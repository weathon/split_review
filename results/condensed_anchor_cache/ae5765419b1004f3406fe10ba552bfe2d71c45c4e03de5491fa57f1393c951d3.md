## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a meta-unlearning framework for diffusion models to prevent malicious fine-tuning from relearning unlearned concepts. It introduces a forward-looking simulation of the fine-tuning process during unlearning, adding a meta-objective that causes related benign concepts to “self-destruct” when fine-tuning is attempted, thereby hindering relearning. The method is evaluated on Stable Diffusion (SD-v1-4, SDXL) for nudity unlearning.

### Strengths
- Addresses a relatively novel and interesting problem: robustness against re-learning of unlearned concepts via fine-tuning.
- The forward perspective (simulating how the model will be fine-tuned and using that to guide unlearning) is well-motivated and sounds novel.
- The proposed solution appears to work well in the evaluated scenarios and is applicable to many models, concepts, and unlearning approaches.
- Extensive experimental results demonstrate effectiveness (noted by one reviewer).
- Related work is described in detail and cites recent advances.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption of known fine-tuning set/process (major).** The method assumes knowledge of the fine-tuning dataset \(D_{FT}\). In practice, malicious fine-tuning could use any set of concept images, making it impossible to directly meta-unlearn against. The paper lacks evaluation on unknown or different fine-tuning processes (e.g., unlearning for one process but fine-tuning on another). This is a key limitation.
- **Problem formulation unclear.** What exactly constitutes “malicious fine-tuning”? If it is simply standard fine-tuning on a subset of \(D_{forget}\), then relearning is somewhat expected, making the problem formulation appear trivial.
- **Concern about destructing benign concepts (major).** The approach deliberately destructs related benign concepts (e.g., “skin” for “nudity”). The goal should be to unlearn the harmful concept while retaining benign knowledge. Effective unlearning should not come at the cost of losing related benign concepts unless absolutely necessary, and the paper does not justify this trade-off convincingly.
- **Lack of ablation for the orthogonal term.** The orthogonal term’s value fluctuates substantially during training. Improvement may come from minimization of the gradient norm rather than the claimed “self-destruct” mechanism. An ablation study isolating the orthogonal term is needed to identify the key factors.
- **Incomplete evidence for claims about existing methods.** The authors claim that existing unlearning methods can re-learn unlearned concepts, but they did not show results for all claimed models (e.g., Wu et al., 2024). Evidence is missing.
- **Evaluation issues.**
  - Table 1: on which images were scores computed? Standard deviations are missing; outliers may appear.
  - Evaluation is done on only one concept setting (nudity).
  - The closeness of generated images to the fine-tune dataset is not discussed.
- **Robustness to adversarial attacks analysis is incomplete.** Comparison is only with RECE (the method the authors build on); other baselines should be included. Additionally, implementation details (type of adversarial attack) are missing from both main paper and appendix.
- **Concept relationship analysis raises questions.** The orthogonal term shows a slight downward trend, suggesting decoupling between the target unlearned concept and the related benign concept. It is unclear whether this is expected or a side effect, and why decoupling occurs if the goal is to destruct the related concept.
- **Technical issue in Algorithm 1.** In lines 6 and 10, gradients should be subtracted (not added) to minimize the objective.
- **Presentation needs improvement.**
  - Figure 1 is confusing and contains a forward reference.
  - Related work lists many citations without adequate explanation; consider focusing on a smaller area (e.g., remove Section 2.2 and expand 2.3).
- **Reviewer disagreement note:** One reviewer was considerably more positive (score 6), praising the novelty and motivation, while the other two (score 3) found the problem formulation weak and the assumptions unrealistic. The weaknesses above reflect concerns from all three.
