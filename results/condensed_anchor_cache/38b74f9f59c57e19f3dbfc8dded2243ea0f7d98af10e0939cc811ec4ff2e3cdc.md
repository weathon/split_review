## Merged Review

### Summary
EduGym is a suite of educational reinforcement learning environments and interactive notebooks, each designed to isolate a specific RL challenge (exploration, partial observability, stochasticity, etc.) and bridge equations with code. The paper reports that 86% of surveyed students and researchers found it useful. Reviewers are sharply split: two (scoring 6) see it as a valuable, well-motivated educational toolkit that addresses a real gap in DRL education, while three (scoring 3) argue it lacks scientific rigor, makes no research contribution, and is ill-suited for ICLR’s main track. The positive reviewers note weaknesses regarding open-sourcing and missing advanced examples; the negative reviewers emphasize the absence of comparative evaluation and novelty relative to existing libraries.

### Strengths
- EduGym addresses a real-world difficulty students face in translating RL equations into code and understanding modern DRL challenges, which textbooks like Sutton & Barto cover only partially for traditional RL and online lectures often treat at a high level.
- The set of environments is well-considered and comprehensive, each isolating a single RL challenge (exploration, partial observability, stochasticity, etc.), and the sequence of presentation is thoughtfully designed for learning.
- The interactive notebooks are detailed, easy to read, and allow hands-on experimentation; they explain each challenge, possible solution approaches, and show experimental performance, helping learners connect theory to practice.
- The codebase is of high quality and provides a quick experimentation loop, making it a good educational resource for beginners and a promising first step toward a community-maintained toolkit for DRL education.
- The motivation is clear, and the paper is easy to follow. In an educational context, EduGym has a positive impact on teaching RL.

### Weaknesses
- The paper is not a research contribution to RL algorithms or methodology; it is essentially a description of an educational tool. Several reviewers consider it unsuitable for ICLR’s main track, suggesting instead the dataset/benchmark track, blog post track, or a venue focused on education.
- There is no controlled comparison or A/B testing against existing educational resources (e.g., OpenAI Spinning Up, Sutton & Barto’s code, or other RL teaching libraries) to demonstrate that EduGym is simpler, easier to learn, or more effective. The reported evaluation relies solely on self-reported survey data, lacking objective measures of learning outcomes or scientific rigor.
- EduGym’s environments are not conceptually novel in RL research; similar environments exist in other libraries, and EduGym does not provide guidance for RL practitioners or best practices for using deep neural networks in RL (e.g., target networks, double Q-networks). A minority of reviewers (those scoring 6) see it as a good first step despite these limitations.
- The background section is not referenced or elaborated in the rest of the paper, making it appear irrelevant.
- The notebooks do not introduce basic RL algorithms (e.g., value iteration, REINFORCE), which would be needed for a complete educational progression.
- The paper does not discuss the pedagogical principles or instructional design models that guided the development of EduGym, nor how the content is structured to support effective learning.
- There is no information about whether the code is open-source, and no plan for community maintenance or expansion to keep pace with evolving RL research (e.g., adding more methods like target networks, double Q-learning). Reviewers ask whether the authors will open-source, invite community contributions, and update the suite in the future.
