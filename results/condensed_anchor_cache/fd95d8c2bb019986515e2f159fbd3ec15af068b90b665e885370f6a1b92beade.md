## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TesseraQ, a post-training quantization (PTQ) method for ultra-low-bit LLM weight quantization (2-4 bits) using block reconstruction. It introduces Progressive Adaptive Rounding (PAR) that iteratively transitions soft rounding variables to hard variables via a sigmoid-based schedule, and Dequantization Scale Tuning (DST) to reduce reconstruction error. TesseraQ is designed as an add-on compatible with existing methods like AWQ and OmniQuant, and shows strong perplexity and downstream accuracy improvements on LLaMA models.

### Strengths
- The idea of optimizing weight rounding error in LLMs is interesting; most prior methods focus on weight clipping or distribution transformation.
- PAR is a well-designed strategy that divides rounding variables into "harden" and "soften" categories and gradually hardens them.
- The methodology is well-motivated: block-wise reconstruction captures inter-layer dependencies better than layer-wise optimization.
- The writing is generally clear and professional, with systematic explanation from basic quantization to advanced optimization; Figure 1 and algorithms are informative.
- Results show substantial improvements over strong baselines: e.g., improving WikiText2 perplexity from 37.37 to 8.05 on LLaMA-2-7B with W2A16, and from 14.65 to 6.82 under AWQ initialization.
- TesseraQ is orthogonal to other PTQ methods (AWQ, OmniQuant), enhancing their performance without adding inference overhead.
- Practical utility is demonstrated through real-world evaluations across hardware configurations, throughput, and memory consumption.
- The method pushes ultra-low-bit quantization, which is significant for deploying LLMs in memory-constrained environments (consumer devices, edge processors).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear writing in Section 3.2 (core methodology):** The description of PAR lacks clarity on how "harden" and "soften" variables are determined and updated; criteria for transitioning variables are not explicitly defined, making practical implementation hard to grasp. Some variables are not explained.
- **Insufficient comparisons with similar rounding methods:** No experiments compare TesseraQ with AdaRound, AdaQuant, SignRound, FlexRound, or other rounding optimization methods. This makes it impossible to isolate the contribution of the proposed progressive adaptive rounding from the block-wise reconstruction.
- **Outdated baseline comparisons:** Table 1 and other tables omit more recent advanced weight-only and weight-activation methods (e.g., SpinQuant, DuQuant, QLLM). The paper claims state-of-the-art but does not convincingly demonstrate superiority over these.
- **Lack of justification for key design choices:**
  - No comparative analysis showing why PAR is better than a well-tuned Straight-Through Estimator (STE) for rounding optimization. The paper does not explore STE’s potential instability or convergence issues.
  - Rationale for progressive freezing instead of a dynamic regularization term (AdaRound) is not clearly articulated; evidence in Lines 075-077 is insufficient.
  - Choice of sigmoid reparameterization in Eq. 9 is not validated experimentally over alternatives (e.g., simply initializing v to 1).
  - The Harden Score metric (Eq. 6) is introduced without clear justification of its effectiveness compared to alternative selection methods.
  - The decision to restrict rounding to ±1 steps (rather than multiple step sizes as in AdaQuant) is not empirically validated.
  - Claims that optimizing the scale parameter s is detrimental (Lines 230-231) lack experimental evidence.
- **Missing theoretical analysis:** No theoretical comparison between (A) block-wise reconstruction with standard rounding and (B) block-wise reconstruction with rounding optimization. It is unclear gains come from better loss landscape exploration or simply better data fitting.
- **Evaluation inconsistencies and incompleteness:**
  - Model selection differs across Tables 1, 2, and 3, making conclusions hard to draw.
  - Runtime and GPU memory analysis is limited to the 7B model; comprehensive efficiency metrics across all model sizes are needed.
  - Incomplete results for newer models (e.g., LLaMA-3) across all experiments.
  - When comparing W4A4/W3A3 using QuaRot initialization, QuaRot alone is not included as a baseline, so the advantage of TesseraQ’s rounding optimization over QuaRot’s rotation alone is unclear.
  - No results for the proposed method without transformation-based initialization (e.g., AWQ or OmniQuant).
- **Concerns about overfitting:** TesseraQ optimizes many parameters (rounding variables) with limited data. A user study or chat test would help validate generalisation.
- **Incorrect/misleading statements:**
  - Line 141 claims GPTQ cannot improve transformation-based methods, but GPTQ can significantly enhance rotation-based transformations like SpinQuant and QuaRot.
- **Minor points:**
  - Line 473 states a significantly larger value than AWQ/OmniQuant, but it is unclear what comparison this signifies since those methods do not optimize rounding variables.
  - The method mentions not relying on regularization loss (like AdaRound) but introduces HS and sorting; no evidence of advantage in computational complexity or convergence speed.
  - Need evaluation of robustness to different PAR schedules (larger t values in the sigmoid schedule, other scheduling functions).
- **Lack of discussion of limitations:** e.g., computational cost of PAR, hyperparameter sensitivity, applicability to different architectures.
