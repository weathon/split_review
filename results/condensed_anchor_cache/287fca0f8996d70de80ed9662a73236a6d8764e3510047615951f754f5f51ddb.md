## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes DRAGO, a continual model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) method that prevents catastrophic forgetting of the world model when an agent faces a sequence of tasks sharing dynamics and state/action spaces but differing in reward functions and initial state distributions. The key challenge is that the agent cannot store data from previous tasks. DRAGO combines two components: (i) *Synthetic Experience Rehearsal*, which learns a separate generative model (VAE) for state-action pairs and uses it to synthesize past experiences for replay, along with a continual training strategy to reduce forgetting of the generative model itself; and (ii) *Regaining Memories Through Exploration*, an intrinsic reward mechanism (Eq. 7) defined by the discrepancy between the current and previous dynamics models to guide the agent to revisit relevant states from prior tasks. DRAGO is built on top of the TDMPC agent. Experiments on three domains (MiniGrid, continuous control benchmarks, and a custom Walker environment) show that DRAGO outperforms vanilla TDMPC (both re-training and continual-training) and an EWC-augmented TDMPC baseline.

### Strengths

- Clear motivation and intuitive explanation of why naive MBRL methods fail, supported by helpful visualizations (Figures 1 and 2).
- The paper is well organized, well written, and easy to follow.
- The problem setting (no access to previously collected data) is interesting, underexplored, and worth discussing.
- Experimental setup is clearly described, including the degree of state-space overlap between tasks.
- Empirical results are promising and demonstrate DRAGO’s advantage over baselines in the studied domains.
- Comprehensive experiments with ablation studies and few-shot transfer provide additional insights into DRAGO’s performance.
- Related work section is detailed (Reviewer 1). (However, some relevant prior art appears to be missing – see Weaknesses.)
- The experiments are reproducible and the results are clearly presented.

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear experimental results and forgetting signals:** In the MiniGrid domain (Figure 3) it is hard to determine when task switching occurs, and the baseline results do not show a clear drop immediately after task switches, making the forgetting phenomenon unconvincing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing baseline: exploration-only method.** DRAGO uses an intrinsic reward for exploration, but the experimental comparison does not include a strong exploration baseline (e.g., RND or curiosity) combined with continual TDMPC. This makes it unclear whether DRAGO’s specific intrinsic reward design and generative replay are superior or if standard exploration is sufficient. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited diversity of environments.** Only three domains are tested, and none involve POMDPs or image-based observations, so the method’s generality is uncertain. (Reviewer 4)
- **Only one MBRL backbone (TDMPC) is used.** It would be beneficial to test DRAGO with other MBRL algorithms such as Dreamer to assess generalization. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential mode collapse in synthetic experience rehearsal.** In the second term of Eq. (5), the dynamics model is trained on synthetic state-action pairs from the generative model, with the target next state provided by an older version of the dynamics model. This could lead to mode collapse, especially when the real data \( \mathcal{D}_i \) covers a very different region from the synthetic data, limiting correction. (Reviewer 2)
- **System complexity and unclear role of the “reviewer” policy.** DRAGO requires an extra set of models (reward model, value model, and policy for the reviewer). The intrinsic reward in Eq. (7) does not inherently need a separate reviewer; if the reviewer shares the same policy/architecture, the added complexity needs better justification. The authors should clarify the proportion of time the learner vs. reviewer policy acts during training/data collection, and whether baselines use the same total number of steps for fair comparison. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Missing ablation: prediction horizon for inference.** An ablation study over different prediction horizons would help understand how well the world model adapts and how long it can maintain accuracy. (Reviewer 1)
- **Inference reward and value models are not continually learned.** The agent is assumed to have access to reward and value models for the current task (relearned at each task boundary), yet the paper focuses on continually learning only the world model. This may explain why baselines perform similarly in continuous control tasks (Figure 5, middle/bottom rows) where there is sufficient state overlap. The authors should discuss the potential advantages and drawbacks of continually learning the reward and value models as well. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Missing related works and contradiction.** Section 5.1 states that no prior work investigates continual learning for MBRL, but Section 5.2 then cites such works. This is contradictory. Additionally, relevant works are omitted: [1] Liu et al. (ICLR 2024) on online world model learning with minimal forgetting, and [2] Huang et al. (ICRA 2021) on continual MBRL with hypernetworks. The similarity of the generative model’s continual training to knowledge distillation is also not discussed. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Baseline selection could be more recent.** While EWC is a reasonable continual learning baseline, more recent approaches exist and should be evaluated. (Reviewer 1)
- **The intrinsic reward (Eq. 7) may penalize visiting states unfamiliar to the previous model, potentially harming final performance.** The authors should explain how this side effect is mitigated and whether it affects results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor writing issues:** Multiple sentences start with “And”; orphan words appear; the last two sentences of the conclusion are repeated. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Unaddressed question about ORSO:** Why cannot ORSO (mentioned in the paper) handle changing dynamics? (Reviewer 1)
