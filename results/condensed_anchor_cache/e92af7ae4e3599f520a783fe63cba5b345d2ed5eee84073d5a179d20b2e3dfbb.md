## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviews are mixed but predominantly positive (scores 5, 8, 8, 6). Several reviewers find the paper well formalized, the adaptation from Neural-LP to DRUM non-trivial, and the theoretical analysis impressive. However, significant concerns are raised about accessibility, generality, motivation, and evaluation focus.

**Key common points:** The paper studies faithfulness (soundness and completeness) of rules extracted from the DRUM model for knowledge graph completion. It shows that existing extracted rules can be unsound/incomplete, proposes a new algorithm ensuring both properties (at the cost of inefficiency), and suggests additional constraints that trade expressive power for tractability.

**Notable disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 2) considers the abstract overpromising and doubts completeness is achieved; this contrasts with the more positive assessments of Reviewers 2/3 (score 8) and Reviewer 4 (score 6). Reviewer 2 (confidence 2) cannot judge technical depth but praises formalism; Reviewer 4 (confidence 3) finds the motivation and presentation lacking.

### Strengths

- **Important problem formalized.** The rule learning problem and the quest for high-quality, faithful rules are critical (Reviewer 4).
- **Well structured and formally presented.** Stringent formalization of Datalog and the DRUM model; the adaptation from Neural‑LP is well motivated and not trivial (Reviewer 2).
- **Detailed theoretical analysis.** Impressive derivation of an algorithm that ensures both soundness and completeness for DRUM (Reviewer 4).
- **Standard evaluation on KG completion.** Results are interesting for this specific task (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 2 notes evaluation on a standard benchmark).
- **Good ideas and experiments overall** (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Abstract overpromises.** The abstract claims applicability to question answering, classification, and recommendation, but the techniques only address KG completion. Furthermore, while soundness may hold, completeness does **not** seem to be assured by the results shown (Reviewer 1, confidence 2). *(Preserving the minority negative view: Reviewer 1 states the paper does not match its claim.)*
- **Unclear applicability beyond KG completion.** It is not obvious how the algorithm would perform for general ML tasks (e.g., classification, recommendation) or whether the guarantees carry over (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Poor accessibility to outsiders.** The paper is not written for an audience outside the immediate Neural‑LP/DRUM community. The formal presentation of DRUM (e.g., path‑counting) is not explained, leaving readers lost. Even after reading, one knows what parts exist but not what they do. The formalization is stringent but the references are sloppy (lower‑case abbreviations, conference names, weird volume numbers, backslashes in URLs) – Reviewer 2.
- **Sufficiency of experimental setup questioned.** Is the heavy machinery necessary only for toy family‑relation examples? Are there no better tasks to showcase the method? (Reviewer 2, question.)
- **Missing citations.** A number of relevant related works appear to be omitted; they should be added and discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation for faithfulness is unclear.** Why is faithfulness an important metric? What concrete benefit does it bring (e.g., better generalizability, legal compliance)? A motivating example would help readers understand (Reviewer 4).
- **Solution is too specific.** The proposed algorithm applies only to the DRUM model, not to general rule‑learning models, limiting its applicability (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation lacks illustrations.** The paper presents a lot of technical content without appropriate visualizations or running examples to intuitively explain notations and equations. Prior works (DRUM, Neural ILP) used such illustrations to convey intuition (Reviewer 4).
- **Evaluation does not assess faithfulness.** Experiments focus only on performance; there is no quantitative evaluation of how many rules from vanilla DRUM violate faithfulness, nor what the negative impact of such rules would be. A case‑study approach is insufficient (Reviewer 4).
