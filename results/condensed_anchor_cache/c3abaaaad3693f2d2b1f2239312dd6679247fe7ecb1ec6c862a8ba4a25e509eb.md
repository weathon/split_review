## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper observes the *attention sink* phenomenon: LLMs allocate disproportionately high attention to initial tokens, even when those tokens are semantically unimportant. Based on this, it proposes **StreamingLLM**, a framework that caches only the most recent tokens and a few initial “sink” tokens, discarding intermediate ones. This enables models trained with a finite-length attention window to generalize to infinite sequence length without any fine-tuning. A placeholder token can be added as a dedicated sink during pre-training to further improve streaming deployment. Experiments show that StreamingLLM works with Llama-2, MPT, Falcon, and Pythia, achieving stable perplexity up to 4 million tokens and outperforming sliding window recomputation by up to 22.2× speedup.

### Strengths
- **Attention sink phenomenon is simple, surprising, and enables a no-training solution** that can be applied to any transformer architecture (R2, R4).
- **Problem is well motivated and timely**, and the paper is clearly written with a logical flow from observation to solution to validation (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Extensive experiments across multiple LLMs (Llama-2, MPT, Falcon, Pythia) and metrics** (perplexity, zero-shot accuracy) convincingly demonstrate effectiveness (R2, R4).
- **Up to 22.2× speedup** is achieved over sliding window with recomputation (R3, R4). The method also works with existing relative positional embeddings (RoPE, ALiBi) with minimal effort (R2).
- **Code and datasets are provided** for reproducibility (R1). Required ablation studies are included (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** Only dense attention, window attention, and sliding window with recomputation are tested. One reviewer (R3) notes that sliding window with appropriate strides (less frequent recomputation) might find a better speed–perplexity trade-off, making the 22.2× claim potentially extreme. No comparisons to other long-context methods (e.g., Sparse Transformers, LongFormer, BigBird, ETC) are made (R2, R3).
- **Context utilization is not adequately evaluated.** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3) question how well the model uses information from evicted intermediate tokens. Reviewer R1 asks how performance decays when querying far-back tokens (e.g., line 1k after 100k lines). Reviewer R3 suggests synthetic tests (e.g., Little Retrieval Test) and long-context benchmarks (e.g., LongBench, MuLD, LRA) to gauge true long-context capability. Reviewer R3 expresses scepticism that a small cache can capture long-context information and doubts the practical usefulness of “infinite sequence length” without such evidence.
- **Theoretical explanation is weak.** The empirical demonstration of attention sink is convincing, but the reasons *why* window attention blows up and *why* very few (≤4) initial tokens suffice are not theoretically grounded (R3). Visualization on short sequences (length 16, Figure 2) may not be representative; quantitative values of attention sink occupancy for longer inputs are missing (R3).
- **Model-specific concerns.** MPT shows a different attention pattern, likely due to ALiBi vs. RoPE, but this is not explained (R3). The pre-training experiment that reduces sink tokens from 4 to 1 uses a 160M-parameter model; it is unclear whether conclusions hold for larger models (R3, R4). Reviewer R4 also asks whether the phenomenon exists in bidirectional models (e.g., BERT), non-transformer models, and very large LLMs (Falcon-40B, LLaMa2-70B), and whether Group Query Attention affects it.
- **Minority reviewer (R3) raises stronger concerns.** While other reviewers acknowledge limitations, R3 questions the core significance: “having an attention sink is a kind of artifact … can we train LLMs not to have it for better performance?” R3 also notes that reducing 4→1 sink tokens is marginal given cache size and asks why a model with a sink token is consistently better, and whether conclusions generalize. Figure 10’s memory-usage discrepancy for 4096 cache size is also flagged.
- **Missing discussion of related work.** Connections to sparse attention patterns (Sparse Transformer) and prior global-token architectures (LongFormer, BigBird, ETC) are not drawn (R2, R3). A concurrent work (LM-Infinite, arxiv 2308.16137) is not compared (R3).
- **Missing societal impact section** (R1).
- **Unanswered reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms.** R4’s questions about combining attention sink with sliding window recomputation, applying it during pre-training, and scaling to very large models remain as missing experiments/comparisons. These are not addressed in the paper.
