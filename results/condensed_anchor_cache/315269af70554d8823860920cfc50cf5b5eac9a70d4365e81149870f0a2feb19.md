## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Label Influence Propagation (LIP) for multi-label node classification on graphs. It decomposes message passing into propagation and transformation operations, analyzes positive and negative label influences, constructs a label influence graph, and dynamically amplifies positive contributions while mitigating negative ones. LIP is plug-and-play with various GNN backbones and shows consistent improvements across datasets. One reviewer found the paper particularly strong (score 8), while three were more reserved (score 6). Their concerns center on missing comparisons, incomplete ablation/efficiency studies, writing clarity, and scope of experimental validation.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a new way to analyze label relationships by examining mutual influences (rather than just correlations), supported by empirical observations in Figure 1.
- It provides theoretical analysis and guarantees for label influence during propagation and transformation operations (reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- LIP is a plug-and-play module that yields consistent performance gains across diverse datasets and GNN backbones (all reviewers).
- The idea of constructing a label influence graph to model positive/negative label interactions is interesting and well-motivated (reviewers 2, 4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (reviewer 3).
- The work considers both encouraging positive and suppressing negative label influences (reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and related work:** The paper does not compare with or cite label propagation methods (e.g., GMNN, “Resurrecting Label Propagation for Graphs with Heterophily and Label Noise”). A dedicated subsection in Related Work discussing decoupled GNNs (APPNP, GBP, etc.) is also missing (reviewers 2, 3).
- **Incomplete ablation and efficiency studies:** 
  - Ablation is only shown on some datasets (Figure 4b); it should be run on all datasets to validate each module’s contribution (reviewer 3).
  - No hyperparameter analysis of α and β (e.g., performance vs. α/β plots, chosen values) (reviewer 2; also reviewer 4 asks about β in eq. 12).
  - The impact of the number of label categories k is not studied (reviewer 2).
  - Efficiency is claimed via time complexity but no actual training time or GPU memory costs are reported (reviewer 3).
- **Writing and clarity issues:**
  - Multiple grammatical errors (e.g., “Illustrate” → “Illustration of” in Fig. 3 caption; line 300 “contain” → “containing”; line 313 “analysis” → “analyze”; line 320 “which can be change” → “which can be changed”) and table captions placed below instead of above (reviewer 4).
  - Critical definition errors: line 301 “negative influence” should be “positive influence”; eq. 6 is inconsistent with the textual explanation of positive influence; Ω_j may need to be ψ_j for consistency (reviewer 4).
  - Theoretical justification in Sec. 4.2 / Appendix A lacks clarity: the connection between feature influence I(i,j), PPR, and the proposed label influence metric is not explicit; the augmented form of A (possibly multi-hop adjacency) is not clearly defined (reviewer 4).
- **Scalability and limitations:** The paper does not discuss how LIP scales with more labels or larger graphs. No limitations of the method are addressed (reviewers 1, 4). Additionally, the decomposition of message passing into propagation/transformation may not hold in all cases, and sensitivity to the initial construction of the label influence graph is unexplored (reviewer 1).
- **Experimental scope:**
  - Only tested on transductive settings; inductive evaluation (more realistic for many applications) is missing (reviewer 4).
  - Performance on heterophilous graphs is observed but not explained, despite the method appearing to assume homophily (reviewer 4).
  - Only a limited set of backbones are used; more advanced decoupled GNNs (APPNP, GBP) and GIN should be tested (reviewers 2, 3).
  - No case-study visualization showing which labels positively/negatively influence others and how that affects predictions (reviewer 2).
- **Minor presentation issue:** Figure 4c x-axis label is missing (reviewer 2).
