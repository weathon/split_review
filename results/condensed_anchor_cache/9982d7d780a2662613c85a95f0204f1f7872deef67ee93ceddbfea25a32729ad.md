## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses class imbalance in deep classification by decoupling the backbone and classifier. It defines a Model Imbalance State (MIS) based on bias in the softmax layer and proposes a Bias Adjustment (BA) method that minimizes KL divergence between the current and an ideal class probability distribution. BA uses a search strategy followed by gradient descent to update only the bias term, keeping the backbone fixed. Evaluations on binary text (SST-2, AG) and image datasets (CIFAR-10 subsampled) are compared with six baselines, including class weighting, two-stage methods, and one SOTA, reporting accuracy, minority-class F1, and G-means. The method is claimed to be computationally efficient (10× faster than the compared SOTA, orders of magnitude faster than weight tuning) and effective under extreme imbalance. Two reviewers gave a score of 6, praising the broad evaluation and efficiency; two gave a score of 3, citing an incomplete central section, missing comparisons with recent long-tailed recognition methods, and concerns about dataset choice and theoretical guarantees.

### Strengths
1. **Intuitive idea**: BA minimizes the difference between the observed model imbalance state (MIS) and an ideal class distribution, making the approach straightforward to understand. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
2. **Handles extreme imbalance**: The method performs well at very high imbalance ratios (e.g., 500:1) where many baselines drop to near-zero performance. (Reviewer 1)
3. **Computational efficiency**: BA is much faster than two-stage methods (reported 10× faster than the SOTA used in the paper) and 2–3 orders of magnitude faster than tuning class-level weights. (Reviewers 1,3)
4. **Simplicity of implementation**: Only the bias term of the classifier is updated; no modification to the backbone or training pipeline is needed. (Reviewers 2,4)
5. **Broad evaluation across data types**: Experiments cover tabular, text (SST-2, AG with BERT), and image datasets (CIFAR-10, ResNet-32), with per-measure comparisons. (Reviewers 2,4)
6. **Solid mathematical rationale**: The method is grounded in a formal definition of model imbalance and a bias-correction objective. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical and notation gaps in Section 3** (Reviewer 3):
   - The statement “$P_i$ records the model prediction probability of label $C_i$” needs a concrete example.
   - Equation (4) claims “$P_i$ can be estimated…” but $P_i$ does not appear in Eq. (4); the text is misleading.
   - The terms $p(C_i|\Phi)$ and $p(x)$ in Eq. (4) are not explicitly defined in that section.
   - Section 3.3 (the search strategy for optimal $r^*$) is too sparse; a figure and more explanation are needed.
   - No theoretical guarantee is given that the searched $r^*$ has bounded error, nor that BA remains effective if $r^*$ is inaccurate.
2. **Missing comparisons with recent long-tailed recognition works** (Reviewer 4):
   - The paper does not compare against methods such as RIDE (ICLR 2021), Balanced-Softmax (NeurIPS 2020), CMO (CVPR 2022), CUDA (ICLR 2023), BCL (CVPR 2022), or NCL (CVPR 2022). This omission is not addressed in the related work.
3. **Dataset choices raise concerns** (Reviewers 1,3,4):
   - Only BERT (text) and ResNet-32 (image) are used; the authors should justify these choices and discuss whether results generalize to other backbones. (Reviewer 1)
   - CIFAR-10 is subsampled into binary subsets, which is not the standard benchmark for multi-class long-tailed recognition. Full CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, or iNaturalist would be more appropriate. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Evaluation metrics are insufficient** (Reviewer 1):
   - Besides minority-class F1, the paper uses overall accuracy and G-means, but does not report precision, recall, or macro-averaged F1, making it unclear whether the model is biased toward any metric or particular class.
5. **Variance in results** (Reviewer 1):
   - Figure 3(b) shows high variance in F1 on CIFAR-10. The authors should explain the possible cause.
6. **Open questions about applicability** (Reviewers 3,4):
   - The paper focuses on binary classification; it is not discussed whether the method extends to multi-class imbalance (reviewer 3) or to imbalanced regression (reviewer 3).
   - At 500:1 imbalance, baseline methods in the paper report zero performance, but prior work (Mirza et al. MMM, 2021) shows non-zero results under similar ratios; the discrepancy is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
7. **Lack of bias-value analysis** (Reviewer 2):
   - The assumption that only the bias term in the softmax layer controls the class imbalance is strong. A study relating bias values to predictive performance should precede the presentation of BA.
