## Merged Review

### Summary
This work studies a Dynamic Incentive and Information Design (DIID) framework for finite-horizon Markov games where a principal learns to maximize her reward (minimize regret) while ensuring that agents’ incentive compatibility (IC) constraints are not too violated (constraint violation). The environment is unknown; the principal recommends actions and agents are assumed to follow them. The paper provides a lower bound on the regret/violation trade-off and proposes an explore‑then‑commit (EC) algorithm with reward‑free exploration that achieves \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{2/3})\) regret and violation. With additional unilateral deviation information (richer feedback), an improved algorithm attains \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1/2})\) for both metrics.

### Strengths
- The setting itself is a key contribution: it is the first work to study online information design (Bayesian persuasion) in Markov games when the environment is completely unknown.
- The DIID framework is interesting and can model many applications.
- The proposed algorithm is straightforward and intuitive in its explore‑then‑commit structure.
- The paper achieves tight regret guarantees in the bandit feedback case and proves tightness with a lower bound.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: Most results appear to follow from adapting previous works (e.g., Bernasconi et al. 2022, “Sequential information design: Learning to persuade in the dark”; Cacciamani et al. 2023, “Online mechanism design for information acquisition”; Bacchiocchi et al. 2024, “Markov persuasion processes: Learning to persuade from scratch”) to Markov games, without introducing genuinely new techniques. The algorithmic approach (explore then commit, UCB for the bandit setting) is standard. Several reviewers noted that the extension from tabular MDPs (Bacchiocchi et al. 2024) to general Markov games does not meet the acceptance bar given community standards.
- **Missing related work**: The paper does not cite Bacchiocchi et al. (2024), which studies a similar setting in tabular MDPs and obtains comparable lower bounds and resolution techniques. This omission weakens the claimed novelty.
- **Upper bounds not tight**: While the trade‑off is optimal, the upper bounds on regret and violation individually are not tight. Only the optimal trade‑off is attained, unlike the tighter characterization in Bernasconi et al. (2022) for different values of \(\alpha\).
- **Model confusion**: The description of the interaction is unclear. The text first says “the principal has the power to take actions on behalf of the agents” and later says “based on the principal’s action and the recommended action, the agents take an action.” It is ambiguous whether agents play an active role or the principal self‑plays. Also, it is not stated whether agents are myopic.
- **Insufficient explanation of algorithmic details**: The reward‑free exploration procedure is a core contribution but is not discussed sufficiently (Reviewer 1). The challenges in applying known techniques (e.g., from Bernasconi et al. 2022) to the MDP setting are not articulated (Reviewer 2).
- **No empirical validation**: The paper provides no experiments or simulations to validate the theoretical results.
- **Presentation issues**: The paper is difficult to follow due to many notations (a notation table is needed). Section 5 should be moved to the appendix and a conclusion/discussion section added. The plot describing the interaction is circular and does not help understanding the sequential nature. There is a missing full stop at line 193, and Eq. (1) contains a suspicious “\(\nu_h - \nu_h\)” term.
- **Missing ablation/analysis**: The lower bound and upper bound are only shown for the trade‑off; it is not clear whether the algorithm can achieve tight guarantees for different values of \(\alpha \neq 2/3\) (Reviewer 3). The tightness of the \(\sqrt{T}\) upper bound in the additional‑feedback setting is not proven; the analysis for multi‑armed bandits may not carry over because different feedback is received at each round (Reviewer 3).
- **Suggestions for improvement**: Reviewers suggest splitting regret and violation bounds and directly bounding the sum of the two metrics (to simplify the presentation), and discussing limitations and future directions.
