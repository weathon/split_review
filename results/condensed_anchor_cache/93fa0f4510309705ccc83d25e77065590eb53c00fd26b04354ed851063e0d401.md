- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 5, 1, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a parameter‑free “relative context” operation (fixed‑coefficient convolution with +1, –1, 0) and uses it in a lightweight speech separation architecture called RCSep (under 500 k parameters). Comparisons are made against Sepformer, TF‑GridNet, MossFormer2, TDANet, and S4M‑tiny on WSJ0‑2mix and WHAM! at 8 kHz. RCSep is not as good as the more complex TF‑GridNet/MossFormer2 but is comparable or better than similar‑sized alternatives. Replacing BLSTMs in TF‑GridNet with the proposed temporal relative context networks (TRCN) yields a slight improvement. Reviewers disagree strongly: one (rating 1) finds the paper extremely poorly written and the core concept unclear, while others (ratings 5–6) acknowledge the efficiency gains but raise concerns about presentation, insufficient evaluation, and unrealistic datasets.

### Strengths

- Very low parameter count ( < 500 k) leading to reduced computation time and memory usage compared to state‑of‑the‑art models (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The fixed‑coefficient convolutional module (weights +1, –1, 0) allows gradients to flow well and can replace any sequential module (e.g., BLSTM). Replacing BLSTMs in TF‑GridNet improves both separation accuracy and efficiency (Reviewer 1).
- The initial experiment (Section 1.2) that investigates the benefit of further separation quality improvements via a downstream ASR task is interesting and motivates the pursuit of lightweight models (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Good results reported for the TF‑GridNet variant with TRCN layers (Reviewer 4).
- The topic of low‑footprint speech separation is relevant and interesting (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

**Presentation, clarity, and technical errors**  
- The paper is extremely poorly written; core concepts are very difficult to follow (Reviewer 3, supported by Reviewer 4).  
- The term “channel size” is confusing: the paper states “single‑channel” input (line 44) but later sets channel size E = 5 and uses it in the explanation of the relative context operation (Reviewer 3).  
- The “relative context operation” is not explained clearly. Figure 2 shows channel size = 5 but does not clearly depict sub‑channels; the relation between number of sub‑channels K and channel size E is ambiguous (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- Notation is introduced on the fly; constants (K, E, M, …) are not defined together early on around Eq. 1, which only gives L and C (Reviewer 4).  
- The shifting operation appears to be a circular shift, but this is not explicitly stated (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- Miniformer architecture details are insufficient: Figure 3 just shows a block; how attention is computed with only three weights is not explained via equations (Reviewer 2).  
- Technical error: line 140 claims phase is the imaginary part of complex STFT while magnitude is the real part, which is incorrect and not supported by Williamson et al. (Reviewer 4).  
- Wrong claim: line 160 states that all methods approach a threshold value (Figure 1) and thus more efficient models are needed; the threshold conclusion is only valid for the less‑realistic two‑speaker‑no‑noise setting (Reviewer 4).

**Experimental evaluation**  
- Tested only on 8 kHz artificially mixed data (WSJ0‑2mix, WHAM!); 16 kHz and more realistic datasets (including reverberation, e.g., LibriCSS) are needed to confirm real‑world translation (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- The initial conclusion that further separation quality improvement is unnecessary is not fully justified because the experiment considered only clean two‑speaker mixtures, ignoring the common presence of background noise (Reviewer 4).  
- Ablation studies are insufficient – missing:  
  - Influence of parameter K on TCRN performance (different K values) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Comparison with K=1, K=10, …, K=E (Reviewer 4).  
  - Analysis of the effect of using a convolutional interpretation (forced zeros, dilation) (Reviewer 4).  
- No flops or device specification provided for the time/memory measurements in Figures 4 and 5 (Reviewer 1).  
- SI‑SDR loss version (scaled estimate as in TF‑GridNet or not?) is not stated (Reviewer 1).

**Missing details and explanations**  
- Why is the method called “relative context”? It appears to be an alternative implementation of a Conv operator with some weights fixed to zero (Reviewer 4).  
- For K = E (subchannels of size 1), why would that be a problem? (Reviewer 4).  
- No discussion of why the method works well with so few parameters; the explanation that “the network places relevant information into shifted channels” is not justified – the network does not control the receptive field (Reviewer 4).  
- It is unclear how shifting groups of channels can be computationally efficient compared to highly optimized convolutions (Reviewer 4).  
- Architecture of TF‑GridNet with TCRN should be included for clarity (Reviewer 2).  
- For the time/frequency sub‑modules, the specific time‑delays used for each of the 7 or 3 sub‑channels should be stated explicitly (Reviewer 1).  
- The sequence length reduction description (Section 4.1.1) says “halved” but technically it is M / 2 reduction; since M = 4 it is halved, but M is not yet introduced at that point (Reviewer 1).  
- Section 4.1.3: channel size should be C · E_T, not M · E_T, and then further reduction to C · M is needed (Reviewer 1).
