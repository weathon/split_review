## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UQ-Merge, an uncertainty-guided algorithm for merging multimodal large language models (MLLMs) fine-tuned from LLaVA-1.5. It uses perturbation-based uncertainty on both text and vision inputs (without requiring labels) to select beneficial candidate models, determine merge order, and perform iterative merging. Experiments on 12 held-in and held-out vision-language benchmarks claim up to 44.3% average accuracy improvement over existing merging methods.

### Strengths
- Novel concept of using uncertainty quantification to guide MLLM merging, addressing overconfidence and vision-language specificity. (R1, R3, R4)
- Instance-specific merging method that is intuitive and can inspire similar approaches. (R2)
- Extensive experiments with many baselines and tasks; clear experimental sections with stated research questions and answers. (R2)
- Clear writing and well-organized presentation with effective figures and tables. (R1)
- Avoids need for labeled samples in uncertainty quantification, increasing practical applicability. (R3)
- Section 4.4.1 demonstrates that selecting based on uncertainty yields better results than relying on superficial performance metrics. (R4)
- Model merging for MLLMs is underexplored, making this work promising. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baseline**: No single model trained on the combined dataset is provided; if such a model performs well, the merging approach may be unnecessary. (R1)
- **Limited model scope**: Experiments only on LLaVA-1.5, not on Intern-VL, Qwen2-VL, MiniCPM-V, or other MLLMs. Merging is restricted to the same architecture fine-tuned on different datasets; cross-architecture merging and cross-family uncertainty quantification are not explored. (R1)
- **Computational cost**: The method is time-intensive due to instance-specific uncertainty calculation per input, multiple perturbations per step, and iterative merging. Discussion on time complexity and inference computations is needed. (R2, R4)
- **Insufficient justification of the heuristic**: The claim that merging models with lower uncertainty excludes overconfident models lacks quantitative support. If all candidate models are overconfident and wrong, uncertainty-based selection may still produce an overconfident merged model. This critical view (R2) contrasts with the more positive assessment (R4) that the method is intuitive and experiments demonstrate effectiveness.
- **Experimental concerns**: The best merged model significantly underperforms LLaVA-1.5 on some tasks (e.g., VQAv2: 71.77 vs. 78.5). Some results fall within the average ± standard deviation of baseline numbers, showing no clear improvement. (R2)
- **Missing oracle numbers**: The upper bound (oracle merging) per task is not reported. (R2, question)
- **Scalability for real-world use**: MLLM usage is often not task-specific, and measuring uncertainty on refined datasets may not transfer to unseen datasets. Discussion on application with limited or no test sets is absent. (R4)
- **Insufficient analysis of perturbation strategies**: Section 4.4.3 claims vision-language perturbation is crucial but does not compare different perturbation methods or alternative uncertainty measures (e.g., gradient-based, Monte Carlo dropout, temperature scaling). (R4, also R3)
- **Missing error metrics**: Table 1 does not report error metrics (e.g., error bars, confidence intervals). (R3)
- **Unclear multi-modal motivation**: The main multi-modal contribution is perturbation on images, but in ScienceQA the visual modality may be unnecessary; perturbing images might compromise visual information validity. It is unclear why the approach is specific to MLLMs rather than applicable to broader LLM tasks. (R3)
- **Prior work comparison missing**: Reference [1] introduces a fusion technique based on gradient-related uncertainty; a detailed comparison with this approach is not provided. (R3)
- **Reference [2] concern**: Some collected knowledge may lack essential commonsense elements, potentially affecting the validity of the uncertainty-guided selection. (R3)
- **Calibration assumption**: The method appears to assume candidate models are poorly calibrated. Would it still be effective if all models were well-calibrated? No discussion. (R2, question)
