## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes LiDAR-PTQ, a post-training quantization method for 3D LiDAR-based object detection. It introduces three components: a sparsity-based calibration for initializing quantization parameters, a Task-guided Global Positive Loss (TGPL) to align final predictions before and after quantization, and an adaptive rounding-to-nearest operation for layerwise reconstruction. Evaluated on the CenterPoint detector (Pillar and Voxel variants) largely on Waymo (LEVEL_2 metrics) and claimed on NuScenes, the INT8 model achieves accuracy close to FP32 with a 3× inference speedup on an Jetson AGX Orin edge device. The method is cost‑effective (30× faster than quantization-aware training). The paper includes a qualitative analysis of why direct 2D PTQ fails on 3D LiDAR data.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow, with a clear introduction to neural network quantization.
- It addresses an important and timely problem: compressing 3D LiDAR detectors for edge deployment, where little prior work exists.
- The analysis of why 2D PTQ methods degrade on 3D LiDAR tasks is reasonable, and the proposed solution is practical.
- LiDAR-PTQ achieves competitive INT8 detection performance on CenterPoint (both Pillar and Voxel) that is nearly identical to the FP32 model, while delivering a 3× inference speedup.
- The method is fast and practical for real‑world use.
- The qualitative explanation of performance degradation causes is a useful contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Narrow evaluation scope**: The method is tested only on the CenterPoint detector on Waymo; generalizability to other 3D LiDAR detectors (e.g., Transformer‑based) or other applications (e.g., segmentation) is not shown. Moreover, results on the NuScenes dataset are claimed but not presented in the paper.
- **Limited novelty**:
  - The “sparsity‑based calibration” is simply max‑min calibration without unique designs. The justification that max‑min avoids the influence of sparse data is not sufficiently strong, as it discards distributional information of non‑zero values. No exploration of alternative calibration techniques designed for sparse data (e.g., percentile clipping, adaptive ranges) is provided.
  - The TGPL is a trivial technique that selects high‑confidence predictions, widely adopted in distillation. The paper lacks analysis of the selection criteria, thresholds, or number of selected predictions, and no ablation studies are shown to justify its design. Furthermore, Reviewer 3 notes that the gains from TGPL and adaptive rounding are small compared to grid search, and more experiments are needed to demonstrate their efficacy.
- **Missing details and comparisons**:
  - Insufficient detail about optimization steps: which parameters are tuned, when, and what percentage of training data is required for PTQ.
  - TGPL minimizes the distance between quantized and non‑quantized outputs, which appears to require labeled data, deviating from the PTQ assumption of using only a small unlabeled calibration set. This also raises the question of whether the method is equivalent to knowledge distillation from the FP32 model to the INT8 model.
  - No comparison of inference acceleration performance with other baselines (e.g., in Table 3). Accuracy together with speed would better highlight advantages.
  - No code release for independent validation.
- **Inaccurate claims**: The statement “point cloud coordinates have explicitly encoded the shapes and geometries of objects, so the larger arithmetic range preserves essential geometric information” is misleading because LiDAR detectors typically use relative coordinates within voxels, not absolute coordinates. Thus the argument about preserving a large range is inaccurate.
- **Applicability to emerging methods**: For fully sparse detectors (e.g., [1][2][3]) that do not produce zero feature maps, the sparsity‑based calibration offers no advantage, further limiting the method’s scope.
- **Unanswered methodological questions** (overlap with missing experiments/analysis):
  - How is γ set for TGPL?
  - Why is LEVEL_2 chosen for Waymo results?
  - What is LEVEL_2 mAPH in Table 1? (should be clarified closer to the table)
- **Minor presentation issues**: typos – “Sparisity” (should be “sparsity”), equation reference (Eq 11 should be Eq 10), “NuSecens” (should be “NuScenes”).
