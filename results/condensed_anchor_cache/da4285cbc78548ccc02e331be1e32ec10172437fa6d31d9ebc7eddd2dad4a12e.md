## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces VAE-Var, a neural data assimilation method that combines a variational autoencoder with traditional variational DA to capture non-Gaussian background error distributions and assimilate real-world irregular observations. Using FengWu as the forecast model, ERA5 reanalysis for training, and both simulated and real GDAS prepbufr observations, VAE-Var consistently outperforms DiffDA, 3DVar, and interpolation in sparse settings and demonstrates year-long stability. The method learns a latent representation of background error fields and solves the variational problem in latent space, reducing dimension. Reviewers agree the paper is clearly written and tackles an important problem, but disagree on novelty (some find it well-known) and on evaluation rigor (ERA5-as-ground-truth, missing operational baselines). A minority reviewer (score 5) considers the lack of 4DVar comparison and missing computational cost discussion as critical omissions.

### Strengths
- Paper is well-written, communicates goals clearly, and explains limitations honestly (e.g., supplementary section A.2) [R1, R2, R4, R6].
- Justification of the independence assumption between background error and background state (Section 3.1) and the non-Gaussian error distribution assumption is well-supported [R1].
- Training set construction and loss function are clearly formulated [R1].
- VAE-Var naturally handles partial and irregular observation operators, giving it an advantage over supervised ML surrogates and DiffDA [R3, R6].
- Consistently outperforms DiffDA and 3DVar in accuracy on both simulated sparse observations and real GDAS observations over one year [R2, R3, R5].
- Original use of a VAE to relax the Gaussian background error assumption addresses a major limitation in traditional variational DA [R4].
- Real-world data experiment moves the work beyond proof-of-concept [R4, R6].
- Cyclic forecasting-and-assimilation setup using a learned forecast model (FengWu) is appealing for operational weather prediction without traditional NWP models [R6].
- Variational problem is formulated in latent space, which can reduce the high dimension of the DA problem—a key practical advantage [R6].
- Scalability demonstrated on high-dimensional operational weather data [R6].

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The combination of (variational) autoencoders with variational DA is already well established (>10 prior works), e.g., Peyron et al. 2021 (QJRMets), Melinc & Zaplotnik 2024 (QJRMets), Cheng et al. 2023 (J. Sci. Comput.), Mohd Razak et al. 2022 (SPE J.), Zhong et al. 2023 (NHESS). [R3] This contrasts with the positive assessment of novelty by other reviewers.

2. **Problematic use of ERA5 as ground truth**: ERA5 is a reanalysis product with known biases (terrain effects, missing coverage, equatorial mesospheric jet, near-surface wind/temperature/humidity mismatches, precipitation/temperature biases in N. America). Evaluating only on ERA5 does not reflect true state errors. Standard non-ML DA papers validate by withholding real observations and computing departures (e.g., [4] MWR-D-22-0260, [5] QJRMets). This is a common flaw in AI-for-weather research. [R4]

3. **Missing comparisons to operational/state-of-the-art baselines**:
   - No comparison to 4DVar, the operational standard in NWP, which enforces temporal dependencies via a physical model and is known to outperform 3DVar. Even a toy-system comparison to 4DVar and its learning-based counterparts (diffusion models [4], end-to-end approaches [5,6]) is necessary. [R6]
   - No comparison to ensemble-based DA (EnKF), which is known to handle chaotic dynamics well. [R3]
   - Why does 3DVar outperform DiffDA in some experiments (e.g., Fig. 3)? If DiffDA captures non-Gaussian statistics, it should be better—this raises fairness concerns. [R6]
   - Configuration details for DiffDA and 3DVar (U_p, S, U_v, U_h) are missing; it is unclear whether baselines were properly tuned. [R4]

4. **Objective function simplification weakens non-Gaussian claim**: The determinant term in Eq. (8) (involving log det of the decoder Jacobian) is omitted because it makes optimization intractable, and a scaling factor is introduced instead. This ad‑hoc fix is not mathematically rigorous and means the method does not fully capture the non‑Gaussian statistics it claims to. [R1, R4] The reference given for the omitted term (Giaquinta & Modica 2012) does not contain Chapter 2.5.3 or Equation (7). [R1, R4]

5. **Performance does not improve with more observations**: VAE-Var shows minimal or no accuracy gain as the number of observations increases (Figs. 3, 4, 8–13), whereas 3DVar and DiffDA improve. This is unusual for DA and suggests possible saturation or model capacity limits. An ablation study or discussion is needed. [R4] Relatedly, in supplementary Fig. 8 (mslp RMSE with 8000 observations), 3DVar sometimes outperforms VAE-Var, though VAE-Var is better most of the time with 1000 observations. [R1]

6. **Lack of reproducibility**: Code is not provided; details missing for observation operator construction (especially for real obs), 3DVar background‑error parameters, and DiffDA configuration. [R4]

7. **Method-specific retraining**: VAE-Var is trained for a specific forecast model (FengWu). Any change or retraining of the forecast model requires retraining VAE-Var, limiting generalizability. [R5] (Noted as minor given performance.)

8. **No discussion of computational cost**: Time/memory comparisons between VAE-Var, 3DVar, and DiffDA are absent despite being a primary motivation for deep‑learning DA. [R6]

9. **Gaussian assumption for observation error** *p*(**y**|**x**): Stating that Gaussian observation error is a “well-defined characteristic” is misleading. Observation errors are often non‑Gaussian (e.g., AIRS brightness temperatures), which can cause substantial biases (Pires et al. 2010, Physica D). The paper should frame this as an accepted approximation. [R4]

10. **Ambiguities and clarity issues**:
    - The right part of Equation (2) is unclear; more details or a reference are needed. [R1]
    - Why is the observation error covariance **R** assumed diagonal? [R1]
    - Why are ERA5 data from 1979–2015 used? [R1]
    - Line 243–244: dimensionality confusion—Chen et al. use 189 × 721 × 1440; what does “69” correspond to in this study? [R1]
    - Line 262–263: claim all components of L are differentiable; why is the observation operator *H* differentiable? [R1]
    - Figure 1 and Algorithm 1: is τ the number of time steps or the time gap? [R4]
    - Lower half of Figure 1 vs Algorithm 2: how is the hidden state initialized (zero or a random field)? [R4]
    - Algorithm 3: model time step *T* is not defined. [R4]
    - Last term in Equation (8) should be ½ log det[ (*∂D*(**z**)/*∂z*)ᵀ (*∂D*(**z**)/*∂z*) ]. [R4]
    - Line 261: rescaling of the background term is unclear. [R6]
    - The baseline name “interpolation” is confusing because the term has a specific meaning in DA. [R6]

11. **Other unresolved questions**:
    - During generation, why are forecasts integrated 0 → 2t and t → 2t instead of 0 → t vs. ground truth at t? [R5]
    - What is the speed of VAE‑Var assimilation relative to other methods? [R5]
    - If the initial state is very ill‑estimated, can VAE‑Var still produce reasonable analyses, or does it require the state to be within a stability region? [R5]
    - Role of the parameter λ; why λ = 4? [R3]
    - Fairness of comparing VAE and diffusion models: if the true error distribution is not complex and data are limited, diffusion models may be expected to underperform. [R2]
    - Uncertainty quantification and the ability to sample multiple reconstructions from the VAE latent space (relevant to chaotic dynamics) are not discussed. [R6]
