## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UStereo, a real-time stereo matching network that introduces a "general inductive bias" (scale invariance and long-range dependencies) alongside a "specific inductive bias" from the cost volume. Two main modules are presented: Dense Scale-aware Fusion (DSF) for volume-level scale invariance and Mixed Direct Long Range Compensation (MDLC) for long-range dependencies without Transformers. Additionally, the authors study the low-resolution volume representation and propose three statistical indicators to control disparity distribution. Experiments are conducted on KITTI 2012/2015, virtual datasets, and the SCARED abdominal scene dataset. All five reviewers note serious writing and clarity issues. Reviewer 2 (score 1, confidence 4) is the most critical, citing severe readability problems and missing benchmark verification. The other four reviewers (scores 3) also identify major weaknesses but see potential contributions.

### Strengths
- A new stereo matching network, UStereo, designed for real-time inference with competitive accuracy (Reviewers 1–5).
- The concept of Dense Scale-aware Fusion (DSF) is interesting, though its relation to traditional scale management is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of capturing long-range dependencies without Transformers is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- UStereo has a small number of parameters and fast computation, partly due to skipping intermediate resolutions (Reviewers 1, 4).
- An in-depth study of the low-resolution volume structure and its role as a confidence volume is provided, along with three original statistical indicators (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- The model shows generalization across virtual/real datasets and diverse natural scenes, including the SCARED small abdominal cavity dataset (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Competitive results compared to other volume-based fast stereo methods (Reviewers 1, 2).

### Weaknesses
**Writing, clarity, motivation**
- The paper is very hard to read due to poor writing quality and lack of clarity; many sentences make little sense (e.g., p.6: “The learning process would make internal relationships vague unlike Eq.9”) (Reviewers 1–5). Reviewer 2 and 4 rate Presentation as 1 (poor) or 2 (fair); Reviewer 5 rates Presentation as 1.
- The framing around “inductive bias” is confusing; it is unclear why IB is introduced instead of simply discussing scale invariance and long-range dependencies (Reviewers 3, 5).
- The motivation for the problem is unclear: there is no experimental demonstration that current methods are insufficient due to lack of inductive bias (Reviewer 4).
- Figures 1, 2, and 3 are not clear enough to understand the architecture (Reviewer 2).
- Suggestions from Reviewer 3 include: review the literature on deep learning for stereo matching, explain how the chosen IB has not been seen, note the use of attention mechanisms for long-range dependencies, better introduce the model, showcase results, and drop the real-time aspect.

**Ablation study and module justification**
- The ablation study is poorly written and does not clearly show how DSF and MDLC improve performance (Reviewer 2, 4).
- Many architectural decisions are not justified; e.g., using Top-5 for disparity regression (Reviewer 2).
- In the MDLC ablation, replacing softmax with Sigmoid is unclear; it would be better to simply remove the MDLC module (Reviewer 4).
- The role of deep supervision (Fig. 4, many losses) is not clearly defined; a specific ablation study is needed (Reviewer 2, 4).
- The two additional statistics indicators (δ 50%top, δ 50%mean) are not well justified; the provided pseudocode is not useful (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of deep supervision on the low-resolution cost volume is not new (see CFNet, CVPR 2021) and its benefit over prior work is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- The performance improvement appears incremental, and more exhaustive comparisons with various layer fusion methods and global/local attention methods are required (Reviewer 4).

**Results and evaluation**
- Claim of robust generalization is not well justified; the model is finetuned on the target dataset, unlike true cross-dataset generalization evaluations (e.g., CVPR 2023 paper [A] cited by Reviewer 4).
- Generalization evaluation on common settings (KITTI, Middlebury, ETH3D without finetuning) is missing (Reviewer 5).
- Insufficient evaluation overall: the paper does not convincingly demonstrate superiority in accuracy, running time, and memory (Reviewer 1).
- Table 1 results on KITTI 2012/2015 are not available on the online leaderboard; this must be fixed for credibility (Reviewer 2).
- Several cited results in Table 1 are incorrect or outdated: BGNet+ KITTI 2015 D1 is 2.19, not 2.91; CoEx and FastACV have better online results than reported (Reviewer 2).
- Among real-time methods, CGI-Stereo, HITNet, and Fast-ACVNet+ achieve better performance on KITTI (Reviewer 5).
- Table 4 is hard to understand, including the coding (32L, 8L, 32F, etc.) (Reviewer 2).

**Technical and methodological issues**
- Equation 12 suggests minimizing the maximum of a softmax (a probability); justification is needed (Reviewer 2).
- The term “scale-invariance” may be inappropriate; “scale-equivariance” might be more accurate because disparity varies with scale (Reviewer 2).
- The specific inductive bias in volume representation: using cost volume as confidence has been explored in Multi-View Stereo; the authors should clarify if any additional specific IB is proposed beyond that (Reviewer 5).
- The baseline network and its performance are not stated (Reviewer 4).

**Spelling and formatting**
- Many spelling errors (e.g., p.1 “invarience”, “for almost vision tasks”, p.2 “knowledgement”, p.3 “wildly” instead of “widely”, p.5 “our contemplation”) (Reviewer 2, 3).
