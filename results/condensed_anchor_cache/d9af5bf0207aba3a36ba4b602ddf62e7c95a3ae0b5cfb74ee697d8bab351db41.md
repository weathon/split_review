## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MC-MoE, a training-free compression framework for MoE-LLMs combining Pre-Loading Mixed-Precision Quantization (PMQ) and Online Dynamic Pruning (ODP). Reviewers generally agree that the work addresses an important, albeit under-explored, area and provides extensive empirical validation across benchmarks and model sizes. However, opinions diverge on novelty and practical impact: two reviewers rate it highly (8), one moderately (6), and one critically (5). The minority reviewer raises fundamental concerns about the motivation and system-level benefits of applying these techniques to MoE models, while other reviewers note missing baselines, limited task variety, insufficient ablation, and lack of efficiency metrics.

### Strengths
- MoE efficiency is relatively under-explored; this work is a welcome addition to the area (R1).
- PMQ delivers decent task performance, notably outperforming BSP in Table 2 (R1).
- Pre-loading is an intuitive and effective method for reducing expert loading overhead (R2).
- The method leverages uneven features learned by different expert heads to guide quantization using integer programming, with valid expert significance analysis (R2).
- Token relevance extracted from attention heat maps provides salient pruning instructions without requiring external clues (R2).
- PMQ innovatively considers multiple factors (activation reconstruction error, routing scores, and activated frequencies) in bit-width allocation (R3).
- The framework offers a comprehensive solution that addresses both static model compression and dynamic inference optimization (R3).
- Extensive empirical validation across multiple benchmarks and model sizes demonstrates robustness and scalability (R3; R4 also acknowledges the breadth of experiments verifying accuracy drop).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty disagreement:** One reviewer finds novelty limited since both mixed-precision quantization and token-dependent expert pruning are well-explored avenues for efficient MoE inference (R1). Another reviewer highlights the novelty of using multiple factors and attention-based token relevance (R2).
- **Missing baselines for ODP:** BSP is the only relevant comparison for PMQ; no pruning baselines are provided for ODP (R1). A reviewer also requests comparing other popular pruning methods in the second stage to demonstrate that weight-only pruning is sufficient (R2).
- **Limited evaluation tasks:** Most datasets in Table 2 are common-sense intelligence tasks; more challenging tasks such as GSM8k, HumanEval, and LongBench should be evaluated (R1). Additionally, a reviewer asks for a comprehensive error analysis to identify which types of tasks or linguistic phenomena are most affected by compression (R3).
- **Unclear efficiency metrics:** The speedup metric in Table 4 is not defined (latency or throughput) and does not correspond to the featured task; input and output lengths are missing. Comprehensive reports on latency, throughput across tasks/compression rates/batch sizes, and memory are needed (R1, R4).
- **Lack of ablation:** No ablation studies are provided for key hyperparameters such as the μ threshold and protection ratio (R3).
- **Compounding quantization errors:** The paper does not address potential compounding effects of quantization errors across multiple MoE layers, especially in deeper networks (R3).
- **ODP computational overhead:** The overhead of token importance calculation is not thoroughly analyzed, which could be significant for real-time applications (R3).
- **Connection between PMQ and ODP:** The two components appear standalone; their interplay is not discussed (R1 – originally a question, now a weakness).
- **Robustness and generalizability:** Robustness under different input sequence lengths is not analyzed; generalizability to other MoE architectures with different routing mechanisms is not discussed (R3).
- **Performance on specific tasks:** PMQ shows relatively meager performance on ARC-c and MMLU; an in-depth analysis of challenges particular to those tasks is requested (R2).
- **Motivation and practical concerns (minority reviewer, lower score):**
  - The target platform (cloud or edge) is unclear; mixed precision may worsen expert load balance, especially when the number of experts is large.
  - Weight-only quantization and dynamic pruning introduce irregular computation patterns, causing load imbalance and lower GPU utility; these methods perform worse with larger batch sizes, yet large batches are essential for SMoE performance.
  - The motivation for using MoE in resource-constrained environments is questioned: e.g., Mixtral 8×7B is twice as slow as Llama-2-13B and much larger, offering no clear advantage without strong system support.
  - Accuracy comparisons between models trained on different datasets (e.g., Llama-2-13B vs. LLaMA-3.1-8B) are not meaningful in a compression study.
  - The single-GPU serving implementation (e.g., whether a for-loop iterates over tokens for the same expert) is not clarified (R4).
- **Minor clarity issues:** The “LM-Eval” column in Table 4 is not defined (R1).
