## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies 11 ranking loss functions (pointwise, pairwise, listwise, weighted) for training performance predictors across 5 NAS search spaces and 13 tasks. It evaluates them on multiple metrics (Kendall Tau, Weighted Kendall Tau, N@K, Rel@K) and tests two predictor-based NAS frameworks. The authors find that loss choice significantly influences predictor performance and that top-centered rank metrics correlate better with final search quality than traditional metrics.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive comparison**: 11 ranking loss functions covering pointwise, pairwise, listwise, and weighted categories across 5 search spaces and 13 NAS tasks (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Clear writing and structure**: Paper is easy to read and well-organized (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Detailed metrics**: Uses global (Kendall Tau), rank-weighted (Weighted Kendall Tau), and Top‑K metrics (N@K, Rel@K) for a thorough assessment (Reviewer 4).
- **Some weighted losses show promising results** in the NAS frameworks (Reviewer 2).
- **Extensive experiments and analysis** with fair comparisons among loss function types (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and impact**
- The work is incremental: adding a weighted term to existing ranking losses yields small improvements, and ranking losses are not widely adopted in NAS due to limited consistent gains (Reviewers 1, 2). One reviewer (3) states the paper makes no new contributions in methods, evaluation, or conclusions.
- The two main insights are not novel or persuasive: (i) the importance of ranking loss in predictors is already widely recognized; (ii) that top-centered metrics help find high-quality architectures is straightforward and lacks theoretical support (Reviewer 3). A stronger contribution would be, e.g., guidelines for choosing a loss based on model type or dataset properties, or mathematical principles behind loss design (Reviewer 3).

**Presentation and reproducibility**
- **Missing mathematical definitions**: Formal equations for loss functions (e.g., WARP weight calculation) and metrics (Weighted Kendall Tau, Rel@K, N@K) are absent, even in the appendix. Textual descriptions are confusing, harming reproducibility (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **N@K metric interpretation**: It is not clearly stated that N@K is “lower is better” (only in Figure 4 caption); the scale and context (rank among which architectures) are unexplained (Reviewer 1).
- **Confusing figures**: 
  - Figure 1 uses color to highlight poor losses rather than best ones, making it hard to see which loss is superior on each dataset (Reviewer 1).
  - Figures 2 and 4 show winning loss in grids of train/test portions—captions are not informative, and trends are nearly impossible to discern (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - Figure 2 legend obstructs the x-axis label; caption should read “under different training and test portions” (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation**: Instances of “@K” should be “@$K$” for consistency (Reviewer 4). Figure 1 should highlight best results (e.g., with superscripts) (Reviewer 4).

**Experimental design concerns**
- **Hyperparameter fairness**: All losses are compared with the same learning rate and weight decay, which is unfair because pairwise losses typically need lower `lr`/`wd` and listwise losses higher `lr`. A sensitivity analysis across a wider range of hyperparameters is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Single predictor architecture**: Only a 4‑layer GCN encoder + 3‑layer MLP is tested. Results should be verified on additional predictor types (e.g., MLP-only, transformer) (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing metric**: Important Top‑K ranking metrics from recommender systems (NDCG, NDCG@K) are not considered. NDCG can be adapted to NAS by sorting by predicted performance and using DCG = Σ(2^{y_i}−1)/log₂(i+1) (Reviewer 4).

**Analysis gaps**
- **Inconsistent loss performance**: e.g., ListNet achieves top-1 Kendall Tau on NAS-Bench-101 but lowest on TransNAS-Bench101-Micro Autoencoder. No explanation or deeper discussion of why this happens, e.g., relation to dataset or task type (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Lack of selection criteria**: After the experiments, the paper should summarize which loss type to choose for a given dataset size, NAS task, or training portion (Reviewer 4).
- **Support for insights**: Insight (ii) does not show how it guides future NAS method or loss function design; an explanation from the mathematical essence of different losses is missing (Reviewer 3).
