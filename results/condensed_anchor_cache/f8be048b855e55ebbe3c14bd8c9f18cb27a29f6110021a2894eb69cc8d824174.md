## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GuideCO, a two-stage generate-then-decode diffusion solver for combinatorial optimization (CO) that can be trained on imperfectly labeled data. It uses objective-guided diffusion with classifier-free guidance to improve solution quality. Experiments on TSP (Traveling Salesman Problem) and MIS (Maximum Independent Set) show improvements over DIFUSCO when training data is heuristic-labeled. The paper also observes an exponential scaling law between optimality gap and required training data.

### Strengths
- The exponential scaling law relating optimality gap and data quantity is an interesting observation (Reviewers 1,4).
- The idea of introducing objective guidance through conditional training of the diffusion model is intuitively appealing (Reviewer 2).
- Evaluation on two distinct and challenging CO problems: TSP and MIS (Reviewer 4).
- Framework addresses the practically relevant problem of training with low-quality labels and outperforms DIFUSCO in that scenario (Reviewer 3).
- Paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2,4).
- Employs an "Objective-Aware Graph-Based Denoiser" architecture (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty.** The generate-then-decode pipeline (heatmap generation + decoding) is standard in non-autoregressive neural solvers such as DIMES, DIFUSCO, and T2T (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The objective-guided training is similar to plug-and-play priors for diffusion models (Reviewer 1) and has been explored for conditional diffusion in decision-making and black-box optimization (Reviewer 2). Overall contribution is incremental.
2. **Empirical performance is weak and leaves large gaps.** 
   - On small instances where exact labels are cheap, DIFUSCO+exact still outperforms GuideCO given enough data (Reviewer 1).
   - On larger instances (500–1000 nodes) with LKH-3 labels, GuideCO still lags behind DIFUSCO; the paper does not show superiority when optimal labeling is truly impractical (e.g., TSP­≥10k) (Reviewers 1,3).
   - On TSP-50 the optimality gap is >10%; on TSP-1000 the gap is 37%, raising questions about practical meaning (Reviewer 3).
   - No evidence is provided that the method improves over current state-of-the-art diffusion-based CO solvers (e.g., T2T, consistency models) (Reviewers 2,4).
3. **Missing baselines and comparisons.** Several relevant baselines are absent, including DIMES, T2T, LwD, OR‑Tools, physics-inspired GNNs, dataless neural networks, and Gurobi for ER graphs (Reviewer 4). Out-of-distribution generalization (as in DIFUSCO Table 5) is not evaluated (Reviewer 4). Inference runtime is not reported for TSP (Reviewers 3,4).
4. **Scalability is insufficiently demonstrated.** Experiments only go up to 1000 nodes; no evaluation on larger instances (e.g., Santa Claus TSP, large MIS graphs from LwD) (Reviewers 1,4). The method’s scalability for real-world problems (≥10k nodes) is not shown (Reviewer 1).
5. **Questionable motivation regarding optimal labels.** The claim that DIFUSCO is trained with optimally labeled data is inaccurate; DIFUSCO uses KaMIS (a heuristic) to label data, the same setting as GuideCO with a different heuristic (Reviewer 4). This undermines the core premise.
6. **Weak connection between bi-level optimization formulation and the actual framework.** The approximation of the lower-level problem is not well justified; the lower-level essentially just ensures constraint satisfaction (Reviewer 4).
7. **Decode process needs elaboration.** The greedy decoding choice is not justified; MCTS often works better for TSP in other works (Reviewer 3). The influence of the diffusion model’s output (or its logits) vs. a purely heuristic decoding is not ablated (Reviewer 4).
8. **Missing ablations and sensitivity analysis.** 
   - No results for GuideCO with γ=0 in the MIS table (Reviewer 4).
   - Sensitivity of DM training to the parameter p is not reported (Reviewer 4).
   - It would be informative to test whether GuideCO can further improve performance when trained on exact labels (Reviewer 1).
9. **Presentation issues.** Typos exist (lines 227, 212, 181, 248); variable I is undefined in Eq. (1); missing transpose in Eq. (5); the symbol ⊙ is not defined until later; citations lack hyperlinks and sometimes point to incorrect references (Reviewers 1,3,4).
10. **Unaddressed high-level concerns.** 
    - If GuideCO’s combined method still cannot outperform problem-specific heuristics (KaMIS, Concorde), the benefit of using it is unclear (Reviewer 4).
    - The claimed simplicity of training diffusion models for CO is unsupported; it is very time-consuming (Reviewer 4).
    - The paper does not discuss why supervised learning is needed when unsupervised or data-less methods perform well (Reviewer 4).
    - For MIS, f_tar is set using the average optimal objective value from a validation set, but under heuristic labels there is no ground-truth optimal value (Reviewer 3).
    - The difference between Q1 and Q2 in the Introduction is unclear (Reviewer 3).
