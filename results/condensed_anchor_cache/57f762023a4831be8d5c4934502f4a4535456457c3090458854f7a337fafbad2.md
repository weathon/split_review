## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces difFOCI, a differentiable parametric approximation of the model-free variable selection method FOCI (Feature Ordering by Conditional Independence). difFOCI is applied in three contexts: (1) as a variable selection method, (2) as a trainable model parametrized by a neural network, and (3) as a regularizer to reduce spurious correlations. Experiments on toy examples and real datasets demonstrate its use, including a fairness application for classification without relying on sensitive features. All four reviewers rated the paper 6, with generally positive views on motivation and versatility, but raised several concerns about experimental scope, theoretical consistency, and clarity.

### Strengths

- Clear motivation: FOCI is non‑differentiable, limiting its use in deep learning; difFOCI provides a learnable, differentiable alternative.
- The method is clearly defined, with toy examples giving intuition.
- Three applications (variable selection, trainable predictor, regularizer) are well‑chosen and effectively demonstrate the method’s versatility.
- The proposed correlation term is “plug‑and‑play” and adaptable to multiple tasks.
- The paper is well‑structured, with clear sections, theorems, and extensive evaluation.
- The method is sound and shows good potential for feature selection and debiasing.
- Results on both synthetic and some real datasets are presented.

### Weaknesses

- **Scalability and dataset limitations**: Experiments use small‑scale datasets (e.g., Waterbirds is a simple binary task, datasets in Section 5.1 are small). Only one architecture (ResNet‑50) is evaluated; Transformer‑based models are not tested. No experiments on large‑scale, high‑dimensional data are provided. Computational cost is not discussed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Outdated benchmarks**: Real datasets are from 2019 or earlier; more recent and complex benchmarks (e.g., WILDS) should be used to strengthen the results. (Reviewer 2)
- **In distribution and OOD concerns**: Train and test sets share the same distribution; out‑of‑distribution generalization (e.g., CIFAR10 vs. CIFAR10.1, DomainNet real vs. sketch) is not assessed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Spurious correlation evaluation**: The method improves worst‑group accuracy but reduces average accuracy by 2–4%. It is unclear whether the improvement stems from reduced reliance on spurious features. Synthetic experiments (as in [1]) would help confirm. (Reviewer 2)
- **Fairness experiments need clarification**:
    * Are explicit features \(X_s\) a subset of \(X\)? (Reviewer 3)
    * How is independence from sensitive features enforced via maximizing \(T(Y,\theta\odot X \mid X_s)\)? Does the model condition on \(X_s\)? (Reviewer 3)
    * No metric is reported to verify that predictions actually avoid using sensitive attributes; only performance (accuracy) is given. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited fairness scope**: Only a binary classification task with a single sensitive attribute is considered. Multiple sensitive attributes and more complex fairness criteria (including accuracy‑fairness trade‑offs) should be examined. (Reviewer 1)
- **Disconnect between theory and practice**: The theoretical result assumes the Softmax coefficient \(\beta\) is infinite, but experiments use \(\beta=5\) — far from the asymptotic regime. This makes the theoretical claims tenuous. (Reviewer 4)
- **Ambiguous symbol definitions**:
    * In Line 096, the definition of rank \(r\) (“\(Y_{(j)} \le Y_{(i)}\)”) is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
    * In Line 076, \(\sigma\) is declared as Sigmoid but used for Softmax; clarification needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Visualization issues**: Figure 2 lacks clear labels and annotations; the main takeaway is not obvious. Additional examples and explanation of the visualization process would help. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited baselines**: Comparisons are restricted to conventional methods (LDA, PCA); state‑of‑the‑art feature learning or debiasing methods are not included, limiting assessment of relative advantage. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor**: In the Waterbirds context, \(X_G\) should refer to the spurious variable (background), not “group variable.” (Reviewer 3)
- **Reviewer confidence contrast**: Three reviewers express confidence 3 and soundness 3, while Reviewer 4 gives confidence 2 and soundness 2, indicating lower certainty in the work’s rigor and theoretical grounding.
