## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies post-training for domain-specific LLMs, focusing on finance. It proposes a "Joint-and-Replay" recipe combining Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to build LLaMA3-8B-Fin. The work claims to outperform larger models (GPT-4o, LLaMA3.1-70B) on seen/unseen financial tasks while retaining general knowledge. Reviewers note the practical problem and comprehensive experiments, but raise major concerns about novelty, dataset selection, significance of claims, and missing methodological details. One reviewer (score 5) is notably more positive than the other two (score 3 each).

### Strengths
1. **Practical problem and recipe**: The paper tackles the important task of adapting LLMs to domain-specific use (finance) while preserving general capabilities. The Joint-and-Replay approach offers a straightforward recipe for practitioners. (R1, R2, R3)
2. **Comprehensive evaluation**: Experiments cover sufficient existing evaluation datasets (general benchmarks and finance-specific tasks), and the paper is well-organized with clear figures/tables. (R1, R3)
3. **State-of-the-art result**: The approach yields LLaMA3-8B-Fin, a financial LLM that outperforms larger models (e.g., GPT-4o, LLaMA3.1-70B) on seen and unseen financial tasks. (R1, R2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Overclaimed scope and insufficient novelty**: The paper asserts a "comprehensive study on post-training" but fails to review recent advances in post-training (e.g., RLHF, self-training methods like RFT, STaR, ReST^EM, self-reward, unification of SFT and RLHF). The claim should be scoped to finance-specific post-training. (R1)
2. **Common training techniques**: Masking instruction content in SFT and using replay to mitigate forgetting are standard practices (e.g., Llama 2, Qwen2, continual learning literature). The proposed Joint-and-Replay method itself lacks significant distinction from existing methods; differences from prior work should be highlighted. (R3)
3. **Dataset selection and missing descriptions**: 
   - The inclusion of evaluation datasets (Aqua, MATH, GSM-8k) during DAPT defeats the purpose of evaluation; generic pretraining data (Wikitext, C4, PILE, FineWeb) would be more appropriate. (R2)
   - No details are given on the filtering criteria for FineWeb documents (only "URL filtering" mentioned), nor rationale for dataset choices, undermining reliability of results. (R2)
   - It is unclear whether answers to datasets like GSM-8k were also included during DAPT. (R2)
4. **Unclear significance of general benchmark results**: LLaMA3-8B-Fin does not degrade vs. LLaMA3-8B-Instruct but underperforms other LLMs; domain-specific models like Palmyra-Fin-32K also maintain general performance, so the takeaway is unclear. (R1)
5. **Ablation on fine-tuning methods adds little**: The finding that full fine-tuning outperforms LoRA (Table 8) is expected and unnovel. (R1)
6. **Evaluation section lacks novelty**: Section 7 on evaluation adds no new insights given extensive existing work on LLM evaluation. (R1) R3 suggests exploring dynamic evaluation methods that measure both specific and general capabilities after post-training. (R3)
7. **Missing comparison or justification for Palmyra**: No description of Palmyra’s training data or exposure to the same datasets; the fairness and reliability of the comparison are unclear. (R2)
8. **No consideration of distillation**: The paper explores only SFT and does not examine distillation (e.g., from larger models, or synthetic document generation) as a remedy for forgetting. (R2)
9. **Insufficient details on joint training**: 
   - The training ratio between DAPT and SFT during joint training is not specified; no experiments with different ratios (even split vs. higher DAPT proportion). (R3)
   - Missing training hyperparameters (learning rate, training time, etc.). (R3)
10. **Unclear definition and evaluation of unseen tasks**: The paper defines unseen tasks only within the financial domain; it does not test on tasks from other domains. Evaluation metrics and task types for unseen tasks are not provided. (R3)
11. **Method section overly focused on basics**: Section 3.1 should introduce the proposed method, not extensively recap basic SFT and pre-training. (R3)
