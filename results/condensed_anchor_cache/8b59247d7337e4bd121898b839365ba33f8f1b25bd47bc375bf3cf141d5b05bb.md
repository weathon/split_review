## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper observes that after fine-tuning CLIP on long-tailed data, textual features remain more balanced and discriminative than visual features. It proposes using the balanced textual features as prototypes to guide disentangled robust representation learning for biased visual features. The framework: (1) fine-tune CLIP via contrastive learning on the imbalanced dataset; (2) freeze the vision encoder and use a linear adapter to refine biased visual representations; (3) for final classification, a linear classifier initialized from fine-tuned textual features is integrated, with classifier weights treated as prototypes. An optimal transport distance (L_OT) is minimized between refined visual features and prototypes, and a supervised contrastive loss (L_SCT) based on the transport plan is added. Experiments on ImageNet-LT, Places-LT, and iNaturalist 2018 report state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths
- The empirical finding that fine-tuned CLIP textual features become balanced in long-tailed classification is intriguing and goes beyond simple observation by contributing a concrete methodology (Reviewer 1, confidence 2; Reviewer 2, confidence 4).
- The idea is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 3, confidence 5).
- Extensive experiments on three long-tailed benchmarks (ImageNet-LT, Places-LT, iNaturalist 2018) demonstrate effectiveness (Reviewer 2, confidence 4; Reviewer 3, confidence 5).
- The ablation study thoroughly validates the contributions of L_OT and L_SCT (Reviewer 1, confidence 2; Reviewer 2, confidence 4).
- Comprehensive visualizations are provided to support the method (Reviewer 2, confidence 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of innovation.** The core idea of using balanced prototypes to guide visual feature learning and adding supervised contrastive loss is similar to previous long-tail methods, with the main differences being the use of a stronger pre-trained model (CLIP) and text-based prototypes instead of target anchors. These changes are considered incremental by Reviewer 3 (confidence 5). Reviewer 2 also notes that the approach is not specifically tailored to long-tail data; the same design could apply to any scenario where image feature quality is limited (e.g., few-shot, noisy data), reducing the contribution to long-tail recognition specifically.
- **Missing discussion of closely related long-tail works.** The paper does not discuss Suh and Seo (2023) nor Kang et al. (2021), the latter being a seminal work on achieving balanced and discriminative feature spaces in long-tailed classification (Reviewer 1, confidence 2).
- **Underperformance on “many” classes.** The method underperforms for head classes in long-tail datasets. Reviewer 2 asks for an analysis and questions whether the method is primarily beneficial only for few-shot / medium/few classes.
- **Questionable design choices and module motivations.** (Reviewer 3, confidence 5)
  - Why is a learnable linear classifier needed? Its weights change during training and may not close the gap between textual and visual feature distributions.
  - Why is cosine similarity chosen as the distance metric in the unsupervised prototype-guided feature learning instead of, e.g., minimum entropy?
  - The modules (learnable classifier, unsupervised prototype-guided feature learning, supervised contrastive loss) appear to be incremental improvements rather than complementary; the motivation for using them together is not clear.
- **High computational cost.** The method uses a two-stage training process and fine-tunes the full CLIP, requiring significant computational resources and long training time. Reviewer 2 questions the cost of setting up optimal transport plans. Reviewer 2 also notes that a simpler concurrent method ([1] arXiv:2301.06267) that directly uses text and image features in a linear classifier achieves strong few-shot performance with far less overhead.
- **Presentation issue with architecture comparison.** Since comparing performance across different architectures (RN50 vs ViT-B/16) is not meaningful, results should be grouped by architecture (Reviewer 1, confidence 2).
- **Use of random sampling in initial CLIP fine-tuning.** Reviewer 2 points out that balanced sampling is standard in long-tail methods and asks why random sampling was chosen; the concern is that it may deliberately induce bias in the CLIP encoder.

*Note:* Reviewers disagree in overall assessment: two reviewers (scores 6 and 5, confidence 2 and 4) see value in the empirical finding and extensive experiments, while one reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) strongly criticizes the lack of novelty and poor motivation of modules.
