## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the "Normalized Float Trick" (NoFlo) as an alternative to the LogSumExp trick for numerically stable evaluation and training of probabilistic circuits (PCs). NoFlo carries normalization constants throughout the forward and backward passes. The authors claim it improves performance on density estimation benchmarks (MNIST family) and brings computational speed-ups. One reviewer (Reviewer 2) rated the paper higher (5) than the others (3), believing the trick has significant potential to replace the LogSumExp standard. The remaining three reviewers were more critical, citing insufficient justification, missing experiments, and likely implementation issues.

### Strengths
- The core idea is simple and easy to follow, which could facilitate adoption in practice and lead to more efficient implementations. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The trick has the potential to replace the ubiquitous LogSumExp trick for PCs and possibly graphical models, and it is surprisingly simple and vectorizable for GPU execution. (Reviewer 2)
- It brings a significant computational speed-up with almost no memory increase by avoiding exponential function evaluations, which could impact many papers. (Reviewer 2)
- The inclusion of an experimental section comparing NoFlo and LogSumExp uncovered that simply changing how layers are evaluated improves empirical performance – a novel discovery worth future investigation. (Reviewer 2)
- The computational cost estimation in Section 3.3 is a good initial comparison between the two methods. (Reviewer 3)
- The authors are upfront about important limitations (e.g., neglect of IO costs, lack of understanding of the phenomenon in Section 4.3). (Reviewer 3)
- The paper provides a fresh perspective on numerical stability in PCs, going beyond the standard LogSumExp trick. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification for improved stability/performance.** The claim that NoFlo is more numerically stable than LogSumExp is not verified or explained. It is unclear how caching α_k terms leads to improved stability (e.g., fewer log/exp computations?). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4) Without a rigorous error analysis (e.g., bounding error propagation in terms of weight matrices), the observed BPD decrease cannot be interpreted as a significant improvement – the true BPD with infinite precision is unknown, and the boost could be due to additional unknown numerical distortions in NoFlo. (Reviewers 2, 3) A reasonable hypothesis is that NoFlo’s distortions act as regularization, reducing expressiveness and improving generalization; this is not discussed. (Reviewer 3) Reviewer 4 states the paper provides no convincing explanation beyond “simply works better.” (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing or insufficient experiments to validate correctness and generality.**
    - No comparison with existing methods/libraries that handle numerical stability well (e.g., Peharz et al., Liu et al., Zuidberg Dos Martires, 2024). (Reviewer 1)
    - No train-data performance is reported; without it, one cannot rule out that NoFlo introduces higher numerical distortions that act as regularizers, inflating test performance. (Reviewer 3)
    - Experiments are only on large circuits (MNIST scale). No small-scale tests to determine ground truth. The paper would be much more solid by comparing NoFlo, LogSumExp, and evaluation with no tricks on small circuits in float64 to see which BPD is closer to infinite precision. (Reviewer 2) Reviewer 4 strongly suspects a bug: the difference in test-BPD in Figure 4 is too large to be mere numerical precision, and suggests building a small model (e.g., 16 binary variables) to verify that the sum of all 2^16 log-likelihoods is (very close to) zero for both methods. (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational cost analysis is limited and inconsistent.** The analysis suggests the cost is dominated by C², giving very limited practical advantage. (Reviewer 4) Section 3.3 uses cycle-counts estimated for x86 architecture while experiments run on GPUs, reducing the value of those estimates. (Reviewer 3) IO costs are neglected, which the authors themselves acknowledge. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing, terminology, and presentation issues.**
    - Many typos, artificially stretched text, unclear phrasing, and mathematical writing that is not tight (missing domains, typos). (Reviewer 3)
    - The term “partition” is used differently from common literature (region graphs); “partition circuit” and “layered circuit” terminology conflicts with existing work (e.g., tensorized circuits, Mari et al. 2023). (Reviewers 2, 4)
    - Structured-decomposability is never formally defined and seems to refer to simple “decomposability” (L86-87). (Reviewer 4)
    - Proposition 2.3: it is unclear whether it is based on prior work and what role it plays. (Reviewer 1)
    - The claim that layered circuits are introduced in Shih et al. (2021) is incorrect; Shih et al. uses neural models for parameter sharing, and the architecture is from Peharz et al. (2019) and Peharz et al. (2020). (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing relevant literature.** No comparison with Yao et al. “On Hardware-efficient Inference in Probabilistic Circuits” (UAI 2024) (Reviewer 4) and with Zuidberg Dos Martires (2024) (Reviewer 1). The relationship with tensorized circuits (Mari et al. 2023) is noted but not discussed. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Code not provided despite claim.** The paper states code will be made available, but it was not. The justification “lab’s policy” is questionable and could compromise anonymity if that policy is unique to a single lab. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of discussion of floating-point analogy.** Given the resemblance between NoFlo and floating-point representation (adaptive exponent offset β), the paper surprisingly does not discuss this connection, which could lead to useful bit-hacks. (Reviewer 3)
- **Motivation and design choices are poorly explained.** For example, why use canonical polyadic layers? What are the alternatives and their implications? Arbitrary choices are not clearly signaled. (Reviewer 3)
- **Questions that double as criticisms.**
    - Is the NoFlo trick only applicable to partition circuits? (Reviewer 1)
    - The log-likelihood improvement in Table 2 seems too good to be true; if the circuit is fully-factorized with one layer, does the improvement persist? (Reviewer 1)
    - How does NoFlo perform on models beyond PCs (any computation requiring LSE)? (Reviewer 4)
    - Can the authors provide a figure similar to Figure 4 using 64-bit precision to support the claim that results do not change? (Reviewer 2)
    - The times in Figure 3 seem unusually long; is everything consistently running on GPU? (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor issues:** Typo L155: what is p_ki? (Reviewer 4) The sentence about Hadamard products (L123-124) is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
