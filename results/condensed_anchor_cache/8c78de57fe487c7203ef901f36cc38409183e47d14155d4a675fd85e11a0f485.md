## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes M³-Impute, a graph neural network-based method for missing value imputation that explicitly incorporates missingness information during embedding initialization and models feature and sample correlations via novel masking schemes (Feature Correlation Unit FCU, Sample Correlation Unit SCU). Experiments on 25 benchmark datasets under three missingness settings are reported, with the method achieving the best MAE scores in most cases and outperforming existing methods including its predecessor GRAPE.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach**: The combination of missingness-aware embedding initialization, feature correlation (FCU), and sample correlation (SCU) is clearly stated and justified. The mask-guided representation learning explicitly leverages missingness information, which is uncommon in prior work. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluation on 25 datasets and three missingness patterns (MCAR, MAR, MNAR) is noted as the most broad experimental setup among missing value imputation studies. (Reviewer 1)
- **Detailed description**: The paper provides a step-by-step explanation of each module (embedding initialization, FCU, SCU, final imputation). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Significance of contribution**: The method addresses explicit gaps in current imputation methods by jointly modeling missingness and correlations, offering a foundation for more sophisticated data analysis in complex datasets. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete discussion of missing data mechanisms**: The paper is limited to MCAR settings, whereas the literature (e.g., Ibrahim et al., 2005; Kim & Shao, 2021) covers MCAR, MAR, and MNAR. This restricts the method’s applicability and theoretical grounding. (Reviewer 3 – strong critique, high confidence)
- **Scalability and computational efficiency concerns**: 
  - The training time is not reported; only test-phase time is given. (Reviewer 1) 
  - The graph-based approach does not scale well to large datasets, as its efficiency depends heavily on sample size and number of variables (like the method of You et al., 2020). (Reviewer 3) 
  - Memory usage and optimization details are missing; resource limitations in practical applications are not addressed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Marginal improvements and lack of compelling significance**: Most gains over baselines (including GRAPE) are marginal; it is unclear if improvements are statistically significant. When improvements are small, GRAPE could be a simpler alternative. (Reviewer 1). A minority reviewer (Reviewer 3) finds the method fails to model underlying missing data mechanisms and overlooks causal inference considerations, further limiting impact.
- **Missing comparisons with most recent state-of-the-art methods**: The paper should compare against the latest imputation techniques (e.g., HyperImpute, Jarrett et al., 2022) to highlight unique advantages. (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 3 notes FCU/SCU concepts are similar to prior works: Zhong et al., 2023; Jarrett et al., 2022; Gupta et al., 2023; Qiao et al., 2016; Um et al., 2023.)
- **Need for additional ablation and tuning details**: 
  - The ablation study shows only marginal changes; no deep insights are provided. (Reviewer 1) 
  - Hyperparameter tuning for the proposed method is not described, while baseline tuning is in an appendix. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limitations in low-dependency data**: M³-Impute struggles when data points have low dependencies, because FCU and SCU rely on similarity to form graphs, potentially leading to sparse/inadequate graphs. (Reviewer 2) 
- **Interpretability**: A section on model interpretability is missing, which is critical for trust in fields like healthcare and finance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Comparison with GRAPE on graph construction**: The method of You et al. (2020) also uses graph construction and graph representation learning. A comparative analysis regarding graph construction efficiency is needed; it is unclear which approach is more effective. (Reviewer 2)
- **When does the method outperform?**: An analysis of scenarios where M³-Impute significantly outperforms vs. where it does not would help practitioners assess when its additional complexity is justified. (Reviewer 1)
