## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper empirically evaluates random dropping methods (DropEdge, DropMessage) for oversmoothing in GNNs, finding them ineffective at test time, and proposes Learn2Drop—an information-theoretic, learned dropping approach via the information bottleneck principle. Experiments show Learn2Drop reduces oversmoothing (measured by normalized Dirichlet energy, NDE) and improves accuracy on several datasets.

### Strengths

* The paper studies the oversmoothing issue, a key challenge for deep GNNs. [R1]
* Both analysis and empirical results demonstrate limitations of DropEdge and DropMessage. [R1]
* The oversmoothing experiments in Section 3 are thorough, particularly Section 3.2 (Figure 2) investigating the role of randomness in DropEdge. [R2]
* The proposed information bottleneck approach (Section 4.1) is principled and appears effective. [R2]
* Informed dropping is well-motivated. [R3]
* The observation that random dropping methods applied only during training have little effect on test-time oversmoothing (if NDE is accepted as a valid metric) is interesting. [R3]
* The paper outlines a novel application of the information bottleneck principle for mitigating oversmoothing. [R4]
* The paper shows no discernible correlation between oversmoothing (NDE) and test performance. [R4]
* Open-sourced code aids reproducibility. [R1]

### Weaknesses

* **Metric validity for oversmoothing (NDE):** The paper relies heavily on a normalized Dirichlet energy (NDE) metric, but multiple reviewers question its validity. NDE does not satisfy the criterion proposed by Rusch et al. (2023) (NDE=0 does not imply all nodes have identical representations), making conclusions drawn from NDE (“oversmoothing is still occurring according to the formal definition”) invalid. [R3] NDE is not well-defined for 1-d features (all 1-d features yield NDE=0) and can be zero even when classification is perfect (e.g., two linearly separable 1-d features). [R3] The reasoning in the paragraph above Section 3.2 is heuristic and obscure. [R3] NDE does not correlate well with accuracy; e.g., in a stochastic block model with two classes, NDE can be near zero while test accuracy remains high—underscoring that oversmoothing primarily matters across classes rather than within a class. [R2,R4] The paper should either plot NDE vs. accuracy frontiers or use additional metrics (e.g., influence scores from Xu et al. 2018) to better interpret oversmoothing. [R2]
* **Insufficient evaluation and baselines:** Comparison is limited to DropEdge and DropMessage; other methods designed to address oversmoothing (e.g., skip connections, residual connections) are not included as baselines, despite Figure 1 suggesting skip connections may be a perfect remedy. [R1,R3] The paper should add skip connections as a baseline in Figure 3 and 4, and discuss why not to simply use them. [R3] The authors claim DropMessage at test time introduces high variance, but provide no experimental evidence; DropMessage applied at test time with multiple forward passes (averaging to reduce variance) is a sensible baseline for Learn2Drop. [R1,R2,R3] Performance on more complex architectures and on homophilic vs. heterophilic graphs is unclear. [R4] Only node classification tasks are considered; the method’s actual benefits remain unclear. [R4]
* **Clarity and writing issues:** Sections 4.1 and 4.2 are hard to comprehend and need significant revision. [R4] The writing overall could be improved. [R1]
* **Missing interpretation:** More interpretation of results in Table 1 is needed (e.g., what does “DO” mean?). [R1]
* **Missing literature and incorrect citation:** The citation for Dirichlet energy usage in oversmoothing is incorrect: the original work is Cai and Wang (2020), not Rusch et al. (2022). [R3] The paper omits recent theoretical work on the “sweet spot” between smoothing and oversmoothing (Keriven 2022, Wu et al. 2023), making the claim “the true causes and consequences of oversmoothing are vaguely understood” inappropriate. [R3]
* **Reproducibility concerns:** No code or hyperparameter ranges were provided at the time of review, hampering reproduction. [R4]
* **Minority positive view:** One reviewer (score 6) found the experiments thorough and the approach principled and effective, but all other reviewers (scores 3) raised significant concerns about the metric validity and evaluation, reducing overall confidence. [R2 vs. R1,R3,R4]
