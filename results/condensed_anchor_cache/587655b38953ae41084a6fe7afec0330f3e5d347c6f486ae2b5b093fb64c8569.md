## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies user-level local differential privacy (LDP) for mean estimation, stochastic optimization, classification, and regression. It proposes adaptive strategies, derives information-theoretic lower bounds, and shows that under the local model user-level and item-level convergence rates are nearly identical for bounded support and faster for heavy-tailed distributions. Reviewers generally agree the topic is significant, the presentation is organized, and the bounds are tight. However, concerns include unclear statements, missing references, limited scope of the optimization setting, unclear technical novelty, and gaps in the lower‑bound proofs for \(\epsilon>1\). Reviewer 3 (score 3) was notably more critical than Reviewer 1 (score 5) and Reviewer 2 (score 6), especially regarding novelty and proof clarity.

### Strengths
- Tackles significant learning problems under user-level LDP and establishes tight minimax lower and upper bounds (R1, R2).
- Paper is very organized and presents results clearly (R2).
- Derivation of matching information-theoretic lower bounds enhances completeness (R2).
- Addresses user-level DP, a relatively less explored but extremely relevant area (R3).
- Studies a wide variety of tasks: mean estimation, stochastic optimization, nonparametric classification, and regression (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear statements and proofs**: Some statements throughout the paper are unclear, making parts of the presentation difficult to follow (R1). Some proof details are also unclear (R3). Specifically, the transition from Eq. 62 to Eq. 64 in Theorem 2 is justified only for \(\epsilon<1\), and similar gaps appear in Theorems 6 and 7 (R3, Q1). The paper would benefit from highlighting the difference in lower-bound proofs between item-level and user-level LDP, especially the \(\epsilon>1\) regime (R3, Q2).
- **Limited stochastic optimization setting**: Only bounded-gradient strongly convex objectives are considered; the paper does not use its own unbounded-support mean estimation for the unbounded-gradient case, which would be more interesting and practical (R1, Q6).
- **Missing references and inaccurate claim**: Several relevant papers are omitted (e.g., Li et al. 2024 on item-level/user-level agnostic learning, Cummings et al. 2022 on mean estimation with user-level privacy under heterogeneity, Charles et al. 2024 on fine-tuning LLMs with user-level DP) (R2). The claim on lines 132‑133 that the paper provides the first analysis on nonparametric classification and regression under user-level \(\epsilon\)-LDP is inaccurate, as Ma et al. 2024 studies regression under ULDP with a sparsity constraint. Sparse estimation works (Zhu et al. 2023, Zhou et al. 2022) could also be added to related work (R2).
- **Unclear technical novelty**: The technical novelty of the work is unclear (R3). (Contrast: Reviewer 2 found the paper clear and complete.)
- **Non-interactive vs. interactive model**: The paper does not discuss whether the local model is non-interactive and whether the proposed algorithms can be extended to an interactive setting (R3, Q3).
- **Specific unclear points**:
    - Why is the case \(\epsilon>1\) considered interesting for LDP? (R1, Q1)
    - In Proposition 1(2), the text says “\(n\) users with \(m\) samples per user” but the reasoning randomly picks one sample per user, which seems inconsistent (R1, Q2).
    - Definition 1: the explanation of why user-level \(\epsilon\)-LDP does *not* imply item-level \(\epsilon\)-LDP is needed (R1, Q3).
    - Theorem 1: it is unclear why the mean squared error never converges to zero with increasing \(m\) if \(n\) is fixed (R1, Q4).
    - The meaning of \(n_0\) in Equation (6) is not explained (R1, Q5).
