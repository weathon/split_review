## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Diffusion², a framework for 4D content generation from a single image or video. It combines a video diffusion model and a multi-view diffusion model via score composition to generate dense multi-view multi-frame images, which are then reconstructed into a dynamic 3D representation. The method avoids training on expensive 4D data by leveraging existing models separately trained on 3D and video datasets. A variance-reducing sampling strategy is introduced to mitigate conflicts between the two heterogeneous scores. The approach is designed for parallel generation and aims to produce consistent 4D assets within minutes.

### Strengths
- Novel conceptual approach that reconciles video and multi-view diffusion models for 4D generation, circumventing the need for synchronized 4D training data (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Well-formulated theoretical framework for score composition, with clear proofs of the denoising process and probability structure (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Thoughtful parallel generation architecture, enabling efficient multi-view video generation (Reviewer 1).
- Variance-reducing sampling strategy thoughtfully designed to reconcile conflicts between the two distinct diffusion priors at higher noise levels (Reviewer 2).
- Clear and thorough ablation studies demonstrating the effect of key components (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The framework requires no additional training, only uses off-the-shelf diffusion models (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Experiments are sufficient to support the anticipated conclusions (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **High computational requirements**: The method needs 8–16 A6000 GPUs for basic operation and still takes 20 minutes per generation, creating a memory-speed tradeoff that limits practical applicability. The authors should discuss paths to reduce these extreme hardware needs (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).
- **Limited motion complexity in evaluation**: Most demonstrations show relatively simple motions; more challenging scenarios such as rotational movements and articulated motions are needed to better assess generalization. Challenges in extending to diverse scenarios should be addressed (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).
- **Missing baseline comparison**: A natural baseline is to generate a video with a video diffusion model, then generate multi-view images frame-by-frame using a multi-view model, and finally apply a 4D reconstruction method (e.g., Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting). The paper should compare against this to highlight the advantage of score composition (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
- **Contradiction in Figure 4**: The qualitative results for stage-1 and stage-2 appear almost identical, yet the text (L524-525) states that stage-1 images show blurring details. This inconsistency needs explanation (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
- **Ablation studies lack quantitative results**: The ablation section only shows qualitative comparisons; quantitative metrics similar to those in Table 1 and Table 2 should be provided (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
- **Oversmoothing on unseen viewpoints**: The generated 4D content, especially the backside, lacks details and appears oversmoothed. The problem is exacerbated when variance reduction is applied. Results are worse than those from SDS-based methods or models trained directly on limited 4D data (Reviewer 3, confidence 4). The assumption of independent probability distributions (e.g., Figure 3) may be too strong (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Suitability of SVD for foreground objects**: The video diffusion model (SVD) performs poorly on videos of single dynamic foreground objects. Would finetuning SVD on foreground-only videos improve quality? (Reviewer 3, confidence 4 – question turned into weakness as it overlaps with quality criticism).
- **Independence assumption justification**: In Figure 3, if viewpoints differ by only 30 degrees, can they still be considered independent variables? The reasoning needs more rigorous justification (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
- **Variance-reducing sampling explanation**: The rollback operation in variance-reducing sampling needs a more specific explanation of why it works and how it reduces variance. Additionally, would simply increasing the number of sampling steps achieve a similar effect? (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
- **Generalization to other models**: Can the proposed method work with other video and multi-view diffusion models besides the specific ones used? (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
- **Time breakdown**: The time consumption for stage-1 (multi-view multi-frame generation) and stage-2 (4D reconstruction) should be reported separately (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
- **Multiple objects**: Can the method generate 4D content with multiple objects? (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
