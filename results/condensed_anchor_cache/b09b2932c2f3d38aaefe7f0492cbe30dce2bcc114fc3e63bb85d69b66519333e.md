## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sibling Augmented Generative Flow Networks (SA-GFN), a dual-network architecture (Behavior Network on true rewards, Sibling Network on intrinsic rewards with data relabeling) to improve exploration in GFlowNets. Reviewers generally find the idea novel, clearly presented, and effective in the tested tasks, with one reviewer (score 8) being more positive and praising the decoupling and comprehensive experiments. The three reviewers scoring 6 note significant limitations in evaluation breadth, missing comparisons, and insufficient analysis. All agree the method is simple and achieves the best results on the selected benchmarks.

### Strengths
- Well-structured, clearly written paper; the storyline from GFlowNet to GAFN to SA-GFN is easy to follow (R2,R3,R4).
- Addresses a relevant and interesting problem: improving exploration in GFlowNets with sparse rewards (R1).
- The idea of a decoupled sibling network with intrinsic rewards is novel, simple yet effective for combining intrinsic rewards with GFlowNets; decoupling samples and rewards for training has rarely been explored (R1,R2,R3).
- Achieves best performance compared to all baselines in the selected datasets (R1,R2,R3).
- Experiments are comprehensive in terms of method comparisons and ablation studies (R2,R3,R4); includes a wide range of comparisons with existing methods (R4).
- Detailed ablation study provided (R3).
- Compatible with multiple GFlowNet training objectives and intrinsic reward methods (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation environments**: Only grid world tasks are tested; more complex/hard-exploration environments (e.g., Montezuma’s Revenge, Mujoco robot manipulation) are missing. Even a qualitative analysis or discussion would be beneficial (R3,R4).
- **Missing baseline intrinsic reward methods**: Only RND is used as an intrinsic reward scheme. Other methods like NovelD and DEIR, which have shown better performance, are not included as baselines; NovelD is only used in the appendix but not as a separate baseline (R1,R3).
- **Incomplete ablation and analysis**:
  - No ablation of hyperparameters, e.g., training frequency ratio between sibling and behavior networks (R3).
  - No study of the effect of using only terminal states vs. all states for intrinsic reward calculation (Eq. 2) (R2).
  - No discussion or experiments on the instability caused by the absence of a critic network, which may lead to high variance (R4).
  - The L1 error curves of some baselines (e.g., 8x8x8x8) in Fig. 4 increase unexpectedly; this behavior is not explained (R2).
- **Presentation issues**:
  - The related work section is too short, making it difficult to assess novelty and relevance (R1).
  - The description of GFlowNets (Section 3.1) is too brief; the policy, states, and objects are not clearly defined (R1).
  - The claim in the introduction about expanding benchmarks to include non-zero rewards is inaccurate because HyperGrid with non-zero rewards was already used in prior work (R2).
  - Many references are incomplete, missing publisher information (R2).
  - The indices and findings in the last paragraph of Section 5.2 use the same labels (a,b,c), causing confusion (R2).
- **Training of sibling network unclear**: It is not detailed how the sibling network is trained—whether it uses data from the behavior policy, directly copies weights, or is trained solely on its own collected data. Also, whether training only on RND-collected data is sufficient for exploration is unanswered (R3).
- **Positioning as non-RL contested**: The authors seem to position GFlowNets outside reinforcement learning, but the reviewer argues that GFlowNets maximize cumulative discounted reward and use off-policy RL training, thus falling under RL. The authors should clarify this positioning (R4).
- **Missed analysis**: Given the GFlowNet setting, is there a way to improve RND beyond direct application? (R1).
- **Visualization of convergence**: In Fig. 4, the curves for baselines should be plotted for more steps (e.g., 32x32, 64x64, 96x96) to show plateaued values (R2).
