## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the emergence of In-Context Learning (ICL) in transformers using a controlled, often few-shot, setup with vision data (Omniglot, SVHN, CIFAR-100). It argues that conceptual repetitions (iCopy – exact copies of the query image in the context) are crucial for ICL, more so than previously studied properties like burstiness or skewness. It further claims that ICL performance depends on balancing in-weight learning (IWL) difficulty with in-context solving ability: harder IWL tasks reduce ICL transiency. Two reviewers (rating 3,3) find the work poorly presented and insufficiently connected to LLMs; two (rating 5,5) see the perspective as novel and experiments as convincing but note limitations in clarity and generalizability.

### Strengths
- Timely topic with a straightforward methodology (R1).
- Figure 1 (n-gram repetition in real corpora) provides good motivation and grounding (R1).
- Related work section is comprehensive and useful (R1).
- Experiments in Section 5 (e.g., label distribution, noise, instance discriminative task) are well-done (R1, R3).
- Builds on Chan et al. 2022, confirming findings on skewness and burstiness, but also showing that under iCopy, skewness may not be important – with analysis from an induction head perspective (R2, R3).
- The core ideas and techniques are sound; e.g., it is believable that copying the query into the context jump-starts a “lookup” ICL mechanism (R3). The iCopy mechanism is shown effective (R4).
- Experiments are carried out across multiple vision datasets (Omniglot, SVHN, CIFAR-100), increasing confidence (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity** (R1, R4): The core experiment in Section 4 is poorly explained. The difference between sequences like (3xQ-3xA-B-C) and (iCopy versions) is not clear: is it that in iCopy the three A and three Q images in the context are exact copies, while without iCopy they are different images with the same label? This applies to training and test data. The notation (Q-A-B-C-D-E-F-G iCopy) does not show which symbol is repeated. The use of “Q” and “A” for query/answer pairs adds confusion (R4). A clear figure or earlier description is needed (R1).
- **Figure 5 (induction heads) is unclear** (R1): It is not explained how to identify induction heads from attention maps (assumed darker columns in subplot d). A description of induction heads should be added to the related work section.
- **Missing justification for experimental design choices** (R2):
    - The baseline model uses a mix of 10% standard sequences and 90% in-context sequences – no ablation on this ratio is provided.
    - The training objective only computes and backpropagates the loss on the final query pair, not on all y_i along the sequence (unlike standard language modeling). How do results change with a fully autoregressive objective?
    - For Figure 6 experiments with fewer classes, it is unclear if models are trained over multiple epochs (potential overfitting) and no IWL validation curves are shown.
- **Connection to LLMs and general ICL is weak** (R2, R3, R4):
    - No experiments are conducted on pure text tasks (R2, R4). It is unclear what iCopy corresponds to in natural language (exact token copying vs. paraphrasing) (R2).
    - The simple copying mechanism may be inappropriate or even damaging for more complex forms of ICL (e.g., reasoning, multi-step tasks). There exists work on “mitigating copy bias” (Ali, 2024) (R3). The paper does not discuss potential tasks where iCopy could be beneficial (R3).
- **Novelty concerns** (R3): The work is incremental on top of Chan et al. 2022. The central idea that label copying can be incentivized by directly copying the query sample is considered obvious. The iCopy setup is a small generalization of previous setups with a single exemplar image.
- **Overly simplistic setup** (R3, R4): Real ICL tasks involve complex sequence processing and example selection, which can influence results due to correlations between examples and the query. The paper does not evaluate or mitigate such influences (R4).
- **IWL performance measurement** (R1): It is not specified whether IWL performance is measured zero-shot.
- **Other missing analyses** (R2): The interplay of model size with the emergence of ICL under different data properties (burstiness, skewness, number of classes) is not explored.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: The two rating-5 reviewers find the perspective novel and experiments convincing, while the two rating-3 reviewers highlight significant presentation and generalizability issues, with one (R3) questioning contribution. The claim that “conceptual repetitions” are crucial for ICL is seen as under-supported for language tasks.
