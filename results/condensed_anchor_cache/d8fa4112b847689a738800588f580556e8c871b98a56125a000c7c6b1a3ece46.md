## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the paper’s meaningful motivation and effort in building a large, hierarchically organized RTL dataset (repository, file, module, block) with CoT annotations, netlists, and PPA metrics. The dataset aims to address gaps in existing RTL datasets (narrow scope, missing circuit implementation details, privacy restrictions). However, the majority of reviewers (scores 3,3,3) find that the paper fails to convincingly demonstrate dataset quality and effectiveness, with unclear contributions, weak experimental methodology, and insufficient details. One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, stating the paper is well-written and the fine-tuned models outperform baselines, but still notes weaknesses in benchmarking methodology. All reviewers express concerns about the dataset’s multimodal claims, annotation reliability, and lack of fair comparisons with existing datasets and methods.

### Strengths
- Meaningful motivation to advance hardware design automation via a high-quality open-source dataset.
- Large, comprehensive collection of complete RTL designs organized at repository, file, module, and block levels, covering chip, IP, module, and RISC-V designs.
- Use of CoT and LLMs (GPT-4, Claude) to generate hierarchical natural language annotations, aligning RTL code with descriptions at multiple levels.
- Enrichment of dataset with synthesized netlists, AST, CDFG graphs, and PPA metrics (Power, Performance, Area).
- The organization at Repository, File, Module, and Block levels is reasonable and aligns with practical hardware design.
- The paper benchmarks several LLMs (CodeLlama, CodeT5+, CodeGen, DeepSeek-Coder-V2-lite) and demonstrates that fine-tuning on DeepCircuitX improves performance on code understanding, completion, and generation tasks compared to baselines.

### Weaknesses
**Contribution Clarity & Scope**
- First contribution mentions HLS, but HLS is never discussed later in the paper.
- Dataset is described as “multimodal,” but it is unclear what modalities beyond text are included (e.g., are AST/CDFG graphs in image/PNG format?).
- It is unclear whether the main contribution is a training dataset or a benchmark. If a training dataset, comparisons with prior datasets (RTLCoder, BetterV, CodeV, AutoVCoder) are missing. If a benchmark, the paper should provide a more detailed comparison with existing benchmarks, ensure exact NL-code correspondence, test correctness, and evaluate whether BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR are suitable for code understanding.
- Dataset focus is on LLMs; other AI research in EDA (e.g., synthesis, PPA prediction) could also benefit, but this is not discussed.

**Dataset Quality and Annotation Reliability**
- LLM-generated annotations (GPT-4) may have a gap compared to human-written descriptions; VerilogEval found that LLMs perform better on machine-generated descriptions than human ones. The paper evaluates annotation quality via human engineers but does not compare GPT-generated annotations against human-written descriptions for consistency.
- Human evaluation (Section 4.2) indicates quality needs improvement, especially in accuracy. No examples of scores 1,2,3,4 are shown, making the scores meaningless. Also, no comparison with baseline dataset quality.
- Selection criteria for “high-quality” data used in annotation are not explained.
- Quantity of PPA data is not reported; most open-source repositories cannot be synthesized easily, and synthesis setups vary.
- Does each design contain multi-level natural language descriptions and synthesized netlists? Or only a subset?
- Does DeepCircuitX separate original Verilog design files from testbenches?

**Dataset Organization and Parsing**
- Why is RISC-V treated as a separate level from Chip, IP, and Module?
- Why not use AST to divide RTL code into distinct functional blocks (instead of regular expressions and LLMs, which are not good parsers)?
- In Table 2, what is the difference between module-level and repo-level descriptions for a module-level design? Why do RISC-V and module-level designs lack block-level descriptions?
- The concepts “Chip, IP, Module, RISC-V” vs. “module-level, block-level, repository-level” are confusing; concrete examples are needed.

**Experimental Methodology and Evaluation**
- RTL Code Understanding: The training-test split is not described. If models are trained and evaluated on the same dataset, results are less meaningful, especially with text-similarity metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR).
- RTL Code Completion/Generation: 
  - For completion, providing only the module header seems limited; completing from more incomplete code (beyond header) would be more meaningful.
  - How many codes (n) are sampled to compute pass@k? Is the standard pass@k estimator used?
  - Does the dataset deduplicate against test sets (RTLLM, VerilogEval) before training?
  - Table 6 shows one result (code generation) but two tasks (code completion and generation) are described in Section 3.2.2.
  - Pass@1 and pass@5 for CodeLlama fine-tuned on DeepCircuitX are lower than results reported in RTLCoder. This may indicate lower dataset quality compared to RTLCoder’s dataset.
- Fine-Tuning and Benchmark Comparisons: 
  - The paper fine-tunes only on DeepCircuitX and then benchmarks against RTLLM and VerilogEval. It is unclear whether the baseline models were already fine-tuned on RTLLM/VerilogEval. For fair comparison, models should also be fine-tuned on other benchmark datasets (from Related Work) and metrics compared.
  - Citations for RTLLM (Liu, Shang et al., 2024) and VerilogEval (Liu, Mingjie et al., 2023) are missing.
  - PPA prediction experiments do not use LLMs, making this section inconsistent with the rest of the paper. The authors could evaluate whether LLMs can learn PPA-related knowledge. Also, PPA is reported using Synopsys DC, not an open-source tool like Yosys, which may hinder reproducibility.
- The paper should discuss a recent relevant work: Dehaerne et al. (2023) “A deep learning framework for Verilog autocompletion towards design and verification automation.”

**Minor Issues:**
- Why were Verilog-specific models like RTLCoder not used in the experiments?
- Why was DeepSeek-Coder-V2-lite used for code understanding but not for code generation?
