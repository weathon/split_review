## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes negative-prompt inversion, a fast image inversion method for text-guided diffusion models that avoids the per-step optimization required by null-text inversion. Instead of optimizing the null-text embedding, the method replaces it with the fixed conditional prompt embedding, enabling forward-only inference. The authors show that this achieves reconstruction quality comparable to null-text inversion while being more than 30× faster (≈5 seconds for 512×512, 50 steps). The method is evaluated on 100 COCO images using PSNR, LPIPS, and speed, and is combined with prompt-to-prompt for editing.

### Strengths
- **Original idea**: Replacing the optimized null-text embedding with the fixed prompt embedding to eliminate optimization is novel and leads to a simple, fast inversion.
- **Significant speed-up**: 30× faster than null-text inversion with similar reconstruction quality; practical impact on reducing compute cost for editing.
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The paper is organized, symbols and concepts are clearly defined, and the background on DDIM, CFG, and null-text inversion is informative.
- **Technically sound** (Reviewer 2): The paper provides a clear summary of preliminaries and extensive justifications for the proposed approach.
- **Relevant literature well-discussed**: Related work is organized and context is provided for the contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Core assumption of equal predicted noise at adjacent steps is not justified** and appears unrealistic. The approximation error may accumulate over multiple steps, especially with small step counts (e.g., 50). The impact of step number on fidelity is not analyzed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Lack of human subjective evaluation**: Only objective metrics (PSNR, LPIPS) on a small dataset (100 COCO images) are provided. Human studies are needed to assess perceptual quality, as metrics may miss subtle artifacts. (Reviewer 1)
- **Failure cases not presented**: The paper mentions poor face reconstruction but does not show any failure examples (faces or other structures), making it hard to understand the nature of failures. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing comparisons with key baselines**: The paper does not compare with recent editing methods such as Imagic, UniTune, or prompt tuning inversion, limiting assessment of relative editing performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Editing performance below DDIM + CFG**: The authors report that editing results with negative-prompt inversion are worse than those from DDIM with CFG, raising questions about practical utility. (Reviewer 2)
- **Assumptions relegated to supplementary material**: The main paper lacks justification for the equivalence of null-text inversion features and DDIM trajectories, and the equality of predicted noise at adjacent steps. The supplementary Proposition 3 is unclear, especially the claim that α_t ≈ α_{t-1} implies z_t ≈ z_{t-1}. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Justification in supplementary relies on conditioning on z₀**, which is not available in actual DDIM sampling; the relevance of those derivations for the real process is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
- **The method may be equivalent to DDIM inversion without CFG**, but a direct comparison with DDIM w/o CFG is missing, which would be needed to verify soundness. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing computational cost breakdown**: The paper claims speed as a main contribution but does not provide FLOPs, step-by-step timing, or memory footprint compared to null-text inversion. (Reviewer 3)
- **Too much background in Introduction and Method**: The Introduction includes excessive discussion of related studies, and the Method section covers well-known concepts too extensively; these could be shortened. (Reviewer 3)
- **Name "negative-prompt inversion" is confusing**: The term "negative prompt" typically refers to undesired features, which does not match the proposed use of the conditional prompt. (Reviewer 4)
- **Editing experiments not on the same benchmark as Mokady et al. (2023)**: The evaluation should follow the same protocol to facilitate direct comparison. (Reviewer 4)
- **The approximation of the optimized null-text embedding with C could be achieved by null-text inversion with a shared embedding across time steps; this comparison is missing.** Could some optimization around C (not per step) improve results while retaining speed? (Reviewer 4)
- **Experiments should be presented given a fixed time budget** to better highlight the efficiency advantage. (Reviewer 4)
