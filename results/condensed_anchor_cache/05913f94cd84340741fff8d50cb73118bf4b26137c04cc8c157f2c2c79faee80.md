## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper targets generating images of Chinese ancient buildings using diffusion models. The authors collect a dataset (reviewer 1 states about 1200 large-resolution images; the abstract says more than 400) and introduce Pinyin sequences as prompts, fine-tuning the diffusion model with LoRA. Experimental comparisons (visual only) show that pinyin prompts outperform English prompts and LoRA outperforms prompt tuning. The work is intended to benefit the Chinese ancient-building generation community and downstream tasks.

### Strengths
- The topic (generating Chinese ancient buildings) is meaningful, interesting, and addresses a rare cultural area.
- The collected dataset is useful for the community, though the dataset size is noted inconsistently across the paper (400 vs. 1200).
- Introducing Pinyin as a prompt is a promising, novel approach for bridging English-centric models with non-English cultural concepts.
- Experimental visual results demonstrate that pinyin and LoRA are effective: pinyin > English, LoRA > prompt tuning.
- The benchmark can benefit Chinese ancient-style building generation and related downstream tasks.

### Weaknesses
- **Paper writing and presentation are poor.** Numerous typos (e.g., “It si similar to” for “It is similar to”), incomplete symbols (e.g., “,,,” and “...” in the first paragraph), missing references (e.g., “Big models … like ChatGPT and Stable Diffusion” without citation), incorrect citation formatting (e.g., “Shen et al. (2023)” instead of “(Shen et al., 2023)”), and improper figure citations (e.g., “visualized results5”, “in different perspectives in 5”, “by SD in 6” instead of Fig. 5/6). Formatting and completeness of references need a full check.
- **Lack of quantitative evaluation.** The results rely solely on visual comparisons; standard metrics such as FID, CLIPIQA, or others are not reported. This severely weakens the evidence for the proposed method’s superiority.
- **Insufficient comparisons.**  
  - A pre-trained diffusion model with detailed English caption keywords (e.g., “realistic”) may already generate realistic Chinese ancient buildings. This baseline is missing.
  - The paper should compare with alternative prompts beyond pinyin (e.g., Chinese characters, descriptions, or detailed captions). One reviewer questioned “what about other prompts?” and “Why the Pinyin sequence?”.
  - There is no comparison against existing specialized methods or ablation on the choice of prompt.
- **Novelty and contribution are disputed.** One reviewer (rating 5) finds the benchmark novel, while another (rating 1) argues that LoRA is an existing approach and the use of pinyin is trivial. The technical novelty beyond applying known techniques (LoRA, prompt tuning) is unclear.
- **The learned representation and prompt design are simple.** The paper does not explain why pinyin is more effective than other representations, nor does it provide a detailed analysis of the learned embedding.
- **Missing ablation or justification.** No ablation study on LoRA rank, prompt length, or the contribution of the dataset size. The need for special representation versus using richer English prompts is not justified.

*Reviewer confidence varies: one reviewer (rating 5, confidence 4) is confident but not absolutely certain; another (rating 1, confidence 4) is similarly confident but strongly critical; a third (rating 3, confidence 2) is less confident and notes potential misunderstandings. This disagreement highlights that the paper’s central contributions are not clearly demonstrated.*
