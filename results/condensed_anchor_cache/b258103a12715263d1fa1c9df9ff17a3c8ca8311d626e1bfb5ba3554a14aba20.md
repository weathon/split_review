## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RF-POLICY, an offline imitation learning algorithm using rectified flow, a flow-based generative model. The method aims to improve inference efficiency of diffusion-based policy generators by adapting the number of function evaluations (NFE) to state uncertainty: for uni-modal (low-variance) states it reduces to a one-step generator, for multi-modal states it uses multiple steps. It trains a rectified flow policy via a straightness-penalized objective and a separate variance prediction network to determine state uncertainty. Experiments on a 2D maze environment and a simulated robot manipulation benchmark evaluate success rate, behavioral diversity, and inference speed.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of rectified flow to imitation learning with an adaptive mechanism** that controls generation efficiency based on demonstration variance (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The method directly addresses the trade-off between inference speed and behavioral diversity that has been a challenge in traditional BC and diffusion-based IL (Reviewer 1).
- **Proposes new evaluation metrics** beyond success rate, including behavioral diversity and computational efficiency, and provides detailed empirical analysis (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Straightforward implementation and rapid training** offering practical advantages (Reviewer 1).
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The combination of rectified flow and offline IL seems intuitively sound (Reviewer 2).
- **Achieves a balance between computational complexity and policy diversity** (Reviewer 3). Empirical results show high performance on both efficiency and multi-modal generation (Reviewer 4).
- **Provides a proof (Theorem 1)** to explain why rectified flow can improve training/inference efficiency and incorporates state variance estimation to maintain multi-modal generation (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical gap between Eq.(8) objective and Alg.1 implementation**: The implementation trains the rectified flow policy and a variance prediction network separately. The variance prediction network determines the number of update iterations at inference. The paper does not explain why this implementation is justified from Eq.(8), and the performance gain in training may only come from rectified flow rather than the proposed solution as a whole (Reviewer 1, high confidence). A direct question remains: does using rectified flow to replace DDPM within DDIM lead to similar performance? (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient novelty and contribution magnitude** (Reviewer 2, high confidence, contribution rating 1). The unique technical contribution beyond standard diffusion-based offline IL is only the use of rectified flow for acceleration (apart from a log-variance regularizer). The paper does not demonstrate that rectified flow is unique or best for this setting—other models such as Consistency Models or TRACT could claim similar speedups (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation studies and comparisons with low-NFE samplers** such as UniPC, Heun, Consistency Models, TRACT. The baseline comparison only uses DDIM with 20 steps (Figure 5). It is unclear how much relative loss would be incurred by DDIM10, DDIM5, or other samplers, undermining the claimed NFE speedup advantage (Reviewer 2).
- **Weak empirical support for core claims**:
    - Only two benchmark datasets are evaluated (2D maze and simulated robot manipulation; a third is in the appendix with comparable performance). The abstract claims high performance across all dimensions, but this is not sufficiently demonstrated (Reviewer 4, high confidence). More realistic domains (e.g., Atari-100k with expert trajectories) and real-world robot benchmarks are needed (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
    - **Assumption 1** (that most demonstration datasets exhibit deterministic/uni-modal states) is not validated empirically or mathematically. No data distribution visualization is provided; the paper should support this claim with experimental evidence (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 2's concern about domain scope).
    - **Theorem 1** and its implication: the loss function (Eq.5) goes to zero as the variance of action given state reaches zero. While this yields efficient inference for deterministic states, it *prevents* the model from generating multi-modal behaviors in high-variance states—counter to the purpose of using diffusion models for multi-modal imitation learning. A detailed proof of Theorem 1 is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with established baselines**: No offline RL methods (CQL, BCQ) nor other diffusion-based IL methods (Diffuser, Diffusion-QL, Decision Diffuser, Diffusion BC) are included. Comparisons are limited to BC and Diffusion Policy (DDIM-based) (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Mechanism of the variance prediction network unclear**: It is trained on offline data containing both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, but the paper does not explain how it isolates aleatoric uncertainty (critical for identifying multi-modal states) from epistemic uncertainty (Reviewer 3). How does the network determine whether a state is uni-modal or multi-modal? (Reviewer 3)
- **Figure 1 is not visually convincing**: The claimed difference between DDIM (red) and RF-Policy (blue) in deterministic regions (x<0) is not readily apparent. This undermines the core argument that rectified flow generates straighter trajectories for computational efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- **Confusing experimental presentation**:
    - Figure 4 textual description may not correspond correctly to the picture (Reviewer 4).
    - Table 1: Maze5 only shows SR (success rate), Maze6 only shows DS (diversity score); incomplete reporting (Reviewer 4).
    - Measurement units for training and execution time differ (as stated in Section 5.3), making combined visualization in Figure 5 potentially inappropriate (Reviewer 4).
    - Experimental results calculation (e.g., number of seeds, epochs) is missing for Table 1 and Table 2 (Reviewer 4).
