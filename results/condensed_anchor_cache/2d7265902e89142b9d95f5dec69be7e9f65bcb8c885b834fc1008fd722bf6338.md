## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) when state observations are adversarially perturbed. It introduces a new optimality definition, Resilient State Optimality (RSO), for State-Adversarial Markov Decision Processes (SA-MDPs) and proposes the SAMM-IRL algorithm, which adapts Max-Margin IRL to this setting. The algorithm learns reward features from perturbed demonstrations. Theoretical convergence and optimality guarantees are provided, and empirical validation is conducted in a grid‑world environment.

### Strengths
- **Novel optimality definition and adaptation**: The introduction of RSO for SA‑MDPs and the adaptation of Max‑Margin IRL to adversarial settings are considered novel by Reviewers 1 and 3. Reviewer 1 highlights this as a novel approach in IRL. Reviewer 3 notes the work is novel for IRL under adversarial perturbations.
- **Theoretical foundation**: Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 praise the thorough and sound theoretical analysis, including proofs for convergence and optimality (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 finds the theoretical analysis solid.
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is well‑structured, clear, and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewers 2 and 3 note the logical outline and self‑contained presentation.
- **Empirical demonstration**: Experiments in a grid‑world show that SAMM‑IRL approximates expert behavior under adversarial conditions and improves over traditional IRL techniques (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and practical relevance**:
  * The combined problem of IRL with adversarial state observations lacks a real‑world use‑case or clear motivation (Reviewer 2). The paper does not argue why treating both simultaneously is important.
  * Reviewer 3 is not convinced that adversarial disturbances are common in real applications; the connection from robust RL to IRL remains unclear.
  * Expert’s behavior under perturbations is not well defined: are the demonstrations themselves perturbed, or does the expert use an adversarially robust policy? (Reviewer 3)
- **Novelty concerns**:
  * Reviewer 2 finds the work incremental: the objective function is a minor modification of Zhang et al. (2020) (taking expectation under initial state distribution instead of vector value function), and the algorithm simply combines existing concepts (SA‑MDP + Max‑Margin IRL).
  * Reviewer 4 states that the RSO optimality definition is **not new**; a similar approach already appears in Appendix C.4 of Han et al. (2022) for multi‑agent adversarial RL. Moreover, the bi‑level optimization (Figure 3a) resembles the alternative training method of Zhang et al. (2021), and applying Max‑Margin IRL to a robust agent policy is straightforward.
  * In contrast, Reviewers 1 and 3 view the work as novel; this disagreement is noted.
- **Technical issues and missing details**:
  * Several definitions are missing: formal definition of policies in Section 3.1 (Reviewer 2); the role of \(\pi_E\) and dataset \(\mathcal{D}\) is unclear in Eq. (3) (Reviewer 2). Sections 3.2 and 3.3 should be merged (Reviewer 2).
  * Assumptions Assmp 1 and Assmp 2 are presented as modeling choices or methodology outlines, not as theoretical assumptions (Reviewer 2).
  * Definitions 2 and 3 are confusing: Eq. (10) and (11) differ in handling the observed perturbed state; the term “believed” is misleading because the agent actually observes the perturbed state, not a belief (Reviewer 2).
  * Line 146 raises a difficulty: the hyperplane separation condition \(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mu_{\pi_{\mathrm{E}}} > \mathbf{w}^{\top}\mu_{\pi}\) may not hold for all policies if multiple optimal policies exist (Reviewer 2).
  * The perturbation set \(B(s)\) is an arbitrary subset of \(\mathcal{S}\); no compactness/convexity assumption is given, which may be needed to find optimal perturbed states (Reviewer 2).
  * Lines 306‑308 assume the expert policy is optimal under the optimal weight; this strong assumption should be presented as an environment assumption (Reviewer 2).
  * Typo in Eq. (12) (Reviewer 2).
  * The concept of RSO is not fully clear: can \(P_0\) be any distribution, and how does it differ from Eq. (7)? (Reviewer 3)
  * The bounds in Theorems 3‑4 use \(\delta\) for state disturbance, but the problem statement uses set \(B\) – is \(B\) a ball of radius \(\delta\)? The relevance and the separate use of \(\epsilon\) and \(\epsilon_0\) in (15) and (16) are unclear (Reviewer 3).
  * Corollary 1 appears redundant – it seems to be a variable substitution of Eq. (14) (Reviewer 3).
  * SA‑MDP Figure 1 is inaccurate: the gray dashed actions should not be drawn between perturbed states because actions still apply to original states (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental limitations**:
  * Experiments are confined to a grid‑world environment, which may not capture the challenges of dynamic or continuous environments (Reviewer 1). Reviewers 2 and 4 call for more complex environments (e.g., Atari used in SA‑MDP literature). Reviewer 2 notes that Fig. 5 lacks running seeds and standard deviation; Fig. 5 also appears before Fig. 4.
- **Missing comparisons and positioning**:
  * The paper does not compare against out‑of‑the‑shelf IRL methods that handle reward misspecification (e.g., Hadfield‑Menell 2017, Skalse 2023). Could the problem be reformulated as IRL under reward misspecification with reward \(r^* \circ \nu\)? (Reviewer 2)
  * The paper lacks positioning within the broader IRL literature and cites too few related works (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions indicating missing analyses or experiments** (converted to weaknesses per instructions):
  * Could SAMM‑IRL work for settings beyond SA‑MDPs? (Reviewer 1)
  * Can it be adapted to multi‑agent adversarial settings? What modifications are needed? (Reviewer 1)
  * How would performance change if adversarial strategies vary dynamically (time‑varying perturbation set)? (Reviewer 1)
  * Can the algorithm scale to Atari or more complicated environments? (Reviewers 1, 4)
  * Could the authors restate the contribution in light of prior work (Han et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2021)? (Reviewer 4)
