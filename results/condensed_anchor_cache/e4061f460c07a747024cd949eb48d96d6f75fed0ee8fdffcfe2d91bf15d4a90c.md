## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PIRATE, a deformable image registration method that integrates an explicit data-fidelity penalty with a pre-trained CNN denoiser as a regularizer in a plug-and-play (PnP) framework. The denoiser is pre-trained on additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) and plugged into an iterative solver. PIRATE+ then fine-tunes this denoiser using deep equilibrium models (DEQ) to form a task-specific regularizer. The methods are evaluated on OASIS and CANDI brain MRI datasets. Reviewers note the novelty of applying PnP to registration and the use of DEQ for fine-tuning. However, they also raise concerns about the lack of intuitive explanation of why denoising is a suitable prior, insufficient ablation studies, limited evaluation (single modality, no group analysis), high inference time relative to other deep learning methods, and readability issues from heavy acronym use.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of plug-and-play to deformable registration.** The paper is the first to use PnP priors for this task, and the integration of a CNN denoiser as a regularizer is an innovative contribution.
- **Use of deep equilibrium models (DEQ).** The adaptation of DEQ to fine-tune the regularizer within PnP iterations is unique and reasonable, yielding noticeable gains in Tables 1 and 2.
- **Strong quantitative results on standard benchmarks.** PIRATE and PIRATE+ achieve state-of-the-art Dice scores on OASIS and CANDI, outperforming recent methods including Mok and Chung (CVPR 2020, MICCAI 2020) and other deep learning and iterative approaches.
- **Comprehensive coverage of related work.** The review of the state of the art is well done and provides solid context for the proposed methods.
- **Qualitative visual results.** Figure 3 convincingly demonstrates superiority over existing methods in visual comparisons (though one reviewer notes this is a single instance, see weaknesses).
- **Training loss convergence.** The training loss curves show stable convergence.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of intuitive and theoretical justification for the plug-and-play denoiser prior.** The paper does not explain why a denoiser pre-trained on AWGN is appropriate for regularizing deformation fields. What statistical properties of deformation fields make denoising a plausible prior? Is the noise assumed Gaussian, and if so, why? The connection between the learned denoiser and desired field characteristics (smoothness, topology preservation) is absent. This makes it unclear if the pre-trained denoiser provides meaningful regularization or an arbitrary constraint.
- **Missing ablation studies.** No ablation justifies the use of a pre-trained denoiser versus a randomly initialized denoiser or a model without any denoiser. The performance gain could stem from other factors (e.g., iterative optimization) rather than the denoiser itself.
- **Limited evaluation scope.**
  - Only brain MRI datasets (OASIS, CANDI) are used. Generalization to other anatomies (CT, body images) or other imaging modalities remains unproven. One reviewer suggests using a non-brain dataset with landmarks (e.g., EMPIRE10) to compute target registration error (TRE).
  - Figure 3 presents a single-instance qualitative analysis rather than a group or statistical analysis. The paper needs group-level validation to demonstrate generalizability.
  - The compared methods use very different loss functions, cost functions, and deformation parameterizations, making the Jacobian-based comparisons difficult to interpret; Dice is more meaningful.
- **High inference time.** The iterative optimization-based nature of PIRATE leads to significantly longer run-times than feed-forward deep learning methods (e.g., VoxelMorph). This is a major disadvantage for clinical applications. The paper does not analyze the trade-off between accuracy gains and computational overhead. Runtime and memory consumption should be reported for fixed-size inputs.
- **Incremental quantitative improvements.** Many improvements are in the second decimal place of Dice. Given the much higher inference time, the practical significance for downstream tasks is unclear.
- **Insufficient analysis of the DEQ model.** The paper does not analyze convergence of the number of function evaluations (NFE) during training. Understanding NFE convergence is crucial for assessing the stability and efficiency of the deep equilibrium approach.
- **Unclear motivation for PIRATE+.** What specific limitation of the generic denoiser does PIRATE+ address? The paper does not explain what “task-specific regularization” means or how the fine-tuned regularizer differs from the pre-trained one.
- **Readability and organization issues.**
  - The methods section starts with iteration updates before stating the optimization objective or explaining the prior, making it hard to follow. It would benefit from an intuitive overview (e.g., using pointers to Figure 1).
  - Acronyms are overused and not always defined (e.g., “DU” is mentioned without clarification). A comprehensive list of acronyms is missing.
- **Limited comparison against other regularization techniques.** The paper does not discuss why PnP is advantageous over more traditional smoothness priors (e.g., diffusion, bending energy) or other learned regularizers.

*(Preserving minority contrast: Two reviewers (scores 6,6) find the evaluation insufficient and the method lacks rigorous justification and ablation, while the other two (scores 8,8) emphasize the novelty and strong results, though they also note the high runtime and limited modality generalization.)*
