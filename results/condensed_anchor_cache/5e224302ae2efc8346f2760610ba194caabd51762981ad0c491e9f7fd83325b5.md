## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for decentralized training of large transformer models on heterogeneous and unreliable devices, combining parameter server architectures, pipeline parallelism, and a task pool to handle disconnections. Reviewers find the problem interesting and relevant, but criticize insufficient novelty, unclear formalization (missing decision variables, undefined terms), inappropriate experiment setups that fail to validate the key design, lack of ablation studies, and poor presentation (small figures, unclear writing, missing code). Two reviewers (scores 1,1) judge the work severely limited by missing technical contributions and formal rigor; two reviewers (scores 3,3) acknowledge the empirical coverage but still see major gaps in novelty and evaluation.

### Strengths
- Leveraging underutilized globally-distributed GPUs for training large models is promising and could offer a cost-effective alternative to expensive data center-based training.
- The hybrid parallelism method (parameter servers + pipeline parallelism) addresses inefficiencies in existing decentralized frameworks and demonstrates the possibility of using geo-distributed heterogeneous devices to train foundation models.
- Distinguishing between unreliable and reliable devices – where reliable devices act as parameter servers for data parallelism and relay activations/gradients – is a different approach from Yuan et al. (2022).
- Experiments cover multiple scenarios including varying device reliability, communication bandwidth, and hardware heterogeneity, providing broad empirical support.
- The research question is highly relevant given the growing interest in decentralized and cost-effective large-model training.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and unclear contribution relative to prior work.** The asynchronous pipeline scheduling largely mirrors asynchronous parameter server methods already studied. The paper does not clearly delineate which technical parts are new compared to Yuan et al. (2022), DeDLOC, PipeDream, DAPPLE, DT-FM, and Swarm. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3; high confidence)
- **Optimization problem lacks formal definition.** No decision (unknown) variables are introduced. In Yuan et al. (2022) the decision variables σ defined the mapping between tasks and devices; this work omits such formalism. (Reviewer 3; high confidence)
- **Key notation is undefined.** It is unclear what \(t_i\) and \(t_s\) represent – whether \(i\) indexes a client, task, or device – and whether they are known or unknown variables. (Reviewer 3; high confidence)
- **Missing algorithmic description.** The clustering algorithm and its post-processing steps are mentioned but no formal description or pseudocode is provided. (Reviewer 3; high confidence)
- **Experiment setups inappropriate to validate the core design.** ALBERT-xxlarge (223M parameters) does not require pipeline parallelism. For Llama-33B training with only pipeline parallelism, 16 V100 GPUs (requiring offloading >500GB to CPU/disk) shifts the bottleneck to data movement, making the impact of heterogeneous GPU/network conditions small. If data parallelism is enabled as claimed, ~2000GB memory is needed even with degree 2. Thus experiments cannot show how asynchronous pipeline scheduling affects training efficiency. (Reviewer 4; high confidence)
- **Insufficient ablation studies.** The improvements from scheduling for heterogeneous communication, heterogeneous devices, and unreliable devices are not isolated. Case 2 in Table 2 only varies communication, yet the scheduling by [1] (Yuan et al.) already solves that scenario. Cases 3 and 4 should compare with PipeDream, Dapple, and Swarm using their own scheduling optimized for heterogeneous communication. (Reviewer 4; high confidence)
- **Unrealistic assumption about device reliability.** The framework assumes stable-network devices can serve as parameter servers, but GPU failures on reliable networks can still cause total parameter loss. (Reviewer 1; confidence 5)
- **Scalability and complexity not discussed.** The complexity of the solution and its scalability to larger, more variable setups are not addressed. (Reviewer 2; confidence 4)
- **Missing runtime analysis of the optimization algorithm.** No numbers are reported for how long the optimization takes, and it is unclear whether failures in unreliable devices require a full restart or can be handled online. (Reviewer 3; confidence 4)
- **Missing integration of other parallelism forms.** The framework does not discuss how tensor parallelism or sequence parallelism could be incorporated. (Reviewer 4; confidence 4)
- **Presentation and writing issues.** All figures are too small to read; the paper contains writing errors (e.g., wrong title, citation format); mathematical formulations could be replaced by textual descriptions or illustrations. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4; high confidence)
- **Source code not provided.** Given the substantial engineering effort, reviewers request code to better understand the system. (Reviewer 4; confidence 4)
