## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MIGA, a Mixture-of-Experts with Group Aggregation framework for stock market prediction. It dynamically switches between specialized style experts and introduces inner group attention to share information among experts in the same group. Experiments on three Chinese stock index benchmarks (CSI300, CSI500, CSI1000) show improved performance over previous end-to-end models.

### Strengths
- The underlying problem—variations in stock styles and market trends—is well-motivated and sound.
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow; Figure 2 clearly illustrates the MIGA framework.
- The approach of combining multiple specialized experts with a group aggregation mechanism is novel and distinguishes the work from prior methods.
- Extensive experiments are conducted on three major Chinese Stock Index benchmarks (CSI300, CSI500, CSI1000), providing robust evaluation across different market conditions.
- The choice of metrics is suitable and comprehensive: annualized return (profit), information ratio (risk), ICIR and RankICIR (predictive power).
- The model shows consistent improvements across these metrics, with a large margin over previous SOTA (e.g., MIGA-Conv achieves 24% excess annual return on CSI300, surpassing prior SOTA by 8% absolute – Reviewer 1 highlights this as a major strength).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing MoE baseline comparisons:** No existing MoE baselines are compared. Specifically, the paper does not compare against conventional MoE or the closely related AlphaMix [Sun et al., *Mastering stock markets with efficient mixture of diversified trading experts*]. The authors should either add these comparisons or explain why they are infeasible.
- **Novelty concern relative to AlphaMix:** The motivation and core idea are very similar to AlphaMix. The main differences appear to be the router (which filters out some experts) and the inner group attention module; this incremental novelty was noted as limited by two reviewers. The paper should explicitly clarify the differences and include AlphaMix as a baseline.
- **Ablation results do not strongly support claimed contributions:**
    - Inner group attention ablation (Table 3) shows performance on par or degraded in several metrics, failing to demonstrate clear effectiveness. The authors should report statistical significance.
    - Scaling the number of experts has minimal impact on performance, raising questions about the necessity and effectiveness of using many experts.
- **Figure 3 is unclear:** No detailed explanation is given for why model performances differ at the beginning of training while training losses are similar. The visualization should be improved and this phenomenon explained.
- **Limited related work discussion:** The related work section is short and misses several relevant works, including but not limited to [2] Feng et al. (adversarial training), [3] Xu et al. (HIST), [4] Gao et al. (DiffsFormer), [5] Huang et al. (generative learning for financial time series).
- **Dataset generality:** Experiments are only performed on Chinese stock datasets; results may not generalize to other markets.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not analyzed:** The paper does not discuss sensitivity of the model to hyperparameters for the inner group attention and dynamic switching mechanism, nor how changes affect performance.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 4) was notably more positive, emphasizing the large performance margin; Reviewer 2 (score 3, confidence 3) and Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 4) were more critical regarding novelty, missing baselines, and weak ablation evidence.
