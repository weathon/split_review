## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Data-free Mesh Mover (DMM) that uses the Monge-Ampère equation to adapt mesh nodes in an unsupervised manner, integrated with a GNN-based neural PDE solver (MM-PDE) to improve accuracy. Experiments on 2D Burgers' equation and flow past cylinder show improved accuracy over fixed-mesh baselines. Reviewers find the idea novel and the method sound in principle but raise concerns about efficiency, missing runtime comparisons, lack of mesh tangling evaluation, absence of ablation studies for the loss components, limited experimental setup (fixed resolution, no generalization), and incremental contribution relative to existing neural PDE solver.

### Strengths
- Novel application of a data-free neural mesh adapter (DMM) using a Monge-Ampère motivated physics loss for mesh movement, trained without optimal mesh data. (Reviewers 2,3)
- Detailed explanation of the framework and background; appendix provides sufficient technical detail on results. (Reviewer 2)
- Method applies well in practice, yielding accuracy improvements (e.g., from 2×10⁻⁶ to 6.7×10⁻⁷ in Table 1, and from 0.0258 to 0.0141 in Table 2). (Reviewers 1,2)
- Use of BFGS for memory-intensive training and the sampling method for the Monge-Ampère equation are sound design choices. (Reviewer 3)
- The adaptive mesh approach has the potential to improve accuracy over static meshes. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Efficiency and runtime not properly demonstrated.** Missing runtime comparisons with baselines (GNN, CNN, FNO, LAMP) and overhead of DMM in terms of time and extra trainable parameters. (Reviewers 1,2) One reviewer questions whether using a larger GNN without DMM could achieve the same error more quickly. (Reviewer 1)
- **Contribution is incremental.** The core neural PDE solver is essentially the message-passing GNN from Brandstetter et al., with DMM as an add-on, limiting novelty. (Reviewer 1) (Note: Reviewers 2 and 3 rate contribution as 3/good and highlight novelty, so there is disagreement.)
- **Mesh tangling is not evaluated.** The r-adaptive (mesh movement) method is prone to tangling (negative Jacobian), and the soft physics loss does not guarantee tangling-free meshes. No analysis or evaluation of mesh quality (e.g., cell volume, Jacobian) is provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of ablation study for the loss components.** The physical loss contains three terms (loss_equation, loss_bound, loss_convex) but their individual contributions are not analyzed. The necessity of each term is unclear. (Reviewer 3) Also, training DMM with Adam+BFGS finetuning lacks justification; why Adam alone is insufficient, and memory consumption numbers are not given. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited experimental scope.** Experiments are conducted only at one fixed mesh resolution (48×48 or 2521 triangular nodes) and on two 2D problems. No tests on generalization to different resolutions, boundary conditions, or physical parameters. (Reviewer 3) Missing baselines on non-uniform grids (e.g., GeoFNO) for the flow past cylinder case. (Reviewer 2) For time-dependent problems, it is unclear whether the mesh moved for u_k remains suitable for u_{k+1}. (Reviewer 1)
- **Evaluation metric and reporting gaps.** Why not compute interpolation error directly instead of an upper bound? (Reviewer 1) Missing details on MSE computation (velocity only or pressure) in Table 2; values appear high, and no rollout visualizations provided. (Reviewer 2) The claim about "two-order" improvement should be "second order". (Reviewer 1)
- **Cell volume metric not connected to final performance.** It is unclear whether reducing cell volume standard deviation and range monotonically decreases test loss; end-to-end training may violate the DMM equations and the post-training mesh quality is not quantified. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation issues.** Figure 2(a) does not show the DMM model architecture; Figure 2 shows 20×20 mesh rather than the stated 48×48. (Reviewer 3) Overall presentation and text need improvement. (Reviewers 2,3)
