## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LaneSegNet, an end-to-end mapping network that predicts “lane segments”—a new representation combing geometry (lanelines) and topology (centerlines) for road structure. Built on a BEVFormer-like architecture with a lane attention module and identical initialization of reference points, the method achieves improved results on OpenLane-V2 across map element detection (+4.8 mAP), centerline perception (+6.9 DET_l), and lane segment perception (+5.6 mAP), and runs at 14.7 FPS. Reviewers acknowledge the novel representation and strong performance but raise concerns about motivation, dataset breadth, writing clarity, and the necessity of the new task.

### Strengths
- **Strong results on OpenLane-V2:** The model outperforms prior methods substantially across three tasks, and the tiny variant also surpasses baselines. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Novel joint prediction of geometry and topology:** The lane segment representation integrates centerline and laneline in one output, a unique contribution compared to prior works such as MapTR (hierarchical queries) or VectorMapNet. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Ablation studies validate design choices:** Ablations on attention mechanisms and reference point initialization confirm the efficacy of the proposed components. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Innovative architectural modifications:** The heads-to-regions mechanism and distributed reference points improve feature aggregation for both long-range and local image features; identical initialization enhances training stability. (Reviewer 4)
- **Discussion of regression in AP_div:** The paper acknowledges and discusses the observed regression in AP_div. (Reviewer 1)
- **Generalization to other datasets (shown in rebuttal):** Additional experiments on other datasets (mentioned in rebuttal) indicate generalization. (Reviewer 1)
- **Research direction:** The work contributes to the trend of directly predicting structured representations for mapping. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for new task insufficiently justified:** Reviewers question why lane segments (explicit left/right boundaries) are superior to centerlines with attributes. The redundancy in representing boundaries (e.g., V1_right and V2_left) raises efficiency and necessity concerns. The paper does not thoroughly discuss limitations of existing representations or the specific advantages of the proposed one. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Writing and presentation:** Section 3 (method) lacks sufficient detail, and Section 3.3 (training loss) describes losses (segmentation, regression) too briefly without citations or hyperparameters. Figures 2 and 3 are too abstract; Figure 2 looks like a generic map prediction architecture, and Figure 3 does not clearly illustrate how the lane attention module operates. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited dataset evaluation:** Only OpenLane-V2 is used for all tasks. No experiments on established datasets like nuScenes to calibrate results, making reported gains hard to interpret. The paper mentions Argoverse 2 on page 7 but does not include it in experiments. (Reviewers 1,3)  (Minority: Reviewer 1 notes rebuttal shows generalization, but the paper itself lacks this.)
- **Definition of “long-range” unclear:** The OpenLane-V2 dataset reannotates lanes into shorter segments, so “long-range” is not a clear challenge. The paper does not quantitatively define what constitutes long-range in this context. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential artifacts from manual lane segmentation:** Dividing lanes into segments without visual cues may introduce artificial discontinuities and inconsistencies, challenging model learning and not clearly critical for autonomous driving. (Reviewer 4)
- **Hungarian assignment is slow/unstable:** The one-to-one optimal assignment with Hungarian algorithm is typically slow and difficult to train, especially early in training. (Reviewer 2)
- **Low absolute metrics:** While showing improvements, the absolute AP and DET values are still low; it would be valuable to see which lane types the model handles well enough for real autonomous driving use. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited novelty in topology prediction:** The topology prediction does not introduce new mechanisms; gains may be a byproduct of the lane segment representation rather than a topology learning advancement. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing comparisons with stronger baselines:** MapTRV2 is a stronger baseline but not compared in the experiments. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient detail on reference point initialization:** How multiple reference points are uniformly initialized inside a lane segment through positional query is not discussed in detail. (Reviewer 4)
- **Overlapping lane segment outputs:** For a lane segment {A_left, A, A_right}, each point appears in three segments; the method for combining these to get a final result for e.g., point A is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
- **Clarity of acronyms:** Acronyms like OLS and DET should be defined for readers. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lane segment length:** The paper does not specify the length of each lane segment. (Reviewer 2)
- **Impact on downstream tasks:** No evaluation of the map’s effect on motion planning/prediction. (Reviewer 1)
