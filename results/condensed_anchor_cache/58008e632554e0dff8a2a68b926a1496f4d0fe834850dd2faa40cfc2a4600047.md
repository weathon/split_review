## Merged Review

### Summary
DiffVAS proposes a target-conditioned visual active search (VAS) framework for multi-target search in partially observable environments. It uses a diffusion-based Conditional Generative Module (CGM) to reconstruct the full search space from partial observations, and a reinforcement learning-based Target-Conditioned Planning Module (TCPM) to balance exploration and exploitation. Experiments on DOTA and xView claim superior performance over baselines. Reviewers note practical motivation and good results, but raise major concerns about unrealistic simplifications (teleportation, fixed altitude), insufficient novelty (overlap with DiffMAE, MDT), missing training details, incomplete ablation studies, and lack of real-world applicability validation.

### Strengths
- Problem formulation (TC-POVAS) is original and practical, addressing the realistic constraint that an aerial agent cannot know the entire search space upfront and must search for multiple target categories simultaneously. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Integrating a diffusion model to reconstruct the scene from partial glimpses is a technically sound and simple approach, enabling the planning module to reason with more complete information. (R1, R3, R4)
- The TCPM reward design (combining reconstruction quality, active search success, and local uncertainty) is a clear and effective way to balance exploration and exploitation for the RL policy. (R1, R3)
- Extensive experiments on multiple satellite datasets (DOTA, xView) cover single-class, multi-class, and zero-shot settings, showing quantitative improvements over prior VAS methods (E2EVAS, MPS-VAS). (R1, R2, R3, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic action space:** The framework allows the agent to teleport to any grid cell, ignoring the visual information that would be gained during movement between cells. This oversimplification may produce policies that cannot transfer to physical UAVs where path planning and continuous motion matter. (R1, also partially noted by R4)
- **Overly simplified search environment:** The task abstracts UAVs to a fixed altitude and a discrete grid. Real-world search requires variable altitudes, multi-scale observations, and dynamic adjustments. The current setup does not capture this complexity. (R4)
- **Limited novelty relative to prior diffusion/MAE works:** The core idea of reconstructing the full scene from partial observations has already been explored in DiffMAE (ICCV23) and MDT (ICCV23). The technical contribution over these works is incremental and not clearly articulated. (R2) *[Disagreement: R1 and R3 praise the originality of the problem formulation, but R2 considers the technical framework itself insufficiently novel.]*
- **Lack of real-world validation:** The experimental data/environments may not adequately simulate real-world incomplete information and sensor noise. Without demonstration that the policy works under realistic conditions (e.g., variable weather, detection noise, occlusions), the practical benefit claimed by the motivation remains unsubstantiated. (R2, R4)
- **Missing training details:** The paper omits optimization algorithms, learning rates, batch sizes, number of epochs, and a clear description of how the diffusion model (CGM) is trained and integrated with TCPM. This hinders reproducibility. (R1)
- **Incomplete ablation on reward terms:** The combination \(R^{AS} + R^{LU}\) is not tested, leaving unclear whether the two rewards interact synergistically or one dominates. (R3)
- **Cross-attention role insufficiently analyzed:** The cross-attention between query and target features in TCPM is only briefly mentioned. An ablation or case study showing its specific impact on performance is missing. (R4)
- **Over-reliance on a single metric (ANT):** The evaluation uses Average Number of Targets (ANT) but does not report search efficiency metrics such as path length or cost-effectiveness, which are important for real deployment. (R4)
- **High computational cost:** The diffusion model reconstruction introduces significant overhead; the authors do not provide runtime estimates or discuss feasibility for real-time UAV deployment. (R4)
- **Unclear experimental setup for multi-class and zero-shot:** Only three multi-class combinations are tested without justification, and it is not stated whether zero-shot categories are completely disjoint from training categories. This makes it hard to assess generalization. (R1, R3 also noted ambiguous phrasing "solely on DOTA on xView")
- **Baseline adaptation not explained:** It is unclear how prior methods (E2EVAS, MPS-VAS) that assume full scene knowledge are adapted to the partially observable task without full information. (R1)
- **Missing upper-bound baseline:** Not including a version of TCPM that receives ground-truth full observations to measure the ideal performance of the planner and quantify how much CGM helps. (R4)
- **Oversimplified detection assumption:** The framework ignores detection noise, occlusions, and variable scene complexity. In practice, object detection in aerial images is imperfect and would affect search decisions. (R4)
- **Writing and formatting issues:** Inconsistent capitalization of symbols ("Sgn" in eq. 6/7), Figure 3 has font/capitalization problems and a missing parenthesis, and the zero-shot section contains a confusing expression ("solely on DOTA on xView"). (R3)
