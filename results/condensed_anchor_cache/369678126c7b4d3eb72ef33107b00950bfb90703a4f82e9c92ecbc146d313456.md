## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EraseDiff, an unlearning method for diffusion models. Reviewer assessments range from scores 3 to 5, indicating disagreement. Two reviewers (R1, R2) found the problem important, the approach novel, and the method computationally efficient, but they also noted that empirical performance was not convincingly better than baselines and that some parts needed clearer explanation. Two reviewers (R3, R4) were more critical, emphasizing poor clarity (especially in notation and problem definition), limited contribution relative to prior work, and insufficient experimental evidence.

### Strengths
- **Problem importance**: All reviewers agree that machine unlearning for diffusion models is a timely and significant problem for privacy and copyright.
- **Novelty of approach**: R1 and R2 note the bi‑level/constrained optimization formulation is novel and includes Pareto‑optimal guarantees; R4 highlights that EraseDiff is computationally friendly.
- **Specific evidence of computational efficiency**: R1 points out that EraseDiff reduces computation time compared to competitive baselines, and R4 notes the source code is provided.
- **Empirical insight**: R1 highlights Figure 2, which empirically shows that EraseDiff reduces gradient conflict on CIFAR‑10.
- **Literature review**: R2 considers the review of prior work comprehensive.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and notation problems (multiple reviewers)**:  
  - Equation (2): The definition of \(\epsilon_f\) is never rigorously given; the expectation depends on \(\epsilon\) but \(\epsilon\) does not appear in the equation (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
  - Equation (4): \(L_f\) takes an extra undefined argument \(\phi_{init}\) compared to (2) (R1, R3).  
  - Equation (6): \(a_t\) is not explained (R4).  
  - The notation for \(\epsilon_\theta(x,t)\) and \(\epsilon_\theta(x|c)\) is used without clear definition (R3).  
  - R1 notes that Line 205 (“\(\nabla_{\phi=\theta} L(\phi,D_f)=0\)”) is ambiguous: unclear whether this holds for any \(\phi\) reachable by optimization and whether it depends on algorithm/seed.  
  - R4 notes that Line 200 says “It is well known that” without a citation.

- **Problem definition not clearly stated**:  
  - R3 and R4 state that the unlearning problem is never well defined until the experiment section. The paper seems to conflate removing influence of specific data points versus erasing a class/concept, and the goal is not clearly conveyed (R3). R4 echoes that “unlearning” is not clearly defined in the introduction.

- **Empirical performance not convincingly superior to baselines**:  
  - Table 1: SA outperforms EraseDiff in both FID and \(P_{\psi}(y=c_f|x_f)\); precision and recall are nearly equal (R1).  
  - Table 2: ESD has a lower FID and nearly equal CLIP score (R1).  
  - Table 3: EraseDiff’s 99.91% is very close to 99.88% of a baseline – not a convincing win (R4).  
  - R2 notes that in Figure 3 and Table 2, SalUn performs very close to EraseDiff, if not better in some aspects.  
  - R1 and R4 both request standard deviations / error bars for all tables to judge significance; R1 mentions that without them a statement about recall decrease (Line 399) cannot be evaluated scientifically.

- **Limited experimental scope**:  
  - Table 1 only uses one class (airplane) for forgetting; R2 suggests more instances should be considered.  
  - R4 asks about performance on other diffusion models beyond the few tested.  
  - R1 asks whether experiments were run over multiple random seeds (affects required error bars).

- **Theoretical justification and formulation concerns**:  
  - R1 questions why the optimization problem in Eq. (2)–(3) uses a loss that is minimized on the forget set, while the introduction claims to “maximize that over the forgetting data.” R4 similarly asks whether Eq. (2) should be maximized instead.  
  - R1 asks how the proposed formulation relates to traditional definitions of unlearning.  
  - R4 asks about the relationship between solving Eq. (5) and bilevel optimization.

- **Contribution and quality**:  
  - R3 states the methodology contribution to prior works seems limited, and overall paper quality does not meet the bar for the conference.

- **Minor**: R4 notes that a separate supplementary file is unnecessary if the appendix is already included in the main submission.
