## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Well-NeRF, a plug-and-play regularization module for sparse-view NeRF based on two assumptions: (1) points outside the intersection of all view frustums are unlearnable, (2) points inside opaque surfaces are unobservable and should not contribute. The method uses a Frustum Score to mask loss and scale gradients, and a Shadow Zone regularization that blends colors along rays to encourage interior points to take on surface colors. Experiments are conducted on Blender synthetic (NeRF Synthetic) and real-world datasets (NeRF datasets, plus an in-the-wild scene captured by the authors). The method is compared mainly against nerfacto and FreeNeRF. Reviewer scores are 6, 6, 5, 3, 6. Reviewer 4 (rating 3) is notably more negative, citing insufficient comparisons, unclear opacity handling, and unconvincing automatic adjustment claim.

### Strengths
1. **Simple and intuitive ideas.** The two assumptions (view frustum intersection and shadow zone) are insightful, logical, and well-founded (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Turning simple observations into concrete implementations that improve results is a strength (Reviewer 2).
2. **Plug-and-play modularity.** The method can be integrated seamlessly with existing NeRF frameworks without scene-specific optimization (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). It automatically adjusts the training space without additional parameter tuning (Reviewer 4).
3. **Clear presentation and visuals.** Figures 1 and 3 are helpful for understanding (Reviewer 2). Paper organization is well done (Reviewer 1).
4. **Impressive experimental results on tested datasets.** Results on Blender synthetic and real-world benchmark datasets (NeRF datasets) show improvement (Reviewers 2, 3). Ablation studies are extensive for the covered settings (Reviewer 2).
5. **Fundamental analysis.** The problem of boundary conditions in the learning space is fundamental in 3D reconstruction and may inspire other 3D tasks (Reviewer 3).
6. **Ease of combination.** The method does not require changing other network settings (Reviewer 5). Some reviewers (2, 3) also note the method’s simplicity and general applicability.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient theoretical proof and incremental contribution.** The paper lacks strong theoretical justification for why the method works; the contribution is simple and incremental without insightful design (Reviewer 1: soundness 2). Reviewer 5 raises technical questions: the Frustum Score is constant per sample point; why not directly use σ_masked in integration instead of further constraining sparsity? Why clip gradients in Equation 6 instead of directly using masked densities? (Reviewer 5). These design choices lack explanation.
2. **Insufficient comparisons to state-of-the-art methods.** The only direct baselines are nerfacto and FreeNeRF (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). Missing comparisons with RegNeRF, ZeroRF, PixelNeRF, and other learning-based sparse-view approaches (Reviewers 3, 2). The FreeNeRF comparison is potentially unfair: different network structures and hash acceleration make direct comparison questionable (Reviewer 5). No comparison with 3D Gaussian Splatting, despite being the current mainstream approach (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
3. **Limited dataset diversity and missing experiments.** Experiments are predominantly on NeRF Synthetic dataset (small scenes); this weakens real-world applicability (Reviewer 1). No experiments on large-scale datasets (Reviewer 1). Missing ablation on number of views: method’s sensitivity and scalability as input views vary from very few to many are not explored (Reviewer 3). Missing ablation on the loss weight λ in Equation 9, the number of sampling points (Equation 7), and different near/far plane settings (e.g., [2,6] vs. [0.05,1000]) (Reviewers 1, 3). Results appear sensitive to near/far plane parameters, suggesting potential overfitting to the chosen setting (Reviewer 3).
4. **Shadow Zone Regularization concerns.** The method lacks an explicit opacity threshold to determine the opaque surface. Equation 7 blends colors along rays, but without a clear mechanism to ensure the regularization applies only to the interior of objects, it may affect other regions (Reviewer 4). This could cause color bleeding from interior surface colors into other views or unconstrained foreground areas (Reviewer 5). The implicit opacity from the NeRF model makes it unclear how the regularization is constrained, especially with sparse views (Reviewer 4).
5. **Missing video results and novel view synthesis evaluation.** No video results are shown, making it hard to evaluate temporal consistency and view-consistency (Reviewer 4). While the method constrains NeRF, novel view synthesis results should be explicitly shown to rule out overfitting to the training data (Reviewer 5).
6. **Real-world in-the-wild results are of concerning quality.** The supplemental material shows poor quality for a real-world scene captured by the authors, suggesting a gap between synthetic/benchmark and true in-the-wild performance (Reviewer 2). This raises the broader question: with only 6 views, per-scene learning may be inferior to multi-scene learning-based approaches (e.g., PixelNeRF) that leverage priors (Reviewer 2).
7. **Automatic adjustment claim not fully convincing.** Manual adjustment of the bounding box is common and may achieve similar or better results; the proposed method’s advantage over a well-tuned bounding box is not clearly demonstrated (Reviewer 4).
8. **Writing and presentation issues.** Incorrect quotation marks at L45,47,50 (Reviewer 1, 4). Sentence at L406 unclear (Reviewer 1). Figures are too simple for full understanding (Reviewer 1). Title does not reflect the “sparse” context (Reviewer 5). Literature review missing recent 2024 works (Reviewer 5). Inconsistent symbol use: σ in Equation 3 should match Equation 1; unclear how S_norm in Equation 6 is computed (S/num_views?) (Reviewer 5).
9. **Missing visualizations requested.** Additional Frustum Score visualizations for qualitative results (Reviewer 4). Loss curves for the proposed regularizers (Reviewer 4). Visualization of RGB values of samples along rays to illustrate Shadow Zone behavior (Reviewer 4). Visualization of frustum scores for input views in the training setting (Reviewer 1).
10. **Lack of scalability to 3DGS.** The analysis is independent of scene representation; combining with 3DGS would strengthen the contribution and demonstrate broader applicability (Reviewers 3, 4).
