## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally find the paper well-structured, clearly written, and the results promising, with significant gains on less robust backbones like ViT-B/16 (e.g., +5.1 F1 on iWildCam, +3.1% WC Acc on FMoW). The insight of learning dataset-specific knowledge to complement frozen CLIP is considered convincing and effective. However, concerns are raised about limited novelty (most techniques except greedy text ensemble are not new), unclear necessity of two distinct mechanisms for injecting domain knowledge (CPNet and domain prompt appearing redundant), fairness of comparison given extra parameters in CPNet, and missing ablations and comparisons. The minority reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive about the contribution to the FSTT-DA community, while other reviewers (scores 5–6) have stronger criticisms regarding motivation, conceptual clarity, and analysis gaps.

### Strengths
- Writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to read (R1, R3, R4).
- Promising experimental results; achieves state-of-the-art on all five large-scale benchmarks (WILDS, DomainNet) (R1, R3, R4).
- Significant improvement with less robust backbones like ViT-B/16: gains of +5.1 in F1 for iWildCam and +3.1% in WC Acc for FMoW (R2, R4; also noted by R1).
- Comprehensive evaluations and thorough ablation study demonstrating effectiveness of proposed modules (R1, R4).
- Extensive supplementary material providing additional support (R3).
- The research task Few-shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation is practical and meaningful (R2).
- The insight of learning dataset-specific knowledge to complement CLIP’s general knowledge is convincing and makes a significant contribution to the FSTT-DA community (R4 – minority review is notably more positive).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is limited: except for the greedy text ensemble strategy, techniques such as domain prompts and cross-attention-based fusion are already present in prior work (R1).
- Motivation is unclear: CPNet and domain prompt appear to serve overlapping functions (both inject domain-specific knowledge), and the necessity of having two separate mechanisms is not sufficiently justified, raising concerns about redundancy, efficiency, and conceptual clarity (R3).
- Fairness of comparison is problematic: L2C introduces a new trainable network (CPNet) with additional parameters compared to baselines like VDPG; it is unclear whether performance gains stem from genuine domain adaptation or simply increased model capacity. A more rigorous ablation isolating CPNet’s contribution is needed (R2, R3).
- Performance improvement on the larger CLIP ViT-L model is limited. This suggests the method may be primarily effective for smaller or less well-trained models and might become less effective as models and data scale up (R2).
- Setting concerns: L2C modifies training on the source domain (Figure 2), which may not be permitted in traditional test-time adaptation settings (R2). Potential catastrophic forgetting when multiple target domains are encountered (as in CTTA) is not discussed (R2). The choice of FSTT-DA (unlabeled few-shot) over CD-FSL (labeled few-shot cross-domain) is not justified (R1).
- Missing comparisons with relevant methods: PODA and ULDA for prompt-driven domain adaptation (R2); HybridPrompt and ProD for domain-aware prompting in cross-domain few-shot learning (R3); no experiments on smaller datasets like PACS (R4). The paper also does not analyze the specific challenges of the FSTT-DA task (R4).
- Ablation and analysis gaps:
  - Text refinement modules M_c and M_d (for greedy text ensemble) are not ablated (R3).
  - In Table 3, domain-aware fusion leads to performance decline across most indicators, but the reason is not explained (R3).
  - The impact of the number of unlabeled samples is not analyzed; sensitivity analysis is needed (R3).
  - Increasing CPNet layers sometimes decreases accuracy (Figure 4), with no explanation given (R1).
  - External losses (e.g., from prior work) are not used; no discussion of whether using them would improve or degrade results (R1).
  - The effect of similarity between target and source domains on method performance is not studied (R1).
- Advantages of the greedy text ensemble and improved text dispersion over prior work are not sufficiently discussed (R4).
- Requested details: additional parameter count and computational cost of CPNet and other added modules (R1); source code availability for reproducibility (R4); clarification on the distinction between “data-specific visual knowledge” and “dataset-specific knowledge” (R4).
