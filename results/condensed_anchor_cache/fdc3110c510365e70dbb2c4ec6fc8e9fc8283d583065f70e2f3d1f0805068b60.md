## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes STICL, a self-training approach for ICL that disentangles the latent shift from demonstrations via a teacher–student framework. A teacher model (prompted with demonstrations and query) generates pseudo-labels; a student model (LoRA adapter) is finetuned to mimic the teacher on query-only inputs. The method aims to improve ICL stability, reduce inference cost, and achieve weak-to-strong generalization. Evaluations span multiple datasets and models, showing gains in both in-domain and OOD settings. Reviewers are split: two rate 8 (high confidence 4 and 3) and two rate 6 (confidence 3). The positive reviewers find the method simple, novel, and significant; the more critical reviewers question its framing as ICL, the motivation of “disentangling,” missing comparisons, and computational trade-offs.

### Strengths
- **Method novelty and clarity.** The idea of treating ICL as an alignment/self-training task is straightforward, interesting, and well-motivated. The paper is clearly written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The formalization of latent shift disentanglement, though debated, provides a clean framework.
- **Empirical validation and generalization.** Strong empirical support, especially for OOD settings (Reviewer 1, 3). STICL outperforms baselines on both in-domain (Table 1) and OOD (Table 3) tasks (Reviewer 3). The self-training analysis connects to classical local consistency and coverage expansion (Reviewer 2). Diverse datasets and models are used (Reviewer 2).
- **Handles ICL instabilities.** The method naturally addresses ordering/selection biases via training sample construction (STICL‑S in Table 2) – ordering issues are no longer a problem at inference (Reviewer 3).
- **Inference efficiency.** Removing demonstrations from the prompt reduces inference cost and context length bottlenecks (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewers consider this a significant practical benefit.
- **Weak-to-strong generalization.** The discussion of weak-to-strong generalization is interesting and valuable (Reviewer 4). The student progressively refines predictions, sometimes outperforming the teacher.

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology and framing.** Calling the method “in-context learning” is confusing because it involves finetuning (adapter training) rather than pure ICL. The whole point of ICL is to avoid finetuning; STICL should be pitched as a finetuning/distillation method (Reviewer 2, score 6). Two reviewers find the “disentangling latent shifts” motivation unnecessary – the core idea is simply distilling ICL into an adapter (Reviewer 2). A minority is more negative on this point.
- **Missing comparison with MetaICL.** The adapter approach resembles MetaICL, but the paper does not cite or compare against it. This omission weakens the contribution claim (Reviewer 1, score 6).
- **No limitations section.** Critical missing discussion: when is the method not applicable? How does computational cost of training compare to using demonstrations? No exploration with very large demonstration sets (Reviewer 4, score 8). The training cost is acknowledged but not quantified or fully discussed (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Unclear algorithmic details.** 
  - Pseudo-label correction algorithm is not detailed (Reviewers 3, 4). Only rough descriptions are given.
  - Calculation of the Lipschitz constant (Figure 2) is unclear, even in the appendix (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - Design choice between STICL‑R and STICL‑S is not justified: why construct k separate 16‑shot sets for k adapters (STICL‑S) instead of taking 16 random samples from the k×16 examples (STICL‑R)? (Reviewer 3, score 8).
  - LoRA parameter configuration (rank, approximation level) is not reported (Reviewer 1, score 6).
- **Missing in-domain vs OOD cross-tabulation.** Table 3 shows OOD performance, but there is no direct comparison of in-domain performance across the same datasets to quantify the gap (Reviewer 1, score 6).
- **Weak-to-strong generalization explanation is vague.** Section 2 and the discussion do not clearly explain why the student should outperform the teacher or what local-to-global consistency means in an ICL context; readers must heavily refer to cited works (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Theory is limited.** The theoretical motivation relies on an approximation of quadratic attention; no experimental verification that demonstrations are truly “disentangled” beyond performance comparisons (Reviewer 4, score 8).
