## Merged Review

### Summary
TOSS introduces text as high-level semantic guidance to constrain the single-image novel view synthesis (NVS) problem, building on Zero123. It fine-tunes Stable Diffusion with image, camera pose, and text conditioning, and proposes a dense cross-attention mechanism to align reference and target images. Expert denoisers are used for pose correctness and fine-detail preservation. Experiments on Objaverse show improved plausibility, controllability, and 3D consistency over Zero123, supported by extensive ablations. Automatic captioning is used for training data.

### Strengths
- The core idea—using text as semantic guidance to constrain the under-constrained NVS problem—is intuitive, reasonable, and effective. Since the reference image alone is insufficient, the text prompt provides high-level information while preserving the generative capabilities of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (R1, confidence 5; R2, confidence 4; R3, confidence 2; R4, confidence 4).
- The proposed dense cross-attention mechanism is well motivated, clearly explained (e.g., Figure 3), and persuasive. It enables better alignment between the reference and target images, and experiments confirm that it substantially improves 3D consistency and 3D reconstruction results (R1, confidence 5; R2, confidence 4; R4, confidence 4).
- The additional text condition increases controllability, allowing diverse NVS results from the same input image when different text prompts are used (R2, confidence 4; R4, confidence 4). This is shown in supplementary results, though R4 suggests moving more such examples to the main paper to emphasize this advantage.
- The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and presents a clean architecture. The motivation and explanation of each proposed module (dense attention, expert denoisers, text conditioning) are logical and well supported by experiments (R1, confidence 5; R2, confidence 4; R3, confidence 2; R4, confidence 4).
- The experiment section is thorough, including not only main comparisons but also comprehensive ablations on a wide range of components (R4, confidence 4). The ablation tables quantify the impact of each module (R3, confidence 2).
- The design of expert denoisers is a valuable practical contribution for image-to-image diffusion models (R4, confidence 4). The paper appropriately reports the impact of each module (R3, confidence 2).
- The proposed automatic captioning pipeline and training on Objaverse are valid and strengthen the experimental setup (R4, confidence 4).
- As a study that improves upon existing work (Zero123), the paper contains elements that can be utilized in follow-up research (R3, confidence 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear contribution of text prompt alone.** In the ablation study (Table 4), “Token-level attention” provides the largest improvement, while “Text prompt” does not give the best quantitative gain. The paper lacks a direct visual comparison isolating the text prompt’s effect (e.g., “Zero123 + Token-level attention” vs. “Zero123 + Text prompt”), making the text prompt’s specific contribution unclear (R1, confidence 5; R2, confidence 4). R2 similarly notes that more qualitative results should be presented to support the claim that text prompts increase plausibility—systematic visual comparisons with and without text are missing (R2, confidence 4).
- **Minor quantitative improvements over Zero123.** Tables 1 and 2 show only minor improvements over the baseline Zero123 (R2, confidence 4). R2 also points out that PSNR is known to be sensitive to camera pose (e.g., as demonstrated in One-2-3-45), so the paper should clarify how metrics were averaged, the distribution of test camera poses (uniform or biased), and whether any special steps ensure fair comparison. Without such clarification, the reader cannot judge whether the small gains are meaningful (R2, confidence 4).
- **Missing comparisons with other relevant methods.** Only Zero123 is compared. R2 (confidence 4) requests comparisons with Magic123 and Consistent123 for 3D reconstruction; R3 (confidence 2) also notes the paper is limited by comparing only with Zero123 and would benefit from comparisons with other single-image NVS methods or diffusion-based models adapted to the same task, even if not identical.
- **Over-claim on multiview-consistency.** R4 (confidence 4) disagrees strongly with the paper’s claim that TOSS improves “multiview-consistency”. TOSS lacks explicit multiview-consistency mechanisms (unlike SyncDreamer) and in Figure 13 (e.g., microphone example) the generated views are clearly inconsistent. The paper should revise claims and avoid presenting TOSS as multiview-consistent (R4, confidence 4). Other reviewers did not comment on this point.
- **Lack of qualitative, in-depth analysis.** While quantitative ablation experiments are present, qualitative analysis is lacking for each module’s effectiveness (R1, confidence 5; R3, confidence 2). R1 specifically requests visual cases on human portraits (e.g., Taylor Swift, young male) because Zero123 performs poorly on such subjects—the paper should demonstrate behavior in these challenging cases (R1, confidence 5).
- **Insufficient analysis of expert denoisers and computational cost.** The effectiveness and motivation of expert denoisers are not deeply analyzed (R3, confidence 2; R2, confidence 4 asks for more elaboration). Also, compared to just using cross-attention with CLIP embeddings, the paper should analyze computational cost and memory of the proposed dense attention (R3, confidence 2).
- **Section numbering inconsistency.** There is no Section 3.2.2, making Section 3.2.1 unnecessary; authors should restructure (R3, confidence 2).
- **Controllability not emphasized enough in main paper.** R4 (confidence 4) notes that the paper should feature more results where different text prompts from the same input image produce different novel views, and move such visualization from the supplementary to the main paper to highlight TOSS’s controllability.
- **Missing experimental details.** In Table 1, there are no experiments for “inference w/o text” and no “w/ expert denoisers” for the 160M version (R3, confidence 2). Also, what does “replace the SD v1.4 model” mean in Sec 4.5? Is it only the decoder, or also the encoder? Is it done only at inference or does it require retraining? (R4, confidence 4)
- **Questions about cross-attention design.** R2 (confidence 4) asks whether other orders or combinations of text, image, and camera pose were tried in the cross-attention process—this is a missing ablation.
