## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SPDIM, a geometric deep learning framework for source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SFUDA) in EEG under conditional and label shifts. It introduces a generative model and shows that prior Riemannian statistical alignment methods compensate for marginal/conditional shifts but harm performance under label shifts. SPDIM uses an information maximization principle to learn one SPD-manifold-constrained bias parameter per target domain. Validated on synthetic data, motor-imagery BCI, and sleep-staging datasets, SPDIM outperforms prior alignment methods. Reviewers broadly agree the problem is relevant and the modeling insightful, but raise concerns about experimental rigor, presentation clarity, and missing comparisons. One reviewer (score 8) is highly positive; another (score 5) is most critical; the others (score 6) are positive but with substantive caveats.

### Strengths

- Rigorous and clear presentation of technical details and full analytic workflow; theory-guided method design for EEG (R1).
- Impactful and realistic problem choice: label shifts in EEG are a ubiquitous clinical and BCI concern (R1, R5).
- Introduction of an SPD-manifold-constrained bias parameter is a novel and principled advancement for SFUDA under label shifts (R1, R3).
- Clear motivation: SPDIM explicitly addresses label shifts, a common challenge in real EEG datasets, with theoretical analysis explaining deviations (R2, R5).
- Simulation experiments qualitatively validate SPDIM’s benefits under varying label shifts (R2).
- Tested on three setups: synthetic data, motor-imagery (cross-subject/session), and sleep-staging (cross-subject) (R4).
- SPDIM consistently outperforms conventional SPD-based alignment methods (R3).
- Some propositions may inspire further work on label shift in SFUDA (R5).
- The modelisation (generative model, domain-specific forward model) is original and insightful (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Assumption issues**: The assumption that the number of latent brain sources equals the number of observed channels *P* may be unrealistic; over-completeness could affect identifiability and interpretability. The method should address this sensitivity (R1).
- **Notation and presentation clarity**: Equations are dense and sometimes confusing — e.g., sum index in Eq.(2), use of *upper⁻¹* in Eq.(10), ambiguous *jᵢ* vs *j*, and *Q* in Eq.(15) not introduced (R2). The main text is overwhelmed by equations with insufficient intuitive explanation, making the core concepts hard to follow (R3). Figures: Figure 1 resolution is low (R1, R5); Figure 2 sub-figures lack subtitles (R5).
- **Missing comparisons**: 
  - No comparison with non-Riemannian SFUDA methods for EEG/multivariate time series (e.g., He et al. 2023, Garg et al. 2023) (R1).
  - For motor imagery, only SPD-manifold baselines and IM are compared; classic EEG decoders like EEGNet, EEG Conformer are missing (R5).
  - For sleep staging, only “recenter” is compared; should include STMA or TMA (spatio-temporal Monge alignment) (R4).
  - Broader comparisons with newer unsupervised/semi-supervised EEG methods are needed (R3). 
  - The “ablation study” label for method comparisons is misleading (R4).
- **Experimental setup gaps**:
  - Motor imagery experiment uses artificially unbalanced labels; real-world naturally unbalanced data would strengthen the work (R4).
  - The mean accuracy of the two SPDIM variants is within one standard deviation of the recenter baseline for motor imagery, questioning significance (R4).
  - Sleep staging does not compare adaptation methods beyond recenter (R4).
  - Experimental details unclear: hyperparameters, source/target domain partitioning, learning rate schedule, batch size (R5). 
  - Whether a train/test split was used on the target domain during unsupervised training is not stated (R4).
  - It is surprising that spatial covariance alone is sufficient for sleep staging without temporal information (R4).
- **Motivation and justification**: The paper does not clearly explain why existing SPD methods fail under label shifts and how the SPD manifold geometry specifically addresses the challenge; the connection is implicit (R3). 
- **Generalizability**: The method appears general and not specifically tailored to EEG, but it is only validated on EEG data, reducing evidence for broader SFUDA applicability (R5).
- **Interpretability**: How learned SPD-manifold parameters relate to EEG neural mechanisms is not discussed, limiting clinical utility (R3).
- **Limitations and reproducibility**:
  - No discussion of study limitations or future directions. E.g., sensitivity to noise/outliers in estimating the bias parameter, computational complexity compared to other alignment methods (R1).
  - Anonymous code link is broken (“repository not found”) (R1, R4).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms or missing ablations**:
  - How does the framework define a domain? (one subject/recording? with both/multiple classes?) (R1).
  - How does the framework compare to non-Riemannian SFUDA? (R1; see missing comparisons).
  - What factors cause high variability in Table 1 beyond dataset size? (R1).
  - Is the domain-specific forward model *Aⱼ* learned or predefined? (R2).
  - How are domain-specific parameters *Φⱼ* and geodesic step-size *φⱼ* learned via information maximization? (R2).
  - Is performance sensitive to hyperparameters like rank of *A* or number of domains in *Dₛ*? (R2).
  - How does the paper model conditional shifts (p(x|y) changing) in its generative model? (R4).
  - What is *D* in Remark 1? (R4).
  - Does Proposition 2 hold when *Mⱼ* is not infinite? (R4).
  - Why is *Q* in Eq.(15) claimed domain-invariant when it depends on *Aⱼ* through *Cᵢ*? (R2).
  - The relationship between the information maximization section (3.3) and SPDIM (bias)/(geodesic) is unclear (R2).
- **Typos**: “?” at lines 218 and 236 in citations; line 443 “standard-deviation in brackets” should be removed; *D* and *P* both used for data dimension; *Q* and *U* both used for domain-invariant part of the mixing matrix (R1, R4).
