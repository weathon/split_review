## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes cognitive prompting, a method that guides LLMs through a sequence of human-defined cognitive operations (goal clarification, decomposition, filtering, abstraction, etc.) to improve structured reasoning. Three variants are introduced: a deterministic sequence, a self-adaptive (reflective) variant where the LLM selects the operation sequence, and a hybrid using generated solutions as few-shot prompts. Experiments with LLaMA, Gemma 2, and Qwen models (two sizes each) on the GSM8K arithmetic benchmark and a commonsense reasoning benchmark show performance improvements over standard QA. One reviewer (R3, score 5, confidence 2) is more positive, noting rigorous experiments and clear presentation, while the other three reviewers (R1, R2, R4; scores 3, confidence 4–5) identify significant weaknesses in evaluation, baselines, and generalizability.

### Strengths
- The idea of organizing problem-solving through a structured sequence of cognitive operations is potentially interesting, intuitive, and well-motivated.
- The paper is well-written, clearly explaining each cognitive operation and the overall approach. It provides insights into exploring thinking modes of LLMs and future prompting design.
- The analysis of the frequency of operation combinations is valuable for understanding the LLM’s internal thought process (R2).
- Experiments are conducted rigorously with thorough comparisons, and the approach improves performance on GSM8K (R3). The results on the 8B model show effectiveness, though a small discrepancy in reported numbers exists (Line 190 states 0.7 across all techniques, but reflective CP achieves ~0.73 – see weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of baselines**: The paper only compares cognitive prompting with vanilla models. Missing comparisons with standard prompting methods such as Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022), Tree-of-Thought (Yao et al., 2024), Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2024), and few-shot CoT (R2, R4), as well as finetuning (R1). Without these, the relative contribution of cognitive prompting is unclear.
- **Limited evaluation datasets and models**: Only two datasets are used (GSM8K for arithmetic, one unspecified commonsense benchmark). More datasets of varying difficulty (e.g., MATH, Hendrycks et al., 2021) are needed (R2). Experiments should also cover a broader set of models, including open (Mistral, Bloom) and proprietary (GPT, Gemini, Claude) (R1). The current evaluation is insufficient to support the claim that cognitive prompting “is adaptable across a wide range of tasks” (R4).
- **Heavy reliance on a human-defined operation list**: The method’s effectiveness is tied to the handcrafted set of cognitive operations, limiting generalizability (R2, R4). It is not adequately distinguished from related work – e.g., how is “filtering” realized differently from prior instruction or CoT approaches? (R1). Transfer to other tasks or domains (e.g., reinforcement learning decision-making) is not examined (R3).
- **Unstable and minor performance gains**: Improvements on arithmetic reasoning are limited; on commonsense reasoning, use of cognitive prompting severely degrades performance for the 70B model (R2). This raises concern about robustness.
- **Missing qualitative analysis**: No representative cases illustrating success or failure of specific cognitive operations are provided (R1). Such analysis would help understand when and why the method works.
- **Missing experimental details**: The baseline “no CP” in Figure 2 is not described (R4). The commonsense reasoning evaluation dataset is not specified (R4). The dynamic selection process for the reflective variant and detailed implementation of each operation require more explanation (R3).
- **Overprocessing issue not addressed**: Larger models with reflective CP may suffer from overthinking or overprocessing, but this problem is not discussed (R3).
- **Potential error in reported numbers**: Line 190 claims “the 8B model achieves scores of 0.7 across all prompting techniques,” but Figure 2 shows reflective CP on the 8B model achieving approximately 0.73 (R4).
