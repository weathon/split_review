## Merged Review

### Summary

TimeDiT trains a diffusion Transformer for time series using a masked reconstruction pre-training. The model handles multiple tasks (forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection) via a unified masking strategy, incorporates physics knowledge (PDEs) as an energy-based prior during sampling, and adapts to varying channel numbers and resolutions. Experiments across tasks and zero-shot settings show potential as a foundation model. **One reviewer (R4, score 3) strongly disagrees on novelty, baselines, and experimental rigor; the other three rate the paper 6 but note several missing details.**

### Strengths

- **Integration of masked reconstruction with diffusion models for time series** is effectively demonstrated (R1, R2). The unified masking strategy allows handling forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection in one framework (R2, R3).  
- **Extensive experimental validation** across multiple tasks and datasets, including zero-shot forecasting (R1, R2, R3). The approach shows strong empirical results (R3) and addresses real-world challenges like missing values and multi-resolution data (R2).  
- **Incorporation of physical knowledge (PDEs) into diffusion sampling** is novel and intriguing (R1). The theoretical framing via an energy-based prior is considered proper (R2).  
- The paper discusses potential challenges for a generic diffusion-based time series model (e.g., variable channel sizes, missing values, multi-resolution) and the promise of diffusion models for uncertainty and randomness (R4). The idea of exploiting multimodal textual information to improve zero-shot forecasting is seen as promising (R4).

### Weaknesses

**1. Novelty & Contribution (serious concern from R4; noted by R1)**  
- The core idea (masked reconstruction + diffusion) has been explored in the image domain; the paper does not clearly articulate novel technical challenges or innovations specific to time series (R1). R4 argues that the plain Encoder-only Transformer backbone (Input Embedding, Multi-layer Encoder, Output Projection) lacks architectural innovation for multi-scale, multi-period temporal characterization.  
- The conditioning scheme simply splices observed sequences into the input; R4 argues this cannot guarantee high multivariate temporal correlation, contrasting with methods like ControlNet, mr-Diff, MG-TSD that design more efficient conditional denoising networks.  
- R4 concludes the model is incremental rather than innovative; e.g., adding a cls-token for classification does not justify a foundation model for both classification and prediction.

**2. Methodological Clarity & Missing Technical Details**  
- **Token definition**: Unclear whether tokens are individual time points, segments, or other representations (R3).  
- **Embedder architecture**: Not specified (linear projection, convolutional, etc., and no details in appendix) (R3).  
- **Noise embedding**: Rationale for embedding the noised sample instead of original observation is not explained; implications for learning temporal dependencies are unclear (R3).  
- **Handling variable channel counts**: The method for accommodating different channel numbers is vague (padding? predefined max resolution?). Unclear how the model generalizes when input resolution exceeds that maximum (R3).  
- **Training stabilization and diffusion schedule influence** on physical constraints are not discussed (R2).

**3. Pre-training Data & Unified Dataset** (raised by R1, R4)  
- The paper does not clarify whether pre-training datasets are task-specific or a single pre-trained model is used across tasks. A clear breakdown of datasets for pre-training, fine-tuning, and testing is missing (R1).  
- Missing description of the “unified pre-trained temporal dataset”: how cross-domain datasets (different channel counts, sampling rates, sizes) are combined into a general dataset. This is critical for foundation model claims (R4).

**4. Missing Tasks & Comparisons**  
- **Sequence-level classification** is absent; important for claiming a complete foundation model for time series (R1).  
- **Irregular time-series handling** is claimed but only evaluated via imputation, not on real irregular sampling with missing values at arbitrary time points (R3).  
- **Incomplete baselines**:  
  - Table 1 missing recent models (TimeMixer, TimeLLM, MG-TSD, mr-Diff) (R4).  
  - Table 2 (PDE results) missing PDE-Diffusion and DiffusionPDE; comparison only with TSDiff, PPDM, PPIM (R4).  
  - Table 3 (zero-shot) missing Timer, GPD, TimeLLM (R4).  
  - Tables 4–5 best SOTA is OneFitsALL (NIPS 2023), considered outdated (R4).  
- **Inconsistent baseline results**: Table 6 Discriminative Score for Diffusion-TS under Stocks is 0.1869, but original paper reports 0.067. All baselines in Table 6 show large discrepancy (R4).  
- **Limited performance gains**: In Table 1, TimeDiT is worse than CSDI on PhysioNet (a) and NASDAQ; <10% improvement on MIMIC-III and PhysioNet (b). In Table 2, improvement <5% on most PDEs (e.g., Advection 2.27%, Navier-Stokes 2.7%, Diffusion-Sorption 3.88%) (R4).  
- Zero-shot forecasting missing state-of-the-art baselines like Timer, GPD, TimeLLM (R4).

**5. PDE Integration**  
- PDE experiments (Table 2) show solving classical PDEs, not applying PDE theory to time series prediction. The claim of “incorporate physics knowledge as an energy-based prior for time series forecasting” is overstated and not explained (R4). R1 questions the setting: if ground truth dynamics are known, can one simply generate training samples numerically and compare?  
- Practical value unclear: if we have the PDE, can we solve it numerically and train a separate model? (R1).

**6. Architecture and Conditioning Limitations**  
- The “each moment as token” strategy is used; PatchTST and others have shown “Patch as Token” is more effective. No ablation or design to establish cross-channel connectivity (R4).  
- Random masking in pre-training destroys temporal coherence (trend-season, multi-resolution, multi-period). SimMTM shows such self-supervision requires vast data and slow learning. Single-scale reconstruction ignores multiscale supervision (R4).  
- Differences between masking (reconstruction from randomly masked discontinuous subsequences) and forecasting (capturing global dependencies) suggest learned representations may not transfer well. R4 argues the reconstruction paradigm allows local information usage, while prediction requires observed-only future prediction.

**7. Missing Ablation & Scalability**  
- No ablation experiments for key components: Transformer-based condition injection, Time Series Mask Unit, Physics-Informed sampling (R4).  
- No analysis of model scalability for very long sequences (R2).  
- No analysis of failure cases or sensitivity to mask ratios during pre-training (R2).  
- Computational costs and training efficiency not discussed (R2).  
- No discussion of missing data mechanisms (MCAR, MAR, MNAR) in forecasting experiments (R2).  
- Sensitivity to threshold choice (99th percentile) in anomaly detection not evaluated (R2).  
- No justification for architecture choices (number of heads, layer depth) (R2).

**8. Multimodal Alignment Details Missing**  
- The paper mentions aligning frequency and category information with textual data to improve zero-shot capability, but implementation details are absent from both main text and appendix (R4).

**9. Reviewer Disagreement Explicit**  
- R4 is a strong minority opinion (rating 3, soundness 1, contribution 1) vs. the other three reviewers (rating 6, soundness 3, contribution 2–3). R4 considers the paper incremental, with insufficient novelty, missing baselines, inconsistent results, and no ablation experiments. Other reviewers note missing details but see potential.
