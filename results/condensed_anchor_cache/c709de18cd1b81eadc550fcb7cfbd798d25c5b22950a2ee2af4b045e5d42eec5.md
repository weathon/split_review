## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces OpenVid-1M, a dataset of ~1M high-resolution (≥512) video clips paired with detailed captions, curated from existing datasets (ChronoMagic, CelebvHQ, Open-Sora-plan, Panda) via an automated filtering and recaptioning pipeline. It also proposes MVDiT, a video diffusion transformer that processes text tokens jointly with visual tokens in self-attention and temporal-attention. Two sub-datasets are created: OpenVid-1M and OpenVidHD (433K 1080p clips). The paper’s contributions are assessed with a clear split: some reviewers rate the work highly (8), while others find it limited (6), with disagreements centered on dataset novelty, caption quality, and architectural novelty.

### Strengths
1. **High-quality dataset with detailed captions**: OpenVid-1M contains over 1M videos at resolution ≥512, each with long, expressive captions generated by LLaVA, addressing the need for a precise, high-quality T2V dataset, especially for smaller models. Two scales (1M and 0.4M HD) are provided.
2. **Meticulous data curation pipeline**: The pipeline integrates aesthetic score, temporal consistency, motion difference, clarity assessment, clip extraction, and recaptioning to ensure quality. The design is carefully described.
3. **Clear experimental validation**: Extensive experiments show that models trained on OpenVid-1M achieve superior generation results compared to models trained on existing datasets (e.g., WebVid-10M, Panda-70M). Ablation studies verify dataset and MVDiT component effectiveness.
4. **Reasonable MVDiT architecture**: Repeating text tokens for each frame and jointly attending to visual and semantic information in self-attention and temporal-attention is sound. Comparisons with SoTA models (Tables 3, 4) demonstrate effectiveness.
5. **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper clearly explains the need for high-quality datasets and better text utilization, and is easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
1. **Dataset composed solely of existing videos**: OpenVid-1M is derived from other public datasets and does not introduce new video data. This limits its contribution as a dataset paper, and models trained on it may not learn entirely new visual concepts.
2. **Caption quality not validated**: Using raw LLaVA-13B as captioner without comparison to GPT-4V or Gemini is questionable. Longer captions do not guarantee higher accuracy or informativeness; no human evaluation or retrieval analysis is provided.
3. **Limited architectural novelty**: MVDiT is closely inspired by MMDiT and seen as a straightforward extension to video. Differences (added temporal-attention and cross-attention layers) are not detailed or ablated, making the technical novelty weak. An ablation study on scaling parameter α and the Multi-Modal Temporal-Attention module is missing.
4. **Insufficient justification for filtering choices**: Reasons for selecting top 20% of Panda-50M vs. 90% from other datasets, and for using temporal consistency to discard extremes while not doing the same for optical flow, are not explained. These choices need rationale for reproducibility.
5. **Missing experiments and comparisons**:
   - Ablation on filtering clips shorter than 3s or 5s is needed, as 37% of clips are under 3s.
   - Table 3 should include recent models like CogVideoX-5B and OpenSoraPlanV1.2.
   - Table 4 comparison is unfair: Panda-50M at lower resolution vs. OpenVidHD 1080p; a controlled same-resolution subset (e.g., Panda50MHD) is required.
   - The effect of super-resolution on original video quality in Table 4 is not addressed.
6. **Presentation issues**:
   - Video frames in Figures 6 and 8 are too small; larger frames or higher-resolution appendix versions are needed.
   - The “Acceleration for HD Video Generation” section is not a contribution and should be moved to the appendix.
   - No project/demo webpage is provided, making it hard to assess overall video quality from static frames.
   - The paper does not compare video duration distribution with other datasets, despite the large number of short clips in OpenVid-1M.
7. **Request for model release**: One reviewer asked whether the trained model will be made publicly available.

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: While some reviewers rated the dataset and contributions highly (8), others found the dataset’s reliance on existing data and the incremental architecture to be major weaknesses (6). This contrast is reflected in the above strengths and weaknesses.
