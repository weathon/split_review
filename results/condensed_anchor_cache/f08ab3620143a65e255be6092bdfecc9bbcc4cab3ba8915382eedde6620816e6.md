## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers evaluate this paper introducing a Non-Contrastive Learning framework for Sequential Recommendation (NCL-SR) with a preference-preserving profile generation method inspired by differential privacy. Scores: 6,6,6,8. Reviewer 4 is notably more positive (8) about solving the computational cost problem of contrastive learning and achieving higher performance; other reviewers acknowledge novelty and theoretical contributions but raise concerns about clarity, missing details, and experiments. All agree the method is novel and technically sound, but weaknesses center on incomplete methodology explanation, insufficient efficiency evaluation, and presentation issues.

### Strengths
- First non-contrastive learning framework for sequential recommendation, eliminating negative sample overhead.
- Preference-preserving profile generation based on differential privacy provides theoretical guarantees for uniformity and alignment (reviewers 1, 4).
- Offer theorem analysis for the proposed method (reviewer 3).
- Introduces novel loss calculation methods, including matrix cross entropy (MCE) – first use in recommendation (reviewer 2).
- Extensive experiments across multiple datasets and architectures (reviewers 1, 3) show NCL-SR outperforms traditional SR and CL-based SR models (reviewers 1, 2).
- Investigation reveals alignment is usually more important than uniformity in data-sparse settings (reviewer 1).
- Paper is easy to follow and well-organized (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Figure 1 needs major revision.** Multiple reviewers find the three polygons and exponential mechanism unclear; the third red text lacks a corresponding polygon (reviewers 1, 2). The visual connection between differential privacy and the augmentation process is not intuitive.
- **Missing experiments on computational cost/efficiency.** Despite the paper’s main motivation to reduce CL’s high cost, no experiments verify the effect of NCL-SR on computation/memory (reviewers 1, 4). A complexity analysis (time and space) is needed, especially given the eigenvalue computation and matrix logarithm in Eq.(9) (reviewer 3). Scalability to large datasets should be discussed.
- **Notation and method details underspecified.**  
  * What does “s()” represent in Eq.1? What does “f()” represent in Eq.4? (reviewer 1)  
  * Calculation of \(C(Z,Z’)\) in Eq.(9) and construction/application of the centering matrix \(H_B\) (dimensions, steps) are missing (reviewer 3).  
  * Why set utility score \(\Delta_u = e - 1/e\)? Provide insight (reviewer 2).  
- **Choice of Matrix Cross Entropy (MCE) not sufficiently motivated.** Why MCE over alternatives like Barlow Twins, MEC, DirectAU? Theoretical/empirical advantages for SR should be clarified, and potential limitations discussed (reviewer 3).
- **Ablation/parameter analysis incomplete.**  
  * Perturbation quantity (number of item-level perturbations) – effect on recommendation performance not tested (reviewer 1).  
  * Effect of \(\gamma\) on model performance not studied (reviewer 2).  
- **Dataset split ratio not stated.** Training/validation/test split not explicitly provided (reviewer 1).
- **Fairness of baselines.** Non-CL SR baselines should also be implemented with the same embedding model (e.g., e5) for a fair comparison (reviewer 4).
- **Missing related work citations.** The paper should discuss/compare with similar works, e.g., [1] and [2] (reviewer 2). Also, a deeper literature review on how differential privacy inspired the profile generation is needed (reviewer 4).
- **Text inconsistencies.** “NCL SR” vs “NCL-SR” in line 178; inconsistent capitalization of “differential privacy” vs “Differential Privacy” (reviewer 1). Overall refine presentation to improve readability (reviewer 1).
