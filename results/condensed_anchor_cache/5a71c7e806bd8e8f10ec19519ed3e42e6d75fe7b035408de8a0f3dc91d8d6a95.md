## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Perp-Neg, a method to address the semantic overlap problem in negative prompting for text-to-image diffusion models. The key idea is to use the component of the negative prompt gradient that is orthogonal to the positive prompt gradient, preventing the negative prompt from suppressing desired concepts. The method requires no training or fine-tuning. It is applied to 2D image generation (view-dependent editing) and integrated with DreamFusion for text-to-3D to mitigate the Janus (multi-head) problem. The authors provide qualitative and quantitative results showing improved view consistency and successful generation of 3D assets that previously suffered from the Janus problem.

### Strengths
- The motivation (Section 2.2.1) is insightful: the semantic overlap drawback of naive negative prompting is not mentioned in prior work. (Reviewer 3)
- The proposed method is elegant, intuitive, and leverages geometric properties of score space. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The method is simple to implement, easy to follow, and does not require offline training or fine-tuning, thus preserving the generalizability of the original diffusion model. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- It provides a tractable way to balance between the scores of positive and negative text prompts. (Reviewer 1)
- Good qualitative results are shown for view-dependent image generation (Figure 6). (Reviewer 3)
- Sufficient evaluations are provided, including quantitative success rate and qualitative results for side/back views. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is clearly written, well organized, technically sound, and reproducible. (Reviewer 2)
- Code is provided to reproduce the results. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- The assumption of perpendicular gradient sampling is heuristic and lacks theoretical proof. (Reviewer 1)
- The effectiveness on text-to-3D generation is limited; according to an implementation in threestudio, the Janus problem is almost not mitigated. (Reviewer 1)
- The balance factor between Eq. 8 and Eq. 9 may be hard to tune in practice. (Reviewer 1)
- Although the method is applicable to many tasks (e.g., gradient mixing during inference), the paper focuses almost exclusively on the Janus problem in 3D generation. The authors should demonstrate effectiveness on more applications. (Reviewer 3)
- The quantitative evaluation lacks fidelity metrics such as FID, CLIP similarity, or user preference; only a success rate is provided. (Reviewer 4)
- The experiments focus primarily on 2D image generation despite 3D being a key claimed application. (Reviewer 2)
- The generated 3D objects are simple; the method’s effectiveness on detailed 3D objects needs further verification. (Reviewer 2)
- The qualitative results for 3D generation are too few: only 9 examples in Figures 2, 7, and 20, with overlapping prompts (e.g., lions). More diverse prompts should be shown. (Reviewer 3)
- The view-dependent image generation results (Figures 6, 10–18) reuse the same prompts (lion, panda, peacock) repeatedly; more varied prompts are needed. (Reviewer 3)
- The comparison to other methods in 3D generation is limited and incomplete. Only Figure 2 with 3 examples is shown; for Magic3D the last example shows no obvious difference, and Figure 7 claims Magic3D fails but does not show those unsatisfactory results. (Reviewer 3)
- The baseline CEBM is not properly justified; it is not designed for this task, and no explanation of its choice is given. A relevant view-consistent text-to-3D work [1] should be cited and compared. (Reviewer 2)
- It is unclear whether simply using negative prompts (without Perp-Neg) in 3D generation could also alleviate the Janus problem; this ablation is missing. (Reviewer 2)

**Reviewer contrast:** Two reviewers (scores 5) expressed stronger concerns about the heuristic assumption, limited 3D validation, and weak comparisons. Two reviewers (scores 6) found the method clever and feasible, with minor shortcomings regarding quantitative metrics and experimental breadth.
