- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 3, 5, 5, 6, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents λ-SecAgg, which integrates Partial Vector Freezing (PVF) into Secure Aggregation Protocols (SAPs) for federated learning. PVF reduces computation by “freezing” most vector entries and processing only 1/λ of them through SAP; frozen entries are later “thawed” via public linear transformations. The Disrupting Variables Extension (DVE) adds Local Differential Privacy noise to frozen entries. Experiments on seven SAP baselines claim up to 99.5× speedup and 32.3× communication reduction. Reviews are sharply divided: two reviewers (R1, R6) assign score 1 and deem the protocol fundamentally insecure, one (R2) gives score 3 and identifies serious privacy/security proof flaws, while three (R3, R4, R5) give scores 5–6 and view the method as novel and efficient but note various weaknesses. All reviewers agree the problem is timely and the evaluation is extensive, but the core security claim is contested.

### Strengths

- Addresses an important and timely problem: reducing computational and communication overhead in secure aggregation for federated learning. (R1, R2, R6)
- Novel approach: the concept of freezing/unfreezing partial vector entries to reduce SAP burden, and the DVE extension for additional privacy, are innovative. (R3, R4)
- Comprehensive evaluation across 7 baselines covering multiple SAP types (homomorphic encryption, SMPC, mask-based). (R2, R3, R4)
- Significant empirical gains: up to 99.5× speedup and 32.3× communication reduction when λ=100. (R3, R5)
- Theoretical security proofs (semi-honest and active adversary models) are provided. (R3, R5; but see Weaknesses for critiques)
- Presentation is fairly clear overall (R6; though others note density issues).

### Weaknesses

**Critical Privacy/Security Flaws – Disagreement Among Reviewers**  
*(R1, R2, R6 strongly argue the protocol is insecure; R3 and R5 accept the proofs but note limitations; R4 finds analysis informal.)*

- R1: The claim in Section 4.1 that revealing \(y^i\) causes no privacy leakage is flawed. Example with λ=2, two-element vector: server learns \(x_1\) in terms of \(x_2\), reducing conditional probability from \(1/p^2\) to \(1/p\). This reduces entropy and provides a privacy leak beyond the aggregated sum. (Severe)
- R2: PVF substantially reduces standard privacy guarantees of SAP. The server can detect whether two clients have similar vector chunks; the LDP mitigation does not fully restore input privacy. (Severe)
- R2: The security proof of Theorem 1 is unsound: (i) Hybrid 1 is not indistinguishable from Hybrid 0 because frozen vectors \(y_i\) are not uniformly random (they are inputs with bounded-variance Gaussian noise). (ii) In Hybrid 3, the protocol incorrectly states the adversary calls the ideal functionality; standard simulation expects the simulator to do so. (Severe)
- R6: The protocol is completely unsafe. Revealing \(\breve{A}x\) (an underdetermined linear system) does not provide any standard notion of security. Example: a client whose vector is \((x_1,0,\dots,0)\) yields \(y\) multiples of \(x_1\), making it distinguishable from uniform – contradicting Theorem 1’s Hybrid 1 claim. The enhancements (DVE) are “privacy by obscurity” and lack rigorous proof. (Severe)
- R4: Security analysis is informal; it does not define attack models or provide formal proofs. The choice of magnitude/distribution of disrupting variables is not justified, and how they impact privacy is unclear. (Moderate)
- R5: The threat model is imprecise – adversary’s knowledge (e.g., access to intermediate values, ability to collude) and capabilities are not adequately described. (Moderate)
- R6: The work ignores relevant prior work on compression under privacy constraints and differentially private aggregation using correlated noise, which are directly related. (Moderate)

**Clarity and Presentation Issues**  

- R3: Mathematical derivations and security analyses are dense and hard to follow; diagrams (Sections 3 and 4) have low readability. Suggest providing more intuitive explanations and simplified figures. (Moderate)
- R5: The Introduction contains an apparent contradiction: DP is said to be insufficient against attacks but then used in DVE – the specific attacks and rationale need clarification. (Moderate)
- R5: The classification of “compression-based techniques” (Figure 3, Section 2) feels abrupt; the link between compression and secure aggregation is not made explicit, and the criteria for categorizing existing methods are unclear. (Moderate)
- R4: The paper would benefit from deeper theoretical grounding – the core idea (freezing parts of the vector) appears straightforward and lacks fundamental novelty; a direct comparison to dimensionality reduction or sparse coding techniques is missing. (Moderate; note R3 and R5 consider the approach novel.)
- R6: Minor technical inaccuracies: “mask-based” is an instantiation of “SMPC-based”; Definition 1 is unclear on “additional knowledge” and the use of rank; the statement “impossible to determine that specific confidential vector” is overly strong; “security of FL remains an open issue” is too broad. (Minor)

**Experimental Shortcomings**  

- R2: Baseline runtimes (e.g., PracAgg: client 14s, server 140s for 100k elements) are significantly higher than reported in the original PracAgg paper (client ~300ms, server ≤5s). The discrepancy needs explanation. (Moderate – question becomes criticism)
- R3: The effect of DP noise on accuracy is not empirically detailed; only a claim of negligible impact is made. Accuracy-loss experiments are needed. (Moderate)
- R3: Scalability to multiple concurrent users is not addressed; experiments only consider single-user/server setups. (Moderate)
- R5: Experimental settings lack description of neural network architectures (layers, activations) and datasets (preprocessing steps); this impairs reproducibility. (Moderate)
- R5: The choice λ=100 is not justified; a sensitivity analysis of λ’s impact on performance and security is missing. (Moderate)
- R5: Generalizability to other models and data types is limited; the paper should discuss potential limitations and future work. (Moderate)
- R4: How to determine the optimal λ in practice is not discussed. (Minor)
- R4: The difference between aggregation of \(k^i\) and that of \(y^i\) is not clarified. (Minor)

**Additional Concerns**  

- R2: The computational gains of PVF may not justify the privacy drop: e.g., in PracAgg the masking operations (field ops, PRG) are already lightweight, and the heavy pairwise key agreements are independent of vector length, so PVF offers limited benefit. (Severe)
- R5: In Section 3.3, the claim that data accuracy is maintained after DP noise in the freezing/melting process needs a detailed explanation of noise-level control and the privacy–accuracy trade-off. (Moderate)
- R6: It is not clearly shown which masking operations of the compared SecAgg protocols are avoided; matrix–vector multiplication or masking still depend on vector dimension. (Moderate)
- R6: How does the SecAgg overhead compare to the cost of local training steps for different models? A breakdown would help assess practical relevance. (Moderate – question becomes criticism)
