## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers rated the paper (scores: 6, 5, 3, 3). One reviewer was positive (score 6, confidence 2), praising the problem motivation and synthetic results; others were more critical (scores 3–5), raising concerns about experimental scope, clarity, and evidence for the main claims. The paper studies distributed asynchronous (“hogwild”) inference for GNNs, identifies “implicitly-defined” GNNs as provably robust under partial asynchrony, and proposes a novel implicit architecture, Energy GNN (EGNN), which uses input-convex networks to guarantee convergence. The problem is considered novel and interesting, and the theoretical analysis connecting partial asynchronism to GNNs is non-trivial. However, reviewers consistently find the experimental evaluation insufficient for the claimed use cases, and note weaknesses in terminology clarity, baseline selection, and presentation.

### Strengths

- The problem of distributed asynchronous GNN inference is novel and has practical applications (e.g., robotic swarms, sensor networks) but has been seldom studied before. (R1, R3, R4)
- The paper provides detailed background and related work, and the proposed method is well motivated. (R1)
- The proposed Energy GNN architecture is interesting and intriguing; the theoretical analysis and the mathematical tools used to guarantee convergence under partial asynchrony are non-trivial contributions. (R1, R4)
- Good grammar and clear introduction; the paper is generally well-written. (R2)
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation on multiple datasets (synthetic, PPI, MUTAG) makes the benefits of the proposed architecture stand out. (R2)  
  *Note: Reviewer 2 finds the evaluation comprehensive; other reviewers disagree (see Weaknesses).*
- Energy GNN works well on synthetic datasets, especially for long-range communication tasks. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient experimental evidence for main claims.** No runtime, convergence curves, or efficiency/time complexity metrics are reported. The paper claims benefits for larger graphs but only evaluates on graphs up to 100 nodes; no scalability to millions of nodes (e.g., ogb MAG240M) is demonstrated. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Limited baselines and comparisons.** Only three GNN architectures are tested (GCN, IGNN, Energy GNN). Missing comparisons with other methods that could improve robustness (e.g., Scarselli’s contraction-based approach, dropout-like schedules from Appendix A.5, deeper GNNs, graph transformers). The choice of a 2-layer GCN as the main MPNN baseline is insufficient, especially for long-range tasks. (R2, R4)
- **Performance on real-world datasets is weak.** On PPI and MUTAG, Energy GNN underperforms GCN (synchronous) and is significantly worse than IGNN (e.g., on PPI, Energy GNN is near GCN level while IGNN is near perfect). No hypothesis or comment is offered for this performance drop. (R1, R4)
- **Missing asynchronous baselines.** The paper does not report the performance of synchronous GNNs (e.g., GCN, IGNN) when executed asynchronously. This comparison is crucial to establish the need for a special architecture. (R1, R4)
- **Lack of clarity in terminology and presentation.** Key terms are not explicitly defined: “finite-depth GNN,” “contractive” (in the GNN context), “energy function,” “implicit GNN” vs “fixed-point GNN” (used interchangeably), “IGNN” introduced in experiments without definition. Section 4’s last paragraph on fixed-point GNN advantages is unclear. The paper is math-heavy with insufficient intuition and figures. Multiple references in the appendix are broken (showing “?”). The definition of \(\tau_j^i(t)\) in Eq. (8) is confusing (should it be staleness bound or last-update time?). The update in Eq. (9) should use multisets. (R2, R3, R4)
- **No averaging over multiple runs.** Results in the main paper appear to be from single runs (or not clearly averaged). Standard deviations are missing from some table entries. (R4)
- **Unclear task setup for Section 6.3.** A precise mathematical formulation of the problem and training objective is needed. (R4)
- **Missing ablation studies.** Effects of the staleness bound \(\tau\), step-size scaling \(O(1/nb)\), and graph size on convergence and efficiency are not explored. Why does the step size depend on \(n\) rather than, e.g., node degree? (R1, R4)
- **Limited to distributed inference only; distributed training is not addressed.** If graphs are so large that inference requires distribution, how was the model trained? (R3)
- **Energy GNN performance is only reported for specific task sizes.** It is unclear whether models are trained on the same graph sizes as inference or whether they generalize across sizes. (R4)
- **Missing discussions on practical runtime.** How many iterations do implicit GNNs and EGNN need to converge? Figure 2 in the appendix shows ~2,000 updates per node on a graph of size 10; this raises questions about efficiency versus a 10-layer synchronous GNN. (R4)
- **Related work gap.** Asynchronous GNN literature (Faber et al., Dudzik et al.) should be cited and discussed. (R4)
- **Presentation inconsistencies.** Capitalization of “Energy GNN” varies; citation format is inconsistent (some first names abbreviated, some not). (R1, R4)
- **Minor issues:** The appendix contains broken reference links; notation inconsistencies (node-level vs. graph-level definitions in Section 6); some figures/histograms (e.g., analogous to Figure 1(d) for Energy GNN) are missing. (R1, R4)
