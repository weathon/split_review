## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes scaling laws for sparsely-connected foundation models, extending prior scaling laws (parameter count N, data D) to include weight sparsity S. The authors derive a formulation relating validation loss to S, number of non-zero parameters, and training data, validated experimentally on Vision Transformers (ViT/JFT-4B) and T5 (C4). They investigate optimal sparsity (including the finding that for a fixed number of non-zero parameters optimal sparsity increases with training data), and extend the study to hardware-friendly structured sparsity (n:m pattern) and pruning from a pretrained dense model. The work is the first large-scale theoretical and experimental analysis of sparsity scaling laws, and the derived laws can predict optimal sparsity without retraining.

### Strengths

- Investigates a highly relevant topic – scaling laws of sparse large models – with clear formulation derivation and validation on two architectures (ViT and T5).
- Provides comprehensive experiments, including hardware-friendly structured sparsity (n:m pattern) and pruning strategies, with detailed experimental results and good writing quality.
- In-depth investigation of the relationship between validation loss and sparsity, non-zero parameters, and training data; offers useful insights for understanding sparsification.
- Derives optimal sparsity for a given inference size and training budget, and shows that the benefit of long training is more obvious for sparse models than dense.
- First paper to combine large-scale experiments (ViT on JFT-4B, T5 on C4) with theoretical analysis to produce scaling laws that can accurately predict model performance under sparsity.
- Findings help configure training parameters scientifically and suggest dynamically adjusting sparsity during training; the result that optimal sparsity increases with data volume provides practical guidance.
- Empirically, ViT and T5 show strong scaling curves for sparsity, and their performances are well predicted by the constructed scaling law.

### Weaknesses

- (Reviewer 1) The paper posits that sparsity reduces training costs, but current sparse training methods often have the same computational cost as dense training. This raises a question about the meaningfulness of studying optimal sparsity if sparse training does not actually lower costs. (Note: this reviewer gave a score of 8 despite this concern, so it is seen as a minor weakness.)
- (Reviewer 2) The scale of the experiments (model and data sizes) is significantly smaller than those used in Chinchilla scaling law studies. More elaboration is needed on what specific improvements in downstream task performance or operational efficiency are guaranteed by the theoretical claims.
- (Reviewer 3, both as criticism and question) Section 2′s fair evaluation argument (comparing sparse and dense models with the same number of parameters and compute) is very similar to the “training budget” argument in prior work [1]. The paper should clarify the differences. (Question: Have the authors explored the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) setting for sparse training on foundation models? If not, a discussion on LTH [2] should be added.)
- (Reviewer 3) Evaluation is limited to the encoder-decoder T5 architecture; the more popular autoregressive decoder-only architecture (on which the original scaling laws were built) is not tested. This limits the generality of the findings.
- (Reviewer 3, question) Can the proposed scaling laws be applied at extreme sparsity levels (e.g., 95% or 99%), or are they only valid within a certain sparsity range? The paper should address the regime of applicability.
- (Reviewer 4) Only two model families (ViT and T5) are tested. Despite resource constraints, this may not be sufficiently convincing for a claimed scaling law.
- (Reviewer 4) The scaling law involves 7 free parameters, which could be hard to generalize to new datasets without sufficient data to fit them. The paper should discuss how these parameters change across different datasets and hyperparameters (N, D, S) and whether the number of degrees of freedom is justified.
- (Reviewer 4) The procedure to identify the optimal sparsity \(S_{opt}\) in Section 2.2 is confusing; a clearer explanation is needed.
