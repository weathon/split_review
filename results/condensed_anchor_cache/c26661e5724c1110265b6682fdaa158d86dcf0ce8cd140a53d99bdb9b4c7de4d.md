## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM), a method for training continuous normalizing flows on Riemannian manifolds. It constructs conditional flows using a premetric (a generalization of distance) that yields closed-form target vector fields. This avoids expensive simulation or divergence computation, enabling scalable training. The method is applied to simple geometries (e.g., spheres, tori) and general geometries such as triangular meshes and manifolds with boundaries.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and efficiency:** The construction of conditional flows via premetrics is novel, efficient, and applicable to general geometries, including non-trivial curvature and boundaries (e.g., triangular mesh, maze). (R1, R2, R3)
- **Key advantages:** The method is simulation-free on simple geometries, does not require divergence computation, and is scalable to high dimensions, overcoming limitations of prior Riemannian diffusion models. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-written, easy to follow, with sufficient background and clear explanation of aims, methods, and results. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Empirical support:** Convincing experiments across diverse real-world non-Euclidean datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, supporting the conclusions. Theoretical claims appear complete and correct. (R1)
- **Extensibility:** The framework can handle manifolds with highly non-trivial curvature and boundaries, whereas previous diffusion models were limited to simple manifolds (e.g., sphere, torus) and their products. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Simulation-free advantage not quantified:** The practical speed advantage over simulation-based methods like RSGM is unclear. RSGM reports achieving similar test NLL with only 5 in-training simulation steps (Section O.1). A comparison of training time or wall-clock speed is needed to justify the claim. (R2)
- **Unexplained performance gaps:** The paper claims state-of-the-art but on the Earthquake and Flood datasets, CNF Matching outperforms RFM (cf. Table 2, where Flood result is bolded despite CNF Matching being better). The reasons for these gaps and why RFM outperforms Riemannian Diffusion are not explained. (R2)
- **Missing ablation on spectral distance truncation \(k\):** No experiments study the effect of the number of terms \(k\) in the spectral distance (Eq. (16)). How does \(k\) affect learned vector field accuracy, sample quality, and computational cost? Is there a point of diminishing returns? (R1)
- **Missing ablation on scheduler \(\kappa(t)\):** The choice of scheduler is not explored. Experiments comparing different schedulers (e.g., linear, cosine, exponential) would provide practical insight into training stability and sample quality, especially on different manifolds. (R1)
- **Premetric design rationale insufficient:** The paper would benefit from a discussion of what makes a "good" premetric. For instance, why choose diffusion distance over biharmonic distance? Are spectral distances chosen primarily for computational convenience, or are there deeper reasons? Why use the spectrum of the Laplace-Beltrami operator instead of another operator? (R1)
- **Non-uniform base distributions not explored:** While the theory allows arbitrary base distributions, no experiments or discussion address practical challenges or potential benefits of non-uniform base measures beyond the uniform distribution. (R1)
- **Clarification needed on applicability of prior methods:** Can other diffusion models (e.g., RSGM) be applied to general manifolds like a triangular mesh (e.g., using denoised score matching with Varadhan approximation)? A comparison or discussion would help contextualize the paper's contribution. (R2)

*Note: Minor clarification required: In Table 2, the Flood result for RFM is bolded even though CNF Matching shows a better result; this appears to be an error in highlighting. (R2)*
