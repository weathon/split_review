## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RE-Adapt and its low-rank variant LoRE-Adapt to adapt instruction-tuned LLMs to new domains without losing instruction-following ability. The method extracts an instruction-following adapter as the weight difference between an instructed model and its pretrained base, then combines it (via weighted addition) with a domain-knowledge adapter obtained by fine-tuning the base model on new-domain data. Experiments are conducted on StreamingQA and RetrievalQA (with and without RAG), evaluating in-domain performance and out-of-domain performance on Natural Questions, using three 7B LLMs (Gemma, Llama 2, Mistral). Reviewers agree the problem is important and the method is simple and well-motivated, but there is significant disagreement: two reviewers (scores 5) find the approach practical and well-grounded, while two reviewers (scores 3) raise major concerns about lack of novelty, insufficient empirical validation, missing baselines, and inadequate analysis of hyperparameters and experimental details.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and practically relevant challenge: adapting LLMs to new domains while preserving pre-existing instruction-following capabilities.
- The proposed method is straightforward, simple to understand, and easy to apply in practice; it builds on well-known concepts (weight arithmetic, adapter combination) in a clear manner.
- The method is well-grounded in existing literature on LLM arithmetic and the modular nature of model weights.
- The low-rank variant LoRE-Adapt reduces memory usage compared to RE-Adapt, leveraging the advantages of low-rank approximation.
- RE-Adapt and LoRE-Adapt are validated on three different LLM families (Gemma, Llama 2, Mistral), showing some generalization across models.
- The approach is compatible with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), demonstrating potential for integration with training-free methods.
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, with a comprehensive Related Work section.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient task and evaluation scope.** The experiments cover only question-answering tasks (StreamingQA, RetrievalQA, Natural Questions). There is no evaluation on broader instruction-following benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, HellaSwag, open-ended generation, summarization) to confirm that instruction-following abilities are preserved after adaptation. Multiple reviewers (2, 3, 4) stress that this is a critical gap.
2. **Missing baselines and comparisons.**  
   - No baseline of fine-tuning an instruction-tuned model directly on data reformatted to match the instruction style (e.g., converting StreamingQA into an instruction-following dataset). This would be a more straightforward comparison. (Reviewer 3)  
   - No baseline with a simple downscaled version of the instruction adapter (e.g., Φ + 0.5Δ) to separate the effect of downscaling from the effect of learning the domain adapter. (Reviewer 4)  
   - No LoRA-only baseline for comparison with LoRE-Adapt; since LoRE-Adapt uses LoRA for the domain adapter, a direct LoRA fine-tuning of the pretrained model (or instructed model) should be included. (Reviewer 1)  
   - No comparison with other adaptation approaches beyond DoRA (e.g., full fine-tuning with careful learning rate tuning). (Reviewer 4)  
   - The baseline of fine-tuning an instructed model on domain data uses a fixed learning rate of 2e-4, which may be too high to avoid catastrophic forgetting (Chirkova et al., 2024); lower learning rates should be explored. (Reviewer 4)
3. **Lack of novelty and theoretical insight.** The core techniques – extracting an adapter via weight difference, using SVD for low-rank approximation, and linearly combining adapters – are well-established (LoRA, task arithmetic). The partial adaptation strategy (learning a scalar factor for each adapter) is similar to prior work (Inarco, 2023). The paper does not provide new theoretical insights into why the method works; the significant improvements are surprising and could stem from regularization effects (e.g., weight averaging) rather than the claimed mechanism. (Reviewers 3, 4)
4. **Insufficient hyperparameter and sensitivity analysis.**  
   - No analysis of the sensitivity of the scaling factor λ (controlling the strength of the reverse-engineered adapter) despite a mention in Section 4.1. (Reviewer 2)  
   - No experimental study of the downsampling ratio τ in LoRE-Adapt and its impact on performance and inference cost. (Reviewer 3)  
   - No analysis of the balance between α and β in Equation (4). (Reviewer 3)  
   - No analysis of why τ = 0.5 yields the best performance; increasing τ degrades performance but also increases parameters, which is unexplained. (Reviewer 3)
5. **Incomplete and unclear experimental details.**  
   - Tables 2 and 3 report identical scores for some models (e.g., Gemma Instruct, Gemma Instruct+DORA, Gemma RE-Adapt all show 26 on Natural Questions); results should be reported to two decimal places for clarity. (Reviewer 2)  
   - Figure 3 claims that at τ = 1 the parameter size reaches 100%, but due to LoRA’s structure (two matrices A and B), the parameter size should be 200%. (Reviewer 3)  
   - The SVD decomposition process is not clearly explained: which parameter matrices are decomposed? Is it the entire parameter matrix of each layer? The rank per layer differs? (Reviewers 3, 4)  
   - The paper does not specify the decoding strategy, metric preprocessing (e.g., lowercasing), or details of the retrieval datastore construction and BM25 parameters. (Reviewer 4)  
   - The analysis of cumulative explained variance is only performed on three layers (k_proj); the choice of layers and exclusion of other layers are not justified. (Reviewer 1)
6. **Questionable interpretations and claims.**  
   - The claim that “any fine-tuning approach is applicable” (line 201) is misleading: full-parameter fine-tuning prevents combining weights as in Equation (4). (Reviewer 1)  
   - The interpretation that RE-Adapt improves out-of-domain results because “instruction-tuning likely degrades knowledge from pretraining” (line 374) does not necessarily follow; the improvement could be due to regularization from weight averaging. (Reviewer 4)  
   - The interpretation that RE-Adapt reduces incorrect interpretations of RAG context (line 434) is similarly unsupported; improvement may come from downscaling the Re-adapter alone. (Reviewer 4)  
   - LoRE-Adapt sometimes outperforms RE-Adapt (e.g., on Mistral-7B) but no explanation is provided. (Reviewer 3)
7. **Methodological and practical limitations.**  
   - The method requires both a pretrained base model and its instruction-tuned variant; applicability is limited if only one version is available or if the instruction-tuned model is weaker. (Reviewer 1)  
   - The RAG setup in RetrievalQA uses a BM25 retriever that searches only within in-domain data; a more realistic scenario would use a diverse external datastore. (Reviewer 1)  
   - Experiments are conducted only on 7B-scale models; it is unclear whether the benefits hold for smaller (<7B) or larger (>7B) models. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The paper does not report the time cost of SVD decomposition relative to the DoRA tuning process. (Reviewer 4)
8. **Missing citations and textual issues.**  
   - Relevant work on composing parameter-efficient modules with arithmetic operations (Zhang et al., 2023, NeurIPS) is not cited. (Reviewer 1)  
   - Several typos and unclear phrases: “Parameter Efficient Fine Tuning” should be “Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning” (L28); “data driven” should be “data-driven” (L114); “(RAG) Lewis et al. (2020)” should be “(RAG; Lewis et al., 2020)” (L409); “lacking the annotations expected from instruct models” (L37) is unclear; “when answering unrelated questions” (L261) is vague; “In section 5” appears inside section 5 (L284); “derived from our subset of articles” (L300) is ambiguous. (Reviewers 1, 4)  
   - Table 2 has several cases where a baseline achieves the same performance as the proposed method but is not bolded (e.g., Gemma Instruct+DoRA, second column: 18). (Reviewer 4)
