## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MEEPO, a point cloud segmentation architecture that integrates CNN and Mamba (state-space model) to achieve efficient contextual modeling with linear complexity. It identifies key requirements for effective segmentation—spatial locality and robust contextual understanding—and enhances Mamba by removing its causality and introducing a Strided Bidirectional SSM to capture spatial relationships in unordered point clouds. The method surpasses prior state-of-the-art PTv3 by up to +0.8 mIoU on ScanNet, ScanNet200, S3DIS, and nuScenes, while being 42.1% faster and 5.53× more memory efficient. The paper is commended for its analysis-driven approach and thorough ablations, but concerns are raised about limited novelty, insufficient evidence for certain claims, scaling/generalizability, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Analysis-driven methodology:** The paper provides clear insights from prior work (CNN, Transformer, Mamba) to derive design decisions, making the approach convincing and easy to follow (R1, R5).
- **Strong performance and efficiency:** MEEPO achieves state-of-the-art mIoU (±0.8 over PTv3) on four major benchmarks (ScanNet, ScanNet200, S3DIS, nuScenes) with 42.1% faster inference and 5.53× memory savings (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- **Comprehensive ablations:** Extensive experiments validate the contributions of causal-free Mamba, bidirectional strided SSM, and the integration of CNN local features; the paper is detailed and clearly structured (R2, R3).
- **Addresses Mamba’s original limitations:** The causal-free modification and bidirectional strided SSM effectively mitigate the unsuitability of enforced causality and directional bias for unordered point clouds (R3, R4).
- **Meaningful comparison of CNN/Transformer/Mamba blocks:** The paper systematically examines the roles of local and contextual modeling, helping to understand the architecture design space (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited architectural novelty / incremental changes**  
  * Multiple reviewers (R2, R4, R5) note that the proposed modifications—causal-free convolution and bidirectional strided SSM—are small fixes or “tricks” on top of existing Mamba, and that the overall architecture remains largely unchanged from prior Mamba-based vision models.  
  * The bidirectional SSM has already been introduced in VisionMamba (Zhu et al., 2024); this paper only adds a stride mechanism (R2, R3).  
  * The causal-free block is not clearly explained or illustrated; a more detailed description is needed (R2).  
  * R4 questions whether the Causal-Free Conv1D is redundant given an additional CNN module already captures local features.

- **Insufficient evidence for block comparison and key claims**  
  * The conclusion that “CNN is more effective for local modeling and Transformer is better at handling contextual information” is based on a single example per block type, with no systematic exploration of hyperparameters, network depth, or architectural variations. R2 calls for theoretical support or extensive multi-dataset experiments.  
  * R5 challenges the claim that Mamba is effective for local processing: the “locally-biased forget gate” lacks detailed analysis or statistical data beyond visualization; it is not obvious how the forget gate promotes local processing without a notion of time in point clouds.  
  * The paper states that long-range attention is unnecessary (Sec. 4.2), but this may only hold due to data sparsity or insufficient training data. Evidence such as experiments with varying data amounts or different transformer architectures is missing (R5).  
  * R3 notes that no figure shows Mamba’s performance with varying input lengths (analogous to Fig. 4 for PTv3), leaving the claim of superior long-sequence handling unsubstantiated.

- **Scaling and generalizability concerns**  
  * Key analyses (e.g., PTv3 window-size ablation) are done on ScanNet (∼1,500 samples) trained from scratch. Properties may change with larger data; the degradation at window sizes >1024 may reflect overfitting rather than an inherent limitation. Larger-scale and multi-dataset training (e.g., ScanNet+S3DIS+Semantic3D) is needed to demonstrate scalability (R1).  
  * The paper focuses solely on semantic segmentation. Extending to downstream tasks (instance segmentation, object detection) would strengthen claims about representation quality and backbone utility. R1 and R5 both request such evaluations, even if not state-of-the-art.

- **Missing ablation / comparison details**  
  * In Table 7 (strided SSM), strides 2–16 are compared but stride 1 (i.e., no stride) is omitted, making it hard to assess whether the stride mechanism itself is beneficial over a simple bidirectional approach (R3).  
  * The parameter count of CNN+Mamba is reported as smaller than pure Mamba, which is counter-intuitive given the added CNN module. R3 requests a detailed explanation (e.g., channel sizes, kernel choices).  
  * Notation inconsistency: “Strided Bidirectional SSM” and “Bidirectional Strided SSM” are used interchangeably (R2).  
  * A minor but reported error: hyperlink in Sec. 3.3 points to Fig. 4 instead of Fig. 5 (R2, R3).  
  * R4 asks about similarities with “Point Cobra” and requests clarification on the source of latency/memory improvements relative to that work.  
  * The bidirectional structure in Fig. 6(b) is not clearly depicted; R2 questions whether it matches VisionMamba. If it does, the reported +0.1% mIoU improvement seems inconsistent with VisionMamba’s larger gains, raising questions about necessity (R2).
