## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies long-context extrapolation for Transformer models by proposing two periodic-extension-based positional encoding methods, Extra-PE and Extra-MPE, that aim to extend context length without disrupting the positional encoding distribution within the training length. It provides a theoretical analysis of RoPE extrapolation and experiments on Llama 2, reporting that a model fine-tuned on 32k tokens can extrapolate beyond 80k tokens, outperforming NTK-32k and approaching YaRN-64k, with only 100 fine-tuning steps (one-fourth of YaRN’s steps). The methods maintain stable attention at ultra-long distances.

### Strengths

*   The research topic is well-motivated and important for real-world AI systems (R1, R2).
*   The two proposed methods (Extra-PE and Extra-MPE) preserve the original positional encoding distribution within the training length, ensuring that in-distribution attention is maintained while enabling longer-context handling (R3). The core implementation is straightforward (Eq. 4) (R3).
*   The paper combines theoretical analysis with experimental validation, exploring multiple facets (fine-tuning steps, context lengths, perplexity) (R3). The methods are well-motivated, with grounding in a review of existing work (R2).

### Weaknesses

*   **Poor writing, organization, and presentation.**  
    *   The paper places nearly all experimental results and evaluation details in the appendix, leaving the main text with empty space and insufficient experimental exposition (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
    *   Figures are poorly designed: Figure 1 has entangled curves, repeated colors, and shows proposed methods worsening perplexity while comparing mismatched context lengths (proposed ≤32k vs. YaRN-64k) (R1). Figure 2 is unreadable because curves are hidden under the purple line (R1).  
    *   Text primarily compares Extra-PE vs. Extra-MPE (two variants of the same method) rather than external baselines, and Tables 1–2 show proposed methods are *inferior* to baselines (R4).  
    *   The paper is not well-written overall and requires major revision (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer (R4, rating 1) strongly disagrees with the other three (all rating 3), stating the paper is “far from ready to be accepted”.  

*   **Lack of rigorous theoretical justification.**  
    *   Section 3 (“Theory Perspective”) presents only hypothesis and intuition, not a formal theory (R1). Even the more positive reviewer (R3) finds the theoretical insights present but the paper still lacks rigorous justification. Another reviewer (R4) could not follow the theoretical results.  

*   **Missing baselines, incomplete comparisons, and unconvincing evaluation.**  
    *   In Figure 3, YaRN-64k is shown as best performing, but YaRN-32k, YaRN-16k, and YaRN-8k are omitted for a fair comparison (R3).  
    *   In perplexity figures (Appendix B.1.2, B.1.3), key baseline methods are missing or show lower perplexity than the proposed methods, making the claimed improvement unclear (R2).  
    *   The paper relies primarily on perplexity, which may not correlate with real-world experience (R1). The LongBench evaluation uses only a subset of tasks, which is not convincing (R1).  

*   **Unclear motivation for the two methods and their practical choice.**  
    *   Section 4 proposes Extra-PE and Extra-MPE without clear motivation for each design, and it is unclear which method should be used when the generation length is not predetermined (R1).  

*   **Undiscussed ambiguity risk from periodic extension.**  
    *   The periodic extension assigns the same positional encoding values to tokens beyond the training length, potentially causing the model to confuse distant tokens with those at earlier positions. The paper does not discuss this risk or provide any mitigation strategy (R2, also asked as a question).  

*   **Unexplained anomalous experimental results.**  
    *   In Table 2 (Appendix B.1.4), Extra-PE‑8k surpasses all longer-context variants by a significant margin, but no analysis is provided for this observation (R3).  

*   **Clarity and notation issues.**  
    *   The term `d_extra` in Section 4 is not defined before use (R2).  
    *   Appendix B.1.4’s table lacks explanation of the evaluation metrics shown (R2).  
    *   Citation style uses `\cite{}` for all citations instead of `\citep{}` for parenthetical references (R4).
