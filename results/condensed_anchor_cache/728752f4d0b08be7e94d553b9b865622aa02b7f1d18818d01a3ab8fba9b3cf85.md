## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ResTran, an unsupervised feature transformation that uses the pseudo-inverse of the graph Laplacian (related to effective resistance) to embed both node features and graph topology into a node-wise vector.  Standard vector-based classifiers (e.g., SVM) are then applied for node classification.  The authors argue that ResTran is robust to the homophilous bias of GNNs and is simpler than message‑passing architectures.  Theoretical connections to effective resistance, k‑means, and spectral clustering are provided.

### Strengths
- **Potential robustness to homophilous bias and simplicity**: The transformation is simple and may effectively capture topology while avoiding the homophilous bias of standard GNNs (Reviewer 1).  The idea of devising novel positional encodings to bypass message‑passing is worthwhile (Reviewer 3).
- **Contextualization and exposition**: The paper provides an extensive overview of spectral clustering preliminaries and is well‑written and easy to follow (Reviewer 2).  Placing the method in the homophily/heterophily narrative is useful (Reviewer 3).
- **Connection to unsupervised graph learning**: The link to unsupervised graph learning methods is interesting (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient literature survey and limited novelty**: Multiple reviewers note that the paper overlooks a large body of relevant work.  
  - Spectral GNNs (e.g., [1,2,3]), LINKX, and recent GNNs that handle heterophily without over‑smoothing (e.g., JKNet, H2GCN, Geom‑GCN, GPR‑GNN, GPNN) are not cited or compared, making the claim that existing GNNs fail on heterophilic graphs inaccurate (Reviewers 2, 4).  
  - The core idea of obtaining unsupervised node embeddings from features and topology is similar to SIGN (and its extensions SAGN, GAMLP), which computes propagated features \(X, AX, A^2X, \dots\) and concatenates them.  ResTran’s use of Krylov subspace methods is conceptually close, yet no comparison or differentiation is provided (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 2 finds the paper offers little novelty beyond traditional spectral clustering methods; Reviewer 4 calls the novelty “extremely limited.”
- **Missing complexity analysis**: The claim that ResTran is simpler than GNNs is unsubstantiated.  
  - No formal time or space complexity analysis is given (Reviewer 2).  
  - Reviewer 3 requests a breakdown of the runtime for computing the Laplacian basis (full eigendecomposition vs. Krylov subspace) versus the downstream learning cost, especially on sparse graphs where the naïve \(O(n^3)\) cost is prohibitive.  
  - Reviewer 1 notes that the need for Krylov approximation and the poor performance of simple downstream models (e.g., SVM) undermine the simplicity argument; competitive results require complex semi‑supervised methods (VAT, AVAE).
- **Fairness of experimental comparisons**:  
  - Reviewer 1 points out that ResTran is evaluated with semi‑supervised methods (VAT, AVAE) that include adversarial training, while baseline GNNs are not given those additional components—a confounding factor.  The choice of a non‑standard dataset split is also questioned.  
  - Hyperparameter selection (e.g., the shift parameter \(b\) in the Laplacian) is not justified; no ablation studies are provided (Reviewer 2).  
  - Reviewers 4 and 2 recommend comparing directly with spectral GNNs and SIGN to properly benchmark the method.
- **Limited expressive power of single transformation**: Using only one transformation of the form \(L^+X\) (analogous to a 1‑layer global MPNN) may avoid over‑smoothing but also restricts the model from capturing hierarchical or multi‑hop relationships that are critical for many graph tasks (Reviewer 1).  This limitation is consistent with the poor performance of simple downstream classifiers and the reliance on complex semi‑supervised methods.
- **Spectral embeddings miss relational structure**: As noted by Reviewer 3, spectral methods treat all nodes homogeneously and cannot represent node/edge attributes or relational context the way combinatorial message‑passing can.  The paper includes no experiments on graphs with rich features (e.g., molecular or synthetic relational graphs) to test this limitation.
- **Preliminaries take disproportionate space**: Over two pages of the main paper are devoted to well‑known spectral clustering results that could be condensed or moved to an appendix (Reviewer 2).
- **Remaining open questions**:  
  - How does ResTran differ from standard spectral positional encodings used in the literature? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Would ResTran + a simple MLP + cross‑entropy loss work, or are the complex semi‑supervised methods essential? (Reviewer 1)  
  - How should the shift parameter \(b\) and other hyperparameters be chosen in practice, and how sensitive is the method to them? (Reviewer 2)

**Note**: One reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 5, low confidence 2) is more positive about the potential of the idea but still raises significant concerns about complexity, relational expressiveness, and missing experiments.  The majority (Reviewers 1, 2, 4) view the novelty as low and the literature coverage as insufficient.
