## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers agree the paper investigates collaborative decoding between small and large language models (SLMs/LLMs) using speculative decoding, contrastive decoding, and proxy tuning. The FS-GEN framework (System 2 = LLM, System 1 = SLM) is introduced, and findings include that collaboration is needed in only ~20% of steps, follows a parameter-ratio scaling law, and is most necessary at the start of decoding and for high-uncertainty tokens. Experiments are conducted on MMLU-STEM, GSM8k, and MBPP using Qwen and Pythia model families. Reviewers rated the paper 6,6,6,3 — one reviewer (score 3) finds the presentation weak and the analysis unconvincing; the other three (scores 6) consider the work interesting and the results promising despite several weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel FS-GEN framework for unifying and analyzing collaborative decoding methods (Reviewer 1; also noted by Reviewer 3).
- Tables and figures are well used, contributing to clarity (Reviewer 1).
- The paper compares models based on parameter counts, providing a systematic view (Reviewer 1).
- The research addresses an under-explored but important and emerging area (Reviewer 2).
- The findings are interesting, particularly the “2:8 law” (collaboration frequency ~20%), the observation that collaboration is most needed at the beginning of decoding, and that high-uncertainty tokens in System 1 are more likely to require intervention (Reviewers 2, 3).
- These findings could spur targeted research in collaborative decoding (Reviewer 2).
- Experimental benchmarks cover different capabilities (knowledge: MMLU-STEM, math: GSM8k, coding: MBPP) and two LLM families (Qwen and Pythia) (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of interpreting collaborative decoding through a System 1/System 2 lens is interesting (Reviewer 4, despite reservations about the execution).

### Weaknesses
- **System 1/2 analogy poorly fleshed out.** The analogy feels like a distraction from the main contributions (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4 also questions the motivation, noting that LLMs might be better at both intuition and deliberate reasoning). The claim that different collaborative distributions follow from human cognitive processes is considered flawed reasoning (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of practical application details and trade-offs.** The paper could discuss practical applications more and analyze trade-offs between inference time and cost (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited task/domain coverage.** Experiments only on MMLU-STEM, GSM8k, and MBPP; domain-specific datasets would give better understanding (Reviewer 1). One reviewer questions generalizability to other language tasks or domains (Reviewer 1’s question, which overlaps with this criticism).
- **Scaling law line fits not convincing.** The parameter-ratio scaling plots, especially for Pythia, do not show convincing line fits (Reviewer 2).
- **Uncertainty analysis is only qualitative.** Quantitative metrics to support the uncertainty hypothesis across tasks and model families are missing, making it hard to have confidence in the finding (Reviewer 2).
- **Actionable insights limited to speculative decoding.** It is not clear how the analysis provides executable insights for contrastive decoding and proxy tuning (Reviewer 3; also noted in Reviewer 3’s question).
- **Writing and presentation issues.**
    - Several minor errors: “allow” should be “allows” (L053), “produce” should be “produced” (L195), and O_g is used instead of O_f for fused logits in Section 2.2 (Reviewer 3).
    - Figures 11, 12, and 13 are unclear—what are the different features? (Reviewer 3).
    - The paper reads like a slide deck with many conclusions and graphics but little detail on experimental setup or how conclusions are drawn; figures are hardly described (Reviewer 4).
    - No accuracy scores reported to show how well collaborative decoding actually works or how performance depends on frequency/position of collaboration (Reviewer 4).
    - No discussion of how results differ between benchmarks (Reviewer 4).
    - Related work is pushed to the appendix and not referred to in the main text—an unusual and questionable choice (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Missing alternative measurement.** Greedy decoding is used to decide whether collaboration is needed; next-token perplexity could be another measure (Reviewer 3’s question, which overlaps with a methodological weakness).
- **Different sampling techniques not explored.** One reviewer asks how collaborative patterns might change with different sampling techniques (Reviewer 1’s question, overlapping with missing ablation).
- **(Minority reviewer criticism)** One reviewer (score 3) expresses strong dissatisfaction with the presentation: soundness 1, presentation 1, and states the paper does not convince them that exploring collaborative decoding in this way is meaningful. This reviewer also notes that the System 1/System 2 motivation is not sufficiently justified.
