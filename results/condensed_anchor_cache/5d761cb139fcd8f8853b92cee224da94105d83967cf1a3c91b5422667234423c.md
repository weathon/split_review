## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers split 5, 5, 3, 3. Reviewers 1 and 2 (high confidence 5) praise the method as achieving SOTA on classic benchmarks and having an insightful idea linking optimal transport to class relationships. Reviewers 3 and 4 (confidence 4-5) find the novelty limited, the experimental improvements small, and the paper lacking in necessary analysis, ablations, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The proposed method (OTMatch) provides state-of-the-art performance on classic semi-supervised learning benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, STL-10) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The paper establishes a connection between optimal transport and thresholding-based SSL methods (Reviewer 1).
- The idea of exploiting inherent relationships among classes via optimal transport is interesting and insightful to some extent (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- The motivation behind the work is evident and the paper is well organized, making it easy to follow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and significance**: The novelty is limited. Using optimal transport for SSL has already been proposed in prior work [1]. Compared to FreeMatch, OTMatch only adds an additional loss term \(L_{un3}\) without significant improvement (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). While Reviewers 1 and 2 find the performance SOTA, Reviewers 3 and 4 argue the improvement is not significant and the paper lacks necessary experimental comparisons to existing OT-based SSL methods (Reviewer 4). The improvement on many-class datasets like ImageNet and CIFAR-100 is particularly limited (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation studies**:
  - No ablation on the loss weight of the proposed OT loss term; how it affects training is not investigated (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
  - No ablation on the parameter \(\epsilon\) for optimal transport; how the appropriate value is determined is not explained (Reviewer 3).
  - No ablation on the hyperparameters of the \(L_{un3}\) loss (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experiments and analyses**:
  - Evaluation on datasets of other modalities (as suggested by [1]) is expected but absent (Reviewer 1).
  - The claim of O(K) complexity relies on mild assumptions; actual runtime comparison is needed to justify this (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 further notes that these conditions may not be satisfied in real-world tasks, making the loss difficult to compute.
  - No comparison regarding convergence speed or loss curves (Reviewer 4).
  - No analysis of the impact of long-tail classes on the OT approach (Reviewer 4).
  - No analysis or discussion explaining *why* class relationships are helpful for SSL, or whether an easier method could exploit the same relationships (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - Section 4 lacks clarity and would benefit from further elucidation (Reviewer 3).
  - A structured algorithm or a comprehensively defined overall loss function is strongly recommended (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 1 is ambiguous; it is unclear what constitutes the input to the OT loss (Reviewer 3).
  - The ground truth matching matrix \(T\) in formulation (1) is not explained (Reviewer 3).
  - The rationale for choosing KL divergence as the loss function is not justified (Reviewer 3).
  - The statement "to push \(f(x)\) in the direction of \(w_k\)" needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
  - There is a typo: "q_ui" should be corrected to "Q_ui" (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing analysis on the advantages/disadvantages of threshold-free methods (Reviewer 4).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**: The difference between OT and contrastive-learning methods like SimMatch and CoMatch that also consider class relations is not addressed (Reviewer 1). All above missing analyses are requested as experiments or ablations.
