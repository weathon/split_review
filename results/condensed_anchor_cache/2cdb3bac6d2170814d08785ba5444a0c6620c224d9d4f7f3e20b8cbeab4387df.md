- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 6, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a model-aware debiasing method for conformal prediction applied to threshold ridge regression. Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty of explicitly tackling model bias in conformal prediction but raise significant concerns about theoretical correctness (Theorems 2–4, Lemma 2, unverifiable assumptions), flawed exchangeability claims for Assumption A1, insufficient empirical validation (limited datasets, missing baselines such as conformalized quantile regression, negligible improvement in several settings), and serious presentation and proofreading problems.

### Strengths
- Several reviewers note the novelty and importance of addressing model bias in conformal prediction. Reviewer 3 states the contribution is “fairly novel” and “first in tackling explicitly the issue of model bias in conformal prediction” in a “structured and interesting way.”
- Reviewer 2 acknowledges that debiasing predictors is “a topic that has attracted interest” and that the paper aims to provide theoretical results, which is “ambitious and relevant.”
- Reviewer 4 agrees the work considers an “important question (efficiency of predictive inference)” and contains “interesting observations that could potentially lead to improvement.”
- Reviewer 1 states the proposed technique “addresses some shortcomings of the basic conformal prediction method” and that “the results are adequately explained.”
- The paper provides theoretical analysis aiming for finite-sample marginal coverage, asymptotic conditional validity, and convergence to the oracle prediction band under certain conditions.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical correctness concerns:**  
  - Reviewer 1 questions Theorems 2–4: Theorem 2 uses Hölder continuity with respect to the inverse of the empirical CDF, but the empirical CDF is not continuous. After the rebuttal, the theory was only partially corrected and still contains misprints and wrong references.  
  - Reviewer 2 states Theorem 1 offers “absolutely no new insights, lacking substance and applicability.” Theorem 2 relies on “no less than ten assumptions” that are “fundamentally unverifiable in practice” and “systematically violated” in real applications.  
  - Theorem 3 is not properly proven; the proof is stated as “the rest of the proof is the same than Theorem 2,” but the conclusion differs; an explicit constant η should be provided.  
  - Lemma 2 appears to claim that F₁(F₁⁻¹(q)) = q without any assumptions about F₁, leaving important conditions unconsidered.  
  - Reviewer 2 points out an inconsistency in Eq. (25): |X_i θ̂ - X_i θ| = O_p(n^{α_θ - δ} + n^{-η}) = O_p(n^{-η}) is claimed, but the text says “Theorem 2 demonstrates that the conformal interval converges to the oracle prediction band if δ as n → ∞. Unfortunately, α_θ is typically not less than δ… There is something wrong.” A proof of Theorem 4 is also demanded.  
  - Reviewer 1 asks how Hölder continuity can be applied to the empirical CDF.  
  - Reviewer 4 notes an error on line 199: the width of the band is stated as 2T_{1-α}(|Y_i - Ŷ_i|), which is not true for e.g., the standardized absolute fitted residual; also notes repeated referral to (3) on line 193.

- **Assumption A1 and exchangeability:**  
  - Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, and Reviewer 4 all note that Assumption A1 does not imply exchangeability. Reviewer 2 calls the claim that A1 “ensures that the data fulfill the condition of interchangeability” “absolutely false as written.” Reviewer 4 gives a counterexample (X₁,Y₁) = (X₂,Y₂) and (X₃,Y₃) independent. The paper needs to clarify or correct this assumption.

- **Conditional coverage claim not validated:**  
  - Reviewer 1 argues the claim of “asymptotic conditional coverage” is not properly checked with experiments; even if conditions are not fully satisfied, it would be valuable to see to what extent conditional coverage is achieved. Reviewer 1 explicitly asks: “Have you checked the conditional validity experimentally? Is it achieved for any of the considered dataset?”

- **Limited and unconvincing empirical evaluation:**  
  - Reviewer 3: “My main concern about the paper lies in the simulation… The authors focus themselves only on a limited number of real world datasets… in some of the proposed tests the impact of debiasing… seems to provide very limited benefits with respect to non-debiased alternatives.” Reviewer 3 asks for a better “simulative” case, performance on other datasets, and identification of stylized situations where the method is more competitive.  
  - Reviewer 4: “numerical results are not convincing… in several settings, the improvement over CRR is not significant (after accounting for the variability). Second, there are many other ways to construct nonconformity scores (e.g., conformalized quantile regression [Romano et al. 2019], conformalized distributional regression [Chernozhukov et al. 2021]) that have not been compared.”

- **Restrictiveness of the proposed method:**  
  - Reviewer 4 notes the discussion is under a linear model (inferred from Assumption B.3) with ridge regression prediction and requires several assumptions for provable efficiency improvement; under these conditions, better ways to construct prediction intervals exist and are not discussed.  
  - Reviewer 1 questions: “The title of the paper does not mention ridge regression specifically. How can ‘model-aware de-biasing’ be applied to a wider class of prediction models?”

- **Presentation and clarity issues:**  
  - Reviewer 1: notation in Sections 2–3 mixes full and split conformal; Section 2.2 needs improvement; formula (2) leaves “μ^{y}” undefined; formula (5) should be “until (n)” and dependence on y is strange; Section 3 has the same mistake with quantile; Figure 3 is hard to read (numbers/marks too small).  
  - Reviewer 2: “The presentation… was obviously produced in haste and suffers from poor proofreading, with numerous instances of awkwardly worded sentences.” Specifics: a placeholder “according to xxx” in the supplement; the A1 claim (see above); Theorem 3 proof issue; lack of rigor and precision makes it difficult to extract coherent insights.  
  - Reviewer 4: repeated referral to (3) on line 193; incorrect statement about width on line 199.

- **Computational cost:**  
  - Reviewer 1 notes the proposed approach requires cross-validation to tune parameters, increasing computational cost and bringing it closer to methods like neural network quantile regression.

- **Other specific questions and missing details:**  
  - Reviewer 2 asks for proof of Theorem 4 and clarification of the convergence rate inconsistency in Eq. (25).  
  - Reviewer 4 questions Assumption A2: is it supposed to be “the density of ε is symmetric about 0 and nonincreasing on ℝ₊”? Otherwise the density is a constant function.
