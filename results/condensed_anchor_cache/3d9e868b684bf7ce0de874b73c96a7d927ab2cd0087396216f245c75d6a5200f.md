## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes **NODEDUP (NodeDup)**, a data augmentation technique for Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that improves link prediction (LP) on low-degree ("cold") nodes while preserving performance on high-degree ("warm") nodes. The method duplicates low-degree nodes and connects each to its duplicate, providing a "multi-view" representation before standard supervised LP training. It is designed as a plug-and-play module with minimal computational overhead. Evaluated across multiple datasets, the authors report average relative improvements of **38.49% on isolated nodes, 13.34% on low-degree nodes, and 6.76% on warm nodes** compared to GNN baselines and existing cold-start methods. The paper claims this addresses the cold-start problem, particularly for recommendation systems. Reviewer scores were **5, 3, 3, 3**, with one reviewer being notably more positive than the other three.

### Strengths

- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation:** The paper presents extensive experiments across a wide range of datasets and comparisons (including traditional GNNs, cold-start GNNs, and data augmentation methods), providing strong empirical evidence for the claimed improvements. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Clear presentation and visualization:** The paper is well-organized and easy to follow. Figures and tables effectively communicate the method’s impact across different settings. (R1, R3)
- **Simplicity and ease of integration:** NodeDup is a simple augmentation technique that requires only duplicating low-degree nodes and adding edges to duplicates, and can be seamlessly applied to various GNN architectures. (R2, R4)
- **Addresses a practically significant problem:** Focusing on cold-start link prediction is important for real-world applications such as recommendation systems. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and unclear differentiation from self-loops:** The core idea of duplicating nodes is not novel, and the distinction from simply adding self-loops is insufficiently justified. With general message-passing GNNs, isolated nodes already can treat themselves as neighbors using separate projection matrices. Without clear differentiation, it is hard to argue why NodeDup is not just a re-weighting mechanism or equivalent to self-loop augmentation. (R2, R4) A direct comparison with self-loop augmentation as a baseline is missing. (R3, R4)
- **Lack of theoretical foundation:** The paper provides no theoretical analysis (e.g., theorems, lemmas, spectral analysis of graph Laplacian changes) explaining why node duplication improves cold-node representations. Multiple reviewers requested theoretical justification for the effectiveness. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Outdated and insufficient baselines:** Comparisons are made only against older baselines. Missing comparisons with recent link prediction models (e.g., **HGWaveNet, Neural Bellman-Ford Networks, LineGNN** [cited by R3]; **MPLP** [cited by R4]) and recent graph contrastive learning methods (**GCLMI, Sp²GCL, POT, GraphACL**, all 2024 [cited by R1]). This raises doubts about whether NodeDup achieves state-of-the-art performance. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Problem-dataset mismatch and missing recommendation datasets:** The cold-start problem is motivated by recommendation systems, but experiments use only general graph datasets (e.g., citation networks). Recommendation datasets such as **Movielens-1M or Amazon-Books** are not included, weakening the practical relevance. (R3)
- **Missing experiments on OGB benchmarks:** The authors state OGB datasets were not used because they “lack a substantial number of isolated or low-degree nodes,” but reviewers argue OGB datasets still contain low-degree nodes and would provide a more scalable evaluation, especially for verifying that warm-node performance is not compromised. Datasets like **collab, ppa, and citation2** should be tested. (R3, R4)
- **High hyperparameter sensitivity:** The threshold δ for defining cold-start nodes (fixed at degree ≤ 2) is critical, yet no clear rationale is given for this specific value. The method may require per-dataset tuning, limiting generalizability. (R1, R2)
- **Unclear explanation for warm-node improvements:** The augmentation is only applied to cold nodes, but NodeDup consistently improves performance on warm nodes (e.g., on citeseer). The paper does not adequately explain how this occurs. One reviewer noted that adding self-loops to cold nodes could introduce noise that degrades warm-node predictions (e.g., famous user with cold followers), but results show the opposite – this contradiction is not resolved. (R2, R4)
- **Potential computational and scalability concerns:** In graphs with highly skewed degree distributions, duplicating many low-degree nodes could nearly double the graph size. The paper does not analyze this overhead or discuss scalability to large graphs. (R2) Additionally, a reviewer pointed out that **Table 18 shows GCN+NodeDup declines in overall metrics** compared to GCN, which is not explained. (R3)
- **Method description lacks clarity:** Algorithm 1 does not specify how the cold-node set V_cold is deterministically obtained. (R3) The time complexity of the decoder is also questioned: it should be O(|V_cold|D) rather than O((M+|V_cold|)D). (R4)
- **Missing ablation on threshold and on self-loop equivalent:** The paper does not ablate the choice of threshold δ or compare NodeDup to simply adding self-loops to cold nodes. (R2, R3, R4) It also does not clarify whether self-loops are already present in the datasets used. (R4)
- **Minor issues:** A typo exists (line 400, “did” used incorrectly). (R4)
