## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes two foundational maximum state entropy (MaxEnt) algorithms: MaxEnt-H (Hazan et al.) and MaxEnt-LA (Liu & Abbeel). It claims to unify them by showing they share the same kNN-based reward and that the policy sampling parameter η in MaxEnt-H is unnecessary. The authors propose MaxEnt-Veritas (MaxEnt-V), which combines the uniform policy mixture of MaxEnt-LA with the policy optimization scheme of MaxEnt-H (freezing the reward until convergence). They provide a theoretical suboptimality bound and empirical results on three Mujoco environments. Reviewers agree the paper is clearly written and tackles an important problem, but three of four reviewers find major weaknesses in empirical evaluation, literature understanding, theoretical assumptions, and originality.

### Strengths
- **Clear comparison and motivation**: The paper provides a clear and detailed comparison between MaxEnt-H and MaxEnt-LA, with intentions stated coherently from the abstract (R1, R2, R3). The mathematical writing is strong and the explanation of why small η in Hazan leads to unrealistic T is helpful (R1).
- **Theoretical contribution**: Theorem 1 closes a gap between theory and practice in policy sampling schedule; the use of the Euler-Mascheroni constant in the telescoping sum analysis is novel (R3). The proof result is a significant improvement over Hazan’s bounds (R1). The algorithm is well-motivated with theoretical guarantees (R2).
- **Problem significance**: Maximum state entropy is a fundamental problem for RL exploration, making this investigation highly relevant (R3). The proposed freezing of intrinsic rewards resembles the target network trick and is an interesting idea (R4).
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 2, score 8)**: The paper is well written, the problem is interesting, and the algorithm outperforms alternatives both in theory and practice on several problems. Reviewer 2 found the contribution strong and the presentation clear.

### Weaknesses
**Literature understanding and originality** (multiple reviewers, especially R3 and R4):
- **Prior art overlap** (R3): The work by Mutti et al. (2021) already presents the algorithmic ideas linking Hazan and Liu & Abbeel, including nearly the same algorithm (MEPOL) as MaxEnt-V (choose high δ to freeze policy updates while using Liu & Abbeel’s sampling). The authors misinterpret Mutti et al. as optimizing trajectory-wise state entropy, but MEPOL does **not** use a trajectory-wise objective. This significantly limits the claimed novelty (R3). The claim that Liu & Abbeel introduced kNN estimators is inaccurate; Mutti et al. (2021) introduced that technique (R4).
- **Misunderstanding of Hazan and Liu & Abbeel goals** (R4): Hazan et al. provided a provably efficient algorithm as a theoretical tool, not a practical competitor. Liu & Abbeel is a practical method; claiming MaxEnt-LA outputs a uniform mixture is incorrect—it samples uniformly from a replay buffer but outputs the **last** policy (R4).
- **Trajectory-wise vs. state-wise distinction** (R4): The distinction between trajectory-wise vs. state-wise entropy is blurred. Mutti et al. (2021) uses the same objective (Eq. 2) but computes kNN within trajectories, while Liu & Abbeel uses buffer-wide kNN. The paper’s claim that “other works optimize trajectory-wise entropy” is a misinterpretation of Mutti et al. (R4).
- **Missing references** (R2, R4): The related work omits important state-of-the-art references, e.g., Lee et al. (2019), Mutti & Restelli (2020), Guo et al. (2021), Liu & Abbeel (2021a, APS), Mutti et al. (2022/2023), Yang & Spaan (2023), Zisselman et al. (2023), and Zamboni et al. (2024). In particular, methods that explicitly maximize entropy via neural density estimators (not just uncertainty) should be discussed and compared (R2).

**Theoretical weaknesses**:
- **Assumptions of Theorem 1** (R4): The kNN estimator (Eq. 6) is assumed smooth and bounded, but can become unbounded when the kNN distance is zero (e.g., duplicate states). The paper does not justify when these conditions hold. Additionally, the “estimation oracle” is ambiguous: does ε₀ refer to error on biased kNN estimate or true entropy? (R4)
- **Insufficient support for key claims** (R4):
  - *“Tuning η is unnecessary”* lacks theoretical justification. Hazan’s analysis gives η a specific convergence role; showing small η in practice does not prove its removal is theoretically sound (R4).
  - *“Freezing reward is better”* is supported only by an upper bound. The bound may not be tight; the paper does not demonstrate it correlates with actual performance or that frozen rewards empirically provide a benefit (R4). A comparative convergence analysis with Hazan’s bound (which appears exponentially decreasing in T) is needed (R2). The paragraph (lines 313–317) about the order of ε when η→0 is unclear and requires explicit derivation and comparison (R2).
- **Trade-offs not discussed** (R2): When might the fixed replay buffer of MaxEnt-V hinder performance, and when might frequent reward updates (MaxEnt-LA) be beneficial despite instability? A more detailed discussion of these trade-offs is needed (R2). The action vs. state entropy distinction at line 486 is oversimplified: both exploration objectives have different merits depending on the problem; neither is universally superior (R2).

**Empirical evaluation weaknesses** (all reviewers, especially R1, R4):
- **Single seed runs**: Plots appear to show results from a single seed; this is unacceptable for statistical significance (R1). The paper should follow community standards (e.g., multiple seeds, confidence intervals) (R4). Curves should be run with multiple seeds and reported with error bars (R1).
- **Missing experimental details** (R1):
  - Amount of training data gathered per epoch, number of training steps per epoch.
  - How “train to convergence” (ε-accurate stopping) is implemented in practice for high-dimensional environments.
  - What an “epoch” means for MaxEnt-LA (since it updates reward every step vs. at epoch boundaries).
  - Scale of the NGU bonus and whether projection functions (random projections) are consistent across methods for fair comparison.
  - Clarify: projecting states to 7D (as in appendix) is only for entropy calculation, not for control? (R1)
- **Downward tick in Ant plot**: The blue line for “total unique states visited during training” shows a slight downward tick, which seems impossible given the metric definition (R1).
- **Wall-time comparison missing** (R2): Results are shown in epochs, but an epoch in MaxEnt-H/V is much longer in wall-time (full policy optimization). A wall-time comparison is crucial to understand computational cost.
- **Comparison with official implementations** (R4): The paper does not compare with the official codebases of Hazan et al. (2019) and Liu & Abbeel (2021), which are publicly available. The experiments should use those implementations to ensure fair comparison.
- **NGU bonus with non-recurrent policy** (R1): The NGU bonus is history-dependent and designed for memory-based policies (e.g., LSTM). Using it with a feedforward policy seems conceptually incorrect. The authors should explain and comment on the non-stationarity introduced by NGU in relation to their criticism of MaxEnt-LA (R1).

**Presentation and clarity issues**:
- The first five pages are loose and hard to follow; presentation could be sharpened (R3). Propositions 2 and 3 are simple calculations that could be integrated into the text (R3).
- The name “MaxEnt-Veritas” implies the method is the “one true MaxEnt” and unfairly denigrates prior work; a more neutral name is recommended (R1). The title is not informative about the methodology (R1).
- Minor errors: Eq. 2 says “min” instead of “max” (R4); line 48 “While these importance sampling-based methods” is unclear (R4); line 488 “entropy function is convex” should be “concave” (R2). Lines 195–199 may confuse MaxEnt-V and MaxEnt-H (R2). Lines 183 and 508 need clarification on stationary/non-stationary rewards (R2, R4). Algorithm 2, line 2: how many states are sampled? (R4)
