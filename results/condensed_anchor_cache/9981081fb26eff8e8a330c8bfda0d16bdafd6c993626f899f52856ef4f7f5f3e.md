## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DiffAD, a method for graph anomaly detection using forward diffusion dynamics (adding noise to node attributes) and a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) for graph generation. It introduces two recipes: (1) capturing the faster drop in egonet dissimilarity (LX) of anomalous nodes during forward diffusion via non-GNN sequence models (LSTM/Transformer/MLP), and (2) generating synthetic graphs with a denoising network that recovers low-frequency energy, used as data augmentation to boost other detectors. Evaluated on six datasets (Cora, ACM, etc.) against GNN baselines, DiffAD achieves SOTA results. Code is provided.

### Strengths

- Interesting analysis of forward diffusion dynamics on graphs, with two well-illustrated propositions and theoretical principles supporting the design of both approaches. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 6)
- The approach avoids over-smoothing limitations of GNNs by using non-GNN methods (LSTM/Transformer/MLP) to capture egonet dissimilarity trajectories. (Reviewers 2, 6)
- The graph generation technique can enhance many GNN models (e.g., GraphSAGE) with a slight modification, leading to substantial performance improvements. (Reviewers 1, 6)
- DiffAD outperforms recent baselines by a large margin on six datasets, and the loss function addresses class imbalance. (Reviewers 4, 6)
- Overall writing is clear and organized; exploration experiments are detailed; appendices are used thoughtfully; code and data are provided for reproducibility. (Reviewers 2, 3, 5, 6)

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity of presentation:** The paper does not clearly explain how the generic diffusion process (Section 3.2) applies to graphs with structure (A) and attributes (X), e.g., how A contributes to X_{t+1} and the effect of neighbors. The jump to observations lacks this foundation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited and inadequate baselines:** Comparisons omit recent graph anomaly detection methods such as CoLA, CONAD, SL-GAD, GHRN, GDN, H2-FDetector, and GAGA. The justification that methods assigning only anomaly scores are not classifiers is weak—a simple threshold could be applied. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5)
- **Questionable necessity of Approach I:** The first principle (denoising network must capture local egonet information) is not novel and is the bedrock of nearly all GAD models. The direct difference in initial egonet dissimilarity (Fig. 2) might be exploited more simply. Table 1 shows that proposed FSC and TRC often fail to outperform baselines. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Focus only on contextual/attribute anomalies:** The method primarily targets attribute anomalies with high variance; it is unclear how it handles structural anomalies (e.g., unusual connections, bridging communities, unique subgraphs). The diffusion process may not capture topological deviations. (Reviewers 5, 6)
- **Preliminary study setup overestimates effectiveness:** The synthetic anomaly injection (Liu et al., 2022) uses attribute distributions substantially different from normal, making the faster egonet dissimilarity drop for anomalies unsurprising. Real-world distinctions may be more subtle. (Reviewer 6)
- **Training process is unclear:** How the transformer, diffusion model, and anomaly detector are trained together (step-by-step interaction and dependencies) is not explained. The forward process uses a separate loss function with label information—why is this needed? (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **High computational complexity:** No discussion of training/inference time or complexity. Transformer self-attention is quadratic in nodes, and DDPM iterative denoising adds overhead. Comparisons in complexity and efficiency are missing. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Auxiliary graph generation motivation is weak:** The link between generating training samples and the core idea of learning diffusion trajectories is not clearly established. It is also unclear how to ensure that generated nodes (e.g., an anomaly node in graph G) remain anomalies in the generated graph G_a (loss function Eq. 22). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Lack of ablation and hyperparameter tuning:** No ablation studies or discussion of hyperparameter sensitivity. (Reviewer 2)
- **No discussion of inference for the DDPM.** (Reviewer 2)
- **Baseline results appear lower than reported in their papers** (e.g., GCN/GAT/GraphSage on Cora/ACM). This discrepancy needs explanation. (Reviewer 5)
- **Minor issues:** Some typos and word choices (e.g., "Eventual" should be "Eventually" p.5). The definition of egonet dissimilarity (LX) is imprecise—LX is essentially a graph convolution operation; a breakdown of how the Laplacian captures node-neighbor differences would help. (Reviewers 4, 5, 6)
- **Data scarcity:** The method still needs sufficient labeled data; generating auxiliary data may be ineffective when anomalous nodes are extremely scarce. (Reviewer 4)
- **Question about structural anomaly detection performance** on nodes with low initial egonet dissimilarity or where anomalies keep local density unchanged. (Reviewer 6)
