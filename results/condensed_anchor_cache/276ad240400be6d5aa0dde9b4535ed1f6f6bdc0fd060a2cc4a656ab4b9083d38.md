## Merged Review

### Summary
All reviewers investigate “Editing Overfit” in parameter-modifying knowledge editing—where edited LLMs assign disproportionately high probabilities to the edit target, hindering generalization (especially in multi-hop reasoning). The paper introduces the EVOKE benchmark with fine‑grained metrics to measure overfitting, evaluates common mitigation strategies (finding most insufficient except Specificity Augmentation), and proposes the plug‑and‑play Learn to Inference (LTI) strategy that uses a multi‑stage inference constraint to guide models to recall new knowledge similarly to how unedited LLMs use in‑context learning. Two reviewers (R1, R2) rate the paper highly (8/8, confidence 4); one reviewer (R3) is moderately positive (6/6, confidence 3) but still finds contribution high (4/4, vs. 3/3 from the others). All agree the overfitting problem is important and the benchmark is valuable.

### Strengths
- **Identification of a new, pervasive problem:** The overfitting phenomenon in parameter‑modifying knowledge editing is clearly identified and supported by extensive experiments (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Dedicated benchmark and metrics:** EVOKE enables systematic and comprehensive evaluation of editing overfit, including in multi‑hop reasoning scenarios (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Comprehensive evaluation of existing mitigation strategies:** The paper tests various previously suggested techniques and finds only Specificity Augmentation partly effective (R1, R2).  
- **LTI is a plug‑and‑play, highly adaptable module:** It integrates easily with existing editing methods and effectively reduces overfit without sacrificing editing success (R1, R2).  
- **Thorough experimental analysis:** Results span multiple LLMs (GPT‑J, GPT‑2) and editing methods, with detailed analysis (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Clear writing and well‑aligned experimental design** (R3).  
- **Contrast in perceived contribution:** One reviewer (R3) rates contribution 4 out of 4, the highest possible, indicating a very strong perceived contribution beyond the other two reviewers’ 3 out of 3 (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **LTI limited to knowledge‑triple format:** The method is only applicable when the edited knowledge can be explicitly represented as (s, r, o, o*), which may not cover more complex or nuanced real‑world edits (R2).  
- **LTI relies on the unedited model’s in‑context learning ability:** It requires the unedited model to generate correct answers from context alone, providing constraints. For smaller models less proficient in ICL or for very rigid facts, this may harm editing performance (R1).  
- **Experimental scale limited to small LLMs:** The analysis is only on GPT‑J and GPT‑2; it is unknown whether larger models (e.g., LLaMA‑2/3, Mistral, LLaMA‑1) still suffer from Editing Overfit and how LTI would perform at scale (R2, R3).  
- **Missing comparison with in‑context editing approaches:** The paper does not include or discuss in‑context editing methods (e.g., [1][2][3]) in Related Work or Baselines, even though such methods may be better suited for multi‑hop editing tasks and could serve as a natural alternative (R3).  
- **Model editing’s known poor performance on multi‑hops is not explicitly linked to overfitting:** The paper could strengthen its motivation by directly arguing that much of the failure in multi‑hop editing is due to overfitting, as the authors suggest (R3).  
- **Section 5 (basic mitigation techniques) may be extraneous:** This section does not directly align with the paper’s main contributions and could be condensed or replaced with more details on LTI (R3).  
- **Lack of analysis on fact type / domain variance:** It is not explored whether Editing Overfit severity differs across factual vs. commonsense knowledge, or whether LTI is less effective on certain knowledge types (R2).  
- **Uncertainty about Paraphrase Augmentation’s role in constraint quality:** The paper notes Paraphrase Augmentation can exacerbate overfitting, but it remains unclear whether applying it to the new knowledge (e.g., to compute an average KL divergence over paraphrases) would improve the constraint or reduce editing effectiveness (R1).
