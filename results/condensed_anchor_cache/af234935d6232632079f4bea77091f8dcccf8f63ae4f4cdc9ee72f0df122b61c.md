## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a training-free method for generating images at resolutions much higher than the training image size (e.g., 4096×4096) from pre-trained diffusion models, while also supporting arbitrary aspect ratios. The authors identify the limited perception field of convolutional kernels as the cause of object repetition and implausible structures at high resolutions, and introduce re-dilation to dynamically adjust the convolutional perception field during inference. They further propose dispersed convolution and noise-damped classifier-free guidance to enable ultra-high-resolution generation. The method is evaluated qualitatively and quantitatively, with results claimed to be state-of-the-art. The paper is well-organized and the samples look plausible. However, several reviewers noted concerns about experimental clarity, metric suitability, and the significance of the results compared to baselines.

### Strengths
- The problem of generating high-resolution images with arbitrary aspect ratios from pre-trained diffusion models is novel and important (R2, R4).
- Key insight: identifying the limited perception field of convolutional kernels as the cause of object repetition, and proposing re-dilation to dynamically adjust the convolutional perception field (R2, R3).
- The method is simple, efficient, and requires no training or optimization (R1).
- Capable of ultra-high-resolution generation (e.g., 4096×4096) and also extends to video (R1).
- Extensive experiments are presented, claiming state-of-the-art performance, particularly in texture details and addressing the repetition issue (R2).
- The paper is clearly written, with well-explained motivation, concise method description, and plausible generated samples; implementation details in the appendix are appreciated (R4).
- The observation and explanation for the object repetition problem are sound, and the method is evaluated on multiple models (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Table 1 does not clearly indicate whether the reported metrics reflect differences among various Stable Diffusion (SD) solutions or the impact of the proposed “Method” column. It is unclear how results vary with changes in resolution and whether the metrics remain acceptable at higher resolutions. The practical interpretation of FID/KID scores for real-world image quality is not defined (R1).
- In Table 2, the proposed method is not superior to SD+SR; the justification for using the proposed method is weak, and the paper does not adequately address the trade-offs between efficiency and quality. No clear use case is given where the proposed method demonstrably outperforms existing techniques (R1).
- The significance of the work is unclear: results are similar or only slightly better than baselines, which is insufficient to justify the contribution (R1). (Note: Reviewer 2 strongly disagrees, calling the contribution valuable and the results state-of-the-art.)
- FID and KID both downsample images to 229×229 for evaluation, making them unsuitable for assessing high-resolution details and textures. The reviewers recommend reporting additional metrics such as patch-FID (to evaluate high-resolution textures) or sFID (to better assess spatial variability) (R3, R4).
- The paper lacks discussion of using the method to magnify outputs from the original model at standard resolution; adding this to Eq. 5 could make the comparison with SD+SR more conclusive (R4).
- The re-dilation schedule function D(t,l) is not fully defined; no experiments are provided to validate or optimize its selection (R3).
- The convolution dispersion step uses a linear transformation R and structural-level calibration to enlarge the convolution kernel; it is unclear whether this increases computational complexity, and if so, by how much (R3, R4).
- The guidance scale w in noise-damped classifier-free guidance: how is it chosen, and does it require adjustment for different tasks or datasets? (R3)
- Comparisons with other recent high-resolution generation methods are missing; such comparisons would further validate effectiveness (R3).
- Minor issues: Eq. 1 has a typo (variable o appears on LHR but not RHS); Eq. 4 should use argmin instead of min; the best metrics in tables should be highlighted consistently (e.g., Tab. 2); the caption of Fig. 1 in Appendix A2 mentions a block MB3 that does not exist (R4). Also, Fig. 5–7 and Table 2–4 could be presented in a better format (R2).
- More ablation studies in the main text and a discussion of limitations are suggested (R2).
- The claim that the method is completely training-free may be inaccurate if the optimization problem in Eq. 4 requires an iterative solver (no closed-form solution is provided). If a closed-form solution exists, it should be included; otherwise, the claims should be adjusted (R4).
- The description of re-dilation in Sec. 3.2 may be misleading: in practice, only dispersed convolutions are applied in the modified UNet, suggesting the description needs polishing (R4).
