## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies an out-of-distribution (OOD) issue in SDS-based text-to-3D generation: rendered images from an initial low-quality 3D model fall outside the distribution of the pretrained score network, causing instability. To address this, the authors propose Path-Tracing Distillation (PTD), a two-stage method. First, a feed-forward 3D generator (SF3D) synthesizes an initial 3D model whose rendered views are used to fine-tune the pretrained diffusion model, saving checkpoints during this fine-tuning. Second, the saved checkpoints are interpolated (weights averaged) and used in reverse order as distillation targets to optimize the 3D representation (e.g., 3DGS or NeRF). The method aims to provide intermediate score networks that better align with the distribution of partially complete rendered images, accelerating early convergence and improving stability.

### Strengths
- **Motivation**: The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates the OOD problem as a source of instability in text-to-3D generation, which is well-motivated and clearly explained (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5).
- **Presentation**: The paper is well-written with strong motivating examples; the methodology is presented in a clear, easy-to-follow manner, and the appendix provides comprehensive background and implementation details (Reviewer 1,3,4,5).
- **Simplicity and novelty**: The proposed PTD technique is simple and straightforward, consisting of two standard stages (fine-tuning a diffusion model with checkpointing, then interval score distillation using interpolated checkpoints). The idea of using a series of intermediate score networks instead of a single model, with negligible additional cost, is novel and interesting (Reviewer 1,2).
- **Generality**: PTD shows applicability across different 3D representations (3DGS and NeRF), indicating broader potential (Reviewer 4).
- **Code availability**: Code is provided to aid reproducibility and community building (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparisons due to initialization**: The proposed method initializes the 3D representation using SF3D (a feed-forward 3D generator), while baseline methods (e.g., LucidDreamer, ProlificDreamer) are either randomly initialized or use weaker initialization (Point-E). This makes it unclear whether the performance gain comes from PTD or the stronger initialization. LucidDreamer should be evaluated with the same SF3D initialization to isolate PTD’s effect (Reviewer 1,2,3,4,5). A reviewer specifically notes that SF3D is a DMTet model and extracting a point cloud for 3DGS initialization seems indirect; alternative feed-forward Gaussian methods could be explored (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 5 additionally argues that relying on SF3D breaks the core narrative of SDS: geometry is essentially determined by SF3D (trained on synthetic data with limited prompt alignment), reducing the advantage of 2D-prior-based text-to-3D generation.
- **Insufficient evaluation**: 
    - Only CLIP scores are reported (mainly text alignment from single rendered views); no 3D metrics (e.g., geometry quality, multi-view consistency) are provided (Reviewer 5).
    - No user study is conducted despite an ethics statement mentioning one; such a study is considered the bare minimum to judge visual quality (Reviewer 4,5).
    - Video renderings are absent, which are essential for evaluating 3D results (Reviewer 5).
    - Qualitative comparisons are sparse; more examples are needed to visually demonstrate PTD’s effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
    - The results appear oversaturated and cartoonish compared to ProlificDreamer (e.g., Fig. 1: “mouse playing a touba,” “duck swimming”), exhibiting less appearance realism and looking similar to LucidDreamer, which is known to be lower quality than ProlificDreamer (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited ablation studies and missing justifications**:
    - The choice of interval score distillation is not justified against other possible distillation methods (SDS, VSD, and related works [Katzir et al., SteinDreamer, Taming Mode Collapse]) (Reviewer 1).
    - The paper claims faster convergence but provides no empirical evidence (e.g., learning curves) (Reviewer 1).
    - The final method in Section 4.2 only saves the last checkpoint and interpolates parameters, contradicting the path-checkpointing scheme described in Section 4.1 and Algorithm 1 (which saves checkpoints at regular intervals). An experiment comparing interpolated LoRA with fixed-interval path checkpointing is needed (Reviewer 1).
    - Ablation studies appear to cherry-pick the best combination and do not demonstrate generality across different cases (Reviewer 3).
- **Oversimplified literature review**: The paper misses several recent works that address distribution-related or initialization-related issues in SDS, such as DreamMapping, “Rethinking Score Distillation as a Bridge Between Image Distributions,” and “Connecting Consistency Distillation to Score Distillation for Text-to-3D Generation.” A more thorough discussion and clearer differentiation from existing approaches would strengthen the contribution (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical and empirical support for OOD motivation is weak**:
    - No theoretical analysis is provided for the claimed OOD issue (Reviewer 3).
    - The motivation may contradict prior studies (Score Jacobian Chaining, Noise-Free Score Distillation) that argue the pretrained score function naturally brings OOD samples from 3D representations toward the in-domain distribution. If the diffusion model is sufficiently tuned on a well-initialized 3D structure, PTD may have little added effect (Reviewer 1).
    - It is unclear whether the OOD issue during early training affects final results, because at late stages the 3D model renders higher-quality images that align with the diffusion model’s distribution (Reviewer 3,5).
- **Missing integration with baselines**: The authors claim PTD can be integrated with most existing methods, but Table 1 only compares stand-alone PTD to original baselines. A stronger comparison would show results for each baseline with and without PTD integration (Reviewer 3). Table 1 shows ProlificDreamer outperforming PTD in CLIP score, although PTD is 5× faster (Reviewer 3).
- **Implementation overhead**: The first stage requires saving many model checkpoints, causing large disk overhead and introducing discretization artifacts via the hyperparameter of how many checkpoints to store. It is not explained why this cannot be done on the fly (Reviewer 5).
- **Minor issues**: Figure 5 appears blurred (Reviewer 3). There are typos, e.g., “the the” (Reviewer 3). Terms “CLIP similarity” and “CLIP score” appear abruptly in the abstract (Reviewer 3). The ethics statement mentions a human user study but provides no results (Reviewer 4).
- **Contrast among reviewers**: One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, praising the simple method and strong motivation, while the other four reviewers (score 3) raised significant concerns about unfair comparisons, insufficient evaluation, limited ablations, and lack of evidence supporting faster convergence and OOD relevance. The higher-scoring reviewer also flagged inconsistency between method sections and asked for comparisons with interpolation as a low-cost alternative.
