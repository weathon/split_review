## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a unified approach for encoding multi-modal, interleaved documents (text, images, tables) for retrieval. Documents are split into sections, each encoded via a vision-language model (VLM), then section embeddings are averaged to create a document representation. A reranker refines section-level retrieval. Reviewers split: two (ratings 5) find the direction important and the method straightforward, while two (ratings 3) question novelty and evaluation rigor. All agree the motivation is clear, but concerns about baselines, ablations, and practical limitations are widespread.

### Strengths

- Clear motivation and problem framing: documents contain multiple modalities (text, images, tables) and segmentation into passages loses global context; addressing this is worthwhile.
- Technically sound and straightforward: leveraging pre-trained VLMs for interleaved document retrieval is a timely and important direction.
- Effectively integrates multi-modal information and global document context via section averaging, while maintaining efficient training and inference (R3).
- Ablation studies on the reranker are comprehensive and clearly illustrate how to train a multi-modal reranker (R2).
- The paper is well-structured and accessible (R4).
- Originality in identifying and addressing the multimodal segmentation gap (R4).
- Potential significance for real-world applications needing sophisticated document processing (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: applying VLMs to IR is not new (e.g., CLIP, BLIP); the paper lacks substantial methodological innovation beyond this application (R1, R4). The section-splitting approach mirrors existing practices without clear justification of its necessity (R4).
- **Insufficient baselines and evaluation rigor**:
  - No strong text-only retriever baseline (e.g., E5, DRAGON, MistralE5) fine-tuned or zero-shot is provided. This makes it impossible to isolate whether gains come from the multi-modal encoding or simply from using a more powerful VLM that may overfit on small training data (R2, R1, R4).
  - No non-VLM-based evaluation to establish improvement over traditional methods (R4).
  - Missing ablation: how much benefit is contributed by each modality (text, images, tables), and the additional latency cost vs. gain (R1).
  - No comparison of contrastive loss vs. cross-entropy loss for training (R1).
  - No evaluation of generalization across datasets (e.g., training on all datasets jointly) (R2).
  - No analysis of performance with other VLMs (e.g., beyond LLaVA-NeXT) (R4).
- **Unclear experimental settings and results**:
  - The difference in numbers between the last row of Table 1 and Table 2 is unexplained (R2).
  - For Table 2, it is unclear how many documents are passed to the reranker when the retrieved unit is a section (e.g., if top-K sections are retrieved, how are distinct documents identified and ranked?) – this is needed to fairly compare section retriever vs. document retriever (R2).
  - The reranking pipeline for document retrieval is not clearly described: are top-25 documents reranked by scoring all their sections and taking the maximum? (R2).
  - The presentation of results in Section 4.3 lacks a clear narrative or overarching research questions; it reads as a series of disconnected experiments (R3).
  - The paper uses “section” and “passage” interchangeably without definition, causing confusion (R3).
  - Equation on line 240 contains a missing `exp` in the loss calculation (R3).
- **Methodological concerns**:
  - The rationale for dividing documents into sections is not convincingly justified; it may compromise document representation integrity compared to holistic encoding (R4).
  - No empirical or theoretical justification that special tokens like `<EOS>` and `<EOS>` are superior to other representation methods (R4).
  - The modality gap (how text, images, tables are fused) is not adequately addressed; no mechanism or metric is provided to assess modality alignment (R4).
  - Feature fusion is done simply by concatenating visual, text, and table tokens; no comparison with other fusion methods (inner product, addition, subtraction, etc.) (R1). Similarly, query and section are concatenated; no ablation on position or other fusion methods (R1).
  - No experiment with a conventional dual encoder where features are extracted from LLaVA (i.e., using LLaVA embeddings with a standard dual-encoder loss) (R1).
- **Real-world applicability limited**: experiments use clean, source-available corpora where documents are easily segmented by subtitles and text/image/table extraction is trivial. Real-world PDFs require OCR and layout analysis, which this work does not address (R3).
- **Surprising patterns unexplained**: the passage retriever performs very poorly (3.9% R@1) compared to the document retriever (20.5%), yet the reranker (which does not use global information) dramatically improves to 28.6%. This casts doubt on the necessity of global document encoding vs. simple passage-level scoring (R3).
- **Missing details**: How are texts, images, and tables organized into the section encoder input? Is the order fixed? (R3). What does “combine four images into one” mean? (R3). Are “four sections per document” the first four? (R3). How are queries with only text targets better retrieved with multi-modal encoding? (R3).
