## Merged Review

### Summary
Six reviewers gave scores of 5, 6, 6, 3, 8, and 6, reflecting a split between moderate support and strong concerns. Most reviewers acknowledge the novelty of fusing numerical integrators (RK4, learnable finite-difference filters) with neural networks for flow simulation, and note that reported results (high correlation times, Table 2 improvements, ablation study) are competitive. However, serious weaknesses are flagged: unclear writing and missing architecture details, insufficient controls and ablations, unsupported performance claims, limited scope (1D/2D regular grids, small benchmarks, small training data), and lack of computational cost analysis or open-source code. One reviewer (score 8) is strongly positive and recommends accept; another (score 3) finds the entire presentation unclear and unconvincing.

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of numerical methods and learning**: The use of a learnable convolutional filter with symmetry constraints (inspired by finite-difference stencils) and a multi-scale time-stepping approach (fine-scale RK4 + coarse-scale NN correction) embeds the PDE structure directly into the network rather than only using the residual as a loss. Multiple reviewers highlight this as a creative and interpretable design.
- **Strong experimental results**: MultiPDENet shows large performance gains over standard baselines (e.g., Table 2) and maintains high correlation over long rollouts (Figure 3b/e/h). The ablation study (Table 3) convincingly demonstrates the contribution of each component. Results also show robustness to distribution shift (Section 4.2). Reviewer 6 calls the results “really good” and a highlight of the paper; Reviewer 5 praises the thoroughness and rigorous comparisons.
- **Potential significance for ML4PDEs**: The work pushes the envelope of performance for neural PDE surrogates, and the sensible inductive biases are promising for the field. Reviewer 5 also notes potential impact on computational fluid dynamics.

*Minority viewpoint: Reviewer 4 (score 3) states they cannot offer a view of strengths because the presentation is too unclear.*

### Weaknesses
- **Poor clarity and missing exposition**: Multiple reviewers report serious difficulty understanding the method.
  - Writing contains typos (e.g., “spatialemporal”, “e divide”, line 233) and awkward phrasing (R2, R6). The term “PDE-constrained” is criticized as inaccurate because the constraints are not enforced exactly up to machine epsilon (R2).
  - The micro and macro blocks (MiNN, MaNN) and the operator $\mathcal{B}$ are not properly defined in the main text; their architecture and hyperparameters are unclear (R2, R4). Essential components like the Poisson block and correction block are relegated to the appendix, forcing readers to constantly cross-reference (R3).
  - Mathematical descriptions (Eq. 1, Eq. 3) lack full explanation of terms ($\tilde{\textbf{x}}$, $\tau$, $\lambda$, $m$, $\Delta$, indices, $\delta$, square brackets) (R6). The meaning of “sparse grids” vs. “coarse grids” is confusing (R2, R4).
- **Insufficient controls and ablations**:
  - Multi-step prediction makes the network deeper; baselines are not compared with a similar number of effective layers (R1). It is unclear whether gains come from the RK4 scheme or simply from increased depth (R1). The authors should compare MultiPDENet with an architecture having the same number of blocks but without RK4 integration (R1). Also compare RK4 with a lower-order time-stepping scheme (R2).
  - The claim that learnable filters “enable accurate estimation of spatial derivatives on coarse grids” appears many times without theoretical or empirical validation (R3). Do the learned parameters actually replicate finite-difference operators with high-order truncation error? (R2).
  - No data ablation is provided despite the very small training data (KdV 3000, Burgers 700, Gray-Scott 540, Navier-Stokes 24000 snapshots); inductive bias may explain large gains in this low-data regime (R6). Additional robustness tests with noisy or incomplete data are needed (R5).
  - Missing comparisons on computational/memory cost (time, memory, parameter count) during training and testing (R3). Speedup claims (“53×” in abstract) are not backed by details of measurement, resolution, device, or accuracy checks (R4). See also nature article reference (R4).
- **Scope and generalizability limitations**:
  - The method is restricted to regular grids due to convolutional operations (R5), and only 1D/2D problems are tested; 3D extension is non-trivial (R5). Benchmarks are relatively small; testing on larger flow benchmarks like PDEArena would strengthen evaluation (R1).
  - The framework is said to be architecture-agnostic but is only demonstrated with one backbone; experiments with FNO, UNet, Transformers (including recent operator‐learning transformers) are needed (R3).
  - Training data uses uniform timesteps; performance on non-uniform timesteps is unexplored (R5). High Re capability claim (Re 4000) is questionable for turbulence; at least 3D with Re ≥ 10⁴ would be needed (R5, R6).
- **Complexity and reproducibility**: The multi-component architecture (learnable filters, RK4, correction block, Poisson block) adds complexity that may hinder adoption (R5). No open-source code is provided (R5).
- **Missing references**: Prior work on learnable FD stencils as convolutions (e.g., Kossaczká–Ehrhardt–Günther, arXiv:2201.01854) and using NN as correction to time-stepping (arXiv:2003.09573) are not cited (R2).
