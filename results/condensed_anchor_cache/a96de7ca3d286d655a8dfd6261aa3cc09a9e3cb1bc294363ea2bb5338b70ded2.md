## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses outlier sampling for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection under the outlier exposure setting. The authors argue that previous sampling methods based solely on predictive uncertainty fail to capture the full distribution of auxiliary OOD data, leading to biased sampling. They propose Diverse Outlier Sampling (DOS): at each training iteration, normalized features are clustered via k-means, and from each cluster the most informative (high-uncertainty) outlier is selected. This yields a small, diverse set of informative outliers that shape a compact decision boundary. Experiments on CIFAR-100 with TI-300K show strong gains (e.g., FPR95 reduction up to 25.79%). All three reviewers find the problem well-motivated, the method simple and elegant, and the empirical results compelling, though they raise concerns about definitional rigor, limited experimental scope, and novelty.

### Strengths
- **Practical importance & good problem motivation.** The paper tackles a key question in outlier exposure: how to select high-quality auxiliary samples that capture the full outlier distribution (R1, R2, R3).
- **Simple, elegant, and effective method.** DOS clusters normalized features and picks informative outliers per cluster; the design is straightforward and achieves strong empirical performance (R1, R2, R3).
- **Novel technical design.** The use of normalized-feature clustering specifically for achieving diversity in outlier sampling is considered novel (R2).
- **Compelling experimental results.** DOS significantly reduces FPR95 (e.g., 25.79% on CIFAR-100) and outperforms both post-hoc scores and representative sampling baselines (R1, R2, R3).
- **Extensive ablation studies.** The paper provides thorough ablation experiments demonstrating robustness (R3, also noted by R1 and R2).
- **Overall clear presentation.** The method is easy to understand and follow (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous definition of “diversity” and insufficient motivation.**  
  - The notion of diversity is not formally defined; the claim that uncertainty-based sampling leads to “imbalanced performance” is vague and lacks supporting empirical evidence beyond a simple toy example (R2).  
  - The paper should provide concrete evidence (e.g., quantitative results) showing the limitation of uncertainty-only sampling (R2).  
  - The connection between clustering normalized features and achieving the desired diversity in outlier sampling is not explicitly established (R2).  

- **Limited experimental scope and missing large-scale validation.**  
  - Experiments are conducted only on CIFAR-100; results on other ID datasets and large-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet as ID) are missing, which would test scalability and generalizability (R2).  
  - No experiments using large auxiliary datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21k) are reported (R2).  
  - Efficiency (sampling cost) is not evaluated, despite the method being described as “efficient” (R2).  
  - Error bars / variance estimates are not reported; a sensitivity analysis for different clustering algorithms (e.g., von Mises-Fisher k-means) would strengthen the work (R1).  

- **Potential trade-off between diversity and informativeness.**  
  - While the greedy strategy continuously samples outliers that are hard (close to ID), the proposed method also selects outliers already recognized as OOD. The paper does not adequately address whether this introduces less informative samples that hinder learning (R2).  

- **Limited novelty – diverse sampling is well-known in active learning.**  
  - Diverse sampling has been extensively studied in active learning; the paper does not provide deeper theoretical analysis (e.g., generalization bounds) that would elevate its contribution (R3; note: R3 is the most critical on this point, rating contribution 2, while R1 and R2 rate contribution 3).  

- **Technical and presentational issues.**  
  - Equation (2) is incorrectly written as entropy only; the cross-entropy formulation is missing the label term \(p(y)\) (R3).  
  - The paper claims that sampling from more clusters yields better OOD detection, but the explicit relationship between “number of clusters” and “diversity” is not clarified (R2).  
  - A comparison with K-means++ seeding–based diverse sampling (as in Li et al., 2023) would help isolate the importance of the clustering step (R3).
