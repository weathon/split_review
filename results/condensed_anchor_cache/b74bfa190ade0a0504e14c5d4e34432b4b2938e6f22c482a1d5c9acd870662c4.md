## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically studies one-layer linear self-attention transformers trained on synthetic noisy linear regression tasks. The authors prove that the global minimizer of the pre-training loss implements one step of gradient descent (GD) on the least-squares objective when covariates are isotropic Gaussian. For non-isotropic Gaussian covariates, the minimizer corresponds to one step of preconditioned GD. When the response comes from a nonlinear family satisfying rotational symmetry, the minimizer still implements one step of GD on a linear regression objective. The paper is well-organized and addresses a crucial topic in LLM theory, but opinions on significance vary: Reviewer 3 (score 8) finds the results innovative and inspiring, while Reviewers 2 and 4 (score 5) consider the contribution incremental given the one-layer setting and prior work; Reviewer 1 (score 6) sees promise but notes limitations.

### Strengths
- Clear writing and organization, with main analysis techniques highlighted and good summaries of concurrent work (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Addresses an important theoretical problem for in-context learning in transformers (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Provides a global optimal analysis: proof that the one-layer minimizer is exactly one step of GD on the least-squares problem (Theorem 1; Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Novel results on preconditioned GD under non-isotropic Gaussian covariates (Section 4; Reviewers 1,3).
- Extension to nonlinear target functions under rotational symmetry, showing the same one-step GD result (Section 5; Reviewers 1,3).
- Good comparison with existing and concurrent works (Reviewers 1,4).
- Opens natural directions for future theoretical study of transformers (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model complexity and assumptions**:
  - Only considers one layer with linear self-attention, already extensively studied; the contribution over prior work [von Oswald et al., 2022] is incremental (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewer 3 disagrees, finding the preconditioning and nonlinear extensions innovative.
  - Data is i.i.d. and Gaussian (or rotations thereof), relying on rotational invariance; does not address long-range dependencies or non-Gaussian/structured data (Reviewer 1).
  - For the nonlinear case (Section 5), the result is unsurprising given the one-layer linear model; no additional analysis for multi-layer or multi-head attention (Reviewers 2,1).
- **Lack of empirical validation**:
  - No experiments or simulations to verify theoretical claims (e.g., training dynamics with nonlinear target functions or multi-layer architectures; Reviewers 1,4). Reviewer 4 acknowledges rebuttal experiments exist but expected more.
  - No verification of the predicted learning rate η and effect of noise σ² (Reviewer 4, Q1).
- **Missing details and clarity gaps**:
  - Theorem 1 does not specify the constrained minimization space (e.g., what are the allowed values for (Wₖ, Wₕ, Wᵥ, h)?) and whether the minimizer is unique (Reviewers 1,3; Reviewer 1 Q3).
  - The derivations of constants in Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 may be zero in some cases—conditions not discussed (Reviewer 1 Q3).
  - The learning rate η in Theorem 1 is not compared to the Hessian’s largest eigenvalue (Reviewer 1 Q4).
  - In Section 4, the renormalization to w ∼ N(0, Σ^{−1}) is not justified; alternative covariances for w are not considered (Reviewer 1 Q5).
  - Theorem 3 uses an undefined distribution 𝓓 (Reviewer 1 Q6).
  - The proof of Lemma 1’s minimizer for g(u) with one data point is insufficiently explained (Reviewer 1 Q7).
  - No proof given that fully-connected neural networks satisfy Assumption 1(1) for the nonlinear case (Reviewer 4 Q2).
- **Practical insight unclear**: The theoretical results do not explain observed in-context learning phenomena or guide training; the gap to practice is large (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Incremental contribution of Section 5**: Without Section 5 the contribution is mainly incremental; more focus on nonlinear extensions would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 4 Q3; see also Reviewer 2).
- **Missing explicit comparison** with related works such as Bietti et al., Tarzanagh et al., and others (Reviewer 1 Q8).
- **Downstream phase not analyzed**: in-context learning’s critical downstream phase is not studied (Reviewer 3).
