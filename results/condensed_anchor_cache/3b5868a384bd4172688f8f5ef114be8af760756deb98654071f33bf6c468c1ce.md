## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces “uniform wrappers,” a framework to reduce online optimization with upper-quadratizable functions to standard online convex optimization (OCO). The authors then apply a variant of Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) from Saha & Tewari (2011) to obtain improved regret bounds for several function classes under zeroth-order and bandit feedback. The core idea is similar to the reduction in Pedramfar & Aggarwal (2024a). One reviewer (R1, score 5, confidence 3) sees a challenging problem and a general framework; two reviewers (R2 and R3, both score 3, confidence 3 and 4 respectively) find the technical novelty limited and the contribution marginal, with R2 stating the framework is “nearly identical” to Pedramfar & Aggarwal (2024a) and R3 describing the results as natural extensions.

### Strengths
- The framework provides improvements in state-of-the-art regret bounds for several settings (R1, R2, R3). Specific numerical gains: from \(\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})\) to \(\tilde{O}(T^{2/3})\) for three function classes under zeroth-order feedback, and from \(\tilde{O}(T^{4/5})\) to \(\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})\) under bandit feedback (R3). R1 notes that for weakly DR-submodular functions under zeroth-order feedback the framework yields “superior regret guarantees, surpassing prior results.”
- The idea of uniform wrappers is general and can be applied to a wide range of function classes, bridging convex optimization and quadratizable functions (R1).
- The presentation is “sufficiently clear” (R2), though R1 finds it dense.
- The paper considers a challenging and important problem: extending algorithms from convex to non-convex settings (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and differentiation from prior work:** Both R2 and R3 see the technical contribution as very limited. R2 states: “the framework appears to be nearly identical to that in Pedramfar & Aggarwal (2024a) … Nearly every technical result in the paper is due to Pedramfar & Aggarwal (2024a), e.g. Lemma 1, Lemma 2, Lemma 3 … I do not see any novelty in techniques or approach.” R3 adds that because upper-quadratizable functions satisfy a first-order variational inequality similar to convex functions (Lemmas 1–3), it is “quite natural” to use a variant of FTRL from Saha & Tewari (2011) to improve regret bounds.
- **Insufficient motivation and explanation of key concepts:** R1 is “confused about the necessity of the uniform wrappers” and finds the paper lacks a clear differentiation from prior work. R1 and R3 both ask for more context: R1 asks about the significance of upper‑quadratizable functions and their real‑world applications; R3 asks for examples where the target function is upper‑quadratizable but not a \(\gamma\)-weakly continuous DR‑submodular function, and why the paper does not focus on the latter instead.
- **Missing empirical evaluation and application examples:** R3 criticizes the lack of “in‑depth discussion of the applications” and “practical evaluation of the newly introduced algorithms,” making it seem like “merely exploring a new function without clarifying its relevance.” R1 also asks about conditions and limitations on the applicability of uniform wrappers.
- **Paper is dense and difficult to follow:** R1 notes the multiple layers of definitions (quadratizability, up‑super‑gradients, uniform wrappers) limit accessibility and suggests more intuition and examples.
- **Concerns about incremental improvement:** R1 describes the framework as “an incremental improvement rather than a groundbreaking advance.” R2 calls the improvements “marginal” and the generalization “does not introduce significant new technical challenges or insights.” R3 agrees the contribution is limited.
- **Specific unanswered questions that reflect criticisms:**  
  - R1 asks whether the regret results (line 203) can reduce to dynamic regret or adaptive regret, and how they compare in those settings.  
  - R2 asks: “What is the technical novelty with respect to Pedramfar & Aggarwal (2024a)?”  
  - R3 asks: “Could the authors provide a detailed personal perspective on how their technical contributions differ from those in references [1] and [2]?”  
  - R3 also asks for a detailed explanation of applications and an empirical evaluation of the proposed algorithms.
