## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Unknown Domain Inconsistency Minimization (UDIM), which extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) by adding data perturbation to the parameter perturbation. Unknown domains are simulated by perturbing source instances, and the loss landscape inconsistency between source and perturbed domains is minimized. The authors provide a theoretical upper bound for DG when merging SAM with UDIM and show empirical gains on benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10-C, PACS). Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the experiments supportive, but raise concerns about novelty (similarity to CrossGrad/domain augmentation), theoretical guarantees on the coverage of unknown domains, sensitivity to the choice of perturbation, insufficient baselines (only up to Fisher, 2022), limited experimental benchmarks (no DomainBed), inconsistencies in reported results, and lack of efficiency analysis.

### Strengths
- Extends SAM from parameter perturbation to data perturbation, aligning loss landscapes across source and unknown domains. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- Theoretical analysis establishes an upper bound for DG by merging SAM with UDIM. (Reviewer 1)
- The method can be combined with multiple SAM optimizers and shows further performance improvements. (Reviewer 1)
- Empirical results demonstrate effectiveness across multiple benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C, PACS). (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- The paper is well-structured and clearly written. (Reviewer 3)
- The approach is considered novel in some settings. (Reviewer 2)
- Addresses an important challenge in domain generalization. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and similarity to existing methods**: The data perturbation component closely resembles existing adversarial augmentation approaches, such as CrossGrad (Shankar et al., 2018), and can be seen as a combination of CrossGrad and SAM. The paper does not clearly differentiate UDIM from such techniques or discuss the advantage of the proposed objective over simply combining SAM with standard domain augmentation. [R1, R4]
- **Theoretical guarantees on covering unknown domains**: There is no guarantee that perturbing input within a ball of radius \(\rho\) covers the space of unseen domains. The perturbed data may be noisy or unrealistic, and the unknown distribution may lie outside the perturbed region. Analysis of the effect of \(\rho\) on generalization risk versus sampling efficiency is missing. [R1, R4]
- **Sensitivity to the choice of data perturbation**: The method’s sensitivity to different perturbation strategies (e.g., adversarial vs. random, geometric transformations vs. pixel-based perturbations) is not explored. The sampling strategy for selecting instances to perturb is not specified, nor is guidance provided on how to choose the perturbation type or magnitude. [R1, R2, R4]
- **Insufficient baselines and experiments**: The baselines are not up-to-date (the latest is Fisher, 2022). Newly introduced baselines underperform ERM on most benchmarks, raising concerns about their utility. The experiments do not use the standard DomainBed benchmark suite; they only include CIFAR-10-C and PACS, missing challenging datasets such as CMNIST and DomainNet. The decision to use only the leave-one-out setting is not justified. [R3, R4]
- **Inconsistency in reported results**: The reported PACS accuracy for Fisher (85.5) differs significantly from the original paper (81.3). The reason for this discrepancy is not explained. Additionally, Reviewer 4 notes that the PACS results are inconsistent with DomainBed results and should be double-checked. [R3, R4]
- **Empirical evidence for loss landscape alignment**: Figure 5 does not show a clear advantage of UDIM over SAGM and GAM in terms of loss landscape flatness; the bottom-right plot appears comparable. [R4]
- **Computational efficiency**: The time complexity of UDIM, even after applying BackPACK, is not analyzed. Efficiency concerns remain, especially for larger perturbation magnitudes. [R1, R4]
- **Optimization procedure ambiguity**: It is unclear whether optimization is performed only on the perturbed samples during training. Training on both source and perturbed samples simultaneously might lead to more stable convergence. [R1]
- **Lack of qualitative analysis**: There are no examples illustrating what the generated perturbed domains look like, making it hard to assess whether they are semantically meaningful. [R4]
- **Rationale for combining both perturbations**: The interaction between parameter and data perturbation and the precise mechanism for improved generalization over parameter perturbation alone (e.g., as in SAM or [1]) is not intuitively explained. [R2]
- **Connection to subgroup distribution shift and fairness**: A brief discussion on how UDIM might relate to fairness concerns (e.g., subgroup performance disparities, as in [2–3]) would be valuable, but is absent. [R2]
