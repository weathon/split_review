## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes GATE, a GAT extension that uses separate attention parameters for the target node and its neighbors to enable switching off task-irrelevant neighborhood aggregation. The authors claim this alleviates over-smoothing, allows deeper architectures to remain effective, and improves performance on heterophilic graphs. They provide theoretical analysis and experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and logically organized (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).
- The research problem is interesting and timely; the idea of switching off irrelevant neighbors in GAT is novel and well-motivated (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- The proposed GATE mechanism is simple yet effective, with sufficient theoretical guarantees (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Experiments are comprehensive (synthetic and real-world datasets) and theoretical analyses are solid, making the claimed performance gains convincing (Reviewer 1, 4).
- The work addresses an important component of GNNs: selective aggregation of relevant neighbors (Reviewer 3).
- Contributions are clearly stated and the flow of the paper aids readability (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Logical flaw in theoretical motivation**: The paper links “switching off neighborhood aggregation” to the condition αij/αii << 1 (relative attention to neighbors vs. self-loop). It is unclear why this ratio is used rather than αij << 1 (absolute attention). Separating attention parameters for self-loops and neighbors may affect the ratio but not the absolute values; the theoretical justification needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical analysis restricted to weight-sharing version**: The proofs only consider a weight-sharing variant of GATE. The gap between this simplified version and the actual implementation is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Baseline comparisons are limited**: Only GAT is used as a baseline. The paper should compare against other GNNs that employ separate aggregators for self and neighbor nodes (e.g., FAGCN, GraphSAGE, Residual Gated Graph ConvNets) to show whether the gains are specific to GAT limitations or reflect more general over-smoothing mitigation (Reviewer 3, 4).
- **Need for more real-world benchmark experiments**: Most experiments are on synthetic or small-scale heterophilic datasets that have known pitfalls (see Platonov et al., Lim et al.). Results on standard public splits (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed with 20 nodes/class; ogbn-arxiv, ogbn-products) are required to better assess general performance (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **When switching off a neighborhood, all nodes in that region (including potentially helpful ones) are shut down simultaneously** – the method may also remove useful information (Reviewer 1).
- **How deep should GATE be?** The paper shows GATE reduces performance degradation with depth, but does not clarify whether deeper GATE is always beneficial compared to a shallower version (Reviewer 1).
- **Reporting of experimental results**: Table 1 reports min loss and max accuracy; it is unclear why these extremes are chosen. Table 2 should report mean accuracy over multiple runs rather than the maximum at some epoch; single-run results offer limited information (Reviewer 4, 5).
- **Figures need improvement**: Figure legends are not clear (Reviewer 1). The 20%-90%ile lines in Figure 4 are difficult to distinguish, and the figure’s purpose is not well explained (Reviewer 4). Adding a pictorial illustration of the GATE mechanism would aid reader intuition (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing references**: Several closely related works are not cited or discussed, including Wang et al. (2019), Kim & Oh (2021), Fountoulakis et al. (2023), and Lee et al. (2023) (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation perspective is narrow**: The paper argues that a successful attention mechanism must block heterophilic neighbors, but other criteria (e.g., projecting same-class nodes to similar regions) are also valid; this narrow framing weakens the motivation (Reviewer 2).
- **Section 4 is hard to digest**: The paper would benefit from a quick summary at the end of Section 3 that highlights key insights from the theoretical Section 4, helping readers who may skip the detailed theory (Reviewer 2).
