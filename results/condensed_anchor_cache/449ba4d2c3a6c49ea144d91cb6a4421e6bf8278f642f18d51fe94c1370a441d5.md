## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ISARA, a self-alignment method for LLMs that uses retrieval-augmented in-context learning with a small seed set (<100 examples) to generate training data, then iteratively fine-tunes without human-crafted instructions or reward models. It is tested on safety (BeaverTails), truthfulness (TruthfulQA), and instruction-following (AlpacaEval). Two reviewers (scores 5) found the approach original, effective, and clearly presented, praising its reduction of human involvement and applicability to small models. Two reviewers (scores 3) criticized weak baselines, inappropriate dataset splits, lack of comparison to SOTA self-alignment methods, and insufficient experimental rigor.

### Strengths

- Works with very limited initial samples (<100), significantly reducing human labor compared to traditional alignment methods. (R1, R2)
- Effective with small models (as small as 350M parameters), making alignment accessible and cost-effective. (R1, R2)
- Uses retrieval-augmented in-context learning to generate high-quality samples without human guidance, achieving 6x+ data generation efficiency. (R1, R2)
- Implements iterative self-improvement mechanism that enhances alignment over multiple cycles. (R1, R2)
- Demonstrates effectiveness across three benchmarks (safety, truthfulness, instruction-following) and shows robust domain generalization capabilities. (R1, R2, R3)
- Applicable to various model sizes and domains without redesign or retraining reward models. (R1)
- Originality: novel combination of retrieval-augmented ICL with iterative fine-tuning for alignment with minimal human supervision; challenges the need for large models (65B+). (R2) [R3 disagrees on novelty – see Weaknesses]
- Quality: comprehensive empirical evaluations with ablation studies on model size, domain generalization, iterative vs. one-time training; strong experimental controls, clear baselines, filtering mechanisms. (R2)
- Clarity: well-structured, detailed algorithm descriptions, pseudocode, implementation notes, transparent experimental setup, effective figures and tables, qualitative examples. (R2)
- Significance: addresses major challenge in LLM development; automated scalable process valuable for practitioners. (R2)
- Highlights high computational costs of existing alignment methods and proposes retrieval-augmented strategy to reduce costs. (R4)
- Practical near-zero human supervision for real-world applications. (R3)

### Weaknesses

- **Inappropriate dataset splits**: BeaverTails, TruthfulQA, and AlpacaEval are split for both training and test, which is inappropriate because real-world seed examples cannot be assumed in-distribution. Most alignment works use other datasets (e.g., ShareGPT, Alpaca). (R1) The source of the initial QA pairs is not detailed; if they include human annotations, it contradicts the near-zero human supervision claim. (R4) What are the seed answers for AlpacaEval – are they from a specific model? (R1)
- **Weak baselines**: Only compared to basic SFT (which uses only 64 samples, 6–7 times fewer than ISARA) and pre-trained/ICL baselines that do not fine-tune. No comparison with state-of-the-art methods like Self-Instruct or other self-bootstrapping methods listed in Table 1. This omission leaves claims of superiority unverified and relative performance unclear. (R1, R2, R3, R4) The authors should include comparisons to self-bootstrapping methods (even if they require reward models or human instructions) to contextualize SOTA and limitations. (R3)
- **Evaluation metric for TruthfulQA**: Using ROUGE-L is questionable; the original TruthfulQA paper shows ROUGE is inaccurate, and most evaluations use multiple-choice accuracy. (R1)
- **Inconsistent experiment settings**: The iterative mechanism, a key design, only appears in harmlessness experiments. TruthfulQA experiments lack ICL and ICL-kNN baselines. (R1) Most experiments only show results for the first two iterations, even when those are worse than ICL-kNN (e.g., domain generalization in Fig. 2). (R3) No ablation study for the stopping threshold hyperparameter. (R4)
- **Novelty concerns**: The method appears to simply apply Retrieval-Augmented ICL to self-alignment, lacking sufficient novelty. (R3) This contrasts with the strength claimed by another reviewer (R2). The authors should provide a more detailed summary of contributions. (R3)
- **Simplistic filtering mechanism**: Thresholds (ROUGE-L>0.7, 5-word minimum) lack empirical or theoretical backing. No advanced quality checks (e.g., semantic coherence, alignment-specific filtering). Without bias correction, the model may amplify its own biases over iterations; incorrect or non-helpful examples should be filtered out. (R2)
- **Computational efficiency unaddressed**: No analysis of training expenses, retrieval costs, or full pipeline costs. No comparison to one-time training to understand trade-offs between iterative training and performance gains. (R2) Compare resource consumption or runtime between ISARA and ICL-kNN, since ICL-kNN achieves comparable performance without fine-tuning (Tables 2 & 3). (R3)
- **Unclear motivation and missing analysis**: The claim that existing methods lack “systematic mechanism to continuously improve” is not explained or analyzed in the introduction. (R4) The problem statement (self-align to a target domain with few examples) is misaligned with the general-domain critique. (R4) The iterative enhancement mechanism is only justified empirically, not motivated conceptually. (R4)
- **Quality of generated data not evaluated**: The paper does not provide a detailed evaluation of the quality of self-generated samples; existing methods use partially annotated datasets to ensure quality. (R4)
- **Domain generalization**: The domains tested (e.g., malicious speech) are not significantly different; provide performance on larger disparities like truthfulness vs safety. (R4)
- **Missing comparison with manual annotation**: No performance metrics for simple alignment strategies using manually annotated data, which would better highlight the contribution. (R4)
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The preliminaries on LLM generation are unnecessary. (R1) Typos: “ISARIL” in Fig. 2 should be “ISARA”; missing “)” in text; “Figure 4: Data scaling ratio…” should be “Table 4”. Authors should carefully review the manuscript. (R1, R3)
- **Missing references**: References for k-NN and embedding model in Section 4.1 are missing. (R4)
- **Unclear statements**: In Table 1, the meaning of “No Human Instructions” is unclear. Why does Self-Instruct need human instructions while ISARA and ReST do not? Why cannot Self-Instruct achieve continuous enhancement? The claim that “current self-alignment can work only on large models” is not experimentally validated. (R1)
