## Merged Review

### Summary
All three reviewers agree that the paper introduces a transmittance lower bound and divide-and-conquer sampling strategy for Neural Density-Distance Fields (NeDDF) to improve ray sampling in volumetric rendering. Two reviewers (ratings 3 and 3) see the analysis as interesting and acknowledge improvement over NeDDF, but consider the work incremental and lacking comparison with recent state-of-the-art methods. The third reviewer (rating 1) finds the paper not yet ready for publication due to major clarity issues, poor motivation, insufficient validation, and unconvincing results compared to baselines like ngp. All reviewers note that the experimental results do not demonstrate clear superiority over current SOTA, and there is a lack of ablation studies, baseline comparisons, and clear explanation of the methodology.

### Strengths
- Derivation of a transmittance lower bound for each interval along the ray, enabling more efficient point sampling during volume rendering.
- The divide-and-conquer sampling approach based on these bounds is interesting and provides a novel quantitative criterion for effective sampling of transmittance functions using distance fields and their gradients.
- The proposed method shows improvement over the baseline NeDDF in the presented experiments.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art:** The paper only compares with NeDDF and a few older methods. Many recent works report better metrics, such as 3D Gaussian Splatting and Voxurf (which also combines multi-resolution hashing with distance fields). No direct comparison with these methods is provided, making it unclear where the proposed approach stands.
- **Missing baseline comparisons:** Several simple baselines are not tested, e.g., naive dense sampling along the ray with the same number of samples, or sampling weighted by distance values. A baseline with voxelized distance fields is also needed to isolate the effect of the neural representation.
- **Uncertain handling of non-solid objects:** The main claimed advantage of NeDDF is handling shapes with no explicit boundary (e.g., fur, smoke). The paper does not validate whether this advantage is retained in the proposed approach; no experiments on such scenes are shown.
- **Lack of geometric reconstruction results:** Since NeDDF (like NeuS and UDF) is designed for high-quality geometry from multi-view images, the absence of any geometric evaluation or comparison undermines the claimed benefits. A reviewer explicitly asked why no geometric reconstruction results are presented.
- **Vague novelty in Section 3.3:** Combining multi-resolution hash encoding with distance fields is already well-addressed by Voxurf. The novelty of this section is unclear, and qualitative/quantitative comparison with Voxurf is essential.
- **Poor clarity and context (several points):**
    - The abstract is confusing and misleading; it claims help with rendering transparency or landscapes, but no experiments support this.
    - Section 3 lacks motivation and explicit statements of novelty; it is not well connected to prior work, and key citations are missing (e.g., proper historical attribution of the rendering equation, more recent plane-based neural fields like K-planes or triplane diffusion).
    - Notation is confusing: the vertical component \(h(t)\) is introduced without intuitive explanation; subscripts \(n\) and \(f\) are used in equations before being defined.
    - All figures need better explanation. For example, Figure 7 (top panel) has mixed colors and an uninformative caption; the bottom panel makes \(M=32\) invisible, and the impact of different \(M\) on rendering is a mystery.
- **Insufficient validation and experimentation:**
    - Only minor quantitative improvement is shown on 8 synthetic test cases; on larger datasets (e.g., Mip-NeRF 360) the method does not improve over ngp.
    - It is unclear how the synthetic examples were chosen (e.g., random or cherry-picked).
    - Experiment details are sparse: e.g., number of view angles, distance from training views, and evaluation protocol are not described; the paper is not self-contained on these points.
- **Confidence and reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer found the paper not yet ready for publication (rating 1) due to the above clarity and validation issues, while the other two (rating 3) saw potential but noted incremental contribution and missing comparisons. The minority reviewers’ strong concerns about presentation and experimentation should be carefully addressed.
