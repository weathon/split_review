## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q-SHAP, a polynomial-time algorithm for computing Shapley-value-based feature-specific coefficients of determination (\(R^2\)) in tree ensemble models (e.g., gradient boosting machines). It leverages the tree hierarchy, polynomial representations of feature contributions along leaf paths, and the inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) to achieve polynomial complexity for quadratic-loss Shapley computations aggregated over the whole training set. Simulations on synthetic problems (3 variables with known ground truth) and a real prostate cancer gene expression dataset (17k features, 551 samples) demonstrate computational speedups over SAGE and SPVIM and improved estimation accuracy for feature-specific \(R^2\).

### Strengths
- **Novelty and significance**: The first adaptation of Shapley values to tree ensembles for quadratic losses, enabling dataset-level decomposition of \(R^2\). The work is original, and the problem (feature importance for widespread tree ensembles) is important. [Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 5]
- **Computational efficiency**: Q-SHAP achieves polynomial-time complexity, orders of magnitude faster than previous methods (SAGE, SPVIM) that are NP-hard in general. This scalability is a creative and practically valuable contribution. [Reviewers 1, 4, 5]
- **Theoretical foundation**: The paper provides mathematical proofs, e.g., the use of polynomial forms, supporting the algorithm’s correctness. [Reviewer 1]
- **Clarity for some reviewers**: The manuscript is well-written, explains motivation in a structured manner, and presents contributions sequentially, making it easy to follow. [Reviewers 4, 5]  
  *(Note: Other reviewers strongly disagree on clarity – see Weaknesses.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and exposition**: The explanation of the polynomial representation and weight derivation is difficult to follow, lacking a step-by-step breakdown and a clear justification of choices (e.g., handling features that appear multiple times along paths). The core algorithm is presented on only one page, and it is not obvious how it differs from previous work. Multiple reviewers request a simplified explanation, a flowchart, a one-paragraph summary before formulas, and a dedicated limitations section. [Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4]
- **Limited and thin experimental evaluation**: Only one real-world dataset is used (prostate cancer gene expression, with extreme \(p \gg n\)). The synthetic experiments are also limited (3 variables). Missing evaluations on diverse datasets (imbalanced classes, highly correlated features, moderate dimensions). Only one real-world example weakens the empirical validation. [Reviewers 3, 4, 5]
- **Unclear motivation and interpretability**: The motivation for using Shapley values to estimate \(R^2\) is not clearly justified, especially given that Shapley values themselves are controversial as an interpretability tool. How the improved \(R^2\) estimates translate to increased trust or decision-making in practice (e.g., healthcare, finance) is not demonstrated. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4 requests concrete case studies]
- **Lack of comparison and contextualization**: The theoretical advantages over SAGE and SPVIM are not sufficiently explained. A detailed comparison of mathematical foundations, complexity breakdowns, and a table/diagram contrasting methods would clarify Q-SHAP’s gains. The failings of permutation importance (a prominent alternative) are not illustrated with a concrete example. [Reviewers 4, 3 (about permutation importance)]
- **Restricted generalizability**: Q-SHAP is designed specifically for tree-based models and does not directly generalize to non-tree ensembles (e.g., neural networks, SVMs), limiting its broader applicability. The potential extension to other quadratic losses is mentioned but not explored, missing an opportunity to underscore broader relevance. [Reviewers 1, 4]
- **Unanswered technical questions**:  
  - Does the algorithm exactly estimate Shapley values or approximately? [Reviewer 2]  
  - How does the runtime scale with increasing features/trees beyond static benchmarks? Provide a step-by-step breakdown of which operations contribute most to the time complexity reduction. [Reviewer 4]  
  - For the real data, Q-SHAP and SAGE agree on top-15 features but disagree with SPVIM. The discrepancy is not explained. [Reviewer 5]  
  - What are the limitations and potential pitfalls when applying Q-SHAP to different tree model variants? [Reviewer 4]
- **Citation error**: “Elecctronic Journal of Statistics” is misspelled. [Reviewer 3]
