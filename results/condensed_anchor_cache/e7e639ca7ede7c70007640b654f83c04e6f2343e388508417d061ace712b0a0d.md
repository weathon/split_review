## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Analogical Prompting, a method that first prompts the LLM to self-generate relevant knowledge and exemplars before solving the given problem. This approach eliminates the need for manually collected few-shot examples or retrieval. The method involves three phases: related knowledge retrieval, exemplar generation, and answer prompting. Experiments on math reasoning (GSM8K, MATH), code generation (Codeforces, 50 problems), and BIG-Bench tasks show consistent improvements over zero-shot CoT and manual few-shot CoT, with average accuracy gains of about +4% on reasoning tasks.

### Strengths

- **Interesting and well-motivated approach**: The method is inspired by analogical reasoning from cognitive science. It addresses the practical problem of requiring manually collected exemplars for few-shot prompting.
- **Simple and convenient**: No task-specific prompts or training examples are needed. The method is easy to implement.
- **Consistent improvements across diverse benchmarks**: Outperforms zero-shot CoT and manual few-shot CoT on GSM8K, MATH, Codeforces, and BIG-Bench.
- **Ablation studies**: The paper includes ablations on scalability, the effect of knowledge, and the number of exemplars, demonstrating robustness.
- **Qualitative analysis**: An error analysis of generated exemplars (relevant/correct, irrelevant, incorrect) in Section 6.6 is provided, showing the relationship between exemplar quality and downstream performance.
- **Test set contamination addressed**: The Codeforces dataset uses recently published problems, reducing contamination concerns.
- **Well-written and clear**: The paper is well-organized, with illustrative figures and examples that make the method easy to follow.

### Weaknesses

- **Experimental setup inconsistencies**: Some studies include in-context demonstrations while others do not; some adopt the knowledge paradigm while others do not. The caption for Table 1 mentions an in-context demonstration for davinci models without explaining whether this is a training example or merely formatting.
- **Missing ablations on distinctness**: The paper emphasizes the importance of generating *distinct* exemplars, but provides no ablation to verify this design choice.
- **Limited dataset for code generation**: Codeforces contains only 50 problems; the claimed ~2% improvement corresponds to one additional solved problem. Larger benchmarks (e.g., HumanEval) are not evaluated.
- **No GPT-4 experiments**: The experiments are limited to smaller models (text-davinci-003, etc.), omitting a natural comparison with GPT-4.
- **Incomplete error analysis**: 
  - The analysis does not specify how many MATH problems fail due to incorrect vs. irrelevant exemplars.
  - No error analysis for *correctly solved* problems is provided: it is unknown how often exemplars are wrong but the model still produces the correct answer (which could indicate disregard for exemplars).
  - Separate error analyses for GSM8K and MATH are missing, leaving dataset-specific behavior unclear.
- **Questions about exemplar quality and LLM reliance**:
  - The provided analysis shows that in 56% of unsolved problems, generated exemplars are not useful (irrelevant or incorrect), and in 30% of solved problems the LLM disregards the exemplars entirely. This suggests the method has difficulty altering LLMs' reasoning from their trained biases, raising doubts about whether current LLMs are effective analogical reasoners.
  - It is not demonstrated that LLMs can generate helpful exemplars for very challenging questions; more qualitative examples are needed.
- **Lack of comparisons with related automatic prompting methods**: The paper does not compare against other automatic prompt engineering methods such as "Large Language Models Are Human-Level Prompt Engineers" or neural-symbolic approaches (e.g., Zhang et al., 2023).
- **Limited technical contribution** (minority concern from Reviewer 3): The method is more of an engineering trick (zero-shot CoT → self-generated few-shot) than a deep technical advance.
- **Unaddressed conceptual distinction**: The generated exemplars are likely novel problems based on statistical knowledge, not literally retrieved from memory as in human analogical reasoning. The paper does not discuss this distinction or its implications.
- **Integration possibilities not explored**: It is unknown whether the method can be combined with self-consistency decoding (e.g., majority voting over multiple reasoning chains) or whether adding a verification stage to filter out incorrect/irrelevant examples would improve performance.
