## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes NoPoSplat, a feed-forward model that reconstructs 3D scenes (as 3D Gaussians) from unposed sparse multi-view images. It anchors one input view’s coordinate system as canonical space, uses a ViT with cross-attention for cross-view fusion, and converts camera intrinsics into a token embedding. Trained with only photometric loss (no depth supervision, loss masks, or metric poses), the method achieves real-time inference. It is evaluated on RealEstate10K, ACID, and generalizes to DTU/in-the-wild. All three reviewers rate the paper 8/10, but with different levels of concern: Reviewer 1 is strongly positive (only requests geometry quant comparisons), Reviewer 2 finds the method simple and effective but notes missing ablations and metric discrepancies, and Reviewer 3 raises major concerns about ignored prior works, unfair comparisons, and the need for several additional experiments.

### Strengths
- Well written and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- A simple feed-forward approach (ViT) that is competitive—even better than pose-aware methods like MVSplat—especially when input overlap is minimal (R2, R3).
- No need for ground-truth depth, explicit matching loss, loss masks, or metric poses during training; this allows scaling to larger datasets (R1, R2).
- Using pretrained Mast3r weights with pure photometric loss on more data is a good strategy to improve NVS and relative pose estimation (R2).
- Good generalization capability on in-the-wild data (R1, R2).
- Real-time reconstruction (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing geometry evaluation**: Quantitative geometry reconstruction results (e.g., point cloud error) should be provided against Mast3r/Splatt3r, since Fig. 5 only shows qualitative (R1, R2). R2 suggests showing depth maps or 3D points instead of Gaussian visualizations. R3 also stresses that DUST3R and its followers are designed for geometry, not NVS, so comparing NVS metrics is misleading.
- **Unfair / inappropriate baselines**:
  - DUST3R and Mast3r are not designed for NVS; including them in Table 1/Fig 4 is unfair (R2, R3). They do not require camera intrinsics (R3).
  - The proposed model is trained on a larger dataset (RealEstate10K + DL3DV + ACID) and uses pretrained Mast3r weights, while baselines like MVSplat or pixelSplat are trained on different splits. Comparisons are not controlled (R3).
- **Canonical space motivation not clearly differentiated**: In the 2-view setting the method predicts per-pixel per-view Gaussians in a common coordinate, which is analogous to DUST3R-style prediction. The main advantage appears to come from finetuning the Mast3r backbone on more data with photometric loss, which is orthogonal to the claimed contribution (R2). R3 adds that canonicalization and cross-view fusion have already been studied in PF‑LRM, LEAP, DUST3R.
- **Missing related work**: PF‑LRM, LEAP, GS‑LRM, GRM, SRT, and other pose-free methods are not discussed. The architecture is nearly identical to GS‑LRM (differences: ViT vs. tokenization, cross-attention vs. concatenation+self-attention). This is a direct combination of prior work and does not constitute a solid contribution (R3). R1 suggests adding ReconX, a concurrent work.
- **Only two views tested**: The paper claims “sparse views” but all experiments use exactly 2 input images. Multi-view (N > 2) results (qualitative, quantitative, performance trade-offs) are needed to support the claim (R2, R3). R2 asks about the upper limit given compute constraints and how cross-attention scales.
- **Metric discrepancies**:
  - In Table 1, MVSplat and pixelSplat report higher PSNR in their original papers than the numbers shown here. Why? (R2)
  - In Table 4, Splatt3r’s numbers are lower than reported in its original paper. Why? (R2)
- **Necessary additional experiments** (R3 requests strongly):
  1. Train on the same data as prior pose-free works (object-level) and compare performance.
  2. Train with only RealEstate10K + ACID to compare fairly with MVSplat.
  3. Train a pose-conditioned variant (using plücker rays) to measure the gap from pose-aware methods.
  4. Ablate the Mast3r weight initialization: train from scratch on the current dataset to see if the pose-free capability comes from initialization or the learning process.
- **Training requires poses and intrinsics**: The paper should explicitly note that ground-truth poses are used during training (for rendering), and camera intrinsics are needed at inference (R3). The intrinsic embedding method (global concat) is the same as LRM/PF‑LRM but not discussed (R3).
- **Minor issues**:
  - Contribution 4 is not a contribution (R2).
  - L274: “intrinsic token option” should be “Global Intrinsic Embedding – Concat option” (R2).
  - Appendix Tab. 6 ablation is incomplete: NVS metrics depend on the 3DGS field, not just the backbone; a better ablation would be on pose estimation given pure 3D points (R2).
  - L773, L775 typo: “experiences” → “experiments” (R2).
- **Clarifying questions**: (R1)
  - Is the main difference from Splatt3r using/not using depth supervision and metric poses? Does depth supervision limit dataset choices?
  - L838-840: does “using ground truth intrinsic during training also causes failure” refer to training Splatt3r?
  - What are training hardware and training time?
  - Are the pretrained Mast3r weights frozen or fine-tuned?
