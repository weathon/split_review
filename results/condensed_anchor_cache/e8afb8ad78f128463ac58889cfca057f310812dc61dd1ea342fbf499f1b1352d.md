## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that the problem of weakly incremental 3D object detection (reducing novel-class annotation cost by using 2D supervisions) is novel and valuable. The proposed framework—combining pseudo‑label refinement, cross‑modal knowledge transfer, and reweighted distillation—is technically sound and well‑motivated. However, all reviewers raise substantial concerns about clarity of presentation, insufficient evaluation (especially missing baselines and settings), and unclear technical novelty relative to prior work (e.g., SDCoT, BoxPC, WEAKM3D). The concerns are serious enough that the current version does not convincingly demonstrate a clear advance.

### Strengths
1. **Novel task formulation** – Weakly Incremental 3D Object Detection (WI3D) is an unexplored but important direction for reducing annotation requirements in class‑incremental 3D detection (all reviewers).
2. **Clear problem illustration** – The paper makes clear illustrations of the major challenges (misalignment between 2D/3D, forgetting) and proposes a logical set of components to address them (R1, R3).
3. **Effective baseline result** – The method outperforms the main considered baseline SDCoT in the WI3D scenario (R1).
4. **Sound integration of existing techniques** – The modular architecture (pseudo‑label refinement, cross‑modal transfer, reweighting) is well‑founded and straightforward (R2, R3).
5. **Extensive ablation study** – Ablation experiments and analysis demonstrate the contribution of each component, although one key ablation (reweighting factor) is missing (R2, R3 mixed; R3 sees it as a strength, R2 notes the omission).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient comparison and evaluation scope**
   - Only one baseline (SDCoT) is compared; other closely related works (Zhao & Lee 2022; Zhao et al. 2022; Liang et al. 2023) are acknowledged but not evaluated against (R1).
   - No results are reported for the sequential incremental learning setting, which is common in class‑incremental learning (R2).
   - The number of incremental stages and total classes is small; scalability to more stages/classes (e.g., 10 stages for ScanNet200) is not tested (R3).
   - The quality of the generated pseudo‑labels (coarse and refined) is not quantitatively evaluated; mAP, recall, or precision metrics for pseudo‑labels are missing (R3).

2. **Many missing or unclear technical details**
   - **Coarse pseudo‑label generation** – The method for generating coarse 3D pseudo‑labels from 2D predictions is never clearly explained. The paper cites WEAKM3D (Peng et al. 2021/2022), which was designed for outdoor LiDAR point clouds, but does not describe the adaptation to indoor RGB‑D data (clustering algorithm, parameters, determination of rotation/center/size) (R1, R2, R3).
   - **PLR and BCH module training** – The training of the Pseudo‑Label Refinement module (PRF) and the Binary Classification Head (BCH) is not described. It is unclear: (a) how ground‑truth offsets and presence labels are obtained, (b) whether training uses base classes only, novel classes, or both, (c) whether normalization (Fig. 4) is for point coordinates, box center, or dimensions, and (d) whether the module is trained end‑to‑end with the 3D detector or in a separate stage (R1, R2).
   - **Adaptation of SDCoT to WI3D** – The paper states it “modifies the training of SDCoT to fit our weakly incremental setting” but does not specify the modifications. The exact use of pseudo‑labels in the SDCoT loss function, the “fine‑tuning” vs. “freeze‑and‑add” setups, and what parameters are frozen are not defined (R2, R3).
   - **Definition of “vanilla”** – The term “vanilla” in Table 5 is ambiguous; what components are included/excluded is not stated (R2, R3).
   - **Class splits** – The procedure for splitting classes into C_base and C_novel is claimed to follow SDCoT, but Table 1 differs from SDCoT’s Table 1; no explanation is given (R2).
   - **Reweighting modulation factor** – How the teacher predicts the reweighting factor alpha_i (based on objectness scores) is not clearly explained, and the formula for the objectness score is missing (R3).
   - **IOU‑based cost function** – Section 3.4 briefly mentions using IOU between 2D predictions and projected 3D predictions in “the cost function,” but no equation is provided (R1).
   - **Regional representations** – The term “regional representations” appears first in Section 3.4 without prior definition (R1).
   - **Human‑visual‑system analogy** – The connection to the human visual system (“Inspired by the human visual system…”) is not clear (R1).

3. **Technical novelty is limited or questionable**
   - The PLR module closely resembles the BoxPC network in [REF1], which also refines pseudo‑labels by predicting 3D box residuals and binary presence. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison of architectural differences, loss functions, or training procedures (R2).
   - The core ideas—pseudo‑label generation for weak supervision and knowledge distillation for incremental learning—are not novel; specific modifications (feature projection, reweighting modulation) appear incremental (R3).
   - The reweighting factor alpha_i (the key claimed modification to SDCoT’s knowledge distillation) is not ablated against the version without alpha_i in Table 7, making its contribution unverifiable (R2).
   - Knowledge transfer is applied only to novel classes, which may cause representational divergence between base and novel classes and suboptimal joint performance (R3).

4. **Specific weaknesses of the methodology**
   - The use of WEAKM3D (designed for outdoor, sparse LiDAR) for indoor, densely‑packed scenes may lead to poor pseudo‑label quality; DBSCAN clustering may merge or fragment objects in cluttered indoor scenes (R2, R3).
   - Figure 2: (a) the misalignment example is questionable—the shown case likely only occurs when the 2D box is entirely wrong; (b) the 3D boxes are poorly aligned with the 2D box edges, but the generation method used for this figure is not described (R1, R2).

5. **Presentation quality issues**
   - Typos: “how to introduces” → “how to introduce”, “Pseuod” → “Pseudo”, “preditions” → “predictions”, “objectiveness” → “objectness” (R1).
   - Reference year error: WEAKM3D was published in 2022, not 2021 as cited (R3).
   - No visualizations of 2D‑3D bounding‑box pairs (bipartite matching) are provided (R2).
