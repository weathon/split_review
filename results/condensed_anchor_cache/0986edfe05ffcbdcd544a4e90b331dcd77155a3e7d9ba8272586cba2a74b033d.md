## Merged Review

### Summary

Auto-Arena is a framework that automates LLM evaluation using LLM-powered agents through three stages: an examiner generates questions, two candidates engage in multi-round peer battles, and a committee of judges discusses the winner. It achieves a 92.14% correlation with human preferences across 15 recent LLMs without manual effort. The method is validated with ablation studies, contamination analysis, and shows extensibility across languages and domains (English and Chinese). While novel in its complete automation and use of questioning, the work is seen by some as incremental over existing multi-agent evaluation techniques. Cost is estimated at ~$5 per model, but this is not fully broken down.

### Strengths

- Clear motivation addressing limitations of static benchmarks (inflexibility, data contamination), costly human evaluation, and biases in single-judge setups.
- Comprehensive experiments on 15 modern LLMs achieve 92.14% correlation with human preferences, surpassing prior automated methods.
- Detailed contamination analysis and ablation studies demonstrate the value of multi-round battles and committee discussions.
- Architecture is novel: using an LLM examiner to generate questions and having models act as peers in battle is a new contribution.
- Scalable and automated; reduces reliance on manual effort.
- Works across different languages (English and Chinese) and tasks, showing versatility.
- Peer-battle mechanism reveals subtle model strengths/weaknesses through competitive interactions.
- Well-structured, well-written, and clearly explained.
- Appendix mentions cost savings compared to comparable approaches (though breakdown is missing).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty:** The application of multi-agent debate for automated evaluation is not particularly novel. Closely related work (ChatEval) is not discussed.
- **No formal guarantees:** There are no formal guarantees on the reliability of the evaluation.
- **Missing limitations section:** A dedicated limitations section is absent.
- **Limited evaluation questions:** Only 30 questions were used; a larger and more diverse set is needed given the central role of questions. More detailed analysis of these questions in the main paper would strengthen the work.
- **Reliability of LLM-as-judge:** Using LLMs to evaluate other LLMs is inherently unreliable; performance as judges often stems from data leakage. The authors claim extensibility to unseen tasks, but this is not experimentally supported. Testing on newer tasks/data not seen by any LLM agent would be needed.
- **Divergence from human judgments:** The paper lacks analysis of cases where Auto-Arena disagrees with human evaluators, especially in nuanced or high-stakes cases. Such examples could highlight where refinement is needed (both R1 and R3 request this).
- **Sensitivity to committee composition:** It is unclear how sensitive results are to the choice and number of initial committee members and their ranking initialization. Including low-capability LLMs in the judge committee might reduce performance (R1 question).
- **Question quality over time:** No mechanism is provided to ensure questions remain challenging as LLMs evolve (R1 question).
- **Resource intensity and cost justification:** Auto-Arena involves many agent interactions (peer debates + committee discussions), making it more resource-intensive than existing static benchmarks. The paper mentions ~$5 per model evaluation but does not break down costs (compute, inference, rounds, discussions) or compare cost-efficiency against baselines. One reviewer (Reviewer 4) specifically questions whether the increase in accuracy justifies the added computational costs.
- **Real-time evaluation of uncertain data:** There is a need to explore performance on unseen data and ensure all models have equal time-stamp cutoffs for fair peer-review and judging (R2 question).
- **Effect of weak models:** It is unclear how results change if a weaker/smaller model is used as a peer, judge, or question generator (R2 question).
- **Choice of judges:** The paper selects stronger models as judges rather than original debate participants; it is not justified why using the original debaters as judges wouldn't improve answer quality (R3 question).
- **Incorporating external information:** The framework relies heavily on the foundational model's strength; incorporating external sources (e.g., like Bing) is not explored (R3 question).
- **Code anonymization:** One reviewer (Reviewer 4) noted the provided code at `https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Auto-Arena-Code/analysis_scripts/result_analysis.ipynb` was not properly anonymized and reveals the author's name.
- **Incremental contribution:** Beyond introducing the framework, the paper does not provide significant new findings about model capabilities (e.g., individual model performances per domain). The contributions are seen as incremental. (This is a stronger criticism from Reviewer 4, who scored the paper 5, while others rated 6.)
