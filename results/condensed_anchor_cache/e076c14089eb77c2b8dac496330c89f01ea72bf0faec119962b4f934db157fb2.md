## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a meta-learning framework for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) that dynamically optimizes per-synapse learning rates via a two-loop process. In the inner loop, weights are updated with a local plasticity rule; in the outer loop, the per-synapse learning rate parameters \(\eta_{ij}\) are updated using gradients of the loss with respect to \(\eta\), inspired by MAML. Evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and DVS-CIFAR10 with Spiking ResNet-18 and Spiking VGG-11, the method reports improved classification accuracy and faster convergence compared to baselines like STDP, STBP, and EIHL, with claimed links to biological metaplasticity.

### Strengths

- The general concept of using a local learning rule to define the weight update and a global learning rule to set the learning rate is interesting and well-motivated from biological metaplasticity.
- The problem of efficient SNN training is timely and significant for neuromorphic computing, and the two-level training approach is distinct from previous SNN training approaches.
- Large performance improvements are demonstrated, and the method effectively performs gradient descent with a partially swapped role of learning rate and parameters.
- The experimental setup includes comparisons against relevant baselines (STDP, STBP, EIHL) on standard datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, DVS-CIFAR10), using standard SNN architectures and reporting both training and validation accuracies.
- The paper provides some insights into why the new framework performs well, such as the importance of adaptability (Table 3).
- The training method is clearly demonstrated via pseudocode and explanations, and sufficient details about hyperparameters and network architectures are given (though one reviewer notes missing details—see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Critical missing details**: The function \(f(s_i,s_j)\) in the plasticity rule \(\delta w_{ij} = \eta_{ij} f(s_i,s_j)\) is never defined or explained, making the core formulation incomplete (R1, R3, R4, R5). Conversely, one reviewer (R5) states algorithmic detail is sufficient, but the majority find this and other key details omitted.
- **Unclear meta-learning connection**: The method is claimed as meta-learning but differs starkly from MAML. In MAML, the outer loop learns to make the network adaptable to new tasks; here the outer loop modulates weight changes and also directly changes weights, relying on a distinct batch to introduce generalizable properties—more akin to contextual/attentional modulation (citations: Lindsay & Miller, 2018; Cheung et al., 2019; Thorat et al., 2019; Hummos, 2023). The link to MAML and the exact gradients followed for optimizing learning rates are underspecified (R1, R2).
- **Missing gradient computation details**: How \(\frac{\partial L_{\mathcal{D}'}(w'_{ij})}{\partial \eta_{ij}}\) is computed is not explained; the computation graph presumably includes the plasticity update (Eq. 8) and automatic differentiation, but this is not stated (R1). Algorithm 1 mentions a learning rate \(\alpha\) that is never used, and the variable table lists two \(\alpha\)s, causing confusion (R5).
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**:
  - No comparison to standard adaptive optimizers like Adam or RMSProp, which also adjust per-parameter learning rates (R1, R3).
  - HP (homeostatic plasticity) is not compared in all experiments; only STBP appears in Table 2 without explanation for why other baselines are omitted (R5).
  - The method could be applied on top of other baselines (STDP, STBP, EIHL) as a generic way to tune per-parameter learning rates—this is not discussed (R3).
- **Incomplete ablation studies**: Section 4.5 (ablation studies) appears unfinished, referencing no tables or figures. Very few controls are shown (only the impact of \(\beta\)), leaving many questions about the method’s sensitivity (R3, R4, R5). EIHL is not included in the adaptability/generalization analysis of Table 2 (R4).
- **Performance dependency on hyperparameter tuning**: The method’s performance heavily depends on tuning \(\beta\) (Figure 5); without precise tuning, accuracy drops below EIHL, questioning the approach’s efficiency and robustness (R4).
- **Unsupported or contradictory claims**:
  - “Synapses critical for task performance receive higher \(\eta_{ij}\) values” would seemingly conflict with the expectation that critical synapses should become fixed by training’s end; the paper does not explore links to continual learning approaches (e.g., Zenke et al., 2017) (R1).
  - The Epoch-number comparison in Figure 3 is unfair because per-epoch complexity differs between methods; the proposed method likely has higher time complexity per epoch due to frequent \(\eta\) updates (R5).
  - Exceptional results (e.g., Spiking ResNet-18 underperforming VGG-11 with STDP on CIFAR-10 and DVS-CIFAR10) are not explained (R5).
- **Limited analysis of learned learning rates**: Figure 4 shows a histogram of final \(\eta\) distributions with multiple modes—it is unclear whether these correspond to different layers, whether the distribution is sensitive to network architecture or random seeds, and how it should be interpreted (R2, R3).
- **No evaluation on standard SNN benchmarks**: Spiking datasets such as SHD and SSC are not used; the method’s effectiveness on these is unknown (R1).
- **Computational cost not reported**: No comparison of FLOPs with vanilla ResNet/VGG trained with backprop is provided, hindering positioning beyond SNN field (R2).
- **Reproducibility concerns**: Code is not provided, hyperparameter tuning details (network size, batch size) for each model are missing, and train-test split is unspecified (R4).
- **Presentation and writing quality**: Several typos, citation formatting errors (e.g., \citet vs. \citep in lines 076–079, 086, double period in line 129), low-resolution Figure 1, uninformative Figure 2 (lacks kernel size, stride, padding, width), repeated abbreviation definitions, and unclear logic in line 36 (R1, R4, R5). The number of cited papers is lower than typical for top conferences (R4). Surrogate gradient details are not introduced at line 317 but appear later in Eq. (12) (R5). References for meta-learning in continual learning for SNNs are missing at line 169 (R2, R5).
- **Open questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - What is the nature of the readout—spiking neurons or a softmax layer with cross-entropy loss? (R2)
  - How is finetuning performed in Section 4.4? Is a new readout included and retrained while keeping \(\eta\) constant? (R2)
  - What does “fixed eta” mean in Section 4.7—\(\eta = 1\)? How does that condition differ from standard STDP, and why does performance exceed STDP? (R2)
  - How good is the STDP baseline? Would training only a readout on top of a random network give comparable performance? (R2)
  - If weights are reinitialized to random but trained \(\eta\)s are kept, would training be faster than with \(\eta=1\)? (R2)
  - How does the parameter count compare to benchmarks? (R4)
