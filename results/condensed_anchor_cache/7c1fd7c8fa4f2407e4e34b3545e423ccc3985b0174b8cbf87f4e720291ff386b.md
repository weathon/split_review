## Merged Review

### Summary
MeshAnything treats mesh extraction as a generative problem using a VQ-VAE and a shape-conditioned decoder-only transformer to produce Artist-Created Meshes (AMs). The method converts any 3D representation (NeRF, Gaussian splats, etc.) into point clouds for conditioning. A noise-resistant decoder (Gumbel noise trick) and conditioning of the VQ-VAE on the shape are employed. The model achieves high-quality meshes with far fewer faces than reconstruction-based methods, aiming to bridge the gap between generated 3D assets and real-world industry usage.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to follow across all sections (R1, R3, R4).
- The problem of generating artist-created meshes is important and seldom explored; the work could serve as an improved remeshing tool and is significant for bridging 3D generation research into practical applications (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The architecture is well-motivated: VQ-VAE with shape conditioning and a noise-resistant decoder (Gumbel noise trick) improves reconstruction robustness (R1, R2). The use of coarse meshes to sample point clouds as inputs narrows the gap between training and inference (R2).
- Converting all 3D representations to point clouds for shape conditioning is an interesting and reasonable approach that makes the model agnostic to the source representation (R3).
- Quantitative results (Table 2) show the method outperforms recent baselines; qualitative results are high-quality (R1, R2, R3, R4). Visualization is good (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and scope**: The focus on “artist-created” meshes is not clearly justified. Would the same method work on reconstruction-based meshes? The assumption appears to be that the method cannot scale to a realistic number of vertices (R1). The task is more accurately described as mesh reconstruction/remeshing rather than generation; the authors should rename the paper accordingly (R2). R2 specifically disputes calling it a generation paper.
- **Scalability and data limits**: Memory scales quadratically with the number of mesh faces. The authors filter out meshes with more than 800 faces (Sec. 4.4?), which limits representation of complex structures (R1, R2). Detailed memory/runtime numbers and the maximum number of faces/vertices the model can handle are needed (R1). Only 56k high-quality artist-created meshes were filtered from Objaverse and ShapeNet, raising concerns about scaling to more data or parameters (R2).
- **Unfair evaluation and comparison issues**:
  - Using meshes generated by the Rodin engine as shape conditioning for comparison with MeshGPT gives MeshAnything an unfair advantage (R2).
  - The quantitative evaluation in Table 2 is biased: MeshAnything uses point clouds sampled from the evaluation set as inputs (aligning generation with ground-truth shapes), while baselines generate unconditionally. This inflates MeshAnything’s performance, especially on diverse Objaverse. ShapeNet (constrained categories) would be more suitable (R3).
  - No qualitative comparisons to baselines (MeshGPT, PolyGen) are provided (R3).
- **User study concerns**: No information is given about whether the 41 participants (L517) are qualified artists with experience in 3D creation/editing. If the users are not qualified, the user study results (Table 1) are not convincing (R3).
- **Generation procedure ambiguity**: Only one mesh is shown per input; the model likely uses greedy/max logit sampling. The authors should clarify whether max sampling was used, and provide diversity variants (e.g., beam search) and discuss how diverse the generation can be (R3). The meaning of “stable” in the claim “our method is not as stable as reconstruction-based…” is unclear (R4).
- **Missing demonstrations**: Real-world application examples (e.g., shape manipulation by artists, rendering efficiency gains) are needed to highlight the significance of AMs (R4).
- **Technical clarity**: The statement “poorer topology quality (relying on dense faces) affects geometric characteristics” is logically unclear – topology is about connectivity, geometry about vertex positions. Please clarify (R4).
- **Missing references**: Recent works should be acknowledged: Part123 (SIGGRAPH 2024), SyncDreamer (ICLR 2024), CLAY (SIGGRAPH 2024) (R4).
- **Reproducibility and analysis**: Will data and code be released? Limitations and failure cases (e.g., watertightness, open surfaces) should be expanded in the main paper (R4).
- **Citation format**: All citations use `\citet` instead of `\citep`, which breaks sentence flow and does not match template instructions (R3).
