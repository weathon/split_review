- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GroupMamba, a parameter-efficient vision state-space model that divides input channels into groups, applies four directional SSM scans, and uses a channel modulation operator plus a distillation loss to boost performance. All three reviewers rate the paper 3/5, with major concerns about limited novelty, unfair comparisons due to distillation, and insufficient experiments.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to understand (Reviewer 1).
- Grouping different scanning directions significantly reduces parameter count while maintaining high performance, making GroupMamba suitable for resource-limited deployment (Reviewer 2).
- The channel modulation module somewhat improves performance, and distillation techniques bring significant improvements to vision mamba models (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty of the core contributions**  
   - The Modulated Group Mamba layer is essentially a channel-reduced version of VMamba (four scanning directions, but with channels reduced from C to C/4 for each group) plus a channel integration module. The authors should clarify fundamental differences versus a channel-reduced VMamba and provide trade-off analysis (Reviewer 2).  
   - The grouped scanning strategy has significant overlap with UltraLight VM‑UNet (Wu et al., arXiv 2403.20035), which uses nearly identical implementation (Reviewer 3).  
   - The grouping operator should be viewed as an engineering refinement rather than an original contribution, as it only modifies VMamba’s SSM by reducing channel numbers and decoupling SSM parameters (Reviewer 1).

2. **Channel Affinity Modulation (CAM) is very similar to Squeeze‑and‑Excitation**  
   - CAM is almost identical to the SE block (Hu et al., CVPR 2018). Formulation in Eq. (8) mirrors Eq. (3) of SE: both use global pooling, a scaling factor per channel, and similar σ/δ operators. The paper neither mentions nor cites this well‑known work (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
   - This lack of citation and the identical design mean CAM should not be considered a technical contribution (Reviewer 1). The authors must clarify differences within the SSM context or properly cite SE (Reviewer 2).

3. **Unfair experimental comparisons due to distillation**  
   - All competing models in classification, detection, and segmentation are trained *without* knowledge distillation, while GroupMamba uses a distillation loss. For a fair evaluation, the authors should compare against models distilled using the same teacher (RegNetY‑16G) or report non‑distilled performance (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
   - Excluding distillation, GroupMamba achieves results comparable to recent mamba models (VMamba, LocalMamba), and under fair comparison it lags behind GrootVL (Xiao et al., arXiv 2406.02395) by a large margin (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
   - Performance gains are largely attributed to distillation (e.g., classification improvement from 82.5% to 83.3% on GroupMamba‑T). The authors should provide specific detection/segmentation results without distillation (Reviewer 2).

4. **Insufficient motivation and evidence for distillation improving scalability**  
   - The claim that distillation enhances scalability of Vision Mamba is insufficiently supported. The argument “accuracy improves with distillation” is trivial; stronger evidence such as theoretical analysis or ablations is needed (Reviewer 1).  
   - The motivation that vision state‑space models lack stability when scaling is outdated: subsequent optimizations (e.g., VMamba v3) have already achieved stable training for base‑size models (Reviewer 3).

5. **Incomplete experiments and unfair baselines**  
   - Downstream tasks (detection, segmentation) are reported only for the tiny variant. Results for small and base variants are missing; they should be provided (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
   - A larger model and a longer training schedule (e.g., 3× on COCO detection) are required (Reviewer 3).  
   - Figure 3 shows only qualitative results of the proposed method, lacking comparisons with methods like VMamba or LocalMamba (Reviewer 2).

6. **Efficiency concerns: throughput slower than baseline**  
   - Despite parameter reduction, the model’s throughput is significantly slower than the baseline. VMamba v2 reports a throughput of 1340; the authors should explain this gap (Reviewer 2).

7. **Writing quality issues**  
   - The introduction lacks an overview of the proposed methods; it does not describe how the paper addresses the stated problem (Reviewer 3).  
   - Equation errors: in Lines 209‑210 the dimensionality of **C** is incorrect (should be 1 × N, not N × 1) (Reviewer 3).  
   - Mathematical notation in Section 3.3 should be clarified, especially Eqs. (3), (6), and (9), to align with Eq. (2) in Section 3.1 (Reviewer 2).  
   - Fonts in some parts of the manuscript differ from the official template, particularly for formulations (Reviewer 1).
