## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a retraction-free optimization algorithm on the Stiefel manifold that does not require a penalty parameter, proves exact convergence with constant step size (improving over Ablin & Peyré, 2022, which only converged to a neighborhood), and applies it to LoRA fine-tuning (Manifold‑LoRA) by constraining the **B** matrix to the Stiefel manifold. Experiments on NLP benchmarks show training speedup and improved evaluation metrics compared to standard LoRA.

### Strengths

- The theoretical establishment of **exact convergence** for the retraction‑free method with a **constant step size** is a promising improvement over Ablin & Peyré (2022), which guaranteed only convergence to a neighborhood whose size depended on the step size (Reviewer 1, rating 8; Reviewer 2, rating 5, also noted improved convergence).  
  *However, one reviewer (rating 3) contests this novelty, stating that more general results (e.g., [3, Theorem 2.8]) already proved convergence for any fixed penalty parameter (Reviewer 3).*
- The algorithm is **computationally friendly** by avoiding retraction operations (Reviewer 2).
- The **application to LoRA fine‑tuning is novel**, and the empirical validation on a range of NLP tasks demonstrates that Manifold‑LoRA can accelerate training speed and improve evaluation metrics compared to standard LoRA (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3).
- The paper is **well‑presented** in the view of one reviewer (Reviewer 1), though another reviewer found numerous typos affecting readability (Reviewer 3).
- The theoretical components appear **sound** to one high‑confidence reviewer (Reviewer 3, though with reservations about novelty).

### Weaknesses

1. **Novelty and relation to prior work** – The contribution is unclear. The update rule (Equation 3) closely follows existing landing methods ([1] Ablin & Peyré 2022; [2] “Optimization flows landing on the Stiefel manifold,” IFAC 2022; [3] “Optimization without retraction on the random generalized Stiefel manifold,” ICML 2024). A landing algorithm on the Stiefel manifold was already proposed in [2], and deterministic/stochastic versions on the generalized Stiefel manifold were studied in [3]. The claim of a constant penalty parameter is not new: [3, Theorem 2.8] already established convergence for any fixed penalty parameter (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (rating 8) considered the exact convergence result significant, but this disagreement is noted.

2. **Insufficient literature review** – The paper omits important related works:
   - Riemannian Preconditioned LoRA (arXiv:2402.02347), DoRA (arXiv:2402.09353), and LoRA+ (arXiv:2402.12354) (Reviewer 2).
   - The landing algorithms on Stiefel/generalized Stiefel manifolds [2][3] (Reviewer 3).

3. **Missing comparisons with appropriate baselines**:
   - No comparison with existing low‑rank fine‑tuning methods that incorporate the Stiefel manifold constraint (e.g., via retraction); thus it is unclear whether improvements are due to the proposed algorithm or the Stiefel constraint itself (Reviewer 1).
   - No comparison with the standard **penalty method** (which simply uses gradients of the objective plus penalty) for Stiefel optimization; the penalty method is easier to implement and might enjoy similar convergence guarantees; such an experiment could justify the advantage of the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
   - For the LoRA experiments, all methods achieve comparable accuracy; the paper does not make a strong case that fine‑tuning LLMs is an appropriate application for the proposed Stiefel optimization (Reviewer 4).

4. **Lack of runtime comparisons** – The paper reports savings in epochs but does not compare actual wall‑clock runtime per iteration; the proposed method may have higher per‑iteration cost due to additional computations (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 specifically calls the comparison in Figure 2 misleading in this regard.

5. **Theoretical gap for the actual method used in experiments** – The theory is developed for optimization over the Stiefel manifold only, but the experimental formulation (Equation 13) and Algorithm 1 involve the **oblique manifold** (**B** constrained to have unit‑norm columns). The paper does not develop a landing algorithm for the oblique manifold nor justify its use; the Stiefel part of the constraint is not actually enforced in the algorithm (since **B** is taken from the oblique manifold), creating a contradiction. Furthermore, enforcing the oblique manifold constraints is trivial, so the need for a penalty‑type method is questionable (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 also notes that Equation (13) is not rigorous (it describes a union of Stiefel and oblique, which is just the oblique manifold).

6. **Motivation for the Stiefel constraint in LoRA is unclear** – Even if **B** is constrained to the Stiefel manifold, the representation **BA** remains non‑unique (e.g., **B** can be multiplied by an orthogonal matrix and **A** compensated). It is not explained why this specific decomposition accelerates convergence; vanilla LoRA has more parameter freedom (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3).

7. **Step size selection remains an issue** – Although the theory allows constant step sizes, in practice step size tuning is still needed for the experiments (Reviewer 2). The authors mention that step size should scale with ∥**A**∥₂ and ∥**B**∥₂, but it is not clear whether this is actually used in the experiments and how the step size is chosen for the proposed method vs. baselines (Reviewer 2).

8. **Presentation and clarity issues**:
   - The paper is not carefully written, with numerous typos that affect readability (Reviewer 3). Examples: the second “**X**” in inequality (2) should be “**Y**” (Reviewer 3); lines 820‑821 in the appendix need details for “induction”; line 849 is confusing (Reviewer 3).
   - Sections 2.2 and parts of 2.1 are not used in the main paper (Reviewer 4).
   - Algorithm 1 introduces ADAM‑related quantities (lines 335, 338) without any prior introduction, making it inaccessible to a novice reader (Reviewer 4).
   - The claim in lines 173‑174 that the Stiefel manifold is 1‑proximally smooth is given without proof, yet it is a cornerstone for the appendix proofs; more explanation is needed (Reviewer 3).

9. **Code not provided** – The code is not included in the supplementary material, which is necessary for reproducibility (Reviewer 1).

10. **Parameter choices** – Theorem 1 requires penalty parameter μ = 1/3, while prior work [3] allows any positive value; the reason for this specific value is not explained (Reviewer 3).
