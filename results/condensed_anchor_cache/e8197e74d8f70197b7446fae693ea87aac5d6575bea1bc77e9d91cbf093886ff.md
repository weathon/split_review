## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PointACL, an attention-driven contrastive learning framework for point cloud understanding. It addresses the tendency of Transformer-based models to overlook under-attended regions by introducing an attention-driven dynamic masking strategy that selectively masks high-attention patches, forcing the model to learn from less prominent areas. It also adds a contrastive learning loss to the original pre-training objective. Experiments integrate PointACL with Point-MAE and PointGPT-S backbones on ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40, and ShapeNetPart. Reviewers strongly disagree: one reviewer (R3, score 8) found no critical weaknesses and praised the contribution, while three reviewers (R1,R2,R4, scores 3,3,5) identified significant limitations in novelty, experimental rigor, and generality.

### Strengths
- **Novel attention-driven dynamic masking**: Unlike fixed or random masking, PointACL adaptively masks high-attention regions, forcing the model to learn from under-attended areas (R1).
- **Well written and easy to follow**: The paper is clear, the motivation is reasonable, and the problem statement is well explained (R2,R3,R4).
- **Visualization of attended regions**: The plot showing that PointACL learns a more global understanding of the input point cloud is helpful and supports the claims (R3,R4).
- **Extensive robustness analysis**: Thorough experiments under Gaussian noise, rotation, scaling, and point dropout demonstrate robustness to perturbations (R1,R3).
- **Results show robust improvements**: The primary takeaway that focusing on less prominent patches yields robust results is supported (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No mathematical foundation explaining why masking high-attention regions leads to better global understanding, how the dynamic probability distribution affects learning, or what properties of the contrastive loss ensure better feature discrimination. This limits generalizability (R1).
2. **Limited innovation**: The method essentially generates an attention-guided masked point cloud and adds a contrastive loss to the original loss. The core ideas are incremental (R2).
3. **Marginal performance improvements**: Gains are small, especially for part segmentation (only 0.1 Ins. mIoU on ShapeNetPart) and few-shot learning (R1,R4). It is unclear whether improvements are statistically significant or due to additional 300-epoch pre-training, increased parameters, or the masking strategy itself (R2).
4. **Missing comparisons and not SOTA**: No comparison against recent state-of-the-art methods such as PointMamba (Liang et al., 2024), Point-FEMAE (Zha et al., 2024), or Point-GPT-L. Comparisons are limited to Point-MAE and PointGPT-S, and other masking strategies from recent works are omitted (R2). Additionally, PointACL does not achieve overall SOTA performance (R2).
5. **Generality not demonstrated**: The method is claimed to be plug-and-play for Transformer backbones, but experiments are only shown with Point-MAE and PointGPT-S. No results with PointBERT, Point-FEMAE, or Point-GPT-L (R2). It is also unclear whether the approach applies to simpler architectures like PointNet (R3).
6. **Missing efficiency analysis**: No comparison of computational cost or efficiency with baselines (R2).
7. **Ablation results raise concerns**: In Table 7, the dynamic high-attention mask only achieves the highest accuracy (92.3%) at mask ratio 0.6; all other settings yield lower and very close scores, questioning the consistency of the strategy (R4). In Table 4, the low-attention mask strategy sometimes outperforms Random Mask (e.g., on OBJ-ONLY), which lacks a reasonable explanation (R4).
8. **Unclear hyper-parameter selection**: It is not stated whether hyper-parameters (e.g., mask ratio R=0.6, probability temperature, contrastive loss weight) are fixed across all datasets or tuned per experiment (R3).
9. **Unclear explanation of Eq.5**: The second part of Equation 5 is not clearly explained; its role as a perturbation of the probability distribution is ambiguous (R3).
10. **Minor typo**: Line 245: “regzions” should be “regions” (R3).

*Note on disagreement*: One reviewer (R3) found no critical weaknesses and rated the paper highly (8). The other three reviewers (R1,R2,R4) rated the paper low (3,3,5) and raised the above points as major concerns.
