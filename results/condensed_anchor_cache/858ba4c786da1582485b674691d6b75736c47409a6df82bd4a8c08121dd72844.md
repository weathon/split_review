## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Semantically Diverse Language Generation (SDLG) to quantify predictive uncertainty in LLMs by generating semantically diverse yet likely alternatives for an initial text. SDLG uses importance sampling and a small NLI model to compute token-level attribution, substitution, and importance scores, then estimates semantic entropy. Experiments on TruthfulQA, CoQA, and TriviaQA with OPT models (6.7B, 13B, 30B) show SDLG outperforms baselines (multinomial sampling with semantic entropy from Kuhn et al. (2023), diverse beam search from Vijayakumar et al. (2018)) while being more computationally efficient. A minority reviewer (reviewer 3, score 8) is substantially more positive than the others (scores 5, 5, 6), praising simplicity and clarity; the other reviewers raise concerns about experimental clarity, missing baselines, ablation, and generalizability.

### Strengths

- SDLG provides an innovative and effective method for uncertainty quantification by considering semantic diversity rather than traditional sampling, addressing a key NLG challenge.
- The method is simple and clearly written; using a smaller NLI model for semantic assessment improves computational efficiency, making it practical.
- Evaluation setup closely follows Kuhn et al. (2023) on the same three datasets (TruthfulQA, CoQA, TriviaQA) and with the same open-access OPT models, allowing direct comparison and replication.
- The use of importance sampling to generate semantically diverse outputs is a strong methodological contribution.
- An additional baseline (diverse beam search, SE_DBS) is included, which does not require an external scoring function like NLI.
- The observation that fewer SDLG samples can approximate the performance of 10 generations is interesting and favors efficiency (results in appendix).
- Computational cost implications are discussed (lines 446-461).
- Unlike methods relying on optimal sampling temperature, SDLG does not require hyperparameter tuning.
- The empirical results across multiple datasets demonstrate SDLG’s effectiveness and efficiency.

### Weaknesses

- **NLI dependence and biases**: SDLG relies heavily on an NLI model for semantic equivalence; biases in the NLI model could skew uncertainty estimations, and the paper lacks discussion on handling or mitigating such biases. The dependency on an NLI model that shares the same tokenizer as the LLM may restrict applicability across different models with varied tokenizers.
- **Missing ablation of token scores**: The method uses three scores (attribution Ai, substitution Sij, importance Iij) but no ablation study investigates their individual or combined contributions. For example, what would results be with only (Ai and Sij) or (Ai, Iij)? The different similarity calculations (Euclidean for Ai, Cosine for Sij) are not motivated. Experiments testing different score combinations are needed.
- **Need for simpler semantic similarity comparison**: It is unclear whether the NLI model is necessary; a comparison with a simpler token-based similarity method (e.g., word2vec distances) would empirically justify the choice.
- **Insufficient discussion of competitive baseline**: SE_DBS is competitive with SDLG on TriviaQA (Table 1), but the paper does not sufficiently discuss this result or provide examples comparing SDLG and DBS generations (Table 3 only compares SDLG and multinomial sampling). A detailed analysis of where and why SDLG outperforms DBS is needed.
- **Experimental clarity**: The evaluation protocol is unclear, especially how ROUGE and BLEURT metrics were applied (L.409 requires citation, L.411-414 are confusing). The phrase “AUROC is used as a metric for classifying” is misleading; the connection between thresholding ROUGE/BLEURT for correctness and the AUROC calculation needs clarification. Results are mentioned without linking to result tables or figures (e.g., lines 432-433, 441-442).
- **Missing relevant baselines**: Previous uncertainty quantification methods, such as self-consistency with beam-search (Wang et al. 2022, Xie et al. 2024), are not included as baselines. The paper’s argument that the sampling method is complementary does not justify the lack of comparison, as the final uncertainty score calculation differs significantly from these works.
- **Limited model family**: Experiments are restricted to the OPT model family (2022), which is not among the most recent or widely used (e.g., LLaMA, Mistral, GPT). Expanding to newer models or discussing limitations/challenges for applying SDLG to them would strengthen generalizability.
- **Discrepancy between motivation and evaluation**: The paper motivates SDLG as a tool for detecting hallucinations, but the empirical evaluation focuses on uncertainty quantification on QA tasks and does not include any hallucination detection experiments (e.g., using the HADES dataset). This is misleading.
- **NLI model as a replacement for attention?**: The use of an extra NLI model to compute token contribution could potentially be replaced by attention score distributions; this alternative is not explored.
- **Incomplete results presentation**: Interesting results (e.g., impact of number of generations) are only in the appendix and not discussed in the main paper. The paragraph on “improper semantic entropy” (lines 437-440) is not explained. The meaning of “19% increase of semantic clusters” (line 441) is unclear.
- **The 30B OPT model often shows lower AUROC than the 13B model (Table 1)**: This is not discussed or compared with Kuhn et al.’s findings.
- **Method details**: The scoring mechanism and how token pairs are selected and replaced are not fully illustrated in the main paper (due to space constraints). The use of starting subwords only (not internal subwords) is mentioned in results (line 430-431) but should be explained in Section 4. Algorithms 1 and 2 would benefit from captions.
- **Example analysis questions**: In Table 3 examples, SDLG generations yield lower probabilities than multinomial sampling (e.g., one token difference "day" vs "month" causing a drop from 0.793 to 0.6x); reviewers sought explanation for why this occurs and what drives the key advantage of SDLG over DBS.
