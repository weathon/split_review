## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies multi-draft speculative sampling, where proposal sequences are sampled independently from different draft models. The optimal token-level selection scheme (maximizing acceptance probability) can be cast as a linear program. The authors show this optimal scheme decomposes into a two-step solution: first use importance sampling to select one intermediate token, then apply single-draft speculative sampling. For two identical draft models, they establish a necessary and sufficient condition for acceptance probability = 1 (Theorem 2) and an explicit expression for the optimal acceptance probability (Theorem 3). They also propose a truncated LP algorithm that solves the optimization faster than the standard O(n²) solution and a weighted importance-sampling-based scheme. Experiments on various tasks show consistent improvements in block efficiency and token rates over baselines like SpecInfer and SpecTr.

### Strengths
- Original and significant theoretical contribution: the decomposition, characterization of optimal acceptance probability for two drafts, and conditions for perfect acceptance are novel (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Theorems 2 and 3 stand out as key novel results (Reviewer 2, 3).
- The two-step scheme is simple and intuitive, making the approach easy to understand (Reviewer 4).
- The truncated LP algorithm is novel to speculative decoding and theoretically better than SpecTr (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental results are convincing and demonstrate consistent improvements in block efficiency and token rates across multiple datasets, decoding temperatures, and draft models (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Results appear sound with no obvious errors (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well written and easy to follow (Reviewer 2).  (Note: this contrasts with presentation concerns raised by Reviewers 1 and 3.)

### Weaknesses
- **Background exposition is insufficient**: Key concepts such as the acceptance probability and its role in speculative decoding are not clearly explained; readers unfamiliar with speculative decoding struggle to understand the paper and must consult the original speculative sampling paper (Reviewer 3).
- **Section 4 presentation is unclear**: The full linear program with all variables and constraints before truncation is not shown. The truncation procedure is not explained in detail, and the relationship between the full and truncated optimal solutions (including trade-offs) is missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Need a clear notation section** with distinct descriptions (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing experiments with target model temperature < 1.0**, which is more common in practice. Only temperature=1.0 is used for the target model; results for lower temperatures and how acceptance rate / block efficiency vary as target temperature changes from 0.0 to 1.0 are needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Experiments on additional benchmarks** (e.g., HumanEval, GSM8K, MT-bench) are needed for fair comparison (Reviewer 4).
- **Report real-world speedup** (e.g., tokens per second) to demonstrate practical effectiveness, not just block efficiency and token rates. Clarify what “token rate” means (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Truncated LP sorting complexity O(n log n) may impact speed**: Real-world time consumption should be reported and compared to vanilla multi-draft selection, especially for models with large vocab (e.g., Llama 3.1 with 128k vocab). For K > 2 the multi-stage scheme may also affect speedup (Reviewer 4).
- **Draft temperature choice in Figure 3** is not justified: experiments use draft temperatures > 1.0, but target temperature is typically ≤ 1.0, so draft temperatures ≤ 1.0 would be expected. Clarify how these draft temperatures were chosen (Reviewer 2).
- **Condition evaluation in Theorems 2/3** seems to require 2^{vocab size} computations – is there a faster way to evaluate these quantities? (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions on potential alternatives**: Are Sinkhorn methods applicable for approximate optimal transport? (Reviewer 2). Can the proposed truncated LP be combined with Medusa or Eagle? (Reviewer 4). Why was RRS (Recursive Speculative Decoding) not discussed or compared? (Reviewer 4).
- **Intuition for the conjecture in Remark 2** is not clear: why should the exponent over the sum of p-weights match K, and why wouldn’t lower-order terms appear? (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor**: Clarify early that “canonical architectures” refers to drafting methods, not neural architectures (Reviewer 2). Compare to results from the reference at https://openreview.net/forum?id=9KxnxWOBA5 (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 6) considers the contributions weak**, arguing that multi-draft sampling is not appropriate for speculative decoding because it performs poorly with batch size > 1, and suggests the authors should better emphasize contributions to real-world applications. (Other reviewers rate contribution as 4; this contrast should be preserved.)
- **Discuss limitations of the paper** (Reviewer 4).
