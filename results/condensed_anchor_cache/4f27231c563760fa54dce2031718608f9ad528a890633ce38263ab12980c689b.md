## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Highlighter module that computes explicit pixel-word correlations to enhance visual features for visual grounding, plus a cross-layer regularization loss to maintain alignment consistency. Reviewers acknowledge the problem importance and the efficiency of the module, but raise significant concerns about novelty, limited experimental validation, and incomplete comparisons. Reviewer 1 is positive (score 6, low confidence) and emphasizes lightweight design and thorough ablations. Reviewer 2 is moderately positive (score 5, high confidence) but notes limited technical innovation and missing analysis. Reviewer 3 is negative (score 3, high confidence) and argues that both main ideas are common, performance is not state-of-the-art, and the analysis is incomplete.

### Strengths
- **Lightweight and efficient**: The Highlighter module introduces minimal computational overhead while effectively incorporating cross-modality information (R1).
- **Backend compatibility**: The method consistently improves performance across various backbones (ResNet, Swin Transformer) (R1).
- **Thorough ablation studies**: Extensive experiments verify the efficacy and robustness of the Highlighter module (R1).
- **Important problem**: Visual grounding is an active and important field in vision and language (R2).
- **Good performance on multiple benchmarks**: The approach demonstrates strong results across a broad number of downstream tasks (R2).
- **Clear motivation and writing**: The motivation is well-written and easy to follow; Figure 1 helps readers understand the motivation (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty in pixel-word alignment**: The main idea of pixel-word alignment has been extensively explored in earlier works such as CoupAlign (NeurIPS 2022), ALBEF (NeurIPS 2021), RIS-DMMT (ICCV 2023), TRIS (ICCV 2023), and SegVG (ECCV 2024). The authors do not provide a comparison in effectiveness/efficiency to justify the need for the proposed Highlighter, nor do they extract a further takeaway beyond “pixel-word alignment” (R3).
- **Lack of novelty in cross-layer regularization**: The idea of using a bbox-to-binary mask with KL divergence for supervision is highly similar to AttBalance (Kang et al.) and SegVG (ECCV 2024), both of which also perform cross-layer supervision. The authors need to explain what specific aspect of the proposed loss is novel and whether it is better than previous similar approaches (R3). Note: AttBalance is also cited by R1 as a missing comparison.
- **Performance not state-of-the-art**: The paper lacks a comparison with SegVG (ECCV 2024), and the reported performance is consistently inferior to SegVG in a fair comparison (R3). The claim of state-of-the-art is therefore unsupported.
- **Limited training scope**: The models are trained on single datasets without joint training across visual grounding datasets or with other tasks (e.g., VQA, captioning), unlike recent works such as PolyFormer, MDETR, LLaVA-1.5, and Ferret. It remains unclear whether the proposed ideas are effective when scaling up training scope (R3). Reviewer 3 questions the meaningfulness of focusing on single-dataset training given that LLaVA-1.5 and Ferret achieve impressive results, and asks why the authors do not scale up to cover all VG datasets like MDETR and PolyFormer at similar parameter ranges (R3).
- **Incomplete analysis in Figure 1**: The K-means analysis intended to show that previous methods lack discriminative visual features omits results for key compared methods (e.g., VG-LAW, TransVG++, LUNA, LG-FPN, PVD, SegVG). A comprehensive analysis including recent methods (VG-LAW, SegVG, TransVG++) is necessary to support the claim that previous methods suffer from this problem (R3).
- **Limited technical innovation**: The layer-wise highlighter injection (cross-attention + word-wise attention) is only a minor modification to existing attention mechanisms such as VLTVG’s Visual-linguistic verification module and QR-Net’s Query-aware Dynamic Attention. Moreover, despite claims of explicit alignment, the method still relies on attention scores and thus remains implicit (R2).
- **Missing quantitative analysis of correlation loss**: The paper does not quantitatively analyze the effect of the cross-layer regularization loss, e.g., by showing layer-wise correlation map consistency with and without \(L_{cr}\) (R2).
- **Redundant writing**: The paper contains overlapping content, particularly between the abstract and introduction in their discussion of related research (R2).
- **Terminology issue**: The term “cross-layer regularization loss” is imprecise because the loss does not consider interactions between layers but simply sums individual differences between mask and correlation values at each layer independently (R2, question).
