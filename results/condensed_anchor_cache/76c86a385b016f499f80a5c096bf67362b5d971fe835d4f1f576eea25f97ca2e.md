## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DeMul (Description-free Multi-prompt Learning) for few-shot image classification. It distills knowledge from LLMs into learnable prompts by aligning their embeddings, avoiding explicit text descriptions. A weighted multi-prompt strategy captures prompt importance. Experiments on 11 datasets claim superior performance. One reviewer (R3, rating 8) finds the method novel and significant, while others (rating 6) note limitations in evaluation, interpretability, and practical gains.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-crafted, clear, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Description-free distillation is a novel, effective, and more cost-effective alternative to description-based methods (R1, R2, R3).
- Weighted multi-prompt learning effectively captures the relative importance of prompts and enhances model adaptability (R1, R5, R3).
- Extensive experiments across 11 datasets and comprehensive ablation studies demonstrate the method and its modules (R1, R3, R4).
- The approach avoids the need for text descriptions or specific prompt design while preserving rich semantics (R5, R3).
- The study on diverse semantics and the importance of multiple learnable prompts is well-motivated (R1).
- The method simplifies prompt engineering and improves reliability, with potential for other VLM tasks (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation**: The method is only tested on few-shot classification; missing comparisons on domain generalization, base-to-new generalization, and cross-dataset transfer, which are standard in recent works like CoCoOp, MaPLe, and GalLoP (R2). A reviewer also suggests open-vocabulary experiments (R1).
- **Negligible gains on many datasets**: On 6 of 11 datasets, DeMul's performance is negligible compared to GalLoP (R2, referencing Figure 3). This contradicts claims of consistent outperformance.
- **Mapping function pretraining and generalizability**: Pretraining requires a dataset covering all category names, limiting generalizability and introducing noise/bias (R2). The sensitivity to training data size, diversity, and distribution shift is not analyzed (R3). The choice of MLP mapping is unjustified; alternative strategies are not explored (R3).
- **Computational costs**: Training/inference time and memory are not compared to baselines. Multiple prompts may incur substantial cost, especially for many classes like ImageNet-1K (R1, R2).
- **Dismissing beneficial description variability**: Figure 1 treats GPT description variability as a limitation, but such diversity can help handle varied within-class images; dismissing it may reduce robustness (R4, R5).
- **Reduced interpretability**: Mapping prompts to the GPT embedding space loses human-interpretable structure; optimization relies on implicit semantic relationships (R4, R5).
- **Algorithm 1 not explained**: The training procedure is not referenced or described, leaving the step-by-step process unclear (R4, R5).
- **Equation (4) discrepancy**: The text says classification probabilities are averaged, but the implementation appears to average text embeddings (R4, R5).
- **Missing citations**: The Preliminaries and Background section lacks appropriate citations (R4, R5).
- **Hyperparameters not justified**: Fixed values for regularization weight and loss balance parameter lack ablation; sensitivity analysis for \(\alpha\) and random splits is missing (R2, R4, R5).
- **Related Work lacks clarity**: Limitations of existing methods and how DeMul addresses them are not clearly articulated (R4, R5).
- **Weights normalization contradiction**: The paper claims weights \(\sum_{j=1}^M w_{ij} = 1\), but Figure 5 appears to show sums exceeding 1 (R4, R5).
- **Marginal benefit of weighting**: In 16-shot setting (Table 1), weighted approach improves only 0.1% over unweighted (85.2 vs 85.3), raising doubts about practical impact (R4, R5).
- **Shared learnable vectors trade-off**: Using shared vectors for memory efficiency may limit class-specific capture; interference and alternatives (e.g., class-specific or hybrid) not explored (R3).
- **Lack of theoretical support**: No theoretical analysis explains why distillation works; only empirical results are provided (R3).
- **Notation issue**: Symbol \(t\) is used for both prompt embeddings (L198) and learnable prompt vectors (L202) (R1).
- **Algorithm 1, line 7**: Why do text embeddings \(g(c_i)\) go through the encoder again? (R1)
- **Prior work overlap**: Distilling LLM text to prompts and prompt weighting have been explored in recent works (e.g., [a], [b]); the paper should discuss and distinguish itself (R2).
