## Merged Review

### Summary

OpenRCA is a novel benchmark (335 failures, 68+ GB of telemetry logs/metrics/traces from three enterprise distributed systems) that evaluates LLMs on root cause analysis (RCA) of software failures. The paper also proposes RCA-agent, a multi-agent framework using code execution to help LLMs process telemetry data. Evaluation of six LLMs (Claude 3.5, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, DeepSeek V3) shows that even the best (Claude 3.5 with RCA-agent) solves only 11.34% of failures. Reviewers praise the dataset’s careful construction and the task’s importance, but raise concerns about limited system diversity, unrealistic oracle sampling, lack of domain-specific insight in the agent, missing ablation studies, and insufficient analysis of why models struggle.

### Strengths

- **Novel benchmark for a neglected phase:** OpenRCA pioneers LLM application to post-development RCA, a realistic and high-impact problem (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). One reviewer noted the benchmark is “clean and good dataset with clear structures” and “huge effort made towards ensuring fairness and denoising” (R4).
- **Rigorous data-cleaning process:** The construction includes system selection, data balancing, calibration, and query synthesis (R2). The dataset is carefully designed with emphasis on consistency and quality (R3).
- **Comprehensive experiments:** Evaluation spans six LLMs (both closed and open), three sampling strategies (Balanced, Oracle, RCA-agent), and includes breakdowns by system complexity and task difficulty (R1, R2). A detailed case study illustrates real-world applicability (R1).
- **Goal-driven task design:** RCA tasks are framed in a generalized natural-language query format (R2).
- **Useful analyses:** The paper provides analysis of reasoning length vs. accuracy (Figure 6), error tolerance (Figure 9), and performance correlation with system complexity (Table 5) (R1).
- **RCA-agent shows improvement:** Although limited in originality, the agentic approach increases accuracy from ~6% to 11.34%, motivating further research (R3). Two reviewers (R1, R3) found the overall paper well-written and organized; R4 also called the paper structure clear.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited system diversity:** Only three distributed software systems are included, which may prevent generalization to other architectures (monolithic, embedded) and introduce significant variance—performance could be overly influenced by these systems’ specific characteristics (R1, R2, R3, R4). R2 explicitly raises concern about generalizability; R1 notes the dataset “narrow focus on distributed systems.”
- **Oracle sampling is unrealistic:** The “golden set” identified by oracle sampling assumes perfect KPI selection, but in real life, understanding and selecting the correct KPIs is itself a key challenge (R4). Another reviewer (R3) argues that removing cases where root cause cannot be identified from given telemetry is a flaw—a real system should flag when data is insufficient.
- **Agent approach lacks domain-specific insight:** RCA-agent uses a generic planner-executor framework with domain knowledge limited to a 42-line rule. One reviewer (R4) found the agent “redundant and unnecessary” and notes the instruction given to baseline models is far less comprehensive than the agent’s prompt, raising fairness concerns. R3 called the agent’s originality limited as multi-agent systems with code execution are widely available.
- **Vanilla LLM evaluation is questionable:** Reviewers note that LLMs are not designed to handle the huge telemetry volume (R4), making direct evaluation on long-context input almost impossible without sampling. The performance of LLMs may be entirely bounded by the sampling strategy, not by RCA ability (R4). Missing ablation study: the benchmark does not disentangle long-input retrieval from non-natural language understanding from software problem identification (R2). Suggested: evaluate on simplified input (e.g., only logs or only traces) (R2).
- **Lack of error bars and variance reporting:** Results are reported as median without any measure of variance (R3). Given the stochastic nature of LLMs and agents, this makes it hard to assess robustness. One reviewer (R2) questions whether Figure 6 actually supports the claim “accuracy increases with more reasoning steps,” as accuracy varies significantly across models and reasoning lengths.
- **Superficial analysis of low performance:** The paper states models “can only handle the simplest cases” but does not deeply analyze why—whether due to context window limits, heterogeneous data, or complex dependencies (R1). Missing error analysis with concrete failure examples (R1). R2 also notes the benchmark does not isolate the difficulties introduced by long-form telemetry vs. the core RCA reasoning.
- **Insufficient contextualization:** The claim that software failures cost “billions of dollars” lacks specific references or citations (R1). The literature review on existing RCA benchmarks (e.g., MIMIC in healthcare, other software engineering frameworks) is inadequate (R1).
- **Scaling and update challenges:** Data collection is highly human-intensive (manual oracle sampling), making it difficult to update and scale; frequent updates are crucial to prevent data leakage in LLM benchmarks (R2).
- **Missing broader impact & ethics discussion:** No discussion of consequences of incorrect RCA in critical systems, or how OpenRCA could improve maintenance workflows (R1).
- **Clarity issues:** Complex tables/figures lack adequate explanation (e.g., Table 2, Figure 5) and crucial details are relegated to appendices (R1). Spelling errors: “post-development phrases” (line 035) (R2), “Partitial” in Table 2, “Accuray” in Table 3 (R4). CSV files in artifact contain excessive newlines, making them confusing (R4).
- **Questions that indicate missing experiments or comparisons:**
  - How were failure cases selected? (R1) No criteria (frequency, severity) are provided.
  - Why was element count chosen as the primary difficulty criterion, given system complexity varies? (R2)
  - Is the benchmark for LLMs or agents? Why were specialized RCA tools/agents not tested? (R4)
  - Does the 42-line rule generalize to other cases? (R4)
  - Does ensemble of LLMs improve performance? (R3)
  - For the “Hard” category, why do all models get 0% even with the agent? (R3)
  - How does performance scale with system size/complexity? (R3)
  - Which Llama3.1-instruct model size was used? (R3) What does “drop ratio” mean in Table 4? (R3)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R4, rating 5) was substantially more critical, especially of the agent’s novelty and the value of evaluating vanilla LLMs, while two reviewers (R1 and R3, both rating 8) were positive about the benchmark’s contribution. R2 (rating 6) raised concerns about generalizability and disentanglement of abilities.
