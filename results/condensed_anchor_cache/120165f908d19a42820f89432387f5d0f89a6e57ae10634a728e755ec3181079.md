## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper revisits FedExProx and provides a refined convergence analysis for non-strongly convex quadratic problems that accounts for computation and communication costs—showing it can provably outperform vanilla GD. It extends the analysis to partial participation, adaptive extrapolation, and functions satisfying the PL condition. However, the focus on quadratics, synthetic-only experiments, and several practical concerns (detectability of parameters, stepsize practicality, missing noise) raise doubts about significance and generality. Reviewer 2 is more positive (rating 6) but note low confidence (1); reviewers 1 and 3 are more critical.

### Strengths
- The analysis is solid and improves over previous FedExProx results, with refined convergence guarantees for quadratics and extensions to partial participation and PL condition.
- Practical considerations of communication and computation lags are explicitly modeled and reflected in the performance characterization.
- A concrete diagonal quadratic case is presented to illustrate the improvement over GD.
- The paper is clearly written and well-structured.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope limited to quadratic problems** – The analysis relies on linear gradients (quadratic case) and does not generalize; the significance is undermined without stronger motivation for why quadratics are sufficient (reviewers 1, 3).
- **Claimed outperformance vs. GD depends on additional parameters (μ, τ)** – It is unclear whether the communication parameter μ is easily detectable in practice, and τ assumes uniform computation cost across devices, ignoring heterogeneity. More discussion is needed to justify these assumptions (reviewer 1, skeptical).
- **Stepsize impractical in Theorem 6.1** – The stepsize choice requires knowledge of the exact Moreau envelope or its lower bound, which is impractical (reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparison with FedAvg** – For quadratic functions, Woodworth et al. show that FedAvg achieves optimal convergence; the paper does not compare FedExProx to FedAvg or explain what additional benefit FedExProx provides (reviewer 3).
- **Closed-form proximal operator available but not used** – In quadratic problems the proximal operator has a closed-form (Eq. (22) in Appendix C); the paper instead uses GD as a subroutine, which is questionable and undermines the motivation for the algorithm’s complexity (reviewer 1, question re: section 4.1).
- **No sample noise considered** – The analysis does not account for stochastic noise from data sampling, nor does it reflect the cost of computing the prox operation under noise (reviewer 3).
- **PL extension lacks clear applicability** – While extending analysis to PL functions is nice, it is not illustrated in an interesting or standard case, leaving its relevance unclear (reviewer 3).
- **Confusing parameter L_{S,γ}** – The parameter should presumably increase with S (like in randomized coordinate descent) but this is not clarified (reviewer 3, question).
- **Experiments are only on synthetic data** – No experiments on realistic datasets are included; this weakens the empirical support (reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 1 specifically requests adding vanilla GD for validation.
- **Minority concern: low confidence** – One reviewer (rating 6) gave only confidence level 1, indicating the positive assessment is not certain (reviewer 2).
