## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents SALMONN-omni, a codec-free, full-duplex speech large language model (LLM) for streaming speech understanding and generation. It extends the SALMONN model using continuous embeddings (not discrete tokens), cross-attention (instead of self-attention), a streaming Mamba encoder, a text LLM, and a VoiceCraft-based streaming TTS module. A two-stage "understand then generate" training strategy is used, with a novel "thinking" step to align frame rates. Evaluated on ASR, spoken QA, zero-shot TTS, speech enhancement (SE), dereverberation, and target speaker extraction (TSE), and turn-taking. Results are reported on a subset of LibriHeavy.

### Strengths
- End-to-end speech LLMs for unified understanding and generation, especially full-duplex operation, is a very hot and important problem.
- Novel use of continuous embeddings instead of discrete speech tokens avoids some difficulties of neural codec representations.
- Cross-attention (rather than self-attention) in the TTS module is a novel architectural choice for streaming; this also avoids jointly modeling two modalities in a single sequence.
- Streaming capabilities for both input and output are important for real-time applications.
- Use of a streaming variant of the Mamba encoder is a novel component.
- A large amount of engineering effort is evident.
- The model is evaluated across several tasks (ASR, spoken QA, zero-shot TTS, SE, dereverberation, TSE, turn-taking), demonstrating practical viability.
- Ablations show effectiveness of the two-stage training strategy and the "thinking" step.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation clarity is poor across the paper** – many explanations are insufficiently detailed, inconsistent, or missing; abbreviations (e.g., SE, TSE) are not defined until the Conclusion; figures lack explanation (e.g., Figure 3 dashed diagonal lines); there are grammatical errors and typos (e.g., line 187 "streaming increased input", line 304 "spilts", line 355 "LoRA" should be "LLM"). The model, architecture, and loss functions are described conceptually instead of with equations, making it hard to follow.
- **No baseline comparisons are provided**, making results difficult to interpret. Missing comparisons include: cascaded systems (e.g., Qwen2 with VoiceCraft); other full-duplex speech LLMs (e.g., Moshi, with pros/cons contrasted); traditional methods for SE/dereverberation/TSE; tokenized speech LLMs for latency/accuracy; head-to-head of cross‑attention vs. self‑attention; head-to-head of continuous embeddings vs. discrete tokens.
- **ASR results fall well behind existing streaming baselines** – on LibriSpeech test_clean the reported 6.0% WER vs. 2.8% [Moritz et al., ICASSP 2020] and on test_other 10.5% vs. 7.3%.
- **Lack of reproducibility** – details missing for: training data generation for SE, dereverberation, and TSE; text LLM initialization (pre-trained? which checkpoint?); hyperparameters (e.g., LoRA rank, number of GPUs, training budget); overall model size; demo link.
- **Insufficient ablation studies** – no analysis of: Mamba vs. other autoregressive encoder architectures; impact of the “thinking” token (compare with removing it); impact of different latency settings (look-ahead length); comparison of cross‑attention vs. self‑attention; comparison of continuous embeddings vs. discrete tokens.
- **No system latency results** are reported for the streaming framework, which is essential to evaluate full‑duplex operation.
- **“Codec-free” claim is misleading** – the TTS module (VoiceCraft) generates codec codes, so codec‑free only applies to the encoder/speech understanding path.
- **The “thinking” token is similar to empty/blank tokens** in Seide et al. (2024) “Speech ReaLLM – Real-time Streaming Speech Recognition with Multimodal LLMs by Teaching the Flow of Time”; that work should be cited.
- **Evaluation metrics are unclear or insufficient** – turn-taking accuracy is not defined with an equation; success rate for spoken QA (judged by GPT-4o-mini) is not properly described (cite or formula); UTMOS (developed for TTS) is used for SE/dereverberation/TSE without justification; “MOS” in Table 3 should not refer to DNN-based MOS; original speech performance is missing from SE/dereverberation/TSE results; no intelligibility test is reported for generated speech (e.g., for zero‑shot TTS, only quality and similarity scores are given).
- **Tables need better description** – Table 2 “Strategy 1” and “Strategy 2” are not defined in the text; evaluation sets for Tables 2 and 4 are not specified (assumed LibriHeavy but should be stated).
- **VoiceCraft selection** is not justified compared to other streaming TTS alternatives.
