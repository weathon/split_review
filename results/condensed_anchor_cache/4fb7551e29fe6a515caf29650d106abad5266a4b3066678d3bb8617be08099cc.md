## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Lower Expectile Q-learning (LEQ), a model-based offline RL method that uses lower expectile regression of λ-returns to obtain low-bias, conservative Q-value estimates. LEQ is simple, easy to implement, and introduces little computational overhead. It significantly outperforms prior model-based offline RL methods on long-horizon tasks (e.g., D4RL AntMaze) and matches state-of-the-art model-free and sequence modeling approaches on dense-reward tasks (NeoRL, D4RL) and pixel-based tasks (V-D4RL). Experimental evaluation is thorough, with ablation studies confirming the importance of lower expectile regression, λ-returns, and critic training on offline data. All four reviewers rated the paper 6. Reviewer 4 is notably more critical of the method’s novelty and significance, while reviewers 1 and 3 praise its innovation.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and effectiveness**: LEQ is a simple, easy-to-implement algorithm with low computational complexity, yet it shows substantial performance advantages across a range of offline RL tasks.
- **Clear motivation and explanation**: The method is well-motivated, the algorithm description is clear, and the paper is generally well-written and easy to follow.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: LEQ is evaluated on a wide variety of tasks, including dense- and sparse-reward environments, state and image inputs (V-D4RL), and long-horizon tasks (larger AntMaze). The authors ensure fair comparisons with a wide range of baselines and provide extensive ablation studies to justify each component.
- **In-depth analysis**: The paper provides both experimental and theoretical analysis of LEQ’s behavior, including an analysis of instability on certain tasks that hints at future research directions.
- **Innovative approach over value penalization**: Unlike traditional value penalization methods, LEQ innovatively uses low expectile regression to alleviate inaccurate model data estimation without requiring uncertainty estimation.
- **Strong baseline significance**: The quality and thoroughness of the work suggest LEQ will provide a strong, computationally fast baseline for future model-based offline RL research.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees**: The paper does not provide theoretical analysis of how low expectile regression impacts the final Q-values of model data, nor does it offer theoretical guarantees for policy improvement (cf. COMBO, MOBILE).
- **Missing distinction from prior expectile-based method**: Expectile regression was already applied to model-based offline RL in IQL-TD-MPC [missing correct citation in Section 5.3]. This method appears as a baseline only for AntMaze results but not in other tables (2,3,4) and is not properly distinguished in the Related Work. The authors should explain the main difference between LEQ and IQL-TD-MPC and include this in the paper.
- **Hyperparameter per environment**: The use of different τ values per environment (Table 10) limits generality, though the results for all expectiles in the appendix are appreciated.
- **Potential error in Theorem 1 proof** (Reviewer 2): The last inequality may be in the wrong direction; the proof seems to require $E[W^τ(Y>X)] ≥ 1-τ$, but $τ ≤ E[W^τ(⋅)] ≤ 1-τ$ for $0<τ≤0.5$.
- **Missing reference to Figure 2**: Figure 2 is not referenced in the paper; it should be inserted to better explain the method.
- **Ensemble world model sampling not specified**: How is the ensemble of world models used for sampling? E.g., random selection per step or per rollout?
- **No analysis of TT and TAC baselines**: The paper includes Trajectory Transformer (TT) and TAC as baselines but provides no analysis or discussion of LEQ’s comparison to them. Why were they included and what can be learned?
- **Policy update uses only model data**: Why does policy training use only model-generated data and not the precise offline data, when most model-based offline RL algorithms use both? (Reviewer 3)
- **Question on deterministic vs. stochastic policy**: Why was deterministic policy gradient chosen for policy update? Are there experimental differences between stochastic and deterministic policies? (Reviewer 1)
- **BC pretraining impact**: Algorithm 1 pretrains the policy with BC on offline data, which is not typical in MOPO or MOBILE. How does this affect results, especially on AntMaze? (Reviewer 1)
- **Uniform scaling of model data errors**: The method scales all overestimated model data loss by τ without considering the varying degree of error among model data. (Reviewer 3)
- **Clarification on gradient derivation** (Reviewer 4): On line 256, what is meant by “unnormalized version of the expectile”? On line 258 vs 266, the surrogate loss approximation seems contradictory; please clarify.
- **Independent hyperparameter confusion**: In Eq. (7), are β and β_EMA independent hyperparameters? If so, using a different symbol for β_EMA would be less confusing.
- **Missing computational cost comparison**: A comparison of run-time/computational cost between LEQ and baselines would be helpful, given LEQ’s claimed efficiency.
- **Failure case analysis**: In Section 5.3, authors suggest increasing world model ensembles to address failures (passing through walls). LEQ uses a single world model – is this correct? Do the authors have evidence that an ensemble would help? (Reviewer 4)
- **τ for variance reduction**: In Section 5.4, does reducing τ (more conservative estimates) address the issue of performance variance from optimistic prediction?
- **Placement of Limitations section**: Section 6.1 on Limitations seems out of place before the conclusion; it might be better moved earlier (e.g., as an additional ablation with a minimal termination predictor). (Reviewer 4)
- **Possible error in Eq. (1) definition** (Reviewer 3): In Eq. (4), $L_2^τ(u) = ‖τ - 𝕀(u>0)‖u^2$ may be incorrect; please verify.
- **Third loss term in Eq. (7)**: Does the addition of the third loss term affect final performance? Simple experiments could analyze this. (Reviewer 3)
- **MBPO vs Dyna classification**: In Table 8, the authors classify MBPO and Dyna as different schemes, but prior works (including MOBILE’s original paper) treat MBPO as a Dyna-style algorithm. Please clarify the distinction. (Reviewer 2)
