## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Centrality Graph Shift Operators (CGSOs) that normalize adjacency matrices by global centrality metrics (PageRank, k-core, fixed-length walk count), analyze their spectral properties, and integrate them into GCN and GATv2 for node classification. The four reviewers gave scores 6, 3, 3, 3, with one positive reviewer (R1, confidence 4) and three negative reviewers (R2, R3, R4, confidences 4,4,5). R1 finds the method effective and practical, while R2, R3, and R4 raise significant concerns about limited novelty, insufficient comparisons, theoretical disconnect, high computational cost, and inconsistent performance.

### Strengths
- **Clear and well‑written**: R1, R3, and R4 praise the clarity, structure, and accessible explanation of CGSOs, especially the background on GSOs and centrality (R1, R3, R4).
- **Interesting and understudied problem**: R2 notes that the role of normalization in GNNs is an understudied question, making the topic valuable.
- **Effective method with promising results** (minority positive view from R1): R1 states that CGSOs show promising clustering improvements and enhance GNN accuracy with low computational overhead during training (pre‑calculation may take time). R1 also highlights the thorough hyperparameter search and the insight into the advantage of negative loops.
- **Rigorous theoretical analysis**: R1 commends the spectral analysis and proofs. R3 and R4 also note that the spectral clustering analysis is good and comprehensive (R3: “good analysis via spectral clustering”; R4: “comprehensive theoretical and empirical analysis”).
- **Table 1**: R2 points out that Table 1 demonstrates that choosing the right normalization can positively influence performance.
- **Discussion of centrality‑exponents‑clustering**: R2 finds the relationship between centrality metrics, exponents, and clustering results in Section 4 interesting.
- **Integration of local and global structure**: R4 notes that CGSOs integrate both local and global structural information, which is promising for clustering and classification.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient comparison with state‑of‑the‑art**: R2, R3, and R4 all argue that the paper compares only with old baselines (GCN, GATv2) and omits recent strong methods. R4 specifically calls for comparisons with GCNII, GPRGNN, BernNet, UniFilter. R2 asks for spectral methods like ChebNet/BernNet and suggests comparing with Graph Transformers (GraphGPS). R3 adds that the paper does not compare with PGSO (Dasoulas et al. 2021) despite claiming to generalize it, nor with other parameterized GSO works: Directional Graph Networks (Beani et al. 2021), ACM (Luan et al. 2022), PEGN (Wang et al. 2022), 1‑hop CKGConv (Ma et al. 2024). R1 (minority) also notes limited comparisons with strong baselines, especially those using global structure.
- **Theoretical disconnect between analysis and experiments**: R2 points out that the spectral analysis focuses on the Markov averaging operator (line 192), while the experimentally used CGSO (Eq. 4) only matches when \(m_1, e_3, m_3 = 0\). The paper does not provide a more inclusive characterization, which is possible (e.g., arXiv:2101.10050). R2 also questions the validity and significance of theoretical claims: Proposition 3.1 is mostly straightforward linear algebra; Proposition 3.2’s mean eigenvalue expression seems incorrect because the Markov averaging operator has zero diagonal (trace zero), so the mean should be zero (unless self‑loops are considered, and the subscript \(\phi\) is undefined). R2 asks about the significance of knowing the mean/std of eigenvalues and the implications of Theorem 3.4.
- **Parameterized CGSO not novel**: R2 notes that the parameterization in Eq. 4 arises from arXiv:2101.10050 by replacing degree normalization with centrality, and the similar labeling suggests the authors are aware of that earlier work.
- **Inconsistent performance across CGSOs and datasets**: R1 observes that no single centrality metric consistently outperforms others; often a simple degree GSO beats the more complex CGSOs, making the choice dataset‑dependent and complicating practical application. R3 adds that 5/8 datasets are heterophily, and on homophily graphs (CiteSeer, PubMed) CGSOs do not significantly outperform GCN with \(\hat{A}\). R4 notes performance is comparable with basic GCN.
- **High computational cost**: R4 states that computing PageRank has quadratic complexity, making CGSOs excessively expensive for large graphs. R3 asks for a memory/runtime comparison with PPNP/APPNP (Gasteiger et al. 2019) to demonstrate the trade‑off. R1’s **question** about the out‑of‑memory issue on Deezer‑Europe (smaller than arxiv‑year) and request for device specs overlaps with this concern.
- **Large number of hyperparameters**: R1 and R4 both note that Eq. 4 introduces many scalar parameters, making hyperparameter optimization challenging.
- **Potential bias in evaluation**: R1 points out that only two‑layer GNNs are used, limiting local power while CGSOs are global. R2 adds that the GSO for GCN is included without self‑loops, which is not the standard matrix used in GCN (asks if this is a typo). R4 asks whether the splits for Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed match the original GCN paper, and why the reported GCN accuracy is lower.
- **Missing demonstration of key advantage**: R3 points out that the paper claims CGSOs preserve original connectivity, yet provides no empirical demonstration (e.g., memory/runtime) to justify this advantage over methods like PPNP/APPNP that also globalize information.
- **Incorrect claim about message passing**: R2 states that the claim that Eq. s (1)–(2) encompass all message‑passing models is false because the message function does not depend on node features; also Eq. 2 appears to describe channel mixing, not an update step.
- **Superfluous introductory material**: R2 considers the historical remarks on matrix representations in the introduction to be unnecessary and suggests using the space for more relevant content.
- **Minor**: R1 notes a typo on line 471: “Singless” should be “Signless”.
- **Questions folded as Weaknesses**:  
  * CGATv2 implementation details (R1): how attention scores combine with CGSOs?  
  * Comparison with positional/structural encodings (e.g., Laplacian, random walks, GraphGPS) (R1).  
  * Applicability to polynomial‑based GNNs (R4): can CGSOs be used with such methods, and how does performance compare?  
  * R2’s questions about the mean in Theorem 3.2, implications of Theorem 3.4, and which matrix is used in GCN (renormalized or not) all overlap with theoretical validity and experiment setup concerns.
