## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers largely agree the paper tackles the novel problem of maximum likelihood estimation for multivariate hypergeometric distributions when both total population and category sizes are unknown. The core method replaces discrete combinatorial terms with continuous Gamma-function relaxations and adds a violation penalty to enforce constraints. The approach is validated on simulated data (small K=3 categories, then large >1000) and extended to a variational autoencoder for single-cell genomics. The paper is generally well-written and clearly presented, with an application that is conceptually appealing (UMI counts as hypergeometric draws). However, reviewers express substantial concerns about theoretical justification, experimental design, missing baselines, and practical applicability.

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly written, easy to read, and provides necessary background (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The problem is interesting and important, especially for single-cell genomics (R1, R3, R4).
- The idea of treating individual transcripts in scRNA-seq as hypergeometric draws is conceptually clean; Figure 6 illustrates this point well (R4).
- The method recovers the true number of transcripts in a practical genomics use case (R1).
- It is surprising that this estimation challenge has not been addressed before (R2).
- The contribution is clearly stated; experiments are illustrative (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical justification is lacking.** The continuous relaxation (replacing factorials with Gamma functions) is known and simple, but there is no analysis of how close the relaxed likelihood is to the discrete one, or whether it defines a proper probability distribution for non-integer \(N_k\) (R1, R4). One reviewer notes that the method’s viability depends heavily on \(N\gg 1\) but this is not justified (R1).
- **Claimed concavity of Equation (4) is incorrect.** A reviewer shows, with a counterexample, that the negative log-likelihood is not concave (the sum of log-Gamma and negative log-Gamma yields a sum of convex and concave functions) and that Figure 1 implies non-convexity. This undermines the optimization justification (R4).
- **Experiments are insufficient and lack baselines.** The paper relies solely on empirical simulations; no comparison against related methods (e.g., other hyper-parameter optimization techniques, Bayesian nonparametric methods, or simpler with-replacement estimators) is provided (R1, R3). The VAE application is not benchmarked against any other approach (R1). Errors in Figures 2 and 3 fluctuate non-monotonically with number of trials, suggesting noise, yet no error bars or replicates are shown (R4). The good performance in those figures appears to rely on a large number of trials; with a single trial, the MLE would collapse to observed counts, making the approach unsensible for individual points—this becomes problematic in the VAE setting where per-observation likelihood is weak (R4).
- **Gap in experimental range.** There is a jump from 3 categories to >1000; intermediate numbers (e.g., 100) are not tested, nor is the behavior as a function of both \(n_{\max}\) and number of samples explored (R2). The study uses knowledge of the number of underlying distributions (e.g., 2 in Section 5); how would the method work for an unknown or larger number? (R3).
- **Motivation and experimental design are questionable.** The method requires repeated independent sampling without replacement from the same population (i.e., sample, return all, resample, etc.). This is contrasted with capture-recapture but essentially replicates the same scenario with more than two rounds; no realistic real-world examples of such an experimental design are provided (R4). The paper mentions recommender systems and language applications but does not develop or demonstrate them (R2).
- **Second part (VAE) is poorly motivated.** It is unclear what the ultimate goal is when estimating category sizes per observation from a mixture of distributions; the explanation of low-rank structure is vague—it merely says the data can be described by a small set of latent patterns, without specifying the mechanism, the impact of latent dimension, or practical implications (R3). The likelihood equations (3) and (4) appear inconsistent with the description at the end of page 3; notation fails to distinguish parameters from observations (R3).
- **Notation and editorial issues.** Equation (1) should have \(c_2\) instead of \(n_2\) (R2, R4). In Equation (7), \(c_i\) should be defined as the largest category count over T samples, not just the maximum draw (R2). Equation (8) is sloppy, defining \(\hat{N}_i\) in terms of itself (R4). In Section 4, \(N_2\) should be 30 (R3). “to elongated along the line of correct” should be “as a line along the correct” (R4).
- **Implementation choices not justified.** The violation penalty \(C_{\text{viol}}\) is used instead of simple bounded optimization (the constraint set is convex and the lower bound is easily computed from data; projected gradient descent would be straightforward) (R4). It is not explained why the relaxation requires \(N\gg 1\) (R1).
- **Missing connections to related work.** The method could be applied to non-central hypergeometric distributions as in Sutter et al. (2020), but this is not explored (R2). The relationship to other hyper-parameter estimation methods (e.g., beta-Binomial or Dirichlet-Multinomial models) is not discussed (R1). The reviewer asks whether similar Gamma relaxations have been used before in estimation contexts (R2).

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R2, confidence 4, rating 6) was more positive, finding the contribution good, the experiments illustrative, and the method a clear extension. The other three reviewers (R1, R3, R4, ratings 5 each, confidences 2–4) were more critical, pointing to the lack of theoretical grounding, missing baselines, and concerns about experimental design and applicability. The above Weaknesses capture the consensus skeptical view, while the minority more favorable opinion is reflected in the Strengths section.
