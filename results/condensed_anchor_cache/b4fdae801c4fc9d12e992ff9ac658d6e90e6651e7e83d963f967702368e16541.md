## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Hierarchically-structured Tree Distance (HTD), a differentiable and modular metric for rooted heterogeneous trees (e.g., JSON, XML). HTD generalizes Tree Mover’s Distance by replacing optimal transport with Hausdorff/Chamfer distances and uses weight parameters to control subspace importance. Experiments on classification, clustering, anomaly detection, and visualization show HTD competitive with neural network methods with far fewer parameters and superior to distance-based baselines. Reviewer opinions are mixed: some find the paper well-written and the approach effective (scores 5,5), while others have concerns about novelty, theoretical depth, missing related work (hyperbolic methods), lack of experiments on motivating data formats, insufficient analysis of limitations and variance, and presentation clarity (scores 3,3).

### Strengths

- The paper studies a natural and important problem: defining differentiable, modular metrics for hierarchically-structured data like JSON, XML, ProtoBuffer. (R1, R2, R4)
- HTD generalizes the Tree Mover’s Distance (TMD) and applies to both heterogeneous graphs and tree-structured data. (R3)
- Extensive experiments across classification, clustering, anomaly detection, and UMAP visualization show HTD’s competitiveness with neural network methods while using orders of magnitude fewer parameters, and superiority over other distance-based methods for tree-structured data. (R1, R2, R3)
- Some reviewers found the paper well-written and easy to follow. (R2, R3)
- Reviewer 2 notes theoretical superiority demonstrated in Table 1.
- The proposed distance is a natural candidate with demonstrated benefits over prior methods. (R1)
- The approach is modular, combining potentially different metrics on different levels of the tree, and the weight parameters are differentiable. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and contribution concerns:** HTD appears as a straightforward extension of TMD, replacing optimal transport with Hausdorff/Chamfer distances, yielding cheaper complexity but limited novelty. (R3; R2 also questions contribution scope, though not an expert.)
- **Theory is very straightforward:** Theorem 1 and 2 could be observations or propositions as they follow from basic definitions. (R1) No proof for Theorem 1 is provided; the justification is hard to follow. (R4)
- **Missing related work:** No citation or discussion of hyperbolic space methods for hierarchical data (e.g., Nickel et al. “Poincaré embeddings”, Chami et al., Monath et al.), which are also differentiable and address similar optimization-over-trees problems. (R1) Reviewer 1 asks for a comparison with these methods and whether there is hope to prove something about the quality of the metric found.
- **No experiments on motivating data formats:** The introduction motivates the distance using JSON, XML, ProtoBuffer, but the experiments use graph datasets and synthetic tasks, not those formats. (R4)
- **Lack of comprehensive limitation discussion:** Sensitivity of HTD to weight parameters for sub-trees, potential instability/bias, and computational complexity not thoroughly analyzed for large, complex trees. Limitations of bag distance for highly diverse or large sets of sub-trees not addressed. (R2)
- **Unclear why HTD outperforms TMD on homogeneous graph datasets** like MUTAG and BZR (Table 3). (R3) Additionally, on heterogeneous datasets, could potentially improve TMD by constructing separate computational trees for each node type. (R3)
- **HTD does not define a valid kernel** (not conditionally negative definite), similar to TMD. (R3)
- **Missing standard deviations** in Tables 3 and 4; likely large variance (e.g., STD ~5 for MUTAG and BZR). (R3, R4)
- **Claim of differentiability lacks theoretical proof.** (R4)
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Motivation and explanation of HS-Trees, sample, schema, and relation to GNNs is confusing; unclear if term HS-Trees is from prior work or newly introduced. (R4)
  - The relation between Eq. (5) and the distances in Table 2 is unclear; a simple proof or derivation needed. (R4)
  - The relation between HTD and TMD is unclear; a simple proof or derivation needed. (R4)
  - The README.md in provided code is insufficient to reproduce HTD and experimental results. (R4)
  - Experimental setup unclear: actual classification/anomaly detection tasks not specified, Appendix A implementation details not referenced in main text. (R4)
  - Colors in Figure 3 and meaning of “—” in Tables 3-5 unexplained. (R4)
  - Proofreading issues: extra parenthesis in line 029, missing period and extra parenthesis in line 250, confusing notation. (R4)
  - Mutagenesis dataset first mentioned without reference or introduction (line 317). (R4)
- **Effectiveness relative to GNN classifiers:** While HTD outperforms other distance-based methods, it does not appear comparable to GNN classifiers in the experiments. (R2)
- Reviewer 4 asks for an illustrative figure showing the relation between HS-Trees and GNNs.
