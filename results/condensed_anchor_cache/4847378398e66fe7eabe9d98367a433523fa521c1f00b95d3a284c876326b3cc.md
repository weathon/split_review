## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents CityAnchor, a two-stage multi-modal LLM for 3D visual grounding in city-scale point clouds. The coarse localization stage projects the point cloud onto a 2D map and uses LLaVA to regress region-of-interest candidates, then SAM generates a heatmap. The fine-grained matching stage uses another LLaVA to compare candidates against the text description and select the best match. Experiments on CityRefer and a newly synthesized CityAnchor dataset show substantial improvements over prior methods. Reviewers praise the well-structured coarse-to-fine design and strong results, but raise concerns about the limited filtering ability of the coarse module, reliance on a closed-set segmentation model, missing technical details, dataset necessity, and overall novelty. One reviewer judged the work more positively (score 8, confidence 4), while the other three gave scores of 6 (confidence 3–4). The minority reviewer found the method well motivated and technically sound, while others noted the approach builds on existing multi-stage frameworks without fundamental new concepts.

### Strengths
- **Well-structured coarse-to-fine design**: The two-stage LLM pipeline (coarse localization on a 2D projection, then fine-grained matching) is highly intuitive for efficiently handling large-scale scenes (R2). It is well motivated and technically sound (R1).
- **Impressive experimental results**: CityAnchor achieves substantial and objective improvements over existing methods on both CityRefer and the new CityAnchor dataset (R1, R2, R3, R4). The ablation study is highly analytical for each module and feature representation (R2).
- **Clear practical potential**: The method shows promise for applications in urban planning and geospatial analysis (R3).
- **Strong integration of spatial context and object features**: The approach effectively addresses the complexity of urban environments, setting it apart from methods that do not handle such challenges (R4). The creation of a new synthetic benchmark (CityAnchor dataset) provides a valuable resource for future studies (R4, though necessity questioned – see Weaknesses).
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The paper clearly states objectives, methodology, and results (R1, R4). However, one reviewer noted vague sentences and grammatical errors (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited effectiveness of Coarse Localization Module (CLM)**:
    - Multiple reviewers question whether CLM actually filters irrelevant regions. As shown in Fig.4, the heatmap highlights almost all urban areas (roads, houses), suggesting it may only distinguish urban from non-urban terrain rather than prune viable candidates (R2, R4). This raises doubt about the claimed computational efficiency gain, as the fine-grained matching module may still need to process many candidate regions (R2).
    - The need for manual adjustment of the RoI threshold in the coarse stage indicates a lack of generalizability (R3).
- **Dependence on a pre-trained, closed-set 3D segmentation model**:
    - The pipeline first segments the point cloud using a pretrained model that only recognizes a closed set of objects. The paper does not discuss CityAnchor’s generality to unknown/unseen object categories (R1).
    - Performance is intrinsically bounded by the granularity and accuracy of that segmentation; errors propagate to the grounding results (R4).
- **2D mapping limitations**:
    - Projecting 3D space onto a 2D map loses spatial information, especially under occlusions or overlapping instances. The CLM assumes objects are nearly flat, limiting applicability to complex environments (e.g., indoor spaces or dense urban areas like NYC) (R4).
    - The method does not address dynamic objects (e.g., moving vehicles, pedestrians), which could affect reliability (R3).
- **Missing technical details** – several reviewers note insufficient descriptions:
    - What is \(T_c\)? (R1, line 157)
    - How is it determined whether an object has a landmark name? (R1, lines 138 & 209)
    - What is \(c\) in \(E^s_x\)? (R1, line 206)
    - Which encoder does the concatenated feature vector feed into? (R1, line 232)
- **Ambiguity in novel-object/novel-description experiments**:
    - It is unclear whether “novel objects” are entirely new categories or variations of existing ones, and whether “novel descriptions” involve significant semantic shifts or minor phrasing changes. A detailed example would clarify whether the setup is truly out-of-distribution (R2).
- **Efficiency concerns and inference-time tuning**:
    - The method involves two LLMs and multiple forward passes in the fine-grained stage. Practical solutions to improve efficiency are not explored (R1).
    - Both inference time and hyperparameter tuning (e.g., RoI threshold, prompt engineering) are discussed only briefly (R4).
- **Dataset justification**:
    - The necessity of the new CityAnchor dataset is not fully justified. Would performance degrade significantly without it? Does it offer unique attributes that CityRefer lacks, such as greater diversity or robustness? (R4)
- **Moderate novelty**:
    - The coarse-to-fine design builds on existing multi-stage approaches without introducing fundamentally new concepts in 3D visual grounding (R2, R3). One reviewer found the method technically sound and well motivated; others considered the lack of innovation a weakness (contrast between R1 and R3/R2).
- **Prompt engineering burden**:
    - Users may need to invest effort engineering prompts for accurate results. A user study with diverse prompts for the same target object would show robustness (R4). A progressive prompting approach (broad query then constraints) could be more intuitive (R4).
- **Positive/negative sample selection in FMM training**:
    - How are positive and negative samples chosen during training of the fine-grained matching module? (R1)
- Minor writing issues: vague sentences and grammatical errors were noted and should be corrected (R2).
