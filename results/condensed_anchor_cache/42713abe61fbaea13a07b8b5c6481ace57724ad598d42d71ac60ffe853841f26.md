## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an encoder-decoder model that uses an image-moment-based loss (L_m) to explicitly disentangle centroid and orientation from other features, achieving translational and rotational equivariance/invariance. The method is evaluated on six datasets (e.g., MNIST, 5HD) against five to six baselines (including TARGET-VAE, IRL-INR). Results show improved disentangling, reconstruction, and computational efficiency (up to 10× training, 9× inference speedup). Reviewers agree the paper is generally well-written but raise significant concerns about the limited scope of experiments, the novelty of the moment loss, missing ablations, and presentation clarity. Reviewer confidence ranges from fairly confident (3) to confident (4). One reviewer rated contribution as poor (1) while the others rated it fair (2), reflecting disagreement on novelty.

### Strengths
*   The idea of guiding equivariant feature learning with spatial image statistics (centroid, orientation) is technically reasonable.
*   The paper is well-organized and easy to follow.
*   The model consistently improves over existing methods across multiple benchmarks and demonstrates effective disentanglement of translation and rotation.
*   The model is computationally efficient, achieving up to 10× faster training and 9× faster inference compared to the average of other models.

### Weaknesses
*   **Limited to toy datasets**: All experiments are on simple 2D objects with near-uniform backgrounds (MNIST, 5HD). In Table 1 all methods exceed 97% accuracy, raising doubts about practical utility for natural images with complex backgrounds or 3D rotations. Missing evaluation on datasets such as CIFAR, ImageNet, or 3D shapes (airplanes, teapots, faces) that would test robustness when shape changes.
*   **Disentanglement evaluation is narrow**: Metrics (z-diff, z-var, IRS) may not fully capture disentanglement quality in more complex scenarios. The GF-Score proposed in the DAE paper should also be reported.
*   **Limited analysis of the moment loss**:
    *   Only first- and second-order moments are used; higher-order moments or other spatial statistics might provide more robust guidance for complex shapes/textures. The paper does not explore these limitations.
    *   An ablation of L_moment is missing. It is unclear how much the model relies on the moment loss versus emergent properties from reconstruction alone. The moment loss could also be applied over other baselines to measure its added contribution.
    *   The novelty of the moment loss is very limited, as it is a widely known concept. One reviewer rated contribution as poor (1), while others rated it fair (2).
*   **Presentation and clarity issues**:
    *   The key contribution (L_m) is not clearly emphasized; the primary focus (disentanglement vs. obtaining moments/orientation) is ambiguous.
    *   The theorems in Section 3.1 are barely referenced elsewhere in the paper.
    *   Experimental results lack sufficient details and discussion (e.g., downstream task setup is barely described; visual results take too much space relative to quantitative results).
    *   Some sentences are hard to understand (e.g., what metric is “training and inference performance”? the sentence on “subtle inaccuracy” before Eq. (13)).
*   **Motivation-experiment disconnect**: The paper claims learning centroids/orientations underpins downstream tasks, but the experimental validation of this claim is weak and lacks detail.
*   **Latent dimension mismatch**: Tables 3 and 4 compare models using d=2 for baselines (e.g., TARGET-VAE, IRL-INR) while those models were originally shown with d≥32. This makes the comparison unclear.
*   **Missing hyperparameter analysis**: Sensitivity to hyperparameters, especially those related to the moment-based loss, is not examined.
*   **Unclear moment computation for other factors**: How are moments computed for factors such as shape and color? (Related to the limitation of using only centroid and orientation.)
