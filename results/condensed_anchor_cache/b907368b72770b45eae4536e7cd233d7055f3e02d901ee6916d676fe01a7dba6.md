## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes the Union-over-Intersections (UoI) method, modifying object detection in two ways: (1) regressing proposals only to the intersection area with the ground truth instead of the full ground truth, and (2) replacing winner-takes-all NMS with a union of regressed intersections from all proposals in a group. The plug-and-play approach is applied to five classic detectors (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, YOLOv3, Def-DETR) and tested on PASCAL VOC and MS-COCO for detection and instance segmentation. Results show consistent improvements in localization and segmentation accuracy with minimal additional computation.

### Strengths

- **Novel and practical insight**: The idea of regressing to the intersection instead of the full ground truth is innovative and addresses the difficulty of extrapolating beyond proposal scope. The union-based post-processing avoids discarding information from non-top boxes.
- **Easy to implement**: The method requires only modifications to the training objective and post-processing, and integrates as a plug-and-play module into existing detection architectures with minimal changes (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, YOLOv3, Def-DETR).
- **Clear writing and presentation**: The paper is well-structured, with intuitive figures, pseudocode, and a logical flow that makes the methodology easy to understand.
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Results cover both detection and instance segmentation across multiple architectures and datasets (PASCAL VOC, MS-COCO). Ablation studies (e.g., group size, IoU thresholds, post-processing variants) and analyses of limitations (crowded scenes, failure cases in Figs. 4-5) are provided.
- **Solid performance gains**: The method yields improved AP and AR while adding only minimal computational overhead. Improvements are observed across object sizes (small, medium, large) in COCO.
- **Good versatility**: Verified on two-stage (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN), one-stage (YOLOv3), and query-based (Def-DETR) detectors.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparison with IoU-based loss functions**: The paper does not compare against GIoU, CIoU, Alpha-IoU, EIoU, or other IoU-based regression losses. Such comparison is needed to justify the superiority of intersection-only regression over learning the full ground truth with proper loss tuning.
- **Inconsistent evaluation across datasets**: Different methods are tested on different datasets without a unified protocol. For example, Mask R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, and YOLOv3 are evaluated on MS-COCO but not on PASCAL VOC (Table 1 vs. Table 2). This makes it difficult to assess robustness and generalizability.
- **Ablation studies limited to Faster R-CNN**: All detailed hyperparameter analyses (group size in Fig. 3c, IoU threshold, post-processing variants) are performed only on Faster R-CNN. The optimal group size (5 for Faster R-CNN on COCO) may not generalize to other architectures; no ablations on Mask R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, YOLOv3, or Def-DETR are provided.
- **Concern about bias toward larger objects**: The union operation tends to enlarge merged boxes, potentially skewing toward larger objects. While mAP improves across all sizes, the method’s benefit for very small objects (e.g., infrared targets under 10×10 pixels) is not analyzed, and the default single-proposal coverage for small targets may limit gains.
- **Regression target dynamics and instability**: The intersection regression target changes depending on the proposal, making the learning target dynamic. The paper does not analyze potential training instability or variance across proposals.
- **Confidence score handling unclear**: The method description does not explain how confidence scores for the union boxes are computed. Since scores are essential for mAP evaluation (including AP@0.5, AP@0.75), this omission is a significant gap.
- **Integration with non-box-proposal methods is not fully demonstrated**: While the method is applied to Def-DETR (query-based) and YOLOv3 (dense grid), it remains a box refinement module that requires proposals. For non-proposal detectors (e.g., FCOS, DETR with non-two-stage queries), the integration as a seamless plug-and-play module is not demonstrated, and additional regression refinement may be needed.
- **Novelty concerns (minority reviewer)**: One reviewer (score 5) argued that intersections are a subset of IoU and that learning IoU (e.g., IoU loss) already covers intersection regression; therefore the novelty is limited. The same reviewer considers the union step similar to established box voting (e.g., test-time augmentation) and notes that relying on proposals limits applicability to proposal-free methods. This contrasts with the positive assessments of the other three reviewers (scores 8), who consider the method innovative and significant.
- **Limited NMS variant ablation**: Only Soft-NMS is tested despite the claim of compatibility with any NMS variant. More variants (e.g., DIoU-NMS, Adaptive NMS) should be evaluated.
- **Missing related work on bounding box regression**: Section 2 does not contain a dedicated related works section for box regression. All compared algorithms (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, etc.) should appear and be cited in the related work.
- **Important ablations relegated to appendix without justification**: Several ablation experiments are only in the appendix but could strengthen the main paper. Figures 4 and 5 are oversized and partially redundant; space could be reallocated to key ablation studies.
- **Formatting minor issues**: Font size in Fig. 3 is too small. Paragraph title formatting inconsistent (e.g., some missing periods, inconsistent capitalization). Pseudocode in Figure 2 may need clarification: the green box uses “iou(H, pi) ≥ k” while the red box uses “iou(M, bi) ≥ k” without justification; the loop “while P ≠ empty” may need a removal step “P ← P \ p_i”. The notation in line 220 uses “R” for target box space instead of “G” or “T” used elsewhere—clarity is needed.
- **Questions requiring clarification**: The difference between Table 1 and Table 4 (Faster R-CNN results) needs explanation (likely Soft-NMS setting). The effect of varying IoU threshold k (not group size alone) should be studied. The regression function r is described as a learnable head, but the baseline lacks an equivalent refinement module—is this a fair comparison? The UoI method produces new boxes but claims the same number of predictions; it is unclear how this is ensured and whether the evaluator receives more predictions before NMS.
