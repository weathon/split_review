## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes NASGraph, a training-free and data-agnostic Neural Architecture Search method that converts neural architectures into computational graphs and uses the average degree of those graphs as a proxy for validation accuracy. The method is evaluated on NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201, NDS, and Micro TransNAS-Bench-101, finding the best architecture among 200 random samples from NAS-Bench-201 in 217 CPU seconds. Reviewers note the approach is simple, novel, and theoretically grounded, but raise concerns about evaluation scope, missing baselines, and assumptions.

### Strengths

*   The method is simple, easy to understand, and novel, going beyond basic DAG conversion by carefully modeling the forward computation within graph blocks. (R1, R2, R3)
*   The final proxy metric (average degree) is theoretically grounded and the method is data-agnostic and GPU-free, drastically reducing search cost. (R2, R1, R3)
*   Experiments are extensive across multiple NAS benchmarks (NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201, NDS, Micro TransNAS-Bench-101) and results are competitive. (R1, R2, R3)
*   The evaluation of complementary nature of the proxy (Table 5) is interesting. (R1)
*   The paper includes a comparison of various graph measures in the appendix. (R3)
*   Techniques to further speed up the method using surrogate models are suggested. (R3)

### Weaknesses

*   **Missing comparison with MeCo proxy**: The paper does not compare against MeCo (https://openreview.net/pdf?id=KFm2lZiI7n), which appears to outperform NASGraph on most benchmarks. (R1, strong criticism)
*   **Limited search spaces**: Evaluation is restricted to cell-based benchmarks. The method should be tested on larger, weight-entangled macro-architecture spaces like AutoFormer, OFA, and HAT, which provide pre-trained surrogates. It is also unclear whether the proxy is directly applicable to transformer spaces (e.g., ViTs, MLPMixers). (R1, R2 – both ask about generalization beyond conv-net spaces)
*   **Assumption of non-negative inputs**: The method assumes inputs are non-negative. Many modern architectures use activations that yield negative values (e.g., GELU). The analysis may not hold for non-ReLU-based architectures. (R2)
*   **Unclear Figure 1 example**: The example in Figure 1 and its caption are unclear and need elaboration. (R1)
*   **Presentation issues**: The definition of the final score (average degree) in Section 4.2 should be moved to Section 3 for clarity. Also, no intuitive explanation is given for why average degree works best among compared graph measures, or what each measure indicates. (R2)
*   **Justification of training-free NAS**: More justification is needed for why training-free data-agnostic NAS is important. Even with near-zero search cost, one must still train selected architectures to verify performance, so most time may still be spent on validation. (R3) – *Note: this is a broader question/criticism.*
*   **Comparison with non-training-free methods**: To demonstrate practicality, the method should be compared with non-training-free NAS methods (e.g., DARTS-PT) on a larger space like the DARTS search space, within the same computational budget. (R3)
*   **Correlation with inference time**: A higher average degree may lead to slower inference speed. The correlation between the proxy metric and model inference time should be examined. (R3)
*   **Reproducibility**: Code was not made publicly available in the submission, which is a concern for reproducibility. (R1)
*   **Method comparison details**: It would be helpful to compare CUDA memory consumption and search time against other proxies. (R1)

*Note: Reviewer 1 (score 3) was particularly critical of the limited evaluation and missing baselines, while Reviewer 2 (score 6) was more positive but still raised significant theoretical gaps. Reviewer 3 (score 5) asked for stronger justification and broader comparisons.*
