## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReSample, an algorithm that solves general inverse problems using pre-trained latent diffusion models. The key contribution is a “hard data consistency” module that enforces measurement fidelity via optimization during reverse sampling, followed by a novel stochastic resampling scheme that maps the measurement-consistent sample back onto the noisy data manifold (theoretically shown to be less noisy). Experiments on linear and nonlinear inverse problems (deblurring, denoising, super-resolution, inpainting, CT reconstruction) demonstrate state-of-the-art performance compared to pixel-space diffusion solvers and other baselines. Three reviewers gave a score of 8, while one reviewer gave a score of 6, noting that the work is solid but with several unresolved questions and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with well-structured background on denoising diffusion models that balances technicalities and intuition.
- The proposed stochastic resampling (ReSample) is novel and theoretically solid: the authors prove that the resampled images have lower variance than standard stochastic sampling.
- Hard data consistency (solving an optimization problem to enforce measurements) is a principled approach and is supported by both theoretical and empirical results.
- The method outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, especially simple extensions of diffusion priors (DPS, DDNM, etc.), across multiple tasks and datasets (deblurring, denoising, super-resolution, inpainting, CT reconstruction).
- Experimental results are impressive in challenging settings: the authors consider measurement operators that are not trivially SVD-decomposable, and they focus on low measurement noise regimes (common in imaging but difficult for diffusion models).
- The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the variance of the resampled images, justifying the advantage of the proposed scheme.
- The paper is well organized, and the core idea is clearly delivered.

### Weaknesses
- The algorithm uses a data consistency update on \( z_{t-1} \) rather than \( z_t \), which differs from prior diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDNM). This requires two feed-forward passes per time step, doubling sampling time. The authors rely on “partial hard data-consistency” to mitigate this. *Reviewer 1 asks:* Could one use \( z_t \) from the previous step without an additional unconditional DDIM step? Would the mean still follow the unconditional path?
- It is not clear how performance and runtime change if hard data consistency is applied at every time step instead of the proposed partial schedule. *Reviewer 1 requests:* quantitative results comparing full and partial hard data-consistency (e.g., performance gap, time cost). *Reviewer 3 similarly asks:* How does the method perform if a data fidelity step is done at each iteration? The current justification (“Because of the continuity of the sampling process…”) is insufficient.
- The parameter \(\gamma\) in Figure 15 is not described.
- Comparison with some baselines appears unfair. ADMM-PnP is run for only 10 iterations, whereas it typically requires 50–100 iterations to converge. FBP-UNet underperforms compared to the results reported by Jin et al.
- The claimed advantages of latent sampling for inference speed are not clearly demonstrated. Table 7 shows only modest speed improvements, which is disappointing given that this is a main motivation. The authors might better emphasize other strengths (e.g., success in constrained settings).
- The paper lacks discussion of related work in latent space optimization and constrained diffusion models. In particular:
  - Methods that optimize latent variables of a decoder (e.g., Deep Image Prior [Ulyanov et al. CVPR 2018], Jalal et al. NeurIPS 2021) are not referenced.
  - Constrained diffusion models (e.g., Mirror Diffusion Models, Reflected Diffusion Models, Diffusion models for constrained domains) are not cited.
  - The theoretical derivations in the appendix appear in a more general form in Efron (2011); this reference should be added when introducing Tweedie’s formula.
- The authors mention that Rout et al. (2023) proposes a similar method but do not elaborate on similarities and differences; such a discussion would be insightful.
- The comparison of DPS is potentially flawed: the DPS paper uses a plain norm \(\nabla_z \|y - \mathcal{A}(\hat{x}_0(x_t))\|\), not the squared norm written in equation (7) of the paper. The authors should clarify whether their implementation uses a squared norm and whether the theoretical analysis still holds in that case.
- The experimental results are mostly limited to random inpainting, which is relatively easy. *Reviewer 4 asks:* How does the method perform on more complex inpainting tasks (e.g., removal of large pixel regions), as demonstrated in Rout et al. (2023)?
- It is not explained why training the model in pixel space (Tables 1 and 2) yields lower quality than the proposed ReSample algorithm. Since latent space is primarily motivated by computational efficiency, the reasons for superior reconstruction quality are unclear.
- Several presentation issues:
  - Figure 6 is unclear: the right plot does not correspond to the problem displayed on the left, and the legend says “ReSample frequency” while the x-axis says “# of iterations” (label should be updated).
  - PSNR metrics should be added to figures showing images (e.g., Fig. 3, 4, and in the appendix).
  - Figure 16 (Stage 2 vs. Stage 1) may be misleading: if Algorithm 1’s condition “if t ∈ C” is not activated before Stage 2, how can measurement information appear in Stage 1?
  - Is Stage 0 really necessary? Solving eq. (10) and sampling from it might provide a good initial step, potentially reducing the number of iterations.
  - Typos: “in replace” (p. 5), “still remain” (should be “retain”, p. 5), “noice” (p. 6 and after Theorem 2).
- The novelty of applying latent diffusion to inverse problems is not groundbreaking (Reviewer 4), but the paper introduces significant advancements. One reviewer rated contribution as fair (2/4) while others rated it good (3/4), indicating some disagreement.
- *Reviewer 2 (score 6) raises additional questions that imply missing analysis:*
  - How to scale ReSample to high-resolution images?
  - For solving eq. (10) (L2 least squares), gradient descent or conjugate gradient is used; can a closed-form expression be used if \(A\) is a linear operator to accelerate?
  - For deblurring tasks, the forward operator \(A\) may be unknown; how can the algorithm be adapted to that scenario?
