## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces PINNsFormer, a Transformer-based framework for physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that uses a pseudo-sequence generator to create sequences of time-stepped inputs processed by a transformer encoder-decoder, along with a novel Wavelet activation function. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty and impressive results on failure-mode problems and high-dimensional PDEs, but raise significant concerns about reproducibility, experimental rigor, missing baselines, and the validity of certain design claims. Scores are divided: two reviewers assign 8 (high, confident), two assign 5 (moderate, with substantive criticisms).

### Strengths

- **Novelty and direction**: First integration of Transformers with PINNs to capture temporal dependencies; adapting transformers to pointwise inputs via pseudo-sequence generation and sequential loss is non-trivial and clever. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Clear problem motivation**: The method explicitly addresses temporal propagation failures in vanilla PINNs (e.g., convection, 1D-reaction) and shows clear benefits in maintaining accuracy across the domain. (R1, R3)
- **Methodology**: Components (pseudo-sequence generation, transformer encoding, Wavelet activation) are well-motivated; ablation studies provide insights into design choices. (R1, R3)
- **Impressive empirical results**: Outperformance of PINNs and variants across multiple failure-mode and high-dimensional PDE benchmarks. (R1, R2)
- **Clear writing and representation** (R1, R2)
- **Flexibility**: Ability to incorporate existing PINNs optimization schemes. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing reproducibility**: No published code or detailed hyperparameters; this makes verification difficult. (R1)
- **Undermining automatic differentiation**: The discrete timestep \(\Delta t\) in pseudo-sequences relies on finite-difference-like approximations, which may compromise the key advantage of automatic differentiation. Justification for this design choice and analysis of its impact on gradient accuracy and convergence are missing. (R1, R4)
- **Sensitivity to \(\Delta t\) and pseudo-sequence length**: No systematic study of how the model performance varies with \(\Delta t\) (fixed or variable) or with the number of pseudo-sequence points \(k\); guidelines for setting these parameters are absent. One reviewer questions whether a fixed \(\Delta t\) is a non-trivial limitation. (R1, R4)
- **Missing baseline comparison to classical solvers**: The method resembles a high-order finite-difference stencil (with 100 elements) but no comparison to a conventional discrete PDE solver under similar computational cost is provided. (R2)
- **Weak experimental setup**:
  - Only three simple PDEs tested; many prior works achieve lower relative errors. (R4)
  - The Navier–Stokes example is too simple; more challenging cases (e.g., from references [2] and [3]) should be considered. (R4)
  - The paper highlights “very low loss” as a metric, but loss is not a fair or comparative metric. (R4)
  - Only isotropic problems are considered; anisotropic and nonlinear problems are not tested, limiting claims of broad applicability. (R3)
- **Wavelet activation inadequately validated**:
  - Needs thorough empirical comparison against standard activations (ReLU, sigmoid, etc.). (R3)
  - One reviewer argues it is a simplified form of positional encoding; a sine activation followed by an MLP could achieve the same effect. (R4)
- **Scalability concern**: Quadratic complexity of self-attention may hinder application to large spatiotemporal datasets; no analysis of computational overhead or memory requirements is provided. (R1, R3)
- **Incomplete ablation**: No quantitative analysis of critical hyperparameters (e.g., pseudo-sequence length, number of coarsening levels) to justify design choices and understand their impact on performance and efficiency. (R1, R3)
- **Poorly specified architecture**: The “Spatio-Temporal Mixer” is not formally defined; the difference between the encoder-decoder and a causal (decoder-only) architecture is not justified. A causal attention (LLM-style) transformer should be compared. (R1, R4)
- **Disagreement on temporal dependency claim**: One reviewer (score 5) strongly disagrees that vanilla PINNs neglect temporal dependency, arguing that MLPs explicitly process time coordinates together with spatial coordinates and see many time points through random collocation sampling; the paper’s claim is considered too strong. (R4)
- **Missing adaptation to non-fixed sampling**: The method relies on temporal discretization; the ability to handle variable or adaptive sampling (as in Daw et al., 2022) is not demonstrated, though transformers could offer flexibility. (R1)
