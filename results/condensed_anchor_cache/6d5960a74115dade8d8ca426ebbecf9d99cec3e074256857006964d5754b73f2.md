## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a systematic empirical study of parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods for Vision Transformers (ViT) on image classification. It compares several PETL approaches after hyperparameter tuning, finding that they achieve similar accuracy on VTAB-1K, but make different errors and high-confidence predictions (complementariness), motivating ensemble methods like weight-space ensembles (WiSE). The study also examines PETL in many-shot regimes and robustness to distribution shifts (e.g., using a CLIP backbone). The paper aims to provide a user guide and insights for practitioners.

### Strengths
- **Important questions and community value**: The paper addresses key questions in PETL and contributes meaningfully to the ongoing discourse; readers can gain a thorough understanding of the PETL direction (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Comprehensive experiments and fair comparison**: Extensive experiments with systematic hyperparameter tuning to fairly compare PETL methods, especially in low-shot tasks (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Intriguing finding on WiSE ensembles**: The observation that weight-space ensembles (WiSE) can enhance performance on the target distribution compared to direct fine-tuning is worth further investigation (Reviewer 1).
- **Good writing and presentation**: The paper is well-written, logical, and easy to understand; charts and graphs are beautiful and properly presented (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Substantial supplementary materials** (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of depth in theoretical analysis**: The paper lacks rigorous theoretical justification for PETL’s behavior, e.g., the claim that “PETL succeeds as a high-capacity learner equipped with an effective regularizer” (lines 132–133) is not backed by theory (Reviewer 4). Other analyses, such as the robustness discussion (lines 519–520), are shallow and do not offer new conclusions beyond existing literature (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited task scope and generalizability**: Experiments are validated only on classification tasks (VTAB-1K and a few others), despite the title claiming “Visual Recognition” which typically includes detection, segmentation, etc. No validation on more challenging or widely-used datasets (e.g., COCO, ADE20K) (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). This limits the generalizability of the recommendations (Reviewer 1).
- **Many known conclusions**: Several findings (e.g., PETL advantages in low-shot and close-domain scenarios) are already well-known in the field (Reviewers 1, 2). For example, the many-shot regime analysis closely resembles prior studies (SSF [1], Bi-Adapter [2], GPS [3] supplementary) (Reviewer 1).
- **Empirical recommendations lack actionability**: The concept of “domain affinity” is not clearly defined or quantified statistically (Reviewer 1). The advice on “Occam’s razor” for similar domains vs. failure with large domain gaps is not thoroughly explored (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing analysis of complementarity sources**: The paper does not investigate whether the distinct and complementary knowledge from different PETL methods arises from different random initializations of tunable parameters (Reviewer 1). It is also unclear whether different types of PETL methods (e.g., adapter-based) tend to make similar incorrect predictions (Reviewer 2).
- **No consideration of computational costs**: While stated, the exclusion of memory usage and FLOPs from the study is a significant practical limitation, as these are critical for large-scale applications (Reviewer 4).
- **Outdated method selection**: The experimental methods are relatively old (e.g., SSF from NeurIPS 2022, FACT from AAAI 2023) (Reviewer 4). Related work omits some of the most recent PEFT studies (Reviewer 3).
- **PETS scope and future direction missing**: The paper focuses on summarizing existing methods without discussing future development trends in PETL (Reviewer 2). Discussing applications to newer areas like image generation would enrich the paper (Reviewer 2).
- **Concerns about similarity across methods**: All PETL methods perform similarly after tuning, but it is unclear whether this is due to task limitations or inherent limitations of PETL; no further discussion is provided (Reviewer 4).

*Note: Reviewer 4 (score 3) is notably more critical, particularly regarding theoretical grounding, age of methods, and lack of complexity consideration, while Reviewer 2 (score 6) is more positive on the comprehensiveness and community value.*
