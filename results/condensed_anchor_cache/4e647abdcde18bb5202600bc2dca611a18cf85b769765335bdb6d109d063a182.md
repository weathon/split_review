## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces TGB-Seq, a benchmark for temporal graph learning curated to minimize repeated edges and emphasize complex sequential dynamics, motivated by the observation that existing methods like GraphMixer and DyGFormer fail on simple sequential patterns (e.g., “a user who followed OpenAI and Anthropic is more likely to follow AI at Meta next”). TGB-Seq comprises large real-world datasets from e-commerce, movie ratings, business reviews, social networks, citation networks, and web link networks, spanning both bipartite and non-bipartite graphs. Benchmarking experiments show current temporal GNNs suffer significant performance degradation and high training costs on these datasets. The paper provides code and dataset access.

### Strengths
- **Compelling motivation and significance.** The focus on repeated edges and sequential dynamics is meaningful, reasonable, and often overlooked. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5) One reviewer (Reviewer 4) specifically considers the idea interesting and significant for the temporal graph learning community, opening new research directions.
- **Diverse and well-curated datasets.** The benchmark spans multiple domains with varying sizes (nodes/edges) and includes both bipartite and non-bipartite graphs, offering diverse resources to investigate unseen-edge scenarios. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Clear presentation and structure.** The paper is well-written, easy to follow, with comprehensive reporting of experimental settings (hyperparameter ranges, computational resources) and state-of-the-art baselines. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Insightful data analysis.** The experiments and Figure 2 are convincing, and the analysis helps re-evaluate current evaluation standards for future link prediction. (Reviewers 2, 5)

### Weaknesses
- **Toy example oversimplification and missing node features.** The toy example (Figure 3) omits node features, which are a key advantage of GNNs over ID-based recommendation models. This omission may not fully demonstrate GNN capabilities. (Reviewers 1, 5) Additionally, the assumption that all nodes have blank (no) features leads to indistinguishability (similar to graph isomorphism), making the limitations appear extreme. Reviewers requested clarification on why this simplification is reasonable for isolating temporal dynamics and how the absence of features impacts conclusions. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of formal analysis in Section 3.2.** The discussion on limitations of memory and aggregation mechanisms is perfunctory and lacks formal definitions of how models store/update node embeddings over time and how these fail to capture sequential dynamics. (Reviewer 2) The section also lacks precise premises: e.g., are node features of u, v, i all blank? (Reviewer 2) A mathematical formulation of SGNN-HN’s success in the toy example and how it overcomes other models’ failures is needed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing relevant baselines.** Several models specifically designed for inductive nodes or repeat/exploration behavior are not evaluated: TREND (WWW’22), Simple Transformer for Dynamic Graph Modeling (WWW’24), RepeatNet (AAAI’19), Repetition and Exploration in Sequential Recommendation (SIGIR’23), Right Tool Right Job (RecSys’24), To Copy or not to Copy (WSDM’24), and Masked and Swapped Sequence Modeling (RecSys’23). Their performance on TGB-Seq should be reported. (Reviewer 1)
- **Claim of SGNN-HN’s state-of-the-art status.** The paper calls SGNN-HN a state-of-the-art recommendation model, but it was published in 2020; it is not current SOTA. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited insights from benchmark analysis.** Tables 3 and 4 only show overall performance decreases. Deeper analysis is needed, such as evaluating models separately on seen vs. unseen edges to highlight handling of distinct cases. (Reviewer 1)
- **Metric limitations.** Only MRR is reported. Other ranking metrics (NDCG, Recall) should be considered, and possibly new metrics designed to measure performance on unseen edges. (Reviewer 1)
- **Validation/test set design.** TGB-Seq retains only nodes seen in training for validation and test sets. This is not practical for real-world evolving graphs, as it excludes generalization to novel entities and may overestimate performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Negative sampling strategy unclear.** TGB-Seq’s negative sampling strategy needs explicit comparison to prior random and inductive NSS from Poursafaei et al. (2022). It is unclear how the proposed constraints differ to justify novelty. (Reviewer 3)
- **Comparison with TGB (Gasteiger et al.).** TGB also features large graphs with high surprise scores, challenging efficiency and performance. The distinctiveness of TGB-Seq’s capabilities is not sufficiently explained. (Reviewer 3) One reviewer (Reviewer 4) also notes that the idea of measuring unseen edges is not entirely new—TGB’s surprise index quantifies test edges unseen during training. A precise definition of “unseen” edges (first-time appearance vs. relative to training set) and how it differs from the surprise index is needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of node/edge features.** Most datasets in TGB-Seq lack node and edge features; some reviewers question whether features were considered for inclusion. Node/edge features can be crucial for learning sequential dynamics (e.g., a user purchasing coffee beans based on item category features). The absence limits evaluation in realistic scenarios where such information is available. (Reviewer 4) Reviewer 5 also notes that omitting node features fails to show GNNs’ full capability.
- **Out-of-time (OOT) errors for memory-based models.** Memory-based models like TGN and DyRep usually scale efficiently (e.g., on TGB), but on TGB-Seq they cannot finish a single epoch in 24 hours. A detailed breakdown of implementation bottlenecks (memory access, data structures for node states/messages) is needed to explain why they do not scale as expected. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing recent benchmarking efforts.** Other recent benchmarking studies in continuous time domains (e.g., [1]) are not mentioned, though they may evaluate a wider range of GNN methods. (Reviewer 5)
