## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Convex Hull and its Neighborhood (CHN) and the Smooth Bellman Operator (SBO) to improve Q-function generalization in offline RL by alleviating over-constraint issues. The practical algorithm SQOG (Smooth Q-function OOD Generalization) adds a regularization term to the critic loss. Reviewers find the problem well-motivated and the algorithm simple, computationally efficient, and performant on D4RL benchmarks (outperforming CQL, IQL, MCQ). However, significant concerns cluster around foundational definitions (CHN, OOD), the mathematical rigor of the theoretical framework, the novelty relative to prior work (DOGE [1], SEABO [2]), and the gap between theory and practice. Reviewer 2 was particularly critical of clarity and mathematical correctness (score 5, soundness 2), while Reviewers 1 and 3 were largely positive (scores 8). All reviewers note the small number of seeds (4) as a limitation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).
- The problem of over-constraining the policy in offline RL is important and very well motivated; the introduction clearly exposes this issue (R2).
- The simple logical motivation (leveraging generalization in the CHN instead of avoiding OOD entirely) is sensible and a natural follow-up to existing literature (R2).
- The practical algorithm is simple to implement (adds a smoothing term to the loss) and computationally efficient, running much faster than competitors like MCQ (R2, R3, R4).
- The sanity check in Figure 1(b) convincingly illustrates the over-constraining problem (TD3+BC keeps high Q-values near high data density) and shows SQOG does not suffer from this limitation (R2, R3). The Inverted Double Pendulum task empirically validates the alleviation of over-constraint (R3).
- The ablation study on the hyperparameter β (which controls OOD generalization loss) and on noise types is a welcome addition (R2, R3).
- Strong empirical performance on the D4RL benchmark reported in Table 1, including improved results over CQL, IQL, and MCQ (R1, R2, R3).
- Theoretical analysis (SBO properties, Proposition 1, etc.) supports the proposed method (R1, R4). Reviewer 1 notes rebuttal addressed all concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Fundamental definitional issues (especially CHN and OOD):**
    * As currently defined, $N(Conv(D)) \subset (S,A)_D \subset Conv(D)$ implies $CHN(D) = Conv(D)$, which is geometrically incorrect. This suggests a fundamental flaw that needs correction for the rest of the paper to be valid (R2, soundness=2).
    * The definition of $(S,A)_D$ is ambiguous: it is introduced as a "space" but seems to denote a set of state-action tuples. Also, the radius $r$ is initially a free parameter but later defined as a function of $D$ (Proposition 1), creating inconsistency (R2).
    * Two notions of OOD collide: the formal one (outside support of behavior policy $\mu$, Definition 2) and an intuitive one (low-density regions in Figure 1, where $[-0.5, 0.5]$ is called OOD despite non-zero density). This undermines the theoretical framework and interpretation of experimental results (R2). The neighborhood definition is given for state-action tuples but later applied to actions only (R2).
    * These unclear definitions make it difficult to understand the core concepts and assess the mathematical claims (R2).
- **Novelty and relation to prior work:**
    * The use of convex hulls and nearest neighbors in offline RL (e.g., DOGE [1], SEABO [2]) is not new and is not discussed. The paper's contribution appears incremental (R1, R4). The authors should clarify the difference or advantage of CHN/SBO compared to existing methods (R4).
- **Disconnect between theory and practice:**
    * The theoretical section introduces SBO and shows properties, but Section 3.3 switches abruptly to proposing a regularization term without a clear justification of how the theoretical insights motivate the practical loss (R2). The link between SBO and the OG loss (Eq. 13) is not explained; are they equivalent? (R2, R4)
    * The practical algorithm constructs OOD actions by adding noise to in-sample actions, but the theoretical justification for this choice and its connection to the CHN is not established. The noise distribution and clipping range (e.g., $[-0.5, 0.5]$) are not explicitly tied to the safety guarantees or neighborhood radius (R4). How do these hyperparameters affect the theoretical guarantees? (R4, Q2)
    * Equation 14: for $s'$ at the boundary of $D$ and $a'$ far from the dataset, the OOD backprop effect is not countered by the smoothing term – is this concern valid? (R2)
- **Assumptions and theoretical validity:**
    * The continuity assumption on the true Q-function is idealistic; in sparse reward environments (e.g., Montezuma's Revenge) the true Q-function can be discontinuous. The assumption applies to $Q^{\pi}$, not the parametric approximation, which raises concerns about the validity of the theoretical analysis (R4).
    * Proposition 1 references the NTK regime without introducing the framework, making it hard to assess the validity and implications (R2).
    * The derivation of the statement before Proposition 2 (l. 138-139) is unclear (R2).
    * The norm used in mathematical statements (except Theorem 4) is unspecified, introducing ambiguity (R2).
    * At L170, the assumption that a unique dataset action exists within a neighborhood is not justified (R2).
- **Missing experiments and ablation details:**
    * The Inverted Double Pendulum is a simple task; locomotion tasks should be used to verify SQOG's Q-value estimation accuracy in higher-dimensional environments (R3).
    * Ablation on noise type was done, but ablation on noise scale (which controls the amount of "OOD-ness") is missing (R2). The clipping range $[-0.5, 0.5]$ in the code should be tuned and its effect discussed (R4).
    * All experiments use only 4 seeds; increasing the number of seeds would improve robustness and potentially shrink confidence intervals (R2, R3).
- **Lack of discussion on limitations and analysis:**
    * Limitations and potential pitfalls are not discussed: how does the choice of distance metric affect results? What is the trade-off between generalization and conservative behaviors? Are some tasks better suited to conservative estimation or aggressive extrapolation? Case studies would help (R1).
    * How does the method scale with different numbers of training samples (changing the convex hull)? Computational cost (time/memory) analysis across methods is missing (R1, R2 noted quick runtime but wants details).
    * The relationship between SBO and behavior cloning loss is not discussed; is SBO's effectiveness limited to TD3+BC? (R3)
- **Minor presentation issues:**
    * Figure 3 (the table) is not clear; reorganizing the information in a different format would help (R1).
    * Figure 1(b) could be shown earlier to convince readers of the over-constraint problem (R2).
    * Details on how "true Q values" in Figure 1 are calculated (Monte Carlo estimation? resetting environment?) and what "standardized and smoothed" means (L315) are missing, affecting reproducibility (R2, R4). The term "true Q-values" may be misleading as the true Q-function in MuJoCo could be irregular; the toy example may be less convincing (R4).
    * Notational inconsistencies: "noise" in Eq. 14 should be an actual variable; action $a^{in}_{neighbor}$ sampled from a support (L170); expectations w.r.t. $\pi$ (L.85) vs $\pi(\cdot|s')$ (R2).
    * "Longest diameter" is redundant (diameter already supremal) (R2).
    * Uniqueness of CHN is stated as "due to the uniqueness" (L135) but never established earlier (should be stated at L115-116 with other properties) (R2).
    * In the proof at L744, "we can easily derive" should be fully derived (R2).
