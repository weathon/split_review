## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a CNN-based approach for diagnosing Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) using single-channel EEG data, testing channels C3, Fp1, O1, T4, and P3. It reports 88% accuracy with leave-one-subject-out cross-validation on a single dataset (58 subjects). Reviewers disagree: two rate it 3 (marginally acceptable) but with low confidence (1) or higher (4), while two rate it 1 (reject) with high confidence (4,5). The core criticisms center on lack of novelty, missing comparisons, insufficient metrics, and weak methodological contribution.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and focus (Reviewer 1):** The single-channel approach reduces complexity and cost for clinical/practical settings. The focused application to MDD and discussion of minimal electrode setups is reasonable.
- **Wearable tech applicability (Reviewer 2):** Single-channel EEG has practical potential for home-based monitoring, and the study addresses this need.
- **Clear writing (Reviewer 2):** Methodology and CNN architecture are explained concisely.
- **Real-time implementation potential (Reviewer 3):** The work addresses the possibility of single-channel EEG in real-time scenarios.
- **Contrast:** Reviewer 4 explicitly states "None" as strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty in methodology (Reviewers 1,3,4):** No original model architecture or tailored network configuration. The CNN (3 layers) is generic, no optimization for single-channel data. No feature engineering or biomarker extraction (temporal, spatial, frequency, statistical, connectivity-based). Recent advances in connectivity (e.g., Delaunay triangulation, chromatic alpha complex) are ignored.
- **No technique for channel selection (Reviewers 3,4):** The paper claims channel selection but merely tests five predefined channels; no ranking, hyperparameter optimization (HPO), layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP), or attention maps. Random selection leaves potential channels unexplored.
- **Missing comparative analysis (Reviewers 1,2,3):** No comparisons with more advanced deep learning or classical machine learning (e.g., SVM, Random Forest) on the same dataset. Specific named baselines missing: Bachmann et al. (2018) achieved 92% on channel Pz with classical ML; other single-channel/CNN studies (DOI:10.1142/S0219622019500342, https://doi.org/10.1177/1550059420916634) are not compared. Outdated state-of-art references; recent techniques (e.g., Jiang et al., ICLR 2024) are omitted.
- **Omission of key performance metrics (Reviewer 2):** F1-score, precision, recall, and AUC-ROC curves are missing, reducing diagnostic reliability.
- **Underperformance (Reviewer 2):** 88% accuracy is lower than Bachmann et al. (92%) with a simpler approach. No demonstration of gains in accuracy, computation time, or clinical relevance.
- **Single dataset limitation (Reviewers 1,2,3,4):** Only one dataset (58 subjects) used; no cross-dataset validation (e.g., MODMA dataset available). Generalizability is unproven. Small sample size (58 subjects) limits conclusiveness.
- **Poor presentation and results section (Reviewers 3,4):** Results lack competing methods; meaningless listing of accuracies from other papers on different datasets in the Introduction. Figure 2 shows C3 electrode activity for 1 sec but time frame is unclear; electrode appears outside head in one figure. References are repeated.
- **High non-stationarity of EEG (Reviewer 3):** Single-channel use in neurological/mental disorder identification is uncertain due to EEG non-stationarity.
- **Hyperparameter tuning (Reviewers 3,4):** Tuning is entirely empirical; inclusion in main Method section questioned.
