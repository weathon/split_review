## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents WinnowRAG, a training-free retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework. It first performs query-aware clustering to group retrieved documents, then assigns each cluster to an LLM agent to generate a response. A critic LLM merges agents with similar answers into super-agents using an ellipse merging strategy. In subsequent winnowing rounds, incorrect super-agents are merged with remaining agents via a hyperbola merging strategy, and the critic obtains the final answer. Experiments on five QA datasets show improved accuracy over several RAG baselines that require training.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and features effective figures and precise formulations, making it easy to follow and implement.
- The research question—how to filter noisy retrieved context—is important and broadly applicable to RAG frameworks.
- WinnowRAG combines query-aware clustering with multi-agent winnowing and a critic LLM to systematically filter out noisy documents, enhancing robustness.
- The experimental design is thorough, demonstrating both the overall efficacy of the method and the effectiveness of its individual modules.
- WinnowRAG is a practical, training-free solution that outperforms several training-required RAG baselines.

### Weaknesses
- **Efficiency concerns (raised by all reviewers):** WinnowRAG uses multiple LLM-based agents in multi-round winnowing, introducing significant computational overhead and time compared to mainstream RAG baselines. The paper lacks an efficiency comparison (inference cost, runtime).
- **Limited novelty (reviewers 2 and 4):** Core methods (query-aware clustering, multi-agent winnowing) resemble existing work (e.g., [1]–[4] cited in review), and the contribution is more engineering-oriented than providing new research insights.
- **Missing baselines (reviewers 2, 3, 4):** The evaluation would benefit from filter-focused baselines:
  - State-of-the-art rerankers (e.g., Re2G [5]), truncation methods (e.g., list-aware reranking-truncation [6]).
  - Single-agent LLM ranker (LLM-based filter).
  - Simpler filters to assess the utility of retrieved texts (e.g., CRAG [1] with corrective retrieval).
  - Comparison with alternative clustering methods (e.g., hierarchical clustering) and discussion of their pros/cons.
- **Ablation concerns (reviewer 1):** Removing the multi-round winnowing module ("Ours w/o W") causes a drastic performance drop, and on MHQA its performance appears inferior to Zero-shot Prompting (Figure 3). This raises questions about the necessity of complex multi-agent winnowing.
- **Dataset inconsistency (reviewer 1):** Figure 4 labels the dataset as "MHQA" while the text refers to "2WikiMQA." This naming inconsistency needs correction.
- **Unexpected retrieval scaling behavior (reviewer 1):** For 2WikiMQA, which has the lowest Recall@5 and Recall@20 (Table 1), increasing the number of retrieved documents did not yield the expected improvement (Figure 4). An explanation is needed.
- **Clustering method choice (reviewer 2):** The number of clusters is sensitive to document count; why not use hierarchical clustering to dynamically adjust it? Also, how is clustering performance evaluated before proceeding to the winnowing steps?
- **Unclear merging description (reviewer 2):** The merging step’s purpose and what is achieved by merging two clusters are not fully explained. The relationship between merged clusters is unclear; more examples would clarify the strategy.
- **Missing details on LLM agents (reviewer 4):** The paper should provide more specifics about the LLM-based agents used (e.g., model sizes, APIs, costs).

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* All reviewers assigned a score of 5, but confidence varied (one reviewer with confidence 3, three with confidence 5). One reviewer rated contribution as 3, while three rated it as 2, indicating some disagreement on the novelty and contribution level.
