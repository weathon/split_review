## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a stochastic vision transformer for self-supervised learning that encodes image patches into elliptical Gaussian distributional embeddings and uses Bures-Wasserstein distance (W₂) to compute attention. A contrastive regularization term based on Wasserstein distance is added to both pre-training and fine-tuning. The method is evaluated on in-distribution generalization, out-of-distribution detection, dataset corruption, semi-supervised settings, and transfer learning.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear and convincing (Reviewer 1, confidence low). The idea of stochastic token embedding and Wasserstein-based attention is interesting and timely (Reviewer 2, confidence high). The paper investigates an important research question that can potentially interest many researchers (Reviewer 3, confidence moderate).
- The paper is written clearly and is straightforward to follow (Reviewers 2 and 3). It is concise (Reviewer 3).
- The method is evaluated on a variety of tasks (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 is positive overall (score 6) but notes inability to assess (confidence 1: “unable to assess this paper”).

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental scale and significance (Reviewer 2, high confidence):** Experiments are limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100). Results on larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet, mini-ImageNet, tiny-ImageNet) are missing, making it difficult to assess scalability and practical applicability. In most experiments the proposed method either matches baselines or is only marginally superior, while being more than twice as costly (~4hrs vs ~9hrs). It is unclear whether results are averaged over multiple runs; without this information the statistical significance of the reported improvements cannot be assessed. The marginal gains with heavy computational overhead raise concerns about practical utility.
- **Similarity to prior work (Reviewer 3, moderate confidence):** The claim of proposing a new stochastic transformer with distributional embeddings and Wasserstein distance-based attention is not proper because these components are the same as in Fan et al., 2022.
- **Baseline implementation details missing (Reviewer 3):** For the MC-Dropout baseline, only the dropout ratio (0.3) applied during pre-training is mentioned, but MC-Dropout requires dropout at both training and test time. No details of testing procedure are given. The choice of dropout ratio 0.3 appears high compared to common options (Reviewer 3’s question).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity (Reviewer 3):** The ablation study suggests the method is very sensitive to hyperparameters.
- **Mathematical typos (Reviewer 2, high confidence):** Equation (4) should be \(W_2^2(z_1,z_2)\) not \(W_2(z_1,z_2)\); the same correction applies to all equations using \(W_2\). In Equation (10), the formulation may be missing a log: did the authors intend \(\mathcal{L}_p - \lambda \log(\sigma(-W_2^2(z_{\text{out}}, f_z(y^+))))\)?
- **Missing experimental design for contrastive loss (Reviewer 2’s question, which overlaps with a criticism):** Equation (10) only considers positive samples. A generalized version using both positive and negative samples (as in SimCLR) would be more appropriate, e.g., \(-\lambda_1 \log(\sigma(-W_2^2(z_{\text{out}}, f_z(y^+)))) + \lambda_2 \log(\sigma(-W_2^2(z_{\text{out}}, f_z(y^-))))\) with \(\lambda_2=0\) recovering the current formulation. The paper does not justify the omission of negatives.
