## Merged Review

### Summary

Three reviewers assessed the paper, with scores of 1 (negative), 8, and 8 (both positive). The minority reviewer found the paper difficult to read, falling between methodological novelty and application, with unclear goals and unsubstantiated claims. The majority praised the method's soundness, novelty, and strong empirical results. The paper proposes DisCo (a method combining contrastive learning and variational inference) for disentangling time series representations, specifically for home appliance electricity usage. It addresses correlated attributes by replacing the independence assumption (e.g., in VAE with KL regularization) with pairwise similarity (cosine similarity instead of Hausdorff Factorized Support from Roth et al. 2023). It introduces l-variational inference layers with self-attention to handle temporal dependencies, and a new metric TDS (Time Disentangling Score). The paper reports improvements of over +60% across benchmarks on three datasets under correlation shifts and better out-of-distribution generalization.

### Strengths

- Tackles the important problem of disentangled representation learning for time series, with potential to reduce carbon footprint (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- Addresses the unrealistic assumption of independence among generative attributes (e.g., appliances) in traditional VAE-based disentanglement; instead, recovers correlated data by encoding a wide range of possible attribute combinations in the latent space (Reviewer 3).
- Uses pairwise similarity rather than independence constraint, enabling work with correlated representations (Reviewer 2).
- Introduces l-variational inference layers with self-attention to effectively model temporal dependencies (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Proposes a new metric TDS for evaluating time series disentanglement quality (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Method is mathematically sound with correct derivations (Reviewer 3).
- Shows strong empirical performance: competitive with downstream methods, with significant improvements of over +60% across a variety of benchmarks on three different datasets under correlation shifts (Finding 5.1) (Reviewer 3).
- Better out-of-distribution generalization, especially with severe shifts, fulfilling a key promise of disentangled representations (Reviewer 3).
- The key finding that simply encouraging pairwise factorized support in the latent space is sufficient for effective disentanglement even when data attributes are correlated (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- The paper falls between methodological novelty and an application contribution, making it hard to identify where the main contributions lie (Reviewer 1). (The two positive reviewers do not share this criticism, noting clear novelties – Reviewer 2, 3.)
- The paper is poorly organized and hard to read; notations are confusing and not defined before use (e.g., $z_m^+$, $\bf{z}$, $z$; also $K$ and $dz$ after equation 2 are not defined) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The paper contains unproven or wrong claims: e.g., the statement that "Disentanglement via VAE **can be achieved** by a regularization term of the Kullback-Leibler divergence" is not necessarily true without strong assumptions (Beta-VAE provides only qualitative evidence). The claim "Rather than training separate auto-encoders for individual appliances" lacks empirical validation and citations; it is unclear how the proposed method handles the multi-appliance scenario when appliances have different activation patterns and power consumption profiles (Reviewer 1).
- The main goal is unclear: the problem statement (NILM) is not defined, the nature of the input data is not specified, and it is unclear how the approach builds upon existing methods or distinguishes itself from prior work (Reviewer 1). There is no related work section (Reviewer 1).
- The application description is abstract and lacks illustration; the experimental results are presented in an abstract way, e.g., without a specific example showing X and Y values for a time window or the rows of Y (Reviewer 2).
- It is unclear how augmentation is performed and how many augmentations are used (Section 4.1) (Reviewer 2).
- The contrastive learning preparation of positive and negative samples is not explicitly clarified: how is appliance dissimilarity in $x$ and $x^{-}$ evaluated? Are labels used? How are cases of similar (but not identical) appliances handled? How are negative and positive samples prepared to avoid unintended overlaps? (Reviewer 2).
- The authors should elaborate on why cosine similarity is used instead of Hausdorff Factorized Support (HFS) (Reviewer 2).
- Section 3.2 (attentive l-variational auto-encoders) would benefit from a dedicated illustration along with notations (Reviewer 2).
- The number of training examples used for linking ("We link the learned latent representation to ground-truth attributes using a limited number of pair labels") is not reported (Reviewer 2).
- The color meaning in tables of the Results section is unclear; it is not clear how TDS has been compared with VAE and Beta-VAE in Table 1 (Reviewer 1).
- The method's sensitivity to hyperparameter selection is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- The paper lacks a related work section (Reviewer 1).
