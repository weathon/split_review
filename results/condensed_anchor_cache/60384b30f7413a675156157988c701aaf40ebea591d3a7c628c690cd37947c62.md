## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes theoretical and empirical connections between loss landscape sharpness (Hessian trace) and compression of neural representations, introducing Local Volumetric Ratio (LVR) and Maximum Local Sensitivity (MLS) as improved metrics. Building on the linear stability insight of Ma & Ying (2021), it derives upper bounds linking sharpness to these compression metrics, extends them across all layers, and replaces the previous bound’s highly variable scaling factor with tighter factors (geometric mean over training points, maximal singular value instead of product). Experiments on VGG-11 (CIFAR-10, 2 classes), LeNet, MLP (Fashion-MNIST), and ViT show positive correlations. The paper argues this dual perspective aids understanding of generalization.

### Strengths
- **Novel bridging of two research directions**: The paper systematically connects loss landscape flatness (parameter space) and representation compression (feature space), which have largely been studied separately—a worthy and important effort (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Mathematical rigor and improved bounds**: The derivations of inequalities linking sharpness to LVR and MLS are technically sound and well-annotated. The bounds improve upon Ma & Ying (2021) by replacing the highly variable scaling factor with tighter factors using geometric mean over training points, maximal singular value instead of product, and considering all trainable weights (not only the first layer) (Reviewers 2, 5). The extension to intermediate-layer representations is a clear contribution (Reviewer 5).
- **Empirical validation across architectures**: Experiments include VGG-11, LeNet, MLP, and ViT, demonstrating consistency of the correlation between sharpness and compression across different network structures (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).
- **Good exposition and organization**: The paper provides clear background, precise notation, and step-by-step derivations that are easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). The limitation regarding the “needle in a haystack” example is acknowledged (Reviewer 2).
- **Insightful nuance**: The argument that local dimensionality is not bounded by sharpness is a thoughtful observation (Reviewer 5). Interesting discussion points (e.g., L400-405) (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient connection to generalization**: Despite framing the paper around generalization, neither theory nor experiments establish a direct or causal link from the proposed compression metrics to generalization performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The abstract and introduction overclaim this connection. The compression metrics (volume compression, small input gradient) do not guarantee that meaningful or best-generalizing features are retained; the authors acknowledge that low sensitivity can sometimes hurt generalization (needle-in-haystack example) but do not resolve this tension (Reviewer 2). The theoretical focus remains on sharpness-compression, not generalization (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of quantitative correlation evidence**: The paper claims LVR and MLS improve the bound and correlation with sharpness, but provides no quantitative correlation coefficients (e.g., Pearson) to support this. Visual inspection of Figures 1 and 2 is anecdotal (Reviewers 4, 5). The systematic correlations in Appendix G.2 are weak, vary across model/dataset, and include an acknowledged negative correlation (Fig G.4 caption: “negative correlation between the left and right hand sides of eq. (11)”). The correlation between NMLS and sharpness is less clear for MLPs in Figure H.9 (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited empirical scope**: (a) The main VGG-11 experiment is performed on only 2 out of 10 CIFAR-10 classes, reducing to a binary classification task that is much easier than full multi-class, raising doubts about generalizability to practical settings (Reviewers 4, 5). (b) Figures 1 and 2 only show a few training runs of one model on one dataset (Reviewer 2). (c) The empirical comparisons are not as extensive as related correlation studies (e.g., Andriushchenko et al. 2023, Jiang et al. 2019) (Reviewer 2). (d) Reproducibility is hindered by the lack of code release (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Unclear contribution framing and insufficient literature review**: The paper’s contributions are not clearly delineated from Ma & Ying (2021); the boundary between recapitulation and novelty is unclear (e.g., L140-160) (Reviewer 1). The metrics are introduced as compression measures but later discussed as robustness notions without explicit justification (Reviewer 1). The paper omits relevant literature (information bottleneck theory, model complexity, algorithmic stability) that would better contextualize its results (Reviewer 1). The term “representation’s compression” is ambiguous between local and global compression (Reviewer 5 suggestion).
- **Local dimensionality issues**: The theoretical treatment of local dimensionality in Section 3.3 lacks explicit bounds, making its practical impact unclear (Reviewer 3). Empirically, local dimensionality increases during training (Figures 1, 2), which contradicts the paper’s central compression narrative; the paper does not adequately reconcile this inconsistency (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experiments and analyses**: Several requested experiments are absent: (a) Testing with adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSprop), dropout, or weight decay (Reviewer 3). (b) Studying how training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) affect the sharpness-compression relationship and offering practical insights (Reviewers 3, 4, 5). (c) Investigating compression metrics and their evolution in earlier training phases (Reviewer 3). (d) Comparison with reparameterization-invariant sharpness measures (Reviewer 2). (e) Exploring alternative compression metrics (mutual information, other sensitivity measures) for a more direct generalization connection (Reviewers 2, 3). (f) Application to domains beyond vision (NLP, RL) (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: The paper contains several technical errors and unclear passages: sentence without a verb (L350-353), undefined notation (Vol(x) in Eq. 7), unconventional terminology (“Gaussian random variable ball”), confusing abbreviations in Appendix G.2 (“D”, “bound”), and typos (Reviewer 2). The definition of \(C_f^{\text{lim}}\) needs clarification (Reviewer 4). The derivations in Propositions 3.4 and 3.10 are mathematically dense and could benefit from diagrams or more intuitive explanations (Reviewer 3). It should be made explicit which parts of the derivations (L140-160) are original vs. from Ma & Ying (Reviewer 1). The reason why the equality in Eq. 8 does not hold in practice should be clarified (Reviewer 5). Better hyperlinks to specific sections for contributions (Reviewer 5).
- **Practical significance limited**: The paper offers little guidance on how the sharpness-compression relationship can be leveraged for model selection, training, or improving robustness (e.g., adversarial robustness). The observed differences in sharpness and input sensitivity under different hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) could be exploited but are not investigated (Reviewers 4, 5).
