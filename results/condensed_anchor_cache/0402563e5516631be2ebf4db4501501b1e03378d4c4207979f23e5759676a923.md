## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Bonsai, a gradient-free graph distillation method for node classification that selects exemplar computation trees rather than synthesizing fully-connected graphs. It addresses three limitations of prior work: full GNN training requirement, fully-connected distilled graphs, and model-specificity. Bonsai is the first linear-time, model-agnostic graph distillation algorithm that retains original node features and unweighted edges, outperforming baselines on six datasets while being 22× faster on average. Theoretical guarantees are provided on approximation strategies.

### Strengths
1. **Novelty**: The gradient-free, computation-tree-based approach is novel (R1, R4). It bypasses expensive gradient calculations, leading to significant speedup (R1, R2). It is the first distillation method to retain original node features and synthesize unweighted edges, more faithfully representing the original graph structure (R2). The proof that maximizing representative power of exemplars is NP-hard is simple but attracting (R3).
2. **Efficiency and scalability**: Bonsai is linear-time and model-agnostic, making it highly scalable and saving effort in hyperparameter tuning when changing condensation models (R2). The experimental results show impressive training time gains (R1).
3. **Performance**: Bonsai achieves high accuracy across varied datasets and GNN models, including cross-architecture generalization results (R3). Solid theoretical analysis supports the approach (R1). The problem is important in the field (R4).
4. **Presentation**: Good presentation and model name (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Connection to MIRAGE**: The idea is very similar to MIRAGE [A] (both select frequent/computation trees). Many theoretical analyses (e.g., Graph Isomorphism) are borrowed from MIRAGE. Extending MIRAGE’s graph-level focus to node-level by following the 1-WL test may not represent substantial novelty. The differences between Bonsai and MIRAGE must be explicitly discussed in the related work section (R2, R3).
2. **Exaggerated claims about prior work**: The claim that prior methods “distill to fully-condensed graph” is inaccurate because most methods include a sparsification step (e.g., thresholding). The edge counts reported in Table 2 are therefore inaccurate; correct counts should follow GCond’s Table 5. Also, the claim that distilled graphs are always smaller than the original needs more careful consideration, as sparsification can produce smaller distilled graphs (R2).
3. **Theoretical assumptions**: Hypothesis 1 (line 201) and Logical Progression 2 (line 215) may not hold. The connection between WL embedding similarity and training gradients needs more rigorous justification (R2). See also R3’s concern about novelty of the 1-WL test extension.
4. **Insufficient explanation of design choices**:
   - Fig. 2: When threshold is large, almost all node pairs are considered, leading to low correlation. How does this observation guide the method design? Clarify how the correlation is used to select representative trees (R2).
   - Diversity (line 235): Which part of the method addresses diversity? Explicitly describe how diversity is achieved in tree selection (R2).
   - Choice of Reverse K-NN instead of clustering: Discuss limitations of clustering and justify the choice of Reverse K-NN (R2).
   - Fig. 4(b) ablation is insufficient (R1). Random exemplar selection still yields considerable results; interpret this outcome. Provide performance comparisons for S_r using different exemplars on datasets like Cora, ogbn-arxiv, and Reddit, including random selection for clarity (R3). Also, PPR and RkNN involve many parameters; the ablation study should cover more (R1).
5. **Non-standard experimental settings**:
   - Compression rates 0.5%, 1%, 3% do not align with previous works (R4). The authors should clarify why these rates were chosen (R4).
   - Different dataset splits (training/validation/test) and compression rate metric used compared to common practice. Clarify reasons for deviating from standard protocols (R2).
   - In some cases Bonsai does not perform best, e.g., on Citeseer (R1).
6. **Missing baselines**: Several recent graph condensation methods should be included for accuracy comparison: SGDD [3], SFGC [4], GEOM [5] (R4). For condensation time comparison, ExGC [1] and GC-SNTK [2] should be included, and time comparisons should be presented as a table rather than a figure (R4). Table 5 should include results for other compression rates (R1).
7. **Method’s relation to graph condensation paradigm**: The approach of selecting datasets via sampling resembles traditional graph reduction, sparsification, or coarsening more than mainstream graph condensation. Consequently, it is hard to accept the claimed significant improvements over random or herding baselines. The authors should provide an intuitive example to support their approach (R3).
8. **Readability**: The paper is not easy to understand (R1).
