## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Language-Guided Abstraction (LGA), a framework that uses natural language and language models to build state abstractions for imitation learning. LTA first textualizes raw perceptual input into a text-based feature set, then uses a pre-trained LM to filter out irrelevant features based on a (possibly incomplete) task description, and finally converts the abstracted features into a policy-ready format (e.g., masked observations). Experiments are conducted on the VIMA benchmark with simulated robotic tasks. LGA reduces the time needed for feature specification compared to manual methods, outperforms non-abstraction baselines in sample efficiency, and improves generalization and robustness to spurious correlations, observational shifts, and language variations.

### Strengths
- The idea to leverage the semantic reasoning ability of LLMs for task-relevant feature selection is interesting, innovative, and intuitive. Using binary masks / masked observations avoids spurious correlations by focusing on goal information rather than raw pixels. The method is powerful for few-shot imitation learning and generalizes across color/texture variations.
- Integration with LMs enables contextually appropriate feature selection, enhancing policy generalization and performance even with limited training data. Exciting application of pretrained LMs in robotics.
- LGA reduces human effort for feature specification while achieving higher sample efficiency than non-abstraction baselines. Policies trained with LGA abstractions are resilient to observational shifts and language variations.
- In multi-task scenarios, LGA effectively resolves task ambiguities and adapts to new language specifications in observations.
- The paper is clearly written and includes comprehensive experiments demonstrating effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of substantial technical contribution**: The approach appears more like a system design to generate heuristics rather than a fundamental method for identifying useful state features. The importance of state features depends not only on task semantics but also on low-level geometric constraints (e.g., collision avoidance); learning of features and motion policies should ideally be coupled (e.g., as in VIOLA). [Reviewer 3, more critical on contribution; Reviewer 2 also notes conceptual novelty is limited – similar ideas explored in robotmoo and VIOLA.]
- **Incomplete evaluation of state abstraction failures**: The paper does not analyze how often the LM predicts insufficient or redundant state abstractions, nor does it explore refining LM choices with feedback from policy execution. It is unclear whether task failures stem from policy quality or incorrect state abstraction. [Reviewer 1, question overlaps with criticism; Reviewer 2 also questions failure modes of segmentation/captioning modules.]
- **Reliance on segmentation and textualization at the desired abstraction level**: The filtering approach assumes that segmentation and captioning operate at the right granularity and are complete. It is unclear how to segment or group objects before the task is known (e.g., if the language instruction refers to a part of an object or a group of objects). Alternative approaches like open-vocabulary detectors or VLMs for identifying target objects are not ablated. [Reviewer 2, more critical on limitations.]
- **Binary masks as state representation may be limiting**: Using binary masks discards texture and detail that could be important for tasks where object appearance matters. The LM should perhaps decide whether to use the mask or original state based on context. Results show that LGA-S (with human refinement) performs better, suggesting the LM-only abstraction may be insufficient. [Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 also asks about using masked image vs. binary mask directly.]
- **Background and landmarks may be essential**: The method may fail if it removes background necessary for localization (e.g., visual navigation tasks). The paper does not discuss assumptions and limitations about which task types the method is suited for. [Reviewer 2]
- **Overly simplified imitation learning setup**: The VIMA benchmark uses high-level primitive actions (“pick”, “place”) with continuous goal poses, which simplifies behavior cloning. A more rigorous benchmark with continuous actions (e.g., RoboSuite) would better test the method’s ability to handle complex manipulation tasks. [Reviewer 3, high confidence]
- **Overlooked related work on state abstraction**: The paper does not discuss extensive prior work on learning state abstraction for reinforcement learning, such as "Approximate State Abstraction" (ICML 2016), "State Abstraction in Lifelong RL" (ICML 2018), "State Abstraction As Compression" (AAAI 2019). [Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3 also suggests referring to related literature.]
- **Practical concerns**: How well do the segmentation and captioning modules work in practice (e.g., can the robot arm be segmented)? Does LGA assume full observability and run segmentation on every frame? This could be expensive given the size of SAM. How good is the LM at guessing relevant objects not explicitly mentioned in the instruction? [Reviewer 2, questions that overlap with weaknesses about reliance and completeness.]
