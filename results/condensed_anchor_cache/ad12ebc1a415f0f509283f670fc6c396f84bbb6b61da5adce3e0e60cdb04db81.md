## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a curriculum view of robust loss functions by rewriting them into a common form with a sample-weighting function \(w\) and a margin difference \(\Delta\), plus an optional implicit regularizer. It uses this framework to analyze training dynamics, explaining underfitting (diminished average sample weights) and noise robustness (larger weights for clean samples). It shows that simple fixes to the curriculum (shifting soft-margin) can make underfitting robust loss functions competitive, and that training schedules affect noise robustness. The work connects several robust loss designs, introduces the marginal effective learning rate, and empirically studies loss functions and regularizers.

### Strengths
- Novel curriculum perspective that unifies many robust loss functions into a sample-weighting and implicit regularizer form, providing a straightforward analysis of training dynamics.
- Helps explain two open questions in noisy-label learning: underfitting of robust losses and the role of sample weights.
- The marginal effective learning rate concept and shifting soft-margin mitigate underfitting, especially when the number of classes is large.
- Experiments are extensive, well-designed, and include mean/std in Table 6; theoretical statements appear correct.
- The simple fix (shifting and rescaling) works well empirically.
- The paper is well-organized, motivation is interesting, and results are reproducible with clearly stated hyperparameters and training setups.
- The common features across losses make the understanding significant; some reviewers find the work valuable with solid theoretical exposition.
- A sufficient number of robust loss functions are reviewed.

### Weaknesses
- The motivation and novelty of rewriting losses into the proposed form is not sufficiently justified. It remains unclear what novel perspective this provides over studying gradients w.r.t. logits or loss values (e.g., Wang et al. 2019a). Some reviewers question why this form is used over a more natural gradient magnitude perspective; the paper does not clearly state which findings are only possible with this framework.
- The paper’s key contribution, the proposed fix for MAE (shifting soft-margin), is significantly limited in novelty and validation. Wang et al. (2019a) already proposed a fix for MAE and studied its underfitting; the current fix is not compared theoretically or experimentally to that prior fix. Moreover, the fix is only tested on MAE; it is unclear whether it can be applied to other robust loss functions and whether it scales to large-scale datasets. Without extensive validation, the contribution appears limited.
- The paper’s findings about MAE focusing on moderate loss/\(\Delta\) are not novel; they were already shown by Wang et al. (2019a) in terms of gradient magnitude vs. \(p_y\). The claim that prior work attributes underfitting to low variance is a misrepresentation – Wang et al. discussed “informative vs. uninformative” samples and already showed MAE underfits on clean labels.
- Several equations lack clarity and implicit assumptions are not stated. Specifically: (i) The derivation between Equations 2 and 3 uses stop-gradient without discussion, defines \(\Delta_y\) unclearly, introduces extra minus signs without explanation, and \(w\) deserves a proper definition. (ii) The equation at the end of page 3 introduces a factor \(k\) and \(1/k\) with stop-gradient without justification. (iii) Equation 4’s derivation is unclear and needs more details. (iv) The motivation for Equation 5 is not explained.
- The general form with implicit regularizer \(R(s)\) is introduced but never studied; its properties are not analyzed.
- Experimental presentation needs improvement: (i) \(\tau\) in Figure 4 is not defined. (ii) How \(\tau\) is chosen for results in Tables 5 and 6 is not explained. (iii) MAE shows very poor performance on CIFAR-100 in Table 3, which is not discussed. (iv) Reporting mean and standard deviation for all tables (not just Table 6) would strengthen conclusiveness; additional runs could provide more reliable histogram estimates.
- The paper lacks discussion and empirical comparisons with curriculum learning and reweighting methods for noisy labels.
- Some relevant robust loss functions are missing: f-divergences, Bregman divergences, and Jensen-Shannon divergences.
- The proposed shifting and rescaling strategy is heuristic and depends on a crucial hyperparameter, hindering practical usage.
- There is a notable contrast in reviewer evaluations: two reviewers (R1, R4, scores 6) find the work valuable and well-written, while two others (R2, R3, scores 5) raise significant concerns about novelty, significance, and limited experimental validation of the key contribution.
