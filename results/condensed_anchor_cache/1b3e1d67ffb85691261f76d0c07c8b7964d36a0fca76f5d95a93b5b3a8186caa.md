## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) as general-purpose preconditioners for iterative solutions of large sparse linear systems. The authors provide a convergence analysis for flexible GMRES (FGMRES), a novel training data generation method that samples from the bottom eigensubspace of the matrix A via Arnoldi, a scale-equivariant GNN architecture with normalization to avoid division-by-zero, and a new evaluation protocol using Iter-AUC and Time-AUC. They test on over 800 matrices from the SuiteSparse collection across 50 application areas, comparing with ILU, AMG, Jacobi, and GMRES. Results indicate predictable construction times, low failure rates, and sometimes faster convergence than traditional preconditioners.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach:** Using GNNs as a general-purpose preconditioner that approximates the matrix inverse without requiring additional problem-specific information. (All reviewers)
- **Theoretical contribution:** Original convergence analysis for FGMRES, which is important because the preconditioner is nonlinear. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Novel training data generation:** Sampling from the eigen-subspace of A using Arnoldi to improve training. (Reviewer 1)
- **Scale-equivariant GNN architecture:** Fresh normalization to prevent division-by-zero and enforce scale-equivariance as an inductive bias. (Reviewer 1)
- **Extensive empirical evaluation:** Tested on over 800 matrices from 50 application areas in SuiteSparse, with novel metrics (Iter-AUC, Time-AUC) for comparison. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Robustness and stability:** Low failure rate compared to ILU/AMG, and predictable construction times versus unpredictable ones for traditional preconditioners. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Relevance:** Preconditioning is a critical problem in scientific computing. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
While three reviewers assigned scores of 6, one reviewer (Reviewer 2) was more critical (score 3) and raised distinct concerns. All points from all reviewers are included below.

- **Scalability concerns (Reviewer 2):** The paper lacks analysis of computational cost and memory requirements for GNNs on larger matrices. There is no discussion of how complexity and memory footprint grow with matrix dimensions, or what practical limitations exist in terms of matrix size for the proposed method.

- **Theoretical foundations (Reviewer 2):** Although empirical results are promising, the theoretical justification for why the GNN architecture can approximate an effective preconditioner is missing. No bounds on approximation error or relation to the optimal preconditioner are provided.

- **AUC metric limitations (Reviewer 3):** Iter-AUC and Time-AUC do not properly weight final residual accuracy. A method that converges gradually to a low residual may be underrepresented, while a method with fast early convergence but a higher final residual can score higher. This can lead to misleading conclusions. The reviewer suggests including a metric that directly measures the final residual.

- **Training data generation and generalization (Reviewers 1, 2, 4):**
  - Reviewer 4 questions the use of Arnoldi for generating training data: if Arnoldi is needed anyway, what is the advantage of the ML approach? The initial sampling from the whole ℝⁿ is not meaningful due to ill-conditioning, and approximating the inverse is difficult.
  - Reviewer 1 asks why the ground truth solution **x = 1** is assumed.
  - Reviewer 1 asks whether there are constraints for using the approach on SPD matrices.
  - Reviewer 2 notes that effectiveness depends heavily on training data quality and diversity. The paper does not sufficiently explore potential biases in the training set or the impact on generalization performance, including overfitting or underfitting due to specific data distributions.

- **Missing comparisons with existing GNN preconditioners (Reviewer 1):** The paper should compare with related works, e.g., Li et al. (2023) (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/li23e/li23e.pdf) and arXiv:2405.15557.

- **Presentation of results (Reviewer 4):** The paper tends to present GNP as the best performer, but other methods may be close, and GNP might perform poorly on other problems. The reviewer would like to see actual numbers or convergence plots for specific examples.

- **Specific technical concerns (Reviewer 4):**
  - Sharpness of Gershgorin estimates: they are not necessarily sharp.
  - Does GMRES really converge in 10 iterations to 10⁻⁶ as implied?
  - AMG works better for symmetric problems. For nonsymmetric matrices, PyAMG offers the AIR approach, which should be considered.

- **Training loss (Reviewer 1):** Why is the ℓ₁ residual norm used as the training loss? Could ℓ₂ or other norms be used?

- **Code availability (Reviewer 1):** The full GitHub repository was not made available for review.

- **Reproducibility of convergence claims (Reviewer 4):** More evidence is needed to support specific convergence rates (e.g., 10 iterations to 10⁻⁶).
