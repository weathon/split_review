## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DODA, a diffusion-model-based data synthesizer for object detection domain adaptation in agriculture. It improves layout-to-image controllability via encoding layout as an image (LI2I) and uses a visual encoder to decouple domain-specific features, enabling generation of detection data for new domains without retraining. Experiments on the Global Wheat Head Detection (GWHD) dataset show that using DODA-synthesized data boosts detector performance by 12.74--17.76 AP₅₀ on shifted domains. Reviewers note the approach is promising and shows significant gains, but raise concerns about limited experimental scope, lack of generalizability tests, insufficient comparisons to existing few-shot domain adaptation and L2I methods, missing ablations and visualizations, writing quality, and the method's agricultural specificity.

### Strengths
- The framework decouples domain-specific feature learning from the diffusion model, improving adaptability across diverse agricultural environments without additional training.
- The LI2I method enhances control over image layouts, leading to improved label accuracy.
- Pre-training with additional unlabeled data is shown to improve the quality of generated detection data.
- The approach enables few-shot domain adaptation using a diffusion model, producing more accurate and contextually relevant synthetic data with minimal training required.
- The research focus on agriculture data is important and will benefit the community.
- Ablation studies explore pre-training dataset size, encoder impact, number of layers, and embedding positioning.
- Significant improvements are observed both in agriculture detection and L2I generation performance.
- The DODA framework is designed to be user-friendly, requiring minimal training.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope and generalizability claims:** Only a single dataset (GWHD, wheat) is tested. This significantly limits the claims about being a unified framework for domain adaptation. Experiments on other agricultural crops/environments and non-agricultural domains (e.g., cityscapes) are missing, making it unclear if gains are unique to wheat detection or transferable. The minority reviewer (rating 3, confidence 5) finds this a critical limitation. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Missing comparisons to state-of-the-art methods:** The paper does not compare DODA with existing few-shot domain adaptation methods (e.g., Jing et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2024, Gao et al. 2022, Gao et al. 2023, Nakamura et al. 2022, Corral-Soto et al. 2021) or general object detection benchmarks. Also, as an L2I generation method, DODA's pipeline is seen as a simplified derivative of ControlNet (ICCV2023), yet the paper does not compare or explain the huge performance gap (ControlNet achieves 25.2 mAP on COCO 2017, while DODA claims 42.5 mAP on GWHD – the reason for this improvement is unclear). [R1, R2, R4]
- **Method lacks agricultural specificity:** The core methodology appears to be a general data augmentation approach using diffusion models; specific agricultural challenges (varying lighting, occlusions due to foliage, small/irregular objects) are not explicitly addressed. It is unclear whether the method offers advantages over existing techniques in such scenarios. [R1, R3]
- **Insufficient related work coverage:** The related work section is dominated by a narrow group of authors in agriculture detection; broader works (e.g., Ghosal 2019, Malambo 2019, Osco 2020, Gene-Mola 2020, Akiva 2020/2021, Hobbs 2021) should be cited. Additionally, the paper does not clearly situate itself within the few-shot domain adaptation setting despite relying on few samples from a new domain in Section 4.2. [R2, R3]
- **Missing ablations and experimental details:** The selection of domain reference images is not explained (random? variance in performance across different sets?). The choice of 200 generated images (line 406) lacks ablation. The specific visual encoder used (line 210) and its generalizability to other encoders are not discussed. [R2]
- **Lack of visualizations and qualitative analysis:** The paper provides only quantitative results. Visualizations of generated images, intermediate feature maps, LI2I effects, detection results before/after fine-tuning, and failure case analyses are needed to understand the method's behavior and limitations. [R1, R2]
- **Writing and presentation quality:** The writing (sentence/grammar level and organization) needs substantial revision, especially in the Related Work and Section 3.3. Fragmented/incomplete clauses and unclear figure captions (e.g., Figure 2b, 2c) hinder readability. Figure 2(c) mostly describes existing text-based L2I methods, with limited description of the proposed method and no depiction of the "Incorporating Domain Embedding" section (3.3). Table 3 should show number of samples, not just percentages. [R3, R4]
- **Lack of innovation:** The layout-text-to-image pipeline is considered a simplified and derived version of ControlNet, lacking novelty in that aspect. [R4]
