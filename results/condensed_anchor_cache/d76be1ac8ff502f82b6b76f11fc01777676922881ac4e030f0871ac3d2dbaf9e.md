## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MCGdiff, a Monte Carlo guided diffusion algorithm for Bayesian linear inverse problems (inpainting, super-resolution, deblurring, colorization). It uses Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) with score-based generative models (SGMs) to sample from the posterior. The method is theoretically grounded with proofs for noiseless and noisy cases. Experiments on simple distributions (GMM, FMM) validate posterior sampling using sliced Wasserstein distance. Image experiments show visually compelling results, but quantitative advantage over baselines on larger datasets is not clearly established.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive background on diffusion models for inverse problems and detailed mathematical proofs covering both noiseless and noisy cases (Reviewer 1).
- Broader applicability beyond images is considered (Reviewer 1).
- Comparative results are impressive; posterior samples align well with exact posteriors in validation studies (Reviewer 1).
- Paper is dense but accessible, well written, thorough and complete; theory is well-developed with all proofs (Reviewer 2).
- MCGdiff provides very good quality samples in all illustrated problems, performing well on difficult cases; code is available (Reviewer 2).
- Addresses a topical problem: faithful posterior sampling with SGMs. The authors claim the first provably consistent algorithm for conditional sampling from the target posterior (Reviewer 3).
- Numerical experiments on simple distributions confirm that the sliced Wasserstein distance between the proposed method and the true posterior (analytical or NUTS) is considerably smaller than for other SGM approaches (Reviewer 3).
- Interesting novel idea to integrate particle filtering with diffusion model; theoretical work is solid and convincing (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 8) was highly positive and highlighted state-of-the-art results, while others were more critical (scores 1, 1, 6; Reviewer 3 and 4 also noted limitations). This contrast in assessment is noted.

### Weaknesses
- Lacks exploration on non-image data; no rigorous analysis of how the choice of prior (GMM, FMM) impacts results across data modalities; sensitivity to hyperparameters not discussed; computational cost and scalability for high-dimensional non-image data are not addressed (Reviewer 1).
- Appendices are hard to read (Reviewer 2).
- The method does not solve inverse problems in the traditional sense (generates realistic samples consistent with observations, not ground truth); cannot yet be used in sensitive contexts like medical imaging; there is a lack of control and interpretability on generated images (Reviewer 2).
- Code issues: the diffusion code appears to be based on DDPM but this is not acknowledged in the main text or appendices; speed issues are not mentioned (Reviewer 2).
- Bibliography omits older foundational references (Fredholm, computer vision since the 1980s) in the first paragraph (Reviewer 2).
- Lacks quantitative summary over a larger set of test images; it is unclear how the method performs under varying noise levels or with more complex image structures (Reviewer 3).
- Contribution point 3 is mainly support for the first two points and hardly an independent contribution (Reviewer 4).
- A pseudo-code or diagram describing the method would make it much easier to interpret (Reviewer 4).
- The method is likely slow (both diffusion model and particle filter are computationally heavy); runtime and limitations should be clearly reported (Reviewer 4).
- It is difficult to conclude that the proposed method outperforms others on image datasets; other ways to quantitatively demonstrate the advantage are needed (Reviewer 4).
- The intrinsic degeneracy issue from particle filter may affect the stability and performance of the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
- Additional questions from reviewers that overlap with these criticisms or request missing analysis: How to make results more interpretable/controllable? (Reviewer 2). Experiments on GM (Fig. 5 and 6) indicate MCGdiff does not sample the posterior in a “thorough manner” – how to handle this? (Reviewer 2). How to deal with noise present in the training dataset? (Reviewer 2). Could the authors propose a metric to summarise performance over a large set of test images? (Reviewer 3). How to quantitatively demonstrate the advantage on image datasets? (Reviewer 4). Does degeneracy affect stability? (Reviewer 4).
