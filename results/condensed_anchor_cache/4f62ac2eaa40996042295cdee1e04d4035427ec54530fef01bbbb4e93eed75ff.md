## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents HumanEval-V, a benchmark of 108 entry-level Python coding tasks adapted from CodeForces and Stack Overflow, designed to evaluate LMMs' visual understanding and reasoning through code generation. Each task includes handcrafted test cases and redrawn images to prevent data leakage. Across 19 evaluated LMMs, performance is low (GPT-4o reaches 13% pass@1, 36.4% pass@10). Ablation studies show that providing textual image descriptions significantly boosts performance (e.g., GPT-4o from 13% to 45.4%). While benchmark curation and experimental analysis are praised, reviewers raise substantial concerns about dataset size, diversity, novelty, and real-world applicability.

### Strengths
- **Novel benchmark for visual reasoning + code generation**: HumanEval-V fills a gap by simultaneously evaluating LMMs’ visual understanding and coding abilities, providing a cohesive indicator for multi-modal models adapted from LLMs with vision encoders.
- **High-quality curation and contamination prevention**: All tasks are manually adapted from original sources and images are redrawn, ensuring that the visual context is essential to solving the problem and reducing data leakage.
- **Comprehensive evaluation and solid analysis**: Each of the 108 tasks comes with handcrafted test cases; extensive experiments on 19 LMMs (proprietary and open-weight) are conducted, with ablation studies showing performance under different settings (e.g., image + explanation vs. explanation alone).
- **Clear motivation and well-organized writing**: The paper clearly articulates the goal of evaluating visual reasoning through coding, and the dataset construction and experimental settings are presented in a detailed, readable manner.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset size and diversity**: The benchmark contains only 108 tasks, derived from 40 core problems. The mutation-based expansion may not introduce sufficient diversity, and many images are reused across tasks. This raises concerns about generalizability, robustness, and susceptibility to overfitting. Most reviewers noted this limitation, with one adding that the 40 core tasks come from as few as 8 Stack Overflow posts from 2020, making the distribution very narrow.
- **Incremental novelty over existing benchmarks**: Several reviewers find the contribution limited compared to MMCode and similar benchmarks (e.g., ChartMimic, Plot2Code). The benchmark pipeline closely aligns with MMCode; the claim of testing higher-order reasoning is not fully justified, as tasks might be solvable through pattern recognition. One reviewer explicitly rated the paper lower (rating 3) citing lack of significant innovation.
- **Rigid integration of code and images**: A minority reviewer argues that many tasks do not necessarily require code-based solutions, making the benchmark feel stiff and failing to reflect how multimodal information enhances code generation.
- **Limited differentiation in model scores**: Many models achieve near-zero scores, making it difficult to distinguish performance between models, particularly among open-weight systems (most 70B models score <4% pass@1).
- **Narrow evaluation scope**: The evaluation lacks detailed metrics for multimodal understanding and code generation separately, and overlooks the potential of using images as feedback signals or for understanding code tasks.
- **Applicability and niche relevance**: The tasks are rarely encountered in real-world scenarios (only 8 relevant Stack Overflow posts from 2020 were found), calling into question whether the benchmark reflects practical challenges LMMs face. Combining multimodal reasoning with code generation may be niche, and the community can test these abilities independently.
- **Missing experiments and clarifications**:
  - Why does “image + explanation” often yield worse results than “explanation alone”? Were prompts adjusted? Does this imply images hinder understanding?
  - Why are LMMs weak even when given only direct explanations, given normal performance on HumanEval+ and MBPP+?
  - Why not consider using images as feedback signals for code generation?
  - No analysis of which image types (graphs, maps, charts) models struggle or excel at.
  - No detailed ablation to verify the effectiveness of modifications against data leakage.
  - More details needed on the structure of human-annotated image descriptions.
  - Clarification needed on the mutation process: to what extent were contexts and images modified? Do adapted questions share similar solutions with originals? How many distinct images are there? Does mutation hurt diversity?
