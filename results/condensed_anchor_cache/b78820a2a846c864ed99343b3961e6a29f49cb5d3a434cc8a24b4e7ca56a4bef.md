## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an inversion and editing method for Rectified Flow (RF) models (e.g., Flux) using an optimal control (LQR) derived guidance that interpolates between a linear path (from the input image to a fixed noise sample) and the unconditional RF ODE. The control parameter γ trades off faithfulness vs. editability. The paper also derives an SDE variant of the controlled flow. Experiments on stroke-to-image synthesis and semantic image editing are shown, with some quantitative and user-study comparisons. Reviewer opinions range from very positive (8) to moderately negative (5), with disagreement about the method’s novelty, empirical strength, and fairness of comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Problem significance**: The paper addresses inversion of Rectified Flow models, which are becoming increasingly important in generative modeling (R2).
- **Theoretical value**: The LQR derivation and the proof that the resulting vector field equals a rectified SDE provide mathematical elegance and insight (R1, R2, R3, R4). Deriving the SDE associated with the RF ODE (Eq. 10) is a nice theoretical contribution (R2, R3).
- **Novel conditioning**: Conditioning the reverse process on the input image \(y_0\) via the controlled ODE is new and interesting (R2). The framework of controlled ODEs may open design space for other applications (R4).
- **Soundness of proposed solution**: The method is sound and results show effectiveness on inversion and editing tasks, with improved performance compared to some baselines (R2, R4).
- **Writing quality**: The paper is well written, clear, and the equations are rigorous (R2). Easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear exposition**: No pipeline diagram, algorithm box, or pseudocode is provided, making the methodology hard to follow. An algorithm or diagram should be added (R1).
- **Insufficient benchmarks for text-guided editing**: Only face editing on SFHQ (“wearing glasses”) is evaluated for text-guided editing, which is too narrow to demonstrate general effectiveness. The paper should compare on well-recognized benchmarks and against state-of-the-art methods from 2024 (R1). R4 additionally notes missing comparison with fixed-point inversion methods (ReNoise, AIDI) that aim to reduce discretization errors (R4).
- **Unfair/non-informative comparisons**: Most comparisons (especially qualitative) are against methods running on different base models (e.g., SD1.5 vs. Flux), making it impossible to attribute differences to the editing method rather than the model strength. The only exception is Fig. 7 with two weak baselines (SDEdit, DDIM inversion) on Flux. Missing comparison to DDIM inversion on Flux with partial inversion (up to a midway timestep) is a notable omission (R3). R2 also requests specification of which models were used for SDEdit, DDIM, NTI in Fig. 5.
- **LQR perspective is equivalent to simpler approaches**: Proposition 3.2 states the LQR solution is simply a linear path between the input image \(y_0\) and a fixed noise \(y_1\), which is exactly what SDEdit proposes. The optimal control framing adds no new insight beyond this straightforward linear interpolation (R2). The main result (Eq. 8) essentially linearly interpolates between SDEdit and DDIM inversion; it could be more efficient to just solve the ODE backward and interpolate with Gaussian noise (R2).
- **Derived SDE not used in practice**: Although Section 3.4 motivates SDEs for robustness to initial conditions, the SDE formulation (Eq. 10) is never used in the main method (Algorithms 1 & 2 in appendix only use the ODE). The SDE only appears in C.3 for numerical simulations. Why not use it? (R2). Similarly, R3 notes the SDE variant is “not explored experimentally.”
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method introduces many hyperparameters (\(\gamma\), \(\eta\)). Only \(\gamma=0.5\) is used throughout, with no sensitivity analysis or two-axis grid showing the interplay of \(\gamma\) and \(\eta\). The paper should specify hyperparameter values per case and analyze how varying them affects results (R1, R2).
- **Unexplored effect of fixed noise sample \(y_1\)**: The inversion is tied to an arbitrarily chosen \(y_1\). The impact of this choice on editing is not studied—different \(y_1\) could lead to very different results, and no clever method for selecting \(y_1\) is proposed (R3). R2 also notes that fixing \(y_1\) is equivalent to SDEdit’s approach.
- **Qualitative results are underwhelming**: On LSUN Bedroom, the edited images (Figs. 13, 15 in appendix) look almost completely unrelated to the stroke input; the L2 metric in Table 1 fails to capture this discrepancy (R3). Age and gender edits (Figs. 16, 17) look very far from the source (R3). The qualitative results in the main text (Fig. 4) appear cherry-picked (R3). R4 also notes that edited faces consistently look straight at the camera, indicating a loss of diversity due to guidance toward the mode (R4).
- **Equations with unknown variables**: Eq. (8) requires \(y_1\), which is unknown during inversion; how is the conditional term computed? (R1). Eq. (15) relies on \(y_0\), meaning the target \(y_0\) is fully known—this raises fairness concerns because reconstructing a known value seems like cheating (almost 100% accuracy inevitable). Many training-free methods do not require \(y_0\) during sampling. Why compare reconstructions in this biased manner? (R1).
- **Controlled ODE may break OT path**: The controlled forward ODE computes a conditioned vector field \(u_t(Y_t|y_1)\) with a fixed noise sample, which may break the optimal transport path of Rectified Flow (R4).
- **Fig. 10 caption error**: The caption states “the number denotes the starting time scaled by 28” but it actually shows the controller guidance value (R2). Also, typo at l.192: “processs” should be “process” (R2).

- **Unaddressed questions about broader applicability**: No experiments on non-rigid editing (e.g., dog → jumping dog), local editing (e.g., red flower → yellow flower), or restoration of blurry/downsampled images via the controlled forward/reverse ODE (R4). No exploration of lower NFEs—would fast sampling (e.g., 4 steps) be possible given that the controlled vector field may break the OT path? (R4). R3 also asks about performance on other flow-based models like SD3.
