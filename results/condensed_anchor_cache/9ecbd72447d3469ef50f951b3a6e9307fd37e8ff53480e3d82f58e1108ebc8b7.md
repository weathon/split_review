## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TLXML, a framework that extends influence functions to meta-learning to quantify the contribution of training tasks to adaptation and inference. It introduces a Gauss-Newton matrix approximation to reduce Hessian inversion cost from O(pq^2) to O(pq). Experiments are conducted on MAML and Prototypical Network for image classification. Reviewers generally agree the approach is novel and technically sound, but find the empirical validation insufficient to support key claims. Concerns include unclear motivation, missing baseline comparisons, ambiguous task grouping, and unsubstantiated storage efficiency claims. Reviewer ratings are 5, 3, 5, 5, indicating a minority (R2) is more negative, especially on clarity and evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Novel extension of influence functions to meta-learning.** The paper is the first to formulate task-level influence for explaining meta-learning, building on the well-known influence functions framework. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Gauss-Newton Hessian approximation.** The proposed approximation is mathematically sound and reduces computational cost from O(pq²) to O(pq). (R1, R2, R3)
- **Task-based explanations via TLXML.** The framework provides explanations that align with user abstraction levels, and the problem is well motivated in the context of safe meta-learning. (R1, R3; R3 explicitly found the motivation clear and well written, contrasting with R2’s view in Weaknesses)
- **Evaluation across multiple architectures.** Experiments with both Prototypical Network and MAML are presented, and the derivation from task-level to group-level influence is clearly explained. (R1, R3)
- **Honesty about assumptions and Hessian pruning analysis.** The authors acknowledge limitations of Hessian inversion, show that pruning improves results, and demonstrate that TLXML consistently identifies the identical task. (R3)
- **Self‑rank distribution exhibits exponential decay**, suggesting that TLXML can reliably pick out the most influential task. (R3)
- **Contributes to an under-explored area** (explainability in meta-learning). (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation is unclear or insufficiently justified.** Multiple reviewers found it hard to understand why task‑level (global) explanations are needed over instance‑level explanations, especially given that meta‑learning models are often evaluated per instance. R2 noted that terms like “local explanations” and “moment of inference” are not adequately introduced, and the link to safety‑critical risks is vague. R4 stated that the advantage of TLXML for XmetaAI is “blurred” and not persuasively shown. *Contrast: R3 considered the motivation well written, but the overall weight of criticism is on clarity.* (R2, R4)
- **Experimental validation is weak and unconvincing.**  
  - The core property 2 (that influence reflects task distribution similarity) is not convincingly demonstrated. Section 5.2 only shows the network ignores noise tasks; the “proper order” condition is too weak. (R3)  
  - No class‑level ranking is provided. In Figure 6, it is unclear whether class “dog” truly influenced the decision for “worm”. (R1)  
  - A suspicious observation (line 465‑466) that class 1 of the test task is similar to three classes in the 1024th training task is not proven to be meaningful; it could indicate TLXML is not optimal for that dataset. (R3)  
  - No direct correlation is shown between the influence value (eqn. 7) and actual testing performance after removing the corresponding meta‑training task. (R4)  
  - Table 1 reports a large gap between training and test accuracy (overfitting); the same analysis for non‑overfitted models (e.g., Omniglot in Appendix Tables 5,6) shows that even with low overfitting, the number of tests with proper score order does not increase, casting doubt on the method’s robustness. (R3)
- **Lack of comparison with a simple baseline.** There is no comparison to a straightforward alternative such as computing cosine similarity between task embeddings. Without this, it is unclear whether the complexity of influence functions adds value. (R2)
- **Storage efficiency claim is unclear.** The paper claims that retaining only I_meta reduces storage, but some form of training data is still required for comparisons with test tasks. The type of information that must be stored is not specified, making the claim misleading. (R2)
- **Task grouping is insufficiently detailed and not justified.** How task groups are formed (e.g., based on clustering or distributional characteristics) is not explained, nor is there a connection to out‑of‑distribution generalization. The rationale for using groups over individual tasks is unclear. (R2, R3 also raised a related question about the apparent contradiction between task grouping and task augmentation in line 219.)
- **Technical novelty concern.** One reviewer suggested that TLXML may be a straightforward implementation of the supervised‑learning to meta‑learning mapping proposed in [1], limiting the conceptual novelty. (R4)
- **Presentation and notation issues.**  
  - Notation is sometimes introduced without explanation (e.g., I_param, I_perf, D_trg, D_src in Section 3; A(T,w) inconsistency in line 159 vs 165). (R3)  
  - Multiple typos: “explain” → “explaining” (line 93), “based” → “Based” (line 121), “metrc” → “metric” (line 262), “validated” → “validate” (line 350). (R3)  
  - Column count mismatch in line 400 (mentions third to seventh but only six columns), and task count discrepancy in line 407 (128 noise tasks + 996 training tasks = 1124, not 1024). (R3)  
  - The paper would benefit from a thorough grammar and consistency check. (R2)
