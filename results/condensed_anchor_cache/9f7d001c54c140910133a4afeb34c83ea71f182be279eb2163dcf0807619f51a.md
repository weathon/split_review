- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 5, 8, 6, 10

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Alignment with Integrated Human Feedback (AIHF), a framework that jointly learns reward and policy from both demonstration and preference data via a bi-level optimization. It recovers RLHF, DPO, and self-play as special cases, and provides a practical single-loop algorithm with finite-time convergence guarantees. Experiments on LLM alignment (Anthropic-HH, Ultrafeedback-binary, Ultrachat200k) and MuJoCo continuous control show AIHF outperforms standard RLHF and SFT, especially under unbalanced data. Reviewers agree the integration is novel and the theoretical insights are valuable, but raise significant concerns about empirical validation, missing comparisons, and insufficient evidence for key claims.

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of IRL and RLHF** for joint reward and policy learning from demonstrations and preferences (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- **General bi-level formulation** that unifies and recovers RLHF, DPO, and self-play as special cases (Reviewers 1,4).
- **Theoretical insights in Section 3.4** explaining why AIHF can outperform two-stage alignment (e.g., variance reduction through weighted averaging) (Reviewers 1,2,4), though one reviewer found the assumptions debatable.
- **Finite-time convergence guarantee** for the proposed algorithm (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- **Empirical gains over RLHF** on Anthropic-HH across multiple Pythia model sizes (Reviewer 1), and improvements over Zephyr-Beta when using AIHF-DPO and Self-Play-AIHF with Ultrafeedback+Ultrachat data (Reviewer 1).
- **MuJoCo evidence** (Appendix A.2.1) shows AIHF outperforms RLHF on continuous control tasks (Reviewer 1).
- **Comprehensive theoretical proofs and explanations** (Reviewer 3).
- **Opens a new direction** for integrating IRL and RLHF in alignment research (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unbalanced data claim unsupported.** Figure 4 (right) lacks an RLHF baseline, making it impossible to verify the claim that AIHF excels when preference/demonstration data are unbalanced (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 further notes the term “unbalanced data” is ambiguous – it should clearly refer to *size imbalance between demonstration and preference datasets* (not label imbalance), and evidence should include comparisons with standard data preprocessing techniques. Minority: Reviewer 4 did not flag this as a weakness.
- **Insufficient comparisons to prior methods.** The specialized variants (AIHF-DPO, Self-Play-AIHF) are not compared against the existing algorithms they derive from (DPO, SPIN, IPO). These comparisons are essential to assess whether the AIHF framework adds value beyond existing methods (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 also asks for direct evaluation of the learned reward model (e.g., correlation with human preferences) to validate the assumption that demonstrations improve the reward model.
- **Evaluation structure is hard to follow.** Figure 2 evaluates the main AIHF algorithm, while Figure 3 separately evaluates AIHF-DPO and Self-Play-AIHF; this makes it difficult to compare the proposed algorithm against its own variants (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 observes that performance gains shrink as Pythia model size increases (Figure 2), leaving this unexplained.
- **No evidence that demonstrations improve the reward model.** The core claim that incorporating demonstrations yields a better reward model is not directly tested – no reward model evaluation (e.g., accuracy on held-out preferences) is provided. Reviewer 3 also worries that the large demonstration volume (orders of magnitude larger than preferences) could lead to overfitting to demonstrations and diminish human feedback influence. Strategies to balance the two data sources are needed.
- **Missing limitations discussion.** The paper does not clearly state its limitations (Reviewer 1). For example, which policy generates samples for human feedback (assumed pre-existing, not collected during training) should be explicitly clarified (Reviewer 3). Minor notation issues (overloaded symbols, unused definition of V_theta) also detract from clarity.
- **Computational comparison unclear.** Reviewer 4 questions whether the true counterpart is standard RLHF (which uses only preferences for the reward model) or SFT+RLHF (which uses the same demonstration data). Standard RLHF also leverages SFT data for KL regularization, which might implicitly average demonstrations and preferences; the paper should clarify this baseline and computational overhead.
- **Section 3.4 could be more rigorous.** Reviewer 2 finds the reasoning over-relies on the assumption |D| >> |P|, while noting preference data (binary comparisons) is often cheaper than demonstrations, making the assumption questionable. Additionally, the claim that RL eventually dominates could weaken the advantage. Reviewer 4 offers a competing interpretation (linearized KL regularization also averages demonstrations and preferences), suggesting the novelty might be less than claimed. The section is not written concisely compared to its importance (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor issues.** Figure 3 should include exact numeric values since differences are small (Reviewer 1). MuJoCo experiments (Figure 5, Appendix A.2.1) miss comparisons to DPO and an analysis of preference/demonstration balance (Reviewer 1). L476 should explicitly reference Figure 4 (Reviewer 1).
