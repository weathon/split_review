## Merged Review

### Summary
TerDiT proposes a QAT method to obtain ternary-weight (values -1, 0, 1) Diffusion Transformers (DiT). It adds an RMSNorm after the AdaLN block to stabilize training by reducing large activation values. Evaluated on ImageNet at 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions with 600M and 4.2B parameter models. Reports 6× reduction in peak memory and 14× reduction in checkpoint size compared to full-precision DiT, with moderate FID degradation. The paper extends the 1.58‑bit LLM idea (BitNet b1.58) to DiTs and deploys 2‑bit CUDA kernels for efficiency. Reviewer opinions diverge: two reviewers (scores 5) consider the work a competent first attempt with convincing results, while two reviewers (scores 3) find the novelty limited and the evaluation incomplete.

### Strengths
- First work to apply ternary quantization (1.58‑bit weights) to DiT via QAT, achieving very low bit-width while maintaining competitive image generation quality (e.g., visual results in Fig. 4, FID in Table 1). Multiple reviewers note this as the primary contribution.
- Identifies the problem of exploding activations in low‑bit DiT training and proposes a simple, effective fix: inserting RMSNorm after AdaLN (inspired by LLAMA). The ablation in Fig. 6 shows clear FID improvement when RMSNorm is used.
- Demonstrates that training ternary DiTs from scratch scales to 4.2B parameters and yields practical memory/checkpoint savings (up to 6× and 14× respectively).
- Paper is well‑written, easy to follow, and presents the architecture and results clearly.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The core ideas (ternary quantization, RMSNorm for activation control) are directly adapted from prior work on 1‑bit LLMs (BitNet b1.58) and LLAMA. The main technical addition is applying these to DiT, which several reviewers consider insufficient.
- **Unclear motivation for ternary vs. other bit‑widths**: No evidence is provided that ternary (1.58‑bit) significantly outperforms binary DiT or offers meaningful efficiency gains over 2‑bit quantization. Reviewer 2 asks directly why a 2‑bit quantizer was not chosen.
- **Incomplete evaluation**:  
  – Evaluation is inconsistent across resolutions/model sizes (e.g., TerDiT‑600M‑G is only shown with CFG in Table 1).  
  – Table 1 results show substantial degradation: TerDiT‑600M achieves nearly double the FID and half the Inception Score of DiT‑XL/2; at 512×512 TerDiT‑4.2B is not much better than the much smaller DiT‑XL/2.  
  – Only one DiT architecture (Large‑DiT) is considered. Other diffusion‑transformer variants (e.g., VDT) are ignored, so the generality of the high‑activation issue is unverified.
- **Inadequate baselines and comparisons**:  
  – The comparison with EfficientDM is unfair because that method uses LoRA, which limits precision. Proper QAT baselines (LSQ, Q‑DM) should have been included.  
  – PTQ methods (Q‑DiT, Q‑Diffusion) are dismissed too quickly; their failure below 4‑bits is well‑known, and a direct comparison with PTQ under similar inference budgets is missing.  
  – No activation quantization is applied, whereas PTQ methods typically quantize activations to 8‑bits, making it impossible to fairly compare inference cost (BitOPs, latency).
- **Efficiency claims lack substance**:  
  – Table 1 reports only parameter counts, not actual model size, memory footprint, or operation counts.  
  – Table 2’s claim of “significantly reduced” inference cost is unsupported; practical speedup from weight‑only quantization is limited, and the paper does not show meaningful latency improvement.
- **Missing analysis and ablations**:  
  – The paper states that using RMSNorm across the full model is ablated in Sec. 4.2, but no such experiments are presented.  
  – The explanation for why RMSNorm alleviates large activations is purely empirical; a theoretical or visualization analysis is needed.  
  – Fig. 5/6 show that training losses with/without RMSNorm converge to similar values, yet FID differs drastically; this inconsistency is not explained (the claim in L260‑263 is not fully supported by the figures).
- **Factual inaccuracy**: The statement (L203‑205) that AdaLN is “more effective than cross‑attention and in‑context conditioning” is incorrect for text‑to‑image DiTs (e.g., PixArt, Hunyuan, SD3 use cross‑attention or concatenation).
- **Minor issues**:  
  – Grammatical error “pachifies” (L192).  
  – Eq. 4 “adaln_norm” repeats “normalization”.  
  – Fig. 2 is overly large for the simple architectural change; Fig. 3 has excessive whitespace.  
  – Several reviewer questions overlap with the weaknesses above: How is α in Eq. 2 initialized? Would a 2‑bit quantizer be better? Why RMSNorm instead of other normalization methods? Would applying RMSNorm to the entire model yield better performance (and thus limit the claimed improvement)? These remain unanswered.
