## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MeshMask, a masked pre-training technique for graph neural networks (GNNs) applied to computational fluid dynamics (CFD). It uses random masking (up to 40% of nodes) during pre-training, an asymmetric encoder-decoder with gated multi-layer perceptrons, and achieves state-of-the-art on seven CFD datasets including a 3D intracranial aneurysm simulation with over 250k nodes. Improvements of up to 60% in long-term prediction accuracy are reported vs. prior best models while maintaining similar computational costs. The three reviewers gave scores of 6, 5, 8 (range: 5-8), reflecting broad interest but also significant concerns. All reviewers acknowledge the novelty of adapting masked autoencoding to GNN-based physics simulation and the extensive experiments, but raise issues about insufficient computational cost analysis, limited baseline comparisons, vague architectural details for masked graphs, and doubts about transfer learning effectiveness across different physics.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach**: The paper introduces masked pre-training for GNNs in physics simulations, adapting ideas from masked autoencoders to the CFD domain (noted by all reviewers).  
- **Consistent performance improvement**: The proposed pre-training consistently improves GNN performance on a variety of physical systems, with notable gains over baseline methods (R1, R3).  
- **Extensive and challenging experiments**: Numerical experiments cover multiple datasets, including a large-scale 3D simulation with over 250,000 nodes (R1, R2).  
- **Appropriate masking scheme**: The authors developed a suitable masking pre-training technique for GNNs in CFD tasks (R3).  
- **Clear presentation and visualizations**: The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, with rich qualitative results and visualizations (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Vague architectural details for masked graphs**:  
  * It is unclear whether the model architecture (multi-grid GNN with W-cycle, encoder-processor-decoder) remains the same during pre-training and fine-tuning (R1).  
  * Random masking may produce disconnected subgraphs; the paper does not discuss how the graph structure is preserved or how message passing is affected by missing nodes (R1).  
  * Following from that, it is not explained how long-range interactions are effectively learned on the masked graph during repeated message passing (R2).  
- **Insufficient computational cost analysis**:  
  * The paper lacks a detailed comparison of training/inference time, number of parameters, and memory footprint (RAM) between MeshMask and baselines (R1, R2, R3).  
  * No breakdown is provided of the computational overhead introduced by gated MLP vs. standard MLP (R2).  
  * A fair comparison requires equalizing total compute budgets (e.g., pre-training + fine-tuning vs. training a baseline for the same total steps). The paper does not provide such an analysis, making claims of “training efficiency” unsubstantiated (R1, R3).  
- **Limited baseline comparisons**:  
  * Only basic baselines (MGN, Multigrid) are compared; more recent GNN-based simulation models (e.g., attention-based architectures) are omitted (R2, R3).  
  * GCN and U-Net baselines are tested on only a few datasets, weakening the comparative analysis (R2).  
  * The absence of comparisons with other recent GNN architectures tailored for physics simulation makes it difficult to assess true novelty and effectiveness (R2).  
- **Concerns about gated MLP and architecture novelty**:  
  * Replacing standard MLPs with gated MLPs is of limited novelty; the paper does not thoroughly justify this choice over alternatives (R2).  
  * An ablation study comparing gated MLPs with other architectures (e.g., attention mechanisms) is missing (R2).  
- **Transfer learning and generalizability issues**:  
  * Table 3 shows that pre-training on a multi-physics dataset performs worse than pre-training on the same physics, and pre-training on a completely different physics is harmful to downstream tasks, raising concerns about the transferability of learned features (R1).  
  * Input/output physics quantities differ across datasets; potential incompatibility in latent spaces and varying input/output lengths are not addressed, leaving it unclear how fine-tuning is performed when dimensions differ (R2).  
  * No high-level discussion is provided about why transfer learning on CFDs should be effective (R3).  
- **Missing experiments**:  
  * The method is not tested on out-of-distribution mesh resolutions (R2).  
  * The effectiveness across varying graph densities (scenarios with different mesh coarseness) is not demonstrated (R3).
