## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FairVLM (called FairerCLIP in the manuscript) for debiasing zero-shot predictions of vision-language models, specifically targeting CLIP. It uses reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS) and the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) to jointly debias image and text representations, with closed-form solutions and iterative optimization. The method is flexible: it works with or without labels and aims to handle both spurious correlations and intrinsic biases. Empirical evaluations across multiple datasets show effectiveness. Reviewers note that the method is computationally efficient due to closed-form solvers and random Fourier features, but raise concerns about scope overclaim, missing details, reproducibility, and certain performance trade-offs.

### Strengths
- HSIC is a promising approach for debiasing, and the paper successfully applies it to CLIP feature refinement (Reviewers 1, 3). Using RKHS allows capturing nonlinear dependencies between representations and sensitive attributes, overcoming linearity assumptions of prior work (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The method provides closed-form solutions via Cholesky decomposition with random Fourier features (RFF), leading to 4×–10× faster training compared to gradient-based alternatives (Reviewer 1). The iterative optimization with closed-form solvers is computationally efficient (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Flexibility: FairVLM debiases both image and text representations jointly (Reviewer 4), works in both label-available and label-absent settings (Reviewers 2, 3, 4), and is effective under sample-limited conditions, significantly outperforming baselines when other methods fail (Reviewers 2, 3; see also CFD results in Table 3).
- Achieves appreciable accuracy gains on benchmark fairness and spurious correlation datasets (Reviewer 1). Ablation studies provide solid understanding of variables affecting performance (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written and clear in organization (Reviewer 3); words are fluent (Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 2 (score 8) is particularly positive, stating the method is a single general method that outperforms specialized solutions for different scenarios, but notes low confidence in understanding the method (confidence 1). Reviewer 1 acknowledges the contributions as sound.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope overclaimed:** The terminology “VLM” is too broad; the paper focuses only on a narrow feature refinement method on frozen CLIP, not the full VLM family (e.g., VQA, VLP, multimodal generation). The title and framing should be toned down to “debiasing CLIP zero-shot predictions” (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing related works:** Several relevant prior works are not discussed, including HSIC-based debiasing (Quadrianto et al. 2019, Bahng et al. 2020), MMD for fairness (Jung et al. 2021), semi-supervised debiasing (Jung et al. 2022), robust fine-tuning methods (Wortsman et al. 2022, So et al. 2022, Vogt-Lowell et al. 2023), and other recent zero-shot robustness works (Chen et al. 2023, Adila et al. 2023, An et al. 2023) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unclear method details and reproducibility issues:** The encoder parameters (linear projection vs. kernel choice), kernel hyperparameters (RBF kernel parameter, dimensionality *r*), and learning hyperparameters (number of iterations, batch size) are not provided. This makes reproduction extremely difficult (Reviewer 1). Reviewers 2 and 3 also note the method is complex and hard to understand; Reviewer 2 admits inability to understand the method even though scoring it highly.
- **Not fully zero-shot:** The method requires parameter tuning (e.g., RBF kernel) and a separate training set or stacking test data for cross-validation. It cannot be trained on a single test sample, limiting its applicability in scenarios with only a few samples (Reviewer 3). Questions: Can this be extended to single-point debiasing? (Reviewer 3).
- **Performance concerns on labeled data:** When labels are available, FairVLM sacrifices average accuracy more than other methods such as Contrastive Adapter (Table 2, w/ labels). On CelebA, performance is similar with and without labels, raising questions about effectiveness when labels are available (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 states that experiment results on datasets with labels are not good enough.
- **CFD limitation:** Table 3 results show that when initial zero-shot predictions are poor, the pseudo-labeling process may suffer, highlighting a potential failure mode (Reviewer 3). Questions: How do other zero-shot methods compare on CFD? (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of theoretical convergence guarantee:** There is no convergence guarantee for Algorithm 1 (FairVLM Training Without Labels); errors from the initialization step may propagate through iterations (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear why RKHS aids debiasing:** While the universal approximation property of RKHS is mentioned, it is not clearly explained how and why this property specifically helps in debiasing the original representations beyond linear methods (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing unimodal baselines:** The paper does not compare with baselines that debias only image or only text representations (Reviewer 4).
