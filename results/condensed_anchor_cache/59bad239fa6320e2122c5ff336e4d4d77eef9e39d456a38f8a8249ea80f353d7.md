## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces LLM-AAI, a framework that evaluates physical common-sense reasoning in large language models (LLMs) by placing them in the Animal-AI (AAI) 3D embodied environment. Tasks replicate cognitive-science experiments on distance estimation, object permanence, tool use, and navigation. The framework allows comparison with deep reinforcement learning agents, human children, and non-human animals. Results show LLMs are outperformed by human children on these tasks. Most reviewers acknowledge the novelty of embodied physical-reasoning evaluation but raise concerns about the environment’s simplicity, the action design’s arbitrariness, and the paper’s lack of deeper insight beyond comparative scores.

### Strengths

- **Novel embodied evaluation paradigm**: The LLM-AAI framework shifts physical common-sense evaluation from static benchmarks to a dynamic, interactive 3D environment, allowing agents to demonstrate understanding through actions rather than question-answering. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3]
- **Controlled, interpretable setup**: The minimalist Animal-AI environment permits systematic testing of fundamental physical concepts (e.g., object permanence, tool use) in a setting where performance can be cleanly attributed and compared across LLMs, RL agents, and human children. [Reviewer 1]
- **Significance and clarity**: The framework could set a new standard for LLM evaluation by addressing the need for ecologically valid, action-based assessments of physical reasoning. The paper is well-organized, with a logical flow from problem setup to results. [Reviewer 3]

### Weaknesses

- **Overly simplistic environment**: The Animal-AI environment is a “toy” setup that does not approximate real-world physics complexity. High performance in such constrained settings may falsely imply deeper physical understanding—a pattern seen in prior toy benchmarks. More robust environments (e.g., Minecraft, Omniverse) would better reveal practical physical competencies. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2; both confident, Reviewer 1 strong]
- **Lack of novel insight**: The paper primarily documents performance differences (LLMs vs. children vs. RL agents) without offering interpretations or mechanisms underlying those differences. It is unclear what the community can learn beyond comparative scores, raising questions about broader applicability and utility. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 soundness 2, Reviewer 2 soundness 1]
- **Unclear central contribution**: Much of the effort appears devoted to API and prompt design rather than advancing understanding of physical reasoning. It is unclear what real-world physical phenomena are represented in the environment and how the results demonstrate that VLMs (or LLMs) actually *reason* about them. [Reviewer 2; low confidence but strong criticism]
- **Misleading terminology (LLM vs. VLM)**: The paper refers to LLMs, but observations are primarily image-based, making “VLM” (Vision-Language Model) more accurate. [Reviewer 2]
- **Arbitrary and unbalanced action design**: The action protocol (“Go”, “Turn”, “Think”) feels unjustified. “Go” and “Turn” are highly specific to the Animal-AI environment, while “Think” is overly generic and not clearly distinguished from alternatives like “Memorize” or “Sense”. The design appears driven by constraints of the environment and the ReAct framework rather than a principled set for evaluating physical reasoning. A more comprehensive set (e.g., “Use Tool”, “Jump”) would better assess physical common sense. [Reviewer 3; high confidence, strong criticism]
- **Potentially unbalanced comparison to children**: The comparison to human children may be unfair because the LLM agent is limited to only three actions, whereas children could perform more flexible behaviors. [Reviewer 3; note: Reviewer 1 sees the comparison as a strength, indicating disagreement]
- **Benchmark only serves testing, not training**: The framework is solely evaluative; it does not clarify how it could directly support model training or help develop improved physical reasoning abilities in LLMs. [Reviewer 3]
