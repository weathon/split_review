## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers rated the paper 5–8, with one particularly positive reviewer (8) praising the novel domain and comprehensive evaluation, while others (6,6,5) raised concerns about task validity, missing experiments, and depth of analysis. OBI-Bench covers 5,523 images across recognition, rejoining, classification, retrieval, and deciphering tasks, evaluating 23 LMMs (6 proprietary, 17 open-source). All reviewers agree the benchmark addresses an important and underexplored problem, but several key limitations are identified.

### Strengths
1. **Novel and impactful domain** – Systematic evaluation of LMMs for ancient script analysis, with potential to accelerate archaeological research and cultural heritage preservation (Reviewer 1). Addresses a completely unexplored area in LMM research (Reviewer 4).
2. **Comprehensive task coverage** – Five key domain problems (recognition, rejoining, classification, retrieval, deciphering) spanning excavation to synthesis, coarse-grained to fine-grained (Reviewers 1,3,4). Tasks go beyond standard evaluations to include recovery and interpretation (Reviewer 4).
3. **Diverse dataset** – 5,523 images from archaeological findings, including multiple font stages (original bone, rubbings, fragments, single characters, handprints) (Reviewers 1,4). Specific data curation per task (Reviewer 1).
4. **Extensive baseline** – Evaluation of 6 proprietary and 17 open-source LMMs, providing a broad and consistent baseline (Reviewers 1,3,4). Results align with other LMM benchmarks (Reviewer 3).
5. **Potential for process supervision** – Design of "How" and "Where" questions offers a framework for exploring process monitoring in OBI research (Reviewer 2). Also tests expert-level challenges requiring deep domain knowledge (Reviewer 4).
6. **Notable deciphering performance** – LMMs comparable to untrained humans in deciphering, indicating potential for creative interpretative perspectives (Reviewer 1, citing abstract). The minority reviewer (8) was particularly positive about the benchmark’s overall contribution.

### Weaknesses
1. **Questionable task definitions** – Rejoining task may only assess high-frequency image textures rather than OBI semantics (Reviewer 3). Deciphering evaluation using BERTScore may be biased due to sensitivity to surface-level textual similarities rather than deeper semantic understanding (Reviewer 3). Overall evaluation is highly sensitive to query phrasing; prompt engineering analysis is missing (Reviewer 1). For example, what happens when the query does not include the word “OBI”? (Reviewer 1, Q). Additionally, "yes-or-no" and "how" queries may be redundant; their irrelevance should be shown (Reviewer 3, Q). Clarify meaning of “with different frequency levels F” in Line 471 (Reviewer 3, Q).
2. **Missing fine-tuned open-source results** – Lack of fine-tuned models limits assessment of domain-specific adaptation, which is crucial for practical OBS analysis (Reviewer 3).
3. **Insufficient analysis of model failures** – No investigation into why open LMMs perform poorly; essential to consider parameter size, training data, architectural biases (Reviewers 1,3). Reviewer 1 suggests testing correlation between performance and parameter size (e.g., fix LMM type, vary parameters). Reviewer 1 also asks whether scaling (larger models, larger context windows) is the issue given stress-testing results in Fig. 3 (Q). The paper does not discuss impact of model architectures or computational requirements (Reviewer 1).
4. **Data imbalance and limited scale** – Long-tail data issues: excessive data for Recognition, insufficient for Deciphering (Reviewer 2). Dataset diversity could be enhanced with more images from various sources and time periods (Reviewer 4). Unclear how many images are original vs. pseudo-OBI, and what the evaluation differences are (Reviewer 1).
5. **Missing overall and human performance scores** – No aggregate metric for comprehensive ability, considering early LMM stages in OBI; a dynamic evaluation score would be beneficial (Reviewer 2). Human performance is only provided in Table 2; should be extended to all tables (Reviewer 1, Q).
6. **Recall metric granularity** – Table 5 only shows Recall@1 and Recall@10; Recall@3 or Recall@5 would be more meaningful as users typically care about top-3/5 (Reviewer 1).
7. **Lack of interpretability and longitudinal analysis** – No insight into how LMMs make decisions, especially on deciphering tasks (Reviewer 4). The paper only evaluates at a single point in time; a longitudinal study tracking model versions would measure progress (Reviewer 4, Q). Also, cultural bias from training data/developers should be investigated (Reviewer 4, Q).
