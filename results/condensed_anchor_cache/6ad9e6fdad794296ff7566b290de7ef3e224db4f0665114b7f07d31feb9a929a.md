## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes GeoMeter, a suite of synthetic benchmark datasets (2D and 3D) to evaluate depth and height perception in large Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It tests 18 state-of-the-art VLMs (open- and closed-source) plus human performance using visual question answering (multiple-choice and true/false) about object order and height. Results show that all tested VLMs struggle with these tasks, even with chain-of-thought prompting, indicating an inherent limitation.

### Strengths
- **Novel focus on depth/height perception**: Assessing VLMs’ capabilities in these specific geometric dimensions is novel and not emphasized by existing VQA benchmarks (R1, R4).
- **Clear and detailed presentation**: Figures are informative; the analysis is thorough and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- **Comprehensive model coverage**: 18 VLMs are evaluated, including both open-source and closed-source models, and human performance is reported to calibrate difficulty (R1, R2, R3).
- **Controlled ablation experiments**: 
  - Verifies that VLMs can recognize basic shapes and lines, isolating the failure to depth/height reasoning (R1).
  - Tests chain-of-thought prompting, showing that poor performance is not due to prompting quality but an inherent limitation (R1).
- **Programmatic generation of synthetic data**: The datasets are generated randomly, ensuring control over factors like object count, shapes, colors, and materials (R2).
- **Exploration of spatial reasoning deficits**: The paper addresses an important area currently lacking a dedicated benchmark in the community (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and scope**:
  - The depth task is highly similar to existing CLEVR, which already requires reasoning about spatial relationships among up to 10 objects; the authors do not adequately discuss what new aspects GeoMeter adds over CLEVR or other spatial benchmarks like BLINK (R3).
  - The benchmark covers only two tasks (depth and height) and uses only synthetic images, while more diverse and realistic benchmarks (e.g., BLINK covering many vision tasks with real images) already exist (R3).
- **Utility and justification of tasks**:
  - The height task (counting blocks in a stack) is more novel but its importance for real-world applications is not convincingly justified; the black platform and toyish setting may cause failures simply because VLMs do not understand the task setup (R3).
  - The benchmark’s applicability to real-world problems is unclear; it remains uncertain how performance on these synthetic scenes correlates with VLM performance in realistic, unconstrained settings (R1, R4).
- **Benchmark design concerns**:
  - Objects often fit poorly with their backgrounds, potentially making the benchmark a trick rather than a test of depth/height perception (R4).
  - In GeoMeter-2D, the numbers used are tiny (~200–300 pixels in VLM input resolution), which may be hard even for humans to recognize (R4).
  - In GeoMeter-3D (Height), the flat base often clashes with the background, potentially confusing the model (R4).
  - The dataset lacks control conditions: no experiments with plain white/clean backgrounds to separate difficulty due to visual complexity from difficulty due to depth/height reasoning (R1, R4).
  - The range of shapes, colors, and textures is limited; despite many samples, foreground objects all look similar (R4).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
  - Recent vision-focused VLMs that improve spatial reasoning (e.g., SpatialVLM, Cambrian-1) are cited but not benchmarked; their inclusion is necessary for a complete evaluation (R1).
  - The paper does not propose or test any mitigation approach to enhance depth/height perception, which would strengthen the contribution (R1).
- **Insufficient insight from results**:
  - The main conclusion (VLMs lack depth/height perception) is not new; several prior works already address spatial reasoning deficits (R2).
  - The analysis fails to explain why models perform randomly (even SOTA closed-source on true/false) and does not derive useful insights for improvement (R4).
  - Reviewer 2 questions the claim that the study paves the way for enhanced geometric understanding, as the paper does not clearly indicate which directions are inspired.
- **Potential bias and evaluation inconsistencies**:
  - Some open-source models show a strong bias toward “True” or the first multiple-choice option; however, the authors do not reconcile how these same models can perform well on other general VQA benchmarks if such biases are pervasive (R4).
- **Redundancy in presentation**: Figure 4 and Table 2 essentially display the same results (merged depth/height scores), making one redundant (R3, minor).
- **Minor errors**: In L59, Fig 1 is described as containing two cats but no cats appear (likely a typo/wrong figure) (R3).
