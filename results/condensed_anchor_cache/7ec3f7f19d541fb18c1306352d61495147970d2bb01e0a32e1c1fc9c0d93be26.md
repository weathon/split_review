## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs) with non-stationary rewards and constraints under bandit feedback. Non-stationarity is measured by a corruption value \(C\). The authors propose algorithms achieving \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T}+C)\) regret and positive constraint violation. For known \(C\), the algorithm (LAG-FTRL) uses UCB with confidence intervals enlarged by \(C/N_t(s,a)\) and policy search over occupancy measures. For unknown \(C\), a meta-procedure (Corral-style with log-barrier FTRL) runs base algorithms with doubling guesses of \(C\).

### Strengths
- First work to consider CMDPs with nonstationary rewards, transitions, and bandit feedback, providing theoretical guarantees. (R1, R4)
- Well-written and easy to follow; the intuition behind the algorithms is natural. (R1, R3, R4)
- The constraint-violation metric is strong: unlike the static hindsight optimal policy, the algorithm is not allowed to violate constraints in some rounds because of the \([\cdot]^+\) notion. (R2)
- The known-\(C\) algorithm is intuitive and well-motivated, especially the enlargement of confidence radii by \(C/N_t(s,a)\). (R2)
- The algorithm works when \(C\) is unknown with almost no performance degeneration, using a meta-algorithm with doubling guesses. (R2)
- The paper adopts \(C\) to quantify non-stationarity, bridging the stationary setup and the adversarial setup. (R3)
- Theoretical guarantees achieve a linearly additive dependence on \(C\). (R4)
- The proof is sound. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Static baseline**: The paper uses static regret against the best-in-hindsight policy, whereas dynamic regret is the typical benchmark in non-stationary environments. The authors do not provide strong motivation for this choice; dynamic regret is often considered more meaningful. It is not surprising that FTRL with a corruption guess works under static regret. Several reviewers question whether the results can be extended to dynamic regret. (R1, R3)
- **Lack of novelty compared to prior work**: The problem formulation and technical approach show limited novelty. Prior works on CMDPs (Efroni et al., 2020; Stradi et al., 2024a,b; Wei et al., 2023) have considered related settings. Moreover, the core technique for handling unknown \(C\) (Corral with different guesses of \(C\)) is largely a direct application of Jin et al. (2024) to the CMDP setting. The paper does not adequately clarify the unique technical challenges specific to CMDPs or provide a detailed comparison with these works. (R3, R4)
- **Technical clarity of algorithms**: The algorithmic definitions, especially \(\ell_{t,j}\) and \(b_{t,j}\), are written in a highly technical way. The role of parameters \(\beta\) is not explained in the main text, making the construction of \(b_{t,j}\) difficult to understand. The effect of \(\nu_{t,j}\) is unclear; the connection between this bonus term and the exploration-exploitation trade-off is not obvious, and the purpose of the max operator in its definition is not well-explained. (R2)
- **Missing lower bound**: The paper does not provide a lower bound for environments between the stochastic and adversarial extremes. While the upper bound adapts to \(C\) and is tight in the stochastic case and adversarial order, the absence of a lower bound limits the justification of tightness in the general non-stationary setting. (R3)
- **Missing simulations**: No simulations are provided to illustrate the efficacy of the proposed algorithms or to compare with baseline algorithms from the literature. (R3)
- **Assumption about constant \(C\)**: The analysis seems to rely on the assumption that the corruption level \(C\) is bounded by a constant (or similar). This assumption is not justified for practical scenarios, and the algorithm's performance is likely to degrade significantly if it is violated. (R1)
- **Generality of the meta-procedure**: The authors claim the meta-algorithm Lag-FTRL is of independent interest, but they do not isolate the required conditions on \(\ell_t\) and \(b_t\) for the framework to work, nor discuss its applicability beyond the specific problem. It is unclear if the specific structure of \(\ell_t\) and \(b_t\) in Eqs. (6)–(7) is necessary. (R2)
- **Computational efficiency**: The efficiency of solving the optimization problem in Algorithm 2 (line 5) is not discussed. (R3)
- **Minor**: The definition of \(C\) on line 155 should be \(\max(C_{G^\circ}, C_{r^\circ})\)? (R2)
