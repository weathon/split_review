## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedPCE, a federated learning method that uses low-dimensional client-specific embeddings to generate normalization layer parameters via a small secondary network (hypernetwork), reducing the number of private parameters (32 in experiments) compared to FedBN. The method aims to handle non-IID data and improve adaptation to new clients. Experiments are performed on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and a mixture of Digits datasets. FedPCE often performs comparably to previous methods (especially FedBN) and may offer an advantage when per-client data is very small.

### Strengths
- The problem (personalized federated learning) is timely and relevant (Reviewer 2).
- The method is simple, makes sense, and is clearly described; the setting is mostly reproducible (Reviewer 2). One reviewer finds the writing and structure clear and the method easy to understand (Reviewer 3).
- The experimental evaluation is mostly described well and appears fair towards the baselines (Reviewer 2).
- FedPCE introduces a novel use of local embeddings to reduce the number of parameters needed for fine-tuning during client adaptation (Reviewer 4).
- FedPCE achieves comparable performance while fine-tuning only a fraction of the parameters; improved adaptation with fewer training samples, which is important for FL applications in e.g., healthcare (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
**Writing, motivation, and positioning:**
- The paper is not well written overall. The introduction fails to clarify why FedPCE solves the non-IID problem, what the main difference from existing approaches is, and articulates the limitations of current methods poorly. The related work section is casual, lacking a systematic review, and does not adequately position the novelty. The method section includes unrelated standard techniques (e.g., normalization) without explaining their specific role (Reviewer 1). Conversely, Reviewer 3 finds the writing clear; this indicates a disagreement among reviewers.
- The paper does not present related prior work on hypernetwork-based personalized FL (Shamsian et al., ICML 2021; Amosy et al., 2022; Scott et al., ICLR 2024) and clustered FL (Mansour et al., 2020; Sattler et al., IEEE TNNLS 2020). These are highly relevant and must be discussed to judge novelty (Reviewer 2). Also missing are FedBABU (no head sharing), MetaVers (embedding vectors for personalization without fine-tuning), and FedAwS (embedding vectors) (Reviewer 4).
- The "federated learning" aspect only covers data not being shared; other aspects like privacy, secure aggregation, client hardware heterogeneity, and robustness to client dropout are not addressed (Reviewer 2).

**Experimental evaluation:**
- Experiments are insufficient: only small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Digits). Generalizability is limited. Most results are worse or similar to three-year-old methods like FedBN; lack of comparison with recent state-of-the-art techniques (Reviewer 1). Suggested additions: TinyImageNet, PACS (Reviewer 3).
- FedPCE does not clearly outperform baselines. In Tables 1 and 2, no method clearly wins when accounting for error intervals; FedBN and FedPCE perform comparably, and FedAvg (without personalization) is also competitive. The main difference is fewer private parameters, but this is not an obvious advantage (no communication cost reduction, computational efficiency not justified formally/experimentally) (Reviewer 2).
- FedAvg’s competitiveness on Digits suggests personalization may not be needed in the covariate-shift setting (consistent labeling function). Tables 3 and 5 show embedding dimension and hypernetwork width have small impact, with large error intervals and no significance test reported (Reviewer 2).
- The method only outperforms FedBN when there are very few training images per client; reasons should be analyzed (Reviewer 3). Figure 4 shows promise in low-data regime but is insufficient alone. A rewrite focusing on low-data regime and real non-IID clients (not synthetic) would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 2).
- Figure 3 is unclear; the experiment should be described in more detail (Reviewer 3). Figure visibility is poor (gray background should be removed) (Reviewer 4).
- Convergence rate analysis for both FedBN and FedPCE is missing (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments need to include client participation rates; full participation is unrealistic (Reviewer 4).
- Ablation study requires comparison with other algorithms to prove FedPCE’s validity in changed environments (Reviewer 4).
- The authors should clarify the technical differences to previous hypernetwork-based personalized FL methods (Reviewer 2).
- Section 4.3 states InstanceNorm is used instead of BatchNorm; does this hold for baselines? If so, would FedBN do better with BatchNorm (as originally proposed)? If not, would baselines benefit from InstanceNorm? Same question for the additional normalization layer in the classification head (Reviewer 2).
- How were all methods’ hyperparameters chosen (learning rates/schedule, batchsize, local iterations, aggregation, data augmentation)? Were the same efforts spent optimizing baselines as the proposed method? (Reviewer 2)

**Other:**
- The reference section has issues: arXiv versions where published versions exist, some lack publication venues, inconsistent spelling and capitalization (Reviewer 2).
- Real-world applicability discussion is needed (Reviewer 4).
- Overall, the contribution is considered trivial and lacks novelty (Reviewers 1, 4).
