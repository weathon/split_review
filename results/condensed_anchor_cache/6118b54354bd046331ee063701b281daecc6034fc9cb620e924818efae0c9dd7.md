## Merged Review

### Summary
MEGA-BENCH is a multimodal evaluation suite containing 507 real-world tasks with over 8,000 samples collected from 16 expert annotators. It embraces diverse output formats (numbers, phrases, code, LaTeX, coordinates, JSON, free-form) and uses over 40 custom metrics. The benchmark offers fine-grained capability reports across dimensions like application, input type, output format, and skill. The authors evaluate several state-of-the-art vision-language models, finding performance variations and emphasizing that task diversity is more valuable than adding more examples per task. Reviewers split: two (scores 8) were enthusiastic about the scale and analysis, while two (scores 6) raised concerns about taxonomy transparency, usability, bias, and missing verification. All reviewers agree the benchmark is an original, large-scale contribution to multimodal evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Large scale and coverage**: Over 500 diverse real-world tasks (507 tasks, 8,000+ samples) enabling in-depth assessment of multimodal models across many applications and skills.
- **Fine-grained analysis**: Tasks are categorized along multiple dimensions (application, input type, output format, skill) using a taxonomy tree, revealing nuanced strengths/weaknesses that aggregate scores obscure.
- **Cost-effectiveness and efficiency**: The paper demonstrates that increasing task diversity is more valuable than adding more examples per task for gaining performance insights.
- **Innovative output format diversity**: Moving beyond multi-choice questions to include varied output formats (code, LaTeX, coordinates, JSON, free-form) and developing over 40 metrics to accommodate them.
- **Open-source resource**: The benchmark is released as open-source, providing a valuable resource for the community.
- **Extensive experiments**: Comprehensive evaluation of popular LLMs/VLMs yields many interesting findings (e.g., GPT-4o 3.5% ahead of Claude 3.5; Qwen2-VL 10% ahead of other open-source models).
- **Rigorous construction**: Meticulous annotation process including an annotation GUI and taxonomy tree; data collected from 16 graduate-level annotators from diverse disciplines.
- **Well-written and organized**: The paper is clearly articulated, with effective figures and tables (e.g., taxonomy tree, task breakdown).
- **Original contribution**: Unprecedented scale of tasks addresses gaps in existing benchmarks (limited output diversity, lack of coverage).

### Weaknesses
- **Taxonomy rationale insufficiently explained**: The paper provides little discussion of how the top-level categories (perception, planning, reasoning) were derived, or how the taxonomy ensures comprehensive coverage of real-world LLM usage. Reviewers ask for the full taxonomy list and the conceptualization process. One reviewer describes the annotation description as “the Feynman method” (write the problem, think hard, write the solution) lacking framing.
- **Annotator selection and bias unaddressed**: The disciplines and selection criteria of the 16 expert annotators are not given, raising concerns that annotator-specific biases may affect task design and that models could overfit to annotation styles rather than underlying tasks. Minority reviewers note this as a potential bias.
- **Usability concerns with 40+ metrics**: The large number of output-specific metrics makes the benchmark “unwieldy” and hard to interpret. For example, “GPT-4o is 3.5% better than Claude 3.5” obscure what meaningful differences entail. The benchmark may be difficult to use in practice, limiting accessibility.
- **Lack of objective answers for some tasks**: It is unclear whether all tasks have a single, objective answer (e.g., writing a story containing two animals, generating LaTeX equations). Failure may stem from formatting differences, not model capability.
- **Unclear task design decisions**: Why is only 4% of tasks using 6–8 images while 8% use 9+ images? Why are 16% of tasks open-ended and 22% structured? These distributions affect aggregate scores.
- **Reliance on LLM judge (GPT-4o)**: Using a proprietary LLM as judge introduces potential bias and raises questions about longevity (if a stronger frontier model emerges, the judge may need replacement). Reviewers ask whether different LLM judges produce stable ratings and how bias is handled.
- **Missing demonstration of advantages for expensive/complex setups**: The introduction claims MEGA-BENCH addresses “expensive inference cost” and “unmanageable setups,” but the paper provides no quantitative comparison of inference time, cost, or setup complexity relative to benchmarks like MMBench or VQA. One reviewer questions how the benchmark itself avoids being unmanageable given the many metrics.
- **Lack of scaling laws verification**: The paper presents a snapshot, not a demonstration that the benchmark obeys scaling laws (i.e., performance improves consistently with model size/ training compute). A good benchmark should be verified by scaling laws.
- **No task difficulty analysis**: There is no discussion of the inherent difficulty of tasks, making it hard to determine whether performance reflects capability or task simplicity.
- **Limited details on non-text (image/video) tasks**: The paper mentions multimodal tasks but gives few concrete examples of image/video inputs. Reviewers request adding such examples in Figure 4 and more description in Section 3.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Not compared to Big Bench (which has overlapping tasks) or Visit-Bench (Bitton et al.). Claim that “many examples or tasks are highly similar” (L83) lacks supporting evidence.
- **Potential environmental impact and tool release**: No analysis of computational resource usage. Reviewers ask whether annotation tools, pre-processing pipelines, and metrics will be open-sourced for reproducibility.
- **Generalizability concerns**: Focus on breadth may include niche tasks, limiting the benchmark’s applicability to broader multimodal problems. The tasks’ mapping to real-world industry needs and future trends is unclear.
- **Copyright/privacy concerns**: Reviewers ask whether tasks raise copyright or privacy issues.
- **Scalability maintenance**: No discussion of how the benchmark will stay relevant as new multimodal tasks emerge.
