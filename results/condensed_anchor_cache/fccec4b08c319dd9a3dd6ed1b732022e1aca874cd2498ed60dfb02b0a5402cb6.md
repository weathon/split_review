## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CSGNN, a GNN architecture that jointly evolves node features and the adjacency matrix using principles from contractive dynamical systems to improve robustness against adversarial attacks. Reviewers generally agree the idea is novel and the theoretical analysis is rigorous, but critical concerns remain about the scope of experiments, theoretical justification of robustness, and reproducibility. Two reviewers rated 6 (positive) but with low confidence (2 and 1, respectively; one admits lack of background, the other inability to assess), while two rated 5 (confident, critical). This disagreement underscores that the positive assessments are less substantiated.

### Strengths
- Novel architecture (CSGNN) integrating contractive dynamical systems to enhance GNN robustness (R1–R4).
- Simultaneous evolution of node features and the adjacency matrix is a unique and innovative aspect (R2, R3, R4).
- Rigorous theoretical derivation and analysis of contractive properties (R1, R2, R3).
- Paper is well-written and clear (R1, R2).
- Empirical results demonstrate competitive or superior performance on several benchmarks (R1, R2, R4).  
  (But note: these strengths are partly offset by weaknesses below, especially regarding the theoretical link to overall robustness and the sufficiency of empirical validation.)

### Weaknesses
- **Inadequate literature review and missing comparisons (R3, R4).** R3 details omission of seminal works [1][2][3][4][5] on robustness via dynamical systems; R4 notes missing comparison to “On the robustness of graph neural diffusion to topology perturbations” (NeurIPS 2022). GNNGuard is mentioned but not used as a baseline in Table 1 (R4). This undermines claims of novelty and thorough positioning.
- **Lack of clear motivation for design choices (R3).** Specific unclarified decisions: piecewise constant assumption in eq(6), the gradient operator of A in eq(8), the ad-hoc form of eq(8) and its alignment with theorems, enforcement of symmetry on $\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_l$, and the adjacency matrix update in eq(14). The paper reads as a mathematical exercise without sufficient justification.
- **Theoretical claims of overall robustness are not adequately justified (R4, R2, R3).** R4 argues that Theorem 2’s contractivity (eq.10) assumes fixed $\mathbf{A}$, but $\Psi_{X_l}^{h_l}$ depends on $\mathbf{A}^*$, so eq(11) does not directly apply. The paper fails to explain how individual contractivity of feature/adjacency updates implies robustness of the coupled system to perturbations. R2 asks what the assumptions are behind Theorems 1 & 2 (not clearly stated). R3 questions whether the theoretical results are specifically tailored to poisoning attacks.
- **Reproducibility concerns (R3).** Code is not provided, making it impossible to verify critical aspects such as the adjacency update mechanism in eq(14) and the positive definiteness of $\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_l$.
- **Computational overheads unaddressed (R3).** The full matrix representation in eq(14) suggests high memory and time costs; the authors should report and discuss these.
- **Narrow attack scope (R3, R4).** Only modification (poisoning) attacks (Metattack, NETTACK) are evaluated; no injection attacks or black-box attacks are tested. R4 questions why only modification attacks are considered, given that the paper title and abstract imply broader robustness. R3 notes reference [6] suggests injection attacks are more threatening. This limits the generality of the claimed robustness.
- **Dataset limitations and weak attack settings (R3, R4).**  
  - Suboptimal performance on Pubmed; efficacy may be dataset-dependent, and no evaluation on heterophily graphs (R3).  
  - No experiments on large-scale datasets like ogbn series (R3).  
  - Attack strength appears weak: e.g., under 25% attack GCN still shows >40% accuracy, whereas related work (e.g., Fig. 1 of Mujkanovic et al. 2022) shows GCN <20-30% under similar settings. This raises doubts about the validity of the robustness gains (R4).
- **Adaptive attack claims are invalid (R4).** The paper uses unit tests from Mujkanovic et al. (2022) that are not adaptive to the proposed model. As the original source emphasized, these do not replace a properly developed adaptive attack. The authors should not claim robustness based on these tests.
- **Missing attack details (R4).** It is unclear whether attacks are inductive or transductive, white-box or black-box.
- **Low confidence of positive reviewers (R1, R2).** R1 (score 6) acknowledges lack of background in contractive systems; R2 (score 6) admits inability to assess the paper. Thus the positive evaluations come from reviewers less able to judge the technical content, while the critical reviews (score 5, confidence 4) offer substantive and specific concerns that should carry more weight.
