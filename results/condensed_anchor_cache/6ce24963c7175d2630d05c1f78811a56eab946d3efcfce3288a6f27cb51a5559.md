## Merged Review

### Summary
MOLLEO integrates LLMs (GPT-4, BioT5, MoleculeSTM) into evolutionary algorithms by replacing the crossover and mutation operations in GraphGA, aiming to improve sample efficiency in molecular black-box optimization. Evaluated on property optimization, molecular rediscovery, and structure-based drug design (including protein-ligand docking), it shows superior performance over baselines and faster convergence. Two reviewers rate it 6 (finding limited novelty and missing analyses) and two rate it 8 (praising the selective LLM integration and practical promise). All reviewers express concerns about correctness, generality, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- Selective substitution: Replaces only the GA operations where LLMs can be effective, rather than using LLMs for end-to-end generation, combining strengths of both paradigms (Reviewers 1,4).
- Framework flexibility: Supports both commercial (GPT-4) and open-source LLMs (BioT5, MoleculeSTM), adapting to different resource constraints (Reviewer 1).
- Strong empirical results: Outperforms baselines across multiple tasks; GPT-4 version achieves best results in 15 out of 23 tasks (Reviewer 1). Superior performance demonstrated across 26 molecular design tasks (Reviewer 3).
- Practical applicability: Improves existing molecules from ZINC 250K and handles complex protein-ligand docking tasks, showing promise for early drug discovery (Reviewers 1,3).
- Straightforward implementation and prompting method: The core mechanism is easy to follow; prompting leverages LLM knowledge to improve performance, opening avenues for further advancements (Reviewers 1,4).
- Novel integration of chemistry-aware LLMs into EA genetic operators enhances proposal quality and accelerates optimization (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- Limited novelty: The core idea is essentially a straightforward substitution of genetic operators in GraphGA with LLMs; this is a simple extension of existing approaches (Reviewer 1). Pareto set selection for multi-objective optimization is standard practice (e.g., NSGA, MOEA/D) and already widely applied in molecular optimization (e.g., DyMol) – the paper lacks comparison with these established multi-objective EAs and should acknowledge prior Pareto sampling methods (Reviewers 1,4). Two reviewers find the contribution limited (scores 2/3) while the other two rate it higher (3/3); the positive reviewers see the selective integration as innovative, but the negative reviewers note the lack of methodological novelty.
- Only applied to GraphGA: MOLLEO is evaluated only on GraphGA as backbone. It should be tested on other models such as GPBO, Genetic GFN, Saturn, or Augmented Memory to demonstrate generalizability and robustness (Reviewers 1,4). Including Augmented Memory in score curve comparisons would provide a more comprehensive evaluation (Reviewer 4).
- Risk of retrieval rather than generation: There is a serious concern that LLMs may simply retrieve known SMILES strings from pre-training data, especially for well-known objectives like JNK3, GSK3β, QED, DRD2 – the authors’ statement that generated molecules do not overlap with datasets does not rule out leakage from the LLM training corpus (Reviewers 1,4). For mutation, it is unclear whether the LLM modifies only certain tokens (true mutation) or reconstructs the full sequence; for crossover, how close is the output to a true crossover operation? The prompt “You can either make crossover and mutations or just propose a new molecule based on your knowledge” means the ratio of crossover/mutation vs retrieval is unknown (Reviewer 4).
- Handling of novel objectives: If the LLM’s prior knowledge is intentionally leveraged, the method may struggle with genuinely new, unseen molecular objectives where no prior information exists. Methods like REINVENT and Augmented Memory optimize from scratch and can adapt to such objectives; MOLLEO’s reliance on LLM knowledge may limit this capability (Reviewers 1,4).
- Computational cost: The inference cost of LLMs could be comparable to training deep neural networks for genetic operators, yet no computational cost comparison with traditional methods is provided. The abstract claims reduced expensive evaluations, but there is no discussion of surrogate models typically used in evolutionary computation to address this (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Multi-objective optimization details missing: The paper does not specify how weights are assigned in the sum-aggregate objective. For objectives involving both maximization and minimization (e.g., maximize QED, minimize SAscore), it is unclear whether simple sign flipping (e.g., 1−score) is used and whether this is sufficient. The “Sum” row in Table 2 (sum of AUC top-10 scores) should be clarified as “Sum(AUC)” or “Scalarization” to avoid ambiguity (Reviewers 1,4).
- Diagram and presentation issues: Figure 1 appears to suggest use of a multimodal LLM (MLLM) despite employing text-only LLMs (Reviewer 2). There is no justification for using different prompts for GPT-4 vs BioT5 vs MolSTM; a table comparing designed strategies would be clearer, and the LLM introductions could be moved to the appendix (Reviewer 2). Figure 2 contains a typo: “LLM editting” should be “LLM editing” (Reviewer 1).
- Rationale for objective choices: The selection of specific objectives (e.g., maximizing JNK3, minimizing GSK3β for selectivity tasks) could be better justified to strengthen context and applicability (Reviewer 4).
- Reproducibility: Reliance on proprietary LLMs (GPT-4) may limit reproducibility of the results (Reviewer 3).
- How to ensure correctness: It is unclear how LLMs can produce better offspring based on text descriptions and fitness values given the complexity of molecular structure – there is no mechanism to guarantee that LLMs naturally incorporate domain-specific chemical knowledge for crossover and mutation. Deep neural networks trained specifically on molecular data can capture data-driven representations that may be more reliable (Reviewers 1,2).
- Chemical space diversity: The diversity of the explored chemical space could be improved; additional mechanisms for balancing exploration with optimization might be beneficial (Reviewer 3).
