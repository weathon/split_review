## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel algorithm for Learning from Label Proportions (LLP) that iteratively performs pseudo-labeling via Belief Propagation (BP) on a Gibbs distribution (built from covariate similarity and bag-level proportion constraints) and then uses the pseudo-labels to train a deep neural network with an embedding refinement step. The final classifier is trained on the resulting pseudo-labels. Experiments on tabular (UCI, Criteo) and image (CIFAR-10) datasets show strong gains (up to 15%) over competing LLP methods, especially for large bag sizes. The paper is well structured and clearly written. The approach is novel and combines ideas from Ising models, BP, and iterative bootstrapping.

### Strengths
- The LLP problem is important and practically relevant, especially for privacy-preserving learning. (R1, R3, R5)
- The proposed method is novel: it combines a Gibbs distribution with BP for pseudo-labeling and an iterative embedding refinement that respects bag-level proportions. (R2, R4)
- The paper is well written, with a clear structure and easy-to-follow presentation. (R1, R2, R5)
- Extensive experimental evaluation across multiple dataset types (tabular and image) with convincing performance gains (up to 15% over several state-of-the-art baselines). (R2, R4, R5)
- The method works particularly well for large bag sizes (e.g., 512, 1024, 2048). (R3, R4)
- The algorithm is computationally efficient for large bag sizes and scales to millions of samples with minimal overhead beyond standard supervised learning, due to BP. (R1, R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No convergence guarantees for the iterative algorithm, no assumptions on data or bag distribution, and no stopping rule. The reliability of the method is hard to assess without such guarantees. (R1, R5)
- **Computational cost**: The proposed method is about one order of magnitude slower than the DLLP baseline (Table 4). Running times of other baselines are not reported, and the k-NN graph construction is expensive for large data. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Inconsistent performance across bag sizes**: While gains are strong for large bag sizes, the method often performs worse than DLLP on small bag sizes. R1 notes that the performance is not significantly better in many setups across Tables 2 and 3. This pattern is not explained. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Binary classification only**: The method is only evaluated on binary LLP; multi-class classification is not addressed, limiting generalizability. (R3)
- **Synthetic bag construction**: All datasets use artificially created bags rather than real bag-level data, hurting the realism of the evaluation. (R1)
- **Disjoint bags assumption**: The approach cannot handle overlapping bags, which restricts real-world applicability. (R5)
- **Equation errors and unclear notation**: 
  - In Eq. (1), the first term becomes zero due to the definition of \(y(S_i)\) (the sum of instance labels in bag \(S_i\)), making the equation problematic. (R3)
  - The derivation from Eq. (1) to Eq. (2) is not provided. (R1, R3)
  - Notation \([m]\) and \(|\cdot|\) in Eq. (2) are not defined. (R1)
- **Parameter sensitivity**: The method requires careful fine-tuning (Tables 9–13) with no sensitivity analysis or practical guidance for selecting parameters (e.g., number of nearest neighbors, number of iterations, stopping rule). (R1, R3)
- **Limited novelty of constraints**: The bag-level and instance-level constraints are common in LLP; the way they are combined in the Gibbs distribution needs stronger articulation to establish novelty. (R3) [Note: R2 and R4 viewed the method as novel; this weakness reflects a minority opinion.]
- **Unclear explanation of key concepts**: The analogy between LLP and parity checks is not clearly explained, nor is the rationale for using a Gibbs distribution. (R3)
- **Missing related work from weak supervision**: Prior work combining weak (aggregate) labels with covariate information via clustering or label propagation is not discussed. (R4)
- **BP convergence not analyzed**: There is no discussion of whether BP actually converged, how convergence time scales with dataset size, or if damping/tricks were needed. (R2)
- **No intuition for performance differences**: Why the method works better on large bags (R3, R4) and why it underperforms on CIFAR relative to other datasets (R5) is not explained.
- **Minor typos**: “the iteration seem to help” (should be “seems”, page 7) and unnecessary capitalization “Bag constraints” in Section 6.1. (R4)
