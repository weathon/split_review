## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel attention mechanism that eliminates trainable Q and K projection matrices by reformulating attention as an optimization problem. It uses a fixed number of optimization steps to find a sparse linear combination of values that approximates the query. The method replaces softmax with raising to the 5th power and renormalization (Eq. 6), incorporates random token regularization and sparsification via ADMM. Experiments are conducted by replacing self-attention and cross-attention components in the DETR model on COCO 2017 object detection. All reviewers acknowledge the novelty of the approach but raise substantial concerns about experimental validation, efficiency metrics, and methodology.

### Strengths
- The optimization-based attention mechanism is novel and offers a fresh perspective, not previously seen in the literature (R1,R2,R3,R4).
- Removing explicit query/key projections has the potential to reduce memory usage and parameter count, which could benefit edge devices (R1,R2,R3).
- The use of ADMM and efficient updates (with random token regularization) introduces new theoretical insights into attention design (R1,R2).
- The explanation of the proposed attention mechanism is clear and easy to follow (R3).
- Reviewer 4 specifically highlights the novelty of “meta-learning the attentional value update as a sparse linear combination of the inputs” as a key strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient task diversity**: Evaluation is restricted to object detection on COCO; the method should be tested on other vision tasks (classification, segmentation) and importantly on NLP benchmarks (e.g., WMT, GLUE, SQuAD) to assess generalizability (R1,R2,R3).
- **Missing efficiency metrics**: No latency, FLOPs, parameter count, or peak memory measurements are reported, despite the main motivation being memory reduction on edge devices (R1,R2,R3,R4). Reviewer 4 emphasizes that without such data the central claim is unsupported.
- **Baseline comparison problems**:
  - Table 1 shows the proposed method is inferior to the full DETR baseline at all average precision levels, contrary to the paper’s claim of “superior results” (R1,R4). Only decoder cross-attention appears consistently better in the row-level comparison (R4). The discussion is inconsistent (“maintain model performance” vs. “superior results”) (R4).
  - The choice to compare against DETR without QK projection weights is questionable; the baseline should be the original DETR with full attention (R3). The paper should also include comparisons with recent efficient attention methods such as FlashAttention and edge-oriented approaches (R4) and simple pooling baselines (e.g., PoolFormer) (R3).
  - No experiment fully replacing both self-attention and cross-attention with the proposed method (R3). Partial replacement (only cross-attention in some rows) reduces the strength of the evaluation.
- **Computational complexity concerns**: The method requires computationally expensive operations (SVD, matrix inversion) that raise scalability questions. A detailed complexity analysis and runtime comparison on larger datasets are needed (R2). Reviewer 4 notes Equation 6 suggests the method may still have \(O(n^2)\) scaling (due to combining all values for each query), undermining the claimed efficiency.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: New hyperparameters (λ in Equation 3, number of random tokens) are introduced without an automated tuning strategy, suggesting costly manual search (R3).
- **Methodology flaws**:
  - The paper contains four uses of “We notice” without empirical or theoretical backing (R4).
  - The role of the MLP in the attention explanation (line 142) is unclear (R1).
  - Qualitative results are inconclusive (R1).
- **Manuscript clarity**: The paper appears rushed and underdeveloped; several sections (e.g., interaction between random token augmentation and ADMM) need expansion and clarification (R2). Reviewer 2 suggests aiming for approximately 10 pages with more thorough polish.
- **Reviewer 4’s minority critique**: The reviewer (score 1) describes severe methodological issues, stating that the paper fails to demonstrate any speed or memory advantage over baselines like FlashAttention, and that Table 1 clearly shows the proposed method is worse than DETR in almost all metrics. This stands in contrast to the more hopeful tone of the other reviewers.
- **Unaddressed questions**:
  - Why should a linear combination of values approximate the query? (R1)
  - Is the method actually faster than FlashAttention on target hardware? (R4)
  - The paper uses softmax normalization in standard attention, but Equation 6 uses a 5th-power renormalization; the justification for replacing softmax with this specific function is missing (R4 notes the comparison is unsubstantiated).
  - The title “Optimizing Attention” is misleading because the paper proposes a new attention method rather than optimizing existing ones (R3).
