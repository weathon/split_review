## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LLM-wrapper, a black-box adaptation method for Referring Expression Comprehension (REC). It uses a fine-tuned LLM (with LoRA) to select the most relevant bounding box from candidates generated by a frozen, zero-shot VLM (e.g., Grounding-Dino, Florence-2). VLM outputs (coordinates, labels, scores) are converted into natural language prompts, and the LLM reasons over them to pick the best-matching box. The approach requires no access to VLM weights or gradients, works with closed-source models, and supports ensembling and transfer across different VLMs.

### Strengths

- Novel black-box adaptation that sidesteps the need for VLM-specific fine-tuning and access to model internals; ideal for closed-source VLMs. (All reviewers)
- Only the LLM is fine-tuned once (using LoRA), reducing training parameters; works with newer VLMs without retraining. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- Demonstrates significant improvements over zero-shot VLMs across three REC datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg). (Reviewer 1)
- Supports ensembling outputs from multiple VLMs, reaching performance that competes with some fully fine-tuned models. (Reviewers 1,3)
- Transferable: LLM-wrapper trained on one VLM’s outputs can be used with another VLM at inference, avoiding re-training. (Reviewers 1,2)
- Modest resource requirements: fine-tuning on a single 40GB-A100 GPU. (Reviewers 1,3)
- Thorough ablation studies on training sample size, zero-shot vs. fine-tuned LLM, transferability, and ensembling. (Reviewer 1)
- Well-written with clear motivation and steps. (Reviewers 3,4)
- One reviewer (score 8) is particularly positive, noting that the method “reaches performance levels that compete with some fully fine-tuned VLMs” and is robust through ensembling. The other three reviewers (all score 6) acknowledge the idea as interesting but raise more concerns about limited performance and evaluation.

### Weaknesses

- **Reliance on labeled REC data**: The method requires constructing a new training dataset with bounding box annotations to fine-tune the LLM, partially defeating the premise of a purely black-box approach and limiting applicability where such annotations are scarce. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Limited comparison with existing black-box methods and SOTA REC models**: The paper lacks comparisons with black-box prompting strategies discussed in the related work. More importantly, it does not benchmark against recent VLMs that natively support REC (e.g., Ferret, KOSMOS-2, Qwen-VL), nor against standard white-box REC models like those listed in related work or cited as [1],[2],[3] by Reviewers 3 and 4. Without these baselines, it is difficult to assess the method’s true effectiveness. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Constrained dataset evaluation**: Experiments are limited to RefCOCO series with relatively simple referring expressions (3.6–8.4 words). Additional benchmarks such as Flickr30K Entities, RefClef, and HC-RefLoCo (longer, more complex expressions) should be included to test robustness on spatial relationships, negations, comparative descriptions, and many entities. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Dependency on VLM proposal quality**: The method’s performance is highly dependent on the chosen VLM’s ability to generate good candidate boxes; if the VLM fails to include the correct object, the LLM cannot recover. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inference cost**: LLM-wrapper doubles the inference cost compared to using the VLM alone. (Reviewer 3)
- **Discrepancy in Table 3**: White-box experiments use Grounding-Dino SwinB, while zero-shot and LLM-wrapper experiments use SwinT. Fair comparison requires SwinT white-box results to avoid inflating gains. (Reviewer 2)
- **Inconsistency in Table 5**: Transfer learning rows (GDrec→Flo2, Flo2→GDrec) are reported on only 300 samples, while other rows use the full validation/test sets. Results on the same 300 samples should be shown for all rows to fairly assess transferability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Performance variability across LLMs**: The fraction of fine-tuned parameters varies widely (e.g., Llama 3 4.2% vs. Mixtral 0.24%), and the paper does not analyze whether gains correlate more with LLM capability or number of tuned parameters. An experiment with Llama at 0.42% parameters is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited performance gap**: Even with improvements over zero-shot, the method still lags behind standard white-box fine-tuning and many existing REC methods that do not use LLMs. The gap is only marginally closed. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Failure case analysis lacking**: No detailed discussion of when the LLM fails to adhere to the required output format (e.g., generating incorrect box indices), how it handles ambiguous queries or misaligned VLM proposals, or the effect of entity count in the referring expression on grounding accuracy. An ablation on the number of entities is needed. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Unclear training data composition**: It is not stated whether the LLM is fine-tuned on training data from a single benchmark or combined across all three (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) when reporting results on each. (Reviewer 2)
- **Paper verbosity**: Section 3.4 is deemed unnecessary and space could be better used for more analysis, baselines, qualitative examples, and limitations. (Reviewer 1)
