## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) with a tailored weight prior for simulation-based inference (SBI) under low simulation budgets, aiming to produce well-calibrated posterior estimates. The authors design a prior that makes the BNN approximately well-calibrated a priori by matching a Gaussian process reference prior, and evaluate on four benchmarks (SLCP, two moons, spatial SIR, a cosmology problem) using NPE and NRE. They claim improved calibration in the low-data regime (as few as O(10) simulations).

### Strengths
- **Novelty and relevance**: The idea of using BNNs for SBI is not new, but the paper provides a valuable contribution by explaining why previous BNN+SBI attempts had limited success and proposing a new prior (derived from a GP reference) that is tailored for the SBI setting. The motivation and derivation of the tailored prior are well done, convincing, and elegant (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Broad applicability**: The method is demonstrated across two SBI frameworks (NPE and NRE) and multiple tasks including a real cosmology simulator with expensive runs (R1, R3).
- **Empirical goal**: The method appears to achieve its primary goal of producing calibrated posteriors for low simulation budgets (R2, R1). The coverage results often show improvement over standard NPE/NRE in the low-budget regime (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper is generally well-written and clearly structured (R1, R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental runs and reporting**: Only 3 runs per experiment are performed, and only medians are reported without error bars or standard errors. This makes it difficult to assess statistical significance and the true performance differences between methods. Several reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) recommend at least 10 runs with error bars (e.g., standard error of the mean). Figures 2 and 3 would benefit from showing variability (R1, R2, R3).
- **BNN-NRE posterior quality is low**: BNN-NRE never reaches the nominal log posterior density of standard NRE, even at 1M simulations, contradicting the claim that it is “on par with other methods for very high simulation budgets” (R3). Similarly, for low budgets (100–1000 sims) BNN-NRE often places less mass on the true parameters than NRE, and some coverage AUC values are negative (R3, R4). The results for NPE are also not convincing in terms of posterior accuracy; BNN-NPE is “massively conservative” even with 65536 simulations on the cosmology task (Figure 3), suggesting overestimation of epistemic uncertainty (R4).
- **Underconfidence / conservatism**: The BNN approach tends to be underconfident in some scenarios (e.g., BNN-NRE on all benchmarks, R1). While coverage improves, the posterior mass is overly spread; a high expectation coverage alone does not guarantee a good posterior estimate (R1, R4). The trade-off between calibration and accuracy is not adequately quantified (R1).
- **Lack of method summary and clarity**: There is no clear summary of the method (e.g., an algorithm or a few sentences at the start of Section 3) (R1, R4). The concept of “a priori calibrated Bayesian model” is not defined early or clearly (R4). The steps for prior tuning (Gaussian process parameter selection) are not well explained: lines 254–261 suggest using simulations for prior tuning, but it is unclear whether these are additional simulations or accounted for in the budget (R1). Figure 1 needs clarification: how are the posterior function samples obtained, and is the calibration computed before or after SBI training? (R1)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The choice of temperature T (set to 0.01) and the GP covariance function (lengthscale, variance) are not discussed in enough detail. The cold posterior reduces the impact of the prior, which seems at odds with the goal of using a prior to enforce calibration (R2). The sensitivity of results to these hyperparameters is not explored (R2, R4). The temperature parameter adds an additional hard-to-choose hyperparameter on top of the GP parameters (R4).
- **Missing comparisons and metrics**: No divergence measure (e.g., MMD) is used to directly compare to the true posterior; the EPLD is used but it is not a direct measure of posterior quality (a high EPLD could indicate overfitting) (R2). Additional evaluation of the posterior predictive distribution is recommended (R1). The NPE/NRE ensemble baseline performs similarly to BNN, but no ensemble comparison is shown for the cosmology example, and computational complexity vs ensembles is not discussed (R1).
- **Questionable practical relevance**: It is not clear that the very low data regime (<100 simulations) is of interest to the scientific community; the authors are asked for a concrete example where this is useful (R4). The convergence to the NPE solution is extremely slow (e.g., 65536 sims still overly conservative), suggesting the prior may need alteration rather than adding a temperature parameter (R4).
- **Overstated claims**: The paper claims that “the nominal log posterior density is on par with other methods for very high simulation budgets” but BNN-NRE never reaches NRE’s log posterior even at 1M sims (R3). Also, BNN-NRE has positive coverage AUC, but some values are negative (R3).
- **Missing related work**: Lueckmann et al. 2017 (Section 2.2), which proposes SVI on neural network weights for continual learning across SNPE rounds, is not discussed (R1). The benchmarks (SLCP from Papamakarios et al., two moons from Greenberg et al., SBI tasks from Lueckmann et al. 2021) are not cited (R1).
- **Figures issues**: Figure 1’s credibility plot may have a typo in standard deviations (R4). Figure 3 lacks a labeled legend (R4) and does not show the reference posterior (R2). Many font sizes are too small (R4). Figure 2 curves are noisy and irregular (R2, R3).
