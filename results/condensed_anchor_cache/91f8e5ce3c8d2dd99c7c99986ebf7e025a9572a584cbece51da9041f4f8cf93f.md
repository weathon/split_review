## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Sparse Subspace Variational Inference (SSVI), a method that maintains a fixed, user-defined sparsity level throughout training and inference of Bayesian neural networks by alternately optimizing the parameters of non-zero weight distributions and updating which weights are included via a removal-and-addition strategy. Two reviewers (scores 6, 6) considered the evaluation insufficient and the novelty claims overstated, while a third reviewer (score 8) found the idea novel, well-defined, and practically useful but noted minor clarity issues. The main request across reviewers is for broader empirical validation, especially uncertainty quantification tasks beyond calibration, comparisons across sparsity levels, and correct contextualization against existing sparse BNN methods.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated: Reducing the computational cost of BNNs is important for adoption.
- Novel idea for sparse BNNs, with an intuitive theoretical model and sound inference steps.
- Working empirically on CIFAR-10/100: achieves 10–20× compression with <3% performance drop and up to 20× FLOP reduction during training compared to dense VI training.
- Training-time benefits are shown (FLOPs reduction) and the method sets new benchmarks on the considered metrics (accuracy, loss, ECE).
- The paper provides implementation, and the method is clearly described so that it should be easy to re-implement.
- A decent range of ablation studies is included (e.g., on updating φ, sparsity level, pruning criteria).
- The selection criteria for weight pruning may be of independent interest.
- Performance on the reported datasets and metrics is better than the baselines considered.

### Weaknesses
- **Empirical evaluation is limited** (raised by all three reviewers, with one deeming it a “significant oversight”).
  - Only tested on two medium-size datasets from the same domain (CIFAR-10/100) with a single architecture.
  - No evaluation of predictive uncertainty beyond calibration (e.g., AUROC/AUPR on OOD datasets like SVHN, corrupted CIFAR, distribution shift). One reviewer argues this is a core motivation for Bayesian methods; another requests downstream uncertainty tasks (e.g., misclassification detection).
  - Only a single sparsity level is tested in the main results (Table 2); needed are more figures like Fig. 2 left showing trade-offs across sparsity levels for all baselines.
  - No demonstration that FLOP reductions translate to real time speedups in practice.
- **Missing or insufficient comparisons to related work**.
  - The paper claims to be the “first fully sparse BNN framework that maintains a consistently sparse Bayesian model throughout training and inference.” This is contested: Ritter et al. (2021) also maintains sparsity throughout training (low-rank parameterization), and the claim is mischaracterized. One reviewer notes this undermines the novelty claim.
  - Not compared to sparse SGHMC (Vadera et al., 2022), which could be extended to VI and would look similar; need to highlight differences.
  - No discussion of other efficient BNN methods (e.g., Cobb & Jalaian, 2021).
- **Presentation and clarity issues** (raised by two reviewers).
  - Abstract is simultaneously wordy and vague; a pruning-based description would be clearer.
  - Complex notations; Eq. (2) is unclear and may not be used directly; could be simplified.
  - Missing definitions: in Fig. 1 baselines are not named; around Eq. (4) variables p, B, q, W are not defined; Eq. (5) variable l is not defined; Table 1 rows 2/3 and 5/6 differences unclear; Eq. (11) vs (12) in appendix – which is used?
  - Abstract numbers like “20× compression, 20× FLOPs reduction” and “surpassing VI-trained dense BNNs” need to be qualified (compared to what? and on which metrics?).
  - Minor: “the optimal” instead of “the” in Figure 3 caption.
- **Insufficient analysis of pruning criteria** (one reviewer).
  - Only ~0.5% accuracy variation across criteria (Fig. 2), suggesting they may not matter much.
  - Need deeper analysis: Spearman correlations between scores throughout training, correlation matrices at different stages, to justify complexity.
- **Method details and questions raised as missing experiments**.
  - How are mean values initialized for weights that are added to the active set? (e.g., zero, or reused from previous removal?)
  - Post-rebuttal update: Appendix D should be referenced in the main text, and some figures moved to the experiment section.
  - Interpretation of Fig. 2 right: one reviewer suggests the observed trend is natural (dense VI has more variance than sparse VI) and asks if the paper’s interpretation is missing something.
- **One reviewer found no major weaknesses** and considered the above points minor; the others stressed them as critical for acceptance.
- **Overclaiming novelty**: The method is not post-hoc pruning—one reviewer explicitly notes the paper should not misrepresent it as such; earlier references (Ritter et al.) already maintain sparsity throughout training via low-rank parameterization.
