- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a model-based off-policy evaluation (OPE) method that uses a conditional diffusion Schrödinger bridge to estimate the Markov transition kernel, then computes a policy value estimator from the estimated model. Theoretical bounds with polynomial horizon dependence (\(O(H^2\sqrt{d})\)) are derived. Experiments on four continuous RL environments are compared against Fitted Q-Evaluation and a model-based baseline. Reviewer scores range from 3 to 5 (three reviewers gave 3, one gave 5), indicating significant disagreement over contribution and experimental validation.

### Strengths
- **First application of diffusion / Schrödinger bridge to OPE** (reviewers 1,3,4). The work brings a generative modeling perspective to off-policy evaluation.
- **Theoretical guarantees that avoid the curse of horizon and dimensionality**, with a polynomial rate \(O(H^2\sqrt{d})\) (reviewer 1).
- **Detailed theoretical analysis** of the estimator (reviewer 2).
- **Initial sections are clear and accessible**, making the core concept easy to grasp (reviewer 3).
- **New perspective for RL and OPE with diffusion models** (reviewer 4).
- **Empirical results are nicely done and complement the theory** in the view of one reviewer (reviewer 4, score 5). Another reviewer noted that numerical experiments demonstrate superior performance on some selected datasets (reviewer 3).
- **One reviewer (score 5) was substantially more positive** than the others, emphasizing the novelty of the perspective and the quality of the empirical work; the other three reviewers (score 3) raised significant concerns detailed below.

### Weaknesses

#### Theoretical Concerns
- **Assumption 6 is very strong**: it requires the behavior policy to thoroughly explore the state-action space (support covers that of the target policy) and the reward function to be well-estimated in the infinity-norm. The bound relies on quantities (integrals w.r.t. the optimal Schrödinger bridge, uniform error over state-action pairs) that are not directly computable from data (reviewers 1,4). One reviewer noted that infinity-norm is unusually strong; \(\ell_2\)-norm error bounds are standard (reviewer 4).
- **No direct assumptions on the behavior policy \(\mu\)** (e.g., its coverage of the target policy’s support) are stated, which is unusual for OPE; in the worst case \(\mu\) may be degenerate (reviewer 1). A similar missing coverage assumption (ratio of behavior to target policy finite) is required to guarantee consistency of the estimated transition kernel (reviewer 3).
- **Liu et al. (2020, Corollary 5) already showed** that, given a good approximation of importance weights, the approximate SIS algorithm also achieves polynomial dependence on horizon. Thus the proposed method is not the first implementable method that avoids exponential horizon dependence (reviewer 1).
- **Theorem 4.2 holds only for DDPM-type models** and repeats the proof of Theorem 10 of Chen et al. (2023a) line-by-line (reviewer 1).
- **Theorem 4.1 may be a simple corollary** of Theorem 4.2 via Bellman equations and backward induction (reviewer 1).
- **The theoretical analysis is missing the effect of sample size**; the error bound assumes a bounded statistical error \(\epsilon\) rather than deriving a term that decreases with \(n\) (reviewer 4).
- **Feasibility of the theoretical assumptions** in practice is not addressed; there is no means to verify whether they hold for trained estimators (reviewer 2).
- **Abstract claim is confusing**: the policy error is not shown to decrease with any parameter of the method, whereas Theorem 4.1 depends on approximation error and discretization step (reviewer 1).

#### Novelty and Prior Art
- **Core methodology (conditional diffusion Schrödinger bridge) is prior art** (Chen et al. 2021, 2023b, 2023c) (reviewers 1,2,3). The paper appears to be an amalgamation of the existing DSB estimator and model-based OPE, without articulating clear advantages over simpler approaches such as Gaussian dynamics models with deep neural networks (reviewer 3, citing several papers).
- **Application of diffusion models to RL is not new**; e.g., Wang et al. (2022) applied diffusion to offline RL (reviewer 1).
- **Claim that “using generative model as transition function estimator in RL has not been discovered”** (page 2) is inaccurate; several works have used Gaussian generative models for policy evaluation and learning (reviewer 3).
- **Incorrect attribution of marginal importance sampling** to earlier works (Jiang & Li 2016, Precup et al. 2000, Thomas et al. 2015, Thomas & Brunskill 2016); the marginal IS ratio was first proposed by Liu et al. (2018) and later extended in DICE estimators (reviewer 3).

#### Experimental Evaluation
- **Extremely limited evaluation**: only four benchmark environments, each with a fixed number of episodes. Experiments consider infinite horizons while the paper focuses on episodic finite-horizon MDPs (reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Missing comparisons** with importance sampling (IS) and doubly-robust (DR) methods, making it difficult to assess practical advantages (reviewer 1).
- **No scaling study**: theoretical claims about favorable scaling with horizon and dimension are not tested; the horizon is simply labelled “infinite” without specifying its expected length, and action/state dimensions are not varied across experiments (reviewer 2).
- **Inconsistent and sometimes poor performance**: the proposed estimator yields larger errors than Fitted Q-Evaluation (FQE) or model-based (MB) baselines in two out of four environments; a clear trend (as theory would suggest) is absent (reviewers 2,3). One reviewer (score 5) considered the empirical results nicely done, but this reviewer did not provide a critical assessment of the baselines or magnitude.
- **Sample size is not varied**; a more comprehensive comparison across settings is needed (reviewer 3).
- **Missing implementation details**: neural network architecture, training procedure, inference baseline models, and evaluation details are not provided in the appendix (only a code link) (reviewer 1). The method used to evaluate the integral in Eq. (1) (importance sampling? SMC?) is not explained (reviewer 2).

#### Clarity and Notation
- **Sections 3 and 4 are challenging to follow**; notation is overly complex and would benefit from simplification (reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Section 3.3 (core innovation) is unclear**:
  - Why does the loss need to be masked? (reviewer 2)
  - What does “adding the time to the training parameters” mean? Does time receive gradient updates at every step? (reviewer 2)
  - The relationship between \(p_{\text{prior}}\), \(p_{\text{obs}}\) in Algorithm 1 and how they relate to \(p_{\text{data}}\) and \(Q^*\) is not explained (reviewer 4).
  - How is \(T\) (the horizon) chosen in general? (reviewer 4)
- **Algorithm 1 typo**: “for \(k\) in \(1:K\)” likely meant “for \(k\) in \(1:N\)” (reviewer 4).
- **Unclear whether gradient descent is sufficient** for minimizing \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{SB}\); is a local minimizer sufficient for the theoretical guarantees? (reviewer 4)
- **Abstract and Theorem claims need discussion**: the implications of Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 for practice are not discussed (reviewer 4).

#### Misprints and Undefined Notation
- Transition kernel definition uses “probability” implying countable support; should be a probability density function (reviewer 1).
- “Expectation of the event” and “conditional expectation of the event” – unclear (reviewer 1).
- Misprint: \(f\#q\) with undefined \(q\) in push-forward density definition (reviewer 1).
- Missing \(\mathrm{d} s_{t-1}\) in the integral defining \(d^\pi_t(s_t)\) (page 3) (reviewer 1).
- Equation (5): misprint in \(\Psi\) (also noted by reviewer 2) (reviewers 1,2).
- Excess comma after equation (10) (reviewer 1).
- “lipschitzness” instead of “Lipschitzness” (page 7) (reviewer 1).
- “Finally, The” instead of “Finally, the” (6th line after Theorem 4.1) (reviewer 1).
- Lemma 1: “where” combined with next formula part (reviewer 1).
- Q-martingales and Q-Brownian motion not defined in Lemma 1 (reviewer 1).
- Page 15: misprint in \(\Psi\) (reviewer 1).
