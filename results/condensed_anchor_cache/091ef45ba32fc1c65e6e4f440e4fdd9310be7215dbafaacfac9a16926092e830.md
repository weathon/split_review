## Merged Review

### Summary
This work addresses robust conformal prediction (CP) under adversarial perturbations, building on the CDF‑aware sets (CAS) framework of Zargarbashi et al. The proposed method binarizes Monte‑Carlo smoothed conformity scores via an adaptive threshold, yielding prediction sets that are smaller, require orders of magnitude fewer MC samples (e.g., 150 vs. 10^4 on CIFAR‑10), and remove the earlier bounded‑score assumption. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in set size and runtime over baselines (RSCP, CAS).

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: uncertainty quantification under adversarial inputs with CP coverage guarantees. (R1, R2, R3)
- Significantly reduces the MC sampling budget compared to prior robust CP methods (RSCP, CAS), enabling faster computation. (R1, R2, R4)
- Produces smaller (length‑efficient) prediction sets than existing baselines, mitigating the trivial‑size issue of RSCP/CAS. (R1, R2)
- The binarization procedure is theoretically grounded (R1) and yields a simple, easy‑to‑compute prediction set form. (R4)
- Experiments are extensive and support the claims; comparisons with prior methods are sufficient. (R1, R4)
- Empirical results are compelling in both runtime and sample size improvement. (R2)
- The method is widely applicable and has favorable theoretical properties. (R3)
- Removes a previous limitation (boundedness assumption on conformity scores). (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Conditional coverage not addressed:** The paper does not consider class‑conditional coverage guarantees, which are of high practical relevance. (R1) One reviewer asked about the effect of binarization on class‑conditional coverage and whether information is lost. (R1, question)
- **Limited scope of adversary motives:** Only the setting where an attacker seeks to reduce empirical coverage is considered; alternative adversarial goals are not discussed. (R1)
- **Lack of comparison to RSCP+:** The recent work RSCP+ (Yan et al. 2024) also transforms smooth scores to reduce set sizes. The paper does not compare to RSCP+ or discuss whether calibration‑time robustness (BinCP) can be combined with test‑time robustness (RSCP+). (R1, question)
- **Readability for non‑specialists:** The paper assumes familiarity with “certificates” and Zargarbashi et al., which may hinder understanding. (R2) Notation is introduced abruptly (e.g., Theorem 1) and some symbols (e.g., r in line 238) are not defined. (R2, R3)
- **Unclear conditioning in (1):** The conditioning on \(\tilde x_{n+1} \in \mathcal{B}(x_{n+1})\) seems formally incorrect because the threat model assumes this holds deterministically. (R2)
- **Confusing claim about binary concentration:** The statement that “working with binary variables allows us to use tighter concentration inequalities” is counterintuitive—binary variables generally have the worst concentration among bounded [0,1] variables. This claim is not formally justified. (R2, R4) The advantage for sample size reduction is not explained with a theorem or intuitive reasoning. (R4)
- **Presentation issues:** Several typos (e.g., “finnaly”, missing bracket, “differ” → “defer”) and inconsistent notation (mixed \(\mathbf{1}\) and \(\mathbb{I}\)). (R3)
- **Confusing paragraphs:** Lines 246–250 claim that the solution to Equation 5 is independent of the point and the function \(f\), which is not obvious and lacks justification. Lines 173–181 were also confusing. (R3)
- **Missing theoretical guarantees for sample size:** The paper does not provide formal theorems or guarantees explaining why binarization requires fewer MC samples. (R4)
- **Abstract numbers without context:** The abstract mentions specific sample sizes (10^4, 150) without specifying the dataset or setup. (R4)
- **Minor corrections needed:** The quantile in split CP requires a modification (e.g., including \(+\infty\) or adjusting the level). (R3) The definition of \(\mathcal{B}(x)\) should formally specify that the ball is centered at \(x\). (R3)
- **Two reviewers scored 8** but noted the above confusion and presentation issues; **two scored 6**, placing heavier weight on the unclear theoretical justification, missing explanations, and presentation flaws. (R3, R4)
