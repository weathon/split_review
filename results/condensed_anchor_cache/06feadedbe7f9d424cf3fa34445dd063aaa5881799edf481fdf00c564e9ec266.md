## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers collectively acknowledge that the paper proposes a novel two-step method (data collapse via log-gradient estimator + PGD on an adversarially trained surrogate) to generate transferable unlearnable examples, and that it is the first to apply the out-of-distribution perturbation idea to unlearnable data. One reviewer (Rating 6) found the mechanism interesting and the numerical results against EM and REM impressive, while a more negative reviewer (Rating 3) described the writing as extremely poor and the motivation/justification as lacking. All reviewers express significant concerns: high computational cost (e.g., training $s_\theta$ via DDPM, unscalable to ImageNet), insufficient evaluation (missing baselines like OPS, missing defenses like ISS/UEraser/AVATAR, only $\ell_\infty \leq 4/255$ adversarial budget, no transformer models), unclear method details (role of two-stage PGD, choice of normal distribution, hyperparameter settings), and poor presentation (unexplained log-gradient estimator, undefined $\rho_d,\alpha_d,K_d$, citation formatting). Some questions overlap with these criticisms.

### Strengths
- Novel formulation: model-independent data collapse (reducing data variability) followed by PGD-noise over an adversarially trained surrogate (Reviewer 1).
- First to apply the idea of out-of-distribution perturbations to generate transferable unlearnable examples (Reviewer 3).
- Interesting mechanism to protect data from adversarial training (Reviewer 2).
- Experimental results demonstrate superior robust transferability compared to EM and REM on CIFAR-10/100 (Reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer found the numerical results impressive in general (Reviewer 2).
- The task of generating effective transferable unlearnable examples is important (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost & scalability:** The method requires training $s_\theta$ using a score-matching method like DDPM, which is very time-consuming. As acknowledged by the authors, the approach is currently unscalable to large datasets like ImageNet (Reviewers 2, 3). Lacks detailed analysis of time/resources per step (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental scope:**
  - Missing comparison with important baseline OPS (single-pixel manipulation, strong against $\ell_{\{2,\infty\}}$ adversarial training). Needed to assess relative strengths (Reviewer 3).
  - Only adversarial training is evaluated as a defense; missing recently proposed defenses: ISS, UEraser, AVATAR (compression, augmentations, diffusion) (Reviewer 3).
  - Adversarial training perturbation budget is too low ($\ell_\infty \leq 4/255$). Need experiments with $\rho_a = 8/255, 16/255, 32/255$ to assess robustness against stronger adversarial training (Reviewer 3).
  - Table 2 only includes ResNet-based models; need transformer-based models (e.g., ViT, BERT) to evaluate architecture transferability (Reviewer 3).
  - In Table 1, the method is less appealing than EM on larger datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet Subset) when no adversarial training is applied – explanation requested (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear method details and justification:**
  - The design of the log-gradient estimator $s_\theta$ is unexplained (Reviewer 3).
  - The intuition for setting the predefined distribution as $N(\tilde{x};x,\sigma^2I)$ is not given; could a different distribution be used? (Reviewer 2)
  - The purpose and advantage of the two-stage PGD (lines 13–16 vs. 17–20 in Algorithm 1) is unclear; why not one stage? Could more stages improve performance? (Reviewer 2)
  - The trade-off between perturbation magnitudes $\rho_u$ and $\rho_a$ is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
  - Does the sequence of collapse and adversarial perturbation matter? (Reviewer 1) The model-independent modification step should be positioned against related works; could it be combined as a pre/post-processing step? (Reviewer 1)
  - How does the modified adversarial training (second step) specifically help the generated examples remain effective under adversarial training? (Reviewer 4)
  - The definition of transferability is not clearly interpreted relative to robust unlearning methods; consider whether the aim is universality rather than transferability (Reviewer 1).
  - Lack of motivation for needing transferable unlearnable examples; the contributions and why the method works are not well-justified (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation and readability issues:**
  - The writing is extremely poor, not well-organized, difficult to follow (Reviewer 4).
  - Arguments for how the approach tackles transferability should be presented upfront (Reviewer 1).
  - The second step description (what $f$ represents, how PGD over adversarially trained model is carried out) is insufficiently clear (Reviewer 1).
  - Notation: models $f$ and $s$ are defined with shared parameters $\theta$ – clarification needed (Reviewer 1).
  - Hyperparameters $\rho_d$, $\alpha_d$, $K_d$ are not properly defined; $\rho_d$ is never assigned or used in the paper, and the update on $x$ is unconstrained (Reviewer 3).
  - Citations are not properly parenthesized (e.g., `\cite{...}` instead of `\citep{...}`) (Reviewer 3).
- **Parameter sensitivity:** Algorithm 1 has many parameters; no discussion on how to set them in practice based on data, or how they relate to performance/robustness against adversarial training (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority contrast:** While one reviewer (Rating 6) found the numerical results impressive, the strongest critic (Rating 3) considered the writing extremely poor and the method poorly motivated; other reviewers (Ratings 5) also raised multiple experimental and clarity gaps.
