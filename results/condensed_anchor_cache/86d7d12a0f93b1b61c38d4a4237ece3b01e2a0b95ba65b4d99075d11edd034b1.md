## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies policy gradient (PG) methods for finite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with softmax parameterization. It analyzes two approaches: the standard simultaneous PG (training all epochs together) and a novel dynamic PG (training backward in time, exploiting DP structure). For tabular softmax, the paper provides global convergence rates for both exact and stochastic (model-free) gradient settings, without regularization. The dynamic PG achieves an improved rate of \(O(H^3)\) compared to \(O(H^5)\) for simultaneous PG. Convergence analyses leverage smoothness and gradient dominance properties adapted from infinite-horizon MDPs.

### Strengths

- The idea of using dynamic programming to capture the finite-horizon structure and improve PG efficiency is interesting and novel; the backward-updating concept is a novel application not previously used to prove global convergence for MDPs.
- The paper provides convergence guarantees with explicit constants, backed by experiments.
- The dynamic PG method is new to finite-horizon MDPs and is complemented by strong theoretical guarantees.
- Smoothness and gradient dominance conditions from infinite-horizon MDP literature are successfully adopted to the finite-horizon case.
- The paper considers the coefficient \(\inf_{n} \min_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \pi^{\theta}(a^{*}(s)|s)\) and further shows how to determine its lower bound, providing new insight.
- For the model-free setting, two stochastic PG methods are introduced with proven convergence results; dynamic PG and stochastic dynamic PG take fewer steps than their simultaneous counterparts.
- The paper is comprehensively developed, articulately written, and easy to follow; proof soundness is generally solid.

### Weaknesses

- More comparisons with state-of-the-art PG methods for tabular finite-horizon MDPs could increase convincingness; e.g., a numerical comparison with Guo et al. 2022 (listed in the paper’s references) would be beneficial.
- The claim of being the first to analyze the global convergence rate for unregularized softmax simultaneous PG is contested: there are existing works on convergence rate and sample complexity for stochastic policy mirror descent, which encompass softmax as a special case (Alfano, Yuan, Rebeschini, 2023).
- Given known limitations of softmax (e.g., exponential time to converge, Li et al. 2021), the practical benefits of using softmax (aside from unconstrained optimization) should be clarified. In the unregularized case, optimal policies can be deterministic, making softmax an incomplete policy class—elaboration on the benefit of using an incomplete class in tabular MDPs would be helpful.
- The global convergence results are not especially surprising; the proofs follow established ideas (smoothness and gradient dominance from [Agarwal et al., 2021; Mei et al., 2020]). A deeper discussion of the technical challenges specific to the finite-horizon setting—and how the analysis differs from the infinite-horizon case beyond adaptation—is needed.
- Motivation is somewhat weak: the introduction could include a more comprehensive literature review emphasizing the significance and practical relevance of finite-horizon MDPs (e.g., robotics, resource management), and it does not adequately highlight the unique challenges and benefits compared to infinite-horizon MDPs.
- The presence of the term “\(\exists s \in \mathcal{S}\)” in the equation of Theorem 4.2 makes the result weaker; the reason for this should be clarified.
- Experimental results are limited (though understandable given the theoretical focus).
- One reviewer (Reviewer 1) disagreed with the paper’s statement that “very little is known about convergence to global optima even in the discounted case” (though this reviewer increased their score after the rebuttal).
