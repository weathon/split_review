## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the first Multi-Modality adversarial Attack (MMA) for latent diffusion models (LDMs), modifying both text and image inputs in a unified iterative framework. In each step, MMA constructs perturbed candidates for both modalities based on input attribution, then selects the candidate with the largest L2 distortion on the cross-attention module. Experiments in white-box and black-box settings aim to show higher effectiveness (triggering LDM failures) and higher invisibility (smaller perturbation budget than single-modality attacks).

### Strengths
- Explores an intriguing and relatively unexplored research topic – multi-modal adversarial attacks on latent diffusion models.
- Straightforward, intuitive approach that is easy to follow and implement.
- First study to consider a unified multi-modal adversarial attack framework for LDMs, demonstrating advantages over single-modality attacks on img2img tasks.
- Evaluation metrics cover two perspectives (imperceptibility and effectiveness), which are better suited for comparing baselines (Reviewer 4; contrast: Reviewer 1 and 2 argue these metrics are insufficient – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Evaluation metrics do not directly measure attack success rate.** Metrics like FID and CLIP score changes only indicate a shift in generated output, not whether the adversarial objective (targeted or untargeted failure) was achieved. The paper lacks a clear definition of successful attack and a reported success rate. (Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 agree the evaluation is not convincing.)
2. **Confusing metric choice.** Comparing PSNR between the original generated image and the adversarial generated image is unstable because both are generated images, not inputs; directly comparing two generated images lacks reliable indication of attack imperceptibility. (Reviewer 2)
3. **No human evaluation of imperceptibility.** The paper relies solely on automated metrics (e.g., CLIP similarity) as a proxy; CLIP may not capture human visual perception nuances. A proper human study with diverse evaluators and a well-defined protocol is needed. (Reviewers 1 and 3; Reviewer 2 also notes no visible verification of imperceptibility.)
4. **Weak rationale for multi-modal attack necessity.** The paper does not convincingly demonstrate why attacking both modalities simultaneously is significantly more effective or necessary than attacking each separately, especially given the added complexity. The adversarial relationship between the two modalities is not exploited – Table 1 even suggests the two modalities have no meaningful interaction. (Reviewers 1 and 2)
5. **Lack of novelty and theoretical depth.** The approach is overly simplistic, especially the text search mechanism (simple gradient-based search that does not explore the semantic space effectively, potentially producing easily detectable adversarial text). No theoretical analysis is provided on how image-text interplay affects latent space traversal or distortion compared to single-modality attacks. (Reviewer 3)
6. **Constrained to untargeted attacks only.** The method does not handle targeted adversarial scenarios, limiting practical applicability. (Reviewer 3)
7. **Missing analysis of computational efficiency and overhead.** No comparison with single-modality baselines in terms of time or resource consumption; practical feasibility is not assessed. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Missing references.** Related works on adversarial attacks for diffusion models and vision-language pretraining models are omitted. (Reviewer 4: [1] Diffusion Models for Imperceptible and Transferable Adversarial Attack; [2] Towards Adversarial Attack on Vision-Language Pre-training Models.)
9. **Insufficient comparisons in black-box setting.** Transfer-based black-box experiments lack comparisons with established image transfer attacks like TI-FGSM, SINIFGSM, DI-FGSM. Could Co-attack (a multi-modal attack for vision-language models) be deployed against LDMs for contrast? (Reviewer 4)
10. **No error analysis or limitation discussion.** The paper does not examine when the method fails, why, or what the limitations are. (Reviewer 4) Differences between “Image vs MMA w/o Image” and “Text vs MMA w/o Text” are not explained. (Reviewer 4)
11. **Minor presentation issue.** Citation styling inconsistency (\citep vs \citet) could be improved. (Reviewer 1)
