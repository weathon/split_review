## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sharpness-Aware Bayesian Model Averaging (SA-BMA), a flatness-seeking optimizer for Bayesian Neural Networks, and a Bayesian Transfer Learning scheme. The authors empirically show that BNN posteriors tend to be sharp and that using flat samples improves BMA performance. They provide theoretical and empirical analysis connecting flatness to generalization, and evaluate SA-BMA on CIFAR10/100 for few-shot classification and distribution shift. Reviewers agree the problem is important but are split on novelty, justification, and experimental validity. Reviewer 3 is most positive (score 6), praising the analysis and comprehensive experiments. Reviewers 1 and 4 (score 3 each) and Reviewer 2 (score 5) raise fundamental concerns about the need for flatness in BNNs, the measurement of flatness over a distribution, the clarity of the objective’s benefit over naive SAM+VI, and limited evaluation scale. Reviewer 4 sees Figure 2 as a negative result: a larger ensemble naturally reproduces the effect of flat-mode selection, questioning the need for SA-BMA.

### Strengths
- Targets generalization of BNNs, an important problem (R1, R3).
- Provides empirical and theoretical analysis to support the need for flatness (R1, R3). Sec 3 and the Hessian-eigenvalue analysis (Fig 1,2) clearly demonstrate why flat modes matter (R3).
- Well written and easy to follow (R2).
- Connection between the proposed objective and existing works (e.g., SAM) is well analyzed (R2).
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate effectiveness of SA-BMA (R3). The paper is a resubmission with additional analysis on loss landscape geometry (R3).
- Proposes an interesting combination of sharpness-aware minimization with Bayesian inference (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Need for flatness optimization in BNNs is disputed.** Bayesian model averaging naturally gives more mass to wide optima; using a large enough posterior sample (e.g., 30 models in Fig 2b-c) yields the same effect as selecting flat models (R4). The argument may hold for VI but not for full posterior approximation (R4). This makes the core motivation unclear (R1, R4). The flatness difference in toy experiment 5.1 is marginal (2× radius with worse likelihood); a sharper comparison with equal likelihood and much narrower optimum would be more convincing (R4).
- **Measurement of flatness over a distribution.** The paper uses average Hessian eigenvalues of sampled weights. It is not clear this is a good measure of flatness for a distribution (R2). Also unclear how flatness of a BNN (a set of NNs) is measured – do the authors average flatness of all samples in Fig 1 and 2? (R1)
- **Unclear benefit of proposed objective over naive SAM+VI.** Variational inference (VI) can naively apply SAM to the ELBO. The difference and added benefit of Eq. 4 and 5 over that baseline are not explained (R1). Theorem 1 merely relates flatness of weight-averaged and individual models, but does not show why ensuring flatness helps BNNs (R1).
- **Compatibility with SWAG and MCMC is unclear** – the paper applies SA-BMA to VI, SWAG, and MCMC but does not describe how it works for the latter two (R1).
- **Experiments limited to CIFAR-10/100**; need large-scale datasets like ImageNet to show scalability (R3). Accuracies reported are far from state-of-the-art for the models used (R4). A comparison of LA on SAM solution vs. SA-BMA is missing, though LA is implemented in the code (R2). Performance-over-time comparison is missing given SAM already increases per-step cost (R4).
- **Training cost is high.** SAM training is already expensive; adding VI (sampling for ELBO) makes it more expensive (R2). Reviewer 2 questions how much improvement is gained over LA on SAM solution.
- **VI training issues.** In several cases, VI ends up with higher NLL and ECE than MAP, which seems strange; it is not clear how successful VI training is ensured (R2).
- **Bayesian Transfer Learning scheme is a straightforward application** with low novelty (R1).
- **Figure 5 may mislead** that PTL and SA-BMA change the loss surface (R3).
- **Language** missing articles, reads like a draft (R4).
