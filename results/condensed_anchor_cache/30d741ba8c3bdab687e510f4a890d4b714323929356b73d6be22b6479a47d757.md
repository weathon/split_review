## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers assess a paper proposing unbounded activations for monotonic MLPs, introducing an activation-switch parametrization to remove weight constraints and proving universal approximation for monotonic functions. Opinions diverge sharply: one reviewer (score 8) finds the theory novel and impressive, considers experiments adequate, and sees the work as mostly theoretical; another (score 3) argues the theory overclaims generalization (adding new restrictions like alternating activation sides or non-positive weights), the empirical gains are marginal (no improvement on 3/5 datasets, large variance, no loss curves or optimization analysis), and the paper does not justify that the new architecture is practically useful. Two reviewers (scores 6, 6) note missing references, novelty doubts, insufficient comparisons (e.g., [2], [3]), lack of analysis on gradient vanishing, sensitivity to initialization, and limited datasets; one of them raises unclear writing and ethical concerns with COMPAS.

### Strengths

- Novel parametrization for monotonic MLPs: removes weight constraints by dynamically adjusting activations based on weight signs (activation switch), potentially improving optimization stability and flexibility. [R1, R3, R4]
- Solid theoretical result proving that MLPs with non-negative constrained weights and alternating left-saturating/right-saturating unbounded activations are universal approximators for monotonic functions. Also shows non-positive weights with convex monotone activations (e.g., ReLU) are universal approximators. Proof techniques are clear, well-motivated, and easily understood. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- Proposed method preserves monotonicity and universal approximation while using unbounded activations (e.g., ReLU), addressing key need in interpretable AI applications (fairness, transparency). [R3, R4]
- Experiments on real-world datasets were conducted appropriately and show improved approximation accuracy on some datasets, with adequate support for the theoretical claims. [R2, R4]

### Weaknesses

- **Empirical improvement is very limited:** No gain on 3 out of 5 datasets; on the other two, mean improvement is marginal and variance is larger. Improvement is notable only on the heart disease dataset (Table 1). [R1, R4; R1 is particularly critical, calling the experiments “preliminary” and insufficient to justify “enhancing optimization stability and performance.”]
- **No analysis of optimization behavior:** No training loss curves, scaling behavior, convergence rates, or sensitivity to initialization provided; the claim that activation switch mitigates optimization difficulty (e.g., gradient vanishing) is not supported beyond Table 1. [R1, R3, R4; R3 notes lack of empirical evidence for optimization stability across different initialization schemes.]
- **Theoretical contribution overclaimed:** The paper claims to generalize prior results, but it removes one restriction (bounded activation) while adding new ones: alternating activation sides every two layers (cannot use the same activation in all layers) or non-positive weights (instead of non-negative). This is not a true generalization. R1 argues the paper is not “mostly theoretical” because it proposes a new architecture without demonstrating practical usefulness; R1 also questions whether the proof adds significant insight over existing methods. [R1; minority: R4 finds the theory “impressive” and “novel,” but this disagreement should be noted.]
- **Missing comparisons with recent works:** Papers [2] and [3] are not included in experiments despite using the same datasets and showing better performance on some. This raises concerns about cherry-picking. [R2; R2 notes [2] constrains weights via re-parametrization (exponential) and uses saturating activations, achieving strong performance; R2 says [3] should be added.]
- **Missing references and unclear taxonomy:** Related work categorization (soft vs. hard, constrained vs. heuristic) is incomplete; many relevant recent references are missing. [R2]
- **Gradient vanishing not addressed:** Monotonic networks can work with shallow depth (e.g., 4 layers), but deeper networks using bounded activations may still suffer from gradient vanishing. No discussion or experiments showing that the proposed method alleviates this. [R2; R2 notes batch normalization may already help existing methods.]
- **Insufficient validation of theoretical claims in practice:** The proof relies on specific activation behaviors (alternating saturation sides) without demonstrating robustness to noisy or non-ideal data; real-world validation is limited. [R3]
- **Limited dataset diversity:** Only 5 datasets, none with high dimensionality or complex feature interactions; no experiments on other monotonic domains (e.g., environmental modeling, medical risk assessment). [R3]
- **Writing issues:** Numerous typos (e.g., line 267 missing γ, Fig. 2 messy caption, line 473 incomplete sentence), comma splices, incomplete sentences, and unclear phrasing (e.g., “Such guarantees usually come at the cost of effectiveness” on p.1). Presentation needs thorough revision. [R1, R2, R4; R4 finds the paper “barely written,” though notes proof is clear.]
- **Ethical concerns:** Using the COMPAS dataset without discussion of fairness, bias, or sensitive data implications. [R2]
- **Missing analysis of computational overhead:** Activation switch requires double matrix multiplications; no reporting of training time or memory usage compared to baselines. [R3]
- **Minor questions from reviewers:** 
  - R1 asks why empirical gains are marginal despite theoretical insights.
  - R2 asks whether “Constrained MLP” means “Constrained MNN,” why convex vs. left/right-saturating distinction matters, and why [3] (a recent work) was omitted.
  - R3 asks about choice of unbounded activations over bounded ones in practice, sensitivity to initialization, and computational overhead.
