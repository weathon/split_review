## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes C-MELT, a cross-modal pre-training framework combining generative (masked auto-encoding) and contrastive learning for ECG-text multimodal representation. Reviewers had mixed opinions (scores 3, 5, 6, 8). Positive aspects include novel combination of methods, strong empirical results across datasets, and a new negative sampling strategy. However, significant concerns were raised about novelty (similarity to BLIP), insufficient ablation, clarity of losses and negative sampling, result inconsistencies, missing comparisons, generalizability claims, and reproducibility.

### Strengths

- Outperforms MERL baseline on multiple downstream datasets (Reviewer 1).
- Clear contributions and comparative interpretation with MERL (Reviewer 2).
- N3S provides an effective negative sample selection strategy tailored for medical datasets (Reviewer 2).
- Multiple novel ideas, particularly the combination of contrastive and generative approaches (Reviewer 3 – positive minority; scores 8).
- Strong results across a wide range of datasets (Reviewer 3).
- Clear explanation of the various parts of the approach (Reviewer 3).
- Many empirical studies encompassing diverse datasets and methods (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- [Reviewer 4 – negative minority; score 3] Lack of novelty: MEM, ETM, and MLM losses are very similar to BLIP (2022); this is essentially a direct reimplementation in the ECG domain, which is not novel enough.  
- [Reviewer 3 disagrees] Theoretical justifications for combining contrastive and generative approaches are limited; no clear working hypothesis is provided (lines 60-68). Unclear what complementary strengths are expected.
- Missing ablation experiments:
  - The role of reconstruction components is not properly ablated. Settings of ‘reconstruction only’ and ‘without reconstruction’ are needed; Siglep and ETM should be ablated individually to understand their contributions (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Impact of removing ETM loss is not tested (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
  - The supervised task used in ablation is not specified (Reviewer 1).
- Loss and sampling clarity issues:
  - N3S technique is confusing: selecting the most distinct reports as negatives makes them easier, not more challenging; this also contradicts the principle of nearest neighbours (Reviewers 1, 3). Implementation unclear: does the contrastive loss still consider the rest of the batch as negatives? (Reviewer 1).
  - Siglep vs. ETM: their differences are not clearly defined; both appear to serve cross-modal alignment, so why retain both? (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). ETM performs binary classification with fused features; the hypothesis that alignment of both unfused and fused features is beneficial is not tested (Reviewer 3).
  - Direction of alignment (projections, joint representation, or inputs) and definition of positive/negative pairs are unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - “Siglep” is mentioned without explanation; the cited paper describes “SigLIP”, not “Siglep” (Reviewer 1).
- Comparison to related methods:
  - Missing comparisons to Lalam et al. 2023 and Liu et al. 2023 (Reviewer 3).
  - The method may not be directly comparable to SSL pretraining because it is exposed to diagnostic text labels, which trivializes label extraction (Reviewer 1).
- Result inconsistencies:
  - Table 3 reports zero-shot average 77.71, but ablation Table 7 reports 72.5±9.1 – these are inconsistent. Moreover, MERL’s original paper reports 75.24±1.7, which is higher than C-MELT’s claimed performance (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 2 layout differs from the original MERL table, causing confusion and hindering comparison (Reviewer 2).
- Reproducibility: Authors do not appear willing to share code (Reviewer 4).
- Generalizability concerns:
  - Performance on novel features not mentioned in the textual reports (e.g., age, sex) may be adversely impacted; pre-training on automatically generated reports (known to contain inaccuracies) introduces bias towards concepts in the reports and limits applicability (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper claims scarcity of labeled data, but textual reports can be trivially converted to labels, so the data are not truly “unlabeled” (Reviewer 1). The categorization of the method (self-supervised, semi-supervised, or supervised) needs clarification (Reviewer 1).
- Missing experimental clarifications and details:
  - Text encoder: frozen or fine-tuned? (Reviewer 1)
  - Masking and reconstruction details: Is masking applied on leads or segments? Is MEM loss computed in feature space or signal space? Potential data leakage if features are input to decoder (Reviewer 1).
  - Positional encoding sequence information in multi-head attention calculation (Reviewer 2).
  - Whether implementation necessitates multi-modal data or can incorporate a mix of ECG-only and multi-modal data (Reviewer 1).
  - Automatic ECG report generation possibility not discussed (Reviewer 1).
  - Test on concepts not in reports (e.g., age, sex) as a generalization test is suggested (Reviewer 1).
  - Whether comparative approaches are re-implemented and hyperparameter-optimized by the authors; if not, citations for specific evaluation numbers should be given (Reviewer 1).
- Presentation issues:
  - Abstract percentages (15%, 2%) need specific context (Reviewer 1).
  - References: repeated Li et al. 2024 (Reviewer 1).
  - Test setup in the introduction should be moved to Section 4.1 (Reviewer 1).
  - Table captions missing metrics, labeled dataset, training configuration (linear probe/fine-tune), and score (Reviewer 1).
  - Table 5: metric not stated; why a student model outperforms a cardio resident? Scores need verification (Reviewer 1).
  - Class imbalance in PTB-XL for 1%/10% splits may explain poor SSL performance for labels other than superclasses (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure captions need improvement and more detail (Reviewer 1).
