## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ProtoN-FM, a prototype-guided dynamic normalization mechanism that replaces standard LayerNorm in Transformers to handle distribution shifts in time series foundation models. Learned prototypes represent distinct data distributions, and sample-to-prototype similarity dynamically selects the appropriate normalization layer. The method is validated on fault diagnosis (FD) and human activity recognition (HAR) classification tasks using PatchTST as backbone.

### Strengths
- Novel use of prototype-guided normalization to dynamically adapt to different data distributions, offering a fresh approach for improving time series foundation model robustness (R1, R3, R4).
- The ProtoNorm method is transferable and can be applied to any model using LayerNorm, not limited to a specific architecture (R2).
- Dynamic selection of the most suitable LayerNorm is simple and effective without significantly increasing parameters or inference costs (R2).
- Stable performance improvements over direct pretraining and finetuning when using PatchTST as backbone on downstream time series classification tasks (R2).
- Outperforms existing methods on multiple benchmark datasets; detailed ablation experiments ensure reliability (R3).
- Well-organized paper with clear problem statement, logical progression, and effective figures (R1, R3, R4).
- Demonstrates robustness to distribution shifts, including scenarios with added noise, suggesting practical applicability (R4).
- Provides insights into the impact of distribution shifts, important for high-stakes domains like healthcare and finance (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited task scope**: The method is only tested on classification tasks (FD, HAR). Strong need for experiments on time series forecasting and anomaly detection to demonstrate generalizability (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Backbone choice insufficient**: Using PatchTST—which is state-of-the-art for forecasting but not for classification/representation learning—may make even minor improvements appear significant. Recommend applying ProtoNorm to top-performing time series classification models, or directly comparing PatchTST with/without ProtoNorm against those SOTA models (R2).
- **Missing comparisons with related methods**: No comparison with simple data augmentation, other contrastive pretraining methods, or adaptive normalization techniques like RevIN. Combining ProtoNorm with RevIN would also be informative. This is crucial because RevIN adds minimal cost while ProtoNorm requires pretraining (R2).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No deep explanation of why prototype-guided dynamic normalization improves learning or generalization. How do prototypes relate to dataset characteristics? The orthogonal loss (to keep prototype vectors orthogonal) significantly improves performance despite only 3 prototypes in a 128-dimensional space—what collapse occurs without it? (R2, R3, R4).
- **Dataset diversity insufficient**: Selected datasets may not adequately represent different fields. Expand to finance, healthcare, climate, or weather data to verify universality (R1, R3, R4).
- **Computational overhead not analyzed**: Missing reporting of training time, memory usage, parameter counts compared to baseline; no complexity analysis of the method relative to standard LayerNorm (R3). May scale poorly when the number of datasets becomes very large due to maintaining multiple LayerNorms (R4).
- **Overfitting risk and out-of-distribution behavior**: Reliance on learned prototypes as centroids risks overfitting to pretraining datasets; behavior under more significant distribution shifts is not explored (R4).
- **No exploration of prototype evolution**: How prototypes adapt during training and whether they capture meaningful patterns or overfit is not studied (R4).
- **Unclear implementation details**: 
  - Gating network structure not specified (gated MLP? how does it receive gradients if its output is not fed into subsequent layers?) (R2).
  - How LayerNorms are selected per patch token per layer: is distance computed once for the whole model or per token per layer? (R2).
  - Justification for using Euclidean distance and the prototype update mechanism is missing (R4).
- **Choice of prototype number and scaling**: Why does increasing the number of prototypes (LayerNorms) lead to performance drops (Figure 4)? How is the number of prototypes determined? More diverse datasets should need more prototypes, but scalability is unclear (R2, R3).
- **Missing analysis of failure cases**: No discussion of scenarios where the method fails (R3).
- **Lack of interpretability**: No visualization of how samples map to different prototypes or how normalization parameters change for different inputs (R3, R4).
- **Generalization across different task domains not evaluated**: E.g., pretrained on FD+HAR, tested on weather/finance (R4).
- **Sensitivity to prototype initialization**: Additional exploration of different initialization methods would enhance understanding (R1).

**Note**: Two reviewers (R1, R3) assigned score 5 and were more positive, highlighting the novelty and experimental results; two reviewers (R2, R4) assigned score 3 and raised substantial concerns about baselines, comparisons, implementation clarity, and task scope. R2 indicated possibility to raise score if concerns are addressed.
