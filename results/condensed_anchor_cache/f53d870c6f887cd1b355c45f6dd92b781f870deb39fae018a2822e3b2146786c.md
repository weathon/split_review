## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TV-HMM, a time-varying hidden Markov model for offline change point detection. It proposes a variational EM algorithm that simultaneously detects the number and locations of change points, with theoretical consistency under a Gaussian likelihood. The model is extended to a semi-parametric version using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to relax distributional assumptions. The reviewers note novelty and flexibility but raise multiple concerns about assumptions, experimental setup, reproducibility, and computational comparisons.

### Strengths
1. The variational EM algorithm is designed to be resilient against misidentification of the number of change points, and stochastic approximation reduces the traditional computational burden (Reviewers 1, 4).
2. Statistical consistency of change point location estimation is established under a Gaussian likelihood (Reviewers 1, 2).
3. The semi-parametric TV-HMM extension via MMD removes restrictive distribution assumptions, enhancing robustness to model misspecification and outliers (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
4. The model formulation is natural for change point detection and offers flexibility in learning the number of change points and the likelihood specification (Reviewers 2, 3).
5. The approach is novel and shows good performance and robustness compared to standard methods, particularly when the number of change points is unknown (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. A significant portion of the theoretical analysis relies on the Gaussian likelihood assumption; the paper does not analyze robustness to deviations from this assumption, limiting practical applicability for non-Gaussian series (Reviewer 1).
2. Simulation studies do not demonstrate a clear advantage over competing methods. Performance differences are marginal in both underperforming and outperforming cases, and the paper does not clarify scenarios where the proposed method offers a clear benefit (Reviewers 1, 3).
3. The experimental setup is limited: equally spaced change points are simplistic, and Table 1 lacks standard deviations over 100 runs (Reviewer 3).
4. No computational complexity comparison with other methods is provided, leaving the speed-performance trade-off unclear (Reviewers 3, 4).
5. The ARD prior placed on elements of the transition matrix does not guarantee nonnegativity and sum-to-one constraints. The paper does not compare with a standard Dirichlet prior or explain how Algorithm 1 line 8 ensures the estimates stay in the simplex (Reviewer 2).
6. The left-to-right Markov chain assumption (no returning to previous regimes) may be unnecessary; identical regimes could repeat. Its necessity is not justified (Reviewer 2).
7. The SGD-type update for the posterior (Algorithm 1 line 8) does not respect the simplex constraint on the transition matrix (Reviewer 2).
8. Paper clarity needs improvement: writing is at times unclear, there are numerous typos, and some sections (e.g., role of Section 4) are ambiguous. Implementation details are insufficient for reproducibility (Reviewers 3, 4).
9. Notation inconsistent and confusing: $M_{K+1}$ is used before $M_k$ is defined; $\theta_k$ changes meaning in Section 4; $K$ in Algorithm 2 should be $\tilde K$ (Reviewers 4). Also, $\tau$ denotes both change points and step size (Reviewer 2).
10. The semi-parametric extension (Section 4) lacks experimental evaluation. The choice of the mapping $\phi$ in simulations is not explained, and Algorithm 2 does not update $\pi_{k,m,n}$ but still references these parameters (Reviewer 4).
11. Below Algorithm 2, the phrasing “$Q(\theta)$ must be derived using variational inference” is too strong (MCMC is an alternative), and the discussion of non-parametric density estimation remains vague (Reviewer 4).
12. Figures sometimes lack labels and captions are not self-contained; some references are missing journal/arXiv identifiers (Reviewer 3).
13. The paper lacks a discussion on practical applicability to real-world datasets, including performance with noisy or high-dimensional data (Reviewer 4).
