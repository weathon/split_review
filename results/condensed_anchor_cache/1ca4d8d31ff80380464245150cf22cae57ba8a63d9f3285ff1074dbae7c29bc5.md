## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally agree that M-VAR proposes a reasonable decoupling of VAR into intra-scale and inter-scale modeling, using bidirectional self-attention for intra-scale and Mamba for inter-scale. The statistics on attention cost are appreciated. However, there is significant disagreement about the paper’s contributions and presentation. Two reviewers (scores 5) find the method effective and the paper clear, while two reviewers (scores 3) criticize limited novelty, insufficient justification for design choices, unfair comparisons due to increased parameters, and readability issues. The paper achieves good FID/IS on ImageNet but concerns about generalization beyond class-conditional generation remain.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear and reasonable (R1, R4). Decoupling intra-scale and inter-scale modeling is a sensible approach to improve computational efficiency of VAR (R1, R4).
- Statistics on attention scores and computation cost in VAR are interesting and used to motivate the design (R1, R2, R3). Table 1 shows intra-scale attention is computationally expensive (R3).
- On ImageNet 256×256 and 512×512, M-VAR achieves better FID and IS than VAR, and the largest model (M-VAR-d32) registers 1.78 FID, outperforming prior autoregressive models LlamaGen/VAR (by 0.4/0.19) and diffusion models LDM/DiT (by 1.82/0.49) (R1, R2, R4). Ablation study shows intra-scale attention + Mamba outperforms global attention on VAR (R4).
- The paper includes numerous objective metrics and tables (R3). Some reviewers found the paper well-written and easy to follow (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Readability and presentation**: The paper is very difficult to read according to some reviewers (R2). There are broken sentences, grammatical issues, and unclear statements (e.g., ll. 201-202 incorrect; ll. 213 “The sequence S of multiple scales is much longer than each scale (s1, …, sn)” is not clear; ll. 229-231 broken). Equation (2) does not define parameterization θ (R2). There are typos, e.g., L480 says “As shown in Table 5” instead of “Figure 5” (R1). Figures 2, 3, 5 are overly large and impact readability (R3). Table 6 lacks significant information and could be more concise (R3). **Contrast**: Some reviewers found the paper well-written (strengths above), while R2 highlighted serious readability issues.
- **Limited technical novelty and insufficient justification for design choices**: Replacing global attention with hybrid architectures (intra-scale attention + inter-scale Mamba) has been extensively explored (R4). The choice of Mamba seems arbitrary; other efficient self-attention variants (e.g., linear attention, RWKV) should be considered and ablated (R1, R4). The paper lacks detailed ablation studies comparing alternative intra/inter-scale modeling approaches (R4). It is unclear why M-VAR can deliver better FID than VAR given that global attention has stronger or similar capacity (R1, R4). The improvements on ImageNet appear incremental (R4). Additionally, no evidence is provided that intra-scale dependencies are easier to model due to local pixel correlations (e.g., a plot of pixel correlation as a function of inter-pixel distance on the dataset) (R2).
- **Unfair comparisons and computational efficiency claims are not convincingly supported**: In Table 2, M-VAR-dX models generally have more parameters than corresponding VAR-dX models (R1, R2, R3). For example, M-VAR-d32 (3B params) outperforms VAR-d30 (2B params) but uses 50% more parameters, and inference time increases from 0.7s to 1.0s (43% increase) despite only slightly better FID/IS (R3). The paper claims reduced computational complexity but does not discuss FLOPs (R1). There is inconsistency in inference time results: Table 4 shows M-VAR inference time is lower than VAR, but in Table 5 it is slightly higher or comparable (R2). The claimed quadratic reduction in computational efficiency is not clearly demonstrated by the results (R2). More details on speed comparison settings (hardware, batch size, inference engine) and measured latency/throughput are needed (R4).
- **Missing definitions and explanations**: The term “attention score” in Table 1 is not defined or explained; it is unclear how it was computed and on how many test images (R2). The motivation for decoupling could be better visualized by illustrating Table 1 data as a figure (R3).
- **Missing comparisons to prior multi-scale autoregressive image generation work**: Prior works such as PixelPyramids (Mahajan & Roth, ICCV 2021), MaCow (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2019), Subscale Pixel Networks (Menick & Kalchbrenner, ICLR 2019), and Parallel Multiscale (Reed et al., ICML 2017) are not discussed in the related work (R2).
- **Limited scope and generalizability**: The paper only presents ImageNet class-conditional results; it is unclear whether advantages preserve after scaling up to text-to-image generation or real-world use cases (R4). Some reviewers question whether VAR-d36 was trained by the authors (R3).
- **Counter-intuitive result**: Figure 5 shows global attention performing worse despite its global modeling capacity; this is not explained (R1).
- **Minor presentation suggestions**: Table 1 data could be plotted as a figure to better highlight motivation (R3). Table 6 could be made more concise (R3).
