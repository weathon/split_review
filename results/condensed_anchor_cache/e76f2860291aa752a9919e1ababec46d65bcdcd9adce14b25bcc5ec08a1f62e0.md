## Merged Review

### Summary
TAPE3D addresses dense 3D tracking from monocular video by combining a joint global-local attention mechanism (inspired by CoTracker) with a transformer-based upsampler. The method processes reduced-resolution tracks and upsamples to full resolution, uses log-depth representation, and is trained on 5.6K synthetic Kubric videos. It achieves SOTA on CVO and TAPVid-3D benchmarks, running over 8× faster (Fig. 1) than prior methods (SceneTracker, SpatialTracker). The paper also ablates depth representation, spatial attention, and the upsampler (Sec. 4.3, Table 6).

### Strengths
1. **Efficiency**: TAPE3D runs over 8× faster than previous methods (Fig. 1) while achieving SOTA accuracy – a ~10× inference speed improvement (R1, R2, R3, R4).
2. **State-of-the-art performance**: New SOTA on CVO and TAPVid-3D for both 2D and 3D dense tracking (R1, R2, R4).
3. **Comprehensive ablations**: Design choices (depth representation, spatial attention module, upsampler) are thoroughly ablated in Sec. 4.3 and Table 6, demonstrating the value of each (R2, R4).
4. **Novel components**: The local-global cross-attention reduces computation while improving performance; the transformer-based upsampler outperforms ConvNet-based ones from optical flow; using log-depth gives better results (R4).
5. **First feedforward TAP-3D model**: Alongside SceneTracker and SpatialTracker, TAPE3D is among the first to address TAP-3D without test-time optimization, making it a valuable contribution (R2).
6. **Visualizations**: Supplementary material shows clear superiority over SceneTracker and slightly superior results vs. SpatialTracker, aligning with quantitative TAPVid-3D results (R2).
7. **Experiments are comprehensive and convincing** (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity of concepts and notation**:
   - Why anchor tracks are needed vs. sampling dense tracks directly is not well explained (R1).
   - The difference between sparse and dense tracks is unclear; parameters \(N'\) and \(L\) are not defined (R1, R4). \(N'\) differs between training and evaluation; \(M\) (number of anchors) is not described (R4).
   - How “dense tracks” in Fig. 3 relate to tokens \(G^i_t\) in eq. (1) is not clear (R2).
   - Eq. (3) is mathematically incorrect or vague: it lacks explanation of what \(q\) and \(k\) are (e.g., linear layers, what they are applied to), what the softmax is over, and values are missing for cross-attention (R2).
   - Fig. 4 and eq. (3) are not clearly connected; it is unclear how the \(\tau\) cross-attention blocks operate (e.g., multiple heads in parallel?) (R2).
   - L290–L295: “local random patch of tracks” is confusing; Fig. 3 shows virtual tracks attending only to anchor tracks and themselves (R4).
2. **Novelty concerns**: The model design appears to be mainly a combination of ideas from SceneTracker and CoTracker; novelty is not clearly stated (R2). Contributions feel scattered and lack depth (R4). The spatial-temporal transformer idea is not new (R3).
3. **Missing experiments and comparisons**:
   - Only 2D tracking results on CVO are reported; results on TAPVid-DAVIS would help demonstrate SOTA for 2D tracking (R2). *Request: evaluate on TAPVid-DAVIS* (R2).
   - Table 5: BootsTAPIR+ZoeDepth is *worse* than CoTracker+ZoeDepth, which is unexpected given BootsTAPIR’s superior 2D performance. More comparisons (e.g., LocoTrack, TAPTRv2) are needed to justify this (R4).
   - Depth is predicted from monocular video without scale specification; consistency across frames is not ensured (R1).
   - The attention upsampling method is related to the guided filter; this relationship should be discussed (R1).
4. **Training details and data**:
   - Why only 5.6K synthetic videos are used when more can be generated easily; whether adding PointOddysey would improve TAPVid-3D performance is not addressed (R2).
   - The compilation cost of each module is not provided; inference machine specs (GPU/CPU) are missing (R3). *Request: provide per-module compilation cost and machine information* (R3).
5. **Parameter selection**: Key parameters (M, r, N', L) are not explained or justified (R1).
6. **Local-global attention compute**: The use of anchor tracks and local attention adds extra computation; during evaluation, local-global requires \(O(TKM + TLN^2)\) more operations than CoTracker’s global attention (R4).
7. **Unexpected result in Table 2**: (R1) “Why the 2D version consistently better than 2D version in Table2?” – the phrasing is unclear, but an analysis of this unexpected result is requested.
8. **(Minority reviewer contrast)**: R4 was more critical, finding contributions lacking depth and the paper difficult to understand in several parts, while R1 considered experiments comprehensive and convincing; R2 and R3 acknowledged the contributions but noted clarity/novelty issues.
