## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a sample selection method (UPA) for annotation in semi-supervised learning, using a modified Frank-Wolfe algorithm to minimize a weighted MMD-squared distance (\(\alpha\)-MMD) between the full unlabeled dataset and the selected subset, explicitly controlling the trade-off between representativeness and diversity via the parameter \(\alpha\). Kernel herding is adapted to avoid sample repetition, and an error bound is provided (Theorem 3.2). Empirically, UPA is combined with SSL methods (FlexMatch, FreeMatch) and compared against random sampling and some AL/SSAL baselines.

### Strengths
*   The idea of \(\alpha\)-MMD\(^2\) and its interpretation (Equation 5) are clear and suitable for active learning tasks.
*   Selecting an optimal subset for annotation is an important problem in scenarios where labels are costly.
*   The main contribution — demonstrating that minimizing MMD for such scenarios improves underlying classification accuracy — is supported by theoretical results relating the approach to coreset construction.
*   An approach that explicitly optimizes both diversity and representativeness has a conceptual advantage.
*   Some theoretical results are presented to support the advantage of the method.
*   Empirical results show some advantage over random baselines, and for CIFAR-10 a clear advantage when using FlexMatch with UPA.
*   The experimental section provides a set of comparisons with baselines that demonstrate advantages of using MMD (noted by one reviewer).

### Weaknesses
*   **Limited comparison to other subset selection or AL methods:** Many existing methods for diverse and representative selection (e.g., coreset, BADGE by Ash et al., and others referenced [1*]-[3*]) are not discussed or empirically compared. Comparisons in Tables 1 and 2 are primarily against random sampling and a few AL/SSAL baselines; the improvement over random is modest (e.g., ~0.5% with STD of 0.5 on some datasets, and overlapping confidence intervals in Table 1). Missing comparisons make it difficult to evaluate the significance of the proposal.
*   **Missing ablation of SSL backbone:** Since UPA is employed on top of FlexMatch/FreeMatch, results without UPA (i.e., SSL alone) are not shown. Without this ablation it is unclear whether improvements come from the SSL method or from UPA.
*   **Insufficient justification of \(\alpha\) parameter:** 
    *   The trivial case \(\alpha=1\) makes all bounds tighter than any other \(\alpha\), contradicting the motivation to balance representativeness and diversity.
    *   Experimental ablation (Table 3) is limited to three datapoints on one dataset, and results for \(\alpha=1\) vs. the “optimal” \(\alpha=1-1/\sqrt{m}\) are quite close. No comprehensive analysis across datasets or \(\alpha\) values supports the choice \(\alpha=1-1/\sqrt{m}\) over \(\alpha=1\).
    *   One reviewer notes that without \(\alpha\) the method has little novelty, though the justification for using MMD is still good.
*   **Soundness concerns:** 
    *   Empirical validation lacks additional datasets beyond those shown.
    *   Only 3 independent runs are reported; more runs are needed for statistical confidence.
    *   The choice of specific \(m\) values (budgets) for each dataset is not justified; a graph over a range of \(m\) values would be more informative.
    *   The requirement \(m/n \leq 0.2\) is weak and may be unrealistic for active learning with very limited budgets.
*   **Clarity issues:**
    *   No pseudo-code or step-by-step description of the method.
    *   The input space over which the method operates is not specified (raw data? penultimate layer representation?).
    *   Remark 1 is confusing (it claims Theorem 3.1 does not always work).
    *   Figure 1 lacks a legend/explanation for colors.
    *   In Equation 6, the function \(f_{I_p}(x_i)\) is defined only for \(x\) without clarification.
    *   In Equation 8, the constant \(K\) in \(B=2K\) is not defined.
    *   Figures (e.g., Fig. 2) do not clearly show the claimed benefits.
*   **Theoretical concerns:**
    *   In the proof of Theorem 3.2, the definition of \(j\) as \(\text{argmin}_{i=1\ldots n \setminus I_p}\) and the subsequent use of \(f_{I_p}(x_{j_{p+1}}) \leq f_{I_p}(x_j)\) may not be correctly justified (question from one reviewer).
*   **Computational complexity:** The claimed complexity \(O(mn)\) is not explained; kernel construction may incur additional cost.
*   **Overall suitability:** One reviewer questions whether diversification and representativeness alone are optimal for active learning, given other important trade-offs like exploration-exploitation. The same reviewer doubts the chosen SSL application is the right one, suggesting the paper should focus on the selection step and compare to related work before sketching applications.
*   **Missing experiments/analysis (from reviewer questions):** 
    *   Results of a supervised classifier (not SSL) trained on the selected set, showing at what subset size accuracy matches full training set.
    *   How the approach copes with low vs. high dimensions, different distributions (overlapping clusters, well-separated, etc.), and what its limitations are.
    *   Why FlexMatch in particular was chosen for UPA.
    *   Why diversification and representativeness is the best (or a good) criterion for active learning.
