## Merged Review

### Summary

BADROBOT is a novel attack paradigm targeting embodied LLMs, exploiting three vulnerabilities: jailbreak (vulnerability propagation from the LLM), safety misalignment (discrepancy between linguistic output and action planning), and conceptual deception (incomplete world knowledge). The authors construct a benchmark of malicious physical action queries and evaluate attack performance across state-of-the-art embodied LLM frameworks (Voxposer, Code as Policies, ProgPrompt) in both simulated and real-world settings. The paper demonstrates that embodied LLMs can be manipulated to violate safety and ethical constraints, highlighting critical security concerns.

### Strengths

- **Well-motivated and timely**: As embodied LLMs increasingly control physical systems, their safety-critical nature demands attention. The paper addresses a significant gap in AI safety for physical systems.
- **Comprehensive attack framework**: Identifies three unique attack paradigms (contextual jailbreak, safety misalignment exploitation, conceptual deception) that exploit the unique vulnerabilities of embodied AI, especially the action/language misalignment. The methods are formally described.
- **Extensive evaluation**: Experiments cover multiple tasks, simulated and real-world platforms, and multiple SOTA LLMs (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4-turbo, Llava-1.5-7b) and frameworks (Code as Policies, ProgPrompt). Practical real-world examples (Sections 4.3 and 4.4) convincingly demonstrate attack feasibility.
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written, logically organized, and easy to follow. The discovered safety risk patterns are clearly stated.
- **Significant impact**: The results are expected to influence the community; source code and website are open for further research.

### Weaknesses

- **Attack surfaces limited to language instructions**: The attacks rely solely on voice-based user instructions. Embodied LLMs could be attacked via other surfaces, such as environment observations, system prompts, or multimodal inputs (visual/audio). Expanding to multimodal attacks (language combined with visual/audio manipulations) would provide a more comprehensive security assessment. *[Reviewers 1, 4]*
- **Claim of “first” is inaccurate**: Recent preprints (e.g., “Exploring the Robustness of Decision-Level Through Adversarial Attacks on LLM-Based Embodied Models”, arXiv:2405.19802; “Can We Trust Embodied Agents? Exploring Backdoor Attacks against Embodied LLM-based Decision-Making Systems”, arXiv:2405.20774) also focus on attack/safety of embodied LLMs. The authors should discuss these to clarify their contribution. *[Reviewer 1]*
- **Missing analysis of finetuning defenses (Reviewer 2 major concern)**: It is unclear what would happen if the LLM were fine-tuned to increase similarity between linguistic output \(f_\phi\) and action planning \(f_\Psi\). The expectation is that the safety misalignment risk (Bsm MSR) would drop significantly. Such an experiment would strengthen the understanding of the vulnerability. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Insufficient insights on fine-tuning effects (Table 3)**: Why do Bcj and Bsm values drop after fine-tuning the LLM for the world model \(w\)? How can this drop be avoided? *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Lack of details on attack prompts used in Table 2**: The number of attack prompts per category and the diversity within each category are not reported. This is necessary to validate that the benchmark covers a diverse set of prompts. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Figure 6 limited to one LLM model**: Showing the harmfulness score trend for at least two more LLM models would demonstrate consistency across architectures. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Attack models tested primarily on GPT architectures**: Evaluation should be extended to other architectures (e.g., BERT, T5, PaLM) to test universality. In particular, encoder-only models like BERT may reveal different vulnerabilities. *[Reviewer 4]*
- **No analysis of attack transferability or why some attacks work better on specific systems**: The paper lacks a detailed investigation into which architectural or training features make models more susceptible to contextual jailbreak vs. conceptual deception. *[Reviewer 4]*
- **Missing thorough limitations discussion**: Scenarios where the attacks might fail or be less effective (e.g., more complex environments, different robot hardware) are not discussed. The assumptions of the attack framework should be explicitly listed. *[Reviewers 1, 4]*
- **Scalability to multi-agent scenarios is unclear**: Many real-world applications involve multiple interacting agents. How attack effectiveness changes in such settings is not addressed. *[Reviewer 4]*
- **Lack of discussion on attack cost/feasibility and trade-offs**: A quantitative or qualitative analysis of the cost and practicality of the three attack methods, as well as their trade-offs, is missing. *[Reviewer 1]*
- **No attempt to defend against the attacks**: Two reviewers explicitly asked whether the authors tried to defend the developed attack prompts. *[Reviewers 1, 3]*
- **Methodology for discovering risk patterns is unclear**: Reviewer 3 noted that it is not explained how the three safety risk patterns were discovered, and whether more undiscovered patterns may exist. *[Reviewer 3]*
- **Minor**: Typos (e.g., L428: “Code as Polocies” → “Code as Policies”) and low-resolution figures (Figures 2, 3, 5, 6). *[Reviewers 2, 4]*
