## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes InterConv, a pure-convolution mechanism for query-to-image feature interaction, and builds Detection ConvNet (DECO), a fully CNN-based end-to-end encoder-decoder detection/segmentation framework. Experiments on COCO show competitive accuracy-speed trade-offs (e.g., 40.5% AP at 66 FPS with ResNet-18, 47.8% AP at 34 FPS with ResNet-50). While all reviewers acknowledge the simplicity and potential of the approach, opinions diverge on its practical impact: two strongly positive (scores 8/8) and two more reserved (scores 5/6), noting limited gains over recent transformer variants and missing details.

### Strengths
- **Simple and effective pure-conv design**: The proposed Self-InterConv and Cross-InterConv capture the essence of DETR’s attention using only standard convolutions, providing an elegant replacement (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Computational efficiency and hardware compatibility**: DECO requires only standard convolutions, making it computationally efficient and more compatible with low-end AI chips compared to DETR-based methods (Reviewer 1). The pure-conv encoder/decoder can capture long-range details without attention mechanisms (Reviewer 2).
- **Competitive performance without complicated components**: DECO achieves comparative performance and efficiency over DETR despite its simplicity (Reviewer 2). Comparable results to DETR are shown (Reviewer 3), and the method can be further improved with multi-scale designs from Deformable-DETR (DECO+) (Reviewer 2).
- **Explores a new direction**: The paper systematically explores a convolution-based DETR framework with InterConv (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and experimental strength**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 4). Experiments are strong and demonstrate the effectiveness of a CNN-based model (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient clarity on key components**: The Fusion operation (method section) is crucial but inadequately discussed; different fusion methods and query map upsampling strategies are unclear and should be detailed in the method section (Reviewer 1). The statement on Lines 074-075 (“whose weights and inputs are both generated during runtime”) is imprecise: convolutional weights are trained, not generated at runtime, while attention weights are dynamically computed—this distinction needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
- **Performance inferior to recent transformer methods**: DECO’s overall performance lags behind recent transformer-based detectors like DINO, Co-DETR, and Stable-DINO, highlighting the limitations of purely convolutional architectures (Reviewer 3). In Table 2, DETR (with ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-T) achieves better accuracy than DECO, while speed improvements are not significant; DETR can be further optimized for speed and accuracy, limiting DECO’s effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with recent works**: The paper lacks experimental comparisons with many recent methods in terms of both accuracy and speed, including Stable DINO, RT-DETRv2, DDQ, and SpeedDETR (Reviewer 3). Additionally, references are missing for the large kernel CNN paper and SparseInst, which is also a pure-conv query-based detection/segmentation method (Reviewer 1).
- **Transferability and generalization concerns**: 
  - It is hard to transfer well-established DETR techniques (e.g., denoising queries, deformable attention) into DECO (Reviewer 3). Reviewers specifically ask whether the denoising training technique from DN-DETR/DINO can be introduced (Reviewer 2) and whether deformable convolutions (v1–v4) could be incorporated into the encoder/decoder, and if so, what the efficiency trade-offs would be (Reviewer 2).
  - The object queries require prior knowledge to determine their shape/layout (e.g., 30×10 for COCO); it is unclear whether a pre-trained DECO would generalize to datasets with different aspect ratios (e.g., 1:3) (Reviewer 3). A reviewer also questions whether object queries can be randomly initialized for different images (Reviewer 3).
- **Segmentation extension unclear**: The details of extending DECO to segmentation tasks are unclear (Reviewer 3). A reviewer asks whether the paper combines DECO with TinySAM in Sec. 4.4 (Reviewer 3).
- **Cross-scale fusion lacks novelty differentiation**: The cross-scale feature-fusion module borrows from RT-DETR, weakening the contribution. The paper does not adequately detail how its fusion mechanism differs from RT-DETR’s approach beyond a high-level claim. A more granular comparison at the level of mathematical operations or specific network layers is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient efficiency argument**: The claim in Lines 073–074 that convolutions are preferable over transformers for efficiency is not convincing. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of computational bottlenecks, FLOPs, memory access patterns, or hardware utilization characteristics that support this claim. It also does not consider recent transformer optimization techniques (e.g., quantization, pruning) that could mitigate speed disadvantages (Reviewer 4).
