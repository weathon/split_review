## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends the seminal result of Matthews et al. (2018) – that randomly initialized infinite-width deep neural networks converge to Gaussian processes – to a larger class of weight distributions termed “pseudo-IID”. This class includes centered, uncorrelated entries with row/column exchangeability and bounded higher‑order moments, and subsumes IID, orthogonal, normalized, low‑rank, and permuted block‑sparse initializations. Results are proven for fully connected networks and convolutional networks under simultaneous scaling of all widths. Numerical simulations for fully connected networks (widths up to 300) verify the convergence.

### Strengths
- The writing and exposition are clear; connections to prior literature are explicitly stated (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The pseudo-IID framework is a natural generalization of the IID requirement, and includes many non‑IID initialization schemes used in practice (orthogonal, low‑rank, permuted block‑sparse) that can yield computational speed-ups (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The proof handles simultaneous scaling of all layers, which is more challenging than the sequential scaling considered in previous work (Reviewer 3).
- The results provide a first step toward identifying the Edge‑of‑Chaos for a broader class of networks and enable tuning at criticality (Reviewer 3).
- The proof technique for fully connected layers is clear and accessible (Reviewer 3).
- The class of distributions is significantly larger than those previously investigated, which may stimulate future research (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 4 notes this as a strength but questions novelty).

### Weaknesses
- **Practical interest not immediately clear**: The practical relevance of the newly covered families (low‑rank, structured sparse) is not demonstrated; justifications are deferred to future work (Reviewer 1, and implicitly Reviewer 3’s question about developing regularisers).
- **Focus on initialization, not Bayesian inference**: The paper studies random initializations for deep learning, whereas Matthews et al. (2018) also examined implications for posterior inference in Bayesian NNs; this limits the paper’s scope (Reviewer 1).
- **First‑layer restriction**: In all relevant examples, the weights of the first layer must be Gaussian IID, which reduces the generality of the claim (Reviewer 2; confirmed in question).
- **Limited CNN validation**: For CNNs, only the orthogonal definition of Wang et al. (2020) fits the pseudo-IID regime, and no numerical simulation for CNNs is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty concern (minority view)**: The proof techniques are largely the same as in Matthews et al. (2018), with only technical modifications (e.g., a CLT for exchangeable random variables); this may be seen as an incremental contribution (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Definition 1: the vector **a** is not defined (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper would benefit from explicitly stating how/why its focus differs from the Bayesian‑NN focus of prior work (Reviewer 1).
  - Notation N₀ for the width of the first layer (input layer) is potentially confusing (Reviewer 1).
  - The assumption that the input space is countably infinite is not motivated (Reviewer 1).
  - Section 3.1 should be more explicit about why each example distribution was not covered by previous results (Reviewer 1).
  - The definition order and explanation of assumptions could be improved; e.g., Definition 2 should come before Definition 1, and each assumption should be immediately discussed (Reviewer 4).
  - The paper contains many grammatical mistakes, suggesting insufficient effort in writing (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing empirical verification**: A numerical simulation for CNNs would strengthen the study (Reviewer 3).
