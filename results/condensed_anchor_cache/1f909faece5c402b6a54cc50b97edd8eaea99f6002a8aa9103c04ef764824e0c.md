## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LUNCH, which replaces fixed hyperparameters in continual learning loss terms (new task, output space, parameter space) with learnable parameters derived from homoscedastic aleatoric uncertainty. An exponential moving average (EMA) is used to stabilize training. Experiments on Split CIFAR‑10, Split CIFAR‑100, and Split Mini‑ImageNet report improvements (e.g., up to 11.26% and 5.64%). The method integrates as a plug‑in with ER and DER. Reviewer scores range from 3 to 6, with significant disagreement on novelty, scope, and evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach.** LUNCH adaptively adjusts hyperparameters based on uncertainty, bringing a fresh perspective to CL (R1, R2, R4). It addresses the important yet often overlooked issue of hyperparameter tuning in CL algorithms (R2).
- **Clear presentation.** The paper is well‑organized and straightforward to follow (R1, R3). Code is provided and easy to run (R3).
- **Consistent improvements.** LUNCH shows consistent performance gains across multiple metrics in both online and offline CL settings (R1, R2, R3, R4). For example, up to 11.26% on Split CIFAR‑100 and 5.64% on Split Mini‑ImageNet.
- **Easy integration.** The method works as a plug‑in with existing techniques such as ER and DER (R1, R4).
- **Thorough evaluation.** The paper includes sensitivity analyses, ablation studies, and cost comparisons that demonstrate stability and robustness (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer highlighted that adequate hyperparameters can improve CL method performance with a large gap (R4, referring to Table 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation and scope.** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3) found the motivation unclear. The paper does not clearly articulate what challenge LUNCH addresses that existing dynamic task balancing methods do not, nor does it pinpoint limitations of current adaptation strategies (R1, R3). One reviewer noted that stating hyperparameters should be adapted is insufficient without demonstrating why existing approaches are inadequate (R1). The method is similar to adjusting EMA parameters in prior work (Online Boundary‑Free Continual Learning by Scheduled Data Prior, ICLR 2023) (R4). LUNCH is only demonstrated on regularization‑based CL methods, ignoring prompt‑based (L2P CVPR 2022, DualPrompt ECCV 2022, DAP ICCV 2023) and other paradigms, making the contribution too narrow (R4). Another reviewer argued that the algorithm only considers hyperparameters for the three loss terms while overlooking “basic” hyperparameters (mini‑batch size, learning rate, optimizer) that significantly influence performance (R2, citing Cha & Cho 2024).
- **Insufficient and outdated baselines.** Reviewers (R1, R2, R3) agreed that the baselines are limited and outdated; comparisons should include state‑of‑the‑art methods from 2023‑2024. For online CL, missing comparisons with EARL, REMIND, SCR, X‑DER (R2, referencing CVPR 2024). For offline CL, missing BiC, PODNet, iCaRL, WA (R2, citing PyCIL). Experiments should vary the number of tasks and include larger datasets like ImageNet (or ImageNet‑100) to verify broad applicability (R2). One reviewer questioned the selection logic given hundreds of existing works (R3). The current set does not convincingly demonstrate competitiveness.
- **Lack of insight into why the method works and its limitations.** Reviewers (R1, R3) found the analytic experiments insufficient to understand when LUNCH succeeds or fails and what criteria determine failure modes or larger margins. Specific analyses requested include:  
  (a) An ablation isolating individual uncertainty components (output‑space vs. parameter‑space) to assess their distinct impact (R1).  
  (b) An ablation clarifying the effectiveness of uncertainty (Unc) versus EMA, because Table 4 shows EMA often gives larger improvement than Unc; a suggested control is applying EMA only at later iterations (e.g., 1/4 × I) to isolate Unc’s benefit (R2).  
  (c) Reporting backward transfer as a forgetting metric, since forgetting is a major focus (R3).  
  (d) Reporting multi‑task learning (MTL) upper bound and sequential task learning lower bound (R3).  
  (e) Investigating applicability to larger transformer‑based models, as current interest has shifted to them (R3).  
  (f) Several results in Table 2 appear inconsistent with prior work: FAA scores for ER and DER on split CIFAR‑10 are much lower than expected (R2, referencing Table 1 of [1]); in many cases using 1/4 × λ yields better performance even without LUNCH (R2); ER shows superior performance compared to other algorithms contrary to common knowledge (R2). These discrepancies need clarification.
- **Notable reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (score 6) praised the novelty and presentation but still noted unclear motivation and outdated baselines. The other three reviewers (scores 3, 5, 3) were more critical, additionally citing similarity to prior work, narrow scope, and insufficient analysis.
- **Minor presentation issues.** Orphans/widows in the paper, references not starting on a new page, appendix not using \autoref, and redundant spaces in eq. 11 and 12 (R4).
