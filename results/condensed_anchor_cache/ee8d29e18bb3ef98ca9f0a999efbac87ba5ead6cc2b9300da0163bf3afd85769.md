## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CRAFT, a self-supervised multi-frame depth estimation architecture that replaces the conventional epipolar-based cost volume with cross-attention maps. The key idea is that training cross-attention layers for image reconstruction (with masked image modeling pre-training) implicitly learns a warping function, eliminating the need for explicit pose and static-scene assumptions. The method is evaluated on KITTI and Cityscapes, reporting state-of-the-art results. Reviewers are split: two rated the paper 5 (with one expressing low confidence) and two rated it 3 (both with higher confidence). The positive reviewers highlight the solid motivation and clear writing; the negative reviewers question novelty, fairness of comparisons, and the independence of the core contribution from pre-training.

### Strengths
- The motivation is well-founded: addressing the limitations of epipolar-based cost volumes (static-scene assumption, need for pose) by using cross-attention as a full cost volume.
- The paper is well-written, clearly structures the problem, and explains the approach.
- The idea is simple, clear, and easy to reproduce.
- Applying cross-attention for cost volume computation and using masked image modeling (MIM) to improve matching similarity are appropriate design choices.
- The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on KITTI and Cityscapes compared to previous self-supervised multi-frame depth estimation methods.
- Thorough experimental evaluation on multiple outdoor datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** Using cross-attention to replace cost volume has been explored in prior work (e.g., Revisiting Stereo Depth Estimation from a Sequence-to-Sequence Perspective with Transformers, ICCV 2021). The structural differences between CRAFT and that method are not clearly explained; it currently appears the differences are mainly at the output level. Cross-attention and coarse-to-fine strategies are widely used in depth estimation, limiting novelty.
- **Independent validation of CRAFT is missing:** All experiments include MIM-based pre-training (CroCO), so there is no ablation to verify whether the CRAFT module itself functions as intended. The large performance gap between models with and without CroCO pre-training further raises doubt about whether CRAFT contributes independently or relies entirely on the pre-training.
- **Unfair comparisons:** The method uses a Transformer backbone while most baselines (e.g., ManyDepth, DynamicDepth) use CNN backbones (ResNet, HRNet). This makes the comparison biased. Quantitative results on Cityscapes (Table 4) are worse than recent methods (e.g., Ds-depth, IEEE TCSVT 2023). The authors should compare using the same feature extractor and also include more recent methods on Cityscapes.
- **Missing analysis of computational complexity:** No comparison of model complexity, inference time, or parameter count with ManyDepth or other methods.
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Only outdoor datasets (KITTI, Cityscapes) are used. The model’s effectiveness on indoor scenes (e.g., ScanNet, NYU) is not shown; such evaluation would strengthen the generality claim.
- **Qualitative results are unconvincing:** In Figure 4, the highlighted red-boxed regions show improvement, but non-highlighted areas display worse depth predictions. For example, trees in the second image are not sharper than DynamicDepth and ManyDepth, and background buildings in the first image are clearer for those baselines.
- **Lack of justification for cross-attention over CNN:** The paper does not sufficiently explain why Transformer cross-attention is more advantageous for feature matching than CNNs, given Transformers’ weaker inductive bias for such tasks.
- **Request for 3D reconstruction comparison:** Since the task is closely related to multi-view stereo, the authors should provide a 3D reconstruction view of the scene to compare with earlier MVS methods.
- **Language issues:** The paper contains vague sentences and grammatical errors that should be corrected.
