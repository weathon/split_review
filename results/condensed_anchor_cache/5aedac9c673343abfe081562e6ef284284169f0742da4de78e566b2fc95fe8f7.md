## Merged Review

### Summary
The proposed ContraSim framework consists of Weighted Headline Augmentation (using LLM-generated reworded, slightly ablated, and negative variations with manually assigned semantic distances 1, 0.5, 0) and Weighted Self-Supervised Contrastive Learning (WSSCL) to create an embedding space for financial headlines. An Info-kNN metric evaluates clustering density. Experiments are conducted on the NIFTY dataset for stock market direction prediction, reporting 7% improvement in classification accuracy and 13% in balanced accuracy. Reviewer disagreement is significant: one reviewer found the work lacking in contributions, experiments, and presentation (score 3, confidence 5), while others rated it moderately (scores 5 and 6) and praised the innovative framework and Info-kNN metric (scores 6).

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an interesting and practical problem of stock market direction prediction using financial news headlines, and the contrastive learning approach is reasonable (R1).
- The example in Table 1 (rephrasing, slight ablation, and negative modification of a headline) is illustrative and to the point (R1).
- The integration of Weighted Headline Augmentation and WSSCL provides a fresh perspective on the interplay between financial news and market behavior (R4).
- The Info-kNN metric, based on information-theoretic principles to assess semantic clustering in the embedding space, is a notable contribution that may have broader applications beyond finance (R3, R4).
- The framework can assist financial analysts by identifying historical market days similar to current headlines (R3).
- Empirical improvements are reported: 7% increase in classification accuracy and 13% boost in balanced accuracy over the baseline (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Single dataset and narrow scope**: Only the NIFTY dataset is used, with a specific market and time period, limiting generalizability. The paper does not test on other stock markets or non-financial domains, nor does it discuss how the method might adapt (R1, R3). The conclusion’s comments on broader application are unsupported (R3).
- **Lack of serious experiments and comparisons**: The experiment section lacks proper baselines and state-of-the-art comparisons to justify the proposed model’s performance (R2). It is not clear how ContraSim contributes relative to other approaches (R4). The baseline in Table 3 (mean of random samples following the 23%, 60%, 17% label split) is questionable—if a classifier always predicts ‘Neutral’, would it achieve 60% accuracy? (R1)
- **Limited and missing ablation studies**: There is no analysis of the individual contribution of each augmentation type (reworded, slightly ablated, negative) to classification accuracy, F1 score, or clustering purity. The paper should show the effect of removing each augmentation, using a standard k-NN instead of Info-kNN, and using standard contrastive loss instead of WSSCL (R3). Similarly, the need for the Info-kNN and WSSCL components is not justified (R3).
- **Poor presentation quality (multiple reviewers; most severe from R2)**: The paper suffers from many grammar mistakes and typos, which impair understanding (R2). Specific issues include: the first paragraph of Section 3.1 contains almost one error per sentence, obscuring the motivation (R2); Equation 3 has unclear text and notation errors (N_i should be N_k; h should be \hat{h}) (R2); the motivation for sampling 10–30 news articles per day is not justified theoretically or empirically, and random sampling risks omitting important topics (R2); the phrase “keep newslines and ignore tabular data” is unclear (R2); confusion between “prompts” and “headlines” in the creation process (R2); the augmentation method lacks quality control, and the definitions of “slight ablation” and “negative” are unclear—the example only generates fake company names without clear motivation (R2); the assignment of semantic distances 1, 0.5, and 0 to augmentations appears arbitrary with no justification (R2); the “generating newsline buckets” subsection is hard to follow due to grammar issues and undefined terms (R2); in the WSSCL section, labels are erroneously listed as “Fall, Natural, and Fall again” rather than including “Rise” (R2); these issues persist throughout the paper (R2).
- **Bias and limitations from LLM-based augmentation and headline use**: The LLM used for headline augmentation may introduce its own biases, affecting data quality and predictions (R4). Additionally, predictions based solely on headlines have limited scope and are error-prone because headlines are often written to attract attention rather than convey accurate information (R4).
- **Missing details and insufficient justification**: The paper does not clearly describe the types of augmentations used (R4). The characteristics of the learned embedding space that influence interpretability are not explained (R4). Hyperparameter tuning is not elaborated (R4). The method for achieving a “known semantic distance” via manual assignment lacks motivation (R2).
