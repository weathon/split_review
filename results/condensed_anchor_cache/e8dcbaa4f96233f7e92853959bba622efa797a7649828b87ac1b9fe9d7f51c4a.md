## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ACADEMICEVAL, a benchmark that uses arXiv papers to evaluate LLMs on long-context generation tasks—Title, Abstract, Introduction, and Related Work. It leverages a co-author graph to provide few-shot demonstrations and features live evaluation to mitigate label leakage. Models are assessed via BERTScore and RougeL.

### Strengths
- By using arXiv papers, ACADEMICEVAL leverages readily available, high-quality academic content, reducing reliance on labor-intensive manual annotation. (R2)
- The benchmark’s periodic updates from arXiv mitigate risks of label leakage and maintain relevance in LLM assessment. (R2)
- The benchmark includes multiple academic writing tasks with different abstraction levels, offering a broad evaluation framework for long-context generation. (R2)
- The motivation of testing LLMs’ ability to perform long-context text-generation tasks is strong. (R3)
- Writing different sections of a paper is a nice challenge for this kind of task. (R3)
- Having a benchmark that requires no human labor is beneficial. (R3)
- The idea of using live evaluation to avoid label leakage is decent. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- The proposed benchmark does not introduce fundamentally novel insights or methodological contributions for evaluating long-context LLMs; it reuses existing concepts (e.g., hierarchical task structure and few-shot learning demonstrations). (R2, strength: strong criticism)  
  - Related question: How does ACADEMICEVAL’s hierarchical structure provide insights not achievable with existing benchmarks like LongBench or L-Eval? (R2)
- ACADEMICEVAL focuses on a narrow set of tasks related to academic writing (only four arXiv subsections), limiting its applicability and failing to test LLMs across a wider range of real-world long-context scenarios. The title should be qualified (e.g., “evaluating LLMs on a subset of research paper generation”). (R2, R3)  
  - Related question: How do you ensure this benchmark generalizes to other long-context generation tasks? (R3)
- The paper does not adequately demonstrate the limitations of existing benchmarks to justify the need for ACADEMICEVAL. Issues like rigid context limits and label leakage are only partially addressed by ACADEMICEVAL’s live evaluation and flexible context lengths. (R2)
- RougeL and BERTScore are likely inadequate for these free-form academic generation tasks: BERTScore’s BERT was trained on small chunks for tasks like sentiment, not long-text generation; RougeL measures word-for-word overlap, which is unsuitable when many valid phrasings exist (e.g., for an Introduction or Related Work). The reviewer suggests G-Eval as a potentially better metric. (R3, strength: strong criticism)
- There needs to be human evaluation to verify that BERTScore correlates with human judgment for these tasks. (R3)
- For the Related Work section, there are many valid subsets of citations and ways to relate the paper, so penalizing generations that differ from the ground truth is questionable. (R3)
- The live evaluation approach creates scalability issues: evaluating a new model requires re-evaluating all prior models on the new data, which is costly and unscalable. A hidden test set not on arXiv would allow consistent, scalable benchmarking. (R3)
- The effectiveness of few-shot demonstrations from the co-author graph has not been validated by user studies. (R2 question overlapping with missing validation)  
- The live evaluation may not sufficiently prevent model contamination given the rapid pace of LLM development and their potential exposure to recent datasets. (R2 question overlapping with criticism of live evaluation)
