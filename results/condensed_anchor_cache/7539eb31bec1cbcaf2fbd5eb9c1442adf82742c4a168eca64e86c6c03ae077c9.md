## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents DLGrapher, a dual latent diffusion framework for generating large attributed graphs with high-dimensional node features and large node counts. It models node features via continuous diffusion and graph structure via discrete diffusion, applied on a joint latent representation obtained from a structure-aware VAE and a reversible graph coarsening scheme. The dual denoising objective jointly learns the dependencies between features and structure. The method is evaluated on three social graph datasets, molecular graphs (QM9), and structure-only synthetic graphs, reporting improvements in feature-structure interaction statistics (12.9×), downstream task performance (25.2%), and throughput (2.5×). Reviewers rated the paper mostly 3 (weak reject) except one rating of 6 (accept). The higher-rated reviewer found the paper well-written and the coarsening intuitive, while others raised concerns about motivation, baselines, presentation clarity, technical novelty, and evaluation completeness.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses the generation of large and complex attributed graphs, an important research direction in graph generation.
- The method jointly models discrete latent graph structure and continuous latent node features via dual diffusion (DDPM for nodes, D3PM for edges).
- Using a compressed graph as the latent space for diffusion is a novel and efficient approach; coarsening in the latent space is intuitive and convincing.
- Integrating discrete and continuous diffusion models is a novel exploration for attributed graph generation.
- The method is tailored for large graphs via efficient compression of node features and graph structures, leading to lower training and sampling times.
- Generating node features in the latent space yields performance improvements.
- The paper evaluates effectiveness across multiple metrics (e.g., degree, clustering coefficient, orbit statistics, feature statistics, MMD, novelty, validity, uniqueness).
- The paper is well-written and contributions are clearly presented.

### Weaknesses
- Motivation for generating social graphs (and using a generative model for social networks) is not clearly justified.
- Insufficient baselines: only two variants of DiGress and GruM are compared. Missing comparisons with recent diffusion-based large-graph generators, including Chen et al. (ICML 2023), Trivedi et al. (ICML 2024), and latent graph diffusion works (Nvdiff, Graphusion, Latent Graph Diffusion).
- Evaluations on standard graph generation benchmarks (SBM, Planar, Cora) are missing. Also missing comparisons on large-scale molecular datasets like MOSES and GuacaMol.
- The dual diffusion combination is described as straightforward; the paper lacks discussion of technical challenges and how they were overcome. An ablation study on the dual diffusion design is needed.
- Lack of rationale for choosing a compressed graph as the latent variable for diffusion instead of numerical tensors used in related works.
- Model description is unclear in several places:
  - Algorithm 1 notations are undefined (what are i, j, Pair_i, max(Pair), how is X initialized before appending concatenated node features?).
  - The decoarsening process is not clearly explained.
  - It is unclear how nodes dynamically change during diffusion/denoising.
  - Figure 1 has a typo (iv→vi) and equation (4) is confusing.
- The reversible coarsening method does not reduce the asymptotic computational complexity (still O(N²)). Limited to a single coarsening step, hindering application to even larger graphs.
- Using MSE loss on softmax/sigmoid outputs of the structure-aware VAE may be inappropriate; cross-entropy loss would be more suitable.
- Results in Tables 1 and 2 are reported without standard deviations, making significance assessment difficult. Table 2 lacks baseline comparisons entirely.
- Questions raised that overlap with criticisms or missing experiments:
  - How does the weight of cross-entropy loss for edge types affect performance?
  - What criterion is used to identify trailing zeros in node features generated by continuous diffusion?
  - Why does continuous modeling on QM9 (small graphs with discrete features) lead to higher novelty, and why does GruM not show similar novelty?
  - In Table 3, does “Dual Diffusion” include graph coarsening?
  - Can virtual nodes actually embed structural information?
  - How is the number of virtual nodes after coarsening determined? What is the starting node count for sampling?
  - Why are DiGress-only results and standalone GruM results not included in Table 1?
- The paper needs to differentiate its work from GDSS (which also uses dual diffusion) and from latent graph diffusion methods.
- Theoretical and empirical time complexity analysis is needed; a sensitivity analysis of wall-clock time vs. number of nodes would strengthen the paper.
- The method introduction is repetitive; presentation can be clarified by removing unnecessary information.
