## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper improves the analysis of EF21, reducing dependence on the quadratic mean of smoothness constants to their arithmetic mean. This is achieved by proposing EF21-W (a weighted variant using client-specific smoothness constants) and also via a refined analysis of the original EF21. Extensions cover stochastic gradients (EF21-SGD) and partial participation (EF21-PP). Experiments on non-convex logistic regression (libsvm datasets) and non-convex linear regression (synthetic data) show faster convergence when smoothness constants vary widely.

### Strengths
- Very well-written with a clear, instructive discovery narrative (reviewers 1,3,4).
- Exhaustive theory under general assumptions (Assumptions 1-3; PL inequality for linear convergence) (R1).
- Improvement in admissible stepsize is relevant for distributed optimization with highly varying smoothness constants (R1).
- Extensions to EF21-SGD and EF21-PP (R1).
- Intuitive algorithm, simple examples, and clear math in the main paper (R3).
- Experiments (figures) clearly illustrate faster convergence in high-variance smoothness cases (R3).
- Improves theoretical understanding of a popular existing method (R4).
- Contribution meaningful for promoting EF21 under certain cases (R4).
- Proposed weighted variant with better theoretical guarantees and empirical out-performance (R2).

### Weaknesses
- (R2, strong) The core idea of EF21-W (weighting gradients by smoothness constants) is independent of EF21 and communication compression. Without compression, the same improvement (quadratic → arithmetic mean) would hold for simple gradient averaging. The paper’s framing within EF21 seems deliberate and obscures this simplicity. (R2 also asked: would re-weighting alone, without compression, yield the same improvement?)
- (R1) It remains unclear whether observed improvements come from the larger admissible stepsize or from re-weighting clients. Experiments only compare EF21 (original stepsize) with EF21-W (improved stepsize), without isolating the two factors. An ablation study is needed; R1 requested clarifying experiments.
- (R1) Insufficient discussion of novelty: weighting by Lipschitz constants is closely related to importance sampling for stochastic optimization (e.g., Zhao & Zhang, ICML 2015). This connection should be addressed.
- (R2) Experiments are too simple and small: logistic regression on libsvm datasets and linear regression on synthetic data can be trained quickly on a laptop and do not require distributed training. The settings feel synthetic and limit meaningfulness.
- (R2) EF21-W is far from practical because obtaining smoothness constants is very difficult for real-world models (e.g., neural networks). R2 asked how to obtain such constants and apply the method in practice.
- (R3) No discussion of compressor choice: only top1 is used in all experiments, without varying compressors or step sizes as in the original EF21 paper. R3 asked why top1 was chosen.
- (R3) Limited justification for parameter choices: number of clients set to 1000 (logistic regression) and 2000 (linear regression). R3 asked how behavior changes with different client counts.
- (Disagreement) One reviewer (R4) considered the paper’s contribution as a refined theoretical analysis of the original EF21, not a new algorithm outperforming it. Other reviewers (R2, R3) viewed EF21-W as a novel weighted variant with better guarantees. This reflects differing interpretations of the contribution.
