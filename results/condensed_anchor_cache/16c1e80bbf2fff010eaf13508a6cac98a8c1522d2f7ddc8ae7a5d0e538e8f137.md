## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Policy Decorator, a method that uses a small residual policy (Gaussian) to refine large, offline-trained imitation learning policies (Behavior Transformer, Diffusion Policy) via online RL with sparse rewards. It freezes the base policy and trains only the residual, using a Progressive Exploration Schedule that gradually adds the residual action to the base action to control exploration. Experiments are conducted on eight tasks from ManiSkill and Adroit. The approach is simple and effective, but novelty concerns are raised by one reviewer.

### Strengths
- Simple and effective method that is easy to implement and understand (all reviewers).
- Thorough experimental evaluation with ablation studies across multiple tasks and two base policy architectures (Behavior Transformer and Diffusion Policy). Performance is strong compared to baselines (R1, R2, R3).
- Clear writing and presentation; assumptions are clearly stated (R1, R2, R3).
- Addresses an important research question: how to efficiently fine-tune large imitation learning policies with online RL, which could lead to major enhancements in robot learning (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern (minority view):** Reviewer 2 argues that Policy Decorator is essentially identical to old "Residual Policy Learning" ([1], [2]) – the only difference is using a frozen pretrained policy instead of a fixed controller. They contend that rebranding it as "Policy Decorator" is misleading and the core finding (residual policy learning is more effective than full finetuning for offline-to-online RL) does not constitute a new framework. This view contrasts with other reviewers, who found the method novel and practical.
- **Missing wall-clock times:** No wall-clock times are reported for the proposed method, making it impossible to assess whether Policy Decorator truly mitigates the cost of gradient updates on large models compared to baselines. (Wall-clock times for baselines RLPD and Cal-QL are only given in Appendices B.5 and F.3.) (R1)
- **Missing baseline: non-large policy models:** The paper does not compare against standard, non-large policy model baselines (e.g., GAIL with MLP or CNN policy). Such a comparison is needed to determine whether large policy models are even necessary on these tasks. (R1)
- **Multi-modality concerns (R1, R3):** The paper does not justify whether adding a Gaussian residual policy retains the multimodal output distribution that is a key advantage of large policy models. Reviewers question whether mode collapse occurs during fine-tuning. Suggested remedies: (a) benchmark on environments from Jia et al. (ICLR 2024) that provide diversity metrics for multi-modality; (b) plot the policy distribution for a multi-modal state (e.g., initial state) for both pre-trained and fine-tuned policy. (R1, R3)
- **Warm-starting critic for baselines is incomplete:** The paper investigates warm-starting the critic for baselines (Appendix F), but Section F.5.2 notes a blow-up in the entropy coefficient. A specific suggested fix: fix the entropy coefficient during critic pretraining and unfreeze it only during fine-tuning to stabilize the approach. (R1)
- **Dense reward availability:** The paper focuses on sparse rewards, but some tasks may have an easily-specified dense reward that could affect RL exploration. The paper should identify which tasks could use dense rewards for clarity. (R1)
- **Missing related works (R3):** The paper lacks discussion of: (1) Jia et al., "Towards diverse behaviors: A benchmark for imitation learning with human demonstrations" (ICLR 2024); (2) "Information maximizing curriculum: A curriculum-based approach for learning versatile skills" (NeurIPS 2023); (3) "Goal-conditioned imitation learning using score-based diffusion policies" (RSS 2023).
- **Minor issues:** Define "diversity" of demonstration data (Line 038); add missing comma between "quantity" and "quality" (Line 013); specify number of seeds for Figure 7 (presumed same as Figure 6?); correct typo "schedule" in Figure 4 caption. (R1)
