## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper explores alternatives to overlapped chunking for long audio sequences, a common technique to preserve context but which increases tensor size and computational cost. It proposes removing/reducing overlap via random sequence shifting and variable chunk sizes, then compensating for performance loss by increasing model parameters. Experiments on speech separation (SepFormer) and audio super-resolution (NU-Wave2) show reductions in runtime and memory while maintaining or improving objective metrics like SDR. The central claim—that reducing overlap frees compute budget to enlarge the model, improving overall performance—is demonstrated. However, reviewers note major issues with presentation, missing ablations, insufficient evaluation, and conceptual errors.

### Strengths

- Investigates an interesting and underexplored question: the effect of overlap in transformer-based audio separation models that treat chunks independently. (R1, R4)
- The central claim—that reduced overlap lowers computational cost, and that the saved budget can be reinvested into larger models to improve performance—is experimentally demonstrated. (R4)
- Achieves significant reduction in training/inference time and memory usage for two distinct tasks (speech separation and audio super-resolution). (R1, R2, R3)
- Applies specific modifications to two architectures (SepFormer and NU-Wave2) and shows those modifications enable larger models with maintained or improved performance. (R3)
- The paper explores alternatives to the almost universally used overlapped chunking, making the approach novel. (R1)

### Weaknesses

**Writing, Presentation, and Organization**
- The paper is poorly organized and bloated: Chapter 1 spends excessive space on overlap instead of providing an overview; Figures 1 and 2 are nearly full-page with limited information; five examples of chunking are unnecessary. (R1, R2)
- Text descriptions are very dense and difficult to follow; architecture diagrams are too brief (only a sequence of module names) and do not illustrate details; the more complex NU-Wave2 architectural changes lack any diagram. (R2, R3)
- Many experimental details are missing: shift threshold and how “too small” is defined; actual chunk sizes used; training infrastructure (device, hyperparameters); whether reported computation time includes only forward pass or also backward pass; whether inference still uses overlap-add for longer sequences. (R1, R3, R4)
- The introduction misstates well-known signal-processing facts: down-sampling is not equivalent to chunking without overlap; wav2vec produces latent features, not down-sampled audio; the Fourier transform does not assume periodicity; window tapering does not make the signal “somewhat periodic” but modifies spectral leakage. (R4)

**Insufficient Experiments and Ablations**
- No ablation studies: e.g., how much performance degrades with same model size as overlap is reduced; how much each proposed technique (shifting, variable chunk size, increased parameters) contributes individually; or how performance changes with varying overlap percentages (e.g., 50%, 25%, 10%, 5%, 0%). (R1, R2, R3)
- Limited scope: only two models tested, both from the specific dual-path architecture class; the methods are not trivial to apply to spectrogram-domain models (would break STFT invertibility) and the frequency-domain handling (NU-Wave2) risks spectral leakage, making conclusions less generalizable. (R3)
- Experiments use a single dataset per task with no confidence intervals, no cross-validation, and no evaluation of generalizability. (R3)
- No perceptual evaluation or task-specific metric (e.g., word error rate for ASR) to validate that the reduced overlap does not introduce audible artifacts (clicks) at chunk borders; objective SDR alone is insufficient to judge quality for human listeners. (R4)

**Conceptual Issues and Missing Related Work**
- The paper presents as new what is a very old and well-understood concept (overlap-add processing from digital signal processing) without differentiating DNN-specific aspects. (R4)
- No discussion of prior work that reduces computational overhead in long audio sequences (e.g., Demucs shift trick, which uses extreme overlap to gain ~0.3dB SDR). (R2, R4)
- The argument that increasing model parameters is a contribution is weak, since larger models commonly improve performance; it is unclear whether the improvements come from parameter increase alone or from the proposed overlap-reduction tricks. (R2, R3)
- The paper claims that down-sampled representations (like Encodec, DAC) cannot be used for audio generation tasks — a statement contradicted by current active research. (R4)
- Several reviewer questions overlap with missing experiments or unclear reasoning:
  - Performance with same chunk sizes? (R1’s Q1)
  - Performance with same number of parameters? (R1’s Q2)
  - Impact of chunk size on model performance? (R1’s Q3)
  - How does subtracting positional embeddings from transformer outputs achieve positional decoding? (R1’s Q4)
  - Are subjective evaluations or sample audio available? (R2’s question)
  - What device was used for timing, and does training time include backward pass? (R3’s questions)
  - For inference, is overlap-add still used? (R3)
  - What would be the effect on models that work in STFT domain but do not use overlapped processing internally? (R3)
