## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies an overlooked issue in batch-oriented evaluation for dynamic link prediction: fixed-size batches cause inconsistent time windows for continuous-time temporal graphs (CTDGs) and introduce temporal dependencies/data leakage for discrete-time temporal graphs (DTDGs). The authors define a Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) metric to quantify information loss and empirically demonstrate how fixed batch sizes distort the task. They propose a new evaluation protocol called “link forecasting” that uses fixed time windows instead of fixed-size batches. The paper reproduces experiments from major temporal graph neural networks (TGNNs) on several public datasets under this new protocol and provides code built on DyGLib.

### Strengths
* The paper addresses a significant but seldom-considered issue in temporal link prediction evaluation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).  
* Extensive experiments illustrate the limitations of the current batch-based approach and the effect of the new protocol. The analysis covers a wide range of TGNN models (memory-based, etc.) and major datasets for both CTDGs and DTDGs (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
* The problem statement is clearly articulated; the motivation for why fixed batch sizes alter the task is well illustrated with concrete examples (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
* The new “link forecasting” formulation is well-defined, and the provided code implementation is thorough and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).  
* The paper offers a novel perspective on evaluation biases that have been overlooked by prior benchmarks (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
* **Missing discussion of performance changes.** The paper explains why memory-based methods degrade on DTDGs, but does not adequately discuss why other methods improve or why CTDG results are mixed. Deeper analysis of reasons for performance shifts across diverse settings is needed (Reviewer 1).  
* **Unclear motivation and terminology.**  
  - The title uses “temporal graph learning” while the paper focuses only on evaluation, not training algorithms (Reviewer 2).  
  - The claim that “within each batch, edges are treated as if they occurred simultaneously, discarding temporal information” is disputed because TGNNs use temporal encodings. More toy examples are needed to clarify why information is still lost (Reviewer 2).  
  - The definition of dynamic link prediction in Section 2 (predicting edges in a batch) contradicts the standard view that prediction should be at a specific timestamp (Reviewer 2).  
  - The new term “link forecasting” is not clearly distinguished from “link prediction”; these are near-synonyms, causing ambiguity. Using “batch-based” vs. “window-based” evaluation is recommended (Reviewer 2).  
* **Flaws and missing discussions in the proposed approach.**  
  - The proposed window-based evaluation still uses batch splitting for GPU gradient computation (to avoid memory overflow), which may reintroduce the same temporal information ignoring that the paper criticizes (Reviewer 2).  
  - The method does not discuss the “empty window” issue—when a fixed time window contains zero edges—nor propose a solution (Reviewer 2).  
  - It is not explained whether link forecasting suffers from temporal training leakage when the window size \(h\) is larger than infinitesimal (Reviewer 3, Question 2).  
* **Incomplete methodological details.**  
  - No formal definition or computation method for NMI is provided; lines 266–268 are confusing without a formulation. Adding technical details (e.g., equations) in the appendix is necessary (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).  
  - There is no parameter sensitivity analysis for the window size \(h\). An empirical study similar to Figure 3 for \(h\) is expected (Reviewer 2).  
  - The experimental protocol is hard to follow: it appears models were trained with horizon-based sampling and then evaluated on both horizon-based and batch-based protocols. This needs clarification (Reviewer 3).  
* **Insufficient validation that the new protocol is better or that old evaluations are flawed.**  
  - The paper does not experimentally demonstrate biases or flaws in prior evaluations that used the batch-oriented protocol. The current experiments only compare results under the new protocol, not a direct head-to-head showing of “better” (Reviewer 4).  
  - No theoretical guarantees are provided for the evaluation protocol (Reviewer 2).  
* **Lack of hyperparameter tuning for the new task.**  
  - The models were not re-tuned for the link forecasting task; performance may be underestimated because the original hyperparameters were optimized for a different batch structure (Reviewer 3).  
* **Missing efficiency analysis.**  
  - There is no comparison of training/evaluation time between batch-based and window-based protocols. The brief comment on computational cost (line 393) is insufficient; experimental timing results are needed (Reviewer 4). A question about how training time scales with \(h\) is also raised (Reviewer 3, Question 4).  
* **Minor issues.**  
  - Figures 2 and 3 contain trivial/intuitive results and should be moved partly to the appendix (Reviewer 4).  
  - Typos: line 143 and line 244 notation is incorrect (should be \(\{t_{b\cdot i}, \dots, t_{b\cdot (i+1)-1}\}\)); line 146–147 missing constraint that \((u,v)\) must be in \(B^+\) or \(B^-\); line 346 ambiguous wording on negative edges (Reviewer 4).  
  - The paper would benefit from a separate Related Work section that discusses how existing TGNN benchmarks relate to the identified issue (Reviewer 4).  
  - Code modifications to DyGLib should be documented with comments or code snippets in the appendix (Reviewer 4).  
  - For ranking-based temporal link prediction methods, more discussion on pros/cons of time-window vs. ranking approaches is needed (Reviewer 4, minor).  
* **Unclear or missing comparisons.**  
  - It is unclear whether the claim that DLP yields different information loss for different models is justified, given a fixed batch size (Reviewer 3, Question 1).  
  - The relative significance of variable time gaps vs. temporal leakage is not investigated (Reviewer 3, Question 3).  
  - An experiment where a model is trained with DLP pipeline and then evaluated on both DLP and LF is requested to show the impact (Reviewer 3, Question 5).  

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** While Reviewer 3 describes the experiments as “nearly exhaustive” and the problem as clearly presented, Reviewers 2 and 4 raise more fundamental concerns about missing definitions, flawed assumptions, and insufficient validation of the proposed protocol.
