## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper analyzes robustness and generalization in DreamerV2-style world models by framing latent dynamics as a stochastic differential equation (SDE). It models two types of latent representation errors (LRE): zero-drift and non-zero-drift. The theoretical analysis shows that modest zero-drift errors can act as implicit regularization, while non-zero-drift errors harm stability. The authors propose a Jacobian regularization to mitigate non-zero-drift errors and validate it on Walker and Quadruped MuJoCo tasks.

### Strengths

- Novel theoretical perspective: applying continuous-time SDE formulation to discrete latent transitions in world models is a useful contribution, leveraging tools from stochastic dynamical systems and differential geometry that are rarely used in this area. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Addresses a popular class of world models (Dreamer-family) that have strong empirical success but lack substantial theoretical analysis. (R1, R3)
- The Jacobian regularization scheme is simple, to my knowledge novel for Dreamer-style models, and leads to empirical performance improvements (faster convergence, better robustness to noisy observations, perturbed dynamics, and encoder errors). (R2, R4)
- Theoretical analysis leads to a derived regularization term that actually improves empirical performance. (R3)
- Overall, the paper is well-written and clear. (R1 – note: this is disputed by R2/R4 regarding readability; preserved as minority positive view.)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited experimental evaluation**: Experiments are restricted to only two MuJoCo environments (Walker and Quadruped). It is unclear how the method generalizes to other DMC or Atari environments. (R1, R2, R4) Also, the paper does not compare against existing robustness-enhancing methods for world models, such as Repo or RPC, or even simple L2 regularization on dynamics weights. The only baseline is the non-regularized version of the same model, which lacks similar inductive bias. (R1, R2, R4) One reviewer (R1) notes that the hyperparameter sensitivity of the Jacobian regularization weight λ is not discussed either theoretically or empirically. (R2)
- **Poor readability and unclear presentation**: The paper is dense and difficult to follow; important concepts like Latent Representation Errors (LRE), zero/non-zero drift, and Jacobian regularization are introduced very late and not explained in accessible natural language before the heavy mathematics. Several reviewers found the presentation inadequate (R2, R4). Specific issues:
    - Theorems are weakly interpreted: primary variables and parameters are not sufficiently explained (e.g., Theorem 3.6 says ε can be assumed small without detailing the conditions); this hampers understanding of the theoretical contributions. (R1, R3)
    - The motivation for why this particular analysis approach is needed is insufficient. (R2)
- **Strong theoretical assumptions**: The analysis assumes C3 functions with bounded Lipschitz derivatives, which can be unrealistic for practical neural network world models. (R2)
- **Missing experimental and implementation details**:
    - The experimental settings for Table 1 are not explained or referenced in the introduction. (R3)
    - How the Jacobian regularization term (Equation 20) is implemented in practice is not described in the main paper or appendix. (R3)
    - The paper focuses on representation errors but does not address errors in the transition model, which are arguably the most critical in latent dynamics models (Equations 5-6). (R3)
- **Unclear or insufficiently justified theoretical connections**:
    - Corollary 4.2 regarding Q-functions is not derived; there are no reward variables in the SDE formulation. (R3)
    - The ground truth probability measure P in Line 232 is not specified; how the two distribution-minimization losses in Dreamer (reconstruction + prediction) relate to the W1 distance and ε is unclear. (R3)
    - How Theorem 4.1 correlates with Hafner et al. (2019)'s empirical finding about predictive accuracy degradation over long horizons is not elaborated. (R1)
- **Typographical errors**: Missing parentheses on citations in Related Work; duplicated “choice choice” on Line 247. (R1)

Note: Reviewer 1 was more positive (score 5) despite these weaknesses, while reviewers 2 and 4 were more negative (score 3). Reviewer 3 gave 5 but raised many unresolved questions, indicating a conditional positive assessment. The minority positive view on clarity (from R1) contrasts with the majority complaint about readability.
