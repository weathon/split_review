## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a supervised learning framework for zero-shot time series imputation in dynamical systems governed by ODEs. The framework generates a large synthetic dataset of ODE solutions with noisy sparse observations, and trains a neural recognition model (inspired by deepONet) offline to map observations to initial conditions and time derivatives, then integrates to impute missing data. Two models are introduced: FIM (point-wise missing) and FIM-l (temporal missing). Evaluation is across 63 time series and 10 high-dimensional settings including human motion, air quality, traffic, electricity, and Navier-Stokes, often outperforming SOTA methods that are trained on the target datasets.

### Strengths
- First work to introduce zero-shot time series imputation with dimension-free inference; novel and interesting direction.
- Novel training objective and supervision: recognition model directly learns to fit initial conditions and time derivatives.
- Zero-shot performance is surprisingly good, achieving SOTA on multiple datasets without any fine-tuning.
- Methodology well-structured, linking amortized inference and neural operators.
- Experiments cover a wide range of datasets and both point-wise and temporal missing patterns, proving zero-shot capability.
- Synthetic data generation method offers a general approach for creating meaningful synthetic time series, valuable for future research.
- Paper is well written and organized; includes faithful details for reproducibility.
- Authors are honest about limitations (e.g., synthetic distribution assumptions, ODE modeling vs. real-world data).

### Weaknesses
- Figure 2 is difficult to read: colors are too similar (Reviewer 1).
- Paper is too "buzzwordy"; would prefer more proper technical discussion (Reviewer 1).
- Results are not always close to SOTA or better than SOTA; e.g., Table 2 results are not that impressive (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The FIM-l model only works for time series that follow a "simple" distribution, limiting practical value since existing imputation models already perform well (Reviewer 1).
- Authors did not clearly explain how FIM handles processes of any dimensionality, and how performance varies with dimensionality in practice (Reviewer 1).
- Baseline comparisons may be unfair because the authors use reported values from other papers; ODE-based baselines should be included given the paper's concept (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation studies for measuring generalization capability would be beneficial (Reviewer 2).
- When the actual application does not match the simulation data assumption, performance can deteriorate; no discussion of fine-tuning or adaptation to specific applications. Since training requires ground-truth initial/time derivatives, such training cannot directly apply to real datasets where these are unknown (Reviewer 3).
- Design of the neural recognition model (branch-net) is not well justified: why use RNN instead of attention? Given RNNs/LSTMs are largely replaced by attention, authors should explain rationale theoretically or empirically (Reviewer 3).
- Hyperparameter selection (e.g., 1024-dimensional embeddings) is unclear; not specified whether a rigorous validation process was used (Reviewer 3).
- The idea of zero-shot amortized inference is somewhat incremental: there are existing works on amortized inference for few-shot time-series forecasting (e.g., [1][2][3][4]) that learn prior knowledge of dynamics. Missing discussion of these works and comparison in experiments (Reviewer 4).
- Technical clarity: In Equations 3, 4, and 5, do φ^θ_i and ψ^θ share the same parameters θ? Similar question for parameter ψ in FIM model, Equations 7 and 8 (Reviewer 4).
