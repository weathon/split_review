## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper conducts a detailed experimental study of training instabilities in Transformers, focusing on reproducing at small scale two instabilities previously observed in large models: growth of attention logits and divergence of output logits. By increasing the learning rate (LR), the authors show these instabilities appear in small models and that mitigations (qk-layernorm, z‑regularization) remain effective. They introduce LR sensitivity as a metric and ablate warm‑up, weight decay, μParam, and model scaling. Findings include that LR sensitivity grows faster with depth than width, that default AdamW epsilon (1e‑8) can be too large, and that instability can be predicted from the scaling of maximum attention logits and gradient norms. The experiments are thorough, well‑controlled, and clearly presented, providing useful heuristics for practitioners.

### Strengths
- **Reproducing large‑scale instabilities in small models** – Demonstrates that attention logit growth and output logit divergence (previously seen only at scale) occur in small Transformers when LR is increased, and the relationship between LR and loss shifts with model size (Figures 1, 6). This strongly suggests the same mechanisms are at play across scales.
- **Verification of mitigations** – qk‑layernorm (Figure 1) and z‑regularization (Figure 2) are shown to significantly reduce LR sensitivity and expand the stable LR range across model sizes, confirming their effectiveness at small scale.
- **Predicting instabilities from scaling behavior** – Shows that the maximum attention logit value and the onset of instability can be extrapolated from smaller runs and different LRs (Figure 6).
- **Novel observations** – The independent parameterization of weight decay and LR, and the analysis of gradient RMS scaling, are highlighted as relatively new insights. The finding that default AdamW epsilon can be too large (causing updates to vanish) is practical.
- **Overall thoroughness and clarity** – Extensive ablations are well motivated and documented (Reviewer 1: “very thorough and supported the claims very well”). The writing is clear, figures are informative, and the experimental setup is described in abundance of detail (Reviewer 2). The paper is well organized and the analysis tools (LR sensitivity) allow a synthetic summary.
- **Impact and utility** – Providing a small‑scale proxy for expensive large‑scale training is impactful (Reviewer 4). The work has the potential to offer useful heuristics for practitioners working with large Transformers (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of concrete rules of thumb** – Despite thorough ablations, the paper does not provide explicit, actionable recipes for setting hyperparameters based on small‑scale runs (Reviewer 1). A short discussion on how practitioners could apply the findings to fix instabilities would strengthen the work.
- **Limited to C4 (text) data and AdamW optimizer** – All experiments use the C4 dataset and AdamW; it is unclear whether the scaling behaviors and conclusions transfer to other modalities (e.g., images, speech) or other optimizers (Reviewer 1). Reviewers request comments on generalizability to other optimizers (Reviewer 1) and architectures (Reviewer 3 asks “do such problems arise in other architectures?”).
- **Ecological validity and niche audience** – One reviewer (Reviewer 2) questions whether the small‑model instabilities are truly representative of the complex dynamics of large‑scale training, and notes that the paper does not provide a thorough correlation analysis between instabilities at different scales. The same reviewer feels the immediate audience for this work is very small, raising concerns about practical relevance. (Reviewer 2’s confidence is lower and contribution rating “fair”.)
- **Replication vs. novelty** – A significant portion of the paper replicates observations made in large Transformers to small models; the utility of this is debatable because it does not demonstrate new insights about large models that could not have been inferred from prior work (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 suggests that making previously unknown observations at small scale and then verifying them at large scale would be more impactful.
- **Mechanism and intuition for logit instabilities** – The paper does not make fully clear why attention logit growth or output logit divergence cause training failure, beyond showing that high LR triggers them (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 notes that divergence at high LR is unsurprising and questions whether the evidence validates the hypothesis that these specific instabilities are the root cause for large Transformers. The same reviewer finds the paper’s structure odd (short conclusion, results mostly in figures) and requests more intuition.
- **Section 3.3 feels anecdotal** – The prediction of instabilities from maximum attention logits is not studied systematically enough; a more rigorous approach would be stronger (Reviewer 4).
- **LR sensitivity metric not normalized** – The metric (average excess val loss over a range of LRs) may not be comparable across model sizes because optimal loss scales with size; normalization should be considered (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarifications needed** – The meaning of “optimal” in Figure 1 (Reviewer 1) and the phrasing in Section 3.3 about predicting instabilities (which could be misinterpreted as within‑run prediction rather than cross‑run extrapolation) should be clarified (Reviewer 1). The faster growth of LR sensitivity with depth vs. width deserves further intuitive explanation (Reviewer 1).

*Note:* Some concerns raised by Reviewer 3 were noted as “addressed” in the author response, but the original criticisms are preserved here as part of the review record.
