## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method for learning spatiotemporal dynamics from randomly sampled point process observations (e.g., sparse sensor networks). It combines neural ODEs, neural point processes, implicit neural representations, and amortized variational inference. Sparse context points are encoded into a latent state; dynamics evolve via a neural ODE over large time steps, with fine temporal resolution obtained via interpolation in the latent space. A decoder maps to observation space. A speedup technique using interpolation to avoid dense ODE evaluations is introduced. Experiments on synthetic (Shallow Water, Navier-Stokes) and real-world datasets show improved predictive accuracy and computational efficiency over baselines including AutoSTPP and Neural-STPP. Reviewers praise the originality, clear presentation, and comprehensive experiments, but raise concerns about theoretical justification, interpretability, computational costs, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The challenge is well-grounded: sparse data over a large spatial domain is common (e.g., few agent trajectories, sensor networks). (Reviewer 1)
- The method appears scalable with low-resolution linear interpolation. (Reviewer 1)
- The mathematical formulation is clear and the empirical results are fair. (Reviewer 1)
- Extensive ablation studies are provided, accounting for context size, resolution, and removal of components. (Reviewer 1)
- Original and elegant combination of different ML fields (neural ODEs, point processes, implicit neural representations, amortized variational inference); does not rely on complex architectures, mostly emerges from well-placed MLPs. (Reviewer 3)
- Claims are well supported by experiments against recent competitive ML architectures; figures are well designed and support the message. (Reviewer 3)
- The manuscript is well organized, structured, and written; a rich appendix provides model design details. (Reviewer 3)
- Results appear significant, especially under sparse data conditions; three synthetic problems (varying difficulty) and a real-world problem demonstrate applicability. (Reviewer 3)
- Metrics are reported with standard deviations. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper is technically sound and the experimental setup is detailed; ablation studies are comprehensive, showing the impact of many parameters. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- Lack of theoretical analysis or visualization explaining how the low-dimensional latent space captures full dynamics from sparse observations; no discussion of information-theoretic bounds on what can be learned from sparse data. (Reviewer 1)
- No clear justification for using a transformer to generate initial latent states; why it is superior to other sequence models is not analyzed. No ablation on this architectural choice. (Reviewer 1)
- Neural ODEs are still slow and may not scale well; detailed training/inference time comparison relative to number of spatial locations or temporal sequence length is missing. (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 3 requests runtime comparison for generating a rollout of spatiotemporal frames.)
- The advantage of using an ODE solver in latent space over directly solving the PDE (Eq. 5) with a neural network is unclear; potential stiffness issues in high-dimensional PDE systems are not addressed. (Reviewer 2)
- The latent approach reduces interpretability; there are no theoretical guarantees on the quality of the learned latent space, and it is unclear how the encoder architecture influences it. (Reviewer 2)
- It is unclear whether the method can learn general systems; certain PDE models may not be well captured by a low-dimensional latent space, especially those with complex or multi-scale behavior. No analysis of how latent dimensionality affects accuracy and generalization. (Reviewer 2)
- The claim of continuity is not rigorously validated; no interpolation experiment shows recovery of completely hidden portions of trajectories during training. (Reviewer 2)
- The observation function is constrained to a normal distribution with fixed variance; justification and discussion of expressivity limits are missing. (Reviewer 3)
- Ablations showing performance under different spatial and temporal sparsities would be highly informative; extending this ablation to other benchmarked methods would provide insights about data efficacy. (Reviewer 3)
- Limitations are not reported; it is unclear how much fine-tuning is required for a new task. (Reviewer 3)
- The model may require many calls per spatial position per time step; a runtime comparison with other models when generating a full rollout is needed. (Reviewer 3)
- The differences between the proposed method and AutoSTPP (which performs almost equally well) need more elaboration to explain why the method is superior. (Reviewer 3)
- Minor: typo in line 306 (“withing”); N_ctx is unclear in Figure 4 (see Appendix C.1). (Reviewer 3)
- More discussion on the limitations of the Poisson process assumption and potential solutions would be welcome. (Reviewer 4, confidence 2)
- Interpretability of the learned dynamical system could be enhanced. (Reviewer 4)
- Additional questions that overlap with missing experiments/ablations:
  - Why predict the next sensor signal's location? (Table 2 suggests removal; what is the benefit and computational cost?) (Reviewer 3)
  - Have you tried higher-order interpolations (quadratic, cubic)? What error is incurred from interpolation compared to modeling the full temporal grid? (Reviewer 3)
  - Have you explored other ODE solvers (e.g., Euler) instead of dopri5? Does the method depend on an adaptive solver? (Reviewer 3)
  - How does latent dimensionality d_z affect runtime and parameter count? (Reviewer 3)
- Reviewer disagreement: One reviewer (score 6) was more critical, raising fundamental concerns about clarity, justification, and interpretability, while the other three reviewers (scores 8,8,8) were very positive. Reviewer 4 also had lower confidence (2). This contrast should be considered in assessing the paper's impact.
