## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CoLa-DCE (Concept-guided Latent Diffusion Counterfactual Explanations), a method for generating controlled counterfactual explanations for image classifiers. It extends LDCE by introducing concept selection (via Concept-wise Relevance Propagation) and spatial conditioning to guide the diffusion process, aiming to improve transparency about which features change and where. Experiments are performed on multiple datasets (ImageNet, etc.) and classifiers, with quantitative and qualitative evaluations. While one reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) stressed the novelty and practical usefulness for model debugging, two reviewers (score 3, confidence 3 and 4) raised major concerns about writing clarity, inadequate quantitative results compared to the baseline, and whether the method actually delivers on its core promise of local, concept‑restricted edits. The overall assessment is mixed, with the positive reviewer acknowledging the idea but the others unconvinced by the execution and evidence.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and control**: The idea of combining latent diffusion with concept‑level and spatial conditioning for counterfactual generation is novel, providing a finer‑grained control than existing methods (e.g., Dataset Interfaces, LANCE). (R1, R2, R3)
- **Applicability across settings**: Experiments cover multiple datasets (ImageNet, etc.), classifier architectures, and diffusion models, demonstrating broad applicability. (R2, R3)
- **Practical usefulness for debugging**: The approach shows potential for diagnosing model failures (misclassification cases), which could help evaluate robustness and fairness. (R1)
- **Successful integration**: Concept conditioning is incorporated into the LDCE pipeline without catastrophic performance loss; generated counterfactuals are realistic and flip the classifier’s decision to the target class. (R3)
- **Comprehensive evaluation design**: The experiments address diverse questions (number of concepts, spatial constraints, failure explanation, concept alignment) and include supplementary results in the appendix. (R3)
- **Clear problem motivation**: The paper clearly states the limitations of prior works (lack of transparency in which features change) and positions CoLa‑DCE as a solution. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Quantitative results are not competitive**: CoLa‑DCE consistently achieves a better FID than LDCE, but the improvement is small. On more critical metrics—Flip Ratio and Confidence—LDCE clearly outperforms CoLa‑DCE (Table 1). The L1 norm is also worse, and the L2 norm (mentioned in line 295) is never reported in Table 1. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Missing evaluation metrics and experiments**:
  - No human study is conducted, even though the paper claims improved comprehensibility and usefulness for debugging. (R1)
  - Additional metrics (e.g., cosine similarity between CLIP embeddings or target‑model embeddings) could better capture perceptual differences; these are not reported. (R1)
  - A comparison with an additional baseline (e.g., text‑to‑image counterfactual methods, [WACV 2024]) is not included. (R2)
- **Core claim of local editing is not convincingly demonstrated**: In all figures (1, 6, 8, 9), the counterfactual modifies the entire image, not only the concept‑related areas. This is likely because (a) spatial constraints are only applied at the semantic latent level, and (b) concept conditioning is suspended for the last 50 generation steps. Consequently, it is unclear whether the decision flip is caused by concept‑related edits or by changes in complementary regions. (R3)
- **Writing clarity is poor** (multiple instances from R2 and R3):
  - Figure 2 is unclear: the connection between Decoder and External classifier has no arrow and is unexplained. (R2)
  - Notations are not defined or inconsistently used: e.g., \(d, \kappa\) in line 224; \(\mathcal{N}\) (natural numbers vs. normal distribution); \(h, g, \delta\) appear without domain descriptions; \(\lambda_1, ..., \lambda_k\) and \(\theta_1, ..., \theta_k\) are confusing (line 260) – \(\theta\) changes from 0 to k vs 1 to k, and Eq. 7 suggests \(\theta\) are binary masks but are presented as index subsets. (R2)
  - Differences between datasets \(X'\) and \(\hat{X}\) (lines 219, 226) are not explained. (R2)
  - The external classifier is modelled as \(p(x|y)\) (line 259), but a classifier should be \(p(y|x)\). (R2)
  - The confidence metric is not defined; it is unclear whether it is the difference in classifier probability before/after modification. (R2)
  - The description of how concept influence is obtained is missing from Section 4. (R2)
  - “Using the intermediate … high flip ratios” (lines 319–320) is an unexplained conclusion. (R2)
  - The attribution function \(attr\) in line 469 is not described. (R2)
  - Section 4 keeps the description of concept and spatial conditioning at a high level; the pseudocode (Algorithm 1) collapses both into one line without distinguishing how binary constraints come from each source. (R3)
  - The gradient in line 238 is taken wrt. the latent representation, while Section 4.3 uses \(x\), suggesting input‑space constraints. (R3)
- **Quantitative trends are unpredictable**: In the appendix (Tables 4, 5), increasing the number of concepts sometimes drastically reduces Flip Ratio (e.g., moving from 50 to 100 concepts), which is counterintuitive. (R3)
- **Feature visualizations are incomplete**: Figures only show attribution maps for the source class, not the target class, and likely only the top‑few concepts rather than all used during generation (e.g., Fig. 1 uses >5 concepts). This limits the claimed improvement in comprehensibility. (R3)
- **Minor issues**: The code URL (line 100) is not working. (R1) Citation hyperlinks are broken, forcing manual lookup. (R3)
- **Incorrect statement about prior work**: The paper claims DiME is “limited to robust classifiers explicitly trained on noisy images”; in fact, DiME works with standard classifiers. (R3)

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) was notably more positive, praising the writing, motivation, and debugging potential, while the other two (score 3) were strongly critical of writing quality, quantitative evidence, and the fulfillment of the core local‑editing claim.
