## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces "model cautiousness" — a concept requiring a model to be calibrated on in-distribution (ID) data while exhibiting high uncertainty on out-of-distribution (OOD) data. It proposes the Expected Cautiousness Error (ECauE) metric and a post-hoc method that merges a base confidence model with a separate OOD discriminator. The approach is evaluated on 12 QA and 37 vision datasets. Reviewers are split: one reviewer (score 6) finds the topic innovative and the proposal reasonable, while three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) raise significant concerns about circular reasoning, missing baselines, unjustified assumptions, definitional flaws, and lack of practical motivation.

### Strengths
- **Paper writing and presentation**: Coherent narrative, logical flow, clear schematic illustrations; readers can quickly grasp the main ideas (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). However, Reviewer 2 and 3 note missing definitions and typos.
- **Innovative perspective**: The idea of treating uncertainty on OOD data differently than on ID data is neat and offers a systems-level view of model safety (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Reasonable proposal structure**: The cautious confidence model and its components align with the formulated problem and are supported by theoretical foundations (Reviewer 1).
- **Extensive evaluation**: Broad coverage of datasets across two tasks (12 QA + 37 vision) with one model per task (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Minor contrast**: Reviewer 3 explicitly states no strengths found, disagreeing with the above assessments.

### Weaknesses
- **Circular reasoning / lack of external justification**: The paper defines a metric (ECauE) and then proposes a method that improves upon it without justifying why the metric is meaningful beyond the authors’ opinion. There is no concrete benefit demonstrated for models behaving this way (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewer 4, partially acknowledged by Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baseline comparisons**:
  - No comparison to existing OOD detection methods combined with calibration techniques (e.g., using a well-known OOD detector and hardcoding low confidence for OOD inputs) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
  - No comparison to selective prediction (abstention) methods, which already require a model to abstain when uncertain – a strictly stronger notion that subsumes the proposed cautiousness (Reviewer 2).
  - No baselines that also account for model cautiousness (Reviewer 1).
- **Unrealistic assumptions**:
  - Binary classification assumption (line 128) not suitable for real-world tasks like ImageNet; authors redefine Y as indicator of correct prediction, but it is unclear if the theory extends (Reviewer 3).
  - Assumption that ID embeddings follow a normal distribution while OOD embeddings do not (lines 255-256) – no evidence provided for realistic datasets (Reviewer 3).
- **Flaws in crucial definitions and derivations**:
  - Target label "y" ambiguous: unclear whether it is a class label or an indicator of correct prediction (lines 303) (Reviewer 3).
  - ID and OOD operationalization unjustified: declaring a dataset OOD relative to a pretrained model is questionable – the model may have been trained on similar data, and OOD should be defined relative to the training or instruction-tuning distribution (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
  - Calibration details missing: how models are calibrated on ID is not described beyond “use a standard calibration approach” (line 236); same for fitting the OOD detector g(x) (Reviewer 3).
  - H(p,q) divergently defined: as a probability in Eq. 5 (line 192) and as an expectation in line 202 (Reviewer 3).
  - Equation 7 confusion: appears always true (h(x)=p(1-q)+1/2 q) when f(x)=p, g(x)=q, which would mean all models are trivially “cautious” (Reviewer 3).
  - Derivation of Eq. 5 questionable: reviewer 3 gives an alternative conditioning (Reviewer 3).
- **Weak empirical results**:
  - Downstream task performance not reported for QA or vision; ECE not provided for QA results in Table 1 (Reviewer 3).
  - For vision (Figure 2), adding the OOD detection model sometimes increases ECE, violating the goal of maintaining calibration on ID (Reviewer 3).
  - Computational burden: computing ECauE requires training an additional OOD detection model, unlike standard calibration metrics (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing discussions and presentation issues**:
  - No discussion of limitations of the problem formulation or method (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
  - PRAUC not defined (line 338) – assumed to be AUPRC (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
  - Typos: “we are confidence” (line 142), “in turn” (line 223), “describes” (line 266), “Choleskly” → “Cholesky” (line 276) (Reviewer 3).
  - PDF lacks hyperlinks on sections/equations/citations, making reading tedious (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**:
  - Can the formulation extend to near-OOD and far-OOD instead of binary OOD event? (Reviewer 1) – aligns with criticism that binary OOD is too simplistic (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
  - Why is calibration on ID and uncertainty on OOD the right goal, rather than aiming for calibration on both ID and OOD? (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 2: “if the model is calibrated on OOD, is it really OOD?”) – this questions the entire motivation.
  - Why not solve calibration and OOD detection separately? (Reviewer 3) – closely tied to the missing baseline argument.
  - What is the need for a new OOD detection method (C2) given the plethora of existing methods? (Reviewer 4) – echoes missing comparisons.
  - Why is the Bernoulli formulation in line 145 needed? (Reviewer 3) – relates to unclear binary assumption.
  - In Figure 3, should ECauE be low on OOD and high on ID? (Reviewer 3) – suggests possible misinterpretation.
- **Contrast in reviewer opinion**: One reviewer (score 6) finds the motivation well-explained and the method reasonable, while the other three (score 3 each) view the motivation as circular, the assumptions unrealistic, and the contributions insufficiently distinguished from existing work. This disagreement is particularly sharp on the framing of OOD as a binary event and the justification of the ECauE metric.
