## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PASDA for differentially private synthetic data generation using pre-trained foundation models (Stable Diffusion, CLIP, UNCLIP). It first generates a class-conditional synthetic base dataset, then extracts differentially private CLIP feature statistics from the private data (via clustering and DP mean), aligns synthetic embeddings to these statistics, and decodes to image space. The approach aims to improve the privacy-utility tradeoff without iterative optimization or fine-tuning. Scores vary widely: 5, 1, 3, 1 – reflecting disagreement between reviewers who find the idea promising (R1, R3) and those who consider the DP guarantee fundamentally flawed (R2, R4).

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem (privacy-utility tradeoff in image generation) and is generally well-written (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Straightforward to implement and ready for practical use (R1).
- Promising use of pre-trained foundation models for DP data generation (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Efficient: no iterative optimization of synthetic data or fine-tuning of pre-trained models required (R3, R4).
- Novel combination of one-step DP budget consumption and foundational APIs, extending the DPSDA trend (R3).
- Extensive main experiments and ablation studies that help explain why PASDA works (R3).
- Achieves state-of-the-art results, e.g., surpassing the best baseline by over 13% on CIFAR-10 (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Flawed differential privacy guarantee.** The clustering step is not accounted for in the privacy analysis. The mechanism clips feature vectors, clusters them, adds Gaussian noise to cluster centroids, but assumes cluster assignments are fixed under neighbouring datasets. In reality, inserting or removing one point can completely change the clustering (reviewer provides a one-dimensional counterexample with three well-separated intervals and a single added point), leading to arbitrarily high sensitivity of the centroids. Without a differentially private clustering procedure, the claimed DP is not valid as presented. (R2; also noted by R1 and R3 regarding lack of clarity and missing full proof.)
- **Incomplete privacy accounting.** No explicit L2-sensitivity or neighbouring definition (add-one/replace-one) is given. The number of clusters \(K\) and label categories \(c\) should linearly affect the privacy cost \(\epsilon\), but this is missing from the pseudocode and Corollary 1. If without-replacement subsampling is used, sensitivity should be \(2\kappa\), not \(\kappa\). (R1, R4.)
- **Missing full privacy proof.** Partitioning by label may not satisfy full DP (including label protection). The Hungarian matching step may leak private information. A complete, step-by-step privacy proof is needed. (R3.)
- **Errors and typos in algorithm/notation.** e.g., “Gaussian noise to the domain gap vector” – noise is actually added to the aggregated embedding vector. The equation \(\hat{\Delta}v = \Delta v + \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) omits \(\kappa\). Algorithm 2 line 10 uses \(s\) instead of \(\kappa\). (R4.)
- **Limited novelty.** Feature matching for DP data generation is a well-established approach; reviewers list prior works: DP-MERF (AISTAT 2021), DPSW (ICML 2021), PEARL (ICLR 2022), Hermite (ICML 2022), DP-NTK (JAIR). The paper should also compare to DP dataset condensation methods: Zheng & Li (NDSS) and Vinaroz & Park (TMLR). (R1, R3.)
- **Algorithm presentation needs clarity.** e.g., definition of \(\mathcal{C}_m^{\text{syn}}\) is missing from Algorithm 1; subscripts for \((\mathcal{S}^{\text{(syn)}}, \mathcal{S}^{\text{(priv)}})\) should be explicit in line 11. Name should be consistent: “Privacy-Aware” vs. “Private-Aware” (mixed usage). (R1, R3.)
- **Missing experimental investigations.**  
  - Robustness to large domain shifts (e.g., medical data) is not tested. (R1, R3.)  
  - Test on data definitely not in pre-training sets (e.g., the cat benchmark used by DPSDA) to ensure features are learned from private data, not from pre-training memorization. (R3.)  
  - Compare methods across varying \(\epsilon\) values (utility vs. \(\epsilon\) curves) for fairer comparison. (R1.)  
  - The DPSDA results on CIFAR-10 (Table 1: 25.7%, 47.1%) contradict the original paper (Figure 5: >85% under \(\epsilon=3.34\)). This discrepancy needs clarification. (R1.)  
- **Pre-training data transparency.** The pre-training datasets for all models (diffusion, CLIP, UNCLIP) must be explicitly stated for both the method and baselines; otherwise gains may merely reflect stronger pre-training. (R1.)  
- **Minor questions.** Clarify the neighbouring dataset definition (add-one vs. replace-one). (R4.)
