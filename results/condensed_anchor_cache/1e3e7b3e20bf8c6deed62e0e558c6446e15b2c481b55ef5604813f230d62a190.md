## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ProPETL, a method that adds a midstream adaptation phase to Parameter Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) for semantic segmentation. It addresses the perceptual granularity gap between pre-trained models (typically from classification) and segmentation tasks by progressively aligning representations via intermediate tasks (e.g., different perception granularities and supervision diversity). ProPETL achieves state-of-the-art performance on most benchmarks and, notably, surpasses full fine-tuning on COCO-Stuff10k. The authors analyze Generalized Parametric Adaptation (GPA) and Decoupled Structured Adaptation (DSA), selecting DSA to mitigate forgetting.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation: identifies that semantic segmentation requires larger parameter adjustments than classification due to a perceptual granularity gap, which existing PETL methods cannot accommodate.
- The intermediate task design (perception granularity and supervision diversity) is considered interesting and novel by multiple reviewers.
- Detailed analysis and comparison of two progressive adaptation strategies, GPA and DSA, with DSA used to avoid the forgetting issue observed with GPA.
- Extensive experiments across multiple segmentation benchmarks, thorough ablation studies, and comparisons with state-of-the-art PETL and full fine-tuning methods. ProPETL outperforms full fine-tuning on COCO-Stuff10k, demonstrating that parameter-efficient methods can match or exceed traditional fine-tuning.
- The paper is well-written and clearly organized, with visualizations illustrating weaknesses of existing methods and strengths of ProPETL.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient analysis for pre-trained models with strong segmentation capabilities** (e.g., SAM). The assumption that a perceptual granularity gap exists for all pre-trained models is not justified; no experiments or analysis are provided for models already pre-trained on segmentation tasks. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear necessity and theoretical foundation of the midstream adaptation.** The intermediate tasks resemble data augmentation; the paper does not compare against integrating these tasks directly into the fine-tuning phase (e.g., scaling images to different sizes, varying categories at random ratios). The selection of intermediate tasks (e.g., alpha=1, specific perception granularity levels) appears heuristic without systematic justification or a clear methodology for adapting to new datasets/tasks. No mathematical or conceptual framework explains why these specific choices are optimal. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Fixed reduction ratio \(r\) in the bypass branches of GPA/DSA may lose critical feature information** for high-perceptual-demand tasks like semantic segmentation. A uniform reduction across channels could discard discriminative information for small or less prominent objects, affecting segmentation boundaries and accuracy. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational overhead of the midstream phase is not adequately quantified.** The paper lacks detailed comparisons of training time, inference time, FLOPs, GPU memory, and storage versus other PETL methods. Without this, it is difficult to assess the practical trade-offs, especially in resource-constrained environments. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Freezing the midstream parameters \(\phi\) during downstream fine-tuning may limit optimization capacity.** For complex downstream tasks, restricting the tunable parameter space forces the model to seek solutions in a smaller space, potentially impairing performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Generalization experiments are insufficient.** The paper claims strong generalization but does not evaluate cross-scenario tasks (e.g., domain generalization) or use a wider variety of backbones (e.g., DINOv2, EVA02, smaller models) and segmentation heads (e.g., Mask2Former, SemFPN). Comparisons to recent related work such as Rein [CVPR 2024] are missing. (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 2 questions about smaller models)
- **Unclear descriptions of the proposed approach** in places, including overall training/inference process and the intuition/theory behind GPA’s architecture. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential one-to-one correspondence between \(\alpha\) (supervision diversity) and pooling window (perception granularity) is not explained.** (Reviewer 4)
- **Fail examples, limitations, and future work are not discussed.** (Reviewer 4)
- **Figure 1 (a) and (b)** have cramped x-axis labels and could be more elegant. (Reviewer 4)
- **Code is not released,** which hampers reproducibility. (Reviewer 4)
