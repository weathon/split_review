## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MMTEB, a massive multilingual extension of MTEB with over 500 quality-controlled tasks across 1,000+ languages. It includes novel task categories (instruction following, long-document retrieval, code retrieval) and computational optimizations (downsampling, hard negative sampling) to reduce evaluation cost. Two reviewers gave score 8 (positive, confident), two gave score 6 (less positive, one with high confidence). All agree the community effort is valuable, but there is disagreement on presentation and the depth of analysis.

### Strengths
- Community-driven effort with diverse contributors, fostering inclusivity (Reviewer 4).
- Coverage: over 500 tasks across 10 categories in 1,000+ languages, with strong focus on low-resource languages and domains (Reviewers 1,4).
- Computational optimizations (downsampling, hard negative sampling, caching embeddings) reduce evaluation cost to 3.11 hours for a 7B model on H100, making it accessible to low-resource settings (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- Optimization strategies are described in detail and easy to implement, facilitating adaptation of custom datasets (Reviewer 3).
- Expands traditional benchmarks with novel task types: instruction following, long-document retrieval, code retrieval (Reviewers 1,4).
- Open-source public leaderboard encourages continuous contributions (Reviewer 4).
- Paper is well-written and clearly communicates main points (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Dataset quality and construction** – Most crowd submissions are based on existing public datasets, not original collection (Reviewer 1). Lack of clarity on dataset construction, annotation methods (human vs model-generated, e.g., FollowIR), and statistics (sample counts, query-document ratios, language imbalance) (Reviewers 2,4). No details on whether further data collection/annotation/synthetic creation was done beyond existing datasets (Reviewer 2). Need examples for the 1,000 languages and 500 tasks (Reviewer 2).

2. **Missing experimental details and comparisons** – Need evaluation of more models (SFR-Embeddings, NV-Embed, bge, Qwen; include English-based results in Table 15) (Reviewer 2). Need sample counts per task in Table 9 and breakdown of language representation (Reviewer 2). Need comparison with other benchmarks like AIR-Benchmark to check overlap (Reviewer 2). Code and long-document benchmarks are not properly explained; need description and performance numbers from literature (Reviewers 2,3). Evaluation metrics for novel tasks (instruction retrieval, reranking, multi-label classification) not described (Reviewer 4). Section 3 should be titled “Experimental Settings” instead of “Results” (Reviewer 4).

3. **Analysis depth and justification** – Inconsistent depth: clustering provides average Spearman correlation but retrieval and bitext mining sections lack similar metrics; retrieval strategy selection based only on NQ and TREC-COVID, which may bias hyperparameter choice (Reviewer 3). No clear articulation of the knowledge gap beyond MTEB; multilingual scores align closely with English, making unique value difficult to discern (Reviewer 4). Novel tasks lack explanation: why include instruction retrieval when it is currently only available in one language; how long-document retrieval is integrated under “retrieval” may require training on long-doc data, countering the low-resource goal; impact on model performance is not discussed (e.g., multilingual-e5-large-instruct context length limitation, Mistral-based models perform better on code, so claim of exceptional performance may mislead) (Reviewer 3).

4. **Benchmark design and interpretability** – Which model(s) were used to select the top-250 hard negatives per query? Not specified (Reviewer 4). Combining 132 tasks makes it hard to interpret performance on specific languages/language families; need finer-grained segmentation by language, domain, or capability beyond geopolitical categories (Reviewer 4). Justification required for including summarization (low correlation in MTEB) (Reviewer 4). Should programming language benchmarks like CoIR be included? What are the criteria for multilingualism inclusion? (Reviewer 4). Are bitext mining and STS considered closely-related categories? (Reviewer 4). Need contributor statistics (distribution across countries, native speakers, domains) (Reviewer 2). Need explanation for why smaller models perform better in multilingual contexts while larger models excel on English; is this pattern unique to e5 models? (Reviewer 2). Provide a more intuitive compute resource metric, e.g., time on a single A100 GPU (Reviewer 3).
