## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the nested matrix-tensor model, an extension of the spiked rank-one tensor model motivated by multi-view clustering. It theoretically quantifies the performance gap between a tensor-based approach (best rank-one approximation) and a matrix-based unfolding approach. The main result is the precise algorithmic threshold of the unfolding approach, exhibiting a BBP-type transition. The analysis is rigorous and provides insights into why tensor methods can surpass matrix methods in handling structured tensor data.

### Strengths
1. Clear and well-motivated problem: providing theoretical analysis for tensor-based multi-view clustering is important. The paper does a good job connecting to both theoretical and applied related work. (R1, R2, R4)
2. Rigorous theoretical analysis with solid proofs. Derives the precise algorithmic threshold for the unfolding approach and quantifies the performance gap relative to the tensor method. This is a novel and non-trivial contribution. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
3. Vital result: under the nested matrix-tensor model, analysis of the unfolding method demonstrates the theoretical superiority of the tensor-based method in accuracy. (R1)
4. Good presentation of the main results, with numerical simulations and graphics that make the findings accessible. (R2)
5. Provides insights into the advantages of tensor-based methods over matrix unfolding for structured tensor data. (R3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Model assumptions and generality**  
   - The nested matrix-tensor model requires equal dimensionality across views; real-world multi-view data often have different dimensions. How can this be addressed? (R1)  
   - The assumption that both noise terms Z and W are Gaussian with zero mean and unit variance is too strong. Their variances affect performance differently, but this is not discussed. (R5)  
   - The analysis is limited to third-order tensors; it is unclear if the results extend to tensors of order greater than 3. (R3)  
   - The paper does not clarify whether “possible”/“impossible” recovery refers to information-theoretic or computational limits. In tensor problems these differ, and here it seems the focus is on a specific algorithmic scheme (unfolding), not an information-theoretic phase transition. (R2)  

2. **Lack of comparison with other multi-view clustering methods**  
   - No detailed comparison with state-of-the-art multi-view clustering methods in terms of computational complexity, convergence rate, and robustness to noise. (R3)  
   - It is unclear which specific tensor decomposition methods (e.g., CP, Tucker, other) the theoretical analysis applies to. This lack of specificity reduces practical relevance. (R4)  
   - Experiments compare only tensor vs. its unfolding; broader comparison with matrix-based methods (e.g., spectral clustering on concatenated or averaged views) and various tensor-based methods is missing. (R4)  

3. **Insufficient experimental validation**  
   - No experiments on real-world multi-view datasets are provided; validation of theoretical findings on real data with appropriate evaluation metrics is needed. (R3)  
   - Experiments are limited in scope—varying SNR, number of views, and dimensionality would give a more comprehensive understanding of the performance gap. (R3)  
   - It is unclear how the parameters (p, n, m) were chosen for Figure 3. (R3)  
   - The plots suggest the main bottleneck is the matrix SNR ρ_M; the practical relevance of the tensor formalism over unfolding is questioned. (R2)  

4. **Presentation and clarity issues**  
   - Error in the definition of T^(1): the element should be T^(1)_{i, n_3 (j-1) + k}. (R1)  
   - The acronym “SNR” is not defined. (R1)  
   - The significance of all variables in Eq. (2) should be specified. (R1)  
   - Definition of the inner product of two tensors is missing. (R1)  
   - Theorems lack necessary remarks; e.g., in Theorem 2 the formula for ζ is complex and its closeness to 1 is not interpretable. (R1)  
   - The detailed deduction of the last equation on page 17 is not apparent and should be shown. (R1)  
   - Cauchy’s integral formula on page 20 is not in standard form; give a reference. (R1)  
   - On page 2, the list of contributions should point to the corresponding theorems. (R1)  
   - Provide a citation for “BBP-type” transition. (R3)  
   - Define the symbol ⊗ in the Nested Matrix-Tensor Model. (R5)  
   - The discussion of the high-SNR regime mentions that tensor power iteration works without unfolding—are there cases where a good initialization for tensor power iteration is available, and are those of practical interest? (R2)  
   - Highlighting easier limiting cases (e.g., n_3 → ∞ while others fixed) could provide useful intuition but is missing. (R2)  
   - The paper does not clearly articulate how the theoretical results can guide the development of new tensor-based multi-view clustering methods; implications for future research are vague. (R4)
