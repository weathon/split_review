## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a realistic noise scenario called feature-dependent graph-noise (FDGN), where noisy node features may entail both structure and label noise, breaking the common assumption that feature noise is independent of graph structure and labels. The authors propose PRINGLE, a deep generative model based on variational inference that captures the causal relationships among variables (X, A, Y) in the data-generating process of FDGN. Experiments on six benchmark and newly collected e-commerce datasets show strong performance on node classification and link prediction tasks.

### Strengths
- The problem of feature-dependent graph-noise is essential and novel. The paper breaks the existing assumption that noise in node features is independent of graph structure or node labels, providing examples and empirical evidence that this new assumption is realistic.
- The causal perspective to justify FDGN is intuitive and sensible under mild assumptions. The proposed PRINGLE method is theoretically grounded with a deep generative model that directly models the data-generating process, and the paper provides detailed justification, case studies, and evaluation results in the appendix.
- Strong empirical performance across multiple noise settings (graph noise, edge noise, label noise, feature noise) on both existing benchmark datasets and newly collected e-commerce datasets.
- The paper is well-presented with clear figures and is generally easy to follow. The presentation and drawn figures are clear and understandable. (One reviewer noted high presentation quality, while another found it slightly less so; see weaknesses.)
- Extensive experiments and the introduction of a new dataset simulating FDGN in e-commerce.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: Using VAE to model causal relationships for handling noise has already been proposed by [1]; this paper only incrementally adapts that solution to graphs. The core idea of variational inference for causal modeling is not entirely new, and the specific implementation, while sound, does not introduce a groundbreaking methodological advancement. The novelty is primarily in the application to the specific problem of feature-dependent graph noise. (Note: one reviewer rated contribution as excellent, while others found it only fair or neutral.)
- **Insufficient justification of causal structure**: The practical existence of the assumed causal relationships (A←X, Y←X, Y←A) is not convincingly justified with real-world evidence, especially in e-commerce. The examples provided are not detailed enough to establish the prevalence of this specific causal structure; more mechanisms and evidence (e.g., how a user’s noisy profile directly causes both noisy interactions and miscategorization) are needed.
- **Unclear rationale for two latent variables**: The paper learns two separate latent variables Z_A and Z_Y but does not sufficiently justify why it does not instead directly infer a clean latent Z_X, which seems more natural given the causal structure (X causes A and Y). A more compelling argument for this indirect approach is required.
- **Missing complexity analysis and efficiency evaluation**: The method has high complexity with three encoder-decoder pairs and three objectives, yet the paper lacks a detailed analysis of computational complexity (scaling with graph size and feature dimensions), running time, or training curves. This is needed to assess practicality for large-scale graphs. Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (e.g., impact of varying model parameters and recommended selection strategies) is also missing.
- **Mixed theoretical grounding**: While one reviewer found the paper theoretically grounded with detailed justification, another argued that the theoretical support is limited because the derivation of the ELBO is well-known and the authors only restate standard results.
- **Missing related work and outdated references**: The paper misses important related works on GNN with label noise (e.g., [2]) and does not discuss technically relevant methods [1] that learn latent variables Z_A, Z_Y for structural denoising. Most references are before 2023.
- **Presentation and writing issues** (despite some positive comments): The abstract does not summarize the methodology, making the paper hard to read. There are too many uninformative “i.e., A/X/Y” notations in Section 3.1. Minor typos: “graph structure OF node labels” should be “OR” (abstract line 4); “introduces” should be “introduce” (page 5 line 5).
- **Unaddressed design choices and questions**: Why do the last three losses share the same weights in Eq. 4? The paper does not explain why generative methods are effective at handling label noise. (These questions overlap with missing justification/ablation.)
