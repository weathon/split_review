## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HC-MPNNs, a framework for link prediction on relational hypergraphs that extends C-MPNNs and HR-MPNNs. It provides theoretical analysis via a relational Weisfeiler–Leman test and graded modal logic, and achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets in both inductive and transductive settings.

### Strengths
- **Clear, well-organized presentation** making complex definitions accessible.  
- **Solid theoretical contributions**: analysis of expressive power using the relational WL hierarchy and logical expressiveness via graded modal logic.  
- **Strong empirical performance**: HC-MPNNs substantially outperform existing baselines on multiple relational hypergraph benchmarks for both inductive and transductive link prediction; results include Table 2 (transductive) and several datasets.  
- **Interesting extension to inductive link prediction**, which is less studied in this area.  
- **Comprehensive experiments** covering transductive and inductive settings, plus ablation studies on initialization methods and positional encodings (sinusoidal PE found best).  
- **Code is available**.  
- **Minority reviewer (rating 8) is notably more positive**, emphasizing the innovative nature of generalizing GNNs to k-ary link prediction and the theoretical rigor, while the other reviewers (rating 6) see the contributions as useful but more incremental.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The adaptation of C-MPNN to hypergraphs is seen as a straightforward migration of existing concepts; the paper does not clearly identify specific challenges unique to hypergraphs or describe how they are overcome in a non-trivial way.  
- **Insufficient justification for hypergraph modeling over sequence models**: Relational hyperedges have fixed arity and positional structure, so the paper should explain why a hypergraph approach is superior to sequence-based models (e.g., Transformers/BERT) and what limitations such models would face on these tasks.  
- **Inadequate analysis of computational and training complexity**:  
  - No detailed breakdown of time/space complexity per step (message passing, aggregation, update).  
  - No comparison with baseline models on execution time, memory usage, or scaling behavior with hypergraph size/arity.  
  - Training complexity: query‑dependent message passing requires per‑query representation recomputation; unclear how queries are selected and how computational cost scales (exponential neighbor explosion possible, similar to NBFNet).  
  - Scalability to very large hypergraphs (millions of nodes) is not demonstrated.  
- **Limited scope of evaluation**:  
  - Model is evaluated only on link prediction; applicability to other tasks (node classification, hypergraph‑based query answering, clustering) is not explored.  
  - Necessity of the problem setting is questioned: hyper‑relation prediction may be decomposable into relational paths, and no real‑world dataset is shown to require a pure hypergraph method.  
- **Assumptions on data quality**: The method assumes complete and accurate hypergraph structures; robustness to noisy, missing, or partially observed hyperedges is not discussed. The impact of hypergraph structure/density (sparse vs. highly connected) on performance is also not investigated.  
- **Theoretical analysis limitations**:  
  - The graded modal logic analysis is too abstract; more evidence (e.g., appendix) is needed on whether it can represent all logical queries on relational hypergraphs and what its limits are.  
  - The expressive power of HC-MPNNs may still fall short in certain graph structures, which is not discussed.  
- **Lack of confidence intervals in empirical results**: Randomness from negative sampling requires error bars; the paper does not provide them.  
- **Insufficient handling of relational hypergraph vs. hyper‑relational graph distinction**: The paper’s justification (lines 145‑153) that these are irreconcilably different is not convincing; reviewers suggest experimenting on hyper‑relational graph datasets (e.g., WD50K, WIKIPEOPLE, JF17K, FBAuto) to demonstrate generalizability.  
- **Significance of inductive setting**: Many baselines in Table 1 are from 2020 or earlier, suggesting the inductive setting is not a current major focus; the paper should better emphasize why this setting is important.
