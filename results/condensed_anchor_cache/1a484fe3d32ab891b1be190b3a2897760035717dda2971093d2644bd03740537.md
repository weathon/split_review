## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses text-conditioned object addition to images without user-provided masks. It proposes a “Paint by Inpaint” framework: because object removal (inpainting) is easier, the authors construct a large-scale dataset (PIPE) of original images paired with object-removed versions (automatically generated by Stable Diffusion inpainting, then filtered). A VLM+LLM pipeline produces diverse natural-language instructions. A diffusion model (based on InstructPix2Pix) is trained to reverse the inpainting process, i.e., add objects. The model outperforms baselines on object-addition metrics, and a user study confirms better instruction faithfulness and visual quality. The dataset and models will be released. Reviewer scores are split: two “3” (reject) and two “5” (accept), indicating disagreement on overall merit.

### Strengths
- The Paint by Inpaint framework provides a novel perspective for object addition.
- PIPE is a large-scale, automatically constructed dataset of image pairs (original vs. object-removed) with natural-language instructions; its filtering pipeline may be useful for the community.
- The combination of this framework and dataset enhances performance on object addition, as shown via comprehensive automatic metrics and a user study against diverse baselines (SDEdit, Null-text inversion, InstructPix2Pix, etc.).
- The paper is well-written, clear, and provides significant detail for reproducibility.
- The authors promise to release the dataset and models, which could advance future research.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope (multiple reviewers):** The method only supports object addition, whereas baselines like MagicBrush handle multiple editing operations (addition, replacement, removal, action changes). Some reviewers find this too limited; they suggest extending to at least 3–4 operations.
- **Practicality and controllability (minority concerns heightened):** The model randomly places new objects without specifying size or position. Unlike bounding-box-guided methods (e.g., Grounding-DINO), it offers no control over these attributes. One reviewer notes that for specific placement (e.g., a cup on one of three tables), the method may fail. Another notes that user-provided masks add little overhead and provide precise control.
- **Technical novelty questioned (repeated):**  
  * The core idea – learning to add objects by reversing inpainting – already appears in prior published work (e.g., ObjectDrop, Winter et al., ECCV 2024). The paper should tone down this claimed contribution.  
  * The “paint by inpaint” mechanism is similar to GAN-based approaches (ManiGAN, SIMSG) that use image reconstruction for editing.
- **Dataset construction and generalization concerns:**  
  * The dataset is generated by Stable Diffusion inpainting; the model may only learn to reverse *that* model’s process, not general editing. Domain generalization experiments are needed (e.g., train on Stable Diffusion data, evaluate on images inpainted by GLIDE, Imagen, or real-world images).  
  * The authors repeatedly claim advantages of using real target images (vs. synthetic), yet provide **no ablation** to support this. More ablations are needed on: (i) filtered vs. unfiltered data (and ideally per-filter tests); (ii) real vs. a larger set of synthetic examples. The fact that InstructPix2Pix fine-tuned on MagicBrush (real data) often outperforms the proposed method on metrics suggests these claimed advantages may not be critical.
- **Missing comparisons and evaluations:**  
  * Comparisons should include mask-based editing methods (e.g., BrushNet) that are more appropriate for object addition than SDEdit/Null-text inversion.  
  * The original IP2P is presented as SOTA, but MagicBrush (NeurIPS 2023) outperforms it. A direct comparison with MagicBrush’s model should appear in qualitative figures and in the user study (e.g., against MagicBrush, not just IP2P).  
  * Image quality degrades noticeably (loss of high-frequency details, facial distortions). The authors should report fidelity metrics (e.g., L2 on background for VAE-only reconstruction) to isolate the cause.
- **Minor issues (not affecting score, but noted):**  
  * CLIP citation in line 400 incorrectly attributes to Ruiz et al.; should cite Radford et al.  
  * Use \citet for author-year citations (e.g., “Name et al. (2020)”).

**Reviewer disagreement:** Two reviewers (scores 3) emphasize weak technical novelty, limited applicability, and missing domain generalization; they view the dataset as the main contribution. The other two (scores 5) find the approach reasonable and the evaluations comprehensive, but still call for ablations and improved comparisons.
