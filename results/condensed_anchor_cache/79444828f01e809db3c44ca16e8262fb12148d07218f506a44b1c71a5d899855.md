## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Decentralized Partially Observable Mean Field Control (Dec‑POMFC), a model that extends mean field control to decentralized and partially observable multi‑agent systems and shows it can be reduced to a tractable single‑agent MDP. Theoretical results connect the mean field problem to the finite‑agent swarm and provide optimality guarantees. Algorithmically, a PPO‑based method is applied within the centralized‑training‑decentralized‑execution (CTDE) paradigm, with policy‑gradient approximation guarantees. Experiments are performed on adapted Kuramoto and Vicsek swarming models and compared against IPPO as baseline.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a challenging combination of partial observability, decentralization, and large‑scale multi‑agent systems, an area worth exploring.
- The theoretical contributions are rigorous, including a dynamic programming principle and optimality guarantees (reviewer 1, 3).
- The paper is well‑written and provides a detailed algorithm description and complete theoretical proofs (reviewer 2, 3).
- Numerical experiments are carefully carried out, reporting training curves and returns as the number of agents increases (reviewer 1, 3).

### Weaknesses
- Assumption 1b (uniformly Lipschitz policies) is restrictive; a question is whether it could be replaced by an assumption on the model that would ensure the optimal policy satisfies the Lipschitz property (reviewer 1).
- The particular difficulty arising from partial observation is unclear. Many Dec‑POMDP problems can be grouped into weakly‑coupled POMDPs where partial observation is already sufficient. The paper does not explicitly explain what the difficulty is and how the proposed method resolves it, nor does it specify how much information each agent can observe (reviewer 2).
- The CTDE framework is common. More importantly, the baseline IPPO is not a fair comparison because IPPO cannot use global information during training while the proposed method uses more information (reviewer 2; note that this reviewer had lower confidence (2) and rated the paper lower (5) than the other reviewers).
- The benchmarks (adapted Kuramoto and Vicsek) are simple; more realistic and complex benchmarks are needed to demonstrate the importance of the studied question and the proposed method (reviewer 2).
- The experimental evaluation lacks an explicit summary or discussion of significant improvement over the baseline, making it hard to judge the contribution from the experimental results. Moreover, only one baseline (IPPO) is compared; direct comparison with additional state‑of‑the‑art methods is missing (reviewer 3).
- The feasibility of extending the method to handle additional practical constraints and sparser interaction is unclear given the adopted assumptions; the paper does not discuss this limitation (reviewer 3, from question).
- The claim about generality of the proposed method is not sufficiently justified: it is not explained which design elements (besides using RL itself) contribute to this generality (reviewer 3, from question).
