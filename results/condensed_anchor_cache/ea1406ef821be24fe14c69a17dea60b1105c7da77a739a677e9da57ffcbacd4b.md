## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FlashMask, an extension of FlashAttention that uses a column-wise sparse representation of attention masks, storing only boundary indices per column. This reduces mask memory from quadratic to linear in sequence length and enables skipping of fully masked blocks. The approach is implemented in Paddle and evaluated against FlashAttention-2 with dense masks and FlexAttention, showing end-to-end speedups of 1.65–3.22× over the dense method and 12.1–60.7% higher kernel TFLOPs/s than FlexAttention. Both forward and backward passes are modified to exploit the representation. The paper is well-written, with comprehensive benchmarks, insightful synthetic-data analysis, and clear algorithmic exposition.

### Strengths
- Novel column-wise sparse representation for attention masks (first application of such techniques to attention), achieving linear memory complexity O(N) and enabling efficient block-skipping.
- Practical open-sourced implementation that yields wall-clock speed-ups over FlashAttention-2 dense masking and outperforms FlexAttention (12.1–60.7% kernel TFLOPs/s, reaching 37.8–62.3% of theoretical peak on A100).
- Extensive evaluation across fine‑tuning and alignment tasks (SFT, LoRA, DPO, RM) and thorough kernel-level analysis.
- Clear presentation of the forward/backward algorithms and the memory/compute advantages.
- Insightful appendix and synthetic-data analysis that corroborate the claims.
- Well‑structured paper with broad benchmarking.

### Weaknesses
- The method is limited to **structured attention masks** that can be represented as compositions of left‑triangular and right‑triangular ranges per column; irregular or arbitrary masking patterns cannot be handled even naively, restricting real-world applicability.
- It is **unclear** whether the computational advantage over Block‑Sparse FlashAttention and FlexAttention stems from the column‑wise representation itself or from the block‑skipping optimization that those baselines also employ. A detailed breakdown of the performance gains is missing.
- The implementation is **tied to Paddle**; generalizing to PyTorch/Megatron would broaden impact.
- **Inference support is absent**; the paper does not discuss how the sparse mask can be integrated into actual inference/serving pipelines.
- The paper **omits citations** to [1], an earlier published general sparse self‑attention training & serving framework.
- **Missing baselines**: FlashInfer with dense masks and FlashInfer sparse masks are not compared, even though FlashInfer is a state‑of‑the‑art attention implementation (FlashInfer does not support backward but is still an important baseline for kernel performance).
- The **overhead of maintaining boundary indices** for very long sequences or very sparse masks is not discussed.
- No experiments on **hardware other than A100** (should include P100, V100, A10G, H100).
- The effect of **block/chunk size** on speed‑up across different mask types is not analyzed.
- When tiling, **workload imbalance among thread blocks** can arise for certain masks; mitigation strategies are not mentioned.
- **No comparison between theoretical FLOPs reduction and wall‑clock speed‑up** for different mask types.
- The impact of **tensor parallelism and pipeline parallelism** on the achieved speed‑up is not examined.
- Figure 4b shows that **FlexAttention has lower memory utilization than FlashMask for sequence lengths below 16K** – this is not explained.
- The paper does not clarify whether **Block‑Sparse FlashAttention can handle the mask types shown in Fig 1(a)**; if it can, the DenseMask variant used for baseline comparisons may be an unfair choice.
- Integration with **PageAttention** and potential benefits for **tree‑based speculative decoding** are left unaddressed.
