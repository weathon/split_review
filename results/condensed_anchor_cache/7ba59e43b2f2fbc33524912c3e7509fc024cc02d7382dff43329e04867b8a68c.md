## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data-free post-training quantization (PTQ) method that generates synthetic calibration data via backpropagation, agnostic to batch normalization (BN) statistics. It claims that including samples from every target category is unnecessary. Reviewers note the method is simple and applicable to various DNNs, but raise concerns about marginal gains at higher bit-widths, missing low-bit (2‑3 bit) experiments, insufficient validation on large-class datasets, lack of comparisons with recent works (e.g., GENIE, Unified data-free compression, non‑data‑free PTQ), and the need for ablation on hyperparameters and computational cost. One reviewer (score 5) is positive about innovation and extensive experiments, while others (scores 1 and 3) find the contributions limited and the writing dense.

### Strengths
1. The synthetic image generation process is agnostic to BN statistics, making it helpful for networks without BN layers (e.g., AlexNet) and simplifying optimization. (Reviewer 1, also implied by Reviewer 2, 3)
2. The method uses very few synthetic samples for calibration (no more than 35), reducing computational overhead. (Reviewer 1, 3)
3. The paper is easy to follow and the methodology is simple, natural, and effective. (Reviewer 2, 4)
4. The proposed method is easily applicable to various DNNs. (Reviewer 2)
5. Extensive experiments are provided across multiple network architectures (e.g., ResNet18, InceptionV3) and datasets, with consistent accuracy improvements over SOTA, especially at 4-bit quantization (e.g., 3.42% top-1 gain for ResNet18, 3.14% for InceptionV3 compared to DSG). (Reviewer 3; confirmed by Reviewer 4: “nice boost at low bit quantization (W4A4)”)
6. Tables and figures are well-detailed, offering clear comparisons with other PTQ methods. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing clarity and methodological detail**: The paper’s writing is dense, especially around Algorithm 1 and synthetic data generation. The backpropagation process and loss function are not explained with sufficient step-by-step clarity, leaving ambiguity about gradient usage and loss formulation. (Reviewers 1, 3) Terminology is inconsistent: “sample” is used ambiguously to mean either a category (class) or the number of synthetic images per category. (Reviewer 2)
2. **Marginal performance gain at higher bit-widths and need for lower-bit experiments**: Data-free quantization already performs well at 4‑8 bits; the claimed improvement is marginal. The method should be evaluated at 2‑bit or 3‑bit quantization, where prior work like GENIE (Jeon et al., CVPR 2023) shows strong performance. (Reviewer 2)
3. **Insufficient validation of the “few classes” claim**: CIFAR-10 has too few classes to robustly demonstrate that selecting only a few target classes is sufficient. Validation on datasets with more classes (e.g., CIFAR-100) is needed. (Reviewer 2) Additionally, the authors should test whether categories are selected randomly or by a design, and whether randomness significantly affects results. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Missing comparisons with recent methods**: The paper lacks a comparison with more recent data-free PTQ works, e.g., Unified data-free compression (Bai et al., ICCV 2023). (Reviewer 4) It also does not compare against non-data-free PTQ that uses real data, which would help contextualize trade-offs. (Reviewers 1, 3)
5. **Limited model scope – outdated and missing architectures**: Testing on AlexNet in 2024 is outdated. The method should be evaluated on modern networks, such as ConvNeXt, ViT, DeiT, and Swin Transformer (which use LayerNorm rather than BN), to support the claim of BN-agnostic performance. (Reviewers 2, 4)
6. **Lack of ablation on key hyperparameters**: The impact of key hyperparameters (e.g., number of iterations T, learning rate α) on synthetic data quality and final quantization accuracy is not ablated, making it difficult to tune the method in practice. (Reviewers 1, 3)
7. **Inadequate discussion of limitations and failure modes**: The paper briefly mentions BN-based dataset homogeneity but does not discuss scenarios where the synthetic generation might fail (e.g., highly non-linear activations, very deep networks). (Reviewers 1, 3)
8. **Computational cost and practical deployment not addressed**: The time complexity and memory footprint of generating synthetic data via backpropagation are not discussed or compared with traditional calibration. There is no analysis of resource requirements for deployment on constrained devices (e.g., edge, mobile, IoT). (Reviewers 1, 3) (Reviewer 3 additionally suggests adding use-case scenarios.)
9. **Missing comparisons with other zero-shot quantization approaches**: The method should be compared with approaches that use latent embeddings (e.g., Qimera, Choi et al., NeurIPS 2021) or text feature distribution (e.g., TexQ, Chen et al., NeurIPS 2023) to show broader applicability. (Reviewer 2)
10. **Specific technical questions about the method**: 
    - What is the difference (in final synthetic images and in BN distribution, even for networks with BN like ResNet) between the proposed BN-agnostic method and DSQ? (Reviewer 4)
    - Why is additional noise introduced to x during each training step (line 228)? More explanation is needed. (Reviewer 4)

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 5) is notably more positive than the others, emphasizing the innovativeness and experimental thoroughness, while the minority low-score reviewer (score 1) was highly critical of writing, missing comparisons, and lack of practical analysis. The above weaknesses represent points raised by at least one reviewer, with duplicate concerns across reviewers noted only once.*
