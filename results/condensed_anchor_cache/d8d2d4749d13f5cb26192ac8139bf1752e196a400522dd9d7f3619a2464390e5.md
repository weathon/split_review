## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses the problem of distractor tasks in LMs, where models pick up secondary tasks from the prompt and deviate from the intended instruction. The authors propose a theoretical framework interpreting LMs as a product of experts, and introduce Prior-Aware Decoding (PAD), a contrastive inference method that combines logits from the original prompt and a “weakened” prompt (without the task description). They test PAD across 11 models (including Llama 2, GPT-2, GPT-3.5) and 4 datasets, reporting improvements in 41 out of 44 task-model combinations. Reviewers find the problem important, the approach interesting, and the experiments broad, but raise concerns about clarity, baselines, practical applicability, and analysis of limitations.

### Strengths

- The problem of LM distraction due to prompt misinterpretation is important and clearly described; it affects models of various sizes and has wide-ranging applications (R1, R2, R4).
- The theoretical framework characterizing LMs as a product of experts provides a novel and compelling perspective on “inverse scaling” and distractor tasks (R1, R4).
- PAD is a simple and effective contrastive decoding method that is well-integrated with the framework; it can be interpreted as scaling the model prediction by the ratio of the model with and without the task description (R1, R3, R4).
- Extensive experiments across 11 different models (open-source like Llama 2, GPT-2 and proprietary like GPT-3.5) and 4 datasets (44 LLM-task combinations) demonstrate robustness and gains over standard inference (R1, R2, R3).
- The method is described with adequate detail for reproducibility; authors plan to release code and datasets (R2).
- The related work section is well-structured and highlights unique contributions (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity of PAD methodology**: The process of generating the “weakened” prompt and the rationale for the specific linear combination of logits need further elaboration. The contrastive mechanism remains abstract and the mathematical formulation could be expanded (R1). The motivation for introducing the product of experts to represent the model distribution is not clearly justified (R3). Figure 1 is confusing as it shows three experts while the framework only uses two; it should be redrawn to match the proposed method (R4).
- **Practical applicability and real-world scope**: Evaluation focuses heavily on synthetic inverse scaling tasks where strong local prior assumptions hold. More complex, noisy, or user-generated data (e.g., real-world prompt injection attacks, multi-step reasoning, tasks with less clear task/data distinction) are not tested (R1, R4). An alternative implementation using system prompts (Appendix C) shows limited performance gains (R4). PAD may fail in settings where primary and distractor tasks are closely interwoven or with certain prompt structures; this is not discussed (R1).
- **Ambiguity in prompt splitting**: The authors assume that splitting prompts into task and data components is unambiguous for their selected tasks (line 354-355), but this is not generally true. No guidelines or automated methods (beyond regex) are provided for less clear cases, limiting the method’s practical applicability (R1, R4). The ε-δ definition in Section 3, relying on strict separation between T and D, also restricts applicability (R4).
- **Lack of strong baselines and novelty discussion**: The paper does not clearly compare with prior methods for mitigating LM distraction, and no simple prompt-based baseline is provided (e.g., a baseline using a known distractor and adding an instruction to avoid it). This makes it difficult to assess the relative contribution of PAD (R2). The novelty of the work relative to existing contrastive or distraction-mitigation approaches is not clarified (R2, R3).
- **Missing analysis of failure cases and parameter sensitivity**: Three cases where α=2 performs worse are mentioned but not analyzed. Detailed case studies of failure modes are needed to understand when PAD is inappropriate (R1). The sensitivity of the contrastive scaling factor α (and its optimization across datasets/models) is not discussed (R1). The relationship between total variation distance (TVD, δ), α selection, and empirical performance is not deeply explored (R4).
- **Computational and diversity costs**: PAD requires two forward passes per generation; the computational impact is not assessed (R1). It is unclear whether the method reduces output diversity or fluency, and no cost-benefit analysis is provided (R2).
- **Proprietary model decoding**: It is not explained how PAD is implemented for proprietary models like GPT-3.5, since the method requires taking the product of two model outputs during inference (R3).
- **Minor issues**: A missing period at line 129; table numbers and titles should appear before the table (R4).
