## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LeanFlex‑GKP, a fine‑grained structured pruning method based on grouped kernel pruning (GKP). The key idea is to make the number of groups (`Conv2d(groups)`) flexible across layers under an integral optimization, avoiding the dynamic operations (e.g., expensive clustering schemes or dynamic pruning rates) that complicate prior GKP methods. The method is one‑shot, post‑train, and data‑agnostic, with minimal hyperparameter tuning. Experimental comparisons with five baseline approaches on ResNet and VGG (and a few other architectures) show promising accuracy and efficiency.

### Strengths
1. The paper clearly identifies limitations of existing GKP methods (complexity from dynamic operations) and proposes a sensible alternative via flexible group counts that aligns well with standard grouped convolution support.
2. The proposed fine‑grained pruning method achieves higher accuracy performance compared to coarse‑grained alternatives.
3. The introduction and background are detailed, clearly explaining different structured pruning granularities, Grouped Kernel Pruning, and Dynamic Structure Pruning.
4. As a one‑shot, post‑train, data‑agnostic technique with little‑to‑no hyperparameter tuning, LeanFlex‑GKP is far easier to use out‑of‑the‑box than many existing methods.
5. Empirical results are shown across a range of model architectures and datasets.

### Weaknesses
1. **Paper structure**: The Introduction is overly long (first 3.5 pages), while Section 3 (the main contribution) is too small to adequately explain the method. The overall structure needs improvement.
2. **Insufficient baselines and evaluation scope**:
   - Only five baselines are compared. Many related works are missing: iterative structured pruning methods [1‒10], one‑shot pruning [11‒15], and lossless pruning methods.
   - Most evaluations are on ResNet and VGG; small models (MobileNet, EfficientNet, ShuffleNet) should also be tested.
   - A direct performance comparison with the closely related method TMI‑GKP is missing, making it hard to assess the incremental contribution. It is unclear whether LeanFlex‑GKP also includes dynamic choices of clustering schemes like TMI‑GKP.
   - Section 4 mostly lists numbers; providing more insights would improve the evaluation.
3. **Missing ablation study**: An ablation study isolating the effect of key design choices (e.g., flexible group counts) is not provided. Additionally, experimental settings (hyperparameters used for different models) should be reported.
4. **Limited contribution**: Two reviewers considered the contribution limited and incremental over TMI‑GKP (the main difference being the group count evaluation). One reviewer found the contribution higher (score 3 vs. 2), noting the interesting idea of flexible `Conv2d(groups)`. This disagreement reflects the need for clearer differentiation and a stronger novelty argument.
5. **Theoretical depth**: It is not clear whether the proposed method can achieve lossless pruning. Theoretical analysis would strengthen the paper.
6. **Related work discussion**: The discussion of related works (iterative/one‑shot pruning, automatic pruning with little tuning, etc.) is insufficient.
7. **Writing issues**: Several grammar errors are present (e.g., “an higher”, “they can be run”, “with in”, “is determine by”, “with lowest”). The phrase “dependable experience” may be “independable experience”.
8. **Open questions from reviewers** (which overlap with criticisms or missing experiments):
   - Can the benefits of dynamic group counts extend to other structured pruning granularities (e.g., filter or channel pruning)?
   - Is there an optimal strategy for setting group counts, or do they need to be exhaustively evaluated?
   - Can the method be applied to other architectures beyond those tested?
