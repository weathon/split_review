## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Enhanced Graph-Less Neural Network (EGLNN), a knowledge-distillation framework for anomaly detection in Ethereum-based IoT data storage and transactions. EGLNN uses a Bayesian graph structure estimator to improve graph structure learning and distills knowledge from a teacher GNN into a student MLP to achieve faster inference and reduced model size. The four reviewers are generally critical: three gave score 3, one gave score 5 (more positive overall). Reviewers acknowledge the practical motivation and some experimental results, but raise significant concerns about dataset and task framing, missing comparisons, unclear methodology, insufficient justification of the student model’s advantages, and problems with reproducibility and rigor.

### Strengths
- **Knowledge distillation for faster inference and reduced model size**: The paper addresses IoT network anomaly detection using a knowledge distillation framework that significantly reduces model size and inference time, making it suitable for memory-limited industrial environments (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Bayesian reasoning to optimize graph structure**: The use of a Bayesian graph structure estimator enhances the model’s ability to learn realistic topological information (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental results suggest strong performance in data-scarce scenarios**: Experiments indicate EGLNN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in anomaly detection, especially when training data is limited (Reviewer 3; note: Reviewer 3 is notably more positive than the other three reviewers).
- **Interesting combination of knowledge distillation and MLPs**: The design offers an interesting solution for anomaly detection with potential industrial value (Reviewer 2).
- **Well-structured and easy to read**: The paper is well-structured, comprehensive, and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient dataset description and limited experimental scope**: The paper does not explain the dataset collection process (Section 3.2 lacks details), uses only very small training subsets (60/70/100 nodes) from a full graph of 376,759 nodes and 1,048,576 edges without clarifying how the full graph was utilized, and lacks experiments across other datasets or comparisons with existing Ethereum anomaly detection algorithms (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The significance of the Ethereum platform as a constraint and the added challenges of IoT are not clearly motivated (Reviewer 1). The claim of large-scale applicability is contradicted by the small experimental settings (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete related work and missing citations**: The related work does not cite or compare against relevant papers (e.g., AdaGMLP, Teach Harder, Extracting low/high-frequency knowledge) (Reviewer 1). Comparative experiments with the Ethereum anomaly detection algorithms mentioned in the paper are absent, reducing persuasiveness (Reviewer 4). The citation of AMBGAT is erroneous: it is attributed to paper [1] but actually appears in paper [2], raising concerns about research rigor (Reviewer 4).
- **Methodology insufficiently described and unclear**: Several symbols are undefined (e.g., Q in Eq2); the term "distillation loss" in Eq4 misleadingly combines distillation with local supervision loss; the training and joint optimization of MLP1 and MLP2 are not clearly explained; the semi-supervised learning approach is not supported by a clear explanation (Reviewer 2). The rationale for selecting training sizes (60/70/100) and testing size (1000) is unclear (Reviewer 2). The ablation study does not sufficiently explain why positional encoding (PE) is so helpful (Reviewer 3). No experiments target potential permutation invariance issues caused by PE (Reviewer 1). The paper does not address whether the Ethereum network is dynamic and if the method adapts accordingly (Reviewer 1). The advantage of using two MLPs (one on structural knowledge and one on node features) over a simpler single MLP distilling from a GNN trained on both PE and node features is not clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Main motivation (student model efficiency) not convincingly demonstrated**: The teacher model (EGLNN-T) outperforms the student (EGLNN) in the experiments, yet no direct resource comparison (inference time, model size) is provided to justify the distillation approach (Reviewer 3). The explanation that “MLP is simple and efficient” is not supported by experimental evidence showing the student’s advantage under resource constraints (Reviewer 3). The paper states the model is suitable for dynamic IoT networks but focuses only on static graphs and does not address time-series information (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of innovation and reproducibility**: The paper appears as a fusion of existing technologies rather than presenting original contributions (Reviewer 4). Source code is not provided, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- **Poor clarity and errors in figures and tables**: Framework and experimental diagrams/tables lack clarity and need better labeling (Reviewer 4). Figure 2 is incorrectly referenced as Figure 4, and Figure 7 is referenced but absent (Reviewers 2, 3).
