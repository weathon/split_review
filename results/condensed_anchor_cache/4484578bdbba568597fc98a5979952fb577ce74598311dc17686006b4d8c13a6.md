## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Ctrl123, a zero-shot novel view synthesis method built upon Zero-1-to-3. To enforce multiview and pose consistency, it adds a closed-loop transcription loss that compares the generated view with ground truth in the CLIP patch feature space after unrolling the denoising process (50 steps). The model is finetuned on Objaverse, alternating between closed-loop and standard diffusion training. Reviewers note the method improves consistency over Zero-1-to-3, achieves state-of-the-art quantitative results on GSO, RTMV, and OmniObjects3D, and introduces metrics like Angle Accuracy and Mask IoU.

### Strengths
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on three common benchmarks (GSO, RTMV, OmniObjects3D) over prior methods including Zero123 (reviewers 2, 5). Quantitative improvements are shown in PSNR, SSIM, rotation accuracy (AA) and IoU.
- Effectively improves multiview consistency while retaining the flexibility to generate arbitrary novel views (reviewers 1, 3). Overfitting experiments on 25 objects show significantly improved PSNR (reviewer 2).
- Provides extensive ablations, investigating the number of denoising steps, feature extractor choice (VAE vs. CLIP), and simultaneous vs. alternating training (reviewer 2).
- Offers useful insights: path features are more informative than class features for NVS; applying MSE loss in VAE latent space leads to vulnerability to training collapse; Sec 3.1 gives a good explanation of current NVS methods (reviewer 1).
- The use of MegaPose for assessing generated viewpoints is appreciated (reviewer 5).
- The proposed tweak is conceptually simple and yields significant practical improvement (reviewer 5; reviewer 2 notes it is simple conceptually).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and significance.** The method is essentially a finetuning of Zero-1-to-3 with an added CLIP feature loss; this is considered a cosmetic rather than fundamental change (reviewer 1). CLIP feature space alignment has already been used in prior work (e.g., Jain et al., Putting NeRF on a Diet) to supervise scene reconstruction (reviewer 4). The approach is not transferable to other NVS methods (reviewer 1).
- **Computational cost.** Backpropagation through 50 denoising steps multiplies GPU memory significantly, likely restricting training to very small batches (reviewer 5). The authors do not provide details on speed and memory cost (reviewer 2). Only ultra-tiny batches are presumed possible (reviewer 5).
- **Poor presentation and clarity.** English is difficult to read; the Introduction is hard to parse (reviewer 1). There are mathematical notation errors (e.g., mismatched parameters in Eq. 3, inconsistencies with decoder \(g()\) and its parameters, repeated formulas in Eqs. (5)–(9)) (reviewer 1, 5). Section 3.2 describing the method (especially closed-loop backpropagation) is unclear; a diagram showing unrolling through denoising steps is needed (reviewer 2). Figure 2 has formatting errors (reviewer 2). Reviewer 5 emphasizes that the crucial point of unrolling the denoiser is not made clear until line 523.
- **Missing comparisons.** No comparison with Zero123++ (which is indirectly referenced as “restricted fixed view generation”) is provided; it is unclear how Ctrl123 improves over Zero123++ (reviewer 3). LPIPS metric (used in Zero123++) is not reported (reviewer 3). CLIP score is not included despite the method using CLIP features for alignment (reviewer 3). No comparative experiments for 3D reconstruction from generated views (reviewer 4). Missing supplementary videos that would help assess qualitative multiview consistency (reviewer 5).
- **Limited evaluation scope.** The overfitting analysis uses only 25 objects (reviewer 4 questions representativeness). The SOTA comparison in Section 4.3 uses only 20 objects; using more objects would strengthen generalization claims (reviewer 4). The shown objects (toys, dolls, avatars) are relatively simple; it is unclear whether the method handles more complex objects, such as those tackled by Zero123++ (reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 notes that Zero123++ addresses more challenging problems.
- **Potential mode collapse.** By applying the regression loss after unrolling, the denoiser tends to regress to the mean, killing variance. The authors should explain how this connects to choosing CLIP space (rather than VAE or RGB) to “soften” variance killing (reviewer 5).
- **Lack of innovation beyond adding a loss.** Reviewers suggest that more thorough innovations (e.g., geometric constraints, regularization on ray casting, smooth camera trajectories, explicit 3D modeling) would strengthen the method (reviewer 4).
- **Minor issues.** Zero-1-to-3 is not styled correctly throughout the paper (reviewer 5). Citations lack spaces or punctuation (reviewer 5).
