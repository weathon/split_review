## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PrefixQuant, a static activation quantization method that isolates outlier tokens offline by prefixing high-frequency outlier tokens in the KV cache to prevent their generation during inference. The goal is to make per-tensor static quantization outperform per-token dynamic quantization. Experiments on Llama-3-8B (W4A4KV4) show improvements over QuaRot in perplexity and accuracy, with inference speedups. However, multiple reviewers raise concerns about novelty (prefix‑based outlier mitigation already explored by QFeP and CushionCache), unclear technical details (how prefixing truly removes outlier impact), incomplete experimental validation (limited to 2048 context length, missing long‑context and large‑scale models, lack of realistic tasks like MMLU/GSM‑8K), and cases where the method does not consistently beat dynamic quantization.

### Strengths
- Shows that per‑tensor static quantization can outperform per‑token dynamic quantization (e.g., 7.43 WikiText2 PPL and 71.08% avg. accuracy on Llama‑3‑8B vs. QuaRot).  
- Introduces a novel approach to address the token‑wise outlier problem for quantization; the method is simple and has low execution cost (noted by one reviewer).  
- Reports real wall‑clock inference speed on multiple devices (RTX 3090, A100) and time‑to‑first‑token speed‑ups, which is often missing in quantization papers.  
- Carefully measures generative quality with many ablation studies; presentation is clear and visualizations are well‑prepared.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** – The core idea of adding prefix tokens to mitigate outliers has been previously proposed by QFeP (Yang et al., May 2024) and CushionCache (Son et al., EMNLP 2024). CushionCache already argued that such prefixes make static quantization useful. The claimed advantages (lower optimization cost, performance improvements) stem from components that are not original contributions (Hadamard rotation, grid search, block‑wise fine‑tuning). One reviewer considers the method novel, while two others find the conceptual/technical contribution minimal.
- The paper’s claim that prefix tokens complement Hadamard rotation by addressing “token‑wise outliers” while rotation addresses “channel‑wise outliers” is unclear and potentially misleading. Prior work (Sun et al., COLM 2024) shows outliers are localized in both dimensions, so removing channel‑wise outliers should also resolve token‑wise outliers. A concrete justification is requested.  
- **Unclear mechanism** – How prefixing outlier tokens in the KV cache prevents the generation of new outliers is not explained. It is unclear whether prefixed tokens are treated differently during attention (e.g., with a separate scale factor in the 4‑bit KV cache or kept in higher precision). If they simply participate in attention like normal tokens, the outlier issue would persist. If they are stored in higher precision, additional overhead and hardware‑unfriendly aspects arise. Clarification is needed.  
- **Experimental scope too narrow** – Evaluations are only performed with a context length of 2048. On longer contexts (e.g., 8192), per‑tensor static quantization is expected to incur more quantization error (more numbers must be represented by a single integer). Comparison with dynamic quantization methods (e.g., QuaRot) at longer context lengths is missing, making the claim of “first to enable per‑tensor static quantization to outperform dynamic” unconvincing.  
- **Missing large‑scale model results** – Runtime speedups are shown only for smaller models; for 70B models, which are memory‑bound, the advantage of static vs. dynamic quantization diminishes. No results for 70B models are provided; limitations should be clearly stated.  
- **Performance without fine‑tuning is weak** – Table 18 shows that at W8A8 on overparameterized models, QuaRot consistently outperforms PrefixQuant without fine‑tuning. Even with fine‑tuning, QuaRot may still be better. A column indicating whether compared methods include fine‑tuning would help interpretation. Also, performance on 70B models without fine‑tuning is notably not strong.  
- **Task coverage insufficient** – Only perplexity and common‑sense reasoning tasks are reported. Broader, more realistic benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, GSM‑8K) are missing.  
- Request for head‑to‑head comparison with CushionCache and QFeP after removing grid search, Hadamard rotation, and block‑wise fine‑tuning to assess the value of the prefix optimization alone.
