## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a benchmark for evaluating generalization in offline reinforcement learning using datasets of varying sizes and skill levels from Procgen and WebShop. It tests state-of-the-art offline algorithms (BC, CQL, IQL, BCQ, DT, etc.) and finds that all methods perform worse than online RL on both training and testing environments, and that BC generalizes better than other offline methods. Reviewers agree the problem is important and experiments are extensive. However, opinions diverge on novelty and depth: one reviewer (R1, score 8) finds the results new and sufficient, while three reviewers (R2, R3, R4, score 6) raise concerns about limited novelty, lack of theoretical analysis, baseline tuning, and restricted algorithm/benchmark scope.

### Strengths
- Important and relatively understudied problem: understanding generalization in offline RL (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- New benchmark: collection of offline RL datasets from Procgen and WebShop with different data sizes and skill levels (R1, R2, R3).
- Thorough experiments covering multiple offline RL algorithms (CQL, IQL, BCQ, DT, etc.) and sequence modeling approaches on Procgen and WebShop (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Experimental results are sufficient and convincing; hyperparameters are provided and results appear reproducible (R1, R3).
- Well-written, clear, and easy to follow; red text boxes effectively highlight key takeaways (R2, R4).
- Results may have broad impact by drawing attention to the generalization issue in offline learning (R1).

### Weaknesses
- Lack of in-depth analysis or theoretical understanding of the root causes of poor generalization in offline RL; no discussion of why offline RL methods struggle compared to BC (R1, R2, R4). R1 explicitly requests thoughts on causes; R2 and R4 note missing deeper analysis such as theoretical explanations (e.g., similar to [arXiv:2204.05618]).
- Limited novelty: the findings are largely expected because existing offline RL algorithms are not designed to prioritize generalization (R2). (R1 considered the results new and important, indicating a divergence in perceived novelty.)
- Limited algorithm scope: missing model-based RL, curriculum imitation, and other recent methods (R3). Also missing justification for not using IQL on WebShop—expectile regression should not depend on the number of actions (R4).
- Limited benchmark scope: most experiments are on Procgen; only a small portion uses WebShop. Some reviewers suggest including D4RL continuous control tasks (e.g., modifying gravity) or more real-world problems (R3, R4).
- Baseline tuning concerns: large gap between BC and CQL suggests CQL may not be properly tuned, as CQL combines TD learning and BC loss (R4).
- Missing details and clarification requests:
  - Figure 2(a): line colors are wrong; results on Leaper are missing in Figures 11 and 12 (R1).
  - Figure 3: blue line (behavior policy) sometimes appears higher than red (expert), which is confusing given data is collected by expert PPO (R4).
  - Section 4.5 plot: insufficient data points (only three) to draw valid conclusions about the trend (R4).
  - WebShop environment setup insufficiently described (state/action/reward/transition) (R3).
  - Fine-tuning and model-selection procedures are unclear; how results are selected is important for reproducibility (R3).
  - No mention of whether the benchmark and code will be open-sourced (R3).
- Duplicate references in the bibliography (R2).
- Overall conclusions may be too definitive given the benchmark; there exist examples of offline RL agents achieving strong generalization (Agarwal 2020, Kumar 2023), so the paper should moderate its claims (R2).
