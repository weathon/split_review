- Scores: 8, 6, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CLIP-MUSED, a Transformer-based method for multi-subject visual neural semantic decoding that uses learnable subject-specific tokens to handle individual differences without linear parameter scaling, and applies representational similarity analysis (RSA) guided by the CLIP representation space to align neural responses across subjects. The method is evaluated on two fMRI datasets (NSD and HCP) and achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing multi-subject and single-subject decoding methods.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important problem in neuroscience: cross-subject functional alignment for visual neural decoding. The proposed multi-subject alignment method is novel and interesting.
- Using Transformers to model long-range dependencies in brain regions and the CLIP representation space to guide neural representation learning in a shared space is a nice idea.
- The use of RSA to incorporate topological relationships of visual stimuli in the CLIP space as prior knowledge for neural representation learning is novel and contributes to the method.
- The method shares model parameters and only varies a per-subject token, allowing use of multi-subject data without a linear increase in model parameters, which is beneficial for scalability.
- The paper provides a detailed methods section covering technical aspects, and the high-level approach is clear. The paper is well-organized and generally easy to read.
- Experimental results on two fMRI datasets demonstrate a proof-of-concept and verify the benefits of the proposed method, achieving state-of-the-art performance among multi-subject methods.

### Weaknesses
- The evaluation is rather limited, especially since both datasets are small (NSD: 9 subjects; HCP: only 9 subjects used from 158). Reviewer 1 notes that results serve as a proof-of-concept but are limited by dataset size. Reviewer 2 considers the decoding task weak: only category decoding is performed. They suggest stronger evaluation such as full image reconstruction for NSD (single image passive viewing) or at least image retrieval (show top-5) on a test set with unseen images. Reviewer 3 notes that experiments verify benefits but does not question the decoding task.
- The abstract is not as clearly written as the introduction; the problem and contributions are harder to discern from the abstract alone.
- There are inconsistencies in notation and typos: e.g., "The contributes are summarized as follows" → should be "contributions". In the abstract, $X^{(n)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_i \times d}$ should be $\mathcal{X}^{(n)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_i \times d}$ (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 notes letter inconsistency (e.g., representation of X) and suggests unifying notation.
- The subject-specific token is fixed per subject rather than computed via amortized inference (e.g., an encoder conditioned on a few BOLD responses). This limits the approach: the paper claims the method does not require subjects to view the same stimulus, but the fixed token prevents few-shot application to new subjects.
- The use of 3D-CNN for dimensionality reduction may affect results, but no ablation or comparison is shown (Reviewer 3).
- The high-level feature RSA employs the average of CLIP image and text features from the last layer. No comparison or justification is given for why averaging is better than using either the image encoder or text encoder alone (Reviewer 3).
- The paper extracts low-level and high-level feature RSMs separately, but the rationale for this separation is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- For the HCP dataset, only 9 of 158 subjects were used. How were the 158 subjects selected from HCP? Randomly? What is the bottleneck that reduces to 9? (Reviewer 1)
- Several methodological details are unclear or missing:
  - In Section 3.2, the backbone network is not defined. Does it take fMRI BOLD responses and output a category? Is the ViT a 3D model? (Reviewer 2)
  - It is not clear what exactly is being decoded. For HCP, the paper mentions a WordNet label; for NSD, 80 categories (binary 0/1 mask per object category). Clarify the exact task and labels. (Reviewer 2)
  - For NSD, is ROI-based patching used? For HCP, is CNN-based patching used? Is the CNN jointly trained with the Transformer? (Reviewer 2)
  - The meaning of "B" in "B visual stimuli" (Section 2.2) should be explained. (Reviewer 3)
  - For HCP, since the stimulus set consists of video clips, how are visual features extracted for the RSA guide? (Reviewer 2)
- Math notation issues:
  - Equation 10 uses the Frobenius norm, but the code (losses.py line 42) uses the squared Frobenius norm. Clarify/fix (Reviewer 2).
  - Equation 15 applies an orthogonal constraint between low-level and high-level representations via regularization loss. Clarify whether the squared norm is used here as well, and clarify that the orthogonality is intended between representations for each stimulus (Reviewer 2).
- Minor formatting: The underline in the AUC results of MS-SMODEL-ViT in Table 1 might be redundant; keep format consistent with other tables (Reviewer 3).
- Figure 1 text mentions "two bird images t1 and t2" and later says "t1 and t2 are birds, while t3 (duck) and t4 (building blocks) are not." Please clarify—ducks are birds. Use other examples. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper states that "Linear transformation and MLP are unsuitable for high-dimensional voxel responses," but no justification is given for the MLP aspect, and the cited work (Yousefnezhad & Zhang) uses a kernelized MLP approach. Clarify. (Reviewer 2)
