## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MISA for General Continual Learning, using forgetting-aware initial session adaptation (ISA-FAM) and non-parametric logit masking. All reviewers agreed the paper is well-written and the experiments are comprehensive. Reviewers were divided on novelty: two (R3,R4) found the approach SOTA and the main contribution (FAM) noteworthy, while two (R1,R2) considered the methodology effective but lacking substantial novelty. The method eliminates the need for a replay buffer, but this relies on availability of pretraining data.

### Strengths
* The writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow (R1,R2,R4). Reviewer 4 specifically called it “brilliant.”
* Comprehensive experiments, ablations, and analysis are provided (R1,R3,R4). The ablation section is particularly rich and thorough, probing every aspect of the method (R3).
* Method achieves SOTA performance on GCL benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-R) with up to ~18–22% improvement over replay-free competitors (R3,R4). All authors met their claims (R3).
* Key components (ISA-FAM and logit masking) are plug-and-play and can be applied to other prompt-based methods, as shown in Table 5 (R3).
* By cleverly leveraging pretraining data, the need for a replay buffer is eliminated; the paper also shows benefit when a buffer is allowed (R3).
* Code is provided in the supplementary material, aiding reproducibility (R3).
* The idea behind FAM (forgetting-aware initial session adaptation) is noteworthy and constitutes the main contribution (R4).
* While two reviewers (R1,R2) questioned the novelty, they acknowledged the effective implementation, the interesting combination of ideas, and the usefulness for the GCL community (R1,R2). In contrast, two reviewers (R3,R4) considered the combination and modifications effective and SOTA.

### Weaknesses
* **Novelty concerns**: Two reviewers (R1,R2) found the approach lacks substantial novelty, noting that SAM for initialization and masking have been used many times in continual learning. Conversely, two reviewers (R3,R4) considered the combination and modifications effective and SOTA. This disagreement should be noted.
* **Reliance on pretraining data**: The ISA-FAM phase assumes availability of the pretraining data, which may be unavailable in practice (e.g., open-sourced models without publicly released data) (R1,R3). Performance may degrade if the pretraining data is not representative of target tasks (R3). This is a strict constraint (R1).
* **Limited evaluation on out-of-distribution datasets**: All tested datasets are natural images similar to the pretraining data. Missing experiments on significantly different domains (e.g., completely different views, 3D data, medical imagery) would assess robustness to distribution shifts (R2,R3).
* **GCL scenario not fully realistic**: The paper does not handle cases where classes appear one by one (strict online), labels unknown ahead of time, novel class detection, unknown number of classes in the beginning, or noisy data (R1,R2). These practical challenges are not discussed or evaluated (R1,R2).
* **Missing comparisons and citations**: The paper fails to cite and compare with several related works (R1 mentions [2,3,4]; R3 notes omission of [4] from R3’s list for GCL and questions why DualPrompt was chosen over Coda-Prompt or HiDe-Prompt). The choice of DualPrompt over more recent methods is not justified (R3).
* **Technical concerns and missing ablations/discussions** (from R3,R4):
  * In ISA-FAM, the classification heads are trained with prompts but later discarded (replaced by previously trained heads). Why is this done? It seems prompts would then “communicate incorrectly” with the replacement heads (R3 Q1).
  * The optimization in Eq. 2 is puzzling: fine-tuning prompts with pretraining data likely yields near-zero gradients; the rationale should be explained earlier in Sec. 4.1 (R4).
  * Eqs. 5–6 resemble MAML (meta-learning); this connection should be discussed in the main paper (R4).
  * The prompt augmentation using an MLP is not convincingly better; testing with addition-based prompting (instead of concatenation) is suggested, as concatenation-based strategies are often less effective (R4).
  * The masking strategy is identical to ER-ACE (ICLR 2022); the paper should be transparent about this similarity (R4).
  * FAM could also be applied to standard class-incremental learning; the paper misses a discussion of this broader applicability (R4).
