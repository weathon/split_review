## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes EBMDock, an energy-based model for rigid protein-protein docking. It parameterizes an energy function over SE(3) transformations (rotation R, translation t) using a modified Equiformer architecture and statistical potentials (negative log-likelihood of residue-pair distances, modeled with GMMs). Training uses contrastive divergence with Langevin dynamics for negative sampling. At inference, poses are sampled via Langevin dynamics. The method is evaluated on DIPS and DB5.5 benchmarks. Reviewers describe the idea as well-motivated, clear, and a nice contrast to prevailing ML methods, with practical inference-time advantages. However, several weaknesses are raised regarding performance relative to AlphaFold-Multimer and classical docking, the simplification to alpha-carbon only, missing baseline comparisons, insufficient architectural and optimization details, and limited evaluation scope.

### Strengths

- The energy-based formulation provides a nice probabilistic framework for docking, enabling efficient sampling and clear integration of training and inference.
- Presenting the method is clear and follows a standard EBM paradigm (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- Leveraging binding site prediction to restrict energy function parameterization to relevant residue pairs is a crucial design choice (Reviewer 1).
- The idea is general and could apply to other rigid-docking settings (Reviewer 1).
- The focus on inference-time performance is practical and helpful for practitioners; EBMDock is substantially faster than AlphaFold-Multimer and PatchDock (Reviewer 3).
- Contrastive divergence training is clever for data augmentation, given limited protein complex data (Reviewer 4).
- The method maintains equivariance via geometric deep learning, which is important for generalizability (Reviewer 4).
- Ablation studies on different binding site prediction methods add useful empirical analysis (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- The method uses only alpha-carbon positions, ignoring backbone and side-chain geometry. This limits the model’s ability to capture steric clashes and specific interactions, potentially leading to inaccurate poses, especially where those interactions dominate (Reviewer 1).
- DockQ score includes native contact preservation using all-atom representation; using only alpha carbons raises validity concerns. The authors should clarify how DockQ is adapted and acknowledge potential bias (Reviewer 1).
- Performance is much worse than AlphaFold-Multimer (DockQ 0.16 vs 0.48), making it unlikely to be useful in practical settings (Reviewer 2). While Reviewer 1 notes AFM results may be optimistic due to training set overlap, the lack of a truly independent post-cutoff benchmark hinders direct generalizability assessment (Reviewer 1).
- EBMDock is evaluated only in a rigid-body setting (ground-truth monomer structures given). This limits applicability because many interactions involve conformational changes upon binding; the comparison with AFM (which folds from sequence) is not fair (Reviewer 2). Additionally, if ground-truth structures are not available, one must first predict structures (e.g., with AF2), making the whole workflow not much faster than AFM and introducing dependence on external structure prediction (Reviewer 2).
- The gradient of the rotation ∇_R E(R, t) is not clearly defined; rotation matrices are not elements of a vector space, so the optimization process is unclear (Reviewer 2, Question from Reviewer 2).
- The Equiformer-PP architecture is not described; details on layers, equivariant operations, and parameterization are missing (Reviewer 2).
- The paper does not sufficiently address the performance of binding site and contact prediction components before docking; comparison with dedicated binding site and contact prediction classifiers is absent (Reviewer 3, Question from Reviewer 3).
- The performance gap to HDock (in DockQ) is significant and requires more in-depth analysis and discussion (Reviewer 3). One reviewer notes that DiffDock-PP, EquiDock, and EBMDock without ground-truth binding sites appear not yet usable for accurate pose prediction; this needs explicit statement and proposals for improvement (Reviewer 3).
- Method variations (e.g., EquiDock+plugin, EBMDock (5)) are not clearly defined in a single place, and naming conventions lack specificity, making results hard to interpret (Reviewer 3, Question from Reviewer 3).
- Only 100 samples are used for the DIPS-Het test set; it is unclear if constraints prevent larger sampling; authors should test on a more extensive dataset (Reviewer 4).
- xTrimoDock is omitted from baselines; according to the cited work, it can outperform both HDock and AlphaFold-Multimer and is relatively fast. It should be included for a comprehensive comparison (Reviewer 4).
- EBMDock-interface relies on ground-truth binding interface, which is not a fair comparison to other baselines; this could cause misunderstanding. Authors should present these results separately or clarify (Reviewer 4).
- Training an EBM with contrastive divergence and on-the-fly Langevin dynamics is time-consuming; details on training time and cost-reduction techniques are missing (Reviewer 1, Question).
- The method has not been compared to a simple alternative: training an inter-residue distance predictor and recovering poses by gradient descent on the predicted distance map (Reviewer 1, Question).
- The insight or motivation for using EBM instead of a cheaper diffusion-based approach (like DiffDock-PP) is not clearly illustrated (Reviewer 1, Question).
- It is unclear why AF-Multimer (sequence-only) outperforms rigid PP docking that uses structural information; authors should explain this phenomenon (Reviewer 4, Question).
- The energy formulation as statistical potential using distance likelihoods is rather simplistic; could more complex formulations improve accuracy? (Reviewer 4, Question)
- Why choose a 6-component GMM for the MDN? Is performance sensitive to the number of mixtures? (Reviewer 4, Question)
- How was EquiformerPP designed? What modifications were made to Equiformer? (Reviewer 4, Question)
- How does inference time scale with protein complex size? Could efficiency be an issue for very large complexes? (Reviewer 4, Question)
- Have the authors considered extending to flexible protein docking? What changes would be needed? (Reviewer 4, Question)

**Minority reviewer contrast:** One reviewer (Reviewer 2, rating 3) is substantially more critical, emphasizing that EBMDock’s performance is far below AFM and that the rigid-body setting and reliance on predicted structures limit practical utility. Another reviewer (Reviewer 3, rating 8) is more positive, highlighting the well-motivated approach and practical speed advantages, while still acknowledging significant weaknesses. The remaining reviewers (ratings 6) express a balanced view with notable reservations.
