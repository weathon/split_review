## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes that pure message-passing can estimate common neighbor (CN) and other structural heuristics for link prediction by assigning each node a quasi-orthogonal (QO) vector as its signature. The authors introduce the Message Passing Link Predictor (MPLP), which uses QO vectors to estimate link-level features while preserving node-level complexities. Experiments on 13 attributed and non-attributed graph datasets are conducted. Three reviewers rate the paper 5 (weak reject or borderline) and one reviewer rates it 8 (strong accept). The minority opinion is notably more positive, especially regarding the overall impact and empirical performance.

### Strengths
- **Originality and theoretical contribution**: The paper studies the fundamental limitation of MPNNs in capturing joint structural features (e.g., CN) and proposes a way to overcome it using orthogonality of input vectors. This is a meaningful discussion and extension of previous work (Zhang et al., 2021) on whether GNNs can capture structural link representations [R1, R2, R3, R4].
- **Novel use of quasi-orthogonal vectors**: Exploiting QO vectors (borrowed from DotHash) enables estimation of CN, DE, AA, and RA heuristics within a pure message-passing framework. This idea is novel and may inspire future work [R1, R2].
- **Clean theoretical analysis**: Theorems (especially Theorem 2) provide a formal basis for the connection between message passing and CN estimation. The paper includes analyses of expressiveness, walk counting, and the effect of orthogonality [R1, R2, R3].
- **Comprehensive experimental setup**: Experiments cover 13 datasets from diverse domains, including both attributed and non-attributed graphs. The non-attributed benchmarks are an interesting inclusion that highlights MPLP’s capabilities [R1, R2, R3].
- **Thorough inference and ablation studies**: The paper includes investigations of signature dimension, inference behavior, batch size effects, and an ablation study (Table 5) [R2].
- **Performance results**: Reviewer 4 finds that MPLP outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on both attributed and non-attributed benchmarks [R4]. (Noted: Reviewer 3 contests the significance of these improvements – see Weaknesses.)
- **Discussion of attribute orthogonality**: The paper discusses how GNNs can perform link prediction on attributed datasets due to the orthogonality of attributes, an interesting point supported by Table 4 [R4].
- **Clarity (mixed opinions)**: Reviewer 1 finds the paper well-written and easy to follow; Reviewer 2 praises succinctness and respect for the reader’s background. However, Reviewer 3 raises writing concerns (see Weaknesses) [R1, R2, R3].

### Weaknesses
- **Ablation study (Table 5) shows shortcut removal is the main working component; some proposed components do not work well** [R3].
- **Marginal improvements in many cases**: While Reviewer 4 claims MPLP outperforms baselines, Reviewer 3 notes that the improvements over baselines in Tables 1 and 2 are often marginal and not statistically significant (mean and standard deviation) [R3, R4].
- **Missing standard OGBL datasets in attributed benchmarks**: Table 2 does not include results for ogbl-ppa, ogbl-ddi, and ogbl-citation2. Only ogbl-collab is compared, and only against ELPH, not the more scalable BUDDY [R2].
- **Limited evaluation metrics**: Experiments only report Hits@50. Current SOTA models typically use Hits@100, Hits@20, and MRR; the paper does not justify this choice [R2].
- **Estimation capability testing limited to GCN and SAGE**: To support claims about expressiveness and the effect of QO vectors, testing should be extended to other GNNs such as GIN or GAT (or a provably expressive GNN). The inner product calculation may not naturally extend to attention-based models, but GIN is a suitable candidate [R2].
- **Node-label estimation limited to CN and DE**: MPLP estimates only CN and DE, not DRNL or DE+. Since SEAL explicitly tests DRNL and DE++ as labeling tricks, their inclusion would provide a more complete evaluation [R2].
- **Loss of non-linearity**: The pure message-passing adopted in MPLP loses the non-linearity of standard MPNNs, which compromises expressiveness [R1].
- **Heuristic-based and fixed QO vectors**: The QO vectors are heuristic-based and predetermined, reducing flexibility and capacity compared to learned representations [R1].
- **Scalability concerns and missing runtime comparison**: Equation (5) relies on an \(r\)-hop induced subgraph to estimate \(r\)-order intersections and differences. Theorem 3 states that \(\mathbf{h}_u^r\) and \(\mathbf{h}_v^r\) cannot be used for CN, raising questions about overall complexity. The paper lacks a side-by-side runtime comparison with BUDDY (including preprocessing and link-wise estimation) [R1].
- **Unclear effect of neighborhood sampling**: The impact of sampling (e.g., in SAGE) on Theorem 1 and the estimator in Eq. (3) is not analyzed [R1].
- **Cost and dimension selection of QO vectors**: The computational cost of generating QO vectors, a principled way to choose their dimension, and scalability to graphs larger than Collab are not addressed. The effect of graph density on runtime and estimation error is unclear [R1].
- **Order of features \(r=2\)**: Experiments use \(r=2\) for computational reasons; it is unclear whether MPLP can be applied beyond 2 hops [R1].
- **Ambiguity in GNN definitions**: In Eq. (4) and Eq. (8), it is not specified whether \(\text{GNN}(\cdot)\) refers to vanilla GNNs or the linearized version from Eq. (3) [R1].
- **Lack of baseline differentiation**: More details are needed to distinguish ELPH, BUDDY, and NCN/NCNC-k when interpreting Table 2 and Figure 4 [R1].
- **Theorem 3 walk operation impractical**: The walk operation counting the number of length-\(l\) walks between nodes appears time-consuming and its practical implementation is unclear [R3].
- **Inconsistent results for SAGE**: The results for SAGE on Computers/Photo differ between Table 2 and Table 4, raising a question about reproducibility [R4].
- **Unjustified Gaussian assumption for attributed benchmarks**: While using random Gaussian vectors is reasonable for non-attributed graphs, the paper does not justify why this initialization would be optimal when node attributes are available. It is not clear whether random vectors should supplement or compete with informative attributes [R4].
- **Novelty concern: CN injection via simpler means**: The core idea injects CN heuristics via QO vectors. It is not clear if performance gains stem from the novel use of orthogonality or simply from injecting CN information, which could be achieved by directly using CN as node attributes (e.g., substituting the first element of input vectors with the CN value) [R4].
- **Missing exploration of attribute + random feature concatenation**: The paper does not test whether concatenating attribute vectors with random features yields better results than using either alone, which could automatically select the better representation [R4].
- **Limited discussion of practical impact**: The paper does not discuss scenarios where MPLP would benefit industry applications or the broader impact of enabling MPNNs for link prediction [R4].
- **Writing needs improvement**: While some reviewers found the paper well-written, Reviewer 3 notes that Figure 3 lacks a caption and color explanation, and that the overall writing requires heavy improvement [R3]. (Disagreement noted: R1 and R2 found the writing clear.)
