## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses hyperparameter optimisation for unsupervised GNN node embeddings without labels. It proposes a framework with two steps: building prior beliefs that characterise high-quality embeddings, and quantifying to what extent those beliefs are satisfied. The framework is instantiated as two metrics: Consensus-based Space Occupancy Rate (CSOR, spatial perspective) and Spectral Space Occupancy Rate (SSOR, spectral perspective). Extensive experiments on seven GNN models and four citation-network datasets (all small, homophilic) are conducted. Reviewers find the problem interesting and the presentation clear, but raise significant concerns about limited dataset diversity, unclear advantages over the existing RankMe baseline, lack of theoretical grounding, computational cost, missing comparisons to contrastive learning, and several technical issues with the proposed metrics.

### Strengths

- The problem of evaluating node embedding quality without labels is important and timely, and the study is motivated clearly.
- Presentation in the main text is well-organised and easy to follow.
- CSOR and SSOR are novel metrics that assess embedding quality from complementary spatial and spectral perspectives.
- The observation-driven approach (building prior beliefs from empirical observations) is simple and logically intuitive.
- Extensive experiments cover multiple GNN models (see Tables; models include VGAE, GCN, GAT, GIN, etc.) and several citation datasets, demonstrating the ranking ability of the metrics.
- Visualisations and empirical analysis of the experimental data are comprehensive.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited dataset scope**: Experiments are only on four small, homophilic citation networks. This does not reflect real-world large-scale or diverse graph structures. Reviewers recommend evaluation on larger datasets (e.g., Reddit, OGBN-ARXIV) and graphs from other domains (social networks, protein interaction, recommendation systems) to test generalisability.
- **Unconvincing motivation experiments**: Figure 1 (motivating sensitivity) uses only VGAE, Cora, and node classification. Stronger evidence is needed with more popular GNNs (GCN, GAT, Transformer) and on other datasets/tasks.
- **Inferior or limited improvement over RankMe**: Multiple reviewers observe that CSOR and SSOR often perform no better and are sometimes inferior to the existing RankMe baseline, yet the paper does not clearly justify why CSOR/SSOR are needed or in which scenarios they outperform RankMe. This limits practical utility.
- **High computational cost**: The SVD operation in SSOR is computationally expensive, making it less scalable to large graphs.
- **Missing comparison with contrastive learning**: Contrastive methods (e.g., GraphCL) are a dominant paradigm in unsupervised graph representation learning but are not included in the evaluation. The paper should compare against them.
- **Ambiguous distance computation for different embedding sizes**: When hyperparameters change the embedding dimension (e.g., \(Z_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_1}\), \(Z_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_2}\)), it is unclear how pairwise distances are computed between embeddings of different sizes.
- **CSOR may favour poor configurations**: CSOR measures distance from the averaged embeddings of all other hyperparameter configurations. This can give high scores to badly performing configurations, as observed in Figure 42. The paper provides no fix or insight for this problem.
- **SSOR gives high scores for random embeddings**: If the learned embedding matrix is random, SSOR still yields a very high score, which undermines its ability to discriminate quality.
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No theoretical justification links the singular values of the embedding matrix (SSOR) or spatial distances (CSOR) to embedding quality such as separability.
- **Unclear relation to UDR**: It is not clear how the Uniformity–Distortion Rate (UDR) from Section 2 relates to the proposed CSOR and SSOR metrics.
- **Modification of GAT/GIN not clearly stated**: In the code, GAT and GIN are used as encoders inside the Graph Autoencoder (GAE) framework for unsupervised training, but this architectural change is not explained in the paper. The results may not be comparable to original implementations and the choice needs justification.
- **Seed sensitivity not addressed**: Even with the same hyperparameters, different seeds can place the same node’s embedding at very different positions. Directly using embedding distance as an evaluation measure requires more evidence that it is robust to this variability.
- **Minor issues**: 
  - Figure 3 caption says RankMe is “green circles” but the legend uses white diamonds.
  - “Hyperparameters” is misspelled as “hyperparamters” in multiple places.
  - The SSOR formula (Section 5) may have an error: \(r\) should possibly be \(r-1\).
  - Code specifications (e.g., software versions) are missing.
  - The number of datasets used to establish the prior beliefs—or whether randomly generated graphs were used—is not reported.
  - Only Manhattan distance is used; the impact of other distance metrics is unexplored.
