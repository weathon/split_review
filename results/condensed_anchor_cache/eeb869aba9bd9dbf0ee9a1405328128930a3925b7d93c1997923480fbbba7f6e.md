## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies zero-shot transfer of object-centric representation learning models that use pre-trained DINO (or DINOv2) features. It introduces a benchmark of seven synthetic and real-world datasets to evaluate transferability, analyzes factors affecting zero-shot performance, and proposes a fine-tuning strategy for the pre-trained encoder (based on DINOSAUR) that achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised object discovery with strong zero-shot transfer. The work is the first systematic study of zero-shot transfer in object-centric learning, and the proposed fine-tuning method effectively prevents model collapse and improves performance. However, reviewers disagree on novelty, evaluation design, and clarity. Two reviewers rate the paper 6, one rates it 5, and one rates it 3 (confusion, missing details). The most positive reviewers find the benchmark and fine-tuning approach valuable; the most negative reviewer finds the writing confusing, technical details absent, and the evaluation of zero-shot transfer poorly explained.

### Strengths

- **First systematic study of zero-shot transfer in object-centric representation learning.** The paper introduces a benchmark comprising seven diverse datasets (synthetic and real-world) to evaluate transferability, addresses an important and under-explored topic, and provides insights into factors affecting performance. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **The proposed fine-tuning strategy (DINOSAUR fine-tuning on COCO) is effective.** It improves object-discovery performance and closes the gap between zero-shot and supervised learning. The method is straightforward yet successfully addresses the model collapse problem. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation.** Extensive experiments across multiple datasets and metrics, with thorough ablation studies validating each component (e.g., high-resolution adaptation, top‑k decoding) and a detailed appendix. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Well-motivated and clearly structured.** The paper begins with a clear question, proposes a testbed based on experimental findings, and is clearly written (though one reviewer disagrees strongly on clarity). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Interesting topic.** Object discovery in unsupervised settings is a relevant and interesting problem. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty of the proposed method.** The fine-tuning strategy (full encoder fine-tuning, high-resolution adaptation, top‑k decoding) relies on well-known engineering improvements and does not introduce significant conceptual insights beyond improved hyperparameter tuning. Similar techniques have been applied in broader ML literature; their application to object-centric learning does not represent a meaningful advancement. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of why these specific choices lead to improved performance, making the findings difficult to generalize. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Obvious or already known conclusions.** Several findings are considered self-evident: that SAM already demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer in object-centric models, that training on complex natural datasets (like COCO) is crucial for zero-shot transfer, and that using real data improves generalization. The claim that “training on diverse real-world images improves transferability” adds little to current knowledge, as data quantity and diversity are widely known to improve performance. The paper does not provide new insights into which specific types of diversity are most beneficial or analyze trade-offs between data sources. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Evaluation design relies heavily on pre‑trained DINO features.** The object-centric methods use DINO (or DINOv2) features, which already have strong OOD capabilities due to large-scale pre‑training on ImageNet. Consequently, it is difficult to ascertain whether the observed zero-shot generalization stems from the object-centric grouping method or from DINO’s inherent robustness. The model primarily learns to cluster pre‑existing semantically meaningful features rather than learning object representations from raw pixels. This limits conclusions about the independent generalization ability of object-centric learning. (Reviewer 4; also noted by Reviewer 1 regarding dataset scale)
- **Segmentation is the only downstream metric.** Using object segmentation as the primary evaluation metric may not fully capture representation quality. Additional tasks (e.g., object classification, property prediction) would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of semantic understanding. Good segmentation can be achieved by grouping similar-looking regions without understanding what the objects represent. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing technical details and poor writing clarity (Reviewer 2).** The paper lacks clear explanations of:
    - **Rationale for dataset selection for zero-shot transfer** – not explained why these particular datasets were chosen or whether they are representative.
    - **Procedures for zero-shot transfer** – how the model is applied to new datasets without additional training is not detailed, making it hard to assess validity.
    - **Implementation of slot attention** – number of slots, dimensionality, attention mechanism, and specific architecture are not provided.
    - **Top‑k MLP decoder** – number of layers, activation functions, and method for selecting top‑k outputs are missing.
    - **Loss function** – the objective used for fine‑tuning is not specified.
    - **Origin of output images in Figure 3** – it is unclear whether the masked images are direct outputs of DINOv2 or results of further processing from the object‑centric module.
- **Scaling behavior not addressed.** The paper shows (Fig. 2b) that models, especially on real‑world data like COCO, do not scale well with dataset size, yet it provides no analysis or solution for this limitation. Understanding the factors that improve scaling could increase the impact of the work. (Reviewer 1)
- **Benchmark datasets are relatively small-scale for pre‑training.** Only COCO, PASCAL, and EntitySeg are consistent with natural image distributions; ScanNet and YCB contain images without background. This limited scale may explain why no clear scaling law was observed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Fine-tuning on COCO lacks a specific design for zero-shot tasks.** The method does not incorporate any mechanism tailored for zero-shot generalization; task-specific fine-tuning on a general pretrained model typically leads to performance improvements anyway. (Reviewer 3)
- **Requested experiments and analyses:**
    - Evaluation without the DINO encoder (e.g., using raw pixels) to accurately assess model transferability. (Reviewer 4)
    - Investigation of alternative slot initialization (e.g., learnable queries from BO‑QSA, OSRT, SPOT) instead of blockwise exponentially decaying learning rates; the current ablation shows little improvement, and the problem may not be caused by random slot initialization. (Reviewer 1)
    - Identification of common failure cases and exploration of their underlying reasons to provide deeper insights from the testbed. (Reviewer 3)
    - A detailed comparison of similarities between COCO and the seven testbed datasets to understand the source of transfer. (Reviewer 3)
