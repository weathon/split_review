## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper aims to theoretically characterize the shape of the accuracy-fairness Pareto frontier (FairFrontier) for binary classification under equalized odds. The main claimed contributions are: (1) when non‑sensitive attributes fully encode the sensitive attributes, the frontier is mostly continuous; (2) otherwise, the frontier may be discontinuous; (3) by decomposing unfairness into data unfairness and model unfairness, one can eliminate the trade‑off. The paper includes experiments on synthetic data to illustrate the findings. Reviewers acknowledge that understanding the accuracy‑fairness trade‑off is an important and relevant problem, and that the approach of linking the frontier’s shape to how closely non‑sensitive attributes encode sensitive ones has potential. However, the majority of reviewers find the paper lacking in rigor, clarity, and novelty, with major weaknesses in definitions, proofs, experiments, and practical insights. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, highlighting the novel use of envelope curves and the focus on continuity as a new perspective.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses an important and relevant problem of understanding the accuracy‑fairness trade‑off. (All reviewers)
- The approach that looks at the trade‑off in the light of how closely non‑sensitive attributes encode the sensitive attributes has potential and is worth pursuing. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper establishes theoretical results that identify different scenarios leading to different continuity behaviors of the trade‑off curve, providing new insights. (Reviewer 3)
- The proof techniques involving envelope curves are quite novel. (Reviewer 2, minority positive)
- The focus on the continuity of the Pareto frontier is an important aspect that has not been looked at before. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper introduces several specific claimed results: Lemma 1 (sharp decline in fairness or both cannot occur), Lemma 2 (when sharp decline in accuracy occurs), Theorem 1 & Proposition 1 (when both accuracy and fairness can be maximized simultaneously), Theorem 2 (conditions for sharp decline in accuracy), Theorem 3 (when model and data unfairness are decoupled and additive). (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of real‑world data experiments.** The paper claims to demonstrate the trade‑off in real‑world settings, but no real datasets are used; only synthetic experiments are presented. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Imprecise and undefined terminology.** Many key terms are used without rigorous mathematical definitions. Examples include: “over‑pursuing fairness”, “mostly continuous”, “sharp decline” (ambiguously meaning discontinuity or rapid change), “local maximum of fairness”, “highly unfavorable prediction for one group”, “non‑trivial classifier”, “consistent sign relationship”. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Hand‑wavy proofs and lack of rigor.** The proofs, especially for Lemma 1 and Lemma 2, are sloppy. For example:
  - Figure 9 lacks points M, A, B that are referenced in the proof.
  - Variable B is defined but not used.
  - The term “sharp decline” is not given a mathematical definition.
  - The space $\Omega$ is claimed to be a probability space, but its outcome space, sigma‑algebra, and probability measure are not defined; it appears $\Omega$ is the feature space.
  - Lemma 1 and 2 are not written rigorously. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Theorem statements lack explanation.** The theorems often state conditions without explaining what they mean or imply mathematically, making them hard to interpret. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unsubstantiated or trivial claims.** 
  - The paper asserts that the Pareto frontier is convex without proof, yet the accompanying figure shows a concave frontier. (Reviewer 1)
  - Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 convey a trivial argument: if optimizers for different groups are aligned, there is no conflict between fairness and accuracy. (Reviewer 4)
  - Theorem 2: if $T_\Theta^f$ is the optimal classifier for fairness, it corresponds to an extreme point on the global Pareto front with the lowest accuracy; the case where accuracy of $T^*_a$ is lower than that of the optimal classifier for fairness does not seem possible. (Reviewer 4)
  - The claim that the FairFrontier may be non‑convex is too strong; a randomized combination of two classifiers on the front should lie within the Pareto front, suggesting convexity. (Reviewer 2)
  - Theorem 1 seems a straightforward consequence of prior results by Chouldechova and Kleinberg on inherent trade‑offs. (Reviewer 1)
- **Experimental section poorly described.** 
  - There is insufficient explanation of how experiments match the theoretical formulation (e.g., why the distributions in Sec 6.1 correspond to the “ideal” setting). (Reviewer 3)
  - Figure axes and captions lack details; e.g., what does “value” mean in the figures? (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - Sections 6.1 and 6.2 use the same setting, but it is unclear how two different Pareto front shapes (Figure 2 vs Figure 4) were obtained. (Reviewer 4)
  - Section 6.4 uses triangular distributions without justification. (Reviewer 3)
  - The claim in Section 6.4 that “maximum accuracy becomes unattainable” is not based on clear evidence. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figures 6 and 7 captions are misleading; both are demonstrations for Example 4. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of intuition and toy examples.** The paper would benefit from a simple toy example explaining the intuition of the results early on. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unexplored references.** Use of envelope curves is mentioned, but no references are discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Proposed methodology for eliminating trade‑off is vague.** The approach is abstract and conceptual, without an actionable solution. It is unclear how to “transform” data to tackle data unfairness, or why one should tackle data unfairness first. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Clarity of definitions and setup is poor.**
  - Definition 1 (well‑defined classifier) is impractical; it requires agreement with optimal classifiers for both groups on the set where they agree, even if those classifiers are inaccurate. (Reviewer 1)
  - Notation in Section 2: $\hat{Y}$ depends on $T_a(x)$; what happens when $T_a(x)=0$? (Reviewer 3)
  - The claim that “other definitions of fairness can also be incorporated” needs concrete specification of which results hold and under what requirements. (Reviewer 3)
  - Where are $TPR^*$ and $TNR^*$ defined in Eq. 12? (Reviewers 3, 4)
  - Theorem 1 equation (7) appears to mean the two groups are not discriminative at all; Condition 2 performs arithmetic over probability density functions, which makes no sense. (Reviewer 4)
  - Theorem 1 P5 Condition 1 essentially means the sensitive feature is completely aligned with target labels, leading to a random data scenario. (Reviewer 4)
  - Section 6.4’s elimination of trade‑off stems from unbiased distribution of sensitive attributes across positive/negative groups, not from any model architecture change. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor presentation issues:** “Non‑sensitive” should be “non‑sensitive” (p2); constraint $w_1+w_2=1$ is not specified; Eq. 4 should write out “y=1”; “empirically prove Theorem 3” should be “empirically validate” (p6); $\Theta$ in $T_\Theta$ is introduced without definition (p2); “FairFrontier is monotonically non‑increasing” is misleading – it is non‑increasing in accuracy and non‑decreasing in fairness, and monotonicity is trivial from Pareto front definition; Figure 4 y‑axis label needs fixing; Section 4.2 “optimal classifier used for each group” should specify “for the whole dataset” (Reviewers 3, 4)
