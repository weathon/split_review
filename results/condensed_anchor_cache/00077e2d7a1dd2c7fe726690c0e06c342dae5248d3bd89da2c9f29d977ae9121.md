## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies how to design additional rewards to steer multi-agent systems toward desired policies without prior knowledge of the agents' learning dynamics, focusing on a new class of "Markovian agents" whose decisions depend only on current states and immediate rewards. It proposes a model-based non-episodic RL formulation with history-dependent steering strategies to handle model uncertainty, introduces a novel objective function balancing steering outcome and cost, and identifies theoretical conditions for the existence of effective steering strategies. The authors develop algorithms including a belief-state-based strategy for small model classes and a First-Explore-Then-Exploit (FETE) framework for larger classes, and present empirical results under the assumption that each agent has only two actions.

### Strengths

- **Conceptual novelty and relevance**: The paper introduces a new approach to steering by focusing on Markovian agents with limited cognitive resources, and the overall method is intuitive and logical (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Theoretical contributions**: The paper provides comprehensive theoretical analysis, including sufficient conditions for achieving low steering gaps, Pareto optimality, and the existence of history-dependent strategies (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Algorithmic development**: The proposed algorithms effectively balance tractability and optimality: a belief-state-based steering strategy for small model classes and the FETE framework for larger classes (Reviewer 3).
- **Objective function**: The new objective function explicitly balances the goal of steering agents toward a target policy while minimizing overall steering cost (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental transparency**: The paper provides detailed experimental assumptions in the appendices, including a wide range of learning rate combinations (up to 3^10), and presents results in different environments (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Presentation**: The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow, with justification and explanation in most places (Reviewer 1). However, one reviewer found the writing poor and the problem formulation unclear (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Model class selection and identifiability trade-offs**: The paper does not clearly articulate how the choice of the model class \(F\) impacts the identifiability condition or provide guidance on selecting an appropriate class in practice. If \(F\) is too small, the true agent dynamics may not be representable; if too large, the identifiability condition becomes difficult to satisfy and may require excessive exploration. The theoretical sections could more explicitly connect understanding of \(F\) to reducing model uncertainty (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited applicability to real-world complexity**: The algorithm relies on accurately specifying agents' learning dynamics and is currently limited to tabular MDPs with a finite model class and binary/categorical decision settings. Real-world scenarios involving non-tabular MDPs, infinite model classes, inter-agent dependencies (e.g., herd behavior, nonconformity), and continuous action spaces are not addressed, and the experiments are basic. The current framework may require significant adaptations for broader, more realistic scenarios (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing comparisons**: 
  - The paper does not compare against non-Markovian agent models, which could help justify the emphasis on Markovian agents in the introduction (Reviewer 1).
  - No comparison is made with exploration-focused approaches such as Random Network Distillation (RND) versus the random exploration baseline FETE-RE used in Section 6.2 (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper does not specify in which scenarios the proposed method outperforms control methods and in which it shows similar or inferior performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Problem motivation and clarity (disagreement among reviewers)**: While Reviewer 1 found the paper well-written, Reviewer 2 noted significant issues:
  - The steering problem is not well-motivated; key concepts (e.g., "desired policies", "desired behaviors") are ambiguous and their precise meaning is unclear.
  - The "mediator" is introduced without explanation, and the notation \(f^*\) appears abruptly at line 210.
  - The new learning objective (line 220) is hard to understand and lacks sufficient clarification regarding its purpose and relevance.
  - Transitions between paragraphs are weak, leading to a lack of cohesion; the problem formulation section becomes increasingly difficult to follow.
- **Conceptual gaps**: 
  - If the desired policy is already known, the necessity of learning is not explained. It remains unclear in what situations learning is still required even when the desired policy is known (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper does not explain why steering is necessary in multi-agent systems when existing algorithms could find an equilibrium or approximate equilibrium (Reviewer 2).
- **Extension to non-tabular and infinite settings**: The paper does not address what happens when the model set \(\mathcal{F}\) is uncountably infinite or for non-tabular MDP setups, leaving open questions about the scalability of the framework (Reviewer 3).
