## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SINDy-SHRED, a hybrid model that combines Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) regularization with the SHallow Recurrent Decoder (SHRED) framework for spatio-temporal modeling. The approach uses GRUs to model temporal sensor measurements, a shallow decoder to reconstruct the full field, and SINDy to regularize the latent dynamics for interpretability and long-term prediction. Reviewers are divided: one (score 6) finds the method flexible, efficient, and robust with extensive experiments, while the other three (scores 3,3,3) see limited novelty, missing critical comparisons (especially the original SHRED), unclear formulations, and insufficient experimental validation.

### Strengths
- Incorporation of SINDy into the SHRED framework introduces a degree of interpretability to the latent space and promotes identification of governing physics (Reviewers 1,2,4).  
- The method is straightforward, flexible, efficient, and robust, demonstrating good long-term prediction on synthetic PDE data, real-world sea surface temperature, and video data (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
- Experiments span multiple datasets (pendulum, cylinder flow, SST, video) and include comparison with several baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, ResNet, SimVP) for the pendulum case (Reviewers 1,3).  
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,3).  
- SINDy regularization enables accurate long-term video predictions with state-of-the-art performance (Reviewers 1,3).  
- Training is efficient (laptop-level computing) with minimal hyperparameter tuning (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The method is a straightforward combination of two existing approaches (SHRED and SINDy); modifications are restricted to the recurrent component (LSTM→GRU + SINDy regularization) (Reviewers 2,4).  
- **Missing critical baselines**: The original SHRED model is not included in any comparison, making the claimed improvements impossible to evaluate objectively (Reviewer 4).  
- **Incomplete experimental validation**:
  - Baseline comparisons are performed only on the pendulum dataset, not on all cases (Reviewers 1,4).  
  - No comparison with other symbolic regression methods (e.g., symbolic learning) to justify the choice of SINDy (Reviewer 1).  
  - No comparison with established reduced-order model (ROM) methods for the cylinder flow problem (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing ablation and component analyses**:
  - No ablation study to show the effect of SINDy loss (i.e., train the same pipeline without SINDy) (Reviewer 3).  
  - No ablation study comparing GRU vs. LSTM to support the claim that GRU produces a smoother latent space (Reviewers 3,4).  
  - Component-wise analysis (roles of SINDy regularization and recurrent unit choice) is absent (Reviewer 4).  
- **Formulation and algorithmic issues**:
  - Inconsistency: Eqns (1-2) define latent dynamics as \(\dot{z}(t)=f(x(t))\) (driven by input), but Eqn (3) and the rest of the paper treat \(z_t\) as autonomous \(\dot{z}_t=f(z_t)\), restricting the method to stationary processes without exogenous forcing (Reviewer 2).  
  - The ensemble SINDy strategy is questionable: minimizing \(\sum_i \|Z^{\text{GRU}} - Z^{\text{SINDy}}_i\|\) collapses to a single SINDy; it is not explained how “varying levels of sparsity” are achieved or how \(\Xi^i\) is set (Reviewer 2).  
  - Algorithm 1 is unclear (e.g., what does line 8 “optimization is completely solved for every \(i\) and fixed \(X_{t-L:t}\)” mean? How is it solved? What is \(n\) in line 2?) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Purpose of sparse discovery is unclear if GRU is still used to compute \(Z\); SINDy does not replace the GRU (Reviewer 2).  
- **Claim about spectral bias is misleading**: The paper claims the method avoids spectral bias because it has no encoder, but spectral bias is a property of neural networks themselves (not just encoders) and would require structural changes (e.g., Fourier features, periodic activations). SINDy-SHRED does not directly address spectral bias (Reviewer 4; also questioned by Reviewer 3).  
- **Training requires full-field data**: To train the model, the whole field \(X\) must be available, creating a chicken-and-egg problem for real-world applications where only sparse sensors are available (Reviewer 2).  
- **Unexplained behavior in Table 1**: SINDy-SHRED performs worse in short-term forecasting but improves at longer leads—a trend that is not convincingly explained (Reviewer 4).  
- **Lack of sensitivity and convergence analyses**:
  - No study of how noise in the data affects the discovered latent dynamics (will the analytical form remain the same?) (Reviewer 1).  
  - No study of the impact of library size (e.g., including more terms) on the SINDy result (Reviewer 1).  
  - No sensitivity analysis with respect to ensemble size (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing clarifications on experimental setup**:
  - Computing configuration (laptop-level claim not validated; training time in Table 1 lacks hardware details) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Training procedure for SINDy loss: is the entire time sequence fed as one batch? How many GRU steps? (Reviewer 3).  
  - How is error propagation from discovered latent dynamics measured? (Reviewer 3).  
  - How are basis functions selected for each test case? (Reviewer 3).  
  - How is the “original latent space” obtained in Figure 14? (Reviewer 3).  
- **Citation formatting** is inconsistent (e.g., “NOAA (Reynolds et al., 2002)” vs. “Williams et al. (2024)” on page 6) (Reviewer 3).
