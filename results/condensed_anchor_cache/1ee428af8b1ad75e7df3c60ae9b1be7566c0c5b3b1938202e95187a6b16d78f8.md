## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reformulates autoregressive language models as Generalized Structural Equation Models (GSEMs) using the Gumbel-max trick to separate deterministic logits from exogenous Gumbel noise. It proposes a hindsight Gumbel sampling algorithm that infers latent noise variables from an observed string, then generates a counterfactual string under an intervention while preserving that noise. Experiments on GPT2-XL and LLaMA3-8B with MEMIT, linear steering, and instruction tuning evaluate side effects and effectiveness of existing intervention techniques, revealing unintended semantic changes. The paper aims to enable true counterfactual reasoning per Pearl’s hierarchy.

### Strengths
- **Novel framework:** Reformulating LMs as GSEMs offers a new causal perspective for interpretability, decomposing generation into deterministic computation and stochastic Gumbel noise. (R1, R3)
- **Theoretical contribution:** Propositions 2.1 and 3.1 and Corollary 3.1 formally establish the LM-GSEM equivalence and the hindsight Gumbel sampling algorithm for counterfactual generation. (R3) (One reviewer notes Proposition 2.1 is extremely weak and Proposition 3.1 is not precisely proved in the appendix; they are included despite this criticism.) (R2)
- **Algorithm:** Algorithm 1 provides a principled method for noise inference and counterfactual generation while maintaining sampling consistency across model modifications. (R1, R3)
- **Empirical analysis:** The thorough evaluation of MEMIT, linear steering, and instruction tuning on GPT2-XL and LLaMA3-8B reveals previously unrecognized side effects and provides useful insights for future intervention design. (R1, R4)
- **Presentation:** The paper is well-written, with clean math and sharp English, making complex concepts accessible. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical concerns with GSEM formulation:**
    - The definition of GSEM is not correctly applied: constraints on multiple solutions, intervention effects, and fixed-point issues are ignored. Easier causal models (e.g., dynamic Bayesian networks) would be more appropriate. (R2)
    - Proposition 2.1 is trivial and does not use the primary data of a GSEM (response to interventions); it simply states that any autoregressive distribution can be captured by a GSEM. (R2)
    - Proposition 3.1 is not precisely stated and its proof is not given in the appendix; only a reference to another work is provided. The marginal distribution of \(U_t(\bar{w}_t)\) is claimed to be Gumbel(0,1), but the authors do not fully justify this given that it satisfies a max condition. (R1, R2)
    - The paper misses independence assumptions for the noise variables \(U_t(w)\). Multiple undefined terms and symbols appear (e.g., \(\Pi\), \(\mathbf{b}\)). (R2)
- **Choice of Gumbel noise is not uniquely justified:**
    - The Gumbel-max trick is just one of many ways to sample from a softmax; the paper does not acknowledge alternatives or explain why Gumbel noise yields the “true” counterfactuals. The title’s “True Counterfactual Generation” is misleading. (R2)
    - No investigation of how counterfactuals would differ under other causal models or noise distributions. (R2)
    - The independence of Gumbel noise across timesteps is assumed without discussion of possible dependencies captured by endogenous variables. (R3)
- **Methodological issues with the algorithm:**
    - Infinitely many noise vectors \(U_t\) can satisfy the counterfactual sampling condition (Corollary 3.1), but only one counterfactual string is generated per original. Experiments showing consistency across multiple \(U_t\) samples are missing. (R1)
    - The choice of hindsight Gumbel sampling is presented without theoretical or empirical justification that it is the correct way to infer noise; it is essentially rejection sampling, but the paper introduces non-standard terminology (“truncated probability distribution”). (R1, R2)
    - No discussion of how the method extends to beam search or how temperature affects counterfactual quality. (R4)
- **Empirical evaluation does not validate the proposed counterfactual generation method:**
    - The experiments only use the generated counterfactuals to evaluate existing intervention techniques; no evaluation of the counterfactuals themselves is performed. There is no comparison with baseline counterfactual generation methods (e.g., naive resampling, other causal models) to demonstrate that the proposed method produces “true” or higher-quality counterfactuals. (R2, R4)
    - Missing controlled experiments that compare counterfactual generation with vs. without noise preservation to quantify the reduction in divergence or the stabilization effect. (R3)
    - The paper does not validate that all stochastic elements are captured by the Gumbel noise; compounding effects at longer sequences are assumed to be purely from intervention. No quantification of residual unaccounted noise. (R3)
    - No experiments showing counterfactuals of counterfactuals to assess consistency and reliability. (R1)
- **Evaluation metrics and experimental design:**
    - \(l_{\text{prefix}}\) (length of shared prefix) is overly sensitive to early divergences and may not capture later differences. (R3)
    - \(\text{sim}_{\text{E5}}\) may miss subtle linguistic changes. (R3)
    - Why use prefix length rather than edit distance or word mover’s distance? (R2)
- **Missing discussion:**
    - No detailed comparison with prior work on counterfactual generation or discussion of limitations of existing methods. (R4)
    - Ethical considerations about potential misuse for generating misleading content are absent. (R3)
- **Minority strongly negative perspective:** One reviewer finds that the story is strong but the substance is severely lacking: the theoretical results are weak or misapplied, the evaluation does not involve the proposed method, and the presentation is so polished as to be deceptive. Other reviewers are more positive but still identify significant gaps. (R2)
