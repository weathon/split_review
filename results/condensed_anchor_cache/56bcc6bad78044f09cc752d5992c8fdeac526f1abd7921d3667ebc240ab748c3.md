## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces NAFlow, a normalizing flow framework for modeling and synthesizing realistic sRGB noise. It encodes noise from multiple cameras into a common latent space using Gaussian mixture models (GMM), enabling noise generation without metadata during inference. Multi-scale encoding captures long-range spatial noise correlations. Trained on the SIDD dataset, the generated noise is used to train denoisers, improving denoising performance. An ablation study investigates the contributions of the proposed components.

### Strengths
- The technical contribution is solid. The idea of encoding noise from different cameras into different Gaussians in a common latent space is convincing and works with multiple camera models without retraining (Reviewer 1).  
- The Noise-Aware Sampling (NAS) algorithm eliminates the need for noisy image metadata during inference, which is novel and practical (Reviewer 2).  
- The idea of using normalizing flows to model in-camera setting distributions is interesting, as previous noise modeling mainly uses GANs (Reviewer 3).  
- Results on self-supervised image denoising are good and solid; the method outperforms baselines by a large margin on noise modeling and synthesis (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- The presentation is clear, well-organized, and easy to follow; the appendix provides many visual results showing advantages (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- The ablation study is sufficient to demonstrate the effectiveness of the approaches (Reviewer 3).  
- Learning noise distribution implicitly without metadata is practical for devices where metadata is hard to acquire (Reviewer 4).  
- Comparisons were conducted convincingly, e.g., reporting the best value for NeCA models which require per-camera training (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Noise realism and dataset diversity**: Generated noise shows heavy chromatic noise that is unrealistic for modern cellphone cameras (e.g., Figure 4); the SIDD dataset contains only 10 scenes, limiting diversity and generalization to real-world cameras. This limitation should be acknowledged (Reviewer 1).  
- **Alternative datasets not used**: Reviewer 1 suggests considering datasets from Jaroensri et al. (“Generating Training Data for Denoising Real RGB Images via Camera Pipeline Simulation”) and raw-only datasets (Chen et al., “Learning to See in the Dark”) for more realistic noise distribution.  
- **Need for broader evaluation**: Generalizability to other datasets (DND, PolyU, Nam) is questionable; experiments on a broader range of noisy datasets beyond SIDD are required (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- **Incremental novelty and missing ablations**: The method draws heavily from Flow-sRGB (noise generation with normalizing flows) and NeCA (multi-scale spatial correlation). The effect of adding multi-scale modeling on Flow-sRGB specifically is not ablated; a related ablation experiment should be added (Reviewer 2).  
- **Motivation for normalizing flow vs. other generative models**: Why use NF over GANs, autoencoders, or diffusion models? The paper states NF’s precise likelihood estimation without further elaboration on why the invertible mapping property is specifically advantageous for noise modeling compared to latent space representations of GANs/VAEs (Reviewer 3).  
- **Novelty relative to NoiseFlow unclear**: The paper does not clearly articulate the architectural differences and methodological advancements over NoiseFlow; specific technical contributions beyond applying a generic normalizing flow should be highlighted (Reviewer 3).  
- **Technical limitations of the flow model [Reviewer 3]**:  
  [1] The NF requires a statistic (mean of the normal distribution for each configuration c) as a strong prior; real-world noise often has unknown or varying means, limiting applicability.  
  [2] Training on SIDD may overfit to SIDD-specific noise profiles; evidence of avoiding overfitting and adaptation to other datasets is needed.  
- **Evaluation metrics**: KLD may not be suitable for noise generation; metrics like PSNR Gap (from DANet) or Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) should be used or at least justified (Reviewer 3).  
- **Incomplete experimental comparisons**:  
  [1] Only self-supervised adaptation is shown; performance in fully supervised settings and with SOTA denoisers like Restormer should be evaluated (Reviewer 3).  
  [2] Comparisons with other SOTA noise generation algorithms (DANet, PNGAN, GDANet, CycleISP, C2N) should be included in the main paper, not just in supplementary (Reviewer 3).  
- **Unfair comparison with C2N**: C2N is trained with only noisy images while other methods use paired images (clean/noisy); this training setting should be pointed out in the experiments (Reviewer 4).  
- **Reproducibility**: The meta-train and fast adaptation procedures are complicated; code and models are not provided, making reproducibility difficult to verify (Reviewer 3).  
- **Writing errors**: Inconsistencies such as “NECA-W” and “NAflow” appear (Reviewer 2).  
- **Application relevance question**: Denoisers trained on synthesized noise pairs perform inferior to those trained on original real pairs; the paper should clarify the practical applications of the proposed noise synthesis method (Reviewer 4).  

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (Score 8) finds the ablation sufficient and the results good and solid, while others (Score 6) question noise realism and request more ablations and broader evaluation.
