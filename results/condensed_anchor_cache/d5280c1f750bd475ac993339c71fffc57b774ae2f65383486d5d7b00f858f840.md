## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Radial Basis Operator Network (RBON), an operator network using radial basis functions to learn mappings between function spaces in both time and frequency domains, with adjustments for complex-valued inputs. Experiments include PDEs (wave, Burgers, Euler-Bernoulli beam) and a CO₂-to-temperature forecasting task. Reviewers generally find the core idea interesting but raise major concerns about presentation clarity, insufficient experimental comparisons, lack of methodological detail, and limited theoretical novelty. Two reviewers (2 and 5) gave the highest scores (5) and praise the writing, results against LNO, and theory, while three reviewers (1, 3, 4) gave 3 and emphasize missing baselines, unclear exposition, and weak justification.

### Strengths
- Proposes a new operator network entirely based on radial basis functions, which is an interesting extension of DeepONet by replacing the DNN with an RBF network (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).
- Paper is generally well-written and clearly written; motivation and literature review are good (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- Quantitative results show RBON, NRBON, and F-RBON outperform Laplace Neural Operator (LNO) on the wave, Burgers, and Euler-Bernoulli beam equations (Reviewer 2; but note Reviewer 4 questions why LNO appears better on Burgers – see Weaknesses).
- Experiments include both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) data (Reviewer 4).
- The theoretical component enhances the paper (Reviewer 5; but Reviewer 3 finds the theoretical contribution marginal – see Weaknesses).
- The practical application to CO₂-to-temperature forecasting (global and local) is included (Reviewers 2, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental comparisons and baselines**: The method is compared only to LNO; comparisons with DeepONet, FNO, LSTM, and other standard operator learning or time-series methods are missing (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). The PDE and TSP tasks are not convincing without such baselines (Reviewer 1). The CO₂-to-temperature experiment lacks any baseline (Reviewer 2). The overall experiment set is considered small (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Methodological description is too short and not self-contained**: The paper lacks a clear outline or pseudocode of the training algorithm, details on training parameters, and how to select M and N (Reviewers 1, 3). Figures 1 and 2 are uninformative (Reviewer 1). Most equations about the network are embedded in a theorem, making the method difficult to re-implement (Reviewer 1). The method appears very close to classical RBF networks with a product on top (Reviewer 1).
- **Frequency-domain details missing**: Section 2.3 provides no algorithm for obtaining frequency-domain data, adapting radial basis functions to complex-valued inputs, or handling k-means clustering (which is typically real-valued) for complex numbers (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear figures and explanations**: Figure 1: what does x’ represent? Does the linear transformation L include tunable parameters? (Reviewer 3). Figures 5 and 6 are too compact and lack detail; a zoomed version would be helpful (Reviewer 5). Table 1 lacks explanation for why different algorithms perform variably across different problems (e.g., LNO works better on Burgers? Why some networks perform better on OOD data?) (Reviewer 4).
- **Ambiguities in architecture and training**: The difference between RBON and NRBON is not clarified (Reviewer 2). How many centers are chosen via KNN and by what criteria? Using KNN for center selection may introduce instabilities (Reviewer 4). A sensitivity study for varying the number of k-means centers on model performance is missing (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited experimental analysis on the weather dataset**: The CO₂-to-temperature experiment lacks discussion of other influencing factors; prediction quality is hard to assess due to fluctuation (Reviewer 2). No insight is given on why the model underperformed on local predictions versus global predictions (short-term fluctuations vs. long-term trends) (Reviewer 5). The weather data is simple; more complex time-series benchmarks would be beneficial (Reviewer 5).
- **Theoretical contribution is marginal**: The paper does not provide significant new theory beyond what is already known (Reviewer 3; contrast with Reviewer 5 who found theory enhancing).
- **Missing literature context**: The review of operator learning in both time and frequency domains could be expanded; the advantage of learning simultaneously in both domains vs. separately and combining is not justified (Reviewer 4).
