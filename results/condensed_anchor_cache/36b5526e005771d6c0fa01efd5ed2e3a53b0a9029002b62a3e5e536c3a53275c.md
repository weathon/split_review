## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper establishes that deep residual networks exhibit an implicit regularization toward neural ODEs when trained with gradient flow. It proves that if the network is initialized as a discretization of a neural ODE, then this discretization holds throughout training, both for finite training time (with clipped gradient flow) and as training time tends to infinity (under a Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition). The results assume weight-tying across layers and a linear overparameterization in width (\(m > c_1 n\)). Numerical experiments on synthetic data and CIFAR-10 (with smooth activations) support the theory.

### Strengths
- **Novel direction**: The paper investigates the converse of the usual ResNet‑as‑ODE discretization view—whether a ResNet remains an ODE discretization after training—which is a previously undocumented contribution (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Rigorous theory**: The theoretical analysis is thorough, the derivations are checked (Reviewer 4), and the results are stated clearly (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The proofs overcome simplifying assumptions of prior works (e.g., linear activations) (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well written, easy to follow, with great attention to detail (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reproducibility is addressed through both derivations and experiments (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical relevance**: The settings are realistic: the theory covers nonlinear activations, finite depth, and finite training time, and the experiments on CIFAR-10 with smooth activations confirm that the ResNet dynamics stay close to a neural ODE (Reviewer 2). The results allow transferring ODE generalization bounds to ResNets (Reviewer 2).
- **Impact of initial conditions**: The work shows how initialization as an ODE discretization preserves weight smoothness throughout training, establishing a new link between initial conditions and model behavior (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Weight‑tying assumption**: All weights are tied across layers. It is unclear whether this is strictly necessary for the ODE limit or if alternative initialization schemes could avoid it. The proof may fundamentally rely on this assumption, which limits practical applicability. The authors should clarify necessity and discuss possible relaxations (Reviewer 1).
- **Linear overparameterization \(m > c_1 n\)**: The width must exceed a linear function of depth. This is considered somewhat unreasonable in practice, and the paper does not discuss sensitivity to this parameter, trade‑offs between theoretical guarantees and feasibility, or known mitigation techniques (Reviewer 1).
- **Clipped gradient flow**: The results require gradient clipping, which is rarely used in practice. The impact of the clipping threshold on convergence and final performance is not explored (Reviewer 2).
- **Polyak‑Łojasiewicz (PL) condition**: The infinite‑time limit relies on the PL condition, which may not hold for complex datasets. Justification for its applicability in the considered scenarios is insufficient, and analysis of how violating the condition affects convergence to the ODE limit is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experimental detail (Frobenius norm across layers)**: For the CIFAR‑10 experiment, only a plot of a random coefficient over depth is shown. Showing the difference in Frobenius norm of the weight matrices across layers would give a more complete picture of whether the smoothness assumption holds in practice (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalization to convolutional/sparse architectures**: The analysis focuses on fully‑connected networks. The applicability to convolutional or other sparse scenarios (where ODE parameter matrices \(A, V, W\) would have many fixed zeros) is not addressed. Figure 3 suggests Theorem 4 might extend to sparse settings, but this is not confirmed (Reviewer 4, Q1).  
- **Lipschitz constant behavior under random initialization**: The paper does not explain why the Lipschitz constant remains small during optimization when weights are initialized randomly, as observed in Figure 3. This appears to go beyond a literal application of Theorem 4 (Reviewer 4, Q2).
- **Out‑of‑distribution robustness**: The potential benefit of a smaller Lipschitz constant for out‑of‑distribution recognition is not discussed. The paper also does not clarify how the derivation would generalize to networks with more than two‑layer perceptrons as residuals (Reviewer 4, Q3).
- **Layer normalization**: Whether the approach can incorporate layer normalization is not considered (Reviewer 1, question).
