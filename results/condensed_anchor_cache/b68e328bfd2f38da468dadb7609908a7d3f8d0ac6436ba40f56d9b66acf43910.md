## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a novel causal discovery framework for stochastic processes modeled by stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Key contributions include: (i) conditional independence (CI) constraints over coordinate processes on selected intervals that are Markov with respect to an acyclic dependence graph; (ii) a sound and complete causal discovery algorithm recovering ancestral graphs from observational data (fully or partially observed); (iii) a consistent signature kernel-based CI test (SigKer) to infer these constraints from irregularly sampled, continuous-time data. Extensive benchmarks on synthetic SDE models and a real-world pairs-trading case study show that the proposed method outperforms several existing causal discovery baselines (e.g., PCMCI, tsFCI) in small graphs, especially under incomplete data and path-dependence, without heavy hyperparameter tuning.

All reviewers agree the paper is well-motivated, theoretically rigorous, and presents a practical CI test. However, concerns are raised about the restrictiveness of assumptions (stationarity, acyclicity), the limited real-world validation for the causal discovery algorithm itself, the handling of diffusion-dependent SDEs, and the breadth of baselines and evaluation metrics. One reviewer (score 6) is most critical, citing overstated claims and insufficient comparisons; another (score 10) is enthusiastic but notes the lack of real-world CD experiments. The remaining two (scores 8) are supportive but identify specific methodological and presentation weaknesses.

### Strengths

- Solid framework for causal inference in continuous-time SDEs, overcoming limitations of traditional discrete-time models.
- Novel signature kernel CI test for path sequences; suited for path-dependent random variables.
- Strong performance with incomplete data, irregularly sampled time series, and path-dependence.
- Real-world demonstration (pairs trading) validates the CI test’s practical effectiveness.
- Method not heavily dependent on hyperparameter tuning.
- Well-written, with thorough theoretical proofs and careful attention to reproducibility.
- Extensive synthetic experiments showing consistent superiority over several baselines (PCMCI, tsFCI, etc.).
- The paper earns its place as a valuable tool for causal discovery in dynamical systems.

### Weaknesses

- **Restrictive assumptions**: The method assumes stationarity and acyclicity, limiting applicability to systems with time-varying causal relationships or feedback loops. The acyclic assumption is more restrictive than in many time-series causal discovery methods (e.g., PCMCI allows cycles across lags); pairs of causal relations across intervals (e.g., X_{[0,s]}^i → X_{[s,s+h]}^j and later X_{[s+h,s+2h]}^j → X_{[s+2h,s+3h]}^i) can effectively create cycles that the algorithm forbids. The paper does not fully discuss the additional limitations introduced by this assumption compared to discrete-time methods. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of real-world experiments for the causal discovery algorithm**: Only the CI test is validated on real data (pairs trading); the main contribution (causal discovery) is evaluated solely on synthetic data. The paper could use real-world benchmarks such as the industrial system dataset by Mogensen et al. (2023) or the Causal Chambers (wind tunnel) by Gamella et al. (2024), where ground-truth causal graphs are available. (Reviewer 3)
- **Overstated claims and incomplete comparisons**: The claim that the algorithm “far exceeds” existing methods is not fully justified. Many prior algorithms already handle confounders (LPCMCI, tsFCI), non-stationarity (CD-NOD), and periodic patterns (PCMCI_Ω). The baselines used are limited to a small set; no metrics like F1, precision, or recall are reported (only SHD). This limits a nuanced understanding of performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Identifiability issues in diffusion-dependent SDEs**: The paper’s claims about handling diffusion-dependence are overstated. When the diffusion coefficient depends on the state variable X_t (driving noise), the causal graph may not be identifiable from observational data alone (Hansen & Sokol 2014, Example 5.5). The lower performance reported in Table 1 for diffusion-dependent settings likely reflects this fundamental limitation. The paper does not address this or propose alternatives (e.g., generator identification as in Wang et al. 2024). (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited analysis of CI oracle errors**: The paper does not thoroughly explore how errors in the conditional independence test (Type I/II) propagate through the causal discovery algorithm. In practice, these tests are based on finite samples and are prone to errors; the impact on final graph accuracy is not discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- **No computational complexity or runtime analysis**: The paper omits any discussion of running time or scalability, which is critical for practical deployment. (Reviewer 2)
- **Accessibility of mathematical exposition**: Section 2 on signature kernels is mathematically dense and assumes familiarity with rough path theory. A more gradual introduction with illustrative examples of how the signature transform captures path features would benefit readers. A summary table of notations would also help. (Reviewer 4)
- **Experimental setup concerns**: In synthetic experiments, self-loop parameters are set small (a_ii ~ U([-0.5,0.5])) while cross-variable causal effects are set larger (a_ij ~ U([-2,-1]∪[1,2])); practical scenarios may not exhibit such clear separation. It is also unclear whether errors for self-loops are counted in the reported SHD. (Reviewer 4)
- **Generality of the CI test**: It is not established whether the proposed signature kernel CI test works beyond the specific SDE setting described, e.g., for general time-series data. (Reviewer 2)
- **Paper length**: The appendix constitutes more than two-thirds of the page limit; the paper might be more suitable for a journal venue. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unexplained methodological details**: 
  - The algorithm’s use of two copies of vertices (V_0, V_1) selected intervals essentially subsamples the process; the resulting causal graph is different from the full time causal graph targeted by many prior algorithms, and the effect of interval selection on the inferred graph is not fully discussed. (Reviewer 2)
  - The method to obtain bi-directed edges (as in Figure 4) is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
  - It is unclear whether PC/FCI and the CI tests (KCIPT, SDCIT) require IID samples, given the non-IID nature of path segments. (Reviewer 2)
  - Power analysis for the conditional test across different conditioning sets is missing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor presentation issues**: Several typos and stylistic inconsistencies (e.g., hyphens instead of em dashes, double parentheses in citations). (Reviewer 3)
