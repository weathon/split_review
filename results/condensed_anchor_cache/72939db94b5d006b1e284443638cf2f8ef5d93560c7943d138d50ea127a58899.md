## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces EfficientDM, a data-free, quantization-aware fine-tuning framework for low-bit diffusion models. It proposes Quantization-Aware Low-Rank Adapters (QALoRA) that can be merged with quantized weights, a scale-aware optimization for LoRA, and temporal learned step-size quantization (TLSQ) for handling activation variations across denoising steps. Using knowledge distillation from the full-precision model, the method achieves QAT-level performance (e.g., FID 6.17 for W4A4 on ImageNet 256×256) with PTQ-like efficiency (16.2× faster than QAT, ~10 GPU hours).

### Strengths
- The paper achieves strong quantization performance at low bit‑widths (W4A4, W2A8) with efficient fine‑tuning, significantly outperforming PTQ while matching QAT performance.
- QALoRA is novel among quantization‑aware adapters; it merges with quantized weights and avoids extra floating‑point computation during inference (unlike QLoRA).
- The data‑free distillation eliminates the need for training data, and the method demonstrates promising results across various bit‑widths (W2, W4A4, W8A8, etc.) on CIFAR‑10, LSUN, and ImageNet.
- Actual speedup with CUTLASS is reported.
- The paper is clearly written, well‑organized, and includes comprehensive supplementary visualizations that enhance credibility (reviewers R2, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental inconsistency:** In Table 2, the W4A4 model (FID 6.17) is much better than the FP model (FID 11.28), and the W8A8 model (FID 11.38) is comparable—both outcomes are counterintuitive and lack explanation. The gap between these FID values and the 3.60 reported in the original LDM paper is also not addressed. Meanwhile, in Appendix A (unconditional LSUN), the W4A4 FID is worse than FP. (R1)
- **Novelty concerns:** Several reviewers (R1, R3, R4) note that the main components (LoRA, distillation, LSQ scale optimization) are combinations of existing works. R4 specifically argues that TLSQ is a simple temporal extension of LSQ and not sufficiently novel as a main contribution. In contrast, R2 considers QALoRA novel.
- **Clarity of the distillation process:** The method to obtain \(\mathbf{x}_t\) in Eq. (7) is not properly described—it is unclear whether \(\mathbf{x}_t\) is sampled from \(\mathcal{N}\) and then processed by the FP model, or generated otherwise. (R1, R3)
- **TLSQ analysis incomplete:** The paper does not show learned activation scale factors across time steps or analyze how they vary per layer/dataset. It also lacks a comparison with simpler alternatives (e.g., using calibration data or run‑time dynamic quantization) to justify the need for LSQ. (R3)
- **TLSQ temporal interpolation:** While the method interpolates learned scales for different numbers of sampling steps, the paper does not test whether this works well when using fewer steps during inference. (R3)
- **Lack of text‑to‑image evaluation:** The method is not evaluated on recent text‑to‑image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion), which would strengthen practical relevance. (R2)
- **Incomplete ablation on LoRA formulation:** The paper does not provide the gradient formulation of LoRA weights to explain why scale‑aware optimization is needed to address ineffective learning. (R2)
- **Figure/notation inconsistency:** In Figure 2, the notation for scale‑aware optimization does not match Eq. (8). (R2)
- **Missing baseline comparison:** It remains unclear whether other quantization methods would also benefit from LoRA and distillation, which are standard techniques. (R4)
- **QALoRA scope:** The paper does not specify whether QALoRA is applied to all weight types (convolutions and attention layers). (R3)
- **Parameter optimization details:** It is unclear whether the quantization parameters (\(l, u, s\)) in Eq. (3) are all trainable and whether optimization follows LSQ or LSQ+ (R1). Also, Table 3 does not confirm whether QALoRA uses LSQ to fine‑tune the adapter and step‑size parameters. (R1)
- **Limited discussion of limitations:** The current discussion of limitations is not specific; the authors should raise more concrete questions for future work. (R3)
