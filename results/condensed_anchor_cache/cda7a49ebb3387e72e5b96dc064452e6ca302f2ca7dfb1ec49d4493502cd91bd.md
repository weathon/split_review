## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers appreciate the theoretical grounding (Nash equilibrium, game-theoretic formulation, convergence guarantee) and strong empirical results of SPPO on multiple benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, Arena-Hard, Open LLM Leaderboard). All four reviewers assign a score of 6, with moderate to high confidence (3–4). However, they raise consistent concerns about missing comparisons (conventional RLHF, other game-theoretic methods, strong offline methods), limited experimental scope (only two 7–8B models, one preference model PairRM, one dataset), and insufficient analysis of training dynamics, hyperparameter sensitivity, and algorithmic details (e.g., iteration/epoch definitions, K² preference model calls). Some specific questions remain about length bias, compatibility with other reward models, and the role of PairRM selection.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical foundation**: SPPO casts RLHF as a constant-sum two-player game and provides a proof of convergence to the Nash equilibrium (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Relation to other methods**: Theoretical connections are drawn to policy gradient and token-level Q* learning, which strengthens validity (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Practical simplicity**: The proposed loss function is simple and motivated by theory, avoiding pairwise comparisons (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Strong empirical performance**: SPPO achieves state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks (e.g., 28.53% length-controlled win-rate against GPT-4-Turbo on AlpacaEval 2.0 with Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2; 38.77% with Llama-3-8B-Instruct), outperforming iterative DPO and IPO (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Monotonic improvement**: Training iterations lead to consistent improvements, unlike DPO/IPO which degrade in later iterations (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Broad evaluation**: The algorithm is tested on AlpacaEval 2.0, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench, and Open LLM Leaderboard (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with conventional RLHF**: The paper does not compare SPPO to standard PPO with a Bradley-Terry reward model, making it hard to assess practical advantages over established techniques (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- **Missing comparisons with other game-theory-based methods**: No comparison with Nash-MD, Nash-EMA, DNO, or Nash Preference Optimization (NPO), which also target Nash equilibria (Reviewers 2, 4). NPO is explicitly named as a missing baseline (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient comparison with strong offline/online methods**: SimPO (Reviewer 1), Snorkel-Mistral-PairRM-DPO (Reviewer 3), and other RL-based methods are not compared. The paper does not clarify whether the gap between Snorkel-Mistral-PairRM-DPO and SPPO is due solely to the loss function (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited experimental scope**: Experiments are restricted to two models of similar size (7–8B), one dataset (UltraFeedback), and one preference model (PairRM). Generalizability to other model sizes, tasks, and preference models is unclear (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Dependence on PairRM**: The preference model is critical, but only PairRM (0.4B) is tested. It is not shown how SPPO performs with other RMs (e.g., UltraRM-13B) or whether the preference model should be updated jointly with the policy (Reviewer 3).  
- **Lack of analysis of hyperparameter η (β)**: The relationship between η and KL divergence from the base policy is not explored. Results would be more convincing if presented as a Pareto front, as done in Rame et al., because comparing algorithms at different KL divergences can be misleading (Reviewer 4).  
- **Insufficient training dynamics analysis**: The paper does not analyze how policy changes over iterations, win rate against the reference model, loss trends, or sensitivity to number of iterations/epochs/learning rate (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unclear terminology**: “Iteration,” “round,” and “epoch” are not clearly separated, making the exact training procedure difficult to follow. Hyperparameters for DPO/IPO should be explicitly stated per iteration (Reviewer 1).  
- **Computational cost**: The annotation step in Algorithm 1 requires K² calls to the preference model, which could be time-consuming (Reviewer 3).  
- **Length bias concern**: Since SPPO uses single responses and the sum of log ratios, length variance may induce spurious length biases, similar to DPO. Table 1 shows longer generations, raising this concern (Reviewer 1).  
- **Decline of baselines**: DPO and IPO worsen in iterations 2 and 3; SPPO improves. The authors are asked to explain why, and why Mistral-7B-DPO/IPO underperform the base policy on GSM8k (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- **Equation clarification**: The equality between equations 3.9 and 3.10 may be up to a constant \(\frac{2}{\eta}\); this should be clarified (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing ablation on response selection**: In SPPO, the highest/lowest PairRM scores are selected as winning/losing responses. Is this setting also used for DPO and IPO? If so, the only difference is the loss function (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing comparison with win-rate-based RL updates**: Since SPPO computes K² preference scores convertible to win rates, a comparison with PPO updates using win rate (like SPO) should be included (Reviewer 3).
