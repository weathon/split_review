## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Stochastic Downsampling (SD), a Monte Carlo-based downsampling layer that replaces standard downsampling (e.g., strided convolution) to defend against adversarial attacks. It combines bilinear interpolation with Monte Carlo integration to introduce randomness, aiming to disrupt adversarial gradients while preserving clean accuracy. The authors claim it is non-parametric, requires only a few epochs of fine-tuning, and is easy to integrate. Reviewers gave scores of 3, 3, 3, and 1. The first three reviewers found the idea interesting but identified major gaps in evaluation and comparison; the fourth reviewer (score 1) was much more negative, considering the experiments insufficient and the method’s usefulness over adversarial training questionable.

### Strengths
- The method is lightweight and computationally efficient: it introduces no additional learnable parameters, requires as few as five epochs of fine-tuning, and can be integrated into existing architectures (e.g., ConvNeXt-tiny, ResNet18) with minimal cost. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Stochastic downsampling adds diversity to adversarial defense techniques by incorporating randomness directly into feature map transformations. (R3)
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art defenses.** The paper compares only against weak baselines (e.g., AddNoise, BlurPooling, ASAP) and not against leading robust models from RobustBench. For example, on ImageNet-1k with ConvNeXt-B, SD achieves only 3.32% AutoAttack accuracy, while robust models on RobustBench reach up to 55.82%. (R1, R3) Adversarial training methods such as PGD-AT and TRADES are completely omitted; their inclusion is needed to understand clean-accuracy/robustness/computational trade-offs. (R2, R4) Moreover, the improvement over AddNoise (std=0.75) is marginal or sometimes negative: in Table 3, AddNoise gives far better robust accuracy with low clean-accuracy drop, yet in Table 4 AddNoise appears weaker—this inconsistency requires explanation. (R4)
- **Missing evaluation against adaptive attacks.** The authors claim SD goes beyond gradient obfuscation, but only test against the non-gradient Square Attack. To rule out gradient obfuscation, evaluations with EOT (with hyperparameters recommended in Tramer et al.) and Backward Pass Differentiable Approximation (BPDA) are essential. (R2, R3) Additionally, adversarial examples crafted by Feature Adversaries with EOT should be tested to see if samples robust to noise withstand SD. (R2)
- **Incomplete reporting and inconsistencies.** Accuracy metrics are reported inconsistently: some tables include mean±std, others omit standard deviation (e.g., Table 1 vs. Table 2 for ImageNet100). Trade-off metrics (e.g., mCE, drop in i.i.d. accuracy) are not clearly defined or consistently used across experiments. (R1) Performance is not comprehensively displayed for all mentioned datasets. (R3)
- **Limited experimental validation.** Table 1 only shows results for one model (e.g., ConvNeXt-tiny) without broader comparisons. Table 2, though including more architectures, does not compare against other defenses. Table 3 shows SD is weaker than AddNoise (std=0.75) on clean and robust accuracy. (R4) Evaluations are conducted on smaller datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100), raising scalability concerns for larger, more complex datasets like full ImageNet-1k. (R1)
- **Sensitivity and parameter tuning.** The method requires careful tuning of the number of samples per pixel (SPP). No analysis is provided on how SPP affects the robustness-accuracy trade-off; curiously, increasing SPP sometimes reduces robust accuracy (Table 5, as noted by R4). This needs explanation and a sensitivity study. (R1, R4)
- **Missing related work on stochastic defenses.** The paper does not discuss or compare with prior stochastic defense methods (e.g., Liu et al., “Towards robust neural networks via random self-ensemble”; Wang et al., “AdvMS” and “Hierarchical random switching”). This omission leaves important context for positioning the contribution. (R1)
- **Questions about transfer and gradient direction.** Table 4 shows that an attack designed on a model without SD performs poorly on the same model with SD (after fine-tuning), which is surprising if SD only changes gradient direction; the authors should explain why transfer fails. (R4)
- **Limited applicability.** SD cannot be applied to robustify transformer networks (e.g., Vision Transformers) without downsampling layers, unlike adversarial training. (R4) The paper tests only on ConvNeXt-tiny, ResNet18, etc.; generalization to models that do not use downsampling layers is unclear. (R1)
- **Minor issues:** The term “Addnoise” is used both for a baseline and for an ablation, causing ambiguity. (R4) Network architectures are not introduced in Section 4.2 and references are missing. (R4) The ASAP and Blurpooling benchmarks show unexpectedly low robustness; possible implementation issues should be checked. (R2)
