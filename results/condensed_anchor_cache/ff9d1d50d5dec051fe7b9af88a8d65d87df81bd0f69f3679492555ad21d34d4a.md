## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes REFINE, an inversion-free backdoor defense based on model reprogramming. It consists of a trainable input transformation module and an output remapping module, plus cross-entropy and supervised contrastive losses. The method is evaluated only on ResNet-18 across several datasets and attacks. Reviewers generally agree the idea is novel but raise substantial concerns about experimental scope, theoretical grounding, and fair comparison with prior work.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach**: The integration of model reprogramming (changing only input/output, not model parameters) to neutralize backdoors without trigger inversion is considered bold, imaginative, and worth exploring (R2, R4). One reviewer specifically notes the threat model and proposed solution are well-set (R4).
- **Theoretical analysis**: R1 and R2 praise the theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) that quantifies how domain transformations bound defense performance and explains the trade-off limitation in prior transformation-based defenses. However, this point is disputed by other reviewers (see Weaknesses).
- **Extensive experiments and ablation studies**: R1 and R3 highlight the thorough experiments and ablation studies that validate each component's contribution (e.g., the impact of the output remapping, contrastive loss).
- **Practical data efficiency**: R4 notes that the method works with only a small amount of unlabeled data, and ablation experiments confirm minimal data requirements, adding practicality.
- **Well-written (minority opinion)**: R4 finds the paper well-written and easy to follow even for someone unfamiliar with model reprogramming. (Note: Other reviewers found the presentation poor—see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient model architectures**: All four reviewers agree that experiments are conducted **only on ResNet-18**. The paper lacks evaluations on more complex or structurally diverse models such as ResNet-50, VGG16, DenseNet, or InceptionV3 (R1, R2, R3, R4). This severely limits generality.
- **Theoretical support is incomplete or disputed**:  
  - R4 argues that Formula 1 is insufficient: it explains only half the story, does not tie the performance guarantee for clean samples back to the theory, and still holds under model reprogramming, so it does not fundamentally support the claim.  
  - R3 questions whether REFINE actually overcomes the utility–effectiveness trade-off: in experiments, the BA with REFINE is not the same as the original model, indicating the same problem persists.  
  - R2 and R1 consider the theoretical analysis a strength, creating a clear disagreement. The paper does not resolve this tension.
- **Unfair or incomplete comparisons with prior work**:  
  - R3 notes that ShrinkPad (used as a baseline) is not the best defense from that paper; the chosen BTI-DBF and other baselines may not represent the state of the art.  
  - R3 and R4 both question the experimental results for BTI-DBF—specifically, why it cannot reconstruct even the basic BadNet trigger, contradicting the original paper’s demonstrations.  
  - R3 lists missing SOTA works (e.g., [1], [2]—explicit references omitted in the reviews) and calls for explanation of why results differ.  
  - R4 seriously questions the validity of the BTI-DBF experiment.  
  - R2 and R1 complain that the paper spends too much time contrasting with BTI methods when REFINE itself is an input-transformation method; the categorization of defenses is not sound (R3: BTI-based defenses do not work only in that one way).
- **Assumptions about clean/unlabeled data may be impractical**:  
  - R1 notes that requiring **20% clean data** may be unrealistic in many real-world scenarios.  
  - R2 asks for clarification on how to acquire an unlabeled dataset i.i.d. with the training set, and warns that distribution shift could affect performance.
- **Computational cost**: R1 points out that training a U-Net for the transformation module incurs significant computational overhead, which is a practical limitation.
- **Adaptive attack setup is flawed**: R4 argues that the adaptive attack setup is unreasonable because the attacker is assumed to know the defender’s hard-coded choices, but the paper disallows the defender from making real-time adjustments (a strategy directly targeting adaptive attacks). Additionally, R4 sees no clear trade-off between the adaptive loss and the backdoor loss.
- **Unclear motivation and connection to prior analyses**:  
  - R1 states the connection between the motivation (critique of BTI) and the proposed method is not close; the paper does not discuss whether/how BTI purification alters the image domain from a domain-based perspective.  
  - R2 notes the paper is hard to read due to many long sentences (e.g., the sentence starting "Additionally, we treat ... the transformation" on Page 4).  
  - R2 asks that “Pad Size” and “Mask Ratio” be defined before first use.  
  - R2 also requests a discussion on why traditional model reprogramming (Section 4.2.1) is insufficient, and a more detailed analysis of feature distance changes in the input transformation module.
- **Missing examples and deeper insights**: R4 asks for more transformed sample images, questioning whether samples from the same class share visual similarities or contain almost no human-recognizable information, and invites the authors to offer higher-level insights about the significance of these transformations.
- **Minor technical questions**:  
  - R1: In Figure 3, why did the color of the part of the image outside the trigger also change?  
  - R2: Two sentences in Section 1 seem to contradict each other regarding “prior information” vs. “prior knowledge”; the dataset used for the limitation analysis in Section 3.2 is not specified.  
  - R2: The paper focuses on dirty-image attacks; would it work on clean-image attacks without trigger features?  
  - R3: The soundness of the limitation study should be improved; the reasonability of the defense categories needs to be discussed.
