## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IDEA (Invariant Causal Defense), a framework that learns causal features with strong label predictability and invariant predictability across attacks, aiming to improve GNN adversarial robustness against both evasion and poisoning attacks. The method derives node-based and structure-based invariance objectives from an information-theoretic perspective and includes theoretical proofs. Experiments on five datasets under five attack types show state-of-the-art performance. The paper also includes ablation studies. **Reviewer 3 (score 8) is notably more positive, praising the complete theoretical basis and experimental generalization, while Reviewer 1, 2, and 4 (scores 5–6) raise concerns about causal justification, threat model, experimental completeness, and generalization to unseen attacks.** (Reviewer 1 and 4 updated their scores to 6 after rebuttal.)

### Strengths
- Presents a new perspective using causal inference for graph adversarial defense (R1, R3).
- Achieves significant improvement over existing defenses on both evasion and poisoning settings (R1).
- Includes complete theoretical derivations and provable defense guarantees (R2, R3).
- Overall experimental methodology is sound, with extensive experiments and an ablation study (R2, R4).
- Interesting premise and motivation for leveraging causal features (R4).
- Good presentation overall (R4); note that other reviewers noted presentation issues (see Weaknesses).
- Novel approach to learning invariant features across attack domains (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Causal graph justification and assumptions (R1, R4):**  
  The assumed causal structure (causal features directly influence label and input graph) is not rigorously justified. It is unclear whether the graph is derived automatically or based on an assumption; potential confounders influencing both causal features and labels are not addressed. The paper also does not explain why the learned features (encoder output H) are causal rather than merely invariant to the attack; a theoretical link between invariance and causality is missing.

- **Threat model and adaptive attacks (R1):**  
  The defense includes a detector neural network, but none of the tested attacks have knowledge of this detector. Improvement may be an artifact of attacker ignorance rather than inherent robustness. The paper does not evaluate against adaptive/white-box attacks that are aware of the full defense (including the detector), making current comparisons to other defenses potentially unfair. The need for such adaptive evaluation is a significant concern.

- **Missing comprehensive attack evaluation (R3):**  
  Tables 1 and 2 only show results for one poisoning attack and one evasion attack; the other three attack types mentioned in the paper are not reported. Without full results across all five attacks, the claimed state-of-the-art performance and generalization are not fully validated.

- **Insufficient baseline comparisons and architectural analysis (R2):**  
  The contribution of causality to improved defense is not isolated. Performance should be compared against a wider range of baselines, including those not focusing on causality. Additionally, the paper does not explore inherent learning-capability differences between GNN architectures, which may confound conclusions (e.g., significance of Figure 1(b) is unclear).

- **Presentation, notation, and figure issues (R1, R3, R4):**  
  - Node j in Figure 2 should be Node k.  
  - Z in Section 3.2.1 is undefined; \((.)_\mathcal{N}\) is never defined.  
  - Figure 3 (overall framework) is shown but not introduced in the text.  
  - Figure 5 contains an empty graph.  
  - No clear symbol table or glossary; many symbols are mixed, hindering understanding of mathematical derivations.  
  - The overall block diagram is rough, lacking sufficient detail to understand architecture and data flow (R3).  
  - Minor language issues throughout (R4).

- **Code availability (R2):**  
  The code link (https://anonymous.4open.science/r/IDEA_repo-666B) is expired, preventing validation of empirical results and reproducibility.

- **Generalization to unseen attacks (R4):**  
  It is unclear how the method generalizes to attack types not seen during training. The reliance on specific attack types in training raises concerns about robustness against novel attack strategies; no analysis of cross-attack generalization is provided.

- **Lack of discussion of related purification methods (R3):**  
  The paper does not discuss or compare with purification methods that could be extended to graphs (e.g., Shi et al. 2021, Liao et al. 2018, Zhou et al. 2021, Naseer et al. 2020). It is unclear whether those methods face the same limitations that IDEA claims to overcome.
