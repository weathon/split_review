## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LatentQA, a task to interpret LLM activations by answering open-ended natural language questions about them, and proposes Latent Interpretation Tuning (LIT), which fine-tunes a decoder LLM on a dataset of over 1M activation-question-answer pairs. The decoder is used for reading latent information (e.g., relational knowledge, system prompt personas), controlling behavior (e.g., debiasing, sentiment steering), and revealing harmful capabilities. Experiments compare LIT against training-free methods like Patchscope, DExpert, and RepE. Reviewers find the problem novel and the dataset valuable, but raise significant concerns about fairness of comparisons, generalization, methodological clarity, and interpretability claims. Scores: 5,6,5,5.

### Strengths
- The problem of decoding activations directly into natural language is novel, important, and user-friendly compared to abstract interpretability outputs. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The creation of a large-scale, dedicated dataset (LatentQA with 1.2M data points) is a valuable resource for the community. [R1, R2]
- The method covers a diverse range of applications: extracting relational knowledge, decoding personas, controllable sentiment generation, debiasing, and eliciting harmful capabilities. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The decoder provides a differentiable loss that enables both interpretation and control of model behavior, a unique combination. [R3, R4]
- The approach shows promising results across these applications. [R2]
- It offers a potential tool for safety auditing by revealing harmful model behaviors. [R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparisons with training-free baselines:** LIT is a trained method (fine-tuned on 1.2M examples) while many baselines (Patchscope, DExpert, RepE) are training-free. Multiple reviewers consider this comparison unfair and state the performance gains may be overstated. Training-based baselines should be included or latency/training cost compared. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Lack of cross-model generalization:** The decoder is trained only on LLaMA-3.1-8B activations; it is unclear if it can interpret activations from a different model (e.g., Mistral). Without this, it may simply overfit to one model’s representations rather than learning generalizable features. [R2, R3]
- **Unclear link to interpretability:** The paper claims interpretability, but the applications focus on manipulating behavior and reading specific attributes rather than explaining internal mechanisms. It is unclear how this approach deepens understanding of model internals. [R2]
- **Questionable activation patching mechanism:** LIT replaces layer 0 activations of the decoder with layer 15 activations from the target model. Reviewers find this counterintuitive—layer 15 activations already incorporate outputs from layers 0–14 and may be numerically incompatible with the decoder’s layer 0 distribution. The justification and analysis for this choice are insufficient. [R3, R1, R2, R4]
- **Method is model-specific and data-hungry:** Each new target model likely requires collecting a new 1.2M-size dataset and re-training, which is a heavy cost. Generalization to unseen personas or tasks (zero-shot) is not evaluated. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Poor writing and clarity:** The paper is hard to follow; key terms like “activation” are not defined early. Crucial steps (dialog definition, STEER procedure, LoRRA finetuning) are not clearly explained. A better pipeline diagram is needed. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Missing ablation on layer choice:** Activations are taken from layer 15 and injected at layer 0, but no ablation study tests other layers. Intuitively, later layers may be more informative. [R2, R4]
- **Missing comparisons with task-specific methods:** Debiasing is not compared against a dedicated debiasing activation-steering method (e.g., arXiv:2406.03631). [R3]
- **LIT performs worst on the “Generate Positive” control task** in Section 5.2, which weakens the claim of effectiveness for sentiment control. [R3]
- **Potential data leakage from WikiText-103 contextualization:** When extracting phrases from WikiText-103 for the relational knowledge task, the same dataset may inadvertently expose object entities, and it is unclear if baselines also use this contextualization. [R2]
- **Synthetic dataset concerns:** The LatentQA dataset is entirely generated by GPT-4, which may introduce biases or noise. More details on construction and diversity are needed. [R4]
- **Only one LLM tested:** Experiments use only LLaMA-3.1-8B; results may not generalize to other architectures or scales. [R1]
- **Limited control evaluation:** Control experiments focus on persona and sentiment; other types (e.g., topic control) are not explored. [R1]
