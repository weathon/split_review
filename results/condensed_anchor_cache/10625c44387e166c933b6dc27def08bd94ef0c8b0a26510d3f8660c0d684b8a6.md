## Merged Review

### Summary
VFDiff is an SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model for structure-based drug design (SBDD) guided by vector fields from VFNet, a pretrained model that predicts atomic positions and Vina Scores from perturbed data. It incorporates three components: energy-planning (adjusting diffusion trajectories based on energy gradients), force-guiding (refining molecular generation), and position-tuning (improving sampling accuracy). Experiments on CrossDocked2020 show state-of-the-art Vina scores (Avg. -7.37) and competitive molecular properties/diversity. Two reviewers (R1, R2) rated the paper 5, citing major concerns about novelty overlap with IPDiff, incomplete evaluation, and presentation issues; one reviewer (R3) was more positive (8), commending the explicit energy complementarity innovation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1).
- The position-tuning mechanism is interesting and original (R2).
- VFDiff explicitly considers energy complementarity between protein and ligand during the diffusion process, an innovation that is commendable (R3).
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art binding affinity (Avg. Vina Score -7.37) on CrossDocked2020, with competitive molecular properties and diversity (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and similarity to IPDiff**  
   - Energy-planning and force-guiding are adopted from previous work (IPDiff) without clearly distinguishing contributions (R1).  
   - The paper appears very similar to IPDiff; many parts (text, equations, figures, overall structure) are almost copy-pasted (R2).  
   - Technical mechanisms such as prior-shifting and energy-planning are claimed as contributions but are taken from IPDiff; the only novel component appears to be position-tuning (R2).  

2. **Unclear novelty attribution and promotional language**  
   - The authors do not explicitly point out their unique contributions relative to IPDiff (R1). Section 4.2 is questioned about what is new compared to IPDiff (R1).  
   - Overly promotional language reduces objectivity; actual contributions are fewer than claimed (contributions 2 and 3 are details of the method already presented) (R2).  

3. **Presentation and clarity issues**  
   - Unclear language, grammar errors, and vague terminology (e.g., "spatial complementarity," "conditional prior") (R2).  
   - Explanation of position-tuning (end of Section 4.1) is abrupt and unclear; the relation between position-tuning loss (4) and VFNet training losses (2) is not explained (R2).  
   - Motivation for design choices, such as the scaling coefficient η ∼ U(0, 0.5) for noise added to coordinates in VFNet training, is not justified (R1, R2).  
   - Equation and algorithm details are confusing: difference between "p" in eq6 and "q" in eq7 (R2); difference between ε₁ and ε₀ in Algorithm 1 (R2); multiplication by 10 in position-tuning in Algorithm 2 (R2).  

4. **Insufficient evaluation of generated molecules**  
   - Basic sanity checks (e.g., PoseBuster filters for validity, connectivity, correct geometry, steric clashes) are missing (R2).  
   - The paper does not provide enough molecular examples to demonstrate that generated molecules avoid difficult-to-synthesize substructures and protein-ligand collisions (R3).  
   - In Figure 10, conformations of generated molecules appear worse than the reference ligands; authors should provide Jensen-Shannon divergences of bond angle distributions (R1).  
   - Discussion of QED and SA metrics is inconsistent; it would be more informative to report distributional distances (JS or Wasserstein) between training and sampled molecules for a wider range of metrics (bond angles, atom types, property approximators like QED, SA, logP) (R2).  

5. **Missing efficiency analysis**  
   - Generation efficiency of VFDiff compared to baseline methods should be reported (R1, R3).  

6. **Technical questions about VFNet and position-tuning**  
   - When predicting forces, VFNet requires specific molecular information (atom types, bond types), but during sampling VFDiff only clearly provides these at the final step; clarification is needed on how accurate force and position-tuning information is obtained without the full molecular graph in advance (R3).  
   - The authors should experimentally validate the hypothesis about position-tuning (lines 223-225) and show how the average error between ĥX_{0|t} and X₀ changes before and after position-tuning as t approaches 0 (R2).  

7. **Reproducibility**  
   - Code was not provided during the review period, hindering independent verification of results and assessment of synthesis and atomic collision issues (R3).  

8. **Missing technical details**  
   - Details on bond assignment, number of molecules sampled per input, pocket definition, and pocket size distribution are needed (R2).
