## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CtrlSynth, a controllable image-text synthesis pipeline for data-efficient multimodal learning. It decomposes visual semantics into basic elements (objects, attributes, relations) using a vision tagging model, leverages an LLM to generate new captions from the tags and original text, and uses a diffusion model to synthesize new images. The pipeline is training-free, modular, and designed to improve CLIP performance by augmenting training data. Experiments on CC3M, CC12M, and 31 downstream tasks (zero-shot classification, retrieval, compositional reasoning, long-tail recognition) show improvements over baseline CLIP.

### Strengths
- Interesting approach to distill knowledge from pretrained LLMs and diffusion models into synthetic training data for CLIP, effectively injecting new information.
- Addresses a critical challenge: noise and misalignment in large-scale image-text datasets.
- Introduces an innovative fine-grained control mechanism via decomposition and recomposition of visual elements; the pipeline is training-free and flexible, allowing integration with different generative models.
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate significant performance gains across diverse vision and vision-language tasks (zero-shot classification, retrieval, compositional reasoning, long-tail recognition).
- Methodology is clearly explained with diagrams and examples, making the synthesis process easy to understand.
- Modular design enables individual components (tagging model, LLM, diffusion model) to be replaced without affecting other parts.

### Weaknesses
- The LLM instruction design in the pipeline is under-analyzed: the example instructions in Appendix A1 only ask for a better description, not explicit editing. Gains may stem from LLM hallucination rather than controlled modification. No ablation or analysis is provided on different editing instructions, nor on which types of edits yield better negatives for contrastive loss.
- The proposed pipeline shares significant similarity with GenArtist (image editing) and with other works on synthetic image generation for target datasets or cross-domain retrieval. The paper does not clearly specify its differences or provide direct experimental comparisons with GenArtist, InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, or recent synthetic data methods (e.g., arXiv:2406.05184, arXiv:2401.00420).
- The claimed fine-grained control is inadequately designed: the text controller uses simple prompt concatenation, making the mapping between visual tags and user policies unclear. For the image controller, the method does not address how image consistency is maintained after editing. Overall, the pipeline lacks a clear demonstration of how editing conditions are provided to the generative model in a fine-grained manner.
- Experimental scope is limited: only small datasets (CC3M, CC12M) are used; no results on larger datasets (LAION-400M, LAION-5B). Only CLIP with specific ViT backbones is tested; experiments on BLIP or other CLIP variants are missing. The paper does not validate data-efficiency claims by comparing with dataset refinement/filtering methods such as SemDeDup, Filter-&-Align, and Sieve in terms of both performance and amount of training data used.
- The pipeline incurs substantial resource and time costs due to reliance on multiple models (vision tagging, LLM, diffusion). Generation is not end-to-end, raising scalability concerns for large-scale pretraining. No efficiency metrics are provided (e.g., time to generate 1 million image-caption pairs, per-component costs).
- Ablation results show that CtrlSynth-img (synthetic images alone) yields minimal improvement. It is unclear whether one could remove synthetic image generation entirely and instead focus resources on improving the diversity and quality of synthetic text, and whether that would lead to consistent gains across all tasks.
- The self-filtering mechanism for synthetic data quality is not explained; details on how the alignment threshold for visual tags is selected are missing.
- The paper does not clearly differentiate CtrlSynth from caption augmentation methods like VeCLIP and LaCLIP. It is unclear whether the improvements stem from increased diversity due to decomposition and user control, or from the generation of more fine-grained semantic captions.
- Only two LLMs (Mistral-Nemo, Qwen2-7B) are tested; it is unknown whether larger LLMs would further improve results.
