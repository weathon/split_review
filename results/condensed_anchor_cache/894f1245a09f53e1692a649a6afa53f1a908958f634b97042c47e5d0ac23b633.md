## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CoDiCast, an autoregressive conditional diffusion model for probabilistic global weather forecasting using ERA5 data at 5.625° resolution. The key design choice is encoding conditioning states with a pretrained autoencoder and using cross-attention in a U-Net denoiser. The model aims to provide ensemble forecasts with uncertainty quantification. Reviewer 1 (score 5) found the problem important and the motivation strong, and appreciated the use of CRPS and ablation studies. The other three reviewers (scores 3) were critical of novelty, baseline selection, evaluation of uncertainty, sampling speed, and limited scope (short forecast horizon, coarse resolution). The main points of disagreement center on whether the pretrained-encoder contribution is sufficient novelty and whether the experimental setup convincingly demonstrates state-of-the-art capability.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important problem: probabilistic weather forecasting with uncertainty quantification, motivated by the high computational cost of NWP ensembles. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The use of a pretrained autoencoder to encode conditioning states before feeding into the denoising network is novel in the weather forecasting domain and yields a documented performance boost. (R2, R3, R4)
- The paper includes helpful ablation studies (Section 5.6) showing the contribution of each modeling component and the impact of the number of diffusion sampling steps. (R3)
- CoDiCast achieves good short-term forecast accuracy (up to 36 h) on ERA5 compared to deterministic baselines trained on the same data. (R1, R3)
- The paper is well-written, clearly motivated, and captures key details about the diffusion model framework for ensemble generation. (R1, R3, R4)
- The use of CRPS to evaluate uncertainty is a positive step, and the test case on global forecasting at 5.625° provides a relevant baseline. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty**: Diffusion models for probabilistic weather/spatiotemporal forecasting have been proposed before (PreDiff, GenCast, SEEDS). CoDiCast largely applies a similar framework to a coarser, shorter-range setting. (R1, R2, R3, R4)  Attention‑based U‑Net and cross‑attention are not novel; similar designs appear in ClimaX and earlier work. (R4)  The central novelty—using a pretrained autoencoder for conditioning—is only evaluated in a short ablation; it is not compared to a learnable encoder trained jointly, nor to jointly noising conditioning and target states (as in e.g., 2309.01745). Robustness to architectures, noise schedules, and hyperparameters is not shown. (R2)
- **Weak and inappropriate baselines**: The baselines (FourCastNet, ClimaX, ClimODE) are mostly deterministic; they are not fair comparisons for a probabilistic model. Missing comparisons to open‑source diffusion weather models (e.g., SEEDS) or to probabilistic variants of deterministic models (MC‑dropout, IC perturbation). (R1, R3, R4)  PreDiff and GenCast should have been included even if codes are unavailable. (R1, R2, R3)  ClimODE provides uncertainty estimates that were not compared in CRPS (Table 2). (R3)  CoDiCast’s performance is inferior to the IFS control run at longer lead times, whereas many SOTA ML models (GraphCast) already outperform IFS control at short lead times. (R3, R4)
- **Limited scope and coarse resolution**: The forecast horizon is only 3 days at 6‑hour steps, while ensemble forecasting is most valuable at medium‑ to long‑range (10–15 days). At longer lead times (appendix Table 6) CoDiCast performs worse. (R1, R3)  The 5.625° resolution is very coarse (≈625 km, ~400× smaller horizontal domain than 0.25°), and only 5 variables are used versus 100+ in operational models. This makes the claimed inference time (12–27 minutes) much slower relative to other ML methods and even to IFS when adjusted for resolution. (R4)
- **Sampling is prohibitively slow**: Sampling a forecast takes 12 minutes for 5 variables at 5.625° using 1000 steps. In comparison, GenCast samples 80 variables at 0.25° in 8 minutes with 20 steps. Using fewer diffusion steps in CoDiCast drastically reduces performance, and other ML ensemble methods run in seconds. The argument that CoDiCast is faster than IFS is misleading because IFS operates at far higher resolution and variable count. (R3, R4)
- **Uncertainty evaluation is insufficient**: The only quantitative uncertainty metric is CRPS, and it lacks comparison to any ML baseline. No calibration metrics (spread‑skill ratio, rank histograms) are provided to show whether the ensemble spread is trustworthy. (R3)  The claim that smaller uncertainty is better is not properly justified; calibration with error is more important. (R1)  It is unclear whether RMSE is computed on the ensemble mean or single samples, and if the ensemble mean is used, the number of members is not stated. (R3)
- **Missing contextualization of related work**: The paper does not discuss alternative ML ensemble methods (perturbation‑based: Chen et al. 2023, Bülte et al. 2024; multi‑model: Weyn et al. 2021; latent variable generative: Hu et al. 2023, Oskarsson et al. 2024) and how CoDiCast compares given its high sampling cost. (R3)  Clear methodological differences from PreDiff and GenCast are not explained. (R1, R2)
- **Technical and presentation issues**:
  - The standard U‑Net does not respect periodic boundary conditions in longitude. (R3)
  - Background on DDPM (Section 3.2) and straightforward instantiations (Sections 4.1, 4.2) can be shortened or moved to appendix. (R1, R3)
  - Figure 6 shows 6‑h lead time, which is not informative; later lead times would be more revealing. (R3)
  - The dimensionality of \(\tilde{Z}^t\) (computed as 2×4×512 = 4096) is not much smaller than the input (64×32×5 = 10240); the authors should comment on this choice. (R3)
  - Error ranges in Table 1 are not explained (variation across seeds?). (R3)
  - The paper claims that deterministic methods cannot achieve UQ, which is contradicted by PreDiff and GenCast. (R1)
  - The number of ensemble members used in experiments and the total parameter count of CoDiCast are not reported. (R3, R4)
  - It is not specified whether CRPS in Table 2 is computed on the full test set or only the case study of Figure 5. (R3)
