## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ADAPT, a dual-decoder Mask2Former framework for continual panoptic segmentation (CPS). It freezes backbone and pixel decoder, selectively fine-tunes cross-attention and feedforward layers, and uses attentive self-distillation with query-level fusion (QLF) to retain prior knowledge while handling new classes. Experiments are conducted on ADE20K under continual learning setups from prior work, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to understand. Clear figure illustration.
- Dual-decoder framework with minimal computational cost: freezing backbone and pixel decoder enables single forward pass for teacher–student distillation (teacher inference requires only the transformer decoder).
- Attentive self-distillation focuses on informative regions by down-weighting non-object areas, making knowledge retention more targeted.
- Query-level fusion (QLF) eliminates scale mismatch issues that occur in probability-level fusion.
- Achieves state-of-the-art on ADE20K, significantly outperforming existing methods. Selective fine-tuning of cross-attention and feedforward layers (freezing self-attention) demonstrates a good balance between plasticity and rigidity.
- Ablation study (Table 2, etc.) confirms positive contributions of each proposed component.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited adaptation for long sequences:** Freezing the encoder and self-attention layers (L.209) may restrict adaptation over very long task sequences or as new classes become more diverse/complex. The authors only provide empirical results (Table 2) without theoretical justification for why freezing self-attention does not hinder adaptation, especially given its role in capturing long-range dependencies. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Computational efficiency claims unsubstantiated:** No experimental results (training time, FLOPs, GPU memory) are provided to support the claimed efficiency advantage. A comparison of training iteration throughput or FLOPs against CoMFormer and ECLIPSE is needed, including a breakdown per component (cross-attention, feedforward, self-attention). The overhead of dual-decoder prediction fusion at test time is also unmeasured. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Only one dataset (ADE20K):** Experiments are limited to ADE20K. Evaluation on at least one more dataset (e.g., COCO with growing classes) is necessary to validate generalization. Open-vocabulary evaluation settings are also suggested. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)  
  - Related: Reviewer 2 asks whether a CLIP-based open-vocabulary panseg model could serve as another baseline.
- **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  - Description of query-level fusion (Sec. 3.4) lacks implementation details and equations; the figure (Fig. 2) is unclear (color meanings, misclassification arrows, why only 4 scores when 8 queries exist). The fusion process and how overlapping instances are handled need clarification. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
  - Ablation and validation experiments are poorly structured (subsection 4.5, first table reference is Table 5 causing confusion). Table 5’s baseline setting is ambiguous – it appears identical to CoMFormer, but whether this is intentional or coincidental is not stated. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
  - Table size and position distribution need adjustment. (Reviewer 3)
- **Assumption about initial phase data:** Dual-decoder fusion assumes most data/classes are available in the initial phase. If this is not the case (e.g., many classes appear later), the method’s applicability and performance are unclear. This should be discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited technical novelty:** Self-distillation and weight freezing have already been considered in continual panoptic segmentation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Probability inconsistency analysis missing:** The activation function used for class probabilities (softmax vs. sigmoid) is not specified. If softmax is used, probability inconsistency from relative scoring may affect fusion; if sigmoid is used, the inconsistency may be reduced. An analysis of the impact of the activation function is needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of qualitative analysis:** The paper lacks visual segmentation masks for both base and novel classes to complement quantitative results, especially for panoptic segmentation where semantic and instance accuracy are both important. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unsubstantiated criticism of prior methods (lines 057–063):** Claims that “raising computational costs” (training only?) and that “introducing additional learnable query features” constrains model capacity lack theoretical or numerical evidence. The authors should avoid judging other methods without support. (Reviewer 3)
- **Scalability to larger models/higher resolution:** The paper reports results only with ResNet-50; it is unclear how ADAPT scales with larger backbones or higher-resolution images given the freezing and distillation mechanisms. (Reviewer 1)
