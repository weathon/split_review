## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Kolmogorov-Arnold Attention (KAA), which integrates the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) architecture into the scoring function of attentive GNNs. The authors also introduce Maximum Ranking Distance (MRD) to theoretically bound the expressive power of scoring functions. Extensive experiments on node-level and graph-level tasks using various backbone models show that KAA-enhanced models outperform their original counterparts. Reviewers generally find the idea novel and the theoretical analysis rigorous. However, concerns include: weak motivation relative to existing strong attention/Transformer models, limited novelty (KAN plus existing components), outdated and weak baselines (shallow layers, not state-of-the-art), potential overfitting from higher expressive power, lack of ablation on MRD ranking methods, no comparison of computational cost, unfair baseline hyperparameter choices (e.g., GAT dropout 0.6), and absence of comparisons with non-attention SOTA GNNs or hierarchical attention mechanisms. One reviewer (score 8) is more positive, viewing the improvements as moderate and consistent; the other three (scores 6) are more critical, particularly of the baseline selection and incremental contribution.

### Strengths

- Exploring KAN for graph attention is novel and creative (R1, R2).
- The paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis: recaps KAN components in attentive GNNs, defines Importance Ranking and Ranking Distance (based on GATv2 analysis), and proves KAA exhibits the strongest expressive power among linear/MLP-based scoring functions (R1, R2, R3).
- Extensive experiments across a wide range of benchmarks on node-level, link-level, and graph-level tasks show that KAA-enhanced models outperform their original counterparts, with substantial improvements in some cases (R2) and moderate consistent improvements across tasks (R3). One reviewer notes that experiments are extensive (R1).
- The architecture writing and KAN background are clear; Table 1 (unified formulas for scoring functions) is informative with many benchmarks listed (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Motivation insufficient:** The paper does not convincingly illustrate when KAA significantly outperforms current strong graph attention (e.g., GATv2, graph Transformers) or why GAT’s simple learned alpha over local neighborhoods has limited expressive power (R1, R4).
- **Limited novelty:** The work mainly combines KAN (already proposed) with existing components such as multi-head attention and MLP-like structures; the modification to the attention computing mechanism is not large (R2, R4). The GNN backbones and baselines tested are old and weak (e.g., shallow layers ≤ 4), which weakens the claim of improvement (R2, R4). Other KAN-based benchmarks should have been included (R4).
- **Unrealistic assumption in theory:** The MRD analysis assumes “the selected central node is connected to all other nodes in the graph,” which is unrealistic even for graph Transformers (R1).
- **Overfitting concern:** Higher expressive power (as claimed for KAA) could lead to severe overfitting; the authors do not discuss how they address this (R1).
- **Computational cost not compared:** KAN likely increases both time and space cost; the paper should compare cost with current graph attention and graph Transformers (R1).  
- **Missing ablations and comparisons:**
  - No empirical ablation study over the different ranking methods proposed in Section 4.2.2 to demonstrate the effectiveness of MRD (R3).
  - Does KAA still need multi-head attention if its single-head ranking is already near-optimal? (R1)
  - No comparison with SOTA GNN architectures that do not use attention (e.g., [1] for node classification, [2] for link prediction) (R3).
  - No discussion or comparison with hierarchical attention mechanisms (e.g., Heterogeneous Graph Attention Network [WWW 2019], Bi-Level Attention Graph Neural Networks [IEEE ICDM 2021]) (R4).
- **Unfair baseline settings:** For example, GAT’s original paper uses dropout 0.6 and achieves better results than reported here under the same data split; the authors may not have chosen recommended hyperparameters (R1).
- **Outdated model selection:** The GNN backbones used are outdated; many are just variant models of the proposed KAA (R4).
