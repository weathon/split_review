## Merged Review

### Summary

Three reviewers assessed this paper. Scores were 3, 5, 3 (one largely positive, two negative). All acknowledge the novelty of using a conditional generator for high-dimensional marks in temporal point processes and consider the presentation clear. However, significant concerns were raised about methodological clarity, evaluation rigor, missing baselines, lack of quantitative and temporal metrics, and insufficient validation of high-dimensional mark modeling.

### Strengths

- The paper proposes a model-agnostic generative framework for marked temporal point processes with high-dimensional marks (e.g., texts, images), an underexplored direction. The conditional generator directly outputs time and mark from a noise vector and history embedding, avoiding sequential generation of time then mark or vice versa. (R1, R2, R3)
- Considerable representational power by not restricting the parametric form of the conditional intensity or density, unlike traditional models. (R2)
- Clear presentation: The methodology is presented in an intelligible and easy-to-follow manner. (R2, R3)
- The framework is flexible and can be extended to other models and learning algorithms beyond those used in the paper. (R2)
- Interesting idea combining TPP with generative models, potentially benefiting the TPP community. (R3)
- Comprehensive experiments across synthetic, semi-synthetic, and real datasets. (R3)
- Reviewer 2 (score 5) found the framework scales well to high-dimensional marks, considers the contribution and presentation good, and is more positive overall than the other two reviewers (score 3 each). The minority positive view is explicitly noted.

### Weaknesses

- **Methodological clarity and learning objective**:
  - The derivation of equation (5) for the learning objective is unclear. The negative log-likelihood circumvents the integral in (3) but appears to omit the conditional CDF evaluated at the observation window. How is the conditional CDF handled or approximated? (R2, R1, R3)
  - It is unclear whether equation (6) or (7) is used for density estimation in the experiments. The paper only shows density estimation of times without marks, which is insufficient to support the model. (R3)
  - The specific losses and their weights for the joint density of time and mark are not clearly defined. How is the reconstruction loss calculated when x includes both time and mark, and how are different components weighted? (R1)
  - In Appendix C, the second term of the ELBO is stated as minimizing NLL p(x|z,h), but it is unclear how this works when x includes both time and mark. (R1)
  - Equation (6) appears to include samples, which is stated as a drawback for some competing methods. (R1)
- **Balancing timestamp and high-dimensional mark errors**:
  - Error in estimating the 1D timestamp is likely to be engulfed by error in estimating high-dimensional marks, as optimization may prioritize mark accuracy at the expense of temporal dynamics. No clear strategy is presented to balance these aspects. (R3)
- **Missing quantitative evaluation and temporal metrics**:
  - For semi-synthetic (T-MNIST, T-CIFAR) and real-world data, no quantitative metrics are provided to assess baseline performance. Visualizations on hand-picked sequences are insufficient. (R2, R3)
  - There is no evaluation of time prediction accuracy in the experiments. It is crucial to assess how well the model predicts event timing, not just marks. (R3)
  - On Figure 6, only the proposed CEG method is shown; baseline results (e.g., DNSK) are missing, preventing direct visual comparison. (R2)
- **Insufficient validation of high-dimensional marks**:
  - The high-dimensional mark experiments (T-MNIST, T-CIFAR) are essentially image generation tasks conditionally, not true temporal point processes with complex interactions between different mark types. A more realistic setup would involve temporal interactions between distinct marks, e.g., where each pixel in an image arrives at a time with interactions between pixels. (R1)
  - The earthquake catalog is a spatio-temporal point process with 2D marks; the crime reports use a reduced 10D representation. These do not fully validate the method for high-dimensional marks (e.g., 50–100 dimensions). (R3)
  - The synthetic data experiments with 1D and 3D marks do not support the main claim; they could be moved to an appendix. (R3)
  - A more meaningful comparison would involve datasets where the temporal point process itself is non-trivial and marks have complex interactions, e.g., sequences of video frames to reflect generated dynamics. (R3)
- **Missing baselines and hyperparameter tuning**:
  - The paper lacks comparison to methods that allow direct sampling, such as normalizing flows or diffusion models. The focus on thinning-based baselines is a significant oversight. (R1)
  - For synthetic data, at least a fully neural network should capture simple intensities; the poor performance of baselines suggests they were not tuned correctly. (R1)
  - Did the authors experiment with other hyperparameter configurations? If not, results may be biased. (R2)
  - In the semi-synthetic high-dimensional mark tasks, why not augment RMTPP with a strong image generator? That could beat the proposed method. (R1)
  - A baseline with ablation: removing timestamps so that x is just the mark from f(x|Ht(x)) in equation (5) should be tested. (R3)
- **Claims about expressiveness and baselines**:
  - The claim that the model enjoys additional expressiveness because it does not impose restrictions on intensity is not unique; many neural TPP models (e.g., FullyNN) also have flexible intensities. The paper should clarify what specifically provides additional expressiveness beyond the flexibility of neural networks in general. (R2)
  - The statement in Section 3.1 that only the conditional intensity can be computed for baselines is inaccurate. Fully neural network models can compute closed-form intensity and cumulative intensity, providing density. (R1)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Citation for (Graves & Graves, 2012) is incorrect in both the reference and the formatting. (R1)
  - Computation cost of Algorithm 2 compared to other methods is not provided. (R1)
