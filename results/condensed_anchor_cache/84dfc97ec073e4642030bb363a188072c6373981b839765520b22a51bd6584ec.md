## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes the label noise robustness of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM). The key insight is that SAM's peak performance under label noise occurs with early stopping, before convergence. The authors decompose SAM's sample-wise gradient into a logit term and a Jacobian term. In logistic regression, the Jacobian term is ineffectual and the logit term upweights gradients from clean examples, delaying fitting of noise. In neural networks, the logit term plays a similar reweighting role but intervening to remove it does not degrade performance; the beneficial effect is instead explained by the Jacobian term. Theoretical analysis on a two-layer linear network shows the Jacobian term regularizes the representation and last-layer weights. Motivated by this analysis, cheaper alternatives that explicitly induce these regularization effects largely recover SAM's benefits on CIFAR-10.

### Strengths
- Provides theoretical analysis and experiments investigating an important practical phenomenon – SAM's label noise robustness.
- Careful decomposition and ablation studies (logit vs. Jacobian SAM) elucidate the source of gains.
- The theory, though simple, is novel and not trivial.
- Proposes a simplified, cheaper method motivated by the analysis that recovers much of SAM's gains.
- Originality: surprisingly shows the logit effect is in fact not necessary for robustness in neural networks.
- Overall well-written and easy to follow.
- Related work is adequately covered.

### Weaknesses
1. **Inadequate empirical support.** Large-scale experiments are only on CIFAR-10. At least one larger/medium dataset (e.g., Tiny ImageNet, ImageNet, MS-COCO, flowers102, places365, food101) should be included – not too similar to CIFAR-10 (like CIFAR-100) nor too small.
2. **Little evidence on the role of early stopping.** Although the narrative emphasizes early stopping, the ResNet experiments in Fig. 1 and Fig. 3 show little effect from early stopping; the performance gap is already present at convergence. The caption for the middle plot in Fig. 1 (“SAM fits much more clean data before beginning to overfit to mislabeled data”) is hard to infer from the plot. Authors should either provide more evidence or deemphasize early stopping in the abstract, introduction, and Sections 5 and 6.
3. **Analysis limited to 2-layer linear networks.** It is unclear whether insights extend to deep nonlinear networks; limitations should be discussed.
4. **Lacks comparison to other label noise robust methods** (e.g., MentorNet). Such comparisons would strengthen the evaluation of the proposed simplified method.
5. **No analysis or empirical demonstrations on tasks other than classification** (e.g., regression tasks).
6. **Missing analysis on how robustness depends on key hyperparameters** such as network width (d), number of training samples (n), overparameterization regime, and learning rate. Could be investigated in the setting of Section 3.1 and with empirical studies on CIFAR-10.
7. **Equation 4.5** includes a stop-gradient operator in a minimization target, which is non-standard; it should be rephrased as an update rule.
8. **Missing references** in Related Work: [arXiv:1609.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04836), [arXiv:1705.10694](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10694).
9. **Section 4.2 does not properly connect the regularization effect to the overall narrative** about robustness to label noise and the role of early stopping. The claim that “In linear models, weight decay has somewhat equivalent effects to SAM’s logit scaling in the sense that it balances the contribution of the sample-wise gradients and thus prevents overfitting to outliers” lacks basis, and there is no discussion of the effect of regularizing the representation.
10. **Proposed regularization method's performance without label noise** is not reported; it is unclear whether gains persist or vanish in clean settings.
11. **Unclear whether SAM's gains are additive** to more sophisticated robust training algorithms (beyond plain SGD) or if the performance gain diminishes.
