## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper combines task-specific scratchpads with multi-level position coupling to achieve length generalization in arithmetic tasks (parity, multi-operand addition, multiplication). Reviewers agree the paper is clearly written, the methods are well described, and the experimental results are compelling—showing 2–3× generalization on operand length and count. However, there is strong disagreement on novelty and generality: two reviewers (scores 8, confidence 4) find the results impressive and the contribution substantial, while two other reviewers (scores 5, confidence 5 and 4) consider the contribution limited and the approach highly task-specific, with insufficient comparison to existing methods. All reviewers note that the method’s heavy reliance on task-designed positional encodings and scratchpad structure limits its applicability beyond arithmetic. Multiple missing ablations, comparisons, and clarifications are identified.

### Strengths

- Well-written with clear description of methods and intuition.
- Impressive length generalization on both operand length and operand count for addition; also on both operand lengths for multiplication.
- Extensive ablations on zero-padding, trained-lengths, and connection to theoretical construction.
- Tri-level position coupling enables length generalization on both operands in multiplication—an interesting observation.
- Parity experiment achieves perfect length generalization under position coupling.
- Theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows a 1-layer Transformer can solve multi-operand addition for lengths exponential in embedding dimension.

### Weaknesses

- **Task-specificity and limited generality**: Position coupling and scratchpad are highly tailored to each arithmetic task (different positional encodings for addition vs. multiplication). It is unclear how to extend the approach to non-arithmetic tasks or to settings where numbers appear among text. Multiple reviewers note this as a core limitation.  
- **Insufficient novelty relative to prior work**: Two reviewers (scores 5) argue that position coupling was introduced in earlier work and that bi-level / tri-level extensions, padding, and reversing are straightforward adaptations. They consider the contribution limited. One reviewer also notes that heavy use of prior techniques is not acknowledged by a proper Related Work section in the main text.  
- **Disputed claim of “first” length generalization**: One reviewer points to prior work (e.g., using RPE) that claims generalization up to 50 digits trained on 5 digits; the paper’s statement should be more nuanced and better contextualized.  
- **Lack of comparison with other positional encoding methods**: Position coupling is compared only with a NoPE+scratchpad baseline. Comparisons with FIRE, Rotary, or multi-level relative PEs (e.g., He et al., Zhang et al.) are missing. One reviewer questions why NoPE was chosen and whether those models were trained longer.  
- **Test set size too small**: For the 30-operand, length-30 setting, the test set may be insufficient to reliably estimate accuracy; a larger test set is needed to avoid overestimating generalization.  
- **Single-digit tokenizer assumption**: The method assumes single-digit tokenization. It is unclear whether it extends to multi-digit tokens or subword units.  
- **Quadratic input blowup from zero-padding**: Padding all operands to max length yields O(n²) input length even when only one operand is long, raising computational and scalability concerns.  
- **Unexpected performance of 1L8H vs. 1L4H**: One reviewer observes that the 1-layer 8-head model performs worse than the 1-layer 4-head model, an unexplained anomaly.  
- **Clarification needed for Theorem 4.1**: Does “solve” mean perfectly? Are there hidden constants in the maximum digits or operands?  
- **Only positive integers considered**: Restriction to positive integers greatly reduces practical interest; one reviewer suggests extending to negative numbers (e.g., via sign or balanced base representation).  
- **Evaluation based on both scratchpad and output**: The main experiments require the scratchpad to be correctly predicted. One reviewer asks whether models are also evaluated on the final result alone, as this could affect ablations and comparisons.  
- **Missing ablations on reversing digits**: Is reversing the digit order in the scratchpad and output necessary? What would be the impact of reversing digits in the input?  
- **Architecture depth and dimension**: Shallow models achieve generalization but use a large embedding dimension (512). One reviewer asks whether smaller dimensions (128, 64) also work. Deeper models (4,6 layers) sometimes perform worse than 2-layer models; this could be due to small training set (500k) or insufficient training steps—an ablation on training set size is requested.  
- **Parameter count inconsistency**: Main text claims 63M parameters, appendix claims 25M; this needs clarification.  
- **Purpose of addition scratchpad design**: One reviewer questions why 0 and a copy of the first operand are included in the addition scratchpad.  
- **Potential benefit of encoder-decoder architecture**: Bidirectional attention over the input (where digits are not reversed) might help; this is not explored.  
- **Alternative prompting approach**: One reviewer asks whether a human-readable numbering scheme (e.g., numbering each step) could achieve similar generalization for multiplication; this is not tested.
