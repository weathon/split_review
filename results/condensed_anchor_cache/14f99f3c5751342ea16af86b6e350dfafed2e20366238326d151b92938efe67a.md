## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Intra-Fusion, a novel approach that integrates pruning and model fusion via Optimal Transport (OT). Instead of simply discarding low-importance neurons, Intra-Fusion uses OT to fuse them with surviving neurons, aiming to preserve the output of the original non-pruned model and recover accuracy without fine-tuning and without requiring any data (data‑free pruning). The method is agnostic to the neuron importance metric used. The paper also explores how fusion can factorize model training (FaP) by splitting a dataset across two independently trained models, potentially speeding up training. Results are benchmarked on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet, showing that Intra-Fusion improves upon a default pruning baseline, especially in the data‑free setting.

### Strengths

- **Data‑free pruning is highly valuable.** The method recovers a substantial amount of accuracy without any fine‑tuning or additional data, challenging the conventional requirement for an expensive retraining step.
- **Novel use of Optimal Transport for pruning.** Rather than discarding less important neurons, OT elegantly integrates them into surviving ones – a thoughtful, well‑reasoned strategy.
- **Conceptually interesting framework.** Unifying pruning and fusion into a principled approach makes intuitive sense and is a fresh alternative to designing ever‑better importance metrics.
- **Consistent accuracy improvements.** Intra‑Fusion yields higher test accuracy than the default pruning baseline across multiple benchmarks, especially in the data‑free scenario.
- **Potential for training speed‑ups.** The FaP (fusion‑aided pruning) idea of factorizing training by splitting data across models is practical and engaging; it could provide near‑2× speedups as an alternative to data parallelism.
- **Comprehensive evaluation and analysis.** The paper includes a wide range of experiments and an insightful analysis section (Sec 5) with interesting findings.
- **Metric‑agnostic.** The method works with any given importance metric, making it flexible.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited comparisons against state‑of‑the‑art.** The main results (Tab 1, Fig 3, Fig 4) only compare against a basic default pruning scheme. Several reviewers strongly recommend adding comparisons with top‑performing structured pruning methods – e.g., group‑lasso based approaches, Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, and other recent methods on the standard ResNet50/ImageNet benchmark.
- **Marginal improvement after fine‑tuning.** The gains over the baseline become small after fine‑tuning. One reviewer (score 8) considers this a major weakness, arguing that “data‑free pruning” is barely practical today and that the method’s practical utility is questionable when fine‑tuning is feasible.
- **Missing evidence for split‑data speed‑up.** The claim that splitting training yields near‑2× speedup is only theoretical; the paper should provide actual wall‑clock experimental results to support it.
- **Writing and presentation problems (noted by multiple reviewers).**  
  – Pages 3–4: “Figure 6” appears where “Figure 1” is clearly intended.  
  – Algorithm 1 notation: “$neuron\ j \in layer \land i[j] \ge t$” incorrectly uses the logical AND symbol.  
  – Many long, clause‑laden sentences that hinder readability (e.g., Section 6, third line).  
  – The text description of Figure 7 is truncated.
- **Unclear terminology.**  
  – What does “meta” in the title refer to?  
  – “Cardinality” is used for “layer’s cardinality” in Sec 3.1 and “group cardinality” in the “Group‑by‑Group” section; the distinction is confusing.
- **Choice of uniform distribution for OT not justified.** It is unclear why a uniform distribution for both source and target is the most robust choice (Sec 3.2.3).
- **Generalizability of FaP.** The FaP approach assumes two models with identical structures. Can it fuse models of different depths or heterogeneous architectures (e.g., CNN and ViT)?
- **Compatibility with pruning‑at‑init metrics.** Can Intra‑Fusion work with metrics like SynFlow or ZiCo that are computed at initialization?
- **Citation error.** The paper cites (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023) as a structured pruning paper, but it is actually on unstructured pruning.
