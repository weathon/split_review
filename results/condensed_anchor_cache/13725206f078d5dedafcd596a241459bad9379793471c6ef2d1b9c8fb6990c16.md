## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SIaM, a self-improving code-assisted mathematical reasoning paradigm that uses a critic model to verify code execution results against golden answers, enabling iterative optimization on large-scale web QA pairs. The method leverages large-scale, expert-written, diverse math question-answer pairs. A code-based critic model guides question-code data construction, quality control, and evaluation. The paper explores alignment algorithms (rejection sampling fine-tuning and DPO) with self-generated data. Experiments are conducted on in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks in both English and Chinese.

### Strengths
- Proposes a method to continuously self-improve LLMs by utilizing large-scale, well-formatted math data from the web, focusing on efficient comparison of reference answers and code execution results via a critic model.
- Evaluates models on both English and Chinese benchmarks.
- Employs iterative refinement with a substantial number of QA pairs to enhance mathematical reasoning capabilities.
- Main experiments are solid and validated across multiple datasets and models, supporting reliability.
- The proposed method exhibits strong scalability and potential for broader applications.

### Weaknesses
- The paper's novelty is limited; the methodology closely resembles existing approaches such as rejection sampling fine-tuning and DPO (ChatGLM-Math, ReFT) and is similar to RFT, STAR, V-STAR, with the main difference being application to code-augmented CoT. The paper appears more as an experimental report than introducing a genuinely novel approach (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The method appears data-inefficient: it uses over 1,000K samples but achieves lower accuracy on MATH (80.5%) and GSM8K (41.9%) compared to Dart-Math (Llama3 base, 590K query-response pairs, 81.1% and 46.6%). The discrepancy may be due to differences in response style and quality (Reviewer 1).
- The paper lacks crucial baselines: fine-tuning the model directly with the large collected data (without the critic model) is missing (Reviewer 2), and traditional methods like RFT or V-STAR are not trained on the same web datasets for fair comparison (Reviewer 3). This leaves the necessity of the critic model unjustified.
- The motivation is unclear and not compelling. The abstract and introduction do not clearly explain the specific problem addressed. Adding TIR to CoT is generally expected to improve performance, and the unique benefits of code-augmented CoT over traditional CoT with RFT are not clearly explained (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The critic model is trained using GPT-4, so training labels are not guaranteed correct. The paper should clarify how this approach compares to using exact match metrics to substantiate the critic model's reliability (Reviewer 3).
- The reported baseline scores for QWEN2Math_instruct on English benchmarks (GSM8K: 79.5, MATH: 48.0) significantly differ from the official blog scores (GSM8K: 89.9, MATH: 75.1). Additionally, tools are indicated for QWEN2Math in Table 3 despite its post-training with CoT data, requiring clarification (Reviewer 1).
- For more complex reasoning like MATH, performance improvements are marginal. The paper attributes variations to evaluation scripts but needs a more comprehensive explanation, including cases where code implementation may not be feasible and how that limits accuracy and scalability (Reviewer 3).
- Grammatical issue: missing definition of variable j in line 177 (Reviewer 2).
- There is a typo: Table '??' in A.4, line 850 (Reviewer 1).
