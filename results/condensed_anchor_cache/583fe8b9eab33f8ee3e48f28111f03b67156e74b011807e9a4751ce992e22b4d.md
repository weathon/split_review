## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GyroAtt, a general attention framework for gyro spaces that unifies attention mechanisms on SPD, SPSD, and Grassmannian manifolds. It introduces gyro homomorphisms for feature transformations, uses geodesic distances for attention scoring, and weighted Fréchet mean for aggregation. Seven homomorphisms with proofs are provided. Experiments on four EEG datasets show competitive performance. Reviewer 3 (score 8) is notably more positive than reviewers 1, 2, and 4 (scores 5, 6, 6), particularly regarding mathematical novelty and clarity; however, all reviewers raise concerns about experimental rigor, architectural clarity, and comparison depth.

### Strengths
- The paper demonstrates strong technical mastery of mathematical tools and provides a rigorous, clearly structured mathematical framework with precise definitions (R1, R3). The use of gyro homomorphisms and geodesic-based attention is mathematically general and elegant (R3).
- It offers a unified approach to manifold-based attention mechanisms, overcoming limitations of ad-hoc designs for individual geometries (R2, R1). This is the first work to construct an attention network on the SPD manifold using the gyrovector space approach (R3).
- The key contribution of introducing and proving seven specific gyro homomorphisms across different manifolds and metrics is well-documented (R4).
- Empirical results on four EEG datasets show that GyroAtt frameworks outperform or are competitive with existing methods, demonstrating effectiveness for non-Euclidean data (R2, R4, R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Architecture and implementation details are insufficient.** The overall model structure used in experiments remains unclear; a more precise description (beyond five lines of “Implementation details”) is needed for reproducibility (R1, R4). The attention mechanism for GyroAtt-SPD appears identical to that in MAtt (Pan et al., 2022), yet performance differences are observed—this needs clarification (R4). A figure of the architecture is provided but does not fully clarify all critical implementation choices (R1).
- **Missing focused comparisons with directly related manifold attention methods.** The paper compares broadly but should specifically compare with MAtt and GDLNet, which also optimize attention on SPD manifolds, to isolate differences (R3, R4). A direct comparison of GyroAtt, MAtt, and GDLNet on performance and computational efficiency is needed (R3).
- **The motivation for the generalization is not fully convincing.** It is unclear whether the generalization for a particular geometry is strictly equivalent to prior specific layers or merely similar (R1). The connection between gyro homomorphisms and linear layers is not sufficiently justified (R1). The framework primarily applies known manifold forms (SPD, Grassmannian, SPSD) and does not facilitate discovery of novel geometric structures (R2).
- **Experimental results lack consistent statistical significance.** Performance improvements are not always significant across datasets; standard deviations in Tables 4 and 5 suggest many results fall within the margin of error (R2, also noted by R3 in context of “overwhelming” advantage). This raises concerns about robustness (R2). It is unclear whether the compared models were tuned to their best performance; alternative tuning could change results (R3).
- **Need for theoretical or empirical justification of performance advantage.** Reviewer 3 specifically asks why the gyrovector space approach gives an “overwhelming” performance advantage (even a few percentage points on EEG is substantial). The mapping between tangent space operations and gyro operations needs deeper analysis; an ablation substituting gyro operations with Euclidean/tangent space operations would isolate the impact (R3). The activation function (power parameter) is one difference; its contribution should be isolated by adding a row without activation in Table 6 (R4). The role of the additional layer in Eq. (9) is unclear—is it empirical or theoretically grounded? (R2).
- **Notation and presentation issues hamper readability.** Abbreviations are excessive and disrupt flow (R1). The commutator [A,B] is introduced in line 92 but only defined in line 146 (R2). Symbols like \(\downarrow\) in Theorems 5.1–5.4 and parentheses in Eq. (16) are not defined (R2). The Karcher flow algorithm description is too brief (R1). The metric used in SPD experiments (Table 4) is not stated (R1). The writing is dense and barely understandable despite improvements (R1).
- **Missing analyses and ablations.** Computational complexity and scalability are not discussed; comparison with baseline methods is needed for resource-constrained applications (R2). The discussion on the power activation coefficient, while moved to appendix, is a distraction from space constraints (R1). Can the attention mechanism be extended to multi-head configurations in different geometries? (R4). Provide intuitive explanation for why different GyroAtt variants (SPD vs. SPSD) excel on different datasets (Tables 4 and 5) (R2).
