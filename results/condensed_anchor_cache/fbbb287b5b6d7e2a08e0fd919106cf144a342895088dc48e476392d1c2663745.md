## Merged Review

### Summary
This work investigates high-density regions in diffusion models. The authors develop an augmented SDE framework to track sample likelihoods during SDE sampling at no extra computational cost, and introduce a high-probability sampler (HP-ODE) designed to generate samples with provably higher likelihood. Empirical analysis reveals that such high-likelihood samples consistently appear as cartoon-like drawings or blurry images, even though the training data (CIFAR-10, FFHQ-256) contains none of these styles. The paper also theoretically bounds the bias of the proposed likelihood estimator and offers a theoretical mode-tracking ODE.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written and uses clear figures/color‑coded equations to aid presentation.
- The augmented SDE for likelihood estimation is a novel theoretical contribution; it enables likelihood tracking during generation without additional computational cost.
- The high‑probability sampler is a practical tool that reliably produces higher‑likelihood samples than standard samplers, and the theory is accompanied by bias bounds.
- The empirical landscape analysis is valuable: it provides a theoretical justification for the known phenomenon that distorted/less‑detailed images have higher likelihood, and it identifies the curious emergence of cartoon‑style images in models trained on non‑cartoon data.
- The derivation of the augmented forward/reverse dynamics and the bias estimation are detailed and theoretically sound.

### Weaknesses
- **Invalid likelihood comparison and incorrect ODE assumption (severe criticism).**  
  (Reviewer 4) The likelihood comparison between normally sampled images ($x_0$) and HP‑ODE images ($y_0$) is flawed because the two are evaluated under different distributions: $p(y_0|x_t)$ vs $p(x_0|x_t)$. A proper comparison would require evaluating $y_0$ under the same distribution as $x_0$.  
  (Reviewer 4) More fundamentally, using the ODE in Remark 1 for sampling constitutes a mistake: real‑world data is multimodal (e.g., CIFAR‑10 has at least 10 modes), but the ODE assumes a Gaussian data distribution. This incorrect assumption likely biases the sampler toward unrealistic modes, explaining the cartoon‑like outputs.  
  (Reviewer 2) The paper lacks analysis of the underlying mechanisms (e.g., frequency components, color palettes) that cause cartoon‑like images to have high likelihood.

- **Limited practical impact and missing guidance for generation quality.**  
  (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3) The paper does not discuss how these findings can be leveraged to improve sample generation strategies or balance likelihood with image quality. No adjustments to the sampler are proposed to mitigate low‑quality outputs.  
  (Reviewer 3) It remains unclear whether the proposed analysis can lead to better stochastic samplers or enhance generation quality.  
  (Reviewer 2) For practitioners prioritizing both likelihood and visual quality, guidance on modifying the high‑probability sampler is absent.

- **Experiments are confined to small, simple, unconditional models.**  
  (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3) Experiments use only CIFAR‑10 and FFHQ‑256 with small diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, score‑based models). No tests are performed on modern, large‑scale models such as Stable Diffusion, Flux, etc.  
  (Reviewer 1) The analysis is unconditional; the effect of text guidance (e.g., in conditional generation) is not explored.  
  (Reviewer 2) It is unclear whether the high‑probability sampler is compatible with different noise schedules or requires specific network architectures.

- **Missing discussion of limitations, computational cost, and implementation details.**  
  (Reviewer 1) The mode‑tracking ODE has high computational cost, but the paper does not discuss scalability or quantify complexity with respect to data dimensionality or convergence steps.  
  (Reviewer 1) No limitations section is provided; assumptions in the theoretical analysis and experimental generalizability are not discussed.  
  (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4) Implementation details, code, and hyperparameters used during sampling are not released or described, hindering reproducibility.

- **No assessment of sample diversity or mode collapse.**  
  (Reviewer 2) The paper focuses solely on likelihood and does not evaluate whether the high‑likelihood sampling reduces output diversity or leads to mode collapse. Poster‑review questions about diversity remain unanswered.

- **Unclear intuition for Figure 4 observations.**  
  (Reviewer 3) The paper does not explain what it means that a model optimized for sample quality yields a smaller difference between $p_0^{ODE}$ and $p_0^{SDE}$.

- **Missing related work.**  
  (Reviewer 1) The paper does not discuss “Null‑text Guidance in Diffusion Models is Secretly a Cartoon‑style Creator” (Zhao et al.), which is highly relevant to the phenomenon of cartoon‑style high‑likelihood samples.

- **Limited quantitative evidence.**  
  (Reviewer 4) The paper provides sparse quantitative results (e.g., no systematic evaluation of the bias bounds or comparison to other likelihood estimators on standard benchmarks).
