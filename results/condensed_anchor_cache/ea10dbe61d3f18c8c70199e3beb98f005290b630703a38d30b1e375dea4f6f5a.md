## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers assess Gaga, a framework that segments open-world 3D scenes by associating inconsistent 2D masks from zero-shot segmentation models using a 3D-aware memory bank that assigns universal IDs before lifting to 3D Gaussians. The paper’s core contribution is replacing video-based mask association with spatial overlap in 3D space. Reviewers’ scores range from 8 to 3 (R1: 8, R2: 5, R3: 3, R4: 5), reflecting substantial disagreement. R1 (confidence 4) is the most positive, praising the simple yet effective idea and extensive experiments. R3 (confidence 5) is the most negative, arguing the method lacks novelty, omits critical comparisons (especially OmniSeg3D), and is too similar to SAM3D. R2 and R4 (confidence 3 and 5) acknowledge promise but cite hand-crafted design, hyperparameter sensitivity, and missing baselines.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and key idea.** R1 highlights the intuitive approach of using spatial overlap in 3D Gaussians to achieve multi-view consistency, avoiding reliance on continuous view changes.
- **Simple, effective, and well‑written.** R1, R3, and R4 agree the method is straightforward to implement and the paper is easy to follow. R3 specifically notes the new mask association algorithm leveraging 3D information is simple yet effective.
- **Comprehensive experimental validation.** All reviewers acknowledge extensive testing: R1 mentions “various datasets,” R2 “multiple tasks,” R3 “different 2D models and sparse views,” R4 “challenging scenarios, datasets, and limited data.” Datasets include LERF-Mask, Replica, ScanNet, and MipNeRF 360. R1 further notes a demo video and detailed supplementary materials.
- **Robustness to sparse training views.** R2 and R4 highlight that performance remains high even with significantly reduced training images, enabled by the memory bank’s lessened dependence on view continuity.
- **Versatility with different 2D segmentation models.** The framework works with multiple open‑world zero‑shot models (e.g., SAM), increasing practical applicability.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and heavy reliance on existing components.** R2 calls the approach “straightforward but highly hand‑crafted” with limited innovation. R1 notes that the framework is heavily based on the Identity Encoding of Gaussian Grouping and that the abstract does not mention the use of 3D GS. R3 adds that the mask association algorithm closely resembles SAM3D (which groups point clouds from 2D poses) and fails to discuss similarities/differences or provide a quantitative comparison.
- **Insufficient comparison with state‑of‑the‑art methods.** R3 and R4 strongly criticize the missing baselines. R3 demands comparisons with methods that use linear assignment, contrastive learning, or clustering to bypass mask association, specifically OmniSeg3D (which has a 3D‑GS implementation allowing a direct comparison). R4 requests comparisons with Panoptic‑Lifting and Contrastive‑Lift. R1 also recommends discussing important works [1,2,3,4] (among them methods that merge 3D results in 3D space and assign universal mask IDs from prompts). R3 further notes the qualitative comparison with GARField (Supp Figure 1) is unfair because the model appears not to have converged and rendered RGB is much worse; clustering settings are not provided.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and manual tuning.** R2 and R3 both highlight that key hyperparameters – percentage of nearest Gaussians (e.g., 20%), patch size, overlap ratio – are manually chosen and likely scene‑dependent. R2 worries about robustness under distribution shifts. R3 provides specific evidence from Tables 1, 2, 3 in Supp showing sensitivity. R4 directly questions the fixed 20% nearest‑Gaussian selection, arguing it may exclude relevant Gaussians when objects are sparse or occupy a small portion of the view, leading to incomplete segmentation. R3 additionally notes that using a fixed percentage ignores opacity properties; the optimal percentage depends on Gaussian densities and thus preset parameters may limit generalization to new scenes.
- **Question about fine‑grained accuracy.** R4 asks how the method handles cases where ground‑truth segmentation is more fine‑grained than the mask partitioning (e.g., left and right panels in Replica ground truth that are different masks but merged by the partition), which reduces segmentation accuracy.
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** R2 states that Fig. 4(b) is unclear for showing the problem with the pixel‑wise strategy and that the Gaussian selection process could be more concise. R4 also notes that the approach in Fig. 4 (View 1) may lead to omitted Gaussians from the target partition when background does not influence foreground.
- **Missing outdoor/generalizability tests.** R2 notes that testing is mostly on indoor datasets (Replica, ScanNet) and that outdoor or mixed environments would better demonstrate the method’s generalizability. R2 also asks how performance and hyperparameter sensitivity behave on outdoor data.
- **Black boundary artifact.** R2 observes that in Figures 6 and 7, Gaga with SAM consistently produces a black boundary around objects not seen in other methods, and asks what causes this effect.
- **Applicability to other 3D representations.** R1 asks whether the 3D‑aware memory bank can be used with NeRF or point clouds, and suggests discussing potential adaptations or challenges.
