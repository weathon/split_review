## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a learning framework for Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) based on cognitive appraisal theory, using an LLM-powered agent for emotion reasoning from a third-person perspective. The framework has two phases: a self-evaluation phase where the agent generates appraisals using counterfactual thinking/reflection, and a meta-evaluation phase using offline RL (reflective actor-critic) to fine-tune a specialized LLM on those appraisals. Experiments on IEMOCAP and DailyDialog show improvements over LLM baselines (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma1.1-7B-Instruct, LLAMA-3.1-8B-Instruct). The method provides interpretable predictions.

### Strengths
- Addresses a very relevant problem: LLMs often fail to capture intricacies in emotion recognition within dialogue (Reviewer 1, score 6, confidence 4).
- Improves interpretability of the prediction process, which is useful for downstream applications (Reviewer 1).
- The automatic data labeling method using reflection and offline RL fine-tuning shows better understanding of speakers’ motivations, attitudes, and goals (Reviewer 2, score 5, confidence 3).
- First to introduce reflection mechanisms into emotion evaluation; ablation studies prove the importance of this design choice (Reviewer 2).
- Well-designed experiments with promising results and clear engineering value (Reviewer 2).
- Interesting approach to ERC; emotion reasoning makes sense to mimic human emotion formation (Reviewer 3, score 3, confidence 4).
- Language of the paper is easy to follow (Reviewer 3).
- Motivation for emotional reasoning from a third-person perspective is interesting and promising (Reviewer 4, score 5, confidence 4).
- Outperforms Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma1.1-7B-Instruct, and LLAMA-3.1-8B-Instruct on the IEMOCAP test set (Reviewer 4).
- Large number of ablation experiments verifying effectiveness of appraisal knowledge generation, self-evaluation phase, and meta-evaluation phase; experiments on DailyDialog demonstrate generalization (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Major flaw (Reviewer 3, score 3, confidence 4):** The initial appraisals are generated by providing the true emotion labels in the prompt, thus swaying the LLM in favor of generating appraisals for that emotion. This violates cognitive appraisal theory, where emotions *arise* from appraisals, contradicting the claim in Section 2.3 that "This LLM simulates human cognitive appraisal when reasoning about emotional states." Table 2 shows the "AppraisalKnowledge Prompt" is the main contributor to model performance, likely because it contains the true emotion label, as seen in Figure 1 (appraisal contains "frustrated" and "angry" – the emotion labels).  
  *Contrast:* Reviewer 1 (score 6) praised the prompt as "well crafted" without noting this issue.
- **Over-packaged innovation and limited novelty (Reviewer 2, score 5, confidence 3):** The core work is applying reflection to emotion evaluation, automatic data collection via reflection, and offline RL fine-tuning. Despite complex Figure 2, there is no real feedback loop or collaborative decision-making between modules; the pipeline is one-way: M_X → (M_G ↔ M_E) → M_A. The method uses classic RL methods, no novel system design. Innovation is limited; the interesting contributions are in data collection and engineering optimization. The paper should describe contributions more honestly (suggest submission to applied-innovation venues).
- **Insufficient task generality (Reviewer 1, score 6):** Tested only on a single problem (ERC); would be more impactful if it addressed at least one other reasoning task. It is not clear how to generate a prompt for other tasks without prompt engineering and testing.
- **Inaccurate or inconsistent claims (Reviewer 2):**  
  – Line 77-78 "first to integrate counterfactual thinking into a verbal RL-based strategy" should be "one of the first" to avoid implying no prior RL with counterfactual reasoning.  
  – Two "first" claims (line 77-78 vs line 111-112) are inconsistent; clarification needed.  
  – The prompt details are only in the appendix, making it unsuitable to list the prompt as a contribution in the Introduction (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear terminology** (Reviewer 3): "appraisal knowledge xi" (Section 2.3.1) and "appraisal context xi" (Algorithm 1) are not defined.
- **Typos and formatting issues** (Reviewer 3): Figure 3: "reply" should be "replay"; Table 6: "three datasets" should be "two datasets" (only IEMOCAP and DailyDialog).
- **Figure quality and presentation** (Reviewer 4): Fig. 1, Fig. 2, and Fig. 5 need optimization – font size of emotion labels in Fig. 1, module sizes and alignment in Fig. 2, text box alignment in Fig. 5.
- **Performance not competitive with prior ERC models** (Reviewer 4): Previous ERC works (e.g., [1], [2], [3], [4]) have significantly higher emotion recognition accuracy without using emotional reasoning. Experiments only on a subset of IEMOCAP and DailyDialog; insufficient to demonstrate effectiveness. Additional datasets suggested: MELD, M3ED, EmoryNLP.
- **Missing multimodal and multi-party considerations** (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 3): Multimodal scenarios (visual, audio, textual) are relevant but not addressed. Multi-party conversations and speaker information are not discussed.
- **Unexplained hyperparameters and efficiency** (Reviewer 4): Fixed window length = 5, reflective cycle = 2 – no a priori or empirical justification. Speed of agent’s emotion reasoning compared to previous ERC models is not reported.
- **Unclear whether baselines in Table 1 are finetuned** (Reviewer 3): "Are the LLMs mentioned in Table 1 also finetuned?" (missing detail).
- **Overcomplicated mathematical presentation** (Reviewer 2): Algorithm 1 and equations in Section 2.3.1 express simple "try-feedback-improve" processes in unnecessarily complex ways; should be simplified with a figure instead.
- **Weak related work** (Reviewer 2): Section didn’t clarify why adding reflection to emotion reasoning is necessary – specific limitations of prior work not identified. Suggested to expand on: (a) self-reflection/feedback in emotion evaluation, (b) self-reflection in other LLM agent aspects, (c) cognition in LLM agents. Should explain why direct transfer of reflection from other tasks is insufficient for emotion evaluation.
- **Missing details on data collection** (Reviewer 2): The dataset collected during the "self-evaluation phase" is not characterized (size, quality, statistics). No plan to release it. Only 600 training examples used. Quality assurance mechanisms for automatic data collection are not described (no comparison of cost/quality with human annotations).
- **Unclear why RL/offline-RL was chosen over SFT** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not explain why direct supervised fine-tuning is unsuitable (e.g., handling complex emotional transitions). Also missing advantage in reasoning efficiency from fine-tuning one LLM rather than keeping two frozen reflection LLMs.
