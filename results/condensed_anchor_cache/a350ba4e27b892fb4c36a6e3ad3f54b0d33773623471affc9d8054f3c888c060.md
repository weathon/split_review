## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Candidate Set Query (CSQ), an active learning framework that reduces labeling cost by presenting annotators with a narrowed set of candidate classes instead of all classes. Conformal prediction is used to dynamically generate reliable candidate sets, and an acquisition function balances information gain against labeling cost. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet64x64 show cost reductions (e.g., 42% on ImageNet64x64). Reviewers find the motivation clear and the framework novel, but raise concerns about theoretical soundness, hyperparameter sensitivity, generalizability, missing real-world experiments, and clarity of certain components.

### Strengths
- The Candidate Set Query (CSQ) approach is novel and effectively reduces labeling costs by narrowing candidate classes for annotators.
- Leverages conformal prediction to dynamically generate accurate candidate sets, adapting to model improvements over AL rounds.
- The acquisition function prioritizes samples with high information gain while considering labeling cost, leading to greater efficiency.
- Strong empirical performance across multiple image recognition datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet64x64), consistently outperforming baseline methods by a significant margin.
- Comprehensive ablation studies validate the effectiveness of all components.
- The paper is well presented and the theoretical framework is mostly straightforward to follow.
- Studies the cost of AL queries in a more realistic way and proposes a practical solution.

### Weaknesses
- The method depends on conformal prediction and a calibration set; theoretical soundness is not guaranteed—convergence cannot be established in a proper label complexity analysis, and the labeling cost assumption in Theorem 3.1 is only a rough approximation.
- Explanation of the cost-efficient acquisition function (Eq. 8) is unclear: the interplay between the information gain term and the cost term is not sufficiently detailed, and the role of hyperparameter \(d\) in balancing them is not well explained.
- Performance is sensitive to hyperparameter \(d\) (Fig. 9a), yet the paper provides limited guidelines for setting \(d\) on different datasets and no analysis of how it affects the exploration–exploitation trade-off.
- Benchmarks are limited to image classification datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet64x64) and only a small number of baseline AL methods; it is unclear if CSQ generalizes to other domains or more advanced acquisition methods.
- No experiments on real-world datasets containing label noise, class imbalance, or other imperfections; robustness to noisy annotations is not evaluated.
- For uncertain samples with low confidence across nearly all classes, the proposed framework may be unsuitable—the paper does not address this scenario, and experiments are needed (e.g., sifting out candidate labels with probability < \(0.1 \times 1/C\)).
- The trade-off between labeling cost and information gain is not fully analyzed: a smaller candidate set implies higher certainty, which may contradict the AL goal of selecting uncertain samples. A plot of performance versus number of queried samples would clarify whether CSQ truly maximizes information gain or merely reduces cost at the expense of informativeness.
- Explanation of the empirical quantile in Section 3.2 is unclear: how it is calculated, how it relates to the desired coverage, and how conformal prediction ensures reliability when generalizing from the calibration set to actively sampled data. More detail is needed.
- Several questions highlight missing analyses or potential issues:
  - Is random selection effective for the calibration set?
  - Can the candidate set from Eq. (5) be empty?
  - Is there a guarantee that using the quantile from the previous round works?
  - How are outlier or anomalous data points with high uncertainty handled?
  - Is there a risk of overfitting or drift of confidence scores across successive AL rounds, and how could CSQ address it?
- Table 1: it is unclear whether these results are derived from the cost-efficient acquisition function.
- Minor issues: Fig. 5c has data points for top-1 prediction that fall outside the scale; line 274 contains a typo (“calcuclate” should be “calculate”).
