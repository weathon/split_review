## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the one-to-many mapping problem in point cloud completion by proposing a completion consistency loss consisting of Self-guided Consistency (SgC) and Target-guided Consistency (TgC) losses. The loss enforces that the network generates consistent completions for incomplete objects from the same source point cloud. Experiments on multiple datasets show performance gains when added to several existing networks without modifying their architecture.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and well-organized (R2, R3, R4).
- The motivation (mitigating the one-to-many mapping issue) is important and interesting (R3).
- The method is simple and is demonstrated to be effective to some extent (R4; note that R4 scored the paper 5 while R1, R2, R3 scored 3).
- Extensive experiments are conducted and show promising performance improvements across several networks (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited.** The target-guided consistency (Eq. 3) closely resembles a term from SeedFormer (R1). The self-guided consistency appears to be a many-to-one mechanism rather than addressing the one-to-many issue, and it has been studied previously in weakly-supervised 3D shape completion (Gu et al. 2020) (R1, R2). No theoretical analysis is provided for how the proposed losses actually solve one-to-many mapping; the paper seems to target the many-to-one issue instead (R1, R2).
- **Insufficient validation of key claims.**
  - The claim that predicting only missing points improves performance (Section 2.3) is tested on only a single model (AxForm); more models are needed (R1).
  - The authors argue that “CD(P_a^com, P_b^com) >> CD(P_a1^inc, P_b1^inc)” at the end of the “Complete Loss Function” section, but this is not justified because CD calculates average error (R1).
  - The analysis of the consistency loss in Section 3 is unclear. The claim that contradictory signals are suppressed by L_c-tg and L_c-sg lacks explanation; the two losses appear to provide very close constraints, as shown in Table 6 where equal weights cause performance drops and the weight of L_c-sg must be small (R4). More analysis from a gradient perspective is needed (R4).
- **Missing experiments and evaluations.**
  - No evaluation on real-world datasets (e.g., KITTI) (R1).
  - F-Score is not reported; only CD is used (R1).
  - No ablation study (R1).
  - No time or GPU memory analysis (R1).
  - The proposed loss is plugged into only a subset of methods in Tables 4 and 5; it is unclear whether gains hold for the remaining methods (R3).
  - A SOTA method, AnchorFormer, is missing from the comparisons on ShapeNet55/34 (R2).
- **Questionable experimental details and results.**
  - It is unclear how the incomplete point clouds used for the consistency loss are generated. In PCN, each object has 8 partial views – do these overlap with the point clouds used in training? (R1)
  - The CD results in Table 2 may be biased because they are calculated on the whole point set (including zero-CD partial points). Qualitative comparisons are needed to support the claim about predicting only missing points (R2).
  - The PCN result for PCN baseline in Table 4 (10.55) differs from the original paper (9.636); explanation is needed (R1, R2).
  - AxFormNet is not tested on PCN in Table 4 (R1, R2).
- **Marginal improvement on recent methods.** The improvement on AdaPointTr is small, which suggests that state-of-the-art models may already handle the ambiguity implicitly, making the proposed loss unnecessary (R3). The enhancement for “chair” in Fig. 3 is not obvious (R1).
- **Manuscript organization problems.** Section 2.3 contains experiments rather than related work and should be moved to Section 1. Section 2.2 discusses the optimizer, but the proposed method is a loss function; its motivation is unclear (R1).

*Note:* One reviewer (R4, score 5) judged the method simple and effective to some extent, while three reviewers (R1, R2, R3, all score 3) raised the above substantial concerns about novelty, validation, and experimental rigor.
