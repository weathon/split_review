## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Implicit Knowledge Learning (IKL), a plug-and-play framework for long-tailed recognition consisting of two components: Implicit Uncertainty Regularization (IUR) and Implicit Correlation Labeling (ICL). IUR regularizes the current epoch’s predictions using those from the previous epoch via KL divergence; ICL constructs a label matrix from inter-class similarity (using median features) to complement one-hot labels. Experiments on multiple long-tailed benchmarks show consistent improvements when IKL is added to existing methods.

### Strengths

- The motivation for IUR is clear and well-supported by Figure 1, which shows that uncertainty is especially high for tail classes. The idea of reducing this uncertainty via cross-epoch regularization is reasonable.  
- Learning from inter-class correlations (ICL) is a sensible direction for long-tailed recognition.  
- IKL is practical: it is plug-and-play and can be combined with many existing long-tailed methods, achieving consistent improvements across diverse baselines.  
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow.  
- The discussion of potential limitations (e.g., computational cost) is appreciated.  
- The authors commit to open-sourcing the code to facilitate reproducibility.  
- Experiments are extensive, covering more than three or four baseline methods.  
- The method is simple yet effective.

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical/empirical justification for IUR is insufficient.**  
  All three reviewers question why minimizing the discrepancy between adjacent-epoch predictions should improve generalization, especially when both predictions could be inaccurate. Reviewer 3 calls for a rigorous analysis of the loss landscape and theoretical grounding rather than relying solely on empirical observations. The necessity of using the *last epoch* model (as opposed to a fixed, well-trained teacher model) is not demonstrated (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 notes that the L₍IUR₎ loss is equivalent to cross-entropy with soft labels from the previous epoch, making the method essentially an ensemble of distillation with a biased classifier (L₍IUR₎) and a rebalanced classifier (L₍ICL₎); the novelty of this design is limited.

- **Technical novelty is limited; connections to prior work are insufficiently discussed.**  
  IUR strongly resembles Temporal Ensembling (Laine et al., ICLR 2016) and Mean Teacher (Tarvainen et al., NIPS 2017), but the paper does not compare with or properly reference these methods in the long-tailed setting (Reviewer 1). Similarly, ICL is related to class‑co‑occurrence learning (Chen et al., CVPR 2019) and other label‑dependency approaches; the paper lacks a discussion of what is new. Reviewer 3 adds that IKL is a regularization approach, yet it is not compared to other regularization‑based long-tail solutions such as weight decay or label smoothing.

- **Ablation results raise concerns about practical significance.**  
  In Table 7 (CIFAR-100-LT), IUR and ICL individually yield only marginal improvements. Given the added computational cost (storing previous epoch’s predictions and computing class‑wise similarity matrices), the practical utility is questionable (Reviewer 3). The paper does not analyze the conditions under which IKL provides substantial benefit versus when it is less effective.

- **Missing comparisons with distillation‑based methods.**  
  Reviewer 2 notes the absence of comparisons with other knowledge‑distillation methods for long‑tail recognition. Reviewer 1 also requests comparison with Temporal Ensembling and Mean Teacher in the long‑tail scenario.

- **Incomplete description and justification of key components.**  
  - The median‑feature computation (Eq. 7) is unclear: what is the “medium function”? How are features ranked and the median selected? (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  - No verification is provided that median features are superior to simple averaging for class prototypes; supporting experiments or references are missing (Reviewer 1).  
  - The progressive scaling of α is described only in the experiment section; it should be stated in Section 3 for clarity (Reviewer 1).

- **Effect of ICL is not fully validated.**  
  Reviewer 1 asks for quantitative and qualitative visualization of the learned class‑level correlation matrix on a specific dataset to show that ICL captures meaningful inter‑class similarity.

- **Inconsistent improvement when combined with different baselines.**  
  Reviewer 3 asks why IKL yields a larger gain with RIDE (+1.4 %) than with Softmax (+0.5 %) in Table 5. This inconsistency is not explained.

- **Paper presentation needs improvement.**  
  There are typos (e.g., missing spaces, missing punctuation) and reference errors (e.g., “Balanced meta-softmax” by Ren et al. is NeurIPS 2020, not arXiv). (Reviewer 1)  
  The parameters for each augmentation operation in RandAugment should be provided to aid reproducibility (Reviewer 1).  
  A concrete demonstration of the increased memory/space (saving previous epoch predictions and computing class‑wise similarity matrices) as a function of total samples and number of classes would be helpful for understanding the stated limitation (Reviewer 1).
