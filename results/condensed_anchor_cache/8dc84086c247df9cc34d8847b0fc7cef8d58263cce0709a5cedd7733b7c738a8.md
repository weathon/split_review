## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper systematically evaluates “decoding-free” candidate-selection methods that use logits from autoregressive LMs to score candidates without generating the output sequence. It compares five estimation methods against full decoding, classification, and retrieval baselines on five multiple-choice QA tasks (e.g., CommonsenseQA, MMLU) and four clinical decision tasks (some with 10k+ candidates). Experiments span models of different architectures, sizes, and instruction-tuning. The main finding is that estimation methods sometimes improve performance for models not instruction-tuned, but generally underperform decoding-based selection, especially for instruction-tuned models and longer candidates.

### Strengths
- The problem is practically relevant: avoiding full decoding in candidate-selection tasks (multiple-choice QA, extreme classification) could reduce runtime and simplify gradient-based optimization in preference learning (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The approach is simple to implement and can be faster than retrieval or full-decoding baselines (Reviewer 1).
- The paper provides a systematic evaluation of several logits-based methods, offering a useful reference as LLM applications expand (Reviewers 3,4).
- Experiments cover multiple datasets and model types, giving some actionable insights into method strengths and limitations (Reviewer 3).
- Some positive results are observed for classification tasks with very large candidate pools (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Overall performance is lackluster.** The proposed estimation methods significantly degrade performance compared to standard decoding-based selection in nearly all settings. In Table 3 (short candidates), estimation methods are handily outperformed by instruction-tuned versions of the same LMs. In Table 4 (large candidate pools), improvements are only seen over Flan-T5, which is itself a very weak baseline relative to instruct-tuned LMs (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- **Performance degrades with longer candidate lengths** – precisely the setting where decoding-free methods would be most beneficial for speed (Reviewer 1).
- **No actionable recommendations.** The paper lacks clear guidance on which method to use in which context. Insights are limited to numerical comparisons without deeper investigation into why certain techniques work better on some tasks than others (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Missing key comparisons and factors:**
  - No comparison with chain-of-thought decoding, which could change both speed and accuracy trade-offs (Reviewer 2).
  - Calibration of logits (e.g., Zhao et al. 2021) is not addressed, yet it is a standard best practice for decoding-based selection (Reviewer 4).
  - Standard classification benchmarks (e.g., GLUE) are missing, and the choice of clinical decision tasks is not well-motivated, potentially biasing results (Reviewer 3).
  - Speed measurements are not reported for large candidate‑pool tasks; the authors expect decoding-free methods might be slower than decoding-based ones in that setting, making the practical speed benefit unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of novelty.** The paper is primarily an analysis/evaluation of existing methods and proposes no new techniques. This limits its contribution for a top-tier venue (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Terminology is inconsistent: “decoding-free methods,” “estimation methods,” and “candidate selection without decoding” are used interchangeably (Reviewers 2,4).
  - Some claims are imprecise: e.g., “cut off the gradient flow and prevent optimization” without specifying the optimization context; “trajectory biases” is undefined; “estimation methods rely solely on the logits without capturing token dependencies” lacks a precise explanation (Reviewer 4).
  - The experimental setup and results are not organized in a standard format; combining all setups in one section and results in another hurts clarity (Reviewer 3).
  - Writing could be more concise (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing or unclear explanations of observed patterns:**
  - In Table 4, estimation methods consistently reduce performance for instruction‑tuned models, yet one might expect instruction‑tuned models to have more calibrated logits. The paper offers no analysis or interpretation (Reviewer 4).
  - Different estimation techniques vary across tasks, but the paper only notes this without investigating why (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear definitions.** The term “decoding” in “full decoding” is not explicitly defined – logits are part of the decoding process, so what specifically makes logits‑based methods “decoding‑free”? (Reviewer 3). The mapping function from candidate logits to scores is task‑dependent but not reported in the comparison table (Reviewer 3). Also, line 462: why is “first token” not part of the prompt? Usually the first token refers to the first token of the answer (Reviewer 4).
