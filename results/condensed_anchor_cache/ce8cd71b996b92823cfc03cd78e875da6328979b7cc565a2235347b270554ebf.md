## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the LCA-on-the-Line framework, which uses Least Common Ancestor (LCA) distance derived from a class hierarchy (e.g., WordNet) as an in-domain metric to predict out-of-distribution (OOD) top-1 accuracy. Experiments on 75 models (vision-only and vision-language) across ImageNet and five OOD variants show a strong linear correlation between in-domain LCA distance and OOD accuracy. The paper also explores constructing taxonomies via K-means clustering and using LCA-based soft labels to improve generalization.

### Strengths
- The idea of using LCA distance for OOD generalization prediction is simple, intuitive, well-motivated, and effective (Reviewer 1, score 8).
- Experimental analyses are extensive: 75 network variants covering ResNet, VGG, EfficientNet, ViT, and VLMs; this comprehensiveness enables conclusions that may generalize across architectures (Reviewer 1, score 8; Reviewer 2, score 5).
- The analysis demonstrates a useful correlation: images from OOD datasets with more distant LCA relationships to the training data tend to be easier to detect as anomalies, with an intuitive explanation based on semantic separation in the hierarchy (Reviewer 2, score 5).
- The work is likely significant for industry applications where predicting OOD performance before deployment is important (Reviewer 1, score 8).
- Addressing the challenge of assessing model generalization under OOD conditions is interesting (Reviewer 3, score 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of LCA explanation (Reviewer 1, score 8):** The description is complicated and relies heavily on the reader’s prior knowledge of WordNet and hierarchical structures. A visual illustration with a small tree (e.g., ‘dog’, ‘cat’, ‘wolf’, ‘husky’, ‘siamese cat’) showing LCA values for example pairs would greatly improve understanding.
- **Fig. 1 readability (Reviewer 1, score 8; Reviewer 3, score 3):** The figure is very difficult to read; the legend is too small and too much information is crammed in. Reviewer 1 suggests showing only top-1 with larger fonts in the main paper and moving the full figure to supplementary. Reviewer 3 notes that more interpretation of Fig. 1 is needed.
- **Clarity of the inferred class taxonomy method (Reviewer 1, score 8):** The explanation of how the taxonomy is built from K-means clustering is unclear—specifically the phrase “establishing the cluster level where both classes share the same cluster as the height of LCA.” Important details are missing: which distance metric is used for clustering, and how the number of clusters at each level is determined. A figure illustrating the cluster hierarchy and how LCA is derived would help.
- **Missing comparisons with OOD scoring methods (Reviewer 2, score 5):** The paper does not evaluate multiple OOD scoring methods (e.g., energy scores, ODIN, Mahalanobis distance, ReAct). The interaction between scoring method and LCA distance remains unclear, and the LCA calculation is limited to ImageNet variants using WordNet.
- **Lack of concrete actionable techniques (Reviewer 2, score 5):** The paper claims “invaluable insights and actionable techniques” to enhance robustness and generalization, but no concrete solutions leveraging LCA distance are proposed or analyzed. Further theoretical or empirical analysis connecting these insights to improved generalization would be beneficial.
- **Introduction clarity and motivation (Reviewer 3, score 3):** The Introduction is unclear. Statements like “most methods involve modeling correlations with in-domain accuracy or agreement” and “many studies evaluate generalization on OOD datasets that feature limited visual shifts” are vague. The authors do not clearly interpret whether the research is meaningful nor sufficiently introduce the advantages of the taxonomy loss. A figure describing the motivation is recommended.
- **Novelty of experiments and architectures (Reviewer 3, score 3):** The evaluated methods in Table 1 are somewhat old. The paper should verify the proposed method on more state-of-the-art models (e.g., works from CVPR 2023, ICLR 2023). Additionally, only classification performance is evaluated; other tasks such as object detection or semantic segmentation should be considered.
- **Typos and formatting (Reviewer 1, score 8; Reviewer 2, score 5):**
  - Abstract: “Beside” → “Besides”.
  - Introduction: missing space in “effective robustness(Taori et al., 2020)”.
  - Above contributions: “in measure model’s semantic awareness” → “in measuring a model’s semantic awareness”.
  - Sec. 2 and Sec. 3.3: brackets and missing spaces in equations (e.g., “lcad(y′, y) := f(y) − f(lca(y, y′), where f(y) ≥ f(lca(y, y′) and lca((y′, y) denotes…”).
  - Incorrect LaTeX quotes in several places.
  - Paragraph below Table 3: “Table3” → “Table 3”.
  - Sec. 3.3 (last paragraph): “natural image(ImageNet)” → “natural image (ImageNet)”.
- **Disagreement on significance:** Reviewer 1 (score 8) finds the contributions likely significant for industry, while Reviewer 3 (score 3) questions whether the research is meaningfully framed and finds the experiments insufficiently state-of-the-art.
