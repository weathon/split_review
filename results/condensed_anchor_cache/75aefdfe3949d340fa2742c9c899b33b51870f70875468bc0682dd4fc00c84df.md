## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies depth-width tradeoffs in neural networks by introducing “intra-layer links”—direct additive connections between neurons within the same hidden layer. It re-examines depth separation theory (Telgarsky’s hard “sawtooth” functions) and shows that a shallow network with intra-layer links requires significantly less width (e.g., at most \(\log(w)k+2\) vs. \(w^k\) for a standard network) to represent such functions. The authors also bound the number of linear pieces that intra-linked networks can generate (Theorems 4, 6) and provide explicit constructions (Theorem 10, Lemma 11). Experiments on synthetic data and real-world datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet) are included. Reviewers generally found the question interesting but raised major concerns about the validity, novelty, and practical relevance of the intra-layer link architecture.

### Strengths
- The paper identifies an important limitation of existing depth separation theory, which is based solely on fully-connected feedforward networks, and adds novel points about the power of width when augmented with intra-layer links. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The conceptual contribution—that intra-layer links can substantially reduce the width needed to represent certain hard functions (e.g., sawtooth)—is nice and could spark community interest. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The theoretical analysis is comprehensive, with clear theorems and proofs: the bounds on linear regions (Theorem 4 vs. Theorem 6 for extra-linked vs. intra-linked networks), and the explicit approximation guarantees for sawtooth functions (Theorem 10, Lemma 11). The differences between extra-layer and intra-layer links are well explained via these results. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The paper provides both synthetic and real-world experiments (CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet) that support the theoretical findings. (Reviewer 3)
- The results are mathematically correct (Reviewer 5), even though this reviewer considers them weak in contribution.
- An added practical insight: intra-layer links do not contain trainable parameters, so they may reduce the total parameter count when replacing deeper networks with shallower intra-linked ones. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Practical relevance and justification of intra-layer links are severely lacking.** The proposed intra-layer architecture is not used in practice; it is artificial and may have limited appeal to experimental researchers. Existing popular networks like ResNet and DenseNet use *across*-layer links (with restricted identity mappings), which are fundamentally different from the unrestricted intra-layer links studied here. The paper does not adequately justify why one should study this specific architecture. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5)  
- **The architecture’s computational limitations are not addressed.** Intra-layer links force sequential computation within a layer, preventing parallelization via GPUs—a critical drawback for practical deployment. This trade-off is ignored. (Reviewer 4)  
- **The theoretical novelty is questionable.** The analysis largely follows the well-known pattern of counting linear regions (Telgarsky, Montufar) and does not highlight specific new technical challenges. The results on sawtooth functions are “relatively straightforward” given prior work. (Reviewer 2)  
- **The fundamental claim that intra-links are different from adding depth is unconvincing.** Several reviewers (2, 3, 4) argue that an intra-layer link is effectively equivalent to inserting an extra layer (increasing depth), because information flows sequentially from one neuron to the next through the link. The paper’s rebuttal (page 5) does not provide sufficient technical depth to distinguish the function classes achievable with intra-links vs. deeper standard networks. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)  
- **A minority reviewer (Reviewer 5) argues that the intra-link assumption is *too strong* and renders the results trivial.** Specifically, Reviewer 5 claims that a two-layer intra-linked network with width \(\sum_i w_i\) can simulate any classic deep network of width \(w_1,\ldots,w_k\) by partitioning neurons into groups and using intra-layer links to propagate sequentially—effectively making a single intra-linked layer equivalent to an entire deep network. This would make the limited sawtooth result weak; the minimum expectation would be a proof that intra-linked networks can represent any standard deep network. (Reviewer 5 – high confidence, score 1)  
- **The theoretical results are confined to a specific class of functions** (sawtooth, used in prior depth-separation lower bounds). It is unclear whether intra-layer links would be beneficial in more general settings, limiting the impact. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Missing comparisons, clarifications, and ablations needed:**  
  - The definition of “zero-points” is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
  - Are bi-directional intra-layer links helpful? (Reviewer 1)  
  - What are the trade-offs for adding as many intra-layer links as possible in a fixed architecture? Why not connect all neurons? (Reviewer 1)  
  - The experiments should indicate how many intra-layer links were added and whether increasing their number affects optimization, generalization, or computational efficiency. (Reviewer 1)  
  - When is it better to use intra-layer vs. extra-layer links? Comparisons with plain ResNets are needed. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The authors should provide a concrete, simple example showing why an intra-linked network cannot be directly “unrolled” into a deeper standard network, to clarify the claimed distinction. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The significance of the theoretical improvement over prior work (e.g., exact width savings) should be made clearer for readers outside learning theory. (Reviewer 3)
