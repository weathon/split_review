## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Graph-Constrained Reasoning (GCR), which converts a KG into a KG-Trie (trie-based index of reasoning paths) and integrates it into the LLM decoding process. This ensures that generated reasoning paths are entirely grounded in the KG, eliminating hallucination. GCR uses a lightweight KG-specialized LLM for constrained decoding and a powerful general LLM for inductive reasoning over multiple paths. Experiments on KGQA benchmarks (WebQSP, CWQ, CSQA, MedQA) show strong performance and zero-shot generalizability to unseen KGs without additional training.

### Strengths
1. **Faithful reasoning guarantee** – All generated reasoning paths are verifiable in the KG (Figure 5). The method achieves 100% faithful reasoning (Reviewer 1).
2. **Token efficiency** – Requires fewer average LLM tokens and fewer LLM calls during inference compared to related methods (Table 2) (Reviewer 1).
3. **Methodological soundness** – The motivation is clear, and the conversion of a KG into a KG-Trie with graph-constrained decoding is technically sound and reasonable (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
4. **Strong empirical results** – Extensive experiments on multiple KGQA benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of GCR (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 4 specifically highlights the interesting zero-shot generalizability results.
5. **Paper quality** – Well written and organized, easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **KG-Trie preprocessing is computationally expensive** – Building the KG-Trie requires BFS (Equation 3) to extract all paths of length *L* around every entity (since the query entity is unknown ahead of time). With *L*=2 used in experiments, and even larger *L* needed for multi-hop reasoning, this becomes prohibitive on graphs with millions of entities and edges. The time/space complexity is not analyzed theoretically or empirically, and the query‑dependent nature means offline pre‑construction may not generalise to new inputs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 notes that the tokenizer‑level decoding further increases runtime (Table 2). Reviewer 3 specifically asks for space complexity discussion.
2. **Missing graph reasoning baselines** – State‑of‑the‑art link prediction GNNs such as NBFNet and ULTRA (applicable on Freebase and ConceptNet) are not included in the experiments (Reviewer 1).
3. **Need for more diverse datasets and analysis of significance** – Only two datasets (WebQSP, CWQ) are used in the main KGQA table (Table 1); on WebQSP the margin over GNN‑RAG + RA is small. Only two more datasets (CSQA, MedQA) are used in zero‑shot experiments (Table 6); the improvement on MedQA is not significant. More datasets would strengthen the conclusions (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 also notes the MedQA improvement is not particularly significant.
4. **Definition and analysis of faithful reasoning** – The definition in Section 5.2 is too broad: wrong reasoning paths can still lead to correct answers, as many papers point out. The paper claims zero reasoning hallucination, but fails to analyse cases of unfaithful reasoning or provide error examples. Real‑world KGs are incomplete and contaminated; GCR’s behaviour when entities are missing or paths are unreliable is not addressed (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
5. **Tokenizer‑level decoding limitations** – Because decoding operates at the tokenizer level, the generated entities or relations may not be recognisable in the KG, invalidating the model. Moreover, this decoding approach substantially increases runtime compared to similar methods (Table 2) (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 asks for the additional time cost of GCR over vanilla ChatGPT / GPT‑4o‑mini.
6. **Lack of comparison with simpler alternatives and design justifications** – The key reason GCR outperforms RoG (which seems a simplified version of GCR) is not fully explained. Only one example is given; more cases would clarify. The choice of BFS vs. DFS and tokenizer‑ vs. word‑level decoding is not justified (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 asks how post‑validation of beam‑search paths via graph querying compares to step‑by‑step Trie constraining in time/space. Reviewer 4 asks about the sensitivity of beam size (set to 10) and how GCR handles multiple valid paths or irrelevant introduced information.
7. **Insufficient discussion of related KG‑enhanced methods** – The paper should discuss more KG‑enhanced approaches for reducing knowledge hallucinations, e.g., [A, B] (Reviewer 4).
8. **Limited handling of temporal/dynamic KGs** – The pre‑constructed KG‑Trie strategy is not designed for temporal or dynamic KGs; the paper does not address how it could be used in such settings (Reviewer 4).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 3) is markedly more critical, emphasising the runtime increase from tokenizer‑level decoding, the broad definition of faithful reasoning, and the insufficient comparison with RoG. Another reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive, praising the clear motivation, sound technique, and extensive experiments. The remaining reviewers (both score 5) are moderately positive but raise substantial concerns about preprocessing complexity and evaluation breadth.
