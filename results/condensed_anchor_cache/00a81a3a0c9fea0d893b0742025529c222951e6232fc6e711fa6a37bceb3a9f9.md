## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a new problem setting – privacy-aware lifelong learning – in which a model must learn a sequence of tasks while supporting exact unlearning of a previously encountered task upon request. The authors introduce PALL, which uses task-specific sparse subnetworks within a single architecture (via learned masks) to isolate and share knowledge. Upon unlearning, the weights allocated to the forgotten task are reset and a light-weight retraining with memory buffer replay and logit regularization is performed to restore performance on subsequent tasks. The method is evaluated on image classification benchmarks, compared to adapted baselines from lifelong learning and several ablations.

### Strengths
- Novel problem setting combining lifelong learning and exact task unlearning, well-defined and less explored; first work to provide an exact unlearning solution in a lifelong setting of interleaved learned and unlearned tasks.
- The proposed method PALL is sound and performs well on experiments, enabling learning without catastrophic forgetting, forward knowledge transfer, and exact unlearning with memory efficiency.
- Subnetwork masks provide a unified perspective for controlling catastrophic forgetting and enabling exact task unlearning.
- Memory-efficient compared to baselines (only binary masks stored per task).
- Extensive experimental evaluation covering various baselines and ablations; scalability demonstrated on both convolutional architectures and attention-based vision transformers (e.g., ViT).
- A better metric proposed for measuring performance in the sequential task learning-and-unlearning setting.
- Well-written and easy to follow.
- Discussion of future direction for class-incremental learning.

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and contribution**:
- The problem setting lacks significant innovation compared to existing problems; objectives could also hold for domain-incremental learning and unlearning, and it is unclear what specific challenges require a new formulation.
- The methods presented are well-established (based on Winning Subnetworks, WSN); core mechanism of subnetwork masks is directly adopted from WSN; modifications (e.g., reinitializing scores and unused weights, adding experience replay) appear incremental rather than fundamentally novel. Overall contribution is limited.
- Equation 6 (logit regularization loss with buffer) is not novel; it is essentially the same as DER++ (Buzzega et al., 2020) with a tunable β; phrasing gives a misleading impression of original generalization.
- Claim of exact unlearning is only empirical; may not hold on real-world data or foundational models.

**Clarity and description**:
- Insufficient description (in main paper) of how baseline methods were adapted from standard lifelong learning to the privacy-aware setting; e.g., how regularization-based methods were applied to unlearning tasks.
- Clarity issues: difference between m_t and \bar m_t not explained; metric A_t ambiguous (whether final checkpoint or per-task checkpoint is used); static vs. dynamic sparsity configurations not clearly described (e.g., initialization and optimization processes).
- Notation issues in Equations 1–3 (e.g., θ_0 in loss, U and L not introduced, constraint in Eq. 3 missing inequality).
- α (weight for logit regularization) is not clearly introduced; later used without redefinition.
- The two ablated configurations “Static Sparse Ind.” and “Dynamic Sparse Ind.” need clearer explanation of their setup and how they differ from PALL; the claim that PALL additionally supports knowledge transfer is not obvious given that new tasks only modify previously-unused weights – the mechanism for transfer (use of other weights in forward pass) should be clarified.

**Experiment design and baselines**:
- Missing important baselines: (i) training sequentially with Differential Privacy (DP) to achieve approximate unlearning without weight resetting; (ii) approximate unlearning methods from the unlearning literature (e.g., post-hoc influence removal); (iii) retraining the entire model from scratch upon unlearning (using buffers); (iv) an “oracle” variant with access to all past data.
- Missing comparison with latest state-of-the-art continual learning models (the cited references for regularization-based methods are dated).
- The “independent subnetworks without knowledge transfer” ablation performs very similarly to PALL (Table 3, ~1% difference, no confidence intervals), making it hard to justify the additional complexity; it is unclear if the differences are significant and whether other task sequences would better showcase the benefit of knowledge transfer.
- Compared methods are not originally designed for this setting; PALL’s better performance, especially on unlearning, is expected and not surprising.
- Fixed memory budget α=1/T may be unfair across datasets with different numbers of tasks (e.g., CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100).
- Some experiments are not directly illuminating (e.g., Table 2 on buffer size effect on CIFAR-10 is obvious).
- Training efficiency (time per task) is not compared; WSN is known to be slow due to parameter-level gradient masking, and PALL likely inherits this cost.

**Metrics and privacy guarantees**:
- The A_u metric (accuracy on unlearned tasks) is not sufficient for unlearning guarantees; lower accuracy does not imply privacy, correlates poorly with more rigorous unlearning metrics, and can even be a vulnerability (accuracy too low may leak information). The authors acknowledge this but still rely on it.
- No empirical privacy auditing or comparison between exact and inexact unlearning regarding privacy strength is provided (though acknowledged as substantial work).

**Memory and scalability**:
- Memory complexity grows linearly in number of tasks because binary masks are stored per task; for many tasks (e.g., 50) and large models, mask storage can become non-trivial, diminishing memory efficiency gains.
- Scalability needs further demonstration on larger datasets (more tasks, more unlearning requests); current experiments limited to 40 tasks (S-TinyImage) and 9 unlearning requests; no evaluation on larger task sets like Omniglot-Rotation (100 tasks).
- Dynamic sparsity benefits not clearly demonstrated; static sparsity may produce comparable performance if model size is increased.

**Setting limitations**:
- Only task-incremental setting considered; applicability to class-incremental or domain-incremental settings (where task IDs are unknown at inference) is not addressed.
- Choice of task-incremental over class- or domain-incremental is not justified.
- Explicit task identification poses a privacy vulnerability by creating a direct link between data and task, potentially revealing sensitive information about the data source.
- The framework assumes user data is cleanly separated by tasks; it cannot support user deletion when users participate across all tasks.
- Reliance on a memory buffer (even small) for replay with logit regularization undermines privacy claims by storing and accessing data from previous tasks.
- No experiments on language data, where the right-to-be-forgotten is especially critical.
