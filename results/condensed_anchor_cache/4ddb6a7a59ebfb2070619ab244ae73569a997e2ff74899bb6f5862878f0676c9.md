## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SingleInsert, a two-stage diffusion-based image-to-text inversion method for single-image concept customization. The approach uses foreground-background disentanglement via segmentation masks and a background loss, along with a semantic loss to prevent language drift. It claims high visual fidelity, flexible editing, single-image novel view synthesis, and multiple concept composition without joint training. The paper also introduces an Editing Success Rate (ESR) metric and an editing prompt list for quantitative evaluation.

### Strengths
- The paper is very easy to follow.
- The foreground-background disentanglement is a reasonable and straightforward approach.
- The method enables more flexible editing for inverted concepts, surpassing its baselines.
- The use of predicted distributions from the original T2I model for regularization is novel.
- The editing prompt list and ESR metric provide a quantitative evaluation framework for editing flexibility.
- The ablation studies clearly demonstrate the value of each design component.
- The motivations and designs of the new loss functions are clearly illustrated.

### Weaknesses
- **Weak novel view synthesis claim** (Reviewer 1): The experiments are insufficient. The method cannot accurately control viewpoint angle, only using text prompts “left side”, “frontal”, and “back side”. No comparison to previous work is provided, and generated images show drastic changes in background and even foreground appearance, undermining the claim.
- **Multiple concept composition** (Reviewer 1): Only demonstrated with a few examples, without comparison to previous methods.
- **Insufficient description of ESR and editing prompt list** (Reviewers 1 and 3): The metric is presented too briefly in the main text, requiring readers to consult the supplemental material for basic understanding. As a claimed contribution, more detail on calculation, significance, and advantages over existing metrics is needed.
- **Missing comparison to related works** (Reviewers 2 and 3): The paper omits several relevant baselines: E4T (requires 5–15 steps, while the proposed method uses 100 steps), Dreambooth (whose Dreambench includes recontextualization, accessorization, and property modification prompts, yet the paper’s prompts are less comprehensive), Taming encoder for zero fine-tuning, InstantBooth, and Enhancing Detail Preservation. These works should be discussed and compared.
- **Lack of novelty** (Reviewer 3, minority critical view): While other reviewers found the approach reasonable, one reviewer strongly argues that the idea is not novel—using masks for object embedding and background loss is common, and the two-stage process lacks sufficient differentiation from existing techniques. The additional complexity (two-stage training plus extra losses) is not adequately justified, especially without comparisons to the omitted baselines.
- **Practicality and generalizability concerns** (Reviewer 3): The model requires retraining for each new concept, relies on LoRA for fidelity in the finetuning stage, and fine-tunes the whole T2I model and a ViT-B image encoder for the single-source scenario, limiting its practicality and generalizability.
- **Limited quantitative evaluation** (Reviewer 2): The evaluation does not follow the Dreambooth setting; the prompts used (for both human faces and objects) are less comprehensive than the standard Dreambench (which includes recontextualization, accessorization, and property modifications). More comprehensive comparisons are needed.
- **Missing details and unanswered questions** (Reviewers 1 and 2):
    - The method is not tested on multiple-source images, despite finetuning many parameters (Reviewer 1 question).
    - Details about web-collected data (composition of common objects vs. human faces, number of samples) are not provided (Reviewer 2).
    - The extra memory required for the frozen T2I model under LoRA is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
    - The total fine-tuning time in seconds/minutes for customizing a new test image is not reported (Reviewer 2).
    - It is unclear whether pre-training on a large-scale dataset could reduce fine-tuning time, or whether Hyper-Dreambooth finetunes the T2I part (Reviewer 3 questions).
