## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ToMA, a plug-and-play token merging method for transformer-based diffusion models. ToMA improves over prior work (ToMeSD) via three components: (1) a submodular function-based greedy algorithm to select a diverse, representative subset of tokens as merge destinations; (2) an attention-based merge/unmerge that leverages efficient GPU attention implementations and is formulated as (inverse) linear transformations, allowing sharing across layers and diffusion steps; and (3) a local-region constraint that limits merging to spatially adjacent tokens, further reducing computation. Experiments on SDXL for text-to-image generation (GEMRec, ImageNet-1k) report 30%-50% speedups with minimal loss in CLIP, DINO, and FID scores. Code is provided.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated and intuitive methodology.** The use of submodular optimization for token selection is reasonable and effectively reduces information loss during merging (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **GPU-aware design.** The method carefully considers modern GPU architectures (e.g., efficient attention implementations, vectorized optimization, invertible merging) to achieve real speedups (R2, R5).
- **Significant acceleration without performance drop.** Reported 30%-50% inference speedups on SDXL while maintaining quantitative metrics (R2, R4, R5). The 30%-50% figure is explicitly noted by Reviewer 2.
- **Practical sharing mechanisms.** Sharing destination selections and merge matrices across layers and steps reduces overhead and enhances practicality (R5).
- **Comprehensive quantitative evaluation.** Experiments use CLIP, DINO, and FID on high-quality datasets; the balance between efficiency and image quality is demonstrated (R3).
- **Reproducibility.** The paper provides detailed implementation and code, which reviewers note supports reproducibility (R1).
- **Clear presentation overall.** The paper is well-crafted and effectively articulates the methodology and experimental outcomes (R1). The method is methodical and straightforward to implement (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** The evaluation compares only to ToMeSD. Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3) note the absence of comparisons to other token merging/pruning methods such as CrossGET, TRIPS, DiffRate, ToFu, FRDiff, Token Downsampling, Flash Attention, and Sparse Transformer. This limits assessment of relative performance.
- **Restricted to SDXL and text-to-image generation.** Experiments are conducted only on SDXL, despite the paper’s claim of applicability to any attention-based T2I model. Reviewers request results on SD2, SD3, and especially DiT-based models (e.g., PixelArt-Alpha, Flux) where larger speedups may be possible and locality assumptions may differ (R2, R4, R5).
- **Concerns about ToMeSD baseline reproduction.** Results in Table 1 show that ToMeSD inference time is longer than the baseline (no merging), contradicting the original ToMeSD paper. Reviewers question whether the same torch/xformer versions were used, casting doubt on the validity of comparisons (R1, R2).
- **Limited novelty.** Reviewer 2 considers ToMA an enhanced version of ToMeSD with updated merge rules and locality optimizations, and Reviewer 3 notes that the locality-based attention restriction is already explored in Sparse Transformer, DiffRate, and ToFu. The minority reviewers (R2, R3) find the contributions not substantial, while the majority (R1, R4, R5) find the method well-designed.
- **Lack of qualitative analysis.** The paper shows almost no visual examples, making it hard to assess generation quality meaningfully. Reviewer 4 specifically points out that in Figure 6, ToMA introduces noticeable noise compared to the original; quantitative indicators alone may not capture such degradation (R3, R4).
- **Unusually high FID scores.** FID values in Figure 5 exceed 25 for ImageNet, which Reviewer 2 notes is unusually high and may indicate quality issues.
- **Limited speedup at lower ratios.** At a merge ratio of 0.25, the speedup is only ~10%, and even at 0.75 a 20% speedup comes with a significant quality decline (R2).
- **Figure quality and clarity.** Reviewer 5 notes that Figure 5 is hard to differentiate between methods (recommends distinct colors/markers and error bars). Reviewer 2 also mentions unclear axis labels (e.g., X-axis in Figure 5). Reviewer 5 additionally flags redundant content (lines L142-L150 and L151-L155).
- **Missing discussion of DiT models.** Reviewer 5 strongly criticizes the lack of discussion on how ToMA can be adapted to DiT-like models without convolution layers, where locality assumptions rely on positional embeddings. Questions about submodular behavior with positional encodings remain unanswered.
- **Missing engagement with related work.** Reviewer 1 notes insufficient discussion of token merging works such as CrossGET and TRIPS.
- **Open questions on design choices.**  
  - How does the submodular destination selection compare to Farthest Point Sampling in terms of efficiency (R1)?  
  - How is the size of the destination set determined (R4)?  
  - Could sharing destinations and attention weights across layers cause notable performance loss in smaller steps (R4)?  
  - The terms "Dino" and "Clip" in line 475 need alignment with Table 3 entries (R4).  
  - Details on tile-shaped region implementation are requested (R1).
- **Title misleading.** The paper focuses only on generative tasks, but the title suggests broader applicability to diffusion models beyond generation (R1).
