## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ARVideo, an autoregressive pretraining framework for self-supervised video representation learning. Two key designs are introduced: (1) grouping adjacent spatiotemporal video tokens into clusters (via a fixed rule based on location) to aggregate richer context, and (2) adopting a randomized spatiotemporal prediction order for autoregressive next-token prediction. With a ViT-B backbone, ARVideo achieves 81.2% on Kinetics-400 and 70.9% on Something-Something V2, on par with VideoMAE, while training 14% faster and requiring 58% less GPU memory.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with a clear introduction, method, and experimental sections (R4, R5).
- The method demonstrates awareness of spatiotemporal structure and the benefit of randomness for pretraining, yielding competitive performance on Kinetics (R1). It introduces a new autoregressive pretraining framework for richer contextual video representations (R3).
- Training efficiency is credibly improved: 14% faster training and 58% lower GPU memory compared to VideoMAE, with a clear side-by-side comparison (R2).
- Extensive ablation studies effectively demonstrate the benefits of the proposed components (R5). Experimental results support the performance and efficiency claims (R4).
- One reviewer (R3, score 5) viewed the framework as a practical paradigm for self-supervised video representation learning.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and marginal improvements.** The method is largely an autoregressive variant of mask modeling, offering incremental contribution (R4). Performance gains over VideoMAE are marginal (R4, R5). Missing comparisons with more recent and stronger baselines: VideoMAE-v2, ST‑MAE, OmniMAE, MVD, MME (R4, R5). The autoregressive random order is not more expressive than predicting all masked tokens at once as in VideoMAE (R2). Overall, the contribution is not enough to be considered a significant advance (R1, R4).
- **Lack of theoretical justification for the randomized order.** The benefit of randomized spatiotemporal prediction order is empirically shown but lacks theoretical explanation; it is unclear whether it merely acts as data augmentation (R1). Ablations suggest the prediction order setup does not significantly impact performance (R2). A deeper analysis of the higher attention rank scores (Fig. 3) and an interpretation of randomness from an autoregressive training perspective are needed (R3).
- **“Clustering” concept is misleading and poorly explained.** Tokens are simply aggregated by a fixed rule based on spatiotemporal location, not by any active clustering mechanism; the aggregation operation (average pooling? max pooling?) is not specified (R2). The performance gains from grouping neighboring tokens are not justified (R5). What is the difference between this grouping and using a larger video token? (R1). The term “clustering” is a misnomer (R2, R5).
- **Reliance on fine-tuning protocol instead of frozen evaluation.** All comparisons and ablations use the fine-tuning protocol, which is less informative. The community should adopt frozen evaluation as the gold standard; recent work like V‑JEPA shows video SSL models can perform well on frozen tasks (R2). The paper does not report frozen linear probing results. How is fine-tuning performed – is it also autoregressive? (R4).
- **Underemphasis on the role of masking.** The method masks 80% of tokens within each unit but underemphasizes its reliance on mask-based modeling; it is essentially a variant of mask modeling in autoregressive form (R5). It is unclear whether the improvements come from the AR formulation or from the clustering/masking itself; a direct comparison with a mask modeling method using the same cluster and masking strategy (e.g., a VideoMAE variant) is needed (R4).
- **Missing justification for autoregressive over alternative SSL paradigms.** The paper does not provide strong justification for choosing autoregressive pretraining over contrastive learning or mask modeling (R4). The ablation on prediction order shows little performance impact, further weakening the case for the autoregressive design (R2).
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** Figure captions (Fig. 1, Fig. 7) lack sufficient detail (R3). Table 5’s “Q” and “Key/Value” columns are unclear (R2). The visualizations in Table 7 are not informative and could be moved to the appendix (R2). Details on the benchmark dataset and hyperparameters used for the analysis in Figure 3 are missing (R3).
