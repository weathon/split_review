## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces InversionGNN, a dual-path graph neural network for multi-objective molecular optimization in drug discovery. It comprises a direct prediction path (GNN trained for multi-property prediction) and an inversion generation path that uses gradient-based Pareto optimization to generate molecules satisfying multiple properties, including a relaxation technique to handle discrete chemical space. Empirical evaluations on synthetic tasks and real-world drug discovery datasets show sample efficiency and exploration of Pareto fronts. Reviewers note the method addresses an important problem, has a good overall presentation, and includes extensive experiments, but raise concerns about comparative evaluations, clarity of core concepts, theoretical assumptions, and practical significance of contributions.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem in multi-objective molecular optimization; overall presentation, writing, and visualizations are good (R1, R3).
- The gradient-based Pareto inversion module aligns with multi-objective optimization intuition and offers strong interpretability (R1).
- Experimental analysis is thorough, validating extensive Pareto front exploration not limited to molecular domain; ablation study provides interesting insights (e.g., incremental substructure addition aligns with weighted vectors) (R1).
- The dual-path GNN effectively combines property prediction with molecular generation, integrating learned chemical knowledge into optimization (R2).
- Demonstrates significant sample efficiency in exploring chemical space, requiring fewer oracle calls (R2).
- Includes extensive experimental evaluations across various real-world datasets and multi-objective settings, providing robust evidence of effectiveness (R1, R2, R3).
- Theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2) offers insights into convergence properties, providing a framework for future research (R2).
- Method performs optimization in discrete chemical space, which is methodologically innovative compared to traditional latent space-based methods (R4).
- Comprehensive literature review situating work well within existing field (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Claimed superiority questionable:** On close examination of Table 1, DST (most competitive baseline) performs nearly on par with InversionGNN across metrics, raising the question whether the gradient-based Pareto inversion module has limited impact and whether the tokenization approach of the scaffolding tree is actually more critical for molecular design. (R1)
- **Insufficient time complexity analysis:** QP-solving complexity can be significant in practice. While the paper suggests computing non-dominating gradient is negligible in theory, a comparison of computation times across methods is recommended. (R1)
- **Oracle budget selection not explained:** Rationale for oracle call budgets for each baseline is unclear. Authors should control for a consistent budget across models to ensure fair supervision signals. (R1)
- **Ambiguous definition of neighborhood set:** How the neighborhood set is specifically defined in the context of molecular design is unclear. (R1)
- **Unjustified theoretical assumptions:** Assumptions underlying Theorem 4.2 (e.g., continuity and differentiability of objective functions, boundedness of molecular sizes, stability of optimization) are not clearly articulated or justified, raising concerns about robustness of theoretical claims and practical applicability. The framework’s narrow focus on InversionGNN’s architecture limits generalizability. (R2)
- **Insufficient comparisons with recent methods:** Majority of experiments compare only with I-LS (a very simplistic linear scalarization model) or older methods, while comparisons with more recent advances (e.g., HN-GFN) are limited to just two lightweight experiments. Primary experiments lack comprehensive comparisons with SOTA multi-objective optimization approaches. A detailed comparative analysis would better contextualize performance. (R2)
- **Clarity of core concepts:** Core ideas are difficult to grasp, especially without a background in multi-objective optimization. More intuitive explanations at the start of Section 4 are needed. Missing preliminaries on differentiable scaffolding trees. Terminology such as “inversion generation path” and “dual-path” lacks sufficient explanation—what does “inversion” mean exactly and how does it contribute to novelty? (R3)
- **Unclear distinction from vanilla GCN:** The “Direct Prediction Path” (Section 4.2) does not clearly differentiate from a standard GCN; its specific contributions should be highlighted. (R3)
- **Unclear role of relaxation techniques:** The relaxation reformulating discrete molecule Pareto optimization into a locally differentiable problem is not explained clearly in how it helps capture trade-offs among conflicting or correlated properties. (R3)
- **Motivation too generic:** The authors’ motivation that existing methods do not consider substructure effects on molecular properties is not specific enough, and InversionGNN itself does not address substructure effects in the method or experimental setup. (R4)
- **Ablation experiments lack clear significance:** It is unclear what the specific significance of designed ablation tasks is. The paper should highlight the impact of removing the direct prediction path or using another proxy model, and what the extent of that impact would be. (R4)
- **Missing details on surrogate oracle:** The training and validation of the surrogate oracle (used in Eq. 3) are not elaborated—specifically, what data and target objectives are used, and how is accuracy assessed across diverse molecules? (R4)
