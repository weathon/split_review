## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MIntRec2.0, a large-scale multimodal multi-party benchmark dataset for intent recognition and out-of-scope (OOS) detection in conversations. It contains 1,245 high-quality dialogues (15,040 annotated utterances, ~12.3 hours) harvested from three TV series, with a fine-grained intent taxonomy of 30 classes and over 5,700 OOS samples. The dataset includes speaker information and supports both single-turn and multi-turn settings. Benchmark evaluations compare classic multimodal fusion methods, ChatGPT, and human evaluators. Reviewers generally find the dataset valuable and the motivation clear, but note limitations in diversity, explainability of multimodal gains, and depth of analysis.

### Strengths
- The dataset is the first to include multi-party dialogues and OOS labels in a multimodal intent recognition benchmark, with a clear and convincing comparison to existing resources (Table 1).
- The expanded intent taxonomy (30 fine-grained classes under “Express Emotions” and “Achieve Goals”) is well-motivated, with classes that are sufficiently distinct to add value.
- Detailed human performance results (71% accuracy with ~7% of training data) demonstrate the dataset is challenging and identify a significant gap between SOTA multimodal methods and humans.
- The inclusion of both in-scope (>9,300) and OOS samples (>5,700) from multi-turn contexts enhances practical applicability and realism.
- Comprehensive speaker information and a general framework for data organization, modality feature extraction, multimodal fusion, and in-scope/OOS handling enrich utility for multi-party conversational research.
- The overall paper structure is well-organized, with clear motivation and a strong resource contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scene and topic diversity**: The dataset is sourced from only three TV series (comedies/dramas), which may not capture the breadth of real-world interactions. Scenarios may be skewed toward dramatic/comedic contexts, potentially hindering generalization to everyday conversations across different demographics. [R1, R2, R4]
- **Unclear performance gains from multimodal fusion**: Improvements from incorporating non-verbal modalities are often only 1–4% on some metrics, and the reasons are not adequately explained. The paper should clarify why gains are modest, whether they are statistically significant, and which specific intent classes benefit most. A more detailed analysis or case studies on multimodal integration—especially for OOS data—are needed. [R1, R2, R4]
- **Insufficient analysis of OOS and multimodal impact**: The discussion of how multimodal information affects OOS detection is superficial (only numeric mentions). A case study or deeper slice showing what primarily constitutes OOS and how modalities help or hinder would be illuminating. [R1, R4]
- **ChatGPT vs. human evaluation lacks detail**: It is unclear whether the 10 dialogues (227 utterances) used for human evaluation were fixed across experiments, whether other few-shot samples with different intent class balances were attempted, and whether metric improvements are conditioned on specific intents. More transparency is needed. [R1, R4]
- **Minor presentation errors**: “Figure 5” should be “Figure 1”, and “Table 7” should be “Table 2” in the text. Figures could be expanded for clarity. [R2, R3]
- **Missing discussion of dataset-specific artifacts**: The influence of laugh tracks, audience reactions, or other TV-specific signals on intent recognition is not addressed. The paper should discuss differences between TV series data and real-world multi-turn conversations. [R1, R4]
- **No querying of annotators about modality importance**: It would be informative to know which modalities annotators found most valuable for understanding conversational intent, to compare human perception with multimodal systems. [R1, R4]
- **Complexity of intent and handling ambiguous signals**: The paper does not discuss how ambiguous or contradictory multimodal signals are handled, nor does it enumerate key influencing factors (e.g., context, speaker role) beyond the dataset features. [R1, R2]
