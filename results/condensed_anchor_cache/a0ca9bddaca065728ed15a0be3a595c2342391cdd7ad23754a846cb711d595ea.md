- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 1, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies adversarial robustness in dataset condensation (DC). It first challenges the hypothesis that DC inherently yields robust models, then proposes GUARD (Geometric regUlarization for Adversarial Robust Dataset), which adds curvature regularization to gradient-matching condensation. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and (partially) ImageNet compare GUARD with other DC methods under various adversarial attacks.

### Strengths
- The topic is valuable and introduces a new direction: studying and improving adversarial robustness in dataset condensation. The motivation and writing are clear. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- The paper identifies that models trained on existing condensed datasets are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, contradicting the intuitive hypothesis that condensation might discard spurious features. (Reviewer 4)
- The authors point out that an adversarially robust model should have a smooth loss landscape and tackle robustness from a curvature perspective. (Reviewer 4)
- GUARD bridges gradient matching (a common DC approach) with CURE-style curvature regularization, leading to a robust condensation method. (Reviewer 4)
- The paper includes both theoretical analysis and experiments to support its claims. (Reviewer 3)
- Compared to standard adversarial training applied to DC, GUARD shows superior performance and efficiency. (Reviewer 4; note contrast: Reviewer 2 finds novelty limited and results marginal.)

### Weaknesses
- **Weak motivation and unclear significance.** The motivation for studying adversarial robustness in DC is weak; it is not clear that DC is a promising technique or why it would inherently promote robustness. (Reviewer 2)
- **Theoretical issues.** The theory in Section 5 and Proposition 1 contains several problems:  
  - The definition of \(h\) is missing in Proposition 1. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The claim that \(\tilde\ell(x,v)\) is convex in \(x\) is questionable; a counterexample is provided. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Equation (12) in Appendix A assumes \(h^{-1}\) is a contraction map without justification, effectively requiring that the distance from \(x'\) to the per-class mean is bounded by \(\sigma\) – an unverified assumption. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The inequality sign in Equation (2) appears reversed (given adversarial examples maximize loss). (Reviewer 3)  
  - The assumption that \(\ell(\cdot)\) is convex (in Proposition 1) is confusing and not justified. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient justification and novelty of the method.**  
  - Curvature regularization for adversarial robustness is already known (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. 2019). The paper’s technical novelty is limited. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The method relies solely on the curvature term without further theoretical or experimental justification; the Hessian approximation is rough, and the hyperparameter \(h\) is not explored. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing related work.** The paper omits relevant prior work on flat minima (e.g., sharpness-aware minimization, implicit regularization) that relates to robustness (e.g., arXiv:2305.05392), and does not cite or compare with an existing adversarially robust condensation method (arXiv:2211.10752). (Reviewer 1)
- **Weak and insufficient experiments.**  
  - Evaluations are limited to small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and a simple architecture (3-layer ConvNet). (Reviewer 2)  
  - Improvements over baselines are marginal and often within standard deviation. For example, under \(\ell_\infty\) attack the proposed method achieves only \(0.7 \pm 0.2\) – no clear improvement and essentially fails. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Under strong attacks, robust accuracy is not above random chance (10% for 10-class tasks), which is a standard benchmark for invisible perturbations. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Clean accuracy drops significantly (as shown in the appendix), contradicting the paper’s statement that “in practice, it is important that the condensed data enable the model to perform well both in i.i.d test setting and under adversarial attacks.” These clean-accuracy results should be in the main paper. (Reviewer 3, also noted by Reviewer 4)
- **Computational cost vs. benefit.** The method incurs extra overhead from curvature regularization on top of gradient matching, yet the robustness gains do not justify this cost. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Missing analysis or experiments on the computational overhead. (Reviewer 4)
- **Additional requested analyses.**  
  - Performance under the standard AutoAttack setting (same epsilon as the strong attack) is not reported. (Reviewer 4)  
  - It is unclear whether adding curvature regularization to other DC methods (not just gradient matching) leads to more than marginal robustness improvements. (Reviewer 4)  
  - The appropriateness of using a weaker attack setting (epsilon=2) in a limited-data scenario warrants justification. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Figures 1 and 2 are unclear in their purpose. (Reviewer 2)
