## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Spawrious, a new image classification benchmark for spurious correlations (SCs) that includes both one-to-one (O2O) and many-to-many (M2M) SCs, with three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard). Images (~152k, 224×224) are generated using Stable Diffusion v1.4 and filtered with a captioning model. The benchmark tests 11 (R4 count; R2 states 10) group robustness methods across six settings. All methods struggle, especially on the Hard splits (accuracy below 73% with ResNet50; none above 70% on the hardest split). Reviewer 2 is very positive (score 8, confidence 4), praising the dataset design and scale of empirical work, while Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 are more cautious (all score 5), citing concerns about verification, missing comparisons, and insufficient analysis.

### Strengths
- The six desiderata for SC benchmarks are well elaborated and can guide future work. (R1)
- The formal presentation of O2O and M2M spurious correlation settings clarifies the paper’s contribution. (R1)
- Overall well-written; Figure 3 is particularly nice; addresses the community’s need for more SC benchmarks. (R1)
- The use of text-to-image models to carefully control the data generation process is a nice design choice. (R2)
- The scale of empirical assessments is substantial: approximately 10–11 methods tested across 6 settings. (R2, R4)
- The dataset is novel: it contains both O2O and M2M SCs, offers three difficulty levels, and comprises ~152k high-resolution images. (R3)
- Experimental results demonstrate that state-of-the-art methods struggle on the dataset (accuracy below 73% on Hard splits); misclassification analysis provides evidence of reliance on spurious background features. (R3)
- Discussion of ethical concerns about using Stable Diffusion (Appendix C) is appreciated. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison with related synthetic SC benchmarks** – notably the PUG dataset, which uses a similar combinatorial construction pipeline. (R1)
- **Lack of understanding of dataset hardness** – no analysis of an optimal re-weighting function, nor how JTT’s assigned weights compare to such an optimum. (R1)
- **No explanation for why MixUp outperforms other methods in the M2M setting** – deeper theoretical analysis is needed; the current work merely observes the performance differences. (R1, R2)
- **Missing baselines** – performance of RWY (uses group info) and BR-DRO / LfF (no group info) is not reported. (R1)
- **Conceptual clarity on M2M** – it is unclear how M2M differs from O2O with superclass/superattribute labels; the M2M case still has a one-to-one relationship between disjoint subgroups of classes and attributes (R1 questions whether overlapping subsets would be more appropriate). (R1)
- **Asymmetric test distribution design** – why is the correlation completely flipped only in M2M (and not O2O)? And why is the test correlation flipped rather than randomized (zero correlation), as is typical in benchmarks like Waterbirds? (R1)
- **Group DRO weight calculation** – since test correlations never appear in training, the theoretical weights would be infinite; it is unclear what weights were actually used. (R1)
- **Verification of image generation output** – the filtering pipeline (captioning model + manual) may be inaccurate: the captioning model can make mistakes, and the manual process is subject to human error and bias. No details are given on how consistency and accuracy were ensured across samples. (R2, R3)
- **No human study of prompt alignment** – a crowd study showing what percentage of generated images actually follow the intended prompt is missing; without it, the dataset cannot be trusted to contain the intended variety of spurious correlations. (R3)
- **Missing validation on existing domain generalization datasets** – results on datasets like FOCUS (Familiar Objects in Common and Uncommon Settings) are not provided, which would strengthen the claim of the benchmark’s challenge. (R3)
- **Missing evaluation of W2D** – none of the evaluated methods handles both correlation shift and domain shift; W2D (Huang et al., 2022) is specifically designed for this setting and should be included. (R4 – major concern)
- **Incomplete architecture analysis** – different architectures (Deit, Swin, ViT) and different sizes within families should be evaluated, following the conclusions of Wenzel et al. (R4 – major concern)
- **Foundation models not tested** – CLIP zero-shot (and possibly other foundation models) should be evaluated to demonstrate how challenging the benchmark is and to analyze whether the synthetic nature introduces easily exploitable biases. (R4)
- **Questionable claim about ImageNet-W** – the paper states that ImageNet-W is synthetic, but the watermark shortcut naturally exists in the real ImageNet dataset. (R4)
- **Table 3 average computation** – it is unclear how the average across different settings/environments is computed and whether such an average is meaningful. (R2)
- **Missing saliency analysis for MixUp** – Appendix E provides saliency maps for some methods but not for MixUp; comparing them would be interesting. (R2 – minor suggestion)

*Reviewer disagreement:* R2 is notably more positive (score 8, confidence 4) and considers the dataset design and empirical coverage a clear strength, while the other reviewers (scores 5) emphasize the gaps in verification, missing comparisons, and conceptual clarity.
