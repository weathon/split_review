## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper trains GPT-2 from scratch on a synthetic grade-school math dataset (iGSM) to investigate whether language models develop genuine reasoning skills versus memorization. The authors design controlled experiments probing internal states, planning, backward reasoning, error causes, and model architecture (depth vs. width). Reviewers agree the paper is highly experimental with interesting findings (e.g., models can generalize to harder problems, learn to plan ahead, show backward thinking), but they raise major concerns about the generalizability of conclusions due to the synthetic dataset’s limited natural language variety and the restricted model scale. Confidence and soundness vary: Reviewer 3 (confidence 2) and Reviewer 5 (confidence 4, soundness 2) are most critical of generalizability; Reviewer 4 (confidence 5) is thorough but notes omitted error analysis; Reviewer 1 (confidence 3) and Reviewer 2 (confidence 4) find the direction interesting but highlight limitations.

### Strengths
- **Controlled experimental design**: Training an LLM from scratch on a synthetic dataset (iGSM) gives full control over data, prevents training-test contamination, and isolates reasoning capabilities (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Probing methods for hidden reasoning**: The paper introduces probing tasks (e.g., V-probing) that align with human problem-solving strategies and can distinguish whether pre-training or fine-tuning is responsible for a probing signal (Reviewers 2, 4). This provides insight into the black-box reasoning process (Reviewer 5).
- **Multiple interesting findings**: Models can generalize to problems of greater depth (unseen during training), learn backward thinking processes (e.g., “because I want to compute X, but X depends on Y and Y depends on Z, so let me compute Z first”), and performance scales with model depth rather than width (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 specifically highlights evidence that the model learns to plan ahead, identifying necessary parameters before generating the solution.
- **Useful synthetic dataset**: iGSM is designed to have high template diversity and reduce contamination, which could facilitate training and evaluation of LLMs on grade-school math reasoning (Reviewer 5).
- **Relevance to model design**: The depth vs. width results and analysis of reasoning mechanisms may help guide future language model architecture decisions (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited generalizability to natural language reasoning**: The synthetic data (iGSM) uses formal, formulaic expressions (e.g., multiplication always mapped from “times” in input) that do not capture the flexibility of natural language (e.g., “three children each eat two eggs”). Reasoning on iGSM is likely much easier than on natural language questions (Reviewer 5). This raises the question of whether conclusions transfer to realistic settings (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 5 considers this the major concern (soundness 2).
- **Model scale too small**: Experiments only use GPT-2-small (100M–200M parameters). A minority reviewer (Reviewer 3, confidence 2) explicitly states that this may affect generalization and requests experiments on billion-parameter LLMs. Reviewer 4 notes that scaling depth is a known fact and questions whether it leads to memorization.
- **Specific CoT form limits applicability**: The paper uses a rigid CoT format (e.g., “Define <param2> as X, so X=…”) that lacks readability and differs substantially from natural language CoTs used in current state-of-the-art models. This artificial structure may not reflect human reasoning and conclusions may not apply to other CoT forms (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 further questions how the authors concluded the model learned backward thinking from this specific CoT (line 447–453).
- **Lack of analysis on data quantity**: Expected a curve or table showing model performance as a function of the quantity of training data (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing error analysis**: The paper omits analysis of model reasoning errors, stating it will be in another paper; reviewers consider this a flaw—at least a summarized version should be included (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also asks how models fix errors (Result 6 missing).
- **No analysis of learning dynamics**: It would be informative to show during which training iterations the model acquired specific reasoning skills, and whether they can be learned through continuous training (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of validation on real-world or other synthetic benchmarks**: No results on real-world GSM8K or similar synthetic datasets like MathWorld. This limits assessment of how much the trained model generalizes to practical problems (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 emphasizes that LLMs are useful because they generalize widely, and “generalization” from one synthetic dataset to a similar synthetic dataset is not ideal.
- **Poor diversity in iGSM categorizations**: iGSM only contains four categorizations of quantitative relationships, which may overestimate the model’s reasoning ability when applied to more diverse datasets (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 questions whether this affects the generalizability of conclusions.
- **Minor**: Use of “AGI” in a paper with synthetic data and GPT-2 is inappropriate (Reviewer 4). Presentation and writing could be improved (Reviewers 1, 2, 5 all give presentation ≤3). Reviewer 4 notes that the conclusion about backward thinking is stated without sufficient discussion in the paper.
- **Disagreement about backward thinking**: While Reviewer 3 and 4 accept the backward thinking finding, Reviewer 2 explicitly states “I cannot understand how the author came to this conclusion” based on the evidence provided. This contrast in reviewer confidence should be noted.
