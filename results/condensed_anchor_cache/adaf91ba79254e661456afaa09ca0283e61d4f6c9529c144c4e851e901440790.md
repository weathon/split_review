## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CSR-ADM, a method that combines an asynchronous diffusion model for denoising with bisimulation metrics to learn causal state representations for POMDPs. The approach aims to improve robustness of RL algorithms under noisy observations. The paper includes theoretical bounds and experiments on Roboschool tasks. Reviewers are split: one finds the idea novel and well-supported (score 8), another sees value but criticizes presentation (score 5), a third finds the theory unclear and the paper hard to follow (score 3), and a fourth appreciates the theory but requests more explanation (score 6).

### Strengths
- Novel combination of causal state representations, bisimulation, and diffusion models for POMDPs (R1, R4).
- Provides theoretical guarantees, including an upper bound on value function approximation between noisy observations and causal states and a bisimulation equivalence under Lipschitz assumptions (R1, R4). This is a “first framework to approximate causal states with diffusion models” with a comprehensive theoretical foundation (R1).
- Method can be incorporated into off-the-shelf RL algorithms, making it easy to apply in practice (R2).
- Experiments compare against relevant baselines and include an ablation study showing the contribution of each component (R2, R1).
- The paper is generally well-written with a clear combination of algorithmic proposal, theory, and experiments (R1). Minority contrast: R2 and R3 find the presentation difficult to follow (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity issues** (R2, R3, R4): The paper is often unclear, making it hard to assess the quality of the method. Specific points:
  - The term “bisimulation” is never defined or explained (R3). Definition 1 is not rigorous: in a POMDP, \(P(s_{t+1}|o_t,a)\) depends on history and policy; the notion of a partition of state space into observation space is unclear (R3).
  - The meaning of “POMDPs with perturbed inputs” in the abstract is confusing—isn’t every POMDP by definition subject to observation noise? (R1, R2)
  - The notation \(\zeta\) is overloaded: it denotes the reward noise model, the predicted state (line 234), the noisy state (line 269), and the bisimulation model (Alg 1, line 2). Do these all represent the same thing? (R1, R2)
  - In Eqs. (1a–1c), the assumptions on dynamics are not clear (continuous/discrete spaces, properties of \(f,g,h\)). The function \(f\) appears both as an observation function and as a different function with different inputs in Def. 1 and Assumption 2 (R2, R3).
  - Potential typos: In Eqs. 7 and 8 and Alg 1, \(\theta\) and \(\zeta\) seem switched (R2).
  - After Eq. 6, variables \(n\), \(\hat{s}_{t+1}\), and \(r_{t+1}\) are undefined (R2).
  - In Alg. 1, the purpose of line 7 (sampled transitions) is unclear (R2).
  - How does the diffusion model predict a future state? It was described as only removing noise (R2).
  - In Assumption 3, what does the assumption intuitively mean, and are \(s_i, s_j\) in \(S\) or \(S\cup O\)? (R2)
  - In Theorem 1, the bound seems to imply that if \(c_T\approx0\) and \(c_R\approx1\), then states with the same immediate reward have zero value gap, which is not generally true. Please explain (R2).
  - In Fig. 4 (Appendix A), how is \(P(\hat{s}_t|o_t)\) computed? (R2)
  - Definition 2 (the bisimulation metric) is stated as a fixed-point equation that “exists and is unique,” which reads like a theorem statement, not a definition. The paper should discuss sufficient conditions (e.g., finite spaces) for existence/uniqueness (R3, R4).
  - Theorem 2 defines \(\mathcal{E}_\zeta\) twice (R4? Actually R3). Clarify.
  - Theorem 4 claims convergence of the algorithm, but the RHS of (15) does not vanish as \(n\to\infty\), and no gradient descent analysis is given (only a vague mention of previous results) (R3).
- **Assumptions and scope**:
  - The method assumes Gaussian noise, limiting applicability to non‑Gaussian noise (e.g., raindrops, colour shifts, heavy-tailed distributions) and not addressing missing data (R2).
  - Assumption 2 (lower boundedness of observation function \(f\)) needs justification: what properties of \(f\) or the data guarantee this bound? What are practical implications if violated? (R4)
- **Lack of computational cost analysis**: The paper does not quantify the added computational overhead, making it hard to assess feasibility in resource-constrained or large-scale settings (R2).
- **Diffusion model error decomposition missing**: The analysis does not provide the standard decomposition into initialization error, score estimation error, and discretization error, nor does it discuss how hyperparameters (e.g., number of diffusion steps) affect each component (R4).
- **Theorems lack explanation**: The significance of each theorem is not clearly stated. For example:
  - Theorem 1: what does the bisimulation metric in (12) represent for value estimation? (R4)
  - Theorem 2: how does the bound on the value gap relate to algorithm convergence? (R4)
  - Theorem 3: what does convergence in Wasserstein-1 distance imply about distribution quality? (R4)
- **Contrasting views on theory**: While R1 and R4 find the theoretical guarantees solid, R3 questions the validity of several theorems and definitions, and R2 finds some bounds counterintuitive (Thm. 1). The paper should address these concerns more rigorously.
