## Merged Review

### Summary
The three reviewers are split. Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 3) sees this as the first framework for solving two-player zero-sum differential games with one-sided information when the information is specifically about a finite set of payoff types, and finds the paper thorough and the algorithm effective. Reviewers 2 (score 3, confidence 2) and 3 (score 3, confidence 4) judge the paper to have major presentation and clarity issues, making it nearly impossible to follow, and question whether the contributions go beyond known results. All reviewers agree that the topic—reducing dependence on action-space cardinality—is important.

### Strengths
- **First framework for the class**: To R1’s knowledge, this is the first paper to give a framework for solving 2p0s differential games where one-sided imperfect information is about payoff types and P1 learns the type before P2. R2 finds finding algorithms with better dependency on `|A|` an important topic. R3 could not identify any significant strengths.
- **Algorithm performance and invariance**: R1 notes that the CAMS algorithm shows time-invariance to increasing action sets because the underlying payoff types are invariant, and game examples from the literature satisfy the conditions.
- **Analysis of the primal-dual reformulation**: R1 commends the thorough description and analysis of the specific game class and the accompanying reformulation, and says the overall structure is good.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity (all reviewers)**: R2 says the paper has important presentation issues – someone unfamiliar with differentiable games struggles even to understand the setup. R3 says it requires extensive rewriting with ambiguous/nonsensical phrasing (e.g., “a computational complexity independent of |A| can be achieved” instead of “a method … can be achieved”; “demonstrate correctness of CAMS” is vague). R3 notes missing definitions: equilibrium is not defined, no-regret is mentioned without explanation, and the transition from the original game to the primal-dual reformulation is not clearly articulated. R3 lists specific errors: Line 128 motivation for running cost `l(u,v)` is unclear; Line 130 “has a value” is misattributed; Line 143 “to have pure Nash” is not explained; Line 175 equation (5) mixes `V_τ` and `l(u,v)` ambiguously; Line 179 “has a pure equilibrium” and “If `U_τ` is not convex in `p`, P1…” are nonsensical. R1 notes dense notation in the preliminaries and suggests a more detailed appendix to explain mathematical steps and intuition. R2 says the authors do not differentiate their contributions from known results (e.g., Theorem 1 and related lemmas seem straightforward from existing results), and the setting (continuous vs. discrete time) is confusing.
- **Assumptions and limitations (R1, R3)**: R1 says there is a lack of explanation for when the assumptions (information asymmetry, finite payoff types) are natural, how to verify that a game belongs to this class, and whether Hexner’s game is the only example. R1 asks for a more detailed discussion of how discretization of continuous payoff sets affects solution quality and convergence, and what information is lost when the true payoff space is continuous. R3 finds the whole motivation and description of the game class insufficient.
- **Missing experiments/comparisons (R1, R2)**: R1 asks whether results can be verified in a game where `|A|=∞` but `I` is finite (the experiments are all with finite `|A|`). R1 also suggests it would be interesting to study Stackelberg equilibria in this setup. R2 asks whether the authors have considered the online convex optimization perspective of learning in EFG (continuous action polytope) and how this relates to continuous-action games. R2 asks for a clearer explanation of what exactly the technical contributions are, as Theorem 1 and lemmas may follow from existing results.
- **Typos (R1)**: References for Diplomacy are broken (“, FAIR”); “tractable solve” in conclusion is ungrammatical; inconsistent capitalization in references (e.g., P Cardaliaguet vs Pierre Cardaliaguet).
