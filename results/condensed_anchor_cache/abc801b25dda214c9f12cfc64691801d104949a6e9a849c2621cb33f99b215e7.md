## Merged Review

### Summary

MolSpectra pre-trains 3D molecular representations by incorporating quantum mechanical information from molecular spectra (IR, UV-Vis, Raman). It uses a two-stage pipeline: first, denoising on a large dataset without spectra; second, contrastive learning between a 3D encoder (TorchMD-Net) and a spectrum encoder (SpecFormer, trained via masked patch reconstruction). The goal is to infuse knowledge of quantized energy levels into representations. Evaluated on QM9 and MD17 benchmarks, the authors report average improvements of 6.46% over prior methods. The work is seen as an interesting and well-motivated step, but concerns about experimental setup, comparisons, and computational demands weaken the conclusions. Reviewer confidence scores vary (3,5,4); scores are 5, 8, 6.

### Strengths

- Incorporating additional data (spectra) is useful; the work may influence subsequent molecular representation learning (R1). The motivation for using spectra—quantized energy levels as direct measurements of molecular dynamics—is well-justified (R3).
- The approach introduces quantum mechanical considerations into molecular representation learning, going beyond classical mechanics (R2).
- The denoising component is sophisticated, incorporating both rotational and vibrational energies into the Boltzmann distribution (R2).
- SpecFormer and the MPR + contrastive objectives are technically sound and well-designed (R3).
- The contrastive setting allows inference without spectral data, enhancing real-world usability (R2).
- Multiple downstream experiments show consistent (R1: small; R2: outperforming conventional approaches) improvements (R1, R2). R2 notes that the model outperforms conventional models; R1 notes the improvements are marginal but consistent.

### Weaknesses

- **Misleading reporting and incomplete baselines:**  
  * In Table 2, bold formatting is incorrect: MolSpectra is bold in 9/12 columns but achieves the best result in only 3/12 (R1, R3). Table 2 and 3 only compare against older, non-SOTA models; newer models like MACE and Allegro achieve significantly better results (R3). Comparisons with recent models that also use similar potential forms (SliDe, Frad) are missing, and if those outperform MolSpectra, it is unclear whether quantum spectral data is the real contributor (R2).  
  * Horizontal separators in tables should be explicitly labeled (R3). The baseline “Coord” is insufficiently described; a fair comparison would pre-train the same architecture with and without spectral objectives (R3).  
  * The model is only applied to one backbone (TorchMD-Net); generalizability to other architectures is unknown (R3). Comparisons with other multimodal molecular representation methods are needed (R3).

- **Complex training and unexplained choices:**  
  * The two-stage pretraining adds complexity without clear justification; could it be done in one step? (R1).  
  * No analysis of computational cost (training time, memory) is provided, and the transformer for spectra is resource-intensive, especially with many contrastive pairs (R2, R3).  
  * Experimental conditions (number of negative samples, noise generation parameters) are not rigorously introduced (R2).  
  * Hyperparameter tuning (stride, patch, mask, objective weights) is mentioned, but it is unclear whether tuning used the pretraining dataset or downstream task performance (R1). Sensitivity to learning rate / transformer configuration is not explored (R1).  
  * Results are sensitive to patch/stride sizes (Tables 4, 5): variation in HOMO (0.2), LUMO (0.5), gap (0.2) is comparable to differences from the next best method (R1). A suspected data error in overlap ratio row needs correction (R1).

- **Insufficient evidence for claimed benefits:**  
  * The claim that MolSpectra “learns the dynamic evolution of molecules” is not directly tested; a suggested experiment is to compare power spectra from MD simulations (derived from the model’s force field) with ground-truth IR/Raman peak positions (R3).  
  * No ablation study isolates the contribution of individual spectral modalities (UV-Vis, IR, Raman) (R3).  
  * No analysis of learned representations (latent space, attention patterns) is provided (R3).  
  * Sensitivity to hyperparameters β_Denoising, β_MPR, β_Contrast (Eq.8) is not reported; tuning runs should be shown (R3).  
  * It is unclear whether the spectra used for spectral representations in Table 1 come from QM9S (assumed but unstated) (R3).  
  * The choice of spectra types (UV-Vis, IR, Raman) is not elaborated; other spectra (NMR, mass) could be beneficial (R3).  
  * The log₁₀ transform on peak intensities is used, but alternative normalizations (e.g., scaling max peak to 1) have not been compared (R3).  
  * Performance scaling with dataset size and diversity is not investigated (R3).

- **Other issues:**  
  * In Table 2, columns for Torch-MD and PaiNN should be bold in first column; second column contains methods that perform better or on par with MolSpectra (R1).  
  * Section 2.2 should explicitly state which energy function (presumably variant I) is used (R3).  
  * Typo on p.8 l.427/428: "yiels" should be "yields" (R3).  
  * Limitations and future directions are not discussed (R3).
