## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper constructs a benchmark of ~2512 QA pairs to evaluate LLMs on understanding and generating structure-rich texts (JSON, YAML, XML, Markdown, Org, LaTeX, Python). Four closed-source LLMs (GPT‑4, Minimax, Spark, Ernie) are tested. The taxonomy categorises structured texts into four classes (e.g., Tree, Tabular). Reviewers note its practical value but raise major concerns about shallow analysis, poor presentation, simple data construction, missing open‑source models, and questionable novelty.

### Strengths
- **Practical value and broad coverage**: Reviewer 1 (score 6) emphasises great practical value and the wide range of structure-rich text covered. Reviewer 2 (score 3) calls it a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating LLMs’ ability to understand and reason on structured texts. Reviewer 4 (score 3) notes the inclusion of JSON, YAML, XML, Markdown, Org, LaTeX, Python, plus categories like Tree and Tabular.
- **Large dataset**: All reviewers acknowledge the new dataset (~2500 QA pairs). Reviewer 3 (score 1) specifically points out the dataset size.
- **Minority positive contrast**: Reviewer 1 (score 6) strongly endorses the contribution and sees high practical relevance, while the other three reviewers (scores 3, 1, 3) are more critical overall.

### Weaknesses
- **Shallow analysis and missing discussion**: Reviewers 2 (score 3) and 4 (score 3) state experiments simply compare performance without any in‑depth analysis. Reviewer 2 expects discussion by category, reasons for LLM failures, and suggestions for improvement; key results are placed in the Appendix with no discussion. Reviewer 4 notes that no tables or figures summarise the main experiment results.
- **Poor presentation and unclear motivation**: Both Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4 observe that the motivation is unclear and no references are cited in Section 1 (Background/Motivation). Reviewer 2 adds that the QA format of the benchmark is not clear until Section 3. Reviewer 4 finds that the related work does not justify the importance of understanding structure‑rich data.
- **Limited model evaluation and reproducibility concerns**: Reviewer 1 (score 6) notes that only closed‑source LLMs are tested; open‑source models should be included for reproducibility. Reviewer 2 agrees that experiments are shallow. Reviewer 4 criticises comparing only four LLMs. Reviewer 3 (score 1) questions why GPT‑4 was not used with prompt engineering and points out that Table 2 and Table 5 (Appendix A.2) show prompt engineering actually decreased performance.
- **Simple data construction**: Reviewer 4 describes the data construction method as very simple (regular expressions + simple rules). Reviewer 1 asks about the cost of using real‑world files for all text classes instead of synthetic generation – a criticism of data realism.
- **Lack of novelty**: Reviewer 3 (score 1) finds the work not very novel and unlikely to be interesting. Reviewer 4 (score 3) also asks about novelty compared to prior work. In contrast, Reviewer 1 (score 6) sees strong contribution, showing clear disagreement.
- **Taxonomy lacking underlying principles**: Reviewer 1 (score 6), despite being positive, notes that the taxonomy would benefit from explaining the underlying principles for task design (e.g., understanding recursive paths) and what capabilities are being evaluated.
