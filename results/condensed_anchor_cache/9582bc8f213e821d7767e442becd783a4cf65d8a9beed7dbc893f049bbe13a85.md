## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies differentially private (DP) inference-time alignment of LLMs via activation editing. It proposes the Private Steering for LLM Alignment (PSA) algorithm, which computes a steering vector per layer by contrasting mean activations of positive and negative demonstrations, clips each example’s contribution, and adds calibrated Gaussian noise to achieve DP. Experiments on seven benchmarks (including Anthropic’s “Advanced AI Risk” evaluation, MMLU) with open-source LLMs of 0.5B–7B parameters (Llama-2, Qwen) evaluate alignment (multiple-choice, GPT-4 ratings), open-ended text generation quality, and general reasoning. A Membership Inference Attack (MIA) tailored for activation editing is also introduced, using canary examples to empirically audit privacy. Results show PSA performs similarly to the non-private Mean Steering, often outperforms PCA Steering, and sometimes exceeds zero-shot baselines; empirical privacy ε is much tighter than the theoretical guarantee.

### Strengths
- **Thorough evaluation** covering alignment performance, text generation quality, general capability (MMLU), and empirical privacy – on seven benchmarks with multiple LLM sizes and families (0.5B–7B, Llama‑2, Qwen).
- **Novel problem formulation**: first study of privacy for activation‑editing–based LLM alignment; addresses an emerging and important issue as alignment datasets may contain sensitive information.
- **Simple, intuitive, and straightforward‑to‑implement approach** (PSA) – applies the Gaussian mechanism to mean activation vectors.
- **New Membership Inference Attack (MIA)** designed specifically for activation editing, providing a tool for empirical privacy auditing that relies only on generated text (no probabilities needed).
- **Clear presentation** – algorithm and experiments are well explained; easy to follow.
- **Promising experimental outcomes** – DP alignment achieves alignment metrics comparable to non‑private methods at relatively low privacy cost; empirical privacy evaluation suggests stronger guarantees than the formal bound.

### Weaknesses
- **Technical contribution is limited** – PSA is a straightforward application of the Gaussian mechanism to privatize a mean vector; the non‑private version (Mean Steering) computes activation means and averages, and the private version simply adds DP noise (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Missing non‑private baseline**: Wu et al. (2024) provides a σ=0 (noiseless) condition for their method and the original non‑private method; the paper only shows one non‑private baseline. The impact of clipping (how norms differ from non‑private activations) is unclear – e.g., if the norm distribution of non‑private steering vectors differs from the norm‑clipped distribution, it is unexplained why private and non‑private performance are similar (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear privacy parameters and reproducibility details**:  
  - The privacy parameters (ε, σ) are not fully specified per layer. For δ=1/(5n), if 5 layers are edited, each layer should allocate δ=1/(25n); it is unclear if ε=2 (Table 2) is for all five layers or a single layer (Reviewer 1).  
  - Algorithm 2 uses σ = 2√(2 ln(1.25/δ))/(nε), implying Δ_f = n/2. Does this mean C_l = Δ_f = n/2? (Reviewer 3).  
  - The empirical privacy estimation shows tight ε (1.6 vs. 2), while prior work (Nasr et al. 2021) required gradient canaries and thousands of models for such tight analysis; the canary properties need clarification (Reviewer 1).
- **Weak motivation for DP alignment** – alignment can largely be achieved with public (non‑sensitive) data, and non‑DP mean steering is generally low‑risk because averaging aggregates population‑level information, reducing individual influence. The threat model is not sufficiently justified, and no attack performance comparisons under different scenarios are provided to highlight actual privacy risks (Reviewer 2).
- **Experiments feel preliminary / proof‑of‑concept** – missing error bars; it is unclear how many examples were used for several evaluations (Reviewer 4). The study would benefit from:  
  - Sensitivity analysis of different clipping bounds (a key DP hyperparameter).  
  - Performance across varying ε / noise scales.  
  - Results on diverse datasets (different modalities/semantics).  
  - Comparison of alignment performance on public vs. private datasets (distribution shift).  
  - Results with language models of varying architectures (Reviewer 2).
- **PCA steering is poorly explained and its low performance is surprising** – is PCA steering conducted in a supervised manner (using labels)? This needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure 3 missing zero‑shot reference line** for “AI Coordination” on Llama‑2 7B (Reviewer 2).
- **Section 6 (attack description) lacks detail** – no formal presentation, diagram, or pseudocode for the MIA; more guidance on canary selection and sensitivity evaluation across different choices is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Placement of qualitative examples** (Appendix C.6, C.7) could be adjusted for readability (Reviewer 2).
- **Unexpected results**: alignment numbers (e.g., MMLU) sometimes exceed zero‑shot – is this expected? Also, for some datasets the δ value may be fairly large (Reviewer 4).
