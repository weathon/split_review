## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a post-processing defense, Stealthy Shield Defense (SSD), based on Conditional Mutual Information (CMI) against black-box model inversion attacks (MIAs). SSD is designed to reduce the output’s dependence on the input while preserving dependence on true labels. The authors theoretically prove that minimizing CMI acts as a special information bottleneck. An adaptive rate-distortion framework optimized by water-filling is introduced to limit output modifications. Experiments on FaceScrub and CelebA, with VGG-16 and IR-152, against BREP, Mirror, C2F, and LOKT attacks, show that SSD outperforms existing defenses in utility-privacy trade-off and robustness.

### Strengths
- Novel perspective: The CMI-based post-processing defense specifically targets black-box MI attacks, a setting not addressed by prior defenses (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- Theoretical grounding: The paper provides thorough theoretical analysis showing minimizing CMI serves as a special information bottleneck, balancing privacy and utility (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- Practical deployment: As a post-processing method, SSD does not require retraining and can be integrated with most pre-trained models, making it easy to adopt (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- Comprehensive evaluation: Validated across multiple attack algorithms (BREP, Mirror, C2F, LOKT), datasets (FaceScrub, CelebA), and architectures (VGG-16, IR-152). SSD achieves state-of-the-art utility-privacy trade-off in both soft and hard label settings, e.g., reducing LOKT attack accuracy to 0% on CelebA while other defenses yield 50–75% (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- Adaptive rate-distortion: The water-filling method efficiently calibrates output probability distributions (Reviewer 4).
- Clear presentation: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Requirement of a validation dataset (D_valid):** SSD requires a separate real-data validation set (potentially the training set or its split) to estimate CMI, which may increase data leakage risk and limit adoption when such a dataset is unavailable. The per-class estimation with potentially few samples (even single samples per class) raises robustness concerns (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Computational cost and inference time:** SSD performs a per-sample optimization step, leading to significantly increased inference time and energy consumption compared to baselines. The authors claim overhead is negligible with GPU optimization, but no detailed benchmarking or comparison of prediction time with NoDef or other defenses is provided (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Susceptibility to adaptive attack via averaging:** An adversary can query the same input multiple times and average the noisy outputs to recover the original prediction, effectively canceling the defense. This vulnerability is not discussed or addressed (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Limited experimental scale and generalizability:**
    - Experiments use only 64x64 low-resolution images; high-resolution scenarios (e.g., common in real-world applications) are not tested. The generalizability of the results to high-resolution images is unclear (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
    - The paper only compares against white-box defenses; comparisons with existing black-box output perturbation defenses are missing, which is a significant oversight for a black-box defense claim (Reviewer 2).
    - The attack [r1] (a state-of-the-art black-box attack) is omitted from experiments; the authors should add it (Reviewer 1).
    - Only lower-complexity models and low-dimensional inputs are tested; when input data is high-dimensional and models are deep, the mutual information I(X;Ŷ|Y) may become too small to optimize effectively. Theoretical or empirical justification for such settings is missing (Reviewer 4).
    - The paper does not evaluate scalability to many classes (e.g., thousands or millions of faces); iterating over all labels could become a bottleneck (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing experimental details and reproducibility concerns:**
    - The dataset used for GAN prior training is not specified; different GAN training datasets can affect the defense (Reviewer 2).
    - Hyperparameters for Figure 1 and the temperature T in Algorithm 1 are not provided (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
    - The value of ε (distortion constraint) for SSD in experiments is not reported (Reviewer 3).
    - For LOKT experiments, it is unclear whether the authors retrained TACGAN and other surrogate models or used pre-trained models; LOKT requires training target models with GANs and surrogates (Reviewer 1).
    - The number of identities used for attacks and the quantity of reconstructed images per identity are not specified (Reviewer 1).
    - A strange result appears: for IR-152, the “no defense” baseline has lower Acc@1 than some prior defenses, suggesting prior defenses improve attack success rate; this requires explanation (Reviewer 3).
- **Key methodological concepts insufficiently explained in main text:** The water-filling method, central to the adaptive rate-distortion framework, is relegated to the appendix. Its rationale, algorithmic implementation, and impact should be in the main body for a comprehensive understanding (Reviewer 2).
- **Related work on MI/CMI lacks depth and breadth**, making it hard to situate this work in the relevant community (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation and typos:** Unconventional notation (non-bold for vectors), duplicated words (line 107), inconsistent use of p (line 215), and a typo (line 314) reduce clarity (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis on distribution mismatch:** The paper does not evaluate SSD’s performance when the public dataset distribution differs significantly from the private dataset (e.g., public=FFHQ, private=CelebA) (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing explanation for effectiveness against LOKT and BREP:** The authors should elaborate on why SSD is particularly effective against these specific methods (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing justification for simplifying the objective:** The paper claims the original CMI objective is too complex for convex optimization and simplifies to KL(p||q^y). It is not explained why gradient descent cannot be used on the original objective or whether that would yield a better solution (Reviewer 3).
