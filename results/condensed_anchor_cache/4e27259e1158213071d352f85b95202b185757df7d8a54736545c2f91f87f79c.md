## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SimpleStrat, a training-free sampling method to increase LLM generation diversity via three stages: auto-stratification (using the LLM to partition the answer space), heuristic estimation, and probabilistic prompting. It also introduces CoverageQA, a dataset of 105 underspecified questions for evaluating diversity. Experiments claim a KL divergence reduction of 0.36 and recall increase of 0.05 over a non-LLM baseline.

### Strengths
- The idea of using the LLM itself to split the answer space into strata (auto-stratification) is interesting and intuitively effective for improving diversity (all reviewers).
- The creation of CoverageQA contributes a needed evaluation dataset for diversity measurement (R1, R2).
- The method is evaluated on both open-source and proprietary models, showing some efficacy in diversity improvement (R2).
- One reviewer (R3, score 5) found the motivation intuitively effective and the diagram/examples clearly illustrate the core idea (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of ablation and analysis**: No ablation of the three stages is provided; the quality of auto-stratification or handling of wrong stratification is not examined; heuristic estimation appears unnecessary and is not used in main experiments (should be moved to discussion) – multiple reviewers note this gap (R1, R3). The impact of the number of stratification dimensions is not studied (R1).
- **Limited evaluation scope**: The method is only verified on a small dataset (CoverageQA, 105 questions) of underspecified questions, leaving generalization and robustness unclear (R1, R3). Reviewers question applicability to practical tasks: reasoning (pass@K), code generation, agents (e.g., Figma AI generating the same weather app) (R1, R2). Impact on single-answer questions is not considered (R2).
- **Insufficient baselines**: The experiments include only one baseline, which is not LLM-based; existing LLM-based diversity methods are omitted, weakening the comparative evaluation (R2).
- **Quality/accuracy not properly measured**: Despite claiming no quality loss, the paper does not report precision or accuracy metrics for generated answers (R1). The impact of SimpleStrat on accuracy is not discussed (R2). The claim that lower temperature can be used is not demonstrated with evidence (R3).
- **Diversity metric concerns**:
  - KL divergence is computed over the model’s own answer distribution, not the full solution space, making it unsuitable for measuring coverage of all valid answers (R3).
  - The “coverage diversity” (recall) measure reflects generation stability rather than true diversity (R3).
  - The answer distribution in Figure 3 is far from uniform, contradicting the stratified sampling assumption (R3).
  - The relationship between the product of next-token probabilities and diversity is unclear; length-normalization should be used (R3).
- **Practical effectiveness questions**: The method requires generating a stratification dimension per prompt; for short generations, it may not be more effective than temperature sampling at equal token cost; for long generations, an easy dimension partition may not exist (R3). The unique rate of generation over 100 samples is not reported (R1).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Several grammar errors and formatting issues (R3 provided specific lines: l046, l102, l135, l181, l196, l218, l337, l351, l236). Heuristic Estimation section should be moved given it is not used in experiments (R3). Some reviewers rated presentation 2-3 (R1, R3).
- **Limited broader insights**: The paper presents a method and a small dataset without providing empirical insights that would extend beyond the specific setting, limiting its contribution (R3). Despite a more positive assessment from one reviewer (score 5), all reviewers agree on significant weaknesses that undermine the current contribution.
