## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes conservation law-encoded neural operators (clawNOs) that enforce divergence-free outputs by replacing the final prediction with the curl of a skew-symmetric matrix-valued function learned via neural operators, implemented through numerical differentiation layers. The method is evaluated on elasticity, shallow water equations, and incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, showing improved accuracy over FNO, G-FNO, and other baselines, especially in small-data regimes. Reviewers generally agree that enforcing conservation laws is valuable and the method shows promise, but raise significant concerns about insufficient novelty due to closely related prior work, missing baseline comparisons, lack of efficiency analysis, limited evaluation scope, and unclear presentation.

### Strengths
- Enforcing conservation laws through the architecture is a useful and principled approach to improve neural operator performance; the use of a skew-symmetric matrix and numerical differentiation layers to hard-constrain divergence-free outputs is novel and enables automatic satisfaction of the continuity equation.
- The method is demonstrated on a variety of scientific problems (elasticity, shallow water, incompressible Navier-Stokes) and consistently outperforms existing neural operators, with particular advantage in small-data regimes.
- The paper provides a detailed review of related work and a smooth transition from existing methods to the proposed approach.
- The built-in conservation law provides a hard constraint, contributing toward more reliable ML models that respect physical laws.
- The presentation of the effect of training data size on performance is insightful and well-executed.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and missing related work**: The core idea is very similar to “Neural Conservation Laws: A Divergence-Free Perspective” (NeurIPS 2022), which is not cited in Section 3.1, making it difficult to judge the contribution. Additional relevant works not acknowledged include projection-based enforcement of integral conservation laws (arXiv:2302.11002) and hard-constraint solvers for PDEs (arXiv:2207.08675). The paper also does not compare with physics-based inductive bias methods like Hamiltonian/Lagrangian neural networks, leaving the distinction unclear.
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: Only FNO, GFNO, and a few others are tested. Advanced operators such as LSM (ICML 2023) and U-NO (TMLR 2023) are omitted. The paper lacks comparison with methods that enforce conservation laws via loss functions or direct parameterization of divergence-free fields, and does not explain when/why the hard-constraint approach is preferable.
- **Missing efficiency and computational cost analysis**: No numerical results on training/inference time, GPU memory, or model parameters are provided. Reviewers request comparisons per iteration and total run time, as well as scaling behavior as conservation laws and system dimensionality increase. The single claim of “comparable network size” is insufficient.
- **Insufficient analysis of divergence-free property**: No quantitative metric for divergence error is reported. Reviewers ask for a comparison of the output divergence of clawNOs versus baselines, and whether improvements in divergence correlate with improvements in prediction accuracy.
- **Limited evaluation scope**: All problems are 2D; there is no test on 3D systems (e.g., 3D Navier-Stokes) or high-dimensional problems, leaving scalability unclear. Although long-term prediction is advertised, time-stamp-wise error curves are not provided.
- **Clarity of experimental setup and presentation**: Many details are missing: why different test cases use different time horizons and sample sizes; whether evaluations are in-domain or out-of-domain; the train-test split procedure; justification for only 3 replicates. Sections 3.1 and 3.2 are described as unclear. The supplementary material only shows results for the proposed method, not comparative visualizations. One reviewer notes that vorticity in 2D is a scalar (not a vector), a minor inaccuracy.
- **Missing physical interpretation**: The physical meaning of the learned skew-symmetric matrix μ is not discussed, raising the question of whether it has any physical significance or is purely a mathematical trick, and whether this imposes limitations.
- **Disagreement on presentation quality**: While some reviewers rated presentation as fair or good (R2: 2, R4: 3), one reviewer (R3) strongly critiqued the presentation (score 1), citing unclear technical descriptions and missing details, indicating uneven clarity.
- **Additional open questions**: The paper does not address applicability to other operator architectures (e.g., Koopman neural operators), potential training stability issues from the divergence-free constraint, whether the method requires prior knowledge of the governing equations, or why alternative spectral basis functions (e.g., Chebyshev for non-periodic boundaries) were not considered.
