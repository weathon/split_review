## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DREXEL and DREAM, two discrete replica-exchange Langevin samplers that combine gradient-based discrete samplers (DULA/DMALA) with replica exchange MCMC to improve exploration of non-convex discrete energy landscapes. Convergence results (asymptotic and non-asymptotic) are provided for log-quadratic targets, and experiments span synthetic multimodel distributions, Ising models, and EBMs on binarized images. The work is an incremental but well-motivated combination of existing ideas; empirical results show statistically significant improvements over prior methods, but the theoretical analysis contains serious flaws and the numerical evaluation lacks crucial details on hyperparameter tuning and statistical quantification.

### Strengths
- The problem of sampling from discrete non-convex energy landscapes is interesting and worth studying (R3).
- DREXEL/DREAM is simple, well-principled, and clearly motivated. The swap function (Eq. 10) is original and appears effective (R1, R3).
- Empirical results are promising: DREXEL/DREAM clearly outperforms competitors (DULA, DMALA) both with and without Metropolis adjustment across synthetic multimodel (8 Gaussians, wave), Ising models, and EBM experiments. Improvements are statistically significant (R1, R2).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with good intuition and clear presentation of methodological bricks and theoretical statements (R1, R3, R4).
- The work is complete, covering both theoretical and numerical results for challenging multimodal sampling, which may be valuable for the sampling community (R4).
- A large variety of numerical settings is considered with convincing results, though main weaknesses exist in the numerical evaluation (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical results are not strong and contain serious flaws.**  
  - Theorem 1 provides no non-asymptotic approximation error. Theorem 2 is difficult to interpret and only gives convergence to a biased stationary measure for DREXEL (when τ₁ ≠ 1). The approximation error of this biased stationary measure is not commented on (R1).  
  - In Theorem 1, it is unclear whether \(\tilde\pi\) and \(\pi'\) refer to the same distribution (R1).  
  - Assumption 3 (and Assumption 4 in the appendix) are too vague; details must be moved to the main text for Theorem 2 to be understood (R1). The validity of Assumption 4 for reasonable distributions is unclear (R1).  
  - Theorem 2 considers the joint distribution of (θ₁,θ₂), but Algorithm 1 returns only θ₁ (the low-temperature particle). The theorem should focus on the marginal of θ₁ (R3).  
  - The target distribution in Theorem 2 cannot be considered an approximation of π even marginally, except when τ₁ = 1. A more rigorous analysis for the τ₁ = 1 case is needed (R3).  
  - The TV bound in Theorem 2 is only an upper bound on the TV between the output of θ₁ and π; the analysis does not isolate algorithmic contribution – improvement could come from the joint-marginal gap (R3).  
  - **Fundamental proof flaw (R3):** Lines 1013–1016 contain a bound \((\mathcal{B}+B)\cdot 1/(1-\rho)\) which is a constant and cannot be made arbitrarily small, rendering the inequality in Eq. (19) impossible unless \(\epsilon > (\mathcal{B}+B)\).  
  - The convergence improvement in the analysis is log-scale and may be practically indistinguishable (R3).  
- **Hyperparameter selection and tuning are not sufficiently discussed or justified.**  
  - The setting of the three main hyperparameters (local Langevin step-size, highest temperature, swap intensity) is critical but not detailed. The authors do not explain how they were tuned; in principle, ground truth samples should not be used for tuning (R4).  
  - No ablation study of hyperparameters is provided (R4).  
  - The rationale for specific step-size choices (e.g., 0.2 for DULA, 0.4 for DMALA on the Ising model) is unclear. Were comparisons done at optimal parameters? How does log RMSE change with step size for DULA and DMALA? (R2).  
  - The dependence of log RMSE on temperature τ in Ising model results (Fig. 3) is not discussed. What about lower target temperature (e.g., τ = 0.1) or more frustrated connectivity (e.g., Sherrington-Kirkpatrick)? (R2).  
  - For DREAM, rejection rates and their dependence on step size and temperature choices (τ₁, τ₂) are not reported (R2).  
  - The gap between τ₁ and τ₂ and its effect on performance is not discussed (R2).  
  - The bias σ in equation (9) is not explained how chosen in practice (R2).  
  - How to determine scaling of temperatures in general? (R3).  
- **Numerical evaluation suffers from missing details and lack of statistical rigor.**  
  - Initialization of each experiment is not given, though it is known to influence MCMC results (R4).  
  - Sampling metrics (MMD, KL) may not assess how well true relative weights of modes are estimated; they may not reflect mode collapse (R4). For the EBM experiment, no sample-based metric is provided; estimating the partition function from MCMC can have high uncertainty (R4).  
  - Standard errors are missing from Tables 1 and 2 (R2).  
  - Numerical results from various methods are very close and may not be statistically significantly different; no error quantification over multiple runs is given (R4).  
- **Explanation of bias correction for dDREXEL vs DREXEL is unclear.** Do DREXEL and DREAM incorporate bias correction? (R2).  
- **Incremental contribution:** The work heavily builds on existing methods (DLS and reMCMC) and the improvement in benchmarks is modest (R2). The setup’s hyperparameter choices may affect relative performance (R2).  
- **Missing comparisons:** Langevin dynamics with decreasing step size might achieve similar performance; no discussion or experiments are provided (R3). The annealed version of DULA used for EBM partition function estimation could be a competing method but is not compared (R4).  
- **Minor issues:** Typo “Asymptotic Convergence on Log-Quadratic Distriubions” (R2). In the algorithm, “retains current” and “follows” should be corrected (R1). Missing standard errors (R2). Assumptions should be more highlighted in the main text (R4).
