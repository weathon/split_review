## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Score Regularized Policy Optimization (SRPO), an offline RL algorithm that leverages a pre-trained diffusion model to approximate the behavior distribution’s score function and uses it to regularize a deterministic policy gradient, thereby avoiding slow iterative diffusion sampling during training and evaluation. The method is derived from the behavior-regularized objective and integrated with Implicit Q-Learning (IQL). Experiments on D4RL locomotion tasks show significant inference speed improvements while maintaining competitive performance. Reviewers are split: two reviewers (score 8) found the approach novel and well-motivated, one (score 6) considered it interesting but had distributional concerns, and one (score 3) was critical of the clarity and experimental thoroughness, questioning the empirical benefits beyond marginal gains.

### Strengths
- The paper derives a novel connection between the gradient of the divergence term in behavior-regularized offline RL and the score function of the behavior distribution, enabling the direct use of a pre-trained diffusion model via Equation (9) (R1, R2, R3).
- By using the score function for regularization, SRPO completely avoids iterative diffusion sampling during policy optimization and evaluation, resulting in substantial computational speedups (R1, R3, R4).
- The motivation and derivation are clearly presented (R3).
- The experimental comparison is relatively comprehensive, covering both conventional behavior-regularized methods and recent diffusion-based policies on D4RL, with reproducible details (R3).
- The paper addresses an important and under-explored problem—improving inference efficiency of diffusion models in offline RL (R4).
- The experiments demonstrate evident improvement in time cost without a major performance drop (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Expressivity and distributional confusion**  
   - The final policy is a Gaussian, which may not capture the complex multi-modal structure of the optimal policy even when regularized by a diffusion behavior model (R1).  
   - The role of distributions in SRPO is unclear: if the learned policy \(\pi_\theta\) is a Gaussian with learnable mean and fixed variance, how is this different from standard Gaussian policies that lack expressivity? (R4).  
   - The choice of timestep \(t\) in Section 4.2 and Equation (9) is not justified; if the actual data distribution \(x_0\) is used, the entropy term in Eq. (9) may not hold because there is no prior on the variance of \(x_0\) (R4).

2. **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**  
   - The method appears to be an incremental extension of IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023), with the main innovation being a different policy extraction procedure, which does not represent a substantial contribution to the field (R3).  
   - It is unclear whether the proposed approach leads to significant empirical benefits over existing methods; the improvements appear marginal, and a more thorough evaluation (e.g., applying SRPO to other behavior-regularized objectives, toy examples, qualitative studies with different pre-trained diffusion models) is needed to demonstrate its value (R2, R3).  
   - One reviewer (score 3) found the paper hard to follow and the experimental evaluation insufficient to support the claims, while other reviewers were more positive about the contribution.

3. **Insufficient experimental evaluation**  
   - No experiments are performed in robotics domains with real data, even though computation-sensitive robotic contexts are cited as motivation (R3).  
   - Training time is not reported or is unclear (Appendix C does not specify actual training cost) (R4).  
   - The source of baseline results in Table 1 is not stated (whether from original papers or re-implemented) (R3).  
   - Figure 5: it is unclear whether the results are averaged across all locomotion tasks; this needs clarification (R3).  
   - The claim that SRPO “completely avoids diffusion sampling throughout both training and evaluation” is misleading because the pre-training of the diffusion behavior model itself requires iterative sampling; this should be clarified (R3).

4. **Clarity and presentation issues**  
   - The paper is considered convoluted and difficult to follow (R2).  
   - Some implementation details are missing, e.g., why different Gaussian-based policies have significantly varying inference times in Figure 1 (R1).  
   - A minor typo: “DIQL” should be “IDQL” in the first and second paragraphs of Section 6.1 (R1).
