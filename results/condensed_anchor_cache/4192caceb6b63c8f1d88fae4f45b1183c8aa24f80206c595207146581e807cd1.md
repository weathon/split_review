## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Masked LoRA Experts (MLAE), which decomposes LoRA’s low-rank matrices into rank-1 “experts” via cellular decomposition, then applies masking (dropout at submatrix-level) during training to promote diversity and reduce redundancy. Experiments on VTAB-1k and FGVC benchmarks show improved performance over prior PEFT methods, including GLoRA, with fewer parameters. Reviewers generally find the paper well-organized and clear, with extensive experiments and useful comparison of masking strategies. However, they raise concerns about novelty, the marginal impact of adaptive coefficients, insufficient analysis of the decomposition’s benefit over standard rank-8 LoRA, lack of statistical significance tests, missing comparisons with dynamic-rank methods and LLM benchmarks, limited analysis of computational cost and masking configurations, and insufficient experiment configuration details for reproducibility. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 5) is more positive overall but also critical of novelty and adaptive coefficients; the other two (scores 5, confidence 4) are somewhat less favorable, citing similar weaknesses but also noting the expert-level masking as novel.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly presented, well-organized, and includes a detailed discussion of related works and variants.
- The concept of treating each rank in a LoRA adapter as an independent expert and applying rank-wise dropout is innovative, providing a fresh perspective on PEFT.
- MLAE compares multiple masking strategies (fixed, random, mixed) and presents detailed experimental results for reference.
- Extensive evaluations across 24 tasks (VTAB-1k and FGVC) demonstrate strong performance, surpassing GLoRA on most datasets and achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters.
- The method combines adaptive dropout with cellular decomposition to effectively address redundancy in low-rank matrices.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and prior work**: The core ideas (mix of LoRA experts and MoE dropout) have been discussed in previous studies (e.g., [1],[2]), limiting technical contribution. The masking is a straightforward submatrix-level dropout, and the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of why this particular masking strategy is superior to other techniques (e.g., structured dropout, importance-based masking). Additionally, the method shares similarities with recent works (Lin et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024); clearer distinctions are needed.
- **Decomposition vs. standard rank-8 LoRA**: The claim that cellular decomposition imposes independence and diversity lacks clarity. It is not explained how 8×rank-1 matrices differ from a single rank-8 LoRA (which can be viewed as concatenating row/column vectors), and whether performance gains in Table 6 are statistically significant (no confidence intervals or p-values provided). A controlled comparison (rank-8 LoRA with equivalent parameter count) is missing.
- **Adaptive coefficients**: The learnable adaptive coefficients for each expert contribute only a small improvement (e.g., 0.2% average in Table 5). The paper does not include a detailed ablation isolating their impact nor justifies their inclusion given marginal gain. Statistical significance of this performance increase is not shown.
- **Missing comparisons with dynamic-rank methods**: Methods that dynamically adjust rank (IncreLoRA, AdaLoRA) are not compared. The paper should contrast computational cost, parameter efficiency, and performance with these approaches.
- **No evaluation on LLMs**: The paper focuses only on vision tasks. Since LoRA is widely used for LLMs, evaluation on that major PEFT benchmark is needed to demonstrate general applicability.
- **Limited analysis of masking configurations**: The paper does not explore different dropout probabilities (e.g., 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9) or dynamic dropout scheduling across dataset types (specialized vs. structured). This leaves uncertainty about optimal sparsity levels and training dynamics.
- **Computational cost**: Longer training time for MLAE is mentioned but not quantified. A thorough analysis of training time vs. performance across datasets is required for practical usability.
- **Why lora+dropout is not more effective**: The paper does not explain why simple dropout on LoRA (without decomposition) fails to match MLAE’s performance, leaving the benefit of the decomposition itself unclear.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Hyperparameters (e.g., masking schedule, initialization coefficients) need searching per dataset (Table 10), but sensitivity is not analyzed.
- **Reproducibility**: VTAB-1k experiment configuration details (masking schedule, initialization coefficients, hyperparameters) are insufficiently specified, making replication difficult.
- **Table 1 source**: The source of some numbers (e.g., from GLoRA paper) is not specified.
- **Theoretical justification**: No theoretical foundation is provided for why rank-1 expert decomposition benefits generalization beyond empirical observation.
