## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses loss of plasticity in continual learning by proposing a spectral regularizer that keeps the maximum singular value of each layer close to one. This is motivated by the observation that neural network weight initializations exhibit good trainability and that singular values grow during training, harming gradient diversity. The regularizer is designed to sustain beneficial initialization properties while minimally interfering with single-task performance. Experiments across supervised and reinforcement learning with ResNet-18 and Vision Transformers on several continual learning benchmarks show competitive performance and robustness to hyperparameters.

### Strengths
- The continual learning topic is important, and loss of plasticity is a fundamental challenge.
- The idea of regularizing the maximum singular value toward one is novel and intuitive.
- The motivation via spectral analysis of continual learning is clear (Sections 1, 2, 3, 4), with illustrative examples that connect singular value growth to training issues.
- The paper addresses trainability directly as a means to combat loss of plasticity.
- Experiments cover a wide range of existing continual learning algorithms, two architectures (ResNet-18 and ViT), multiple datasets (including non-stationarities), and both supervised and reinforcement learning settings.
- Spectral regularization attains competitive performance and is robust to the choice of regularization strength.
- One reviewer (R3, rating 8) was particularly positive, praising the well-written introduction and problem setting, the motivating spectral analysis, and the extensive empirical evidence for competitive performance and hyperparameter robustness.

### Weaknesses
- Novelty relative to L2 regularization (or L2 regularization toward initialization) is marginal; a deeper analysis comparing spectral norm vs. L2 norm in this context is missing.
- Computation cost of spectral regularization for large models and large data is not addressed.
- The connection between initialization, singular values of the Jacobian, condition numbers, and their influence on new parameters (e.g., rank) is not clearly established.
- The paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art continual learning methods; performance is not consistently superior to vision transformers and ResNets across all experimental settings.
- Experiments are limited to models on the smaller side (ResNet-18, ViT; ViT size not specified) and datasets that may not be sufficiently diverse or challenging (Tiny-ImageNet input size not stated; larger datasets exist in recent continual learning literature).
- Presentation of experimental results needs improvement: Figure 1 and Figure 2 have legends only on the top-left panel, making it hard to follow; Figure 3 does not define all plotted quantities; the paper shows only graphs and plots, no tables with exact values for quantitative comparison.
- The sudden decrease in the y-axis of Figure 5 is unexplained; a thorough analysis of its cause and implications for stability is needed.
- The baseline Continual Backprop was not evaluated; a rationale for its omission is missing.
- The method bears similarity to arXiv:2303.06296 (keeping top singular value small), which warrants discussion.
- The claim that the datasets and architectures “encompass all continual supervised learning experimental settings considered in the loss of plasticity literature” appears overstated.
- In some graphs (e.g., shrink-and-perturb baseline), another method shows better trainability as measured by singular value; this should be discussed.
