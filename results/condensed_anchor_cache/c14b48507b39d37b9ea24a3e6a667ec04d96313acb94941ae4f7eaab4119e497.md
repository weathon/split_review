## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes training vision-language models (VLMs) on receiver behavior data (likes, comments, replay graphs) from Reddit and YouTube, arguing that such behavior carries signals about the content. It contributes BLIFT, a 730k-sample instruction fine-tuning dataset (400k images, 330k videos), and fine-tunes LLaMA-Vid into Behavior-LLaVA and Ad-LLaVA. The authors report improvements over strong baselines across 46 tasks, especially high-level tasks (emotion recognition, persuasion, QA). Reviewers generally acknowledge the dataset and broad empirical gains, but express disagreement: Reviewers 1 and 2 rate the paper highly (8/8), while Reviewer 3 rates 5 and questions the theoretical foundation and novelty; Reviewer 4 rates 6 and asks for more experimental and data-analysis details.

### Strengths
- The motivation is well-presented: leveraging freely-available user behavior data (no extra annotation cost) to improve VLM content understanding.
- The BLIFT dataset (400k images, 330k videos) is a large-scale, cross-modal resource that reviewers agree is a useful contribution for VLM research.
- Data collection and preprocessing from Reddit and YouTube are described in detail, with careful cleaning and conversion of behavior into instruction-tuning formats (Reviewer 2).
- Training setup includes sensible ablations (e.g., Ad-LLaVA matched for additional data), and the evaluation is rigorous across 46 tasks and multiple benchmarks, showing clear improvements over strong baselines (LLaMA-Vid).
- The perspective of examining VLM understanding through human behavioral performance is innovative (Reviewer 4).
- The paper shows that using behavior data can be essentially “free-lunch” since the data is collected by default on platforms, a point appreciated by Reviewer 2.
- *Contrast*: Reviewer 3 acknowledges the dataset and empirical improvement but finds the core idea trivial and insufficiently novel; this minority view should be noted.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing training details:** Reviewer 1 asks for average text input length and how extremely long ASR results are handled. Reviewer 4 further notes no specific prompts, training steps, or learning rate schedules are provided for instruction tuning, hindering reproducibility.
- **Insufficient data analysis:** Reviewer 4 points out that filtering criteria appear empirical; no analysis of feature distributions (topics, emotions, object categories in images/videos vs. comments) is given, making it hard to assess dataset biases. Reviewer 4 also questions whether distributional bias was considered and whether other data sources were used beyond YouTube/Reddit.
- **Lack of theoretical foundation:** Reviewer 3 states the connection between VLM performance and behavior fine-tuning is questionable; the paper lacks formal theoretical analysis and only makes abstract references to human behavior research. The conclusion that “learning behavior improves content understanding” is called trivial: adding any data could improve performance. Reviewer 3 suggests the authors curate the same dataset without behavior labels as a control to substantiate the claim.
- **Novelty concerns and related work positioning:** Reviewer 2 notes that the paper claims “behavior data is ignored while training large language models” (Line 50), ignoring prior text-only work (e.g., using Reddit upvotes/comments). Reviewer 2 suggests the paper should acknowledge existing LLM work and claim novelty only for VLMs. Reviewer 3 adds that many studies on LLM/VLM connection with human behavior (social behavior, trust) already exist, and the paper should clearly position itself and state differences.
- **Poor organization of results:** Reviewer 2 finds Section 3 difficult to follow; recommends one summary table (with all scores normalized to baseline 100%) and paragraph-level headings for clarity.
- **Missing comparison of time efficiency:** Reviewer 4 notes no comparison of training time with vs. without behavioral data, making practical trade-offs unclear.
- **Weak support for sensor vs. behavior claim:** Reviewer 4 states the paper claims sensor data is inferior based on insufficient data quantity; more evidence and nuanced discussion are needed.
- **Legal/ethical compliance:** Reviewer 2 asks how the authors ensured that collecting and releasing behavioral data from YouTube and Reddit complies with platform Terms of Use. Reviewer 4 also questions legality and processing. Reviewer 3 asks for analysis of harmfulness, trustworthiness, and bias in generated content from Behavior-LLaVA, given that the dataset may contain harmful content.
- **Spelling inconsistency:** Reviewer 4 notes “Ad-LLaVA” vs. “AdLLaVa” should be consistent.
- **Limited model improvements:** Reviewer 4 asks whether any model improvements beyond fine-tuning were considered, noting that only llama-vid code is provided and supplementary materials show only a small amount of Reddit data.
