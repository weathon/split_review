## Merged Review

### Summary

Two reviewers rated 5 (high confidence 4 and 3) and three rated 3 (confidence 4). The positive reviewers (R2, R3) found the work well-organized, the first SNN for edge detection, and the method effective with reasonable performance and low energy consumption. The negative reviewers (R1, R4, R5) considered the novelty limited, the architecture too similar to existing DNN methods (PiDINet, HED-UNet, SDMCM), and the claimed “first SNN for edge detection” disputed by existing SNN edge detection works. All reviewers agreed on the need for stronger justification of SNN-specific benefits, more architectural details, and additional comparisons.

### Strengths

- **First SNN-based method for edge detection** (R1, R2, R3). R2 and R3 emphasize this as a primary contribution, while R5 notes prior SNN edge detection works exist.
- **Well-organized and easy to follow** with helpful figures and sample results (R2, R4, R5).
- **Competitive performance with low theoretical energy consumption**: achieves BSDS500 ODS=0.804 (vs. human 0.803), NYUDv2 ODS=0.750, BIPED ODS=0.891, consuming only 14.64 mJ (R2, R5). R1 also notes comparable inference to DNN with low power.
- **Comprehensive ablation studies** on SMSB, decoding method, and time steps (R2). R3 highlights visualizations showing good model performance.
- **Concept of Spiking Multi-Scale Block (SMSB) and Membrane Average Decoding (MAD) is reasonable** and the use of multi-scale information is sensible (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and strong similarity to existing DNN methods**:
  - SMSB closely resembles the multi-scale block in PiDINet and the second-order derivative-based multi-scale contextual enhancement module (SDMCM) in [1] (R1, R2). R2 notes the method is essentially a simplified version swapping ANN activations for IF neurons.
  - The overall U-Net architecture is similar to HED-UNet (R1) and other U-Net variants (R5). R1 asks for explicit differences.
  - The Spiking VGG encoder and Membrane Average Decoding have been widely used in deep SNN literature; differences are not clarified (R1).
- **Claim of “first SNN for edge detection” is contested**: Three existing SNN edge detection papers are cited by R5 ([1] Segmentation and edge detection based on spiking neural network model; [2] GPU implementation … ; [3] Emulating spiking neural networks … on FPGA). This weakens the claim of novelty and requires a clearer demarcation.
- **Ablation improvements are marginal**: Replacing SMSB with a standard 3×3 convolution gives only a drop of ODS -0.004, OIS -0.006, AP -0.003 (Table 1). R2 and R3 question whether this is “significant”. Similarly, MAD yields minimal accuracy improvement over alternatives (R2, R3).
- **Missing justification for SNN-specific benefits**: It is unclear why SNNs are particularly suited to the core challenges of edge detection (precise localization, clean edge maps) compared to other efficient architectures like BNNs (R4). When time step T=1, SNN behaves like a BNN, further weakening the case for SNN-specific advantages (R4).
- **Lack of necessary comparisons and visualizations**:
  - No comparison with other SNN architectures for edge detection to demonstrate that the proposed modifications to the SNN architecture are necessary (R3).
  - No precision-recall curve comparison with existing methods (R1).
  - No visualization of activation maps from the five convolutional layers in SMSB to demonstrate their practical effectiveness (R2).
  - Energy consumption is not compared to DNN baselines quantitatively (R1).
- **Missing architectural details**: The structure of the Spiking-VGG Block and Up Pred Block (Figure 1) is not described (R1). Symbols in equations (e.g., Eq. 3, 4) are insufficiently explained, and the mathematical formulation is not rigorous (R5).
- **Potential computational overhead of multi-scale convolutions** is not analyzed: R3 asks whether the same computational budget applied to other methods would yield similar results, and whether performance gain is solely due to multi-scale.
- **Energy consumption claims need practical proof**: The low energy values are theoretical; no details on hardware implementation or how to achieve them in practice are provided (R5). R1 asks for direct energy comparison to DNNs.
- **Table inconsistency**: In Table 1, energy consumption for the second row of the last block is 7.56 mJ while identical configuration rows show 7.59 mJ (R2). Also, bold values in tables are not defined; the caption should explain which metric each bold value corresponds to (R5).
- **Presentation issues**: Duplicate abbreviation definitions (R1). Citation formatting inconsistent (e.g., “Maass (1997)” vs. “(Maass, 1997)” – R4).
