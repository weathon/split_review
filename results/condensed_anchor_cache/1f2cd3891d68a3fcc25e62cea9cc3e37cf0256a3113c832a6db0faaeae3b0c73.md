## Merged Review

### Summary

Segmentation Dreamer (SD) extends the Dreamer MBRL framework to visually distracting environments by introducing an auxiliary task that reconstructs only task-relevant image components guided by segmentation masks. A selective L2 loss handles imperfect masks from pre-trained segmentation models (e.g., SAM). Evaluated on DMC and Meta-World tasks with added 2D background distractions, SD achieves better sample efficiency and final performance than prior work, especially in sparse-reward settings.

### Strengths

- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written, with easy-to-understand figures, text, and mathematical formulations (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- **Intuitive motivation**: The idea of focusing on task-relevant parts mirrors human perception and is reasonable for reducing representational complexity (R1, R3).
- **Solid experimental comparison**: Comparisons with strong baselines and detailed ablations (e.g., on segmentation quality, selective loss) are provided (R1, R2, R3). Comprehensive evaluation on two benchmarks (R2).
- **Thoughtful robustness mechanism**: The selective L2 loss is a thoughtful addition to handle imperfect segmentation masks (R4, R5). Originality in addressing segmentation errors (R5).
- **Significance**: Addressing visual distractions is an important problem for policy deployment (R5).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited generality of experimental setup**: The evaluation focuses on 2D background shifts, where the segmentation mask directly targets the distractor. Generalization to other domain shifts—viewing angle, color, lighting, texture—is not demonstrated (R5). Environments are relatively simple (DMC, Meta-World) and may not capture real-world complexity (R1, R4).
- **Reliance on prior knowledge of task-relevant regions**: The method requires specifying which parts are task-relevant (e.g., agent body), a strong assumption that may be difficult to obtain in complex, cluttered, or dynamically changing environments (R3, R1, R4). This limits the scope of potential usage (R3).
- **Simplified problem setting**: By reconstructing only masked regions, the problem becomes simpler than original full-image reconstruction. This could limit generalization if task-irrelevant regions contain useful contextual information or if the task definition shifts (R3). Potential overfitting to the specific segmentation masks (R3).
- **Stability concerns**: Results on Meta-World show large reward variance, suggesting robustness issues in more complex/dynamic environments. Further analysis and mitigation strategies (e.g., hyperparameter tuning) are needed (R1).
- **Computational overhead**: Using fine-tuned SAM for mask generation introduces additional computational cost during training (R2).
- **Lack of thorough comparison with segmentation-augmented RL methods**: The paper does not sufficiently compare with existing works that combine foundation models (e.g., SAM) with RL, e.g., using segmentation as preprocessing or feeding masked images directly to agents. A quantitative comparison beyond ablation would clarify advantages and limitations (R4, R5).
- **Need for deeper analysis of segmentation quality and impact**: 
  - How does mask quality affect performance? Provide results comparing native L2 loss vs. selective L2 loss (R2). 
  - How many samples are needed for fine-tuning the pre-trained segmentation model? Would the selective loss become less important with more samples? (R3)
  - What is the training objective for the binary mask predictor head? (R3)
  - Visualize the predicted masks from the SD mask predictor and the foundation model to understand similarities/differences (R3).
  - How does the model perform when segmentation errors are systematic or masks omit critical information? (R4, R1)
- **Questions on mask correctness in sparse-reward scenarios**: In sparse-reward tasks, rewards alone may be insufficient to drive good mask predictions, risking omission of essential features (e.g., background context) (R1). How is correct identification of task-relevant parts ensured without strong reward signals?
- **Ambiguity in dynamic environments**: How does the method ensure consistent performance when distractions and task-relevant features are dynamic, or adapt to new environments without retraining? (R4)
