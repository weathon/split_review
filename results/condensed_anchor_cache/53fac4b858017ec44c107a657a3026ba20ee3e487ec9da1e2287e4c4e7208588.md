## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes incorporating Variational Inequality (VI) methods (Extragradient and Nested-Lookahead) into the MADDPG algorithm to improve convergence and stability in multi-agent reinforcement learning. It introduces LA-MADDPG, EG-MADDPG, and LA-EG-MADDPG and evaluates them on zero-sum games and MPE tasks, showing performance improvements and policies closer to equilibrium.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and includes detailed pseudocode (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- The idea of viewing MARL as a VI problem and introducing VI solvers (Extragradient, Nested-Lookahead) into MARL is novel, bridging the gap between the MAPG (cooperative) and VI (zero-sum) literatures (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
- The paper clearly shows how MADDPG can be reformulated into a VI problem and how gradient descent is a special case of solving such VI problems, making the algorithm accessible (Reviewer 4).  
- Addressing hyperparameter sensitivity and training instability is an important problem in MARL (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 3 and 4 report that the proposed empirical methods yield improvements over standard optimizers, and Reviewer 4 emphasizes the mathematical promise of the framework. (Note: Reviewer 1 disagrees about the presence of improvement.)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and problem alignment**: The paper claims to address hyperparameter sensitivity, but no experiments or analysis are provided to demonstrate reduced sensitivity (Reviewers 1, 2). It does not identify which hyperparameters are sensitive, nor show how the proposed method stabilizes that sensitivity (Reviewer 2). Rotational dynamics inherent to MARL are not eliminated by switching optimizers, and VI methods can introduce additional hyperparameters (Reviewer 1). The specific problem with MADDPG that the paper addresses is unclear; the focus appears to be on a general optimization issue rather than a specialized MARL challenge (Reviewer 3).  
- **Theoretical justification**: There is insufficient explanation of why standard optimization fails and how VI methods specifically benefit MADDPG/MARL. The paper does not analyze the optimization landscape (non-convexity, saddle points) and does not explain which specific issues the proposed methods address (Reviewer 3). Extragradient methods are typically confined to coherent saddle point problems, an assumption that may not hold in MARL; without addressing this theoretical mismatch, there is no assurance of convergence to differential Nash equilibria (Reviewer 1).  
- **Experimental limitations**: Results in Figure 2 do not show improvement and exhibit high variance, casting doubt on the method's reliability and robustness (Reviewers 1, 2). Figures 2 and 6 lack learning curves for MPE experiments; only evaluation episodes are shown (Reviewer 3). The experiments are simplistic—only rock-paper-scissors, matching pennies, and MPE predator-prey—and do not test on more complex domains (e.g., SMAC) or with more agents, leaving scalability unaddressed (Reviewers 3, 4). The method is only applied to MADDPG, which is outdated and has known instability; newer algorithms (MATD3, MAAC, MAPPO) are not referenced or compared, limiting the contribution (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **Incremental contribution and performance questions**: The proposed methods are essentially swapping Adam for existing VI methods without deeper modification (Reviewer 3). In some tasks (e.g., MPE predator-prey, rock-paper-scissors), EG does not show significant improvement and LA-EG-MADDPG underperforms (Reviewer 4). It is unclear why the combination does not work well, and no theoretical proof is provided that VI methods are superior for this specific VI formulation (Reviewer 4). The paper states that full convergence to Nash equilibrium was not achieved even with the proposed methods, and it is unknown whether tuning hyperparameters could reach it; such an ablation on hyperparameter sensitivity is missing (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minor issues**: Incorrect reference order (line 150–151: MADDPG cites DDPG but DDPG is a later paper); imprecise notation “each i=1,…,N agent” should be “each agent i=1,…,N” (line 224); missing references for MARL applications listed in the introduction (Reviewer 4). One reviewer (Reviewer 4) also questioned the claim about “stronger evaluation metrics” than reward.  
- **Conflict among reviewers**: While Reviewer 4 rates the approach as mathematically promising and novel (score 5), Reviewers 1–3 are more critical about the motivation, experimental design, and limited evidence of improvement (scores 3).
