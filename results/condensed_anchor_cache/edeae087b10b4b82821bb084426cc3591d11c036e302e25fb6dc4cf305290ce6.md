## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LoRAPrune, a structured pruning framework for LLMs that integrates with LoRA. It uses a LoRA-guided pruning criterion to estimate importance, avoiding gradients of pre-trained weights to save memory. An iterative pruning procedure removes redundant channels and heads. Experiments on LLaMA show improvements over LLM-Pruner in perplexity and memory usage.

### Strengths
- The paper targets an important domain: improving LLM inference efficiency and compatibility with LoRA fine-tuning. (R1)
- The proposed method reduces memory overhead during pruning by using LoRA weights and gradients instead of pre-trained weight gradients, potentially enabling pruning on a wider range of devices. (R1, R3, R4, R5)
- Experimental results show promising performance improvements over baselines (e.g., at 50% compression, PPL reduction of 4.81 on WikiText2 and 3.46 on PTB, and 52.6% memory reduction). (R1, R2, R5)
- One reviewer (R5, score 6) found the LoRA-guided weight importance criterion original, interesting, and a good practical contribution, though other reviewers raised novelty concerns. (R5, contrast with R1, R3)
- The framework concurrently enables structured pruning and LoRA fine-tuning. (R2, R4)
- The pruning criterion guided by LoRA principles is novel and useful. (R2)
- Comprehensive experiments using LLaMA models are presented. (R2)
- Presentation is clear with high-quality figures; derivations in Section 3.2 are intriguing and effectively show memory savings. (R3, R4)
- The evaluation is impressively thorough. (R3)
- The method achieves practical speedup via structured sparsity. (R4)
- Unifying parameter-efficient fine-tuning and structured pruning saves memory efficiently. (R4)
- Reduced resource requirements for pruning and fine-tuning constitute a good practical contribution. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to LLM-Pruner:** Multiple reviewers (R1, R3) noted that the methodology is very similar to LLM-Pruner. The key difference is shifting importance computation from backbone weights to LoRA adapters. R1 and R3 found this incremental; R3 stated the overall methodology is nearly identical. (R1, R3)
- **Questionable claim about LLM-Pruner not supporting tuning:** In Table 1, the authors state LLM-Pruner does not support tuning. However, LLM-Pruner uses structured pruning and is compatible with LoRA adapters, as per its abstract and experiments. (R1)
- **Missing LLM-Pruner baseline in Figure 3:** R1 notes that Figure 3 omits the LLM-Pruner baseline, which would help contextualize LoRAPrune's performance. (R1)
- **Significant perplexity degradation even at low pruning rates:** Reviewers (R2, R3) observed substantial PPL increases in Table 2. For LLaMA-7B, even at 20% pruning, the PPL degradation is noticeable. At 50% pruning, the PPL drop is severe (e.g., degradation of ~4 points). R3 notes that a PPL degradation of just 1 surpasses the performance gap between LLaMA-13B and LLaMA-30B, and a ~0.2 difference separates LLaMA from Llama-2. Thus, a degradation of 4 renders the model practically unusable. R2 questions whether such drops are acceptable and if 50% pruning is overly ambitious. (R2, R3)
- **WANDA results contradict previous claims:** R2 notes that the WANDA results in the paper show significant PPL degradation, contradicting the claims in the WANDA paper. (R2)
- **Lack of context from contemporaneous studies:** R2 requests PPL and other metrics as reported by contemporaneous works (e.g., Sheared-Llama, Wanda) for better comparison. (R2)
- **Fine-tuning on large corpus may diminish importance of pruning criteria:** R3, citing Sheared-Llama, argues that fine-tuning a pruned model on a relatively extensive corpus (e.g., RedPajama) reduces the disparities between LLM-Pruner and more sophisticated pruning criteria. Thus, the practical value of LoRAPrune's importance criterion is questionable when the pruned model is finely tuned. (R3)
- **Memory constraints of LLM-Pruner can be mitigated by off-loading:** R3 notes that the memory overhead of LLM-Pruner can be reduced via CPU off-loading, weakening the claimed advantage of LoRAPrune. (R3)
- **Lack of motivation for LoRA-guided criterion:** R4 finds that the paper does not sufficiently explain why LoRA can guide pruning; it simply shows it estimates importance without justification or analysis of why it is better than other criteria. (R4)
- **Confusing and unclear equations and notation:** Multiple reviewers (R4, R5) report issues with the equations. Examples: abnormal superscripts/subscripts (R4); Figure 2 numbers not explained; notation abuse (I_ij vs I_g); top-s% not formally defined; eq 11 notation mathematically wrong; algorithm 1 missing subscript ('|_t'). (R4, R5)
- **Missing experimental validation of importance approximation accuracy:** R5 asks for supporting results on how accurate the LoRA-guided importance approximation is, and whether better approximation leads to better performance. (R5)
- **Memory usage concern:** R5 questions whether \hat{I} has the same dimension as model weights W, and if so, compute the same as gradients w.r.t. W, undermining the memory saving claim. (R5)
- **Missing theoretical FLOPs comparison:** R5 requests theoretical FLOPs comparison with dL/dW-based importance criteria to quantify savings. (R5)
