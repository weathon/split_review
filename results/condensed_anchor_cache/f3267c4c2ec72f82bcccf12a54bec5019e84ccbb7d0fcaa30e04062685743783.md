## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for 2D video compression. It introduces a Toast-like Sliding Window (TSW) orthographic projection to adapt 3DGS to video, and develops GSVC, an end-to-end pipeline using deformable Gaussians and optical flow guidance. On the UVG dataset, GSVC achieves better rate-distortion performance than NeRV, while providing ~30–40% faster rendering and inherent stream decoding. However, reviewers raise concerns about limited novelty (many components adapted from prior work), lack of comparisons to traditional codecs (HEVC/VVC) and advanced neural methods (VCT, DCVC), per-video training requirements, and unclear implementation details.

### Strengths
1. **Improved inference speed over INR methods**: GSVC achieves ~30–40% higher FPS than NeRV (R1, R2, R3).
2. **Pioneering application of 3DGS to video compression** – the first work to adapt 3D Gaussian models for 2D video, with minimal assumptions about content (R2).
3. **Toast-like Sliding Window (TSW) and orthographic projection** are interesting, reasonable designs for treating time as an extra dimension and adapting perspective projection (R3, R4).
4. **Flexible bitrate-availability tradeoffs** valuable for streaming applications (R2).
5. **High adaptability** to various 3DGS-based compression methods (R2).
6. **Comprehensive experiments** on UVG show GSVC achieves favorable rate-distortion vs. NeRV (R1, R2, R3, R4).
7. **Clear writing and logical flow** (R4).
8. **Code release promised**, fostering reproducibility (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: Key techniques (time-position embeddings, Gaussian attribute variation over time, offset extraction) are adapted from NeRF and 4DGS [1]; the compression framework aligns closely with HAC [2]; coordinate compression with GPCC was previously introduced; optical flow loss is from 4DGS [3]; GPU-based ANS ae/ad is from CompressAI [4] (R1). This raises questions about the paper’s contribution to RGB video compression (R1).
2. **No comparison with traditional codecs** such as HEVC/H.265, VVC (R1, R3, R4). Such comparisons are needed to gauge practical utility and computation costs for encoding (R3, R4).
3. **Limited evaluation baselines**: Only NeRV is compared; other neural video compression methods (e.g., VCT, DCVC) are missing. GSVC is evaluated only at low bitrates/bpp, while methods like VCT/DCVC demonstrate PSNR >34 on UVG (R2).
4. **Per-video training required** – a limitation for real-world usage (R3).
5. **Unclear bidirectional rendering process** in TSW: how future frames are rendered and what additional information they provide beyond past frames is not clearly explained (R4). The mixing of Vb and Vf images (e.g., averaging) is not specified (R2).
6. **Missing ablation of FiLM vs. MLP** to justify the design choice with performance/computation cost comparisons (R4).
7. **Potential depth-sorting inconsistency**: When Gaussians cross from Vf to Vb between consecutive frames, relative depths may reverse, leading to different coloring after alpha blending. The authors should report if this has been observed (R2).
8. **Gap in frame availability** after complete bit decoding – what factors cause this? (R2)
9. **Figure 1 ground truth image appears low quality**; 3DGS reconstruction is prone to high PSNR but missing details – the authors should address this for compression tasks (R3).
10. **Temporal inconsistency hard to assess without video examples** – video comparisons should be provided (R3).
11. **Unclear anchor point selection** in TSW (R4). Rasterization process under orthographic projection should be included (R4).
12. **Typos**: e.g., line 243 “us” → “use” (R4).
