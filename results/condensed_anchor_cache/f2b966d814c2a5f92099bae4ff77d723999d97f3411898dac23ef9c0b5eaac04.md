- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Meta Self-Distillation (MSD), a meta-learning method for few-shot image classification that aims to reduce reliance on shortcut features by enforcing consistency between models fine-tuned on different augmented views of the same support set. The inner-loop produces multiple finetuned models (each from a different augmented view), and the outer-loop trains the meta-initialization to maximize the similarity of their predictions on the same query. Experiments on miniImageNet and TieredImageNet under various shot settings and cross-domain scenarios show improved performance over MAML and some variants. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (soundness 2–3, presentation 2–3, contribution 2) and one reviewer rated it 5 (soundness 3, presentation 3, contribution 2). The more positive reviewer found the idea simple and plausible with broad applicability; the others saw the novelty as incremental and raised concerns about presentation, experimental rigor, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The motivation — that learning with higher consistency reduces shortcut feature reliance and improves generalization — is plausible and technically sound (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). The approach is a clear adaptation of contrastive/consistency principles to the meta-learning inner/outer loop (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed method is simple and generally applicable to MAML-based few-shot learning algorithms (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Validation on multiple datasets (miniImageNet, TieredImageNet) under different shot settings and cross-domain experiments shows consistent improvement over MAML (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The idea of learning representation consistency across different tasks is novel in the optimization-based meta-learning scope (Reviewer 2, 3).
- The visualization in Figure 5 strongly supports the validity of the core idea (Reviewer 3).
- The paper provides a thorough survey of related work (Reviewer 3).
- Figures 1 and 3 are innovative (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is easy to follow overall, with clear formulation (Reviewers 2, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The approach is seen as incremental by three reviewers. Reviewer 2 notes that data augmentation for feature consistency is already used in few-shot learning (e.g., Ni et al., ICML 2021; Seo et al., Neural Networks 2021). Reviewer 3 points to three additional papers that undermine novelty: “Towards Reliable Neural Machine Translation with Consistency-Aware Meta-Learning,” “Consistency-Guided Meta-learning for Bootstrapping Semi-supervised Medical Image Segmentation,” and “DAC-MR: Data Augmentation Consistency Based Meta-Regularization for Meta-Learning.” Reviewer 4 describes the method as a simple combination of MAML + multi-teacher distillation (cites “Learning from multiple teacher network,” KDD 2017). (The more positive Reviewer 1 considered the motivation plausible but did not comment on novelty relative to these works.)
- **Presentation and clarity issues** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4):
  - Section 4.1 heavily overlaps with Section 1; Figure 2 is far from its reference (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 3 (overview of MSD) is unkempt and draft-like (Reviewer 2). Several figures need more careful drawing (Reviewer 3).
  - Tables are far from where they are referenced, hindering reading (Reviewer 2).
  - Algorithm 1 is not used; variable names in Algorithm 1 and 2 are inconsistent (C vs L_cls, L_ck). The symbols S and Q are reversed in Equation 4. Variables n and f(.) are not defined (Reviewer 3).
  - The term “self-distillation” is misleading — the method is not actually related to distillation (Reviewer 3). Some arguments do not align with the referenced papers (Reviewer 3).
  - Unicorn-MAML is not cited (Reviewer 3). 
  - Many typos: inconsistent “maml” vs “MAML” in Sec 5.2.1; “Table 10” should be “Table 1”; “inconsistency knowledge” vs “inconsistent knowledge”; “Table 5” should be “Table 6”; “alignment In various settings” (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - The title should add “few-shot image classification” to avoid confusion (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing detail on the number of inner-loop steps used for quantitative comparisons (Reviewer 4; only provided for visualization). Number of finetuned models (views) in inner-loop not specified (Reviewer 4).
  - The claim that “models’ weights are pre-trained on the meta-train set to initialize” is not standard for MAML; it is unclear whether all MAML baselines were pretrained in the experiments, raising fairness concerns (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental scope and comparisons**:
  - Missing comparisons with more MAML-based algorithms, especially in cross-domain settings (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). Cross-domain tables compare mainly metric-learning methods; need optimization-based baselines (MAML, Unicorn-MAML) (Reviewer 2). MAML baselines are absent from cross-domain experiments (Reviewer 4).
  - Missing comparisons with augmentation/hallucination-based few-shot methods (Ni et al., ICML 2021; Seo et al., 2021) (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing comparisons with task-adaptive cross-domain methods (e.g., “Learning to Balance,” ICLR 2020; “Meta-Learning with Task-Adaptive Loss Function,” ICCV 2021; “Learning to Forget,” CVPR 2020/TPAMI 2022) and with causal-intervention approaches for shortcut learning (“Interventional Few-Shot Learning,” NeurIPS 2020) (Reviewer 4).
  - Need for a simple baseline: Ensemble of MAML models (each trained with a different augmentation) to isolate the effect of multi-view consistency (Reviewer 4).
  - Omniglot dataset is not used, which is uncommon in meta-learning (Reviewer 3). 20-way tasks should be tested on other datasets as well (Reviewer 3).
  - The augmented few-shot learning evaluation (Section 5.2.1) is redundant; the paper’s conclusion that “MSD has an approximate 2% increase irrespective of weak or strong perturbations” makes the section seem unnecessary. Investigate how the gap changes under varying degrees of augmentation (Reviewer 3).
- **Sensitivity and hyperparameter tuning**: The choice of data augmentations and hyperparameters is critical. Tables 8 and 9 (Appendix) suggest very specifically fine-tuned augmentations are required; sensitivity analysis is lacking (Reviewer 1, 2). Is MSD sensitive to the selection of augmentation methods? (Reviewer 2)
- **Theoretical analysis needed**: Several conflicts with prior meta-learning theory are not discussed. Studies [4][5][6] (from Reviewer 3’s list: “A Closer Look at the Training Strategy for Modern Meta-Learning,” “Meta-Learning Requires Meta-Augmentation,” “Meta-Learning without Memorization”) show that with enough tasks, generalization error is unaffected by intra-task overfitting (shortcut features). The paper neither cites these nor explains why its conclusions differ. A theoretical analysis on how consistency affects generalization error (e.g., PAC-Bayes, uniform stability, information-theoretic bounds) is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited modality**: The method is only applicable to image data, losing the modality-agnostic advantage of MAML (Reviewer 1, 3). Does MSD work beyond image classification? (Reviewer 3, Question 3)
- **Semi-supervised extension**: Could unlabeled query data be used for consistency as in self-supervised learning? (Reviewer 1)
- **Grad-CAM++ visualizations insufficient**: MSD sometimes shows higher attention on background, and MAML already focuses on the object. Better examples are needed, and visualizations for other methods (e.g., ProtoMAML, task-adaptive methods) should be included to support the claim that MSD reduces shortcut learning (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details on consistency computation**: Is consistency the cosine similarity between embeddings or between class distributions? Figure 3 suggests distributions, but not clearly defined. Equation 5 compares v_i to the average v̄; is this the best choice? What about using max or min? (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing accessibility**: Reference code is not provided (Reviewer 3).
