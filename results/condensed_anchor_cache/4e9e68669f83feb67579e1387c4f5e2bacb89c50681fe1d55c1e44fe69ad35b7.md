## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Successive Policy Re-weighting (SPR), an online alignment method that uses a weighted supervised learning approach (similar to AWR/RWR) to reduce memory requirements relative to PPO and DPO. SPR requires loading only one model per update step, matching SFT's peak memory. The authors derive the method from TRPO, using a W-function to avoid learning a Q-function. Experiments on TL;DR and Open LLM Leaderboard show memory reduction (~50% vs. PPO) and mixed performance—outperforming DPO on some benchmarks, but PPO still appears stronger in some plots. Reviewers largely rate the paper as weak (scores mostly 3), with one reviewer giving 5 for the interesting integration of TRPO and outperformance over DPO. Key criticisms include limited novelty (method aligns with existing offline RL and prior alignment works), missing comparisons (e.g., RWR/AWR, GRPO, SimPO, Remax, SPIN, iterative DPO), lack of ablation (especially on the W-function estimation), unclear advantage of the proposed estimator over closed-form alternatives, incomplete experimental comparisons (e.g., no standard deviations, no runtime or FLOP comparisons, PPO/DPO baselines not fully characterized), and unclear writing (preliminaries too long, proofs incomplete).

### Strengths
1. The problem of memory-efficient LLM alignment is significant and underexplored. (R1, R3, R4)
2. SPR matches SFT peak memory usage, offering ~50% reduction vs. PPO by not requiring a value function. (R1, R2, R3)
3. The method outperforms DPO and SFT on 3 out of 6 benchmarks (R1). One reviewer specifically notes outperformance over DPO across different environments and LLMs. (R2)
4. The idea of integrating TRPO into alignment is intriguing. (R2)
5. The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (R3)
6. The method is interesting and appears to have decent performance. (R3)
7. The paper provides empirical evidence supporting effectiveness. (R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The method closely follows existing offline RL literature (AWR/RWR, Advantage-Weighted Regression) [11] and prior alignment works (e.g., Implicit Language Q-learning [Snell et al., 2022]) with the main technical contribution being the estimation of the normalization factor. (R4) No ablation study on this design choice is provided. (R1, R4)
2. **Missing comparisons**:  
   - Absent comparisons with other memory-light alignment methods such as RWR/AWR, GRPO (DeepSeekMath), SimPO, Agent-Q, Remax, iterative DPO, SPIN, and training-free decoding (Controlled Decoding). (R3, R4)  
   - No comparison with DPO variants that avoid storing a reference model (e.g., pre-computing reference log probs). (R3)  
   - No baselines shown in Figure 3 (only SPR presented). (R3)  
   - Reward model used is weak; suggest using GPT-4 or stronger models from RewardBench. (R3)  
   - TL;DR is not comprehensive; experiments on Ultrafeedback would strengthen the evaluation. (R3)
3. **Unclear advantage of the proposed estimator**:  
   - The advantage of Equation (26) over the closed-form estimator in Equation (23) is not justified. The additional optimization for \(W_\phi\) consumes time and memory; a discussion of solver quality and why it outperforms the empirical estimator is needed. (R2)  
   - The Gumbell noise motivation for Equation (23) is not adequately explained. (R3)
4. **Algorithm 1 and implementation details**:  
   - How the minimization problems in Steps 4, 5, and 6 are solved is unclear. (R2)  
   - The sequential optimization may cause error propagation: if Step 4’s \(\phi\) is poorly solved, it affects \(\varphi\) learning in Step 5. (R2)  
   - In Algorithm 2, \(Q_\phi\) is introduced but never used. (R4)  
   - The implementation of best-of-N needs clarification (e.g., is it similar to ReST or SuperHF?). (R3)
5. **Incomplete experimental reporting**:  
   - In Figure 2, SPR N8 takes 5 iterations to match DPO’s single iteration; DPO and PPO are shown as horizontal lines, which is not a fair comparison—additional iterations for DPO are required. (R2)  
   - No standard deviations or statistical significance (e.g., across random seeds) are provided for Table 5. (R2)  
   - PPO still appears much better than SPR for most of the training in Figure 2. (R3)  
   - Why does SPR sustain performance through 7 iterations when prior iterative methods (e.g., iterative DPO, SPIN) decline after about 3 iterations? Are there critical design factors? (R4)
6. **Missing runtime and memory analyses**:  
   - No runtime comparison between DPO and SPR on the same dataset and hardware, or FLOPs analysis, is provided. (R3)  
   - Request for space complexity analysis across methods to theoretically understand memory reductions. (R2)
7. **Unclear proof steps**:  
   - The derivation of \(\pi^*(a|s)\) in Lines 905–906 (Appendix) is incomplete. Steps to go from \(\sum_a \pi(a|s)=1\) to the given formula are not shown; the connection with \(\zeta_s/\alpha d_{\pi_{\text{old}}}(s)\) is missing. (R2)  
   - What is \(\alpha\) in the definition \(\beta = 1/((1-\gamma)\alpha)\) in Theorem 1? (R3)
8. **Writing and presentation**:  
   - Three pages (3–5) of preliminaries and well-known derivations could be moved to the appendix; retain only essential information in the main text. (R1, R4)  
   - Equations 16–20 are directly sourced from [1] (AWR) and should be placed in preliminaries. (R1)  
   - Algorithms: two algorithms are presented but only the latter is used; delete the first or move to appendix. (R4)  
   - Overall writing lacks clarity; large portions of standard derivations and theorems obscure the novel contributions. (R4)
9. **Missing citations**: Key literature on online/offline data trade-offs for LLM alignment (e.g., “Preference Fine-Tuning of LLMs Should Leverage Suboptimal, On-Policy Data”, 2024) and related methods (ReST, SuperHF) are not cited. (R3)
