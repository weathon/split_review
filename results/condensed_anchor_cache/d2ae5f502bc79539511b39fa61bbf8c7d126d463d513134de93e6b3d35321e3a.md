## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a video inverse problem solver that uses only pre-trained image diffusion models, avoiding the need for computationally expensive video diffusion model training. The method treats the time dimension of a video as the batch dimension of image diffusion models and applies spatio-temporal optimization within denoised batches. A batch-consistent diffusion sampling strategy is proposed: the same noise is used across all frames at each diffusion step, synchronizing stochastic components to encourage temporal consistency. Combined with multi-step conjugate gradient optimization (inspired by DDS) on the data term, the method achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction on various spatio-temporal degradations (e.g., deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting) with significant speed and VRAM savings. The method is demonstrated for the non-blind case where the degradation operator is fully known.

### Strengths
1. **Innovative use of image diffusion models for video tasks**: Avoids the difficulty of training video diffusion models, reducing computational resources while still achieving strong performance. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
2. **Batch-consistent sampling**: The simple but original idea of using identical noise across frames at each diffusion step provides temporal consistency without explicit video modeling. This is a key technical novelty. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
3. **Clear and self-contained presentation**: The paper is well-structured with intuitive formulations and symbols, making the method easy to follow. (Reviewers 3,4)
4. **Computational efficiency**: VRAM savings enable deployment in lower-resource environments, and processing is faster than video diffusion alternatives. (Reviewers 1,2)
5. **Comprehensive ablation study**: The role of each component (batch-consistent sampling, multi-step CG) is clearly demonstrated. (Reviewer 3)
6. **State-of-the-art results**: Outperforms prior methods on multiple video inverse problems (e.g., deblurring, super-resolution) on the DAVIS dataset. (Reviewers 1,2,4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited evaluation scope**:  
   - Only tested on the DAVIS dataset, which may not capture diverse real-world content (e.g., high-frame-rate videos, highly dynamic scenes, varying frame rates and motion complexities). (Reviewer 1)  
   - Test degradations are synthesized using point spread functions (PSF), not real-world degradations such as those in the GoPro dataset (deblurring), low bitrate compression artifacts, or old film noise. (Reviewers 1,2)  
   - Missing comparisons with non-diffusion-based video restoration methods (e.g., optical flow based or recurrent neural network based methods). (Reviewer 1)  
   - Missing comparisons with recent blind video restoration methods that handle unknown degradation operators; the paper only compares to older ADMM methods. (Reviewer 4)

2. **Incremental technical novelty**: The main contribution is adapting batch-consistent sampling to image diffusion models. The method heavily relies on established techniques (Tweedie denoising, multi-step conjugate gradients from DDS). While results are impressive, the technical contribution is considered relatively incremental by some reviewers. (Reviewer 3)

3. **Non-blind assumption limits practical significance**: The method requires full knowledge of the degradation operator A(X). In practice, obtaining such an operator for real videos is very difficult. The paper does not explore or provide insights on how the prior would work in blind restoration where the operator is unknown. The same-noise sampling implicitly forces frames to be similar but does not model temporal dynamics; the only temporal constraint comes from the degradation operator. If the operator must also be estimated, the approach may not generalize. (Reviewer 4)  
   - This point is echoed by the request to compare with blind video restoration methods and to clarify the applicability beyond the non-blind case. (Reviewer 4)

4. **Incomplete ablation on pretrained models**: The paper does not study how the choice of different image diffusion model architectures (e.g., DDPM, DDIM, LDM) or different pre-training datasets affects final performance. It is unclear whether the algorithm yields consistent conclusions across various backbones. (Reviewer 2)

5. **Missing analysis of step count and failure cases**:  
   - The paper uses 20 steps for DDIM but 1000 steps for NFE for baselines; it is unclear if the baselines would show a notable performance drop with 20-step DDIM. (Reviewer 3)  
   - No specific failure cases are provided; metric evaluations may not fully reflect viewing experience, and a user study is requested. (Reviewer 2)  
   - No demonstration on video frame interpolation or high frame rate datasets, leaving the generalization to other tasks unclear. (Reviewer 1)

6. **Small technical complaints**: The image quality in the manuscript is low; a high-quality version is needed. (Reviewer 2)  
   - One reviewer (Reviewer 1, rating 8) was notably more positive about the contribution and efficiency, while other reviewers (ratings 6,6,6 with one contribution 2) were more critical about novelty and practical scope. This contrast is preserved.
