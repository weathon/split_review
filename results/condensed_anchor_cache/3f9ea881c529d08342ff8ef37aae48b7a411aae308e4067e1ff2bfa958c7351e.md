## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper provides a formal theoretical analysis of how residual connections and normalization layers mitigate oversmoothing in linearized Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). Based on insights about the centering step in normalization, it proposes a new principled normalization layer, GraphNormv2. All reviewers acknowledge the clear presentation and the value of the theoretical foundation. However, there is disagreement about the novelty of the residual connection analysis (one reviewer finds it obvious and similar to prior work). Several reviewers raise concerns about the scope of the theory (limited to linearized GNNs with random weights), experimental evaluation (only homophilic graphs, few architectures), and some inconsistencies in the empirical results (large standard deviations, weaker improvement for certain architectures). Two reviewers gave scores of 8 and two gave scores of 6, indicating a split in overall assessment.

### Strengths

- Provides a thorough theoretical analysis of oversmoothing for residual connections and normalization layers, supported by detailed proofs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Introduces a novel, principled normalization layer (GraphNormv2) that addresses a specific limitation of existing methods; the method is simple but effective (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and logically structured, with a good balance of theory and empirical evaluation (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Technical results are extensive and detailed, with gradual exposition and connections to existing literature (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments are conducted on multiple real-world datasets and show convincing performance in support of the theoretical findings (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical scope is limited.** The analysis considers only linearized GNNs (except Remark 4.6) and random weights, which corresponds mainly to initialization. It is unclear how the insights transfer to non-linear GNNs (e.g., with ReLU or tanh) or to trained networks where weights are no longer random (Reviewers 1, 3). The authors mention bridging this gap in future work but do not discuss specific anticipated challenges (Reviewer 3).
- **Residual connection analysis is not surprising and has prior art.** One reviewer notes that the update rule (Equation 4) directly adds the initial features, so preventing collapse is obvious. Proposition 4.2 should state the condition strictly (if x_i=0 the conclusion is invalid). Proposition 4.3 is similar to Theorem 2 in GCNII (Chen et al., ICML 2020) and should cite that work (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental results on homophilic graphs only.** The method is tested solely on homophilic datasets (e.g., Cora, ogbn-arxiv, MUTAG). Oversmoothing is especially pronounced on heterophilic graphs; experiments on heterophilic datasets (e.g., Texas, Cornell, Arxiv-year) are needed to demonstrate generalizability. It is also unclear whether the theoretical analysis suggests any challenges for applying GraphNormv2 to heterophilic graphs (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited range of GNN architectures.** The authors test only GCN, GAT, and GIN. Other architectures susceptible to oversmoothing, such as GraphSAGE and diffusion-based models (GDN, DCNN), should be evaluated (Reviewer 4).
- **Large standard deviations and inconsistencies in Table 1.** Standard deviations for GIN on MUTAG, PROTEINS, PTC-MR, and Cora are quite large, reducing confidence in the results (Reviewer 2). Moreover, on ogbn-arxiv, GIN with BatchNorm, PairNorm, GraphNorm, PowerEmbed, and GroupNorm performs worse than no normalization (6% vs. 25.7%) for #layers=20, which contradicts the claim that batch normalization prevents collapse. The reviewer finds this confusing (Reviewer 1).
- **GraphNormv2 improvement is not consistent across architectures and tasks.** On ogbn-arxiv, GraphNormv2 shows clear improvement for GIN but not for GCN and GAT. The reasons for this are not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **No quantification of relative importance.** The paper shows that residual connections and normalization help through different mechanisms, but does not attempt to quantify which technique has a more significant impact against oversmoothing, either through bounds or empirical measurement (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues.** There is an error in line 118 (Reviewer 2). Also, a reviewer asks: if residual connections alone (Figure 1) outperform all normalization variants (including GraphNormv2), why use normalization at all? This overlaps with the criticism that normalization may be unnecessary when residual connections are present (Reviewer 1).
