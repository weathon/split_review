## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes to improve video decoding from fMRI by aligning brain responses across subjects using an optimal transport functional alignment method, followed by linear regression to predict latent video frame representations from pre-trained encoders (CLIP, VD-VAE). Compared to an anatomically-aligned baseline, out-of-subject decoding performance improves by up to 75%, and the approach outperforms classical single-subject approaches when fewer than 100 minutes of data are available for the tested subject. The study uses only three subjects. One reviewer (rating 6) finds the approach novel and noteworthy; other reviewers (ratings 3, 3, 5) raise significant concerns about novelty, sample size, potential confounds from shared stimuli, and missing comparisons with existing methods.

### Strengths

- The paper provides interesting insights into the use of functional alignment for studying BOLD responses in naturalistic stimuli (Reviewer 1).
- One reviewer finds the use of functional alignment to improve decoding of visual representations across subjects novel and noteworthy (Reviewer 2).
- The quantification of decoder performance with respect to available data size is noteworthy (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed ideas are simple, intuitive, and generic, applicable to different models (Reviewer 3).
- The experimental setup is well designed to examine the hypotheses, and findings are clearly presented and motivated (Reviewer 1).
- The method is simple and easy to implement (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Very small subject sample**: Only three subjects are used, making it unclear whether findings generalize to a larger population (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The small dataset also raises concerns about model generalization (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential confound from shared stimuli**: The left-out subject watches the same video as the reference subject, creating a possible data leakage mechanism. The alignment may learn to map responses based on stimulus similarity rather than genuine functional correspondence. The paper lacks a clear demonstration that the alignment is based on underlying functional organization, not simply matching responses to the same stimuli. The shared stimulus could drive the observed performance boost and inflate decoding performance when tested on the same stimulus category. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparison with state-of-the-art methods**: The proposed method is not compared with other models, so its effectiveness is not convincing and requires further validation (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). A comparison with state-of-the-art methods should be included (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty**: The novelty of the approach requires further elaboration (Reviewer 3). Although the alignment methodology itself is not entirely novel (Reviewer 1), one reviewer (Reviewer 2, rating 6) found the application novel; other reviewers (Reviewer 3 and 4, rating 3) cited insufficient novelty.
- **Difficulty in choosing loss hyperparameters**: The loss function has three parameters; how to select them is not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **Uses only one reference subject**: The method relies on a single reference subject; handling multiple reference subjects in practice is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Sensitivity to latent representation not explored**: The choice of latent representation (CLIP, VD-VAE) and feature granularity is not justified, and sensitivity to this choice is not examined. The restriction to linear regression for decoding is also not justified. (Reviewer 1)
- **Choice of retrieval metric not justified**: It is unclear why standard metrics such as mean average precision or NDCG are not used (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing details and unclear presentation**:
  - Equation (1) lacks sufficient context, requiring the reader to refer back to Thual et al. 2022 (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 2 should have higher resolution to interpret false retrieval cases; more description in the appendix is needed (Reviewer 1).
  - It is unclear what “one repetition” means in line 268 (Reviewer 2).
  - There is an apparent contradiction: line 281 says left-out subjects watch the same videos, but line 265 says test stimuli are shown for the first time—are the videos new to subjects? (Reviewer 2)
  - The claim that the approach aligns brain responses in accordance with brain anatomy (line 12) is not demonstrated (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing controlled experiment**: To strengthen evidence of cross-subject generalization, a comparison where at least one subject is shown different stimuli should be added (Reviewer 2).
