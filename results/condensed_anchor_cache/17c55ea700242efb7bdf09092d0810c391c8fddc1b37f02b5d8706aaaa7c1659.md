## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper challenges the assumption that post-hoc importance attribution methods are independent of training details. It empirically demonstrates a strong dependency on the training of the pre-trained model’s classification layer (less than 10% of parameters), showing that this dependency outweighs the pre-training scheme itself. The study finds that using binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss instead of conventional cross-entropy (CE) loss for the final layer consistently improves interpretability metrics across supervised, self-supervised, and CLIP pre-training frameworks. Additionally, non-linear B-cos MLP probes enhance class-specific localization. One reviewer (score 5) found the contribution insufficient, noting that the evaluation metrics and B-cos model are not the authors' innovations.

### Strengths
- Clarity and organization: exceptionally well-written and structured (Reviewers 1, 4).
- In-depth motivation section clearly outlines issues with generating consistent attributions (Reviewer 3).
- Discovery that BCE loss yields better interpretability than CE loss, with insightful analysis of the Softmax Shift-Invariance issue (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Improvements are consistent across multiple visual pre-training frameworks (supervised, self-supervised, contrastive vision-language) (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Experiments cover a diverse set of attribution methods and evaluation metrics, demonstrating that the issue is attribution-method-invariant (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Extensive validation over numerous models, datasets, attribution methods, and metrics (Reviewer 4).
- Plenty of qualitative results (Reviewer 3).
- Simple but impactful findings that are relevant for future interpretable model design (Reviewer 4).
- Reveals and demonstrates the strong dependence of post-hoc attribution methods on classification layer training details (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- Limited architectural diversity: experiments use only ResNet50 backbone; results may not generalize to other architectures, especially Vision Transformers (ViTs). One reviewer (R1) stressed the need to test ViTs with attention-based explanation methods, as their behavior can differ significantly from gradient-based methods on CNNs. Another reviewer (R2) noted the results are not extensive enough to be convincing (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Missing additional explanation methods: the analysis relies primarily on gradient-based attribution methods; including perturbation-based methods such as RISE and Score-CAM would provide complementary perspectives and strengthen conclusions (Reviewer 1).
- Potential cherry-picking of examples: Figures 1 and 6 may show only favorable cases; since GridPG scores in Figure 5 indicate BCE does not always outperform, a broader range of examples (including low-GridPG cases) should be shown to demonstrate variability (Reviewer 1).
- Contribution considered insufficient by one reviewer (R2): the evaluation metrics used and the subsequent B-cos model are not original contributions of the authors; the paper only discovers the impact of training details but does not introduce novel methods (Reviewer 2; contrast with other reviewers who found the findings impactful, see Strengths).
- Figures have obvious flaws and should be carefully revised (Reviewer 2).
- Lack of discussion on why accuracy and attribution quality increase with more complex output layers (e.g., B-cos MLP). Is it simply due to layer size or another reason? Also, the paper should clarify if proper train/validation/test splits were used (Reviewer 3).
- Missing analyses to verify the Softmax Shift-Invariance explanation: reviewer (R2) requested results with more loss functions, specifically cross-entropy loss, to further substantiate the claim (Reviewer 2).
- Unclear why CE produces worse attributions than BCE; reviewer (R3) asked for further explanation of this phenomenon and why the final output layer is so important relative to the rest of the model (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer (R2) questioned the necessity of the B-cos MLP design: could a plain larger MLP achieve the same benefits? (Reviewer 2).
- Minor spelling mistakes present, though they do not hinder understanding (Reviewer 4).
