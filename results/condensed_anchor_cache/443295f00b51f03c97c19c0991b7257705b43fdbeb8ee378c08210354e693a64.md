## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses incremental training for Temporal Knowledge Graph (TKG) completion, focusing on generalizing to new knowledge and handling entities with sparse connections. It proposes a model-agnostic enhancement layer that uses global entity similarity (beyond local GNN neighborhoods) and a weighted sampling strategy that emphasizes infrequent entities. Experiments on ICEWS14 and ICEWS18 (reconstructed by the authors) compare variants of Titer (FT, ER, EWC, weighted sampling, enhancement layer, full) and report MRR improvements of 10% and 15% on the two datasets.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles the relatively new and practical problem of incremental learning for TKG completion, relevant to real-world applications where data arrives continuously.
- It proposes a novel model-agnostic enhancement layer that leverages global entity similarity, providing a creative approach beyond local neighborhood proximity used in existing GNN-based methods.
- The framework is adaptable to existing TKG completion models, and results demonstrate substantial quantitative gains (10% MRR on ICEWS14, 15% on ICEWS18).
- The paper is well-written and well-structured, clearly describing challenges, limitations, related work, proposed solutions, and experiments around three research questions.
- The target problem is interesting, and the authors highlight important challenges (generalization, assimilation, sparse connections for unseen entities).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset diversity and homogeneity.** The experiments use only ICEWS14 and ICEWS18, which differ only by year; no datasets like WIKIDATA or GDELT are tested. The authors reconstruct ICEWS without providing a detailed description of the new dataset. (R3, confidence 5)
- **Insufficient comparison with other TKG completion methods.** Only Titer is used as the base model. The paper mentions many related works but does not include comparisons with recent state-of-the-art models such as:
  - Xu et al., 2023 (historical contrastive learning)
  - Zhang et al., 2023 (long- and short-term representations)
  - Zhu et al., 2021 (sequential copy-generation networks)
  - Cygnet, LCGE
  This limits evidence for the universality of the proposed enhancement layer and weighted sampling. (R2, R3)
- **Method is too simple and lacks deep insights.** One reviewer (rating 3, confidence 5) strongly criticizes that the global similarity and weighted sampling are superficial training strategies without breakthrough contributions. Another reviewer notes that the motivation appears to be a simple combination of two methods without a well-established rationale. (R2, R3)
- **Incremental training premise is questioned.** One reviewer asks: if global similarity is used to enhance entity representations, why not train representations of all entities together from the start? More generally, incremental training typically only reduces training cost and should not outperform full training; the paper's starting point for using incremental training to improve TKGC may be fundamentally wrong. (R3)
- **Real-world deployment considerations are missing.** The paper does not discuss memory footprint, computational cost, scalability with graph size and data arrival rate, or ability to handle streaming data. (R4)
- **Weighted sampling justification is weak.** The paper uses a specific inverse frequency-based sampling function without exploring or justifying alternatives (e.g., mean/max/min inverse frequencies, learned importance weights). (R4)
- **Run time and complexity analysis is absent.** Adding the enhancement layer and weighted sampling introduces computational overhead, but no quantitative analysis of trade-offs is provided. (R1)
- **Inconsistent performance between full method and its components.** In Figure 2.a, the full method is outperformed by the enhancement-layer-only variant on HITS@10 and by the weighted-sampling-only variant on some metrics, raising questions about how the components combine. (R4)
- **Reliance on a single base model (Titer) raises novelty concerns.** While the framework is claimed to be model-agnostic, experiments only demonstrate it on Titer. Adding other base models (with/without the proposed extensions) would significantly strengthen the evaluation. (R1, R3)
- **Minor issues:** In Section 5.2, a LaTeX citation is not compiled properly. (R1)
