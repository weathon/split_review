- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 1, 1, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a continual learning method that selectively freezes important neurons when training on new tasks. Neuron importance is computed using Hebbian learning in a separate network (dual-network design). The method is evaluated on a simple two-task MNIST split (digits 0-4 vs. 5-9) and only compared against a vanilla network. The idea of using Hebbian learning to guide neuron freezing is noted as interesting, but the work is criticized for insufficient conceptual justification, severely limited experiments, lack of comparisons to established continual learning baselines, and missing implementation details. Three reviewers gave scores of 1 (strong reject); one reviewer gave a score of 3, noting the direction may be promising if the conceptual reasoning is sound.

### Strengths

- The use of Hebbian learning to evaluate neuron importance is interesting, as Hebbian learning is a well-known synaptic plasticity rule in animals. This connection may provide valuable insights into how biological mechanisms could support continual learning.
- The approach of combining Hebbian learning with neuron freezing (selectively freezing weights based on importance scores) is recognized as potentially valuable if properly justified.
- One reviewer (score 3) noted that if good conceptual reasoning behind the proposed use of Hebbian learning is provided, the direction explored in the paper may be promising.

### Weaknesses

1. **Conceptual motivation and justification are insufficient.**  
   - It is unclear why a separate network trained with Hebbian learning (a different learning rule) is needed to compute importance scores for neurons in the main network trained with backpropagation. The hypothesis linking Hebbian association by activity to the selection of task-relevant nodes while excluding past-task nodes is not explained.  
   - The paper does not adequately justify the dual-network design or how Hebbian learning in the separate network correlates with importance in the main network.  
   - No explanation is given for why (conceptually) Hebbian learning prevents catastrophic forgetting. A more detailed conceptual background is required for readers lacking this background.

2. **Experimental evaluation is extremely lacking.**  
   - The method is only tested on MNIST with a simple 0-4/5-9 split into two tasks. This setup is too simple to properly evaluate the algorithm’s effectiveness.  
   - The only baseline is a vanilla network; no comparisons are made to any established continual learning methods (despite the paper mentioning prior work). This makes it impossible to assess relative performance.  
   - The absence of comparisons to other continual learning baselines and the use of only a single, trivial scenario severely limit the evidence for the method’s effectiveness.  
   - Multiple earlier studies have used MNIST for continual learning evaluation, yet none are compared.

3. **Missing crucial implementation details.**  
   - The second equation lacks indices, making its interpretation difficult.  
   - The architecture of the neural network used is not specified.  
   - The training and testing processes are not described in sufficient detail (e.g., how the network is tested on different tasks: whether the whole network is used or a mask is applied in the forward pass).  
   - Figures and tables are rudimentary and lack details.

4. **Task identification is unaddressed.**  
   - The method assumes externally-defined task boundaries and does not tackle task identification or new-task recognition. The authors acknowledge this, but without such a component it is questionable whether the method truly realizes continual learning. The relevance of the destructive adaptation problem is diminished without this aspect.

5. **Redundant evaluation metrics.**  
   - All experiments essentially measure the same thing with different metrics. One metric would suffice to demonstrate the core result.

6. **Limited novelty and insufficient survey of prior work.**  
   - The paper does not sufficiently summarize previous work. The novelty of the idea is limited.  
   - No advantages over prior Hebbian-based continual learning methods are demonstrated or discussed.

7. **Practical computational complexity is not evaluated.**  
   - Modern deep learning models have a massive number of neurons. The proposed approach may be impractical if it requires too much computation to evaluate importance per neuron sequentially.

*Note: Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 rated the paper 1 (strong reject) and criticized its experimental weakness, missing comparisons, and lack of conceptual clarity. Reviewer 4 rated the paper 3, indicating that the direction might be promising if the conceptual reasoning is properly established, but still agreed with the core weaknesses about explanation and evaluation.*
