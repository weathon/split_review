## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces 3DRealCar, a large-scale real-world RGB-D car dataset of 2,500 cars scanned using iPhone 14 devices, providing ~200 high-resolution 360-degree views per car along with sparse LiDAR point clouds, part annotations, and three lighting conditions (standard, reflective, dark). The dataset is intended to support research in 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, relighting, car parsing, and perception tasks. Experiments demonstrate applications in 2D/3D downstream tasks, including detection, segmentation, depth estimation, point cloud completion, and 3D car generation. Benchmarks show that state-of-the-art methods struggle under reflective/dark lighting.

### Strengths
- **Diverse, high-quality data**: Includes 2,500 cars from over 100 brands, covering sedans, sports cars, small trucks, with high-resolution images and part annotations (doors, hood, wheels). The dataset is openly available online.
- **Three lighting conditions**: The inclusion of standard, reflective, and dark environments addresses a gap in existing datasets and enables benchmarking of reconstruction robustness. This is considered an interesting and valuable feature.
- **Extensive downstream evaluation**: The paper demonstrates the dataset's utility across multiple tasks: neural rendering, 3D generative modeling, camera-based perception in corner-case scenarios (e.g., cars swerving off road), 2D detection/segmentation, depth estimation, and point cloud completion. For perception, synthesizing corner-case images with the car assets is shown to improve real-data performance.
- **Clear organization and presentation**: The paper is well-structured and clearly presented.

### Weaknesses
- **Data collection method not novel**: The reconstruction pipeline (iPhone RGB-D + COLMAP SfM + 3DGS) is a standard approach; the paper lacks technical novelty in data processing or 3D/2D car understanding.
- **Uniqueness and necessity not convincingly demonstrated**: Prior works (e.g., CADSim, GeoSim) already reconstruct cars from real data; the paper claims higher quality but does not demonstrate that this quality is necessary for downstream tasks or yields clear advantages over existing alternatives.
- **Missing multi-lighting-condition data for the same car**: The dataset includes three lighting conditions, but no car appears in more than one environment. This limits the ability to study lighting effects on appearance and prevents using the dataset for relighting ground truth. Reviewers 1 and 3 identified this as a major gap.
- **Lack of technical contribution**: The contribution is purely the dataset; there is no new method for 3D/2D car understanding. Some reviewers rated contribution as low (1–2).
- **LiDAR quality concern**: The data is captured with iPhone 14 LiDAR, which is sparser than typical automotive or survey-grade LiDAR. The term “3D scanner” in the introduction is misleading and should be clarified.
- **Novel View Synthesis results below state-of-the-art**: Figures 8 and 9 show reconstructions that are considerably inferior to SOTA methods, reducing the impact of the benchmark.
- **Incomplete COLMAP reconstructions**: The provided sample COLMAP output mesh (`textured_output.obj`) is missing the upper half of the car, raising questions about the standard pre-processing quality.
- **Missing details**: Several aspects are unclear: whether the dataset is restricted to academia or allows commercial use; how car parsing maps are used as input to segmentation (binary mask or fine-grained?); and low-level data collection details (e.g., how raw LiDAR points are obtained from iPhone).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Typos (“well-lighting” → “well-lit”, “Dreamcract3D”) and a missing citation format fix (Stable Zero123 author name). Reviewers also suggested adding usage tutorials/examples to increase impact.
