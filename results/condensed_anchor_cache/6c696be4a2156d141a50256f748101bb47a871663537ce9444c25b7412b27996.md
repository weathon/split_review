## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Time-LLM, a reprogramming framework that repurposes frozen large language models (LLMs) for time series forecasting. It aligns time series data with LLM modalities by reprogramming input patches into text prototypes via cross-attention and introduces Prompt-as-Prefix (PaP) to augment input context with declarative prompts (domain knowledge, task instructions, statistics). The approach is evaluated on short-term (M4) and long-term (ETT) forecasting, showing strong performance in supervised, few-shot, and zero-shot settings. Reviewers agree the paper is well-written and the method is novel, but concerns exist about limited datasets, missing baselines, unclear generation mechanism, and the fundamental plausibility of transferring NLP knowledge to time series.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and organization:** The paper is articulately written, systematically structured, and provides sufficient details for reproducibility (Reviewers 1,2,3,5). The motivation and desiderata for enabling LLMs to forecast are clearly presented (Reviewer 2). The explanation of the proposed method is easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty and effectiveness of reprogramming:** The approach of converting time series into text prototype representations via cross-attention using word embeddings as a discretization medium is innovative and promising (Reviewers 3,4,5). This strategy offers a new perspective for cross-modality adaptation in LLMs and shows that not only trained LLMs but also their learned embeddings can transfer to time series problems (Reviewer 4). The use of Prompt-as-Prefix to steer LLM reasoning with declarative prompts (domain expertise, task guidelines) is particularly promising for controlled time series analysis (Reviewer 5). The architectural design, dividing time series into patches and reprogramming each, is logical and well-motivated (Reviewer 5). The paper provides a summary of metrics for pre-trained LLMs (generalizability, data efficiency, reasoning, multimodal knowledge) (Reviewer 1).
- **Thorough empirical evaluation:** The experiments are comprehensive, covering different LLM variations (e.g., GPT-2, Llama, Flan-T5), ablation studies, computational efficiency, and model interpretation (Reviewers 2,3,5). The method demonstrates strong performance, especially in few-shot and zero-shot settings, showing sample efficiency and practical applicability (Reviewers 2,5). Abundant side experiments study the method from various aspects (Reviewer 5). The approach builds upon existing work (PatchTST) but introduces important innovations (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset diversity and scope:** The evaluation is restricted primarily to ETT (long-term) and M4 (short-term) datasets. Multiple reviewers noted that many commonly used benchmarks are missing: Weather, Electricity, Traffic, Influenza-like Illnesses, Exchange Rate, Tourism, M3, and the Monash time series forecasting archive (Reviewers 2,3,4). The ETT datasets involve similar metrics, and their mutual similarities may overinflate performance (Reviewer 3). Only prediction tasks are considered; other time series tasks (e.g., classification) are not evaluated (Reviewer 1). If the paper claims state-of-the-art across many domains, more evidence is needed (Reviewer 4). The paper does not address how the model would perform on multivariate time series with significant cross-correlations (e.g., inter-channel dependencies in complex systems) and does not discuss the limitation that long-term benchmarks favor univariate models while Time-LLM processes each channel separately (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** Several important baselines are absent: statistical methods (AutoARIMA, AutoTHETA, AutoETS, Naive/Seasonal Naive) (Reviewer 2); recent LLM-based forecasting works such as LLM4TS and PromptCast (Reviewer 2); the paper “LLMs are zero-shot forecasters” (optional but would strengthen) (Reviewer 2). N-BEATS and N-HITS were only compared in short-horizon settings (Reviewer 2). The paper does not compare with GPT4TS, which also used a reprogramming approach, regardless of which parts are fine-tuned (Reviewer 1). The provided source code link was empty, preventing detailed verification (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear generation mechanism:** The process for producing the next H steps is ambiguous: it is not clear whether the model operates auto-regressively (predicting one step at a time and feeding it back) or directly predicts all H steps simultaneously (Reviewer 3). This also relates to Figure 3(b), which appears to need a one-step left shift to be auto-regressive, and the role of the projection head is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 5 also asked for clarification on the output projection mechanism (flattening and linear projection) and how \(E'\) is derived from \(E\) (Reviewers 3,5).
- **Fundamental plausibility of modality transfer:** One reviewer expressed strong skepticism about directly transferring natural language knowledge to time series, given that text and time series are distinct modalities and LLMs are not pre-trained on text-time-series pairs (Reviewer 1). While other reviewers found the approach promising, this disagreement highlights a need for deeper justification. Specifically, the paper does not define what constitutes a good time series representation or provide an in-depth discussion of why pre-trained LLMs are capable of producing such representations (Reviewer 1, question). The shared declarative prompts, though providing domain knowledge, do not introduce per-timestep text, raising questions about how they benefit understanding of complex temporal behaviors in different windows (Reviewer 1). Regarding cross-domain adaptation in the zero-shot setup, it is unclear what knowledge from the source domain in time series can be transferred to the target domain (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Novelty concerns and alteration of backbone:** The concept of reprogramming LLMs for time series is not entirely new; prior work (e.g., GPT4TS) explored similar ideas (Reviewer 1). Additionally, the method fine-tunes input and output layers, which constitutes some alteration of the pre-trained backbone, contrary to the claim of keeping it intact (Reviewer 1). While other reviewers considered the approach novel, this criticism should be addressed.
- **Technical clarity and presentation issues:** Several figures and descriptions need improvement. Figure 3 has ambiguous labeling (variables like \(E, Z\) not clearly linked) and the transformation process is hard to follow (Reviewer 3). Figure 5 lacks clarity and would benefit from being displayed at a larger scale (Reviewer 5). In patch reprogramming (Sec. 3.1), the linear projection aligning hidden dimensions is not clearly depicted; the transformation from patch embedding \(Z^{(i)}\) to output embedding \(O^{(i)}\) is unclear (Reviewer 5). The Prompt-as-Prefix section references statistics (trend, lag) without specifying how these are calculated; it would help to elaborate on the specific content and calculation methods (e.g., how trend and lag are determined and what statistical measures are included beyond basic descriptive statistics) (Reviewer 5). The paper omits references to several pivotal reprogramming studies from recent years, which should be integrated in the introduction or related work (Reviewer 5). Reference formatting should be uniform, with a preference for citing formally published works from conferences or journals over preprints (Reviewer 5).
