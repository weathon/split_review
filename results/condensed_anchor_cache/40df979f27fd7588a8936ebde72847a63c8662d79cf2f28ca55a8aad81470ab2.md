## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes EXCOST, a semi-supervised learning algorithm that combines exemplar-contrastive self-training with a Category-Invariant Loss (CIL). Pseudo-labels are assigned to unlabeled samples with high confidence and high exemplar similarity; CIL enforces consistent class probabilities under perturbations. The method avoids weak/strong augmentation and EMA. Evaluation on MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 shows state-of-the-art results, but performance on CIFAR-100 is notably worse. Reviewers are split: two (scores 5) find the approach effective and components novel, while two (scores 3) cite writing flaws, unclear motivation, limited novelty, missing results, and concerns about generalization and reproducibility.

### Strengths

- The idea of combining confidence and exemplar similarity to generate more reliable pseudo-labels is interesting and contributes to trustworthy pseudo-labeling (R2, R3).
- The Category-Invariant Loss promotes consistent class probabilities across perturbations and is independent of weak/strong data augmentation and exponential moving averages (R1, R3, R4).
- Experiments are thorough and demonstrate effectiveness on MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10, with significant performance improvements over prior methods (R1, R3, R4).
- The proposed components (exemplar-contrastive selection and CIL) are considered novel to some extent (R1). One reviewer found the paper easy to follow and well-structured (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and organization:** The motivation is unclear; the connection between prototype/exemplar theory and the method is not convincingly established. The narrative lacks clarity, with inconsistent expressions (e.g., Section 1 says exemplar theory emphasizes uniqueness, Section 3.2 says samples of same class should be similar) and non‑standardized nomenclature (e.g., Φ_c vs φ_c) (R2, R3, R4). The current structure (e.g., ablation study included in the main paper) makes it difficult to quickly grasp core contributions (R2). Table 1 mixes reporting styles (median error of last 20 epochs vs minimum error across all epochs), hindering direct comparison (R2). Two reviewers explicitly found the paper difficult to follow (R2, R3).
- **Novelty and related work:** The integration of contrastive learning into SSL is not new; prior works [1–4] should be analyzed and experimentally compared. The Related Work section is not comprehensive, lacks summary/analysis, and fails to clearly highlight the paper’s contributions (R3, R4). One reviewer considered the components novel (R1), but others strongly question novelty.
- **Performance and generalization:** The method underperforms on CIFAR-100, showing a significant gap compared to other techniques, raising concerns about generalization. No analysis is provided for why EXCOST struggles on this dataset (R2). Many baseline results are missing from Table 1, especially on MNIST (R3, R4). The paper lacks any discussion or breakdown of computational cost/time complexity for the algorithm (R1, R2).
- **Hyperparameters and experimental details:** Missing experimental details, e.g., the threshold in Eq. (1) is not specified. No methodology is given for selecting the CIL threshold, and the sensitivity of hyperparameters (confidence threshold, exemplar similarity threshold, margin, labeling rates) and their interaction are not analyzed (R1, R3). The ablation study is confusing and incomplete; many results remain blank (R3).
- **Missing evidence:** The claim that more pseudo‑labeled samples are incorporated as training progresses is not supported by any plot or table (R3). How prototype theory, exemplar theory, and the typicality gradient are reflected in the method remains unclear (R4).
- **Evaluation inconsistency:** Table 1 reports results with two different evaluation protocols (median error of last 20 epochs vs minimum error across epochs) for different baselines, making fair comparison unreliable (R2).
