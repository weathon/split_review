## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PolyPythias, extending the Pythia model suite with 45 additional training runs (9 seeds × 5 sizes, 14M–410M) and releasing ~7k checkpoints. The authors analyze training stability and dynamics: downstream performance consistency, representational stability via probing and MDL, and training phases via HMM. They find mostly stable behavior except for two outlier runs at 410M, and identify inter-seed agreement peaks during early learning. Reviewers praised the data release, thorough experiments, and well-written presentation. Criticisms center on limited model scale (max 410M, no scaling extrapolation), lack of hyperparameter variation, unclear interpretability of some analyses, missing experiments (e.g., checkpoint frequency, gender bias scale), and presentation issues. One reviewer (R2, confidence 3/4) found no significant weaknesses, deferring to others.

### Strengths
- **Data release and impact:** The release of 45 new training runs (9 seeds × 5 sizes) with ~7k checkpoints, including unstable runs with loss spikes, is a significant contribution for studying LM training stability (R1, confidence 2/4; R3, confidence 4/4). The discovery that inter-seed agreement peaks when models begin learning meaningful patterns is particularly interesting (R3). This resource can enable further research on training dynamics and outlier detection (R4, confidence 3/4).
- **Thorough and well-written analysis:** The paper is well-written and supports every claim with extensive experiments (R2; R3; R4). The investigation covers downstream task performance, algebraic representation analysis via probing, and HMM-based transition analysis—all using recent techniques (R1). The analyses provide valuable insights into training consistency and outlier characteristics (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model scale and lack of scaling extrapolation:** The largest model (410M) is far smaller than commonly used LMs (>1B). The paper does not discuss how findings might extrapolate to larger scales, and lacks controlled variation of other hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, optimizer), making it difficult to attribute instabilities solely to random seed (R1; R4). Additionally, there is no clear definition of a 'stable' run, and no analysis of gradient norms or Hessian eigenvalues to support stability claims (R1). One reviewer (R4) noted that more guidance on predicting outliers to stabilize training would be desirable. In contrast, one reviewer (R2) found no significant weaknesses, deferring to the collective opinion.
- **Unconvincing representational stability interpretation (R3, high confidence):** The probing/MDL setup is cast as investigating representation stability, but actually measures stability w.r.t. task-solving ability rather than direct representation similarity. The sparsity-inducing prior (Eq. 1) is problematic: if random vectors can solve tasks perfectly, tasks may be poorly designed. The authors should more clearly explain the MDL and linear probe interpretation, or remove the analysis. R3 suggests that a more natural interpretation would be investigating model parameter change in a task-oriented setup.
- **Gender bias analysis insufficient:** The gender bias evaluation uses a small benchmark; the authors themselves are uncertain about benchmark size effects. R3 recommends using larger gender bias datasets from other tasks (e.g., machine translation) to strengthen conclusions.
- **Lack of motivation for complex HMM methodology (R3):** Section 5 uses feature maps for HMM analysis, but the authors do not justify why simpler, more interpretable metrics are insufficient. Table 3, which lists actual features determining state transitions, is more illuminating and should be presented earlier (e.g., at the start of Section 4). R3 requests citation of prior work if simpler methods were found inadequate.
- **Missing experiments and analyses:**
  - Fewer checkpoints are plotted than captured; all data points should be shown (R1).
  - More frequent checkpoints in the range \(10^2\)–\(10^4\) steps would better capture early dynamics (R1).
  - It is unclear whether the HMM state transitions are generic across unstable runs, which is critical for using them to forecast training problems (R1).
  - No analysis of loss spike recovery speed (fast recovery within <100 steps vs. catastrophic) as observed in large-scale models; the paper only mentions two outlier runs (R1).
  - Inter-seed agreement metric only compares against seed 0; comparing all seed pairs might yield different results (R4).
  - Lack of highlighted summarizations at the end of each section to distill key takeaways (R4).
- **Presentation errors:** Fig. 1 has mislabeled row/column references (“middle and bottom rows” should be “middle and right columns”) and does not plot the data points for the two outlier runs described in the text (R1).
