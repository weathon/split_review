## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Bi-level Fair Pruning (BiFP), a bi-level optimization framework that jointly optimizes pruning mask and weights under fairness constraints. The empirical comparison uses CelebA and LFW datasets with ResNet10 and MobileNetV2 models. Reviewers agree the problem is important and timely, but opinions diverge: one reviewer (score 5) found the method reasonable and well-organized, while two reviewers (score 3) raised significant concerns about novelty, experimental scope, and evaluation rigor. The main points of criticism include insufficient differentiation from prior bi-level pruning, limited datasets/models, missing fairness-aware baselines, and lack of theoretical analysis.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem setting**: Investigating algorithmic bias in pruning is timely and under-explored; most pruning works ignore fairness. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Method is reasonable and sound**: The bi-level formulation is a sensible approach to embed fairness into pruning. (R1, R3)
- **Good presentation**: The paper is well-written, especially the preliminaries, and the writing clearly conveys the proposed approach. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Experimental results show some interesting findings** (R3). One reviewer noted that the results demonstrate better accuracy–fairness tradeoff compared to pruning methods that do not optimize fairness. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scale**: Only small models (ResNet10, MobileNetV2) and small datasets (CelebA, LFW) are used. Missing larger models (e.g., ResNet50, transformer-based) and larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet). This limits generalizability. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Insufficient novelty and differentiation from prior work**: The bi-level optimization structure is very similar to that in [a]. The paper does not clearly articulate what is new beyond adding a fairness metric to an existing bi-level pruning framework. Equation 5 is a straightforward linear combination of losses; the lack of theoretical analysis (convergence guarantees, impact of weighting strategies) makes the contribution seem incremental. The surrogate function in Equation 8 is taken directly from [c]. (R1, R2)
- **Missing fairness-aware pruning baselines**: None of the compared baselines optimize for fairness. Works such as [b] that explicitly address fairness in pruning should be included to demonstrate the benefit of the proposed bi-level approach. (R2)
- **Lack of statistical significance and marginal improvements**: The gains in accuracy and fairness shown in Figures 3 and 4 appear very small; no significance testing is provided, so it is unclear whether improvements are meaningful or due to random variation. (R3)
- **Insufficient details on random mask initialization**: The paper does not specify the distribution (e.g., uniform, normal) or its parameters for the random initial mask. The impact of different initialization scales/variances on final performance and fairness is not explored, raising concerns about robustness. (R3)
- **No convergence guarantees for the alternating optimization scheme**: The bi-level optimization alternates between mask and weight updates. No theoretical analysis is provided to guarantee convergence to a stationary point or to avoid oscillations/divergence. Different random initializations may lead to different convergence behaviors and suboptimal solutions. (R3)
- **Poor justification for convex relaxation**: The paper invokes convex relaxation to make fairness constraints convex and differentiable, but the neural network itself is non-convex (ReLU, maxpooling, etc.). The motivation is not convincing, and alternative approaches are not discussed. (R3)
- **Redundant definitions**: Definition 2 (Compressed Model Performance Degradation) and Definition 3 (Performance Degradation Fairness) are not used in practice; only Definition 1 (fairness performance metric) is employed. The definitions feel disconnected from the experiments. (R2)
- **Missing methodological clarifications (reviewer questions)**:
    - The choice of surrogate function \( u(\cdot) \) in Equation 8 is not specified. (R2)
    - The mask selection procedure is unclear: how is the sparsity level chosen? What if a continuous mask value becomes negative (e.g., -2.5)? Is the mask gradually pruned or fixed from the start? (R2)
    - Equation 5 does not clearly state what variable is minimized in the upper-level optimization. (R2)
- **Notation error**: In Definition 3 and thereafter, \(\mathbb{F(f_c, \mathcal{D}; \mathbb{R})}\) should be \(\mathbb{F(f, f_c, \mathcal{D}; \mathbb{R})}\). (R2)

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: While one reviewer (score 5) found the method reasonable and the paper well-organized, the two other reviewers (score 3) were much more critical, especially regarding novelty, experimental scope, and lack of theoretical grounding. The above weaknesses reflect the majority view, but the positive aspects from the minority reviewer are retained in the strengths.
