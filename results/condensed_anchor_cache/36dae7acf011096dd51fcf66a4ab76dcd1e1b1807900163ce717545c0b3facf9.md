## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Coordinate-Aware Modulation (CAM), a method for neural fields that modulates intermediate MLP features using scale and shift parameters extracted from grid representations. Unlike typical sequential combination (grid features first, then MLP), CAM injects grid information into the MLP’s hidden layers. This approach is motivated by mitigating spectral bias while retaining MLP compactness and adding feature normalization stability. The method is simple, plug-and-play, and evaluated on image representation, novel view synthesis (static and dynamic NeRF), and video compression, achieving competitive or state-of-the-art results with fast training and low parameter counts.

### Strengths

- **Clear motivation and well-structured paper.** The problem of spectral bias in MLPs and the memory cost of grid methods is well articulated. The paper is easy to follow.
- **Simple yet effective approach.** The idea of using grid-derived scale/shift modulations is novel for implicit neural representations and has a plug-and-play nature that may have broad impact.
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation across diverse tasks** (images, static/dynamic NeRF, video compression) with strong results. Achieves state-of-the-art in dynamic NeRF (fewest parameters, fast training) and under 1MB static NeRF; outperforms neural-field video compression baselines by a large margin.
- **Empirical verification of spectral bias mitigation** (Section 4.3), supporting the core claim.
- **Baselines are generally competitive and recent** (noted for video experiments), and the method demonstrates clear improvements over grid-only and MLP-only variants.
- **Feature normalization shown effective in neural fields** when combined with CAM, a result previously unsuccessful.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing discussion of related feature modulation work.** Should cite FiLM (Perez et al., 2017) and follow-up works in generation, denoising, image restoration, and style transfer. (Reviewer 1, score 6)
- **No comparison of inference speed and memory** between regular MLP, grid-based methods, and CAM. (Reviewer 1, score 6)
- **Insufficient explanation and ablation for choice of input coordinates used to extract scale/shift.** For images only pixel coordinates are used; for video the grid also depends on channel. Table 6 ablates only on NeRF tasks and does not examine dependence on direction or time. The reasoning behind task-specific coordinate subsets is unclear. (Reviewers 1 and 4, scores 6 and 8)
- **Image representation comparison uses only FFN (Tancik et al., 2020) as baseline.** Missing more recent grid-based methods such as instant-NGP. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **Baseline numbers for several methods appear to be from the authors’ own implementation,** which weakens the appeal of the comparison. (Reviewer 2, score 6)
- **Figures 1 and 2 do not convey the mechanism clearly.** A more detailed diagram (e.g., example inputs and values for Γ) and a brief description of the functional form of Γ and B would improve readability. (Reviewers 3 and 4, scores 8 and 8)
- **Notation in Equation 1 suggests that γₙ and βₙ depend on the full batch X,** whereas they should be per-sample (as in Equation 4). Clarify notation (e.g., γ(Xₙ; Γ)). (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **Variance shown in Figure 8 is not explained.** What is being measured (e.g., variance across elements of the last MLP layer input)? (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **The claim that HM (H.265) decodes at 10fps using a GPU is surprising and likely inaccurate** (HM is a CPU-only codec); the paper should clarify the hardware used (GPU vs CPU). (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **Missing experiments that would demonstrate generality and stability.** Reviewers suggest testing CAM in additional architectures (e.g., triplane-based decoders) and in text-to-3D pipelines (DreamFusion) to showcase training stability benefits. (Reviewer 1, score 6)
