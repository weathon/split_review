## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a novel task, Referring Medical Image Sequence Segmentation, which aims to segment anatomical structures or lesions in sequential medical images (2D video frames or 3D slices) guided by text prompts. The authors propose a baseline model, Text-Promptable Propagation (TPP), that integrates cross-modal prompt fusion and a Transformer-based triple-propagation strategy to exploit temporal and cross-modal relationships. A benchmark dataset (Ref-MISS) was curated, covering 4 modalities (MRI, CT, ultrasound, endoscopy) and 20 organs/lesions across 18 datasets. Experiments comparing against several referring video object segmentation algorithms show the proposed method achieves better performance. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear illustrations.

### Strengths
1. **Novel task and approach**: The paper proposes a new task (Referring Medical Image Sequence Segmentation) that combines medical imaging sequences with text prompts, and a model (TPP) that leverages cross-modal prompt fusion and triple-propagation to propagate segmentation across slices/frames. This is considered innovative and original (Reviewers 2,3,4).
2. **Comprehensive dataset**: The curated benchmark spans 18 datasets, 4 imaging modalities, and 20 different organs/lesions, providing a large and diverse testbed that strengthens reliability and generalizability (Reviewers 1,3,4).
3. **Well-written and clear**: The paper is logically structured, easy to follow, and includes helpful illustrations of the model architecture and results (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewer 3 also notes it is well-structured and clear.
4. **Thorough experiments on referring video segmentation baselines**: The method is compared against several referring video object segmentation algorithms, and the proposed TPP demonstrates clear advantages (Reviewer 2). Comparisons with SAM-2 are performed (Reviewer 1).
5. **Triple propagation novelty**: The design enforcing temporal consistency across consecutive slices is a notable contribution (Reviewer 2).
6. **Originality and significance**: The work addresses an important need for context-aware segmentation in clinical settings, and the dataset provides a valuable resource for future research (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear clinical motivation and practical applicability**: Multiple reviewers question the clinical scenarios the task targets. Radiologists typically already have preliminary information about lesions/diseases, and the need for text-prompted segmentation in real workflows is not convincingly justified (Reviewers 1,2,3). The assumption that 3D slices (evenly spaced) and video frames (irregular intervals) can be processed uniformly is questionable without justification or analysis of impact on temporal consistency (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (Reviewer 3, rating 3) is particularly skeptical about the clinical relevance, stating it seems inconsistent with radiologist workflows.
2. **Missing comparisons with strong medical segmentation SOTA baselines**: The paper compares only against referring video object segmentation algorithms, not against established medical image segmentation methods such as nnU-Net (Isensee et al. 2021), Swin-UNet (Cao et al. 2022), UNeXt, 3D UX-Net, UNetR, CLIP-Driven Universal Model (Liu et al. 2023), BiomedParse (Zhao et al. 2024), or SAM-based medical models (Ma et al. 2024). This limits fairness and does not substantiate the claimed advantage (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). For instance, on the BTCV dataset, SOTA methods (e.g., nnU-Net variants) report Dice ~0.8, while the proposed method significantly underperforms (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 requests at least a comparison on abdomen data with CLIP-Driven Universal Model and nnU-Net.
3. **Insufficient ablation and analysis of design choices**:
   - No ablation on the cross-modal prompt fusion strategy versus simpler alternatives (e.g., averaging or concatenating image and text features) (Reviewer 2).
   - The model uses N_q repeated prompts per image, multiplying computation cost; no analysis of FLOPs or effect of N_q is provided (Reviewer 2).
   - The inclusion of a box head alongside the mask head is questioned as redundant; its specific role (e.g., providing coarse localization or aiding training) is not explained (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 notes that the model appears to be a composition of existing design blocks without clear innovation justification.
4. **Unclear added value of text prompts**: The text prompts used are pseudo-text (generic organ/lesion descriptions) rather than personalized clinical reports. The added value over fully supervised segmentation methods is not demonstrated. Experiments evaluating different prompt types, specificity levels, or personalized reports are missing (Reviewers 1,3,4). One reviewer (Reviewer 4) asks why a general description of an organ would provide significant information without seeing the actual radiological report.
5. **No evaluation on 2D datasets and lack of justification for sequential processing**: Despite claiming a unified framework for 2D and 3D, no experiments are conducted on 2D video datasets (Reviewer 2). The paper does not explain why treating 3D volumes as sequences is preferable to direct 3D models that naturally capture spatial coherence; no theoretical or empirical comparison is provided (Reviewer 4).
6. **Minor clarity issues and missing implementation details**: Terminology such as "the referred object" could be clarified (Reviewer 3). It is unclear whether separate models are trained per dataset or a single model is used for all 18 datasets (Reviewer 4). Reviewers 1 and 3 also request ablation on the impact of different text description versions (e.g., varied wording), noting that the prompt appendix provides only one version.
7. **Method complexity**: The design (three prediction heads, cross-modal fusion, triple propagation) may be overly complex for the task, potentially not outperforming simpler fully- or semi-supervised architectures (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 notes insufficient clarity on what is novel versus a combination of existing blocks.
8. **Missing citation/comparison with related work**: The paper does not cite or compare with BiomedParse (Zhao et al. 2024), which also uses text prompts across multiple modalities (Reviewer 1).
