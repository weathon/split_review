## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies that in visual RL, high neuron dormancy (inactive units) correlates with poor exploration early in training. It proposes DrM (Dormant ratio Minimization), which uses the dormant ratio to adaptively schedule three mechanisms: weight perturbation (shrink-and-perturb), exploration noise, and a blended exploitation/exploration value update. Experiments on DeepMind Control Suite, MetaWorld, and Adroit (pixel-based continuous control) show DrM significantly outperforms DrQ-v2, A-LIX, and TACO, achieving the first model-free results on Dog, Manipulator, and demonstration-free dexterous manipulation tasks. Scores: 6, 8, 6, 6. Reviewer 2 is more positive; others are positive but raise concerns about novelty, design justification, and empirical rigor.

### Strengths
- The central insight—that the dormant ratio can serve as an internal, reward-independent indicator of exploration quality—is novel and interesting. The paper verifies this correlation by examining dormant ratio and agent behavior for some seeds.
- Using the dormant ratio to control the exploration-exploitation trade-off is a promising direction that does not depend on external signals like reward.
- The results are strong: DrM is the clear winner on all reported tasks across three domains (DeepMind Control Suite, MetaWorld, Adroit), with step-change improvements, especially in Adroit.
- The paper provides a nice contrast with other adaptive exploration methods (e.g., bandit-based epsilon-greedy) and shows that the method depends on a single easy-to-measure network property.
- The empirical analysis is generally good; improvements appear statistically significant despite low sample size, and the paper includes an ablation study (Figure 10) on Adroit.
- Pseudocode is provided for each component; the paper is overall well written and easy to read. The coverage of related work is thorough.
- The idea of strategically exploring based on representation properties is exciting and could be applied more broadly in RL.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of mechanisms**: Each of the three components (shrink-and-perturb, expectile-based value updates, exploration scheduling) is individually known from prior work (D'Oro et al., Ash & Adams; Ji et al.; Agent57). The novelty is limited to using the dormant ratio to schedule these hyperparameters.
- **Weak empirical evaluation**: All experiments use only 4 random seeds, raising concerns about statistical significance. The paper would benefit from at least 10 runs on selected environments (e.g., the four dense MetaWorld tasks from Figure 7) following suggestions like Patterson et al. (2023). Without this, it is hard to judge if performance gains are significant when curves are close.
- **Insufficient motivation for design choices**:
  - Why use shrink-and-perturb rather than directly resetting inactive units? Could S&P introduce extra exploration like noisy nets (Fortunato et al.)?
  - The exploration strategy uses a max operator that can *increase* noise after the network is "activated," contradicting the motivation of more noise early and less later. This needs clarification.
  - What motivates the assumption that the network becomes "activated" only once? Why wouldn't the dormant ratio drop multiple times?
  - The "exploitation" term is misleading: the expectile-based update biases Q-targets optimistically, which is more like risk-seeking than pure exploitation. It could cause overestimation in noisy environments and attract the agent to high-variance, low-expected-return states.
- **Hyperparameter concerns**:
  - The method introduces 4–5 new hyperparameters (perturbation scale, expectile, exploration schedule parameters, etc.) with different optimal values across environments (e.g., max perturbation rate and expectile are tuned per domain, as shown in Appendix Table 1). This undermines the claim that "all hyperparameters are robust to tasks and domains" (Section 3.1) – a claim that is never substantiated.
  - No hyperparameter sensitivity study is conducted, even on a simple environment. This is not good empirical science; the performance gain could partly come from extra tuning.
  - How much worse would performance be if hyperparameters from one domain were used in another?
- **Insufficient and coarse ablations**:
  - Figure 10 ablates each entire mechanism, but the novel part is the *adaptive scheduling*. A proper ablation would compare DrM against DrQ-v2 + perturbation resets with fixed α + blended operator with fixed λ (i.e., removing only the dormant-ratio scheduling).
  - Ablations consider only one domain (Adroit) and are aggregated; more granular results are needed. Without proper ablations, it is unclear whether lowering the dormant ratio is cause or effect (chicken-egg). Only the perturbation mechanism directly targets the dormant ratio.
- **Overclaims and clarity issues**:
  - Section 3.1 claims "dormant ratio acts as an intrinsic metric" based on a single example; the statement that agents learn relevant behaviors before returns improve is dubious.
  - Abstract states "no broken seeds (76 seeds in total)" – this is vague and not revisited in the main text.
  - Algorithm 2 is unclear: it says "create a deep copy of the network" then immediately "randomly initialize weights" – what did the copy accomplish? Algorithm 1 lines 6–7 need explicit equations.
  - The linear schedule σ_linear (exploration) should be defined in the appendix for self-containedness.
  - What value of β (expectile) is used in Figures 3 and 4? This should be stated in the caption.
- **Fairness of comparisons**: Baselines (DrQ-v2, A-LIX, TACO) do not incorporate sophisticated exploration strategies; DrM adds multiple exploration-enhancing mechanisms, making it hard to attribute gains to the dormant-ratio insight vs. the extra knobs.
- **Missing experiments**:
  - Show whether other baselines (A-LIX, TACO) also fail to minimize the dormant ratio (like DrQ-v2) on at least some tasks.
  - Investigate the periodicity in dormant ratio observed in Humanoid Run and Manipulator tasks.
  - Test each component’s fidelity to its motivation: e.g., does the exploration mechanism have larger impact on tasks that require more exploration?
  - Consider whether the method would be effective with different architectures or in discrete-action domains like ProcGen or Atari.
- **Causality unclear**: Is the claim that interesting behavior leads to more diverse data and thus fewer inactive units, or that low capacity limits the range of behaviors? The paper does not disentangle cause and effect.
