## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces VL-ICL Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multimodal in-context learning (ICL) in vision large language models (VLLMs). It covers 10 tasks spanning both image-to-text and text-to-image modalities, including perception, reasoning, rule-induction, image interleaving, fast concept binding, long context, and shot scaling. The authors evaluate over 20 VLLMs, revealing diverse strengths and weaknesses, and highlight that even GPT-4 finds tasks challenging. The paper is well-motivated and provides extensive analysis, though concerns remain about dataset construction details, evaluation scope, and the practical utility of the benchmark.

### Strengths
1. **Good motivation**: The paper clearly identifies inadequacies of evaluating multimodal ICL via standard VQA and captioning benchmarks, and quantifies why they are insufficient.
2. **Comprehensive benchmark**: VL-ICL covers a wide range of ICL tasks (10 tasks) that test perception, reasoning, rule induction, long-context handling, and both I2T and T2I generation. This provides a more complete picture than prior datasets.
3. **Extensive experimentation**: The authors test a large number of VLLMs (open-source and proprietary, with varying architectures), and provide thorough analysis including shot-scaling trends, model comparisons, and ablation studies. The evaluation is rigorous and yields useful empirical insights.
4. **Documentation of phenomena**: The paper records and explains several observed ICL phenomena (e.g., learning to be concise in captions, format learning in VQA), offering guidance for future research.
5. **Inclusion of text-to-image ICL**: The benchmark includes few-shot T2I generation tasks, which are often overlooked in prior ICL benchmarks.

### Weaknesses
1. **Dataset construction details needed**: The paper does not clarify whether all samples from original sources (Table 1) are used or if filtering is applied. No dataset statistics (e.g., sample distribution, class balance) are provided, making it hard to assess representativeness and diversity of the benchmark (R1, R2, R4).
   - *R4 notes the total sample count appears small and questions whether class imbalance is a problem.*
2. **Metric explanation incomplete**: The “ICL efficiency” metric in Table 2 includes negative values, but the paper does not explain why or how to interpret them (R1).
3. **Saturation of ICL improvements**: Across several figures (e.g., Figure 5, 6, and 3a), models often saturate at 1-shot and show little gain from more examples. The paper does not adequately explain why this occurs or how VL-ICL offers a better ICL evaluation than standard VQA when both show similar saturation trends (R1, R2). Additionally, R2 questions why peak accuracy is used instead of average accuracy over all shots.
4. **Text-figure inconsistency**: The claim (Lines 159–160, 224–225) that ICL improvement is “more significant” on text-to-text (Figure 4) than image-to-text (Figure 3a) is not visually supported—the trends appear comparable, and the magnitude of improvement is marginal in both (R2).
5. **Unclear model selection rationale**: Figure 3 and Figure 4 use different models, and the paper does not explain why specific models were chosen for each figure (R2). This undermines interpretability of cross-figure comparisons.
6. **Weak data contribution**: All data and annotations are collected from existing datasets; no novel annotations or task creation is performed (R2, R3). The benchmark’s contribution is primarily taxonomic rather than data-centric.
7. **Missing analysis of in-context example (ICD) selection**: The paper does not explore how different ICD choices affect ICL performance, despite prior work (e.g., “What Makes Good Examples for Visual In-Context Learning?”, “How to Configure Good In-Context Sequence for VQA”, “Understanding and Improving ICL on Vision-language Models”, “Exploring Diverse In-Context Configurations for Image Captioning”). Testing ICD selection within VL-ICL would strengthen the benchmark (R4).
8. **Concern about textual substitution for images**: The experiment (Section 4.3) that replaces images with textual descriptions (e.g., generated captions) raises concerns about information loss, bias, and hallucination risks if using BLIP or GPT-4V. The paper does not investigate removing images entirely (keeping only query-answer pairs) to understand models’ reliance on visual input (R4).
   - *R4 asks for implementation details: were images simply removed, or were captions used? This remains unclear.*
9. **Insufficient evaluation of T2I tasks**: For image generation ICL, the community often focuses on complex instruction following; the benchmark’s evaluation is too narrow and does not reflect current research priorities (R3, citing an external paper).
10. **Unclear usage scenarios**: The benchmark may not be suitable for either (a) separately evaluating generation vs. understanding, where most recent models excel in only one modality, or (b) evaluating unified models in truly complex ICL settings where both input and output contain images and text. The paper does not address these mismatches (R3).
11. **Detailed ICL phenomena have been previously reported**: Several observations (e.g., answer-format learning, verbosity adjustment) have been discussed in prior work; the paper should cite those studies (R4).
12. **Minor formatting issues**: Some tables (e.g., Table 5) do not bold the best performance numbers (R1).
