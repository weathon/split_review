## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces OracleMamba, the first Mamba-based stock prediction framework, integrating a dynamic market-guided module for short-term forecasting (fusing market indices and GPT-O1-extracted sentiment from analyst reports) and a SelectiveMamba module with a 3D scan mechanism for long-term forecasting (capturing spectral and temporal features). Tested on CSI300 and CSI800 datasets, it claims to reduce noise and outperform baselines. However, the reviewers raise major concerns about data clarity, baseline fairness, novelty, and reproducibility. One reviewer (score 1) finds the contributions unoriginal, the literature review severely lacking, and the experiment non-reproducible due to unspecified data sources. The other three (score 5) acknowledge the hybrid approach but point to missing details, weak comparisons, and insufficient justification.

### Strengths
- First work to apply Mamba (SSM) to stock price forecasting; the approach is promising for the subdomain (R1, R3, R4 – though R4 questions this due to poor literature review).
- Novel hybrid architecture combining a dynamic market-guided module for short-term prediction and a SelectiveMamba module for long-term prediction (R1).
- Novel 3D scanning mechanism to capture interactions across time, stock, and market state dimensions (R2, R3).
- The problem is well-formulated and the paper is clearly written and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- Experimental results show performance gains over the included baselines (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Data and experiment details missing or vague:**
  - The exact data point length (daily vs. hourly) and sequence length for CSI300/CSI800 are unspecified; longer sequences might favor Mamba but this is not controlled (R1).
  - The market context (analyst reports, financial documents) is only vaguely described: sources, market coverage, frequency, volume, and noise levels are absent (R2, R4). Credibility and reproducibility are in question (R4).
  - The use of GPT-O1 for sentiment extraction is not specified: no prompts, no description of sentiment output (scores, labels), and the role of “experts” is unclear (R2).
  - Sectors and regions are mentioned but never integrated into the model or analysis (R2).
  - Only the Chinese stock market is tested, limiting generalizability (R4).
- **Baseline and comparison issues:**
  - It is unclear whether baseline models use the same additional features (market indices, sentiment) as OracleMamba; if not, the comparison is potentially unfair (R1).
  - Important baselines are missing: Mamba itself or other Mamba-based models (R2); state-of-the-art time-series forecasting models (DLinear, NLinear, Autoformer, Fedformer, PatchTST) (R2); only two stock-specific baselines and a vanilla Transformer are included (R3, R4). Two of the three baselines are at least three years old (R4).
  - No computational cost (memory, runtime) evaluation is provided despite using a Mamba-based method (R1).
- **Novelty and related work insufficient:**
  - Using text data (including language models) for market sentiment in stock prediction is already well-established (e.g., NumHTML [1]) (R3, R4). The paper does not cite or compare with these works.
  - Combing short- and long-term contexts in stock forecasting has been widely explored in prior ML/AI conferences (R4). The claimed novelty is thus not new.
  - The Time-Spectral (frequency) method is a generic time-series technique (e.g., FOCAL [2]) with no specific adaptation or theoretical justification for stock data (R3).
  - Inter-stock correlations have been modeled before (e.g., via graph attention, [3]) (R3).
- **Mamba/SSM design and explanation lacking:**
  - The motivation for the SSM design and its advantage for stock prediction is not well explained (R2, R3). Readers unfamiliar with Mamba cannot see where it is applied in the method (R3).
  - In the TSSS structure, B and C are input-independent, which is a time-invariant SSM rather than the improved Mamba design (R2).
  - The variable \( s \) in the DSE calculation is undefined (R2).
  - The design of DTE and DSE lacks explanation; it is unclear how they provide the claimed benefits (R2).
- **Noise handling claim unsubstantiated:**
  - The paper claims the model handles noisy data but provides no theoretical or empirical analysis of how it suppresses noise compared to prior deep learning methods (R3). Stock noise often derives from Brownian motion, which is not addressed (R3).
- **Reproducibility:**
  - Code is not provided (R3).
  - Expert role, document sources, and sentiment extraction details are absent, making the experiment unreproducible (R2, R4).

*Note: One reviewer (score 1) was significantly more negative, emphasizing severe lack of literature review, unoriginality of both claimed contributions, and concerns about data credibility and reproducibility. The other three (score 5) were more positive but still identified major weaknesses.*
