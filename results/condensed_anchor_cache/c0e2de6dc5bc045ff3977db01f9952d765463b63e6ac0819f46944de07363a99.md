## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses action smoothness in deep RL, a problem important for real-world deployment (e.g., robotics). The proposed method, PASRL, extends TD7 by augmenting the state with representations of past actions (via an RNN) and uses pink noise instead of white noise. The method is evaluated on the MuJoCo benchmark (Ant and Hopper environments). The approach aims to produce smoother action sequences without handcrafted heuristics. The idea is easy to follow and implement. However, all three reviewers identify significant weaknesses, particularly in the trade-off between smoothness and reward performance, insufficient evaluation, and missing comparisons with existing smoothness-oriented methods.

### Strengths
- The problem of action smoothness is important for robotic systems that cannot instantly change motor torques and for other continuous control tasks (e.g., autonomous driving).  
- The method uses an RNN to incorporate past state-action representations, and applies pink noise instead of standard white noise for exploration.  
- The core idea of augmenting the state with prior actions is straightforward and easy to implement.

### Weaknesses
- **Performance degradation / over‑regularization:**  
  The method yields smoother actions but at the cost of lower rewards (e.g., in Ant, the reward gap compared to TD7 is unacceptably large). This is seen as over‑regularization; smoothing biases the policy toward a limited set of actions, which hurts exploration and can prevent discovery of optimal or near‑optimal solutions, especially in high‑dimensional environments like Humanoid.  
- **Smoothness is not universally desirable:**  
  The paper treats smoothness as a universal objective, but real‑world problems (e.g., racing cars) require sharp, discontinuous (bang‑bang) control for optimal performance. The work does not address scenarios where smoothness is not the goal.  
- **Constrained RL formulation is missing:**  
  Real systems have specific reactivity constraints, not a need for “smoothest” actions. The paper should be extended to a constrained RL setting where a smoothness parameter is part of the optimization, allowing fair comparison with prior methods (e.g., reward shaping, low‑pass filters). This would demonstrate easier tuning and better performance given a constraint.  
- **Insufficient evaluation environments:**  
  Reviewer 2 notes the paper only evaluates on Ant and Hopper; Reviewer 3 states the experiments are conducted only on Ant. Both agree that evaluation in more complex environments (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Humanoid) is necessary to assess generalizability.  
- **Missing baseline comparisons:**  
  Several recently proposed methods for action smoothness are not included: LipsNet (Lipschitz‑constrained policies), TAAC (switch policy for action repetition), PIC (action inertia), SR²L, L2C2, and other regularization‑based approaches. Without comparisons to these, the contribution is not well‑demonstrated.  
- **Unclear framing of success/failure:**  
  It is not clear what the authors consider a success: the method improves smoothness but degrades performance. The paper does not adequately address this trade‑off.  
- **Experimental methodology concerns:**  
  - Figure 5: It is unclear whether the smoothness results are from training (with exploration noise) or evaluation (without noise); this significantly affects interpretation.  
  - Figure 7: The shaded area for a single seed line raises questions about seed fixing (environment seed, torch/numpy seed).  
  - The paper does not explain why better action smoothness consistently hurts reward (e.g., red line in Figure 5 has lowest reward but best smoothness).  
- **Missing literature review:**  
  The related work section is incomplete; key works on action smoothness in DRL (LipsNet, TAAC, PIC, SR²L, L2C2) should be discussed.  
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - Missing punctuation (commas) in several sentences, causing ambiguity.  
  - Undefined notation: e.g., \(nh_t\) in Section 4.2.  
  - Acronyms introduced without full names: LPF, ERE.  
  - Latex typo: “representational drift” (line 105).  
- **Question about off‑policy replay and temporal information:**  
  The paper claims “reliance on only the current time step causes instability,” but off‑policy methods like TD3/TD7 already use past transitions from a replay buffer, which contain temporal information. The authors should clarify this argument.
