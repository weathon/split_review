## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how symmetry equivariance (p4m group on staggered Arakawa C-grids) and physical conservation laws (mass, energy, enstrophy) can be jointly enforced as hard constraints in neural PDE surrogates via novel input/output layers. Experiments on shallow water equations and decaying incompressible turbulence show that both constraints improve autoregressive rollout accuracy, with symmetries being more effective than physical constraints and the combination yielding the best performance, including improved generalization to out-of-distribution initial conditions and longer horizons.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of constrained layers for staggered grids**: The work introduces input and output layers that impose symmetry and conservation constraints on the Arakawa C-grid, which is the dominant discretization in computational fluid dynamics. This is considered novel by one reviewer (score 6, confidence 5) and appreciated as a practical departure from regular grids in neural PDE surrogates, while two other reviewers (both score 3) question algorithmic novelty beyond the layers and a fair comparison, given prior work on symmetry-enforced neural PDEs (Wang et al., 2020; Helwig et al., 2023; Smets et al., 2023; Huang & Greenberg, 2023; Ruhe et al., 2024) and physics constraints via loss terms (Read et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2020; Stachenfeld et al., 2021; Sorourifar et al., 2023) or hard reparameterization (Mohan et al., 2020; Beucler et al., 2021; Chalapathi et al., 2024; Cranmer et al., 2020; Greydanus et al., 2019).
- **Clear experimental findings**: The conclusion that symmetry constraints are more effective than physical constraints and that optimal performance requires both is clearly supported by the experiments. The ablation study across autoregressive steps, accuracy metrics (e.g., decorrelation time, energy spectrum), and network sizes is appreciated, and the out-of-distribution generalization test (Figure 4) is highlighted as evidence for the importance of inductive biases.
- **Non‑trivial tasks on challenging PDEs**: The two test cases (shallow water equations with closed boundaries and decaying incompressible turbulence) are recognized as challenging and practically relevant. State-of-the-art prediction accuracy is reported.
- **Overall clarity and motivation**: One reviewer (score 6) praises the written quality, clear objective, and exposition. The central idea of building exact constraints into the architecture is well-motivated.

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading scope and messaging** (two reviewers, scores 3): The title claims “PDE integration”, which is inaccurate — the work learns neural surrogates that approximate the integration operator, but does not perform numerical integration as in neural ODEs. The title, abstract, and introduction over-sell the breadth; the study is limited to two examples and a single architecture. The reviewer with score 6 does not raise this issue.
- **Insufficient explanation of the method** (reviewer 2, score 3; also implied by reviewer 1’s questions): The novel input/output layers for constraints are not clearly described in the main text or Figure 1. The appendix formulas (Appendix C) do not convey the core idea of how symmetries and conservation laws are exactly enforced. A better explanation with additional figures is needed. Reviewer 1 asks how physical constraints are applied in the output layer and how mass conservation relates to the surface elevation (Eq. 73).
- **Weak baselines** (reviewer 2, score 3; echoed by reviewer 3, score 6): The method is only compared to a “vanilla” U‑Net, which is not optimal for autoregressive solution operators. Missing comparisons with (a) symmetry-enforcing methods like _Incorporating Symmetry into Deep Dynamics Models_ (Wang et al., 2021) or data augmentation like Lie point symmetry (Brandstetter et al., 2022); (b) physics-constrained surrogates like PINO or FNO with physics losses; (c) stabilization techniques such as noise injection (Stachenfeld et al., 2021), pushforward (Brandstetter et al., 2022), DySLIM (Schiff et al., 2024), and PDE‑Refiner (Lippe et al., 2023). Reviewer 3 states this lack is the main concern for rigor.
- **Limited symmetry scope** (reviewer 3, score 6; also reviewer 2, questions): The work restricts to the discrete p4m group (translations, reflections, 90° rotations). Many PDEs possess continuous Lie point symmetries (scaling, arbitrary rotations) as in Brandstetter et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2021), which are not addressed. Reviewer 2 asks about SO(3)‑equivariance on manifolds and applicability to more PDEs (e.g., non‑hyperbolic). The scope is thus narrow.
- **Missing direct conservation visualizations** (reviewer 1, score 3): Figures 3 and 4 show energy, not the directly conserved quantity (mass). The mass conservation is central to the claim; the authors should show mass evolution explicitly. Also, the generalization capability on SWE may be explained by the linearity of the equation in surface elevation, and on INS the explanation is unclear. A definition or reference for “wavenumber” (used but not in Eq. 9) is needed.
- **Missing experimental details** (reviewer 1, score 3): The hardware and simulation time for generating reference solutions and training data, as well as GPU‑hours for training, are not provided.
- **Theoretical depth** (reviewer 1, score 3): A theoretical analysis linking the conserved quantities to the prediction loss (e.g., loss bounds or convergence rates) is absent.
- **Presentation issues** (reviewers 1, 2, 3): Figures 2, 3, 4 are overloaded and hard to parse (reviewer 2). Section 4.2 is titled “Decaying turbulence” but turbulence is not mentioned in that section — the connection is unclear (reviewer 2). Typos: line 68 “to solutions at time via supervised learning” (missing \(t\)), line 256 “Iinitial” → “Initial”, line 347 “perdition” → “prediction” (reviewer 1; also noted by reviewer 3). References on lines 93, 105, 115 are not in parentheses (missing \citep command) (reviewer 3). Tables 1 and 2 use \(m\) for both reflection in p4m and mass, causing confusion (reviewer 3).
- **Questions that overlap with criticism or missing ablations** (converted to weaknesses):
    - Reviewer 1: In Eq. 73, mass is enforced by removing a global mean rather than through an FVM‑like flux‑based update. Was the latter tried? Wouldn’t the FVM approach better exemplify the staggered grid?
    - Reviewer 2: How are physical constraints exactly enforced in the output layer? Why test only two PDEs? What about non‑hyperbolic PDEs or PDEs on manifolds with SO(3)‑equivariance?
    - Reviewer 3: How does this work tie into classical equivariant moving frame theory for symmetry‑preserving schemes (Rebelo & Valiquette, 2011)?
- **Minor disagreement on overall assessment**: One reviewer (score 6, confidence 5) finds the paper well-executed and novel, recommending marginal acceptance, while the other two (score 3, confidence 4) consider the work incomplete due to weak baselines, limited scope, and insufficient explanation, suggesting major revision or rejection.
