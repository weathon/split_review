## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an unsupervised few-shot learning (U-FSL) method, BECLR, that extends the memory queue to dynamically updated memory clusters (DyCE) to improve positive sampling in contrastive learning, and introduces OpTA, an iterative optimal-transport distribution alignment strategy to address sample bias (distribution shift between the unlabeled query set and the labeled support set) at inference. BECLR is an end-to-end framework that also expands single-image instance discrimination. The method achieves strong empirical results on several benchmarks.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses several issues within a single framework, benefiting from contrastive pre-training, distribution shift handling, and improvements to the memory queue concept.
- The research is robust: DyCE and OpTA are methodically developed and effectiveness is demonstrated through comparisons with established methods like SwaV, SimSiam, and NNCLR.
- The paper is detailed, relatively easy to follow, and well motivated, substantiated by several experiments and ablations.
- The results are promising and impressive, and the method is well grounded.
- The approach shows originality by addressing recognized limitations in U-FSL, merging concepts innovatively, and introducing fresh modules (DyCE and OpTA).
- The paper holds substantial significance for advancing U-FSL and self-supervised learning, especially in few-shot scenarios, and offers a direction for future research.
- *Reviewer 1 (score 8) is notably more positive overall; the other reviewers (scores 6) share the strengths above but raise substantial concerns.*

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons:** SAMPTransfer (Shirekar et al., 2023) is reported only on the miniImageNet→CDFSL task (Table 3) and is missing from other experiments. In Table 1, comparison methods are missing for ResNet-50 and Wide ResNet backbones; Table 3 also omits several methods. The subset of methods across experiments appears arbitrary. Prior FSL works related to distribution shift (sample bias) are not discussed.
- **Resemblance to PsCo:** The overarching idea and structure of BECLR bear a striking resemblance to PsCo, particularly as seen in Figure 2. The primary differences appear to be the concatenation of different views from PsCo and addition of a dynamic clustered memory. The authors should provide a comprehensive comparison with PsCo in the introduction and related work, highlighting unique aspects. Also needed: comparison with PsCo in terms of model size and inference time.
- **Notation and algorithmic clarity:** The notation in the algorithmic section needs elucidation; each symbol should be clearly defined to improve readability.
- **Missing ablations:** No test evaluating performance without merging ‘X’ (i.e., keeping PsCo’s view structure) and no results when PsCo is merged and when masking is applied. The evaluation should consider components in separation—is OpTA the sole source of improvement, or would adding a different baseline method to the framework yield similar gains? Baseline methods such as linear evaluation principles for FSL should be compared when OpTA is replaced.
- **Parameter updates during testing:** Unclear whether model parameters are updated during the testing phase, and if so, how many updates occur. This affects understanding of adaptability and robustness.
- **Pretraining data:** When pretraining with miniImageNet, which specific datasets were used? Was the entire miniImageNet dataset utilized?
- **Scalability:** Why was the method not evaluated on the entire ImageNet dataset?
- **Typos:** Several typos are present, e.g., abstract: “Critical” should be “Clinical” (?) but likely meant “Critical”? Also “downstrea” should be “downstream”.
