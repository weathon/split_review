## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Crystal Hierarchical Generative Flow Network (CHGFlowNet), the first application of GFlowNets to crystal structure generation. It decomposes the generation process into a hierarchy of space groups, lattice parameters, and atomic positions, and combines physics-informed reward terms. Reviews are mixed: two reviewers (scores 5 and 5) praise the motivation, clarity, and alignment with materials discovery goals; two others (scores 3 and 3) criticize the lack of methodological rigor, missing details, weak experimental validation, and questionable performance. One reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) is particularly severe, rating soundness and presentation as poor.

### Strengths
- **First GFlowNet for crystal generation** – The work is the first to apply GFlowNets to crystal structure generation, a significant problem in materials science (R1, R3).  
- **Hierarchical design leveraging space‑group symmetry** – The bi‑level generation graph, which sequentially selects a space group and then samples symmetry‑constrained lattice parameters and atom positions, is well‑motivated and captures physical priors effectively (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Physics‑informed reward functions** – The combination of formation energy, bond distance, structure density, and composition validity rewards helps guide generation toward stable, diverse crystals. An ablation study shows their individual contributions (R1, R3).  
- **Good background and literature revision** – The paper provides a concise and insightful overview of crystallography and related work (R1, R2).  
- **Clarity for some readers** – One reviewer found the method straightforward and easy to re‑implement (R4); however, this view is not shared by the majority (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient details on the generation process** – Crucial aspects are described only vaguely or omitted:  
  - How is the initial state sampled? What is the number of atoms generated, and are they sampled one‑by‑one or all at once? (R2, R3)  
  - Atom positions are drawn from a Multivariate Gaussian distribution, but a Gaussian is unbounded and can produce invalid negative fractional coordinates. A bounded distribution (e.g., Beta, as in Lahlou et al., 2023) would be more appropriate (R2).  
  - The handling of Wyckoff positions is not discussed. For a space group with large multiplicity (e.g., Fm‑3m, multiplicity 192), does the model duplicate atoms 192 times or does it deduplicate special positions? (R3)  
  - The paper merely references Lahlou et al. (2023) for continuous‑space adaptation but does not explain how the hybrid discrete‑continuous state space preserves the assumptions required for correct GFlowNet training. A naive design is likely incorrect (R2).  
  - No detailed algorithm or pseudocode is provided (R3).  
  - The selection of lattice parameters and the construction of the graph (edges) in Section 4.1 are not clarified (R2, R4).  
- **Violation of translation invariance** – Fractional coordinates are directly input to the GCN, which breaks the translation invariance of 3D crystals. This issue has been well‑studied in prior predictive and generative works (e.g., Periodic Graph Transformers, CDVAE) and is a notable omission (R3).  
- **Weak and incompletely described experimental evaluation** –  
  - Only one task (battery material discovery) and one dataset are considered. Testing on MOF, zeolites, or other crystal structure prediction tasks would strengthen the results (R1).  
  - Baselines are limited to PGCGM and a simple GFlowNet; no comparison with CDVAE or recent symmetry‑aware generative methods (R1, R3).  
  - The method does not consistently outperform baselines across all metrics in Tables 1 and 2, raising doubts about its practical advantage (R4).  
  - The reported average formation energies (PGCGM: 4.558, GFlowNet: 1.433, CHGFlowNet: 0.882) are all positive; in practice formation energy must be negative for stability. The PGCGM paper reports 39.6% negative‑energy structures, a discrepancy that is not explained. No distribution of formation energies is shown (R2).  
  - Evaluation criteria are vague: “within certain thresholds” (R2), definition of “mode” and diversity metrics are not specified (R2).  
  - Only three of the four reward functions are ablated; the effect of composition validity is not examined (R3).  
- **Lack of motivation and justification** –  
  - Why GFlowNets are necessary for this problem and how they address specific limitations of prior methods is not clearly argued (R4).  
  - The claim “HGFlowNets generalize GFlowNets” is made without any elaboration or formal justification (R2).  
  - Using penalty terms in the reward function to encode constraints is suboptimal; GFlowNets can incorporate hard constraints directly during sampling, whereas soft constraints introduce multiple hyperparameters (R2).  
  - The design of the four reward functions lacks theoretical underpinning or comparison with alternative physics‑informed rewards (R4).  
- **Presentation issues** –  
  - The writing is hurried and overloaded with technical details without sufficient context; no illustrative figure appears until page 4 (R4).  
  - Numerous typos and formal errors: “atom list L” should be “atom list A” (R4); “atom list L” used in triplet but later “atom list L” and “atom coordinates X” are same variable (R2); lowercase $\tau$ reused for both trajectory and trajectory set (R2); “flow transitions” instead of probability transitions (R2); missing period in Figure 1 caption (R4); lattice parameters in Figure 1 (a=4, b=6, c=4) do not match the drawing (R2).  
  - The predictive model for formation energy is only mentioned in the supplementary (M3GNet) with a vague description: “Any prediction lower than the cut-off is set to 10 eV/atom” – what is the cut‑off and how often does this occur? (R2).
