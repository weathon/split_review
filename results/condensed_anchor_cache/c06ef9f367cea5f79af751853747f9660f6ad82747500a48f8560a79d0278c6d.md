## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the transferability of gradient-based image jailbreaks across a diverse set of over 40 open-parameter vision-language models (VLMs). Through extensive empirical analysis, the authors find that such jailbreaks—optimized against a single VLM or an ensemble of VLMs—exhibit little to no transfer to other VLMs, except when models are very similar (identical initialization, highly similar architectures/training). The results stand in contrast to transferable adversarial attacks on image classifiers and text jailbreaks on LLMs. The paper also demonstrates that attacking larger ensembles of highly-similar VLMs can improve transfer to a specific target.

### Strengths
- The studied problem is of central interest, and the results are counterintuitive and impactful. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper conducts extensive experiments across many reasonable settings (over 40 VLMs, diverse backbones, training data, safety alignment), making the conclusions convincing. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The writing and presentation are clear, and conclusions are stated without overblown claims. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The systematic breakdown of transferability across different granularities (e.g., identical initialization, architecture, training data) effectively demonstrates how quickly transfer drops off. (Reviewer 3)
- The evaluation pipeline and experimental setup appear reasonable; the choice of prismatic VLMs facilitates exploration, and inclusion of newer VLMs like Qwen is appreciated. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper proposes a method to improve transfer by attacking ensembles of highly-similar VLMs, showing significant gains on the target. (Reviewer 2)
- Good care was put into accurately measuring the success of the attack. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Attack methodology limited to a single approach.** The paper relies exclusively on 50k-step Adam optimization, initialized from random noise or a single natural image. One reviewer (Reviewer 3, rating 3) considers this a must-fix: the paper should explore more diverse optimizers, step sizes, weight decays, and the APGD method with an epsilon constraint (as used by Schlarmann et al., 2023). The same reviewer notes that 50k steps is excessive and that checking transfer at 5k steps would suffice. The authors state computational limitations prevent more sophisticated attacks, but the reviewer argues that testing a variety of optimizers is more informative than running 50k steps for every attack. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient diversity in natural image initialization.** Multiple reviewers note that starting from only a single natural image is not enough to claim equivalence between noise and image initialization. (Reviewers 2, 3) Reviewer 2 calls for experiments with multiple diverse natural image initializations and analysis of whether choice affects transferability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Overfitting issue not adequately discussed.** The optimization space for image jailbreaks is enormous (255^(HW) vs. text's V^N), making the likelihood of overfitting extremely high. The reviewer suggests that 8 ensemble models may be far from enough for image transfer, whereas it suffices for text. They recommend using PAC learning theory and strategies to reduce overfitting, citing existing transfer attack research. (Reviewer 1)
- **No test with non-harmful content to isolate transfer difficulty from safety alignment.** Reviewer 1 questions whether the lack of transfer is due to the content being harmful (triggering safety alignment) rather than limited transferability per se, and suggests testing with non-harmful content (e.g., consistent text generation unrelated to safety). (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing quantitative definition of "highly similar" VLMs.** Reviewer 2 asks for a quantitative similarity measure (e.g., via loss landscape analysis) and analysis of its correlation with transfer success, citing prior works on NN similarity. Without this, the claim that models are "highly similar" remains vague. (Reviewer 2)
- **Clarity needed on the jailbreak image design.** Reviewer 4 asks for clarification: is the jailbreak a single optimized image used for all prompts? Could multiple images (e.g., per topic) or topic-relevant initializations improve transfer? The reviewer does not require additional experiments but notes this as a missing clarification that could inform future work. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing ablation on identical initialization.** In Section 3.3, transfer partially succeeds when models are identically initialized. Reviewer 4 asks whether transfer would break if models were initialized differently, and how important identical initialization is. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing explanation for failure with 2-stage model in Section 3.6.** Even when scaling ensembles, no transfer to the 2-stage model was observed. Reviewer 4 asks for intuition on why this setting fails. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential issues with image discretization and clipping.** Reviewer 3 questions whether pixel values are clipped to [0,1] during optimization and evaluation, and whether the VLM sees a uint8 representation in the final evaluation (vs. floating-point precision). If discretization occurs, it could impact results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Effect of attention mechanism and input ordering.** Reviewer 1 asks whether, since the input texts contain no image references, the attention mechanism may ignore the image and reduce transferability. They also ask whether the order of image and text input affects transferability (some VLMs insert the image before the text). (Reviewer 1)
