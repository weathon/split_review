## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Neural Network-ResDMD (NN-ResDMD), a method that combines Residual Dynamic Mode Decomposition (ResDMD) with neural network parametrization of dictionary functions, trained by minimizing the spectral residual. Experiments on a pendulum, turbulence, and neural dynamics are presented. Reviewers generally find the approach technically reasonable but disagree on its significance: three reviewers (ratings 5,5,5) consider the contribution incremental and the results unclear, while one reviewer (rating 6) finds the combination interesting and flexible but still notes theoretical gaps.

### Strengths

- The method is technically reasonable, well-organized, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,4).  
- The idea of using the spectral residual as a loss function with neural network observables is natural but has not been exactly practiced before (Reviewer 1).  
- The approach is theoretically justified by the guarantees of ResDMD (Reviewer 3).  
- Clustering results on the neural dynamics experiment are promising (Reviewer 3).  
- Code for the experiments is available online (Reviewer 3).  
- Minority reviewer (Reviewer 4) finds the combination of neural networks and ResDMD to make ResDMD more flexible, and considers the topic interesting and relevant.

### Weaknesses

- **Incremental contribution.** The use of neural networks as Koopman dictionary functions is well-established in the literature (e.g., Takeishi et al. 2017, Lusch et al. 2018, Mardt et al. 2018, and many others cited by Reviewer 1). Three reviewers (1,2,3) view the paper’s contribution as insufficient for a conference like ICLR; Reviewer 3 suggests it is better suited as a workshop paper. Reviewer 4, while more positive, still notes theoretical limitations.  
- **Overlooked related work and loss similarities.** The paper does not adequately cite or position itself within the rich literature on neural network-based DMD/Koopman (Reviewer 1 lists nine specific papers). The squared relative residual has strong similarities to the VAMP-E score of Wu & Noé (2020); the authors should provide a comparative analysis and clarify differences (Reviewer 2).  
- **Theoretical gaps.** The paper claims EDMD lacks convergence guarantees, but Korda & Mezić (2018) already discuss convergence (Reviewer 1). More importantly, the theoretical impact of neural network approximation error on the convergence and stability of the estimated Koopman spectrum is not addressed; overfitting, generalization, and sensitivity to architecture/hyperparameters are not discussed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Unclear experimental design and results.**  
  - Pendulum experiment: ground truth is not explicitly specified; it is confusing that NN-ResDMD results are shown as shaded areas while other methods use points, and the shaded area has a large radius (Reviewer 3).  
  - Turbulence experiment: no ground truth is provided; it is unclear why NN-ResDMD results (Fig. 5) are considered good while Hankel-DMD results (Fig. 7) are considered bad (Reviewer 3).  
  - Neural dynamics experiment: different numbers of eigenfunctions are used across methods, making the comparison potentially unfair; no ground truth for eigenfunctions is available (Reviewer 3).  
  - Fig. 4: it is unclear whether the dynamical system is measure preserving and why only spectra on the unit circle are considered; if not measure preserving, the distribution of eigenvalues inside the unit circle should also be discussed (Reviewer 4).  
  - The authors should clarify whether EDMD-DL uses exactly the same configuration as NN-ResDMD except for the loss function (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing details and computational concerns.**  
  - The feedforward neural network architecture details are not provided, and no investigation of other architectures (e.g., PINNs/PINOs) is performed; this is left as future work but should have been explored (Reviewer 3).  
  - There is no discussion of computational cost. The iterative process likely requires many evaluations of eigenpairs, making NN-ResDMD significantly slower than classical ResDMD; a trade-off between accuracy and time should be empirically and theoretically discussed (Reviewer 3).  
  - Limitations of the algorithm are not discussed (Reviewer 3).  
- **Minor technical issues.**  
  - The reason for alternating between gradient-based update of θ and least-squares for K is not justified; it would be possible to include the least squares within the gradient computation as done in Takeishi et al. (2017) and Otto & Rowley (2019) (Reviewer 1).  
  - Optimization variables are not specified in equations (3.4) and (3.5) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 1 diagram needs to be cleaner and more professional (Reviewer 3).  
  - Appendix A.5 on Hankel-DMD is left empty (Reviewer 3).  
  - Line 208: should use \((G+\sigma I)^{-1}\) instead of \(\dagger\) (Reviewer 4).
