## Merged Review

### Summary
Most reviewers (scores 6, 6, 8) find the paper solid, original, and well-evaluated, with one more critical review (score 5) questioning novelty and experimental choices. The paper proposes Data Taggants, a non-backdoor dataset ownership verification method using clean-label targeted data poisoning with out-of-distribution (OOD) key samples. Strengths include novel use of OOD keys, rigorous statistical guarantees, and strong empirical results (zero FPR, high TPR, no accuracy loss) on ImageNet. Weaknesses center on incomplete baseline comparisons, insufficient adaptive attack analysis, unclear technical novelty versus prior work (Witches Brew), and potential practical vulnerabilities (OOD detection, collusion). Reviewer 4 is notably more positive and raises distinct concerns about key detection and collusion, while Reviewer 3 is more skeptical about novelty and experimental justification.

### Strengths
- **Novel application**: Using OOD samples as secret keys for dataset ownership verification is a novel idea (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Repurposing gradient matching (Witches Brew) with random key sampling strengthens theoretical guarantees (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong empirical results**: Data Taggants achieve 0% false-positive rate and high true-positive rate while maintaining model accuracy (Reviewer 4). Evaluation covers large-scale ImageNet1k with ViT and ResNet, includes poisoning defenses and OOD detection (Reviewer 2).
- **Statistical guarantees**: Method provides more rigorous statistical certificates than prior work (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The key sampling is random, ensuring independence and uniform null distribution (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Designed for realistic setup**: Harmlessness, stealthiness, and black-box access are important challenges addressed by the method (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Presentation**: Well-structured methodology (Reviewer 1) and mostly easy to read (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete evaluation and baseline positioning**: Only two baselines are compared (Backdoor watermarking v1, v2) without clear rationale for excluding others (Reviewer 2). Table 1 shows Taggants beating baselines even on TPR, suggesting missing baselines (Reviewer 2). Baselines such as BadNet are needed (Reviewer 3) and the relationship to all prior DOV methods should be clearly mapped (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear technical novelty versus prior work**: The paper does not clearly mark that gradient matching, augmentations, and restarts are directly from Witches Brew (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 asks for a detailed technical and setting comparison with the prior gradient-matching work. The contribution is incremental, applying existing ideas to the ownership application (Reviewer 4).
- **Unsubstantiated theoretical guarantee claims**: Reasons why prior work violates independence/uniformity assumptions are not sufficiently explained (e.g., using ground-truth class predictions) (Reviewer 2). Empirical validation of the match between theoretical and empirical FPR for prior work and for Taggants is missing (Reviewer 2). Are model predictions on random [0,1]^d data truly uniformly random? A class like "TV static" might be predicted consistently (Reviewer 2). This needs more clarity and validation.
- **No formal security analysis against adaptive attacks**: The paper lacks analysis of an adversary who knows the exact verification technique (Reviewer 1). Specific practical attacks are not considered: the adversary could use an OOD detector to identify random-key inputs and alter predictions (e.g., output uniform distribution) (Reviewer 4). Also, watermark collusion is possible: an adversary can generate its own key set and signed dataset, creating ownership disputes (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited investigation of downstream task impacts**: Effects of taggants on downstream task performance (e.g., transfer learning) are not studied (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclarified experimental choices**: Why 0.1% modification budget? How sensitive is the method to this choice? (Reviewer 1). Why was SleeperAgent chosen as the backdoor baseline instead of a simpler method like BadNet? (Reviewer 3). Table 1 reports 0% TPR for Backdoor watermarking (Li et al. 2023) – how was this measured? The reference reports much better numbers (Reviewer 4). Possibly due to use of top-k instead of full probability set or the Wilcoxon test (Reviewer 4).
- **Key technical insight underexplored**: The paper shows (Table 3) that forcing predictions on OOD keys is easier than on in-distribution samples, but does not explain why (Reviewer 2). This central idea deserves deeper analysis (Reviewer 2).
- **Robustness concerns with limited top-k**: Verification effectiveness is significantly reduced when only the top-1 prediction (k=1) is available, limiting practical applicability (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential violations in verification assumptions**: The assumption of independence of model predictions on keys is questioned: during inference, predictions are independent, so why does the paper claim prior work violates it? (Reviewer 4). This needs explanation.
- **Errors in literature positioning**: The claim that Radioactive Data (Sabrayrolles et al.) does not support black-box verification is incorrect; their black-box verification uses loss differences (Reviewer 4). Also, the claim that backdoor watermarking "is harmful to the model as it introduces errors" needs clarification – what errors, and how does that contrast with the harmlessness claim of Witches Brew? (Reviewer 3).
- **Scalability concerns**: Imperceptibility and effectiveness may degrade on smaller images (CIFAR-10, grayscale) or other data types (tabular, text) (Reviewer 4). The effect of training on a subset of the dataset or combining datasets (reducing the ratio of signed images) is not explored (Reviewer 4).
- **Typographical issues**: Missing dot after citation (line 151), extra dot (line 518), extra "them" (line 188), and a potentially malformed sentence at line 317 (Reviewer 2). Reference typo at line 148 (Reviewer 4).
