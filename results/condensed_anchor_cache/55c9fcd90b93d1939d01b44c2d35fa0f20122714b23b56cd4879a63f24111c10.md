## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the bias‑variance decomposition for ensembles of deep classification models. It empirically establishes two phenomena: (1) bias and variance are “aligned” – the log squared bias and log variance exhibit an approximate linear relationship with slope 1 for correctly classified samples; (2) squared bias upper‑bounds variance (\(B^2 \ge k \cdot V\)). Theoretical justifications are provided from two perspectives: calibration (showing the bias‑variance gap can be bounded by calibration error) and neural collapse (deriving explicit bias and variance under a Gumbel‑noise perturbation model). Experiments are conducted on CIFAR and ImageNet with various architectures, and the role of overparameterization is investigated.

### Strengths
- **Novel empirical finding:** bias‑variance alignment and the upper bounding of variance by bias are clearly demonstrated in deep ensembles. The linear relation on a log scale is of surprisingly high quality (R1).  
- **Strong empirical evidence:** Figures 1.b, 2, 3, 4 are compelling across CIFAR, ImageNet and multiple architectures (R1, R4).  
- **Two complementary theoretical frameworks:**  
  – Calibration provides rigorous upper bounds and shows that alignment follows from calibration (R1). The general definition of calibration under sub‑σ‑algebras is a nice contribution (R2).  
  – Neural collapse yields an exact, closed‑form solution under Assumption 5.1, and the resulting bound on the variance‑to‑bias ratio is novel (R2).  
- **Well‑written and organized:** the paper is clear, the introduction sets up the structure and main claims well (R1, R3).  
- **Overparameterization** is well‑investigated and its role in the bias‑variance phenomenon is demonstrated (R2).  
- The paper provides new insights into the bias‑variance relation in deep learning, relevant to understanding generalization in overparameterized models (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Related work:**  
  – Theorem 4.3 appears equivalent to Theorem 4.2 in Jiang et al. [1]; the authors should comment on this in the main paper rather than claiming an implication (R2).  
  – The related work is **highly incomplete**: it misses prior ensemble/deep‑ensemble literature on bias‑variance decomposition, the effect of overparameterization on variance [4], and the observation that optimizing variance increases bias [5]. An extensive literature review is recommended (R3).  

- **Calibration assumption is strong:**  
  – Assuming models are well‑calibrated is very restrictive, which weakens the interest of the calibration‑based results (R2). Real‑world models are rarely calibrated, raising the question of whether alignment would still hold approximately for uncalibrated models (R4).  

- **Empirical evaluation scope:**  
  – Only CIFAR and ImageNet are used; results on other domains (NLP, simple polynomial curve fitting) are missing, limiting evidence for generality (R3, R4).  
  – The evidence could be more directly linked to the claims: Fig. 3 should include colors to indicate misclassified points, and the actual bound for eqn (3) is not shown (R3).  
  – The overparameterization analysis is too shallow – it merely varies ResNet width on CIFAR, largely reproducing results already in [4]. A deeper investigation was expected (R3).  

- **Neural collapse section is contested:**  
  – While one reviewer (R1) found it interesting and praised the exact solution, another (R3) considered it adds little, has restrictive assumptions, is too short, and should be moved to the appendix in favor of expanding empirical evaluation.  
  – The choice of the **Gumbel distribution** in Assumption 5.1 is motivated only by analytical tractability; additional justification or evidence beyond the unimodal‑shape check (Sec. G.2) would be desirable (R1).  
  – The variable \(F\) in eq. (59) has an \(F(2(K-1),2)\) distribution – this fact is central and should be explicitly explained (R1).  

- **Exposition and presentation:**  
  – The paper is **dense and notation‑heavy**, with too many disjoint ideas squeezed into 9 pages. Some ideas are under‑explored (overparameterization) while others are over‑explored (neural collapse); the paper would benefit from prioritising its most impactful claims (R3).  
  – **Title**: “alignment” does not precisely describe a linear/proportional relationship and is an overloaded term in AI safety (R3).  
  – The two main claims on page 2 are presented as theoretical truths but are empirical; this should be clarified (R3).  
  – Eqn (10) should either contain a proof (in the appendix) or cite a source (R3).  
  – Many plots are too small, especially the text (R3). Tables 1 and 4 have unusual formatting (double line at top) and should follow style guidelines (R3).  
  – Colors for negative and positive classes in Figures 12 and 13 are swapped relative to the rest of the paper; consistency would help (R1).  
  – Minor typos: histograms missing in the middle row of Figure 12; “\(K\) as the number of samples” in Sec. G.2; “In above” → “In the above” on p. 9, line 5 (R1, R3).  

- **Clarity of definitions and technical details:**  
  – The definition of bias and variance should be **more explicit** about which sources of randomness are included: initial parameters is clear, but variance over the training set is not discussed, whereas works like Adlam and Pennington use a finer‑grained decomposition (R4).  
  – Eq. (52) is correct only up to addition of a vector parallel to \(\mathbf{1}_K\); this should be noted to avoid confusion (R1).  
  – The identity of \(C_{h_\theta}\) in eqn (2) and eqn (3) should be clarified if they are the same (R3).  

- **CE loss experiments:**  
  – The change to CE loss in Appendices E.2 and F.6 is not very readable; the effect mostly disappears. These sections should be expanded and clarified (R4).  
  – After eq. (10) the paper mentions using MSE on probabilities, but training details for the empirical results are not given (R1). If CE was actually used, the scale \(s\) of the ETF complex grows during training, and one could test the theoretical prediction that \(\log \mathrm{Bias}^2 / \log \mathrm{Var}\) changes from \(0.557\) to \(2\). This would also contradict the alignment statement (1) that requires the ratio to approach \(1\) as \(\log \mathrm{Var}\to-\infty\) (R1).  

- **Figures and data analysis:**  
  – Figure 13: it is unclear whether it includes only correctly classified samples or all of them. If all, the absence of a vertical tail of misclassified points in the synthetic data should be explained (R1).  
  – Figure 7: the alignment looks worse than in Figures 3‑4; a more detailed explanation of why is missing (R4).  

- **Missing experiments / suggestions:**  
  – Study bias and variance as a function of feature learning strength; the scale of variance over initializations depends inversely on feature learning strength (citing https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12147) (R4).
