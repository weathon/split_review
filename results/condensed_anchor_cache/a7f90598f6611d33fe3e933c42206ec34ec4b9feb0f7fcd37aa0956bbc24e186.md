## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper evaluates existing LLM-generated text detectors (zero-shot and purpose-trained) on short news-like posts (≤500 characters) generated by six LLMs (Phi-2, Gemma-2B, Mistral-0.1, Gemma-chat, Zephyr, LLama-3-8B-Instruct). It tests three evasion attacks: changing generation parameters (temperature, repetition penalty), prompting attacks, and paraphrasing. Key findings: zero-shot detectors are easily fooled by simple temperature increases; a purpose-trained detector can generalize across unseen LLMs but fails on off-distribution human-written texts (e.g., US vs British English). Two reviewers scored 5, three scored 3, indicating disagreement on overall value.

### Strengths
- The studied question (real-world LLM-generated content detection) is highly practical and crucial for societal concerns.
- A new short-news dataset was developed using six diverse LLMs, with text truncated to 500 characters to reduce length confounds.
- Three attack types (generation parameters, prompting, paraphrasing) are systematically evaluated, revealing substantial performance degradation in existing detectors.
- The evaluation includes off-distribution human texts (e.g., British English) – a factor missing in prior literature.
- The paper provides a comprehensive literature review on LLM detection.
- The finding that zero-shot detectors are vulnerable to simple temperature and repetition penalty attacks (even without quality constraints) is interesting and important for benchmark design.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and surprisingness (R1, R2, R5):** Many results are expected. It is well-known that zero-shot detectors are sensitive to distribution shifts and that purpose-trained detectors can overfit to their training distribution. No new technical insights or methods are offered.
- **Narrow dataset scope (R2, R3, R4):** The dataset is limited to news-like posts from CNN Dailymail. Real-world LLM content spans diverse styles (creative writing, technical documents, conversational text, reviews, etc.). Claims of “real-world” applicability are unsupported without testing broader text types.
- **Short text length (R2):** Restricting to 500 characters excludes long-text detection scenarios. Specialized short-text detectors are not evaluated, limiting the benchmark’s thoroughness.
- **Insufficient source models (R2, R3):** The six LLMs are all small or open-weight. More advanced models (GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude) are missing. The “moderately sophisticated attacker” assumption is weak; attackers can use proprietary APIs. Detection performance on texts from stronger models is needed.
- **Incomplete baseline coverage (R2):** Recent state-of-the-art detection methods are omitted (e.g., Fast DetectGPT, and other works cited by the reviewer). The claim that “LLM detectors still fall short” is not justified without comparing these methods.
- **Missing analysis of trained detector failure (R4, R1):** The failure on unseen human texts (e.g., US vs British English in Table 2) is attributed to overfitting, but the paper does not analyze why. The Electra_RR model uses dataset mixture for improving TPR; the same is not explored for FPR. It remains unclear whether adding diverse human text (both US and British) to training or adjusting the threshold would resolve the issue. No attention visualization or feature importance analysis is provided.
- **No control for quality degradation after attacks (R5):** Increasing temperature or repetition penalty can reduce fluency/coherence, making the attacked text impractical for real disinformation. The paper does not measure or constrain quality preservation, so the threat model is unrealistic.
- **Presentation issues (R3, R4):** The paper contains grammatical mistakes and incomplete sentences. Misuse of \citet and \citep makes it hard to read. The introduction does not clearly explain motivation, problem, or method.
- **Missing experiments/ablations requested (from reviewers’ questions):**
    - Varying LLM size (e.g., 2B–7B parameters) and its impact on detection and adversarial evasion (R1).
    - Methods to improve generalization to different domains of human-generated text (R1).
    - Testing on non-news datasets (e.g., books, reviews) as in RAID benchmark (R4).
    - Will the dataset mixture training approach reduce FPR and solve the US vs British English failure in Table 2? (R4).
    - Identifying subpopulations in human text prone to misclassification as LLM-generated (R5).
- **Overall lack of actionable insights (R3):** The paper is an evaluation, not a solution. It offers observations but no fundamental challenges addressed or novel directions that could advance LLM detector development.
