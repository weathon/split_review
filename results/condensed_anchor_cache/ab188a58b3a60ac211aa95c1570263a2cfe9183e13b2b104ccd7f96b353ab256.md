## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UniHDA, a framework for hybrid generative domain adaptation using multi-modal (image/text) references from multiple target domains. It employs CLIP-based direction vector interpolation and a cross-domain spatial structure loss to retain source consistency. Several reviewers note the idea is simple and interesting, and that the method is generator-agnostic. However, multiple concerns are raised about the validity of comparisons, missing ablations, inconsistent qualitative results, limited exploration of multiple domains, and insufficient evidence for claims. One reviewer (score 3) is markedly more negative about output quality and degradation in 3D/real-image settings.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- The core idea – linearly interpolating CLIP direction vectors for hybrid multi-modal adaptation – is interesting and technically sound. (R1, R2, R3)
- The method is versatile and generator-agnostic, validated on StyleGAN, EG3D, and diffusion-based models. (R2, R4, R5)
- Thorough experiments across a wide variety of generation backbones (GAN, 3D, video, diffusion). (R2, R5)
- Robust performance on incompatible domain combinations, avoiding artifacts that appear when objectives conflict in other methods. (R3)
- Good discussion distinguishing the approach from T2I models and image editing methods, clarifying unique contributions. (R3)
- One reviewer (R5) finds that quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate superiority over prior arts in hybrid domain adaptation. (Note: other reviewers disagree on this point.)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evidence for certain claims.**  
  * The assertion (line 86) that multiple target domains increase overfitting risk lacks empirical support. (R1)  
  * The claim (line 188) that prior works only support single-domain adaptation overlooks multi-domain methods like Domain Re-Modulation, FHDA, and Domain Expansion. (R1)  
  * The claim (line 420) that UniHDA shows superior diversity over T2I generators for multi-attribute images is unsubstantiated; quantitative evidence is required. (R3)

- **Experimental comparisons are unfair or incomplete.**  
  * Using DINOv2 for patch-level feature matching gives UniHDA an advantage over NADA; an ablation adding the same loss to NADA is needed for fair comparison. (R1)  
  * The comparison with IP-adapter is problematic because IP-adapter is an open-set variation model without a source domain, making it unsuitable for generative domain adaptation. (R2, R5)  
  * For one-shot settings, the paper should compare with MindTheGap. (R1)  
  * Missing comparison with recent diffusion-based methods for attribute manipulation and translation (Prompt Sliders, Concept Sliders, SDEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, Imagic). (R3)

- **Qualitative results are not consistently better than baselines, and often worse.**  
  * In Figure 5, NADA better captures reference styles (e.g., green color and wooden texture for Hulk; exaggerated features for caricature). (R1, R2)  
  * FHDA's alleged mode collapse is not evident; FHDA actually maintains diversity. (R1)  
  * Image quality is low compared to current state-of-the-art T2I models. (R2)  
  * EG3D adaptation (suppl. videos) produces flattened geometry and degraded texture quality. (R4)  
  * Real image adaptation (Figure 8) shows blurriness and artifacts. (R4)  
  * The Hulk example (Figure 10) fails to transfer texture, capturing only expression. (R4)

- **Quantitative evaluation concerns.**  
  * The CS-I metric (cosine similarity between source and generated images) is ambiguous – if “real image” means the source image, then high CS-I and SCS may simply reflect a large λ (CSS loss weight) that encourages source consistency over target adaptation. An ablation of λ from 0 to 5 with CS-I and SCS scores is needed. (R2)  
  * The average CS-I loses fine-grained information about per-domain attribute transfer. A user study is critical for comprehensive comparison. (R1)  
  * A minority reviewer (R4) notes that the compared methods’ weaknesses are highlighted while UniHDA’s own quality issues are underplayed.

- **Limited exploration of multiple domains.**  
  * Experiments only cover two-domain hybrids; scalability to more than two target domains is not validated. (R2, R3)  
  * The method requires separate training for each hybrid configuration (domain combination and coefficients), whereas NADA can train n models once and interpolate weights for any hybrid. This makes the efficiency claim partially inaccurate. (R5)  
  * Composition is limited to linear interpolation; non-linear or learnable coefficients are not considered. (R3 question)

- **Writing and presentation issues.**  
  * Section 3.2 essentially reproduces the NADA loss (and MindTheGap) without proper attribution as background. (R1)  
  * Figure 4 is confusing: samples from the source generator on the right side may be misinterpreted as a different source generator. (R1)
