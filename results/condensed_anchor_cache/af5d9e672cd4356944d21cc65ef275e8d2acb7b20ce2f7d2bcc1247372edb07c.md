## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the need for lightweight deep watermarking models by identifying a mismatch between common decoding losses (MSE, BCE) and the actual decoding objective, which causes parameter redundancy. The authors propose two plug-and-play components: Decoding-Oriented Surrogate Loss (DO), which suppresses irrelevant optimization directions, and Detachable Projection Head (PH), a module used only during training to handle those directions. They also introduce a new five-submodule framework enabling independent parameter reduction. The resulting model uses only 2.2% of the parameters of prior state-of-the-art while achieving better efficiency, invisibility, and robustness, including against diffusion-based attacks. The evaluation is comprehensive. Reviewers generally found the motivation well-grounded and the experimental validation sufficient, though one reviewer (score 6) was significantly more critical on clarity and novelty.

### Strengths
- First identification of the mismatch between the optimization objectives of commonly used decoding losses (MSE, BCE) and the actual decoding objective; the existence and impact of this mismatch are confirmed through ablation studies, providing a new perspective for model optimization (Reviewer 4, also noted by Reviewers 2, 5).
- Proposed DO and PH effectively mitigate the negative impact of irrelevant optimization directions, enabling a lightweight model to achieve SOTA performance with only 2.2% of the parameters of previous frameworks (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The methods are simple, easy to deploy, and intended as plug-and-play components for future lightweight models (Reviewer 3).
- Comprehensive evaluation against multiple distortions, including robustness against diffusion-based attacks (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The lightweight model outperforms existing models in invisibility, robustness, and efficiency for resource-constrained environments; the fine-grained framework minimizes performance loss during compression (Reviewer 4).
- The paper discusses watermarking of several types of data (Reviewer 1).
- Experimental results are satisfactory under lightweight structural conditions, with performance surpassing baselines; the motivation is fairly strong (Reviewer 5).
- Most reviewers found the paper well-written and well-organized (Reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer (score 6) strongly disagreed on presentation (rated 2 vs. 3–4), criticizing the clarity of the methods section (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- The intuitive explanation behind the three loss components derived from MSE (deflation, inflation, regularization in Eq. 3) is insufficient. It is not clearly justified why minimizing \(L_{deflation}\) aligns with the decoding objective and why \(L_{inflation}\) and \(L_{regularization}\) are detrimental, beyond the mathematical derivation. This undermines technical rigor (Reviewer 5; one reviewer was particularly critical of this and the overall clarity of the methods section).
- The novelty of the proposed framework (decoupling the decoder into NWIP and ME) is not clearly distinguished from existing methods. It is unclear what specific limitations of integrated decoder designs are addressed by this separation, and why it is crucial for parameter reduction beyond having two modules instead of one (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5).
- The writing in the methods section (Section 3) is poor, especially for the Detachable Projection Head. The purpose is unclear—whether it mitigates the impact of \(L_{inflation}\) and \(L_{regularization}\) or serves another goal—and the connection to the loss analysis is weak. The goal should be stated first, then how it is achieved (Reviewer 5). Consequently, one reviewer rated presentation 2, while others gave 3–4.
- The analysis of the mismatch is limited to MSE loss. The paper lacks discussion of how the framework extends to BCE or other commonly used loss functions in watermarking (Reviewer 5; Reviewer 3 also asked why BCE was not used for deriving DO in Eq. 21).
- The comparison of embedding capacity is unfair: HiDDen embeds 52 bits in a 16×16 grayscale image (0.203 BPP) while this paper uses 64 bits in a 3×128×128 color image (much lower BPP). The embedding capacities of other compared algorithms are inconsistent; the authors should explain how comparisons are made (Reviewer 4).
- The proposed framework omits the discriminative network that has been used since HiDDen to enhance invisibility. The reason for this omission is not explained (Reviewer 4).
- Important details are relegated to the appendix but should be in the main text: visual results, implementation of the projection blocks, and the noise parameter settings for NWIP during training (only testing-phase parameters are given) (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 4 notes lack of training noise details).
- The safe distance (\(\epsilon\)) in DO loss is manually set; automated or adaptive tuning could improve performance and ease of use (Reviewer 1).
- PH and DO do not achieve best performance under geometric distortions; real-world applicability requires improvement (Reviewer 1).
- PH increases training complexity, but its parameter count and computational demands are not reported; whether its size can be optimized is unknown (Reviewer 1).
- The very high PSNR values (72.64 and 67.75 in Tables 6 and 8) are not explained and need clarification (Reviewer 3).
- The parameters A and B in Eq. 4 are not explained; it is unclear if they are learnable (Reviewer 3).
- Robustness against clipping attacks is not evaluated (Reviewer 2).
- The motivation behind the lightweight architecture design itself (beyond the loss analysis) is not clearly stated (Reviewer 2).
- Minor issues: citation format needs brackets (Reviewer 2); spelling error "robust-ness" in Table 9 (Reviewer 4); abbreviations in Figure 1 are used without full terms first (Reviewer 5).
- The decomposition of MSE loss may not be novel; a similar decomposition appears in knowledge distillation, which could affect the assessment of innovativeness (Reviewer 4).
- The conclusion should summarize the advantages and disadvantages of PH and DO (Reviewer 3).
