## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Meta-Value Learning (MeVa), a framework for opponent shaping that uses a meta-value function to judge joint policies by a discounted sum of future optimization iterates. MeVa applies a form of Q-learning to the meta-game of optimization, avoiding explicit representation of continuous policy updates. The method is claimed to be consistent (does not assume the opponent is naïve and is aware of its own learning) and far-sighted (looks beyond one step). Experiments are conducted on a logistic game and repeated matrix games (IPD, Matching Pennies, Chicken, and a logistic game) comparing MeVa against LOLA, HOLA, COLA, and M-FOS.

### Strengths
- **Novelty**: Applying Q-learning-based methods to the meta-game in opponent-shaping is novel (Reviewer 1). MeVa extends “looking ahead” with a discounted sum over returns and avoids policy gradients (Reviewers 2, 3). It is consistent and far-sighted, addressing limitations of one-step lookahead (Reviewer 2).
- **Quality and Significance**: The method is well-motivated and builds on important prior work (Reviewer 4). Opponent shaping is increasingly important for real-world multi-agent systems (Reviewer 1). MeVa achieves superior performance over LOLA, HOLA, COLA in tested games and is competitive with M-FOS (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The paper fairly analyzes limitations (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity**: Generally well-written with lucid theoretical discussions (Reviewers 1, 3, 6). Background on LOLA and related work is clear (Reviewer 2). Design considerations and their motivations are highlighted (Reviewer 6).
- **Minority-positive reviewer**: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, rating 6) was notably more positive, praising MeVa’s consistency, far-sightedness, and value-based formulation as high-quality contributions that could bring new insights to the MARL community.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical guarantees missing**: The paper lacks rigorous analysis of convergence, stationarity, or local optimality in the meta-game. There is no proof or discussion of convergence properties or computational complexity, especially given the extrapolated meta-value approximation (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). A proof of convergence to a stationary point or local optimum is requested (Reviewer 2).
- **Scalability concerns and restricted scope**: Experiments are limited to small matrix games and a logistic game with tiny policy spaces. Scalability to larger state/action spaces, more than two agents, or continuous actions is not addressed (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The method assumes observation of opponent strategy parameters, which may not hold in real-world tasks (Reviewer 2). Computational cost of calculating meta-values could become prohibitive with more agents (Reviewer 1). Head-to-head results between MeVa and M-FOS were not presented due to computational restrictions (Reviewer 4).
- **High variance and sample complexity**: MeVa relies on REINFORCE for gradient estimation, which is known to have high variance. The paper does not discuss practical measures to mitigate variance or provide standard deviations for performance in Tables 2 and 3. Gradient consistency is essential for the iterates (Reviewers 5, 6). Sample complexity may be prohibitive for scaling (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete experimental comparisons**: Comparisons against other meta-learning opponent-shaping methods (Meta-PG, Meta-MAPG) are missing (Reviewers 2, 3). Baseline methods that compute equilibria in zero-sum games could be included (Reviewer 3). Experiments do not clearly demonstrate why extended predictions from the value function are beneficial beyond performance numbers (Reviewers 3, 4). Error bars / standard deviations are absent (Reviewers 3, 5, 6).
- **Insufficient ablations**: Important ablations missing: comparing the surrogate formulation (Eq. 6) with the naïve infinite discounted sum (Eq. 5); comparing the exploration strategy (Gaussian noise with sign-flipping) with standard schedules like ε-greedy or action noise (Reviewers 5, 6). Current ablations only enable/disable exploration without contrasting schemes (Reviewers 5, 6).
- **Clarity issues in methodology**:
  - Notation differs from prior works (LOLA, Meta-PG, Meta-MAPG), making direct comparison difficult (Reviewer 1).
  - Algorithm 1 is hard to follow; more explanation of the meta-value update and practical implementation details is needed (Reviewer 1).
  - The meta-MDP (state space, action space, transition function, reward function) is not clearly outlined; it seems non-episodic but this is not clarified (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
  - The term “extrapolation” is not defined and may be confused with bootstrapping (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - “Self-consistency” and “naïve vs. non-naïve learning” is unclear; LOLA also accounts for own learning to first-order, so the distinction needs sharper explanation (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Description of M-FOS as “solving inconsistency” yet “not learning, but acting with learning awareness” is confusing (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - The advantage of the “more grounded in the game” claim (Section 4.1) is not justified (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - The exploration strategy (flipping sign of final layer) is crude and lacks comparison to standard approaches (Reviewers 3, 4, also noted under ablations).
  - Derivation of the Bellman equation for U (Eq. 4) is not shown; its advantage over standard bootstrapping is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work organization**: Discussion of related work is spread throughout the paper. A dedicated related work section would improve readability and better situate MeVa among LOLA, HOLA, COLA, Meta-PG, etc. (Reviewers 5, 6).
- **Reproducibility**: The source code is not submitted, making reproducibility uncertain (Reviewer 2).
