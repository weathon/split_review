## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviews are split. Two reviewers (R1, R2; scores 5) find the application of the proximal-perturbed Lagrangian to fairness constraints novel and interesting, value the convergence guarantees, and consider the paper well-organized. Three reviewers (R3, R4, R5; scores 3) raise major concerns about algorithmic feasibility, assumptions, empirical evaluation, and positioning relative to existing convex relaxation methods. All reviewers note shortcomings in clarity, justification of assumptions, or connections between the theory and the original fairness problem.

### Strengths
- Novel application of the proximal-perturbed Lagrangian formulation (Kim 2021) to fairness‑constrained classification (R1, R2, R5).
- Provides convergence guarantees for the constrained optimization reformulation (R1, R2, R5).
- Well‑referenced fairness literature (R4).
- Multiple numerical experiments on benchmark datasets (R3, R4).
- Plenty of fairness notions discussed in Section 2 (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Surrogate relaxation vs. original fairness** (R4): The reformulation (3) uses a differentiable surrogate ε‑fairness (Definition 1) instead of the original indicator‑based parity; the algorithm solves this reformulation but no theoretical guarantees are given w.r.t. the original parity. The claim of directly handling the original non‑convex non‑smooth problem is misleading, as surrogates are still used. The practical advantage over convex relaxations (e.g., Donini et al., Celis et al., Goel et al.) is unclear. (Also echoed in R5’s concern about positioning.)

2. **Algorithmic framework and subproblem solvability** (R3): The subproblem for θ_{k+1} (Algorithm 1, line 3) involves ⟨λ_k, G(θ)⟩, where G is non‑convex and non‑smooth; it is not explained how to obtain θ_{k+1}. If the linearization in Eq. (9) is used (first‑order Taylor), the update requires boundedness of λ_k (Assumption 5). The authors assume this boundedness without justification, whereas standard proofs derive it under constraint qualifications (e.g., Boob et al. 2023). (R5 also notes lack of clarity on update rules.)

3. **Theoretical guarantees and assumptions** (multiple reviewers):
   - Assumption 1 (existence of primal‑dual KKT solutions): its validity for the non‑convex non‑smooth Lagrangian is not justified; no discussion of strong duality (R2, R5).
   - Assumption 5 (bounded λ_k) lacks precise references to the literature; justification is insufficient (R3, R5).
   - Theorem 1 states asymptotic convergence, but the proof suggests an O(1/T) sublinear rate; the theorem should state the stronger non‑asymptotic rate directly (R2, R5). The connection between the KKT conditions in (4) and the results in Theorems 1 and 2 is unclear (R5). R1 finds Theorem 2 “not so meaningful”. The link between Lemma 4, Theorem 1, and Remark 1 is not explained (R5). Theorem 2’s guarantee of feasibility for Algorithm 1 is not clarified (R5).

4. **Empirical evaluation insufficient** (R4, R3): Very few baselines compared to the rich fair‑classification literature; notably missing comparisons with convex relaxation approaches. Insufficient reproducibility details: hyperparameter tuning process not specified, values of ε not given, results lack tables with standard deviations. Most empirical claims rely on CPU time without evaluation of convergence rates. (R4)

5. **Clarity and presentation** (multiple):
   - Section 3 lacks an intuitive explanation of the Proximal‑Perturbed Lagrangian, the role of penalty and proximal terms, and the chosen update order (R5, R3).
   - The specific function z(λ,μ) = (λ-μ)/α is presented as a special case without justification for its choice (R1).
   - In Eqs. (9) and (10), second‑order Taylor expansions replace differentiable functions; why not use the differentiable functions directly? (R1)
   - Typo: iteration number sometimes k, sometimes t (e.g., δ_k = κ·(t+1)^{-1}) (R5).
   - Eq. (2) sums over s but no s appears inside the sum (R4).
   - Positioning unclear: the approach is a general optimization framework, not specific to fairness, making the title, abstract, and introduction misleading (R5).

6. **Scope limitations** (R4): The algorithm only handles two protected groups; extension to multi‑group problems is not discussed.

7. **Convergence rate not in Theorem 1 statement** (R2, R5): The theorem only states asymptotic convergence; the sublinear rate is hidden in comments/proof and should be stated explicitly as a main result.
