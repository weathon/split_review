## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses token-level uncertainty in language model cascades, framing deferral to a larger model as rejection in classification with rejection. It identifies length bias in naive sequence-level confidence (Chow-Sum favors short sequences, Chow-Average favors long sequences) and introduces a quantile-based approach (Chow-Quantile) along with post-hoc classifiers trained on quantile features and embeddings. Experiments on FLAN-T5 across multiple NLP tasks (machine translation, QA, etc.) demonstrate advantages over baselines, sometimes outperforming both individual models. The method is principled and easy to implement. Reviewers varied in enthusiasm (two scores of 8, two of 6), with the lower-scoring reviewers raising more concerns about rigor, generalization, and comparison.

### Strengths
- Clear and accessible exposition, connecting LM cascades to classification with rejection (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Identifies a crucial practical issue: length bias in sequence-level confidence measures for generative tasks (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Proposes a simple yet effective quantile-based method (Chow-Quantile) and post-hoc classifiers (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Comprehensive evaluation across several NLP tasks using FLAN-T5 (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Provides interesting insights via post-hoc methods (Reviewer 4).
- Principled uncertainty estimation method for LLMs (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- Limited comparison with other uncertainty estimation methods (e.g., deep ensembles, MC dropout, calibration techniques like temperature scaling, Platt scaling) (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Evaluated only on a single base model (FLAN-T5); generalizability to other architectures (encoder-only, different tokenization/attention) is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- Use of greedy decoding, which is more prone to hallucination than beam search; may affect conclusions about length bias – e.g., Chow-average favoring longer sequences could be due to hallucination loops, and negative correlation between translation quality and length may be hallucination artifacts (Reviewer 1).
- Lacks rigorous analysis of token log-probability distribution to justify quantile approach; Figure 1 is insufficient. Need categorization of sequences by variance, examples where low-probability tokens skew average, and more detailed distribution analysis (Reviewer 3).
- Observation that best generation performance occurs at intermediate deferral rates (not when all is deferred) suggests smaller model sometimes outperforms larger; further analysis of input characteristics behind this is needed (Reviewer 3).
- Claim that Chow-Sum is biased toward deferring longer predictions contradicted by Figure 3(a) (oracle deferral score smaller than Chow-Sum for sequences of 150–250 words); clarification required with distribution of sequence lengths per method (Reviewer 3).
- Missing experimental details: WMT dataset not identified/cited; MNLI experiments not justified as techniques are for generation, not classification (Reviewer 1).
- Post-hoc method details insufficient: performance of Post-Hoc-Embed-1 not reported; computation of Φ(x) unexplained; performance of quantile classifier for golden deferral label unclear (Reviewer 3).
- No discussion or comparison with speculative decoding, another line of work addressing efficiency-performance tradeoffs (Reviewer 3).
- Not evaluated for out-of-distribution robustness or domain adaptation (Reviewer 2).
- Questions about relation of Chow estimates to proper scoring rules (NLL, Brier score) and whether they could be compared to deep ensemble uncertainty estimates (Reviewer 2).
- In intermediate embeddings approach, only decoder representations used; input properties (e.g., length) might be useful features (Reviewer 4).
