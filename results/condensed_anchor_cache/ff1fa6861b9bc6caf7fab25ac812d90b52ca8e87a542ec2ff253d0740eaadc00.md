## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Controllable Continual Test-Time Adaptation (C-CoTTA), which uses Concept Activation Vectors (CAV) to guide (rather than suppress) domain shifts by maximizing inter-class distances and minimizing inter-domain distances. Domain- and class-level shift control losses are introduced.

### Strengths
- Using CAV for the problem of continual TTA is a novel combination (R1, R3).
- Proposing domain and class shift controlling losses to actively control domain shifts and maintain clear classification boundaries (R1, R4).
- The method demonstrates empirically plausible results across widely used CTTA benchmarks (R1, R2).
- R1 specifically notes that performance improves on SOTA benchmarks; R4 acknowledges the approach tackles error accumulation.

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal improvements**: Reported gains are small (0.6% on CIFAR-10C, 0.4% on CIFAR100-C, 0.5% on ImageNet-C), raising concerns about practical significance (R3). Several benchmark comparisons show very tiny gaps (e.g., 15.3 vs 14.7 in Table 1, 30.2 vs 29.9 in Table 2, 59.9 vs 59.4 in Table 3) (R2, R3).
- **Potential test-set overfitting**: Given the low-resource setup and small performance gaps, it is unclear if hyperparameters and design choices (e.g., in Appendix C) were tuned without access to the test/evaluation split. This impacts the fairness of empirical comparisons (R2).
- **Vague terminology**: The terms “guide” and “control” are not precisely defined. The method appears to fundamentally maximize inter-class distances and minimize inter-domain distances – an objective shared by many CTTA methods, making the claimed distinction from “suppressing the shift” unclear (R2). Equation 7 still seems to focus on suppressing shifts (R2).
- **Unfair characterization of baselines**: Summarising all baseline methods as merely “suppressing the shift” is inaccurate; analysis in §4.5 and Figure 4 should be extended to all methods listed in Tables 1–3 to support this claim (R2).
- **Missing comparisons**: EcoTTA (Song et al., 2023) and BeCoTTA (Lee et al., 2024) are not compared (R1). Several other relevant works [1–5] listed by R3 are omitted. The set of compared methods is inconsistent across datasets (e.g., ViDA only on ImageNet-C, not on CIFAR) (R3).
- **Metric validity concerns**: The inter-class and inter-domain distance measures may be scale-dependent (e.g., if feature vectors are doubled, distances increase artificially). Example: ‘Brightness’ vs ‘Contrast’ conditions on ImageNet-C have ~20% accuracy difference despite similar inter-class/inter-domain distances, questioning metric relevance (R3).
- **Pseudo-label reliance**: Using pseudo-labels for prototype computation can cause error accumulation in real-world scenarios (R1, R3, R4). The accuracy of pseudo-labels directly affects class shift estimation (R4).
- **Motivation for CAV**: The paper does not sufficiently justify why Concept Activation Vectors specifically are needed (over other interpretability methods) and what properties make them uniquely suited to CTTA rather than general deep learning (R1).
- **Sensitivity to loss functions and hyperparameters**: The method’s reliance on controlling shifts via losses (lambda_1, lambda_2) may be sensitive to the choice of loss functions; lack of discussion on robustness (R1). How are lambda_1 and lambda_2 tuned? Is any validation corruption used? (R1).
- **Missing ablations**: Ablation with lambda_1=0 and lambda_2=0 is not reported to isolate contributions of each loss (R1). The impact of varying amounts of labeled source data for class prototypes is not studied (R3). The effect of using only a subset of classes per batch on domain shift calculation (eq. 9) is not addressed (R3).
- **Computational overhead**: No analysis of the overhead introduced by CAV computation and the proposed loss terms; this is especially important for TTA where efficiency is crucial (R1).
- **Domain prototype concept**: Originally proposed in UDA, not novel for TTA; obtaining an accurate domain prototype is challenging given the online mini-batch nature of TTA (R4). Mini-batch size is not specified – it is a significant issue in TTA (R4). What happens if a batch contains samples from multiple domains? (R4) The impact of batch size on domain shift direction calculation (eq. 9) needs discussion (R3).
- **Notation, grammar, and presentation errors**: Many grammatical errors hinder reading (R4 lists examples: line 109, 112, 124, 157, 225, 231, etc.). Typo “Concept Ativation Vectors” (line 82-83) (R1). Notational issues: eq2 undefined symbols X_t and X_s; inconsistent subscripts/superscripts in eq3 and eq4; line 216 undefined i (R4). Algorithm 1 does not define p^t_i (R1). Typo “DSCL → CSCL” in lines 71,73 (R3). Computing symbol “cov[]” in eq(1) is undefined (R3). The relationship between eq(7) and eq(8) needs explanation (R3). Why use symmetric cross-entropy loss rather than KL or reverse KL in eq(10)? (R4). “Random order” in §4.8 should be defined (R3). RMT performance reported is lower than the original RMT paper – reason unclear (R4). Figure 3 should show similar plots for different target domains over time (R4).
