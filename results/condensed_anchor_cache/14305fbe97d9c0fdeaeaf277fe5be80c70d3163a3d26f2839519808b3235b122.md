## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MaxMin TD Learning, an exploration strategy that alternates between taking the action that maximizes and the action that minimizes the current Q-estimate. It claims that using the minimum-valued action yields higher TD errors than random actions, accelerating learning in low-data regimes. The method is tested on the ALE 100K and 200M benchmarks, with the 100K results showing a 248% improvement in median human-normalized score. Theoretical propositions (3.4, 3.6) attempt to justify the benefit of min-action experience. Reviewers praise the theoretical framing and low-data improvements but raise major concerns about novelty, experimental rigor, missing comparisons, and the method’s long-term effectiveness.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical analysis of min-action value:** Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 4) highlights that the theoretical assessment showing the minimum-action value leads to higher TD error than random actions and that the difference equals the disadvantage gap is a novel and well-presented finding. Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 4) also finds the analysis of acting uniformly vs. Q-minimizing after parameter initialization interesting.
- **Clear formalization and exposition:** Reviewer 1 notes the paper nicely formalizes concepts and gradually presents propositions for single and double Q-learning, making it easy to follow. Reviewer 2 (score 5, confidence 2) and Reviewer 4 (score 3, confidence 3) agree the paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- **Experimental scope and evaluation choices:** Reviewer 3 acknowledges the testbeds (toy Chain MDP, Atari 100K, Atari 200M) are reasonable, and reporting median/80th percentile aggregate measures with 5 seeds and standard errors is appropriate. Reviewer 4 notes the method’s flexibility across DDQN and QRDQN.
- **Simple, low-overhead method:** Reviewer 2 points out the method requires fairly simple changes to existing algorithms and tends to improve performance, with zero additional computational cost.

### Weaknesses
- **Framing as a TD algorithm vs. exploration strategy:** Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 4) strongly argues the method only changes experience generation, not consolidation, so it should be described as an exploration/behavior strategy, not a TD approach. Baselines like Noisy Nets and ε-greedy are themselves exploration strategies. (Minority view: Reviewer 1 and 2 accept the framing without objection.)
- **Incomplete theoretical justification:** Reviewer 3 states the propositions and proofs do not deliver a full picture, failing to capture long-term behavior after initial uniformity breaks. Reviewer 2 (score 5, confidence 2) questions Proposition 3.4 and 3.6: the RHS uses a fixed state *s* while the LHS uses random variable *s_t*, making the proof unclear and requiring a time-dependent looseness. The paper also lacks a regret analysis for the min-action choice (Reviewer 2) – e.g., cases where large negative rewards are harmful.
- **Counterexample showing introduced bias:** Reviewer 3 constructs a multi-armed bandit example where taking only max/min actions after random initialization can permanently prevent other actions from being experienced, even though they are not the true max/min. The TD error for chosen actions quickly reaches zero while other actions remain unexplored, contradicting the proposition’s premise. Reviewer 3 requests a comment or experiment on this setting.
- **Missing comparisons with exploration baselines:** Multiple reviewers identify missing baselines:
    - Reviewer 1 (score 6) and Reviewer 2 request comparisons with Random Network Distillation (RND) [3] and curiosity-driven approaches. Reviewer 2 also requests “Flipping Coins to Estimate Pseudocounts for Exploration in Reinforcement Learning”.
    - Reviewer 3 suggests comparing with bootstrapped DQN (Osband et al., 2016) and other epistemic uncertainty minimization methods.
    - Reviewer 4 (score 3, confidence 3) recommends benchmarking against alternative sample-efficiency methods (e.g., [4,5] from their review). The claim that count-based methods require “substantial complexity including training additional deep neural networks” (lines 126-128) should be quantitatively compared against such methods.
- **Limited benchmark diversity:** Reviewer 1 notes the paper lacks experiments on exploration-specific benchmarks like MiniGrid or Crafter. Evaluation is confined to ALE.
- **Atari 200M results are weak and truncated:** Reviewer 3 points out that Atari 200M performance is not competitive (plots truncated earlier than 200M frames; e.g., StarGunner stops at 70M). This suggests the method helps early training but cannot match final performance of well-tuned baselines. The “canonical temporal difference learning” label in Figure 3 is ambiguous – reviewers struggle to identify which basis algorithm (DDQN, QRDQN) corresponds to which plot/table.
- **Unfair comparison with ε-greedy scheduling:** Reviewer 4 notes that the ε-greedy baseline in Atari 200M uses a decay that may converge too rapidly (ε decreases every 0.8% of the total interaction budget), penalizing ε-greedy when given a larger budget. NoisyNetworks is used in the 100K benchmark but absent from the 200M experiments. Request experiments with varying ε values and more balanced comparison.
- **Marginal novelty:** Reviewer 4 states the proposed method introduces limited novelty – exploring different selection criteria based on Q-estimations has been previously explored with ensembles [1,2], and optimistic/pessimistic policy update trade-offs exist [3]. A dedicated Related Work section is missing. Reviewer 3 also suggests the benefit may stem from epistemic uncertainty reduction, a well-studied area.
- **Missing experimental details and clarity:**
    - Reviewer 1 asks: does the algorithm consider min actions only early in training? What is the value of *e* in Algorithm 1? What is the motivation for clustering games in Figure 4?
    - Reviewer 2 asks for justification of the choice of ε decay (0.8% value) and details on NoisyNetworks implementation.
    - Reviewer 2 requests clarification of “information gained” in line 264 (is it information-theoretic or loss optimization?).
    - Reviewer 3 asks why Atari 200M curves are truncated and the reasoning behind the specific subset of games. Number of states *N* in Chain MDP is not reported.
    - Reviewer 3 notes that x-axis labels should be “number of frames” not “environment interactions” because of frame skipping >1.
    - Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 4 request clarification of Figure 4: what does it mean for TD error to be stable or drop, and what are “high” negative/positive values?
    - Reviewer 4 asks for interpretation of constant 2 in Figure 1 (UCB) and whether it was optimized; show results for [0.5,1,2,3].
    - Reviewer 4 suggests “zero additional cost” not “zero cost” in line 265.
- **Compatibility limitation:** Reviewer 3 notes the method is only compatible with discrete-action Q-based methods (e.g., DDPG cannot use it) – this should be mentioned.
