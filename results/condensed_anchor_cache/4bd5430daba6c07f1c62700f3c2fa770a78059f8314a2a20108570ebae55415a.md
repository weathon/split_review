## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AnyText, a diffusion-based multilingual visual text generation and editing model. It uses an auxiliary latent module (ControlNet-like) with glyph, position, and masked image inputs, plus a text embedding module that encodes OCR stroke data and blends it with caption embeddings. Training employs text-control diffusion loss and text perceptual loss. The model claims plug-and-play compatibility with existing diffusion models. The authors also introduce AnyWord-3M, a 3M image-text dataset with multilingual OCR annotations, and an AnyText-benchmark for evaluation. The method outperforms prior work under this benchmark.

### Strengths
- **Plug-and-play adapter for multilingual text generation**: The proposed module can be inserted into many existing pre-trained diffusion models to guide visual text generation, and achieves multilingual capability (including languages not addressed in previous works) – noted by R1, R2, R4. R2 particularly highlights this as a notable innovation.
- **Effectiveness of text adapter and text encoder**: These components are proven to improve OCR performance (R1); the text embedding module (visual text token injected into prompt latent space) is a key innovation (R4). R3 notes that generated glyphs harmonize with image content and are not monotonous (unlike simpler ControlNet-based approaches).
- **Text-control diffusion loss and text perceptual loss**: Strategic incorporation during training improves text generation quality (R2, R4). R2 calls it a "catalyst" for enhanced accuracy.
- **AnyWord-3M dataset**: The first large-scale multilingual text image dataset with OCR annotations – recognized as a valuable contribution by all reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Multilingual generation with relatively less data**: R3 highlights the paper's ability to handle multilingual text glyph generation without requiring massive per-language data.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing implementation and dataset details**:
  - Whether the linear projection layer for the OCR encoder is trained or fixed (R1).
  - Inputs to Text-ControlNet (concatenation of z_a and z_t) and configuration of the fuse layer (single or stacked convolution) are unclear (R1).
  - How the tight position mask (l_p) is annotated: PP-OCR does not output tight polygon masks, so it is unclear if they come from OCR boxes, human annotation, or another method; scale/cost of human annotation not discussed (R1).
  - Layouts (position masks) and caption information for many figures (Fig. 1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12) are not provided (R1, R3).
  - Token replacement mechanism: which tokens are identified as text tokens and how they are used in generation requires clearer explanation (R2).
- **Unfair comparison and attribution of gains**:
  - Baseline models were trained on different datasets; fairness of comparison is questionable (R4). The ablation study does not isolate the impact of the model architecture from the training data (R3, R4). R4 suggests comparing with baselines trained on the same dataset, or training AnyText on a previous LAION glyph subset.
  - The paper does not measure FID in the ablation study; only Chinese sentence accuracy and NED are reported – insufficient to assess image quality (R3). Qualitative results for each ablation experiment are also missing (R3).
- **Practical limitations**:
  - The model requires a user-given text mask (ground truth) during evaluation; this is impractical in real-world scenarios. Performance with predicted or noisy masks is not explored (R4).
  - The method adds multiple modules to a vanilla diffusion model, but computational overhead (GPU hours, additional parameters) is not reported (R2, R3).
- **Evaluation gaps**:
  - Korean and Japanese are shown in qualitative results but excluded from quantitative results (categorized as "others") (R3). No comprehensive analysis across diverse scripts or character complexities (R3).
  - Sentence accuracy and NED are measured using the same OCR model (PP-OCRv3) used in training; evaluation with an independent OCR model is needed to avoid bias (R1).
  - The improvement from visual text embedding is shown only for Chinese text; it is unclear whether it also improves English word generation (R1).
- **Reliance on pre-existing technologies and re-implementation challenges**:
  - The architecture heavily builds on Latent/Stable Diffusion and ControlNet, somewhat shadowing its novelty (R2). The complex multi-stage training with many critical hyperparameters makes re-implementation difficult (R2).
- **Technical ambiguity**:
  - The intermediate generative results (x'_0) used in perceptual loss may contain noise or blur, potentially compromising the loss computation (R2).
  - The synergy between Z_a (from Auxiliary Latent Module) and Z_0 (from VAE) in Text-ControlNet is not clearly explained (R2).
- **Lack of investigation into alternative encoders**: The paper does not study the impact of different visual encoders (e.g., CLIP visual encoder instead of PP-OCRv3) on text generation quality (R3). The ablation shows text embedding is critical, but its reliance on OCR module performance is not disentangled (R3, R1).
- **Conflicting reviewer assessments**: R2 (score 8, confidence 4) views the method as a strong innovation, while R1, R3, R4 (score 6, confidence 3) raise concerns about missing details, fairness, and practical applicability. The strength of the contribution is debated.
