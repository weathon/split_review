## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes BoostCL for pre-trained model continual learning, combining a theoretical analysis of Random Projection (RP) with a Multi-View RP scheme that adapts AdaBoost to CL. The approach also includes a self-improvement process for task-specific prompt selection. Experiments show consistent improvements over baselines (including Hide-prompt and RanPac) on multiple benchmarks, though some reviewers note the method’s advantages are concentrated in linear classifiers, with limited gains in anti-forgetting (FFM metric). The theoretical results are considered interesting but partly heuristic, and several trade-offs (e.g., projection dimension, view count, inference cost) are not fully explored.

### Strengths
- Theoretical analysis demonstrates how Random Projection improves feature separability and generalization ability in high-dimensional space.
- The adaptation of AdaBoost to continual learning addresses a previously challenging integration, with a concrete mechanism for multi-view ensemble.
- The self-improvement process for prompt selection is simple yet empirically effective.
- The method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including Hide-prompt and RanPac, across diverse datasets.
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation across multiple tasks and datasets, with real-world continual learning scenarios.
- The motivation for Multi-View Random Projection is clear and well-justified.
- The paper is well-organized; formulas and theoretical derivations are presented unambiguously, and most reviewer queries are addressed in the text or appendix.

### Weaknesses
- The method shows no advantage on the forgetting measure (FFM) in Table 1 (Reviewer 1).
- Lacks discussion of why RP is superior to alternative feature transformations (e.g., learned transformations, non-linear projections) and a trade-off analysis between them (Reviewer 2).
- Insufficient comparison with recent CL methods that use replay buffers or regularization techniques (Reviewer 2).
- Technical details are challenging to follow; the interaction between multiple views and random projection is not clearly explained; a step-by-step breakdown with mathematical formulations is needed (Reviewer 2).
- No analysis of scalability (computational cost, GPU memory, training time) or comparison of resource consumption with other methods; reviewer asks how GPU memory scales with projection dimension (Reviewers 2 and 3).
- Presentation could be improved: introduction should better motivate the problem, and appendices (proofs, extra results) could be more effectively integrated (Reviewer 2).
- Limited discussion on the theoretical upper bound of generalization error under RP (Reviewer 3).
- No theoretical guidance for choosing the optimal projection dimension, which greatly impacts performance across tasks (Reviewer 3).
- The self-improvement process increases inference complexity; its trade-off is not analyzed, especially in scenarios with many tasks (Reviewer 3).
- Minor spelling error: “sammple” in line 250 (Reviewers 3 and 4). Also, undefined notation γ(s,g,ℝᵈ) in line 177 (Reviewer 4).
- Need more ablation studies on key components: prompt selection, self-improvement, number of views, projection dimensions, and threshold for the voting mechanism (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The necessity of having many views is unclear; the final classifier uses only a subset, potentially discarding complementary knowledge – no justification for why a subset is sufficient (Reviewer 4).
- The method is largely heuristic: the voting strategy and self-improvement process lack a principled or theoretical foundation; voting is ad-hoc and self-improvement may accumulate errors in multi-step prediction without error-propagation analysis (Reviewer 4).
- No comparison with simpler ensemble methods (e.g., randomly sampling views, using all views) or ablation studies for each component (view selection, self-improvement) (Reviewer 4).
