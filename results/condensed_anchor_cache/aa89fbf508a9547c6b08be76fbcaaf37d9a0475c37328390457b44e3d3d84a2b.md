## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes LLM-Regress, which combines lifted symbolic regression planning with LLM-based affordances for open-world embodied tasks. The method uses LLMs to ground affordance predicates for unknown objects, while regression planning is used to generate goal-directed plans with fewer LLM calls. Evaluations on ALFWorld and a new ALFWorld-Afford dataset show improved success rates, planning duration, and token usage over several baselines, with demonstrations of generalization to unseen tasks and resilience to affordance shifts.

### Strengths
- The paper is mostly clear, well-written, and well-organized (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The research topic (open-world planning) is of interest to the community (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed method is technically sound; replacing hand-crafted affordance functions with LLM queries is a sensible approach (Reviewer 1).
- Empirical results on ALFWorld and ALFWorld-Afford are strong compared to the picked LLM planners (Reviewer 1). Ablation studies effectively demonstrate the crucial role of affordances (Reviewer 1).
- Rigorous formal reasoning may help ensure safety and interpretability (Reviewer 2). Reasoning backward from the goal could potentially simplify the process (Reviewer 2).
- Using LLMs to ground affordance information might be smart, as affordances are relatively domain-independent properties (Reviewer 3). The combination of symbolic reasoning and LLMs is sophisticated (Reviewer 4).
- Empirical evaluations on both datasets are thorough and well-structured (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited benchmark diversity**: The evaluation is conducted only on ALFWorld, which is not very “open-world” as the number of objects, functionalities, and tasks is limited. More diverse environments (e.g., Minecraft) are needed to assess generalization to truly open-world scenarios (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Insufficient baselines**: Missing comparisons with interactive LLM planning with self-check or reflections, memory-based learning, retrieval-augmented planning, and especially DEPS (which has results on ALFWorld). A more comprehensive comparison would strengthen the paper (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Unclear definition of “open-world”**: The paper does not clearly define what “open-world” means and how ALFWorld fits that definition. Open-world scenarios often imply unknown or infinite objects/predicates; how would the method function in such a scenario and what would the complexity be? (Reviewers 2, 3). Relatedly, the assumption that “the affordances of the objects are unknown” is puzzling: if the next action is determined by prompting a large model, what is the fundamental distinction from works like SayCan? (Reviewer 2).
- **Grounding functions vague**: The definitions of θ_o and θ_a (grounding functions) are vague; it is not clear what input they take and what output they produce (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation and assumptions unclear**: The argument that “LLMs do not require structured inputs or explicit knowledge modeling, which theoretically makes them well-suited for open-world planning” is not justified (Reviewer 3). The paper does not clearly explain why observation predicates (P_o) can be grounded from images (e.g., p(hold(potato_1) | image)) but affordance predicates (P_a) cannot (e.g., p(canHeatStuff(microwave_1) | image)) (Reviewer 3). Also, if grounded propositions P_o are given, how are there any “unknown objects”? (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing connection to related work**: There are existing works on learning abstractions for planning that address similar limitations of classical planning; these should be discussed (Reviewer 3). The claim that the approach “provides complete and verifiable plans” needs clarification: complete with respect to the affordance performance of LLMs? (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear difference from LLM-Affordance+FD baseline**: Both the proposed method and the LLM-Affordance+FD baseline use affordance information generated from the LLM, yet the success rates differ. The only way this occurs is if the domain definition (operators and/or propositions) differs between the two approaches, which would imply that the affordance-related propositions generated by the LLM are inconsistent across methods; this is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Affordance classes seem pre-defined**: In experiments, “determining whether an object can be heated, cooled, cleaned, or turned on” appears to pre-define affordance classes, contradicting the claimed open-ended extraction from LLMs (Reviewer 3). The paper should motivate why affordances need to be extracted from LLMs at all (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure 1 is unclear**: Captions should explicitly explain concepts like “Lifted Regression” and “Grounded Regression” so that readers do not have to refer to the main text (Reviewer 2). The font size of graphics is too small to see clearly (Reviewer 1).
- **Implicit assumptions about object names**: Does θ_o output object labels (e.g., egg_1, microwave_2)? Is there a pre-defined set of object names? (Reviewer 3).
- **Typos**: Citation typo for Russell and Norvig (AIMA) at Lines 189, 192; possible typo at Line 212: “can be regressed *no* longer?” (Reviewer 3).
