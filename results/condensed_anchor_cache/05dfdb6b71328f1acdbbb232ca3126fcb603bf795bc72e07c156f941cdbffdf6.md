## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes AdEMAMix, a modification of Adam that uses a mixture of two EMA gradients (a fast one with small \(\beta_1\) and a slow one with large \(\beta_3\)) to better exploit past gradients. Experiments on language modeling (LLMs, Mamba) and image classification (ViTs) show improvements over AdamW, including up to a \(95\%\) token efficiency gain. One reviewer (score 10) strongly endorsed the work, praising its strong results and thorough ablations. The other four reviewers (scores 5, 6, 6, 6) acknowledged the empirical performance but raised concerns about motivation, novelty, hyperparameter sensitivity, memory overhead, and lack of theoretical justification. The main criticisms center on whether the improvements are explained and whether the additional complexity is justified.

### Strengths

- **Strong empirical performance**: AdEMAMix consistently outperforms AdamW across language modeling (LLMs, Mamba) and vision (ViTs), with up to a \(95\%\) token efficiency gain on a 1.3B parameter model (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5).
- **Thorough ablations**: Extensive hyperparameter and architecture ablations in the main text and appendix demonstrate robustness (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The simple modification to Adam is clearly presented (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- **Comprehensive benchmarking**: Experiments cover multiple architectures, dataset domains, and hyperparameter choices (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Interesting study on forgetting**: The authors show AdEMAMix slows down forgetting during training, potentially useful for continual learning and pretraining beyond Chinchilla-optimal tokens (Reviewers 4,5).
- **Addresses immediate concerns**: The paper includes experiments on hyperparameter tuning and memory overhead, showing awareness of practical issues (Reviewer 4).
- **Raises interesting questions**: The work motivates further study of gradient relevance and momentum design (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Motivation is vague and insufficiently connected to LLMs**: The Rosenbrock function toy example (Figure 2) is used to motivate why older gradients help, but the gap between this simple convex function and LLM training is large and not bridged (Reviewer 1). The paper claims older gradients are relevant for tens of thousands of steps, but more direct LLM-specific evidence is needed to convince that “\(\beta_1 = 0.9\) is suboptimal for LLMs” (Reviewer 1). Several key sentences in the introduction (e.g., “changing the direction of the slow momentum …”) are difficult to understand and lack connection to LLMs (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty – combining multiple momenta is not new**: AggMo already applies the same idea (multiple momentum terms) to gradient descent; adapting it to Adam, while practically useful, is not a significant conceptual leap (Reviewer 4).
- **Additional hyperparameters introduce complexity and sensitivity**: The algorithm introduces \(\beta_3\) and \(\alpha\) (mixing ratio), and requires schedulers for large \(\beta_3\) to avoid early instability. This makes the optimizer less user-friendly and may require extensive tuning per setting (Reviewers 3,4,5). The optimal \(\alpha\) can vary: in switching experiments \(\alpha=2\) or \(\alpha=1\) is used, while optimal values from tuning were much higher (Reviewer 4). The interaction between \(\alpha\), \(\beta_1\), and \(\beta_3\) is not studied (Reviewer 3). Alpha being \(\ge 1\) is large and no intuition is given; could this necessitate a smaller step size? (Reviewer 5)
- **Memory overhead is non-trivial**: Storing a second momentum buffer increases memory by roughly the model size. While the authors propose setting \(\beta_1=0\) to compensate, this causes instabilities and loss spikes (Figure 22) that do not always recover, especially at larger batch sizes (Reviewers 4,5). Using FSDP does not eliminate the memory overhead for many practitioners (Reviewer 5). The wall-time comparison uses \(\beta_1=0.9\), so the memory savings from \(\beta_1=0\) are not utilized there (Reviewer 5).
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees or convergence analysis**: No convergence proof is provided, even in the convex setting. It is unclear what constraints on \(\beta_1,\beta_3\) ensure stability and avoid divergence (Reviewer 3). The paper should reconcile with recent theory showing Adam converges for a wide range of \(\beta_1\) (Zhang et al., 2022) and that Adam works well when gradient/Hessian correlations exist (Crawshaw et al., 2024; Kunstner et al., 2024) – this suggests the new variant may not be well-motivated (Reviewers 1,5).
- **Figure 2 is confusing and possibly misleading**: The color scheme is inconsistent across subplots (Reviewer 5). AdEMAMix appears to overshoot the minimizer more than Adam in some subplots, and it is unclear whether it converges to the optimal solution (Figure 2c) (Reviewers 1,5). At least 10× more iterations may be needed to draw valid conclusions (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient justification for why the algorithm works**: The main explanation (older gradients help because they contain relevant information) is hand-wavy. The claim that the improvement is at least partially explainable without variance reduction (toy justification in Figure 2) lacks empirical support in LLM experiments; the benefit indeed drops with increasing batch size (Figure 17), but the maximum batch size tested (512k) is smaller than that used for large LLMs (e.g., Llama uses 4M) – an experiment with 2M batch size is suggested (Reviewer 2). The concept of “older gradients being outdated” is not quantified or defined clearly (Reviewer 5).
- **Additional experiments are needed**:
    - Effect of weight averaging on both AdamW and AdEMAMix (Reviewer 2).
    - Explore the new momentum term with other optimizers (Adafactor, Signum) (Reviewer 4).
    - Storing the slow EMA in lower precision to save memory – does it affect performance? (Reviewer 4)
    - The optimal learning rate for AdEMAMix was too high for Adam only at larger scales, suggesting it may be harder to tune; further investigation into which parts of the network cause destabilization is needed (Reviewer 4).
    - Experiments with stabilization strategies (e.g., QK-LayerNorm) could help assess ease of tuning (Reviewer 4).
    - The forgetting effect (Figure 4) is dominated by injection time \(t_B\); the algorithm’s additional benefit is small relative to that, and the forgetting analysis is mostly in the appendix while the main experiments focus on loss (Reviewer 5).
    - On ImageNet-1K, performance is not clearly better than AdamW – why? (Reviewer 5)
- **Potential exploding step size**: When gradients are extremely small for many steps, the slow EMA term decays slower in the numerator than the denominator, potentially leading to exploding step sizes (Reviewer 4).
- **Instability concerns**: Even with hyperparameter tuning, the algorithm may show instability in early steps (0–200k) due to the use of schedules (Reviewer 5). Setting \(\beta_1\)=0 causes loss spikes even at moderate batch sizes, raising concerns for scaling (Reviewer 4). No bias correction is applied to \(m_2\), though it may not matter significantly (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing related work**: The connection to Zhang et al. (2022) on Adam convergence for various \(\beta_1\) should be discussed, as it provides a theoretical foundation that playing with \(\beta_1\) is safe (Reviewer 1). Also, AggMo should be more thoroughly compared (Reviewer 4).
- **Figures need improvement**: Inconsistent colors, long captions, and three unrelated subplots make figures hard to interpret (Reviewer 5).
