## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Progressive Knowledge Distillation (PKD), a method for teacher-student pairs with heterogeneous architectures. It groups layers into modules using CKA (centered kernel alignment), then selects representative features via PCA and enforces alignment. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K. Two reviewers (R2, R3; scores 5) find the approach soundly motivated and competitive, while three reviewers (R1, R4, R5; scores 3) raise major concerns about clarity, novelty, complexity, and missing comparisons. Overall confidence is moderate (3–4).

### Strengths

1. **Novel components**: Using CKA to group layers into functional modules (instead of layer indices) and PCA to extract informative features for alignment is interesting and avoids misalignment across architectures (R1, R2, R4).
2. **Sound motivation for progressive training**: The shallow-to-deep sequential modular alignment acts as an implicit curriculum, matching the inherent learning hierarchy of deep networks (R2).
3. **Competitive empirical results**: PKD consistently outperforms selected baselines (e.g., OFA) across many teacher-student configurations on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K, with improvements up to 4.54% and 6.46% respectively (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
4. **Problem significance**: Feature alignment is a critical issue in hint-based knowledge distillation (R5).
5. **Clear experimental settings**: Datasets, architectures, and hyperparameters are well documented (R3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Poor presentation and unclear method details** – multiple weaknesses from R1, echoed by R3 and R5:
   - The main optimization problem is not explicitly stated. It is unclear whether Eq. (2) or Eq. (7) is the loss function; Eq. (2) lacks a teacher-student similarity term, while Eq. (7) optimizes a 0-1 vector *c*, not student weights. The relationship between the equations needs clarification.
   - The PCA process is described as selecting columns of the original data matrix *R_i*, but standard PCA transforms the data to a new basis (subset selection is not typical). A step-by-step explanation is required.
   - Eq. (3) defines *mv* as an average over *m* features, yet the text says to sort *mv*; a single value cannot be sorted. The meaning of `|X|` in Eq. (3) is also unspecified.
   - Eq. (7) is a discrete optimization problem (0-1 vector). The solution algorithm and any approximations/relaxations are not provided.
   - The distinction between *d_SM* and *d_DM* is unclear since they are computed identically; the paper should either clarify or remove it (R3).
   - Figure resolution is low; equation styles are inconsistent (R5).

2. **Insufficient novelty and limited literature review** – from R1, R3, R4, R5:
   - The progressive KD pipeline (shallow-to-deep) has been proposed before (e.g., [1] in R2; works listed by R3 and R4: ICCV 2021, ECML 2021, IJCAI 2018, NeurIPS 2020). The paper does not differentiate itself from these.
   - Using CKA in loss functions is not new [1,2] per R5; R3 lists multiple PCA-based KD works (CVPR 2022, arXiv:2312.08700) and cross-architecture KD works (ACCV 2022, arXiv:2010.02666, FG 2024) that are not discussed. Without clear differentiation, novelty is in question.
   - Compared to (Wang et al., 2024) – the major difference is unclear; the paper gives the false impression of being the first cross-architecture work (R1).

3. **Missing necessary ablations and comparisons** – from R1, R3, R4, R5:
   - Ablation on CKA-based modularization vs. simpler alternatives (e.g., dividing by feature map resolution) is needed to justify its overhead (R3).
   - Ablation on the initial training epochs for each module, the loss function, and threshold *ε_th* (R5).
   - Sensitivity analysis for PCA: number of input samples used for teacher PCA, variance threshold (10⁻⁴), and typical dimension reduction (from/to) for both teacher and student (R1).
   - Comparison against a simple approach: force student to have same number of modules as teacher and distill sequentially, to justify the optimal set selection via Eq. (7) (R1).
   - Complexity comparison with other baselines (e.g., training time, FLOPs, memory) to demonstrate scalability (R2, R4, R5).

4. **High complexity and scalability concerns** – from R2, R4, R5:
   - The pipeline requires multiple initial runs (epochs) to determine configurations, introducing extra overhead and hyperparameters (R5).
   - PCA and Gram matrix computation of deep features become a computational bottleneck as network width/image resolution increase, limiting practical scaling (R2).
   - The method is only evaluated on small/medium benchmarks (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1K); results on large-scale models (e.g., large language models) are not provided (R4).

5. **Unclear module correspondence selection** – from R1, R4:
   - How corresponding blocks/modules for the student are selected is not explained. Does this correspondence impact final results? An elaboration is needed (R4).
   - Why the shallowest module ensures the most representative features is not justified (R3, lines 56–57).

6. **Missing appendix** – the paper references an appendix but it was not found (R1).
