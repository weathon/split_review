## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces noise prompt learning: given a random noise and text prompt, a lightweight network (NPNet) transforms the noise into a "winning ticket" that improves generated image quality. The method includes a dataset (NPD) of 100k {noise, winning noise} pairs collected via DDIM inversion and preference filtering, and NPNet trained on this dataset. Reviewers generally found the idea intuitive and the dataset contribution valuable, but raised substantial concerns about the magnitude of improvements, architectural choices, missing comparisons, and limited evaluation of diversity.

### Strengths
- The concept of learning a "noise prompt" to obtain winning noise tickets is novel and intuitive. (R1, R2, R4)
- The paper compiles a large-scale dataset of 100k noise–winning noise pairs with associated prompts, which could serve as a resource for future research. (R3, R4)
- NPNet is designed as a small, plug-and-play module, adding only marginal inference overhead without modifying the original diffusion pipeline. (R3, R4)
- Extensive experiments are performed across multiple diffusion models (SDXL, DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo, Hunyuan-DiT) and a wide range of image quality metrics (PickScore, HPSv2, AES, ImageReward, CLIPScore, MPS, FID). (R2, R3, R5)
- Analysis of singular values of noise is interesting and the resulting noise prediction targets appear useful. (R1)
- The orthogonal experiment with DPO (Table 3) suggests noise manipulation can provide additional improvement beyond fine-tuning. (R5)
- The method shows improved text-image alignment across different models. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal quantitative improvements:** In most settings and metrics, the improvements over baselines (e.g., inversion) are negligible, raising questions about practical significance. (R1, R2, R3, R4) One reviewer (score 6) found the method compelling, while others (scores 3, 5, 5, 5) highlighted this as a core weakness.
- **Lack of diversity metrics:** The paper only reports FID on class-conditional ImageNet, which has few classes. Diversity metrics (FID on text-to-image benchmarks, precision/recall) are missing for foundation text-to-image models. There is concern that bias toward certain noises could lower diversity. (R5)
- **Missing comparisons with relevant methods:** Only InitNO (in appendix) and Repaint (outdated) are compared. No quantitative comparison against other noise optimization methods mentioned in related work (e.g., Attend-and-Excite, other DDIM-inversion baselines). The credibility of InitNO results is questioned because its reported text-image alignment is lower than that of StableDiffusion-v1-4, contrary to the original paper. (R4, R5)
- **NPNet architecture insufficiently justified:** The design is overly complex (SVD decomposition, separate residual predictor, singular value predictor) and no ablations show that a simpler network (e.g., a small DiT/UNet taking noise+text) or a plain MSE loss would not suffice. (R2, R4)
- **Small test set and limited analysis:** Test prompts are only 100–200 per dataset. In-depth analysis of how NPNet changes the noise distribution and whether the differences have semantic relevance to the text is missing. (R2)
- **Inconsistent conclusions:** Table 3 claims NPNet is orthogonal to DPO, but only one metric (ImageReward) supports this; the other three metrics do not. (R3)
- **Figure 2 (Stage 2) unclear:** The illustration suggests separate loss measurements for the two components, but the method combines them into one loss. (R5)
- **Positioning concerns:** One reviewer (R1) argues NPNet can be seen as a small-scale human-preference distilled version of the diffusion model focusing only on the first denoising steps, raising questions about its novelty.
- **Missing experimental details:** No details on CFG scale used in DDIM inversion, and no training of NPNet exclusively for Hunyuan-DiT is explained. (R2) Details on Repaint and Inversion baselines are also absent. (R5)
- **Lack of ablation on dataset collection:** It is unclear why DDIM inversion is used rather than simply sampling two random noises and picking the better one. (R4)
- **Transferability:** Questions remain about NPNet performance when transferring to a new model without any fine-tuning. (R1)
- **Related work only in appendix:** Repetition of text-image alignment results takes up main-paper space; related work should be moved to the main paper. (R5)
