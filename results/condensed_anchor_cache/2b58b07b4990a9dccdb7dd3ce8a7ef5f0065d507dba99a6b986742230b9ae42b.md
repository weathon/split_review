## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies grokking (delayed generalization) by modeling neural network training as a dynamical system. It proposes a hidden progress measure called Main Embedding Diff (MED) that correlates with grokking, and introduces a method to accelerate grokking by modifying the data embedding. The work is presented in two parts: a theoretical analysis using a reduced 3-D dynamical system, and an empirical demonstration of MED on modular addition tasks.

### Strengths
- The study of grokking is relevant and of interest to the ICLR audience.
- Viewing neural network gradient descent as a dynamical system is an interesting perspective (though not novel), and trying to relate generalization phenomena to properties of such a system is a creative approach.
- The proposed progress measure (MED) is potentially of interest to the field, and the empirical reduction of grokking (Figure 7) is promising.
- The paper offers a good formal description of the properties of the simple dynamical system (noted by Reviewer 3, though connection to the high-dimensional counterpart is insufficiently motivated).
- Reviewer 3 (rating 5) found the idea of studying grokking as emergent behavior from a dynamical system very interesting, stating that formal guarantees from such an analysis would greatly enhance theoretical understanding. (The other three reviewers rated the paper 1, citing severe clarity and quality issues.)

### Weaknesses
- The paper is very difficult to follow due to unclear language, many typos, and repetitive/uninformative figures. Multiple equations (1-3, 4-6, 7-9) leave components unexplained (e.g., what are W, U, x, e, y, gamma, s, c, u, w?). The embedding in Section 3 is unclear: Figure 2 mentions *p* symbols for the range 0 to *p*-1, but it is not specified whether *p* is the prime used in modular arithmetic, nor what *c*, *gamma*, and logit represent. The sums in Eqs. 4-5 are not explained.
- The theoretical part and the MED part appear disconnected. The paper does not establish a clear relationship between the dynamical system analysis and the proposed progress measure; MED is only shown to be vaguely correlated with grokking, without a precise link.
- Key assumptions (Assumption 1 and 2) are introduced without justification. The statement that $\alpha_d$ is "guessed to be approximately linear in specific situations" is vague. The properties mentioned below Theorem 1 (line 233) are not motivated as desirable for modeling.
- Theorem 4 is not stated mathematically and is vague; Reviewer 1 notes it "seems to contain no falsifiable statement," and Reviewer 4 adds that it is unclear what is gained or how it is proven.
- The progress measure (Eq. 12) depends on *n*, but *n* is not present in any of the preceding equations, making the definition of Pro 1 unclear.
- The paper only considers modular addition (a single task), while the original grokking work examined multiple problem types. It is unclear how generalizable the analysis is to other tasks, and why a 3-D reduced dynamical system is always sufficient (Reviewer 3).
- The mitigation of grokking (Figure 7) uses a Toeplitz/circulant matrix that exploits the periodicity of the task, which appears to rely on task-specific features rather than the dynamical systems framework, weakening the claimed connection.
- The rationale for increasing *p* instead of *β* in the dataset-size experiment is not explained; the bounds in Sec. 5.2.2 have *β* and *p* in the numerator, so increasing either increases norm, which seems contradictory to the intended effect on grokking time (Reviewer 4).
- The learning curves in Figures 3-7 show dramatic spikes not present in the original grokking work (Reviewer 2).
- The claim that *d_model* influences grokking rate is not connected to the presented results (Reviewer 4).
- Several sentences do not make grammatical sense or end abruptly, and there are numerous misspellings (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Definition 2 is not clear, and the statement "If the model have learnt" needs mathematical clarification (what does "learnt" mean?) (Reviewer 3).
