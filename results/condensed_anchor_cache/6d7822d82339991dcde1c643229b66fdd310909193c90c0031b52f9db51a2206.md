## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NPBML, a bilevel meta-learning framework that simultaneously learns task-adaptive initializations, optimizers (preconditioners), and loss functions, modulated by FiLM layers for task-specific adaptation. It combines these existing gradient-based meta-learning components into a single end-to-end system. Experiments on few-shot image classification benchmarks (miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CIFAR-FS, FC100) using 4-CONV and ResNet-12 backbones show that NPBML outperforms a range of baselines. Ablation studies confirm each component contributes to performance.

### Strengths
- The combination of meta-learned initializations, optimizers, and loss functions into a unified framework is novel and well-motivated. The framework is flexible, encompassing many prior gradient-based meta-learning approaches as special cases.
- In all experiments, NPBML achieves the best performance among compared methods. Ablations (Tables 3, 4) support the contribution of each component and are presented clearly.
- The paper is clearly written, with well-presented figures and tables. The ablation tables serve as a good example for the field.
- Two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 4 and 4) found the work sound and saw potential significance for meta-learning practitioners aiming for state-of-the-art performance.
- The experimental evaluation is thorough, with many baselines included and strong results across all tested benchmarks.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: Multiple reviewers (3 and 4, scores 3) considered the work an incremental combination of existing techniques with no new theoretical or algorithmic insights. One reviewer (1) highlighted the strong similarity to MT-nets (Lee et al., 2018), which also learns optimizers, initializations, and modulating functions (using binary masks instead of FiLM layers). A detailed comparison—including theoretical analysis of the differences between FiLM layers and binary masks—is requested. Extensions such as a theoretical result, new performance improvements, or demonstration of emergent phenomena are encouraged.
- **Risk of meta-overfitting and generalization**: The increased capacity of the meta-learner (learning initialization, optimizer, and loss) raises concerns about meta-overfitting to the training task distribution, which is not adequately addressed. The paper lacks (a) analysis of the meta-training loss vs. meta-testing performance to show no overfitting, (b) experiments measuring the generalization gap, (c) discussion of sensitivity to changes in task distribution, and (d) exploration of adaptive (meta-learned) regularization instead of manually tuned regularization. Contradicting the goal of automatic learning, manual regularization choices are introduced.
- **Computational cost and practical applicability**: The method is more complex and computationally expensive than alternatives. Comparisons of runtime, memory footprint, and number of parameters against baselines (MAML, ProtoNet, SimpleShot) are missing. One reviewer specifically requested a runtime analysis under the same hardware/software settings. Another noted the bilevel optimization nature adds significant overhead, making the practical advantage over cheaper methods (e.g., pretraining or in-context learning) unclear.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Several important baselines are omitted: (a) ProtoNet and SimpleShot, which are cheaper and could use larger backbones; (b) methods based on extended pretraining of the backbone and in-context learning / sequence modelling (e.g., Refs. [1,2]), which reportedly achieve stronger performance on these few-shot benchmarks with large-scale models. The paper does not adequately justify its baseline choices or discuss trade-offs with these alternative paradigms. It is also unclear whether the reported baseline numbers are reproductions or taken from original papers.
- **Limited benchmark and task scope**: The benchmarks used (miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CIFAR-FS, FC100) are considered outdated and may reward overfitting to the training task distribution rather than testing meta-generalization. All tasks are limited to 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot image classification, which does not reflect real-world scenarios such as segmentation, detection, super-resolution, translation, or text summarization. The paper does not validate the framework on larger domain shifts or more diverse task distributions. Scalability to larger convolutional networks (beyond 4-CONV and ResNet-12) or transformer architectures is not examined.
- **Manual setting of inner-loop steps**: The number of inner-loop updates is manually fixed at 5. No justification for this choice is given, and sensitivity analysis is absent. The paper claims an implicit learned early stopping mechanism but does not use a larger number of steps (e.g., 50, 100) to demonstrate it. A learned stopping criterion would strengthen the method.
- **Meta-learning rate and training budget**: One reviewer noted that the meta-learning rate η=0.00001 combined with only 30k meta-steps seems low, raising questions about how much meta-learning actually occurs. The performance of the model at initialization (η=0) without meta-training but keeping all other components equal was not reported, which would help contextualize the gains.

**Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (1,2) gave scores of 5, while two (3,4) gave scores of 3, reflecting a split on the paper’s novelty and significance. All had confidence 3–4. The more critical reviewers emphasized the incremental nature and missing comparisons/analyses, while the more positive ones valued the solid empirical improvements and clarity.
