## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates whether complementary knowledge exists between any pair of pretrained models (independent of overall performance) and how to transfer it without performance degradation. The authors find that standard knowledge distillation fails in this general setting and propose a continual-learning-based extension with data partitioning (DP) that splits data based on which model has higher confidence. Experiments on over four hundred ImageNet-trained models show consistent improvements, even when the teacher is weaker than the student.

### Strengths
- **Problem significance**: The paper addresses a practical gap – transferring knowledge between any two pretrained models, including from weaker to stronger, without performance loss. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Empirical evidence for complementary knowledge**: The paper provides grounded evidence that different models (varying architecture, size, training settings) encode unique, complementary information localized to particular classes. (R2, R4)
- **Consistent improvements over baselines**: The proposed method achieves consistent performance gains across many model pairings (e.g., Table 1). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Large-scale evaluation**: Experiments cover over 400 models on ImageNet, validating the claims and providing insights into model properties. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Sound and practical method**: The data-partitioning and continual-learning components are easy to implement and reasonable. (R3, R4)
- **Transfer from weaker teachers**: The method successfully transfers complementary knowledge even when the teacher is weaker than the student. (R3, R4)
- **Analysis of student model properties**: The paper studies how student characteristics (e.g., architecture, scale) affect transferability (e.g., Figure 8). (R4)
- **Clear writing**: The paper is easy to follow. (R1)
- **Two reviewers (R3, R4) rate the soundness and contribution as excellent (4/4); R2 rates them good (3/3).**

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal gains and causal attribution**: The reported improvements are often less than 1% on ImageNet (Table 1), and it is unclear whether the gains stem from the proposed transfer method or from uncontrolled training variations (architecture, augmentation, optimization). (R1 – minority more critical, score 5)
- **Key terms not clearly defined**: Terms such as “transfer delta Δ_transf”, “available complementary knowledge per class”, and “transfer rate” are not explicitly defined, hindering reproducibility. (R2)
- **Unclear motivation for continual-learning framing**: The regularization techniques (weight constraints, replay) are presented as a continual-learning solution, but the connection to continual learning is not well justified. The components could be viewed as generic distillation tricks. (R2, R3) R2 further questions whether any multi-step training is necessarily continual learning.
- **Missing detailed breakdown of accuracy changes**: The paper does not report, e.g., (a) the percentage of [teacher correct, student wrong] samples that become correct after distillation, or (b) the percentage of [teacher incorrect, student correct] samples that become incorrect. Such analysis would clarify whether the transfer is truly complementary. (R2)
- **Need for oracle/ensemble comparison**: To contextualize the small absolute gains, the authors should compare with an oracle ensemble of the teacher and student (or similar upper bound). (R2)
- **Limited scope of student models**: The experiments focus on relatively strong architectures (ResNets, Transformers). A systematic study of very low-capacity or randomly initialized models is missing, limiting generalizability to resource-constrained scenarios. (R3)
- **Lack of ablation on data partitioning**: The individual contribution of the data-partitioning heuristic is not isolated. Its interaction with the KL-divergence loss and the replay mechanism is unclear. Ablation studies (e.g., different partitioning strategies) are needed. (R3, R4) R4 additionally notes that the heuristic may maintain overconfidence on mislabeled or hard examples.
- **Sequential transfer training budget unclear**: In Table 4, it is not stated whether each sequential transfer step uses 20 epochs, and whether the total training budget is equivalent to the single-teacher setting. If sequential transfer uses multiple 20-epoch steps, the total budget is larger, skewing the comparison. (R1)
- **Unexplained architecture-specific trends**: The finding that CNN student models improve with scale while transformer student models worsen (Figure 8) is not explained. (R4)
- **Unsupervised DP outperforming supervised DP**: In Table 3, unsupervised data partitioning sometimes outperforms supervised DP. The authors offer no intuition for why distilling from the teacher when the teacher is both confident and incorrect would be beneficial. (R4)
- **Minor**: Inconsistent use of italics for “KL+DP” throughout the paper. (R3)
