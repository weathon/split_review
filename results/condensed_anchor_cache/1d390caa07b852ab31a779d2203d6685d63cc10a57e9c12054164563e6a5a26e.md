## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a simple prompting method, RE2 (Re‑Reading the question), that repeats the question before generating the answer to enhance reasoning in decoder‑only LLMs. Reviewers generally agree the idea is simple and interesting, and that the presentation is clear. Experiments cover 14 reasoning datasets, 112 experiments, and several models (ChatGPT, davinci‑003, Llama‑2). However, reviewers strongly disagree on the breadth and rigor of these experiments: one finds them “comprehensive” (R3, score 8) while another finds them “limited” (R1, score 5) because only vanilla CoT and few backbones are compared. All reviewers note that gains are often small (many around 2%) and inconsistent, raising questions about noise, scaling, and the method’s theoretical justification.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and adoptability**: The RE2 prompt is extremely simple and can be easily used by the research community (R1, R3).
- **Clear and well‑organized presentation**: The paper is easy to follow (R1, R2).
- **Comprehensive coverage of datasets, models, baselines, and ablations** (R2, R3): Evaluations span arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning; multiple LLMs; comparisons with vanilla, CoT, PS, PAL, and self‑consistency; ablations on repetition times, question complexity, and re‑reading instructions. Reviewer R3 notes this as a major strength, while R1 considers the experimental scope insufficient.
- **Reasonable motivation**: Enabling back‑and‑forth interaction in decoder‑only LLMs is a plausible goal (R2).
- **Mostly positive results across tasks and models**: With few exceptions, RE2 improves reasoning performance (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Inconsistent gains and potential noise**: Many improvements are only about 2%, and the paper does not provide standard deviations to assess significance (R3). On ChatGPT, RE2 shows random behavior (sometimes better, sometimes worse) (R2, R3), and on ARC tasks it shows negative or neutral results in both vanilla and CoT settings (R3). Table 1 and 2 indicate that RE2 improves davinci‑003 almost always but is less reliable on ChatGPT‑vanilla (R2). Figure 2 shows RE2 hurts performance on low‑complexity questions (difficulty ≤3) in GSM, yet other arithmetic benchmarks (mostly low complexity) are improved – a contradiction that is not explained (R2).
- **No justification for the claimed mechanism**: The claim that RE2 enables “bidirectional” attention or deeper understanding is misleading and unsupported by evidence (R2, R3). For example, re‑reading 3 times is not better than 2 times (R3). The connection to human re‑reading is a casual, unsubstantiated argument (R2).
- **Limited generalizability beyond QA**: RE2 requires an explicit question, making it unsuitable for non‑QA scenarios like multi‑round conversations, whereas methods like CoT operate on the solving stage and are more general (R2). The paper does not evaluate beyond reasoning tasks (R3).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**: Only vanilla CoT and a few backbones (ChatGPT, davinci‑003, Llama‑2) are compared; many newer CoT variants and other LLMs are omitted (R1). The division of LLMs across reasoning tasks (ChatGPT/davinci‑003 for commonsense/symbolic, Llama‑2 for arithmetic) is not justified; competing methods should be compared on the same tasks with all models (R1).
- **Scaling behavior concerns**: Gains are more pronounced in weaker models (davinci‑003 vs ChatGPT, Llama‑2‑13B vs 70B), raising questions about whether RE2 works better for less capable models and how it scales (R3).
- **Failure case in symbolic reasoning**: Across most datasets RE2 improves vanilla more than CoT, but in symbolic reasoning the opposite holds – no interpretation is provided (R2).
