## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SeMDiff, a diffusion-based model with semantic memory for image-to-long-text generation (I2LTG). It first predicts semantic concepts from images via a Semantic Concept Predictor, then enhances them through a Semantic Conditional Memory module, and finally feeds the memory responses into a diffusion decoder to generate long text. Experiments are conducted on three public datasets and a new COCO-LT dataset (54K instances). Results show improvements over previous state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths
1. The problem of long-text generation from images is interesting and important for real-world applications (Reviewer 1, 2). The proposed approach of predicting semantic concepts as an intermediate representation is unique and intuitively sound (Reviewer 4).
2. The overall method is technically sound, with clear components described in a well-organized paper that includes helpful figures (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). The ablation studies comprehensively validate the contribution of each module (Reviewer 3).
3. The new COCO-LT dataset is a reasonable and potentially useful resource for the community (Reviewer 1).
4. Experimental results are good and well-validated, with comparisons to SoTAs and detailed hyperparameter settings to aid reproducibility (Reviewer 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear initialization and training of key components** (Reviewers 1, 3):
   - The semantic vectors in the initialized matrix (Sec.2.2) are not specified: are they pretrained embeddings from a language model or randomly initialized? Is this matrix fixed during training or sample-specific? (Reviewer 1)
   - The description of the Semantic Conditional Memory (Sec.2.3) is ambiguous: what specific information is stored, how is it obtained, and how are memory vectors updated? (Reviewer 1)
   - The variable **n** in Eq.(12) is not defined (noise vector or learned parameter?). (Reviewer 3)
   - The cross-entropy loss **L_CE** is not formally introduced; which probability distributions are compared and how it relates to the module outputs is unclear. (Reviewer 3)
   - In Figures 15/16, **ĥ₀** is shown as a product of probabilities, but a conditional probability **p(ĥ₀|hˢ, h)** would be more standard. (Reviewer 2)

2. **Insufficient evaluation on short captions and missing standard metrics** (Reviewer 1):
   - No tests on short caption benchmarks (e.g., MS-COCO), limiting comparison with a wider range of existing methods.
   - The CIDEr metric, which is standard for image captioning, is not reported. This omission makes comparison with other image captioning models harder.

3. **Unfair or inconclusive comparisons** (Reviewers 1, 2):
   - In Tables 2 and 3, only SeMDiff uses a transformer backbone; all competitors use ResNet-101. Comparisons should include other transformer-based methods (e.g., [1] a diffusion-based captioning model) to provide a fair assessment. (Reviewer 1)
   - The improvements over SoTAs in Table 3 are small (e.g., BLEU-1: 0.412 vs 0.407; BLEU-4: 0.129 vs 0.126), making it hard to judge significance. The authors could adapt their proposed modules to a SoTA method to prove effectiveness. (Reviewer 2)
   - In Table 2, the simple transformer baseline ("Trans") already achieves strong results on LN, COCO-LT, and CC-SBU, eroding the apparent contribution of the memory and diffusion components. This raises the question of whether the proposed methods are necessary for those datasets. (Reviewer 2)

4. **Potential data leakage and ambiguous settings for LLM baselines** (Reviewer 2, 4):
   - It is unclear whether the semantic concepts are extracted from the training set only, or whether the validation/test set was used (which would constitute cheating). (Reviewer 4)
   - For Table 4, it is not specified whether the results of existing SoTAs (including LLMs like LLaVA) are zero-shot or fine-tuned, nor whether SeMDiff uses the same setting. A 6-layer transformer encoder-decoder achieving 0.054 BLEU-4 on COCO-LT while a finetuned LLaVA only reaches 0.06 is surprising and needs explanation. (Reviewer 2)

5. **Missing analyses on diffusion model properties and computational cost** (Reviewer 3):
   - The effect of diffusion across timesteps is only shown qualitatively. Provide quantitative metrics (e.g., BLEU) at different timesteps to measure the impact.
   - The known property of diffusion models—guidance to improve correlation between generation and condition—is not studied. How does semantic guidance influence long-text quality and coherence?
   - The computational cost of diffusion (thousands of NFE) is not compared with baselines. An analysis of overhead is needed.
   - The diffusion schedule and sampling method (DDIM/ODE or DDPM/SDE) are not specified. These choices significantly affect performance and efficiency.

6. **Naming inconsistency and unexplored hyperparameters** (Reviewer 2, 4):
   - The module is called "Semantic Conditional Memory," yet the word "conditional" is never used in its description (Sec.2.3). The rationale for the name is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
   - The size of the semantic concept set and the number of predicted concepts are not explored. Are larger sets always better? Does predicting more concepts always help long captioning? (Reviewer 2)

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** All four reviewers gave a rating of 5 (borderline reject), but one reviewer assessed soundness as “fair (2)”, while the other three rated it “good (3)”, indicating that at least one reviewer found the technical foundation less convincing.
