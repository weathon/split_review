## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SynthCLIP, a CLIP model trained entirely on synthetic image-text pairs generated by text-to-image diffusion models and LLMs. It introduces SynthCI-30M, a 30-million-sample purely synthetic dataset, and studies scaling trends, design choices (LLM, prompt, concept distribution), and downstream performance. The work is seen as a full departure to synthetic data for both modalities, with extensive benchmarks and ablations. However, several reviewers question the motivation, empirical evidence of advantages over real data, data efficiency, and fairness of comparisons. One reviewer (rating 3) is markedly more negative, citing limited novelty, insufficient empirical demonstration of the core claim, and problematic comparison methodology; the other three (ratings 5,5,6) see merit but identify significant gaps in scaling, analysis, and breadth of evaluation.

### Strengths

- Provides SynthCI-30M, a 30M synthetic dataset of captioned images from diverse concepts.
- Pipeline design is sound, follows community norms, and ablated: LLM selection, prompt strategy, concept distribution, and the impact of each synthetic modality (Figure 4a).
- Approach is noteworthy as a full departure to synthetic data, whereas most prior work requires at least one real modality.
- Benchmarks are extensive and representative, covering diverse datasets and tasks (zero-shot classification, retrieval, etc.).
- Ablations yield interesting insights: the concept distribution study supports the hypothesis that performance differences between real and synthetic data are partly due to conceptual distribution, not just quality; experiments on long-tail mitigation suggest directions for improving unseen/undersampled concepts in real training.
- Synthetic data enables control over the concept distribution and collection at scale without human intervention; because concepts are fixed, subsets can be selected with reduced NSFW concerns compared to unfiltered real data.
- Models trained at similar scales (using synthetic) show similar performance on downstream tasks.

### Weaknesses

- Motivation is insufficiently argued. The introduction does not convincingly answer why purely synthetic data is needed, why a hybrid approach is not preferred, or why the problem is significant.
- Core empirical claim – that synthetic data mitigates the quality-cost trade-off in scaling real datasets – is not convincingly demonstrated. The paper only compares against small real datasets (CC3M, CC12M) with a 30M synthetic set; outperforming CC12M does not prove a quality advantage. Moreover, at matched size (10M), the synthetic model performs *worse* than a real-data counterpart, contradicting the claim.
- Novelty is limited: the framework builds on existing models and approaches; the concept bank construction is taken from prior work (reviewer 4, rating 3).
- Data efficiency: synthetic samples require longer training to match real data performance. Practical adoption is unclear when abundant real data exists (e.g., LAION-5B, DataComp). The gap in data efficiency is only partially explained by concept distribution; the cause of the remaining performance deficit is not addressed (possible lack of real-world nuances, biases in generative models).
- Scalability is not convincingly demonstrated. Prior work (e.g., M3AE) shows that certain pretraining augmentations work at small scale but not at large scale; the paper lacks evidence that synthetic gains hold at scale.
- Fairness of comparisons: models are trained for a fixed number of epochs and batch size, so different dataset sizes result in different iteration counts, impairing validity of comparisons across scales.
- Evaluation depth: zero-shot classification results are reported only on standard ImageNet and few others; missing evaluations on ImageNet variants (ImageNetV2, -A, -R, ObjectNet), and on multimodal LLM understanding tasks (MLLM). Reviewers requested these experiments.
- Only compared against CC3M and CC12M; no comparison with a subset of LAION-400M to test against larger real data.
- Synthetic image quality concerns: the pipeline filters captions but not generated images, so images may be unfaithful to the intended concept or contain NSFW content (since Stable Diffusion was trained on LAION, which contains NSFW). No workarounds are proposed.
- The argument that synthetic data can augment tail classes is weakened because Stable Diffusion itself is trained on long-tailed real data, so its performance on rare classes may be poor.
- Captions are oriented to single objects; this may harm performance on tasks requiring fine-grained or relational understanding (e.g., text within images, object relationships), where current TTI models are limited.
- Questions about MTL metric (how is it calculated? what is it averaging over?) and about comparing synthetic vs. natural distributions: e.g., using CC3M’s concept distribution to generate synthetic data, or converting CC3M images into synthetic pairs. Would help diagnose the gap.
- Request to report scaling coefficients (log-log) for synthetic vs. natural data, and to evaluate effective robustness (e.g., under distribution shift as in Nguyen et al. 2023).
- “Human intervention” argument is weak: real internet data is already curated automatically via alt-text, with automatic filtering (CLIP score) and recaptioning methods available; thus high-quality real data can be had without human intervention, undercutting the claimed advantage of synthetic generation.
- Why generate images at all instead of recaptioning freely available internet images? (Figure 4 suggests synthetic images may be inferior to recaptioned real ones; the paper does not justify the extra cost of image generation.)
