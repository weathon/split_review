## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates scaling inference compute via repeated sampling from LLMs. Reviewers agree the problem is well-motivated and the study is systematic across multiple models (Gemma, Pythia, Llama) and tasks (coding, math, SWE-bench, GSM8K, MATH). With oracle verifiers, coverage improves log-linearly with samples (e.g., DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct on SWE-bench Lite: 15.9% at one sample → 56% at 250 samples, outperforming single-sample state-of-the-art 43%). Without oracle verifiers, performance plateaus due to verifier inaccuracy. Reviewers disagree on novelty and depth: one reviewer (score 3) finds the main claim trivial and previously known; others (scores 6 and 5) value the systematic quantification but note important limitations.

### Strengths
- **Extensive empirical analysis** across diverse models and datasets, showing robust scaling trends over four orders of magnitude in samples (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Covers coding (HumanEval, SWE-bench Lite), math (GSM8K, MATH), and formal proofs.
- **Practical cost-effectiveness**: Repeated sampling from a cheaper model (DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct) with five samples is more cost-effective and solves more issues than one sample from GPT-4o or Claude 3.5 Sonnet under current API pricing (Reviewer 1).
- **Interesting scaling-law parameterization**: Model family affects only the offset, not the slope, of the log-linear coverage plot – a contrast with pre-training scaling laws (Reviewer 1).
- **Figures 6 and 7 compellingly show** that failures of test-time methods (majority voting, reward models) are due to verifier inaccuracy, not lack of coverage (Reviewer 1).
- **Precise quantification** of the scaling relation, with good diversity in tasks from agentic (SWE-bench) to standard reasoning (Reviewer 2).
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Main claim considered trivial by some reviewers**: One reviewer (score 3) and partly Reviewer 3 argue that the improvement from repeated sampling is a well-known consequence of pass@k monotonicity, previously observed in AlphaCode (Li et al. 2022) and mathematically simple (independent Bernoulli sampling per problem). The scaling-law curve-fitting is applied to a formula already known (equation 1: pass@k = 1 – (1 – C_i)^k), so the insight is limited (Reviewer 3, 4). Other reviewers note the systematic quantification adds value (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Novelty concerns**: Similar scaling figures appear in prior work (AlphaCode, GPT-4 technical report) – the paper should more clearly delineate what is new (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical usefulness of the fitted scaling laws is unclear**: The laws are task- and model-specific, cannot extrapolate to new budgets, and are cheap to estimate, unlike pre-training scaling laws (Reviewer 3).
- **Weak verifier analysis**: Only one off-the-shelf 8B reward model (chosen for RewardBench reasoning, but applied to math) is used; conclusions about verifier saturation are too strong. Experiments with verifiers of varying sizes and process supervision (Lightman et al. “Let’s Verify Step by Step”) are needed (Reviewers 3, 4). The paper does not characterize why reward models fail or examine explicit failure examples (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons to alternative test-time compute methods**: Beam search against a verifier (Snell et al. 2024, Setlur et al. 2024) is not discussed or compared (Reviewer 1). The baseline for comparing sampling versus chain-of-thought variants is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Does not examine effects of sampling parameters** like temperature or top-K (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing parameterization of scaling law by model size** (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Lacks deeper analysis of how repeated sampling fixes reasoning traces**: Would benefit from examples showing which part of the reasoning trace gets corrected and characterization of common mistakes (Reviewer 2).
- **Writing clarity issues**: The experimental setup with/without verifier is confusing, especially how the verifier is integrated (iid vs. sequential). Needs more justification for methodological choices (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor**: Line 394: \(k\) should be italic (Reviewer 4).
