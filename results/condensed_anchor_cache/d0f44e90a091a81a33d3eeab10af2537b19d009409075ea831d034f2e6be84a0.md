## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the forgetting issue in sequential LLM post-training (SFT followed by DPO). It provides theoretical proof of sequential suboptimality and proposes two joint training algorithms, ALRIGHT (stochastic linear scalarization) and MAXRIGHT (Chebyshev scalarization), that alternate between objectives to retain balance. Empirical tests on LLAMA-3 and PYTHIA-1B claim up to a 31% higher win rate with minimal additional computational cost.

### Strengths
- **Keen observation on forgetting in LLM post-training:** The paper demonstrates and theoretically proves the suboptimality of the sequential SFT→DPO training pipeline, which is the industrial standard but has received little prior joint analysis (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Well-organized and clear motivation:** The paper is logically developed with a clear problem statement (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty of problem framing:** The treatment of SFT and DPO as a multi-objective optimization problem that reaches Pareto optimal solutions is original (Reviewer 3). (Note: Reviewers 1 and 4 disagree on novelty—see Weaknesses.)
- **Theoretical contributions:** Proofs (Theorem 3.1, 4.1) appear correct and provide convergence guarantees for ALRIGHT; the analysis deepens understanding of labeling logic mismatch between SFT data and human preferences (Reviewer 3).
- **Explanation of computational motivation:** The paper explains why a direct linear combination (“vanilla” scalarization) is computationally prohibitive and introduces the stochastic equivalent (ALRIGHT) and the Chebyshev-based method (MAXRIGHT) as practical alternatives (Reviewer 1).
- **Efficiency and practicality:** The proposed methods require little increase in runtime and GPU utilization, making them practical for LLM post-training (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Empirical improvements:** Experiments on LLAMA-3 and PYTHIA-1B show the methods achieve lower loss and improved win rates (up to 31%) compared to sequential training (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical explanation:** The paper tries to explain its suggestions theoretically (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty in method:** ALRIGHT is simply the stochastic version of linear scalarization, and MAXRIGHT is basically the Chebyshev scalarization—both well-known in multi-objective optimization (MOO) literature (Reviewer 1). The paper does not differentiate itself clearly from existing one-stage methods that bypass sequential forgetting (e.g., SimPO, ORPO); the value proposition versus these approaches is not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **Questionable Pareto-optimality claim:** It is unclear whether the proposed methods actually prove Pareto optimality (Reviewer 4). The toy example in Figure 2 is problematic: the pure SFT and DPO solutions are expected to be Pareto optimal, yet they appear dominated by the “Average Opt. Point” (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing standard deviations:** Figure 1 reports no standard deviations, so the statistical significance of the empirical results cannot be assessed; confidence in the claims is reduced (Reviewer 4).
- **Poor figure clarity:** Figures 2, 3, and 4 are difficult to read. The gradual color change of training trajectories, inconsistent line/color patterns, and a legend that is too large and obscures details (Figure 2) hinder interpretation. Axis labels (e.g., what are the axes—empirical loss?) and trajectory granularity (per gradient update? per epoch? which seed?) are not clearly defined. The figures rely heavily on color and are not readable in grayscale (Reviewer 3, 4). In Figures 3 and 4, bar colors and the legend do not match (Reviewer 4).
- **Questionable assumptions in theory:** Theorem 3.1 assumes T_DPO = T_SFT = T, which may be too strong and misaligned with real-world training schedules (Reviewer 2).
- **Theory–experiment gap:** The theoretical modeling (e.g., line 215) differs from the practical LoRA-based implementation used in experiments, which may weaken the study’s contributions (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** The experiments only compare with sequential and mixed post-training settings. Other methods that balance RLHF and SFT, such as those mentioned in the related work, should be included to strengthen the contribution (Reviewer 2).
- **MAXRIGHT requires apriori knowledge of the ideal point f*:** This knowledge is needed for both SFT and DPO, but its computational cost is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **“Ideal Distance” is scale-dependent:** The ideal distance metric is not scale-invariant; scaling either objective arbitrarily biases the distance toward that objective, making the combination unfair (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear resolution of labeling logic conflicts:** It is not explained how random sampling or multi-objective learning resolves the inconsistency in labeling logic between SFT data and DPO data (Reviewer 3).
- **Optimal proportion of SFT vs DPO steps:** The paper does not investigate what proportion of time for SFT versus DPO would be most efficient (Reviewer 3).
- **All Pareto solutions may not be equivalent from a user perspective:** Since LLM performance is judged by metrics beyond SFT and DPO objectives, it is unclear whether every point on the Pareto front is equally desirable (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation for joint optimization of SFT and DPO:** Why is it necessary to find a point that optimizes both objectives? If DPO optimization harms SFT, that indicates SFT failed to align with human preferences—optimizing DPO alone might suffice. The paper does not justify the need for recovering SFT performance (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation inconsistency:** The definitions of D_RLHF and D_DPO should be unified for clarity (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues:** Theorem 4.1’s left-hand side is similar to Theorem 3.1, but it is unclear what has changed; a modification is suggested to avoid confusion (Reviewer 3).
