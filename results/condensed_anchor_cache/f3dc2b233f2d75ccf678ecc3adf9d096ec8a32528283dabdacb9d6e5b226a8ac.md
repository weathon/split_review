## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a method to efficiently compute summaries of higher-order derivatives (Hessian and third-order tensor) by partitioning the parameter space into groups (e.g., layers) and applying tensor contractions (e.g., with the all-ones vector or gradient direction), yielding a reduced \(S^d\)-sized tensor. These summaries capture inter-layer interactions often ignored by block-diagonal approximations. The authors use them to derive a layerwise learning rate scheme interpolating between Newton and Cauchy steepest descent, as well as a cubic-regularized optimization method. Experiments on CIFAR show mixed results, with the method underperforming K-FAC.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated idea**: Addressing the neglect of inter-layer interactions in most second-order methods is a significant gap; summarizing the Hessian via contractions is a computationally tractable approach (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Principled learning rates**: Deriving layerwise learning rates from partitioned summaries is a neat extension of Cauchy’s steepest descent (Reviewer 1).
- **Broader potential**: The partitioning scheme is the main contribution and could be useful beyond optimization, e.g., for studying feature learning across layers or interpreting neural network training dynamics (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Thorough discussion**: Related work is covered comprehensively, and the paper thoughtfully considers limitations of Newton’s method (Reviewer 2).
- **Evidence of non-trivial inter-layer interactions**: The experiments provide some evidence that cross-layer Hessian blocks contain meaningful information (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Weak experimental validation**: Experiments are limited to small datasets (CIFAR) where no method reaches typical 90%+ accuracy. The proposed method’s accuracy is worse than K-FAC, and results are mixed. This may reduce community interest without more thorough evaluation (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 1 suggests transformers / language modeling may be a more suitable testbed (Hessians are less homogeneous), and comparisons with Adam-Mini [1] are needed. Reviewer 3 argues the optimization method should not be the focus; instead the paper should emphasize the partitioning/computation method and extend Section 5.1 with real-world or solvable examples.
- **Missing ablations and baselines**: Reviewer 1 requests an ablation using diag(Ĥ) for layerwise learning rates to isolate the importance of cross-layer information. More baselines are needed: Cauchy step size, AdaHessian, block-diagonal quasi-Newton, and other recent second-order methods. Reviewer 2 notes the method only searches in the gradient direction per layer (scalar multiples), missing Shampoo-style direction changes; this restricts the subspace and likely limits performance relative to preconditioning methods.
- **Computational overhead**: The method requires multiple Hessian-vector products, and a wall-clock comparison is missing (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 estimates complexity as \(t \cdot p \cdot S\) vs. \(t \cdot p\), making it substantially slower for deep networks.
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper is poorly written. Equations on page 5 are unnumbered. The tensor contraction notation \(A[u,u,\ldots,u]\) is non-standard and undefined; it must be inferred from context (Reviewer 3). There is a lack of literature survey on Hessian properties in deep networks and recent second-order works (Reviewer 3). Proofreading errors exist: “despite it does not go beyond” (line 90–91) and “so its preserves” (line 76) (Reviewers 2, 3). The main contribution (fast computation of the reduced tensor) is not explained in sufficient detail in the main text, and even the appendix should be more explicit (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 suggests an alternative perspective (reparameterizing perturbations as scalar multiples of gradients) to make the presentation clearer, and also suggests a better title.
- **Technical concerns**: 
  - L43: Claim that Hessian singularity prevents inversion is questionable; one can use SVD pseudo-inverse on the nonsingular subspace (Reviewer 3).
  - L71: Why regularize instead of computing the pseudo-inverse? (Reviewer 3).
  - L218–220: The mapping direction of the d-derivative seems incorrect; \(u\) and brackets are undefined (Reviewer 3).
  - L258: The statement “Therefore, the tensors \(D_\theta^d(u)\) extract more information than naive Taylor terms” is not obvious; an explicit example for low \(d\) with a fixed number of parameters is needed (Reviewer 3).
  - Reviewer 1 questions whether cross-layer curvature correction is more important than within-layer correction, given that Hessian energy is often concentrated on diagonal blocks. Also asks whether the method could be relevant in sharpness-aware minimization (Reviewer 3’s suggestion).
- **Methodological limitation**: Searching only in the gradient direction per layer prevents the method from adapting the search direction using full curvature information; this is a significant limitation compared to methods like Shampoo that precondition the gradient (Reviewer 2).
