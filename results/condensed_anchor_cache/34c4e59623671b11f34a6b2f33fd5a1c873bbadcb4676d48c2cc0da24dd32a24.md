- Scores: 5, 3, 8, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper argues that grokking—a delayed drop in test loss long after train loss saturates—arises from a transition from lazy (kernel) to rich (feature learning) training dynamics. The authors propose a toy polynomial regression model (Gaussian data, two-layer net with quadratic activation, single-index target) that exhibits grokking without explicit regularization, and connect the phenomenon to misalignment between the initial NTK eigenvectors and the target function. Reviewers are divided: two (scores 8,8) find the idea clean, important, and experimentally convincing, while two (scores 5,3) consider the theoretical and empirical evidence limited, criticize the definition of grokking, and note that the core explanation is not entirely novel. A minority reviewer (score 3) is notably more negative, arguing the paper’s definition of grokking deviates from the original (Power et al., 2022) and that the explanation does not cover the originally observed phenomenon.

### Strengths
- **Relevance and novelty of the lazy-to-rich transition explanation.** The connection between grokking and the transition from kernel regime to feature learning is viewed as the right idea (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The focus on vanilla gradient descent (without label-noise SGD or weight decay) is considered a novel contribution compared to prior work (e.g., Li et al., 2021) that required explicit regularization or noise (Reviewer 2; also noted by Reviewer 3).
- **Toy model advances understanding.** The polynomial regression setup is simple enough to allow some theoretical decomposition of the loss into interpretable terms, and it demonstrates grokking without regularization, which existing theories cannot explain (Reviewers 1, 4). The model helps clarify that grokking is not inherently tied to increasing norm or weight decay (Reviewer 3).
- **Experiments on diverse tasks.** The paper tests the proposed mechanism on more realistic settings (MNIST with an MLP, arithmetic with a one-layer Transformer, student-teacher networks), lending credibility to the generalizability of the claim (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Presentation is clean.** Reviewer 4 rated presentation 4/4 (“excellent”), and Reviewer 3 found the paper clear and its point sufficiently important.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous theoretical analysis.** Reviewers 1 and 2 note that the paper provides no end-to-end theorem or proof of grokking. The explanation is only empirically verified, even for the simple quadratic model; a derivation of gradient flow trajectories is missing. Reviewer 1 explicitly asks: “Can the authors comment on the technical challenges in analyzing the gradient flow trajectory for this quadratic model?”
- **Definition of grokking is inconsistent with the original phenomenon.** Reviewer 2 strongly criticizes that the paper’s definition (“train loss decreases much earlier than test loss”) does not match the original grokking literature (Power et al., 2022), which requires that test loss/error remains at chance level until after train loss reaches near zero, and that perfect generalization eventually occurs. The paper’s examples often show test loss starting to decrease before train loss is low and final test loss remaining high (e.g., Figure 5(b)). Thus the explanation only applies to the paper’s relaxed definition, not the originally described behavior.
- **Insufficient connection to prior works.** Reviewer 1 points out that the relationship to Nichani et al. (2022) on NTK-to-feature-learning transition and improved sample complexity for low-degree + sparse high-degree polynomials is not discussed. Specifically, does sparsity of the quadratic component affect grokking? Similarly, the target function has information exponent 2 (Ben Arous et al., 2021); Reviewer 1 asks whether the approximate saddle caused by random initialization is related to the test loss plateau, and whether larger information exponents would yield similar results. The quantity \(y^\top K^{-1} y\) from the kernel literature (Arora et al., 2019) is also mentioned as relevant. Reviewer 2 notes that the explanation via lazy-to-rich transition was already known before the grokking paper (e.g., the quadratically overparametrized linear model of Li et al., 2021) and the current theoretical result is less clean than that prior work.
- **Theoretical evidence is thin.** Reviewer 4 finds the theoretical results “very limited (just a decomposition of the loss into three terms with relevant interpretations).” The paper relies heavily on the simple toy model, with only three experiments on more complex data. Reviewer 4 also questions whether a necessary condition for grokking is that the task must be “well adapted to the rich regime,” which is harder to characterize.
- **Sample complexity gap vs. timescale separation.** Reviewer 2 struggles to understand how the claim that a kernel method can learn \(|x|^2\) at \(D\) samples and the full target at \(D^2\) samples relates to the separation of timescales in a single training run.
- **Role of weight decay not adequately discussed.** Reviewer 3 notes that weight decay changes the NTK at speed \(\kappa\) and that if \(1/\kappa\) is larger than the lazy training timescale, a separation of timescales arises—this point should be explicitly discussed as it constructively supports the paper’s argument.
- **Potential alternative cause for failure of grokking with small eigenvalues.** In Section 6.1, grokking disappears when using too small eigenvalues. The paper attributes this to insufficient data, but Reviewer 4 asks: could it also be because the corresponding eigenfunctions are hard to learn regardless of regime? Was this hypothesis tested?
- **Minor citation issue.** Reviewer 1 notes that the cited references on quadratic sample complexity lower bounds for kernel estimators (e.g., Ghorbani et al., 2019) do not rigorously cover the Gaussian data case.
- **First paragraph introduction may be misleading.** Reviewer 2 objects to the statement that “one assumes that a model’s performance on its training set will be a reliable indicator of its generalization capabilities,” arguing this is generally false in overparametrized deep learning. The claim about safety issues from grokking is also not justified.
