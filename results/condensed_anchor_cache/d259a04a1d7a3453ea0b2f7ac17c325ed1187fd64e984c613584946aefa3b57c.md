## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces EVOPROMPT, a framework that combines evolutionary algorithms (EAs) with large language models (LLMs) to automatically optimize discrete, human-readable prompts. EVOPROMPT uses the LLM itself to perform mutation and crossover operations on a population of prompts, iteratively evolving them to maximize task performance without gradients or parameters. The method is instantiated with Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Differential Evolution (DE) variants and evaluated on 31 datasets covering language understanding, generation, and BIG-Bench Hard tasks, using both closed-source (GPT-3.5) and open-source (Alpaca) LLMs. Results show significant improvements over human-engineered prompts and existing methods (e.g., up to 25% on BBH).

### Strengths
- **Novel and powerful idea**: Using an LLM to perform EA mutation/crossover ensures generated prompts remain coherent and readable, solving a key challenge in applying EAs to discrete text. This combination is innovative and inspiring for future LLM+optimization research (R2, R3, R4).
- **Well-written and clearly presented**: The paper is well organized, with a clear motivation, thorough literature review, and clean figures (Figures 1 and 2) that effectively illustrate the method (R1, R2, R3, R4). Useful examples and LLM templates are provided (R3).
- **Extensive and rigorous experiments**: The method is tested on 31 diverse datasets with multiple baselines, on both proprietary (GPT-3.5) and open-source (Alpaca) models, and with two EA variants (GA and DE). This solidifies the empirical results (R2, R3, R4). The ablation studies on mutation strategies and initial population are commendable (R3).
- **Sound methodology**: The approach is well-motivated, leveraging EA advantages for discrete optimization while using the LLM to overcome the standard operator issue of losing coherence. The black-box nature suits API-only models (R2).

**Minority positive view**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) particularly praises the LLM-based evolutionary operator as "very powerful" and finds the experimental setup convincing, while the other three reviewers score 6.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and significance for ICLR**: The framework relies on established EA algorithms (GA, DE) without introducing novel algorithmic contributions. Reviewer 1 finds the approach "uninspired" and not a substantial advance, suggesting it would be better suited for venues like IEEE CEC or PPSN. Reviewer 2 similarly doubts the relevance to ICLR, suggesting GECCO could be more appropriate, and questions the long-term interest of prompt optimization as LLMs become more robust to prompt phrasing (R1, R2).
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: The paper lacks a thorough study of the runtime, financial cost, and resource requirements for the optimization process. Reviewer 2 requests such an analysis in the main paper, including comparisons with baselines. Reviewer 3 also asks for a breakdown of time for prompt generation, evaluation, and EA operations, plus details on the number of LLM calls and whether iterations occur within a single session (R2, R3).
- **Insufficient analysis of EA behavior and diversity**: 
  - Reviewer 3 asks for a deeper analysis of why DE outperforms GA, e.g., by examining the distribution of prompt lengths and vocabulary changes across iterations.
  - The diversity of the evolved prompts should be quantified (semantic similarity, novelty relative to the initial population) to assess whether the search truly explores diverse linguistic space or converges to a narrow region (R3).
  - Convergence is unclear in Figure 6; more iterations should be shown and it should be specified whether the 10 iterations are within one LLM session or across separate resets (R3).
  - Reviewer 1 questions why only DE and GA were chosen, why only one offspring generation strategy for DE was used, and how infeasible offspring are repaired and how that affects timing complexity (R1).
  - Reviewer 4 requests more detailed implementation explanations for each EA variant, including specific steps and how the LLM is prompted to generate candidates. More ablation studies on population sizes, mutation rates, and selection strategies are needed to understand robustness and generalizability (R4).
- **Dependence on initial prompt quality**: Performance is influenced by the quality and composition of the initial prompt population (R3). The paper should analyze the ratio of manually crafted vs. LLM-generated initial prompts, especially when DE works better than GA, and what happens if all prompts are LLM-generated (R3, question 1). The paper also does not discuss negative cases or tasks requiring complex reasoning where EVOPROMPT might struggle (R3).
- **Additional requested analyses**: 
  - Whether combining GA and DE updates during search could be beneficial (R3, question 5).
  - How effectively the evolutionary operators generate novel prompts vs. repeating the same words (R3, question 3).
  - The effect of different population sizes, mutation rates, and selection strategies (R4, question). These requests overlap with the missing ablation studies.
