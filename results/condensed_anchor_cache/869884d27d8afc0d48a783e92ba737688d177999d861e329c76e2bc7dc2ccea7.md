## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses continual learning under noisy labels (CLN) by proposing Alternate Experience Replay (AER) and Asymmetric Balanced Sampling (ABS). AER leverages the observation that noisy or complex samples are more prone to being forgotten, using this to separate clean, complex, and noisy samples in the buffer. ABS selects low-loss (clean) samples from the current task and high-loss (informative) samples from past tasks to balance purity and diversity. Supplementary techniques include consolidation and MixMatch. Experiments on four datasets (three synthetic, one natural-noise: WebVision) with two noise processes show an average 4.71% accuracy gain over existing purification strategies. Reviewers generally agree the problem is important and the method is clearly motivated; however, concerns about novelty, performance on real-world data, limited scale, and missing ablations are raised.

### Strengths
- The problem of continual learning under noisy labels is vital for real-world scenarios (R1, R4, but R4 notes the setting is controversial).
- The intuition and each component of the algorithm are well motivated and easy to understand (R2, R3). The use of “forgetting” as a selection signal is clearly presented.
- Experiments are comprehensive for synthetic datasets: 4 datasets, two noise types, comparison with multiple CL and noisy-label methods, and significant improvements reported (R1, R3).
- A thorough analysis of the algorithm’s behavior is provided (R2 – though R2 later questions some aspects).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and technical contribution** (R3, R4): The combination of existing replay mechanisms (experience replay, loss-based clean sample selection, MixMatch) is considered a direct combination with no essential methodological innovation. The “forgetting” observation for noisy labels is already known from prior work (R4). ABS is seen as a simple threshold-based selection strategy (R3).
- **Performance on real-world / natural noise** (R2, R4): On WebVision (natural noise), AER+ABS does not outperform PuriDivER; only after adding consolidation and MixMatch (from other papers) does it marginally improve. Only 10 out of 1000 WebVision classes are used, with unclear selection criteria. The method appears effective mainly on synthetic symmetric noise (R2). This limits applicability to realistic CL scenarios.
- **Ablation and comparison gaps** (R2, R3):
  - No clear ablation showing individual contributions of AER vs. ABS.
  - Missing comparisons: same ABS + AER vs. same ABS + DivideMix; same AER + ABS vs. same AER + PuriDivER sampling; report DivideMix results on Joint (R4).
  - Not compared to PuriDivER applied to DER++ (R2).
  - The consolidation phase of PuriDivER is not sufficiently explained given it is a baseline (R2).
- **Setting and experimental scope** (R2, R3, R4):
  - It is unclear whether the setting is offline or online CL – a crucial distinction that affects training iterations (R2).
  - PuriDivER is designed for online CL and blurry tasks; applying it to offline CL may not be appropriate (R3). AER should also be evaluated under online CL (R3).
  - Experiments are limited to small datasets (CIFAR-10/100); on 50k data joint learning may be more appropriate (R4). Results in Table 1 are unimpressive (R4) and Table 2 results are inconclusive for solid verification (R4).
  - CIFAR-10 asymmetric label noise results are missing from Table 1 (R4).
- **Presentation and clarity issues** (R1, R2):
  - Method details are vague: meaning of \(D_t\) and \(D_t \cap M\) in Eq. (4) unclear (R1); Eq. (1) seems to fit noisy distribution rather than clean – contradiction with algorithm goal (R2); buffer containing current task data in Eq.(4) is counterintuitive (R2); definitions of \(p_{curr}\) and \(p_{past}\) missing (R2).
  - Figure 1 does not clearly show which task the noisy data originates from; a more rigorous tracking of loss for the same sample across tasks is needed to demonstrate forgetting (R2).
  - Writing issues: abstract logic weak (noise renders buffer vulnerable – but buffers are designed for CL); “adaption is faster” is confusing in context; Section 3.4 line 2 misstates the setting (R2).
  - Only average accuracy reported across tasks; no direct measure of forgetting reduction is provided (R1).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
  - How to guarantee that high-loss samples selected from past tasks are not actually noisy (R1)? This is a key contradiction with the motivation.
  - How does buffer size impact performance (R1)?
  - How does the method perform with shuffled task order (R1)? How does it preserve information from early tasks as sequence length grows (R1)?
  - Can the loss threshold adapt to complex noise distributions (pairflip-45, instance label noise) rather than manual tuning (R3, R4)? The forgetting indicator may not be reliable for diverse noise types – needed analysis (R4).
  - Why is CIFAR-10 asymmetric noise missing (R4)?
