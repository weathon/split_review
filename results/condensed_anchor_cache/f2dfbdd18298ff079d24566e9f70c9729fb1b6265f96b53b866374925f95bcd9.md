## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Pruning via Ranking (PvR), a structured pruning method that uses an importance function (Eq. 2) combining the L1 reconstruction error of the output before/after pruning a neuron and a constant penalty if the prediction changes. Similar neurons/channels are grouped via correlation to speed up the pruning process, and groups are pruned based on group importance. PvR can prune layers, channels, and neurons, reduces model depth, and supports user-specified parameter budgets. Subnetworks are retrained from scratch, and the paper uses a Geometric Complexity measure to justify retraining. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, and IMDB with VGG16, ResNet34, and BERT-base-uncased.

### Strengths
- Novel pruning method with a ranking-based importance function that goes beyond output reconstruction error by incorporating whether the prediction result changes (penalty term).
- Empirically verified across diverse tasks (vision and NLP) and models (VGG16, ResNet34, BERT-base), showing broad applicability.
- Can prune layers, channels, and neurons, reducing both width and depth, and supports user-specified parameter budgets.
- The paper is well-written and the overall approach is easy to follow.
- One reviewer (Reviewer 4) was notably more positive (rating 6 vs. 3), but their concerns are reflected in the Weaknesses below.

### Weaknesses
- Algorithm introduction is hard to follow; a flowchart is missing, making it unclear how the components work together.
- No empirical analysis of the time cost of the pruning process despite grouping being used to reduce computational cost.
- Baseline models compared are not state-of-the-art; more recent models and pruning methods (e.g., Kuang et al. 2022) should be covered.
- Lack of essential ablation studies on the two key hyperparameters: the constant error "d" and the group size. Their influence on accuracy is likely large, and the method would be less useful if careful tuning is required per task/model.
- The claim of supporting a parameter budget is trivial; all pruning methods can achieve this by adjusting thresholds (e.g., L1 norm pruning) at negligible cost.
- Limited creativity: the Geometric Complexity-based initialization method is taken from another paper, not a novel contribution.
- VGG-16 is very redundant for CIFAR-10; the results on that combination may not be convincing.
- The paper does not describe how group importance is computed from individual neuron/channel importance (presumably averaging, but not stated).
- Lack of details on when the method prunes an entire layer and how it achieves lower latency from layer pruning.
- Missing references to relevant prior work: COP (IJCAI 2019) for correlation-based pruning and ThiNet for reconstruction error-based pruning.
- Experiments are limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet); need results on ImageNet-1K to demonstrate generalizability and robustness.
- Lack of thorough analysis of the ranking method: sensitivity to group size, interaction of the penalty term with other components, and the impact of removing weights that do not cause incorrect predictions.
- No validation of the grouping module: how different grouping settings affect pruned model performance and how much it speeds up the overall process.
- Missing discussion of why PvR works for both vision and NLP while other methods cannot (insight not provided).
- No comparison of the total time taken by the grouping and ranking processes versus other pruning methods.
