## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DrugXAS, an interpretable and adaptive graph contrastive learning framework for drug-related biomedical link prediction, combining attention-aware augmentation, an adaptive graph updater/neighborhood sampler, and an information-sharing module with diffusion loss. All reviewers acknowledge technical soundness and strong empirical results on seven benchmark datasets across warm and cold-start scenarios. However, three reviewers (ratings 3,3,3) found the novelty limited, while one reviewer (rating 5) was more positive but still raised significant concerns about novelty, ablation, and visualization. Major criticisms include insufficient interpretability validation, lack of computational efficiency analysis, missing ablations and theoretical analysis, and inadequate hyperparameter justification.

### Strengths
- Technically sound and well-structured framework that integrates attention-based augmentation, adaptive graph updater/neighborhood sampler, and information sharing module with diffusion loss.
- Achieves strong predictive performance, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on seven benchmark datasets across both warm and cold-start scenarios, with particular strength in cold-start settings.
- The attention-aware augmentation provides interpretability via attention maps (though its novelty and validity were debated by some reviewers).
- The information sharing module effectively integrates chemical structures (molecule view) with heterogeneous relational semantics (network view).
- Comprehensive evaluation covering multi-type tasks (drug-drug, drug-target, etc.) on seven datasets.
- Topic is interesting and valuable for drug discovery applications.
- Presentation is clear and well-organized overall.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited Novelty**: All reviewers agreed novelty is limited. The framework combines existing techniques (multi-view augmentation, GNN encoder, graph contrastive learning) without substantial technical advances. One reviewer (score 5) specifically noted strong similarity to SNRI (Xu et al., AAAI 2022) and BioKDN (Ma et al., TKDE 2024) and requested clarification of key differences. Another reviewer described it as a mixture of many existing studies.
- **Interpretability Claims Insufficiently Validated**: The interpretability relies primarily on qualitative attention map case studies without systematic evaluation or domain expert validation. One reviewer (confidence 4) noted the debate on whether attention provides valid explanations (Jain & Wallace 2019; Serrano & Smith 2019) and called this a major concern. Another reviewer (confidence 3) argued any GNN with attention can produce similar maps, undermining the claimed contribution.
- **Lack of Computational Efficiency and Scalability Analysis**: A major concern raised by one reviewer (confidence 4). No runtime comparisons against simpler baselines, memory profiling, or scaling experiments are provided. The multiple complex components (attention augmentation, dynamic neighborhood sampling, information sharing) add overhead without analysis, critical for real-world applicability on larger graphs.
- **Insufficient Ablation and Component Analysis**:
    - The dynamic neighborhood sampler did not show significant improvement in the ablation study; one reviewer (score 5) questioned its necessity and requested further experiments.
    - The visualization in Section 5.5 (drug embeddings) for DrugXAS vs. DrugXAS w/o-SMN showed no significant difference for most drug types; further discussion needed.
    - One reviewer asked how the adaptive graph updater and neighborhood sampler specifically impact performance in cold-start scenarios.
    - One reviewer requested varying GNN encoders for molecule- and network-view to verify model robustness.
- **Missing or Incomplete Hyperparameter Analysis**: The model has many hyperparameters (γ, d, k_n, s_n, p_0, p_x) but limited sensitivity analysis. One reviewer (confidence 4) noted that selecting different hyperparameters per dataset is inefficient and impractical. The attention-aware augmentation hyperparameters (p_0, p_x) were described as poorly justified. Limited analysis of how augmentation impacts different tasks/datasets was also noted.
- **Lack of Theoretical Analysis**: One reviewer (confidence 4) pointed out that the effectiveness of the adaptive graph updating strategy (selecting top k_n nodes by cosine similarity) and the choice of k_n lack theoretical justification. Another reviewer called for complexity analysis.
- **Incomplete Comparison with Alternative Contrastive Learning Approaches**: One reviewer (confidence 4) noted the paper omits momentum contrast, multi-view contrastive, and hierarchical contrastive methods. The effect of different positive/negative sampling strategies, especially in cold-start scenarios, is not explored.
- **Minor Presentation Issues**:
    - Figure 1(b): what do grey edges mean?
    - Figure 2: many characters are hard to recognize.
    - Figure 1 includes light blue arrows, but Section 3 defines the graph as undirected.
    - Dynamic neighborhood sampling (Section 4.2): if the number of 1-hop neighbors exceeds s_n, how are neighbors selected?
- **Reviewer Disagreement**: Three reviewers (ratings 3) were critical of novelty and contributions, while one reviewer (rating 5) was more positive but still noted limited novelty, insufficient ablation (dynamic sampler improvement), and visualization issues. Even the more favorable evaluation acknowledges substantial limitations.
