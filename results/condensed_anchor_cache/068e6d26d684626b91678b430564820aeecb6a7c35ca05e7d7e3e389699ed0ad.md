## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses private data selection for transformer fine-tuning over multi-party computation (MPC). The model owner wants to purchase a subset of data from the data owner without revealing the model or the full dataset. The authors propose SelectFormer, which uses proxy models that approximate the target transformer by replacing expensive nonlinearities (softmax, layer norm, GeLU) with low-dimensional MLPs (ReLU), combined with a multiphase selection protocol that progressively filters data using coarser to finer proxies, and overlapping computation/communication. Empirical results show large delay reductions (from thousands to tens of hours) with ~0.2% accuracy degradation compared to an oracle that selects high-entropy examples openly. Reviewer assessments range from 6 (three reviewers) to 8 (one reviewer). The majority see a useful contribution but raise concerns about clarity, baseline comparisons, and thoroughness of analysis.

### Strengths

- The problem formulation (private data selection via MPC for transformers) is interesting and novel. [R1, R2, R4]
- The algorithm cleverly combines techniques: (1) fusing and approximating nonlinear operators with MLPs trained on a small bootstrapped dataset, (2) multiphase selection to reduce delay while maintaining or improving accuracy, and (3) overlapping computation and communication in MPC. [R1, R2, R3]
- Empirical results show strong improvements in delay and accuracy over baselines (Random, oracle with no privacy, MPCFormer) across both vision and NLP benchmarks. [R1, R2, R3]
- The multiphase selection technique is a nice contribution: using coarse, cheap proxies early and progressively more accurate proxies reduces total delay and slightly improves accuracy. [R3]
- The MLP approximation of nonlinearities is a useful technique, especially the dimension reduction (e.g., from 768 to 2). [R2, R3]
- Evaluation is broad, covering multiple transformer models and tasks, with granular ablation studies (e.g., Figure 4, Figure 6). [R3]
- The method can handle unlabeled and imbalanced data, which is important for practical data markets. [R3]
- The authors evaluate the impact of parallelism (overlapping communication and computation), which is valuable even if not entirely novel. [R3]
- One reviewer (R3, score 8) finds the overall contribution strong, and notes that the techniques are sufficiently different from past work and could be independently interesting.

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and formalism:** The paper is not polished and often hard to read. Notation is imprecise or undefined (e.g., selectivity αi = Si/Si−1 – is that a ratio of cardinalities?; M_t vs M_target vs M̂_i vs M_p; W_i vs w_i; Mg in Figure 2 defined only later and inconsistently). The threat model does not clearly specify *who* learns *what* data—e.g., could the data holder, knowing the ranked entropies of their samples, train the model locally on the most informative ones to approximate the private model? This leakage is not explored. The MPC algorithm is not formally written out, and no privacy guarantees are proved. [R1, R4]
- **Pipeline description insufficient:** The multi-phase selection algorithm is not clearly explained. It is unclear why candidate data need to be queried, how the Gaussian distribution for layer outputs is used to supervise fine-tuning, and which parameters are hyperparameters. An explicit algorithm pipeline showing steps and hyperparameters is needed. [R4]
- **Baseline selection and comparison:** The main baseline (MPCFormer) may be a strawman because its distillation approach is ill-suited to data selection (as the authors note). The paper should compare against stronger recent MPC inference baselines that do not rely on data-dependent approximations, such as THE-X (Chen et al., 2022) or Bolt (Pang et al., 2024, S&P). [R1, R3] It is also not clear whether Table 3 compares methods under the same communication and computation budget. [R4] The delay should be directly compared with MPCFormer. [R4]
- **Grid search hyperparameters:** The MLP dimensions, number of proxy layers, and selectivity per round are chosen via grid search. This limits practicality; guidance or justification for choosing these parameters without multiple MPC runs is needed. Could the grid search be performed on a non-private test set first? [R2]
- **Limited scope:** The techniques are tailored to MPC data selection and “impractical for model inference directly,” which may limit their broader impact. [R3]
- **Parallelism novelty:** The claim that “No prior MPC systems exploit such parallelism” is questionable. Meta’s Private Computation Framework and an arXiv preprint (2209.13643) describe overlapping computation and communication in MPC training/inference. The parallelism contribution needs a more nuanced comparison to prior work. [R3]
- **Time-accuracy tradeoffs unclear:** The results should be presented as a plot of accuracy vs. delay for all variants and baselines, including a 1-phase variant with dimension-2 MLP, to see if it achieves a better tradeoff. Current tables (e.g., Table 1) are hard to read and often lack metric definitions. Captions should be self-contained. [R1]
- **Surprising Table 2 results:** Some configurations where all nonlinear layers are replaced by MLPs outperform no emulation. This suggests MLPs may be sufficient to approximate the entire model. The paper should analyze the embedding-layer-level gap between MLP approximations and the true nonlinearities, not just final accuracy. [R4]
- **Missing analysis of communication/computation costs:** There is no detailed breakdown of computation and communication costs (e.g., how many communication rounds, how much data is transferred). Figure 1 should include a baseline showing time and memory without MPC. [R4, R2]
- **Alternative approximation methods not explored:** Given that ReLUs are still a bottleneck in MPC, why not use polynomial approximations with learnable coefficients instead of MLPs, which introduce more ReLUs? [R3]
- **MPS delay reduction not clearly quantified:** The paper claims MPS reduces total delay by 33% to 61%, but it is unclear which figures support this (e.g., is it the difference between PM and PMT in Figure 6?). A delay column in Table 4 would help. [R3]
- **Minor editing issues:** Line 82: citation “??”; line 398: “under 3 compute parties” repeated; line 498: d_i not formatted; typo in Beaver triple citation; missing citation for BOLT. [R1, R2]
