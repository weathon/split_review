## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes a supervised causal learning approach, PAIRE, that improves upon a transformer-based baseline (CSIvA). It addresses two claimed limitations: inconsistency from using Bernoulli-sampled adjacency matrices as learning targets, and inadequate encoding of causal information. PAIRE introduces a pairwise encoder module with unidirectional attention and outputs a skeleton matrix plus a v-structure tensor (v-tensor) to represent the Markov equivalence class (MEC). Experiments compare against several baselines on synthetic and real (Sachs) data.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly identifies and motivates two limitations of existing SCL methods (CSIvA), and incorporates skeleton and v-structure information to address them. (R1 did not list strengths; R2, R3, R4 agree on this point.)
- The proposed workflow (pairwise encoder, skeleton/v-tensor output) is novel and interesting. (R3)
- The paper is generally well-written, including helpful “Remarks” and precise details. (R2: presentation good; R3: definitions and writing generally clear; R4: well written, good theoretical knowledge.)
- Experimental results are convincing and include multiple baselines. (R3; note R1 and R2 disagree on convincingness – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees for identifiability and consistency.** The problem setting is unconventional; it is unclear how the network aligns with standard assumptions (e.g., linear non-Gaussian, additive noise) to ensure identifiability. Training on only 100 simulated graphs with 30 nodes provides no consistency guarantee, even with infinite data. Unlike constraint-based or score-based methods, PAIRE offers no interpretable evidence (e.g., conditional independence, score) for edge decisions. (R1 #1; R2 #1; R4 #6; all three reviewers express this concern.)
- **Motivation for avoiding Bernoulli sampling is questionable.** The non-identifiability example in Section 4.3.1 stems from the linear Gaussian model assumption, not from Bernoulli sampling itself. Additionally, the v-structure output also relies on a logit decision, subjecting it to the same risk of inconsistency. (R1 #2; R2 minor last point)
- **Missing experiments on identifiable scenarios.** All experiments are on non-identifiable settings (linear Gaussian, general nonlinear). The method should be evaluated on identifiable cases such as linear non-Gaussian models (e.g., LiNGAM) or post-nonlinear models. (R1 #3; R2 notes GOLEM specially designed for linear-Gaussian with nonequal variances.)
- **Scalability is severely limited.** The pairwise feature tensor has dimension \(d^2 h\) (e.g., 40,000 for d=100, h=4), making the method unsuitable for larger graphs. Experiments are only on 30-node synthetic graphs and the 11-node Sachs network. Performance on larger graphs (e.g., >100 nodes from Bnlearn repository) is not shown. (R2 #2; R3 #1; R4 major concern #1)
- **Experiment details are insufficient.** Setup details are lacking: how are “general nonlinear” data generated (which nonlinearity)? What is the average degree of graphs? Why does GES fail to finish in 24 hours for 30 nodes? The application to Sachs data is not fully described (how was the trained model used?). Synthetic data generation mechanisms (ER, SF, WS, SBM) need more explanation (at least in appendix). (R2 #3 first part; R4 Section 5.1 & 5.2)
- **Missing competitive baselines.** Several state-of-the-art methods (GraN-DAG, RL-BIC, NOTEARS-ML, GOLEM) are not compared, even though their outputs can be converted to CPDAGs for fair comparison. It is also unclear whether heuristic/Meek rules were applied to baseline algorithms. (R2 #3 second part; R4 #9, #10)
- **Omitted important results from prior work.** CSIvA reported SHD values of 5 and 6 on Sachs data, while PAIRE achieves SHD=10. This discrepancy is not discussed. (R2 #3 third part)
- **Unclear and insufficiently explained technical details.** Several concepts are not precisely described: what is “pairwise relationship” (pairs of (D,G) or nodes)? internal/external information? attention operations (value, key, query for a pair of nodes)? permutation equivariance justification? node feature encoder usage? v-structure conflict heuristics? forward sampling \(P(D|G)\)? The figure captions need more explanation. (R1 overall; R3 minor; R4 Sections 1, 2, 4.1, 4.2; multiple minor points)
- **Inadequate discussion of related work and overclaiming.** Earlier transformer/sampling–based methods (e.g., causal discovery with reinforcement learning, Neural Causal Structure Discovery from Interventions) are not cited. The paper also overclaims the advantages of DNN-based SCL. How PAIRE differs from its closest baseline AVICI is not clearly stated. (R2 minor #1; R4 #2)
- **Acyclicity enforcement not specified.** Unlike AVICI, which optimizes for acyclicity, PAIRE does not explain how cycles are avoided during skeleton learning. (R4 major concern #2)
- **Generalization to different data distributions is not analyzed.** The model is trained on synthetic data with a specific distribution and may not transfer to real-world datasets with different distributions; constraint-based methods could be more robust in such cases. (R4 major concern #3)
- **Parameter counts and computational cost (FLOPs) are missing.** The number of parameters is only given in Section 5.3, not for Table 2; a FLOPs comparison is needed because the pairwise decoder is more computationally intensive than standard self-attention. (R3 #1; R2 #2)
- **Section 5.3 appears redundant.** It largely restates Table 1 with two extra metrics, and its purpose is unclear. (R3 #2)
- **One reviewer (R3) found the experimental results convincing** and the workflow novel, while **others (R1, R2) raised severe concerns about theoretical grounding, motivation, and experimental rigor**, leading to significant disagreement on overall quality. (Contrast preserved.)
