## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents LT3P, a data-driven typhoon trajectory prediction model that uses real-time Unified Model (UM) data rather than reanalysis data (ERA5), enabling 72-hour ahead forecasts at 6-hour intervals. The model employs an encoder-decoder architecture with cross-attention between encoded past trajectory points and global weather fields (geopotential and wind). It is pretrained on ERA5 to predict physics variables, then fine-tuned with UM data and bias-corrected embeddings for trajectory prediction. The authors open-source their PHYSICS TRACK dataset, including ERA5, typhoon best-track, and UM forecast data. Reviewers highlighted the novelty of using reanalysis data at inference time, strong empirical gains over physics-based models, and valuable open-source contributions. However, concerns about missing implementation details, insufficient ablations, unexplained performance jumps, limited regional scope, unclear handling of lead times and stochasticity, and grammatical issues were raised. Reviewer 1 (score 3) was significantly more critical than the other reviewers (scores 6, 8, 8).

### Strengths
- **Novel use of reanalysis data for inference**: LT3P is one of the first data-driven models to use a real-time NWP dataset (UM) for prediction, sidestepping the offline-only limitation of reanalysis (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The bias-correction scheme that leverages ERA5-pretrained embeddings for UM forecasts is an interesting methodological contribution (Reviewers 3, 4).

- **Strong empirical results**: Significant gains over physics-based baselines from various meteorological institutions are reported (Reviewer 1). Extensive evaluations demonstrate state-of-the-art performance (Reviewer 2). The framework combining learned physics variable maps with cross-attention and trajectory prediction yields very good results (Reviewer 4).

- **Open-source data and code**: The release of the PHYSICS TRACK dataset, training/test codes, and pre-trained weights is a valuable contribution to climate AI (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewers noted that careful documentation is needed for accessibility (Reviewer 1).

- **Innovative approach**: The method trains on NWP data like UM instead of relying solely on ERA5, a good study that distinguishes the work from prior data-driven methods (Reviewer 1 noted limited originality but valued the study; Reviewer 2 called it innovative).

- **Potential for broader impact**: The approach could change how reanalysis data are applied, expanding from offline computations to real-time use (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing implementation details** (multiple reviewers): Architecture details (number of layers, embedding dimensions, total learnable parameters) are lacking (Reviewer 3). The method was tested with GAN, CVAE, and diffusion models, but their architectures, loss functions, latent space structures, and training procedures are not described (Reviewer 1). The loss function \(L_{trajectory}\) is undefined (Reviewers 3, 4). The ensemble method (e.g., simple averaging or complex weighting) is unclear (Reviewer 1). Acronyms (LSTM, MLP, MID, SSIM) are not defined (Reviewer 4).

- **Unclear data handling and matching**: The phrase "matched through latitude and longitude mapping and interpolation method" is vague; the interpolation method (bilinear, nearest neighbor) and handling of different resolutions are unspecified (Reviewer 1). The bias correction phase matching UM data with ERA5 by timestamp is not fully explained (Reviewer 1). The notation in Section 3.1 is confusing (e.g., \(C_p\) vs. pressure levels \(p\), variable indices missing) (Reviewer 1).

- **Insufficient ablations and analysis**: The ablation study is hard to understand and should be expanded (Reviewer 1). Specific ablations are missing, e.g., training with UM and joint training and bias-correction **without** pre-training, or removing bias correction (Reviewer 1). The effect of using only UM data without pre-training is unclear (Reviewer 1). The paper does not sufficiently explain why the model achieves order-of-magnitude improvements over other recent works (Bi+ 2023, Lam+ 2022); the ablation suggests joint training with reanalysis is key, but deeper analysis is missing (Reviewer 3). The reported quantitative results are surprisingly good but by an order of magnitude; the paper must clarify what drives this jump (Reviewer 3).

- **Inconsistent baseline comparisons**: The first set of baselines in Table 2 (e.g., SocialGAN) appear to be off-the-shelf pre-trained human trajectory prediction models, which likely explains their poor performance; they should be retrained on the typhoon data (Reviewer 1). The trends for MGTCF seem inconsistent with its original paper (MGTCF should be clearly better than SGAN, but Tables 2 and 3 show different results) (Reviewer 3). Ruttgers et al. is not included in the benchmarking (Reviewer 1). It is unclear which of the compared methods use UM data vs. only trajectory information (Reviewer 4).

- **Limited regional and generalizability scope**: The UM map has fixed latitude/longitude values; it is unclear how typhoons near the borders or outside are handled, and whether the model can be adapted to other regions without retraining from scratch (Reviewer 4). The study only applies to typhoons, not other tropical cyclones (Reviewer 2). The fixed region and lack of generalization discussion are limitations (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing practical details for real-time use**: Computation times (training and inference) and hardware specifications are not reported, which is critical for assessing real-time applicability (Reviewer 4). The 3-hour delay for UM data means effective lead times are shorter; the reported "6-hour leadtime" should be understood as "3-hour leadtime"? (Reviewer 3). The model's dependency on real-time NWP dataset availability and accuracy may be a limitation, especially when data are sparse or inaccurate (Reviewer 2).

- **Unclear stochasticity and probability cones**: The method section does not mention stochasticity, yet probability cones are shown in results; it is unclear how stochasticity is introduced (sampling, direct estimation) and how cones are generated (Reviewer 4).

- **Data split and hyperparameter tuning not specified**: It is unclear whether a validation set was used or if hyperparameters were tuned on the 2019–2021 years; the exact split into training, validation, and test sets should be clarified, and the hyperparameter tuning process detailed (Reviewer 4). The number of different typhoons in the training set is not given (Reviewer 4).

- **Additional reviewer questions that overlap with criticism/requests**: (These are included in the points above or listed here for completeness.)
  - Why are geopotential height and wind inputs taken from output timesteps \(i=t_p+1,\dots,t_p+t_f\)? Should they be \(i=1,\dots,t_p\)? (Reviewer 1)
  - The claim "too large scale to train a model from scratch" should be toned down; models are trained from scratch on ERA5 (Reviewer 1).
  - Why is bias-corrected UM sometimes better than ERA5 only? (Reviewer 4)
  - Figure 4 shows bias-correction losing details; the SSIM and mean values are not convincing (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 1 scale (km or lat/lon) is missing (Reviewer 4).
  - How does the model handle uncertainties in trajectory prediction and ensure reliability? (Reviewer 2)
  - It would be valuable to show a worst-case example in Figure 3 with a comment (Reviewer 4).

- **Many typos and grammatical errors** (Reviewers 1, 4): Examples include "preliminarily" instead of "preliminaries", "bais-corrected", missing definition of "the UM", "L_trajectory not defined", "on a conservation laws", "hyperparameters & a architecture", "we are viewing probability cones?", "SSIM not defined", etc. A proofread is needed (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 noted numerous grammar mistakes that inhibit understanding.

- **Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (score 3, confidence 4) found the clarity and missing details severe issues and considered the contribution limited, while Reviewers 3 and 4 (both 8, confidence 4) found the methodological framework interesting and fitting for ICLR. Reviewer 2 (6) was moderately positive. The contrast in assessment should be noted.
