## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies LLM training stability using an 830M parameter model and high learning rates to force divergence. It finds that the L2 norm of outputs from QKV, Proj, and FC2 linear layers grows with high LR, causing instability. The authors explore several interventions: applying layer normalization after Proj and FC2, applying LN after QKV (removing pre-normalization), and combining QK layer normalization with softmax capping. The last two methods allow a 1.5x increase in learning rate without divergence, and all three show perplexity improvements over the baseline (QK LN only). The paper reports both successful and unsuccessful methods.

### Strengths

1. Interesting finding that multiple layers (QKV, Proj, FC2) are responsible for training instability due to output growth. (R1)
2. Good comparison of different training stability methods from the literature. (R1, R3, R4)
3. Proposes new methods of applying layer normalization and softmax capping across layers. (R2)
4. Reports both which methods worked and which did not offer benefit; both results are valuable for intuition. (R3)
5. Perplexity improvements seem significant. (R4)
6. Good number of comparisons. (R4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Figure quality and presentation**: Figure quality is low (need vector images). Many figures lack axis labels (e.g., Figure 3 caption insufficient, Figure 2 needs more LR examples). (R1, R3)
2. **Formatting and typos**: Paper does not adhere to ICLR25 standard. Multiple typos (e.g., “H10” in Section 2). No line numbers shown. (R1, R2)
3. **Lack of scaling evidence**:
   - No evidence that the method scales to models with 10B or 100B parameters. (R2)
   - Only tested on 830M model; not obvious how findings scale down to smaller models. (R3)
   - Missing scaling to larger architectures (e.g., 2–3B) with benchmarks beyond perplexity. (R4)
4. **Insufficient experimental evidence**:
   - Results section relies on two tables; missing training curves showing how different methods improve stability. (R1, R2)
   - No training loss convergence curves; only a binary divergence/convergence table is insufficient. (R2)
   - No evidence of faster convergence at different scales beyond avoiding divergence. (R1)
   - Missing ablation studies: how much training time saved by higher LR? How is convergence impacted beyond binary? (R4)
5. **Missing dataset disclosure**: Training datasets not disclosed, harming reproducibility. (R4; also asked by R3)
6. **Precision issues**: Training used bf16, not fp32. Impact of precision on stability not discussed. Also interesting to test fp8 (greater instability) but not done. (R1, R4)
7. **Training time and optimality**: Training to 1T tokens is excessive for a 830M model; chinchilla-optimal would be ~16B tokens. This consumes resources that could be used for ablations. (R4)
8. **Limited evaluation**: Only perplexity and divergence/convergence reported. Missing evaluation on standard benchmarks for LLM quality. (R2)
9. **No evidence on Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) applicability**. (R2)
10. **Missing motivation**: Further motivation and examples needed for why training instability is a serious issue. (R3)
11. **Writing needs work**: Several typos; overall paper requires more work in writing and contributions. (R1, R4)
12. **R3 (score 5, minority more positive)**: Still agrees with many weaknesses above (e.g., missing smaller model results, figure labels, dataset question). Their positive assessment is noted in Strengths.
