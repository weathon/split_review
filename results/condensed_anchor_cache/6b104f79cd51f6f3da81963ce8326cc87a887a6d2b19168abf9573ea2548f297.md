## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a gradient-based algorithm, MLM-CMOO, for constrained multi-objective optimization (CMOO). It provides a convergence analysis showing an $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ rate to Pareto stationary solutions and tests the method on multi-task learning problems against NSGA-II and PSL.

### Strengths
- Novel gradient-based algorithm specifically designed for constrained MOO, an underexplored area.
- Provides a convergence analysis with theoretical proofs (including in the supplementary material).
- Paper is well-written and easy to follow; technical and mathematical details are clearly presented and proofs appear correct.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation.** Only Table 1 and Figure 1 are presented, missing standard MOO metrics (hypervolume, IGD, generational distance) and Pareto front visualizations. Many reviewers noted that loss and time values alone are insufficient to understand effectiveness. Additional missing details: number of independent runs per algorithm, the number of objectives for each dataset, and whether the constraints in the MTL problems are clearly explained. One reviewer (score 1) argued the comparison with NSGA-II is unfair because NSGA-II is a population-based black-box method that will lose on convex problems; fair comparison requires state-of-the-art gradient-based approaches (e.g., gradient ascent, gradient sliding).
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** No CMOO algorithms (gradient-free or gradient-based) are compared. The paper compares only NSGA-II and PSL; it does not include SPEA2, MOEA/D, or other gradient-based MOO methods. Two reviewers specifically asked why these widely used baselines were omitted.
- **Restrictive convexity assumption and unclear practical relevance.** Assumption 4.1 requires each objective $f_i$ to be convex (and strongly convex in Theorem 4.6). Multiple reviewers questioned the realism of this assumption—real-world MOO problems rarely consist of purely convex components and may have multimodal local optima that trap gradient methods. It is also unclear whether convexity refers to the search space or objective space. One reviewer (score 1) called this an “extreme assumption [that] severely limits the contribution.”
- **Misleading title and framing.** The title “Constrained Multi-Objective Optimization” suggests a broad survey, but the paper covers only one gradient-based algorithm. The paper (line 132) states the goal of MOO is to find a Pareto optimal solution, whereas the standard goal is to find a good approximation of the entire solution set. This downplays the complexity of the field.
- **Undefined terms and missing definitions.** The convergence rate uses $T$ without specifying what $T$ refers to (function evaluations, iterations, runtime?). The term “Pareto criticality” needs clarification: is it equivalent to a multi-objective local optimum?
- **Incomplete justification of gradient-based scope.** The paper does not discuss limitations of gradient-based methods (local optima, sensitivity to initialization, differentiability requirement) or provide conditions under which they are suitable. More justification for focusing solely on this subclass is needed.
- **Lack of ablation studies.** The algorithm has multiple components; no experiments isolate which parts are most important or whether all are useful.
- **Role of constraints not justified.** One reviewer asked why constraints are necessary: would the framework look similar if the problems were unconstrained? The paper does not clarify this.
- **Presentation issues.** Inconsistent notation (e.g., $f_i(x)$ vs. $f^i(x)$); Figure 1 caption is too brief and subfigures unexplained; citation formatting errors (e.g., “The NSGA-II Deb et al. (2002)” instead of “The NSGA-II (Deb et al., 2002)”); missing review of constraint handling techniques (CHTs). One reviewer noted the paper appears to have been submitted in a hurry without being fully completed.
