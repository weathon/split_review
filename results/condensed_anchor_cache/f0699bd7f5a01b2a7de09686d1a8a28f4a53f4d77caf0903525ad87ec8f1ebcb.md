## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OMNI (Open-endedness via Models of human Notions of Interestingness), which uses LLMs as models of interestingness (MoI) to prioritize tasks for open-ended RL agents. The method combines learning progress (LP) with LLM-based interestingness scores to select tasks that are both learnable and interesting. Experiments in Crafter and BabyAI environments show OMNI outperforms uniform sampling and LP-only baselines. Reviewers are divided: two strongly positive (scores 8,8), one moderately positive (6), and one very negative (3). The majority finds the idea novel and promising, but the negative reviewer considers the experiments unconvincing and presentation poor.

### Strengths
- The paper targets an important and well-motivated problem: the inability to quantify interestingness in open-ended learning. (R1, R2, R3)
- The idea of leveraging LLMs as MoIs is novel, promising, and timely; it makes a previously infeasible task feasible. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The proposed method is technically sound: LLMs infused with human priors can help determine task interestingness for curriculum generation. (R3, R4)
- Experiments in two domains (Crafter, BabyAI) show clear advantages over uniform task sampling and LP-only baselines. (R2, R4)
- Additional experiments in the appendix are thorough. (R4)
- The problem of open-ended learning and auto-curricula is relevant to a broad audience. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons**: The paper only compares to uniform sampling and LP-only, but not to established intrinsic motivation methods (prediction error, information gain) or novelty search algorithms that explicitly aim to discover novel behaviors. Including such baselines is needed to contextualize OMNI’s contribution. (R2, R3) Also missing comparisons to other curriculum learning for RL methods discussed in related work. (R3)
- **Contrived environments and simple heuristics**: Experiments use only two simple environments (Crafter, one MiniGrid task). In Crafter, “Impossible” tasks are generated by assigning zero reward and “Boring” tasks by repeating the same task, making the setup solvable with very simple heuristics. An LLM is not strictly needed; evaluating on more challenging domains (e.g., Minecraft, NetHack) would strengthen the paper. (R1)
- **Poor performance on BabyAI**: OMNI and all baselines achieve extremely low success rates (< 0.008) on BabyAI. The authors should analyze the policies and consider plotting results for only interesting tasks (as in Crafter) to clarify what is happening. (R2)
- **Lack of open-ended task generation**: The experiments use a pre-defined set of interesting/boring/challenging tasks, not a procedural generator that could produce unlimited novel tasks. Testing in a more open-ended setting is needed to match the paper’s claims. (R2)
- **Evaluation only on seen tasks**: Models are tested only on previously seen tasks; held-out, especially out-of-distribution (OOD), tasks should be evaluated to assess robustness. (R2)
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: The algorithm description is vague; no pseudocode or precise equations for task selection are provided in the main text. Criteria for “doing a task well” (success rate or reward) are not clarified. (R1) The Conclusion/Discussion/Future Work section is too long (>1 page or >1.25 pages), taking space from concrete methods and results that are relegated to the appendix. (R1, R2) The writing uses lyrical, atypical language (e.g., “ephemeral fuel”, “eons of human experience”), which compromises scientific clarity. (R2)
- **Overstated claims**: The paper’s claims about potential for self-improving AI and AI-Generating Algorithms are disproportionate to the presented work. The gap between the simple experiments and the grand implications should be narrowed. (R2)
- **Practical implementation issues**: The current algorithm assumes all candidate tasks are known in advance, which is unrealistic in an RL setting where agents must discover tasks through exploration. (R3) The downsampling method for boring tasks (multiply by 0.001) is simplistic and its applicability needs discussion. (R3)
- **Human alignment not discussed**: Using LLM interestingness as a proxy for human interestingness raises alignment concerns (e.g., safety). The paper does not address how alignment issues (like those tackled by RLHF) affect the use of LLM-based MoIs. (R3)
- **Missing related work**: The paper arXiv:2302.06692 (uses language model to suggest plausibly useful goals) shares a similar idea but is not cited or discussed. (R3)
- **Removal of survival component in Crafter**: Survival is a key aspect of open-endedness; removing it reduces the complexity and goes against the motivation for using LLMs. (R3)
- **LLM understanding of interestingness**: The paper pre-defines interesting tasks; it is unclear how the LLM knows what is interesting without explicit instruction or examples. The prompt and query format should be shown. (R3)
- **Contrasting reviewer on LP-only performance**: One reviewer (R4) notes that LP-only should eventually match OMNI with unlimited rounds, but experiments show otherwise. The authors should discuss if LP baseline was run for enough rounds. (R4)
- **Unexpected random agent performance**: Another reviewer (R4) finds it surprising that a uniform random task generator in Crafter performs so poorly; the authors should clarify. (R4)
- **Missing references**: Some references on building open-ended agents in open-world environments are missing. (R4)
