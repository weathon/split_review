## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses edge-level signals on graphs that are either direction-aware (equivariant to edge orientation) or direction-agnostic (invariant). It proposes a GNN that treats these two types separately using orientation-equivariant and orientation-invariant Laplacian constructions, and extends to directed edges via the magnetic Laplacian. The model is evaluated on synthetic and real-world benchmarks, outperforming prior work.

### Strengths
- The problem is well-motivated by real-world applications (e.g., traffic, water flow); Figure 2 clearly illustrates the distinction. (Reviewers 2,4)
- The solution is elegant and principled: the separation of equivariant and invariant features along with the construction of distinct Laplacians (B_equ, B_inv) and stitching them together is a clever design that provably respects the required symmetries. (Reviewers 2,4)
- Theoretical claims are sound and proofs appear correct. (Reviewer 1)
- The empirical evidence is strong; the model consistently outperforms baselines, and the ablation studies convincingly show the source of improvements. (Reviewers 2,3)
- The experiments cover a wide range of tasks (3 synthetic + 5 real-world datasets, 3 tasks); the novel benchmark suite is a significant contribution. (Reviewer 3)
- The decoupling of direction (of edges) from orientation (of edge features) is unique compared to prior work and sets a new standard for the field. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical depth**: The main idea is not entirely novel—both equivariant and invariant edge features have been studied separately, and combining them may account for performance gains simply through a more complex model. Theorem 4.1 essentially follows by design. (Reviewer 1) In contrast, other reviewers found the approach original and principled. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- **Use of magnetic Laplacian for directed edges**: The magnetic Laplacian with complex phase shifts is not the most general way to aggregate information on directed graphs. Aggregation of features with phases q and –q cancels to a real value indistinguishable from undirected aggregation; similarly, after multiple hops along directed edges the direction information can be lost (e.g., when kq = 2π m). A more principled alternative is to separately aggregate outgoing, incoming, and undirected neighbors (as in Rossi et al.). The authors should consider replacing the magnetic Laplacian with such a scheme and compare empirically. (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of comparison with state-of-the-art**: The baselines may not include the most recent relevant methods. In particular, comparisons with a ChebNet-style architecture using the magnetic Laplacian (Zhang et al., 2021) and the extension of the magnetic Laplacian to signed directed graphs (MSGNN, He et al.) are missing. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Scalability and dataset limitations**: The construction of the novel incidence matrices is computationally expensive, and all benchmarks use only small graphs. It is unclear whether the method scales to larger graphs. Also, the method does not appear to work on directed multigraphs. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation issues**: The paper is vague in several arguments (e.g., explanation of direction-consistent orientations in lines 200–203, Figure 2 is not sufficiently explained). (Reviewer 3) Minor writing issues include missing punctuation in equations, undefined notation B^H (Hermitian transpose), and inconsistent capitalization (e.g., “laplacian” vs. “Laplacian”). (Reviewer 4)
- **Model complexity and sensitivity**: The authors do not report the number of trainable parameters or the sensitivity of the model to hyperparameters (e.g., the phase shift q). One reviewer notes that for a q = 1/2, four directed hops can cancel directional information, raising expressivity concerns. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Questions about framework extensions**: It is unclear whether the framework can handle both edge and node signals, or be extended to signed and directed graphs beyond the magnetic Laplacian setup. (Reviewer 4)
- **Doubt about broad interest**: The problem of mixed undirected/directed edges with orientation-dependent features may be considered too niche for a top venue like ICLR. (Reviewer 2)
