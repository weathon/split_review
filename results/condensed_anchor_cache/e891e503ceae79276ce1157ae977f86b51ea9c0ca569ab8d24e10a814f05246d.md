## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends inverse constrained RL (ICRL, Liu & Zhu 2022) to a meta-learning setting. It proposes to first learn meta-priors over reward and constraint functions from related tasks, then adapt to new tasks from few expert demonstrations. The problem is formulated as a bi-level optimization (upper: reward meta-prior, lower: constraint meta-prior). Because exact gradients are intractable, the paper introduces three levels of approximation (first-order gradient approximation, replacing inverse-Hessian with an optimization, and solving the lower-level via multi-step gradient descent). It provides convergence to an ε-approximate first-order stationary point at O(1/ε²) iteration complexity and derives a generalization bound for new tasks. Experiments in a physical drone navigation and MuJoCo (Swimmer, Hopper, HalfCheetah with constraints) show that M-ICRL outperforms ICRL, Meta-IRL, IC-GAIL, and Variational ICRL baselines. Most reviewers rated this paper 6/6/6/6 with contribution scores 3/3/3/2; Reviewer 4 is notably less positive on contribution (rating 2).

### Strengths
1. **Novel problem formulation:** The paper creatively combines meta IRL/RL (Xu et al. 2019; Rajeswaran et al. 2019) with inverse constrained RL (Liu & Zhu 2022) to tackle few-shot learning of both reward and constraint functions. (R1, R3)
2. **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-structured, with careful notation and a clear exposition of challenges, algorithm, theories, and experiments. (R1, R3, R4)
3. **Solid theoretical analysis:** Comprehensive theoretical results including convergence guarantee to ε-FOSP and generalization bound for new tasks; assumptions supporting the proofs are clearly stated. (R2, R3, R4)
4. **Prominent baselines:** The proposed method consistently outperforms ICRL, Meta-IRL, IC-GAIL, and Variational ICRL in both physical drone and MuJoCo settings, demonstrating effectiveness. (R1, R2, R4)
5. **Real-world demonstration:** The physical drone navigation experiment adds convincing evidence of practical applicability. (R1, R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Marginal novelty / significance:** While the combination is creative, the paper largely extends the existing bi-level ICRL objective (Liu & Zhu 2022) by adding a meta-prior. The main technical contribution is on mitigating computational intractability through approximations; several reviewers find this advancement marginal over prior work. (R1, R3, R4) Reviewer 4 rated contribution 2 (fair) vs. 3 (good) from others.
2. **Missing comparison with meta-safe RL:** The paper does not compare or discuss “A CMDP-within-online framework for Meta-Safe Reinforcement Learning” (Khattar et al., ICLR 2023), which studies meta-learning for constrained RL. The relationship and differences in handling constraints and meta-learning should be clarified. (R1)
3. **Unclear role of regularization term:** The necessity of the ||η – ω||² term in Equation (3) is not explained. (R1)
4. **Formulation of lower-level optimization deviates from Liu & Zhu:** In the meta ICRL lower-level (Appendix A.1, Equation 11), constraint feature expectations are directly matched without a Lagrange multiplier, unlike Liu & Zhu where a multiplier λ appears in the dual. This effectively treats the constraint as a negative reward (Appendix A.2). This may lead to suboptimal solutions when the constraint cannot be simply expressed as a reward offset, because the trade-off between reward and constraint satisfaction is not explicitly balanced. (R2)
5. **Lack of scaling analysis with demonstration count:** The paper does not study how the performance gap between M-ICRL and ICRL varies with the number of demonstrations. It would be useful to understand if there are diminishing returns and at what point ICRL approaches M-ICRL. (R2)
6. **Theoretical results have limitations:**
   - Convergence is only to an ε-FOSP; optimality is not discussed, and such results for gradient-based algorithms are standard and not novel. (R3)
   - The upper bound in Theorem 1 scales exponentially with K (the number of gradient steps), implying fast error accumulation; the paper neither explains nor mitigates this effect. (R4)
   - The ε-FOSP claim includes an additional Big O term beyond the √(1/N) term; the impact of the approximations (which could dominate convergence) is not sufficiently justified. (R4)
   - The results are in terms of gradient norm, not regret or PAC sample complexity; it is unclear how they translate to practical performance guarantees. (R4)
7. **Strong assumptions:** Assumption 1 (reward/cost bounded and smooth up to fourth-order gradient) is restrictive, especially for neural networks with ReLU activations and unbounded state-action spaces. (R3)
8. **Bi-level approximation shifts original framework:** The meta-level expands the upper-level to learn both reward and constraint meta-priors (θ, ω), deviating from the original ICRL (Liu & Zhu 2022) where upper = reward, lower = constraint. The task-specific reward adaptation via one gradient update may be inadequate; the paper lacks an ablation study to validate this adaptation. (R4)
9. **Empirical concerns:**
   - **MuJoCo settings questionable:** The reward (target velocity) and constraints (e.g., swimmer front tip angle threshold appear simplistic; it is unclear why the agent would need to violate the constraint to maximize reward, or whether these are representative of constrained RL. (R4)
   - **Meta-IRL results counterintuitive:** Meta-IRL (which does not model constraints) is expected to achieve higher reward and higher constraint violation, but the results show lower reward (contrary to expectation). The paper does not explain this. (R4)
   - **Large performance gap:** M-ICRL significantly outperforms all baselines, and baselines are far from expert performance. This suggests baselines may not be well-tuned for the tasks; more details on baseline design, hyperparameters, and training procedures are needed. (R4)
10. **Missing ablations:** No empirical study of hyperparameters α, batch size B, number of gradient steps K, or other design choices. (R2, R4) Additionally, hyperparameter values are not clearly reported. (R2)
11. **Notation and clarity issues:**
    - Line 4 of Algorithm 1 has an extra D_i^{tr} in the gradient expression; the final parenthesis doesn’t correspond to any element. (R4)
    - The δ term in gradients (6) and (7) is undefined. (R4)
    - Algorithm inputs/outputs should be stated in words (e.g., meta-priors), and implicit hyperparameters (α, B, etc.) should be listed. (R2)
12. **Audience alignment:** The paper focuses on gradient approximations and stationarity analysis; its style may fit optimal control/robotics more than the mainstream ML/ICLR audience. (R4, subjective)
