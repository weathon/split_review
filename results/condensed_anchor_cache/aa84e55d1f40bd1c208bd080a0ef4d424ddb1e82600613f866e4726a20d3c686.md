## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes 3DMolFormer, a dual-channel transformer (GPT) framework that handles both protein-ligand docking and pocket-aware 3D drug design via a parallel sequence representation of atom tokens and 3D coordinates, with pre-training and task-specific fine-tuning (supervised docking + RL for generation). Reviewers rate the paper 8, 6, 6, 5. The majority (scores 6,6,5) find the framework novel and promising but raise concerns about experimental rigor, missing benchmarks, fairness of RL reward design, lack of ablation studies, and missing details. The highest-scoring reviewer (8) emphasizes strong results and clear presentation, while the lowest (5) is more critical of benchmarking and threshold choices.

### Strengths
- Novel dual-channel transformer framework that unifies protein-ligand docking and pocket-aware 3D drug design in a single model, leveraging their duality.
- Parallel sequence format (discrete SMILES tokens + continuous 3D coordinates) effectively handles 3D information.
- Large-scale pre-training on mixed dataset alleviates data limitations; GPT-like architecture provides scalability in model parameters and data volume.
- Strong empirical results on both fine-tuning tasks, outperforming previous approaches.
- Clear and well-structured presentation (noted by one reviewer).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing figure:** Several reviewer references to “Figure 1” are absent from the preprint, hindering understanding of the architecture.
- **Insufficient ablation and statistical rigor:** Results are based on a single run with no variance analysis, making statistical significance unclear. No ablation studies are performed on key components (dual-channel architecture, pretraining strategy, freezing of weights during RL ligand sampling). The RL reward function is overly simplistic, treating QED and SA as binary constraints (e.g., SA > 0.59 → 1, else 0; QED > 4.39 → 1, else 0) rather than continuous objectives. More advanced multi-objective RL techniques (Pareto optimization, adaptive scalarization) are not explored, and the thresholds are chosen arbitrarily without justification.
- **Missing baselines and benchmarks:**  
  - For docking, the method is not evaluated on PoseBusters against state-of-the-art models like AlphaFold3 and Chai-1 (which achieve ~76–77% accuracy) or Uimol-docking v2. How 3DMolFormer compares in accuracy and speed is unclear.  
  - The docking evaluation reports only RMSD; other pose-quality metrics (steric clashes, strain-energy) are omitted.  
  - For 3D generation, standard benchmarks such as CheckPose or DrugPose are not used. The molecule distribution (size, logP, number of rotatable bonds) is not reported, and the protein targets used for generation are not described (diversity, selection criteria).
- **SE(3) symmetry not explicitly modeled:** The method relies only on data augmentation rather than an SE(3)-equivariant architecture. This limitation is acknowledged but not sufficiently discussed or ablated.
- **Potential overfitting in drug design:** The RL fine-tuning directly uses Vina score, QED, and SA as the reward function, which may unfairly skew evaluations toward these metrics. Additional metrics (e.g., the delta score from [1]) are needed to assess whether the model overfits to the Vina oracle and to validate general drug-likeness.
- **Docking formula unclear:** The formula for redocking lacks a standardized reference energy; different targets would require different reference scores, and no standard docking energy is applied.
- **SA threshold misinterpretation:** The paper states a score above 0.59 is given a value of 1 for synthetic accessibility, but usually lower SA scores indicate better synthetic accessibility. While the appendix may clarify, the main text is ambiguous. The choice of this threshold and the QED threshold (4.39) is not justified.
- **Speed-accuracy trade-offs:** No comparison of inference speed or computational cost relative to AlphaFold3, Chai-1, or other docking/generation methods is provided.
