## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the lack of conditional coverage in conformal prediction by proposing a method that targets coverage conditional on a reduced set of two variables: the classifier’s confidence and a nonparametric trust score measuring deviation from the Bayes classifier. The work builds on the method of Gibbs et al. (2023) and introduces a function class optimized for improving conditional validity. Experiments on ImageNet, Places365, and Fitzpatrick 17k show improved conditional coverage properties compared to standard conformal prediction, though with larger prediction set sizes. Reviewers generally agree the problem is important and the paper is well-written, but raise substantial concerns about the effectiveness of trust scores, missing theoretical support, insufficient baselines, computational costs, and limited domain scope. One reviewer (score 3) is notably more critical, arguing that the method does not achieve its intended goal and offers negligible improvement over naive approaches.

### Strengths
- Addresses a central limitation of conformal prediction—lack of conditional coverage—especially in high-dimensional settings. The targeted approach improves coverage where classifiers are overconfident, enhancing practical utility for high-stakes applications.
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and well-positioned within recent advances in conformal prediction. Extensive reproducibility efforts are appreciated.
- The use of trust scores as an interpretable proxy for agreement with the Bayes optimal classifier is a practical innovation, providing a low-dimensional signal for conditional coverage in high-dimensional input spaces.
- The method is tested on diverse image datasets (ImageNet, Places365, Fitzpatrick 17k), including long-tailed variants and a clinical dataset, demonstrating robustness across class imbalances and demographic subgroups.

### Weaknesses
- **Doubts about trust score effectiveness**: Multiple reviewers question whether trust scores and confidence adequately capture miscoverage. Reviewer 3 provides specific evidence: across all tables, the coverage gap for the Conf×Trust column differs substantially from the Conf×Rank column, indicating the trust score does not successfully proxy the rank function. Moreover, the improvement over Naive in terms of the Conf×Rank coverage gap is consistently negligible, suggesting limited practical benefit.
- **Lack of theoretical support**: Reviewer 1 notes the field expects strong theoretical underpinnings; the paper provides no theory (even in large-data regimes) that trust scores are sufficient to capture conditional coverage. Reviewer 3 calls the method theoretically sound in strengths but this is contradicted by the empirical evidence against trust scores.
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: Reviewer 1 requests comparisons with simpler low-dimensional function classes (e.g., a linear head on the representation layer or on CLIP embeddings). Reviewer 4 asks for comparisons to Cluster CP and Mondrian CP, especially on low-class datasets like CIFAR-10/100 where Mondrian CP achieves strong class-conditional coverage. Reviewer 1 also notes a missing reference to a related work on learning features for conditional coverage (arXiv:2404.17487).
- **Computational cost and scalability**: Reviewer 2 points out that computing trust scores and confidence across large datasets may be expensive despite using FAISS, especially with higher-degree polynomial functions; the paper does not fully address scalability for real-time or resource-constrained environments. Reviewer 4 suggests an ablation on the k parameter of the k-NN radius used in trust score computation.
- **Difficulty in setting binning thresholds**: The method requires binning samples by confidence and trust scores. Reviewer 2 notes that choices of bin sizes/boundaries significantly impact results, yet the paper provides no guidance on selecting these thresholds and no sensitivity analysis. Reviewer 3 asks whether different discretization methods were explored.
- **Limited domain scope**: Experiments are restricted to image datasets. Reviewer 2 asks about performance in NLP or tabular data, where nearest neighbors in embedding space may be less semantically meaningful and confidence-misclassification relationships differ.
- **Proposition 1 issues**: Reviewer 4 states that Proposition 1 is overly strict and its claim that higher temperature always yields larger prediction sets is inconsistent with prior work where the relationship is not monotonic; the constraint on \hat{q} is unrealistic since \hat{q} varies with temperature. The purpose of comparing coverage at different temperatures is unclear.
- **Larger prediction set sizes with minimal gain**: Reviewer 3 argues the method becomes overly conservative (larger average set sizes) while offering minimal improvement in the desired conditional coverage. Reviewer 4 agrees that significant conditional coverage improvement comes at the cost of larger sets. Reviewer 3 further notes that additional metrics common in prior work (e.g., WSC from APS, SSCV from RAPS) are missing and would help evaluate the method.
- **Additional reviewer questions treated as weaknesses**:  
  - How to adapt the approach if confidence/trust scores are unreliable?  
  - How to choose bin thresholds for different datasets?  
  - What about performance on non-image domains?  
  - Are there computational benchmarks for trust score and confidence threshold calculation?  
  - Did the long-tailed test sets maintain the same imbalanced distribution, and could that bias minority class evaluation?  
  - Could alternative encoders (e.g., CLIP) be used for trust score calculation to improve discriminative features?
