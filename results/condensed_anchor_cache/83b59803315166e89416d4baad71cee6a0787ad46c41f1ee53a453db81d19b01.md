## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RealTracker, a point tracking model with a simplified architecture and a semi-supervised training recipe that uses off-the-shelf trackers to generate pseudo-labels on real videos without annotations. It achieves state-of-the-art results on Kinetics, RGB-S, and DAVIS while using far less training data than prior work, and also studies scaling behavior with additional real unlabeled data.

### Strengths
- Addresses an interesting and important problem: enabling TAP models to train on real videos without manual annotations, moving beyond the common reliance on synthetic data.
- RealTracker’s simplified architecture (removing or simplifying components from prior trackers) is both lighter and faster, yet outperforms state-of-the-art methods such as BootsTAPIR and LocoTrack.
- The semi-supervised training protocol is simple and effective, achieving better results on multiple public datasets.
- The paper explores the scaling law of using more real unsupervised data, providing insights into how dataset size affects performance.
- Experimental analysis is comprehensive, and visualizations are impressive in demonstrating the model’s capabilities.
- The paper is well written and organized.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of pseudo-labeling approach**: Using trackers to pseudo-label unlabeled datasets is well explored (e.g., Dino-Tracker, DinoTracker3, and other related works [1,2,3,4] that should be discussed). The method is more an engineering combination of existing ideas than a theoretical contribution; no novel loss, optimization, or architecture component is introduced.
- **Heavy reliance on teacher model**: Performance depends strongly on the teacher model’s capabilities, creating a ceiling effect. The paper does not explore mitigating this dependency, such as using an ensemble of diverse teacher models or techniques to reduce the impact of inaccurate pseudo-labels. It is also unclear whether a single teacher model can adequately support training.
- **Missing ablations and experiments needed**:
  - Ablation studies verifying that removing/simplifying specific modules from prior trackers is beneficial, including both tracking performance and computational cost.
  - The authors should train CoTracker and TAPTR on the same Kub+15M data to verify RealTracker’s effectiveness under equal data conditions.
  - Visualizations of confidence and visibility outputs are suggested to further demonstrate the model’s behavior.
- **Lack of domain adaptation innovation**: The paper aims to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap but only uses real-data fine-tuning and teacher voting. No advanced domain adaptation techniques (adversarial domain adaptation, domain-invariant feature learning, discrepancy-based methods) are explored, limiting the contribution in this area.
- **Unclear or missing analysis of results**:
  - Table 2: Training set matters – why does Kub+15M outperform Kub on average? What is the difference between the two training sets?
  - Why does the offline variant perform better than the online variant? Intuitively the opposite might be expected.
  - Table 6: Why does SIFT give the best results among the options? This needs explanation.
- **Motion and occlusion analysis absent**:
  - It is unclear what types of motions were tested (e.g., parallax required, zooming with no parallax).
  - The percentage of occlusion (both in terms of coverage of the object and time occluded) is not clearly tested.
- **Failure cases and limitations**: The paper does not explore failure cases or limitations of the algorithm, which should be discussed.
- **Terminology issue**: The term “self-supervised fine-tuning” is questionable; the approach is better described as teacher-student or pseudo-labeling, as supervision comes from external models, not from the data itself.
- **Missing question about speed**: For the online variant, what is the overall tracking speed (fps) given an online testing video? (Table 2 reports per-frame and per-point times, but overall speed is not given.)
- **Missing references**: The related work should include more pseudo-label-based tracker training approaches [1,2,3,4] as indicated by one reviewer.
