## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a general guidance framework for discrete state-space generative models (diffusion and flow) based on continuous-time Markov chains (CTMC). It derives predictor-guided and predictor-free guidance rate matrices, and a Taylor-approximated acceleration. Experiments on small molecules, DNA sequences, protein sequences, and small images demonstrate feasibility, with comparisons to DiGress and Dirichlet FM.

### Strengths
- Provides a general guidance mechanism for discrete state-space diffusion and flow models, applicable to models built on CTMC (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Derives both predictor-guided and predictor-free rate matrices with intuitive comparison to continuous guidance (Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrated on diverse and relevant applications: small molecules, DNA, protein sequences (Reviewers 1, 2, 3), and small images (Appendix F.1) (Reviewer 3).
- Targets an important and difficult problem (Reviewer 4).
- The use of continuous-time processes to enable guidance is well-founded (Reviewer 4).
- Paper has logical structure, clear expression, and is nicely presented (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Novel contribution to the field (Reviewer 2).
- Reproducibility ensured by providing source code (Reviewer 3).

One reviewer (Reviewer 4, rating 8 vs 6 from others) was more positive, emphasizing the importance of the problem, clear presentation, and well-founded approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of error analysis:** No precise theoretical guarantees or error analysis for CTMC numerical integration and approximation from trained predictors; it is unclear how errors propagate and affect sample quality (Reviewer 1). Also missing discussion of trade-offs between efficiency and accuracy (Reviewer 1).
- **Generation quality overlooked:** The paper focuses on showing guidance effects rather than generation quality; no clear benchmark comparison to existing methods to assess practical utility (Reviewer 1). Empirical results are relatively close to DiGress and Dirichlet FM (e.g., "as predictor-free guidance for DirFM-CFG and DG-PFG behaved comparably") (Reviewer 3).
- **Innovation concern:** The core idea extends continuous-state guidance to discrete state spaces without introducing significant novel theoretical insights (Reviewer 1). (In contrast, Reviewer 2 considers it a novel contribution.)
- **Missing background on discrete diffusion models:** The paper omits a clear introduction to forward/backward processes, how the forward process gradually adds noise, and the objective function for training the rate matrix \(R_t(x,\tilde{x})\) (Reviewer 2). 
- **Insufficient algorithmic details:** Missing step-by-step descriptions of training procedures for both predictor-guided and predictor-free guidance (optimization, loss functions, predictor network architecture) (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear distinction between predictor-free and predictor-guided:** Equation (4) appears to be a direct substitution of Equation (2); it needs clarification on how predictor-free guidance eliminates the classifier-like likelihood \(p_t(y|x_t)\) (Reviewer 2).
- **Taylor-approximated guidance practicalities:** Unclear how it operates (e.g., storing \(\nabla_x \log p_t(y|x)\) instead of full distribution); what if discrete states have no discernible order for continuous embedding? (Reviewer 2).
- **Gamma range and reporting:** Line 216 suggests \(\gamma \in [0,1]\), but experiments use values 2, 10, 100; discuss range immediately. Also, \(\gamma\) values per experiment not clearly reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Mathematical presentation too quick:** Equation (3) only valid for \(\tilde{x} \neq x\); flow conservation for diagonal not included in main text. In Appendix C, Equation (14) differs from Eqs (2)/(3) in numerator, with simplification only discussed later in C.6 (Reviewer 3).
- **Experiments only on flow matching:** Despite claiming the method works for both diffusion and flow models, experiments use only flow matching (line 297) (Reviewer 4). Is this a weakness of the model? (Reviewer 4).
- **Simultaneous jumps in discretized time:** In continuous time, probability of jumps in two dimensions simultaneously is zero, but with Euler integration, two dimensions can jump simultaneously; does this cause problems? (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear conditioning and retraining effort:** The conditioning mechanism is not clearly explained. For Discrete Guidance, an unconditional flow-matching model was trained and then predictor guidance was applied; how was the predictor trained? Are training-free guidance strategies achievable? (Reviewer 3). Also question about training-free guidance for inverse problems (e.g., from ICLR 2023 papers) (Reviewer 3).
- **Term "discrete guidance" too general:** Given competing method DiGress; title potentially problematic (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues:**
  - Line 109: Claims gradient of predictor is easily accessible in continuous DMs, but it is based on heuristics (Reviewer 3).
  - Line 140: "Why 'However'?" (Reviewer 3).
  - Line 145: Clarify that the rate function is positive but can take negative value when \(\tilde{x} = x\); precise the Kronecker delta meaning (Reviewer 3).
  - Line 412: "an conditional flow-matching model" typo (Reviewer 3).
  - Line 897: Accent issue for Clément Vignac (Reviewer 3).
- **Specific question about guidance strengths:** Why use the same \(\gamma \in \{1,3,6,10,20\}\) as reported by Stark et al. (2024) for DirFM-CG? Is the formalism similar? (Reviewer 3).
