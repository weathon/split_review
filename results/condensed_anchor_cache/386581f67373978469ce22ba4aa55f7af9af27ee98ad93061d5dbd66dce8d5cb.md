## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Conv‑LoRA, a parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning (PEFT) method for the Segment Anything Model (SAM). Conv‑LoRA adds ultra‑lightweight convolutional parameters and a Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) module into Low‑Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to inject image‑related inductive biases into SAM’s plain ViT encoder. The method freezes the pretrained SAM and trains only the lightweight additions, enabling end‑to‑end multi‑class semantic segmentation without prompts. Extensive experiments across medical, natural, agriculture, and remote‑sensing benchmarks show superior results compared to vanilla LoRA, VPT, and other adaptor‑based methods, and in several settings it outperforms full fine‑tuning of all parameters.

### Strengths
- Novel combination of LoRA with convolutional bottlenecks and a Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) module to reintroduce local image priors into a ViT encoder – a valuable contribution to PEFT for vision transformers. (R1, R2, R3)
- Thorough experimental assessment across diverse domains (medical, natural, agriculture, remote sensing) with most experiments run three times and compared against a substantial set of baselines (LoRA, VPT, adaptors, full fine‑tuning). (R1, R2, R4)
- Consistently outperforms vanilla LoRA and other PEFT baselines; improvements are small but robust, and Conv‑LoRA even surpasses full fine‑tuning (100% parameters) on several benchmarks. (R1, R2, R4)
- Improves fine‑grained segmentation (e.g., slim edges) and enables high‑level semantic segmentation, addressing SAM’s original foreground‑background limitation. (R1, R3, R4)
- Paper is well‑structured, clearly written, and the concepts are coherent and well‑motivated. (R1, R3, R4)
- Expands SAM’s applicability to specialized domains and provides an end‑to‑end multi‑class segmentation solution without requiring prompts. (R1, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear explanation of mechanism**: The claim that Conv‑LoRA reinjects convolutional inductive biases into ViT features that may have already lost spatial locality is unintuitive and not sufficiently justified; the authors should present it as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion (R1). The motivation for combining MoE with convolutions is not clearly articulated – the design is reminiscent of Inception structures, and the paper does not justify why MoE is necessary over simple averaging or fixed multi‑scale convolutions (R2, R4). The work is purely empirical and lacks reasoning for *how* the model improves performance (R3).
- **Missing ablations and component analysis**: No ablation study separates the individual contributions of the convolutional layer and the MoE module, making it unclear which part drives the gains and whether they interact (R2). Missing also: ablations on different kernel sizes and their combinations with/without MoE (R2); an ablation on each component’s effect (R3); and a row for scaling factor 8 in Table 4 to examine the effect of upsampling scales (R1, Q4). A comparison with an Inception‑like structure (parallel convolutions without MoE) is needed to evaluate the novelty of the proposed architecture (R2).
- **Misrepresentation of prior work**: The paper claims that prior SAM adaptations (Zhang & Liu 2023 – SAMed, and Shaharabany et al. 2023) fail to address SAM’s limitations regarding high‑level semantic information and end‑to‑end automatic deployment – but both works actually achieve these. The characterization should be revised; a direct experimental comparison with SAMed is not required, but accuracy in describing those works is (R1, Q3).
- **Lack of training details and computational cost comparison**: The paper does not specify which parts of SAM are frozen, how Conv‑LoRA is integrated into the ViT encoder, which loss function is used, or the optimization strategy (R2). Missing also are comparisons of training time, inference time, and memory consumption among the PEFT methods (R2). The authors should clarify the rationale for freezing the prompt encoder (R3, Q).
- **Scale selection and tuning effort**: The MoE scaling factors (expert scales) vary across datasets (Table 4), requiring extra tuning for new datasets. The paper provides no guidance for scale selection or sensitivity analysis (R2). This contrasts with simpler Inception‑style fixed multi‑scale convolutions that may need less tuning (R2).
- **Performance improvement is marginal**: While robust, the gains over vanilla LoRA and other PEFT baselines are very small, raising questions about practical significance (R3). (R1 also notes the improvement is small but robust, but R3 explicitly calls it marginal.)
- **Missing baseline comparison**: No comparison with training a segmentation model from scratch (random initialization), making it unclear whether the pretrained SAM backbone transfers effectively to downstream semantic segmentation tasks (R4).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Clarify where in the ViT encoder Conv‑LoRA is added (R1, Q5); define all variable names consistently (e.g., in Figure 4 and Appendix A) (R1, Q9); label Figures 8, 9, 10 individually (R1, Q8); add example images for the medical domain (R1, Q10); spell out “low‑rank adaptation” in the abstract before the abbreviation (R1, Q11). These do not affect the core findings but should be addressed.
