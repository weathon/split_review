## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies learning additive Gaussian multi-index models of the form \(f_*(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^{P} \big( \operatorname{He}_2(\langle \mathbf{v}_k^*, \mathbf{x}\rangle) + \operatorname{He}_{2L}(\langle \mathbf{v}_k^*, \mathbf{x}\rangle) \big)\) (with \(L\ge2\) even, directions orthonormal, and link function known), using a two-layer neural network trained with a two-phase online SGD scheme. The main claim is that the model can be “strongly” learned (exact direction recovery) with \(\tilde{O}(d\,\mathrm{poly}(P))\) samples/steps, whereas relying only on the lowest-degree (quadratic) Hermite term would only give subspace recovery. The analysis reveals two learning phases: first the relevant subspace is recovered via the second-order term, then individual directions are recovered via the higher-order term.

Scores: 5, 8, 5. One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 8) was notably more positive, praising the clarity and importance of the problem, while the other two gave 5 and raised concerns about scope, motivation, and lack of experiments.

### Strengths
- Exceptionally well written and easy to follow; the proof outline (Sections 3 and 4) is incredibly clear and gives an insightful, intuitive picture of the two learning phases.
- The problem is important for developing a theory of two-layer networks, and studying the interaction between different Hermite terms is a needed piece.
- Nice to show that higher-order terms can be useful, contrasting with the majority of work that focuses solely on the first non-zero Hermite coefficient.
- The informal discussion of how the SGD argument derives from gradient flow analysis and the “user-friendly” martingale argument (Ben Arous 2021) is a nice addition to the literature.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope and specificity.** The setting is very restricted: orthonormal directions, link function fixed to \(\operatorname{He}_2 + \operatorname{He}_{2L}\), known link function, noiseless labels. It is unclear whether the results extend to more general link functions with information exponent 2 (e.g., \(\operatorname{He}_2 + \operatorname{He}_3\)), to non-orthogonal or approximately orthogonal weights, or to noisy labels.  
  - Oko et al. (2024) already showed that for functions with information exponent >2, only the first non-zero term matters, so the presented picture is never relevant there. Even for information exponent 2, after subspace recovery one can subtract the quadratic activation to obtain a model with higher information exponent, potentially bypassing the claimed benefit.
- **Lack of numerical experiments.** The paper would greatly benefit from experiments implementing Algorithm 2.3. The learning algorithm differs from standard joint gradient descent (weights in both layers updated together), and it is not clear that the same qualitative behavior would hold in practice. Numerical experiments could also help justify assumptions such as the independence of first-layer weight dynamics.
- **Misleading motivation around “information exponent.”** The information exponent (IE) was introduced for well-specified single-index models with one-pass SGD, and several recent works (Abbe et al. 2022; Dandi et al. 2024; Damien et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2024; etc.) already show that IE is not a robust or fundamental notion of computational complexity for multi-index models or for other algorithms. Motivating the results around IE is therefore a stretch.
- **Missing technical clarification/comparisons.**  
  - The discussion in L99‑103 about differences from Ben Arous et al. (2024) is confusing, especially in light of Appendix A (which uses a tensor decomposition reduction). The claim “not comparable” needs more precision.
  - The role of the regularization parameter \(\lambda\) in learning the second layer is not commented on; it is uncommon in standard two-layer network training.
- **Assumptions and realism.**  
  - The algorithm requires the first-layer weight vectors to be kept orthonormal. Would the results hold if they were initialized from a Normal distribution?
  - The dynamics of different first-layer neurons are assumed independent; this is not true in practice, especially when the second-layer coefficient \(a_0\) is not very small. The impact of such interactions (e.g., when \(a_0\) is larger) should be explored, at least numerically.
- **Typos and readability issues.**  
  - L278: Is \(k\in[d]\) a typo (should be \(k\in[P]\))?
  - L304: \(\|v_{\le P}\|^2\) is missing from the numerator of the last term.
  - L352: The RHS should be \(4L\big(1 - \|v_{\le P}\|^2 + c_L(\|v_{\le P}\|^2 - v_1^2)\big)v_1^{2L} \ge 4L c_L(\|v_{\le P}\|^2 - v_1^2)v_1^{2L}\).
  - The definition of \(v_k = v_k^{*\top} v\) appears to be introduced only after it is used (L278).
  - Theorem 2.1 mentions \(\epsilon_*\) without relating it to \(\epsilon\).
  - Using the same index for target neurons (\(k\in[P]\)) and model neurons (\(k\in[m]\)) hurts readability; different indices would help.
  - Normalization step in Stage 1 (Section 2.3) may be missing; the ODE in L278 seems to account only for the gradient step and not the projection, contradicting the claim that the second term comes from projection.
- **Unaddressed follow-up questions (overlap with criticisms).**  
  - Is strong recovery possible under label noise?
  - What changes if the target includes \(\operatorname{He}_3\) instead of \(\operatorname{He}_{2L}\)?
  - Could the analysis be extended to approximately orthogonal weights, and what effect would that have?
  - How does the algorithm compare to standard joint SGD (updating both layers simultaneously) – would the same phases emerge?
- **Related literature omissions.**  
  - Reference Lee et al. (2024) is concurrent with Arnaboldi et al. (2024); both should be acknowledged when citing that result.
  - For single-index models, the statistical and computational sample complexity results of Damien et al. (2024) generalize those from Barbier et al. (2019); this should be noted in L44‑47.
  - Beyond SGD, computational weak-learnability results for multi-index functions appear in Chen & Meka (ICML 2020) for polynomials and in Troiani et al. (2024) for general link functions with linear sample complexity.
  - Convergence rates for gradient flow on multi-index targets (Bietti et al. 2023) are closely related; the relationship should be discussed.
