## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes FairLoRA, a fairness-regularized variant of LoRA that reduces per-class variance in loss to mitigate bias in vision models (ViT, DiNO, CLIP). Experiments on Aircraft (100 classes) and Waterbirds (2 classes) show some effectiveness. The paper also studies factors like LoRA rank and distribution shifts. Reviewers agree the topic is important and the method is simple, but disagree on novelty and experimental rigor: Reviewer 1 finds limited novelty and poor experiments, while Reviewers 2 and 3 consider the experiments extensive and results convincing.

### Strengths
- The investigation of fairness in LoRA is interesting and timely (R1, R3).
- First to introduce a fairness‑based fine‑tuning technique using LoRA (R2; also implicit in R3’s “important topic”).
- Presents multiple fairness metrics (e.g., Equalized Opportunity Difference) and extensive experiments with colorful figures that enhance clarity (R2).
- Examines factors influencing fairness – LoRA rank, distribution shifts, task types – which contributes to the research community (R2).
- Motivation is clear; the proposed method of reducing group‑wise variance is simple yet effective (R3).
- Evaluated on several visual foundation models (ViT, DiNO, CLIP); results are convincing (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Paper readability and framing** (R1, R3):
  - Figures 1, 2, and 5 are poorly illustrated with tiny fonts; hard to read (R1).
  - Fairness is not well defined for the classification tasks used; no discussion of the relationship between fairness, good performance, and good generalization (R1).
  - The formulation on page 4, line 208 lacks a numbered equation (R3).
  - The definition of “Equalized Opportunity Difference (EOD)” is abstract; a textual explanation is needed (R3).
- **Group‑division ambiguity** (R2, R3):
  - How groups are divided (page 4, line 212 and Section 5) is not clearly explained (R3). Are they inherent dataset divisions, random splits, or based on VLM inference performance? Reviewer 2 warns that different division strategies could affect fairness conclusions.
  - The relationship between these groups and “category groups” (e.g., cats vs. dogs) is unclear; fairness across categories is a natural interpretation but is not directly addressed (R3).
- **Experimental limitations** (R1, R2):
  - Only two datasets – Aircraft (100 classes) and Waterbirds (2 classes) – are used, which is insufficient; Waterbirds in particular is a poor choice for demonstrating fairness (R1).
  - No experiments on long‑tail distributions or comparisons with standard long‑tail strategies (R1).
  - Only per‑group *min* and *average* metrics are reported, not the best‑group metric; it is doubtful whether the best group is degraded by the proposed method (R1).
  - The analysis of distribution‑shift handling relies solely on FID scores; t‑SNE visualizations before/after fine‑tuning (or comparing vanilla LoRA vs. FairLoRA) would more directly show whether learned representations become less clustered by sensitive attributes (R2).
  - On the Geode dataset, FairLoRA achieves a significantly lower “Eod Max” value than other methods (Figs. 4, 7). No detailed analysis is given to explain why, or whether this trend holds across architectures and LoRA ranks (R2).
  - Sudden performance drops at specific LoRA ranks (e.g., rank‑32 in Fig. 5 for Accuracy and Min F1; rank‑16 in Fig. 9) are not analyzed; it is unclear whether this pattern is consistent across models/datasets and what it implies for rank selection (R2).
- **Scope and novelty** (R1, R2, R3):
  - Minimizing group‑loss variance is straightforward and not deeply explained; more justification is needed for why the method does not degenerate performance (R1).
  - Several concurrent works [1][2] (May 2024) also study LoRA fairness (with similar metrics like EOD and LoRA rank analysis) but are not discussed. Their findings (e.g., “lower ranks retain original model bias” vs. the paper’s conclusions about rank necessity) need clarification (R2).
  - Only recognition tasks are addressed; the method’s generalizability to fine‑grained tasks like segmentation (where category imbalance is common) is unexplored (R3).

**Minority view:** Reviewer 1 is significantly more negative, finding the paper difficult to read, the novelty limited, and the experiments (two datasets, no long‑tail) unconvincing. Reviewers 2 and 3 are more positive, praising the first‑of‑its‑kind fairness regularizer and the extensive experimental analysis.
