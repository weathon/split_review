## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers rated the paper 6, finding it generally clear and well-presented. The core idea—decomposing a model into submodules (layers, attention heads, MLPs) that exhibit higher linearity than the full model, then independently merging those submodules with a closed-form solution for the merging weights—is seen as novel and intuitive by most. The non-linearity score, the ablation on merging granularity, and the ability to work with only 30 data points per task are appreciated. However, several reviewers criticise the modest and inconsistent performance gains over vanilla task arithmetic, the limited evaluation (only Llama-2 7B/13B, only math/coding/translate tasks, no GLUE or CV benchmarks), and the omission of important baselines from related literature (Git Re‑Basin, Zipit!, MuDSC, MoE merging, router-based methods). The observation that submodules are more linear than the whole model is considered by two reviewers to be obvious (nonlinearity accumulates), while the other two reviewers view it as a novel contribution. Additional concerns include the lack of discussion on non-linear activation functions, unstable head-level merging, and the need for experiments with more than three tasks.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, easy to follow, and provides detailed explanations in the appendix (all reviewers).
- The idea of focusing on submodule linearity (layers, attention, MLP) rather than global linearization is novel and well-motivated (R1, R3, R4).
- The proposed non-linearity score is intuitive and effectively visualised (Figures 1 and 3) to motivate the approach (R3, R4).
- Deriving a closed-form solution for the optimal merging weights based on linearity is an elegant contribution (R1).
- The ablation study on merging granularity (model-level vs. layer-level vs. attention/MLP-level vs. head-level) is valuable and supports the claims (R1, R4).
- The method is data-efficient, requiring only 30 data points per task (R1).
- The approach shows consistent performance across different model scales (Llama-2-7B and Llama-2-13B) and merging granularities (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Obviousness of finding**: Two reviewers note that the observation that submodules are more linear than the overall model is unsurprising because the nonlinearity of each submodule accumulates when composing the full model; Figures 1 and 2 therefore add little surprise (R2, R3). The other two reviewers do not raise this objection, and consider the contribution novel.
- **Modest and inconsistent performance**: The performance improvement over vanilla task arithmetic is slight and not consistent across settings. In particular, Table 1 and Table 2 show that the proposed method is still outperformed by prior methods on “coding & translate” and “math & coding” (R2, R3). The added complexity of layerwise merging is therefore not clearly justified (R3).
- **Limited evaluation**: The paper tests only two Llama-2 architectures (7B, 13B) and only on math, coding, and translation tasks. Previous works (e.g. Task Arithmetic, DARE) also evaluate on GLUE, OpenLLM, and computer vision benchmarks. A more exhaustive evaluation is needed to rule out lucky choices of models and datasets (R3).
- **Missing baselines**: The paper does not compare against several relevant works: (i) global linearization approaches extended to layerwise merging, such as Git Re‑Basin, Zipit!, and MuDSC (R2, R3); (ii) Mixture‑of‑Experts merging approaches that linearly weight submodules at the activation level (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00433) (R1); (iii) router-based methods that can achieve task-specific specialisation without linearity assumptions (R1). Reviewer 1 suggests that the method in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13025 could serve as a useful baseline.
- **Lack of discussion on non-linear activation functions**: It is unclear how submodule non-linearity scores (Figures 1‑2) can be near zero when the submodules contain non-linear activations (e.g., GELU, Swish). The paper does not explain whether these activation functions were removed or how they affect the linearity assumption. A discussion is needed, including whether certain activation families (ReLU, GELU, Swish) are more compatible with the framework (R4).
- **Ignoring interdependencies between merging weights**: The current optimisation updates each weighting coefficient independently, assuming no interaction between submodules. Reviewer 1 suggests that incorporating second-order (Hessian-based) information or using routing methods could better account for submodule interdependency and might be more powerful.
- **Unstable head-level merging**: Table 3 shows that merging at the head level gives unstable outcomes, hypothesised to be due to averaging out representation functionalities. The paper does not propose a method to avoid this instability (R4).
- **Effect of model depth and data size**: The 7B model shows stronger non-linearity than the 13B model. The paper does not discuss the influence of varying depth or data size, nor the implications for the universal approximation theorem (R4).
- **Need for more tasks and trend analysis**: The paper merges up to three models and shows that merging three gives a lower projection distance than merging two, but only two data points are insufficient to confirm a trend. Experiments with more tasks (e.g., 4 or 5 compatible tasks) would reveal whether the advantage over baselines increases or diminishes (R3). Additionally, what if no validation data is available? (R3)
- **Potential for finer granularity**: It is conceivable to decompose the model into even smaller components (groups, channels, neurons) that would exhibit even higher linearity, but this would introduce more merging parameters. The paper lacks a discussion of this trade-off (R2).
- **Redundant properties**: The three properties defined in Section 2 are somewhat redundant and could be simplified (R2).
- **Minor formatting issue**: In Property 2, the footnote marker placed over an equation looks like a power, causing confusion (R3).
