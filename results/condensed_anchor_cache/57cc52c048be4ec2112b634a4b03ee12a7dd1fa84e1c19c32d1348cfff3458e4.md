## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FPTQ, a novel W4A8 post-training quantization method for LLMs that combines 4-bit weight quantization (memory bandwidth saving) with 8-bit activation quantization (fast INT8 matrix computation). It uses group-wise weight quantization and a Logarithmic Activation Equalization (LAE) layer – applied when activation ranges lie between heuristically set thresholds (v0=15, v1=150) – along with per-token dynamic quantization for other layers. Experiments on BLOOM, LLaMA, and LLaMA-2 show competitive accuracy on MMLU, commonsense QA, etc. Reviewers note good readability and simplicity, but also highlight limited novelty (close to SmoothQuant), missing real inference speed benchmarks, heuristic hyperparameters, significant MMLU degradation vs FP16, and several unclear or missing details. One reviewer (score 6) was more positive about the clarity and practical usability; two reviewers (score 3) were more critical on novelty, evidence, and presentation.

### Strengths
- Well-organized, easy-to-follow paper with clear motivation and method description.  
- The proposed method is simple and straightforward to implement, making it potentially usable in real-world applications.  
- First PTQ approach to target W4A8 quantization for LLMs.  
- Extensive experiments on multiple model sizes from the LLaMA family, covering a variety of benchmarks.  
- Ablation studies with different calibration datasets demonstrate robustness of the approach.  
- Shows superior accuracy over QAT methods while requiring less data and time.  
- Achieves competitive W4A8 scores, making the configuration practically viable.

### Weaknesses
- **No real inference speed benchmarks**: The paper lacks actual speed measurements; Fig. 2b appears speculative and may exaggerate the benefit of W4A8. In compute-bound context decoding, the overhead of W4→W8 conversion could make W4A8 slower than W8A8. Real hardware results are needed to substantiate speed claims.  
- **Performance gap vs FP16**: Significant MMLU degradation (e.g., 2–3 points on LLaMA‑7B/13B) compared to FP16 may block real-world deployment.  
- **Limited novelty**: The method is very similar to SmoothQuant; the only difference is replacing the scaling factor formula with a logarithmic function of max activations. No proof is provided that this formula is superior, and no W4A8 SmoothQuant baseline is shown.  
- **Heuristic hyperparameters v0 and v1** (set to 15 and 150): No justification for these values, no ablation study on their sensitivity, and no principled way to choose them.  
- **Variations with calibration datasets (Table 6)**: The paper notes but does not analyze or mitigate the variations caused by different calibration data.  
- **Unclear meaning of bold numbers in Table 4** – not defined in the caption or text.  
- **Two strategies mentioned at bottom of page 5** are not clearly explained.  
- **KV cache quantization** is mentioned on page 6 but no details are provided.  
- **Missing determination of clipping value** when using static activation quantization.  
- **No comparative analysis with LLM‑QAT** using MMLU scores.  
- **Typo in Table 5**: The original accuracy for HS, LLaMA‑7B appears abnormally low.  
- **No results showing how poorly SmoothQuant performs at W4A8**, which would clarify the advantage of the proposed method.
