## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel XAI method called Sharing Ratio Decomposition (SRD) that explains model decisions from a vector perspective (instead of neuron level), incorporates inactive neurons via APOP, and recursively decomposes Pointwise Feature Vectors. Experiments on ImageNet-S50 (752 samples) using VGG16 and ResNet50 backbones evaluate Localization, Complexity, Faithfulness, and Robustness. SRD outperforms prior methods (pre-2021) across these desiderata. The writing is well-organized and includes rich visualizations.

### Strengths
- **Vector perspective and APOP novelty**: SRD adopts a vector viewpoint to capture nonlinear interactions between filters, and introduces APOP (Activation-Pattern-Only Prediction) – an interesting observation that retention of activation states alone preserves inference capability, emphasizing the role of inactive neurons. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Comprehensive evaluation along multiple XAI desiderata**: The paper systematically evaluates SRD on Localization, Complexity, Faithfulness, and Robustness, with Table 2 showing strong quantitative results against many baseline XAI methods. (Reviewer 1,3)
- **Rich qualitative results**: Visual comparisons in the main paper and supplement clearly contrast SRD with prior methods, aiding human judgment of explainability. (Reviewer 1,4)
- **Rigorous deduction**: The forward and backward propagation calculations of SRD are clearly derived and sound. (Reviewer 2)
- **Strong readability and organization**: Language is fluent, and the paper includes extensive visualizations and thorough experimental validation. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset and test set**: All experiments are conducted on ImageNet-S50 (752 samples), not the full ImageNet validation set. This raises concerns about generalizability and comparability with standard benchmarks (e.g., perturbation, deletion/insertion). Testing on a larger, more diverse dataset is needed. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Restricted backbone architectures**: Only VGG16 and ResNet50 are tested. Experiments on other architectures (e.g., DenseNets, Transformers) and different depths/shallow vs. deep layers are missing, limiting evidence of general applicability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons with recent methods**: All compared methods are from 2014–2021; many CAM-based methods from the last two years are omitted. This weakens the claim of state-of-the-art performance. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Method description (Section 3) needs clarity**: The presentation is overly verbal; a stepwise or pseudocode description would help readers (especially beginners) follow the procedure (feedforward, compute PFV, decompose, compute sharing ratio backward, compute relevance). (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear source of SRD gains**: The paper does not clearly analyze which factor drives improvements – vector perspective, inactive-neuron consideration, PFV decomposition, or a combination. An ablation analysis isolating contributions is missing. (Reviewer 1)
- **APOP connection to attribution unclear**: While APOP is an interesting observation, its relevance to the core interpretability method is not well articulated. The paper does not explain how APOP helps attribute model decisions or how it directly informs SRD’s design. (Reviewer 3)
- **Equation 4 normalization issue**: The modified sharing ratio in Eq. (4) does not sum to 1. This may cause problems; normalization would be expected. (Reviewer 2)
- **Choice of target layer not justified**: The paper targets Conv5_3 for VGG16 and avgpool for ResNet50 without explaining why these specific layers were chosen or how the choice affects results. The impact of using adjacent layers is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Input resolution inconsistency across methods**: Different methods are evaluated at different input resolutions; the paper does not explain how this ensures fair comparison. (Reviewer 4)
- **No discussion on adversarial attack susceptibility**: The introduction claims existing methods are susceptible to adversarial attacks, yet the paper does not provide any related experiments or discussion on SRD’s robustness in that context. (Reviewer 4)
- **Imbalance in content organization**: The experimental description is limited, while the related work section is extensive; results need more in-depth analysis and discussion. The conclusion is lengthy and verbose. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient persuasion of performance gains**: The experimental comparisons and performance improvements lack strong persuasiveness without broader datasets and backbones. (Reviewer 4)
- **Custom metrics differ from standard XAI evaluation**: The metrics used are quite different from those commonly used in other XAI works; the authors do not explain why these particular metrics were chosen. (Reviewer 2)
