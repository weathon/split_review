## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents CryoFM, a flow-matching-based foundation model for cryo-EM density maps. It comprises two components: CryoFM-S for high-resolution local details and CryoFM-L for medium-to-low-resolution overall shape, trained on 38,626 maps from EMDB. A flow posterior sampling method is introduced to use CryoFM as a flexible prior for multiple inverse problems without fine-tuning. The model is evaluated on four tasks—density modification (spectral noise denoising), anisotropic denoising, missing wedge restoration (cryo-ET), and ab initio reconstruction from 2D projections—achieving state-of-the-art performance on most tasks compared to baselines such as DeepEMhancer, EMReady, spIsoNet, and cryoSPARC.

### Strengths
- **Novel foundation model for cryo-EM density maps.** Learning a data-driven prior from a large database of high-quality maps is promising and novel, with potential benefits for many downstream tasks (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Versatile across multiple tasks.** CryoFM is applied to four relevant inverse problems (3D denoising under spectral and anisotropic noise, missing wedge filling, and ab initio reconstruction) and performs as well as or better than baselines in most cases (Table 1, Table 2, Figure 7) (Reviewers 1, 2). The approach requires no fine-tuning for new tasks.
- **Technical soundness of flow matching and posterior sampling.** The use of flow matching to capture the prior distribution and the flow posterior sampling method is technically sound (Reviewer 3).
- **Competitive speed.** CryoFM appears faster than other baselines (Reviewer 1).
- **High-quality figures.** All figures are of high quality (Reviewer 2).
- **Testing on real-world ab initio data.** Ab initio reconstruction experiments include two real-world datasets (Reviewer 2).
- **Well-organized paper.** Overall structure is clear (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Mathematical description is sloppy and contains errors.** Several equations have sign mistakes, missing expectations, and unclear notation (Reviewer 1). Examples:
   - Eq. (2): implied vector field should be \(\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0\) not \(\mathbf{x}_0 - \mathbf{x}_1\) (Reviewer 1).
   - Line 172: loss missing expectation over \(t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]\), \(\mathbf{x}_0 \sim p_0\), \(\mathbf{x}_1 \sim p_1\), and sign error (Reviewer 1).
   - Line 196: gradient update adds likelihood term instead of subtracting it, inconsistent with positive \(\lambda_t\) in Algorithms (Reviewer 1).
   - Algorithm 1 missing time increment \(\Delta t\); loop inclusive of \(t=0\) can produce \(\mathbf{x}_{-\Delta t}\); “increasing the loss” is counterintuitive (Reviewer 1).
   - Symbol \(\theta\) overloaded (model parameters vs. tilt angle) (Reviewer 1).
   - Notation inconsistent: \(v_t(\mathbf{x}_t)\), \(v_\theta(t,\mathbf{x}_t)\), and vector fields not boldfaced as per guidelines (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 also finds the flow-matching theory very difficult to understand, with key concepts like “probability density path” unexplained and the variable \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0\) undefined (random variable or deterministic function?) (Reviewer 2).

2. **No evaluation on experimental data (real noise).** All denoising experiments use synthetic noise (spectral noise model). Testing on real cryo-EM datasets with realistic noise (microscope artifacts, ice contamination, etc.) is necessary to validate real-world effectiveness (Reviewers 1, 3). For ab initio, the paper uses synthetic noisy projections (Reviewer 1 questions the success rate; Reviewer 4 notes only class averages are used rather than raw noisy projections).

3. **Insufficient detail and context for baselines.** Baselines DeepEMhancer, EMReady, spIsoNet, cryoSPARC are introduced in only 4 lines; their mechanisms, differences, and why they were chosen are not adequately explained (Reviewers 2, 4). A comparison with the classical Gaussian prior (Scheres et al.) is missing (Reviewer 2).

4. **Missing discussion of related work and novelty.** The related work omits recent protein diffusion models like Chroma (2023) and DiffModeler (2024) that also operate on density maps or structures; the novelty of the flow-matching variant used is unclear—standard vs. modified elements should be explicitly stated (Reviewers 2, 4). The motivation for using a flow-based model over other generative models is not clarified (Reviewer 4).

5. **Anisotropic denoising experiment is poorly motivated.** The setup (noise affecting some orientations more than others) is not clearly connected to a real measurement setting beyond the cryo-ET missing wedge (which is separately evaluated); it appears intermediate between cryo-EM and cryo-ET noise models without clear added value (Reviewers 2, 4).

6. **Ab initio reconstruction comparisons are limited and potentially unfair.** The method uses clean class averages as input, while cryoSPARC uses many noisy projections directly; this difference raises questions about comparability (Reviewer 2). Results are similar or slightly worse than cryoSPARC, making them not compelling (Reviewer 4). Comparisons with state-of-the-art methods like CryoDRGN2 or CryoFIRE are missing (Reviewer 3). The ability to handle heterogeneous datasets or conformational variability is not demonstrated (Reviewer 3). The paper does not show whether CryoFM can be used for high-resolution refinement beyond initialization (Reviewers 2, 4).

7. **No release of code or pretrained model.** No source code is provided, which would help clarify mathematical details and enable reproducibility. The dataset list for pretraining is not reported, making it unclear whether test examples overlap with training data (Reviewers 1, 4). While the authors mention making the dataset public (figshare) in the rebuttal, the link is not yet in the paper (Reviewer 2).

8. **Training dataset and data preprocessing choices unclear.** “Problematic cases” and the reason for removing maps >576 Å are not defined. Helical structures are removed from pretraining, yet test evaluation involves enhancing helices – this inconsistency is confusing (Reviewer 4). The complete list of PDB/EMDB entries used for pretraining is not provided (Reviewer 1). Data augmentation motivations are not explained (Reviewer 4).

9. **Ab initio experiments show only a few samples (4 examples), one per setting.** The success rate or expected variability across runs is not reported; it is unclear whether shown results are representative or cherry-picked (Reviewer 1). Figure 9 omits ab initio results (Reviewer 4).

10. **Minor writing issues.** Abstract number (38,626) will soon be outdated (Reviewer 1). “Approximate” should replace “generate” for posterior distribution description (line 095) (Reviewer 1). Typo: “scaler” (page 7), “Transformer” misspelled (line 231) (Reviewers 1, 4). “Leave for future work” phrasing (Reviewer 1). The term “degradation operator” \(\mathcal{A}\) is imprecise; “forward model” would be more standard (Reviewer 4). Figure 3 line colors for CryoFM and DeepEMhancer are too similar (Reviewer 4). The acronym “FM” in the title could mean “foundation model” or “flow matching” (Reviewer 4). Additional algorithmic details for specialized applications are in the appendix rather than main text (Reviewer 4).
