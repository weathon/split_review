## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper provides a theoretical analysis and comparison of existing data augmentation (DA) methods in image-based deep reinforcement learning (DRL). It introduces a unified actor-critic framework that classifies DA into explicit and implicit regularization, analyzes the variance of Q-targets and actor/critic losses, and proposes a principled algorithm that incorporates a KL divergence term. A novel adaptation of the tangent prop regularization from computer vision to DRL is also presented. Experiments on DeepMind Control Suite and dmcontrol-generalization-benchmark demonstrate state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths

1. **Comprehensive theoretical analysis and unified framework**: The paper provides a detailed analysis of DA techniques, including variance decomposition of Q-targets and losses, and offers a unified framework that covers explicit and implicit regularization. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
2. **Introduction of tangent prop to RL**: The adaptation of tangent prop regularization from computer vision to RL is novel and empirically shown to improve performance. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
3. **Thorough theoretical proofs and derivations**: Propositions are supported by detailed formulas and derivations in the appendix, with high logical clarity. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
4. **Insightful variance analysis in SAC**: The analysis of variance under DA raises interesting questions for future research. (Reviewer 4)  
5. **Analysis of KL regularization direction**: The paper examines the optimal direction for applying KL regularization. (Reviewer 4)  
6. **Clear experimental presentation and code release**: Results are clearly shown, and code (with reproduction comments) is promised. (Reviewer 1)  
7. **Considerable experimental data**: The empirical evaluation covers multiple environments and comparisons. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of limitations and overfitting discussion**:
   - The paper does not discuss shortcomings, failures, or future extensions of the proposed algorithm. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - When enforcing invariance via more updates for transformations with low cosine similarity (e.g., random convolution, rotation), potential overfitting is not addressed. (Reviewer 3)

2. **Insufficient comparison and generalization scope**:
   - More comparisons with a wider range of methods (e.g., SODA, SGQN) and domains are needed to validate generalizability. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - The experimental section lacks in-depth analysis of why previous algorithms fail and how the proposed method improves upon them. (Reviewer 3)
   - SVEA is not included in the initial experimental setup; a comment on why this is omitted is expected. (Reviewer 4, question)

3. **Oversimplified unified framework**:
   - Representing implicit DA solely via KL regularization is limiting; methods like SODA (contrastive loss) and SGQN (generative model) use auxiliary objectives that are not captured. (Reviewer 4)
   - The framework fails to capture the diversity of DA approaches. (Reviewer 4)

4. **Unverified claims about target augmentation variance**:
   - The claim that using complex image transformations (e.g., random convolution) in the target does not cause significant variance is unsupported. Table 5 actually shows a non-negligible increase in variance for such transformations. (Reviewers 2, 4)
   - The paper does not provide empirical evidence (e.g., variance relative to target magnitude) or theoretical justification to substantiate the claim. (Reviewer 4)
   - The specific experiment to verify the claim about "even using complex image transformations in the target" is missing. (Reviewer 2)

5. **Missing explanation for RAD vs. SVEA discrepancy**:
   - SVEA (which avoids target augmentation) significantly outperforms RAD. The paper should analyze why avoiding target augmentation yields better performance and how that relates to the variance analysis. (Reviewer 4)

6. **Reproduction and hyperparameter issues**:
   - DrQ is reproduced with batch size 256 instead of 512, leading to lower scores (ball_in_catch, walker_walk, walk_run) compared to official DrQ results. Authors should either use original hyperparameters or report scores from the DrQ paper for fair comparison. (Reviewer 2)
   - The grid search for α_KL and α_tp is too sparse ({0, 0.1, 1.0}); the final choice of 0.1 leaves results within [0,0.1] unexplored. Similarly, the SVEA comparison uses only 0.5 from {0.1, 0.5}. (Reviewer 2)
   - The method for determining α_i (weights per augmentation) is missing reasons; it is unclear if each augmentation gets a specific value or an average weight. (Reviewer 2)

7. **Excessive reliance on appendix**:
   - Frequent deferral to the appendix (especially sections 5.2 and 6) disrupts readability; essential information should be integrated into the main text. (Reviewer 4)
   - Limitations are placed in the appendix without explicit reference in the main body. (Reviewer 4)

8. **Clarity and presentation issues**:
   - Table 4 is perplexing: the logic for measuring variance against augmentations not used during SVEA training is unclear, and the specific "DA" is not mentioned. (Reviewer 4)
   - Bolding in Tables 4 and 5 needs justification. (Reviewer 4)
   - Figure 7 lacks a legend (the green line is not identified). (Reviewer 4)
   - Equation ambiguity: the KL divergence terms in Eq. 8 need checking; there is ambiguity with Eq. 4 and Eq. 9. (Reviewer 2)

9. **Technical questions / missing justification**:
   - How to prove that the distributions of ν and μ in Eq. 11 and 12 satisfy Lemma 1? (Reviewer 2)
   - The work appears equivalent to adding explicit regularization to implicit regularization; is it functionally equivalent to DrQ combined with DrAC? (Reviewer 2)
   - How does the proposed algorithm address the initial question (“which technique should be preferred”)? The main idea is not analyzed. (Reviewer 2)
   - What is the intuition that the KL term helps reduce variance of the target critic? (Reviewer 4)
   - The only difference between the authors’ method and DrQ+KL is tangent prop; Table 1 suggests tangent prop is the primary factor reducing critic loss variance, not the generic algorithm. What is the role and impact of tangent prop? (Reviewer 4)
   - From Table 2, RAD+ has lower critic loss variance than DrQ but underperforms DrQ, contradicting the claim that reducing variance stabilizes training. Explanation? (Reviewer 4)

10. **Novelty and contribution concerns**:
    - The proposed principled algorithm is a minor extension of DrQ by simply adding a KL term; tangible benefits in Figures 1 and 2 appear largely due to tangent prop. The novelty of tangent prop in this RL context is limited, as it originates from computer vision. (Reviewer 4)
    - One reviewer rated contribution as fair (2) and presentation as fair (2), contrasting with others who rated higher. (Reviewer 4)
