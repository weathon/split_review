## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UniComposer, a complex system for band-level multi-track music generation. It introduces a hierarchical music representation (note-level attributes, bar-level encodings, song-level bar feature prediction) and uses four cascaded diffusion models to progressively generate rhythm features and unified symbolic/audio latent features learned by autoencoders. The system decomposes music into monophonic, polyphonic, and percussion tracks, each with a reduction layer. Training is done in three steps. The main innovation is the inclusion of an audio encoding branch concatenated to symbolic features. Objective metrics are reported compared to baselines.

### Strengths
- The design reflects hierarchical planning in music generation: music is generated from latent ideas, multi-track music is categorized into instrument types (monophonic, polyphonic, percussion), and generation is coarse-to-fine (using music reduction as an intermediate representation). (Reviewer 2, 3)
- The paper presents a working scheme for hierarchical music modelling (note-level attribute learning, bar-level encoding, song-level bar feature prediction) with a scheduled training strategy. (Reviewer 3)
- By learning a unified audio/symbolic encoding space, the model can also work as a transcriber for AMT tasks, supporting instrument and note velocity along with other note attributes. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear contribution and motivation:** With such a complex system, it is hard to understand the contribution; differences from existing works are not clearly highlighted. (Reviewer 1) The specific goals of the model (e.g., enhancing generation quality or addressing longer-term generation) are unclear. (Reviewer 2) The introduction states “music generation” broadly, but the actual task appears to be accompaniment generation; the input and output should be stated upfront. The direction of the audio-symbolic mapping is confusing (Line 058 says “converts audio into symbolic format” but training primarily synthesizes symbolic into audio). (Reviewer 3)
- **Audio branch adds no real information:** The audio used as input to the audio encoder is obtained by rendering the MIDI file with FluidSynth; such audio features are then concatenated to symbolic features from the same MIDI. The model only predicts MIDI data (rendered into audio). Therefore, there seems to be no additional information obtainable from these audio features. (Reviewer 1)
- **No subjective evaluation or working demo:** The accompanying website (last checked 11/3/24) only features placeholder content (e.g., “Where we are today… What has your team accomplished?”). This makes it challenging to assess the model’s capabilities and output quality fully. There is no subjective evaluation; objective metrics alone cannot provide a comprehensive understanding of the model’s effectiveness. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3) A human study on naturalness, musicality, and creativity might be necessary. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of ablation on the audio branch:** It is necessary to demonstrate whether the inclusion of the audio branch improves the quality or diversity of generated symbolic music, or if it primarily serves as a transcription pathway. (Reviewer 3) An ablation study comparing the model trained with and without the audio branch is needed. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Methodology difficult to follow:** The connections between graphs are unclear; nodes are not consistently used across figures (e.g., “Note feature” in Figure 3 is not shown in other figures). Many terms are introduced without effective linking. (Reviewer 2) The writing is unclear: the division into monophonic and polyphonic (Line 164) is confusing because Bass (mostly monophonic) is grouped into Poly.; using “melodic” and “harmonic” would better reflect functional roles. (Reviewer 3) The diffusion models are described as “Transformer-based” (Line 308) but appear to be U-Net based with self-attention, a significant distinction. (Reviewer 3) No interpretation of evaluation results in Section 4.4; the paper should explain the significance of metrics and how they relate to performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Narrow evaluation:** Missing baseline comparisons to alternative models (e.g., [1] Gardner et al. MT3, [2] other recent models). (Reviewer 3) How is the Figaro model applied as a baseline if it is a style transfer model? (Reviewer 3) Evaluation of music quality using only statistical metrics is arguably insufficient. (Reviewer 3)
- **Specific technical questions/omissions:**
  - Does the model support user control over the generation process? (Reviewer 3)
  - How is the chord of a piece extracted (Line 208)? What is the chord resolution (#chord per bar)? (Reviewer 3)
  - What is the loss for the diffusion models (Line 308)? Is it MSE loss over bar encodings? (Reviewer 3)
  - Could the model compare to MT3 on the multi-instrument music transcription task (Section 4.2 A&B)? (Reviewer 3)
  - The overall system seems overengineered with no real novel insight; the quality of generations is impossible to evaluate. (Reviewer 1 [minority stronger criticism])
