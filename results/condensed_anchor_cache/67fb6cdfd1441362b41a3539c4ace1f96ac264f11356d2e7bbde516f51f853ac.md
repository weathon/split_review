## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GroupCoOp, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that uses group-specific prompts and a pseudo-labeling scheme to improve group robustness of VLMs without requiring group labels. It achieves state-of-the-art results on five benchmarks across multiple CLIP architectures, training only 0.016% of parameters, and demonstrates robust performance even with extremely limited minority group samples.

### Strengths
- Simple and effective debiasing method: combines decoupled CoOp prompts for group initialization with heuristic pseudo-labeling, achieving strong results without group-level ground truth (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Exceptional parameter efficiency: trains only 0.016% of network parameters, yet matches or exceeds full fine-tuning methods (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Strong empirical performance: consistently outperforms existing debiasing baselines on five group robustness benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 3, 4); not an ensemble of multiple CoOp models (Reviewer 3).
- Effective under extreme label scarcity, including cases where minority group has only one sample (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Proves effectiveness of leveraging rich text features in VLM for group robustness, particularly in label-scarce scenarios (Reviewer 3).
- Writing is polished and easy to understand (Reviewer 4). Technical quality is on par with previous accepted papers (Reviewer 2).
- Well-structured presentation and thorough explanation of the research process (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient motivation and theoretical analysis: the paper does not fully explain why VLMs are susceptible to group bias during fine-tuning, nor does it provide task-specific analysis of bias causes (Reviewer 1). The connection between group-wise prompt learning and the underlying motivation appears tenuous (Reviewer 3). Overall, the method does not introduce a fundamentally new idea to address major VLM limitations (Reviewer 2). The difference from previous methods is not clearly stated, and there is a lack of theoretical or practical evidence supporting the debiasing strategy (Reviewer 1).
- Method section is inadequately written: requires reading many references to understand; lacks detailed explanations of key concepts (Reviewer 1). The structure could better clarify the inference process, especially how group prompts are generated and matched to test images (Reviewer 2).
- Lack of qualitative analysis and visualizations: no visualization or in-depth explanation of how group prompts and pseudo-labeling reduce bias, aside from some supplemental material on category classification (Reviewer 1). The correlation between text prompt tuning and group robustness could be demonstrated with text embedding visualizations or analysis of learned prompt tokens (Reviewer 3). More empirical analysis (e.g., extension of Fig. 4 comparing GroupCoOp and k-means, classical failure cases) is needed to justify the pseudo-labeling strategy over alternatives like k-means (Reviewer 4).
- Inconsistencies and reliability concerns: the subscript of Fig. 1 seems inconsistent with lines 186-187 and 215 (Reviewer 1). Results may have errors in parameter settings or code implementation (Reviewer 1). The number of datasets used in evaluation is ambiguous (four or five?) (Reviewer 2).
- Missing comparisons with stronger prompt-tuning baselines: more effective methods such as MaPLe should be considered as competing methods (Reviewer 3). The novelty is limited without specific comparisons to CoOp-CSC or ERM methods with subgroup recognition (Reviewer 2). Group-wise Adapter/LoRA alternatives remain unexplored, as they are often more effective than prompt tuning (Reviewer 3).
- Limited scope in experimental settings: the method distinguishes only two groups per class; it is unclear if it can capture finer intra-class variations (Reviewer 2). Benchmarks involve only two classes; performance with more classes and spurious attributes is unknown (Reviewer 2).
- Pseudo-labeling ablation analysis is incomplete: the explanation for why k-means underperforms despite intuitive advantages is unconvincing (class imbalance can be adjusted) (Reviewer 4). Theoretical justification is optional but needed (Reviewer 4).
- Interpretation of learned prompts: in Table 6(c), the result that learnable prompts are not equivalent to linear vectors for the same group is unexplained; more analysis is required (Reviewer 4).
- The method, while effective, is relatively naive and lacks in-depth problem/result analysis; mathematical visualization of key points could enhance contributions (Reviewer 1).
