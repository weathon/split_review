## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Hybrid Directional Graph Neural Network (HDGNN) that combines strictly equivariant operations with learnable (non‑equivariant) modules, aiming to improve expressiveness by relaxing the strict equivariance constraint. It achieves strong results on QM9 and OC20 (IS2RE) benchmarks. Two reviewers (R1, R2) were positive overall; R1 raised several technical and clarity concerns but after author responses raised the score to 8. The third reviewer (R3) was more critical, noting that performance gains are limited and inconsistent, and questioned generalizability. The main innovations involve using Wigner‑D matrices to convert directional features to a global basis, applying MLPs, then mapping back.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a relevant and timely topic: investigating whether the sometimes restrictive inductive bias of full equivariance can be relaxed to improve expressiveness. This is a fruitful direction (R1, R3).
- The paper is well written and easy to follow, especially the theoretical preliminaries (R1, R2).
- Excellent results on several benchmarks, including state‑of‑the‑art on some QM9 tasks (e.g., U0, U, G, H, ZPVE) and competitive or best performance on OC20 IS2RE (e.g., energy forces, forces above threshold). Comprehensive ablation studies demonstrate the benefit of the hybrid approach (R1, R2, R3 acknowledges partial success).
- Novel architectural design using Wigner‑D matrices to project features into a global frame, apply an MLP, and project back – enabling partial equivariance without full Clebsch‑Gordan tensor products for high‑degree representations (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Ill‑defined rotation matrix**: The rotation matrix \(R_{ij}\) that maps \(\vec{r}_{ij}\) to \([0,0,1]\) has one degree of freedom left (rotation around the z‑axis). Therefore Equation 9 is not correct. This issue should be fixed or explicitly commented on. The trick appears in Zitnick et al. (2022) but a discussion of local reference frames and the ill‑defined rotation was already given in Kofinas et al. (2021); that work should be cited. (R1)
2. **Model complexity**: The architecture (Figure 1) is heavily engineered with many interacting blocks. While results are strong, the engineering effort may overshadow the scientific message; the core advantage of partial equivariance could be analyzed more simply and thoroughly. (R1)
3. **Clarity of partial equivariance**: The key innovation – why some operations break equivariance and others do not – is not clearly explained, especially around Eqs. 10–11 and the source of non‑equivariance. The role of the ill‑defined rotation vs. the MLP use should be disentangled. (R1)
4. **Performance gains are inconsistent and often marginal**: On QM9, improvements are only on 2 out of 4 metrics; on OC20, 2 out of 11 tasks. This raises questions about the method’s generalizability and whether the claim of state‑of‑the‑art is warranted. (R3 – minority reviewer, more critical)
5. **Misleading claims about equivariant networks**: The statement that equivariant networks lead to “inefficient learning” because of excessive inductive bias is not justified; physics demands equivariance. The paper should provide a counterexample or clarify. (R1)
6. **Expressive power analysis hidden in appendix**: Section 2.4 (expressive power) is relegated to an appendix not included in the main pdf; a brief discussion should appear in the main text. (R1)
7. **Incorrect claim in related work**: Stating that CG‑based networks are limited because learning weights are only in the radial scalar overlooks the spherical harmonics – the actual limit is the band‑limit \(L\). (R1)
8. **Unsubstantiated claims**: “capture more general physical properties” is not supported beyond raw performance scores. (R1)
9. **Missing mechanism for “learned equivariance”**: How the network learns equivariance is never explained. (R1)
10. **Exaggerated claim**: “HDGNN has shown superior performance compared to strictly equivariant models” – results show it outperforms sometimes, not always. (R1)
11. **Ambiguous experiments**: The paragraphs after Tables 3 and 4 are unclear about what research questions are being tested. (R1)
12. **Missing speed/efficiency comparison**: No runtime comparison with other equivariant models, despite the motivation of efficiency. (R1)
13. **Need for theoretical justification**: The claim that the architecture enhances expressive power beyond finite low‑degree group representations should be supported by a proposition or theorem, not only an ablation study. (R3 – question turned into weakness)
14. **Limited benchmark scope**: Experiments should be expanded to other recent GNN benchmarks (e.g., those used by Equiformer). (R3 – missing comparison)
15. **Detailed technical issues**: Over a dozen specific points needing clarification or correction:
    - Use \(\mid\) in neighborhood definition.
    - Provide details on estimating \(R_{ij}\).
    - Explain why an MLP on \(x_i'\) (Eq. 9) would not break equivariance if the rotation were well‑defined; clarify that the ill‑defined rotation is the actual source.
    - Clarify Eqs. 10–11: why decomposing? What is the idea?
    - Define \(a_{ij}\) in Eq. 12.
    - Simplify the Fourier‑based activation function (Eqs. 13–14) and explain why concatenation is performed.
    - Correct the statement “convolution and correlation” (they are the same up to a flip).
    - Support the claim that QM9 is “susceptible to overfitting” with evidence or remove.
    - Locate the “ODD average scores” given in text (GDGNN 564 vs. SCN/Equiformer 592/613) which cannot be found in the table.
(All from R1 detailed comments)
