## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of exploring continual learning for weakly supervised video anomaly detection (CL-WSVAD) and appreciate the use of ChatGPT-generated texts for normality pre-training, the normality coreset memory, and the prompt learning framework. Results on UCF-Crime and XD-Violence are seen as promising by most, and the paper is generally clear. However, reviewers raise substantial concerns about unrealistic task assumptions, missing comparisons with prior continual learning and recent UVAD/WSVAD methods, insufficient ablation, and a weak theoretical proof. Scores range from 5 to 8; one reviewer (score 8) is markedly more positive about the real-world value and structure, while several others (score 5) are critical of the setting’s practicality and the method’s competitiveness.

### Strengths
- Novel exploration of continual learning for weakly supervised video anomaly detection (CL-WSVAD), addressing a realistic streaming anomaly scenario (Reviewers 3,4,5,6,7).
- Normality representation pre-training using ChatGPT-generated anomaly texts is innovative; the normality coreset memory helps maintain robust normality representations (Reviewers 3,5,6).
- Prompt learning framework with task-specific textual cues effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting (Reviewers 6,7).
- Promising results on UCF-Crime and XD-Violence datasets, with reasonable baselines (Reviewers 2,3,4,7).
- Clear writing and well-structured paper (Reviewers 4,7).
- Intuitive CLIP-based framework (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic task assumptions.** The setting assumes each task introduces exactly one new anomaly type and that all normal videos are collected initially. Real-world scenarios often involve multiple simultaneous anomalies and a continuous stream of normal videos. Scalability to rapid or parallel anomaly introductions is untested (Reviewers 1,6,7; Reviewer 1: “scalability … to handle such rapid changes remains untested”).
- **Missing comparison with prior continual learning for VAD.** The paper overlooks Doshi and Yilmaz (CVPR 2020 Workshop on Continual Learning for Anomaly Detection in Surveillance Videos) and Doshi and Yilmaz (WACV 2022, “Rethinking Video Anomaly Detection - A Continual Learning Approach”). A discussion and comparison are needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of comparison with recent UVAD/WSVAD methods.** The method is not compared against state-of-the-art unsupervised video anomaly detection (UVAD) and weakly supervised VAD from 2024, e.g., Zhang et al. (CVPR 2024), Chen et al. (CVPR 2024), Jain et al. (2024). Such comparisons are critical to demonstrate the advantage of the proposed setting (Reviewers 6,7).
- **Potential information leakage from ChatGPT-generated anomaly descriptions.** The 2000 ChatGPT-generated terms may overlap with anomalies in UCF-Crime and XD-Violence, compromising the continual learning evaluation. The impact of the number of terms on performance is not analyzed (Reviewer 4). The realism and quality of these synthetic texts are also a concern (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient ablation.** Ablation studies are missing for: (a) contribution of each loss component (cross-modal alignment loss, textual semantic contrastive loss); (b) comparison with and without the pre-training phase for Task 1; (c) hyperparameter sensitivity across different distributions (Reviewers 1,4). Specific evaluation metrics for the mixed-up cross-modal alignment method are also absent (Reviewer 5).
- **Weak theoretical proof.** The CPC-like proof in Appendix A.1 introduces undefined notation (e.g., t_(l,n), x_n) and lacks rigor. The derivation is confusing and not solid (Reviewers 6,7; Reviewer 7: “unreliable”; Reviewer 6: “not elaborate and confusing”).
- **Lack of clarity in key components.** The paper does not fully explain how normality pre-training distinguishes normal from incremental anomalous events, nor how the prompt retention mechanism works across tasks (Reviewers 1,5). The semantic meaning of mixed features is unclear – they may be just data augmentation (Reviewer 3).
- **No qualitative visualization.** No example outputs (e.g., frame-level anomaly scores) are provided to demonstrate effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing discussion of limitations.** The paper does not discuss the practical difficulty of collecting all normal videos initially, the burden of specifying anomaly types in advance, scalability to larger datasets, or handling ambiguous cases where anomalies resemble normal events (Reviewers 1,3,5,6,7).
- **Minority view on competitiveness.** One reviewer argues that performance is uncompetitive compared to standard WSVAD and questions whether continual learning adds value over retraining on the full dataset (Reviewer 7). Other reviewers find the results promising, so this is a contrasting view.
- **Questionable evaluation design.** The definition of “seen testing videos” in Section 4.2 needs clarification; the current metric may not properly highlight forgetting (Reviewer 3). The mismatch between numbers of normal and abnormal videos in Config.1 and Config.2 may bias results (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing cross-dataset continual learning.** It would be useful to test continual learning across datasets (e.g., UCF-Crime → XD-Violence and vice versa) (Reviewer 4).
- **Typos.** Incorrect citation command (\citet instead of \citetp) in lines 108,115,118,119,120; “Equ.” should be “Eq.” in lines 263 and 312 (Reviewer 7).
