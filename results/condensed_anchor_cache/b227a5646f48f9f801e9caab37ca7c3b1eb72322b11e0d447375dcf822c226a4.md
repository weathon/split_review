## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Catastrophic Cyber Capabilities Benchmark (3CB), a framework with 15 challenges (one per MITRE ATT&CK category) to evaluate LLM agents’ offensive cyber capabilities. 14 LLM agents are tested, each challenge attempted 10 times with best elicitation, and a linear mixed-effects model quantifies performance. Results show larger models (GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) outperform smaller open-source models, and communication protocol (XML > Markdown) affects success. The authors argue for regulatory implications.

### Strengths
- The problem is interesting, timely, and has real-world implications (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).  
- Experiments are extensive, though not covering all tactics (Reviewer 1).  
- Valuable basis for further engineering (Reviewer 1).  
- Paper is well-written and enjoyable to read (Reviewer 2).  
- Comprehensive approach covering all MITRE ATT&CK categories and major LLMs (Reviewer 2).  
- First benchmark for evaluating catastrophic cyber capabilities of LLMs (Reviewer 3).  
- Reviewer 2 is notably more positive (rating 8) than Reviewers 1 and 3 (ratings 3 and 5) – see contrast in weaknesses.

### Weaknesses
- Claim that “3CB ensures that each challenge is designed such that successful completion by a model accurately reflects its ability to apply the technique described in that challenge” lacks citations for the “some” benchmarks mentioned and provides no evidence for the claim itself (Reviewer 1).  
- Novelty of challenges is unclear: they are broadly in the ATT&CK categories, and LLMs can relate similar textual concepts; no evidence that the challenges are conceptually new (Reviewer 1).  
- Only one challenge (binpwn) is described in detail (Appendix B.2); the other 10 publicly released challenges lack description, and 4 are kept confidential. The GitHub repository contains only a demo of binpwn and lacks clear installation/running instructions (Reviewer 1, 2).  
- Completion rates are counterintuitive: nmap (a seemingly simple task) has lower completion than binpwn (more demanding), and setuid also has higher completion than nmap, which is not explained and prevents gauging LLM sophistication (Reviewer 2).  
- The paper does not clearly show LLM capabilities in cybersecurity attacks; apart from the binpwn example, the reader cannot assess how simple or sophisticated the challenges are (Reviewer 2).  
- There are unresolved open questions that overlap with criticisms:  
  * How are the prompts chosen for each challenge? (Reviewer 2)  
  * How are environment requirements (VMs, network) handled? (Reviewer 2)  
  * Why do some LLMs complete sophisticated challenges but fail simpler ones? (Reviewer 2)  
  * Would a cybersecurity-finetuned smaller LLM (e.g., Llama) perform better? (Reviewer 1)  
  * A more fine-grained comparison is needed to explain why large LLMs succeed and small ones fail (Reviewer 1).  
- Technical contribution is not strong enough for ICLR; presentation is more like a technical report than a well-organized research paper. Experiments yield only superficial results (e.g., larger LLMs are stronger), making the paper unsuitable as either a technical or benchmark paper (Reviewer 3).
