## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HyperMask, a continual learning method that uses a hypernetwork to generate semi-binary masks for a target network, drawing on the lottery ticket hypothesis to obtain task-specific subnetworks. Experiments are on Permuted MNIST, Split MNIST, and Split CIFAR-100. Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 5) finds the approach easy to follow and connects ideas novelly. Reviewers 2 (score 3, confidence 4) and 3 (score 3, confidence 5) acknowledge technical soundness but raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental scope, and justification.

### Strengths
- The hypernetwork is used to generate masks (hypermasks) for each task, offering a different mechanism for continual learning. [R1]
- The method connects to lottery ticket theory, enabling a single network with weighted subnets for multiple tasks. [R1, R3]
- The combination of hypernetworks and lottery ticket hypothesis is a novel approach to mitigating catastrophic forgetting. [R2]
- The idea of semi-binary masks produced by a hypernetwork to create target subnetworks is a fresh and creative approach. [R2]
- The method details are technically reasonable and well described. [R3]
- The evaluation includes comparisons against several state-of-the-art baselines on multiple benchmark datasets. [R2]

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and differentiation**: The paper does not clearly articulate why generating masks with a hypernetwork is superior to other masking approaches (e.g., directly learning masks via gradient descent) in terms of performance, computational cost, or memory. [R1] The method appears to be a combination of HAT and PackNet without sufficient distinction. [R3] HyperMask does not adequately differentiate from existing hypernetwork-based weight/mask generators like PackNet and Piggyback. [R2] The core idea of dynamic parameter adjustment through masks is not unique; the paper needs to clearly articulate the novelty of its specific implementation. [R2] The primary contribution may not be highly novel given prior work on hypernetwork-generated task-specific weights and mask-based model adaptation. [R2]
- **Insufficient ablation and justification**: The method uses multiple loss functions (hypernetwork regularization, L1 regularization), but no ablation study isolates their individual contributions or shows that the hypernetwork component is the main driver of performance. [R1] It is unclear whether improvements come from the proposed hypernetworks or from added regularizations. [R1]
- **Limited experimental evaluation**: Experiments are only on very small benchmarks (Permuted MNIST, Split MNIST, Split CIFAR-100). [R1] Performance gains in Table 1 are marginal and not significant. [R1] The amount of experimental results is severely insufficient; HyperMask is only superior on Split CIFAR-100, and even there it is not state-of-the-art—e.g., FeCAM outperforms without needing task identity. [R3] Baselines compared are relatively outdated; regularization- and representation-based methods (like FeCAM) are ignored. [R3] The comparison should include other hypernetwork variants and architecture approaches, and discuss how HyperMask compares to them. [R2]
- **Missing theoretical analysis**: The paper lacks theoretical justification for why semi-binary masks from hypernetworks minimize forgetting, or why the method should outperform other CL techniques. [R2] A theoretical analysis of mask properties, impact on the loss landscape, and conditions for effectiveness would be beneficial. [R2]
- **Hyperparameter guidelines lacking**: Hyperparameters β, λ, and p are mentioned, but no specific recommendations or insights are given for their selection, making reproduction difficult. [R2] The paper should provide a more detailed analysis of how these hyperparameters affect performance and give practical recommendations. [R2]
- **Computational cost and memory not analyzed**: No details on training time, memory usage, or parameter count for the hypernetwork approach. [R1, R2] The method's higher complexity is not justified against marginal gains. [R1] The output layer of the hypernetwork must match the number of parameters in the target network, leading to memory consumption—strategies to mitigate this are not discussed. [R2] (Reviewer 2 repeated this question; kept once.)
- **Techniques largely borrowed**: Almost all techniques are from prior work; the lottery ticket hypothesis is not leveraged beyond existing mask-based methods. [R3]
- **Minority contrast**: Reviewer 1 (score 5) gives a more positive view of the idea's novelty and clarity, while Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 3) strongly question the contribution, novelty, and sufficiency of experiments. [R1 vs R2, R3]
