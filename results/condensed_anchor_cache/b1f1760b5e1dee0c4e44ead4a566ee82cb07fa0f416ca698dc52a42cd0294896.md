## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DCWM, a model-based RL method for continuous control that uses quantized discrete codebook representations (specifically Finite Scalar Quantization, FSQ) for the latent state in a world model. Building on TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3, DCWM is evaluated on DeepMind Control Suite and Meta-World manipulation tasks, showing improvements over TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3. The paper also ablates encoding type (one-hot, label, codebook), loss objective (categorical vs. regression), and transition stochasticity.

### Strengths
1. **Valuable investigation into latent space design for continuous control.** The paper systematically compares quantized discrete codebook encodings against one-hot and label-based encodings, demonstrating the superiority of codebook (FSQ) encodings. [Reviewers 1, 3, 5]
2. **Strong experimental results and ablation studies.** DCWM outperforms or matches SOTA baselines (TD-MPC2, DreamerV3) on multiple tasks. Ablations in Sections 4.3–4.5 provide insights into encoding, loss, and stochasticity choices. [Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 5]
3. **Clear, well-written presentation.** The paper explains background material, method details (e.g., FSQ codebook illustration, Figure 2), and tricks (lines 309, 352, 355). Supplementary material is thorough. [Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 6]
4. **Computational efficiency.** FSQ simplifies the loss function by removing the need for commitment losses, and may reduce training time compared to one-hot or continuous latent alternatives. Reviewer 6 notes this is a benefit. [Reviewers 2, 6]
5. **First successful application of discrete latent spaces for continuous control with world models.** [Reviewer 6]

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing ablation of key components.** REDQ (ensemble of 5 critics with random 2 for bootstrap) is use but the work; the baseline (TD-MPC2) uses a similar ensemble but it is unclear whether the performance gain stems from REDQ or the latent choice. An ablation without REDQ is needed. [Reviewer 2]
2. **Insufficient experiment scale and missing comparisons.** 
   - Comparison with additional baselines is lacking: EfficientZero-V2, MAMBA, Hungry Hungry Hippos (H3). [Reviewers 1, 6]
   - No multi-task learning evaluation, which could reveal task-dependent codebook differences. [Reviewer 4]
   - No head-to-head comparison by replacing the latent space in DreamerV3 or TD-MPC2 with the proposed codebook encoding. [Reviewer 5]
   - No direct comparison between FSQ and VQ (dictionary learning) despite mention in Line 206. [Reviewer 5]
   - Experiments are limited to DMC and Meta-World; tasks used by TD-MPC2 (ManiSkill2, Myosuite) are missing. [Reviewer 5]
3. **Performance variability and robustness concerns.** On some Meta-World tasks (e.g., assembly, peg-insert-side), DCWM’s performance is comparable to TD-MPC2 with higher variance, suggesting robustness issues. A detailed analysis of why DCWM does not consistently outperform across all tasks is needed. [Reviewer 4]
4. **Lack of computational cost comparison.** No numerical analysis of training time or memory usage vs. TD-MPC2 is provided, despite a claim of efficiency in Line 485. Codebook encoding only outperforms one-hot in one task in Figure 6, so the efficiency claim requires support. [Reviewers 2, 5]
5. **Missing visual input experiments.** The method is only tested on state-based tasks; results on pixel-based continuous control (e.g., DreamerV3’s setting) are absent. Reviewer 6 explicitly requests visual input results, even on easier tasks. [Reviewer 6]
6. **Insufficient detail on stochastic transition dynamics.** The description of how the categorical distribution is parameterized and how logits are generated from the previous latent state and action lacks clarity. No analysis of calibration or stable training is provided. [Reviewer 4]
7. **Unclear handling of expected code.** Taking the expected code (average of discrete codes) can produce invalid codebook indices. The paper states this works but does not explain how such an "invalid" code is fed into the dynamics/reward model (e.g., after normalization). [Reviewer 3]
8. **Need for normalization justification.** Normalizing the code to \([-1,1]\) improves performance, but no reference or theoretical reasoning is given (e.g., differing quantization levels across channels). [Reviewer 3]
9. **Overstated claims and unclear motivation.**
   - The abstract claims discrete latent spaces "typically underperform" in continuous control, but this overlooks that TD-MPC2 uses state-based tasks and DreamerV3 does not use inference-time planning; comparing methods without control for planning is misleading. The paper could naturally motivate itself from TD-MPC2 alone. [Reviewer 5]
   - The distinction between "label encoding" and "one-hot encoding" is unclear at first read; a preliminary section clarifying discrete encodings would help. [Reviewer 5]
   - The methods section mixes novel contributions with elements directly from TD-MPC2 (e.g., ensemble of 5 critics with random 2 for bootstrap) without clear differentiation, confusing non-expert readers. Algorithm 1 is standard and could be moved to appendix. [Reviewer 5]
10. **Minor clarifications.**
    - How does "codebook encoding captures similarity between observations" contrast with one-hot encodings? (Line 248) [Reviewer 5]
    - Why did configuring the transition model for label and one-hot encodings result in inability to learn? [Reviewer 5]
    - How does the hyperparameter \(b\) (number of channels) in codebook encoding affect the label encoding's "incorrect ordinal assumption"? (Line 248) [Reviewer 5]
    - In Line 514, why is \(|C| = 2^4\) for \(L=[5,3]\) when \(|C| = \prod L_i\) as claimed? [Reviewer 5]
11. **Other minor issues.** 
    - Formatting: line 217 inconsistent use of `i-th` and `d-th`; line 352 typo "warms starts"; line 465 missing "and" before "models". [Reviewer 3]
    - Hyperparameter sensitivity: which parameters affect performance on specific tasks? [Reviewer 4]
    - Is the codebook a direct application of quantization-aware training (QAT)? [Reviewer 4]
    - How are the weights of the expected code determined? Is this a hyperparameter? [Reviewer 4]
    - Could FSQ-based discretization be used for action space discretization? (action sampling) [Reviewer 6]

**Reviewer disagreement:** While some reviewers (e.g., Reviewer 3) consider the paper free of major flaws and strongly recommend acceptance, others (Reviewers 1, 4, 5, 6) raise significant concerns about missing experiments, insufficient scale, unclear claims, and missing ablations. Reviewer 5 is willing to improve their rating if concerns are addressed, indicating that the weaknesses are addressable but currently limit the paper’s impact.
