## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Bootstrap3D, a framework to automatically generate synthetic multi-view images for training text-to-multi-view (T2MV) diffusion models. The pipeline uses (1) a text-to-image model (PixArt-Alpha) to generate a single view, (2) an image-to-multi-view model (SV3D or Zero123++) to produce four views, and (3) a fine-tuned LLaVA variant (MV-LLaVA) to filter inconsistent data and rewrite captions. The authors generate 1 million synthetic multi-view images with dense captions and introduce a Training Timestep Reschedule (TTR) strategy to balance learning from synthetic vs. real data. Qualitative and quantitative evaluations (CLIP score, FID) are reported. Reviewer 1 (score 6) is substantially more positive than the other reviewers (all score 3), who raise concerns about unfair comparisons, insufficient evaluation, and limited novelty.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear: current multi-view models are limited by the scarcity of high-quality 3D data with detailed captions. (R1, R3, R4)
- The bootstrapping idea—using existing multi-view models to generate synthetic training data—is novel and interesting, similar to pseudo-labeling in semi-supervised learning. (R2)
- The quality filtering via MV-LLaVA is a reasonable approach; training a specialized multimodal model for multi-view consistency is interesting. (R1)
- The Training Timestep Reschedule (TTR) that uses synthetic data for large timesteps and real data for small timesteps is a clever way to benefit from larger data volume while maintaining real-data quality. (R1)
- The paper shows that the bootstrap-trained model outperforms the data generator (SV3D, Zero123++) on the reported metrics. (R1)
- The pipeline is practical, addresses significant challenges, and is well-documented with clear illustrations and diagrams. (R2, R4)
- The generated dataset is large (1 million multi-view images with dense descriptive captions). (R3)
- The writing is good and easy to follow. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparisons due to different backbones**: The proposed method uses PixArt-Alpha as the diffusion backbone, while compared methods (Instant3D, MVDream) use SDXL. The performance gain may come from the backbone rather than the training scheme. The authors should compare with a version fine-tuned on Stable Diffusion (2.1 or XL) and report those results. (R2, R4; R3 also notes missing comparisons to methods like Zero123, SyncDreamer, Stable-Zero123, and single-image-to-3D methods like Lara, DMV3D, Dreamcraft3D)
- **Missing multi-view consistency and 3D reconstruction evaluation**: Only CLIP and FID scores are reported; there is no direct evaluation of view consistency (e.g., reprojection error via LRM, COLMAP-based evaluation as in SyncDreamer, or optical flow as in HarmonyView). The claim of improved 3D-awareness is unsubstantiated without 3D reconstruction metrics or a 3D generation comparison. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient ablation studies**: There is no ablation isolating the quality check component (MV-LLaVA). Table 3 only ablates the entire data generation process, not individual components. It is unclear whether GPT-4V alone could replace MV-LLaVA for filtering and caption rewriting. (R2, R4)
- **No qualitative comparison to a model trained without synthetic data**: The paper does not show uncurated qualitative results comparing the full model to a naive model trained only on real data. This would isolate the benefit of the synthetic data. (R2)
- **Limited qualitative evaluation**: No large set of uncurated random samples from different models is shown, and no user study is conducted. Qualitative comparisons are essential for generative models. (R2, R3)
- **Dependence on SV3D and restriction to 4 views**: The pipeline relies on SV3D for multi-view generation, but SV3D can generate more than 4 views. The choice of 4 views is not justified, and the paper does not explore a more robust generation model to produce higher-quality data. (R3)
- **Purpose ambiguity and limited novelty**: If Bootstrap3D can generate high-quality multi-view images, why can’t it be used directly for 3D generation (e.g., SDS optimization or reconstruction)? The paper claims only to assist other diffusion models, which seems narrow. The contribution is largely engineering/curation, more suited to a dataset track (NeurIPS dataset track). (R3)
- **Unconvincing statement about Objaverse-XL**: The claim that existing datasets (Objaverse-XL) contain insufficient high-quality data is not supported by statistics or visual evidence. The dataset is enormous and likely contains many high-quality samples. (R3)
- **Why results better than SV3D?**: The model is trained on data generated by SV3D yet outperforms it on metrics. No explanation is given (e.g., whether SV3D was finetuned, any post-processing). (R3)
- **Missing categories classification**: In lines 039–045, the paper categories current methods but omits relevant ones like MVDream. (R3)
- **Typographical inconsistency**: The abbreviation for PixArt-Alpha varies (e.g., “PixArt-Alpa” vs “PixArt-α”). (R4)
- **Inference time not reported**: The paper does not discuss inference time, which is important for practical use. (R3)
