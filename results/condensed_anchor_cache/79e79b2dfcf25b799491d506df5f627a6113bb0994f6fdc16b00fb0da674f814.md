## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses continual personalization (CP) of text-to-image diffusion models by introducing regularization methods that leverage Diffusion Classifier (DC) scores. The approach combines parameter-space (Elastic Weight Consolidation, EWC) and function-space (Diffusion Scores Consolidation, DSC) regularization, motivated by the inability of prior replay-free CP methods (e.g., C-LoRA) to balance new concept learning with forgetting. The method is evaluated across multiple datasets, tasks, and metrics, outperforming C-LoRA and other baselines with zero storage overhead (no replay) and minimal parameter overhead (low-rank adapters). All three reviewers score the paper 6, with general agreement on technical soundness and clear presentation, though they differ on the degree of novelty.

### Strengths
- The use of Diffusion Classifier (DC) scores for continual personalization is novel and interesting; the combination of DC scores with EWC is a key innovation. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- The motivation is straightforward and clear; the discussion of C-LoRA’s limitations naturally leads to the proposed EWC+DC approach. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
- The paper is technically sound, well-structured, and provides a detailed explanation of preliminary concepts (e.g., DC scores). (Reviewer 2, 3)
- The method is computationally efficient (no replay, low parameter overhead) and is supported by sufficient experimental results and details. (Reviewer 2)
- Related work is well summarized, and the limitation of prior work (C-LoRA) is well addressed. (Reviewer 1)
- While Reviewer 2 argues the novelty is limited (building on well-established techniques like EWC), Reviewer 3 considers the combination of DC scores with EWC a clear innovation.

### Weaknesses
- **Variance in Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) approximation:** The expectation over DC scores during FIM update is approximated with a single trial per minibatch. While averaging across epochs helps, there is no analysis of how high variance across different timesteps affects FIM stability or whether the approximation introduces biased Monte Carlo estimates. The paper should provide empirical evidence that the approximation does not compromise stability. (Reviewer 1)
- **Loss component interactions:** The ablation study evaluates individual losses but does not analyze interactions (e.g., how the weight of the EWC term affects convergence of the DSC term, or vice versa). A more systematic understanding of these interactions is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing detailed objective function and algorithm integration:** The paper lacks a single objective function that includes the FIM (parameter-space) alongside DSC (function-space). Presenting Algorithms 1-3 without a summary algorithm corresponding to Fig. 2 makes it unclear how the gradients from DC loss and EWC penalty are combined. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear benefit of DSC over simpler EWC+DC:** In Fig. 4 and Table 2, DSC+EWC+DC achieves performance comparable to EWC+DC alone. Given DSC introduces extra complexity (multiple diffusion forward/backward passes per iteration), the paper does not demonstrate clear unique benefits or scenarios where DSC is advantageous. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational complexity:** Each iteration of DSC and EWC+DC requires multiple complete diffusion forward and backward passes (Eq. 4, Eq. 6), while C-LoRA’s explicit regularization is more efficient. The paper should discuss per-iteration complexity, provide quantitative reductions from pruning (e.g., selecting subset of seen concepts in Fig. 16), and report wall-clock time comparisons with C-LoRA. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited architectural and methodological scope:** The method uses only Stable Diffusion 1.5; newer versions (SD 2.0, 2.1, XL) should be tested to demonstrate generalization. Additionally, comparisons with more recent personalization methods like LyCORIS and DoRA are missing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Multi-concept generation not evaluated:** Since multiple-concept generation is common in personalization literature, experimental results on generating images with multiple concepts (e.g., combining learned concepts) are missing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Sanity Check II insufficiently detailed:** The paper does not clearly state what classifier is used, whether the classification is with a single trial, and it conflates improved classification with improved generation quality. A detailed analysis of how DC scores relate to generation fidelity (e.g., whether well-classified regions correspond to high-fidelity regions) is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of strategy for selecting k (number of sampled tasks):** In Fig. 16, picking five tasks works for six total tasks, but no principled guidance is given for choosing k when the total number of tasks changes (e.g., based on task similarity). (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear update of DC scores dictionary in Algorithm 1:** Lines 5-6 are ambiguous: line 5 indicates a probability distribution over n+1 concepts, while line 6 normalizes for a subset of size k. The procedure should be clarified. (Reviewer 3)
- **Notation inconsistencies:** The symbol c is used for text prompts (line 103), class labels (line 146), and one-hot labels (line 245); superscript notation is confusing with exponentiation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing details about the new word vector V_n:** The paper mentions learning V_n (line 172) but does not show it in the algorithm or figure. It is unclear how V_n is obtained and used during training vs. evaluation. (Reviewer 1) Also, providing concrete examples of the pre-trained concept c_0 and V_n would improve clarity. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor errors:** Equation 5 lacks an expectation over data in the second term of the RHS. (Reviewer 1)
- **Misleading title:** The word “secret” is ambiguous and does not clearly reflect the content. (Reviewer 1)
