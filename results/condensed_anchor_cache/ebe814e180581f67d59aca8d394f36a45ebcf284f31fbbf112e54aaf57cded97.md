## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies the GemmaScope sparse autoencoder (Gemma-2-2B at 16K width, layer 11) to search Neuronpedia for mental-health-related features (MHRFs) using GPT-4-generated labels. It reports 29 suicide and 42 sadness features across all 25 layers, but no mania or paranoia features. A single suicide feature is tested with four prompts (two suicide, two homicide) and steering is shown to increase suicide-related outputs. Reviewer opinions are split: one reviewer (score 5, confidence 3) finds the identification of features and gaps valuable and the paper highlights needed safety improvements; the other three reviewers (scores 1, 1, 3, with confidences 5, 5, 3 respectively) consider the contributions preliminary, the methodology lacking in rigor and novelty, and the evaluation severely limited.

### Strengths
- Originality: Meaningful application of sparse autoencoders to uncover latent features in LLMs specifically related to mental health crises.
- Significance: Addresses an important, underexplored topic in AI safety for mental health applications.
- Provides a psychiatry-based perspective on LLM hidden representations and biases.
- Initial exploration of MHRFs in LLMs; evaluates SAEs in a new domain.
- Identifies MHRFs across all 25 layers (29 suicide, 42 sadness features) and finds no features for mania or paranoia, indicating potential critical gaps in model capacity for complex psychiatric symptoms.
- Highlights the need for improved feature identification and modulation to enhance safety in mental healthcare AI (strongly endorsed by one reviewer).

### Weaknesses
- Paper structure is hard to follow; no clear contributions section upfront; important findings (e.g., lack of mania/paranoia features) are not sufficiently highlighted and key counts are relegated to the appendix without academic or statistical presentation. (R1)
- Weak justification for choosing Gemma-2-2B: only accessibility and low cost are mentioned; no comparison to other models (e.g., different sizes, instruction-tuned versions) or different SAE widths/location, so it is unclear whether the findings are unique to this model or reflect broader trends. (R1, R3)
- Selection of search terms (“suicide,” “sad,” “mania,” “manic,” “paranoia,” “paranoid”) lacks theoretical foundation, clinical criteria, or systematic reasoning; comprehensiveness for representing complex psychiatric conditions is not justified. Filtering of irrelevant features (e.g., “Wrestlemania”) is not described (manual or automated criteria). (R1)
- Extremely limited evaluation: only four prompts (two suicide, two homicide) tested without statistical significance testing; no systematic prompt engineering or validation with diverse examples (20–50+ prompts) to test feature robustness. (R1, R3, R4)
- No methodological novelty: simply searches GemmaScope on Neuronpedia; does not compare multiple SAEs (pretrained vs. instruction-tuned) or train new SAEs. (R2, R3)
- Lacks deeper technical/mathematical understanding of why LLMs generate such content; no mathematical framework proposed. (R2)
- Relies entirely on GPT-4-generated feature labels without validating that these labels accurately represent the underlying features; does not use saelens to compute activations across a variety of inputs or evaluate activation patterns. (R3)
- No discussion or justification of activation thresholds for determining feature relevance; max pooling vs. single token activation not explored; no sensitivity analysis of threshold values (e.g., 0.1–0.9). (R3)
- No correlation analysis between feature activation and model outputs; just because the SAE latent activates does not mean the feature is used in the output. Missing analysis of false positives/negatives in feature detection in the output space. (R3)
- No exploration of feature interactions or complex patterns; missing ablation studies (e.g., systematically masking each identified feature to measure causal impact). (R3)
- No comparison with baseline or other methods (e.g., probing methods, random controls). (R3)
- Findings are preliminary; the paper stops at identifying a subset of MHRFs without addressing subsequent questions (e.g., next steps, solutions for the gaps found). Methods are limited in scope and rely on an existing service, raising questions about generalizability from both psychiatry and ML/NLP perspectives. (R2)
- Insufficient justification for focusing specifically on suicide, mania, and psychosis over other MHRFs; no emphasis on why these conditions are prioritized. (R4)
- Missing ethical review. (R4)
- No testing with real crisis conversations (anonymized) to validate feature detection in realistic scenarios; no measure of false positive/negative rates for crisis detection. (R3)
