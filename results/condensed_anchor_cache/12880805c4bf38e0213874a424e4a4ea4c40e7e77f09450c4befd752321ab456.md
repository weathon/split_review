## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical framework for transferability error in model ensemble attacks, decomposing it into vulnerability and diversity terms and bounding it via empirical Rademacher complexity and Hellinger distance. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical novelty and clear writing but raise significant concerns about the restrictive assumptions in the problem formulation (target and surrogate models sharing parameter space/distribution), the definition and practical relevance of diversity (misalignment with gradient-based diversity in prior work), the interpretability of the input-space Rademacher complexity, and the lack of comprehensive experimental validation (limited datasets, missing baselines, unclear figures). One reviewer (R4) rated the paper substantially lower (score 5, soundness 2, contribution 2) than others (scores 6, soundness 3–4, contribution 2–4), citing these assumptions as a major limitation.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical framework:** The paper introduces transferability error, vulnerability-diversity decomposition, and bounds using empirical Rademacher complexity and Hellinger distance. This provides early theoretical grounding for ensemble adversarial attacks. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Clear exposition:** Writing is well-motivated, notations are neat and self-consistent, and Figure 1 provides an intuitive overview. (R1, R4)
- **Avoids independence assumption:** The bound uses the divergence of the joint distribution of model parameters from the product of marginals, rather than assuming models are independent. (R2)
- **Broader applicability of tools:** The analysis techniques for bounding dependent random variables and defining ensemble Rademacher complexity may have impact beyond adversarial attacks. (R1)
- **Practical guidelines match prior empirical findings:** The three guidelines (more surrogate models, increase diversity, reduce overfitting) are correct and consistent with known strategies in the literature. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Overly restrictive assumptions in Eq. (3):** The definition of the most transferable adversarial example \(z^* = \arg\max L_P\) (with \(L_P\) being expectation over \(\theta \sim P_\Theta\)) implicitly assumes that the target model shares the same parameter space \(\Theta\) and the same distribution \(P_\Theta\) as the surrogate models. This implies identical architectures and training configurations, which is unrealistic for practical black-box transfer attacks. (R4, soundness concern; also R1 notes the analysis is limited to L2-bounded attacks and ensemble-based transfer settings.)
- **Definition of diversity misaligned with prior work:** The paper measures diversity via the Hellinger distance \(H_\alpha\) of the joint parameter distribution from the product of marginals. Prior ensemble attack works (e.g., TRS [1], Kariyappa & Qureshi [2]) define diversity based on gradient similarity (low cosine similarity of gradients w.r.t. input). The relationship between these definitions is unclear. Moreover, encouraging model diversity through regularizers introduces model-level dependency, which increases \(H_\alpha\) (seemingly worsening the bound) yet is known to reduce transferability error, suggesting the theory may not capture the full picture. (R2, R1)
- **Interpretation and usefulness of input-space Rademacher complexity:** The bound suggests decreasing the empirical ensemble Rademacher complexity (Eq. 12) gives a tighter upper bound. However, decreasing this complexity implies that any input sample achieves a low loss for the ensemble, which contradicts the attack objective of maximizing loss (Eq. 1). This trade-off is not explored. Additionally, the paper sometimes treats model complexity and input-space complexity interchangeably. One reviewer doubts that Rademacher complexity conveys reliable information for deep networks and notes that a lower upper bound does not guarantee lower transferability error in practice. (R2, R4)
- **Experimental validation is insufficient:**
  - **Figure clarity:** Figures 2–4 lack explanation: x-axis (“# steps” – epochs? iteration steps?), \(\lambda\) is weight decay, and whether loss values are computed on training or test samples is unclear. (R1, R2, R3)
  - **Limited datasets:** Only MNIST, CIFAR-10, and some ImageNet experiments are conducted. Missing more challenging datasets (CIFAR-100, SVHN, full ImageNet) that could test generalizability. (R3)
  - **Missing analysis:** No experiments directly analyze the trade-off between diversity and input-space complexity, nor the effect of different diversity definitions. (R2)
  - **No baselines:** The paper lacks empirical comparisons to existing ensemble-based transfer attack methods (e.g., gradient diversity methods like TRS). (R4)
  - **Figure 4 contradictions:** For CIFAR-10, the variance of logits increases over the few plotted epochs, contradicting Lemma 5 of [1] which states gradient diversity (and logit variance) should naturally decrease. It is unclear whether the trend continues with more epochs, and the authors note a decreasing trend for other datasets without explaining the discrepancy. (R2)
- **Practical guidelines are already well-known and lack concrete trade-off guidance:** The three guidelines (more models, increase diversity, reduce complexity) are already widely applied in the literature. The paper does not provide criteria or metrics to balance the vulnerability-diversity trade-off during ensemble selection or attack generation. (R3, R4)
- **Minor issues:**
  - Line 106: “combine” → “combines”. (R1)
  - Line 153: The hypothesis space maps to a discrete label space, making the loss \(\ell: \mathcal{Y} \times \mathcal{Y} \mapsto \mathbb{R}_0^+\) with domain \(\{-1,1\}\times\{-1,1\}\)—this seems odd and may need fixing. (R1)
  - Line 279: Redundant phrase “provided in Appendix”. (R1)
  - Line 1061: \(R\) should be defined prior to use. (R1)
  - Lines 1281–1290: Possibly missing a \(1/N\) coefficient before \(\sum_{i=1}^N f(\theta_i; x)\). (R1)
  - Questions about equalities: Line 1035 → Line 1038, and Line 1378 → Line 1381 need justification. (R1)
