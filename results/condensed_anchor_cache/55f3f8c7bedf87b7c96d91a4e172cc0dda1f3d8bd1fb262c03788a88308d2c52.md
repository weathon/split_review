## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates three types of outliers in Transformer-based LLMs (activation, weight, attention) and argues they are systemically linked to the softmax attention mechanism. The authors show that these outliers co-occur in low- versus high-residual-stream layers, propose they act as implicit context-aware scaling factors, and demonstrate that replacing softmax with a variant that includes an explicit context-aware scaling factor (or using sigmoid attention) eliminates outliers. Experiments are conducted primarily on GPT-2, with additional analysis across LLaMA2, Mistral, and Phi-2. The paper reports that removing outliers can accelerate convergence (Figure 15, 50 steps) and may benefit model compression. Reviewers are split: two reviewers rate the paper highly (8,8), one moderately (6), one low (5), and one very low (3). Consensus exists on the empirical detail and visual quality, but strong concerns remain about theoretical depth, rigorous definitions, novelty, and sufficiency of validation.

### Strengths

- **Clear exposition and compelling narrative**: The paper is well written with good figures (especially Figures 1–3) that clearly illustrate the clustering of outliers in specific channels and layers. Reviewer 1 called it “a nice and easy to follow story of a complex topic.”

- **Comprehensive empirical analysis across architectures**: The study examines multiple popular LLMs (LLaMA2, Mistral, Phi-2) and provides extensive visualizations of outlier patterns (Figures 1–3, 5b, 8, 11). Reviewer 3 noted that this covers “multiple popular LLM architectures.”

- **Interesting hypothesis about outlier function**: The paper hypothesizes that outliers act as implicit context-aware scaling factors for the attention mechanism and tests this by training GPT-2 with five attention variants (Table 2). Reviewers 1 and 2 found the reasoning process clear and convincing, especially the demonstration that explicit context-aware scaling eliminates outliers.

- **Intriguing convergence result (Figure 15)**: Reviewer 5 highlighted Figure 15 as “a strong argument for smoothing out outliers using the proposed method,” showing faster convergence to lower loss. Reviewer 2 noted that including an explicit scaling factor can also stabilize training.

- **Summary sections and step-by-step structure**: Reviewer 5 appreciated the summary sections that help contextualize each ablation and analysis.

- **Exploration of outlier lifecycles**: Reviewer 3 found the perspective on how outliers emerge and evolve over layers interesting.

- **Addresses an important problem**: Both the study of outliers and the potential for improved model compression are recognized as practical and timely (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **No rigorous definition of “outlier”**: Several reviewers noted that the paper lacks a formal, quantitative definition (e.g., a threshold or statistical measure) for identifying activation, weight, and attention outliers. Reviewer 3 stated that “the absence of a formal definition makes it difficult to reproduce the findings or compare them with other studies.” Reviewer 5 also asked, “how do you determine if a token has or doesn’t have an outlier?” The identification relies on visual inspection of heatmaps (Figures 1–3) without clear thresholds.

- **Insufficient theoretical foundation**: Multiple reviewers (2, 3, 4) remarked that the paper is primarily empirical and lacks mathematical depth. Reviewer 2: “there is no theory involved in the analysis.” Reviewer 3: the claim that outliers serve as “implicit context-aware scaling factors” lacks a mathematical proof or rigorous demonstration. The connection between softmax attention and outlier emergence (Section 6) is described as speculative (Reviewer 3) and would benefit from a formal derivation or simulation.

- **Novelty concerns**: Several reviewers questioned the novelty of the findings. Reviewer 1 pointed out that the successful attention variant (d) in Table 2 was “directly proposed by [1] (presumably Dettmers et al., 2022)” and should be cited in the table. Reviewer 3 listed three previously established results: activation outliers and impact on compression [1], Attention Sink [2], and connection to layer sparsity [3]; the paper presents these as novel. Reviewer 4 asked how this work relates to Sun et al. – whether it is an extension or offers fundamentally new insights.

- **Limited scope of experimental validation**:
  - **Only GPT-2 trained for causal tests**: Reviewer 2 noted that the key training experiments (attention variants, outlier removal) were run only on GPT-2, not on LLaMA-style models, which have architectural differences (e.g., RoPE, pre-norm). Question: would the same modifications remove outliers in LLaMA?
  - **Convergence result is preliminary**: Reviewer 5 emphasized that Figure 15 shows only 50 steps without any baseline comparison; longer training (100, 200, 1000 steps) and quality metrics (perplexity, zero-shot accuracy, MMLU) are missing to confirm that outlier removal improves convergence without harming quality.
  - **No quantitative compression comparison**: Reviewer 3 and 4 noted that the claim of improved compression is not backed by comparisons with existing pruning or quantization methods (e.g., magnitude-based pruning, sensitivity-aware methods). Reviewer 3: “the paper does not provide a quantitative comparison with state-of-the-art compression methods.”
  - **Lack of ablation studies for attention variants**: Table 2 proposes five variants, but no ablation isolates which modification produces each effect (Reviewer 3). The overlap metric for “95% overlap” (Table 1) lacks methodological detail (e.g., intersection over union) according to Reviewer 3.

- **Logical gaps and unsupported causality**:
  - Reviewer 3 pointed out that the paper asserts causal relationships between outlier lifecycles and attention function without controlled experiments. The link between outlier patterns and their proposed role remains correlational.
  - Reviewer 1 had a related question: could the model instead achieve zero updates by making all values in V small, rather than using an outlier with a small vector? The paper does not address this alternative explanation.

- **Unclear takeaway and focus**: Reviewer 5 found the paper’s central message unclear – “the lack of practicality and clarity make it hard to recommend an accept.” The paper moves from outlier identification to lifecycle to attention modification, without a clear, memorable takeaway. Reviewer 3 agreed, describing the investigation as “scattered” and the transition to attention modification as “abrupt and inadequately motivated.”

- **Statistical rigor missing**: Reviewer 3 noted that all quantitative claims lack error bars, statistical significance tests, or confidence intervals, making it impossible to assess whether observed effects are genuine or due to random chance.

- **Missing quality evaluation after outlier removal**: Reviewer 5 asked, “how does this affect quality? What if outliers are mitigated but quality plummets?” No perplexity or accuracy results are reported for the models with modified attention that eliminated outliers.

- **Minor issues**: Notation differences (Reviewer 1 suggested clarifying “down projection input” earlier and referring to Figure 4 when first mentioned). Several figures (2, 3, 8, 11) are not explicitly discussed in the text (Reviewer 5). Some figures lack captions to guide the reader (Reviewer 5). The paper claims a “deeper theoretical understanding” in the conclusion but does not deliver theory (Reviewer 2).
