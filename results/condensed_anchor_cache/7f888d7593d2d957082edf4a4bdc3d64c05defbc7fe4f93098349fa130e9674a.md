## Merged Review

### Summary
AdaWM introduces an adaptive world model-based planning method for autonomous driving that selectively fine-tunes either the dynamics model (world model) or the planning policy during online finetuning to mitigate performance degradation caused by distribution shifts. The authors first identify two root causes of performance drop – dynamics model mismatch and policy mismatch – and propose a method to quantify their severity using total variation distance. Based on this quantification, AdaWM uses alignment-driven finetuning with low-rank updates (LoRA) to update only the mismatched component. Experiments on CARLA tasks (ROM03, RTD12, LTM03, LTD03) show improved success rate and time-to-collision compared to baselines including UniAD, VAD, DreamerV3, and others.

### Strengths
- Provides a motivating analysis of distributional shift during adaptation in model-based planning for autonomous driving, quantifying the gaps in the dynamics model and planning policy separately.
- Proposes an adaptive finetuning framework (AdaWM) with mismatch identification and selective updates based on a switching strategy.
- Includes theoretical analysis and mathematical derivation of the performance gap (e.g., Theorems, constants C1 and C2).
- Demonstrates improved adaptation results (success rate, time-to-collision) in several CARLA scenarios over baseline models.
- The paper is easy to grasp and the presentation of the main idea is clear.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient specification of model, experimental, and metric details** (R1, R3):  
  - Architecture details of the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) and actor-critic network (latent state dimensions, layers, activations, optimization algorithm) are not provided.  
  - CARLA scenario configurations (sensor resolution, FOV, LiDAR parameters, weather, time of day, traffic density) are unclear.  
  - The self-defined reward function’s components and weights are not reported; standard metrics like Driving Score and Route Completion are missing.  
  (R1) This raises concerns about whether the reward curve objectively reflects performance degradation, especially if the reward itself is poorly crafted or subject to distribution shift.

- **Problematic and unfair baseline comparisons** (R1, R2, R4):  
  - AdaWM uses perfect BEV perception input, while baselines like UniAD, VAD use raw sensor inputs, making the comparison inherently unfair. (R1, R2)  
  - The finetuned AdaWM is compared against pretrained baselines that were **not** finetuned on the same tasks, which inflates the advantage; the paper should instead compare against existing finetuning methods, such as alternate finetuning, model-only finetuning, policy-only finetuning, and methods from Feng et al. (2023), Baker et al. (2022), Hansen et al. (2022), Ouyang et al. (2022). (R4)  
  - Missing comparisons with other model-based/world model planning methods specifically designed for autonomous driving (UniAD, VAD are end-to-end, Dreamer is not AD-specific). (R2)  
  - It is unclear whether the baselines were even finetuned on the specific tasks (ROM03, RTD12, LTM03, LTD03). (R2)

- **Limited evaluation of generality and standard benchmarks** (R1, R3):  
  - No benchmark results on standard AD leaderboards like Bench2Drive or CARLA Leaderboard-2. (R1)  
  - Evaluation lacks a detailed breakdown across diverse scenarios (high-density urban vs highways, complex intersections, varying traffic densities, frequent lane changes). (R3)  
  - Not tested on out-of-distribution scenarios (extreme weather, unexpected obstacles, highly aggressive driving behaviors). (R3)  
  - Ablation studies do not test generality across different SOTA model architectures (e.g., VAD v1-v2, UniAD, TCP, PlanT). (R1)

- **Potential computational overhead and efficiency trade-offs** (R3):  
  - Mismatch identification via total variation distance may add significant computational load in real time; no complexity analysis or breakdown of time spent on this component relative to the overall planning cycle is provided.  
  - LoRA-based low-rank adaptation’s effect on prediction accuracy and model stability is unclear; a quantitative analysis of the trade-off between efficiency (rank) and prediction accuracy/overall system performance is needed. (R3)  
  - The method requires model and policy rollouts, which may introduce safety issues in real-world deployment; the simulation domain gap may lead to out-of-distribution problems when transferring to real driving. (R2)

- **Insufficient driving behavior quality** (R2):  
  - The vehicle exhibits jittery motion even on straight roads before turns, which is unsuitable for real-world autonomous driving; this should be addressed in the evaluation.

- **Methodology and theoretical clarity deficits** (R1, R4):  
  - The constants C1 and C2 in Theorem 1 lack clear derivation. (R1)  
  - The purpose and effect of the switching strategy are not fully explained (e.g., what happens when both model and policy have mismatches). (R1)  
  - The introduction and motivation are overly verbose on embodied AI and world models, detracting from clarity; the motivating example could be condensed. The paper does not adequately highlight its novelty relative to prior work (e.g., what distinguishes AdaWM from existing finetuning methods, why existing methods cannot be directly applied). (R4)  
  - Rationale for using LoRA over more advanced SOTA finetuning methods (e.g., QLoRA) is missing; an ablation study comparing different finetuning algorithms would be more convincing. (R4)  
  - In the measurement of Policy Mismatch, the authors use state distribution shift, but in deep RL the policy’s output is action; justification for this choice over the more standard action-based comparison is needed. (R4)

- **Transferability claims need more evidence** (R2):  
  - The claim that the world model’s adaptive nature provides an advantage for handling domain gaps is not sufficiently supported; if the simulation-to-real domain gap is large, retraining from scratch may be required. The paper should explain how AdaWM ensures better transferability compared to standard approaches. (R2)

*(Note: Reviewer 2 (score 5) was notably more critical of safety, driving smoothness, and domain gap transferability; these concerns are preserved above.)*
