## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Artsy framework for class-incremental learning in pre-trained models, inspired by the activation mechanisms of silent synapses via spike-timing-dependent plasticity. Artsy combines a fixed pre-trained encoder (e.g., vision transformer) with task-specific sub-networks and artificial silent/functional synapses that connect the two. During training the pre-trained network is frozen while sub-networks learn new features; during inference a gating mechanism (trained MLPs) activates only the relevant sub-network(s). Experiments on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet show improved accuracy over prior methods, including the closely related EASE approach. Reviewers disagree on the novelty and impact: two rate it 5, two rate it 3, with the lower scores citing marginal gains over baselines, limited task complexity, insufficient biological grounding, and missing implementation details.

### Strengths
- **Innovative biologically inspired approach.** Applying silent synapse dynamics (AMPA/NMDA receptor switching) to continual learning in pre-trained models is original and promising (R1, R2). The emulation of silent and functional synapses in artificial networks is novel for this setting (R2).
- **Clear presentation and biological background.** The paper provides a solid explanation of the biological foundations and a well-structured description of the framework, including training and inference algorithms (R1, R2, R4). The flow is easy to follow (R2, R4).
- **Automatic gating mechanism.** The use of trained MLPs to decide which sub-network to activate during inference is interesting; if perfectly trained, only the correct adaptor fires, enabling highly accurate predictions (R3).
- **Commendable description efforts.** Although some details are missing, the authors made a good effort to explain the architecture and its biological motivation (R1, R4).
- **Promising results on class-incremental learning.** The approach outperforms conventional methods on the tested benchmarks (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal advantage over existing method (EASE).** The own comparison shows only small gains over EASE, and the use of only two simple datasets (CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet) leaves unclear whether the gating mechanism is beneficial for more complex tasks (R3, R4). Additional experiments on larger-scale or more challenging benchmarks are missing.
- **Missing reproducibility details.** Key parameters are not reported: number of artificial synapses, connectivity pattern (all-to-all vs. sparse), architecture of sub-networks, and how the pre-trained encoder is chosen (R1, R4). Code has not been made available (R1, R2).
- **Incomplete evaluation and statistical rigor.** Standard deviations are missing from Table 1 and Figures 3/4; the number of experimental runs is not clarified (R1). Only two datasets are used (R3, R4). No efficiency metrics (computational cost, model complexity, training time) are provided (R1, R4).
- **Biological plausibility and interpretability not validated.** Despite heavy emphasis on biological mechanisms, no experiments demonstrate that the model actually behaves like biological silent/functional synapses (R4). The link between artificial synapses and brain lesions/dementia is claimed without references and without clear explanation (R1).
- **Weak or missing ablation studies.** The ablation only tests different types of input features, not the separate contributions of the fixed encoder vs. the sub-network outputs (R1, R2). The contribution of the gating mechanism itself is not isolated (R2, R3 question its advantage).
- **Under-specified model components and training details.** Several aspects are unclear:
  - How the threshold \(thr_t\) is selected (learnable or fixed?) (R2).
  - How the gating score \(S_t\) is optimized and what its activation function is (R2, R1).
  - The meaning of \(h\) in Eq. (5) – possibly \(h_0\) – and differences between notations in Eq. (1), Algorithm 1, and Algorithm 2 (R2).
  - How often \(m_t=0\) vs. \(m_t=1\) occurs (R2).
  - The rationale for training the sub-network for 20 epochs and the artificial synapse for 2 epochs (R2).
  - The definition of "good features" and "bad features" in Section 4.5 (R1, R2).
  - What "complete the prototypes for former classes" means (R2).
  - The details of the pre-trained network \(E_0(\cdot)\) (R4).
- **Missing related work on biologically inspired continual learning.** Key references on biological synaptic consolidation and neuromodulation approaches (e.g., Parisi et al. 2019, Madireddy et al. 2023, and several others) are not discussed; this weakens the contextualization of the proposed method (R1, R2).
- **Potential scalability issues.** The number of sub-networks and artificial synapses grows with each new class, which may limit scalability and biological plausibility (R1).
- **Replay memory requirement not clarified.** The gating MLPs require training with both old and new data, implying a replay buffer – this is not explicitly stated or discussed (R3).
- **Simplistic mathematical description.** The mathematical formulation of artificial synapse dynamics is too shallow; a more rigorous model linking biology (e.g., AMPA/NMDA receptor kinetics) to the algorithm is needed (R4).
- **Claim about generalization not tested.** The paper states that pre-trained networks lack generalization, but no experiment addresses this claim (R4).
- **Diagram improvement.** Figure 2C should depict multiple sub-networks to accurately represent the architecture (R1).
