## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Robust Diffusion Classifier (RDC), a generative classifier built from a single pre-trained diffusion model. RDC first maximizes the data likelihood of a given input and then predicts class probabilities via the conditional likelihood estimated by the diffusion model through Bayes' theorem. To reduce computational cost, the authors introduce a multi-head U-Net and efficient sampling strategies. The method does not require training on specific adversarial attacks and achieves state-of-the-art robust accuracy (75.67% against various ℓ∞ attacks with ε=8/255 on CIFAR-10, +4.77% over previous methods). The paper provides theoretical justifications and thorough evaluation with adaptive attacks.

### Strengths

- The proposed methodology is technically sound and effective, offering a novel framework for robust classification that leverages the rapid progress in generative modeling. It has high potential for inspiring future research.
- The method achieves strong robust accuracy without requiring knowledge of specific adversarial attacks, showing generalization to multiple unseen threats. It is carefully benchmarked with modern attacks (AA, StAdv, BPDA) and includes strong adaptive attack evaluations with justifications.
- The paper provides both theoretical contributions (e.g., likelihood maximization as a key ingredient, Bayes rule derivation) and empirical validation (including ablation studies on hyperparameters).
- The writing is overall clear and well-structured, with model overviews and illustrative diagrams that enhance comprehension.
- The paper includes a thorough study motivating the multi-head classifier design in the appendix, and the authors are transparent about limitations, providing code for reproducibility.
- Strong coverage of related work and comparison to previous methods.

### Weaknesses

- **Computational complexity and quantitative analysis**: Inference is expensive in both time and memory, yet no quantitative comparison is provided. A table with inference time and memory for this method versus other generative and regular classifiers is missing. A breakdown of costs per stage (diffusion sampling, multi-head classification) would be helpful. Even with the proposed multi-head U-Net and complexity reduction, the method remains heavy. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing experimental evaluations**:
  - No results on CIFAR-100 or larger datasets (Reviewer 1, 3). It is unclear how the method scales to more classes or fine-grained differences. The limited number of classes in evaluation is a limitation (Reviewer 3).
  - No evaluation on common corruptions (e.g., CIFAR-10C) to demonstrate robustness beyond adversarial attacks (Reviewer 3).
  - No generated images from the diffusion model are shown (Reviewer 1).
- **Paper presentation and missing details** (Reviewer 1, 2):
  - Theorem 3.2: "optimal diffusion model" – it should be clarified in the main text that it minimizes noise estimation error. Also, the softmax operation in Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3 needs explanation (what is it operating on? Is it across classes?).
  - Section 3.5: The sentence "instead of calculating the diffusion loss using all timesteps like Eq. (9), we only sample a single timestep" – it is unclear how this timestep is sampled (uniformly?).
  - Section 3.5: "BPDA approximates the gradient with an identity mapping" – the exact application of the identity mapping should be explained, possibly with pseudo-code.
  - The claim that "the optimal diffusion classifier achieves 100% robust accuracy" needs clarification: how was this found? What is the gap between optimal and empirical? Is it limited data or optimization?
  - Figure 2a legend entries are not explained in the caption; it should also be clarified that the figure uses DC, not RDC. The order of referencing figures is odd (2c, 2b, 2a).
  - Some equations exceed page margins.
  - There is an error: "hypothesize" should be "hypothesis" (Reviewer 1).
- **Diffusion model implementation details** (Reviewer 2):
  - The sampling strategy is not clearly specified. It seems to use VP sampling with uniform sliding timesteps similar to Nichol & Dhariwal (2021), but the appendix and code also reference Karras et al. (2022) which includes correction steps; thus NFE may exceed T. The exact sampling algorithm (deterministic/stochastic) and how NFE relates to time discretization must be clarified.
  - The paper relies on the hypothesis that the ELBO is tight. It would be valuable to discuss the implications of the gap between likelihood and ELBO for uncertainty calibration and robustness, and potential biases from using ELBO as a proxy for true likelihood.
- **Scalability and computational burden** (Reviewer 2, 3):
  - The repeated ELBO calculation for each class is expensive. Discussion of strategies to reduce this (e.g., efficient sampling, ELBO approximations) is needed. Scalability to larger datasets should be addressed.
- **Missing comparison with other generative classifiers** (Reviewer 2):
  - The paper should more thoroughly compare with prior generative classifiers (not only those for robustness) in terms of complexity, accuracy, and robustness. Connections to works like Heek & Nal (2019) and Han et al. (2022) should be integrated.
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (incorporated as weaknesses):
  - Does AutoAttack with BPDA include the Square Attack component? Does Square find additional examples beyond BPDA gradient-based attacks? (Reviewer 1)
  - In Figure 2a, why is robust accuracy barely above 30%? What are T and T' for main results (Table 1)? Would larger T' (>1000) improve results? If the diffusion model is trained with fewer time steps, would the classifier work well with smaller T'? (Reviewer 1)
  - With multi-head diffusion, is only the last layer receiving class conditioning? How does this compare to injecting class conditioning at multiple locations? Are there generations from this model? (Reviewer 1)
  - Appendix B claims "only achieves 60% accuracy on CIFAR-10" – how does that relate to ~90% in Table 1? What is different? (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper says it "leverages an off-the-shelf diffusion model" but modifies the last layer – which diffusion model exactly? How does it compare to those used in previous works? (Reviewer 3)
