## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes using transformers with in-context learning (ICL) and continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) with flow matching to perform full Bayesian inference for statistical models (GLMs, latent factor models, GMMs), approximating posterior distributions after pre-training on synthetic tasks. Experiments compare to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) and variational inference (VI) methods on real datasets, showing competitive sample quality.

### Strengths
- Demonstrates that ICL can yield posterior distributions on complex real datasets (GLMs, latent factor models, GMMs) that approximate full Bayesian inference.
- Novel framework combining ICL with CNFs and flow matching for high-dimensional posterior inference, built on prior fitted networks.
- Strong empirical comparisons: results are similar in quality to HMC and better than VI methods (including IAF) in multi-modal cases; Figure 2 clearly shows ICL posterior aligns with ground-truth HMC while VIs (including autoregressive flow-based VI) fail on multi-modal distributions.
- Comprehensive explanations: concepts, roles of components, and connections to prior work are clearly articulated; experimental design and significance are clearly reported.
- Tests on diverse latent variable models (GMM, Factor Analysis, GLM) illustrate model applicability across multiple contexts.

### Weaknesses
- Incremental contribution relative to prior work [1] (ICLR22) that already showed ICL can do Bayesian inference; the advantage of extending to full Bayesian inference is not demonstrated (e.g., via Bayesian optimization or few-shot learning as in [1]).
- Baseline LA and VI models use small GLMs/Factor models while ICL uses a transformer; Tables 1-4 should report and compare model sizes to ensure fair comparison.
- Missing ablation study on the loss function: comparison of CFM loss (Eq.5) with other distribution matching methods (VAE, score matching) to justify its necessity.
- Computational expense and scalability issues during pre-training are not sufficiently addressed; runtime of both pre-training and inference is not reported.
- Lack of detailed architectural justification for using CNFs over simpler or more established alternatives.
- Limitations not clearly presented except for the GMM case; explicit failure cases and scenarios where the model underperforms existing methods under varying model specifications are needed.
- Insufficient exploration of synthetic data design: impact of variations in datasets/hyperparameters and analysis of out-of-distribution robustness are missing.
- Comparison with gradient descent-based VI (e.g., Langevin dynamics [Welling & Teh 2011], stochastic variational inference [Hoffman et al. 2013]) for evolving latent samples would strengthen validation.
- The posterior inference procedure is not explicitly explained; it is unclear how CNFs are used within the proposed method (writing needs improvement).
- The framework appears more like meta-learning than in-context learning, as the model is pre-trained on many related tasks for inference.
