## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes T-Graphormer, a Transformer-based model that extends Graphormer to spatiotemporal forecasting by directly modeling both spatial and temporal correlations via a single attention mechanism over graph sequences. It introduces centrality and positional encodings for spatiotemporal data. Evaluations are conducted on two traffic speed datasets, and the model reportedly achieves up to 10% reduction in RMSE and MAPE over some baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces two novel spatial feature extraction methods: centrality encoding (in-degree/out-degree) and shortest travel distance encoding between graph nodes. (Reviewer 1, high confidence 4)
- The model effectively adapts Graphormer for spatiotemporal prediction, with improvements in both embeddings and tokens. (Reviewer 2, high confidence 5)  
- T-Graphormer shows superior performance on forecasting the next 12 time steps (1-hour window). (Reviewer 2, high confidence 5)
- Related work is well written (Reviewer 3, confidence 4). [Note: Reviewer 3 finds it a strength despite others noting lack of recent citations – contrast preserved.]
- Figure 2 clearly explains the model framework. (Reviewer 3, confidence 4)
- The ablation study is strong and demonstrates the usefulness of different model components. (Reviewer 3, confidence 4)
- The time series forecasting problem is valuable and impactful. (Reviewer 4, confidence 4)
- The centrality+positional encoding combined with attention is adaptable to various domains. (Reviewer 4, confidence 4)
- The presentation and description of the methodology and experimental setup are clear. (Reviewer 4, confidence 4) [Note: Other reviewers found presentation lacking – this contrast is preserved.]

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty:** Several transformer-based methods for spatiotemporal forecasting already exist (e.g., [1][2]), and T-Graphormer does not make structural modifications to the standard transformer attention mechanism. The approach is considered incremental. (Reviewer 1, score 1; Reviewer 3, score 5)  
- **Lack of details on novel contributions:** The paper omits key mathematical formulations for the temporal integration into positional encoding (e.g., how it differs from naive concatenation) and for the special tokens (`cls`, `graph`). Section 4.2 is too brief; Section 4.3 lacks initialization and interaction details for tokens. (Reviewer 2, high confidence 5; echoed by Reviewer 1 on token explanation)  
- **Outdated and missing baselines:** The baselines include old methods like SVM and FC-LSTM. Missing comparisons with recent advanced models: STAEformer (Reviewer 2), STID, MegaCRN, PDFormer (Reviewer 3), DSformer, LDT (Reviewer 4). STEP is used but not convincingly outperformed. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4; suggestions vary)  
- **Insufficient datasets:** Only two traffic speed datasets are used. More traffic datasets (PEMS03, PEMS04, PEMS07, PEMS08) and non-traffic spatiotemporal datasets (weather, crime) are recommended to claim general spatiotemporal capability. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)  
- **Missing visualizations:** No attention weight visualizations are provided to support the claim that T-Graphormer jointly captures spatiotemporal dependencies vs. relying on spatial proximity or temporal smoothness. (Reviewer 2, high confidence 5)  
- **Lack of hyperparameter analysis:** The hyperparameter experiments are insufficient; the range of values explored is too narrow. (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 4)  
- **No code provided:** Absence of publicly available code hinders reproducibility and evaluation of results. (Reviewer 4, confidence 4)  
- **Presentation/writing quality issues:** The manuscript is unbalanced (methodology chapter ~1.5 pages vs. longer others). Problems include: wrong dimension for adjacency matrix (Section 3.1), incomplete sentence in Section 4.1, confusing summary (Section 6), and inconsistent terminology (using “spatiotemporal forecasting”, “multivariate time series”, “traffic prediction” interchangeably). (Reviewer 1, score 1; Reviewer 3)  
- **Limited experimentation variety:** Besides main results and ablation, further experiments (e.g., comparing encoding strategy with GNN-based attention, hyperparameter analysis) are missing. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Questions regarding encoding strategy:** The methodology focuses the embedding preprocessing on the temporal dimension; it is unclear how cross-dimensional (spatiotemporal) dependencies are captured, and the effect of replacing attention with a GNN in the encoding strategy is not ablated. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Graph construction details omitted:** The paper does not explicitly describe how the graph and adjacency matrix are generated for the datasets (though follows another paper). (Reviewer 4)
