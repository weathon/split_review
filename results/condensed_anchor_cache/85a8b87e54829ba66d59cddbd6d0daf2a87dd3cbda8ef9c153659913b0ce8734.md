## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents S7, a state-space model (SSM) that combines S5’s simpler structure with input-dependent state transitions (as in S6/Mamba) and a stable reparameterization (inspired by StableSSM). The authors evaluate S7 on neuromorphic event-based datasets, Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmarks, and physical/biological time series. Reviewers agree that the idea of adding input-dependence to S5 is promising and that theoretical stability analysis is provided, but they also identify substantial weaknesses: limited novelty (input-dependence from S6, reparameterization from StableSSM), missing comparisons to S5 and S6 in several experiments, poor LRA performance relative to prior SSMs, lack of evidence for claimed efficiency/simplicity, unclear notation and implementation details, and overstated contributions. One reviewer (R1, score 5) is significantly more positive, emphasizing the helpful combination and extensive experiments; the other three reviewers (score 3) are more critical.

### Strengths

- Input-dependent dynamics: Adapting S5 to make state transitions input-dependent is a promising approach for handling input variability in long-range tasks (R1, R3).
- Stable reparameterization: The theoretical derivation of gradient stability via reparameterization (Eqs. 6–8, adapted from StableSSM) is a positive feature that aims to control exploding/vanishing gradients (R2, R3, R4).
- Broad evaluation on diverse tasks: Experiments cover neuromorphic datasets, LRA benchmarks, and multiple physical/biological time series, with extensive analysis (R1, R4). The minority reviewer (R1) highlights that this contributes to SSM development.
- General applicability: The model applies across domains without complex domain-specific biases (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and reliance on prior work:** The core idea (input-dependent update) was already introduced in S6 (Mamba), and the stable reparameterization framework (Eqs. 6,7,8) is directly borrowed from StableSSM [Wang & Li, 2023] with insufficient differentiation (R2, R3, R4). The paper does not explain why combining S5 with this reparameterization is a non-trivial contribution.
- **Missing crucial comparisons:** S5 is not compared in many experiments (Tables 5,6,7, mislabeled as Figure 2) even though it is the direct baseline (R1, R4). No efficiency comparison with Mamba/Mamba2 in terms of speed, parameters, or FLOPs to support claims of simplicity (R2, R4). No results on general language modeling tasks central to Mamba’s appeal (R2). The selective copy/induction heads benchmarks (from Mamba and the survey [Tiezzi et al. 2024]) are not used, making it unclear where S7’s input-dependence is beneficial (R3).
- **Poor LRA performance and insufficient analysis:** S7 performs worse than many prior SSMs on LRA (R2, R3, R4). It is better than S5 on only 2 of 6 tasks (Text, Pathfinder) and substantially worse on others (e.g., Path-X, where accuracy drops from S5’s 96.6% to 88.56%) (R1, R4). The authors provide only a brief discussion without proposing improvements, weakening the claim that input-dependence is useful for long sequences (R2, R3).
- **Unclear efficiency and simplicity:** The paper claims S7 is simpler than Mamba, but does not demonstrate with any metric (parameter count, training/inference time, memory usage). Theoretically, updating A_k at each step with input-dependence could be expensive without parallel selective scan (R2, R4).
- **Missing implementation details:** How is Λ_k (or A_k with input-dependence) computed? The equations for the input-dependent mechanism are not shown; the reader sees only the stable reparameterization (R4). Notation for A_k is inconsistent: input dependence appears in Eq. 5 and line 267 but is omitted elsewhere (R3). Δ_k in Fig. 1 is undefined in the caption (R4).
- **Unverifiable assumptions and lack of ablation:** Assumptions 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 are not justified or their feasibility examined (R3). The effectiveness of the stable reparameterization is not experimentally verified (e.g., gradient norm stability or comparison with naive parameterization) (R1). No experiments with different initializations of the state matrix to test robustness (R1).
- **Disconnected neuromorphic design:** Section 3.4 introduces neuromorphic-specific equations (Eq. 11, 12) that seem unrelated to the core model; it is unclear whether these apply to all benchmarks or only neuromorphic tasks (R3).
- **Overstated contributions:** Terms like “superiority” are not supported by the results (R4). The paper lacks a balanced discussion of limitations (R3, R4).
- **Minor presentation issues:** The paper overstates novelty (R4). Figure 2 on page 10 should be Table 7 (R4). Long sentences hamper readability (R4).
