## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper theoretically compares the convergence rates of Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum (SGDM) and a simplified version of Adam (same step-size per coordinate, ignoring corrective terms) under the non-uniform \((L_0,L_1)\)-smoothness condition and an affine noise variance assumption in the stochastic setting. The authors claim that Adam achieves faster convergence than SGDM in both deterministic and stochastic settings: (1) in the deterministic case, Adam attains the known lower bound for first-order deterministic optimizers, while GDM’s rate has higher dependence on the initial function value; (2) in the stochastic case, Adam’s upper bound matches the lower bounds of stochastic first-order optimizers, whereas SGDM can fail to converge for any learning rate. A stopping-time-based technique is introduced to further prove that Adam’s convergence rate in terms of the minimum gradient norm can match lower bounds across all problem hyperparameters, and that Adam with a specific hyperparameter scheduler becomes parameter-agnostic. All four reviewers agree on the core description, but they disagree on the significance, correctness, and novelty of the results.

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly structured, moving from motivation and assumptions to the simpler deterministic setting and then to the more complex stochastic setting. It provides clear exposition and argues for the practical relevance of the non-uniform \((L_0,L_1)\)-smoothness assumptions, which are milder than the standard L-smoothness and bounded variance conditions used in prior work.
- The authors explain the intuition behind the different convergence behaviors (e.g., the effect of the adaptive preconditioner in Adam, and GDM’s inability to handle varying sharpness) and describe the limitations of a previous derivation of the GD lower bound, motivating their own formulation.
- The paper establishes convergence theory for both SGDM and simplified Adam under these relaxed assumptions. The obtained upper bounds for Adam are claimed to be tighter than those in prior work (e.g., Li et al., 2023a) and to match the lower bounds of first-order optimizers in both deterministic and stochastic settings.
- A novel stopping-time technique is developed and used to show that Adam’s convergence rate in terms of the minimum gradient norm matches the lower bound, and that Adam with a specific parameter scheduler is parameter-agnostic (Theorem 12).

### Weaknesses

- **Proof incompleteness and errors**  
  *Multiple reviewers (R1, R3) identify a gap in the proof of Theorem 3 (Appendix C.1). In the derivation of an upper bound on the average gradient norm, the first-order term \(\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_t}[\langle\nabla f(w_t),u_{t+1}-u_t\rangle]\) is decomposed into three components (equation (7)). Equations (8)–(13) provide an upper bound only for the sum of the first two terms, leaving the third term unbounded. The proof lacks justification for bounding this third term. R1 and R3 consider this a critical omission (R3 states their rating is conditional on this gap being resolved).*  
  *Two reviewers (R2, R4) point out that setting the regularization parameter \(\lambda\) to zero leads to division by zero in the proofs (line 857) without any further assumption. This error affects the soundness of the claimed results.*

- **Inconsistent or omitted assumptions in theorem statements**  
  *R1 and R3 note that the proofs of Theorem 3 and Theorem 5 rely on assumptions that are not captured in the formal statements. Specifically, the proof of Theorem 3 (Appendix C.1) assumes \(\beta_1 \leq \beta_2\) (line 1390), \(0\leq \beta_1^2 < \beta_2 < 1\) (Lemma 1 requirement), \(\beta_2 \geq \frac{1}{2}\), and \(1-\beta_2 \leq \frac{1-\beta_1}{1024\sigma_1^2}\) (line 1416). Similarly, the formal statement of Theorem 5 omits the assumptions \(\beta_1 \leq \beta_2\) and \(0\leq \beta_1^2 < \beta_2 < 1\) (see Theorem 11 in Appendix D.1). R3 explicitly asks how the choices of \(\beta_2\) in Theorem 9 and Theorem 11 can be ensured to satisfy all these conditions simultaneously, and also whether the inequality \(\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{1-\beta_2}} \leq \frac{1-\beta_1}{128L_1\sigma_1}\) (page 39) can hold under those choices.*

- **Practical utility of results questionable due to hyperparameter tuning with unknown parameters**  
  *R1 and R3 observe that the convergence results for Adam (Theorem 3 and Theorem 5) require “a proper choice of learning rate \(\eta\) and momentum hyperparameter \(\beta_2\),” but the formal derivations (Theorem 9, Theorem 11) necessitate tuning these hyperparameters with prior knowledge of the non-uniform smoothness parameters \((L_0,L_1)\) and the affine noise variance parameters \((\sigma_0,\sigma_1)\). Since these parameters are generally unknown or difficult to estimate, the conditions on hyperparameters would be challenging to satisfy in practice, limiting the practical applicability of the theoretical results. R3 additionally asks for more details on the choice of \(\beta_2\) and the derivation of the bound \(\|w_{t+1}-w_t\| \leq 1/\sqrt[4]{t}\) when \(t \geq (128\sigma_1 L_1/(1-\beta_1))^4\).*

- **Lack of clarity about which version of Adam is analyzed**  
  *R1 and R3 point out that Algorithm 1 in the main text describes the coordinate-wise Adam, but it is not until Appendix B that the authors state that all subsequent theorems apply to the norm version of Adam. Although a footnote in the appendix mentions that an extension to the coordinate-wise version is not difficult, more detailed discussion is needed for clarity.*

- **Significant concerns about novelty and the value of the lower bounds**  
  *Two reviewers (R2, R4) argue that the paper brings no substantial contribution compared to the literature. They claim that the convergence rates are derived using classical and elementary tools (smoothness inequalities, stopping time in the stochastic setting) that already exist, and that similar rates were established in Li et al., 2023a. R2 and R4 further state that the authors’ arguments about differences from related work (section 4.1) appear wrong or irrelevant.*  
  *R2 and R4 also criticize the lower bounds, especially in the stochastic setting, as being based on trivial and degenerate situations. They note that the lower bounding function (Theorem 10 in Appendix) uses a gradient noise that is not integrable, leading to a vacuous bound where all expectations become infinite. In contrast, lower bounds in the literature (e.g., Carmon et al., Arjevani et al.) are built on more restrictive and realistic properties (stability, gradient boundedness, etc.). R2 points out that the lower bound does not even depend on the smoothness parameters, which was the motivation stated in the introduction. Both reviewers suggest that exhibiting the bounding function in the main text would be more honest and transparent. R4 notes that the paper also provides lower bounds via one-dimensional functions, but still considers the contribution insufficient.*

- **Missing comparison with a closely related prior work**  
  *R2 asks how this paper relates to wang2024provable, which also analyzes Adam and SGD(M) under non-uniform smoothness. This question indicates a missing comparison that could affect the assessment of novelty.*

- **Typos, presentation issues, and inconsistent formatting**  
  *R1, R3, and R4 all note typos and citation format problems. Specific comments from R4 include: remove the blue color in theorems; define the sequence \(w_t\) in Theorems 3, 4, 5; add a proper name for each theorem to identify which algorithm the result applies to; re-read the appendix thoroughly (e.g., line 2015: “Also, as \(\tau\) is a bounding stopping theorem, by optional stopping time, we obtain …”); avoid placing literature reviews in the middle of proofs (makes reading inefficient); fix the quantifier \(\forall\) at line 465; rewrite left quotation marks in the correct direction; avoid phrases like “to the best of our knowledge” and “for the first time” (used multiple times on page 7).*

- **Disagreement among reviewers on the overall contribution**  
  *While R2 (score 8) and R3 (score 3) acknowledge some contribution and see potential value in the theory, R2 also expresses significant reservations about novelty. R4 (score 1) and R2 both strongly criticize the lack of substantial advancement and the soundness of the lower bounds. R1 (score 5) and R3 (score 3) are conditional on fixing the proof gap and inconsistencies. This spectrum of opinions is explicitly preserved here.*
