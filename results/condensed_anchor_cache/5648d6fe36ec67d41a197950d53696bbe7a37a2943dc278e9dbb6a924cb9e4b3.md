## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proves that a convolutional neural network with Batch Normalization (BN) is a universal function approximator even when only the BN parameters (γ, β) are trainable and all other weights are fixed at random initialization. It provides an explicit construction for common activation functions (including ReLUs) and several random initializations, with bounds on the required network width and depth. The result applies to CNNs with and without residual connections. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 validate the claim by matching target networks that outperform experimentally obtained networks with trained BN parameters. The theory also explains a depth–width trade-off driven by architectural constraints and why disabling entire neurons can be beneficial.

### Strengths
- **Interesting theoretical contribution** with a solid foundation: rigorous mathematical proofs and a well‑structured argument (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Well‑organized and clearly written**; easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Effectiveness well supported by experiments** on CIFAR‑10 and CIFAR‑100 (Reviewer 2). The paper practices a sound experimental methodology: it states a hypothesis, describes experiments, and shows results (Reviewer 3).
- **Appropriately placed into contemporary literature** (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear practical relevance.** The contribution is marginal compared to known results about approximation with models that have random fixed parameters. The paper does not sufficiently address the limitations of training only BN parameters, especially when initial random weights are poorly suited for the target function. The experiments do not explore practical limitations such as sensitivity to random initialization or the computational cost of the required width/depth (Reviewer 1).
- **Small‑scale experiments.** The experiments are too small for modern deep learning, leaving scalability unclear (Reviewer 3). (Duplicate point: Reviewer 1 also notes the experiments do not explore limitations.)
- **Conceptual error about normalization types.** The paper describes BN as a subset of Layer Normalization (LN). BN is *not* a type of LN; they are different normalization layers distinguished by the dimension along which normalization is computed. BN aggregates statistics across samples, making the network output depend on the set of input images – during training a BN network is a statistical operator, whereas LN operates within a sample and is a deterministic function. The paper needs substantial rewriting regarding this distinction (Reviewer 3). *Note: Reviewer 4 independently raises a related concern: the introduction states “we delve into the role of layer normalization” while the title emphasizes BN – is this a typo? (Reviewer 4).*
- **Reduction of normalization to a linear transformation.** The paper treats normalization as γh + β, ignoring the statistical (batch‑wise) component (x‑μ)/σ. Many normalizations (BN, LN, InstanceNorm) share this final affine form, so why emphasize Batch Normalization in the title? Where does “batch” come into play? (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing theoretical justification for the existence of f_t.** Section 2 asserts the existence of f_t due to universal function approximation, but such approximations are typically conditional (not mild, idealized conditions). The author should explain whether these conditions affect the present theory (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons to advanced BN variants.** The analysis should be related to recent improvements like IEBN (AAAI) and SwitchNorm (ICLR). These works are missing from the related work and analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Broken references.** The paper contains incomplete references such as “Theorem ??” (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of exploration of limitations.** Beyond the experiments, the paper does not systematically examine when and why training only BN parameters might fail, nor does it provide guidance on the required width/depth in practice (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority reviewer contrast:** One reviewer (Reviewer 2) found no significant weaknesses and rated the paper 8, while Reviewer 3 rated it 3 with strong objections (especially the BN/LN error) and Reviewer 1 raised concerns about practical relevance. Reviewer 4 (rating 8) posed several clarifying questions that, if unaddressed, could affect the score.
