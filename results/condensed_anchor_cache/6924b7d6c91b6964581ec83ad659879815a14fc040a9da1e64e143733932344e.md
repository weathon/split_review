## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes O‑Edit and O‑Edit+, two sequential knowledge‑editing methods for LLMs that use orthogonal subspaces to minimise interference between successive updates. O‑Edit introduces a new loss function to enforce orthogonality; O‑Edit+ directly guarantees orthogonality between different knowledge updates. Experiments on Mistral‑7B and Llama3‑8B with the COUNTERFACT and ZsRE datasets show strong improvements over ROME and MEMIT, especially when the number of edits reaches up to 1,500. The methods preserve downstream task performance with negligible extra parameters. Two reviewers found the paper innovative and well‑structured; one reviewer rated it slightly lower (5 vs. 6) and raised stronger concerns about evaluation breadth and scalability.

### Strengths
- The results are excellent compared to previous approaches (e.g., ROME, MEMIT).  
- The paper is well‑written, easy to follow, and contains a comprehensive analysis that helps understand the key insights.  
- The method is innovative: using orthogonal subspaces to enhance sequential editing is novel and intuitive.  
- Logical consistency: based on the assumption that knowledge from orthogonal domains has minimal mutual influence, the two variants O‑Edit and O‑Edit+ are proposed, and the stronger orthogonality in O‑Edit+ yields superior performance.  
- The ablation study demonstrates that the proposed method indeed brings performance gains.  
- Continuous editing is achieved without introducing additional parameters.  
- The theoretical derivations are detailed and convincing.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation datasets**: Only two datasets (COUNTERFACT, ZsRE) are used. One reviewer noted that only the COUNTERFACT dataset was used for the primary experiment, though another reviewer acknowledged both datasets. More scenarios where knowledge editing is important should be tested.  
- **Scalability to a very large number of edits**: The paper tests up to 1,500 edits. It is unclear whether orthogonality can be ensured when the editing count scales to millions, as in pre‑training corpus updates. One reviewer explicitly asked to increase the number of edits to explore limits (e.g., 3,000 as in GRACE).  
- **Missing comparisons with memory‑based methods**: The paper does not compare against methods that use external memory or knowledge bases for sequential editing (e.g., GRACE, WiSE). Such comparisons are needed to understand the trade‑offs between direct weight modification and memory‑based approaches.  
- **Incomplete time/compute cost analysis**: No elaboration on the additional time costs of O‑Edit and O‑Edit+; one reviewer explicitly asked whether they incur extra time costs.  
- **Lack of analysis on weight norm growth**: Previous work shows that increased parameter norm hurts performance. The paper does not demonstrate whether O‑Edit and O‑Edit+ suppress norm growth, especially in long editing sequences.  
- **Missing multi‑hop editing evaluation**: One reviewer questioned whether O‑Edit/O‑Edit+ can be applied to multi‑hop editing tasks, where conflicts between new and old knowledge are common.  
- **Forgetting of previously edited knowledge**: When editing up to 1,500 pieces of knowledge, the paper does not study how well the model remembers earlier edits.  
- **Generalisation and localisation degradation at T=200**: A reviewer noted that generalisation is reduced at T=200, suggesting that the orthogonality constraints may lead to insufficient editing thoroughness, which increases generation but decreases localisation. The relationship between the number of edits and the `loc` metric (localisation) needs investigation across different model sizes.  
- **Theoretical validity of CGS**: The mapping used to construct the orthogonal subspace (CGS) needs further exploration, such as its relationship to the offset of the model’s hidden state.  
- **Effect of high dimensionality on orthogonality**: In high‑dimensional spaces, different vectors are almost orthogonal by default. One reviewer asked whether this diminishes the practical impact of the method in larger models.  
- **Portability after multiple edits**: The portability of the method after many edits (e.g., how well the edit transfers to related queries) is not explored.  
- **Influence of context length**: One reviewer asked about the effect of context lengths on knowledge editing, which is not addressed.  
- **Missing references**: The paper omits relevant surveys on knowledge editing.
