## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TIF, a tree-like interpretable framework that transforms standard GNNs into hierarchical trees for graph classification. Each level of the tree represents a coarsened graph at a different granularity. TIF uses a graph coarsening module, a perturbation module for diversity, and an adaptive routing module to identify informative root-to-leaf paths, providing multi-granular interpretability. Three reviewers rate the paper 6/6/6 but with varying confidence (4,3,5) and soundness/contribution scores (R2 gives lower soundness 2 and contribution 2). All agree the problem is important and the experiments are extensive, but raise concerns about theoretical justification, evaluation metrics, missing comparisons, and clarity of definitions.

### Strengths
- The multi-granular interpretability problem is important and previously overlooked; the paper addresses a practical need (R1, R2).
- The tree-structure-based interpretation method is intriguing and can inspire future GNN explanation work (R3).
- The paper is well-written and well-organized; the introduction and framework description make the work easy to follow (R2, R3).
- Extensive experiments with sufficient comparative algorithms demonstrate the effectiveness of TIF in both interpretability and prediction performance (R1, R2, R3).
- Figures and tables are insightful and well-analyzed, facilitating understanding of the method (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical foundation**: There is no formal mathematical justification for why the multi-granular approach improves interpretability, nor guarantees on optimality of the tree structure; the algorithms appear heuristic (R1, R2: lacks clear theoretical foundations; R2: “based solely on heuristic designs”).
- **Complexity and efficiency**: The framework requires significant computational resources to maintain multiple branches and perturbation matrices per node; the hierarchical tree adds complexity at each level. The architecture has three major modules and five hyperparameters (α1–α5) requiring careful tuning plus a dynamic hardening mechanism, making implementation and maintenance harder than simpler approaches. There is no analysis of algorithm complexity or running efficiency (R1, R2).
- **Confusing notation and missing definitions**: The methods section uses confusing subscripts, making it difficult to follow (R2). Several key terms are used before being defined: “path importance” in Figure 3(c), “compression ratio q” in Section 4.4, “IAR” and “LV” in Figure 7; the discussion of Figure 7 mismatches with the notations (R3). The relationship between $\hat A$ and $A^{(l+1)}$ is unclear; does $\hat A$ have the same dimensions $n \times n$ as the original? The role of eq (5) in ensuring connectivity of the coarsened graph is not explained (R2: question 2; becomes weakness). In Figure 1, “finer graph” and “moderate graph” are not defined, and the progression from finer to moderate to coarsen is unexplained (R2: question 1).
- **Opaque definition of interpretation**: It is unclear whether the chosen root-to-leaf path or the final leaf node serves as the interpretation, and why a coarsened graph qualifies as an explanation. An example showing the input graph, root-to-leaf path, coarsened graphs at each layer, prediction, and corresponding explanation is needed (R3). The connection between coarsened graphs at each layer and ground-truth explanations (especially in synthetic datasets) is not established (R3).
- **Insufficient and unconvincing evaluation metrics**: The metrics for explanation effectiveness (e.g., explanation accuracy, consistency) are not sufficiently convincing. Using softmax output as confidence lacks theoretical justification (R2: question 3). The capability of graph kernels for the consistency metric is questionable (R2: question 4). The metrics do not reflect sparsity of the explanation; a trivial explanation (the original graph) is meaningless. Compression ratio is mentioned but does not fully capture informativeness and relevance; a more direct measure of sparsity and its impact on prediction accuracy is needed (R3).
- **Missing visual comparisons and experiments**: There is a lack of visual comparisons between different interpretable GNNs (e.g., GSAT vs. TIF) on the same dataset; visual explanations from methods like GSAT on MultipleCycle are needed (R2: questions 6). Explanation experiments on the Mutag dataset are necessary (R2: question 5). An ablation study for the graph perturbation module is recommended to demonstrate its contribution to diversity (R3: question 2).
- **Limited training guidance**: The training process requires balancing multiple loss terms, but the paper offers limited guidance on how to optimize this process (R1: question becomes weakness).
- **Unclear criteria for explanation quality in figures**: In Figures 4 and 5, the principle for measuring explanation quality is not defined; it is unclear which finer-graph instance is closer to the ground-truth. The difference between GIP (grey, no color) and TIF (colorful) is not explained as a potential superiority (R3).
