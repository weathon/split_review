## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a gaze-augmented framework for fine-grained human action prediction in egocentric video by integrating eye gaze data into a Vision Language Model (VLM). Gaze coordinates from Ego4D are aggregated into heatmaps, overlaid on RGB images, and processed by a Vision Transformer (ViT) to produce gaze-aligned features. In a gaze-regularized attention block, these features serve as queries while RGB features act as keys/values; a Kullback–Leibler divergence loss aligns the attention distribution with the gaze heatmap. Compared to a Flamingo VLM baseline trained without gaze, the method achieves a ~13% improvement in semantic score. The dataset is a subset of Ego4D.

### Strengths
- The problem (fine-grained action prediction in egocentric video) is well-motivated and important for practical applications (Reviewer 1, 2).
- The paper is well written and describes the method and dataset creation clearly, aiding reproducibility (Reviewer 1, 2).
- The approach is a straightforward yet effective integration of gaze into a VLM, aligning machine attention with human perceptual cues to improve intent understanding and prediction accuracy (Reviewer 3).
- The ablation study is adequate and thorough, with many experiments validating the method (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- Using gaze features (rather than just heatmaps) by optimizing attention distribution to reflect gaze patterns is intuitive and powerful (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of proper baselines and comparisons**: Only one baseline (plain VLM without gaze) is used. Additional baselines are needed to separate the benefit of gaze information from the benefit of the proposed gaze-regularized architecture (e.g., simply overlaying gaze on images and fine-tuning the base VLM) (Reviewer 1). Comparisons with existing non-gaze-based methods on Ego4D (e.g., PALM, MMG-Ego4D) and other gaze-based methods (e.g., Min & Corso 2020, Voila-A) are missing; the argument that fine-grained predictions preclude coarse-grained comparisons is unconvincing (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). The method should also be tested with other VLMs (e.g., LXMERT) to show generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty concerns**: Guiding attention with eye gaze is not novel (Min & Corso 2020; Awale & Sarikaya 2022; Rehg group works: Ozdel et al., Fathi et al., Zhang et al.). The proposed gaze-regularized attention block resembles Thakur et al. (WACV 2024) which uses object embeddings instead of gaze, making the technical novelty unclear (Reviewer 2, 4). The paper overlooks foundational work showing gaze improves action anticipation/prediction in egocentric video (Reviewer 4).
- **Reliance on gaze at inference time**: The method requires gaze data at test time, whereas many prior works use gaze prediction models to avoid this requirement. The paper describes this limitation as a feature, but it remains a practical drawback (Reviewer 2). It would be better to regress gaze simultaneously rather than use ground-truth gaze directly (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited dataset and generalizability**: Validation is only on a single, small-scale subset of Ego4D. Generalizability to other scenarios/datasets is unclear (Reviewer 1). The dataset split is much smaller than recent SOTA action prediction methods (Reviewer 4). Given the specialized gaze equipment needed, the method must demonstrate clear accuracy gains over non-gaze approaches (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing sensitivity analysis**: The impact of missing gaze data (e.g., tracker failure) is not studied. Could an off-the-shelf saliency model be used as substitute? (Reviewer 1). The paper does not discuss the distribution of gaze points within the selected distance discrepancy threshold (η) or why Δ=200ms was chosen; evaluating larger Δ up to 30 gaze points per frame could be insightful (Reviewer 2).
- **Task definition and evaluation issues**: The title and intro are misleading; the task is action description/captioning, not action recognition/anticipation as defined by Ego4D, and the output contains multiple actions while ground truth has a single action+noun (Reviewer 4). Evaluation metrics diverge from Ego4D’s standard metrics, making comparisons with existing Ego4D action anticipation methods difficult (Reviewer 4). The method's performance is only ablated, not compared to SOTA—results merely confirm that gaze matters, which is already known (Reviewer 4).
- **Methodological concerns and lack of detail**: The gaze-regularized attention block is insufficiently justified: when λ=0, performance drops below baseline, suggesting that the gaze-overlaid image query component may be unnecessary and could be removed to eliminate the need for gaze at inference (Reviewer 2). Equation 4’s global pixel summation normalization is not explained (Reviewer 3). Gaze-overlaid image processing description is ambiguous (“similar to that used for RGB images”) (Reviewer 3). Construction of gaze feature images needs more detail, including study of Gaussian overlay spread/opacity (Reviewer 3). Manual evaluation of text descriptions is not described (how many evaluators, consensus, duration) (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing references and inconsistent formatting**: Several key references are missing (ViT, ChatGPT, GPT-V4, ShareCaptioner); many citations are to arXiv versions despite acceptance at venues; no ICLR papers are referenced, contributing to unclear community fit (Reviewer 2). Abbreviations (ViT, VLM) are used inconsistently (Reviewer 2).
- **Other missing analyses**: Temporal variations over varying input/prediction times should be included in the main paper (Tables 8/9 from supp) (Reviewer 3). Model speed/real-time performance is not reported (Reviewer 3). Limitations of the approach are not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Implications of using VLM for both ground truth and predictions**: This raises concerns about the validity of the evaluation (Reviewer 4).
