## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Vision-Language Bootstrapping (VLB), a dynamic evaluation protocol that generates new VQA samples by bootstrapping both images (via PowerPaint, SAM, GPT-4V) and language (via GPT-4) while preserving answer consistency. VLB aims to reduce data contamination and enable flexible difficulty levels. Extensive experiments on SEEDBench, MMBench, MME across 8 LVLMs show that VLB reveals performance drops and reduces contamination. Most reviewers (3/4) gave 8, but one reviewer (Reviewer 4) gave 6, citing complexity, reproducibility, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- Identifies an important research direction: static benchmarks suffer from data contamination/leakage, making evaluation unreliable; VLB proposes a new evaluation paradigm (Reviewer 1).
- The idea of using insights from user interactions to inform image and language transformations is interesting (Reviewer 1).
- Thorough experiments: judge model ensures consistency; human examination on 2,100 samples confirms <5% inconsistency for individual strategies (Reviewer 2).
- Composition of multiple strategies effectively reduces data contamination and provides a range of difficulty levels, enabling more reliable evaluation than static benchmarks (Reviewer 2, 4).
- VLB can be readily combined with existing LVLM benchmarks (Reviewer 2, 4).
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow; bootstrapping strategies are introduced clearly (Reviewer 3).
- The framework allows probing upper and lower bounds of VLMs’ capabilities on specific tasks (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Role of user interaction not defined in detail** – e.g., how visual attention and linguistic understanding are converted into specific transformations (e.g., V1, V2, L1, L2 in Figure 3(a)) is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Overlap with prior VQA rephrasing/robustness works** – question rephrasing has been explored in VQA Rephrasings, VQA-LOL, VQA-Subquestions; the novelty relative to those is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited task scope** – the framework only handles VQA; can it evaluate capabilities that require other task formats? (Reviewer 1).
- **Verification of data contamination reduction** – it is not shown that generated questions are absent from pretraining data; using VLB does not automatically guarantee this (Reviewer 1).
- **Performance variance due to randomness** – even with the same sample and strategy, GPT-4V and PowerPaint produce different outputs. Experiments do not quantify this variance (e.g., standard deviation across runs). Without it, the statistical significance of observed performance differences is unknown (Reviewer 2). This is a critical concern for reliability.
- **Consistency of combined strategies not verified** – human verification (Figure 11) only checks individual strategies, not compositions (e.g., V1+V3+L4). Errors may accumulate; the consistency verification should be extended to combined strategies (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalizability beyond specific tools** – bootstrapping relies on GPT-4V, PowerPaint, SAM, GPT-4. It is unclear if similar observations hold with alternative models (e.g., different inpainting or language models). The sensitivity of results to the choice of these tools should be tested (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Veracity of modifications** – only human verification on a small batch is used to confirm modifications are faithful. The paper should include automated verification, such as comparing semantic scores or image-text alignment before/after applying VLB strategies (Reviewer 3).
- **Terminology** – “data contamination” is not standard; the phenomenon is widely called “data leakage” (Chen et al., 2024). The paper should use established terminology (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor typo**: Table reference missing at Line 707 (Reviewer 3).
- **High complexity and dependency on multiple external models** – using GPT-4V, PowerPaint, SAM, etc. increases implementation complexity and potential points of failure (e.g., semantic errors, artifacts from PowerPaint). The specific prompts are not provided, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Time and resource overhead** – generating new samples is time- and computation-intensive; no concrete numbers (e.g., time per sample, GPU hours) are provided, making it hard to assess practical feasibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Performance drop not explained** – VLMs’ performance decreases on dynamic samples, but it is unclear whether this is due to increased difficulty or other factors (e.g., artifacts, biases). No detailed error analysis or visualization is provided to understand the reasons (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of visualization for error analysis** – insufficient visualizations to gain insights into model strengths/weaknesses on VLB-generated samples (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details on specific models** – e.g., Table 1 does not specify which InternVL2 variant (1B, 2B, … 76B) was used (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential bias from limitations of tools** – e.g., PowerPaint may introduce artifacts when removing objects; how does VLB ensure fairness and accuracy in such cases? (Reviewer 4).
- **Interpretability** – no method to improve interpretability of evaluation results; how can researchers understand specific areas where the model underperforms? (Reviewer 4).
- **Naming and ordering of strategies** – multiple reviewers suggested reordering strategies by difficulty (e.g., put easier ones first: V2 before V1?, L3 before L4?) and fixing a label error in Figure 3(c) (L3 should be “add relevant context” not “add irrelevant context”) (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- **Systematic automated validation missing** – aside from human checks, there are no automated methods to guarantee semantic consistency across all generated samples (Reviewer 4).
- **Disagreement in overall assessment**: Three reviewers (1,2,3) rate the paper 8 and find it sound and well-presented, while Reviewer 4 rates it 6, citing serious concerns about complexity, reproducibility, interpretability, and missing analyses (presentation score 1). The weaknesses above capture both the majority and minority viewpoints.
