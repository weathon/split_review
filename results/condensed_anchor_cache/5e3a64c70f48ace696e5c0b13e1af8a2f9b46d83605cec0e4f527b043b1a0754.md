## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies training a two-layer ReLU network for binary classification via gradient flow with small initialization, assuming a well-separated dataset (any same-label pairs positively correlated, different-label pairs negatively correlated). Analysis shows an early phase where neurons align with either positive or negative data, yielding an \({\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{\mu}})\) bound on alignment time. After alignment, loss converges at \({\mathcal{O}}(1/t)\) and the first-layer weight matrix becomes approximately low-rank. Numerical experiments on MNIST illustrate the theoretical findings.

### Strengths
- **Interesting alignment behavior**: The dynamics of gradient flow under small initialization and separable data reveal a clear early alignment phase (Reviewer 1).
- **Rate analysis for after-alignment convergence**: The paper provides a quantitative convergence rate after alignment (Reviewer 1, also noted by Reviewers 3,4).
- **Extension from infinitesimal to finitely small initialization**: Prior results required \(\epsilon \to 0\); this work handles finite but small initialization (Reviewer 4).
- **Quantitative bounds**: Proven bounds involving initialization scale, number of neurons, data points, and time (Reviewer 3).
- **Deterministic initialization**: Results do not require random initialization (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4 notes the specific form in Eq.(3) but acknowledges this point).
- **Clear exposition and helpful illustrations**: Relation to prior work is clearly presented; figures aid understanding (Reviewers 1,3,4; Reviewer 2 says “illustrations support explanations”).

### Weaknesses
- **Very strong data assumptions** (multiple reviewers): Assumption 1 (well-separated, positively correlated within class) is highly restrictive and likely not met by real data. Reviewer 2 questions why a neural network would be used on such data instead of linear regression. Reviewer 3 notes that positively correlated labels imply feature representations point in similar directions, which is unlikely when classes are designed to be discriminative. Reviewer 4 states the assumption is not met by almost all real data. None of the reviewers argue the assumption is mild.
- **Specialized, unconventional initialization of the second layer** (Reviewer 4): The second-layer weights must depend on first-layer weights as in Eq.(3), rather than being independently initialized. This setting is not used in practice or in most theoretical analyses. The paper’s discussion does not justify why results would hold in the natural independent setting; Reviewer 4 doubts the results extend without this restriction.
- **Limited novelty / incremental contribution**: Reviewer 4 argues the result is an extension of similar results from infinitesimal to finite but small initialization, limiting significance. Reviewer 1 also notes that the low-rank convergence property was previously known or observed for (stochastic) gradient flow on separable data. Reviewer 2 rates contribution as poor, citing the strong assumptions as limiting practical relevance.
- **Missing analysis of initialization scale** (Reviewer 1): The paper does not qualitatively discuss how small the initialization must be to ensure the two-phase convergence, or whether there is a transition from two-phase to single-phase as initialization increases. Comparison with methods that find global minima under random initialization (e.g., Wang et al. 2019) would help.
- **Missing experiments / ablations** (Reviewer 1): More numerical tests demonstrating the two-phase convergence (e.g., plots of alignment over time) are requested. Also, the effect of imbalanced second-layer initializations (deviation from Eq.(3)) should be numerically validated; Reviewer 1 hypothesizes it only changes timing but would like confirmation.
- **Scope limited to ReLU** (Reviewer 1): Only ReLU activation is analyzed; extension to other activations is not considered.
- **Potential issue with gradient expression** (Reviewer 4): In Eq.(5), the right-hand side appears to lack the usual residual term \(y_i - f(x_i)\) that appears in gradient derivations. The paper does not explain this discrepancy, which may indicate an oversight or missing justification.
- **Inconsistency in Assumption 2 presentation** (Reviewer 3): The description before the assumption does not match the statement; this hinders clarity.
- **Minor presentation issues** (Reviewer 1): Grammar and typos should be corrected.
- (Other reviewers did not raise additional questions beyond those covered above.)
