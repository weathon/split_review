## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an unsupervised control-theoretic framework for inferring latent control signals and dynamics underlying naturalistic animal locomotion (zebrafish larvae). It uses iLQR-VAE to fit an RNN and a high-dimensional linear dynamical system (LDS), then applies balanced model reduction to the LDS for interpretability. Reviewers found the model reduction approach interesting and the problem clearly defined, but raised concerns about lack of comparisons to existing methods (MoSeq, LFADs), limited novelty, insufficient validation, and missing benchmarks. Scores are 6, 5, 6, 6; one reviewer (score 5) considered the modeling novelty minimal and the neuroscience insights not fully validated.

### Strengths
- Applying balanced model reduction together with dynamical and control inference is an interesting contribution to system identification for behavior (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The unsupervised discovery of latent control signals and behavioral motifs is well motivated and clearly formulated (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The learned latent representation disentangles different movement categories and yields good spatial decoding from top 5 PCs (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Model reduction enables interpretable insights into eigenmodes active during different movements (e.g., Figure 4), which would be cumbersome for an RNN (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well written and clearly presented (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of quantitative comparisons to existing methods** – The paper does not compare its segmentation or reconstruction performance to MoSeq or LFADs. Direct comparisons of segmentation accuracy, reconstruction quality, and interpretability of latent control signals are needed (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Limited methodological novelty** – The core iLQR-VAE method is taken from prior work with minimal changes; the balanced truncation technique is well known. One reviewer (score 5) considered the application valuable but not novel from a modeling perspective, and suggested the findings may be more suitable for a different venue (Reviewer 2). Another reviewer noted the paper is “1 compelling figure short of a reasonable submission” (Reviewer 4).
- **Unvalidated driving inputs** – There is no ground truth for the inferred control signals, making validation impossible without perturbative experiments or neural correlates (Reviewer 4). It remains unclear how the number of inferred control inputs relates to the known bout types in zebrafish (Reviewer 4).
- **Robustness of linear approximation** – The reduced model’s insights depend on the initial LDS fitting the observed system accurately. How the framework performs when the true dynamics deviate from linearity (e.g., nonlinear pendulum with larger angles) was not systematically tested. A parameter sweep to explore the limits of the linear approximation was requested (Reviewer 3).
- **Dual-model complexity and error propagation** – Fitting both an RNN and a separate linear system introduces extra steps, and inaccuracies in the RNN propagate to the subsequent linear approximation and reduction (Reviewer 3).
- **Generalizability to more complex animals** – The zebrafish dataset is relatively simple; it is unclear whether the model would capture richer kinematics of mice or monkeys (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited applications / no new experiments enabled** – The analysis did not demonstrate fundamentally new types of experiments or concrete links to neural or behavioral variables (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient explanation of balanced truncation** – The intuition behind Hankel singular values, controllability/observability, and the derivation of the reduced model is lacking. The mathematical presentation needs more accessible explanations (Reviewers 1, 3). Eqn 5 contains a typo: it should state $\tilde{W}_o = \tilde{W}_c = \dots$ (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing supplement sections** – Section “Model Selection C.1” is referenced but absent from the supplement (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure and text errors** – Inconsistent/mislabeled figure references (e.g., “As shown in Figure 3B” when referring to MGU vs LDS, and Section 4.1 mislabels Fig 1C→1B, Fig 1D→1C) (Reviewers 1, 2). Missing spaces in captions (e.g., “udriving” → “u driving”) (Reviewer 2). Typos: “withing” → “within”, “cannot be reach” → “cannot be reached”, “we can the compute” → “we can compute” (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure clarity** – Figure captions need more details (parameters, color meanings, interpretations) (Reviewer 1). In Figure 4C, the circles for the reduced model’s eigenvalues overlap with the non-reduced ones, making them invisible; transparency or unfilled markers would help (Reviewer 3). Figure 1A does not define what blue and orange correspond to (Reviewer 1). Figure 3D lacks adequate explanation in the main text (Reviewer 2).
- **Unaddressed question** – Why does the classification accuracy decrease with observation time in Figure 3? (Reviewer 3).
