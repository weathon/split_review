## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper formalizes the concepts of trainability and dequantization in variational quantum machine learning (QML) using a rigorous learning-theoretic language. It introduces a new family of “vari veryational” models and proves the existence of models that are both gradient-based trainable (with standard cryptographic assumptions) and non-dequantizable. While reviewers agree the paper clarifies key concepts and provides concrete results, they diverge on novelty and practical relevance: two reviewers (scores 6) find the contribution primarily synthetic and the construction contrived, while two (scores 8) value the rigor and explicit quantum-classical separation but note limited impact on real VQA design.

### Strengths
- **Clear definitions and formalization**: Provides precise, operationally motivated definitions of trainability, simulatability, and dequantization, linking these concepts into a coherent framework (R1, R2).
- **Concrete rigorous results**: Constructs a QML model that is gradient-based trainable yet non-dequantizable, providing explicit examples of efficiently trainable quantum networks with provable quantum advantage over classical counterparts (R1, R4). This addresses a pressing question in the field.
- **Good exposition**: Paper is well written and accessible; Figures 1 and 3 aid comprehension; coverage of recent literature is sufficient (R2, R3).
- **Extended discussion of related literature**: Figure 3 and the synthesis of prior works are helpful for understanding the landscape (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Contrived construction**:
    - The model relies on a computationally hard problem (cryptographic assumptions) and a highly specific training method; it does not generalize to practical VQAs (R1). Theorem 3 / Corollary 4 essentially tensor products a trivial trainable system with an untrained classically-hard function, making the broader design impact unclear (R4).
- **Limited technical novelty**:
    - The paper primarily reinterprets existing results rather than introducing new mathematical tools or algorithms (R2). Two reviewers (R1, R2 with score 6) find the theoretical framework excessive relative to the provable results, while even the more positive reviewers note that the paper attempts both a review and novel contributions, weakening each; moving Sections 3.3–3.4 to an appendix could strengthen the novelty (R3). One reviewer questions whether ICLR is the ideal venue and whether the novelty suffices for this community (R3).
- **Lack of practical guidance and missing application to known architectures**:
    - No analysis is provided of how existing QML models (e.g., quantum convolutional neural networks, quantum generative adversarial networks) fit into the proposed classification (R2). The paper offers no actionable insights, heuristics, or prescriptive guidelines for designing models that are simultaneously trainable, non-dequantizable, and highly expressive (R2). The formalization remains descriptive.
- **Definitional and notational issues**:
    - The mathematical definitions in Section 2 are heavy and it is unclear whether they are necessary for the main results (R1). For example, the notation $\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}$ for the risk functional is insufficiently explained (R1). The term “vari veryational” is criticized as uninformative, awkward to spell/pronounce, and unlikely to be adopted (R1, R3). Figure 2 and Table 1 convey redundant information (R3).
- **Scope limitation**:
    - The dequantization criteria focus only on supervised learning; unsupervised sampling problems (e.g., quantum advantage demonstrated in Nature 574, 505 and Nature 626, 58) are not addressed (R4).
- **Overly narrow or missing clarifications**:
    - “Gradient-based trainable” is not defined precisely—e.g., does it mean no barren plateau per Definition 2? (R1). The paper does not address what implications the results have for constructing actual quantum neural network architectures (R4). At the end, the authors could have offered at least preliminary guidelines for building models that are trainable, non-dequantizable, and very variational (R2).
