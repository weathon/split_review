## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper systematically explores efficient U-Net based diffusion model architectures (EDM2+) by substituting standard convolutions with depthwise separable convolutions and reducing condition embedding dimension in bottleneck layers, achieving 2× reduction in FLOPs/parameters while maintaining generation quality on ImageNet. Reviewers largely agree the paper is well-written and presents comprehensive ablation studies, but note limited theoretical depth, missing analyses (training time, GPU latency breakdown, scaling laws, failure modes), and restricted novelty as the architecture reuses established efficient-CNN patterns (MobileNet/EfficientNet). Reviewer 1 (score 3) is significantly more negative than the others (scores 5–6), citing severe gaps in theoretical justification and experimental thoroughness.

### Strengths
- Clear demonstration of 2× computational savings (FLOPs) and improved CPU/GPU runtime performance, with practical focus on both training and inference efficiency (Reviewer 1).
- Systematic ablation experiments verifying the role of each architectural component, incorporating conventional wisdom from prior works (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Impressive ImageNet results: generation quality (FID) maintained with ~2× complexity reduction under deterministic sampling; EDM2+ excels on ImageNet benchmarks (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Paper is well-written and easy to follow overall (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Efficient architectures reduce computing resources, benefiting further research in diffusion models (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical gaps:** No rigorous analysis of why channel mixing becomes more important under computational constraints, missing theoretical justification for the embedding bottleneck design, and insufficient analysis of how modifications interact with diffusion dynamics or probability distribution geometry (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 4 suggests a geometric viewpoint).
- **Limited novelty:** Core architectural components (depthwise separable convolutions, bottleneck embedding) closely follow established efficient CNN designs (MobileNet, EfficientNet); paper lacks in-depth discussion of how the specific arrangement/interconnection of these components drives gains beyond adopting known patterns (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing parameter/trend studies:** No interpolation studies showing trends and sweet spots for architectural design; absence of scaling law analysis across different model sizes; limited exploration of trade-off spaces (Reviewer 1).
- **Incomplete latency analysis:** Depthwise/pointwise convolution may not significantly benefit GPU latency; missing GPU latency results with varying batch sizes and breakdown of latency contributions from different network parts (Reviewer 3).
- **No training time comparison:** How do different architectures affect training time? (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing medium model (M) variant:** EDM2+ omits the medium-sized model experiments that were present in original EDM2—no explanation provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Failure modes and diffusion-stage analysis:** Limited investigation of failure modes and edge cases; missing analysis of how modifications affect different stages of the diffusion process (Reviewer 1).
- **Generalization to other paradigms:** No demonstration that the denoising network architecture generalizes beyond standard diffusion models to other generative paradigms (e.g., flow matching, consistency models). Reviewer 2 questions compatibility with consistency models (e.g., ECT); Reviewer 4 notes the framework does not show superior results in other generative paradigms.
- **Limited transferable insights:** Most architectural choices appear derived from existing efficient CNN literature without sufficient adaptation to diffusion-specific challenges; minimal discussion of why certain modifications work better than others (Reviewer 1).
