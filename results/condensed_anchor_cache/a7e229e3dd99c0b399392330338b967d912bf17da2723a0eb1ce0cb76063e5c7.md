## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies that the uniform timestep sampling in score distillation (SDS) for 3D generation misaligns with the coarse‑to‑fine nature of 3D optimization. The authors propose to replace random timestep sampling with a monotonically non‑increasing schedule (using a Gaussian weight function) that prioritises early (large) timesteps for geometry and later (small) timesteps for texture. The method is simple, adds no computational overhead, and is shown to improve convergence speed, visual quality, and diversity across multiple 3D representations and baselines.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear and well‑grounded: random timestep sampling used in DreamFusion is derived from diffusion training, while 3D optimization resembles inference (coarse‑to‑fine). The paper provides intuitive visualizations (Fig. 3 and Fig. 6) of how different timesteps affect the optimization.
- The proposal is simple to implement, adds zero extra cost, and yields consistent gains in quality, convergence speed, and diversity.
- Extensive qualitative and limited quantitative experiments demonstrate generality across many state‑of‑the‑art 3D generation methods (e.g., Magic3D, Fantasia3D, etc.), making the technique easy to adopt by the community.
- The weighted‑function formulation (Gaussian PDF) gives a principled way to control the timestep sampling, accelerating convergence and improving semantic alignment.
- The paper is well‑written and presented clearly.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with closely related prior work on timestep scheduling:** Several existing works (ProlificDreamer’s two‑stage sampling, HIFA’s non‑increasing annealing scheme, Score Jacobian Chaining) already explore similar ideas. The paper does not compare or discuss how its schedule differs from or improves upon these, making it hard to assess novelty and relative performance. (Multiple reviewers flagged this.)
- **Limited quantitative evaluation:** The experiments rely heavily on qualitative examples. Quantitative metrics (e.g., CLIP score, user studies) are scarce, and the paper would benefit from more rigorous evaluation. (R1, R2)
- **Heuristic, fixed schedule:** The proposed monotonically non‑increasing schedule is fixed across all prompts and iterations. It lacks a theoretical justification for the specific functional form (Gaussian) and may not generalize to complex or diverse prompts. An adaptive schedule that depends on the current rendering could be more robust. (R4; also questioned by R2 regarding theoretical deduction)
- **Limited evidence for addressing SDS‑specific artifacts:** Fig. 2 highlights blurriness and color distortion caused by SDS, but the paper does not convincingly show that the proposed method resolves these issues. (R3)
- **Insufficient demonstration of TP‑VSD enhancement:** Only one example (Fig. 1) is given for applying the schedule to VSD (TP‑VSD). The paper lacks dedicated explanation (e.g., whether the same schedule is used) and more results to validate generalization. (R2, R4)
- **Missing diversity in representations and baselines for evaluation:** Comprehensive experiments across NeRF, NeuS, DMTet, etc. are needed. The quality claims are not convincingly shown to surpass methods like Magic3D, Dreambooth3D, or Fantasia3D. (R3)
- **Missing image‑input results and clarification of which model (Stable Diffusion vs. Zero123) produced which outcomes.** The paper focuses on text‑to‑3D but should also report image‑to‑3D results and clearly separate the two. (R3)
- **Other missing details:** Relevant citations (AvatarCraft) are omitted. (R3) The scope is limited to a “trick” (R1); one reviewer (score 3) considered the contribution incremental for a top‑tier venue, while another (score 8) found it highly effective and well‑motivated.
