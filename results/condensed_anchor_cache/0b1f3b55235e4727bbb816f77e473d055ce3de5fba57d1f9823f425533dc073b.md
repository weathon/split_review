## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TIDY, a universal fake image detector that leverages image-text discrepancies in CLIP's joint vision-language space. It computes global (whole image) and local (per semantic object) distances between an image and its generated caption, then trains an MLP classifier. Experiments on GAN, diffusion, and Deepfake datasets show strong generalization. Two reviewers (R1, R2: scores 3, 3) find limited novelty and practical concerns; two others (R3, R4: scores 5, 5) find the cross-modal approach simple yet effective and the validation extensive. Reviewer confidence: R1 and R2 = 5, R3 and R4 = 4.

### Strengths
- **Novel cross-modal discrepancy idea**: Exploiting image-text discrepancies in a joint embedding space moves beyond traditional visual-only detection (R2). The global-to-local analysis is novel to some extent and simple yet effective (R3, R4).
- **Strong generalization and robustness**: Superior performance across diverse generative models (GANs, diffusion models, Deepfakes, face-swap) and good adaptability to unseen models (R1, R4). Extensive experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate this (R1, R4).
- **Thorough ablation studies**: Design choices and properties of TIDY are well validated (R1). The ablation includes an image-only variant (Figure 4) and local-discrepancy analysis (R1).
- **Clear writing**: The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work**: The use of CLIP-based joint representation for deepfake detection has been proposed in DE-FAKE (Sha et al., CCS'23), which is not cited. TIDY adds object-level local discrepancy, but this component does not significantly boost overall performance according to the ablation study (R1). Similarly, the motivation overlaps with DE-FAKE; a comparison with DE-FAKE is needed (R3). Missing comparisons with recent works like FatFormer (CVPR 2024) and FreqNet (AAAI 2024) (R3).
- **Dependence on captions and object detection**: The method requires generating a caption for every test image. Inaccurate or misleading captions (e.g., for complex scenes or low-quality images) can degrade detection (R2). The approach assumes a caption can always be accessed, which may not hold for abstract or minimal-content images (R2). Errors in object detection propagation (limited to learned classes) and caption generation can hurt final performance (R1, R2). The complex pipeline (caption generation, object detection, distance computation) introduces inefficiency; an image-only inference path would be more lightweight and practical (R4). No systematic efficiency evaluation is provided (R4 question). The question "How to detect fake images without any caption?" (R2) underscores this reliance.
- **Performance concerns and ambiguity**: The image-only variant (Figure 4) shows comparable performance to the full model, which relies on text. The full model is only slightly better than the UniFD baseline, and that improvement may disappear if local discrepancy is excluded (R1). Under unseen perturbations (Figure 3), performance is not outstanding and requires discussion (R3). The paper does not explain *why* fake images exhibit greater image-text discrepancy than real images (R3 question).
- **Potential inaccuracy in local-discrepancy computation**: Formula (4) computes the distance between each object patch and its *corresponding* object token only, yet Figure 2 illustrates a large distance between an object and *unrelated* tokens. This description may be inaccurate (R4). Additionally, the object detector may produce mismatches between object patches and text embeddings; how this is avoided is unclear (R3 question).
- **Outdated evaluation**: The method is only tested on older generative models. Results on more challenging recent models (e.g., FLUX, SD3, DALLE-3) would be more convincing (R2).
