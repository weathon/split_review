## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes the cycle consistency of image-to-text (I2T) and text-to-image (T2I) generation models by pairing various off-the-shelf models and measuring input-output reconstruction. It reports that current models exhibit a degree of perceptual cycle consistency despite not being explicitly trained for it, finds a correlation between cycle consistency and downstream task performance (image captioning and T2I generation), studies how the number of objects in a text prompt affects T2I divergence and cycle consistency, and shows that T2I models are sensitive to prompt variations. All reviewers agree the topic is interesting and the experiments involve multiple model combinations. However, reviewers disagree on the depth and validity of the analysis, with scores ranging from 3 (strongly critical of causal interpretation, experimental design, and unsupported claims) to 5 (appreciating the empirical study but noting missing applications and ablations).

### Strengths
- The paper investigates a less explored aspect of generative models (cycle consistency) and provides useful empirical insights for the community.
- It offers a comprehensive analysis with extensive experiments combining multiple popular T2I and I2T models (e.g., BLIP2, LLaVA1.6, SDXL Turbo, LLaVA 1.5-13b), including both quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations.
- The observation that cycle consistency correlates with downstream task accuracy (image captioning and T2I generation) could serve as a quick performance proxy without requiring curated benchmarks.
- The paper is well-written and clearly presents its claims and findings.
- The question of cycle consistency is especially relevant given the iterative sampling process in modern generative models, which makes explicit cycle objectives harder to inject.

### Weaknesses
- **Causal interpretation is unsupported and likely reversed (minority reviewers, scores 3 strongly criticize this).**  
  Section 3 claims to show that “more cycle-consistent models have better downstream performance,” but the evidence only demonstrates that better individual T2I/I2T models are more cycle-consistent. This is a natural consequence of higher-quality models and does not establish that cycle consistency itself causes or predicts performance. Counterexamples are provided: a perfect image→text→image cycle can be achieved by an I2T model that writes all pixel values as text; a perfect text→image→text cycle can be achieved by a T2I model that visually encodes the entire input text. The paper provides no experiment showing that improving cycle consistency directly improves downstream tasks.

- **Analysis of model differences is superficial.**  
  - The paper lacks an in-depth explanation of why different I2T+T2I combinations yield varying cycle consistency. For example, BLIP2 underperforms LLaVA1.6 in image cycle consistency but surpasses it in text cycle consistency; no analysis of why.  
  - The claim that I2T models have a greater impact than T2I models is hand-wavy; there is no detailed breakdown of architectural factors (e.g., vision encoder quality vs. language model ability), training data, objective functions, or pre-training strategies. A table summarizing these differences is requested.  
  - No exploration of how training dataset characteristics (e.g., data quality, caption detail) correlate with observed cycle consistency performance.

- **The effect of “descriptiveness” on divergence is confounded with caption length.**  
  The experiments in Figure 6 do not control for caption length: the 1-tag caption in the first row is longer and more specific than the 5-tag caption, and the 1-tag caption in the second row already contains information from the second tag (“reflects”). Therefore the claim that “more descriptive text leads to a broader distribution of generated images but also better reconstructions” is not convincing. The experiment needs to be redesigned to control for length and isolate information density.

- **Abstract claims are not supported by the paper’s content.**  
  - “Analyze in what ways they (cycle consistency) fail”: no detailed error analysis or discussion of failure modes is provided.  
  - “How it affects achieving cycle consistency”: no systematic exploration of factors (model architecture, training data) that affect the ability to achieve consistency.  
  - “We show possible challenges of training cycle consistent models”: the paper does not conduct any training experiments or discuss training challenges beyond noting T2I sensitivity.

- **Missing ablations and baselines.**  
  - The fixed models for each modality (SDXL Turbo for image cycle, LLaVA 1.5-13b for text cycle) are not ablated; it is unclear if the same trends hold if the fixed model is changed.  
  - Tables 1 and 2 present cycle consistency values without any baseline or threshold, making it difficult to judge whether a given pair is “good” or “bad.”  
  - Only one dataset (1k MS COCO) is used, and the evaluation is further limited because MS COCO captions often lack detailed descriptions. Experiments on long captions are missing.  
  - No analysis of divergence in I2T models (which are also stochastic) is provided, even though the paper studies T2I divergence.

- **Application scope is narrow and lacks practical guidance.**  
  The correlation with downstream performance is only shown for two tasks (image captioning via CIDEr and T2I generation). Adding more tasks (e.g., VQA) is suggested to test generalization. The paper stops short of proposing concrete solutions or mitigation strategies for the observed T2I prompt sensitivity, and does not clearly state how the divergence/sensitivity results inform the creation of more cycle-consistent models.

- **Section 4 and Section 5 lack clear connections to the main narrative.**  
  Reviewers noted ambiguity in how the divergence study (Section 4) and prompt sensitivity study (Section 5) relate to cycle consistency. The paper should explicitly state how these analyses impact or explain the cycle consistency results.

- **Potential metric validity concern.**  
  Hallucination in MLLMs and compositional errors in T2I models could lead to high cycle consistency despite low actual performance (e.g., an input prompt generates an incorrect image, which the MLLM mis-captions back to something resembling the original). This threatens the reliability of cycle consistency as a proxy metric.

- **Presentation issues.**  
  - Double “a” in “a a broader distribution.”  
  - Incomplete sentence on page 4: “Therefore, examine how a text-to-image mapping can diverge…”  
  - On page 8, “Table 5” should be “Table 6.”
