## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies multi-batch reinforcement learning under linear function approximation (dimension \(d\)), where a learner sends queries in \(K\) batches. For policy evaluation and best-policy identification, it proves a lower bound of \(\Omega(\log \log d)\) on \(K\) required for sample-efficient algorithms using \(n = \mathrm{poly}(d)\) queries. The bound uses techniques from Zanette (2021) extended with subspace packing via chordal distance.

### Strengths
- **Significance**: The lower bound is novel and meaningful—it shows that a constant number of batches is insufficient and that adaptivity must grow with \(d\), leaving open questions about tightness and matching upper bounds (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Soundness**: The theoretical proof is technically rigorous (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Presentation**: The paper is well written, easy to read, and the proof sketch section effectively conveys the high-level intuition (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Novelty**: Extends prior lower bound techniques (Zanette, 2021) with new tools such as subspace packing with chordal distance (Reviewer 1).
- **Importance of question**: The paper addresses an interesting and fundamental question about the role of adaptivity in sample efficiency (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Tightness and relevance of bound**: It is not clear whether the bound is tight; \(\log \log d\) can be considered a constant in practice (e.g., \(\ln\ln 10^9 \approx 3\)) (Reviewer 1). One reviewer (Reviewer 3) asks whether the bound can be improved to \(\log d\) or even polynomial in \(d\), and what technical limitations prevent such strengthening.
- **Assumptions**: The assumption \(\gamma \geq \sqrt{3/4}\) in Section 4 lacks justification (Reviewer 1).
- **Proof overlaps and presentation**: The proofs of Theorems 4.4 and 4.5 are very similar; subsections such as D.4.1 and E.4.1 appear identical, suggesting unnecessary copying (Reviewer 1). The authors should shorten these proofs.
- **Generality of results**: It is unclear whether Theorems 4.4 and 4.5 hold only for specific \((\varepsilon, \delta)\) (e.g., 1, 1/2) or whether general conclusions follow easily (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear exposition**: The second paragraph of Section 4 is dense and confusing; its mention of a “matching” lower bound and relation to the main result is not explained (Reviewer 1). The connection to Zanette (2021) is promised at the start of Section 5 but not elaborated in the proof sketch (Reviewer 1).
- **Significance of setting questioned**: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 5, minority more negative) found the problem setting’s importance unclear. Specifically, the lower bound may disappear if the evaluation metric is changed from worst-case (Def 3.1) to a regret or expectation-based measure, or if additional mild assumptions (e.g., suboptimality gap, coverage of the target policy, bounded action space) are introduced. This reviewer also noted that the hard instance construction is very similar to Zanette (2021), which reduces the technical contribution; more comparison of the technical challenges faced here versus in Zanette (2021) is needed.
- **Intuition and diagrams**: The paper would benefit from diagrams illustrating the hyperspherical cap/sector and the policy class considered in the lower bound (Reviewer 3). The connection between the specific structure of the policy class and the subspace covering results is not intuitive; more explanation is needed to clarify why this particular choice makes the problem hard (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues**: On page 21, the definition of \(W\) is missing an exponential; also \(g(\gamma)\) is used in Lemma C.5 but not properly defined (Reviewer 3).
- **Open questions from reviewers**: Reviewers also raised the following points that overlap with missing comparisons or explanations:  
  - Is the gap between finite-horizon and infinite-horizon, or between linear MDPs and the realizability assumptions (4.1, 4.2)? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Does the bound hold for general \((\varepsilon, \delta)\) without further work? (Reviewer 1)
