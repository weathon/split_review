## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a new large-scale duet dance MoCap dataset (3.93 hours, 15 genres), a motion representation incorporating body vertices, skeleton, and contact labels, and a diffusion-based model with Interactive Refine Guidance for reactive dance generation. The dataset is a key contribution, and the method targets realistic interactions.

### Strengths
- **Valuable dataset contribution.** The dataset captures fine-grained body and finger movements with contact information, is twice the size of DD100 (3.93 vs 1.77 hours), and covers 15 dance genres with strong interactions. It will likely benefit the motion generation community. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
- **Motion representation that prioritizes contact.** Including body shape (vertices) alongside skeleton features and contact labels is a sensible design for close interactions, and addressing contact is often missing in prior work. (R1, R2, R5)
- **Diffusion-based model with interaction guidance.** The Interactive Refine Guidance sampling strategy (using penetration and contact gradients) is an interesting trial to improve interaction realism at inference time. (R2, R5)
- **Well-written and structured.** The paper is clearly organized, and technical details are presented in an easy-to-follow manner. (R2, R4, R5)
- **Comprehensive quantitative evaluations.** The paper includes multiple metrics, a user study, and ablation experiments. (R4, R5; R3 notes comprehensiveness)
- **Code and project page will be released**, supporting reproducibility. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited core technical novelty and significance.**
  - The method is essentially a vanilla diffusion model; the motion representation is a trivial combination of two existing representations (e.g., body shape + skeleton from prior work), and the sampling guidance is a common practice (needs citation). The overall methodological innovation is incremental. (R3, R4, R5)
  - The dataset, while larger, is only a 2× size increase over DD100 and does not represent a fundamental leap in diversity or scenario. (R3, R5)
  - The reactive generation task itself is questioned: duet dance movements are inherently co-created, so generating one dancer’s motion conditioned on the other’s feels artificial. Simultaneous generation, though possible, is not the focus and results look unnatural. Practical applications are unclear. (R5)
- **Insufficient and unconvincing qualitative results.**
  - Very few generated examples are shown, and no visual ablation results exist to verify the impact of proposed components. (R4, R5)
  - The provided videos and Figure 7 show incorrect interactions (e.g., a wrongly placed arm that should hook around the other dancer’s waist, incorrect hand placements). The proposed method’s results do not look significantly better than Duolando’s (per reviewer impression). (R4)
  - Results are limited to short clips; scalability to longer sequences (e.g., >4s) is unclear and not analyzed. (R5)
- **Missing critical evaluations and baselines.**
  - No apples-to-apples comparison on the DD100 dataset (train and test both on DD100). Duolando’s codebook approach suffers on out-of-distribution data, so testing it only on InterDance is unfair. (R1, R3)
  - Simple strong baselines are missing: nearest-neighbor retrieval using motion-based distance, nearest-neighbor using music embedding distance, mirroring the leader’s motion. (R1)
  - It is unclear how baseline methods Edge and InterGen were adapted for this task (they were designed for other tasks). (R1)
  - No metric measuring the correlation between dancers’ motion dynamics (e.g., paired Frechet Distance). Cross-distance likely does not capture this. (R1)
  - The FID calculation is ambiguous (Is it per-sample vs. distribution? Why is FID on ground truth not zero?). (R1, R4)
  - The user study details are insufficient (recruitment, compensation, video format/resolution, whether music was used). (R1)
  - Only cherry-picked videos are shown; random examples are needed. (R1)
- **Dataset description and collection details are incomplete.**
  - Missing per-category frame/sequence counts. (R2)
  - How the 655 vertices were selected from SMPL-X (random vs. topology-preserving) is unspecified. (R2)
  - It is unclear if multiple dancer pairs per genre exist, and whether all dancers signed consent forms. (R2)
  - Why not combine with DD100 to create a larger dataset? (R1)
- **Motion representation questions and issues.**
  - If the leader’s representation includes contact labels, this requires contact information at inference, which may be problematic in real-world use. (R1, R4)
  - Coupling root translation/orientation with body pose may hinder learning, as pose at different locations requires different representation; decoupling might be better. (R1)
- **Ablation study reveals potential problems.**
  - Ablation shows that adding Local Rotation Offset and Vertices components *worsens* motion quality, contradicting their claimed benefit. (R3)
  - The paper’s statement “more interaction brings more penetration” is not a design principle—proper guidance should increase interaction without raising penetration. A combined penetration-contact metric would be better. (R3)
- **Technical writing and notation issues.**
  - Loss \(L_f\) (foot contact) is not clearly defined. (R2)
  - Symbol \(f\) is used for both foot contact and forward motion, causing confusion. (R2)
  - A relevant related work (Listen, Denoise, Action! from SIGGRAPH 2023) is missing. (R2)
  - Table 1 caption is factually incorrect: InterHuman has longer total duration and strong interaction. (R1)
  - Typo: line 72 “priors, without” → “prior without”. (R2)
- **Limitations discussion is inadequate.**
  - No mention of the dataset’s limited scale compared to image-based 3D lifting methods. (R1)
  - The diffusion model’s limitation (generating full trajectory at once without sliding-window training for longer sequences) is not discussed. (R1)
  - The only “societal impact” note (detachment from reality) is misplaced; more imminent issues like replacing human dancers and copyright are omitted. (R1)
