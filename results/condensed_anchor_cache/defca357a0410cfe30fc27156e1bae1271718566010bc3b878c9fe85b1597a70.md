## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ELSA, an active learning-based labeling assistant for heavily imbalanced datasets with few positive examples. It combines three components—iterative Nearest Neighbor Search, a Random Sampler, and a Linear Head with Active Learning—to automatically exclude most negative samples. The authors claim orders-of-magnitude reduction in labeling effort, e.g., detecting 80% of a class in ImageNet-1k by labeling only 0.67% of the dataset starting from ≤5 positives. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets are provided. Reviewers are split: two rate the paper 5 (moderate to high confidence), one rates it 1 (high confidence). The positive reviewers find the method novel and the results sound, while the negative reviewer finds the novelty very small, the solution trivial, the writing poor, and the experimental support insufficient. All reviewers agree that comparisons with popular existing methods and more thorough empirical evaluations are missing.

### Strengths
- **Novel combination of components** – The method integrates Nearest Neighbour Search, a Random Sampler, and a classification head in an active learning loop, achieving orders-of-magnitude performance gains (Reviewer 1, confident; Reviewer 2, low confidence).
- **Empirical evidence on benchmark datasets** – The paper provides experiments on several benchmark datasets and uses evaluation metrics like precision and recall; results show high effectiveness even with very few positive samples (Reviewer 1).
- **Sound technical content** – The mathematical formulations and proofs appear correct, though not thoroughly checked (Reviewer 2, low confidence).
- **Potential for reducing labeling effort** – The problem of positive-class labeling in massive imbalanced datasets is relevant and ELSA is a potential solution (Reviewer 3, high confidence, but sees this as the only strength).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient empirical evaluation and missing baselines**  
  – No comparison with popular existing active learning / self-supervised methods (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
  – Table 5 should include other state-of-the-art tabular benchmarks for a fair comparison (Reviewer 2).  
  – Overall, the experimental work is weak and insufficient to validate claims (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**  
  – The method is a simple combination of well-known techniques (Nearest Neighbor Search, random sampler, linear head) without clear motivation for why they work together (Reviewer 3, high confidence; Reviewer 2, low confidence: “slightly incremental”).  
  – Both active learning and self-supervised learning are mature fields; the proposed modifications are minor (Reviewer 3).  
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees**  
  – No error bounds are provided; the method’s behavior under noisy or mislabeled data is not analyzed (Reviewer 3, high confidence; also raised as a question by Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing hyperparameter analysis**  
  – The impact of key hyperparameters, especially the parameter “a” in the Nearest Neighbour Search, is not studied (Reviewer 1).  
  – Sensitivity to the choice of embedding space and sampler parameters is not explored systematically (implied by Reviewer 1).  
- **Poor writing and presentation**  
  – The paper lacks a formal problem definition (Reviewer 3).  
  – Several symbols are unclear or inconsistent: e.g., \(L_e\) and \(d_r\) in Section 3.1 are not properly introduced; \(M_{ij}\) should be written as \(M_{ij} = \text{MSE}(g(d_i), g(\Lambda_j))\); \(I_i\) in Section 3.1 vs. \(d_i\) in Section 4.1 are used inconsistently (Reviewer 1).  
  – Section 4 has a messy structure, and Section 4.4’s explanation of why the algorithm works is weak (needs references or actual data points instead of Figure 1) (Reviewer 1).  
  – Multiple English typos throughout (Reviewer 2).  
  – The discussion of algorithmic time/space complexity in Section 5 is not essential for this type of task (Reviewer 1).  
- **Reproducibility** – No code is provided (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing analysis for noisy/mislabeled data** – The paper does not address how ELSA handles noisy or incorrectly labeled positive samples, a critical real-world issue (raised as question by Reviewer 1).
