## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviews show substantial disagreement. One reviewer (score 5) considers the method straightforward and effective, while the others (scores 3, 1, 3) raise major concerns about the bbox-based design, reliance on SfM, insufficient baselines, poor metrics (PSNR <20), missing comparisons with octree-based and other grid methods, unclear importance sampling, and lack of novelty. Overall, the paper is seen as having a reasonable intuition but flawed execution and incomplete evaluation.

### Strengths

- The idea of allocating more model capacity to complex scene parts is straightforward and makes sense (R1).
- Being able to render large regions accurately in a few minutes is practically important (R2).
- Taking region importance into account is plausible; the method’s simplicity (using a set of bounding boxes instead of a uniform grid) is effective for focusing training on important regions and achieving fast training (15 min) (R4).
- The paper correctly observes that NeRF optimization needs different granularity and uses hierarchical bounding boxes to compose importance factors (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Bounding box design issues**: Bbox allocation is determined before optimization and heavily relies on SfM. It cannot adapt to reflective, translucent, or complex-but-few-view regions (R1). The fixed N³ voxel grid per object treats large and small objects with the same complexity; larger objects (e.g., textured buildings, statues) may be coarsely represented even if they contain high detail (R3). The bbox generation lacks precise control: lower-importance regions may get no bbox, while high-importance regions may have many overlapping bboxes, wasting compute (R4). A simple alternative (octree) would allow precise per-region depth control and justifies why bboxes are preferred (R2, R4).
- **Importance distribution acquisition non-trivial**: Using SfM point density is problematic: SfM keypoints appear on texture-rich regions, but texture-rich does not necessarily equal important (R4). The method also requires a user-defined importance or SfM point cloud, creating a chicken-and-egg problem (R4). The reported 15 min training likely excludes importance distribution generation (R4). For textureless regions (e.g., sky in Fig 4), SfM may produce no points, raising coverage concerns (R1). The single-Gaussian importance case is not compared with a simple focus-of-attention approach (e.g., NeRF 360 background projection) (R2).
- **Importance-weighted pixel sampler unclear**: The per-pixel importance depends on NeRF’s output (transmittance T, thus σ and t₀); it is not specified when this is computed (every step / every few steps), which could add heavy cost (R2). There is a likely typo: “proportional” should read “inversely proportional” (R2).
- **Inadequate experimental evaluation**: Only two baselines (nerfacto and TensoRF) are compared; many relevant grid-based methods are missing (e.g., DVGO, Plenoctrees, Tetra-NeRF, Zip-NeRF, tiling multiple instant-NGP networks) (R1, R2, R4). LPIPS is worse than baselines, yet LPIPS is more important for visual similarity than PSNR/SSIM (R4). Training times of baselines are not reported, making speed claims questionable (R4). PSNR <20 across all methods in Table 1 is incredibly low, indicating poor convergence and only blurred images – experiments are not convincing (R3). No convergence plots (PSNR vs iters) are provided, which would better illustrate speed comparisons (R2). Results are worse than 3D Gaussian Splatting in Sec 4.4, but the authors downplay this without showing surface representation examples (R1, R2). The paper claims surface representation is limited, but no results are shown for the proposed method (R2). Only outdoor drone scenes are tested; standard NeRF datasets with video are missing (R3). Tables lack averages and proper highlighting (e.g., tower2 SSIM incorrectly highlights 0.46 instead of better 0.55; pipes1 numbers are inconsistent between Table 1 and Table 2) (R1). The type of nerfacto model used in Fig 4 is not specified (R2).
- **Missing related work discussion**: Important methods are not discussed: mixture of volumetric primitives (Lombardi et al. 2021) and progressively optimized local radiance fields (Meuleman et al. 2023) (R1); octree-based and adaptive voxel methods (Plenoctrees, DVGO, Tetra-NeRF) (R2). The related work section is unfocused and even discusses compression techniques (R2).
- **Implementation novelty questioned**: The fast ray–bounding volume hierarchy intersection is already implemented in packages like OptiX or Embree; the paper’s claimed contribution in this area needs clarification (R1). Overall, the core idea of representing the scene with bboxes is a marginal improvement over existing grid-based NeRF methods (R4).
- **Proofreading and presentation issues**: Inconsistent numbers for pipes1 scene across Table 1 and Table 2; duplicated “requiring” in Sec 1; SSIM highlighting error for tower2 (0.46 highlighted but 0.55 better) (R1). Lack of averages and poor table formatting (R2).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R1, score 5) found the method straightforward and effective, but the other three reviewers (R2 score 3, R3 score 1, R4 score 3) raised the significant concerns listed above. The most critical review (R3, score 1) states that the experiments are unconvincing and the method may only work on scenes with small, delicate objects and large repetitive background.
