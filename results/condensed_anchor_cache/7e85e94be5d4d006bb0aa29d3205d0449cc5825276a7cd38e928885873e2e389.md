## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes applying the LLM alignment pipeline (pretrain–SFT–RLHF) to game agents in a 3D console game (Bleeding Edge). Agents are first pretrained via imitation learning on diverse human gameplay, then supervised finetuned on curated demonstrations for a target behavior (navigating to one of three jumping pads), and finally aligned using reinforcement learning from human preferences (REINFORCE with a learned reward model). Experiments show improvements in task success after each stage, but also reveal challenges such as difficulty in re-alignment and sensitivity to reward model initialization.

### Strengths
- **Important and timely topic**: Exploring the LLM alignment scheme in game AI is valuable and could drive interest from both LLM and game AI communities (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Interesting environment**: The game (Bleeding Edge) is distinctive and differs from common benchmarks (Reviewer 3).
- **Thorough experimental details**: The analysis of why the right jumppad agent underperforms despite map symmetry is helpful, and the gap when using a random encoder is clearly shown (Reviewer 1).
- **Combined offline–online contribution**: Using the learned reward model to rank offline trajectories and then finetuning on the top 20% is a meaningful addition beyond prior work that typically studies each setting separately (Reviewer 1).
- **Interesting empirical results**: The investigation yields notable findings, such as the difficulty of re-alignment after RLHF, which aligns with concerns about maintaining diversity during alignment (Reviewer 2). (Note: one reviewer [2] calls these results “interesting but not surprising” but still a strength.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The overall process (bootstrapping RL with imitation learning and finetuning with learned reward models) has been studied before outside LLMs (Reviewer 1). The technique is incremental, combining IL, SFT, and REINFORCE (Reviewer 4).
- **Task is too narrow and not challenging enough** (agreed by majority):
  - Only three locations tested, with a maximum rollout of 100 timesteps (~10 seconds) and 16-dim actions – much simpler than many locomotion control tasks (Reviewers 2, 3). The goal lacks visual distractions, semantic richness, or the need for complex goal understanding that would parallel LLM alignment challenges (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 acknowledges the value of studying a simpler setting but agrees it is too limited.
- **Reproducibility concerns**:
  - No human behavior data or game environment will be released (Reviewer 3).
  - Insufficient details on the environment’s internal mechanics and physics, given the game is not widely known (Reviewer 3).
- **No benchmark comparisons**: The results are not compared with basic model-free or model-based RL baselines; classical control algorithms should provide a baseline (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experiments and ablations** (multiple reviewers):
  - **Scaling law**: Whether performance scales with model size or data is not explored (Reviewer 4).
  - **In-context learning**: Whether preferences can be captured via demonstrations in the model context is not studied (Reviewer 4).
  - **Context length**: The context is only 32 timesteps, far smaller than typical LLMs (≥2k); whether this is sufficient for useful information is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
  - **Alternative RL algorithms**: Why undiscounted REINFORCE rather than PPO? Direct preference optimization (DPO) also not tested (Reviewer 4). Only the last layer is finetuned; full finetuning or LoRA are not tried (Reviewer 4).
  - **Offline-to-online RL**: Comparison and discussion are missing (Reviewer 4).
  - **Generalization**: Only a single environment and a single-task setting; multi-task or other environments not considered (Reviewer 4).
  - **Right jumppad exploration**: The poor performance on the right jumppad might benefit from more sophisticated exploration (e.g., entropy bonus, intrinsic rewards), but this was not attempted (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity issue** (minor): The first stage is called “unsupervised pretraining” in Figure 1, but it uses imitation learning (supervised) (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation suggestion**: The paper would benefit from an overarching heat-map showing how agent behavior evolves across map locations at each stage (Reviewer 1).
- **Confidence/quality disagreement**: While some reviewers (1, 2) praise the thorough investigation, others (3) rate soundness as poor and find the paper premature; one reviewer (2) notes the work is “a bit premature in terms of width and depth.”
