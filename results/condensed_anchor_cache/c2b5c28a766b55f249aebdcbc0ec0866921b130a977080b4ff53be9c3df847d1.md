## Merged Review

### Summary
PROSE (Predicting Operators and Symbolic Expressions) is a bimodal transformer model that predicts ODE solutions and their governing equations from initial numerical trajectory points and a symbolic (possibly corrupted) representation of the velocity field. The numerical trajectory is encoded by a 2-layer transformer, the symbolic equation (represented as a tree in Polish notation) by a 4-layer transformer; the two representations are fused via an 8-layer transformer, then decoded by an 8-layer trajectory decoder and an 8-layer auto-regressive symbolic decoder. Experiments on 15 dynamical systems, with parameters sampled from small intervals around target values (typically [0.9,1.1]), show accurate numerical predictions and symbolic equation recovery.

### Strengths
- The bimodal numeric/symbolic approach is promising, and the proposed architecture is a sensible design for fusing these modalities (R1).
- The paper is well-written and provides careful background (R2).
- The topic of merging numerical and symbolic information in Transformers is interesting and relevant (R2).
- Experiments demonstrate that the model effectively leverages symbolic information to improve trajectory prediction, confirming the benefit of modality fusion (R2).
- The research question (ML for differential equations) is significant, the multi-modal Transformer approach is technically sound, and the novelty is reasonable (R3).
- One reviewer found the experimental results impressive with high precision (R3).

### Weaknesses
- The evaluation is severely limited: only 15 ODE systems, most with very few free parameters (4/15 have 1, 3/15 have 2, 4/15 have 3), sampled from a narrow interval (0.9–1.1). The training set contains 512k examples (~34k per system), and the model runs 80 epochs of 2000 steps with batch size 512 (81.9M total examples). Given the model size (hundreds of millions of parameters) and low diversity, there is a high risk of memorization and train/test contamination (R1). Two reviewers (R2, R1) consider the hypothesis space too small: a simple 15-class classifier plus parameter optimization (e.g., BFGS) could substitute the symbolic decoder, undermining the claim of model discovery (R2). One reviewer noted the experiments were highly precise, but the other two found them insufficient due to small scale and lack of baselines (contrast: R3 vs R1/R2).
- No comparisons with existing methods are provided. Missing baselines include SINDy, DeepONet, and symbolic regression approaches such as Becker et al. (ICML 2023), D’Ascoli et al., Biggio, Kamienny et al., and transformer-based symbolic velocity field predictors (R1, R2, R3). Without such comparisons, it is impossible to assess relative performance or whether the high accuracy stems from the method rather than the simplicity of the equations (R1, R3).
- The methodology lacks justification for many architectural choices. The 8-layer fusion transformer may be overkill – smaller fusion networks (1–2 layers, or even a single linear layer) should be ablated (R1). The 3-token floating-point encoding (vocabulary ~10⁴ words) is cited from Charton 2022, but the exact precision is not stated (R1). The handling of equivalent expressions (e.g., x+2 vs 2+x, x+y+z with different associativity) is not addressed, yet the paper claims “one-to-one correspondence between trees and mathematical expressions” which is incorrect (R1).
- Important training details are missing: the training loss function and how the two decoders are balanced (simultaneous vs separate training) are not specified (R1).
- Missing ablation studies: the contribution of multimodal inputs is not verified via single-modality ablation (R3). The impact of the training window size (currently fixed up to 2 seconds) is not explored; results may be sensitive to this choice (R2).
- The analysis of noisy or corrupted symbolic inputs is incomplete. When input symbolic information is wrong, does the symbolic decoder correct the errors, or simply reproduce the corrupted input? No systematic evaluation is provided (R2). The paper only states that the model handles noise/errors, but does not show that the decoder can recover the true equation from a corrupted version.
- Additional missing analyses: error as a function of extrapolation interval (e.g., training on (0,2), testing on (2,4), (4,6), (6,8)), error as a function of system dimension and number of free parameters, and use of alternative metrics (L∞, L1) beyond those reported (R1). Table 4 lacks a caption indicating it is for the Unknown 3D experiments (R1).
- The novelty is limited when symbolic information is unavailable: without it, the model reduces to a simple forecasting network, whereas the claimed contribution relies on the symbolic decoder; however, with only 15 equations the decoder cannot do more than choose among seen classes (R2).
- Related works are missing: Becker et al. (ICML 2023), D’Ascoli et al., Biggio, Kamienny et al. (R1), and the transformer-based symbolic velocity field predictors (R2). The claim of novelty is weakened by these omissions.
