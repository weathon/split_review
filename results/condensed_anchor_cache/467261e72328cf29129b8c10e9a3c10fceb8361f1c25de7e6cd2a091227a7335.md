## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DiffSketch, a method to generate sketches from text or images given a single reference drawing. It uses features from a pretrained Stable Diffusion (SD) UNet and VAE decoder, selects and aggregates representative diffusion features across timesteps, and trains a sketch generator with a CLIP-based directional loss and a novel condition diffusion sampling scheme (CDST) for diversity. A distilled version (DiffSketch₍distilled₎) is trained on 30k synthetic pairs for fast inference. Experiments compare against style transfer and sketch extraction methods on HED, XDoG, and Anime styles, evaluated with FID and user studies.

### Strengths

- **Novel use of SD features**: Applying SD UNet and VAE decoder features for sketch generation is a fresh perspective, with two-level aggregation capturing both overall structure and high-frequency details (R1, R2). Observations on hyperparameter choices (number of clusters, timestep effects) and the CLIP sampling limitation are well justified (R4, R2).
- **One-shot capability**: Only one reference sketch is needed for training, addressing data scarcity in sketch datasets (R2, R3, R6).
- **Thorough feature analysis**: The paper provides detailed justification for feature selection and the aggregation process (R2, R6). The diffusion-based sampling scheme (CDST) is interesting for generating diverse training examples (R6).
- **New evaluation criteria**: Proposes a set of evaluation metrics for sketch generation (R3).
- **Good supplemental material**: Quantitative and qualitative comparisons with multiple methods are included (R5, R3).

Minority positive views: R3 credits the method for reducing dependence on large datasets and conducting comparative analysis, though finds innovation lacking overall. R4 comments that observations on SD bias are astute.

### Weaknesses

**Method clarity and presentation**  
- The paper is very difficult to follow; sections are poorly organized (e.g., Sec 3 and 4 overlap, Sec 4.1 repeats Sec 3.2–3.3) and many details require moving between sections (R1, R4, R5, R6).  
- Sentence constructions hinder readability and the entire text needs revision (R4, R6).  
- Reference formatting uses `\citet` instead of `\citep`, making text and references hard to distinguish (R6).  
- A summarized algorithm is missing, and the paper is not self-contained—many parts refer to the appendix (R6).  
- The distillation process in Sec 4.4 is vague: it only mentions Pix2PixHD without any detail on how knowledge is transferred (R4).  
- Key variables are undefined (e.g., feature gate G*, v_{i,n}, cluster centers, regularization terms) and figures are poorly labeled (Fig 2: 12 curves unexplained; Fig 4 typo U_{md}) (R1, R6).  
- The objective includes a CLIP similarity loss (CLIPsim) but no ablation study for that loss is provided (R5).

**Ablation studies insufficient**  
- The ablation study is very weak: only L1 loss is ablated, while the contributions of all other losses (CLIP directional, regularization) are not quantitatively evaluated (R4, R5).  
- Hyperparameter search for the weight of L1 and other losses is missing (R4).  
- “One timestep” gives competitive performance; comparison over a range of timesteps (following DDIM) is needed to verify robustness (R4).  
- The proposed CDST and SD feature selection bring only weak improvement over simpler alternatives (R1).  
- No quantitative comparison of SD+VAE vs. SD-only feature extraction is provided, despite that being a major claimed contribution (R4).

**Missing comparisons and benchmarks**  
- Missing comparisons with recent style-injection diffusion works: Style Injection in Diffusion (Chuang CVPR 2024) and Diffusion Hyperfeatures (Luo NeurIPS 2023) (R1).  
- Missing comparisons with existing sketch generation methods: DiffSketcher, CLIPasso, Clipascene (R2).  
- Evaluation is limited to edge-extraction datasets (BSDS500). Real human-drawn sketch datasets (TU-Berlin, Sketchy) are not used, so applicability to authentic human sketches is unverified (R2, R3, R6).  
- The validation dataset is very small, and the requirement that the user must draw a sketch for a generated image severely limits practical use and adaptation to existing sketch datasets (R1, R4).  
- While HED and XDoG are used as styles, they are binarized edge maps rather than human-like sketches; the method also performs poorly on the more human-like Anime style (w/o CDST outperforms the full method on anim) and fails to generate clean sparse sketches for Artist 1 in the supplementary (R6, Fig 22).  
- Specific numbers missing: scores of Equal Feature in Table 2 are not provided (R6).

**Applicability and conceptual issues**  
- The method generates boundaries/edgemaps, not hand-drawn sketches. Hand-drawn sketches have unique abstraction and variation that are not captured by this approach (R3, R2).  
- Definitions of “personalized sketch extraction” and “sketch style” are unclear; treating contour extraction techniques (HED, XDoG) as “styles” conflates them with human stylistic diversity (R3).  
- The paper claims to be tailored for sketches, but the design is not sketch-specific—it can transfer any style (the same CLIP directional loss framework is used for general style editing). Only sketch applications are shown, which is limited (R6).  
- XDoG and HED are known, simple edge extraction methods; training a complex pipeline to imitate them has limited value. Real human-drawn sketches are what the field truly needs (R6).  
- Training time is hidden: DiffSketch takes 4.74s per inference, and generating 30k pairs for distillation takes ~50 hours, making the claim of “fast inference with a single training example” misleading (R1).

**Minority criticism on innovation**  
- The paper lacks technological innovation: it offers no new perspectives or insights beyond applying existing SD feature aggregation and CLIP losses to sketch generation (R3, echoed partially by R5 who questions the source of stylization).  
- One-shot learning capabilities are not clearly articulated or demonstrated in the text (R3).
