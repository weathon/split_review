- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Regularized Distribution Matching Distillation (RDMD) for one-step unpaired image-to-image translation, adding a quadratic transport cost regularizer to DMD and connecting to optimal transport. Experiments are conducted on several I2I tasks at 64×64 resolution (pixel and latent space). Reviewers disagreed: two (scores 5,5) found the method interesting and empirically effective; two (scores 3,3) found the contribution incremental and experiments insufficient.

### Strengths
- The paper proves that the solution of soft-constrained RDMD converges to that of the hard-constrained Monge problem (Theorem 1). Reviewer 1 found this proof complicated but a strength; Reviewer 4 also noted this relationship as a strength.
- Applying distribution matching distillation to unpaired I2I translation is an interesting direction (Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 2 found empirical results effective and the method efficient at inference (one-step generation). Reviewer 3 noted experiments were conducted on various data and resolutions in both pixel-space and latent diffusion.
- Reviewer 4 found the paper easy to follow.

### Weaknesses

**Limited novelty**
- The contribution is incremental (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 observed that RDMD primarily extends DMD (text-to-image with Gaussian prior) to I2I by substituting the Gaussian with a source distribution and adding a transport constraint, lacking deeper justification. Reviewer 3 viewed the quadratic term ‖x−G_θ(x)‖² as a simple regularization and argued that, because Equations 9‑11 replace the Gaussian input with p_source and retrain G_θ, the method should be seen as fully retraining a generator rather than distillation.

**Missing comparisons and related work**
- The paper omits relevant optimal‑transport‑based methods: OTCS (Reviewer 1), NOT, OTM (Reviewer 4), and other OT approaches cited by Reviewer 3 (e.g., [J. Fan et al. 2021, J. Choi et al. 2023, Y. Shi et al. 2023, N. Gushchin et al. 2024]).
- Missing comparisons with GAN‑based I2I methods like CUT (Reviewer 3) and with fully trained I2I models assisted by diffusion models (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 specifically requested a comparison with NOT and OTM, noting that they achieve competitive FID with large backbones (DDPM ++), and asked for clarification of RDMD’s advantages over them.
- Reviewer 2 noted missing references to acceleration techniques without additional training (e.g., [1,2,3]) and unpaired I2I methods that could leverage such acceleration (e.g., [4,5,6]), which would provide stronger baselines.
- Definitions/citations for CycleDiff and DDIB are missing (Reviewer 3).

**Experimental weaknesses**
- Experiments are limited and do not consistently outperform baselines across metrics (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). In Table 1, SDEdit and DDIB achieve competitive FID with inferior transport costs, but no visual examples are provided to justify the trade‑off (Reviewer 4). The practical benefits of the optimal‑transport connection are not fully demonstrated (Reviewer 1).
- The 64×64 resolution for primary experiments is questionable; it limits visual complexity and makes quality assessment difficult, especially for fine details in I2I (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 wondered if this was due to computational or efficiency challenges.
- Qualitative evaluations at this resolution are very hard to judge (Reviewer 1).
- The toy experiments (Figure 2) lack quantitative results – transport cost and Wasserstein distance between generated and target distributions should be provided (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 2 suggested using FID computed on the target distribution only (rather than source+target) because other metrics do not adequately capture translation quality.

**Theoretical and modeling concerns**
- The assumptions required to guarantee bijectivity of the Monge optimal transport map are not clearly stated (Reviewer 4, line 179), making theoretical validity difficult to assess.
- Reviewer 2 questioned the choice of squared‑difference norm for transport cost, arguing it may be too simple to capture semantic details; suggested a more robust cost like the Energy function from EGSDE. This may lead to high faithfulness (low L2) but low realism (high FID), as observed.
- Reviewer 2 asked whether both the one‑step generator and the fake diffusion model can converge with the proposed loss, and requested training curves for both models.
- Reviewer 3 asked whether the method uses LPIPS as a loss term (as DMD does) during training.
- Reviewer 4 asked for clarification on whether all models in Tables 1 and 2 share the same backbones (except the pixel‑space EGSDE) for fair comparison.

**Training resource analysis**
- The training phase requires an additional fake model; Reviewer 2 requested an analysis of the training resources needed for the proposed framework, and also asked whether convergence is challenging for the two models.

**Presentation issues**
- Missing definition/citation for CycleDiff and DDIB (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 1 noted OTCS is missing from the related work.
- Reviewer 4 requested details for assumptions in line 179 and quantitative results for toy experiments.

*Note: The higher‑scored reviewers (scores 5,5) also raised several of these weaknesses; the lower‑scored reviewers (scores 3,3) were more critical on novelty and experimental rigor.*
