## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a flow-based generative model for 3D point clouds that uses an offline precomputation of approximate optimal transport (OT) pairs between noise and data, followed by online subsampling to form training couplings. A hybrid coupling that mixes approximate OT with independent coupling is also introduced to reduce the complexity of the learned flow. The method is evaluated on ShapeNet for unconditional generation and shape completion, outperforming prior diffusion and flow baselines. Reviewers generally recognize the novelty and effectiveness, especially with few inference steps, but raise concerns about missing baselines, limited evaluation scope (diversity, multi-category, other domains), insufficient theoretical guidance for key parameters, and computational/scalability issues. Two reviewers are more positive (scores 8), while three are moderately positive (scores 6).

### Strengths
- The method achieves top performance among approximate OT flow and diffusion baselines, especially in the low-iteration regime, generating fine 3D point results with limited steps.
- The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of existing OT-based methods, systematically identifying their limitations and proposing concrete solutions.
- It is surprising yet convincing that more optimal OT leads to poorer performance due to high Lipschitz requirements; this insight supports the hybrid coupling design.
- The adaptation of optimal transport methods for point cloud generation is a novel and significant contribution that addresses permutation invariance.
- The paper demonstrates a good balance between theoretical foundations and empirical validation, supporting claims with mathematical analysis and extensive experiments.
- The hybrid approach effectively manages the trade-off between computational resources and model performance, resulting in a practical solution.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** Energy-based models (Generative PointNet, Generative VoxelNet) that are naturally permutation-invariant are not discussed or compared. Recent diffusion models that address permutation invariance (point-voxel diffusion, SE(3)-Diffusion, decomposed latent diffusion) are not included as baselines. Other fast sampling methods (e.g., fast point cloud generation with straight flows) are not considered, limiting the comparison of sampling efficiency.
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Only 1-NNA CD and EMD are reported for quality; diversity (COV with CD/EMD, as in DiT-3D) is not measured. Experiments focus solely on single-category generation; multi-category training on full ShapeNet-13, ShapeNet-55, or Objaverse is missing. The method is not validated on other complex domains (e.g., large molecular structures, protein configurations), leaving questions about broader applicability.
- **Scalability and computational cost:** The method still requires offline computation of a dense OT superset, which can be costly for large point clouds. No details are given on the number of points used for precomputation or the time per shape. Training takes about four days on a cluster with four A100 GPUs, indicating high computational overhead. The method also needs separate training per category, reducing efficiency compared to contemporary multi-category approaches.
- **Lack of theoretical guidance for key parameters:** There is insufficient theoretical foundation for determining optimal superset size M and the hybrid coupling blending coefficient β. Only empirical results are provided, making it hard to connect with existing theoretical frameworks or adapt to new datasets/tasks.
- **No study of performance across varying resolutions:** The paper claims good scaling but does not evaluate generation quality at different point cloud resolutions. High-resolution methods (PointInfinity, skip-attention) are not compared.
- **Rotational invariance is not considered or discussed** (noted by two reviewers).
- **Missing experiments at higher inference steps:** It is unclear whether the method continues to outperform baselines when using many steps (e.g., 1000). Also, comparisons after convergence are not provided.
- **Figure 4 visualization:** The number of points used for training in the visualization experiments is not clearly stated (the caption says 30 points for visualization but not for training).
- **Unclear description:** The term “hyperparameters” in Line 374 is ambiguous.
- **Handling of noisy data/outliers** is not discussed.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms:**
  - Can a worse but faster OT approximation (e.g., Feydy et al.) replace the hybrid coupling and achieve the same benefit as Gaussian noise perturbation?
  - How critical is the size of the precomputed superset for model performance?
  - How should β vary depending on dataset characteristics and task requirements?
  - What strategies are planned to address scalability in practice?
