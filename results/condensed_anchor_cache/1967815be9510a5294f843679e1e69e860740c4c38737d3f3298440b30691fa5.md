## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes an iterative method to refine pruned neural networks by combining the sparse weight matrix with a low-rank approximation of the residual error between the dense and sparse matrices. Theoretical convergence analysis is provided. Experiments on LLaMA models (up to 13B) under different pruning techniques (magnitude, Wanda) and sparsity levels show perplexity improvements (e.g., 53.9% reduction at 50% sparsity on LLaMA-7B) and parameter reduction (8.6% at ~50% sparsity).

### Strengths

- The idea of using a low-rank adjustment to bridge the gap between dense and sparse models is interesting and reasonable for compressing LLMs without retraining (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- The method is efficient and shows promising results for refining sparse models without retraining (Reviewer 1, 4).
- Paper is well-written, clear, and well-organized (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- Theoretical analysis is solid and provides a foundation for the method’s validity and convergence (Reviewer 1, 3; Reviewer 3 notes the bound is not tight enough to guarantee practical performance, but acknowledges the effort).
- Multiple baselines and approximation strategies are presented (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: The combination of low-rankness and sparsity is a well-explored topic in matrix decomposition and DNN compression (e.g., CVPR 2017 paper on low-rank + sparse decomposition, Allerton 2009/ICML 2011 Sparse and Low-Rank Decompositions) (Reviewer 1, 3). Scaling to large matrices/LLMs provides little technical contribution. Similar decompositions for mitigating compression error during fine-tuning already exist (LQ-LoRA, LoQT, LoftQ) (Reviewer 1). The method is also similar to LoSparse, SLoPe, OATS; the paper does not clearly differentiate from them (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient and unfair experimental evaluation**:
    - Experiments are limited to LLaMA models up to 13B; no tests on larger models (30B, 70B) or on CNN architectures (despite the title targeting "sparse neural networks") (Reviewer 2, 4).
    - Only perplexity is reported for LLMs; standard benchmark metrics (e.g., downstream tasks) are missing. For Wanda, no downstream task results are provided (Reviewer 2, 4).
    - The method is only evaluated on sparse models from magnitude and Wanda pruning; SparseGPT is not tested (Reviewer 4).
    - Accuracy comparison with sparsity is not fair: adding a low-rank part obviously improves accuracy, but the storage cost of the low-rank component is not reported, so the actual compression ratios are unclear (Reviewer 3).
    - Random sparsity beyond 4:8 and 2:4 patterns is not meaningful for practical GPU acceleration (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 3 asks for practical model sizes and speedups).
    - For 2:4 sparsity on 13B, accuracy drops from 40.4 to 31.2, limiting practical impact (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparisons**:
    - No comparison with prior extensive structured pruning literature (Reviewer 1).
    - No comparison against other training-free refinement methods for sparse LLMs (e.g., DsNot) in terms of time and performance (Reviewer 4).
    - It is not shown whether the method can outperform traditional fine-tuning like LoRA, or if further fine-tuning the low-rank parameters with LoRA could yield better results (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear motivation**: Why should the difference between sparse and dense blocks be low-rank? This is not justified (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical weakness**: Error bound is not tight and cannot guarantee anything in practical assessment (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 2) gives significantly lower rating (3 vs 5,5,5)**, citing insufficient experimental scope and unclear motivation. This reviewer also gave a lower soundness (2) and contribution (2).

*Note: Reviewer questions about decomposition in gradient space (Reviewer 1), further fine-tuning with LoRA (Reviewer 4), and practical speedups/sizes (Reviewer 3) are included as they overlap with missing experiments or ablations.*
