- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a workflow for generating stable crystal structures of multi-component alloys (specifically NiFeCr) by adapting the Crystal Diffusion Variational Autoencoder (CDVAE). The main modification is a phase prediction network (P-CDVAE) that classifies local structure as FCC or BCC. The authors also contribute a dataset (DFT and MD simulations) and a data augmentation method via local search. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of tackling high-entropy alloys (HEAs) with generative models and the dataset contribution, but universally find the presentation unclear, the experiments insufficiently detailed, and the scope limited. All three reviewers gave a score of 3 (borderline reject) with soundness and presentation rated fair to poor.

### Strengths
- The problem – using generative models to discover new HEA structures – is mostly unexplored and significant. (R2, R3)
- The phase prediction network added to CDVAE appears to improve structural integrity of generated crystals, as measured by CP G‑distance and visualisations. (R1)
- The authors contribute a dataset (DFT and MD simulations) and the dataset creation workflow, which they plan to make available. (R2)
- The method shows excellent agreement in identifying stable configurations when compared to first‑principles data. (R1, implied in abstract)

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity are critically poor.** The paper is difficult to follow even for ML specialists with a materials background. Key concepts (e.g., “phase”, “local reconstruction score”, “phase prediction network”) are not defined or explained for the NeurIPS audience. (R1, R2, R3)  
  * R2: “I had significant difficulty in following some sections … the extensive use of materials/physics jargon.”  
  * R3: “I understand the goal is to search for new HEA structures. But I don’t understand how this is achieved by the work … the submission does not have a clear problem formulation.”  
  * R1: “Overall the paper is difficult to follow for machine learning specialists (main ICLR audience) because the main discussion points are with respect to the properties of the generated structures and less about the design choices regarding the model.”

- **Insufficient methodological detail.** Several critical aspects of the model and experiments are unclear or not explained.  
  * What is the structure of one training sample? Is it a graph with node features? (R3)  
  * How is the self-supervised task formed? What is the input to the model? (R3)  
  * Is the phase an observed variable used during training? (R3)  
  * Does the model use different cell sizes (number of atoms) for training and testing? Does it generalise to different atom counts? (R1)  
  * How big is the training set? (R1)  
  * During training, is the output of the crystal phase classifier fed to the decoder, or is the label fed directly? (R1)  
  * The “local search for data augmentation” – is it used to expand the training set or to jump out of local minima during Langevin dynamics optimisation? (R1)  
  * The “local reconstruction score” – is it used as a replacement for the reconstruction error during training or only as an evaluation metric? Why is it not used as the cost function if it is better? (R1)  
  * Section 3 (“Methods”) is particularly hard to parse: reconstruction scores based on MAE/RMSE are mentioned without explaining how these are computed for crystal structures or why they are appropriate. (R2)  
  * Algorithm 1 (property optimisation) has many undefined variables (e.g., z, T, E, x) and is barely explained in the text. (R2)  
  * The concept of “phase” plays a central role but is never defined. (R2)

- **Limited scope and shallow related work.**  
  * The method is demonstrated only on a specific ternary alloy (NiFeCr). It remains uncertain whether the framework will apply to other alloys or whether future research can easily build on this work. (R2)  
  * Related work is identified but not put in context; the discussion is superficial. (R2)

- **Missing comparisons and speed/efficiency claims not justified.**  
  * In Section 4.4, the authors state that the model finds bulk moduli similar to those from local search. How much faster is the model than local search without ML? Did the model use the local search data for training? If so, the claim of “gaining” from the model is not supported. (R1)

- **Data duplication concern.** The authors mention making the “workflow” available, not necessarily the full dataset. It is unclear whether this suffices for the community to reproduce or build upon the work. (R2)

- **Figures and captions are insufficient.** Figure 1 is not referenced in the text; Figure 2 has a very short caption and is not interpretable; Algorithm 1 has only a title as caption. (R2)

- **Minority reviewer contrast (R3):** R3 is particularly explicit about not understanding the problem formulation, stating that they could follow the original CDVAE paper but not this submission. This contrast should be noted: even a reader with weak chemistry background could understand the baseline CDVAE, but the present work fails to convey its core ideas.
