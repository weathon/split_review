## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Feedback-on-Feedback (FoF), a prompting framework that uses meta-feedback (feedback on the feedback) to improve self-correction in LLMs. The idea is to sample multiple pieces of feedback, measure their consistency via TF‑IDF cosine similarity, and generate refined feedback based on meta‑feedback derived from inconsistencies. Experiments are conducted on GPT‑3.5 and LLaMA‑3‑8B across three tasks (arithmetic reasoning, machine translation, code generation). The majority of reviewers (ratings 3,3,3) find the improvements over baselines (self‑refine, self‑consistency) to be small and often within variance, and question the motivation for the meta‑feedback step and the choice of TF‑IDF for similarity. One reviewer (rating 5) is more positive, finding the technique interesting and the insights about answer changes valuable, but also notes that the improvements are modest and the justification for why second feedback is less error‑prone is missing.

### Strengths
- The paper explores an important and under‑explored direction: improving the quality of feedback itself rather than the final answer in self‑correction (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- The analysis of feedback consistency using a symbolic measure (cosine similarity on TF‑IDF features) is shown to be more effective than prompting a model to select consistent feedback (Reviewer 3).
- The technique genuinely improves generation quality over the self‑refine baseline on weaker models (GPT‑3.5, LLaMA‑3‑8B), with gains such as +3.6 points on GSM8K and +6.4 points on MBPP for LLaMA‑3‑8B (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The paper includes robust experimental considerations, such as keeping prompts consistent and controlling for the number of tokens used (Reviewer 3).
- Figures and the description of the framework are clear and helpful (Reviewer 3).
- A case study shows that stronger critic models (GPT‑4) provide better feedback, and the paper also provides a concrete example where FoF fails (Reviewer 2).
- The analysis of the correlation between feedback score and answer accuracy is somewhat interesting (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Statistical significance and effect size:** The performance improvements in Table 1 are not significant—error margins overlap in almost all cases. The difference between FoF and self‑refine is often within the reported variance (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 also notes that the improvement is not large enough to tell whether it comes from the meta‑feedback strategy or simply from an additional refinement step.
- **Lack of evaluation on stronger models:** Experiments are limited to GPT‑3.5 and LLaMA‑3‑8B. It is unclear whether FoF provides any improvement when a stronger model (e.g., GPT‑4) is used either as the base model or as the critic (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The paper assumes access to a stronger model for feedback, which raises the question of why that stronger model is not used for response generation directly (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair comparison due to API calls:** FoF makes several more API calls (multiple feedback samples, meta‑feedback step) than the self‑refine or self‑consistency baselines. This makes the comparison unequal (Reviewer 2). The number of refinement steps and samples drawn should be clearly reported (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear motivation for meta‑feedback:** The premise is that LLMs produce noisy/hallucinated feedback, but the paper does not justify why a second round of feedback (meta‑feedback) would have lower error rates. The same reasoning that causes the first feedback to be error‑prone could apply to the second (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Methodological concern with TF‑IDF for semantic similarity:** TF‑IDF only matches words, not semantic meaning. It is likely a poor choice for measuring the consistency of feedback. The authors should compare against alternatives such as BERTScore, sentence‑level embeddings, or directly prompting an LLM (e.g., GPT‑4) to judge similarity (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The citation for TF‑IDF is also outdated (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient analysis of the feedback quality improvement:** The paper does not provide an isolated evaluation showing that meta‑feedback actually improves the quality of feedback. The claims that FoF generates more diverse answers and results in fewer “mischanges” (wrong‑to‑wrong or correct‑to‑wrong) need quantitative validation: count tables (wrong‑to‑wrong, wrong‑to‑correct, correct‑to‑wrong) for FoF vs. self‑refine, metrics for diversity, and direct metrics for feedback quality are missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation or controls:** Table 2 shows that FoF does not notably outperform self‑refine with 0 samples; the paper should address this directly (Reviewer 3). The “Feedback Sampling Consistency” experiment actually compares self‑consistency to FoF, but the role of consistency is not fully disentangled (Reviewer 3).
- **Results presentation issues:** 
  - The base model used for the first row of Table 2 differs for GPT‑3.5 (Reviewer 2).
  - The mapping between baselines (CoT prompting, self‑refine, self‑consistency) and the column labels in Tables 1 and 2 is unclear; e.g., “+ Initial Answer” is unexplained (Reviewer 3). CoT results are not explicitly shown (Reviewer 3).
  - In Figure 3 and its caption, the feedback score calculation is not explained (Reviewer 2), and there is an inconsistency: the caption reports 42% while the heatmap shows 69% (Reviewer 3).
  - Table 1 and Table 2 scores for GSM8K do not match; clarification is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation quality:** Figure 2 exceeds the page bounding box (Reviewer 1). Figure 3 axis labels need to be more readable (Reviewer 1). There is extended white space below Figure 5 (Reviewer 2). A typo exists at L094 (“abi9ility”) (Reviewer 3). The refined feedback notation in Figure 2 is inconsistent (Rf vs. FR) (Reviewer 3).
- **Case study and analysis depth:** The single case study in Sec. 5.3 illustrates a bottleneck that is not specific to FoF (self‑refine would likely also fail). The paper would benefit from a case study showing a failure caused by incorrect meta‑feedback (Reviewer 3). More examples for the analysis of answer changes would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 1). The line “mischanges from correct answers to incorrect result in self‑correction failures” (L422) is tautological and likely misstated (Reviewer 3).
- **Statistical significance tests:** It is not reported whether the main results in the tables are statistically significant (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 already notes overlapping error bars, but explicit significance testing is missing.
- **Number of tokens used:** The paper mentions controlling for token count (L425‑429) but does not report the actual resulting token counts (Reviewer 3).
