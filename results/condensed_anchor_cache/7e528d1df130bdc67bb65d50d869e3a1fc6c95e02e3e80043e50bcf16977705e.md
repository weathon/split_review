## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MAZero, an extension of MuZero to multi-agent cooperative settings under the CTDE paradigm. It combines a centralized model with MCTS over joint action spaces, using Optimistic Search Lambda (OS(λ)) and Advantage-Weighted Policy Optimization (AWPO) to improve search efficiency. Experiments are conducted on the SMAC benchmark and one Google Research Football task, comparing against several model-free and model-based MARL baselines. The reviews note that MAZero is the first empirically effective application of the MuZero paradigm to MARL, with clear writing and thorough appendices, but raise concerns about limited experimental scope, insufficient statistical rigor, lack of stochastic environment evaluation, and incomplete validation of the proposed techniques.

### Strengths
- First empirically effective extension of MuZero to multi-agent cooperative environments (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- Clear writing and well-laid-out methodology, including cost functions and detailed appendices (Reviewers 1,3).
- Proposed OS(λ) and AWPO improve search efficiency in large action spaces, enabling tractable MCTS (Reviewers 1,2).
- Demonstrates sample efficiency over model-free methods and comparable or better performance vs. model-based methods on SMAC (Reviewers 1,2,3). Reviewer 1 (score 8) particularly highlights the effective use of prediction with reduced search.
- AWPO is an interesting technique for balancing cloning loss and optimistic values (Reviewer 1).
- Method is generally applicable to Dec-POMDP problems under the CDTE assumption (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope and generalizability**:
  - Only tested on the deterministic SMAC benchmark and a single GRF task; results may not generalize to stochastic environments (Reviewers 2,3,4). Reviewer 4 (highest confidence, score 6) strongly criticizes the empirical validation as "very weak," noting that SMAC is outdated and should be replaced by SMACv2, and that diverse benchmarks (e.g., Multi-agent MuJoCo) are missing.
  - No experiments in the very large action spaces that motivated the work (e.g., 27m_vs_30m); the largest setting tested is 10m_vs_11m (Reviewer 4).
  - Few baseline comparisons; missing recent methods like MBVD, RODE, CDS, etc. (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 also notes it is unclear if MAZero offers performance gain over other CTDE methods.
  - Only 3 seeds for ablations; the number of seeds for the main Table 3 is not clearly reported. Reviewer 4 recommends at least 10 seeds for statistical rigor (Reviewers 3,4).
  - No discussion of hyperparameter tuning or how baseline hyperparameters were chosen (Reviewer 3).
  - The evaluation does not adopt the recommended protocol of Gorsanne et al. (2022) for MARL, which weakens the evidence for effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
- **Method-specific concerns**:
  - Parameter sharing may limit heterogeneous agent behaviors; the paper does not adequately address this limitation. The Shared Individual Dynamic Network and positional encoding are described without sufficient detail (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 also notes scalability concerns for heterogeneous environments.
  - The method requires global reward information at each agent during execution (Reviewer 1).
  - The search component (MCTS) may not be significantly beneficial; there is no empirical investigation of the impact of unroll steps or number of MCTS simulations. With a limited budget (e.g., N=100), it is unlikely that many actions are simulated multiple times, weakening the motivation for OS(λ) (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also questions whether MCTS is used at evaluation time and requests quantification of search overhead.
  - OS(λ) and AWPO are not extensively validated on single-agent large action space problems; the bandit experiment (Figure 1) is too simplistic. Missing comparisons with trivial heuristics like sampling N actions from the learned policy and following the best (Reviewer 4).
  - It is unclear if OS(λ) and AWPO can be applied to other model-based MARL algorithms (Reviewer 2). Potential side-effects of optimism are not studied (Reviewer 4).
  - Computational expense of MAZero may limit scalability (Reviewer 2).
  - Potential drawbacks of the proposed network structure are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and writing issues**:
  - Grammatical typos throughout; the paper should be thoroughly proofread (Reviewer 3). Overly enthusiastic language ("ingenious", "audacious") is discouraged (Reviewers 1,3).
  - CTDE is not spelled out at first mention; loss terms in Equation 2 are not explained; Figure 1 appears unusually smooth, raising questions about whether it shows a single seed (Reviewer 3).
  - The distinction between communication and sharing in Figure 6 is unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - Appendix C's discussion on Adam vs. SGD is brief and does not provide new insight beyond known facts (Reviewer 1).
