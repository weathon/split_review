## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates process supervision (reward per reasoning step) versus outcome supervision (reward for final answer) for training reward models in LLMs, using the MATH dataset. The authors show that process supervision significantly outperforms outcome supervision: their process-supervised model solves 78.2% of a representative MATH test subset. They also demonstrate that active learning improves data efficiency by 2.6×. The paper releases PRM800K, a dataset of 800,000 step-level human feedback labels.

### Strengths
1. **Novel and intuitive idea** – Assigning rewards to intermediate steps leverages problem structure better than outcome-only supervision. [Reviewer 1, 3; note: Reviewer 3 later questions novelty relative to RL reward shaping – see Weakness 3]
2. **Very strong empirical results** – Process-supervised model solves 78.2% of MATH subset, significantly outperforming outcome supervision. [Reviewer 1, 4]
3. **Active learning for data efficiency** – Improves efficacy by 2.6×, making process supervision more practical. [Reviewer 1, 2, 4]
4. **Release of PRM800K dataset** – 800,000 step-level human labels support future research on reasoning and reward modeling. [All reviewers]
5. **Well-written and easy to follow** – [Reviewer 3]
6. **Independent and thorough investigation** – Yields new, valuable conclusions on supervision comparison. [Reviewer 4]

### Weaknesses
1. **Reproducibility concerns (Reviewer 1, confidence 3)** – Insufficient details about base models (model sizes are not revealed; only stated “small-scale base models similar in design to GPT-4 but pretrained with ~200× less compute”). It is unclear whether the success stems from the PRM dataset or model scale. The paper should consider experiments with open models. This issue raises a concern that the work may be better suited as a blog post. Missing details also include: who the human data-labelers are (MTurk, math teachers? proficiency level?) and the source of MathMix (1.5B tokens of math problems – textbooks or web scraping?).
2. **Limited to math domain (all reviewers)** – Experiments are conducted only on the MATH dataset (and other exam datasets). It is unclear whether process supervision generalizes to other reasoning tasks (e.g., essay writing, story generation) where step-level feedback is hard to provide. [Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 4 explicitly asks for results on significantly different datasets]
3. **Novelty questioned (Reviewer 3, score 3; contrast with Reviewer 1,4 who found it novel)** – The idea of dense intermediate rewards is well-known in the RL community (reward shaping), so the contribution may lack novelty. This disagreement is noted: other reviewers (1, 4) considered the idea novel and the investigation thorough.
4. **Missing RLHF application (Reviewer 2, confidence 4)** – The paper does not apply the reward models to RLHF, so it remains unclear how process vs. outcome supervision affects final generation model performance. Additionally, if outcome supervision is large and diverse enough, a model trained with outcome supervision might effectively emulate process supervision by evaluating each step.
5. **Hard to draw comprehensive insights (Reviewer 3, confidence 3)** – More dimensions of advantage for step-level rewards would strengthen the paper. For example, under the same labeling budget, is it better to provide step-level rewards on a small set or outcome-level rewards on a much larger set? (This question overlaps with a missing ablation/comparison.)
