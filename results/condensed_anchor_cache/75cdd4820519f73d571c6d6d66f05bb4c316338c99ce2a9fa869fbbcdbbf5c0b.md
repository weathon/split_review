## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Cayley Maze, a universal mathematical framework based on finite automata and group theory, presented as an open-ended reinforcement learning environment with a formal complexity measure. It is implemented in JAX and tested with UED algorithms (PAIRED, PLR). Reviewer 4 found the goals and messages extremely unclear (rating 1, no strengths identified). Other reviewers acknowledged potential significance and originality (ratings 6, 5, 3) but raised substantial concerns about exposition, experimental validation, and practical utility.

### Strengths
- Rigorous mathematical foundation using group theory and deterministic finite automata to define MDP complexity, enabling precise measurement and control over complexity (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- Claim of universality: every deterministic finite sparse MDP corresponds to an instance of Cayley Maze (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Formal representation of problems (e.g., Rubik’s Cube, sorting, integer factorization) improves reproducibility and comparability across studies (Reviewer 1).
- Strong potential for Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) and related fields; the connection between symmetry and complexity (simpler environments are more symmetric) is intuitive and natural (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- JAX implementation of the environment is a concrete contribution (Reviewer 2).
- Novel perspective that removes redundancy in MDP structure; complexity measure is grounded in algebraic properties (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 1 and 2 explicitly noted the environment’s ability to support diverse UED approaches; experiments combined with PAIRED and PLR.

**Minority contrast:** Reviewer 4 could identify no strengths.

### Weaknesses
1. **Exposition and clarity are poor across multiple dimensions**
   - The paper is poorly written with many unclear statements, English mistakes, typos (e.g., missing articles, “it’s” for “its”, missing spaces around parentheses), and informal language (Reviewer 1, 4).
   - The exposition moves too fast for the target UED audience, assuming advanced abstract algebra knowledge. Key concepts (free monoid, syntactic congruence, monoid generator, wreath product, “simple in group-theoretic sense”) are not adequately explained (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
   - Example 1 (grid) is introduced without context and without visuals; Cayley diagrams should be used to make ideas concrete (Reviewer 2, 4).
   - The paper lacks a conclusion and a related work section (Reviewer 3, 4).
   - Definition 5 does not clearly map onto a UMPD (UED-amenable parameterized MDP); the parameters of the Cayley Maze should be explicitly defined (Reviewer 2).

2. **Experiments are insufficient, poorly described, and do not meet UED evaluation standards**
   - No clear test set of levels, no bar charts, no inter-quartile scores as in prior UED works (e.g., PAIRED, PLR) (Reviewer 2).
   - Missing error bars / performance variation across random seeds; plots lack y-axis labels (Reviewer 3, 4). What does the y-axis represent? The x-axis is unlabeled (Reviewer 3).
   - Figure 3 is unclear (“of of” typo, no self-contained caption); figures should explain the environment, what is measured, and what to conclude (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
   - No visual depictions of generated levels (Reviewer 2).
   - The tasks are not clearly delineated or named, making it hard for others to reference them (Reviewer 2).
   - Details of environment instances are missing: group structures, generators, how they are chosen, how these choices affect maze complexity (Reviewer 3).
   - No steps per second reported for the JAX environment during training (Reviewer 2).
   - The domain randomization algorithm is not described or cited (Reviewer 3).
   - PLR appears still improving in Figure 3; longer runs and tuning of methods should be provided (Reviewer 2).

3. **No empirical link between the proposed complexity measure and agent difficulty**
   - The paper does not show how varying complexity scores (as defined) affects UED algorithm performance (Reviewer 1, 3).
   - No theoretical results or empirical justification demonstrating that the complexity measure is predictive or satisfies desirable properties (e.g., monotonicity) (Reviewer 3).

4. **Open-endedness and universality claims are not substantiated**
   - The definition of an “open-ended RL environment” is never given; simply being a parameterized family of MDPs is too broad (Reviewer 4).
   - The paper focuses on potential (“what is possible”) rather than actual demonstrations (Reviewer 4).
   - The universality claim (Proposition 2) is narrower than stated in the abstract (constrained to deterministic, sparse, finite MDPs) – this constraint should be mentioned in the introduction (Reviewer 2, 3).

5. **Missing stochastic environments and generalization**
   - Cayley Maze is deterministic; no discussion of how to handle stochastic transitions, which limits real-world applicability (Reviewer 1).
   - No experiments or theoretical arguments showing generalization across distinct Cayley Maze tasks (e.g., from Rubik’s Cube to sorting) (Reviewer 1).

6. **Missing comparisons and baselines**
   - No comparison to existing environments (e.g., Omni-EPIC, Minigrid) or to other complexity measures (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
   - No ablation studies (Reviewer 4).

7. **Missing concrete implementation and reproducibility details**
   - The JAX implementation was not provided (Reviewer 1).  
   - The paper does not reference Omni-EPIC (also universal) in the context of universality (Reviewer 2).

8. **Minor clarity issues (selected)**
   - Line 16 should read “A formal definition” (Reviewer 2).
   - Line 85: rhs of implication should be xby ∈ L, not xby ∈ M (Reviewer 2).
   - Line 100: referencing matrix multiplication for function composition is confusing (Reviewer 2).
   - Line 107: “corresponding trajectories” is undefined (Reviewer 2, 4).
   - Line 117: definition of congruences after proposition statement is confusing (Reviewer 3).
   - Line 158: “words” and “language” are undefined (Reviewer 3).
   - Line 173: unclear notation Im(T) (Reviewer 3).
   - Line 213: “in this cases” → “in these cases” (Reviewer 3, 4).
   - Line 213: what does “actions of M(T) on state space” mean? (Reviewer 3).
   - Line 247: “But it’s certainly possible for MDP’s” is not connected (Reviewer 3).
   - Line 226: reference appears incorrect (Demaine E., 2018) (Reviewer 4).

9. **Questions that overlap with criticisms or missing experiments**
   - How can Cayley Maze be adapted to stochastic environments? (Reviewer 1)
   - Can agents trained on one Cayley Maze generalize to another? (Reviewer 1)
   - How does the proposed complexity measure relate to other existing measures? (Reviewer 3)
   - How to generate interesting (non-trivial, non-trivial) instances? (Reviewer 3)
   - How can edits like ACCEL best be applied? (Reviewer 2)
   - Is Theorem 1 actually useful for open-ended RL claims? (Reviewer 4)
   - What is the meaning of “simple in group-theoretic sense”? (Reviewer 4)
   - Why is the construction in line 250 only “similar” to the group-theoretic analogue? (Reviewer 4)
