## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces W-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating watermark robustness against four image editing types (regeneration, global editing, local editing, image-to-video generation), and proposes VINE, a watermarking method using SDXL-Turbo as a generative prior. Experiments on eleven existing methods plus VINE show that most fail after editing, while VINE achieves better robustness and image quality.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-organized, and effectively uses figures to explain both W-Bench and VINE (Reviewer 1, 4, 5).
- W-Bench is the first comprehensive benchmark covering diverse editing techniques (regeneration, global, local, I2V), promoting standardization and comprehensive robustness assessment (Reviewer 1, 3, 4, 5).
- The proposed VINE method is novel: leveraging a pretrained one-step diffusion model (SDXL-Turbo) as a conditional generative encoder, with a reasonable combination of losses (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- Solid validation of frequency-domain patterns: watermark patterns in high-frequency bands are removed by both image editing and blurring, while low-frequency bands are less affected (Reviewer 2).
- Rigorous evaluations testing VINE and eleven other watermarking methods across multiple editing techniques (Reviewer 1).
- Focuses on an important but rarely explored problem—robustness against editing enabled by large-scale generative models (Reviewer 3, 4, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing critical baselines and unfair evaluation**:
  * The paper does not compare against frequency-domain watermarking methods (TreeRing, Gaussian Shading, RingID) that are generally considered robust against image editing (e.g., prompt2prompt) and regeneration. If these methods are also robust, VINE’s contribution may be diminished (Reviewer 3).
  * EditGuard is evaluated but is designed for editing detection, not robust watermarking, and was not tested in its most robust configuration, impacting the fairness of comparisons (Reviewer 1).

- **Limited scope and completeness of W-Bench**:
  * The benchmark includes only four types of editing; it omits other methods such as style transfer, which can significantly alter image statistics and potentially disrupt watermarks (Reviewer 4).
  * The “global editing” category is broad and should be separated into color adjustments, geometric transformations, and texture modifications for more granular evaluation (Reviewer 4).
  * Only one image-to-video generation method is included; other methods with distinct architectures (e.g., [1]) are not evaluated, limiting generality (Reviewer 4).

- **Value of the task questioned and robustness doubts**:
  * The task of watermarking against image editing seems worthless (Reviewer 2). If 250-step noise in image regeneration can significantly disrupt the watermark (bit acc), as shown in Figure 5(a), then global image editing (e.g., SDEdit, prompt2prompt) with 250 steps may also completely remove watermarks, failing to demonstrate true robustness (Reviewer 3).
  * The authors should elaborate on the perspective and value of this task (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing validation and disconnect between analysis and model design**:
  * The hypothesis (Line 249) that a powerful generative prior facilitates more invisible and robust watermark embedding is not validated; the assumptions behind this hypothesis are unclear (Reviewer 2).
  * VINE shows higher brightness in the low-frequency center of the frequency domain (Figure 6 in appendix), which aligns with robustness analysis, but the paper does not explain **why** the SDXL-Turbo-based model exhibits this characteristic, creating a disconnect between the frequency analysis and the specific watermark design. If the authors elucidate these principles, the disconnect could be resolved (Reviewer 5).

- **Insufficient novelty of frequency-domain observations**:
  * The observation that watermark patterns are removed from high-frequency bands after image editing and blurring while low-frequency bands remain is not a new discovery, yet the paper spends excessive text discussing it (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing experiments, ablations, and clarifications**:
  * The purpose of finetuning VINE-B to VINE-R using Instruct-Pix2Pix is not explained (Reviewer 2).
  * Resolution is not unified across experiments (Line 1042) (Reviewer 2).
  * It is unclear whether VINE works only on the image editing task or also on other common watermarking tasks (Reviewer 2).
  * The authors have not considered using representative image editing as an attack template with associated attack loss as an objective function during training; incorporating the specific effects of editing on watermarks into the model design could be informative (Reviewer 5).
  * Subjective experimental results are difficult to discern visually; selecting a subset of images and enlarging specific regions would aid reader comprehension (Reviewer 5).

- **Motivation for using SDXL-Turbo**:
  * The paper does not fully justify why SDXL-Turbo is chosen over other one-step generative models (e.g., SDXS [Song et al., 2024]). If the goal is to avoid multi-step sampling, many alternatives exist (Reviewer 4).

- **Contrast**: One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive about novelty and effectiveness, while the majority (scores 6) raised substantive concerns about missing comparisons, benchmark scope, clarity of contributions, and insufficient validation of design choices.
