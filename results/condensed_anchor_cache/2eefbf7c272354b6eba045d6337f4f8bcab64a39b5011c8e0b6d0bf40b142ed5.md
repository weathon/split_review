## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ImagineNav, a mapless, open-vocabulary visual navigation framework that uses a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to select the best next viewpoint from “imagined” future observations generated by a novel view synthesis (NVS) module. The Where2Imagine module predicts candidate waypoints based on human navigation habits, and a point-goal policy executes low-level movement. Experiments on HM3D and HSSD benchmarks show notable improvements over baselines. Reviewers find the idea novel and the experimental evaluation thorough, but raise concerns about the reliability of the NVS module, lack of memory, waypoint reachability, object ambiguity, and missing comparisons. One reviewer (rating 3) is significantly more critical and holds fundamental doubts about object permanence, waypoint feasibility, and object reference handling, while the other three (ratings 6) are more positive but still identify important limitations.

### Strengths
- Novel use of novel view synthesis as a form of “imagination” for indoor navigation, translating navigation into a best‑view image selection problem for a VLM (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 1 notes it reduces pipeline complexity).
- Achieves high success rates on open‑vocabulary object navigation tasks due to the combination of VLM and diffusion model (Reviewer 2).
- Extensive experimental evaluation on HM3D and HSSD benchmarks with thorough ablation studies (Tables 2, 3, 4, 5) that demonstrate the importance of visual prompting, novel view synthesis, and waypoint prediction (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Effective presentation of successful and failed trajectories (Figure 5) and clear explanations of the framework (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 1 says design better integrates sensor observation with large models).
- Approach is well‑motivated and described in sufficient detail for reproducibility (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Reliability of the novel view synthesis module**: There is no guarantee that the diffusion model understands object permanence; it may hallucinate non‑existing objects or omit objects that should be present, leading to incorrect VLM decisions and reduced success rate. The success rate is lower when the NVS model is added (Table 2, row 3 vs row 5) – more explanation is needed. An approximate distribution of failure modes (e.g., how many failures are due to inaccurate imagined views) would be valuable (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Waypoint reachability and reliance on a local map**: The method does not guarantee that waypoints predicted by Where2Imagine are reachable, especially in out‑of‑distribution scenarios; it still needs some form of local map for low‑level path planning. Tests on a real robot or in novel environments would alleviate this concern (Reviewer 1). In multi‑room or occluded scenarios, reliance on human habits without interactive perception may cause the robot to become trapped in local optima, preventing it from locating the target (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of history / memory**: The framework only uses immediate views for navigation decisions without fully utilizing historical information, which may limit exploration and global path planning over long distances or in intricate environments (Reviewer 2).
- **Object reference ambiguity**: The method does not handle ambiguous object references (e.g., “navigate to the chair” when multiple chairs exist) (Reviewer 1).
- **Computational cost not quantified**: The paper criticizes object detection/segmentation/mapping for computational demand but introduces a computationally intensive NVS module. A comparison in terms of FLOPs, memory usage, and inference time versus traditional approaches is needed to substantiate the claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and ablation of intermediate representation**: Comparisons with end‑to‑end approaches (e.g., SPOC, Reviewer 1) and recent open‑source VLMs with 3D spatial understanding (e.g., Spatial VLM, SpatialRGPT, Reviewer 3) would strengthen the work. More fundamentally, why are waypoints the correct intermediate representation for navigation and object search? What is the point of generating images via NVS if it limits performance – could embeddings be used instead? What are the limitations of VLM high‑level planning? How can the VLM learn online from additional data? (Reviewer 4; also asked by Reviewer 1 about comparison with end‑to‑end methods.)
- **Presentation issues**: Text references to tables and figures are often several pages away from the actual elements, hurting readability (Reviewer 3). The structure of the VLM analysis output is inconsistent between Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 – it is unclear which one is used in the evaluation (Reviewer 1). The conclusion and paragraph 2 of the introduction should be rewritten for clarity (Reviewer 4).
- **Error bars needed**: The experimental evidence would be more convincing with error bars to determine significance (Reviewer 4).
- **Addressing failure cases**: While failed trajectories are discussed, it would be beneficial to see how the framework could be modified to avoid or recover from those failures (Reviewer 4).
