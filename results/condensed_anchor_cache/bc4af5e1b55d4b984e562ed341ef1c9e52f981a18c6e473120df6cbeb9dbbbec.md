## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Chat-TS, an LLM augmented with time-series tokens for multi-modal reasoning over time-series and text. It contributes three synthetic datasets (TS Instruct Training Dataset, TS Instruct QA Benchmark, TS Instruct Qualitative/Quantitative Benchmark) generated via GPT-4o-mini by converting time-series to images and using vision-language models to produce instruction-tuning pairs. A discrete tokenizer transforms continuous time-series into discrete bins without training. Experiments show Chat-TS improves ~15% on the new TS Instruct QA benchmark over baselines and retains performance on text-only tasks (e.g., MMLU). Scores range from 1 to 6; one reviewer (score 1) finds the paper lacking in experimental rigor and formatting, while three reviewers (scores 6, 3, 6) acknowledge the novelty but raise concerns about dataset quality, missing baselines, and generalization.

### Strengths
- **Novel dataset construction**: The paper creates a time-series+text instruction-tuning dataset with four conversation types (e.g., QA, math, decision-making) synthesized via GPT-4o-mini from time-series images. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Preservation of text benchmarks**: Time-series pretraining does not degrade performance on standard text tasks (MMLU) and may improve BBH and GPQA reasoning (Reviewer 1), and overall maintains strong language capabilities (Reviewer 3).
- **Discrete tokenizer**: A straightforward, training-free tokenizer for multivariate time-series that achieves high reconstruction quality (low MSE on validation set). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Detailed quantitative analysis**: The evaluation goes beyond accuracy to assess response quality (Reviewer 4). Chat-TS shows ~15% average improvement on the TS Instruct QA benchmark over existing methods (Reviewer 4).
- **Identifies an underexplored area**: Integration of NLP with time-series data is a promising direction (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset quality and alignment**: The synthetic dataset is generated by GPT-4o-mini from images with only basic filtering; hallucination risks remain (Reviewer 1). Alignment between time-series and text uses GPT-4 without additional validation, raising consistency and accuracy concerns (Reviewer 3). The datasets are entirely synthetic; real-world time-series can be more complex and noisy, limiting practical applicability (Reviewer 4). This also means the dataset creation is essentially a distillation from GPT-4o (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: No comparison with vision-language models (e.g., LLaVA) that could directly process images of time-series (Reviewer 1). No direct comparison with GPT-4o, despite its heavy involvement in dataset creation (Reviewer 3). Common multimodal baselines are absent, making it hard to assess state-of-the-art (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited modality interleaving**: The model only outputs text, ignoring time-series forecasting and multi-modal responses (Reviewer 1). A more fine-grained instruction dataset with multi-modal responses would be needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Mixed experimental results**: The base model performs strongly on the TS Instruct QA benchmark but worse on Math Analysis and Decision Making tasks (Reviewer 3). Why TS training improves BBH/GPQA but harms MMLU-Pro? Is this fluctuation or an inherent difference? (Reviewer 1). Generalization to longer sequences or diverse time-series types is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing analyses and experiments**: No scaling experiment with larger models to test if improvements hold (Reviewer 3). Factors affecting time-series pretraining (context length, vision ability, reasoning) are not explored (Reviewer 1). Could multi-modal (vision+text) pretraining and SFT handle the benchmarks? (Reviewer 1). The paper lacks rigorous analysis of limitations, biases, and practical applications (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues**: Formatting does not conform to ICLR template (margins) (Reviewer 2). Typos: “LLava” → “LLaVA” (line 049), “it’s effectiveness” → “its effectiveness” (line 057), “LLama3.1” → “LLaMA 3.1” (line 327); “qualitative” should be “quantitative” (line 23) (Reviewers 1, 3).
