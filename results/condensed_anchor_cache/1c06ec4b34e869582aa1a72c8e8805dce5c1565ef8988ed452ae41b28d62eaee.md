## Merged Review

### Summary
All three reviewers rate the paper 3 (weak accept/neutral) but identify several shortcomings. Reviewer 1 finds the work informative with innovative use of multi‑objective concepts, but notes inadequate illustration of the methodology, unfocused related work, and insufficient substantiation of claims. Reviewer 2 considers the approach timely for semantic consistency but criticises limited novelty (incremental GA→NSGA), an underdeveloped related‑work section (key black‑box jailbreak papers omitted), lack of ablation study, and missing justification for evaluation choices. Reviewer 3 acknowledges comprehensive experiments and reasonable NSGA‑II integration, but points out missing technical background, insufficient genetic operations, over‑claimed stealthiness, and absence of a standard metric for semantic consistency. Overall the paper is seen as having potential but requiring substantial revision.

### Strengths
- **Multi‑objective innovation.** Introduction of multi‑objective evolutionary concepts (MOEAs, NSGA‑II) to jailbreak attacks is an innovative and promising direction (R1, R3). The use of NSGA‑II is reasonable and allows integration of additional fitness functions (R3).
- **Thorough experiments on ASR.** The experimental part is comprehensive and convincingly demonstrates ASR improvements (R3). Numerous visualisations usefully supplement the text (R1).
- **Timely and relevant problem.** The focus on semantic consistency in adversarial attacks and the use of LLMs in both white‑box and black‑box settings are timely and relevant (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The core contribution – switching from a GA algorithm to NSGA‑II – represents only an incremental improvement over existing work (R2). The use of NSGA‑II is presented more as a novel contribution than as an application of a well‑established algorithm (R1).
- **Underdeveloped related work.** The literature review is notably incomplete. Important black‑box jailbreak methods are omitted, e.g. “Jailbreaking Black Box Large Language Models in Twenty Queries”, “Open Sesame! Universal Black Box Jailbreaking of Large Language Models”, “All in How You Ask for It”, and “Tree of Attacks” (R2). The concept of semantic consistency appears to be borrowed from “Open Sesame!” but is not properly cited (R2). Other genetic‑algorithm‑based jailbreak attacks (e.g., GPTFUZZER, Semantic Mirror Jailbreak) are also missing (R3). Traditional adversarial machine‑learning references dominate, while LLM‑specific black‑box attacks receive insufficient focus (R1).
- **Lack of ablation study.** No ablation study is provided to understand the contribution of individual components (e.g., mutation, crossover, fitness functions). Without it, the relative importance of each part is unclear (R2). Reviewer 1 similarly notes that the method’s extensibility to other goals is claimed but not empirically substantiated.
- **Missing computational overhead analysis.** The paper does not detail the computational overhead of the attack, nor does it describe the computational resources used. This is essential for assessing practicality (R2, R1).
- **Use of older LLMs.** Experiments rely on older models like LLaMA‑2, weakening relevance to state‑of‑the‑art models (R2). (Contrast: Reviewer 3 finds the experiments comprehensive, indicating a disagreement on the impact of model choice.)
- **Unjustified evaluation choices.** The use of “5” as the success indicator via GPT‑Judge is not justified (R2). No standard metric (like ASR) is used to fully demonstrate semantic consistency between jailbreak prompt and outputs; the paper lacks evidence that BlackDAN enhances readability/usefulness of outputs (R3).
- **Over‑claim on stealthiness.** The abstract claims optimisation of “stealthiness”, but this is not proved in the experimental part and appears to be an over‑claim (R3). (Reviewer 1 also notes that limitations listed in the introduction are not substantiated by references.)
- **Methodology clarity.** The methodology section lacks a clear, step‑by‑step depiction of the proposed approach; the provided figure could be enhanced with detailed explanation (R1). More technical background on NSGA‑II and a detailed description of Algorithm 1&2 are needed (R3). Concepts like Pareto ranks are used without adequate explanation (R1).
- **Insufficient genetic operations.** Only two genetic operations are considered, which is not comprehensive; leveraging more diverse operations (as in GPTFUZZER, Semantic Mirror Jailbreak, Open Sesame) could improve results (R3). The paper does not justify why only two are used (R3).
- **Hyperparameter selection.** The paper does not clarify how hyperparameters were chosen (e.g., based on empirical experimentation or adopted from prior work); sensitivity analysis is missing (R2).
- **Visualisations lack depth.** Visualisations would be more impactful if accompanied by deeper interpretations that provide further insights (R1).
- **Redundancy and insufficient follow‑up.** The second and third paragraphs of the introduction are somewhat redundant, and the examples raised in Figure 1 are not thoroughly explored in later sections (R1).
