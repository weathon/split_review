## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes HarmoniCa, a learning-based feature caching framework for Diffusion Transformer (DiT) acceleration. It identifies two issues in prior learning-based caching: Prior Timestep Disregard (training ignores cache effects from earlier timesteps) and Objective Mismatch (training target of aligning predicted noise per timestep deviates from inference goal of high-quality image generation). To address these, HarmoniCa introduces Step-Wise Denoising Training (SDT) to maintain denoising continuity during training and Image Error Proxy-Guided Objective (IEPO) to approximate final image error from cached feature reuse. Experiments on class-conditional and text-to-image tasks across DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, and PixArt-Σ families (7 models, 4 samplers, resolutions 256×256 to 2048×2048) show speedups (e.g., 1.5× on 20-step PixArt-α 1024×1024 with improved FID) and ~25% training time reduction compared to the existing learning-based approach, without requiring image data during training. Reviewers’ overall scores are mixed (6,6,5,8), with reviewer 4 being considerably more positive and reviewer 3 more critical.

### Strengths

1. **Clear problem identification and novel framework** – The paper clearly identifies the Prior Timestep Disregard and Objective Mismatch issues in learning-based caching and proposes a new framework (SDT and IEPO) to address them. (R1, R3, R4)
2. **Strong empirical performance and extensive validation** – HarmoniCa is validated on DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, and PixArt-Σ families across 7 models, 4 samplers, and resolutions from 256×256 to 2048×2048, demonstrating effectiveness and generality. It achieves 1.5× speedup on PixArt-α 1024×1024 with improved FID over the non-accelerated model. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
3. **Reduced training cost and no external data** – HarmoniCa reduces training time by ~25% compared to the existing learning-based approach and requires no image data during training. (R1, R2, R4)
4. **Comprehensive ablation studies** – The paper includes detailed ablation studies on cache ratios, loss coefficients, and proxy metrics, providing insight into parameter influence. (R2, R4)
5. **Well-organized and clearly presented** – The paper is clearly written with well-structured sections, effective figures (e.g., Figure 3, Figure 4), equations, and pseudo-code that enhance reproducibility. (R1, R3, R4)
6. **Practical benefits across architectures and scales** – HarmoniCa demonstrates adaptability to different DiT architectures and resolution settings, with broad applicability. (R2, R4)
7. **Clean and effective approach** – The method is clean and produces strong empirical results. (R2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Incremental improvement over Learn-to-Cache** – The work is seen as an incremental improvement upon the existing learning-based caching method (Learn-to-Cache), with only limited performance gains. Table 2 shows HarmoniCa’s results are only slightly better than Learn-to-Cache. (R3)
2. **Marginal gains compared to non-trainable methods** – Under similar latency and compute, HarmoniCa’s generation metrics are nearly the same as those of SA-Solver, a method requiring no training. This raises questions about the practical utility of training-based HarmoniCa. (R3)
3. **Missing comparisons with other cache-based methods** – No experimental comparison is provided with other cache-based acceleration methods such as DeepCache and Faster Diffusion. (R1)
4. **Missing comparison under same acceleration ratio with orthogonal techniques** – Performance comparison with pruning, quantization, or other acceleration methods at the same acceleration ratio is not provided. (R1, R4)
5. **Lack of qualitative visual analysis** – The paper does not include visual comparisons of generated images to show artifacts or quality differences between HarmoniCa and baselines, especially under aggressive caching. (R4)
6. **Limited dataset diversity** – Evaluation is only on ImageNet (class-conditional) and MS-COCO (text-to-image). No discussion of performance on other data types (e.g., video, complex textures, multi-modal). (R4)
7. **IEPO proxy sensitivity not fully analyzed** – The choice of proxy metric (e.g., MSE vs. LPIPS) for the Image Error Proxy-Guided Objective is not empirically explored, limiting understanding of potential biases or trade-offs. (R2, R4)
8. **Insufficient hyperparameter tuning documentation** – Additional hyperparameters (β coefficient, update interval C) are introduced, but the paper lacks details on the search space, sensitivity to their values, and computational resources required for tuning. (R2)
9. **Lack of fine-grained analysis of SDT and cache router** – The individual contributions of SDT and cache router components are not isolated in ablation, making it unclear how each sub-component (caching frequency, router sensitivity) directly impacts performance. (R4)
10. **Complexity and reproducibility concerns** – The framework relies on multiple components (SDT, IEPO, custom cache router) which may limit accessibility for practitioners, and implementation details (e.g., modifications to DiT architecture, memory footprint) are not fully clarified. (R4)
11. **Writing issues** – Long sentences and typos (Line 127, Line 213, Line 261) detract from clarity; terminology “discrepancy” could be replaced with “mismatch” for accuracy. (R2)
12. **Missing implementation details** – Questions remain about whether the routing probability \( r_{t,i} \) uses a sigmoid activation, which optimizer updates the router, and whether stop-gradient is applied to the proxy loss generator (gen_proxy). (R2)
13. **Router transferability across samplers** – It is unclear whether a router trained with one diffusion sampler (e.g., DDIM) can be applied to another (e.g., DPM solver). (R2)
14. **Generalization beyond image generation** – The paper does not explore HarmoniCa’s effectiveness for video generation or other data-intensive DiT applications. (R4)
15. **Combining with other efficiency techniques** – No discussion of synergies or limitations when combining HarmoniCa with orthogonal acceleration methods like quantization or model pruning. (R4)
16. **Potential integration with U-Net architectures** – The paper does not address whether SDT and IEPO can be adapted to U-Net-based diffusion models. (R4)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Reviewer 4 is considerably more positive (score 8), highlighting the originality and significance of SDT and IEPO, while reviewer 3 is more critical (score 5), viewing the improvements as marginal and the approach as an incremental extension of Learn-to-Cache.
