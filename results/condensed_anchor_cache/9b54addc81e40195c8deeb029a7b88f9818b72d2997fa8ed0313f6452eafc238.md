## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers found DistillSpec to be an effective application of knowledge distillation (KD) to improve speculative decoding (SD), with a systematic study of data generation, divergence functions, and lossy sampling. The method yields 10-45% speedups on several benchmarks and explores accuracy–latency trade-offs. However, reviewers noted limited technical novelty (direct application of existing KD), task-dependence of gains, insufficient evaluation on large models and out-of-domain/zero-shot settings, presentation clarity issues, and high computational cost of per-target distillation. One reviewer found the paper well-written, while others found it messy. All three gave a score of 6.

### Strengths
- Leveraging KD to align draft and target models for SD is novel and effective (R1). The paper provides a comprehensive, systematic study of different KD aspects: data generation, divergence functions, and lossy sampling; the conclusion that model-generated data is important is well-supported (R2). Experiments are solid, with clear tables and figures (R3).
- Using acceptance rate as the efficiency measure is well-motivated; the total variation distance (TVD) objective is simple and straightforward (R1).
- The fast alternative of using student-generated (on-policy) data for training is backed by theoretical justification and strong experimental results (R1).
- DistillSpec demonstrates effectiveness on several tasks and datasets under both greedy and non-greedy sampling, with 10-45% speedups over standard SD (R2, also R1). The lossy speculative decoding results offer novel insights and recommendations for combining KD and SD in different scenarios (R2).
- The exploration of the accuracy–latency trade-off is valuable (R3).
- One reviewer found the paper well-written and easy to read (R3). [Contrast: another reviewer found presentation messy/unclear – see Weaknesses.]

### Weaknesses
- Technical novelty is limited; the method is a direct application of existing KD techniques to SD, and the work is more a comprehensive study than a novel method (R1, R3).
- Performance is task-dependent (e.g., marginal speedup on WMT En-De; TVD performance varies across tasks) (R1). All experiments are in-domain, so the paper does not demonstrate effectiveness for zero-shot/few-shot out-of-domain settings where LLMs excel (R3).
- Experiments mainly focus on T5 models (small) and relatively simple tasks (except GSM8K). Results on larger models are unclear – Figure 6 lacks details on which data points correspond to which target and draft model sizes (R1, R2). The paper does not evaluate DistillSpec on recent large models (e.g., LLaMA-7B) and lacks discussion on the difficulty of LLM distillation (R2).
- The method is target-model-dependent, requiring distillation of a new draft model for each new target model; this computational cost is especially high when using online data generation (R1).
- Presentation is messy and unclear: the formal definition of DistillSpec is hard to find among analysis of existing approaches; details of distillation data generation are not clear; specific configurations for Figure 6 are missing; overall clarity and structure need improvement (R2). [Contrast: one reviewer found it well-written – see Strengths.]
- The paper does not compare or discuss DistillSpec with other methods combining large and small models at inference, especially under lossy decoding (e.g., Liu et al. 2023 Online Speculative Decoding) (R2).
- The paper does not analyze generated texts in detail (diversity, coherence); providing examples would be helpful (R2).
- The paper does not explain why different distillation methods vary significantly in Figure 4 (R3).
