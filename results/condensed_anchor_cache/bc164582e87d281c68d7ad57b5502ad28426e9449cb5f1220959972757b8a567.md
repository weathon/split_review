## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Prioritized Generative Replay (PGR), a framework that uses conditional diffusion models to create a parametric replay buffer for online RL. This enables two advantages: (1) densification of past experience by generating new plausible transitions, and (2) guidance via relevance functions (curiosity based on ICM, TD error, Q-values) that steer generations toward useful parts of the agent’s history. The method is evaluated on Gym and DMC tasks in both state- and pixel-based domains, and consistently improves performance and sample efficiency over baselines including SynthER. Scaling experiments show benefits with larger policy networks and higher synthetic-to-real data ratios. The paper provides insights into why curiosity promotes diversity and reduces overfitting.

### Strengths
- Clear and well-structured presentation; the method is logically laid out and easy to read (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Novel perspective on replay buffers by combining generative modeling with guided replay; framing it as a conditional generation problem with diffusion models is novel (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 3 notes it is a simple combination of existing elements, but this is a minority view).
- The method is scalable, simple to integrate into existing model-free or model-based agents, and should be easily reproducible (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Empirical results on various benchmarks (state- and pixel-based) show consistent improvements over model-free, model-based, and generative replay baselines without guidance (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Scaling experiments with larger policy networks and higher synthetic-to-real data ratios are compelling and important for real-world scaling (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The paper provides useful insights into why the approach works—curiosity promotes diversity and reduces overfitting (Reviewer 4).
- The diffusion model requires no additional hyperparameter tuning (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns relative to model-based RL with intrinsic rewards:** The approach appears similar to prior work that applies intrinsic rewards to rollouts from a learned dynamics model. The main differences (e.g., generating instances rather than full trajectories) are not sufficiently clarified (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Robustness of the guidance function:** The curiosity-based relevance function (ICM) is known to be susceptible to the noisy TV problem, where difficult-to-model factors score arbitrarily high. The tested tasks are perceptually simple and do not expose this pathology. The paper does not address how to handle noisy TVs or search for alternative guidance functions in practice. Other intrinsic rewards that avoid this pathology are not discussed or compared (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Limited discussion of alternative generative models:** The paper does not explain why other generative models (e.g., VAEs, Gaussian mixture models) were not considered or used for the replay buffer (Reviewer 2).
- **Dynamics model reliance and compounding errors:** The curiosity-based relevance function depends on a learned dynamics model. The paper does not adequately address the risk of compounding errors in that model, especially in environments with high stochasticity or hard-to-model dynamics, which could generate unrealistic or misleading transitions (Reviewer 4). This connects to the noisy TV concern.
- **Synthetic data ratio saturation and inconsistency:** Increasing the synthetic-to-real data ratio (r) does not benefit PGR and the unconditional baseline (SynthER) equally. PGR scales better at r=0.75 than SynthER, but neither benefits from r=0.875. The paper lacks a clear explanation for why higher ratios stop yielding improvements and why the trends diverge. A sensitivity analysis on this ratio is missing (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 1 also expresses concern about driving up the syn-real ratio too high).
- **Missing comparisons and ablation analysis:** The effect of different exploration bonuses is not compared (Reviewer 3). The sensitivity of the frequency of the inner loop in Algorithm 1 is not analyzed (Reviewer 3). It is unclear whether multiple relevance functions can be combined (Reviewer 3). No comparison is made against PER baselines that use approximate parametric models of prior experience (Reviewer 4 question).
- **Minor writing issues:** The paper has some missing words and formatting problems; Tables 1, 2, and 3 are cramped and slightly misaligned (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Alternative approach not considered:** The paper does not explore whether improving the exploration method itself (instead of sample generation) could achieve similar diversity gains, or whether combining both would be optimal (Reviewer 2 question).
