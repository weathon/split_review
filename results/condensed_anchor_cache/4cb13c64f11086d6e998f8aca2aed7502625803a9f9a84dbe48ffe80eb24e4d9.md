## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SparsitySolver, an RL-based pruning method for LLMs that searches layer-wise sparsity and includes a reconstruction compensation for structured pruning without extra parameters. It is evaluated on LLaMA-V1/V2, Mistral, and OPT families across structured/unstructured granularities, reporting perplexity and zero-shot results. One reviewer (score 6) found the illustrations clear, the method effective across scales, and the sparsity distribution observation interesting; the three other reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) raised major concerns about technical novelty, evaluation bias, missing comparisons, presentation quality, and insufficient details.

### Strengths
- Novel direction: incorporating RL into LLM pruning strategy search, supporting both structured and unstructured pruning (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The reconstruction compensation method for structured pruning restores performance without introducing additional parameters (reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Redesign of the reward function (dense reward) significantly impacts performance compared to previous RL-pruning approaches (reviewer 4).
- Demonstrates effectiveness across multiple LLM families (LLaMA-V1/V2, Mistral, OPT) at various scales, with improvements in both perplexity and some downstream benchmarks (reviewer 2, 4).
- The search strategy reveals diverse inter-layer sparsity within LLMs, an interesting observation for future research (reviewer 2).
- One reviewer (2) found the method description clear and straightforward; another (4) considered the paper well written and easy to follow (contrast with weaknesses below).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation issues (majority concern):**
  - The paper is difficult to understand and contains reverse statements; motivation for using RL is not properly justified (reviewer 1).
  - Claims of being “first to incorporate RL into LLM pruning” are contradicted by existing RL-based pruning methods (reviewer 1).
  - Overall organization is chaotic, with inconsistent paragraph spacing and formatting; Figure 1 is low quality and fails to illustrate the method effectively (reviewer 3).
  - Mathematical notations and equations lack proper explanation and context; citations are inconsistently formatted (reviewer 3).

- **Insufficient technical novelty and theoretical foundation:**
  - The RL environment design is overly simplistic: using only the total pruning ratio as the state space lacks theoretical justification (reviewer 3).
  - The reconstruction compensation method lacks mathematical rigor; many assumptions and steps are not properly justified (reviewer 3).
  - Critical implementation details missing: RL agent architecture, training process, and how the compensation handles different layer types (reviewer 3). Also unclear whether the method requires training or is only forward pruning (reviewer 1).

- **Experimental evaluation is biased and incomplete:**
  - **Reward hacking risk:** The pruning strategy is optimized by minimizing perplexity on wikitext, but perplexity does not always translate to better downstream performance (e.g., FLAP vs wanda-sp evidence). The OPT-6.7B pruned model even has lower perplexity than the dense model, indicating potential reward hacking (reviewer 4, citing Skalse et al. 2022).
  - **Missing downstream evaluations:** Downstream zero-shot tasks are only reported for LLaMA-V1 7B at a single sparsity (20% structured, 70% unstructured). Must include evaluations on other models, sizes, and sparsity ratios (reviewer 4). Also missing evaluation on complex tasks like machine translation and few-shot in-context learning (reviewer 2).
  - **Missing comparisons with existing RL-based pruning methods** (reviewer 3, 4).
  - **Not SOTA:** At 70% sparsity, OWL+SparseGPT achieves better perplexity than SparsitySolver, and SparseGPT alone matches downstream performance (48.03 vs 48.11) (reviewer 4). Reproduced scores have discrepancies (e.g., BESA); paper should include both original and reproduction results (reviewer 4).
  - **No statistical significance analysis** (reviewer 3).
  - **Ablation studies are incomplete:** fail to validate the necessity of each component (reviewer 3).
  - Does not support or evaluate very large models (>70B parameters), such as LLaMA2-70B or PaLM-540B, which is a pressing use case for pruning (reviewer 2, 3). Compatibility with quantization techniques is not addressed (reviewer 2).

- **Missing practical details:**
  - Computational overhead and end-to-end runtime compared to other pruning strategies not discussed (reviewer 3, 4).
  - No stability analysis across different tasks (reviewer 3). RL is known unstable; how does the method revert pruned parameters? (reviewer 1).
  - Reward function inconsistency: Figure 1 shows reward as \(1/\text{ppl}\) but the equation states \(10/\text{ppl}\); which is correct and why was scaling used? (reviewer 4).
  - The compensation method may reduce sparsity because the sparse items differ across channels; how is this handled? (reviewer 1).
  - The meaning of “without need for additional computation” in the reward environment is unclear (reviewer 1).
