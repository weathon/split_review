## Merged Review

### Summary
NeuroQuant is a post-training quantization (PTQ) method tailored to non-generalized Implicit Neural Representations for variable-rate video coding (INR-VC). By adjusting quantization parameters (QPs) of pre-trained weights and redefining variable-rate coding as a mixed-precision quantization problem, it avoids retraining for each target bitrate. It establishes theoretical sensitivity criteria, network-wise calibration, and channel-wise quantization to minimize quantization errors. Experiments show significant gains over existing PTQ and QAT methods, enabling INT2 quantization with minimal reconstruction loss, and 8x encoding speedup (excluding pretraining time). The paper is well-written and easy to follow, providing both theoretical insights and comprehensive empirical evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Single model for multiple bitrates**: PTQ on a pre-trained model eliminates retraining for each target rate, decreasing overall training time (Reviewer 1, confidence 4; Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, confidence 4; Reviewer 4, confidence 4; Reviewer 5, confidence 4).
- **Theoretical foundation**: Mathematical formulation derived from Nagel et al. (2020) for network-wise calibration; a criterion for optimal sensitivity in mixed-precision INR-VC enables bitwidth allocation among layers (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
- **Strong empirical results**: Significant PSNR improvements – e.g., ~0.2 dB at 6-bit and ~3 dB at 2-bit for NeRV (Reviewer 5). NeuroQuant outperforms competitors in rate-distortion, with superior compression efficiency even at INT2 (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Gains are visible on all tested models and sequences (Reviewer 5).
- **Efficiency in obtaining multiple rate points**: Provides a fast alternative to fully retraining or QAT for each bitrate; encoding complexity reduced particularly for multi-rate deployment (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Comparison of multiple quantization methods (AdaQuant, BRECQ, etc.) across INR-VC architectures; thorough ablation and analysis (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and motivation**: Well-structured paper with clear explanation of why traditional PTQ fails on INR-VC models due to inter-layer dependencies, and how the proposed method addresses this (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparison in time/speedup** (Reviewer 3, confidence 4):  
  - Table 2 excludes pretraining time for NeuroQuant, but pretraining is still required. Report pretraining and fine-tuning times separately for both baselines and NeuroQuant.  
  - Claim of 8x encoding speedup is misleading because it omits the initial INR-VC encoding (pretraining) time, even though NeuroQuant avoids full retraining for each rate point.
- **Missing comparison with Neural Network Coding (NNC)** (Reviewer 1, confidence 4):  
  NNC also performs PTQ and can achieve variable-bitrate by adjusting QP parameters. The paper should include this comparison.
- **Unfair comparison with x264/x265** (Reviewer 3):  
  Using `zerolatency` setting disadvantages the INR-VC methods that inherently have non-zero latency. This setting unfairly favors x264/x265 by sacrificing compression efficiency for speed.
- **Unclear advantage over QAT** (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 1):  
  - The paper claims better RD performance than FFNeRV/HiNeRV, but PTQ typically underperforms QAT. Authors should explain why NeuroQuant achieves better RD, and provide detailed performance/complexity trade-offs against QAT-based variable-rate methods (e.g., Gomes et al., other works using quantization-aware training for multiple bitrates).  
  - Clarify whether acceleration comes from avoiding QAT optimization or from skipping retraining per rate point (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited ablation study** (Reviewer 3):  
  Ablation experiments are performed on a single sequence, which may not generalize. Expand to a diverse set of sequences.
- **Ambiguity in mixed-precision realization** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5):  
  - The abstract mentions mixed-precision quantization, but Table 1 appears to use fixed precision. Explain where mixed precision is applied and how bitwidths are allocated.  
  - The quantization step parameters (s in eq.17) for mixed-precision may affect bpp calculation. Clarify whether bpp in Fig.4 and Fig.5 includes these parameters; AdaQuant and BRECQ (fixed-precision) do not require such parameters, making the comparison potentially unfair (Reviewer 5).
- **Similarity to prior PTQ work** (Reviewer 5):  
  The calibration objective (Section 3.2) closely resembles the network-wise reconstruction in BRECQ (Li et al. 2021b). Both use an MSE-form objective. The paper should clarify its novel contributions relative to such existing methods.
- **Missing details for reproducibility** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3):  
  - How is Figure 3 generated? Provide architecture details and data used; is the analysis valid for MLP?  
  - What is the significance of eq.5/6? Is the optimization problem actually solved?  
  - In eq.16, how is s determined? Learned, greedy, or fixed?  
  - For Nagel et al. (2020), specify which equation variant (21 or 25) was used.  
  - Encoding runtime for HiNeRV 3M (22 hours) seems far longer than original paper; memory usage appears unusually high – explain differences (e.g., FP16 usage).  
  - How long does it take to obtain additional rate points via fine-tuning (e.g., QAT for 30 epochs vs. full training) for models like NeRV, HNeRV, FFNeRV, HiNeRV?  
  - Provide computational cost (MACs and wall time) for the calibration process compared to QAT (Reviewer 3).
- **Applicability and generalization** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5):  
  - Could network-wise calibration also benefit generalized neural codecs? Provide experiments on such models.  
  - Is the inter-layer independence property a good or ill-posed characteristic of INR models? Provide insight (Reviewer 5).  
  - Can the method be extended to a more general mixed-precision PTQ with calibration? (Reviewer 5)
- **Presentation**: Provide more intuitive explanations (e.g., diagram, pseudocode) for non-experts in model quantization (Reviewer 5).
