## Merged Review

### Summary

DITS uses state-only interactions to augment offline RL data. It generates high-return trajectories with a conditional diffusion model (conditioned on returns), fills in actions via an inverse dynamics model and rewards via the diffusion model, and blends them with original data through a stitching algorithm that finds compatible (s,s') pairs across trajectories. Evaluated on D4RL with BC and TD3+BC, it shows gains over the underlying algorithms and the SynthER baseline.

### Strengths

- The setting (state-only interaction without reward feedback) is practical and broadly applicable (reviewer 1).
- The combination of diffusion-based trajectory generation and trajectory stitching is novel and interesting (reviewer 2).
- The writing is clear and easy to follow (reviewer 2); Figure 1 clearly illustrates the difference from prior approaches (reviewer 4).
- The goal of generating high-return trajectories is well-motivated and worthwhile (reviewer 4).
- Using BC as one evaluation algorithm is appropriate because DITS aims to generate high-return trajectories; good BC performance directly supports that claim (reviewer 4).
- The experiments are intensive, showing effectiveness and robustness (reviewer 1). DITS performs well compared to baselines on D4RL (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and strong similarity to prior work.** Two reviewers (scores 3) consider the contribution minor and the method a variation of Decision Diffuser, DiffStitch, and SynthER. The “state-only trajectory modeling” is almost the same setup as Decision Diffuser (reviewer 3). The only addition is the stitching, but it is problematic: sampling a next state from a different trajectory is almost always much less likely than the predicted next state (reviewer 3).  
- **Lack of comprehensive baselines.** Table 1 only includes BC and TD3+BC, ignoring state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms such as CQL, IQL, and Decision Transformer (reviewer 2). The data augmentation baseline is limited to SynthER (transition-level); newer diffusion-based methods like MTDiff are missing, making the comparison unfair and incomplete (reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablations and analysis.** The importance of the trajectory stitcher is not analyzed (reviewer 2). Ablations use BC, which does not utilize the generated reward information; TD3+BC would be a more appropriate base learner (reviewer 1). The authors should compare DITS vs. DITS w/o stitcher and w/o generator, as well as the effect of the number of generated trajectories, using TD3+BC (reviewer 1). There is no ablation showing the effectiveness of the generation pipeline against alternatives like Diffuser or DD (reviewer 2). The effect of the iteration rounds K is not examined (reviewer 2).
- **Empirical results are not consistently strong.** DITS outperforms baselines in only about half of the tasks. In many cases, confidence intervals overlap (e.g., all Med-Expert experiments, Medium HalfCheetah, Med Replay HalfCheetah with TD3+BC, Kitchen Partial, Antmaze U-Diverse and Umaze with BC) (reviewer 4).
- **Statistical reporting issues.** Standard deviations are not properly propagated when averaging across runs (they should be added in quadrature) (reviewer 4). Using standard deviation assumes normality, which is often invalid in RL; a bootstrap confidence interval would be more appropriate (reviewer 4).
- **Technical choices are unexplained or questionable.** The paper uses slow autoregressive generation with the diffusion model instead of joint trajectory diffusion, but the advantage of this choice is unclear (reviewer 3). The one-step dynamics model p(s'|s) is already used to verify stitching; why not just use that model and drop the diffusion model entirely (reviewer 3)? In Algo 4, it is not specified whether the state-value function V is trained from scratch each iteration or only fine-tuned (reviewer 1). The annotation in Figure 2 (“trajectories from good policies”) is misleading because the trajectories come from interacting with the real environment using a planning-like method; “real environment” would be better (reviewer 1). Line 16 of Algo 2 says “for behavioural cloning training” but the algorithm trains various offline RL methods besides BC (reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient related work.** The paper misses relevant data augmentation works for offline RL, including Corrado et al. (expert-quality augmented data), Pitis et al. (model-based augmentation), Sinha et al. (offline RL augmentation), and several others (reviewer 4).
- **Unsupported claim.** The statement that “merely generating high-return trajectories is sub-optimal because it may overfit those regions” is not supported by evidence in the paper; Kumar et al. provide a theoretical argument that noisy expert data can be better than pure expert data, which may relate but is not cited or discussed (reviewer 4).
- **Clarifications requested.** The meanings of m (Line 205) and equation 8 are unclear (reviewer 2). In Section 3.2, the authors claim the last C steps are needed for consistency, but the tasks are Markovian, and the description of autoregressive decoding vs. conditioning on history is confusing (reviewer 3).
