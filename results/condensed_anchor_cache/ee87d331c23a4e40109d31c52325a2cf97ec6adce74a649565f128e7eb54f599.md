## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SCOPE-Gen, a sequential conformal prediction method that prunes generated samples via greedy filters to achieve conformal admissibility control. It factorizes admissibility as a Markov chain, enabling separate calibration of each factor and reducing the number of admissibility evaluations needed during calibration. Experiments on natural language generation and molecular graph extension tasks show reduced query counts and prediction set sizes compared to the CLM baseline.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, 4).
- The method is novel along multiple axes: it avoids the simplifying assumptions of earlier conformal wrapper methods, leverages sequential factorization for tractable admissibility control, and draws connections to Pareto methods (Reviewer 1). Differences from Quach et al. 2024 are explicitly detailed (Reviewer 4).
- It reduces the number of admissibility checks during calibration relative to prior methods, which is important when evaluations are costly (e.g., from human oracles) (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- Formulating hallucination control within a risk-control framework provides mathematical guarantees (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results support the method’s effectiveness in reducing query counts and prediction set sizes (Reviewer 2).
- The method can be tuned based on available data for a given domain (Reviewer 1).
- [Minority positive view: Reviewer 1 rated 8, finding the approach simple yet innovative; other reviewers rated 5, 6, 6 and were more critical.]

### Weaknesses
- The method is compute-intensive: it requires sampling multiple (i.i.d.) generations and then pruning, which can generate many samples before filtering. The paper lacks analysis of how the number of generated samples scales with desired admissibility or model complexity (Reviewer 1, 2).
- There is no theoretical analysis of the conditions under which the reduction in admissibility checks is guaranteed, nor how it scales with problem complexity or dataset size (Reviewer 2).
- Missing ablation studies: the effects of individual components (e.g., the update function, coverage level α) are not systematically evaluated, making the empirical evaluation difficult to assess (Reviewer 3).
- The calibration process requires sample splitting for generation and each filtering stage, consuming extra ground-truth samples. The paper does not discuss sensitivity to calibration set size or the impact of the splitting strategy on threshold accuracy (Reviewer 2).
- An independent calibration set is assumed, which is difficult to obtain in practice. In experiments, this either reduces to trivial cases (single valid example) or relies on another model of uncertain quality (Reviewer 3).
- The update and filter (diversity/quality) functions are difficult to formulate in realistic scenarios, limiting practical applicability (Reviewer 3).
- No human evaluation is reported; the validation function is another model (or a simple oracle), which does not fully validate the method’s claimed advantage when a human oracle is used (Reviewer 1, 3). Relatedly, using a human oracle may invalidate assumptions like the need for an increasing update function and could introduce noise that breaks probabilistic conclusions (Reviewer 3).
- Evaluation is limited to QA-style tasks. The paper would benefit from experiments on long-form generation (e.g., segmentation-based conformal wrappers) to demonstrate broader applicability (Reviewer 1).
- The molecular graph extension and TriviaQA experiments are not clearly beneficial: when there is only one admissible example, simply checking validity at generation time may suffice (Reviewer 3).
- Questions about generalization: can the method handle broader admissibility definitions (e.g., requiring multiple correct answers, or maximizing the fraction of correct answers within a fixed set size)? (Reviewer 2).
- The order of filtering stages (diversity first, then quality) is not justified; why is diversity prioritized? (Reviewer 2).
- Equation (6) is unclear: what level of quantile is used, and what is the interpretation of the n-fraction on the right-hand side? (Reviewer 3).
- Fig 1 caption is unclear; it does not explain the symbol ν, which is only defined later in Section 3 (Reviewer 4).
- The gap between SCOPE-Gen and CLM appears to narrow in Appendix Table 2; no explanation is provided (Reviewer 4).
- The main text spills to page 11, exceeding the strict 10-page limit (Reviewer 3).
- The paper does not discuss related works on conditional language validity (Cherian 2024, Mohri & Hashimoto 2024) or confidence scoring for hallucination reduction (Kuhn et al. 2023, Lin et al. 2023, Wang & Holmes 2024), which could bridge separate lines of inquiry (Reviewer 1).
