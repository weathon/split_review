## Merged Review

### Summary
FRUGAL is a memory-efficient optimization method that splits gradient updates into two subspaces: a *stateful* subspace updated with advanced optimizers (e.g., Adam) and a *state-free* subspace updated with stateless methods (e.g., SGD, signSGD). The method is flexible, supports various projection techniques, and provides convergence guarantees. Empirical results on LLaMA pre-training (C4) and RoBERTa-base fine-tuning (GLUE) show perplexity gains over GaLore and BAdam at similar memory budgets. One reviewer (score 6) found the paper well-structured and practically impactful; the other four (scores 5) noted shortcomings in presentation and justification.

### Strengths
1. **Novel combination** of stateful and stateless optimizers for memory-efficient, full-rank updates – a new direction compared to low-rank methods like LoRA and GaLore.
2. **Empirical superiority**: FRUGAL consistently outperforms GaLore and BAdam on pre-training (LLaMA on C4) and fine-tuning (GLUE) across fixed memory budgets, often approaching full Adam performance (R1, R2, R4).
3. **Convergence guarantees**: Provides a convergence rate similar to SGD(M) under nonconvex assumptions; the theory can recover standard SGD(M) rates (R2, R5).
4. **Flexibility**: Supports multiple choices for stateful/stateless optimizers (Adam, SGD, signSGD, Lion) and projection methods (random, blockwise, SVD) (R2).
5. **Ablation studies**: Sufficient ablation on density ρ, projection type, block size, and layer selection (Table 3) gives insight into hyperparameter effects (R1).
6. **Practical impact**: Method is straightforward to implement and broadly applicable (R3).
7. **Strong experimental execution**: Hyperparameter details are well disclosed and implementation provided; extensive experiments on multiple model sizes and tasks (R5).
8. **Good presentation**: One reviewer found the paper well-structured and easy to follow (R3); another praised clear concepts but noted organizational flaws (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Selection criteria for stateful vs. state-free subspaces** is not adequately justified (R1, R2, R4). It is unclear whether parameters are randomly assigned or based on importance, and why only linear layers are considered for the state-free subspace (R2). No explanation is given for *which* parameters need Adam vs. SGD (R4).
2. **Density parameter ρ** is poorly explained. It is unclear whether ρ=0 means all parameters are state-free (i.e., full SGD) and why this sometimes achieves optimal performance despite claims that stateless optimizers underperform on transformers (R1, R4). The density study is placed in the appendix but should be in the main text (R2).
3. **Gap between theory and practice**: The theoretical analysis uses SGD with momentum (SGDM) for the stateful subspace and SGD for the stateless subspace, but the main algorithm uses Adam and signSGD. The paper does not explain how the theoretical results extend to these practical choices (R2, R5).
4. **SVD vs. random projection discrepancy**: Tables 1 and 15 show SVD consistently outperforms random projection, yet the paper claims random projection is superior (line 192: “subsequent training with random projection yields significant improvements”). The logic is confusing (R1, R2, R4). The computational cost of SVD is noted but not compared to the cost of other methods (R1).
5. **Missing baselines and comparisons**:
   - No full-parameter fine-tuning baseline in Table 4 (R2).
   - Lion optimizer is not compared with Adam in any table (R2).
   - GLUE benchmarks are outdated; more recent tasks (common-sense reasoning, MT-Bench) would strengthen evaluation (R4).
   - Running time comparisons beyond memory efficiency are missing (R3).
6. **Notation and definitions**:
   - Terms like “state-full” and “state-free” are introduced without formal definitions; their meaning is hard to grasp (R3, R4).
   - Notations (e.g., g̃) are undefined; concepts like smoothness and unbiasedness are vague (R2).
   - Algorithm 1 uses K, s without definition; inputs are unclear (R4).
   - Definitions for Full-Rank SVD/Random and Low-Rank SVD/Random are scattered across Table 1 (R2).
7. **Paper organization**: Analysis and experimental results appear within the Methods section, disrupting logical flow. Experiments are presented in a scattered manner, making it hard to draw clear conclusions (R2).
8. **Insufficient evidence for core claim**: The main contribution is the stateless optimizer part (signSGD), but only one ablation study (Table 3) is provided. This is not enough to assure generalization to non-LLaMA architectures (R5). The motivation in Figure 2 shows that top gradient directions are similar over time, but it does not prove that random/blockwise selection is always better; an ablation on projector type vs. density ρ is needed (R5).
9. **Deviations from primary algorithm**: Experiments use column-wise projection instead of the proposed block-wise projection; results with the original approach should be included (R2).
10. **Lack of limitations discussion**: The paper does not discuss failure modes or scenarios where FRUGAL may be less effective (R3).
11. **Missing references**: Adam-mini and BlockLLM are not cited but are relevant (R1).
12. **Reviewer disagreement on presentation**: One reviewer praised clear structure (R3), while another found it poorly organized and notation-heavy (R2).
