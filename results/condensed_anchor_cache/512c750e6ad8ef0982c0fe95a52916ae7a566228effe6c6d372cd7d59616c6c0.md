## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CASE-Bench, a context-aware safety evaluation benchmark that incorporates context (based on Contextual Integrity theory) into safety assessments of LLMs. It comprises 900 query-context pairs across 45 categories, uses over 47K human annotations from over 2K annotators (21 per task, determined by power analysis), and evaluates several open-source and commercial LLMs. The authors find a significant influence of context on human judgments (z-test p<0.0001) and mismatches between human judgments and LLM responses, especially for commercial models in safe contexts.

### Strengths
- The core idea – understanding how context might/should affect LLM refusal decisions – is interesting and identifies a key gap in current safety evaluations (over-rejection of legitimate requests in safe contexts).
- The research methodology is extremely rigorous: use of Contextual Integrity theory to formalize context, power analysis to determine annotator numbers, large-scale human annotation with robust quality control, and statistical verification of results (z-test, p<0.0001).
- The dataset creation is thorough: 900 distinct query-context pairs from 45 categories, 21 annotators per task, over 2K annotators total, resulting in a high-quality dataset useful for other research.
- The paper is extremely well-written and clear.
- Experiments are well-done and interesting; extensive analysis of model vs. human alignment.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited usefulness of Contextual Integrity theory**: The connection between CI and the benchmark construction is unclear. CI is mentioned in the prompt used for context generation, but it is not shown to be necessary or whether GPT-4o actually uses it. All contexts were manually revised by the authors anyway. The mapping from CI to LLM safety feels superficial. [Reviewer 1, confidence 3; Reviewer 2, confidence 2]
- **Insufficient detail on contexts**: The paper does not discuss what the contexts look like, how they were crafted, how often they repeat across queries, or share examples of contexts. This is a major omission given contexts are the primary contribution. [Reviewer 1; Reviewer 1 question]
- **Lack of information about annotator demographics**: Choice of whether an LLM should respond is subjective and depends on personal politics, religion, culture, etc. Without demographics, the benchmark measures acceptance/decline only relative to the specific annotator pool surveyed. [Reviewer 1, confidence 3]
- **Premise/motivation concerns**: Context-dependent safety evaluation may be easily exploited by malicious actors fabricating safe-looking contexts to elicit harmful content (jailbreaking). The paper does not address this critical security risk. [Reviewer 3, confidence 3; Reviewer 4, confidence 3; Reviewer 1 also raises this: shouldn’t LLMs decline even with context if users might lie?] Minority view: Reviewer 4 finds the premise weak, stating "I'm personally not convinced that context should dictate perceived harm."
- **Limited evaluation metrics**: The paper uses only basic measures like accuracy and recall. More detailed analysis, such as measuring the impact of different error types, is missing. [Reviewer 3, confidence 3]
- **Unexpected results not explained**: In Fig. 3, some unsafe contexts lead categories to be rated by humans as safe (categories 10-20). No explanation or examples are provided for this counterintuitive finding. [Reviewer 4, confidence 3]
- **Reproducibility concerns**: The data was created using GPT-4o (commercial model). It is unclear whether the automatic content generation is reproducible with open-source models. The manual revision process appears expensive and was not automated. [Reviewer 2, confidence 2; Reviewer 2 questions]
- **Lack of limitations and future work**: The paper barely discusses limitations or potential areas for improvement. [Reviewer 2, confidence 2]
- **Missing discussion on real-world integration**: How could this context-based evaluation be integrated into actual LLM safety mechanisms? [Reviewer 3, confidence 3]
