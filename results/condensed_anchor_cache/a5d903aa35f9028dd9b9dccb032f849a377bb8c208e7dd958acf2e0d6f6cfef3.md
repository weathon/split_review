- Scores: 3, 3, 8, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for circuit analysis—comprising circuit selection (via edge pruning), attention grouping, and logit clustering—and applies it to GPT-2 small on the IOI and Greater-Than tasks. The authors examine how circuits change under prompt ablations (e.g., replacing names with animals) and after various fine‑tuning methods (BitFit, LoRA, full). Key observations include variations in circuit size and component composition across conditions. Reviewers strongly disagree on the contribution: one rates the paper highly (8), citing novelty in clustering heads by functionality and useful empirical findings; the other three rate it low (1–3), finding the framework superficial, the methodology standard, and the insights lacking significance or causal explanation.

### Strengths
- Transformer interpretability is a timely and relevant topic for the community.
- The paper provides a detailed analysis, both quantitative and qualitative, explaining the proposed framework.
- The experimental section is well‑chosen and yields several interesting results: fine‑tuning can change circuit sizes (sometimes larger, sometimes smaller depending on task complexity); fine‑tuning often retains large portions of circuits and sometimes strengthens their effects; IOI circuits vary notably with prompt ablation (animal version is much simpler but retains many components).
- The main novelty is clustering attention heads by functionality (logit clustering, attention grouping), which addresses two key problems in existing circuit discovery: insight on node functionality and variation of templates.
- The discovery of behavior shifts during fine‑tuning could help explain task‑specific accuracy gains.
- The paper is generally well written, with clear intuitions and supportive figures.
- The paper takes a step toward automating circuit interpretation, an area often overshadowed by circuit discovery.

### Weaknesses
- The paper’s contribution is superficial: the main result is only that circuits vary under perturbation, which is either intuitively straightforward or an artifact of edge pruning; the significance of the variation is not explained, nor is it connected to better architecture design, data pipeline, or training algorithms. *(majority opinion; one reviewer found the findings novel)*
- The claim that this is a novel framework that accelerates circuit discovery is weak—the framework appears to be a set of ad‑hoc procedures, not a principled methodology. *(majority)*
- The work builds heavily on edge pruning (Bhaskar et al., 2024) and could be characterized as a technical report or application rather than a novel contribution. *(majority)*
- Experiments are limited to GPT‑2 small; evidence that the approach scales to larger networks is missing. *(both weaknesses and missing experiment)*
- Some methodological steps are standard and small: circuit selection relies on elbow method and established metrics (KL divergence, Kendall’s tau) without novel contribution. *(multiple reviewers)*
- The attention grouping methodology has potential flaws. Grouping heads across different layers can be misleading because information may shift tokens across layers; two heads attending to the same information at different positions would be incorrectly assigned to different groups. The method does not account for cross‑layer information flow. The threshold (mean of top‑k attention values) is arbitrary, with no justification for choosing k or sensitivity analysis. *(reviewer 2)*
- Logit clustering and attention grouping function more as exploratory heuristics than as a rigorous interpretability framework. They do not provide causal understanding of circuit behavior—they identify heads with similar outputs but do not explain why or how they contribute to computation. The paper lacks a clear definition of what constitutes a node’s “functionality.” *(reviewer 2; echoed by reviewer 4)*
- It is unclear how the proposed circuit identification helps understand model function or leads to concrete interventions (e.g., improving model performance on the studied tasks). Missing comparison to other state‑of‑the‑art circuit identification and analysis methods. *(reviewer 4; also question)*
- Specific methodological details are missing or unclear:
    * The logit clustering section does not specify what exactly is being clustered (direct head output vs. dot product with unembedding matrix) nor how logits are aggregated across multiple IOI samples in a batch. Needs formulas. *(reviewer 2, reviewer 3)*
    * The circuit selection “knee” (elbow) point identification is vague—whether based on visual inspection or a quantitative measure, and how it balances faithfulness and sparsity. *(reviewer 3)*
    * Table 4’s “shared” column is unclear: the reported values (e.g., edges(A)+edges(B) < shared(A,B)) are inconsistent with a straightforward interpretation of node or edge intersection. *(reviewer 3)*
- Writing could be clearer; more mathematical descriptions would help. *(reviewer 2)*
- Errors in the submission: missing content in Appendix A and Appendix D; appendix table of contents headers misaligned with figures. *(reviewer 2, reviewer 3)*
- The framework still depends on templated datasets and manual investigation. *(reviewer 3)*
- The paper focuses on attention heads; MLPs are not discussed in detail, and whether logit clustering could apply to them is unexplored. *(reviewer 3)*
- Limitations of the Logit Lens (potential imprecision due to residual stream drift) are not acknowledged. *(reviewer 3)*
- The term “irrelevant” (line 307) for tokens like “M” is imprecise—they provide grammatical information; “syntactically relevant or semantically irrelevant” would be better. *(reviewer 3)*
- The scope of ablation studies is narrow; more template variations are needed to claim the circuits represent the model’s full IOI capability. Future work should test different templates (e.g., present progressive tense). *(reviewer 3, future-work critique but relevant to evaluating contribution)*
- The question of why circuits change is not addressed; variation alone is insufficient as a takeaway. *(reviewer 1, reviewer 2)*
- **Disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8) found the approach novel and the paper well‑written, while the other three (scores 3,3,1) considered the contributions small and the framework insufficiently rigorous. All reviewers agree that the paper would benefit from clearer methodology and stronger evidence of utility.
