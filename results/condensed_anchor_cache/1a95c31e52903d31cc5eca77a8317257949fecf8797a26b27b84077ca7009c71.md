## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces In-Context Unlearning (ICUL), a method for LLMs that unlearns specific training instances by providing them in context with flipped labels at inference time, requiring no parameter updates. Experiments on Bloom 560M and 1.1B models show competitive performance with gradient ascent (GA) on binary classification tasks. The idea is novel and leverages in-context learning, but the evaluation and framing raise several concerns.

### Strengths
- The paper poses a novel and interesting problem that could motivate future work on parameter-free unlearning.
- It is well-written, easy to follow, and clearly presents the approach.
- ICUL requires minimal computational resources, offering a cost-effective alternative to retraining or fine-tuning.
- The ablation study (label flipping, unforgotten samples) is thorough and confirms the importance of the key components.
- The ROC curves for ICUL are close to the diagonal, supporting the claim that the method reduces the likelihood of the forget set being recognized as training data.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model scale and baselines**: Experiments are restricted to Bloom 560M and 1.1B models, with no comparisons to newer/larger models (e.g., LLaMA, Falcon, ChatGPT). Only a single benchmark method (GA) is compared, and GA sometimes outperforms ICUL, making it difficult to claim superiority.
- **Binary classification focus**: The method is tailored to binary tasks (SST-2, Amazon, Yelp) and does not easily extend to multi-class or more complex NLP tasks. Real-world applicability and scenarios are left ambiguous.
- **Scalability concerns**: ICUL is demonstrated only on single-sample unlearning. Handling large volumes of deletion requests in realistic settings is not addressed. Reviewer 2 asks directly how ICUL scales for continuous unlearning.
- **Lack of theoretical understanding**: The paper does not explain why ICUL works. In particular, it is unclear why ICUL outperforms GA (which should, intuitively, be at least as effective since it modifies weights to forget). Reviewer 3 notes this gap: the GA method intentionally forgets via gradient ascent, yet the presented results show ICUL sometimes performs better—this needs clarification.
- **Prompt-dependent robustness**: The method’s effectiveness relies on the specific prompt used (forget instance with flipped label plus context examples). If queried with alternative prompts (e.g., random instead of the forget instance), performance degrades. Unlike gradient-based methods, ICUL does not guarantee forgetting across all queries. Reviewer 3 suggests that this makes ICUL more akin to prompt-based adversarial attacks than true unlearning.
- **Missing comparisons to related lines of work**: Several relevant prior works are not discussed:
  - KGA: A General Machine Unlearning Framework Based on Knowledge Gap Alignment (Reviewer 1).
  - Prompt-based adversarial attack methods such as Raman et al. (2023) "Model-tuning Via Prompts Makes NLP Models Adversarially Robust" (Reviewer 3).
  - Concept erasure works in NLP (Reviewer 2 lists references 1,2,3; not specified in review).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Section 4.2 is redundant and better placed in ablations.
  - Inconsistency in tables: ICUL appears without query length in Table 1 but with (s) in Table 2; the notation should be clarified.
  - Baselines are not clearly defined: “Baseline” in Section 5.2 is unclear; Reviewer 3 assumes it refers to comparing train vs. held-out on the initial model, but this is not stated explicitly.
  - Typos and inconsistent abbreviation of “membership inference” (Reviewer 3).
- **Specific results unaddressed**:
  - Why does GA perform worse than the “Baseline” (sudden spike at 10⁻¹ FPR for Amazon and Yelp)? Reviewer 3 asks: gradient ascent optimizes forgetting; it should be below baseline.
  - Why is the train set accuracy for ICUL(6) on SST-2 low when models are fine-tuned with causal language loss?
  - Reviewer 3 questions the output dynamics when querying the forget instance again using the ICUL prompt format.
- **Stopping criteria for GA not discussed**: Reviewer 1 asks for rule-of-thumb suggestions.
- **Equation notation**: Reviewer 1 questions the definition of $\hat{\theta}$ in Eq.(2) and whether each (x,y) is used as one test sample in the LRT.
- **Conclusion claim**: The statement that “label flipping of context examples had an insignificant impact on smaller LLMs” challenges prior work, but the paper does not explain this discrepancy.
