## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes combining smoothing (quadratic programming regularization of ILPs) with surrogate losses (SPO+, SCE) for decision-focused learning, and uses DYS-Net to improve scalability. Multiple reviewers acknowledge the clear writing, good organization, and thorough background. However, the main concerns center on limited novelty (the combination is seen as straightforward/incremental, mostly assembling prior work), insufficient theoretical justification for gradient benefits and surrogate loss properties, and experiments that are too small or lack comprehensive comparisons. One reviewer (score 8) strongly praises the novelty and efficiency; the other four (scores 3,3,5,5) judge the contribution as weak to moderate.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written, with clear mathematics, good structure, and helpful visualizations (Reviewers 1,4,5).
- It provides a good summary of existing DFL methods (smoothing, surrogate losses) and clearly explains their drawbacks (Reviewers 1,2,3,5).
- Combining smoothing with surrogate losses to mitigate vanishing gradients is a sensible idea, easily implementable by practitioners (Reviewers 2,3,5).
- Using DYS-Net as a differentiable solver significantly reduces training time while maintaining decision quality (Reviewers 2,4).
- Numerical experiments on common DFL benchmarks (shortest path, knapsack, TSP via PyEPO) show promising results; the proposed approach achieves test regret comparable to or lower than state‑of‑the‑art methods (Reviewers 2,3,5).
- One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 8) considers the combination of smoothing and surrogate loss novel, and highlights the efficiency and clarity as major strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and incrementality:** The paper largely compiles prior works; the proposed combination of smoothing and surrogate losses is straightforward and does not present significant technical challenges (Reviewers 1,2,3). Reviewer 3 notes that smoothed SCE with DYS‑Net performs comparably but not uniformly best—SPO+ with computational tricks and CaVE often do similar or better—making the contribution feel incremental. Reviewer 5 states that Section 5 merely explains another paper’s method (DYS‑Net) and is not a contribution of this work.
- **Theoretical justification is insufficient:**
  - The claim that the gradient of the smoothed task loss remains zero over large regions is not well supported; Figure 1b is a single low‑dimensional example that does not generalize (Reviewers 2,5). No theoretical analysis or computational evidence is provided for higher‑dimensional behavior (Reviewer 5).
  - The benefit of applying QP smoothing to SPO+ (which is already convex) is unclear; it may add regularization rather than improve gradients, and the experiments show no significant gain (Reviewer 3).
  - The proof on page 5 lacks a formal statement (Proposition/Lemma) (Reviewer 3). Proposition 1 holds only when both regret and SCE loss are zero, which is rarely true in practice (misspecification, noise). The appendix fails to bound expected regret using the SCE loss (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 notes that Berthet et al. 2020 (“Learning with Differentiable Perturbed Optimizers”) may already address some of the gradient problems discussed.
- **Lack of comparison with related acceleration methods:** The paper does not compare DYS‑Net with other speed‑up techniques such as LP relaxations (Blackbox Differentiation, PyEPO library) or solution caching (Contrastive Losses and Solution Caching) (Reviewer 4). This limits the evaluation of the claim to reduce training time.
- **Experiments are too limited and lack detail:**
  - Only small instances are tested (e.g., 11‑node TSP), so scalability to larger COPs is not demonstrated (Reviewer 4).
  - Missing ablation studies (Reviewer 2) and comparisons on settings where zero regret is achievable (e.g., weighted classification, shortest path with a linear plug‑in model) (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing hyperparameter details: learning rate, batch size, optimizer, and crucially the smoothing parameter μ (Reviewer 4).
  - Lack of gradient behavior experiments (e.g., gradient magnitude comparison with existing methods, regret vs. training epochs) to support the claim of improved gradient flow (Reviewer 5).
  - Inconsistent base models across experiments: different solvers (CVXPYlayers vs. DYS‑Net) are used for different methods, making direct comparison unfair (Reviewer 5 suggests using the same neural network and solver for all methods).
- **Clarity of proposed method:** The exposition does not clearly state where the proposed method is defined; the empirical problem and full training procedure are not explicitly presented (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 3 questions why DYS‑Net is only applied to SCE and not to SPO+ or other surrogate losses.
- **Practical concerns:** Guidance on choosing μ is absent; does DYS‑Net require pretraining? No details are provided (Reviewer 4). How μ affects performance and computation time is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
