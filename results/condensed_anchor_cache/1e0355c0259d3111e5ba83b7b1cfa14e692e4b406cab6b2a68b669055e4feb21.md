## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents the Neural Evolutionary Kernel Method (NEKM) for solving semi-linear time-dependent PDEs. NEKM combines operator splitting, boundary integral techniques, and deep neural networks to construct evolutionary kernels that approximate solution operators. It pre-trains neural network representations of Green’s functions and then solves the PDE by alternating between a nonlinear ODE flow map and a linear convolutional kernel. Experiments on heat equations and Allen-Cahn equations in complex domains and on manifolds are provided.

### Strengths
- The approach is innovative, combining operator splitting, boundary integral techniques, and DNNs in a mathematically grounded way, offering a fresh perspective on solving PDEs.
- NEKM reduces the need for extensive network parameters and sampling points by leveraging boundary integral representations, improving training efficiency and relaxing regularity assumptions on solutions.
- The method can be applied to problems in complex domains and on manifolds, demonstrating generalizability across different settings.
- NEKM can be combined with time discretization schemes that preserve energy stability, a crucial property for modeling physical systems.
- The treatment of singular boundary integrals arising from fundamental solutions is addressed, contributing to the method’s efficiency and robustness.
- Empirical validation on heat equations and Allen-Cahn equations in complex domains and on manifolds shows high accuracy.
- The incorporation of mathematical prior knowledge (e.g., Green’s functions, conservation laws, boundary conditions) into the neural network framework is nice and gives the method theoretical soundness.
- The computational savings from defining the Green’s function on the boundary rather than the interior domain are appreciated.
- The high-dimensional simulations in Figures 6–7 and the ability to handle different manifolds and boundary conditions are positive aspects.
- The paper is well-written and easy-to-follow, with a nice abstract that motivates the use of numerical methods for PDEs.

**Note:** One reviewer (score 6) was very positive about the method’s novelty and potential, while three other reviewers (scores 5, 3, 3) raised significant concerns about limitations, evaluation, and novelty relative to existing work.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited PDE Class**: NEKM only works on semi-linear PDEs. This is a strong limitation; the extension to fully nonlinear PDEs (e.g., hyperbolic problems with shocks) is not discussed, and it is unclear how operator splitting would handle nonlinear parts lacking closed-form solutions. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Reliance on Known Fundamental Solution**: The method heavily relies on a closed-form fundamental solution \(G_0\). For many PDEs, such solutions are not available, restricting applicability. The numerical handling of the singularity of \(G_0\) (asymptotic expansions, integration by parts) is not explained in sufficient detail—specifically, the order of approximation and criteria for applying integration by parts are missing. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient Evaluation Against Baselines**: The evaluation compares only to exact solutions, with no comparison to other numerical methods (e.g., neural operators, PINNs, finite difference/element methods). For the Allen-Cahn equation, results are not directly compared with a known baseline—comparisons relegated to the appendix are not enough. The method is tested only on simple linear heat/diffusion and Allen-Cahn equations; hyperbolic problems (e.g., GPME benchmarking framework from Hansen et al., ICML 2023) are not considered. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Lack of Computational Cost and Scalability Analysis**: The paper does not discuss computational resources required for training and inference, how the method scales to larger 3D problems or longer time scales, or how it compares to classical numerical methods in terms of efficiency. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Dependence on BINet and Unclear Novelty**: The method seems to depend heavily on the BINet framework. The novelty over prior work by the authors (Lin et al., 2023a, 2023b) is unclear beyond extending to time-dependent problems. The exposition of Section 2 is not clear and includes details (e.g., BINet equations) that could be moved to an appendix. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Implementation and Discretization Concerns**: The operator splitting implementation and treatment of the coupling between linear and nonlinear parts are not clearly explained. The time discretization (Forward Euler, first-order) adds first-order error; stability bounds on \(\Delta t/\tau\) are not discussed. The specific time discretization scheme used is not justified relative to alternatives (e.g., RK4). The method seems to require analytical or numerical solutions to the ODE in the first step, which is a strong limitation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Complexity and Accessibility**: The method is complex, combining operator splitting, boundary integrals, Green’s functions, and neural networks. This may hinder adoption by practitioners not expert in all these areas. The interpretability of the neural network components remains a challenge despite incorporation of mathematical priors. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited Real-World Validation**: Examples are in relatively small and simple domains; more complex and realistic geometries (e.g., from arxiv 2309.00583) would better demonstrate real-world applicability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing Connections to Related Work**: The paper lacks discussion of other kernel operator methods such as the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) and methods that use neural networks only at the spatial level with time discretization (e.g., Evolutional Deep Neural Network, Implicit Neural Spatial Representations for Time-dependent PDEs, Neural Galerkin Scheme). The incorporation of boundary conditions into Neural Operators (e.g., Saad et al., ICLR 2023) is not referenced. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Error Propagation from Pre-trained Green’s Function**: The error from pre-training the Green function may propagate to the PDE solution; the paper does not analyze how accurate the numerically approximated Green function must be to avoid affecting performance. (Reviewer 4)
- **Energy Stability Claim**: Energy stability is claimed as a contribution, but it is not clear whether it is empirically observed or analytically grounded. (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation Issues**: Figure 12 is too small; Figure 3 lacks axis labels; the term “evolutionary PDEs” should be defined earlier. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Scope of Empirical Validation**: The validation covers only heat and Allen-Cahn equations. More diverse PDE types across different scientific domains are needed to support generalizability claims. (Reviewer 1)
