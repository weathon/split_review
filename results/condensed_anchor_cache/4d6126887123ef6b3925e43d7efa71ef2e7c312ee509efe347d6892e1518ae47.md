## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes the Consistency Trajectory Model (CTM), a generalization of Consistency Models (CM) and score-based diffusion models. CTM trains a single neural network to predict any final time from any initial condition along the Probability Flow (PF) ODE, enabling one-step generation with score access. The method combines denoising score matching (DSM), a soft-matching loss, and adversarial training to achieve state-of-the-art FIDs on CIFAR-10 (1.73) and ImageNet 64×64 (1.92) for single-step sampling. CTM also introduces γ-sampling, a multi-step procedure that consistently improves sample quality with more steps. Reviewers found the topic timely, the idea novel and natural, and the empirical results strong. One reviewer (R3, rating 8) was particularly positive about the paper’s clarity and connections to prior work. Other reviewers (ratings 6) raised concerns about training complexity, methodological justification, missing experiments, and reproducibility.

### Strengths

- **Timely and novel idea.** The paper extends Consistency Models in a natural, intuitive way. (R1)
- **SOTA one-step FID.** Achieves new single-step generation records on CIFAR-10 (FID 1.73) and ImageNet 64×64 (FID 1.92). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Natural multi-step sampling.** CTM mitigates a key limitation of CM: performance does not saturate with more compute; γ-sampling yields consistent improvements. (R1, R3)
- **Score access preserved.** Unlike CM, CTM can access the score function, enabling exact likelihood computation and compatibility with established conditional / controllable generation methods. (R3)
- **Theorem 2 and Classifier-Rejection Sampling** are interesting, non‑trivial, and novel. (R2)
- **Thorough baseline comparisons** and helpful ablation studies that isolate the effect of each design component. (R1, R3)
- **Clear presentation** (especially Figure 2) with good positioning relative to related work. One reviewer (R3) found the paper well written with valuable insights. (R1, R3, R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Training complexity.** The distillation involves three networks, multiple forward passes per step (at least 4 model passes of CTM), ODE solver calls, DSM loss, and a GAN discriminator. Training also requires tricks such as a warm‑up scheme for λ_GAN and a special time sampling schedule (Appendix D.1) with little ablation of their importance. (R4: methodology “excessively complex”; R3: request detailed forward‑pass count and total training time in Table 3, compared to the teacher; R3: ask for more elaboration on the design and necessity of these tricks.)
- **GAN dependency and motivation.** The strong performance heavily relies on adversarial training (γ‑sampling in Figure 15). Reviewers questioned why one should use a complex distillation pipeline (expensive pretrained diffusion + costly CTM training) instead of simply training a GAN for single‑step generation, given that GANs already excel on standard benchmarks. (R4: “the fundamental component driving performance improvement is the GAN training objective”; R1: Section 3.2 is confusing, and the need for an extra loss term due to numerical instabilities weakens the claimed advantage of recovering the conditional expectation via approximation.)
- **Missing mode‑coverage analysis.** Adversarial training is known to compromise mode coverage. One reviewer (R3) explicitly requested Recall on ImageNet 64 in Table 2.
- **Reproducibility concerns.** The authors reported difficulties reproducing Consistency Models even with source code available. Reviewers asked whether similar stability issues affect CTM and requested a discussion of such factors. (R2) Concrete implementation details for GAN weight tuning (e.g., “tuned similarly to VQGAN”) are also needed. (R2)
- **Insufficient comparisons with Consistency Models.** For extremely small NFE, comparisons should primarily be against CM (not EDM). The reviewer (R4) also asked why CTM outperforms CM when s=0, and why the retrained CM in the paper is much worse than the officially reported FID. (R4, R3 minor a)
- **Mathematical clarity and minor errors.** The definition of g in Lemma 1 may have reversed integration bounds. (R1) The fact that G can be written in terms of g is trivial and should not be part of a lemma. (R1) The explanation of Section 3.2 needs clearer exposition—the main point (a separate network used for ODE solving, with student learning those predictions) is hard to follow. (R1) The paper contains typos (e.g., missing parenthesis in citation at top of page 3). (R1)
- **Missing training‑cost comparisons.** The paper does not report wall‑clock training time; the process appears more expensive than training the diffusion model itself or a GAN. (R4, R3 weakness 2)
- **Ambiguities in reported numbers.** The FID of Rectified Flow (4.85) is from 2‑Rectified Flow with distillation—the paper should specify which model is compared. (R3 minor c) The official DFNO FID is 3.78, not the value listed. (R3 minor b)
- **Unexplained advantage over teacher.** Why does adversarial training enable CTM to beat the teacher model? (R1, R4) Why does Taylor expansion account for discretization error? (R4)
