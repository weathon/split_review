## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a novel attack where an adversary fine-t-tunes a pre-trained language model (LM) to amplify exposure of its pre-training data. Using pseudo-labels derived from the machine-generated probability of each generation (via DetectGPT’s perturbation discrepancy), the method employs reinforcement learning (RLHF) to reward generations that appear more human-written, under the assumption that such texts are more likely to be pre-training data. Experiments on six OPT models show a 4-8× increase in training data exposure for LMs with over 1B parameters. The paper is well structured and addresses a timely privacy issue, but several reviewers raise concerns about the sufficiency of the evidence, the reliance on a single detection method, the lack of diversity analysis, and the practical assumptions of the attack.

### Strengths
- **Novel attack scenario:** Differs from prior post-hoc extraction by adversarially fine-tuning the LM to intensify retention of its pre-training data, rather than modifying the decoding process. This is the first work to explore such an attack (e.g., R2: “originality” R3: “new perspective” R4: “unique attack vector”).
- **Timely and important problem:** Addresses privacy risks in large LMs, a topic of widespread concern (R2, R4).
- **Well-written and easy to follow:** Clear explanation of threat model, adversary capabilities, and steps (R2, R4). Uses figures, tables, equations effectively (R2).
- **Comprehensive empirical scope:** Experiments across six datasets and six OPT models, enhancing generalizability (R1, R2).
- **Thorough technical detail:** Includes discussion of mitigations and open questions (R2).
- **Significant reported effect:** Up to 4-8× increase in training data exposure for models >1B parameters (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evidence to distinguish genuine memorization amplification from overfitting to the pseudo-labeled generations.** This is a major concern raised by one reviewer (R3, score 5 with high confidence) who is more critical than the others. Specifically:
  - No deduplication at the n-gram level; only full-string deduplication is performed. Without a diversity metric or n‑gram overlap analysis, the reported extractions may simply be repeated memorized n‑grams (R3).
  - The analysis does not disentangle how many of the extracted strings overlap with the specific generations used during fine-tuning versus being entirely new regurgitations (R3 Q1).
  - No comparison is shown between the diversity of generations from the fine-tuned model and those from the reference model; the fine‑tuned model may have collapsed to a small set of outputs (R3 Q2).
  - Section 5.2 only validates that the reward model can distinguish machine‑generated from human‑written text; it does not provide evidence that this leads to increased training data extraction (R3).
- **Missing qualitative and utility evaluations:**
  - No example reconstructed texts to illustrate the attack’s effect (R1).
  - No comparison of model utility (e.g., task performance) between the fine‑tuned and standard models (R1).
- **No comparison with existing TDE attacks:** The paper does not situate the proposed attack against prior work in terms of feasibility, scalability, or practicality (R2).
- **Over-reliance on a single detection method (DetectGPT):** The pseudo‑labeling step uses only DetectGPT; there is no robustness evaluation or justification of how perturbation discrepancy correlates with membership probability or actual training data presence. Confounding factors (prompts, sampling, hyperparameters) are not accounted for (R2).
- **Unrealistic attacker assumption:** The attack requires restricted white‑box access (gradient‑based fine‑tuning), which is often unavailable in practice (e.g., API‑only access). The paper does not discuss this limitation in detail nor consider weaker adversarial models (R4).
- **Lack of mechanistic explanation:** It is unclear how pseudo‑labels guide fine‑tuning to favor training data generation. A mathematical formulation of the adversarial fine‑tuning objective and its effect on the output distribution is missing (R4).
- **Ethical/social implications not addressed:** The paper proposes a novel attack that could amplify exposure of sensitive information, but does not discuss potential harms or risks (R2).
- **Clarity issues:**
  - The structure is hard to follow in parts; deduplication procedure is not fully clear (R3).
  - The choice of threshold for \(d(x)\) in Figure 1 is not explained – was it empirical? (R2 Q1).
  - Table 1 has inconsistent highlighting (e.g., lowest test accuracy highlighted in Epoch 1, highest in Epoch 2, no highlights in Epochs 0 and 3) without justification (R2 Q2).
  - Section 5.2’s duplicate token overlap experiment is not clearly motivated (R3 Q4).
- **Open question regarding DP training:** How would the attack’s efficacy change if the pre‑trained LM had differential privacy applied? This is not investigated (R4 Q).
