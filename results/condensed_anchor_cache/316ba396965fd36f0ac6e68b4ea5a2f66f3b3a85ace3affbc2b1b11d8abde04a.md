## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper derives classifier-free and classifier-based guidance for discrete diffusion models, introduces Uniform Diffusion Language Models (UDLM) with an improved continuous-time variational lower bound, and demonstrates controlled generation on genomic sequences, small molecule design, and discretized images. Reviewers are divided: three rate the paper 6 (good, acceptance-level), one rates it 3 (reject-level). The higher-scoring reviewers praise the theoretical derivation, practical guidance mechanisms, and strong results on small-vocabulary tasks. The dissenting reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) challenges the novelty, technical scalability, and experimental rigor, arguing that the uniform prior and guidance formulation are already known and that the method fundamentally cannot scale to large vocabularies. All reviewers note limited scope (small vocabularies only), missing comparisons with closely related discrete diffusion works, and insufficient explanation of key design choices.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a clear theoretical foundation with derivations of the continuous-time variational lower bound for uniform noise diffusion, yielding a simple expression that improves the ELBO. (R1)
- The guidance mechanisms (classifier-free and classifier-based) are practical and easy to implement. (R1)
- Thorough ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis are included. (R1)
- Adapting diffusion models to discrete data is an important task, with applications to molecule and text generation. (R2)
- Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of guidance with discrete diffusion models across several domains (genomic sequences, small molecule design, CIFAR-10). (R2)
- UDLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on small vocabulary datasets. (R2, R3)
- The paper is well-written and well-organized, with a logical narrative. (R3, R4)
- The proposed method is simple and easy to follow. (R3)
- The experimental settings are diverse across various discrete domains. (R4 – though qualified by “difficult to pinpoint further clear strengths”)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (minority view, R4, score 3, confidence 5):** The uniform prior and the corresponding variational lower bound are already known. The analysis is a direct application of the MDLM setup for the uniform path. The paper does not significantly differ from Discrete Flow Matching (Gat et al. 2024) and Dirichlet Flow/Diffusion (Stark et al.). The first-order Taylor approximation to guidance was already introduced (Nisonoff et al. 2024); the adaptation here is a minor change. The dissenting reviewer “very respectfully disagrees” with the claimed contributions and sees nothing novel. (R4)
- **Contribution unclear / overclaimed (R3):** The description of contributions is not clear – it is ambiguous whether the main claim is the guidance mechanisms alone or also the interpolating discrete diffusion model part. The claim that the model is “particularly amenable to guidance” may be overclaimed because the key technique (transition probabilities as interpolation between current state and a prior distribution) is not new. (R3)
- **Missing comparisons with closely related discrete diffusion works (R3, R4):** The paper does not discuss or compare with very similar discrete diffusion methods that also use simplified interpolation (references [1] and [2] in R3; Gat et al. 2024, Stark et al., and MDLM in R4). The lack of comparison makes the claimed SOTA performance unconvincing – it is unclear whether gains come from the guidance methods or the choice of underlying diffusion process. (R3, R4) **Specifically, the omission of MDLM for LM1B (Table 3) is awkward; MDLM’s test perplexity upper bound of 27.04 is better than UDLM.** (R4)
- **Limited scope and scalability (R1, R4):** The improvements are mainly restricted to multinomial diffusion with small vocabularies; there is a persistent performance gap in larger-vocabulary NLP tasks. The dissenting reviewer has “severe concerns” about usefulness when scaling: the need to compute the normalization constant in the guidance formulation prevents extension to larger-scale systems. The reviewer notes that Dirichlet Flow Matching (Stark et al. 2024) explicitly points out this issue with uniform prior paths and suggests Dirichlet paths instead. (R4)
- **Poor justification and incomplete analysis (R1, R2, R3):**
  - The paper lacks computational complexity analysis and detailed runtime comparisons. (R1)
  - Hyperparameter choices, particularly sampling steps T, need more thorough justification. A question asks: “How does the choice of T affect the trade-off between generation quality and computational cost? Is there a systematic way to choose optimal T?” (R1)
  - The theoretical analysis of guidance mechanisms could be more extensive; failure cases when guidance strength γ is high should be explored. (R1)
  - In the Introduction, “D-CBG” is introduced before being explained. (R2)
  - The logic of why UDLM makes guidance easier is unclear – the only difference is setting π, and the intuition behind that choice is not explained. (R2)
  - Section 3.2 claims the proposed method yields a simple expression for the ELBO, but the ELBO is not explicitly shown below that statement; a table comparing the new and old ELBO is suggested. (R2)
  - The equation in Line 181 (page 4) may not be correct: why does the log(p) term equal the inner product term? (R3)
- **Experimental omissions (R4):**
  - Tables 1 and 3 report results on the same dataset (LM1B) with different vocabulary sizes, which feels contrived. It is clear uniform noise does not work well at larger vocabularies. (R4)
  - Generative perplexity under GPT-2 is not reported, unlike in MDLM. (R4)
  - No textual generated samples are shown, even though CIFAR-10 samples are provided – this is a strange omission; visual inspection of generated samples would help assess performance differences. (R4)
- **Key advantage over other discrete diffusion methods not explained (R3):** The paper lacks analysis of why the proposed method performs better than alternatives – e.g., differences in convergence rates, stability, or computational efficiency. (R3)
