## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally agree the paper is the first to study stochastic linear bandits under lexicographic ordering, with a novel algorithm (MTE²LO) and regret bound \(\widetilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})\) plus an additional problem-dependent parameter \(\lambda\). However, critical concerns recur across multiple reviews: the regret definition and its comparison with prior work (Hüyük & Tekin 2021) are disputed, with one reviewer providing a concrete counterexample that challenges the claimed strictness of the general regret over priority-based regret; the necessity, prior-knowledge assumption, and practical feasibility of \(\lambda\) are questioned; several reviewers argue that a simple sequential application of single-objective OFUL might achieve similar or better regret without needing \(\lambda\); the algorithms share structural similarities with SupLinRel/SupLinUCB and lack sufficient intuition for their three-phase design; the decision set assumptions and the absence of a lower bound for general regret are criticized; experiments are limited to synthetic data and show nearly linear regret, not clearly sublinear. Two reviewers are more positive (scores 5), two are more critical (scores 3).

### Strengths
- The problem of multiobjective stochastic linear bandits under lexicographic ordering is novel and under-explored, with plausible real-world motivation (water resource planning, radiation treatment).
- The paper provides clear exposition: it introduces the standard linear setting first, then extends to the multiobjective case, and develops algorithms incrementally from PF-LEX to STE²LO to MTE²LO.
- Regret bounds are proved and stated clearly, with numerical experiments on synthetic data that demonstrate the algorithms’ performance.
- The introduction of the \(\lambda\) parameter to capture the complexity of optimal arms and the new lexicographically ordered arm filter (LOAF) are novel algorithmic contributions.
- A detailed formulation of the model and problem setting is given.

### Weaknesses
1. **Regret definition and comparison with prior work**  
   - Reviewer 3 provides a counterexample showing the claim that \(R^i(T)\) is more stringent than \(\hat{R}^i(T)\) is false. Let \(\theta^1=(1,0),\; \theta^2=(0,1),\; x_A=(0,0),\; x_B=(-1,1)\). For always playing \(x_B\), \(R^1=T,\; R^2=-T\) and \(\hat{R}^1=T,\; \hat{R}^2=0\). Priority-based regret can be worse, but the paper’s assertion about stringency is reversed.  
   - Reviewer 3 argues that the comparison with Hüyük & Tekin (2021) is flawed: their lower bound \(\Omega(T^{2/3})\) is for priority-based regret, while the paper’s upper bound is for general regret. The claim that prior work is “suboptimal” is incorrect because the prior algorithm does not achieve optimal even for single-objective MAB.  
   - Reviewer 2 asks how the new regret formulation differs from the earlier indicator-based formulation; if the indicator is false, regret for that objective inherently increases.  
   - Reviewer 4 questions why we are interested in general regret and whether a lower bound for it can be derived, given that Hüyük & Tekin provided one for priority-based regret.  
   - Reviewer 2 notes that Lu et al. achieved a Pareto-optimal regret bound; does Pareto optimal regret imply lexicographic optimal regret, and how does this work go beyond that?

2. **The \(\lambda\) parameter**  
   - Multiple reviewers question the necessity and feasibility of knowing \(\lambda\) a priori. Reviewer 1 asks for a counterexample if \(\lambda\) is omitted; Reviewer 2 states that prior knowledge of \(\lambda\) seems infeasible.  
   - Reviewer 3 gives a detailed example that \(\lambda\) is not a fixed problem characteristic but can be manipulated. Let \(x_A=(1+\epsilon,0)\), \(x_B=(1,1)\) with the same \(\theta\) as above. Equation (5) gives \(1\leq \lambda\epsilon\), so \(\lambda\geq 1/\epsilon\). If \(\epsilon=T^{-1/3}\), Theorem 2 yields \(R^2(T)=\widetilde{O}(T^{2/3})\), not \(\sqrt{T}\). With more objectives, the time order degrades further. An adversary can choose arms to make \(\lambda\) scale with \(T\), worsening the bound.  
   - Reviewer 3 further contests the optimality claim: comparing to the scalar linear bandit lower bound \(\Omega(d\sqrt{T})\) is inappropriate because the \(\lambda\) assumption is not part of standard mild assumptions; the example shows the bound is not instance-independent.  
   - Reviewer 4 notes that the abstract mentions real-world applications (water resource planning, radiation treatment), but more detail is needed to justify why \(\lambda\) would be known in practice.  
   - Reviewer 1 points out a notation issue: the paper writes \(\lambda\) when it should be \(\lambda^i\) for each objective.

3. **Alternative simpler algorithms**  
   - Reviewers 1 and 2 independently ask why the authors do not simply run a standard single-objective linear bandit algorithm (e.g., OFUL) sequentially for each objective. Reviewer 2 suggests: apply OFUL for the first objective, then if multiple arms remain, apply OFUL for the second, and so on. This would achieve a bound like \(O(md\sqrt{T})\) (Reviewer 1) or similar to Abbasi-Yadkori (Reviewer 2). A detailed comparison between this naive bound and the paper’s \(O((\sum_i \lambda^i)d\sqrt{T})\) is needed.

4. **Algorithm design and intuition**  
   - Reviewer 2 finds little intuition for the three phases in MTE²LO; Reviewer 1 questions why forced exploration is required and whether UCB-like schemes could replace the three-part trade-off.  
   - Reviewer 4 notes that STE²LO and MTE²LO resemble SupLinRel/SupLinUCB; the authors should clarify the analytical challenge and could condense the description.  
   - Reviewer 2 points out a possible typo: on page 5 “SCE²LO” should be “STE²LO”.

5. **Assumptions about the decision set**  
   - Reviewer 2 criticizes that the theorems do not specify whether \(\mathcal{D}\) is finite or infinite. If finite, the algorithm should be compared to SupLinUCB regret bounds; if infinite, it is unclear how to filter arms based on chain relations.  
   - Reviewer 2 questions the claim before Equation (4) that “the decision set for all times are determined before the game start” – how is this possible?

6. **Experiments**  
   - Reviewer 1 notes the regret plots appear too linear even with 10,000 steps; suggests either running longer or showing a smaller setup where the sublinear part is visible.  
   - Reviewers 1 and 4 both suggest that experiments on real-world datasets would make the efficiency of the proposed algorithms more convincing.

7. **Presentation and notation**  
   - Reviewer 4 observes that both \(m\) and \(d\) seem used for dimension; they should be unified.  
   - Reviewer 4 suggests adding a notation table and a table that compares existing results in multiobjective bandits.  
   - Reviewer 2 rates presentation 2/5, citing confusion over several points (decision set, regret comparison, typo).
