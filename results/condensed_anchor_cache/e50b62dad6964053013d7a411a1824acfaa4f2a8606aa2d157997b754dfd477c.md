## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method for efficient few-shot multiple choice question answering (MCQA) by distilling a large language model (LLM) into a smaller encoder-only model (DeBERTa-v3-base). The approach uses an LLM (or smaller decoder-only models) to generate synthetic MCQA data via two prompting strategies (JSON format vs. decomposed/question-answer-per-option) and to assign probability scores to choices. The smaller model is then fine-tuned with a distillation loss using the LLM's soft scores. Evaluation on MMLU shows an improvement from 28.9% to 39.3% over a 5-shot baseline; ablation studies are conducted on ARC.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and presents a practical method for distilling an LLM into a smaller encoder-only model (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- A comprehensive ablation study is provided, including analysis of distillation loss with temperature scaling and use of format correctness as an implicit confidence signal (Reviewers 1, 2, 5). Results are reported across multiple random seeds (Reviewer 5).
- The approach addresses a relevant problem: enabling few-shot MCQA when computational resources are limited (Reviewer 4).
- The paper explores the effectiveness of LLM-driven data generation for MCQA tasks, and the proposed distillation training shows notable performance improvements (Reviewer 2).
- The methodology sheds light on the capabilities of encoder-only models, which have received less attention recently, and is adaptable to domain-specific benchmarks (Reviewer 3).
- The setting is slightly novel: distilling a decoder-only LLM into an encoder-only model for the specific task of MCQA (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The overall method is straightforward. Using formatting prompts (JSON vs. segmented) is not a novel data generation technique; the distillation loss reduces essentially to fine-tuning with soft labels and does not explore advanced techniques like attention-based or feature-level distillation (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). The paper does not introduce a new dataset (Reviewer 3).
- **Does not consistently outperform existing smaller models**: 
  - The best result using the constructed dataset (JSON distillation) does not surpass the multi-task fine-tuned Tasksource model (same DeBERTa backbone) – MMLU improvement is marginal (+0.5) and on ARC the proposed approach performs significantly worse (Reviewers 1, 2, 5). 
  - The improvement over Tasksource is narrow (0.5 percentage points) and a clear advantage is not established (Reviewer 5).
- **Performance gain is limited and depends on base model strength**: Gains are pronounced with the weaker DeBERTa-base but minimal with the stronger Tasksource model; performance even slightly decreases for the Decompose method (Reviewer 2).
- **Practical significance is questionable**: 
  - Existing alternatives (e.g., Tasksource, which needs only one forward pass and no synthetic data generation; or small LLMs like Gemma-2-2b-it after quantization) are compelling and can run cheaply even on a laptop. It is unclear when the described distillation into DeBERTa would be used in practice (Reviewer 5).
  - MCQA is a specific format convenient for evaluation, but results may not directly indicate general QA abilities. For encoder-only models, a classification-based approach (scoring each QA pair) may be more appropriate (Reviewer 1).
  - The findings are not surprising given that knowledge distillation and synthetic data generation with LLMs are well-studied and known to work (Reviewer 5).
- **Reliance on robust LLMs**: The method depends heavily on the availability of high-performing LLMs, which may not be accessible in languages other than English or for domain-specific tasks. The computational cost of running the LLM for data generation can be substantial, potentially negating the efficiency gains of distillation (Reviewer 4).
- **Data quality and generation issues**:
  - The decomposed generation method often yields noisy data due to longer, less structured answers, which can degrade model performance despite reducing parsing errors (Reviewer 4).
  - There is inadequate validation of the generated dataset quality: analysis relies only on semantic similarity, neglecting question complexity, diversity, and correctness of answers (Reviewer 3). No check (e.g., nearest neighbors or answer option overlap) is performed to verify that generated examples are not present in or too close to the MMLU/ARC test sets (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only the DeBERTa encoder model is tested; no comparison is made with other encoder-only architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, which was mentioned as performing poorly without further analysis). Evaluations are limited to MCQA tasks (Reviewer 3). 
- **Lack of analysis on why JSON outperforms Decompose**: The paper does not provide example-based analysis to explain the performance difference between the two generation strategies (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experiments or analyses (from reviewer questions)**:
  - Is the improvement due to learning the MCQA format or actual question-answering ability? (Reviewer 1)
  - Comparison with recently released lightweight models (~1B parameters) is absent (Reviewer 3).
  - Why use few-shot learning in an encoder model instead of a masked language model (MLM) approach? (Reviewer 3)
  - Details on inference for baseline models and on handling improperly formatted JSON samples (reviewer 5).
  - Exact number of generated MCQA examples (line 245 says “approximately 4000” – should be exact) (Reviewer 5).
  - Why set the number of negative examples to 5 when MMLU and ARC have only 4 choices? (Reviewer 5)
  - What temperature was used for the MMLU experiments? (Reviewer 5)
  - How is a sequence transformed into a scalar (e.g., [CLS] token, pooling) for scoring? (Reviewer 5)
  - Does handling an n-option MCQ require n forward passes? (Reviewer 5)
- **Minor**: A couple of typos (missing space in line 69/70, “a scalar values” in line 179) (Reviewer 5).
