## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies domain counterfactuals — what a sample would have looked like if generated in a different domain — in the setting where latent variables follow an SCM and observations are invertible nonlinear mixtures. It introduces Invertible Latent Domain (ILD) models, characterizes domain-counterfactual equivalence classes, proves that recovering the full latent SCM is unnecessary if intervention sparsity is known, and proposes a practical algorithm based on autoregressive generative models with shared parameters. Experiments on simulated and image data demonstrate improvements over baselines.

### Strengths

- The problem setting of domain counterfactuals is novel, intriguing, and practically significant (e.g., combining datasets across expensive data collection domains) (R1, R4).
- The theoretical analysis is serious and provides a significant, novel contribution both in terms of theoretical characterization (equivalence classes, canonical forms) and its application in an algorithm (R2, R3).
- The idea of modeling domain differences via invertible transformations is interesting, and if the theoretical setting holds, it could largely reduce the complexity of finding domain differences (R3).
- The paper is clearly structured, guiding the reader through the theoretical setup (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Terminology (nonstandard usage of “intervention” and “counterfactual”):** Reviewer 3 states that the “intervention” in this paper (changing structural functions between domains) differs from the standard *do*-intervention, and the “counterfactual” does not preserve past exogenous variable values as in standard counterfactual definitions. The paper does not adequately clarify how its soft intervention relates to *do*-intervention or how the counterfactual notion connects to the standard one. The authors strongly suggest avoiding the terms or adding precise definitions (R3).

- **Theoretical concern: Prop. 1 (generality of model class) is problematic:** Reviewer 3 argues that the Rosenblatt transformation is sample-specific (each sample point requires a different conditional CDF), so it cannot construct a single invertible function for the entire distribution as claimed. The paper’s argument that the transformation applies to the whole distribution is incorrect (R3).

- **Theoretical concern: Prop. 2 (invertible autoregressive assumption):** Reviewer 3 notes that the “generalized causal mechanisms” in Prop. 2 are only a subclass of invertible SCMs (invertible autoregressive SCMs). The proof assumes each subsystem \(\hat{f}^{(j)}\) is invertible, but overall invertibility of \(f\) does not guarantee subsystem invertibility. Reordering nodes does not change the functional form, so non-invertible subsystems cannot be made invertible. This assumption is stronger than stated (R3).

- **Lack of intuitive explanation for Theorem 1 and what must be identified:** Reviewer 1 finds the domain counterfactual equivalence result confusing. The paper does not provide an intuitive explanation of which aspects of the true latent causal model need to be identified (e.g., noise variables, intervention set size, structural equation forms) to guarantee counterfactual equivalence, nor which aspects are irrelevant (R1).

- **Definition of intervention set size \(k\) and reference model:** Reviewer 1 asks how to precisely define \(k\) (number of intervened variables) when the reference latent model with no interventions is unknown. What criteria determine whether a change in structural equation constitutes an intervention, especially given unknown causal mechanisms? (R1). Reviewer 4 also asks how to set \(k\) in practice (R4).

- **Canonical domain counterfactual model justification:** Reviewer 1 notes the canonical model requires that only the last \(k\) variables be intervened, which seems stringent. While any ILD can be transformed to this form, the paper should provide real-world applications illustrating the relevance and computational benefits of this canonical form (R1).

- **Selection and impact of \(k\) (sparsity):** Reviewer 1 asks for practical strategies for choosing \(k\) when the true intervention structure is unknown, and what the implications of over/underestimating \(k\) are. Reviewer 4 similarly asks how off estimates could be if \(k\) is wrong or if the ILD form is not precisely known (R1, R4).

- **Potential unstated assumption (sign-flipping example):** Reviewer 2 raises the case where domains have \(g = Id\) and \(f_d = Id\) or \(f_d = -Id\), producing identical observed distributions (standard normals). The paper’s method would be unable to decide between \(x_{d'} = x_d\) or \(x_{d'} = -x_d\). The paper should clarify whether an implicit assumption rules out such cases (R2).

- **Writing and proofreading issues:** Multiple reviewers note writing problems. Reviewer 2 suggests more proofreading and points out specific errors: abstract wording (“non-intervened ancestors” should be “no intervened ancestors”), unclear paragraph assumptions in Section 3, mis-referenced “Prop. 8” in Corollary 3, awkward phrasing “establish on”, missing continuity assumptions in Corollary 5, and unclear “not ill-defined” (R2). Reviewer 3 notes two different definitions of autoregressive functions (end of page 1 vs. Def. 1), a typo in “generalize” (Prop. 2), and mis-referenced “Prop. 8” in Corollary 3. Reviewer 4 suggests moving many theorems to the appendix and focusing on Theorem 2; the current structure uses too much space on less important theorems (R4).

- **Experimental concerns:**
  - Reviewer 4 finds the simulated experiments contrived: the data generation uses only linear transformations followed by Leaky ReLU, and the model knows this exact functional form (details buried in the long appendix). More realistic comparisons are needed (R4).
  - The image experiments (Section 5.2) lack clarity: how is sparsity enforced (L1, hard thresholding, etc.)? The VAE architecture and hyperparameter choices are not described in sufficient detail. These experiments need fuller exposition and methodological explanation (R4).

- **Missing comparisons and discussion:** Reviewer 3 asks about identifiability of canonical ILDs — can the single equivalence class containing the true model be identified when learning? Reviewer 4 asks how this work compares with prior work using sparsity for identifiable representations (e.g., Lachapelle et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2022) (R3, R4). Reviewer 3 also suggests citing Wu & Fukumizu (2020) as related work on invertible causal mechanisms and shared functions between domains (R3).

- **Minor theoretical questions:** Reviewer 3 asks whether Gaussian exogenous noises are without loss of generality (Def. 3, point 3), whether the second equality in Eq. (2) is merely by definition, and notes that “shared observation function \(g\)” should be explained immediately after Def. 3 (R3). Reviewer 3 also questions why Theorem 2 claims “both counterfactually and distributionally equivalent” — isn’t counterfactual equivalence stronger and already implies distributional equivalence? (R3).
