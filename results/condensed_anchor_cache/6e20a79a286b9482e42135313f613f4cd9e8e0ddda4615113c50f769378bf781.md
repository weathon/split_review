## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a test-time domain generalization method that models pseudo-labels as distributions (variational inference) and incorporates variational neighbor labels and a meta-generalization training stage. The reviewers acknowledge the attempted solution but raise substantial concerns about novelty, clarity, experimental scope, the amount of improvement, and reproducibility. Most reviewers rate the paper 5 (weak accept) with moderate to high confidence, but note major revisions are needed.

### Strengths

- **Reasonable core idea**: The approach of using probabilistic pseudo-labels (stochastic variables) and variational neighbor labels to handle uncertainty in test-time adaptation is considered a reasonable direction by multiple reviewers (R1, R4). R1 specifically highlights the use of stochastic variables, distribution estimation, and the meta-generalization method.
- **Empirical results on seven datasets**: The paper conducts experiments on seven widely-used datasets and reports superiority over state-of-the-art methods (R1, R2, R3). R2 notes that the improvement is "subtle" but still a strength, and R1 finds the experiments "highly intuitive and sufficiently comprehensive."
- **Code provided**: R2 positively mentions that code is provided for reproducibility (though R3 later notes it is non-executable – see Weaknesses).
- **Well-written (minority view)**: One reviewer (R1) states the paper is "well-written and easy to read," while three others strongly disagree (see Weaknesses on clarity).

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and presentation (majority criticism)**:
    - R2 finds the paper "hard to follow" and suggests labeling the approach with a meaningful code instead of "this paper," and moving the related work section earlier.
    - R4 calls clarity the "main issue," stating many parts are "not clear or seemingly not correct." Specific complaints: the graphical model in Fig. 1 is confusing (not a real probabilistic graphical model); the rationale of meta-generalization is unclear; the gradient update (Eq. 11) does not constitute formal variational inference; and the paper does not explain why a variational inference formulation is needed over simpler deterministic updates.
    - R3 notes ambiguity in how uncertainty is incorporated into the meta-objective (e.g., whether uncertainty weights the meta-loss or plays a more integral role). R3 also states Table 1 suggests meta-generalization is the key step, but the novelty of combining uncertainty with neighborhood information is not clearly articulated.
    - In contrast, R1 found the paper well-written (minority).

- **Limited experimental scope and missing benchmarks**:
    - R2: experiments are limited to small-scale datasets; missing large-scale benchmarks such as CIFAR10-C, CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C, and VisDA.
    - R3: validation is too narrow; suggests including STL10, CIFAR10-C, or CIFAR100-C (preferably without other corrupted domains, i.e., single source S=1). The paper also lacks single-source domain generalization experiments, which are relevant to real-world applications.

- **Limited improvement and incremental contribution**:
    - R2: the amount of improvement over SOTA is limited; the ablation on variational neighbor labels shows only subtle improvement.
    - R3: the contribution appears incremental rather than transformative; the usage of neighborhood information with uncertainty is not novel, and the paper fails to explain how meta-learning explicitly leverages uncertainty estimates (e.g., whether uncertainty is used to weight the meta-loss).
    - R4 questions why variational inference is needed at all, suggesting many equations could be replaced by deterministic updates and simplified.

- **Insufficient ablations**:
    - R1 requests ablations such as pseudo-labeling with meta-generalization and meta-generalization with probabilistic pseudo-labeling.
    - R3 asks for an ablation study on hyperparameters λ₁, λ₂, λ₃ to indicate the contribution of each factor.
    - R2 notes the ablation on neighbor labels shows only minor gains.

- **Missing hyperparameter and implementation details**:
    - R2: hyperparameter selection (e.g., learning rate) for each dataset is not explained.
    - R3: questions how to balance the n samples per domain; asks about other crucial hyperparameters besides λ.
    - R2 and R3 also question inference speed and computation cost introduced by the neighbor pseudo labels (R2: “How much computation cost does this neighbor pseudo label introduce?”).

- **Meta-generalization uses target domain labels? (strong criticism)**:
    - R3 points out that statements on page 4, page 5, and Algorithm 1 mention the “accessibility of actual labels of the meta-target data during training.” This raises a fundamental concern about whether target domain “training” samples are used, which would violate the domain generalization assumption. R3 asks for clarification on the source of superior performance.

- **Performance discrepancy on Office-Home**:
    - R3 highlights a noticeable performance degradation on Office-Home (Table 3) compared to results reported by Xiao et al. (2022) and asks for an explanation.

- **Rationale of meta-generalization vs. source-free domain adaptation**:
    - R3 asks what advantages meta-generalization offers over existing source-free domain adaptation methods (e.g., SHOT). The current discussion (Section 4) is not concrete enough. R3 suggests including a detailed discussion and also clarifying the negative sign in the meta loss update (L_CE – L_meta).

- **Pseudo-label quality and uncertainty**:
    - R4: the quality of pseudo-labels is still unknown and hard to understand. It is unclear when and why the method improves pseudo-label quality.

- **Code non-executable**:
    - R3: the supplementary code is non-executable; key implementations are missing (e.g., "dg_adapt_sampler.py" has missing/modified segments). Essential pieces for validation are absent.

- **Minor issues**:
    - R1: the symbol δ in Equation 2 is not explained.
    - R2: the paper’s labeling of the proposed approach as “this paper” is unhelpful.

- **Reviewer disagreement on presentation**: While one reviewer (R1) praised the writing, three others found it lacking, with R4 rating presentation as "1 poor" and R3 rating presentation as "2 fair." This conflict is noted.

- **Uncertainty about meta-generalization motivation**:
    - R4 asks for the corresponding graphical model for meta-generalization and questions why gradient-based updates (Eq. 11) should be considered variational inference. R4 also asks for justification of the variational inference approach given that deterministic alternatives could be simpler.
