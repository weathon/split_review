## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes two cycle-consistency losses (slot-feature-slot and feature-slot-feature) to regularize slot-based object-centric models (e.g., Slot Attention). The objectives enforce that features of an object map to a single slot, aiming to improve slot distinctness without relying heavily on architectural priors. The method is evaluated on unsupervised segmentation (synthetic and real-world: COCO, ScanNet) and on downstream reinforcement learning (4 Atari games). The losses are applied on top of several base models (e.g., Slot Attention, BO-Slate, etc.).

### Strengths

- The idea of adding cycle-consistency regularization to encourage distinct slots is reasonable, clearly presented, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- The motivation for the two consistency objectives is clearly conveyed (R2, R4).
- Experiments are extensively conducted across multiple datasets and tasks; the proposed regularization leads to consistent improvements in segmentation (including on BO-Slate, which already aims at disentanglement) and in downstream RL performance (R1, R2, R4).
- The method is evaluated not only on unsupervised segmentation metrics but also on a downstream task (reinforcement learning), addressing a practical gap in the object-centric learning literature (R4).
- The cycle consistency objectives are simple and effective; they may be useful for other q-former-like architectures (e.g., DETR, BLIP-2) (R3).
- Claims are validated through appropriate experiments; the paper is well-structured (R3, R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited real-world accuracy and direction doubts (R1):** Overall segmentation accuracy on real-world scenes (COCO, ScanNet) remains low; the reviewer doubts whether further enforcing slot disentanglement is a promising direction for object discovery on complex imagery.
- **Mixed and narrow Atari evaluation (R1, R4):** Results on the four Atari games are mixed; only four games are tested, raising concerns about generalization. (R1 explicitly criticizes the limited scope; R4 suggests the downstream section should appear after object discovery for more natural flow.)
- **Unconvincing logic about addressing the downstream gap (R2):** The paper claims to overcome a “notable gap” in using learned object-centric representations downstream, but simply showing better downstream performance does not logically address that gap – the claim is doubtful.
- **Limited technical novelty (R2):** The core contribution amounts to two additional loss terms; novelty is considered fair (R2) or moderate (R4), with R4 also noting that the claim to “reduce reliance on architectural priors” is not valid – Slot Attention’s clustering biases remain, and the proposed losses only enforce compact clusters, not a fundamentally new inductive bias.
- **Missing metrics for segmentation (R4):** Only FG-ARI is reported, which is known to favor undersegmentation and to ignore boundary accuracy. Proper metrics like mIoU or AP should have been used, as noted in prior work (Engelcke et al. 2020, Karazija et al. 2021, Monnier et al. 2021).
- **Insufficient justification for unsupervised image-based training (R4):** The paper does not convincingly motivate why training from scratch on RGB images is necessary, given that stronger alternatives exist: (a) supervised data (SAM), (b) contrastive features (DINO→Dinosaur, CutLER), which generalize across datasets, and (c) optical flow / depth, which is often available in video settings. The claim that using flow is infeasible because many datasets lack it is questionable, as unlabeled video is abundant.
- **Missing analyses and ablations (R3, R4):**
  - Does the slot-feature-slot objective reduce the required number of slots or affect slot feature dimension? (R3)
  - Are there failure modes beyond the trivial solution when λ_sfs is increased? (R3)
  - How to determine the number of slots? (R2)
  - Why cannot the model separate multiple semantics in slot 6 (Fig. 2)? (R2)
  - Do the proposed losses improve the Dinosaur model (which uses DINO features)? (R4)
  - What happens if the softmax in Eq. 8 is applied over the full matrix instead of only along the diagonal? (R3)
- **Reviewer disagreement on confidence and overall assessment (all):** Reviewer 1 expresses skepticism about the entire direction (score 5), while Reviewers 3 and 4 are positive (scores 8), and Reviewer 2 (score 6, low confidence 2) finds limited novelty but acknowledges extensive experiments. These differing views are preserved: R1 is the most negative, and the method’s promise remains debated, especially for real-world scenarios.
