## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses continual learning (CL) with pre-trained Vision Transformers, proposing an adaptive method that proactively detects task transfer and interference using the Fisher information matrix, gradient update directions, and a task transfer distance metric. The method employs LoRA fine-tuning with a Mixture of Experts (MoE) mechanism, along with a dynamic parameter update and expansion strategy. Experiments on seven benchmarks are reported.

### Strengths
- The proactive detection of transfer distance and the adaptive continual learning design is generally reasonable and novel (Reviewer 1, score 5, confidence 3). However, Reviewers 3 (score 3, confidence 5) and 4 (score 3, confidence 5) argue the contribution is incremental: Reviewer 3 sees it as a minor combination of GEM and MOE-Adapters with only a change from Adapters to LoRA; Reviewer 4 states that the principal direction extraction (Sec. 3.3.1) is essentially the GPM (ICLR 2021) algorithm and not a contribution.
- The approach of performing continual learning through knowledge transfer between tasks is intuitive (Reviewer 4).
- Extensive evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets are provided (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Empirical validation shows promising results across benchmarks (Reviewer 2, score 5, confidence 3).
- The topic (CL with pre-trained models) is important to the machine learning field (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and presentation** (all reviewers): The paper is hard to follow, with numerous typos, missing spaces (e.g., line 424), small fonts in figures, wrong figure references in Section 4.3 (Reviewer 1), unclear definition of many symbols (Reviewer 2, e.g., line 297 "k_t", line 342 subscript), and a lack of an overview section at the start of Section 3 or an algorithm summary at the end (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also notes the absence of a problem formulation section that specifies which CL setting is used (task-incremental, class-incremental, domain-incremental).
- **Lack of high-level intuition and insufficient differentiation from prior work** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3): The paper does not discuss the intuitions behind its designs (Reviewer 1); techniques such as MoE, FIM, and PCA are used with only brief justification and no reference to their prior application in this field (Reviewer 2); the extensive discussion of the Fisher information matrix largely overlaps with EWC, and the bound in line 228 is directly copied from [2] without contextualization (Reviewer 3).
- **Weak experiments and missing analysis** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4):
  - No ablation studies are provided; missing evaluations on hyperparameter sensitivity, number of experts, removal of individual modules (Reviewer 3), and influence of different pretrained models (Reviewer 2).
  - The analysis in Section 4.4 on transfer and interference is weak and fails to demonstrate the value of distinguishing them (Reviewer 1).
  - Experimental setup details (learning rate, batch size, epoch, hyperparameter settings for the method) are not clearly described (Reviewer 4).
  - The paper only works with pretrained Transformer architectures but does not clarify this in early sections (Reviewer 1).
- **Unfair comparison and task-identity issue** (Reviewer 4, score 3, confidence 5): The method uses task-specific routers for each task (MoE). If the setting is class-incremental learning (where task labels are unavailable at inference), the method cannot select the correct router, making it unclear how it functions. Yet the method is compared against class-incremental baselines, which is unfair.
- **Specific technical questions/errors** (overlap with criticisms):
  - Is the approach only applicable to Transformer models in vision? (Reviewer 1)
  - In Section 3.3.2, how many and which "old branches" are selected to participate in learning the new task? (Reviewer 1)
  - The formulation of β in Eq. (10) seems misaligned with its definition (Reviewer 2).
