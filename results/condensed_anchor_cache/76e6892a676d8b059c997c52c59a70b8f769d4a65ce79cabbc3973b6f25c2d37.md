## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies self-improvement in Large Language Models, proposing a mathematical formulation and a metric called the generation-verification gap (GV-gap). Experiments across model families and tasks (GSM8K, NQ, Sudoku) reveal a scaling phenomenon where the relative GV-gap scales monotonically with model pretraining FLOPs. The paper also examines conditions for self-improvement, an iterative procedure, and attempts to improve verification. Reviewers generally find the study timely and the framework insightful, though concerns about clarity, limited task diversity, missing ablation experiments, and the use of base models only are raised. One reviewer (R1, score 6) found the exposition unclear and the contributions weak, while others (R2, R3, both score 8) found the metric clearly defined and the analysis valuable.

### Strengths
- The research focus on the GV-gap provides a principled way to determine whether a model possesses self-improvement capability at any stage (pre-/post-training). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The GV-gap is clearly defined with real-world examples in different generator-verifier configurations, bringing clarity to the framework. (R2)
- The mathematical formulation and the proposed metric (GV-gap and its relative variant) offer a novel way to quantify the limits of self-improvement and are expected to be beneficial for future research. (R2, R3, R4)
- The paper is well-organized, well-written, and easy to follow. (R1, R3)
- Experiments are extensive and comprehensive, analyzing self-improvement from various perspectives: scaling laws, task suitability, iterative procedures, and better verification attempts. (R1, R3)
- The experimental findings are generally insightful, especially the scaling property of the GV-gap with pretraining FLOPs. (R3, R4)
- The selection of tasks (GSM-8K, NQ, Sudoku) is representative: they correspond to the improvable, the unimprovable, and the “theoretically improvable but empirical findings show unimprovable” cases. (R4)
- The study is timely and addresses an important phenomenon in LLM pre-training, post-training, and test-time inference. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clarity in exposition and methodology:**  
  One reviewer (R1) found the definition of the generation-verification gap not intuitively explained; the core concept is obscured by complex formulas with many symbols, and no illustrative diagram is provided to visualize the flow between generator and verifier. (R1)  
  The overall methodological framework and experimental process are described insufficiently—it is unclear how the generator and verifier models interact, how data is filtered, and how performance is evaluated—making the results hard to reproduce. (R1)  
  In contrast, other reviewers (R2, R3) found the presentation clear; this disagreement should be noted.

- **Limited task diversity and generalizability concerns:**  
  The main experiments are limited to one benchmark, GSM8K, which consists of elementary-level math problems. This dataset is relatively easy for current LLMs, may suffer from data contamination, and the findings may differ across more complex or diverse scenarios. (R1, R3)  
  The lack of experiments with more challenging datasets (e.g., harder math, reasoning, or less likely pre-training data) limits the generalizability of the conclusions. (R3)  
  A minority reviewer (R1) also raised that using only GSM8K makes the results less convincing; others (R4) noted that the Sudoku task helps, but further grid-size sweeps are requested.

- **Absence of instruct-based models weakens takeaways:**  
  The experiments are conducted only on base models. Recent work shows that smaller (8B) instruct models can self-improve well, yet this paper’s signal from base smaller models indicates they lack reasoning capabilities even under few-shot settings. The absence of instruct-based models leaves a gap in the takeaways and makes comparisons to other research difficult. (R2)  
  The paper could benefit from explicitly discussing the limitations of using only base models. (R2)

- **Utility function noise and measurement issues:**  
  The real-world choice of the utility function makes practical measurements of the GV-gap very noisy, as the authors themselves note. While several ways to improve the verification signal are proposed, this remains a weak point in the framework that is not present when directly measuring model performance before and after self-improvement. (R2)

- **Metric—performance correlation not established:**  
  The metric predicts the potential for self-improvement, but the paper does not analyze the correlation between the GV-gap and the *magnitude* of actual performance improvement after multiple self-improvement iterations. A more rigorous analysis linking the gap to the degree of improvement (not just its presence) would strengthen the empirical support. (R3)

- **Missing ablations and questions about experimental choices:**  
  – It is unclear why many hyperparameters in the lm-evaluation-harness setup affect the GV-gap rather than being determined solely by differences between the generator and verifier; the choice of parameters is not justified. (R1)  
  – Is the RL update illustrated in Example 2.1 actually adopted in the experiments? (R4)  
  – Is the “High-fidelity model update” condition from Section 2.1 examined or verified? What is its practical benefit? (R4)  
  – Will different decoding algorithms (e.g., search algorithms) for generation affect self-improvement and potentially alter the paper’s conclusions? (R4)  
  – The Sudoku experiments use 4x4 matrices; non-trivial gaps are observed only with models >70B. Can the authors sweep simpler configurations (3x3, 2x2) to see if smaller LLMs can self-improve? (R4)  
  – Can the mathematical framework provide insights about why diversity deteriorates with iterative rejection fine-tuning? (R4) If diversity deteriorates, does self-improvement become harmful, or is it a tradeoff? (R4)

- **Minority reviewer concern about novelty:**  
  One reviewer (R1) considers the contributions relatively weak, arguing that the main conclusions (e.g., verification gap scales with pre-training flops, saturation limit and cause) are already known and that the paper does not offer substantial new insights beyond empirical confirmation. Other reviewers (R2, R3, R4) find the framework and metric to be a meaningful contribution.

- **Questions about scaling-law discrepancies across verification methods:**  
  The scaling laws in Figure 1 appear to be severely influenced by the verification method; some methods (e.g., MC, To) do not exhibit the scaling law. The paper does not explain why this happens or whether other methods (e.g., CoT-B) share similar findings. (R3)
