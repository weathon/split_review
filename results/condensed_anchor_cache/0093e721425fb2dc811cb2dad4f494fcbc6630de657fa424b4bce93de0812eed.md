## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces OLMoE, a fully open, state-of-the-art sparse Mixture-of-Experts language model with 1B active parameters (7B total), pretrained on 5 trillion tokens and further adapted via DPO. The model Pareto-dominates many state-of-the-art models in the performance vs. active parameters space, outperforming dense models like Llama2-13B-Chat and MoE models like DeepSeekMoE-16B. The paper presents extensive experiments on MoE design choices (expert granularity, routing strategies) and provides novel analysis of routing behavior (router saturation, expert co-activation, domain/vocabulary specialization). All model weights, training data, code, and logs are open-sourced.

### Strengths
- **Clear and well-structured presentation** – writing is easy to follow; Table 1 clearly communicates key design choices early (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Fully open-source** – model weights, data, code, and logs are publicly available, making the work entirely reproducible and beneficial to the community (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Strong empirical results** – state-of-the-art performance for 1B active parameters, competitive with larger dense models (e.g., Llama2-13B-Chat) and partially open MoE models (e.g., Qwen, DeepSeek) as shown in Table 2 and Table 3 (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Thorough exploration of the MoE design space** – provides valuable guidance on building high-performance MoE models, covering expert granularity, routing strategies, and training choices (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Informative analysis of routing behavior** – novel insights into router saturation, expert co-activation, and domain/vocabulary specialization during training and inference, offering fresh perspectives and accelerating community understanding (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Important takeaways** for training good MoE LLMs, which are very helpful to the community (Reviewer 3 – rated the paper 10, confidence 5, notably more positive than the other reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Potential inaccuracy in Table 2** – OpenMOE’s activation parameter count in the original paper is 2.1B, but Table 2 shows 2.9B. Authors should verify the accuracy of these values (Reviewer 1).
- **Parameter configuration mismatch** – other state-of-the-art MoE models are not exactly in the same 1B/7B active/total parameter configuration, so an exact comparison to OLMoE’s performance is not possible (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty limited to design space exploration and routing analysis** – most design and training choices are based on prior work; the novelty lies primarily in the empirical exploration and analysis, not in fundamentally new components (Reviewer 2).
- **Model scale** – the model is still much smaller than state-of-the-art MoE LLMs, although the authors acknowledge resource constraints (Reviewer 3).
- **Unaddressed expert parallelism issue** – the model uses dropless MoE, which causes imbalance when using expert parallelism during training and inference. Without expert parallelism it is manageable at this scale, but for larger models expert parallelism would be required. The paper does not discuss how to handle token dropping in that scenario (Reviewer 3 – asked as a question; placed here as a missing analysis).
