## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers describe TSGM as a score-based generative model (SGM) for time-series synthesis, using an RNN encoder-decoder to transform data into a latent space where an SGM generates samples. The method is designed for both regular and irregular time-series. While reviewers acknowledge strong empirical performance (state-of-the-art on discriminative and predictive scores) and the handling of missing data, they raise serious concerns about lack of novelty, poor writing, missing details, and insufficient comparisons to existing diffusion-based time-series methods.

### Strengths
- The methodology and experiments are clearly written (Reviewer 1).
- Performance is obviously better than VAE and GAN baselines (Reviewer 1).
- Using an RNN encoder-decoder encapsulates temporal information without future leakage and robustly handles missing data (Reviewer 2).
- Combining an encoder-decoder with a score-based generative model achieves state-of-the-art discriminative and predictive scores, with a considerable increase in the discriminative task (Reviewer 2).
- The problem addressed is interesting and the architecture and concepts align well with the issues (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical assessment on benchmark data demonstrates promising potential (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed model is the first SGM-based method for synthesizing both regular and irregular time series (Reviewer 4; note: this is contested by Reviewer 2, see Weaknesses).
- The loss function for the score network on time-series data is novel (Reviewer 4).
- The model achieves state-of-the-art results on different settings (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear justification for the importance of time-series generation.** The motivation (incomplete/insufficient samples) does not adequately differentiate from time-series imputation (TimeGrad, CSDI) or show that generated samples improve learning quality/robustness (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty.** The method relies on two established models (autoencoder + conditional score-based) and is more evolutionary than revolutionary (Reviewer 3). Prior works (TimeGrad, CSDI) also use score-based/diffusion models for time series; the idea is similar even if task differs (imputation vs. generation) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 notes that Song et al. (Score-based SDE, 2021) show diffusion models are score-based, disproving claims of being the first SGM for time-series. The paper falsely states “there is no research using SGMs for this purpose” and dismisses diffusion-based forecasting/imputation methods without citing them (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons for forecasting and imputation** (conditional generation), which are more challenging and important (Reviewer 1). The paper only compares to unconditional generation baselines.
- **Poor writing and lack of crucial model details.** The paper is vague, with much text superfluous and key information missing (Reviewer 2). Examples:
  - The functions **f** and **g** in Equation (1) are never given; expressions for VE, VP, subVP are not defined when first mentioned (Reviewer 2).
  - No description of what the model actually looks like beyond “RNN encoder-decoder” and “score-based model” (Reviewer 2).
  - Theorems and corollaries are statements without proof or explanation; Corollary 3.2 is called “useless” (Reviewer 1) and the index of theorems/corollaries should be unified (Reviewer 1).
  - The text references many appendices that are missing (Reviewer 2). Without appendices, full review is difficult (Reviewer 2).
  - Much text is unnecessary (e.g., stating time series can be represented as a matrix) while essential details are omitted (Reviewer 2).
- **Loss function and procedure for irregular time series are not clearly written.** The loss function (Eq. 12) is not clearly explained, and how it relates to time series generation or solves computation issues is unclear (Reviewer 4). The procedure for generating irregular time series using continuous-time encoder is not clearly written; how irregular-sampled timestamps are generated in the sampling procedure is not explained (Reviewer 4). Which encoder is used for encoding irregular time series in the experiments is not stated (Reviewer 4).
- **Time complexity not discussed.** The time complexity of TSGM compared to baselines is not analyzed (Reviewer 4). The authors acknowledge high runtime but do not provide details (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient validation for irregular data.** The method is only validated on data that is regularly sampled but has missing measurements; it does not handle points at non-integer spacings of the regular sampling period (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of evidence for key claim.** The statement that **h_n** ~ **x_n** (first line under Figure 2) needs more evidence and explanation (Reviewer 2).
- **Technical typos.** In Eq. (5), \hat{x}_n is also an RNN so should have the same arguments as **h_n** (Reviewer 2). “Eq. equation 12” is a typo (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 8) is more positive** but still notes limited innovation and similarity to prior work (CSDI). This contrast should be kept: while one reviewer found the idea valuable and well-aligned, other reviewers strongly criticized novelty, writing, and missing details.

*Note: Many reviewer questions (e.g., about time complexity, diffusion procedure, Eq. (12) calculation, encoder choice, timestamp generation) overlap with criticisms of missing details and are included above.*
