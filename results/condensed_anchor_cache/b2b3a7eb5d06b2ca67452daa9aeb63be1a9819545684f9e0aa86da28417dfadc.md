## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SynFlowNet, a GFlowNet model that uses chemical reactions and buyable reactants to sequentially build new molecules with guaranteed synthetic pathways. It evaluates synthesizability with AiZynthFinder and shows improved diversity over REINVENT and SyntheMol. The authors propose a backward policy training strategy and connect to wet-lab fragment screening (SARS-CoV-2 protease inhibitors). Scores: 8,8,6,8. Reviewer 3 is more critical (score 6), mainly citing limited testing on optimization benchmarks.

### Strengths
- Focus on synthetic accessibility: explicitly incorporating forward synthesis as a constraint addresses a key limitation in generative models for drug design. (Reviewer 3)
- Diverse and optimized sampling: GFlowNet improves both diversity and high reward modes compared to reinforcement learning methods like REINVENT. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- Validation through synthetic accessibility metrics: in silico experiments show advantage over fragment-based approaches (FragGFN) in synthesizability. (Reviewer 3)
- Novel backward policy: proposed training strategy ensures backward-constructed trajectories belong to the MDP and facilitates synthesizable molecules. (Reviewers 2,4)
- Connection to wet-lab experiments: case study with fragment-based crystallography for SARS-CoV-2 demonstrates real-world applicability. (Reviewer 4)
- Comprehensive ablation studies on design choices (e.g., building block set size, backward policy variants). (Reviewers 2,4)
- Strong background and related work section; paper well-structured and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Reproducibility**: No code provided. Reviewer 1 deems this unacceptable for a 2024 CS conference submission and would raise score if code were made available. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of comparison to other synthesizability-constrained models**: The paper only compares to REINVENT and SyntheMol, but models with public codebases like MoleculeChef and SynNet (and the works of Gottipati, Horwood & Noutahi, Koziarski et al.) are not included. The novelty of the action embedding approach (using Morgan fingerprints) is already present in existing GFlowNet and synthesis-oriented work (Koziarski et al. 2024, Gottipati et al. 2020), and the authors should highlight novel differences or provide appropriate citations. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4 Issue 1)
- **Fairness of diversity/novelty metric**: The reported improvement in novelty (Reward/Novelty trade-off) may simply be due to using Enamine building blocks, while REINVENT is trained on ChEMBL. A fair comparison would require training REINVENT on Enamine REAL or using ChEMBL-like building blocks from retrosynthetic decomposition. (Reviewer 2, Q4; Reviewer 4, Issue 3)
- **Limited testing on established optimization benchmarks**: The evaluation uses only proxy models (sEH proxy) and does not include standard molecular optimization benchmarks (e.g., PMO tasks from Gao et al. 2022). Without comparisons to multiple generative baselines on such benchmarks, the true optimization performance is unclear. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 4, Issue 4)
- **Need for molecular rediscovery benchmarks**: Diversity is only meaningful if molecules are accessible. The authors should evaluate whether SynFlowNet can rediscover known synthesizable binders (e.g., celecoxib rediscovery) to complement AiZynthFinder predictions. (Reviewer 1)
- **Excessive oracle calls**: Training uses 300K oracle calls, whereas REINVENT shows good performance after 10K. Experiments with only 10K oracle calls are needed for fair comparison. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing experimental details for multi-objective optimization and ablation studies**:
  - Section 4.1: The rewarding strategy for multi-objective optimization (FragGFN SA, SynFlowNet SA, SynFlowNet QED) is not specified—is it MOGFN or multiplication-based rewards? (Reviewer 2, Q3; Reviewer 4, Issue 2)
  - Section 4.5 (scalability): Missing details—what reward function was used (sEH?), what "average times" refers to (runtime per batch or per molecule), and whether the entire building block set was used for Figure 7 D-F. (Reviewer 4, Issue 2)
- **Scalability concerns**: Increasing the number of building blocks slows training linearly and negatively impacts average reward and speed. To justify the use of larger BB sets, structural diversity metrics (unique Bemis-Murcko scaffolds, average pairwise Tanimoto distance) should be reported. (Reviewer 2, Weakness 2; Reviewer 4, Issue 5)
- **Backward policy effectiveness unclear**: The two learned backward policies (Maxlikelihood, REINFORCE) achieve high ratios of solved routes but do not increase the number of high reward modes compared to Uniform Policy. The paper needs to discuss this discrepancy and clarify which experiment demonstrates improved sample quality/diversity from the new backward policy. (Reviewer 2, Q1; Reviewer 1, Question "Which experiment?")
- **FragGFN performance questionable**: In Figure 4, FragGFN achieves lower rewards despite covering a larger chemical space (Figure 2). This may be due to insufficient training or poor generalization of the SeH proxy. This should be justified. (Reviewer 2, Q2)
- **Missing analysis**: No sample efficiency analysis, failure mode analysis, or evaluation on symmetric targets (e.g., molecules with Cn rotation axes, known weakness of SynNet). (Reviewer 1, Questions)
- **Minor issues**: 
  - Figure 6 last panel: unclear if points are shuffled; need clarification. (Reviewer 1)
  - Figure 1B: building block selection probability depends on reaction as well as state; figure should be revised. (Reviewer 4, Q1)
  - Dot product between Morgan fingerprint and state embedding may introduce bias in forward transition probability (example with 4-bit fingerprints). (Reviewer 4, Q1)
  - Page 7 footnote: "reactants" should likely be "reactions". (Reviewer 4, Q2)
  - Figure 7C: change title "Performance" to "Runtime". (Reviewer 4, Q3)
  - Table A.1: clarify meaning of max_nodes (apparent contradiction with heavy atom count). (Reviewer 4, Q4)
  - References poorly formatted; GSK3β capitalization error in Appendix. (Reviewer 1)

Note: The minority reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 6) is more critical, emphasizing the lack of testing against established molecular optimization benchmarks (PMO) as a key weakness, while acknowledging strengths in synthesizability focus and diversity. Others are more positive overall but raise substantial reproducibility, comparison, and experimental detail concerns.
