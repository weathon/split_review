## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Diff-II, a diffusion-based data augmentation method with three steps: category concept learning, inversion random circle interpolation, and two-stage denoising to balance faithfulness and diversity. Experiments on few-shot, long-tailed, and out-of-distribution classification show improvements over diffusion-based DA baselines (DA-Fusion, Diff-Mix). The paper is well-organized and easy to follow, but reviewers disagree on the method’s effectiveness and novelty (scores 3 and 6). Two reviewers found the method simple, effective, and comprehensive; two others considered the evaluation insufficient, the pipeline overly complex, and the analysis overclaimed.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, easy to understand and follow (R1, R2, R4).
- The method is simple and effective, providing a decent solution to generate faithful and diverse samples for few-shot classification (R4).
- Comprehensive experiments on multiple tasks (few-shot, long-tail, OOD) and clear performance improvements over SOTA diffusion-based methods like DA-Fusion and Diff-Mix (R3, R4). Decent improvement on few-shot fine-grained datasets (R1).
- Addresses a critical challenge in data augmentation, valuable for low-data and imbalanced scenarios (R3).
- Presents the theory for spherical interpolation in the appendix, strengthening soundness (R4).
- The problem is valuable and significant (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient and unrealistic evaluation**: No evaluation on large-scale datasets like ImageNet (R1, R2). Existing works already evaluate at ImageNet scale (e.g., “Synthetic Data from Diffusion Models Improves ImageNet Classification”; “Diversify, Don’t Fine-Tune”) and are not cited (R1). Only diffusion-based augmentation methods are compared; non-diffusion methods (e.g., Zhou et al., “Training on Thin Air”) are not considered (R4). The long-tail experiment does not reflect true tail classes—the tail classes are just randomly sampled from existing datasets (R1).
- **Overly complex pipeline and limited robustness**: The pipeline involves a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model, a caption model, and a large language model, reading like a technical report rather than a principled method (R2). The two-stage denoising uses suffixes summarized from VLMs to capture high-frequency context; this may overfit to validation/test sets and not generalize to real-world in-the-wild data, limiting applicability (R4). The method introduces notable computational demands, especially in high-dimensional datasets, raising scalability concerns (R3).
- **Overclaims on “analysis” of existing methods**: The paper claims to analyze current diffusion-based DA methods (DA-Fusion lacks diversity, Diff-Mix lacks faithfulness) but provides only textual explanation and a few visualizations in Figure 7, with no specific experiments demonstrating that those shortcomings stem from faithfulness or diversity. The conclusion is an assumption, not an analysis (R4). Moreover, given that simple prompt diversification achieves large-scale improvements (R1), the point of this complex tuning is questioned (R1).
- **Lacks sufficient ablation studies**: The effectiveness of each component (category concept learning, inversion interpolation, two-stage denoising) is not adequately separated and tested (R2).
- **Potential issues with fine-grained details and category embeddings**: Circle interpolation and reliance on accurately learned category embeddings may struggle to capture subtle fine-grained details in highly complex or visually similar categories, potentially reducing faithfulness or causing category divergence (R3).
- **Interpolation requires minimum data per category**: The method relies on interpolation between two samples, requiring at least two images per category. With extremely sparse data (e.g., only one sample per category), benefits diminish (R3). At imbalance factor IF=100, gain decreases because many categories have only one image; it is unclear whether augmentation is skipped or alternative strategies (e.g., DDIM inversion with different prompts) are used (R4).
- **Specific errors and clarification requests**:
  - Equation (4) appears to be incorrect (R2).
  - A detailed explanation of DDIM inversion is needed (R2).
  - How does interpolation between two DDIM inversions ensure the resulting latent remains in the same category? Does the text embedding play the primary role? (R2)
  - How does the method maintain both diversity and faithfulness for categories with highly variable context? (R3)
  - For generalization, using LLMs to generate diverse prompts instead of summarizing via VLMs from training data might be more robust; this alternative is not explored (R4).
  - Typos: Line 481 “Qutative” → “Qualitative”; Line 491 “our Diff-II show” → “Our Diff-II shows” (R4).
  - Are there planned extensions to tasks beyond classification (e.g., object detection, segmentation)? (R3)
