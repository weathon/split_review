## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a meta-learning framework (Meta-Dynamical SSM) for learning shared latent dynamics across heterogeneous neural recordings. It parameterizes dataset-specific variations via low-dimensional embeddings that modify the weight matrices of a state space model, enabling few-shot reconstruction and forecasting on synthetic dynamical systems and motor cortex recordings from arm reaching tasks. Reviewers generally found the approach novel and well-motivated, with strengths in addressing multi-session integration, rapid adaptation, and interpretability of dynamical embeddings. However, concerns were raised about missing benchmarks against existing models (e.g., LFADS, POYO, Schneider et al., Ye et al., Caro et al., Azabou et al.), reliance on large-scale pretraining and limited domain (only motor cortex), unclear handling of trial/condition structure, insufficient ablation of design choices, and some presentation issues. One reviewer (score 6) was more critical than the others (scores 8), particularly regarding missing comparisons and inaccuracies in related work statements.

### Strengths

- **Integration of multi-session neural recordings**: The framework effectively addresses the challenges of integrating heterogeneous recordings by using a low-dimensional dynamical embedding to capture variations across datasets. This is an important direction/application.
- **Learning shared dynamical structure**: The model learns a shared dynamical structure across diverse datasets, adapted based on each dataset’s specific characteristics.
- **Rapid adaptation to new data**: The method demonstrates good few-shot learning performance, rapidly adapting to new recordings with limited training data, which is valuable for costly neuroscience experiments.
- **Generalizability**: The framework shows promising generalization to held-out data from both synthetic and motor cortex recordings, leveraging the learned manifold of dynamical embeddings.
- **Well-motivated and well-written**: The introduction frames the paper well, and Section 2 provides a compelling example of limitations of shared latent dynamics with dataset-specific emissions.
- **Strong synthetic results**: The synthetic examples in Figures 2 and 3 form a cohesive story with Section 2, convincingly illustrating the problem and the model’s recovery performance.
- **Extensive motor cortex analysis**: Real data analysis covers two different arm reaching tasks, showing variability in dynamics across stimuli and tasks. Figure 7 demonstrates interpretability via interpolation in the embedding space across tasks.
- **Addresses a long-standing problem**: The approach tackles a key challenge in neural data analysis that will become more pressing as collection methods improve.
- **Proof-of-concept clarity**: Figures 2 and 3 are extremely helpful for introducing the problem.
- **Well-chosen real dataset**: The motor cortex data balances real-world heterogeneity with simplicity for exploring model performance.
- **Timely priority**: Methods for combining insights across datasets are a huge priority in neuroscience.
- **Builds on well-understood components**: The model proposes a sensible ansatz for how embeddings alter system dynamics.
- **Careful experimental exploration**: Experiments examine key variables including dataset size, look-ahead prediction, and number of training trials, clearly presented.

### Weaknesses

*Note: The following issues were raised across multiple reviewers; the first reviewer (score 6) was substantially more critical than the others.*

- **Missing benchmarks and comparisons** (all reviewers):  
  - No comparison against models the authors acknowledge as having similar goals: Ye et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2023, Caro et al. 2024, Azabou et al. 2024, and Schneider et al. 2023 (the latter also not cited).  
  - No comparison with LFADS on single-session data or POYO on multi-session data for the \(k\)-step ahead \(R^2\) metric.  
  - No empirical comparison to models that handle variability across datasets using indicator variables or inputs.  
  - Lack of theoretical connections: how does the SSM relate to known models (e.g., Driscoll et al. 2024, Cotler et al. 2023)? Can they be cast as special cases?  
  - The absence of direct comparisons makes it difficult to assess true performance.

- **Inaccurate statements in related work** (Reviewer 1):  
  - CEBRA (Schneider et al., 2023) does learn underlying dynamics, contrary to the claim that it “does not learn underlying dynamics.”  
  - CS-VAE (Yi et al., 2022) assumes shared dynamics and uses switching linear dynamical systems (SLDS) to discover them; citation should be updated to the eLife reviewed preprint.  
  - The paper conflates learning latent representations with learning the parameters of a dynamical system, and does not justify why the proposed method is the only way to do both.

- **Dependence on large-scale pretraining and limited domain** (Reviewer 1):  
  - Effectiveness depends on large-scale pretraining data; models trained on smaller datasets learn specialized solutions that may not generalize.  
  - Testing is limited to motor cortex settings; more complex settings such as vision decoding are needed.  
  - No information on runtime or what “rapid” adaptation means in practice.

- **Assumption of shared structure** (Reviewer 1):  
  - The framework assumes shared structure across tasks, which may not hold for very different brain regions or unrelated behaviors. The motor cortex data uses highly similar tasks across all subjects, limiting generalizability. Comment on modularity or alternative mechanisms is needed.

- **Accurate embedding inference** (Reviewer 1):  
  - Model generalization hinges on accurate inference of the dynamical embedding for new datasets. Performance under corrupted/noisy data and in other domains should be investigated.

- **Confusion about handling of trials and conditions** (Reviewer 3):  
  - Unclear whether a single dynamical embedding is learned per session or per session-condition. If per session, does this require concatenating data across all conditions?  
  - How are different conditions handled in notation \(y_{1:T}^{1:M}\)? Are test trials or test conditions left out in Section 5.2?  
  - Figure 5B is difficult to interpret and needs more annotations or a clearer caption.

- **Lack of results as a function of number of sessions** (Reviewer 3):  
  - The authors cite large-scale training as motivation, but only compare single-session models to an “all sessions” model. Performance on few-shot held-out sessions should be shown as a function of the number of training sessions.

- **Unclear design choices and incomplete ablation** (Reviewer 4):  
  - Does the choice of dynamical VAE matter? Bowman et al. inference vs. SMC-based autoencoders (e.g., [1,2,3])? What about the bidirectional RNNs for \(\mu_\alpha\) and \(\sigma^2_\alpha\)?  
  - No ablation of how much conclusions depend on these choices.

- **Intuition for low-rank embedding mechanism** (Reviewer 4):  
  - Experiments show low-rank embeddings outperform linear adapters or using embeddings as inputs, but intuition for why rank-1 updates avoid interference is terse.

- **Presentation issues** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4):  
  - Figures 3 and 4 lack details on which parameters/embedding values \(e^i\) are visualized and how they were generated; color schemes are unclear.  
  - Section 3.1 takes unnecessary turns: starts with \(\theta^i = \theta + \epsilon_i\), discards it for eq. (8), then returns to \(\theta^i = \theta + h(e^i)\). A more streamlined exposition from eq. (3) to eq. (8) would help.  
  - Figure 1 does not include \(\Omega\) and the inference networks, capturing only half the model.  
  - Figure 2 lacks a ground truth visualization, making it hard to judge what one should look for.  
  - Minor: L131 “linear function” should be “affine model” (the mean is affine). Eq. (5) \(\phi^i\) is undefined (learned independently per L205). L155: “inductive bias” is misused (structure is by construction, not external constraint). Eq. (14) and L347: \(d_r\) and \(r\) used interchangeably. L390 missing “Fig” reference. It would be helpful to show true dynamics in Figure 12.

- **Questions on specific model components** (Reviewer 2):  
  - How does the read-in network \(\Omega\) handle different data dimensionalities?  
  - A readout matrix \(C^i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,0.01)\) is highly contractive; what effect does that have, and does it induce degeneracy?  
  - Why did the linear adapter and Embedding-Input methods get worse as \(n_s\) increased in Table 1? Is the intuition similar to the high-M case in Section 2?
