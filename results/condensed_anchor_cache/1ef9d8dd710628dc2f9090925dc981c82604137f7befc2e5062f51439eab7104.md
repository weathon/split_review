## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Flash Inference, a method to accelerate exact autoregressive inference of long convolution sequence models (LCSMs) from quadratic to quasilinear \(O(L \log^2 L)\) time using relaxed polynomial interpolation and a tiling strategy that reduces memory movement and shares computation. It identifies key properties enabling this speedup and presents a general framework. A proof-of-concept implementation on Hyena achieves up to 1.6× end-to-end and 50× position-mixing speedup. The work is primarily algorithmic, with no experiments on trained models or downstream tasks.

### Strengths

- The interpolation perspective is original, inspiring, and clearly presented (Figure 1 is especially helpful). The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The algorithm solves the longstanding quadratic inference bottleneck for LCSMs like Hyena and SGConv. It is technically sound, with clear proofs, rigorous algorithm boxes, and careful complexity analysis for both computation and memory.
- The method is exact rather than approximate, maintaining model fidelity. A concrete implementation is provided and all variants and baselines (eager, lazy, Flash FFT, hybrid) are benchmarked empirically.
- Speedups are well documented: up to 1.6× end-to-end and 50× for the position-mixing component, with careful ablation studies of different \(\tau\) implementations and hyperparameters (\(B, M, D\)).
- The framework is not limited to LCSMs; it identifies general properties that could guide efficient architecture design in the future, e.g., for data-dependent or non-causal extensions.
- The paper addresses a fundamental issue (quadratic inference cost) and also focuses on reducing memory movement, making the work relevant for deployment.

### Weaknesses

- **Significance and positioning:** One reviewer (Reviewer 2) strongly questions the significance, arguing the problem does not need solving because performant LCSMs are empirically indistinguishable from efficient low-dimensional SSMs; the paper is only applicable to models that have better alternatives empirically. This reviewer notes that the paper perpetuates a false dichotomy between SSMs and LCSMs (SSMs are a class of LCSMs) and that all LCSMs have restricted expressivity tradeoffs, so there is no intrinsic expressivity advantage. They suggest the contribution is purely algorithmic and not appropriate for ICLR without anchoring to real models (e.g., showing a non-SSM LCSM that outperforms an SSM, or data-dependent extensions that improve performance).
- **Related work omissions:**  
  - Missing comparison to dilated convolution models (e.g., WaveNet, [1,2,3]) that support linear-time inference via cache. These are simpler alternatives and should be discussed.  
  - Limited discussion of implicit convolution (CKConv, FlexConv) and SSM lineages. The paper is heavily anchored on Hyena, but the algorithm is not Hyena-specific; any LCSM convolution (e.g., H3) could be substituted.
- **Presentation clarity and completeness:**  
  - The method remains too abstract; no stand-alone implementation is provided in the appendix. Algorithm 2 lacks step-by-step detail. A concrete example with data flow for a single layer is needed.  
  - The crucial \(\tau\) function is undefined until Sec 4.2 (loosely defined). Seven versions were tested but only four are mentioned, and the selection criteria across ranges/parameters are not specified. A rigorous mathematical definition and trade-off analysis are required.  
  - Notation could be improved (subscripts indicating position, feature, layer are confusing). Imprecise notation on line 159 (\(y \mapsto z\) should be \(y \to z\)). Algorithm 1 should reference equation (3).  
  - Asymptotic complexity expressions should consistently specify the base of the logarithm (\(\log_2\)).  
  - The introduction lacks a clear distinction between prefilling and autoregressive generation phases.  
  - A claim about “popular misconception” (line 109) regarding LTI realization theory lacks citations and appears factually incorrect in control theory.  
  - Typo on line 233 changes the meaning; needs fixing.
- **Experimental scope:**  
  - Experiments are limited to synthetic setups and Hyena; broader validation across models (e.g., SGConv, H3, other non-SSM LCSMs) and real-world tasks (language modeling, time-series) is missing.  
  - No trained model is used; only speed benchmarks.  
  - The impact of non-mixer runtime dominance (e.g., large MLPs) is not analyzed; when the MLP dominates, the 1.6× improvement may become marginal (see Fig 2(a) & 3(c)). In Fig 3(c), it is unclear why non-mixer runtime is smaller for Eager (NP) and Lazy (NP) than for Flash, and why Hybrid shows no mixer reduction compared to Flash FFT.  
  - Hyperparameter exploration (tile size, interpolation degree) is insufficient.
- **Data-dependent filters:** Efficiency relies on data-independent filters. Data-dependent filters may double computational cost (additional FFT). The paper acknowledges this but lacks detailed analysis of practical performance hits and adaptations; this limits applicability.
- **Causality assumption:** The method assumes autoregressive causality, restricting non-causal architectures (e.g., bidirectional). The paper mentions this but offers little exploration of possible adaptations.
- **Novelty relative to prior work:** Unclear how the proposed relaxed polynomial interpolation differs fundamentally from existing dynamic FFT techniques (van der Hoeven). A detailed comparison (algorithmic steps, memory access, parallelization) is needed.
- **Missing baselines/ablation:**  
  - No comparison of inference speed crossover with recurrent SSMs (e.g., at which state size does a recurrent SSM become slower than Flash Inference?).  
  - No comparison with efficient transformer variants.  
  - The trade-off between exact (growing cache) vs approximate inference (constant memory, amortized distillation cost) is not quantitatively compared for practical sequence lengths.  
  - No analysis of performance for very long sequences (L > 32K) beyond memory constraints.  
  - Extension to structured sparsity in filters is not explored.
- **Parallelization discussion:** The analysis of memory bandwidth implications for parallelization across layers is insufficient.
