## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Reflective Knowledge Distillation (RefKD) for Video Moment Retrieval (VMR) to address dependence on training data biases and improve generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The framework uses a dual-teacher structure (ID teacher and OOD teacher) with introspective ensemble and extrospective adjustment to distill knowledge into a student model. Evaluations on Charades-CD and ActivityNet-CD report improvements in both ID and OOD performance.

### Strengths
- Novel approach: Introduces RefKD using a dual-teacher framework for knowledge distillation in VMR, aiming to address dataset bias and improve generalization. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Detailed analysis on both ID and OOD scenarios to verify effectiveness. (R1)
- The method aligns with natural human learning and provides in-depth analysis of the rationale and limitations of label rectification. (R3)
- Interesting and novel idea tackling an important problem, achieving good results. (R4)
- Decent combination of methods (two teachers, label rectification, ensemble). (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Core bias mitigation not evidenced:** The dual-teacher framework does not solve the root cause of dataset bias; the effect is barely noticeable on the evaluated datasets. There is no clear evidence that 1) the proposed method addresses dataset bias, and 2) the reduced bias leads to better performance. The “temporal location-aware bias” mentioned in L053 is neither discussed again nor addressed. (R1, R2)
- **Marginal performance gain vs. complexity:** Performance improvements are modest considering the added complexity. The computational cost (e.g., training/inference overhead) is not discussed. (R1, R2)
- **Lack of up-to-date comparisons:** Table 1 compares only methods published up to 2022. A comparison with recent VMR methods (e.g., QVHighlights, UMT, Moment Detection in Long Tutorial Videos) is missing, reducing the relevance of the reported results. (R3)
- **Insufficient benchmark details:** The creation of Charades-CD and ActivityNet-CD is not well explained. It is unclear how the data splits differ from prior works and how prior methods’ numbers are reported on these modified splits. (R2, R4)
- **Limited/weak ablation studies:** Ablation studies (Table 3, Table 4) show that the proposed components do not improve performance much and provide no evidence of bias mitigation. The term “IntroD” in Table 4 is equated to “w/o both” but lacks explanation and a separate ablation study. Limited experiments on different backbones are presented. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Overstated and imprecise language:** The paper uses overstatements (e.g., “introduce a paradigm shift” – L090) and forced writing that should be toned down. (R2, R4)
- **Notation and formula issues:** Several symbols are not defined (e.g., capital P vs. bold P in Secs 3.2, 3.3; P^T in Eq.5; P^S in Eq.6). Eq.(1)–(2) refer to a “norm operator” but do not specify whether L1 or L2. Eq.(4) may be incorrect. Eqs.(8)–(10) could be written in a single row. “side curriculum” (L241) is introduced with no empirical study. (R2, R3)
- **Missing clarifications:** No description of what “span-based” student model means (Fig.2). The difference between video moment retrieval and moment detection is not addressed. It is not stated whether the dataset splits are newly created in this work or follow prior work; if newly created, how prior methods’ numbers are obtained is unclear. No testing with a truly OOD teacher is reported. (R2, R4)

*Minority contrast: Reviewers 3 and 4 gave a score of 5 but still raised substantial concerns about outdated comparisons, writing clarity, and missing ablations. Reviewers 1 and 2 (score 3) are more skeptical of the core contribution and evidence for bias mitigation.*
