## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel anchor-free contrastive learning (AFCL) method with the Similarity-Orthogonality (SimO) loss. This loss simultaneously minimizes Euclidean distance and orthogonality (squared dot product) between embeddings of similar inputs while maximizing these measures for dissimilar inputs. It aims to create class-specific, internally cohesive yet orthogonal neighborhoods, and the authors argue it mitigates dimensionality collapse. Validations are on CIFAR-10 using a ResNet-18, with visualizations (t-SNE) showing class-separated embeddings. The paper claims applicability in supervised and semi-supervised settings.

### Strengths
- The loss is simple, easy to implement, and the paper provides theoretical proofs (semi-metric property, mitigation of dimensionality collapse) and well-organized lemmas/theorems (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 6).
- Visualizations on CIFAR-10 clearly show semantic structure and class separation with potential to balance intra-class cohesion and inter-class orthogonality (Reviewers 1, 3, 6).
- The work addresses the important challenge of dimensionality collapse in representation learning (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed loss is a novel departure from existing methods that typically focus on either Euclidean distance or orthogonality, not both (Reviewers 5, 6). Reviewer 6 gave the highest score (5), while others gave 1–3, highlighting a contrast in perceived contribution and effectiveness—Reviewer 6 validated the embedding visualizations as evidence of clear semantic structure, but Reviewer 4 (score 1) considered the experimental evidence insufficient.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited empirical scope**: Only one dataset (CIFAR-10), one backbone (ResNet-18), and no baseline comparisons (e.g., cross-entropy, triplet loss, SimCLR, SupCon, BYOL, Barlow Twins, t-SimCNE). Results are a single line in one table; evaluation uses 1-epoch fine-tuning accuracy, which is not standard practice and makes comparison impossible (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 6). The absence of benchmarks on larger datasets like ImageNet or CIFAR-100, and no results under self-supervised or semi-supervised settings despite claims, severely limits evidence of broader applicability (Reviewers 1, 5, 6).
- **Achieved accuracy is low**: For CIFAR-10, the reported 1-epoch fine-tuning accuracy is not competitive (Reviewer 4).
- **Practicability of orthogonality maximization**: The orthogonality term is entangled with distance; in practice distance may dominate, so it is unclear whether the orthogonality objective is actually realized. No ablation or comparison confirms both terms are handled well (Reviewer 4). This also relates to the question: how are SimO embeddings different from those of other methods in practice? (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear justification for orthogonality**: For two classes the optimal solution is opposite, not orthogonal. When the number of classes exceeds the embedding dimension, full orthogonality is impossible. The paper does not justify why orthogonality (instead of opposite or other angular relationships) is desirable, nor how it operates under dimensional constraints (Reviewer 1).
- **Orthogonality measure**: The squared dot product is used to measure orthogonality, but it depends on both angle and norm. The paper does not clarify why normalized cosine similarity (unit vectors) was not used instead (Reviewer 1). The loss definition also lacks intuition and clarity—e.g., what “y is a binary label for the entire batch” means is unexplained (Reviewer 2).
- **Majority found presentation poor**: Reviewers 2 and 3 found the writing unengaging, the motivation missing, Section 3 a disconnected collection of definitions, Section 4.1 lacking intuition, mathematical notations unclean (e.g., Equation line 193 has extraneous “·e_j”), figure titles too small, and the experimental section hard to follow. In contrast, Reviewer 4 found the paper clear and easy to follow (minority positive view).
- **Missing citations**: The introduction omits key recent representation learning methods (SimSiam, BYOL, Barlow Twins, etc.) and relies on older references (Reviewer 2).
- **Specific unclear details**: Figure 2(a) and 2(b) titles include “1060000” which is unexplained (Reviewer 2). The only quantitative result (single table line) offers no ablation or convergence behavior (Reviewer 3). High computational requirements are mentioned but not quantified (Reviewer 5). The method is applied in supervised learning while contrastive losses are predominantly used in self-supervised learning, with no justification for this choice (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 6 notes that unsupervised methods like t-SimCNE (Bohm et al., 2023) already achieve interpretable cluster visualizations without labels, limiting SimO’s applicability.
