## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) can assist long-term action anticipation (LTA) from videos. The proposed AntGPT formulates LTA in two ways: a bottom-up approach that predicts future actions autoregressively from observed actions, and a top-down approach that infers the actor’s goal and plans the procedure accordingly. Visual features (CLIP) are extracted per video segment, action labels are predicted, and these are fed to an LLM. In-context learning and fine-tuning are used for goal inference and temporal dynamics modeling. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on Ego4D LTA v1/v2, EPIC-Kitchens-55, and EGTEA GAZE+. The LLM-based capabilities are also distilled into a compact network (1.3% of original size) with competitive performance.

### Strengths
- The research question—whether LLMs can help action anticipation—is interesting and timely.
- The formulation into bottom-up (direct prediction) and top-down (goal-conditioned) approaches is intuitive and well motivated.
- Experiments are thorough and informative: they compare multiple uses of LLMs (in-context learning, fine-tuning), include ablations, and evaluate on three major benchmarks (Ego4D LTA v1/v2, EPIC-Kitchens-55, EGTEA GAZE+).
- The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on all tested benchmarks.
- The distillation of LLM knowledge into a compact model (1.3% of original size) is a practical and well-demonstrated contribution.
- The limitations section is appreciated and adds nuance.
- The writing and presentation are generally clear (though one reviewer noted improvement room).

### Weaknesses
- The paper’s novelty is limited; AntGPT is a straightforward application of LLMs to the action anticipation task. One reviewer rated the contribution as fair (score 5).
- Performance gains in Table 1 are marginal, particularly on Ego4D LTA v1/v2. The improvement is not surprising given the introduction of additional goal labels and LLM knowledge; comparison with prior methods that do not use LLMs should be interpreted cautiously.
- The role of the visual modality is unclear. Table 2 shows that using action labels as input yields better performance than using visual features, raising the question whether LTA is primarily a language-understanding task rather than a vision task. More insights and experiments on multimodal interaction are needed.
- The reliance on predicted action labels from previous fragments risks error accumulation, and the paper lacks a systematic error analysis (when and why does the method fail?).
- In-context (few-shot) learning is used for the bottom-up approach, but the motivation is tied to LLM constraints (prompt length) rather than the task itself. The selection of few-shot examples is not explained; a discussion about using the full training set or supervised alternatives is missing.
- The goal definition is unclear: in Figure 2, goals sometimes appear to be repetitions of the last action label. The paper states goals are either from video metadata or “designed manually,” but the manual process is not described.
- Distillation results show the smaller model outperforming the original LLM (Table 5), yet no explanation is provided for why this happens.
- Several minor presentation and reporting issues:
  - Figure 1 text is too small to read, and the connections between its subfigures (b–e) are confusing.
  - The experiment section would benefit from placing “Comparison with State-of-the-Art” first.
  - Typo: “an few-shot” should be “a few-shot.”
  - The “visual procedure planning” paragraph in Related Work is unclear and does not give a definition.
  - The reference Mascaro et al. is missing the publication year.
  - In Table 1, a star (*) next to “oracle goals” is not explained in the caption or text.
  - In Table 5, the “from-scratch” setting is ambiguous: is the model randomly initialized or pretrained on some text corpus?
- Missing experiments that could strengthen the work: testing other ego-specific video-language models (e.g., EgoNLP, LaViLa, EgoNLPv2) and evaluating on EPIC-Kitchens-100 (a larger, denser, cleaner dataset) in addition to EPIC-Kitchens-55.
- Reviewer disagreement: One reviewer (score 8) found the paper informative and well-designed, while another (score 5) considered novelty limited and the improvement marginal. The majority were moderately positive (scores 6, 6).
