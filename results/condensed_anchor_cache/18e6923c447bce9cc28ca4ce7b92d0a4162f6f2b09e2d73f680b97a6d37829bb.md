## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper compares classic shallow graph embedding methods (e.g., DeepWalk) with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for node representation learning. It unifies the two approaches under a common framework and identifies two key differences: the learning prior (node attributes vs. random walk embeddings) and the neighborhood aggregation mechanism. Through experiments on ten real-world datasets, the authors observe that GNNs suffer dimensional collapse and performance drops when input features are limited, and that in heterophilic networks, neighborhood aggregation can hurt performance for heterophilic nodes. The paper provides a practitioner guide recommending GNNs for attribute-rich, homophilic, transductive, or large graphs, and shallow methods otherwise.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and meaningful topic: systematically comparing GNNs and shallow embedding methods for node representation learning.
- The paper is well-structured, clearly written, and easy to follow (reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- The experimental setting is clear and code is provided (reviewer 1).
- Extensive experiments are conducted across multiple datasets to analyze and compare performance (reviewer 2).
- The analysis linking GNN performance drops to attribute dimension and dimensional collapse in hidden space is meticulously elaborated and sound (reviewer 4).
- The paper provides a helpful guide for practitioners, clarifying respective strengths and weaknesses of GNNs and shallow methods (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and trivial conclusions (all reviewers).** One reviewer (score 3) found the novelty particularly weak, noting the findings (GNNs suffer from poor attributes and heterophily) are already identified in prior work (e.g., [1][2][3]). Other reviewers (score 5) also agree that most observations are straightforward, already known, or a consensus in the community.
- **Lack of theoretical analysis.** Only empirical results are provided; there is no deep explanation or theoretical justification for the observed phenomena (reviewer 1).
- **Incomplete baseline comparison.** Only DeepWalk is used among shallow methods; it is unclear whether DeepWalk is representative of all shallow methods or if it can outperform others on all datasets (reviewer 1). Several relevant GNN variants that combine walking-based approaches or separate topology and attributes (e.g., APPNP, LINKX) are not compared (reviewer 3). The paper could also discuss hybrid methods that combine network embedding and GNNs (reviewer 4).
- **Missing heterophilic datasets.** The experiments use only homophilic datasets; heterophilic datasets (e.g., from [3]) are absent, limiting the analysis of performance on heterophilic graphs (reviewer 1).
- **Guide for practitioners is not sufficiently specific.** For example, the recommendation to use GNNs for "attribute-rich" and shallow methods for "attribute-poor" networks is vague; it is not clear how to decide attribute richness. Flickr and PubMed both have 500 features but yield opposite results, so the number of features is not a reliable indicator (reviewer 2).
- **Questions and criticisms that overlap with missing analysis:**
  - The claim that GNNs are limited to transformed node attributes as prior is questionable, since GNNs can also take uniform initialization (reviewer 1).
  - Why do classic methods (DeepWalk) not exploit homophily properly? Under the homophily assumption, nodes close in the graph often have the same label, so classic methods should also benefit (reviewer 2).
  - The fact that removing features decreases GNN performance is very natural; can this be mitigated by augmenting features with structural or random features? Also, can small representation effective dimension be explained simply by the dimension of the initial feature set? (reviewer 2)
  - The concatenation experiment (Figure 6) is only on DBLPFull; are results consistent on other datasets? (reviewer 2)
  - Is it correct that GNN w/o Agg uses no graph structure? (reviewer 2)
  - Does APPNP (which uses personalized PageRank) suffer from similar dimensional collapse and feature-poor problems? (reviewer 3)
  - Does LINKX (which encodes topology and attributes separately) also suffer from dimensional collapse? (reviewer 3)
  - The results for E2E-GCN on CiteSeer and PubMed are lower than reported in previous papers; can the authors explain the experimental setting differences? (reviewer 4)
  - Since GNNs use non-linear activation functions, is it reasonable to measure dimensional collapse by the rank of the embedding matrix? (reviewer 4)
  - Is it possible to overcome the weaknesses of both approaches and propose a new paradigm combining their strengths? (reviewer 4)
- **Typos and formatting errors throughout** (reviewer 2 lists several: “output” → “outputs”, “There have been various of GNNs”, “methods preserves”, “respeectively”, footnote before punctuation, duplicate matrix definitions in Section 4.2 and appendix, etc.).
