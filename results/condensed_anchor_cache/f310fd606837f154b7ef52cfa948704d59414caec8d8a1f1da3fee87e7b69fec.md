## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Bound Implication Graph (BIG) to reduce branches in Branch-and-Bound (BaB) neural network verification. BIG captures neuron dependencies by using pre-computed linear bounds from LiRPA/α-CROWN to detect when splitting one ReLU implies stabilization of another. A two-stage check (Theorem 3.2) reduces computational cost. BIG is integrated into αβ-CROWN and evaluated on standard benchmarks and a new VeriHard dataset, showing consistent verification time reductions and solving more problems.

### Strengths
- Tackles the important problem of certified adversarial robustness and reducing the cost of BaB verification.
- First work to propose bound implications between arbitrary layers and among more than two neurons (Reviewers 1, 2).
- First to efficiently reduce branches by explicitly tracking neuron dependencies as active/inactive states (Reviewer 1).
- The two-stage check (Theorem 3.2) is elegant and significantly reduces the cost of finding bound implications (Reviewer 2).
- Combining (sub)BIG with established BaB-based methods consistently improves verification performance, sometimes significantly (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The new VeriHard benchmark (problems solvable only with long timeout) is valuable for future research (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Comprehensive ablation studies are provided (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4); technical details are clearly introduced.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison to GCP (Zhang et al. 2022), the most recent αβ-CROWN version.** This baseline is critical because GCP’s MILP constraints may capture similar neuron relationships. Without this comparison, it is unclear whether the improvements stem from bound implications or a more efficient implementation of the underlying αβ-CROWN framework. The paper should compare number of branches explored and certified accuracy with GCP, and explore combining BIG with GCP (Reviewer 2).
- **Incremental novelty relative to prior work [1, 2] (Anderson et al., Kouvaros & Lomuscio).** The paper presents several ideas as novel when they were first discussed in [1, 2]:
  - Implication constraints were introduced in [1] and shown to help verification times; the paper claims they were only “handled as constraints to a black-box MIP solver without validating their usefulness” (Reviewer 3).
  - Bound implication graphs were previously constructed in [2] and used to reduce branches; the paper presents the graph as novel without referencing [2].
  - The procedures in [1] are GPU-friendly and rely only on matrix operations; the paper claims the opposite. [2] also discussed incorporating implications into any verifier with a branching mechanism (Reviewer 3).
  - The paper does not clarify that implication constraints were first introduced in [1] (Reviewer 3).
- **No comparison with implication constraint identification procedures from [1].** It is not shown that the proposed method performs better. The procedures in [1] use concrete bounds and should be faster (Reviewer 3). [1, 2] show similar gains, so the marginal contribution is unclear (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Significance of improvements on established benchmarks is unclear.** Gains are often less than 1%. The paper needs analysis of which cases improve and which do not, and discussion of limitations (Reviewer 2).
- **General idea of removing sub-problems by recording case-split dependencies has been considered before** (e.g., Planet). The technical contribution is thus incremental and specific to αβ-CROWN (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Figure 2 y-axis scaling is unclear; a linear scale would be better (Reviewer 2).
  - Typos: “Mn-BaB” instead of “MN-BaB”; extra “the” in Section 2; broken sentence after Equation (3); “Activate and inactive” (Reviewer 2). “(inputs ≤ 0)” for the inactive case is incorrect (Reviewer 2).
  - Theorem 3.2 presentation could be improved: it is not initially clear that the two enumerated cases follow from Equation (7) or are additional constraints. The meaning of “linear bounds to not have an intersection with C” is unclear (Reviewer 2). An illustration would help (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experimental details and ablation:**
  - What are the actual perturbation bounds and dimensions on VeriHard? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does the number of implicating neurons K affect performance? (Reviewer 2)
  - Would BIG be applicable beyond αβ-CROWN to other verifiers like NNV and Marabou? (Reviewer 4)
  - Could bound implications be used as part of the split heuristic (e.g., adding scores of implicated neurons)? (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited scope:** Only certifying adversarial robustness on perception networks (Reviewer 4).
- More discussion on the bottleneck of the proposed method would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 1).
