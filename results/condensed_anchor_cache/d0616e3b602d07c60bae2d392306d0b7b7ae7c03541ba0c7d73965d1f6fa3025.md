## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose DOTA, a test-time adaptation method for vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) that continually estimates class distributions (mean and covariance) of test samples instead of storing a cache. Using Bayes’ theorem, posterior probabilities combine zero-shot CLIP predictions with distribution-based similarities. A human-in-the-loop component selects uncertain samples for annotation to further improve adaptation. The method is backpropagation-free and computationally cheap.

### Strengths
- Well written and easy to follow; figures are clear.
- Proposes online estimation of class distributions, revisiting nearest-class-mean principles from continual learning (Mensink et al., Bendale & Boult).
- The adaptive fusion of zero-shot text classifier outputs and distribution-based feature similarities is simple and intuitive.
- Backpropagation-free; light-weight and computationally cheap (Table 3 shows efficiency).
- Extensively tested against prior work; outperforms state-of-the-art (e.g., TDA) on most datasets across multiple backbones (Tables 1, 2). Fig. 3 shows performance advantage over TDA as number of test samples increases.
- First to formally define test-time adaptation with human feedback for VLMs.

### Weaknesses
- **Distribution estimation in single-image TTA**: The update procedure for mean and covariance with batch size 1 is unclear. The paper must clarify whether features are stored or an online update rule is used, and provide the exact equations. It also needs to specify whether per-class or global distributions are maintained.
- **Impact of uncertain samples on distribution**: Using all test samples (including low-confidence ones) to update statistics (Eq. 4) may degrade estimation. The paper should justify or introduce sample weighting/filtering criteria.
- **Human feedback evaluation protocol and baselines**: 
  - The evaluation must clarify whether human-annotated samples are included in the final accuracy. If they are (with ground-truth labels used), accuracy should be inflated by 5% or 15%; reported gains are only 1–2%, suggesting possible exclusion. All results should be on the full test set for fair comparison.
  - Stronger baselines needed: compare random, confidence, and entropy-based selection with the same annotation budget. Table 7 is missing random baseline, which is crucial for context.
  - The chosen feedback rates (5% and 15%) are high for practical deployment. Experiments with lower rates (1–2%) and justification of practicality are required.
  - The proposed active learning strategy shows modest gains (e.g., only ~0.6% gap over random feedback at 5% percentile; Table 6). Model calibration assumptions need discussion.
- **Limited comparison baselines for continual TTA**: TPT/DiffTPT work under episodic (reset) settings and are not directly comparable. Only TDA shares the continual setting. Adding DMN and other continual TTA baselines would strengthen results. Also consider practical TTA and universal TTA settings to account for stream dependencies and non-i.i.d. conditions.
- **Missing analysis of test-time forgetting**: The motivation highlights TDA’s catastrophic forgetting, but no experiment directly demonstrates this phenomenon. A qualitative/quantitative analysis of how DOTA mitigates forgetting is needed.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Hyperparameters (initial variance, shrinkage ε, λs, ρ) are tuned on a validation set, which is unavailable in realistic TTA. Sensitivity analysis across different data orders, batch sizes, and non-i.i.d. streams is missing. This is essential to evaluate robustness and practical deployability.
- **Similarity to existing methods (T3A, prototype-based approaches)**: The paper should clarify the difference from T3A (Test-Time Classifier Adjustment Module) and discuss related work on prototypical networks and online statistics (e.g., open-world recognition). The claim of applicability beyond CLIP (Footnote 1) is unsupported; experiments on SigLIP or other VLMs would strengthen it.
- **Marginal gain on ResNet-50**: On ImageNet-based datasets, DOTA’s average improvement over TDA is only 0.15% (Table 1). The reason for this small gain should be explained.
- **Ablation and visualization gaps**: 
  - No ablation study for the adaptive fusion probability (Eq. 10).
  - Uncertainty estimation uses a simple confidence metric; comparison with entropy-based methods or other uncertainty measures is needed.
  - More details on f_k(x) in Eq. (3) are needed.
  - No case study or visualization focusing on a specific domain/challenging dataset, nor illustration of human-in-the-loop adaptation.
- **Missing reference evaluations**: Table 4 reports results only for DOTA; including baselines (e.g., TDA) would provide context. Related work discussion is limited on TTA settings and continual learning / prototype methods.
