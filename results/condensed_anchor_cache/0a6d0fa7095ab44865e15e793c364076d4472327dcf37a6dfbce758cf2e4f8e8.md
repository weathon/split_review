## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Polar Accumulator Grid (PAGrid) and PagFormer, which integrate directed accumulation and grid sampling into a transformer for medical image segmentation of elliptical/oval objects. Experiments are conducted on ISIC2017, ISIC2018, and ACDC datasets. Reviewers acknowledged the interesting idea, clear writing, and novel encoder-slicer design, but raised concerns about missing baseline comparisons (including SOTA methods that outperform PagFormer), lack of evidence for fast convergence, potential test-set parameter tuning, small improvements without statistical significance, insufficient qualitative and complexity analysis, and questionable generalizability. Novelty was also questioned due to prior work on directed accumulation.

### Strengths
- Introduction of PAGrid and PagFormer, building on directed accumulation and grid sampling, is innovative and addresses geometric bias in transformers (R1, R2, R4).
- The accumulate-process-slice symmetric design simplifies architecture to an encoder-slicer, reducing information loss compared to polar sampling (R1, R2).
- Clear method description with appropriate formality; the paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2, R3, R4).
- Experiments on three public datasets (ISIC2017, ISIC2018, ACDC) with direct comparisons to several alternatives (R2, R4).
- CUDA parallelization enables efficient implementation and back-propagation for training (R3).
- Discussion of complexity and contrast between polar accumulator (PA) and polar sampling (PS) helps readers understand efficiency and performance implications (R1).

### Weaknesses
- The claim of tailoring to “elliptical or oval objects” is problematic: the method uses a radially symmetric transformation, not ellipse-specific. It is unclear whether all ACDC objects (left ventricle, right ventricle, myocardium) are well approximated as ellipses; illustrative examples with segmentations are needed (R2).
- Missing important baselines: For ACDC, Tragakis et al. (2023) “The fully convolutional transformer” reportedly outperforms PagFormer; for ISIC2018, Bozorgpour et al. (2023) “DermoSegDiff” outperforms. The authors should check current SOTA leaderboards (R3). Also missing comparisons with nnUNet, nnFormer, and VT-UNet (R4).
- A core component (Directed Accumulation) has been proposed in prior work (Zhang et al. 2023, “DeDA: Deep Directed Accumulator”) [3], which significantly weakens the novelty of this submission (R3).
- No evidence is provided for the claim (p2) that PagFormer “exhibits faster convergence” — convergence curves or timing comparisons are absent (R2).
- Parameter tuning (scale factor and number of intermediate feature maps, Fig. 5) appears to be performed using test set results to obtain the best Dice scores reported in Table 2. This should be done using a validation set (R2).
- Improvements over baselines are typically <1% Dice on only 20 test cases (ACDC); statistical significance (e.g., bootstrap confidence intervals) is needed (R2).
- Object localization (polar centre) is not described until Sec. 4.2; it should be presented earlier and in more detail in the Methods section. A separately trained SwinUnet was used to identify the left ventricle centre, which might adversely affect results for the right ventricle and myocardium (R2).
- The image quality assessment relies solely on PSNR; additional metrics (e.g., SSIM, user studies) could better capture perceptual quality (R1).
- No direct comparison of computational complexity or resource requirements with other methods (R1, R4).
- Experiments are limited to dermoscopy (ISIC) and cardiac MRI (ACDC); generalizability to other imaging modalities and varied conditions is not addressed (R1, R4).
- Qualitative visual comparisons of segmentation masks are missing; it is important to visualize results from different methods (R4).
- For reproducibility, the authors should provide their implementation and the hyperparameter settings used for competing methods (e.g., a GitHub link) (R2).
- Minor issues: incorrect citation style (equations should be referenced with parentheses, e.g., “Equation (2)”); grammar/typos; some figures need repositioning closer to where they are first mentioned in the text (R2).
