## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **GenRe**, a generative model for algorithmic recourse that jointly optimizes three core objectives: proximity (low cost), plausibility (realistic data distribution), and validity (desired classifier outcome). Unlike prior methods that train objectives separately and then use gradient-based search at inference, GenRe uses an autoregressive generative model trained with a unified objective that includes a cost function and data distribution, and then produces recourse via forward sampling. The authors synthesize training supervision from unpaired data and show that GenRe achieves improved trade-offs across multiple metrics compared to state-of-the-art baselines on synthetic and real-world datasets. Code is open-sourced.

### Strengths
- **Unified optimization of recourse objectives:** GenRe integrates proximity, plausibility, and validity into a single generative model, addressing a key limitation of prior work that optimizes these separately. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Forward sampling reduces inference cost:** By using sampling instead of gradient-based search, GenRe reduces computational cost during inference, improving scalability and speed. (R3)
- **Well-written and organized:** The paper is clearly written, with a clear problem definition and motivation, making the contribution easy to follow. (R2)
- **Thorough experimental analysis:** The method is evaluated on various tasks (synthetic and real-world datasets), demonstrating its effectiveness. (R2)
- **Open-source code:** The code is publicly available, aiding reproducibility and further research. (R1, R3)
- **Intuitive and easy to implement:** The method is conceptually straightforward. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and notation issues (severe for readability):** Many quantities are undefined or inconsistent, making the paper hard to follow. Specific problems: cost function in Eq. (2) is not defined; $N^+$ (Line 251) and $\lambda$ in Eq. (6) are undefined; $\mathbf{x}$ in Algorithm 1 Line 290 is undefined; in Algorithm 2, `projectCategoricals` and $m_c$ are undefined; the sampling of $k$ in Algorithm 2 Line 5 is unclear; bin scales vs. bin widths are inconsistent; $D_1$ vs. $N^+$ inconsistency; $\mathbf{x}$ in Algorithm 2 Step 1 is undefined; Eq. (7) and (8) appear to use different $K$; the choice of variance = 0 (Line 376) makes step 2 of Algorithm 2 redundant. (R1)
- **Missing justification for autoregressive architecture and limited ablation:** The choice of an autoregressive model over other generative architectures (VAE, diffusion, non-autoregressive) is not justified intuitively, experimentally, or theoretically. No ablations comparing different generative architectures are provided. (R1, R2)
- **Missing comparisons with general conditional generation methods:** The problem of cost-aware sampling from $P(x|y=1)\exp(-\lambda C(x,x'))$ is not novel; standard guidance techniques (e.g., classifier-free diffusion guidance, sampling with constraint satisfaction as in "Learning from Invalid Data") can be directly applied. The paper should compare with such baselines. (R2)
- **No clear advantage over nearest-neighbor search:** The necessity of a complex autoregressive model over a simple KNN baseline (e.g., finding the nearest valid sample) is not demonstrated. Missing analysis on: performance with varying dataset sizes (where generative advantage might diminish), impact of different distance metrics on KNN, computational cost/time complexity of training the generative model vs. KNN. (R4)
- **Interpretability concerns:** The generative approach may produce less interpretable outputs. The paper lacks quantification of interpretability (e.g., complexity of changes, number of features modified), user studies, or comparison with simpler, more interpretable methods to highlight trade-offs. (R4)
- **Potential robustness and generalization issues:** The synthetic supervision for training may introduce heuristics or assumptions. The model's success depends heavily on generative model quality and data quality (bias, limited data). Generalizability to diverse datasets or domains with different constraints is not studied. (R3)
- **Missing efficiency analysis:** No comparison of GPU/time requirements, number of parameters, or training/inference time against prior methods. (R1)
- **Unclear details on classifier and baselines:** The paper does not specify whether the classifier is a random forest or MLP (and when each is used), nor the classifier accuracy. How are final checkpoints selected for baselines? (R1)
- **Minor issues:** Duplicate reference entry (Lines 557-560); Algorithm 1 would benefit from step descriptions like Algorithm 2; proofs could be moved to the appendix. (R1)
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms:** 
  - Purpose of "TopK" in Algorithm 1 Line 290 is unexplained. (R1)
  - How are dimensions of $\mathbf{x}^-$ frozen? Are they dataset-dependent? (R1)
  - Is the generative model trained from scratch or with pretrained weights? (R1)
  - In Table 2, LOF definition has an upward arrow, but higher LOF typically means more abnormal – why? (R4)
