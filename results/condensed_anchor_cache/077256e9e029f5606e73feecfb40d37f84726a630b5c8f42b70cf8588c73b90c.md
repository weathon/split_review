## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents video-SALMONN 2, an audio-visual LLM for detailed video captioning, trained with multi-round DPO (mrDPO), LoRA proxies, and rebirth tuning. New metrics for missing information, hallucination, and repetition are introduced. Experimental validation is conducted primarily on a custom benchmark. Reviewer scores range from 3 to 6, with consensus on the promise of audio-visual integration and DPO-based training, but significant concerns about methodological clarity, evaluation scope, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- **Audio-visual integration:** Incorporating both visual and audio channels for video captioning is a significant and well-motivated direction (R1, R2, R4). The model moves beyond vision-only or audio-only approaches.
- **Novel training pipeline:** mrDPO with periodic reference model updates and LoRA merging, combined with rebirth tuning, is a creative and well-structured methodology (R1, R3, R4). R3 finds the pipeline “interesting and inspirational.”
- **Detailed evaluation metrics:** The introduction of missing-information rate, hallucination rate, and repetition rate for video captions is a valuable contribution (R1, R4). Dividing the evaluation into global and local parts is particularly beneficial (R4).
- **Ablation studies:** Good ablation experiments are provided for mrDPO iterations, LoRA proxy updates, and rebirth tuning, offering useful observations on DPO’s effects (R1, R3). R1 commends the “high-quality experimental validation.”
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is generally well-structured and explains complex techniques accessibly (R1). However, R2 and R4 strongly disagree on this point (see Weaknesses).
- **Competitive performance:** On the custom dataset and Video-MME short set, the model shows strong results compared to GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro, demonstrating practical effectiveness (R1, R2).
- **Attention to detailed captioning:** Focusing on generating detailed, accurate video descriptions (rather than just QA) is a worthwhile task (R4). R2 also calls the goal a “promising direction.”

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient methodological detail:**
  - Exact loss formulations for each training stage (audio alignment, SFT, DPO) are not provided (R2). The DPO loss variant and reward function should be explicitly stated (R2).
  - Caption sampling strategy for DPO (temperature, top-k/top-p, number of samples per video) is missing (R2).
  - Key terms are undefined or vague: “basic atomic events,” “information missing and hallucination rates,” “global captions” vs. “local captions,” and how “problematic patterns” are detected for rebirth tuning (R2).
  - The final model includes an additional single round of gDPO (line 407) not described in the method section (R2).
  - Training parameters and DPO loss curves are needed to demonstrate stability (R4).
- **Unclear advantages of mrDPO:**
  - The paper states mrDPO causes “repetitive and unnatural text patterns” (line 337), undermining its motivation (R2). This contradicts the later claim that the mrDPO model “excels at generating complete and accurate video descriptions” (line 349) (R2).
  - Quantitative evidence supporting mrDPO’s improvement over single-round DPO is weak: Fig. 4(a) shows direct DPO yields the lowest total error rate (R2). The claim that a dashed line indicates “unnatural text generation” is not substantiated (R2).
- **Lack of ablation on LoRA’s role:** LoRA is presented as a key contribution, but there is no ablation study demonstrating how it prevents catastrophic forgetting or performance drop (R4).
- **No component analysis of mrDPO:** The individual contributions of LoRA updates, regularization terms, and the multi-round structure are not analyzed (R1, R2).
- **Computational overhead not quantified:** GPU memory, runtime, and FLOPS per training round are missing. The point where mrDPO yields diminishing returns should be identified (R1).
- **Evaluation scope is too narrow:**
  - Results are almost entirely on a custom internal dataset; only one public benchmark (Video-MME short) is reported (R1, R2, R3). Standard video captioning benchmarks (MSR-VTT, VATEX) and additional video QA benchmarks (Video-MME medium/long, MVBench, VideoVista, MLVU) are needed (R2, R3).
  - Comparisons are limited to a few baselines; extensive benchmarking against state-of-the-art video LLMs is required (R2).
  - The rebirth tuning evaluation is limited; its effect on non-captioning tasks (e.g., video QA at each training stage) is not assessed (R1, R3).
- **Reliance on GPT-based evaluation:**
  - Using GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 to compute missing/hallucination rates introduces potential bias and reproducibility issues (R1, R2). Alternative self-contained metrics or correlation with human judgments should be provided (R1, R4). R4 specifically recommends correlating with human Elo (as in AuroraCap).
- **Benchmark details are sparse:** Caption length (word count, noun count), number of video sources, and other dataset statistics are not provided (R4).
- **Writing and figures need improvement:** R4 states the figures “severely hinder the proper understanding of the article.” R2 rates presentation as 1, noting the methods section is hard to follow.
- **Lack of qualitative analysis:** No examples of model outputs at different training stages (initial, after mrDPO, final) or of successful/failed audio-visual alignment are provided (R1, R2).
- **Novelty of audio inclusion questioned:** R2 notes that incorporating audio into VideoLLMs has been proposed in prior work (three references given), and the present model shows “no significant difference” in terms of audio inclusion. Note: R4 considers audio incorporation a “great idea,” indicating reviewer disagreement.
- **Absence of human-aligned metric validation:** The new automated metrics should be validated against human ratings to be convincing (R4).
- **Minority contrast on overall assessment:** R4 (score 6) is much more positive than R1 and R2 (both score 3), with R4 seeing the paper as having “great potential” if weaknesses are addressed. R2 finds the paper needs “large revision” and rates soundness 2, presentation 1, contribution 2.
