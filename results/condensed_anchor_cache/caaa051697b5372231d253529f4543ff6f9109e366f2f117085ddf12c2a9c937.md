## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Curriculum (DisCL), a method that leverages image guidance in diffusion models to generate a spectrum of synthetic images at varying levels of similarity to real data (controlled by the image guidance scale). A curriculum learning strategy then selects which difficulty level of synthetic data to use at each training stage, targeting long-tail classification and learning from low-quality data. Two of four reviewers rate the paper 5/5, citing the simple and effective idea and reasonable curriculum design; the other two rate it 3/5, questioning novelty, missing comparisons, and limited evaluation details.

### Strengths
- The idea of adjusting the image guidance scale to obtain a full spectrum of training data, from near-real to diverse synthetic, is considered interesting and novel by some reviewers (R1, R4).
- Combining curriculum learning with generative data augmentation, allowing the difficulty/quality of synthetic data to be adapted across training stages, provides a new perspective over prior work that uses a fixed guidance scale (R3).
- The method is simple and appears effective for both long-tail classification and low-quality image classification, with substantial improvement in tail-class accuracy (e.g., tail accuracy on ImageNet-LT from 4.4% to 23.64% and 4.02% all-class gain) and OOD/ID improvements on iWildCam (2.7% and 2.1%) (R1, R4).
- Experiments are generally well-designed, with good visualizations (R2).
- The use of a CLIPScorer to filter low-fidelity generated images addresses a potential shortcoming of generative data augmentation (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns regarding image-guidance**: Several reviewers note that using both image and text conditioning in diffusion models for data augmentation, and controlling the influence of the real image via the starting timestep, has been extensively studied (e.g., ICLR 2023 paper "Is Synthetic Data from Generative Models Ready for Image Recognition?" and SDEdit). The paper does not adequately differentiate itself from these works (R2, R3).
- **Missing comparisons with related works**: The evaluation lacks comparisons against methods that use synthetic data for long-tail classification (e.g., Hemmat-Askari et al. 2023, which also employs adaptive curriculum learning), representation learning with generated data (Tian et al. 2024a/b, Astolfi et al. 2023), and other generative augmentation strategies (e.g., fine-grained modifications, local sampling, hard negative generation). ALIA is mentioned as a related work and baseline in §3.1.2 but never appears in the results tables (R1, R3).
- **Experimental setup is unclear or incomplete**:
    - The choice of ResNet-10 (motivated by LDMLR) is inconsistent with main baselines (CUDA uses ResNet-32 on CIFAR-100, ResNet-50 on ImageNet). Reviewer questions if results hold with larger backbones (R1).
    - It is not stated whether baselines and DisCL are trained for the same number of iterations/epochs (R1).
    - To maintain a constant imbalance ratio, the authors undersample non-tail samples at each stage; the rationale for not rebalancing the dataset using synthetic data is not explained (R1).
    - In Table 2 (ImageNet-LT), combining DisCL with Balanced Softmax sometimes yields lower accuracy than CE+DisCL. No intuition is given for this inconsistency (R1).
    - Bolding in Table 2 appears inconsistent (R1).
    - Results for CE+Text-only Guidance and CE+All-Level Guidance are missing from Tables 2 and 3, making it hard to assess the marginal benefit of DisCL (R2).
    - The ablation in Table 1 shows only limited improvement of CE+DisCL over CE+Text-only Guidance (39.10% vs. 39.40%) and CE+All-Level Guidance (39.40%) on CIFAR100-LT; the few-class accuracy improvement with CE is not replicated with Balanced Softmax (R2, R3).
- **Method details are poorly presented**: The paper defers key details (e.g., generation of diverse textual prompts) to the appendix, and the adaptive curriculum for low-quality data is described only briefly in §4.2 without specifying its concrete implementation. Reviewers note that §3.2 (the core curriculum contribution) is too short, while earlier sections recite diffusion theory without novelty (R2, R3).
- **Terminology is confusing**: The terms "diverse to specific" and "easy to hard" are used for different tasks, but it is unclear if they refer to the same concept (R3).
- **Practical limitations**:
    - Generating a full spectrum of images for large datasets consumes a long time; the paper does not address this speed tradeoff (R4).
    - Using a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model without fine-tuning raises concerns about synthetic data quality for small-resolution datasets like CIFAR100-LT (resolution mismatch). It is unclear if the method applies to non-photograph domains (comics, drawings) (R4).
    - The CLIP-score threshold may filter out too many generated images for some classes, potentially preventing the generation of a full spectrum. The authors do not report the percentage of filtered images or strategies to ensure sufficient data per class (R4).
