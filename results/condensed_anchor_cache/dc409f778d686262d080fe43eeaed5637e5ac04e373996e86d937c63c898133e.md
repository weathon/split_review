## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles uncertainty in online surgical phase recognition caused by imbalanced phase distribution and low-quality image acquisition. The proposed MetaDiff combines a classifier-guided diffusion model (CDM) with a meta-weighted optimization algorithm (MOA). CDM generates multiple denoised trajectories per frame to enable uncertainty quantification (Prediction Interval Width, Paired Two-Sample t-Test), and MOA dynamically re-weights the loss to handle phase imbalance. Experiments on Cholec80, AutoLaparo, M2Cai16, and CATARACTS show accuracy improvements (e.g., 95.3% on Cholec80) over state-of-the-art methods. Reviewers find the work innovative and the evaluation extensive, but raise concerns about technical clarity, generalizability, missing comparisons, and unresolved details regarding how the diffusion model actually handles uncertainty.

### Strengths
- **First to identify and formalize the uncertainty problem in surgical phase recognition** (R3), going beyond prior work focused only on spatial-temporal representations.
- **Innovative combination of meta-learning with a classifier-guided diffusion model** to address uncertainty and class imbalance (R1, R2, R4).
- **Use of multiple denoised trajectories** to compute statistical confidence measures (PIW, PTST), enabling more reliable predictions in high-stakes settings (R2).
- **Meta-weighted objective** elegantly rebalances the loss across phases without increasing training time (R2, R3).
- **Extensive evaluation on four diverse surgical video benchmarks** (Cholec80, AutoLaparo, M2Cai16, CATARACTS) with consistent SOTA accuracy improvements (R1, R3, R4).
- **ConvNeXt+LSTM backbone** designed to avoid future information leakage, suitable for online real-time recognition (R2).
- **Methodologically rigorous framework**, with thorough background on generative models and uncertainty quantification (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of technical depth on the classifier-guided diffusion model.** The specific architecture of the classifier, how its output conditions the diffusion process during training and inference, and the impact of classifier bias on imbalanced data are not explained (R1). It remains unclear how CDM actually addresses uncertainty from image quality rather than simply aligning predictions with ground-truth labels (R3). The handling of ambiguous phase boundaries (where the classifier itself is uncertain) and the stability of the diffusion process during those transitions is not discussed (R4).
- **Dependence on ConvNeXt+LSTM pre-training.** The model’s performance may degrade if the pre-trained features do not generalize to new tasks or domains beyond surgical videos (R2). This reliance limits adaptability.
- **Limited evaluation across diverse surgical environments.** The four datasets cover similar laparoscopic/endoscopic contexts; robustness to robotic, open surgery, or highly variable visual conditions is untested (R2). Adaptation to other medical video tasks (e.g., surgical process planning) or datasets with different temporal structures is not explored (R1, R4).
- **Missing comparisons with recent SOTA methods.** Several relevant works are omitted: SurgPLAN++, SR-Mamba, SPRMamba (R2). Additionally, experiments on larger-scale fine-grained datasets (e.g., OphNet) are suggested (R4).
- **High dependency on accurate timestamp annotations.** Surgical video annotations are often noisy, and the model’s sensitivity to inaccurate timestamps or non-surgical frames (camera adjustments, no-procedure views) is not investigated (R2).
- **Potential bias from meta-dataset composition.** The composition of the meta-dataset used by MOA is not detailed; if dominated by certain phases, the learned weights could be biased, undermining performance on minority phases (R4).
- **Uncertainty metrics (PIW, PTST) trade-offs not analyzed.** No analysis of how the prediction interval width might lead to false negatives (overly wide) or false positives (overly narrow) (R4).
- **Multiple writing and presentation issues:** mathematical notation without variable definitions (line 38–39), inconsistent notation (e.g., *i* vs. i), grammar errors (“consisted of,” unclear phrasing like “modestly collected”), unclear Figure 2 (CDM/MOA process, inconsistent DM component), misuse of “frames” vs. “clips,” Table 3 mis-bolded best result on AutoLaparo, Table 6 mislabeled “DM” instead of “CDM” (R3).
- **Training time and memory overhead.** The added cost of MOA and CDM is not reported; one reviewer questions whether the method significantly increases computation (R3).

*Reviewer contrast:* While three reviewers rated the paper 6, reviewer 2 (score 5) expressed stronger concerns about the reliance on a specific pre-trained backbone, the limited diversity of surgical contexts tested, and the omission of several recent SOTA comparisons.
