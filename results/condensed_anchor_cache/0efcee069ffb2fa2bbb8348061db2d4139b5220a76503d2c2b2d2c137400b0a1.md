## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ENAMEL, a benchmark for evaluating the efficiency of LLM-generated code. It proposes a new metric eff@k that generalizes pass@k to efficiency, handling right-censored execution times via Rao–Blackwellization. The benchmark consists of 142 problems selected from HumanEval and HumanEval+ (excluding trivial O(1) problems), each paired with expert-designed reference solutions and rigorous test generators. The authors evaluate 30 LLMs and find that even top models achieve eff@1 around 0.47, far below their pass@1 scores, indicating a significant gap in generating efficient code.

### Strengths
- Clear and well-structured presentation; the metric design is rigorous and the evaluation is convincing (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The eff@k metric provides a theoretically sound treatment of right-censored execution times (Reviewer 1).
- Human expert-provided reference solutions and test cases set a high standard for efficiency evaluation; these hand-optimized implementations are valuable for future research (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Comprehensive evaluation across 30 LLMs offers insights into the efficiency limitations of current models (Reviewers 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty and justification of the eff@k metric are not fully convincing. One reviewer (rating 5) found the contribution not strong enough, noting that code efficiency measurement is well-studied in the compiler community (e.g., speedup metric) and a simple speedup ratio might suffice if properly defined; the paper lacks a detailed discussion of why such existing metrics are insufficient (Reviewer 2). Other reviewers also questioned the metric’s novelty: (a) the paper does not empirically demonstrate how often right-censoring occurs in practice to motivate the need for handling it (Reviewer 4, C1); (b) the choice of taking the maximum efficiency over k samples rather than the average is not justified—it is unclear why max is superior (Reviewers 1, 4); (c) the empirical effect of Rao–Blackwellization is not shown (Reviewer 1). The paper should compare eff@k with naive metrics (e.g., average efficiency, speedup) to show if model/problem rankings change (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The benchmark is limited to the HumanEval dataset, which contains relatively simple tasks. After filtering trivial O(1) problems, the remaining set (142 problems) may be too small for a comprehensive evaluation (Reviewers 3, 4). The method relies on manual expert effort, making it hard to scale to larger, more competitive datasets like Codeforces or CodeContests (Reviewers 3, 4). The paper does not provide the exact size and proportion of retained tasks (Reviewer 4).
- Lack of evaluation with prompts that explicitly ask for efficient code. Reviewer 1 notes that without such prompts, the benchmark may be too weak; a better prompt could shift model behavior and reduce the apparent gap.
- Missing guidance on hyperparameters of the eff@k metric (α and h). No sensitivity analysis is provided, and no automatic hyperparameter selection method is offered (Reviewer 4).
- The evaluation does not consider different test case generation strategies, which can affect efficiency measurements. The paper should assess robustness of eff@k against varying test case difficulty (Reviewer 4).
- Space complexity is not considered. Reviewer 1 provides a concrete example: for problem #40 (has_triplet_sum_zero), the reference solution uses O(n) space while an O(1) space two-pointer solution exists with the same time complexity. The benchmark could be extended to measure space efficiency as well.
- The paper dismisses existing efficiency benchmarks like EffiBench (1000 problems, SOTA efficiency solutions) as sporadic, without sufficient discussion of how ENAMEL differs or improves upon them (Reviewer 1). A more thorough comparison to prior work (including compiler community approaches) is needed (Reviewer 2).
