## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes WATER-GS, a plug-and-play watermarking method for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It uses a pre-trained HiDDeN decoder and treats the 3DGS generative module as a watermark encoder, fine-tuning color-related parameters (especially SH coefficients) to embed a 48-bit message. Novel 3D distortion layers are introduced to improve robustness. Evaluated on LLFF, Mip-NeRF360, and Tanks&Temples, the method reports up to 95% extraction accuracy and a 20% improvement in accuracy from distortion layers. The method is also applied to 3DGS compression frameworks and 2D Gaussian splatting.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and timely problem: protecting IP for 3DGS as it gains popularity (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Plug-and-play design is practical, requiring minimal modifications to existing 3DGS pipelines and adaptable to variants (compression frameworks, 2DGS) (Reviewers 2, 5).
- Introduces 3D distortion layers that enhance robustness against point cloud distortions, with up to 20% improvement in accuracy rate (Reviewers 3, 5).
- Comprehensive experimental setup: multiple datasets (LLFF, Mip-NeRF360, Tanks&Temples), extensive ablation studies, various distortion scenarios, and comparison with baselines (HiDDeN, MBRS) (Reviewers 2, 3, 6).
- The paper is clearly written and well-organized, with thorough quantitative and qualitative analysis (Reviewers 3, 4, 7). One reviewer (3) highlights the extensive experiments demonstrating practical effectiveness.
- The plug-and-play watermarking module is simple yet effective, and the ablation studies provide guidance for future work (Reviewer 6).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The methodology closely resembles NeRFProtector, WateRF, and CopyRNeRF, simply adapting them to 3DGS. There is no significant technical difference from these prior works (Reviewers 2, 5, 6, 7). The approach is “straightforward adaptation” and lacks innovation (Reviewer 7). One reviewer (6) notes the correlation with the nature of 3DGS is weak.
- **Missing comparisons with existing 3DGS watermarking methods**: The paper does not cite or compare against 3D-GSW, GaussianStego, or GeometrySticker, all of which address similar tasks (Reviewers 3, 4, 6, 7). In particular, 3D-GSW also claims to be the first 3DGS watermarking method (Reviewer 4). The paper should directly compare with WateRF applied to 3DGS (Reviewer 3) and with NeRFProtector (Reviewer 7).
- **Insufficient robustness evaluation**: 
  - Only a few attacks are reported (Tables 1, 3). Missing common 2D distortions such as JPEG compression, smoothing, clipping, and Gaussian noise at various levels (Reviewers 1, 7). Attacks should be shown as plots across severity levels (Reviewer 1).
  - Details of applied attacks are vague: “crop attacks” not specified which type; Gaussian noise level not given (Reviewer 1).
  - The method’s resilience to color transformation (e.g., converting SH to RGB) is not evaluated, though this operation would completely change color features and could break the watermark (Reviewer 4).
  - Resilience to reconstruction attacks (adversarial recreation of a new 3DGS from rendered views) is not tested (Reviewer 4).
  - No evaluation of security against sophisticated attacks (e.g., PGD adversarial perturbations, VAE encoding attacks) (Reviewer 5).
- **Performance concerns**: 
  - Bit error rate reaches ~9% even without attacks, and bit accuracy drops significantly under common attacks (Reviewer 6).
  - PSNR values in Tables 2, 3, and 4 are low, which could affect practical usability (Reviewer 7).
  - Fine-tuning overhead is high: 10K–30K iterations taking 0.5–1.5 hours, hindering deployment for frequent updates or real-time embedding (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of explanation for view generalization**: Intuitively, watermark embedding lacks underlying 3D physics; generalization from training to testing views is not well justified. Authors should report BER on both train and test views, show distribution across test views, and discuss vulnerability to random camera views (free viewpoint in a 3D viewer) (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 also notes the claim that watermarks can be extracted from any viewpoint is not supported by methodology or experiments.
- **Decoder architecture justification**: The choice of HiDDeN architecture and parameter settings is not motivated; ablation studies on architectural choices are missing (Reviewer 2). HiDDeN may be vulnerable to JPEG compression; more advanced decoders should be discussed (Reviewer 5).
- **Comparisons with HiDDeN and MBRS are weak**: These methods struggle to withstand 3DGS rendering degradation, making the comparison irrelevant (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited scene complexity**: Experiments are on relatively simple and common scenes; need tests on large-scale environments, dynamic scenes, and real-world applications to demonstrate scalability (Reviewer 2).
- **Unanswered questions that overlap with weaknesses** (require experiments/analysis):
  - How does point cloud reduction (e.g., Compact3D compression) affect watermark accuracy? What compression level is used? (Reviewer 1)
  - Is 48-bit the capacity upper bound? Can WATER-GS hide more bits with acceptable visual quality? (Reviewer 6)
  - Are the 3D distortion layers differentiable during training? (Reviewer 5)
  - How does the self-defined mask select target parameters? Is there a strategy to filter which 3D Gaussians to fine-tune? (Reviewer 5)
  - What is the extraction BER after an adversary trains a new 3DGS from rendered views? How to defend against denoising + reconstruction attacks? (Reviewer 4)
  - The paper treats 3D distortion layers for 2D watermarking – why are these considered 3D? (Reviewer 1)
