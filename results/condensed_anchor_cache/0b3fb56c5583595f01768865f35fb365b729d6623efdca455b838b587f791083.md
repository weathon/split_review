## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to improve generalization in multivariate time-series forecasting by integrating causal knowledge from a learned DAG. It introduces CAIFormer, which uses the Markov boundary (derived via PC or FCI causal discovery) to mask attention in a Transformer, thus restricting the hypothesis space. The paper includes a theoretical argument linking conditional independence to improved generalization and presents experiments on benchmark datasets. Reviewers agree the problem is interesting but diverge on execution: one reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) finds the theoretical analysis solid and the method well-motivated; the other three (scores 3) consider the causal integration superficial, the experiments insufficient, and the novelty limited.

### Strengths
- Interesting and relevant problem (R1).
- Some theoretical analysis on how conditional independence constraints narrow the hypothesis space (R1, R4).
- Simulation experiments included (R1).
- Generally clear writing (R1).
- Uses causal models and conditional independence to motivate feature selection for generalization (R2).
- Presents the CAIFormer architecture (R2).
- Ablation study testing subcomponents (R3).
- Good intuitive explanation of the idea (R3).
- The derived collider-based constraint is a novel angle for reducing hypothesis space (R4).
- Method is simple and could be extended to other causal discovery approaches (R4).
- **Minority review (R4):** Found the theoretical results solid and the motivation strong, contrasting with the majority view that the causal connection is not well established.

### Weaknesses
- **Causal framework for iid data, not time series:** The paper uses PC/FCI (designed for iid) without properly discussing time-series-specific causal discovery (e.g., Granger causality, PCMCI, tsFCI). Causal graphs in time series can be cyclic due to feedback loops; unwrapping with lagged variables is not addressed. Missing references and discussion (R1, R3 Q2, R2 Q5).
- **Limited and insufficient experiments:**
  - No error bars, confidence intervals, or uncertainty estimates; only results over 3 seeds is not enough (R1, R3 Q4b).
  - Model scale (parameter count) not reported; missing comparison of model sizes across baselines (R2, R3 Q4c).
  - Experimental results appear to be a subset of iTransformer results, raising concerns about rigor (R2).
  - Ablation differences are not significant; need per-prediction-length breakdown (R2).
  - No visual comparison of predicted vs. ground truth (R2).
  - It is unclear whether baselines were re-run under identical settings or only reported values were used; if re-run, training regime should match (R3 Q4a).
- **Theoretical and conceptual issues:**
  - Proposition 1 appears to conflict with the standard definition of colliders: when condition set is given, Y becomes independent of X_i, but the paper's derivation needs clarification (R2, R3 Q1).
  - The definition of “maximal linearly independent group” is not explained (R1 Q1).
  - Proof in Section 4.2 is not sufficiently detailed; several steps (e.g., Equation (4) derivation) are missing (R2, R4).
  - Notation conflicts (e.g., edge set vs. operator) (R4).
  - What is \(V\) in Section 4.2? Only \(V_c\) and \(V_s\) specified; what about other nodes? (R4)
  - Equation (7) uses an operator \(E\) without clear definition (R4).
  - Generalization error is not formally defined; unclear how Equation (10) benefits test performance (R4).
  - Several expectations lack specification of the underlying distribution (R4).
  - Motivation for choosing \(L^2(V)\) space is missing; need more background (R4).
- **Method novelty and practical value:**
  - The obtained Markov boundary is used rigidly as a mask; attention already learns which tokens to ignore — the paper does not bring new insight beyond variable selection at the correlation level (R2).
  - Markov boundaries are already known to be sufficient and necessary for prediction; the paper does not clarify what new theoretical contribution it adds (R3 Q3).
  - No consideration of autoregressive self-dependence: Markov boundary derived from DAG typically excludes the variable itself, but for forecasting lagged versions are crucial (R2).
- **Missing details and assumptions:**
  - Variable names in the discovered DAG are not given, reducing interpretability and trust (R2).
  - Unclear if instantaneous relationships are assumed; if not, PC should produce fully directed edges due to temporal ordering (R1 Q4).
  - Robustness to misspecified DAGs or Markov equivalence classes not studied (R3 Q2).
  - Assumptions about linearity between variables are unrealistic for many real-world systems (R3).
  - No discussion of scalability: causal discovery struggles with many variables, which limits applicability to high-dimensional transformers (R3).
  - Does the method assume no time-lag effects or that conditional distributions are invariant? Would it still help when the conditional distribution of \(v_c | (v_i, v_s)\) changes? (R4)
  - Can the method be applied to classification tasks? (R4)
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Figure 2 incorrectly uses addition rather than Hadamard product between DAG and Attention mask (R2).
  - Purpose of using both PC and FCI in the appendix is unclear; what do those experiments add? (R2, R3 Q4d)
  - Experiment settings need more detailed description (R2).
