## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces MGTST (Multi-scale and cross-channel Gated Time-Series Transformer), a new model for multivariate long-term time-series forecasting, comprising four key components: Parallel Multi-Scale Architecture (PMSA), Temporal Embedding with Representation Tokens (TERT), Cross-Channel Attention and Gated Mechanism (CCAGM), and Channel Grouping (CG). The authors claim a 1.5% to 41.9% improvement over state-of-the-art on seven benchmark datasets. All reviewers agree the paper is well-structured and addresses the issues of cross-channel dependencies and multi-scale patterns, but opinions diverge on novelty and depth of justification. One reviewer (score 3) finds the motivation too general and the modules a simple stacking; another (score 5) sees the combined approach as fruitful; the third (score 5) questions the novelty of the multi-scale and CLS token designs relative to prior work.

### Strengths

- **Addresses important issues**: The paper tackles inadequate modeling of cross-channel dependencies and multi-scale temporal patterns in multivariate long-term forecasting (R1, R2, R3). Combined cross-channel attention and multi-scale learning is a fruitful approach in the provided scenarios (R2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive comparisons with multiple state-of-the-art baselines and ablation studies across seven widely used datasets (R2, R3). The ablation studies help demonstrate the effect of each component (R2).
- **Clear structure and presentation**: The paper is well-structured, with clear motivation (R2, R3) and a detailed description of each component’s contribution (R2). The proposed MGTST model, with modules PMSA, TERT, CCAGM, and CG, is technically sound (R3).
- **Novel model design**: Introduction of the MGTST model with innovative modules (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient justification and novelty of module design**: The paper lacks clear explanation of the inductive biases and rationale behind choosing specific designs. For example, the advantage of gated attention over standard attention in time-series forecasting is not adequately discussed (R1). The parallel multi-scale structure and CLS token are not new: the multi-scale design in Crossformer uses Unet-style skip connections (a parallel design), and CARD (Xue et al., 2023) already uses a CLS token. The present designs are not significantly different (R1). The method seems like a simple stacking of modules; deeper analysis of the interplay between PMSA, TERT, and CCAGM is missing (R3). The claim that “inadequate modeling of cross-channel dependencies” is a limitation of prior methods is not fully convincing, as cross-dimension dependencies have been explored in (Zhang & Yan, 2022; Nie et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2022). The advantages over these existing methods are unclear (R3). Similarly, for multi-scale modeling, the advantages over prior work are not clear (R3). (Reviewer 2 finds motivation clear, but Reviewer 3 finds it too general and lacking specific depth.)

- **Numerical comparison concerns**: The reported superiority over PatchTST may be incomplete. In the Traffic dataset, PatchTST/64 (input length 512) achieves MSE scores of 0.360/0.379/0.392/0.432 for forecasting lengths 96/192/336/720, which are better than both MGTST-336 and MGTST-512. The authors should include PatchTST/64 experiments (R1). The 1.5% reduction in MSE claimed appears relatively minor given the substantial computational overhead of the parallel architecture (R2).

- **Missing architectural and reproducibility details**: Specific dimensions of embedding layers, number of attention heads, activation functions within transformer blocks, and positional encoding method are not explicitly stated (R2). The method for selecting the number of channel groups, group sizes, and grouping criteria is not discussed; a sensitivity analysis is needed (R2). Hyperparameter selection (e.g., group size varying across datasets) is unclear; was it chosen using a validation set? (R3). The paper lacks an explanation of how the channel grouping strategy is determined; after a random permutation of channels, different groups would result, and when group number = 1 (as used for 5 of 7 datasets), how does it differ from standard attention? (R1).

- **Figure clarity and notation issues**: Figures 2 and 3 lack sufficient detail. Explicit annotations, arrows indicating data flow, and labels distinguishing between TERT and CCAGM components are needed (R2). Notations in text do not align with figures (e.g., “D” undefined) (R2). The main conceptual diagram is too hastily drawn and occupies too much space (R3).

- **Limited evaluation scope**: The paper lacks experiments on complex, multivariate, multimodal datasets such as air quality (Song Chen, 2019) and interstate traffic (John Hogue, 2019) (R2). Given the parallel architecture overhead, its impact on prediction performance for large-scale multimodal datasets is not assessed (R2). How does MGTST compare with copula-based transformers (e.g., TACTiS; Drouin et al., 2022) for both long-term and short-term prediction? (R2)

- **Dataloader issue**: The sample code may ignore the last several test samples, a known issue from the original PatchTST paper (also noted in the TiDE paper). The authors should fix the dataloader and update results (R1).

- **Questions that double as criticisms or missing experiments**:
  - What is the specific advantage of the gated mechanism over standard attention, and how does it impact different time-series characteristics? (R1)
  - Does channel grouping depend on the order of channels? How stable are results under random permutation? (R1)
  - How was the hyperparameter for group size determined across datasets? (R3)
