## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MI-TUNE, a self-supervised fine-tuning approach for text-to-image model alignment. It uses point-wise mutual information (MI) between prompts and images, estimated from the denoising process, to select top-k aligned images for fine-tuning, requiring no additional datasets or auxiliary models. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on T2I-CompBench and in user studies.

### Strengths
- The idea is novel: first to introduce MI for T2I model alignment (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Self-supervised fine-tuning eliminates the need for human annotation or extra image datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- MI is simple, effective, and acts as a plug-and-play module for most T2I models (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and includes algorithm/pseudo code (Reviewers 3, 4).
- MI is shown to strongly agree with well-established metrics (BLIP-VQA, HPS) and human judgments, and achieves SOTA on these metrics (Reviewer 4).
- The central question “Is MI meaningful for alignment?” is compelling and opens a new direction (Reviewer 3).
- The fine-tuning approach demonstrates predictive power (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Comparison bias**: Baselines (A&E, SDG, SCG) are evaluated on SD1.4, while MI-TUNE uses SD 2.1, which is more powerful and may introduce evaluation bias (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Experiments focus on simple concepts (color, texture, shape) in T2I-CompBench and Figure 1, lacking validation on complex prompts involving spatial relationships, object counts, or compositional interactions (Reviewers 3, 4). One reviewer questioned how the 700 prompts for MI analysis were selected (Reviewer 3).
- **MI directionality limitation**: MI measures how much the prompt helps generation but cannot guide generation in the right direction when misalignment occurs (e.g., color mismatches) (Reviewer 2).
- **Metric choice ablation needed**: The paper does not compare against using SOTA VQA models as the selection metric, which could be more precise than MI (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalizability to non-DDPM models**: The method estimates MI via the score function in DDPM-style denoising; its applicability to rectified flow models (e.g., SD3, PixArt) or DiT-based models is unclear and unaddressed (Reviewer 4).
- **MI scores absent from comparison**: Despite being central, MI scores are not reported in tables or figures alongside other metrics (Reviewer 3).
- **Denoising steps not ablated**: The fixed choice of 50 inference steps is not justified, and no ablation studies test the robustness of MI-tuning with varying step counts (Reviewer 4).
- **Selection of representative images**: The rationale for picking the 1st, 25th, and 50th ranked images for visualization is unclear; a quantitative analysis of MI score distribution is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Out-of-distribution prompts**: The train and test splits come from the same dataset (T2I-CompBench), so results on out-of-distribution prompts are unknown (Reviewer 1).
- **Relation to classifier guidance**: The approach essentially uses MI as a loss for fine-tuning, which resembles classifier guidance; the paper does not discuss similar previous attempts (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and ablation organization**: Several ablations mentioned earlier are hard to locate in later sections; writing clarity could be improved (Reviewer 4).
