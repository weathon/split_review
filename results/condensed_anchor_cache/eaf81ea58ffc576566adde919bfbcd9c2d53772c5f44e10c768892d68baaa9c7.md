## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces AnyView, a method for few-shot personalized view transfer using DreamBooth and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). It aims to learn the concept of a view from a single image of a scene and transfer that concept to a novel object using a few shots, without explicit 3D priors. Experiments on the DTU dataset and natural images evaluate performance against baselines. Reviewers note both merits and weaknesses.

### Strengths
- Studies a new task: learning a view from a single image (R1).
- Provides evidence that diffusion models can learn and transfer high-level concepts like views, with broader implications for generative modeling (R1).
- Clearly articulated methodology with supporting diagrams, effectively motivating the architecture and addressing the "forgetting" problem in personalization models (R2).
- Notable performance improvements over comparable baselines; achieves competitive results against specialized novel view synthesis (NVS) methods despite a simpler approach with fewer input images. Comprehensive results reported across SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS (R2).
- Thorough analysis of design choices, including impact of background in training data and object complexity on performance (R2, R3).
- Originality in applying personalization and LoRA fine-tuning for view transfer tasks (R2).
- Novel approach defining "view" as a high-level concept, requiring only minimal 2D data and no 3D information (R3).
- Robust experimental setup with evaluations on DTU and benchmarks (R3).
- Method requires no prior 3D data (R1, R4).
- Few-shot: 3–4 examples for object, 1 example for view (R4).
- Showcases the importance of background features for view synthesis (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Weak scientific novelty**: The method is a simple combination of DreamBooth and LoRA (ZipLoRA), offering limited innovation (R1, R4). Two reviewers (R2, R3) found the approach more original (R2: "originality noteworthy", R3: "novel approach"), creating a contrast in valuation.
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: SSIM/LPIPS may not capture visual quality of view-transferred images; perceptual metrics or human studies are needed to assess fine details and view consistency (R2, R3). There is also a lack of quantitative metrics to evaluate view transfer itself, e.g., defining orientation/coordinates of both the view and the object (R1).
- **Dependence on background cues**: The model relies significantly on spatial cues from backgrounds (Sec. A.5), limiting adaptability to varying or abstract backgrounds; generalization to novel backgrounds remains unclear (R2, R3).
- **Poor qualitative results**: Figures are low-resolution and unclear (R1). Synthesized images in Fig. 5 and Fig. 12 have poor fidelity; the pig toy images in new Fig. 12 part 2 are obvious failures, indicating a lack of robustness for certain object geometries or textures (R1, R4).
- **Limited dataset and potential cherry-picking**: Results are presented only on the DTU dataset, which is insufficient to prove claims (R4). Competitor model Zero123’s transformation is manually estimated, raising cherry-picking concerns (R4). Most test objects are simple or celebrities where SDXL likely has strong priors; using unique objects would be more convincing (R1). Reference images of rare objects in the appendix already have diverse views similar to the target view, undermining the method’s effectiveness (R1).
- **View disentanglement not convincingly demonstrated**: The model may be learning the scene rather than the view; in top-camera-view outputs, the object always faces the camera while the reference object does not have that orientation. Background changes in synthesized images (likely due to LoRA) contradict the claim of view disentanglement (R4).
- **Complex multi-object scenes**: The method struggles with multiple objects or significant occlusions (acknowledged as a limitation). More experimentation on such cases is needed to validate efficacy and propose modifications (R1, R2, R3).
- **Lack of implementation details**: Insufficient detail on how training is performed and how quantitative metrics are computed (R4). The precise definition of "masking" in the evaluation methodology is unclear (R2).
- **View generation from a single image**: How a view is extracted from a single image is unclear, given views are inherently relative; the method likely relies on the semantic prior of a "canonical pose" from diffusion models. Viewpoint ambiguity is not addressed. Figure 7 shows inconsistencies, raising questions about its interpretation (R2). Would incorporating multiple images of the same view with different objects improve performance? (R2).
- **Failure cases not analyzed**: Specific failure cases and limitations beyond multi-object scenes are not identified (R2). Feasibility of extending the method to effectively handle multi-object scenes is not discussed (R2).
