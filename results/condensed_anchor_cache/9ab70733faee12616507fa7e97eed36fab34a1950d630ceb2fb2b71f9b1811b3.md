## Merged Review

### Summary

All three reviewers describe a framework (Ditto) that enables quantization-aware secure Transformer inference. The key contributions are (1) an MPC-friendly quantization-aware distillation that retrains the model to maintain utility after quantization, and (2) novel MPC primitives (Downcast and Upcast) that support the type conversions required for quantization. The evaluation is performed on Bert and GPT2 models and compares against MPCFormer and PUMA; reported speedups are 3.14–4.40× over MPCFormer and 1.44–2.35× over PUMA with negligible utility loss. Reviewers agree the problem is important, but have mixed opinions on novelty and depth.

### Strengths

*   **Important problem:** The paper addresses the critical challenge of efficient secure inference for Transformers, a widely used model family (Reviewer 2).
*   **MPC-friendly quantization-aware distillation:** The approach smoothly integrates quantization into the MPC pipeline and retrains to preserve accuracy (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
*   **Novel type conversion primitives:** The Downcast and Upcast protocols are a creative solution to the key difficulty of handling dynamic quantization in MPC (Reviewers 1, 2). One reviewer notes they effectively solve the dynamic-quantization bottleneck (Reviewer 3).
*   **Extensive evaluation:** The evaluation covers efficiency, utility, scalability, communication cost, and latency across varying sequence lengths and batch sizes, on Bert and GPT2 models (Reviewers 2, 3). The speedup numbers (3.14–4.40× over MPCFormer, 1.44–2.35× over PUMA) are highlighted as compelling (Reviewer 3).
*   **Bridging ML and MPC:** The framework integrates best practices from both communities (quantization-aware training, MPC-friendly approximations) to achieve end-to-end gains (Reviewer 3).
*   **Minority positive view:** One reviewer (score 6) is more positive than the other two (both score 5), praising the creativity of the protocol contributions (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

*   **Limited novelty of distillation and MPC protocols:** Reviewer 1 notes that distillation is already widely used in MPC-based secure inference. The same reviewer finds the MPC protocol contributions limited relative to prior work (e.g., SecureML truncation). The paper lacks a comparison to the latest related work, specifically Iron (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 asks for a theoretical or experimental comparison of Downcast/Upcast against existing SOTA.
*   **Utility drops from approximations:** Both Ditto and MPCFormer exhibit noticeable utility drops in Bert tasks when using ReLU approximation for Softmax. The quadratic (Quad) approximation for GeLU introduces approximation error that can be significant for certain tasks. A more detailed analysis of when the approximation is most detrimental and exploration of alternative approximation strategies is missing (Reviewer 3).
*   **Missing analysis of Downcast errors and guarantees:** Reviewer 1 questions whether the Downcast protocol has a probabilistic error and how it differs from SecureML’s truncation. For Upcast, the distribution of the random mask \(r\) and the assumption that the input is positive are not justified; the reviewer asks how positivity is ensured (Reviewer 1).
*   **Poor readability and insufficient explanation:** The paper is hard to read; the explanation of the type conversion primitives and their impact on communication overhead is unclear. There is a lack of detailed discussion of limitations and failure modes of the quantization-aware distillation procedure (Reviewer 3).
*   **Unexplored extension risks:** The limitations of using more aggressive quantization are not discussed—whether they would affect model utility or create security vulnerabilities (Reviewer 3’s question, which overlaps with a missing analysis).
