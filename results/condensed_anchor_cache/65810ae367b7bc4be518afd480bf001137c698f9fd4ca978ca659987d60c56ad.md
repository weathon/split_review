## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides a theoretical investigation into the capabilities of State Space Models (SSMs) combined with Feedforward Neural Networks (FNNs) for dynamic token selection. The authors prove that SSMs with FNNs can achieve performance comparable to Transformers on input copying, associative recall, and nonparametric regression tasks. The work is motivated by the observation that practical architectures combine SSMs with nonlinear layers, but prior theoretical work has focused on standalone SSM layers.

### Strengths
- **Timely and important topic:** SSMs are increasingly popular in language and vision applications; theoretical guarantees are crucial.
- **Clear and rigorous presentation:** Precise mathematical notations, well-structured definitions, comprehensive explanations, and solid proofs. The proofs for Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 appear correct.
- **Novel theoretical perspective:** Demonstrates that the combination of SSMs with nonlinear layers can simulate the dynamic token selection mechanism of Transformers, bridging a gap in understanding SSM potential.
- **Well-motivated:** Practical architectures combine SSMs with FNN layers, and the paper correctly raises this important question (page 2, line 77).
- **Multi-task analysis:** Evaluates across three fundamental tasks (input copying, associative recall, non-parametric regression), showing performance parity with Transformers.
- **Empirical validation noted by one reviewer:** The paper includes experiments on DNA base sequences, though other reviewers consider this insufficient (see weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient empirical validation:** The paper lacks sufficient experimental evidence for its theoretical claims. Reviewers request:
  - Experiments comparing (i) SSM, (ii) SSM+FNN, and (iii) Transformer on input copying and associative recall (R4).
  - Results on modern SSM variants (e.g., applying the improvements to Mamba) and ablation studies demonstrating the effectiveness of adding FNNs (R3).
  - Validation on LLM-scale settings beyond DNA base sequences (R3).
  - Experiments on Mamba's selective copying and induction heads tasks to strengthen connections to contemporary SSM research (R4).
  - The theoretical analysis does not address training challenges (e.g., vanishing gradients, instability), leaving open whether claimed capabilities translate to practice (R4).
- **Assumptions deviate from practical scenarios:**
  - The convolutional filter formed by SSMs is assumed to have a defined "window size," whereas in practice it is often as large as the sequence length (R1).
  - The specific SSM parameterization (Lines 151-152) is not commonly adopted in real-world applications (R1).
  - The analysis uses input-independent SSMs, which are outdated compared to selective SSMs (e.g., Mamba) (R2, R4). The comparison is also unfair: the paper compares SSM+FNN against only the self-attention component of Transformers, while the strength of a Transformer block lies in combining self-attention and FNN layers (R2).
  - The problem setting overlooks structural constraints on the state matrix A (e.g., diagonal or structured forms) that are essential for efficient SSM training and practical success. Without incorporating these constraints, theoretical claims may not translate to implementable architectures (R4).
- **Unjustified choice and excessive focus on non-parametric regression:** The connection between piecewise smooth functions and dynamic token selection is not clearly established. This analysis appears loosely related to the paper's core contribution, occupies disproportionate space, and would be more suitable as a separate study. The piecewise gamma-smooth function class lacks connection to real-world data distributions, making practical implications unclear (R1, R4).
- **Theoretical foundation inconsistent and limited novelty:**
  - The paper references results from [1] that analyze a single SSM layer (page 2, line 84), but the current analysis assumes two SSM layers with additional FNN layers (page 5, line 222). The superior performance of multi-layer SSM+FNN over single-layer standalone SSM is self-evident, so the theoretical analysis alone offers limited practical insight (R4).
  - The proof techniques and settings closely resemble those in [1] (Repeat after me). The paper does not clearly differentiate how SSMs differ from Transformers in solving comparable tasks with similar complexity. Lemma 5.1 appears disconnected from earlier proofs and fails to elucidate the underlying mechanism distinguishing SSMs from Transformers (R1).
  - A deeper comparison with [A] (which considers generalized SSMs including FNN as a subclass) is necessary, as [A] suggests GSSMs do not outperform Transformers on input copying, contrary to the current paper's perspective (R2).
- **Missing comparisons with other sequence models:** The paper does not discuss comparisons with RNNs or LSTMs, which have established performance characteristics and computational trade-offs (R3).
- **Readability concerns:** The paper is challenging to read due to dense technical details; it would benefit from figures or remarks after each theorem to illustrate reasoning and provide intuitive explanations (R2, R4 presentation score 2).
- **Increased model complexity:** The integration of SSMs and FNNs adds parameters and computational overhead (FNNs before and after the SSM layer), potentially impacting training and inference times. The performance gains may not justify this added cost (R3).
- **Specific minor points:**
  - The origin of the expression inside the big-O notation at Line 405 needs explanation (R2).
  - It is unclear whether the underlying network construction could be shared between Theorems 1 and 2 (R1).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R2, score 5) is notably more critical, raising concerns about the outdated nature of input-independent SSMs, unfair comparison with Transformers, and lack of discussion on selective SSM generalizations. Another reviewer (R4, confidence 4) emphasizes the absence of experiments and structural constraint issues, while another (R3) notes some experimental validation on DNA sequences but calls for more in LLM settings.
