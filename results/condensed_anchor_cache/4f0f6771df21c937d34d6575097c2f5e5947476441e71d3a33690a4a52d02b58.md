## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes the Graph Distributional Analytics (GDA) framework, which combines Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) graph kernels with distributional distance analysis to provide insights into structural characteristics that may affect GNN predictions, particularly for out-of-distribution (OOD) data. Reviewers generally acknowledge the intuitive nature and linear time complexity of the WL-based embedding, but the majority (four reviewers, score 3) raise major concerns about weak theoretical foundations, lack of baselines and rigorous evaluation, unclear notation and mathematical errors, and questionable utility as a true explainability tool. One reviewer (score 5) was notably more positive, praising the method’s clarity, scalability, and potential for large-scale datasets; this reviewer also noted the need for more theoretical analysis and prior knowledge dependence. No reviewer recommended acceptance.

### Strengths

- The tackled problem of enhancing GNN interpretability through graph distribution analysis is relevant and important.
- The WL embedding approach is intuitive and scalable, with linear time complexity that makes it suitable for large-scale graph datasets (R1, R2).
- The GDA framework provides a novel approach to OOD detection by characterizing graph distributions (R5).
- Experimental results demonstrate graph-level explainability and practical utility in identifying structural features linked to misclassifications (R3).
- The method uses both graph-level and node-level prediction, making it applicable to various types of graph data (R2).
- The writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical contribution is weak**: The method lacks sufficient mathematical proof or theoretical analysis, relying on heuristic insights without formal motivation (R2, R3, R4).
- **No comparison with existing explainability methods**: No baselines (e.g., GNNExplainer, GNN-LRP) are included, making it impossible to evaluate claimed advantages. The tool’s utility as a GNN explainability method is questionable; at best it indicates where WL-compliant GNNs may struggle (R1, R3).
- **Insufficient evaluation**: Only three datasets are used (ENZYMES, ogbg-ppa, MalNet-Tiny) with no graph counts in Table 1. The experiments provide limited examples, and the improvement over standard graph classification is modest. The method requires separate prior knowledge for each dataset (R2, R3, R5). OOD detection is claimed but not clearly demonstrated in experiments (R2).
- **Arbitrary design choices**: The score definition contains multiple arbitrary choices (e.g., κ in line 150, threshold of 3 in line 246, assumption of normal distribution in line 237) without justification. The range and scale of resulting scores are not indicative of potential issues, and conclusions rely on arbitrary thresholds or qualitative reasoning (R4).
- **Presentation and notation problems**: Notation is disorganized and mathematically imprecise. Specific issues: function τ in line 114 defined with dimension d that depends on input (invalid); line 129 “heterogeneity” and “cardinality of a vector” are unclear; set definition L in line 130 is confused with multiset; line 132–133 definitions of T and g are inappropriate; Eq 3 uses unexplained η(G)_j and nonstandard set–vector notation (R4). Missing figure reference in line 375 (R2, R3). Figures have black background making them hard to read (R3).
- **Limited scope**: The method is restricted to graph-level classification of unattributed graphs (no node/edge features). Extensions to attributed graphs or node-label tasks are not discussed (R4). Handling graphs without node labels is not explained (R2).
- **Computational complexity claim is incorrect**: The stated linear complexity is not justified for WL kernels; complexity grows with the number of iterations h and with higher-order WL, and the standard WL kernel has O(n²) complexity (R4, R5, citing Shervashidze et al. 2011).
- **Lack of reproducibility**: No code is provided, and experimental settings (number of layers, learning rate, other hyperparameters) are not specified (R3).
- **Unclear design decisions and missing explanations**:
  - Why choose WL kernel over GNN embeddings? How does the number of WL iterations affect representations and outcomes? (R5)
  - How is φ(G) for dimensionality reduction derived? Is it pre-defined or dataset-adaptive? (R5)
  - How are the unique labels L in Algorithm 1 derived? (R3)
  - Why does large kurtosis (→6.0) indicate substructures causing misclassifications, and how are those substructures identified? (R5)
  - How does GDA differ from existing dataset splitting methods like scaffold splitting? (R1)
  - What is the meaning of the initial node label when no true labels exist? (R2)
  - The explanatory perspective is essentially on data distribution rather than GNN structure; does this reduce to a data enhancement operation? (R2)
