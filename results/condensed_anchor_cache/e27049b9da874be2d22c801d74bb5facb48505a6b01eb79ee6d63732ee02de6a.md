## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FV-NeRV, a neural representation for free viewpoint video compression that encodes multiple viewpoint and frame indices into a compact network via overfitting, followed by pruning and quantization. It aims to reduce traffic and decoding delay in FVV delivery. Reviewers agree that the topic is interesting but raise major concerns: the method is essentially NeRV with a viewpoint index and lacks novelty; prior works (MV-HiNeRV, Zhu et al.) already address similar tasks; the claim of “free-viewpoint” is not substantiated (only predefined views are synthesized); evaluations use only two sequences with few views and no novel-view results; comparisons miss recent INR codecs (HiNeRV, HNeRV, ENeRV) and standard metrics (PSNR, LPIPS). There is significant disagreement: one reviewer (R5) gave a score of 5, highlighting fast decoding and competitive performance at small model size, while two gave 3 and two gave 1, citing weak novelty and validation.

### Strengths
- Addresses the under-explored problem of deep learning-based free-viewpoint video coding. (R2)
- Provides a systematic analysis of traditional 3D video compression techniques and neural representation. (R1)
- Presents good evaluation mechanisms that highlight important factors (traffic vs. decoding latency trade-off). (R1)
- Well-organized and easy to follow. (R3, R4)
- FV-NeRV encodes multi-view videos with a single model, achieving fast decoding (faster than 3D-ATM) and competitive image quality with a smaller model size; at similar decode time it outperforms S-NeRV in quality. (R5 – minority positive reviewer)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The architecture, loss function, and compression pipeline are nearly identical to NeRV; the only addition is a viewpoint index. Several prior works already apply similar multi-view neural representations and view indices (MV-HiNeRV, Kwan et al. 2024; Zhu et al. 2023). No notable architectural changes or novel components are introduced. (R2, R3, R4, R5 – R5 also notes this weakness despite the high score)
- **Not truly “free-viewpoint”**: The method only encodes and decodes a fixed set of input views; it does not demonstrate synthesis of novel, unseen viewpoints. Results for interpolated viewpoints (e.g., v=0.55) or extrapolated ones (v=-0.1, 1.1) are missing. Without such capability, the method is more accurately described as multi-view video compression. (R1, R5)
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: Only compared against NeRV, S-NeRV, P-NeRV, and 3D-ATM. Missing comparisons with more recent INR-based video codecs (ENeRV, HNeRV, HiNeRV) and state-of-the-art FVV codecs (MIV test model). Missing P-NeRV in Tab. 4; a comparison with smaller P-NeRV networks should be provided. (R2, R3, R4, R5)
- **Limited evaluation datasets**: Experiments use only two sequences (from the Fujii dataset) with very few views. Reviewers recommend evaluating on the full Fujii dataset, D-NeRF, MIV standard test sequences, and the Neural 3D Video dataset (CVPR 2022) to cover wider ranges of resolution, framerate, and number of views. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Incomplete evaluation metrics**: Only MS-SSIM is reported. Missing standard metrics such as PSNR, LPIPS, and rate-distortion curves. (R3, R4)
- **Questionable system assumption and unfair comparison**: The paper claims a “sender-side rendering” advantage but ignores the transmission of the compressed model from sender to user. Comparing against 3D-ATM (which uses texture/depth) is confusing because FV-NeRV only handles multi-view video, not geometry. (R1)
- **Lack of technical soundness**: No thorough rationale for the chosen MLP+CNN architecture; the paper does not motivate architectural decisions or ablate components. The possibility of using Transformers for sequence modeling is not explored. (R4)
- **Missing important related work**: Deep learning-based multi-view video compression and free-viewpoint synthesis methods (e.g., Neural 3D video synthesis, Neural residual radiance fields) are not cited or compared. (R4)
- **Limited viewpoint range**: Figure 2 does not show significant viewpoint changes across the scale [0,1]; it is unclear whether the method can support larger viewpoint ranges. Experimental evidence or an explanation of limitations is requested. (R5)
- **Unseen-view scenario not addressed**: The paper does not consider the practical use case where novel views are required on the user side, which is essential for a free-viewpoint video codec. (R2, R5)

*Note: Reviewer 5 (R5) gave a higher score (5) and emphasized the practical speed and model-size advantages, but still acknowledged the lack of novelty and incomplete experiments. This positive assessment is in the minority; the majority of reviewers found the contributions insufficient.*
