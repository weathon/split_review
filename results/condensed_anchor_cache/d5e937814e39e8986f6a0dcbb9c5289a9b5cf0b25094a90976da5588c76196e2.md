## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a high-fidelity and real-time 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) head avatar method that uses a compact tensorial representation: static color is stored in tri-planes, and dynamic texture details (opacity changes) are represented by lightweight 1D feature lines conditioned on expression blendshapes. The approach builds on GaussianAvatars by binding Gaussians to the FLAME mesh. A cluster-based uniform sampling strategy is introduced to handle dataset bias toward large expressions. Experiments demonstrate rendering quality (PSNR) and storage efficiency. One reviewer (score 6) finds the design simple yet effective and a good trade-off between quality, runtime, and storage. Two reviewers (score 5) are less convinced: they see limited improvement over baselines, question the necessity of dynamic textures, and note that the method fails to surpass GaussianAvatars and GBS in FPS or visual fidelity (e.g., facial wrinkles). All reviewers agree that the paper is missing limitations, comparisons with recent methods (FlashAvatar, Saito et al., etc.), and ablations needed to justify key design choices.

### Strengths

- Simple yet effective design that finds a good trade-off between quality, runtime (real-time), and storage. (R1)
- Separately designing dynamic geometric deformation (via FLAME) and appearance change (via dynamic textures) addresses a limitation of GaussianAvatars (coarse 3DMM deformations). (R2)
- Compact and efficient texture representation: tri-planes reduce parameters compared to spherical harmonics; 1D feature lines enable compact modeling of dynamic opacity details. (R1, R2, R3)
- Advances SoTA on high-quality generation of dynamic 3D head avatars with real-time capabilities and efficient storage, practical for VR/teleconferencing. (R1)
- Well-structured and well-written paper. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing limitations and ethical discussion.** No section addressing limitations or societal impact. (R1, R2)
- **Limited novelty and contribution.** Only improves RGB and opacity representation; overall technical contribution is narrow. (R3) *Contrast:* R1 finds the design simple yet effective, while R2 and R3 see insufficient novelty.
- **No clear performance improvement over baselines.**  
  - FPS does not surpass GaussianAvatars (GA) and GBS, contradicting the motivation of temporal/spatial efficiency. (R3)  
  - Visual results not significantly better: in Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 (and demo video), GBS shows more detailed facial wrinkles; the method shows identity preservation issues (e.g., no eye specularity, noise width variation suggesting an identity shift in Fig. 5). (R2, R3)  
  - Supplementary video does not demonstrate clear improvement over GaussianAvatars. (R2)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.**  
  - No comparison with NeRF-based methods (e.g., Neural Head Avatars from Monocular RGB Videos). (R3)  
  - Missing 3DGS-based methods: FlashAvatar (CVPR 2024), Saito et al. (Relightable Gaussian Codec Avatars, CVPR 2024), Teotia et al. (GaussianHeads, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024), Chen et al. (Implicit Neural Head Synthesis, CVPR 2023), Xiang et al. (FlashAvatar, CVPR 2024). (R1, R3)  
  - Should compare against Saito et al. and optionally Teotia et al. (both use 3DGS for head avatars, real-time, sparse multiview). (R1)
- **Unjustified design choices.**  
  - Dynamic texture necessity: authors do not explain why dynamic textures (e.g., blendshape-conditioned opacity) are needed—e.g., to handle occlusions of teeth, eyeballs, tongue. Clarification is recommended. (R2)  
  - Lower dimension for Txz and Tyz tri-planes is a strong assumption that may fail for intricate regions with topological changes (mouth interior). No ablation of higher vs. lower dimensions provided (quantitative and qualitative, including extreme viewpoints). (R1)  
  - Cluster-based uniform sampling: quantitative results (Table 2) show performance “on par or better” without it, so sampling is not worth the complexity. Need breakdown by facial region/viewpoint and visualizations of data distribution. (R1)  
  - Canonical space design may cause artifacts in dynamic areas like mouth interior (no novel viewpoint synthesis for extreme head poses shown). (R1)  
  - Loss function: teeth and hair normalization terms not ablated. Suggestion to add gradient loss (Sobel) or perceptual loss for sharper results. (R1)
- **Insufficient resolution and FPS analysis.**  
  - No results at 2K or 4K resolution; method’s capability at higher resolutions not demonstrated. (R1)  
  - Why does the method fail to achieve higher FPS? What factors limit speed? (R3)  
  - Could improve rendering speed and compare with more baselines. (R3)
- **Figure error.** In Fig. 2, the arrow between the 3DGS rasterizer and the opacity offset is reversed. (R1, R3)
- **Missing reproducibility details.** No high-level architecture diagram in supplementary for tri-plane+MLP and 1D feature lines+MLP. (R1)
- **No identity similarity verification.** Claims of “no identity shift” in Tables 1 and 2 should be supported by face recognition metrics (e.g., ArcFace). (R2)
- **Potential limitations not explored.**  
  - Torso and head rotations, fast facial expression activations. (R1)  
  - Mouth interior topological changes (zoomed-in views needed). (R1)
