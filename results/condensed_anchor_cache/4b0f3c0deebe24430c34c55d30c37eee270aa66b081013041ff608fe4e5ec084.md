## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the CIRCUIT dataset of 510 question‑answer pairs for evaluating LLMs on analog circuit reasoning, covering various topics and including diagram and netlist representations. Experiments with multiple LLMs (GPT‑4o, etc.) show best accuracy of 48.04% and a unit‑test pass rate of 27.45%, highlighting limitations in multi‑level reasoning about circuit topologies. The reviews acknowledge the dataset as a first step but raise concerns about its scope, realism, and connection to actual circuit design.

### Strengths
- First evaluation of LLMs in the circuit design domain; summarizes limitations and provides qualitative error analysis offering valuable insights (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Dataset construction is relatively comprehensive, spanning different difficulties and including both diagrams and netlists (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Evaluation results are extensive with detailed analysis of performance across models (Reviewer 1); experiments test several mainstream models (Reviewer 3).
- Paper is well organized and clear (Reviewer 2).
- The dataset, with over 500 question-answer pairs sourced from courses and textbooks, appears substantial and non‑trivial (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset scope and realism**  
  - The dataset size (510 pairs) may not be sufficient to fully evaluate LLM capabilities in analog circuit design (Reviewer 1).  
  - Most problems are collected from textbooks published more than 10 years ago, assessing general knowledge rather than modern, complex analog IC designs (Reviewer 2).  
  - The benchmark oversimplifies real‑world analog design challenges; it does not cover critical stages such as topology generation, device sizing, layout design, or process variations (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - The dataset lacks diversity in circuit topologies (e.g., current‑mode, switched‑capacitor, RF circuits) and does not include device parameters, device selection, test verification, or physical design (Reviewer 3).

- **Evaluation limited to question‑answering, not design capability**  
  - The evaluation is similar to math problems; it is unclear how accuracy on such questions translates to designing analog circuits (Reviewer 1).  
  - The benchmark cannot verify LLM ability to assist in design tasks like evaluating power, performance, and area (PPA), making design trade‑offs, or optimizing for specific performance targets (Reviewer 3).  
  - Only LLM‑only solutions are tested; it is not shown how combinations of models could use this benchmark (Reviewer 1).  
  - Technical contributions appear limited to prompt engineering (Reviewer 4).

- **Insufficient analysis and reporting**  
  - Categories are imbalanced (Figure 3: most Basic questions are level 1, RF questions are harder); detailed LLM performance breakdown by level and category is lacking (Reviewer 2).  
  - Only overall accuracy is reported; a breakdown per complexity level (e.g., levels 1, 3, 5) would yield more meaningful insights (Reviewer 4).  
  - Results seem unreasonable without this breakdown (Reviewer 4).  
  - The benchmark lacks evaluation of overall circuit performance metrics such as gain, bandwidth, noise, linearity, and power consumption (Reviewer 3).  
  - Insights are limited; the paper does not show how to improve LLMs for analog design automation (Reviewers 3, 4).

- **Technical and methodological concerns**  
  - How to differentiate errors caused by insufficient background knowledge from incorrect reasoning? Should knowledge errors be classified as Direction Errors? (Reviewer 2)  
  - Minor: In Table 4, how is error rate calculated? The sums in each column/row do not equal 1 (Reviewer 2).  
  - How does the benchmark help improve LLMs for EDA‑aided design? (Reviewer 3)  
  - How does the benchmark address different design stages of analog circuits? What is the success ratio per level (e.g., levels 1, 3, 5)? (Reviewer 4)
