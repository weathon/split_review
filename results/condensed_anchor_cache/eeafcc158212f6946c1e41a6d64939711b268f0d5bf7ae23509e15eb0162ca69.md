## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TransLLM, a framework for adapting English-centric chat LLMs to non-English languages without supervised data in the target language. It uses low-rank adaptation (LoRA), recovery knowledge distillation (KD), and translation chain-of-thought (TCOT). Training consists of three stages: target language pre-training, translation pre-training, and transfer fine-tuning with TCOT data, recovery KD data, and bi-direction translation data. Experiments are conducted on LLaMA-2-chat-7B (and variants Llama2/3/3.1) for Thai and four other languages (Arabic, Portuguese, Telugu, Turkish). On Thai benchmarks, TransLLM outperforms baselines including ChatGPT, Chat Vector, and translation-as-a-bridge, and on MT-bench and AdvBench it surpasses GPT-4. Human studies evaluate chat quality in target languages.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge of adapting English-centric chat LLMs to non-English languages, which is crucial for multilingual generalization (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The idea of using translation in chain-of-thought (TCOT) for target language transfer is intuitive and well-motivated; the overall training scheme is effective, outperforming all baselines except GPT-4 (Reviewer 1). Note: one reviewer found the method novel and well-motivated, while another considered it engineering-focused (Reviewer 2); there is disagreement on novelty.
- Ablation studies and analysis provide insights on detailed decisions, e.g., distilling answers from the LLM itself rather than GPT-4, the importance of target language pre-training, and effectiveness in preserving original knowledge (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Human studies evaluate resulting chat quality in target languages (Reviewer 1).
- Experimental results across multiple languages (Thai, Arabic, Portuguese, Telugu, Turkish) and multiple LLMs (Llama2/3/3.1) with solid ablation studies (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- The approach is considered engineering-focused without introducing substantial novelty or new methodology (Reviewer 2). One reviewer (Reviewer 1) found the idea well-motivated, so there is disagreement on novelty.
- The paper is poorly written with contextually unclear terminologies such as "chat LLMs," "Knowledge Distillation," and "chain-of-thought" (Reviewer 2). The term "target chat LLM" may cause confusion with "target language" (Reviewer 4). The "chain-of-thought" in TCOT data format is not sufficiently elaborated (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Evaluation is heavily focused on Thai, with limited results for other languages (Reviewer 2). Moreover, the benchmarks (AlpacaEval, xCOPA, AdvBench) are mainly obtained from translation; the model's reliance on English reasoning and translation training makes it likely to perform well on translated benchmarks, but it is unclear whether it would perform on non-translated benchmarks (Reviewer 3).
- The main baseline is GPT-4, but its multilingual capacity is not properly documented; a multilingual LLM baseline would be more appropriate (Reviewer 2).
- Missing baselines: (a) A baseline that directly uses multilingual supervised fine-tuning (MSFT) on google-translated SFT data; TCOT incurs higher inference overhead and must show clear improvement over this simpler approach (Reviewer 4). (b) A baseline that removes TCOT and only uses target language pre-training + fine-tuning on target QA pairs, with and without English QA pairs to preserve original knowledge, to decouple improvement from new data vs. the training scheme (Reviewer 1).
- Missing ablation: An ablation to demonstrate the necessity of maintaining original model parameters throughout the entire training process. Specifically, compare merging LoRA parameters after pre-training then applying LoRA in transfer fine-tuning vs. not merging until the end of two-stage training (Reviewer 4).
- Further missing analyses: (a) Whether the model maintains high performance in English after transformation (Reviewer 3). (b) Whether the method can be applied to transform a chat model to multiple languages simultaneously, and compare performance across languages (Reviewer 3). (c) Implementation details of the NLLB-bridge baseline are lacking: how NLLB-3B bridges between Llama2 and Thai, in which stage, and whether it uses TCOT or simply translates responses (Reviewer 4). (d) Experiments with other open-source LLM families (e.g., Mistral, Gemma) to demonstrate generalizability (Reviewer 4).
- The evaluation uses GPT-4 as a judge for comparison with GPT-4/ChatGPT; accuracy measures on datasets like xCOPA and example generations for qualitative error analysis would strengthen the results (Reviewer 3). (Note: Reviewer 1 mentions human studies, which partially addresses this, but the request for quantitative accuracy remains.)
