## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Episodic Novelty Through Temporal Distance (ETD), a method for exploration in Contextual MDPs (CMDPs) with sparse rewards. ETD uses temporal distance—estimated via contrastive learning—as a quasimetric for state similarity and computes intrinsic rewards based on the novelty of states within an episode. Experiments on discrete-action benchmarks (MiniGrid, Crafter, MiniWorld) show ETD+PPO outperforms state-of-the-art methods like DEIR, NovelD, E3B, EC, NGU, RND, and count-based exploration.

### Strengths
- Novel use of temporal distance as a (quasi)metric for state similarity to drive episodic intrinsic motivation (R1, R3).
- Extensive evaluations across multiple CMDP environments (MiniGrid, Crafter, MiniWorld) with thorough comparisons to many baseline methods (DEIR, NovelD, E3B, EC, NGU, RND, count-based) (R1, R3, R4).
- ETD+PPO demonstrates robust performance improvements, especially in challenging sparse-reward scenarios; ablations clearly show the method’s advantages and insights into why baselines fail (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper is well-structured, with clear explanations of motivation, methodology, and results; technical details are effectively conveyed through figures and toy-task illustrations (R1, R2).
- Ablation studies and analyses of different technical components provide valuable insights for readers (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **MDP assumption violation** (R2): The intrinsic bonus \(b_t\) depends on the history of visited states within the episode, making the total reward non-Markovian. Since actions are selected by \(\pi(a_t|s_t)\) without knowledge of visitation history, the same action in the same state can yield different rewards, leading to potential instability and suboptimal learning. The paper does not address this fundamental technical issue or compare how other reward-bonus methods handle it.
2. **Limited algorithmic novelty** (R4 – minority critical view): The core components (discounted state occupancy measure, successor distance, symmetrized InfoNCE loss, energy function \(f_{\theta}(x,y) = c_{\psi}(y) - d_{\phi}(x,y)\)) are directly borrowed from "Learning Temporal Distances: Contrastive Successor Features Can Provide a Metric Structure for Decision-Making." The paper’s contribution is therefore incremental.
3. **No inclusion of extrinsic rewards in similarity metric** (R1): ETD ignores reward signals entirely when computing similarity. States that are functionally very different (in terms of task progress) may be treated as similar if they are temporally close, and vice versa. This is a significant limitation, especially when sparse rewards carry critical information.
4. **Limited to discrete action spaces** (R1, R4): Experiments are only on discrete control tasks (MiniGrid, Crafter, MiniWorld). Continuous action domains (e.g., MuJoCo, DeepMind Control Suite, Fetch, Meta-World) are not tested, limiting generalizability to real-world robotics tasks.
5. **Unclear exposition in lines 186–208** (R2): The derivation of successor distance, sampling from the discounted state occupancy measure, the motivation for the energy function decomposition into potential and quasimetric nets, and the rationale for the InfoNCE loss are difficult to follow and require additional intuition and clarification.
6. **Missing code** (R4): No public code is provided, preventing independent verification of results.
7. **Minor issues** (R2, R3):
   - The SpiralMaze example in Figure 2 has an essentially linear structure; a more complex environment with loops would better demonstrate ETD’s distance-learning advantages.
   - No guidance on choosing the hyperparameter \(\beta\) (intrinsic reward weight); the paper should provide heuristics or best practices.
   - Line 188 is missing a \(ds\) in the integral.
   - A more detailed discussion on successor distance assumptions: what happens when the MDP does not allow loops (e.g., real dynamics with velocities)? Would the distance become infinite? (R3)
   - Why are separate networks needed for the potential and the asymmetric distance? Could the potential be removed? An ablation would be helpful. (R3)
   - Why is the InfoNCE loss symmetrized? Does this assume transitions are invertible? An ablation and further discussion are needed. (R3)
8. **Missing experiments/ablation requests** (R1, R3, R4):
   - Why was PPO (on-policy) chosen over off-policy algorithms like DQN or SAC, which are common in sparse-reward tasks? Could ETD be extended to continuous actions, and if so, were any experiments run? (R1)
   - Could temporal distance be combined with reward-based similarity (e.g., weighting by extrinsic rewards)? What are the potential trade-offs? (R1)
   - Test ETD on continuous control tasks (e.g., Meta-World “Push,” “Reach,” “Pick-Place”; MuJoCo “Cheetah-Vel-Sparse”) to validate performance in high-dimensional continuous spaces. (R4)
