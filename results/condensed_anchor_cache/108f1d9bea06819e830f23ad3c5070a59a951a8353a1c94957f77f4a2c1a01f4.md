## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents NeuralWalker, which captures long-range dependencies in graphs by sampling random walks, treating them as sequences, encoding them with a sequence model (e.g., SSM), and interleaving this with local message passing and global attention. Theoretical analysis shows NeuralWalker is more expressive than 1-WL and CNN-based random walk models. Experiments on 19 benchmarks show strong performance, and pretraining on random walk predictions is explored. The approach aims to combine the strengths of random walks (efficiency, long-range capability) with modern sequence models.

### Strengths
- **Addresses an important problem** – long-range dependencies in GNNs (R1, R2, R3).
- **Interesting design choice** – integrates random walks directly into tokens (rather than only as positional encodings), a novel use of SSMs for this purpose (R3, R4).
- **Versatile framework** – can be combined with various GNN, GT, and sequence models (R1, R3).
- **Thorough theoretical analysis** – proves expressiveness relative to 1-WL and CRaWl (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Comprehensive experiments** – multiple benchmarks with detailed results in main text and appendix (R3, R4).
- **Novel pretraining idea** – predicting structural encodings of random walks (R4).
- **Actionable reproducibility details** – training details reported (R2).
- **Scalability** – experiments on graphs with over 1M vertices (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** – combines well‐known techniques (random walks, local/global mixing, SSMs). Graph-Mamba (Behrouz et al., KDD 2024) also uses random walks with Mamba on GNNs, and graph transformers already combine global and local information (R1, R3). When the number of sampled walks per node is 1, NeuralWalker is very similar to Graph-Mamba; differences need clarification (R2).
- **Unclear source of performance gains** – ablation in Table 6 shows that removing local and global message passing causes a significant performance drop, raising the question of whether random walks themselves are the key contributor (R2, R3). An ablation study isolating the contributions of pretraining, positional encodings, and the sequence model is needed (R1). The motivation for including global message passing is also unclear when long walks already capture global information (R3).
- **Missing baselines and incomplete comparisons** – Several recent methods are omitted: GSSC (Huang et al., 2024), GOAT (Kong et al., ICML 2023), GRED (Ding et al., ICML 2024), VCR-Graphormer, LazyGNN, and notably Graph-Mamba (R2, R3). Also missing is a comparison with random walk–based GNNs like GraphSAGE (R2). Polynormer results are reported only in Table 5 but missing from LRGB and other experiments (R2). The claim of “significant improvements on 19 datasets” is unsupported because NeuralWalker does not outperform baselines on all datasets and several SOTA results are absent (R2).
- **Lack of efficiency evaluation** – The paper motivates efficiency but provides no comparison of running time or memory usage against baselines such as Polynormer or graph transformers (R2, R3). The claim that CNNs are 2–3× faster than Mamba on A100 is unsubstantiated (R2).
- **Insufficient dataset scale for node classification** – Node classification benchmarks are mainly heterophilic and small; commonly used large-scale datasets like ogbn-products and ogbn-papers100m are not included (R1, R3).
- **Missing or weak theoretical justification for sequence model choice** – No discussion of why SSMs outperform Transformers (R4) or why classical RNNs (which have superior circuit complexity for unbounded sequences) were not considered (R4). The paper does not discuss whether bidirectional RNNs were tried (R3). Theoretical results require assuming the sequence model is a universal approximator – without this, how is the expressiveness comparison to 1-WL valid? (R2)
- **Unclear design choices** – The walk aggregator uses average pooling without justification (R3). The purpose of integrating random walks into token design (vs. using them as GT positional encodings) is not motivated by a preliminary study (R3). Sensitivity to noisy connections in random walks is not addressed (R2).
- **Disagreement among reviewers** – Two reviewers (R2, R4) rated the paper 8 and found it well‐written, novel in its use of SSMs, and supported by thorough analysis; the other two (R1, R3) rated it 6 and considered the novelty limited and the motivation unclear. This tension is especially evident in the debate over whether the performance gains are driven by the random walk component or the local/global modules.
