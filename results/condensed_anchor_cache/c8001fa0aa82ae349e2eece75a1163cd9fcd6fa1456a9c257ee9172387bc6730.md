## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a method for learning discrete-event-conditioned causal dynamics in model-based RL. It learns a partition of the state-action space into “events” via vector quantization of a learned embedding, then fits an event-specific sparse causal graph (LCG) and transition function for each event. A regularized log-likelihood score guides causal graph selection, while the quantization loss encourages clustering. Experiments on two custom environments (Chemical, Magnetic) and a robot-arm task show improved robustness under distribution shift and recovery of event-specific graphs. Five reviewers rated the paper 6,5,6,6,6. Reviewer 2 is notably more negative (score 5, poor soundness/presentation), questioning the soundness of the joint optimization, the theoretical proofs, and reproducibility. The other four reviewers find the idea novel and the experiment compelling, but raise concerns about theoretical alignment with experiments, the identifiability of events, the mildness of assumptions, and missing details.

### Strengths

- **Novel and intuitive idea**: Discovering fine-grained, event-specific causal relationships by partitioning the state-action space is interesting and tackles a meaningful problem in causal discovery for RL (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The method goes beyond global causal dynamics by addressing local independence that only holds under certain conditions (R2).
- **Theoretical identifiability result**: The paper proves that optimising a regularised likelihood score can identify the optimal event decomposition and the corresponding fine-grained causal graphs (R1, R3, R5). Reviewer 5 highlights that the proof shows the grouping does not depend on the hyperparameter *K* for identifying meaningful context.
- **Experimental demonstration**: On the Chemical and Magnetic environments, the method successfully recovers event-specific causal graphs (Figures 4, 5) and improves robustness to unseen states and OOD generalization compared to prior approaches (R1, R5). Table 3 shows that event-specific causal discovery scores improve over baselines.
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (R3, R4). The presentation is clear for most readers (R1, R5).
- **Comparison to prior work**: The paper attempts to extend (Hwang et al. 2023) by learning the event partition jointly with the causal models, rather than assuming a fixed partition (R2 acknowledges the challenge).

### Weaknesses

- **Soundness of the joint optimization and risk of degenerate solutions** (R2, R4).
  - *Reviewer 2 doubts* whether the learning of the decomposition scheme is effectively signaled by the impact on the causal models. Specifically, it is unclear how the gradient of the prediction loss *L_pred* propagates through the argmin in Eq. 5; if it does not update the codebook *C* or embedding *h*, then the decomposition learning is solely based on the quantization loss *L_quant*, making it not truly joint (R2, Q1).
  - Both R2 and R4 ask how a nontrivial result is ensured: if *h* always outputs a constant vector and all cluster centers equal that constant, *L_quant* is minimised but the decomposition is meaningless (R2 Q2, R4 Weakness).
- **Theory concerns** (R1, R2, R3, R4).
  - *Reviewer 2* finds the main theorem applicable only to a special case (two irreducible phases) and the lemmas (Prop. 1‑3) flawed, with missing explicit assumptions (e.g., the role of λ, requirements on λ for Prop. 2). Proofs rely on unstated assumptions placed in the appendix rather than the main text (R2 Q3‑Q8).
  - *Reviewer 2* points out that the probability factorisation on page 3 assumes conditional independence of next-state components; this assumption is not verified for the environments used and may affect identifiability (R2 Q5).
  - *Reviewer 1* notes a misalignment between theory and experiments: in the Magnetic environment the causal graph may be uniform across partitions, contradicting the theoretical premise of distinct LCGs across events (R1 Weakness).
  - *Reviewer 3* finds the identifiability of events unclear: how does vector quantification lead to identifying meaningful events? The connection between learned discrete codes and actual events is not rigorously established; there is no guarantee that true events align with VQ boundaries (R3 Q2, Weakness 2).
  - *Reviewer 4* argues that Definition 1 only allows edges to be subsets of the original graph, but a change in event could alter the entire causal relationship; this assumption may be too restrictive (R4 Weakness).
  - *Reviewer 4* questions whether the score (Eq. 4) can identify the causal graph without strong assumptions (linearity, non-Gaussianity) or intervention data; the paper lacks rigorous assumptions to substantiate identifiability (R4 Weakness).
  - *Reviewer 1*: the identifiability result is not reflected experimentally – no quantitative measure (e.g., Structural Hamming Distance) is provided to compare learned and true LCGs (R1 Weakness).
- **Reproducibility and missing details** (R2).
  - *Reviewer 2* (major concern): no pseudo-code is provided; method description in Sections 3.3, 4.1.2, C.2 misses critical pieces (exploration policy, which parameters are updated by *L_pred* vs. *L_quant*, gradient computation). Experimentation code is not given, and the two MDP environments are insufficiently described to allow reproduction (e.g., how noise is injected at test time, the distribution of box positions at training time – if positions are sampled uniformly from the whole table at training, then “unseen” locations may not be truly OOD) (R2 Weakness (b)).
  - *Reviewer 2* also notes insufficient contrast to prior work, especially (Hwang et al. 2023); Proposition 1 in this paper essentially rephrases Proposition 4 of that work (R2 Weakness (d)).
- **Experimental limitations and assumptions** (R5, R1, R2).
  - The validation environments are simple, low-dimensional, and assume full observability. Real-world RL often involves partial observability, which limits the generalisability of the findings (R5 Weakness).
  - The method assumes that different events have different causal graphs (different LCGs). *Reviewer 1* points out that in complex real data, all partitions may share the same LCG while having different transition dynamics *p(s’|s,a)*. Can the method distinguish partitions in that case? This question is not addressed (R1 Weakness).
  - In the ID setting, the proposed method is not the best; the paper offers no explanation for this (R1 Question).
  - The choice of codebook size *K* is not justified. Table 3 suggests larger *K* gives better performance, but what happens when *K* equals the number of samples? (R1, R5)
- **Novelty and citations** (R3).
  - The vector quantification technique in Eq. 5 is not new; VQVAE and related works should be cited (R3 Weakness 1).
- **Concept of “event”** (R4, R1).
  - The concept of an event is not clearly defined; is it a specific state value, a region, or analogous to a domain ID in domain generalisation? Examples in RL would help (R4 Weakness).
  - The definition of *ℰ* (event) in Section 3.1 is missing (R1 Question).
- **Intuition behind robustness** (R4).
  - *Reviewer 4* questions why removing unnecessary edges from the full graph improves robustness: if edges are unnecessary, the full causal function should already ignore them; the paper should clarify this (R4 Weakness).
