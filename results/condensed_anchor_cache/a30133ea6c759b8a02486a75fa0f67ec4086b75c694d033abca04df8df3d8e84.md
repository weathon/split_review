- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 1, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces EM-MIA, a membership inference attack (MIA) for LLMs that iteratively refines membership scores and prefix scores using an expectation-maximization-like algorithm. It also presents OLMoMIA, a benchmark built from OLMo resources with controlled difficulty levels (easy/medium/hard/random) via clustering. The method achieves state-of-the-art AUROC on WikiMIA and outperforms baselines (loss, min-k%, zlib, ReCaLL) on OLMoMIA, though all methods near random on the random split. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: one (score 1) argues MIAs are fundamentally impossible per Maini et al. ICLR 2021 Theorem 2 and that the paper fails on standard benchmarks; another (score 6) finds the framework novel and experiments comprehensive; two others (scores 5, 3) recognize contributions but identify significant missing analyses and concerns.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of the EM-MIA framework**: The iterative refinement of membership and prefix scores is a new approach (Reviewers 1, 4). One reviewer (score 5) finds the idea "novel" and expects it to be a good benchmark for future work.
- **Comprehensive experiments**: EM-MIA is compared to strong baselines across multiple benchmarks (WikiMIA, OLMoMIA) and different difficulty settings; the paper also considers recent criticisms by including random splits where performance is ~50% (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **OLMoMIA benchmark design**: Careful use of clustering and embedding models to create controlled difficulty levels is commended (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 1 believes it will be "quite significant and valuable for future work."
- **Analysis of ReCaLL assumptions and weaknesses** is appreciated (Reviewer 2).
- **Relevance**: The work addresses a timely and important problem in LLM privacy and data compliance (Reviewer 4).
- **Contrasting view**: One reviewer (score 1) notes the benchmark is useful for assessing MIA methods, but asserts that Theorem 2 implies all such methods must fail when training data is large and each sample is seen only once.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing TPR@low FPR**: Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3) note the paper reports only AUROC and omits TPR@low FPR, which is the community-standard metric (e.g., from Carlini et al., 2021). Reviewer 3 additionally points out that in Figure 1b, TPR@5%FPR = 93.4 is worse than the blind baseline of 94.4% from Table 1.
- **No results on MIMIR or Pile**: The authors skip MIMIR (a standard MIA benchmark) claiming no baseline better than random. Reviewer 2 counters that Min-K%++ achieved 61.1 and 74.2 AUC-ROC on Pythia-12b for MIMIR splits, contradicting the claim. Reviewer 3 further notes the paper lacks experiments on the Pile dataset used in Maini et al. 2024.
- **Computational complexity not analyzed**: Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 all request a precise complexity description (tokens consumed, wall-clock times, apples-to-apples comparisons). The iterative nature (Alg. 1) requires multiple rounds of scoring over the test set, and its cost relative to baselines like ReCaLL is not given.
- **Insufficient ablations and hyperparameter analyses**:
  - Variation of number of shots (for EM-MIA and ReCaLL) is missing (Reviewer 1).
  - Compare baselines under equal compute budgets (Reviewer 1).
  - How good must the initialization be? (Line 359: "requires a baseline sufficiently better than random guessing") – no ablation (Reviewer 1).
  - Vary the number of iterations (Reviewer 1).
  - Effect of reusing test examples for ReCaLL (Reviewer 1).
  - Impact of prefix size on prefix scores (Reviewer 2).
  - Impact of number of clusters (k=50) and other clustering hyperparameters on OLMoMIA difficulty (Reviewer 2).
  - Ablation of initialization methods: why Min-K%++? How does EM-MIA perform when initialized with Loss, Avg, or other baselines? (Reviewers 1, 4).
  - N-gram overlap ratio between members and non-members as a difficulty metric (Reviewer 2).
- **OLMoMIA design clarity**: The description of easy/medium/hard settings is ambiguous; precise equations, pseudocode, or figures are needed (Reviewer 1). The use of δ at the end of Section 5 is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - Not initially clear if the paper handles pretraining or finetuning MIA (Reviewer 1).
  - "Prefix score" in the abstract is ambiguous and should be explained more concretely (Reviewer 1).
  - Tables are dense; plots would improve interpretability (Reviewer 1).
  - Section 4: "test data labels" what are they? (Reviewer 3). D_test and how S is computed require explicit definition (Reviewer 3).
  - The subsection on external data (members/non-members access) is not fitting because the authors never use it in experiments; should be removed (Reviewer 3).
- **Reliability of WikiMIA for design choices**: The paper uses WikiMIA to decide update rules but Section 2.3 itself states WikiMIA is not a reliable MIA dataset (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing code release**: Multiple reviewers (1, 2 [implied], 3) note no source code is provided; plans for release should be clarified (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing references**: Kandpal et al. EMNLP '24 and Maini et al. NeurIPS '24 should be cited (Reviewer 1).
- **Questionable EM analogy**: The paper strongly emphasizes expectation-maximization, but it is a shallow analogy (only alternating optimization). Without a deeper connection to latent variable models, this framing is misleading (Reviewer 1). One reviewer (score 2) asked for elaboration.
- **Theoretical impossibility claim**: One reviewer (score 1, confidence 5) argues that, due to Maini et al. ICLR 2021 Theorem 2, MIAs on LLMs are impossible when training data is large and seen only once. They assert the paper’s own results confirm this (all methods near random on random splits) and that the method violates the basic MIA definition (no truly held-out in-distribution dataset). This reviewer also states the method fails on the Pile benchmarks from Maini et al. 2024.
- **Missing transferability**: OLMoMIA only works with OLMo; evidence that results transfer to other models under controlled distribution shift is absent (Reviewer 1).
- **Ratio vs difference of log-likelihoods**: Reviewer 1 questions why the paper uses the ratio of log-likelihoods instead of the difference (as suggested by Neyman-Pearson lemma). A variant with difference should be tested.
- **Why mink and mink++ do not improve with difficulty or longer text** in Table 2 (Reviewer 2).
- **Impact of prefix size**: Reviewer 2 requests an ablation study on prefix size.
- **Intuition for insensitivity to initialization** (Section 6.3): Reviewer 2 asks for elaboration.
- **Confidence in member labels**: Reviewer 2 questions assurance that Wikipedia articles are actually in training (may be in validation partitions).
- **How sure that performance is "significantly lower" without non-members** (Section 3): Reviewer 3 asks for precise measurement.
- **What is "working robustly" and "minimal information"?** (Reviewer 3).
- **Using Kendall’s tau or Spearman’s rho?** (Reviewer 3).
