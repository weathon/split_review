## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a gray-box method to determine if a target model is a fine-tuned version of a given base model, even when obfuscation techniques (parameter permutations, scaling transformations) are applied. It also extracts the LoRA rank used during fine-tuning. The method is validated on 29 diverse open-source models (including Llama2, Llama3, Mistral). Reviewers agree the task is important. Reviewer 2 is notably more positive (score 6) about robustness and accuracy; other reviewers gave scores of 3, 3, 5 and raise substantial concerns about presentation, assumptions, missing related work, and practical limitations.

### Strengths
- **Timely problem**: Model attribution and transparency concerns will grow with open-source LLMs.
- **Focus on obfuscation**: Addresses parameter permutations and scaling transformations, which are common ways to hide a model’s origin.
- **Robustness**: The method performs well even under obfuscation, demonstrating robustness against common manipulation tactics (Reviewer 2).
- **Accuracy**: Extraction of the LoRA rank provides a quantitative measure to distinguish fine-tuned models from base models, with detailed numerical comparisons (Reviewer 2).
- **Extensive empirical validation**: Experiments cover 29 diverse models, including Llama2, Llama3, and Mistral families.
- **First formal framework**: Claims to be the first formalized approach specifically for pinpointing fine-tuning origins (though Reviewer 1 questions this claim; see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
**Presentation and clarity issues (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):**
- The paper needs substantial rewriting. Captions of Figures 1 and 2 are poor—Fig. 1 is not understandable without reading the paper; Fig. 2 caption and text are mixed. All figure captions should be improved.
- Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 are not well connected; a paragraph before 4.1 explaining their relation is missing.
- Algorithm 1 (“Random Rank Extraction”) is not self-sufficient—notation used in the algorithm should be defined within it.
- Where is Theorem 4? The proof end is not marked (no end-of-proof symbol).
- The experimental analysis is weak and needs more detailed quantitative exposition; more focus should be given to Sections 4 and 5.2.
- The title “Toward Trustworthy: A Method for Detecting Fine-Tuning Origins in LLMs” is grammatically poor and the term “trustworthy” is ambiguous.
- None of the formulas are numbered; symbols like the one between \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) in line 575 are not defined (not routine like +, -, ×, ÷). The full name of “MLP” should be introduced.
- Typos: In line 200 “scalara”; line 168: reference \(W\) should be \(W_c\); line 186, 194, 200: missing space before “is”; line 223: missing reference for NLTK.

**Methodology and assumptions (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):**
- **Missing related work**: The “Random Rank Extraction” procedure (Algorithm 1, line 279) and the “Rank Extraction Method” are very similar to the “Hidden-Dimension Extraction Attack” in Carlini et al. (ICML 2024, “Stealing part of a production language model”). This paper is not cited or discussed. Also missing: discussion with backdoor-based detection approaches like Double-I Watermark (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong assumptions in proofs**: Assumption in line 742 that a matrix is full rank is strong and unverified; same for assumption in line 785. These assumptions only appear in the appendix and should be clearly explained in the main paper. The linear independence assumption in line 217 is also too strong.
- **Unreasonable insight**: The statement in line 269 “if outputs are nearly identical, their corresponding intermediates are likely similar” is intuitive but not rigorously justified.
- **Claim of “first formal framework”** (L49–51) is an overclaim—fingerprinting techniques with black-box access have been shown to survive fine-tuning.
- **Claim that “the method remains valid regardless of the permutations used”** (Reviewer 3) is questionable: if parameters are significantly altered, it becomes theoretically impossible to determine if the model was trained from scratch or fine-tuned. This differs from the problem’s reliance on LoRA; clarification needed.
- **Technique fails in non-trivial cases**: (a) Obfuscation in MLP layers is excluded—why is this reasonable? An adversary could easily manipulate MLP layers. (b) When the LoRA configuration includes \(W_o\), the method estimates ranks very far from the given rank (Reviewer 1).
- **Scope limitation**: The method is only applicable when the MLP layer is not modified during fine-tuning. If MLP parameters are tuned or architecture changed, effectiveness drops (Reviewers 1, 2). No plan for extension discussed.
- **Challenge with small-amplitude output models**: For models with small output amplitudes, reverse-engineering intermediate states suffers from weak gradient signals, limiting applicability (Reviewer 2).

**Additional reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms:**
- (Reviewer 1) Why is rank(\(W_c - W_b\)) >> s?
- (Reviewer 2) In the iterative optimization update \(y_{m+1}=y_m - \alpha \nabla \| f(y_m)-z_c\|^2\), if \(f(y_m)\) is not smooth near \(y_m\), does that affect gradient stability and convergence? With multiple local minima, how does gradient descent avoid getting stuck, especially when \(z_c\) is obfuscated?
- (Reviewer 2) How well does the approach generalize to different model types and fine-tuning strategies? Are there plans for more extensive experiments?
- (Reviewer 4) The symbol between \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) in line 575 is not defined.
- (Reviewer 4) Section 3.3 needs references to help readers unfamiliar with the field; the discrepancy in ranks of parameter differences is not well explained.
- (Reviewer 4) There should be a reference for PEFT in line 86.

**Computational complexity**: Use of SVD and gradient descent may entail high computational cost, especially with large models (Reviewer 2). No discussion of cost or scaling.

**Applicability to non-fine-tuning derivations**: The method is specific to fine-tuning; not shown to work for distillation or compression (Reviewer 1).

**Missed references and missing discussion**: No reference to Carlini et al. ICML 2024; no discussion of backdoor-based detection (Double-I Watermark); no reference for NLTK; no reference for PEFT.

**Minor formatting issues**: Figure 2 caption overlaps with text; blank space missing before “is” in lines 186, 194, 200; none of the formulas numbered.
