## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends the previous Spike-driven Transformer (Yao et al., 2023) into a meta-architecture, Meta-SpikeFormer, by adding macro-level conv blocks (RepConv2), a new spike-driven self-attention (SDSA) that uses matrix product instead of Hadamard, and ChannelMLP. It is evaluated on four vision tasks: ImageNet-1k (80.0% top-1 accuracy, 55M params), object detection, semantic segmentation, and event-based action recognition (on the largest event-based human activity recognition dataset). The model achieves state-of-the-art results among SNN baselines.

### Strengths
- Clear paper structure and easy-to-follow presentation (R2).
- Improved accuracy over previous Spike-driven Transformer and other SNN baselines: 80.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k with 55M params surpasses prior SOTA SNNs (66M) by 3.7% (R1, R2, R3).
- Extensively evaluated on four distinct vision tasks, including event-based action recognition (R2).
- Interesting ablation study comparing different attention mechanisms (SDSA-1/2/3) and shortcut structures (R1).
- The new self-attention SNN implementation is considered novel and contributes to the accuracy gain (R2); however, R1 finds the overall architecture incremental to Yao et al., with only tweaks (more CNN layers, changed attention) and notes that the matrix product attention has worse complexity than the Hadamard product.
- Proposes use of RepConv2, SDSA, and ChannelMLP, and compares against prior SNN designs (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Contributions are rather incremental; the bulk of the architecture was presented in earlier papers (Yao et al., 2023) (R1). (R2 disagrees on the novelty of the attention module, but R1 and R3 consider the changes incremental.)
- The motivation for SNNs is weak: why is SNN important? (R3).
- Energy consumption comparison is unclear and potentially misleading:  
  - In Table 1, Meta-SpikeFormer reports 52.5 mJ versus Yao et al.’s 6.1 mJ – an almost 10× increase. The cause (e.g., more complex attention) is not discussed.  
  - In Section 4.1, power numbers (11.9 vs. 10.2 mJ) are given for different timesteps T (T=1 vs. T=4) and are not directly comparable; the authors should compare T=4 with T=4 and discuss the dependence of energy on T (R1).
- The metric for “power” is not clearly defined or computed (R3).
- The claim of “state-of-the-art” is only within the SNN domain; compared to conventional ANNs, 80.0% top-1 accuracy with 55M parameters is not competitive – e.g., tinyViT achieves 80% with 5M params, and EfficientNet B3 achieves 81.1% with 12M params (R3). The paper’s claim may mislead readers about the absolute performance level.
- The architecture only focuses on ViT and vision tasks; despite claiming a “general spike-driven Transformer”, there is no discussion of extension to language tasks (R2).
- Missing training cost comparison with vanilla Transformer models (R2).
- The concept of “meta” architecture is unclear; the paper does not explain what makes the architecture “meta” beyond versatility across datasets (R1).
