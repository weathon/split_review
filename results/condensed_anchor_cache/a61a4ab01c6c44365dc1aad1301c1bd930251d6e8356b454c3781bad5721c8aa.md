## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RouteFormer, a multimodal ego-trajectory prediction network that integrates driver field-of-view (FOV) data (first-person video and gaze fixations), GPS data, and environmental context. It also presents the Path Complexity Index (PCI), a new metric for trajectory complexity, and the GEM dataset, which provides synchronized driver FOV, gaze, and GPS data for urban driving. Extensive evaluations on GEM and DR(eye)VE show that RouteFormer significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods, with improvements especially pronounced in high-complexity scenarios.

### Strengths
- Novel dataset (GEM): high-quality, focused on urban driving, with synchronized FOV and gaze data. Fills a critical gap in available resources for trajectory prediction. Will be valuable to the community. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- RouteFormer uniquely integrates driver FOV (first-person video + gaze) with GPS and scene data, enhancing ego-trajectory prediction accuracy. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- Introduction of PCI provides a nuanced assessment of scenario complexity, often overlooked by traditional metrics, and helps evaluate model performance in challenging scenarios. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Method outperforms all baselines on both datasets. (Reviewer 1)
- Sound theory: considering driver attention and intent, as well as surrounding environment, is convincing and a key impact factor for future trajectory. (Reviewer 3)
- Ablation study and supplementary material are helpful, enabling readers to understand contributions of each modality. (Reviewer 3)
- RouteFormer is described as the best solution on the market for this problem; the paper offers genuinely unique insights, providing refreshing clarity and meeting conference publication standards. (Reviewer 2, rating 8)
- Extensive testing demonstrates robust performance in real-world scenarios, highlighting potential for practical driver-assistance and AD systems. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient ablation studies**: Reviewer 1 requests additional ablations: (1) RouteFormer without video and gaze (motion only), (2) RouteFormer without surrounding scenes (motion + gaze), and comparison of ADE and ADE+20PCI results.
- **Weak baselines**: The current baselines (GIMO, Multimodal Transformer) are designed for human motion, not vehicle motion. Comparison with at least one vehicle motion predictor (e.g., MTR, Autobots) is needed to be convincing. (Reviewer 1)
- **Corner-case definition bias**: The PCI-based categorization essentially uses a contrastive approach (predict trajectory, compare with ground truth), but predictions differ across models – how is the used prediction model unbiased? The approach is considered somewhat subjective. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear definition of long-tail phenomenon**: It is not clearly defined what constitutes the long-tail, nor why the proposed method is capable of defining rare scenarios. The described method does not adequately capture the essence of long-tail events. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing citations**: The paper is inspired by human visual attention during driving but misses citing earlier works that introduced similar ideas in trajectory prediction, e.g. Human Observation-Inspired Trajectory Prediction (ICRA 2024) and Less is More: Efficient Brain-Inspired Learning (ECAI 2024). (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited related work discussion**: The related work section could benefit from a broader discussion of recent research (especially 2023–2024 studies). (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing limitations/future work**: The paper would benefit from a discussion section addressing limitations and future challenges. (Reviewer 2)
- **Open-sourcing**: The authors should consider open-sourcing code and dataset to significantly contribute to the community. (Reviewer 2)
- **Failure cases**: It would be better to report failure cases, especially those where predictions are totally opposite of ground truth. (Reviewer 3)
- **Gaze dynamics concerns**: What if estimated gaze positions move quickly (due to prediction errors or rapid driver gaze shifts)? Could there be a negative impact on results? (Reviewer 3)
- **Single gaze per timestamp**: Line 190 states gaze positions are in shape T×2 – does that imply using only one gaze per timestamp? Clarification needed. (Reviewer 3)

*Note: Reviewer 2 is notably more positive about the paper's overall quality and contribution, rating it 8, while Reviewers 1 and 3 rate it 6, with more critical comments on baselines, ablations, and missing comparisons.*
