## Merged Review

### Summary
FedSecurity is a benchmark for simulating adversarial attacks and defenses in Federated Learning (FL), built as a supplementary component of the FedML library. It includes FedAttacker (implements data poisoning, model poisoning, and data reconstruction attacks) and FedDefender (defenses applied before, during, or after aggregation, such as clipping, robust aggregation, and noise addition). The benchmark supports various ML models (Logistic Regression, ResNet, GAN) and FL optimizers (FedAvg, FedOpt, FedNOVA). It is extended to federated training of large language models (BERT, Pythia) and includes a real-world edge device experiment. Experiments evaluate attacks like Byzantine noise and defenses like m-Krum, showing that attacks degrade accuracy and defenses can mitigate them but may also hurt accuracy when no attack occurs. Four reviewers gave scores 5, 5, 5, and 6.

### Strengths
+ Robustness of FL is a hot topic; a systematic benchmark is useful for researchers and practitioners (R1, R2, R3, R4).
+ Implements a good set of attack and defense methods (R1, R3, R4). Covers multiple categories: data poisoning, model poisoning, data reconstruction; and defenses: clipping, robust aggregation, noise (R2, R4).
+ Extension to federated LLM training (BERT, Pythia) shows wider applicability and is timely for the community (R1, R2, R3, R4).
+ Real-world demonstration on edge devices indicates scalability beyond simulations (R2).
+ Analysis provides insights into balancing robustness against potential negative impacts of defenses (R2).
+ Limitations and future directions are discussed (R4).
+ Paper is well presented and easy to read (R1). One reviewer (R4) rated the contribution as “good” and the benchmark as “comprehensive”, while the majority found limited novelty (contrast of overall scores: 6 vs. 5).

### Weaknesses
- The paper introduces no new techniques or advancements; it entirely relies on previously proposed attacks and defenses (R1, also reflected in R2 and R4 noting limited coverage).
- Code implementation was not provided for review, making it hard to assess characteristics and possible weaknesses (R1).
- Many attacks and defenses are missing: test-time attacks (e.g., adversarial examples), privacy attacks (property inference, membership inference), and recent SOTA attacks/defenses (the implemented methods appear to be published before 2021) (R1, R4).
- Defenses implemented are only a small subset from the literature; more defenses are needed for completeness (R2).
- Analysis is limited: lack of convergence visualization and ablation studies (R2, R3).
- LLM experiments are small-scale and only cover BERT and Pythia; more diverse LLMs should be tested (R2, R3).
- Task types are narrow, mostly image classification; should include NLP, recommendation systems, etc. (R2).
- No large-scale experiment with up to 1000 clients, which is typical for benchmark works (R3).
- No table of real training time on different devices (R3).
- Pre-trained models before/after fine-tuning for LLM are not provided (R3).
- Missing explicit comparison with existing federated learning benchmarks (e.g., LEAF, Flower) (R3).
- Unique challenges of applying attacks/defenses to LLMs are not discussed: large parameter size, different training procedures, unique vulnerabilities (e.g., backdoor susceptibility), computational cost of defenses (R4).
- Data reconstruction attacks are privacy-focused, conflicting with the stated security focus; this creates a conceptual inconsistency (R4).
- Need more backdoor attacks/defenses and evaluation metrics (e.g., backdoor attack success rate) (R3).
- Attacks/defenses for NLP tasks specifically should be added for LLM and smaller models (R3).
