## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces BS-MPC, a model-based RL method that incorporates a bisimulation metric loss into the TD-MPC framework to directly optimize the encoder. The goal is to improve training stability, robustness to input noise, and computational efficiency. Experiments on continuous control and image-based tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite compare BS-MPC against TD-MPC and other baselines. The paper includes theoretical analysis bounding cumulative rewards in the latent space. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and the approach sound, with promising experimental results against TD-MPC. However, there is substantial disagreement on novelty: some reviewers view the integration of bisimulation metrics as a valuable new perspective, while others consider the contribution incremental, noting that the framework, loss design, and theoretical results largely borrow from existing work. Additional concerns include hyperparameter sensitivity, insufficient justification for computational speedups, and limitations in the robustness evaluation and theoretical assumptions.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow; ideas are explained thoroughly and the mathematical formulation is rigorous (all reviewers).
- The integration of bisimulation metric loss into a model-based RL objective (TD-MPC) is a sound and sensible approach (R2, R3, R4). Some reviewers highlight this as a novel perspective that directly addresses encoder stability and noise robustness (R1, R3).
- Experimental results are promising: BS-MPC shows improved performance and robustness compared to TD-MPC on several tasks (R1, R2, R3). R1 specifically notes strong results on both state-based and image-based tasks, increased resilience to noise, and faster training due to parallel computation.
- The paper provides theoretical analysis bounding expected cumulative rewards in the learned latent space, adding depth to the approach (R1). The supplementary material is comprehensive, including additional experiments and details (R3).
- The empirical evaluation is extensive, covering more than 20 case studies across different domains (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited and incremental.** The framework is entirely based on TD-MPC, and the bisimulation metric and its loss design are taken from existing literature (R2, R3, R4). Adding an additional regularization loss for the encoder is a common practice in model-based RL (R2). One reviewer notes that the only major novelty is adding the bisimulation loss, and the other two claimed contributions (stability, efficiency) follow naturally (R4).
- **Theoretical analysis has several issues.** 
  - Theorem 1 relies on the “strong assumption” that the learned policy continuously improves and eventually converges to the optimal policy, yet training curves show non‑monotonic returns and no evidence of convergence to an optimal policy (R3). The authors do not discuss whether this assumption can be relaxed (R3).
  - The robustness argument (Theorem 1, Eq. 20) appears hastily added: the authors declined to perform Lipschitz analysis because it is “intractable,” but without Lipschitz constants, the robustness claim is not substantiated; moreover, Eq. 20 is misleading because Lipschitz continuity also applies to the encoder and noise can affect it if global Lipschitz continuity is used (R4).
  - The theoretical results do not provide a performance gap between BS-MPC and the optimal policy under the bisimulation training error, nor a theoretical comparison of performance gain over TD-MPC (R2). There is also no theoretical confirmation of which case studies BS-MPC should outperform TD-MPC (R4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and tuning cost.** The parameter \(c_4\) requires extensive tuning, which may be impractical without automated selection (R1, R4). R1 asks for more discussion on reducing this dependency.
- **Claims of faster computation are not substantiated.** No concrete timing comparisons under same hardware are provided (R1). R2 finds it surprising that adding an extra loss term speeds up training and requests the latency overhead of the bisimulation loss term. R4 asks whether faster convergence and fewer parameters are supported theoretically or only empirically.
- **Robustness evaluation is insufficient.** The perturbations used (visual distractions) are not representative of realistic environmental changes (R1). The paper lacks details on the specific types of noise applied (R1). The authors did not perform a proper Lipschitz constant comparison of the controller (R4). R1 also asks how BS-MPC handles dynamic backgrounds that move with the agent (not just pure noise).
- **Experimental comparison concerns.**
  - Use of TD-MPC (rather than the improved TD-MPC2) in main experiments: the rationale for keeping TD-MPC in the main body and only mentioning TD-MPC2 in the appendix is unclear (R4). Running comparisons on TD-MPC2 would be more fair given that it is the updated version (R4).
  - In Fig. 4, non‑MPC‑based methods are only shown up to 10M steps; a reason is not given (R3).
  - It is not specified whether gradient clipping was applied to the TD-MPC baseline (R4). If it was not, the comparison may be unfair, as gradient clipping would mitigate the gradient explosion that BS-MPC avoids.
- **Missing discussion and analysis.** The paper does not discuss how the bisimulation metric loss interacts with the dynamics model learning, nor how this interaction affects latent space stability (R1). The additional computational cost of the bisimulation loss in high-dimensional latent spaces is not addressed (R1, R2).
- **Typos and presentation issues.** “In BS-MPC, the latent dynamics are modeled using an MLP. We also model the latent dynamics model with an MLP” – “BS-MPC” should be “TD-MPC” (R3). Missing spacing in “meanμ0” (R3).
