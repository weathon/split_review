## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces two exact kernel-based models—a global model (RKBS) and a local model (RKHS with LiNK)—to analyze neural networks of arbitrary width, depth, and topology under only finite-energy activations. The global model yields a Rademacher complexity bound that can be made width- and depth-independent under certain conditions. The local model defines a local-intrinsic neural kernel (LiNK) and shows the NTK is its first-order approximation, leading to a bound on network adaptation complexity. Reviewers agree the work is theoretically ambitious, but opinions diverge sharply on clarity, novelty, and empirical grounding. Two reviewers (scores 5) view the paper as solving a central problem (parametric convergence rate \(\sqrt{n}\)), while two others (scores 3) find the presentation too dense, the novelty limited relative to prior work, and the practical implications unclear. All four note the lack of numerical experiments.

### Strengths
- The paper extends kernel-based models beyond the over-parameterized regime to any network width, depth, and topology, demonstrating a sophisticated understanding of functional analysis and neural network theory. (R1, R4)
- The mathematical derivations (e.g., Hermite polynomials for the RKBS representation, LiNK construction) are novel and intricate. (R1)
- LiNK is a novel notion, and showing that the NTK is its first-order approximation provides a path to generalize NTK outside the over-parameterized limit. (R1, R3)
- Concrete Rademacher complexity bounds are derived, notably Theorem 3 and Corollary 5 showing \(R_N(\mathcal{F}) \leq 1/\sqrt{N}\), which would imply a parametric convergence rate. (R1, R2)
- The global model yields a bound that can be made depth-independent under mild assumptions (e.g., with a modified He initialization). (R1)
- The local model provides a bound (Theorem 8) that offers insight into the benefits of adaptation methods like LoRA. (R1)
- The theoretical frameworks are rigorous and apply to any neural network configuration, going beyond the common over-parameterized setting. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and accessibility:** The paper is extremely compressed; the density of definitions and derivations limits readability. Notation is particularly confusing (e.g., \(W^{[j]}\) and \(W^{[\widetilde{j},j]}\)), making it hard to follow the core contributions in Theorem 1 and Theorem 6. (R1, R2) One reviewer (R2) could not fully understand these two theorems despite their central role.
- **Lack of concrete examples and empirical validation:** No numerical experiments are presented. There are no demonstrations of how the bounds behave for standard architectures (e.g., CNNs, transformers) or on standard datasets (e.g., MNIST, CIFAR-10). (R1, R4) This weakens the practical impact and leaves predictions untested. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Unclear novelty relative to prior work:** The Rademacher complexity analysis—bounding complexity via weight matrix norms and making it depth-independent—appears similar to prior work by Bartlett & Mendelson (2002) and Golowich et al. (2018). The paper does not clearly distinguish its contribution from these results. (R3) Similarly, the local model (Theorem 8) does not obviously provide benefits beyond existing NTK-based analyses. (R3)
- **Assumptions and their necessity:** The paper does not discuss whether its assumptions effectively reduce to the standard assumption that weights live in a compact set independent of width/depth—which already gives a \(1/\sqrt{N}\) bound. The necessity and naturality of the assumptions (e.g., finite-energy activations) are not explicitly justified with examples. (R1, R2)
- **Scope of the local model:** The local model only captures the change \(\Delta f\) around the initial network \(f_0\), and may not capture the full dynamics of general neural networks, which are more than just \(f_0 + \Delta f\). (R4)
- **Missing comparisons:** The paper does not compare its Rademacher bounds with known bounds for standard network topologies, nor does it compare LiNK with NTK in practical settings. (R1, R3)
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with weaknesses:**
  - How do the proposed modifications to He/Glorot initialization affect convergence speed and stability in practice? (R4)
  - What are the implications for non-standard architectures like RNNs/CNNs? (R4)
  - Clarify whether “nodes” on line 126-127 refer to layers in the usual sense. (R3)
  - The paper should include concrete examples (e.g., evaluating bounds on known topologies, comparing LiNK and NTK) and numerical experiments to illustrate the theoretical results. (R1, R4)
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** Two reviewers (scores 5) are highly positive, emphasizing the parametric convergence rate and theoretical generality. Two others (scores 3) question the novelty and clarity, noting that the results may follow from existing assumptions, and strongly request empirical validation. The paper does not address these concerns.
