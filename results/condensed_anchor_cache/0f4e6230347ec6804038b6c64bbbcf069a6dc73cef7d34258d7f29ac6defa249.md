## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a stagewise pretraining framework, RaPTr (Random Part Training), that trains only random subnetworks (depth-wise, width-wise) and progressively increases subnetwork size during training, ultimately training the full network. The method is motivated by stochastic depth, generalizes prior layer dropping, and aims to improve efficiency. Experiments on encoder-decoder models BERT (base) and UL2 show wall-clock speedups (up to 33%) and, for UL2, downstream improvements on QA and SuperGLUE (+1.5%). The paper also provides theoretical justification using a polynomial learning example and a stability bound (Theorem 5.3). Reviewers generally found the paper well-written and the problem important, but expressed concerns about limited experimental scope, insufficient theoretical depth, missing comparisons, and lack of sensitivity analysis.

### Strengths
- The problem of efficient pretraining is increasingly important as models and datasets scale (R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and well-motivated by the stochastic depth literature (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The proposed method (RaPTr) is straightforward and easy to understand (R2).
- Obtained results on a small set of benchmarks show competitive or superior performance compared to full-model training, translating directly to wall-clock savings (R1). UL2 improvements on QA and SuperGLUE (+1.5%) provide evidence of better inductive bias (R1).
- The paper is comprehensive, covering theoretical justification, detailed numerical analysis, and implementation guidelines (R3).
- Efforts have been made to offer theoretical justifications for the method (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental scope is limited.** The paper only evaluates on encoder-decoder models (BERT base, UL2); decoder-only models (much more prevalent) are not included (R1). For BERT, only a base model is used and improvements are marginal compared to other stacking/dropping methods; only 3 GLUE tasks are reported (R4). UL2 improvements are within the variance of the baseline (R4). The paper should include all GLUE tasks, experiments on BERT-large, and evaluations on a large number of tasks from eval-harness (R1, R4). The decision of specific dataset mixtures is unclear; an off-the-shelf dataset like FineWeb/FineWeb-edu would be more suitable (R1).
- **Theoretical foundation is insufficient and unconvincing.** The polynomial learning example with a 2-layer residual network is too simplistic and does not translate to the complexities of deep networks like BERT or UL2; the analysis lacks depth and does not consider different activation functions, network depth, or complex interactions (R2, R4). The theoretical analysis does not address potential overfitting or instability when learning hierarchical features in deep networks (R4). Theorem 5.3 shows the loss gap is upper bounded by stability, but it is unclear whether this holds for other stacking or dropping methods; no comparative analysis of loss gap bounds is provided, making it impossible to assess RaPTr’s relative advantage (R4). The empirical verification in Section 5 (Fig. 3) does not support the theory: there is no linear decrease in loss gap as depth increases (R4). Some reviewers find the theoretical justification comprehensive (R3), while others (R2, R4) consider it overly simplistic and lacking depth—this disagreement reflects a significant weakness.
- **Missing sensitivity analysis and ablation.** The selected Rel. FLOPS settings (e.g., fixing to 1.33x in Table 1) seem arbitrary; the paper lacks clear explanation of how these values are derived from the schedule and what the trade-off is between computational savings and model performance as Rel. FLOPS is varied (R3). The connection between equal stage lengths and average layer usage is not explicitly detailed in the main text (R3). It is unclear how performance holds when more FLOPs are reduced (e.g., to half or 1/3 of baseline) (R2). The paper does not explore whether RaPTr can be used for fine-tuning instead of pretraining (R4). No comparison of loss gap bounds with other stagewise methods is provided (R4).
- **Implementation details are scattered and some are buried in the appendix.** Important details such as sequence length, batch sizes, and how to provide gradients to all layers (e.g., making every layer receive gradients even if not sampled) should be in the main paper (R1). The trivial alternative of multiplying block output with alpha_i to ensure gradient computation (though without wall-clock gain) could be mentioned concisely (R1).
- **Formatting issues.** Many typos, e.g., line 482: "layernorm is enabled or not respectively" is reversed (R4). Figures with subplots are not labelled clearly (R4).
