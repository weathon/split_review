## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SPE-Unlearn, a structure-aware parameter-efficient unlearning method for Transformers. It introduces learnable binary masks to identify influence-critical parameters in heads and filters, optimized via a greedy search with warm start. Unlearning is then performed by updating only those masked parameters using second-order or other methods. The paper claims improved efficiency and effectiveness across multiple unlearning scenarios (memory-free, memory-aided) and presents experiments on several models and datasets.

### Strengths
- The approach of identifying influence-critical parameters using both retain and forget sets is considered original by some reviewers (R1, R3). The derivation of the score function is clear and mathematically rigorous (R1, R4).
- The paper is well-organized, well-formatted, and easy to follow (R1, R4). The mathematical derivations are rigorous (R4).
- Exploring applications of the proposed method for successive machine unlearning (both memory-free and memory-aided) is meaningful (R1).
- The reported time and memory efficiency improvements across tables are a promising benefit (R2). *The strongest reviewer (R2, score 6) highlights this as the main strength, though other reviewers (R1, R4) question whether efficiency is actually achieved.*

### Weaknesses
- **Efficiency claims are not convincingly supported.** The method requires additional computations: twice the differentiation for mask identification (R1, R2). The unlearning phase (Eq. 10) still computes gradients for all parameters, not just masked ones, so the computational cost of the unlearning step itself is not reduced (R1). Storing masks and their gradients adds memory overhead that may negate savings (R1). In Table 2, SPE-SO does not show a significant improvement over SO while requiring more time (R4). A comprehensive complexity analysis (memory and computation) for both mask identification and unlearning phases is missing (R1).

- **Method not clearly structure-aware for Transformers.** The use of mask variables on heads and filters is not unique to Transformers; the method does not exploit Transformer-specific properties (R2). Much of the paper focuses on Transformer content when the approach is generic, making it less focused (R2).

- **Formulation concerns.** The mask variable \(m\) is binary (line 160) but the derivation assumes differentiability with respect to \(m\) (line 190) (R2). The unlearning objective (1) aims for a retrained model on \(D_r\) only, but the pruning-based approach alters the architecture, misaligning with this objective (R2). The transformation from Eq. (4) to Eq. (5) uses a second-order Taylor expansion, introducing extra computational cost and approximation error; the necessity over direct optimization of mask parameters is not justified (R1).

- **Insufficient experimental evaluation and missing details.**
    - The split of \(D_r\) and \(D_f\) is not specified (R2).
    - Hyperparameters controlling unlearning speed (e.g., learning rate) for compared methods (FT, GD, SA) are not discussed, raising fairness concerns (R2).
    - Unlearning accuracy (forget) is very high (85.94% lowest) while remaining accuracy drops; it would be fairer to report remaining/testing accuracy at a common unlearning accuracy threshold (R2).
    - The MIA evaluation should compare to retrained model performance (gold standard), but the paper sets lower/higher values as better without explanation (R2).
    - Fine-Tuning (FT) and Sparsity-Aware (SA) use only \(D_r\), while the proposed method uses both \(D_f\) and \(D_r\); this discrepancy is not clarified, and comparisons with methods using both datasets are needed (R2).
    - The method is only integrated with second-order unlearning (SO); testing with other unlearning methods is missing (R4).
    - Common LLM unlearning benchmarks are absent: TOFU, MUSE, WMDP (R4).
    - Several standard baselines are missing, e.g., NPO, “I don’t know” (R4). The compared baselines are outdated; recent works like SalUn and Boundary Unlearning should be included (R2).
    - Robustness evaluation is limited to memory-free and memory-aided scenarios; other approaches like relearning attacks or jailbreak prompts are not explored (R4).

- **Unclear choices and missing analysis.**
    - Why is a layer-wise optimization (Eq. 9) needed in addition to individual optimization (Eq. 8)? (R1 Question 1)
    - The derivation of the first item in Eq. (13) is hard to follow (R1 Question 2).
    - The threshold for when retraining from scratch becomes necessary after multiple unlearning requests is not defined; its consistency across models/datasets and practical implications are unanalyzed (R3).
    - The choice of 90% sparsity in Table 3 is arbitrary; no justification or sensitivity analysis around this value is provided (R3). *Reviewer 3 (score 6) flags this as a weakness, though other reviewers do not mention it explicitly.*

- **Minor issues.** Line 273 appears to differentiate with respect to \(\theta\) rather than \(m\) (R1). Notation does not follow ICLR guidelines (R1). Duplicate citation of “Jang et al.” in Related Work (R2). *These are noted but not central.*

**Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R4, score 3, confidence 4) is significantly more critical, questioning the method’s improvement over baselines and the lack of thorough evaluation, while other reviewers (R2, R3, both score 6) see more promise in the approach and efficiency claims. All reviewers, however, agree on significant weaknesses in experimental rigor and some formulation issues.
