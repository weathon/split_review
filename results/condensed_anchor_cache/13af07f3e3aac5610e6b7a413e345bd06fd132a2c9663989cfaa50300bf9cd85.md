## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents Ego-Foresight (EF), a self-supervised auxiliary objective that disentangles agent and environment representations by predicting future images conditioned on a sliding window of recent images and future proprioceptive states. EF integrates into DrQ-v2 and is evaluated on DMControl (4 tasks), Distracting Control Suite (2 tasks), and Meta-World (10 tasks). It aims to improve sample efficiency without costly supervisory signals. The paper is clear, well motivated (drawing on human motor prediction), and simple to implement. It includes hyper-parameter details for reproducibility.

### Strengths

- The paper is well written, clear, and easy to follow, with intuitive motivation from human learning. The learning objectives and architectures are presented in a self-contained manner. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The idea of self-supervised agent-environment disentanglement through visuomotor prediction is a fresh and promising approach for improving RL efficiency. (Reviewer 3)
- The method is simple to implement, and all hyper-parameters are clearly stated, making reproduction easy. (Reviewer 4)
- There is sufficient discussion of limitations in Section 5, with technically relevant assumptions (e.g., little variation in environment between time steps). (Reviewer 2)
- The idea of modeling future images solely from proprioception is interesting and likely effective when there is minimal relevant visual external information. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

- **Unfair experimental setup due to privileged proprioceptive information.** The benchmarks (DMControl locomotion, Distracting Control Suite) are visual RL tasks where agents typically only have raw RGB inputs. Extending DrQ-v2 with an auxiliary task that uses additional proprioceptive information (which is not available to vanilla DrQ-v2 or Dreamer-V2) creates an inherently unfair comparison. The improvements in Figures 4 and 5 are minimal, and it is impossible to tell whether they come from the auxiliary objective or the privileged observations. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Insufficient evaluation on complex tasks and limited benchmarks.** The tasks are mostly locomotion or simple object interaction. More challenging scenarios (e.g., multiple moving parts, deformable objects, intricate interactions) are needed to demonstrate the method’s ability to truly disentangle agent and environment. The Distracting Control Suite evaluation is especially limited; the method’s emphasis on disentanglement suggests it should be tested more extensively across all difficulty levels. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Lack of comparisons to relevant self-supervised representation learning methods and recent strong baselines.** The paper does not compare with methods that also learn agent-environment representations via bisimulation metrics or factorized representations (e.g., Zhang et al. 2020, Fu et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2022). It also omits recent work like Seo et al. (2022), Zhu et al. (2023), TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), Dreamer-V3 (Hafner et al., 2023), ALIX (Cetin et al., 2022), TACO (Zheng et al., 2023), and DrM (Xu et al., 2024). Dreamer-V3 has been available for nearly two years and has public results. Without these comparisons, it is unclear how EF performs relative to the state of the art. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Minimal and not statistically robust performance gains.** The proposed method mostly achieves comparable results to baselines, not clear outperformance. Standard deviation alone is insufficient; only 3–5 seeds are used, which is generally considered inadequate in RL (Agarwal et al., 2021). The lack of rigorous statistical analysis undermines confidence in the reported improvements. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **No wall-time or computational cost comparison.** The auxiliary objective decodes visual observations up to 40 time steps into the future, which is computationally expensive. The paper acknowledges this but does not provide wall-time numbers. Such a comparison is necessary to understand the practical cost-benefit trade-off. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limitation: assumes static environment and does not condition on actions.** The method assumes little change in the environment between time steps; otherwise, proprioception alone cannot reconstruct future images. Furthermore, the prediction head does not use actions, so when the agent encounters unseen states during RL, the learned representations may break. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Lack of justification for using proprioception when Dreamer already predicts images without it.** Dreamer-V2/V3 also predicts future images (via imagination) and achieves comparable results without proprioception. The added complexity of requiring proprioceptive data and paired datasets needs clearer justification. (Reviewer 2)
- **Choice of LSTM not justified.** Using LSTM for sequence prediction (instead of Transformers or state-space models like Mamba) is not motivated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Baselines differ per environment.** The paper does not explain why different baselines are used for different benchmark suites. (Reviewer 2)
- **Questions about method details.** It is not clarified which difficulty level was used in the Distracting Control Suite experiments (Reviewer 4). The statement about action repeat of 2 being used because “environment updates are costly” is confusing; action repeat is typically used to reduce control frequency, not because environment updates are costly (Reviewer 2). It is also unclear whether the context representation is recomputed after each step or updated sequentially (Reviewer 4).
