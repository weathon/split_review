## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DeCoRe (Decoding by Contrasting Retrieval Heads), a training-free decoding strategy to reduce hallucinations in LLMs. It identifies retrieval attention heads that extract contextual information; masking these heads is hypothesized to induce hallucinations. DeCoRe then contrasts the output distributions of the base LLM and the masked LLM, guided by conditional entropy to control the amount of contrast. Experiments on summarization (XSum), instruction following (MemoTrap, IFEval), open-book QA (NQ-Open, NQ-Swap, TriviaQA, PopQA, TruthfulQA), and multi-hop reasoning with CoT are conducted using Llama 3 8B and 70B. The method shows improvements on several benchmarks but results are mixed across tasks.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with necessary background and a well-structured presentation.
- The proposed method is training-free and can be applied to pre-trained LLMs without additional fine-tuning, making it practical for hallucination mitigation.
- DeCoRe introduces a novel decoding approach that contrasts the base LLM with a variant where retrieval heads are masked, building on recent insights into attention head functionality.
- The entropy-controlled mechanism dynamically adjusts the contrast based on model uncertainty, which is a principled addition.
- The experimental setup is thorough, comparing against six strong baselines (including CAD) across three tasks (reading comprehension, summarisation, instruction following) on multiple datasets.
- Significant improvements are observed on MemoTrap (instruction following) compared to all baselines.
- The research addresses an important and timely problem: developing reliable methods to reduce hallucinations in LLMs.

### Weaknesses
- **Inconsistent performance and lack of fluency/coherence evaluation:** DeCoRe fails to consistently outperform baselines across settings. On XSum and IFEval it underperforms relative to several methods (e.g., CAD), and on NQ-Swap CAD outperforms DeCoRe by over 8 points (Tab.1). Improvements on factuality (Tab.2) and multi-hop reasoning (Tab.3) are often within a one-point margin of the second-best method, making significance unclear. The paper claims “competitive performance” on NQ-Swap despite CAD’s large lead. Additionally, the evaluation focuses solely on hallucination metrics; DeCoRe yields lower ROUGE-L and BERTScore-F1 in summarisation tasks, indicating potential degradation in fluency and coherence. No analysis of generation length or conciseness is provided, though shorter answers are preferred for QA datasets.
- **Limited baseline comparisons and missing related work:** The paper only compares against contrastive decoding variants and a few other methods. It does not compare against self-consistency or discuss that line of work, even though DeCoRe shares the idea of using multiple outputs to improve grounding. The claim that CAD cannot be applied to MemoTrap is questionable (the task instruction could be used as additional context).
- **Limited originality and incremental contribution:** While some reviewers view the method as a novel extension, others note it appears to be a straightforward combination of existing ideas: applying the retrieval heads identified by Wu et al. (2024) to the Contrastive Decoding framework of Li et al. (2023). The core novelty is incremental.
- **Missing statistical rigor and aggregation:** Three DeCoRe variants (static, DeCoRe, DeCoRe lite) are compared against baselines, which can be misleading without an aggregated score. The paper lacks error bars, statistical tests, or reported variance for the main results, making it hard to judge the significance of reported gains.
- **Lack of qualitative analysis:** No qualitative examples are provided to illustrate when the conditional entropy mechanism is beneficial or harmful, or to compare generations from DeCoRe and baselines in terms of fluency, length, and content differences.
- **Insufficient robustness and hyperparameter analysis:** Masking more retrieval heads is not always beneficial (Fig.3), yet alternative selection strategies (random masking, task-based head profiling, layer-balanced masking) are not explored. The robustness of key hyperparameters (temperature, number of masked heads, number of in-context shots) is not reported. The alpha value for DeCoRe static is not specified, nor how it was determined. The smaller LM used for DeCoRe lite is not identified. Computational cost (e.g., TFLOPs) is not reported.
- **Generalizability and context limitations:** The identified retrieval heads are model-specific and may not generalize across tasks or contexts. Most experiments use short-input datasets; longer contexts and contextual conflicts are not studied.
