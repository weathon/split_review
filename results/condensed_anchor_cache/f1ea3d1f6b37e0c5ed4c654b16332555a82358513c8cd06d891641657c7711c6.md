## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OVOR (OnePrompt with Virtual Outlier Regularization) for rehearsal-free class-incremental learning (CIL) using frozen pre-trained ViTs. OnePrompt replaces task-specific prompt pools with a single learned prompt, reducing computation and storage. Virtual Outlier Regularization (VOR) tightens classifier decision boundaries via synthesised outliers to reduce inter-task confusion. Reviewers find the ideas interesting and results promising, but raise concerns about missing comparisons (SLCA, RanPAC) that achieve higher accuracy on the same datasets, insufficient ablations (especially VOR combined with other prompt methods), lack of experiments on more than two datasets, unclear intuition for OnePrompt and VOR, and several structural/presentation issues. One reviewer (R2) rates presentation as poor; others find it clear.

### Strengths
- OnePrompt is an interesting simplification that reduces computational cost by removing key-query selection over a prompt pool. (R1, R2, R3)
- OVOR outperforms or equals existing prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) in accuracy, computation, and storage. (R1)
- The pipeline is clearly explained (R1) and the background section is helpful for understanding the context and notation (R2).
- Novelty: first use of virtual outliers in prompting for CIL; the simplification from multiple prompts to one is novel; the combination is novel. (R3)
- Quality: good context, clear rationale, and testing on standard datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R) of interest to the CIL community. (R3)
- Significance: the paper addresses a timely need to explore improvements to prompt-based methods with pre-trained ViTs, showing that simplification and regularization can enhance performance. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art alternative methods.** OVOR is not compared to SLCA and RanPAC, which use pre-trained ViTs without prompting and achieve higher final accuracies: on ImageNet-R (T=10) OVOR 75.6% vs SLCA 77% and RanPAC 78.1%; on CIFAR-100 (T=10) OVOR 86.7% vs SLCA 91.53% and RanPAC 92.2%. These results substantially outweigh the gains shown over prompting baselines. (R3)
- **Insufficient dataset variety.** Only two datasets (split CIFAR-100, split ImageNet-R) are used, whereas related works [1,2,3] report on at least 4–7. Ablations, OnePrompt baseline, and OVOR need to be shown on at least 4 datasets (e.g., split CUB, split Cars) to demonstrate trends. This is especially critical because OnePrompt alone underperforms CODA-P on ImageNet-R, and only OVOR beats it. (R3)
- **Insufficient ablations.** 
  - No ablation results for VOR combined with L2P, DualPrompt, or CODA-P, even though the paper states VOR is agnostic to any prompt method. (R2, R3)
  - Ablations for OnePrompt with/without VOR are only given on ImageNet-R; missing on CIFAR-100. (R2, R3)
  - No experiments training only the classifier (plus Eq. 3) are reported. (R2)
- **Lack of intuition and explanation for key components.** 
  - No mathematical or intuitive justification for why virtual outliers (based on a Gaussian mechanism) tighten decision boundaries effectively. (R1)
  - No intuition for why a single prompt works as well as multiple task-specific prompts; specifically, how the prompt changes across tasks and how representations of previous tasks evolve. (R1, R2)
  - The paper does not explain how the virtual outliers are generated; it only cites previous work, but this is central to the contribution. (R2)
- **Missing details and clarity in tables/figures.**
  - Table 3 should include OnePrompt and add vertical lines to separate numbers of tasks. (R2)
  - Results for OnePrompt in Table 4 are missing; the text in the last two paragraphs mentions comparisons without presenting the corresponding numbers. (R2)
  - Table 1’s purpose and its relation to the CIL setting (versus task-incremental) is unclear. The paper should clarify whether OnePrompt operates in CIL or task-incremental with a shared classifier. (R2)
- **Misleading statement about prototype methods.** On page 3, the paper suggests that prototype-based methods (Zhou et al., Ma et al.) may not be truly rehearser‑free, but class prototypes are averaged feature vectors (not original samples) and are not used to update parameters in subsequent tasks. Privacy concerns over class-mean vectors are overstated. This statement appears to unfairly discredit a set of competitive methods. (R3)
- **Overly early assumption about pre‑trained ViTs.** The introduction does not make clear that the methods are designed only for pre‑trained ViT networks; the larger CL community still works with CNNs and without such strong pre‑training. This should be stated explicitly early in the paper. (R3)
- **Structure issues and missing explanations.** Presenting VOR before OnePrompt is confusing because OnePrompt is the baseline; VOR is the main contribution yet its derivation is only cited. (R2) One reviewer (R2) rates presentation as poor; others find it clear, but the need for better organisation is a consistent minority view.
- **Unanswered questions that represent missing experiments/ablations.** 
  - Does VOR increase forgetting? Table 2 shows it does; an explanation is needed. (R2)
  - Why are prompts inserted only into the first 5 layers? No ablation on this hyperparameter. (R2)
  - What is the computational cost of VOR? (R2)
  - How does OnePrompt behave in a true CIL setting (all classes seen at test time with a shared classifier)? (R2)
