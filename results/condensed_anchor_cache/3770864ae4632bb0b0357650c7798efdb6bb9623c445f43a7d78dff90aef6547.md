## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MixturePFN, which extends TabPFN to larger tabular datasets by using a mixture of in-context prompters (MICP) based on K-means clustering and context-aware finetuning (CAPFN) via bootstrapping. This addresses the quadratic space/time complexity of TabPFN with dataset size. On the TabZilla benchmark across 36 diverse datasets, MixturePFN outperforms 19 strong deep learning and tree-based baselines in mean rank and Condorcet ranking.

### Strengths
- The idea of blending Mixture of Experts with TabPFN is novel.
- The paper is well written and provides a justified solution for the context-length limitation in ICL for tabular data.
- MICP effectively reduces memory usage, allowing the model to handle larger datasets than existing TabPFN.
- CAPFN bootstrapping and finetuning appears to be an effective way to mitigate distribution shift in ICL for tabular data.
- Extensive experiments on well-established benchmarks (TabZilla) against 19 strong baselines (including tree-based and deep learning methods) demonstrate good performance in both mean rank and Condorcet ranking across 36 diverse datasets.
- Writing is easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- Reviewer 2 is more positive (score 8), while Reviewers 1 and 3 score 6, citing significant missing comparisons and ablations.
- **Missing comparison with prior work**: The paper does not compare with “Retrieval & Fine-Tuning for In-Context Tabular Models” (Thomas et al., NeurIPS 2024, also called LoCalPFN), which also proposes nearest-neighbor retrieval and finetuning for TabPFN. Authors must compare with this work and clearly highlight what is novel in MixturePFN. (R1, R3)
- **Ablation on number of clusters K vs. performance** is missing. (R1)
- **MICP’s reliance on K-means clustering**: Quality of clusters can vary significantly based on dataset properties and distance metric; poor clustering could lead to suboptimal routing and ineffective prompts. Ablations on clustering quality would be valuable. (R2)
- **CAPFN bootstrapping may introduce bias/overfitting** if sampled subsets are not representative of the entire dataset, especially in cases with imbalanced classes (e.g., 1% positive labels). The paper does not discuss this. (R2)
- **Struggles with feature-rich datasets**: MixturePFN still has difficulty with high-dimensional data (e.g., patient healthcare data), where simple XGBoost performs well. This limits applicability in domains with many features. Authors leave this to future work but should address it. (R2)
- **Categorical feature encoding**: The paper does not clarify whether categorical features are simply encoded as ordinal values, which could imply false relationships unordered elements. (R2)
- **Scalability to very large datasets**: The paper does not evaluate effectiveness on datasets with a million samples, despite aiming at scale-up. (R3)
- **Scalability to many features**: Effectiveness on datasets with hundreds or thousands of features (practical in real-world) is not demonstrated. (R3)
- **Alternative baseline suggestion**: If comparison with LoCalPFN is not possible, the paper should at least use k-nearest-neighbor sampling (not random) for TabPFN* to make the comparison fairer. (R3)
