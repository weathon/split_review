## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Exact Preference Optimization (EXO), a data distillation method for extrapolative protein design. The key idea is to use triplet-based preference optimization, rather than standard pairwise comparisons, to better capture the fitness gradient and improve the generative model’s ability to propose sequences with fitness beyond the training range. EXO is evaluated on AAV and GFP protein optimization tasks (four total) against both scorer-guided and non-scorer baselines. Results show improvements in extrapolation rate and top-100 fitness in three out of four tasks. Ablation studies examine triplet creation strategies (e.g., consecutive bins, hard negatives) and preference optimization methods. The method is praised for its simplicity and strong motivation, but also criticized for reliance on in-silico scorers, computational cost, limited dataset diversity, and clarity issues in presentation.

### Strengths

- Well-motivated use of triplet preference optimization for extrapolative protein design, which is novel to the field (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The approach intuitively approximates the fitness gradient better than pairwise methods.
- Simplicity and ease of implementation; the method is likely to be adopted by practitioners (Reviewer 2).
- Thorough ablation studies on triplet creation strategies (e.g., consecutive bins, hard negatives, scorer distillation) and preference optimization methods, each with 5 replicates, convincingly demonstrating the causal role of different components (Reviewers 1, 2). Figure 2 is particularly compelling.
- Outperforms state-of-the-art scorer-based (e.g., DBTA) and non-scorer-based methods in three of four extrapolation tasks on AAV and GFP (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Evaluation across multiple dimensions (extrapolation rate, diversity, top-100 fitness) gives a broader perspective on the exploration-exploitation trade-off (Reviewer 3).
- Analysis of the importance of difficult triplets is insightful and supports the design choices (Reviewer 4).
- The experimental validation is thorough, including ablations and multiple replicates (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Reliance on in-silico scorers and potential over-optimism:** The method depends on a scorer to define triplets, but the scorer may not generalize in out-of-distribution (extrapolative) regions and could contain biases from being trained on a subset of data. This raises concerns about the practical value without wet-lab validation (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 specifically notes that the mismatch between in-silico and in-vivo fitness may lead to over-optimistic results. The paper lacks discussion on robustness to noisy or imperfect labels, especially in pathological cases where the scorer defines difficult triplets incorrectly (Reviewer 4).
- **Computational cost and scalability:** Generating triplets, especially with scorer-based distillation, is computationally intensive and a significant bottleneck. The resource requirements may limit scalability to larger datasets or more varied protein types (Reviewer 3). This concern is strongly emphasized by Reviewer 3, while other reviewers note the reliance on scorers but focus more on bias than cost.
- **Limited dataset diversity:** Evaluation is confined to AAV and GFP, which have relatively well-studied fitness landscapes. The method’s generalizability to proteins with more complex or rugged fitness landscapes remains unclear (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 further notes that additional benchmarks on diverse protein families are needed to strengthen claims.
- **Clarity and presentation issues:** Several key components are insufficiently explained or scattered across sections:
  - The mechanism for “sampling with a scorer” is not detailed (e.g., direct probability adjustment or iterative refinement) (Reviewer 2).
  - The meaning of “scorer distillation” is confusing because the vanilla version also uses a scorer (Reviewer 4).
  - The triplet definition appears in multiple places (Sections 3.4, 4.3, 6.1) without clarity (Reviewer 4).
  - The preference loss β = 0.1 (line 284) is not disambiguated from AdamW’s β parameters (Reviewer 2).
  - Top-k, top-p sampling and the role of temperature are not explained (Reviewer 1).
  - The term \( r_\phi \) (reward) is not clarified in the main text (Reviewer 1).
- **Baseline hyperparameter selection and fairness:** The text does not explain how hyperparameters were chosen for baseline models, despite Table 11 showing that hyperparameter choice can dramatically affect performance. The lack of clarity on whether baselines were thoroughly optimized raises concerns about the fairness of comparisons (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 also asks what choices went into selecting baseline hyperparameters and whether baseline performance would change with the same level of attention.
- **Hyperparameter selection for proposed method:** The decisions for β and τ are only justified by “performing well” based on Figures 21 and 22/Tables 10 and 11, but the order and metrics used to select these values are not specified (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related work:** The related work section should include latent space optimization methods (e.g., [3]) and recent preference-based latent encoding (ICLR 2024, [2]) that encodes preference into the latent space of generative models, offering robustness to the extent of extrapolation (Reviewer 1).
- **Categorization of extrapolation methods:** The paper’s claim of a separate extrapolation category is questionable; most protein optimization methods are inherently extrapolative, with differences only in the extent (Reviewer 1, citing [1] and [2] on “separation”).
- **Methodological details require justification:**
  - Why are triplets generated from consecutive bins? The fitness landscape may have sharp local variations, making the assumption that adjacent bins are more similar questionable (Reviewer 1).
  - How is a “small meaningful improvement” determined for the perturbed seed sequence (Section 3.3, line 163)? Are there restrictions on sequence dissimilarity? (Reviewer 1).
  - The mask infilling step is not motivated; how frequently do masked infilled samples appear in the challenging triplets? (Reviewer 4).
  - The exploration-exploitation balance tuning is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure 3 (t-SNE):** Claims about “supports by unseen ground truth extrapolation sequences” should be hedged due to the stochastic nature of t-SNE and small symbol size (Reviewer 2).
- **Citation error:** The citation for [1] in the text (2023) should be 2024 (Reviewer 1).
- **Disagreement note:** While all reviewers note the reliance on an in-silico scorer, Reviewer 3 is the most critical of computational cost and generalizability, whereas Reviewer 4 and 2 focus more on the robustness of the scorer and clarity. This contrast should be kept in mind when assessing the severity of these weaknesses.
