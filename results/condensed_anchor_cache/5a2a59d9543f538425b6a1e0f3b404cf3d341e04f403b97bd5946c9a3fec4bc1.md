- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 6, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel Conditional Information Bottleneck (CIB) approach for time series imputation, extending vanilla IB to better preserve temporal dependencies. The method is supported by theoretical bound derivations and empirical experiments on several real-world datasets. Reviewers rated the paper 6, 6, 6, and 8, agreeing on the novelty of the CIB idea and the solid theoretical analysis, but raising concerns about experimental completeness, missing comparisons with recent SOTA models, and clarity of some motivations and derivations.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured, clear, and easy to follow, with a well-described motivation and derivation (including tricky points like the chain rule in Eq.5). The related work section demonstrates awareness of recent missing-data imputation methods. Results are concise and clear.
- The extension from information bottleneck to conditional information bottleneck (CIB) is a novel theoretical contribution that opens promising research avenues for VAE-based time series imputation.
- The theoretical analysis is solid and persuasive; the bound derivations are interesting and provide a principled justification.
- The smoothing of latent representation via the temporal kernel is a novel and interesting idea.
- The model effectively addresses the issue of temporal information loss in the latent space of VAEs.
- The method is inspired from a theoretical IB background and can produce stochastic imputations.

### Weaknesses
- **Notation complexity** occasionally hinders readability (R1).
- **Lack of experimental details:** No information is provided about the encoder/decoder architectures for HealingMNIST, RotatedMNIST, or Physionet; hyperparameters (e.g., β, γ) are not discussed; number of samples used in Eq.4 and sensitivity to that number are not reported; the number of learnable parameters is not given, making it hard to compare computational cost (R1, R4).
- **Code availability:** No code or repository is provided, which is especially important given the consistently strong results (R1, R2).
- **Motivation of conditional term:** The intuitive explanation of why conditioning on the observed time steps preserves temporal structure is insufficient; more rigorous justification is needed (R2).
- **Superior performance concerns:** The method outperforms all baselines across all datasets and metrics, which raises doubts. The three-term loss (Eq.8) is likely difficult to optimize, yet no discussion of optimization trade-offs or β/γ influence is provided (R1).
- **GPVAE reference:** The temporal kernel idea bears similarity to GPVAE; proper referencing should be strengthened (R1).
- **Ablation study missing:** No ablation disentangles the contributions of different loss terms or the importance of the temporal kernel. Would the method still outperform baselines without the kernel? (R1)
- **Equation issues:** In Eq.7, the denominator sums over X but the expression contains only z; the equation could be clarified (R1).
- **Table 3 anomaly:** Mean imputation achieves the best RMSE on some data, suggesting that error metrics may be inconclusive for temporal scenarios. Missing data in bursts/sequences should be considered (R1).
- **Outdated baselines:** The compared baselines are not state-of-the-art; missing comparisons with recent methods including ODE-based models (e.g., mTAN), diffusion approaches (e.g., CSDI), and other generative models. This makes the evaluation less comprehensive and less convincing (R2, R3, R4).
- **Incremental innovation:** While clever, the approach may be seen as a technique or trick rather than a substantial paradigm shift (R3).
- **Lack of quantitative analysis for regularization claim:** The paper claims CIB mitigates excessive regularization, but provides no explicit metrics or criteria to measure regularization extent (R3).
- **Overfitting risk:** Reduced regularization might lead to overfitting, but no empirical evidence or theoretical analysis is given to show how the method balances fit and generalization (R3).
- **Use of MNIST for temporal dependencies:** The MNIST-based example (Fig.1) is dubious for demonstrating temporal dynamics in multivariate irregular time series (e.g., weather, traffic). How CIB handles multivariate dependencies is not addressed (R3).
- **Bound tightness in Eq.4:** Dropping both the entropy term (justified as parameter-independent) and the KL term (assuming good variational approximation) may make the lower bound too loose, resembling reconstruction loss rather than mutual information. The impact of these approximations on optimization and bound tightness needs empirical validation (R4).
- **Contrast among reviewers:** One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, emphasizing the theoretical contributions, but also raised concerns about bound tightness, missing baselines (mTAN, CSDI), and lack of parameter count.
