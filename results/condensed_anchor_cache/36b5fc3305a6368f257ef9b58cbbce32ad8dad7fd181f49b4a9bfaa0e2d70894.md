## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces AVCaps, an audio-visual captioning dataset with separate textual captions for audio, visual, and combined audio-visual content. It contains 2061 video clips (28.8 hours) from VidOR, manually annotated by crowdsourcing (up to 5 captions per modality) and supplemented with LLM-generated audio-visual captions. The authors present multimodal and crossmodal captioning and retrieval experiments using a baseline model (ResNet3D for video, PANNs for audio, GPT-2 decoder) and claim that models trained on LLM-generated captions achieve 14% higher Sentence-BERT similarity on ground-truth audio captions. The dataset is intended to support studies of modality contributions and future applications in multimodal representation learning, QA, and generative AI.

### Strengths
- **Modality-specific captions fill a gap:** The dataset provides separate captions for audio, visual, and audio-visual content, enabling analysis of each modality’s contribution. Reviewers 1 and 2 consider this a significant contribution; Reviewer 2 notes it reduces the common bias where visual content dominates.
- **Manual annotation ensures quality:** All reviewers acknowledge the dataset is labeled by human annotators (crowdsourced) and includes a thorough cleaning process (automated error correction + manual relevance screening). Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 highlight this as a strength. Reviewer 4 also notes that LLM-generated captions provide additional high-quality descriptions.
- **Dataset construction is legitimate:** Reviewer 2 finds the construction reasonable and the bias-reduction approach (generating LLM-based captions and evaluating them) interesting. The authors claim the dataset will be freely available.
- **Experiments illustrate the dataset’s utility:** Reviewers 1 and 4 note that the designed captioning and retrieval experiments demonstrate the value of modality-specific captions. Reviewer 4 finds the experiments “meticulously designed” (though see weaknesses for contrasting views).
- **Potential future applications:** Reviewer 2 points out the dataset supports multimodal representation learning, GenAI, and related tasks.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset scale:** All four reviewers mention the small size (2061 clips, 28.8 hours). Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 specifically question whether this is sufficient for multimodal representation learning and robust evaluation. Reviewer 4 asks: “What can this scale of the dataset do?”
- **Lack of novelty / existing prior work:** Reviewers 3 and 4 argue that audio-visual caption datasets already exist (VALOR, VAST, Panda, InternVid, MMTrail), diminishing novelty. Reviewer 2 also notes missing citations for related works on audio-visual captioning (Multi-modal Dense Video Captioning, [2,3,4]) and criticizes the absence of a dedicated related work section.
- **Outdated/basic models used in experimentation:** Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 criticize the baseline model (ResNet3D + PANNs + GPT-2) as outdated. Reviewer 2 asks why not use a visual transformer or a modern VLM (e.g., LLaVA). Reviewer 3 notes PANNs and ResNet3D are outdated. Reviewer 4 asks for comparisons with modern models on the dataset.
- **Missing comparisons with other datasets/models:** Reviewers 3 and 4 state there are no experiments comparing the proposed dataset against other datasets (e.g., VALOR, VAST) nor using open-source models. Reviewer 4 specifically asks: “What is the performance of your models in other datasets?” and “What is the performance of another model in your dataset and your setting?” This also relates to Reviewer 2’s request for advanced baseline results.
- **Evaluation metric concerns:** Reviewer 1 questions the justification of using similarity between audio-visual captions and audio/visual captions as an evaluation metric, warning about information errors and redundancy. They suggest additional experiments: (1) generating LLM audio-visual captions from only audio and visual captions (without the audio-visual caption) and measuring changes, and (2) using GPT-4 to generate audio-visual captions from audio+visual captions and comparing to references. Reviewer 2 agrees that Sentence-BERT similarity may not capture richness (atomic actions, object attributes, etc.). Reviewer 4 finds the single-modality evaluation confusing in purpose.
- **Unclear task definition and dataset purpose:** Reviewer 4 finds the task definition unclear and asks for clarification on the specific applications this dataset enables.
- **Missing analysis of LLM-generated captions:** Reviewer 1 asks for an analysis table (similar to Table 1 for crowdsourced captions) for the LLM-generated captions to highlight differences.
- **Modality overlap determination:** Reviewer 2 asks how the authors determine whether a caption leans to audio or visual when elements inherently overlap.
- **Disagreement on experimental validation:** Reviewers 1 and 2 find the experiments illustrative and the metric gains interesting (14% improvement), while Reviewers 3 and 4 criticize the lack of comparisons and weak validation. Reviewer 4 states “the proposed experiments do not sufficiently support the method’s claims.” Reviewer 3 sees no comparison at all.
