## Merged Review

### Summary

FlatQuant is a post-training quantization method for LLMs that enhances weight and activation flatness via per-layer learnable affine transformations optimized with a lightweight objective. To reduce runtime overhead, the transformation matrix is decomposed via Kronecker decomposition and fused with quantization into a single kernel. The method also uses per-channel scaling and learnable clipping thresholds. Experiments achieve state-of-the-art W4A4 quantization: less than 1% accuracy drop on LLaMA-3-70B, surpassing SpinQuant by 7.5%. Inference speedup reaches up to 2.3× for prefill and 1.7× for decoding, with transformation overhead reduced from QuaRot's 0.26× to 0.07×.

### Strengths

1. **Theoretical insight and motivation.** The observation that flatness is the main influence on quantization error is insightful and well-supported. The attempt to improve flatness through learnable transformations is theoretically sound. (Reviewer 1)
2. **Kronecker decomposition effectiveness.** Decomposing the large transformation matrix **P** into two smaller matrices effectively reduces memory and computation while maintaining performance. The varying decomposition size has limited perplexity impact, and speedup peaks when the two Kronecker factors are of equal size. (Reviewers 1, 2)
3. **Learnable clipping threshold.** Introducing a learnable clipping threshold improves upon the typical grid search, and the paper shows notable accuracy gains from this component. (Reviewers 2, 4)
4. **Efficient CUDA kernel fusion.** The fused kernel reduces inference overhead substantially: only 0.07× slowdown from transformations vs. QuaRot's 0.26×, enabling up to 2.3× speedup for prefill and 1.7× for decoding. This addresses the doubled computation and memory access from pre-quantization transformations in AffineQuant. (Reviewers 1, 4)
5. **Strong empirical results.** Extensive experiments across model sizes (including LLaMA-3-70B) and tasks show consistent improvements. The method achieves less than 1% accuracy drop for W4A4 on LLaMA-3-70B and outperforms SpinQuant by 7.5%. (Reviewers 1, 5)
6. **Clear figures.** The figures aid understanding of the proposed pipeline. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Invertibility of transformation **P** not clarified.** AffineQuant ensures invertibility via diagonal dominance; it is unclear how FlatQuant maintains **P** invertible during training. The use of a re-parameterization technique (e.g., SVD) should be explicitly stated and justified, along with a discussion of its stability and computational overhead compared to direct matrix inversion. (Reviewers 4, 5)
2. **Missing details on Kronecker decomposition methodology.** The paper does not specify how Kronecker factors are selected, the decomposition method, dimensions of the decomposed matrices, and the computational cost of decomposition. It is also unclear whether the decomposition is applied to the full transformation matrix or to sub-blocks, and how this choice affects performance. Approximation errors introduced by the Kronecker decomposition and their impact on model performance are not analyzed. A comparison between the full transformation matrix and its Kronecker approximation, quantifying error and correlation with performance degradation, is needed. The trade-off between decomposition rank and accuracy/efficiency should be explored. (Reviewer 5)
3. **Missing theoretical complexity analysis.** The evaluation lacks detailed computation complexity analysis, total parameter counts (including decomposed transform matrices), and comprehensive memory consumption analysis for inference. (Reviewer 1)
4. **Unfair comparison with QuaRot and SpinQuant regarding computational overhead.** Reviewer 3 argues that the comparison is unfair because FlatQuant requires many floating-point matrix multiplications online, while QuaRot can merge most transformation matrices into the weights and only the rotation in Query/Key and after SiLU need online floating-point computation. FlatQuant’s per-input pre-quantization must be applied more than six times per LLaMA block (O(n) memory, O(n log n) overhead), whereas SpinQuant requires only one pre-quantization transformation per block. The Speed Up comparison in Figure 6 is biased: QuaRot’s floating-point computation is less than FlatQuant’s, and the Kronecker fusion reduces Hadamard runtime under GEMM (not Walsh–Hadamard). The authors should provide additional comparisons on computational complexity and parameter quantity between FlatQuant and SpinQuant. (Reviewers 3, 4)
5. **Limited model architecture coverage.** Only the LLaMA family is tested. Performance on other architectures (e.g., Gemini, Qwen) is not demonstrated. It is also unclear whether different architectures would require different decomposition strategies. (Reviewer 1)
6. **Learnable clipping threshold analysis lacking.** The method is not compared with existing CNN learnable clipping works (e.g., Leveraging Inter-Layer Dependency for Post-Training Quantization). There is no detailed analysis on why important outliers are not clipped (referencing LLM.int8() concerns). (Reviewer 2)
7. **Missing weight-only quantization results.** The paper does not report zero-shot performance for weight-only quantization. As shown in other works, LWC can cause overfitting, and the perplexity metric may not reflect true performance. Weight-only results should be provided. (Reviewer 4)
8. **Questionable training speed comparison with OmniQuant.** FlatQuant adds the inverse affine matrix, pre-quantization, SVD decomposition, and learnable activation clipping, yet is reported to be 10× faster than OmniQuant, which only trains smooth parameters. A detailed breakdown of timing comparisons, including implementation details and hardware used for both methods, is needed. (Reviewer 4)
9. **Source of perplexity improvement unclear.** In Table 3, the difference between SpinQuant and FlatQuant may be largely due to the learnable clipping threshold. The paper should isolate the effect of the learnable clipping threshold component and also evaluate its pure contribution (without other components). The ablation study should include separate evaluations of “PS”, “LCT”, and “PS+LCT” to comprehensively demonstrate improvements from each component. Additionally, the individual contributions of αₐ and α_w should be separated. (Reviewers 2, 5)
10. **Perplexity without Kronecker decomposition not reported.** The paper does not show performance when training the full (non-decomposed) transformation matrix directly (i.e., without decomposition). (Reviewer 1)
11. **Sensitivity to initialization and calibration data not discussed.** The impact of different initialization strategies for the learned parameters and the choice of calibration data selection is not explored. (Reviewer 1)
12. **Figure 3 is confusing.** The caption and subfigures need better explanation of the relationships and flow of operations. (Reviewer 1)
13. **Asymmetric speedup curve in Figure 5.** The speedup curve is not symmetric; the speedup at dimension 2048 is substantially slower than at 2. (Reviewer 2)
14. **Inference remains non-pure integer.** Even with Kronecker decomposition, online transformation is still required, so pure integer computation is not achieved. (Reviewer 2)
15. **Fundamental flatness-quantization error correlation not strict.** Reviewer 3 provides a counterexample: [3,4,5,6] has significantly higher quantization error at 2-bit than [0,0,0,√86], even though the former is “flatter.” The paper only optimizes Eq. (4) and does not establish a direct correlation with flatness. (Reviewer 3)
16. **Rationale for per-channel scaling unclear.** Since a properly designed **P′** (e.g., **P′** = **P** diag(c)) can already balance outliers between weights and activations, the justification for adding separate per-channel scaling is not provided. (Reviewer 5)
17. **Missing distribution visualizations.** The paper does not show the distribution of **W_d**, **W_q**, **W_k**, **W_v** and their corresponding activations (visualizations like Fig. 2 would be beneficial). (Reviewer 5)
18. **Speedup on Hopper GPUs not reported.** Results are not shown for H100/H800 architectures. (Reviewer 5)
19. **Citation gap.** A relevant learnable clipping threshold work (Duanmu et al., SKVQ, 2024) is not cited. (Reviewer 1)
