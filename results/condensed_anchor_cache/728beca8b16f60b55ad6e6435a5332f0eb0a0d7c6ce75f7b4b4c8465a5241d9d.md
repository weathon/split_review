## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MathGAP, a framework for evaluating LLMs on arithmetic word problems with arbitrarily complex proofs, addressing data contamination and the lack of systematic complexity in existing benchmarks. Problems are generated from proof trees with controlled depth, width, linearity, and ordering, along with chain-of-thought annotations. Experiments on multiple state-of-the-art LLMs show that performance degrades with increasing proof complexity, especially for nonlinear proofs, and that in-context examples from the same distribution are not always beneficial. The paper is well-written and presents its methodology clearly. However, reviewers had mixed views on the depth of new insights, the breadth of the evaluation, and the timeliness of the models tested.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a critical evaluation gap:** Tackles underexplored aspects of LLM generalization by separating data contamination and enabling controlled studies of proof complexity (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Provides a way to generate new problems on the fly, avoiding fixed datasets (Reviewer 3).
- **Well-structured formalism and clarity:** Clear use of proof trees, logical forms, and properties (depth/width/linearity/ordering). Figures and writing are easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Section 3 provides good background (Reviewer 4).
- **Comprehensive experiments across multiple complexity dimensions:** Tests depth, width, linearity, and ordering on several LLMs, giving insights into model limitations and in-context learning behavior (Reviewers 1, 2). Studies OOD generalization in a controlled manner (Reviewer 3).
- **Interesting findings on in-context learning:** Reveals that zero-shot prompting and showing less complex examples sometimes yield higher accuracy than in-distribution prompts (Reviewers 1, 3). Highlights sensitivity to problem structure and ordering (Reviewer 1).
- **Necessary objective evaluation tool:** Provides a solid evaluation metric separate from hype (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of insights:** Several results (e.g., performance drops with depth/width, OOD difficulty) are already reported in prior work (Dziri et al., Fig. 5). The paper does not sufficiently explain *why* models fail, e.g., why Llama3-70B is near-perfect on linear depth, why nonlinear problems are harder even at small depth, and why ordering distance affects performance nonlinearly. (Reviewer 2, 4)
- **Shallow analysis of model errors and failure modes:** Lacks deep error categorization (arithmetic vs. logical, reasoning path errors, parsing errors). It is unclear whether performance degradation is simply due to constant error rate per step (more steps → lower success) or genuine reasoning breakdown. Impact of number of shots and its differential effect on linear/nonlinear setups is not explored. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Missing recent state-of-the-art models:** Lacks Llama 3.1, Llama 3.2, GPT-o1, which are highly competitive on linear problems. Including them could change conclusions about OOD generalization and the claim that nonlinear proofs remain challenging (Reviewer 4). As a result, the current conclusions may be outdated (Reviewer 4).
- **OOD generalization vs. pure difficulty confound:** The performance drop may simply reflect increasing problem difficulty rather than a generalization gap. The fact that in-distribution examples do not always improve performance supports this alternative interpretation (Reviewer 4).
- **Synthetic data and constrained setup:** Only 5 predicates and 5 inference rules are used, leading to repetitive linguistic patterns and simple arithmetic. Models may exploit superficial patterns, overestimating true reasoning ability. The lack of diversity in templates, numbers, irrelevant information, and units limits generalizability. (Reviewers 1, 4) Would be interesting to expand to other reasoning types (Reviewer 3) and study scaling with more predicates/rules (Reviewer 4).
- **Assumption that LLM reasoning aligns with proof trees:** The evaluation assumes models follow the predefined logical form, but LLMs might use heuristics or pattern-matching not captured by the framework, affecting accuracy of the assessment (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing finetuning analysis:** The paper focuses on in-context learning but does not study how finetuning (especially on controlled proof complexities) affects OOD generalization (Reviewer 3).
- **Unexplained choice of predicates:** Different predicates are used for different analyses (e.g., comp/transfer for depth, part-whole for width) without justification, potentially confounding results (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority reviewer disagreement on overall contribution:** While two reviewers rated the paper 8 and found it insightful, two rated it 6, noting that the new insights are limited and the analysis is shallow. Reviewer 2 considers the work borderline in its current form.
