## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Sharpness-Aware Trajectory Matching (SATM), which improves trajectory-matching based dataset condensation by minimizing sharpness in the outer loop of bilevel optimization and using efficient hypergradient approximation techniques. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical contributions and the relevance of generalization in dataset condensation. However, concerns are raised about missing comparisons with recent SOTA methods, insufficient experimental support for claimed computational/memory reductions, limited ablation studies, unclear evaluation settings, and marginal performance gains at higher IPC. Disagreement exists among reviewers regarding the novelty and theoretical rigor of the method.

### Strengths
- Good topic: generalization capability in dataset condensation is underexplored (R1).
- Overall writing and presentation are satisfactory (R1); manuscript is well-structured (R4); easy to follow (R5).
- SATM and the proposed techniques (hypergradient approximation, trajectory reusing) are novel (R1, R2). The integration of sharpness-aware minimization into trajectory matching is theoretically sound with comprehensive mathematical proofs (R2, R4, R5).
- Hypergradient approximation strategies reduce computational overhead in theory and make SATM adaptable (R2).
- SATM demonstrates robust performance gains over baseline methods in low-IPC settings (R1, R2) and is compatible with other sharpness-aware minimizers (R2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing comparisons with SOTA methods**: Several recent methods are not compared against: DATM (R1, R5), RDED, CUDD, “Towards Lossless Dataset Distillation via Difficulty-Aligned Trajectory Matching”, “On the Diversity and Realism of Distilled Dataset”, “Curriculum Dataset Distillation” (R1). In Table 2, DATM is notably absent (R5).
2. **Insufficient experimental validation of claimed efficiency**: The paper claims reduced computation overhead, time complexity, and memory cost, yet provides no practical computational cost comparison or real-time/memory benchmarks with existing methods (R4, R5). The baseline Tesla (Cui et al., NeurIPS 2022) already enables ImageNet-1K distillation; evaluation on ImageNet-1K would be beneficial to support memory claims (R4).
3. **Limited ablation studies**: Only one ablation study (on sharpness-aware optimizer) is conducted. Ablation on the proposed trajectory reusing and hypergradient truncating methods is missing (R5). A trade-off analysis between truncated steps and performance is needed (R2).
4. **Marginal performance at high IPC and limited architecture/dataset scale**: Compared to FTD (which also uses SAM), SATM’s improvement becomes marginal as IPC increases (<0.2% on CIFAR at IPC=50). Is the method only effective for low IPC? (R5). Results on larger architectures (ResNet-101) and datasets (ImageNet-1K) are absent (R1, R4).
5. **Unclear evaluation settings**: It is not clearly stated whether ZCA whitening, EMA, and DSA augmentation are used (R5). Specific settings for DC, DM, MMT experiments should be listed (e.g., learning rate) (R3).
6. **Theoretical questions**:  
   - Proposition 3.1: Clarify whether αJ is >1 or <1; if αJ>1 the inequality appears incorrect. The trade-off between number of truncation steps and performance should be discussed (R2).  
   - Theorem 3.2: θ is a vector, so Δθ<sub>τ</sub> is a vector, not a scalar. The bound on ||Δθ<sub>τ</sub>|| makes more sense; an upper bound on Δθ<sub>τ</sub> does not necessarily imply θ<sub>τ</sub> is close to ˆθ<sub>τ</sub> (R2).  
   - Why is sharpness minimization particularly beneficial for dataset condensation? SAM is known to impose computational burden; a stronger motivation is needed (R2).
7. **Concerns about innovation and originality**: Some reviewers consider the method a minor combination of BO/MTT with SAM, lacking a new data compression method and focusing only on optimization cost (R3). The baseline experimental data is identical to that in “Minimizing the Accumulated Trajectory Error to Improve Dataset Distillation”; authors should clarify any connection or overlapping authorship (R3). (Note: R1 and R2 view the contribution as novel; R3 and R4 find it less innovative.)
8. **Formatting issues**: Missing space between “min” and $\mathcal{L}$; Figure 1 has an incomplete label for “training iteration” (R2).
