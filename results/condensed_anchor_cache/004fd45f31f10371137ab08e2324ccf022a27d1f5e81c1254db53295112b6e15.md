## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles dataset pruning for knowledge distillation (KD) by selecting medium-difficulty samples, arguing they form a smoother decision boundary for the student than hard or easy samples. It additionally introduces a logit reshaping technique that uses global class-wise statistics (standard deviation and mean alignment) to mitigate distributional shift from pruning. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet with multiple teacher-student architectures show that the static method outperforms existing static pruning techniques (e.g., Herding, Forgetting, MoDS) and achieves comparable or superior performance to dynamic methods like InfoBatch, while halving training time on ImageNet at a 50% keep ratio and improving accuracy by 0.4%.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and explanation**: The paper clearly articulates the problem of decision boundary drift in KD due to the student’s limited capacity, and why hard samples worsen this drift. This explanation is considered a main novelty. (R2, R4)
- **Methodical innovation**: Selecting medium-difficulty samples for KD and using global logit reshaping are novel concepts. The visualizations (Figure 3) are clear and insightful. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Thoroughly tested on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet with various teacher-student pairs. Ablation studies and comparisons with multiple pruning methods (static and dynamic) provide a holistic view. (R1, R2)
- **Efficiency gains with maintained/improved performance**: Significant reduction in training time (e.g., 50% on ImageNet with a 50% keep ratio) without sacrificing student accuracy; even improves accuracy by 0.4% on ImageNet at 50% keep ratio. At 70% keep ratio, accuracy exceeds vanilla KD with 30% time reduction. Practical utility for resource-constrained settings. (R1, R2)
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: Methodology is simple to implement and clearly described. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work**: The overall methodological advances feel incremental compared to Moderate Dataset Selection (MoDS) and existing reshaping techniques. (R3 – minority, negative score 3)
- **Weak value proposition**: The time savings are for the KD training phase, not inference (which is more important). Accuracy degradation is always present with pruning; meaningful time savings require very small keep ratios, which cause unacceptable accuracy drops. (R3 – minority)
- **Insufficient theoretical justification for medium-difficulty samples**: The paper lacks rigorous mathematical analysis formalizing why medium-difficulty samples lead to smoother decision boundaries. Needed: analysis of loss landscape Hessian, gradient stability (e.g., variance of gradients per difficulty level), Fisher information, or geometric measures like class centroid distance evolution and boundary curvature across difficulty levels. (R2)
- **Insufficient analysis of logit reshaping**:
  - No sensitivity analysis of how reshaping affects per-class performance, e.g., KL divergence between teacher and student logit distributions per class, especially under class imbalance or noisy labels. (R2)
  - No ablation isolating contributions of the standard deviation adjustment vs. mean alignment component. (R2)
  - No evaluation of whether reshaping introduces bias or overfitting (e.g., train/validation performance gap, adversarial robustness). (R2)
- **Lack of quantitative metric for decision boundary drift**: No proposed metric (e.g., Hausdorff distance, mutual information alignment) to measure boundary drift between teacher and student, nor analysis of how drift changes during training with different difficulty selections. (R2)
- **No difficulty spectrum analysis**: The paper only compares easy/medium/hard categories; a continuous analysis (e.g., student performance as a function of teacher loss per sample) would better justify why medium-difficulty is optimal. (R2)
- **Limited evaluation on student capacity variation**: It is unclear how the method performs when the student’s learning capability is close to or very far from the teacher’s. If the pruned set works only for certain students, the method’s generality fails. (R3)
- **Missing comparisons with other KD methods and techniques**: No comparison with self-distillation, feature-based or attention-based KD, data distillation, or few-shot KD approaches (e.g., Ye, ICLR 2024 on Bayesian conditional distribution estimation). (R1, R3)
- **Scalability and generalizability concerns**: Not evaluated on more complex architectures (e.g., deeper ResNets, Wide ResNets) or on non-vision domains (NLP, time-series). (R1, R2)
- **Figure 2 is out of context**: It shows a “large accuracy gap” between teacher and student, but this is typical in KD and does not specifically demonstrate the degradation due to pruning. Better to show accuracy plots for easy, medium, and hard pruning. (R4)
- **Equation (3) M_DIF motivation unclear**: It is not clear whether M_DIF is a separate pruning algorithm or only used for discussion in Section 3.2. (R4)
- **No hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: The paper does not systematically explore how performance varies with different keep ratios or distillation loss functions beyond the standard KD loss. (R1)
