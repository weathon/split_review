## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes two normalizing-flow-based evaluation metrics for generated images: FLD (single flow) and D-FLD (dual flow). The metrics use exact likelihood from flows to measure alignment between real and generated distributions. The authors claim advantages over FID: sample efficiency (stabilizing with hundreds of images vs. tens of thousands), lower parameter count (1.9M vs. Inception-V3’s 23.8M), and monotonic sensitivity to image distortions. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ with synthetic distortions and DDPM-generated samples are presented. The paper is generally well-structured and addresses a practically relevant problem, but reviewers raise major concerns about empirical breadth, reliability of likelihood as a metric, computational fairness, and presentation issues.

### Strengths

- **Sample efficiency**: FLD stabilizes rapidly with only 100–200 samples, enabling evaluation of small generated sets and validation batches inside training loops (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Interpretability**: Flow-based likelihood distance is straightforward to understand (Reviewer 3).
- **Sensitivity to distortions**: FLD shows monotonic behavior and higher sensitivity to image degradation (e.g., Gaussian noise) compared to FID (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Computational footprint**: The flow network has over an order of magnitude fewer parameters than Inception-V3, and the metric can be computed with smaller models (Reviewer 1). This is especially beneficial for new domains where retraining is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Addresses a real need**: FID has known limitations; developing alternative metrics is topical and practically relevant (Reviewers 2,4). The approach overcomes the normality assumption in FID’s Fréchet distance (Reviewer 4).
- **Flexibility**: Metrics work effectively for different counts of real and generated image sets (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient generative model evaluation**: The metrics are tested only on synthetic distortions and one generative model (DDPM) on two relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ). Reviewers 2 and 4 insist that at least several real (e.g., GAN, VAE) generative models should be compared to demonstrate robustness. Reviewer 1 also asks whether FLD/D-FLD trends match FID trends when evaluating SOTA models.

2. **Limited comparison to other metrics**: Only FID is compared. Reviewer 2 notes CMMD outperforms FID in sensitivity and should be included. Reviewer 4 also asks for comparison to IS and KID. No experiment compares FLD and D-FLD on the same dataset (Reviewer 2).

3. **Reliability of likelihood as a metric**: Reviewer 3 questions whether likelihood is reliable for deep generative models, citing Nalisnick et al. (2019) [1] (flows lack calibration) and Theis et al. (2016) [2] (likelihood and visual fidelity are largely independent in high-dimensional data). Heusel et al. (2017) [3] note FID aligns better with human perception. The paper does not demonstrate that FLD is well-calibrated or capable of out-of-domain detection. Reviewer 3 recommends analyzing data distribution and model curvature to substantiate reliability.

4. **Fairness of parameter efficiency claim**: Reviewer 3 argues that Inception-V3 (23.8M) is over-parameterized for CIFAR-10, making the comparison to a 1.9M flow model potentially unfair. The 1.9M flow may not reliably model ImageNet1K; scalability to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet, Laion5B) is unaddressed. Reviewer 4 also notes a conflict: the conclusion says large-dataset limitations are due to computational constraints, but Section 4.4 claims low parameter count allows training on edge devices.

5. **Convergence and variance analysis**: Figure 1 shows FLD stabilizes quickly, but Reviewer 3 asks for variance bars for FID and comparison of relative variance (variance/mean) between the two metrics. The paper does not state how many real and generated images were used in experiments (e.g., Figures 4,5) (Reviewer 2).

6. **Robustness to small noise**: Reviewers 3 and 4 note that Gaussian noise level 0.1 strongly affects FLD, raising suspicion that FLD may be vulnerable to adversarial noise. Reviewer 4 points out that the hypothesis of high sensitivity being advantageous is not experimentally confirmed.

7. **Computational cost missing**: No comparison of training or inference time between flow models and Inception for computing metrics. Reviewers 2 and 4 request time measurements. The claim that FLD is faster is unsubstantiated.

8. **Diffusion model presentation confusion**: Reviewer 4 notes that the paper refers to diffusion timesteps as x_1000 (noise step) instead of standard timestamps (x_0 is real data), which may confuse readers. Figures 6 and 7 use this non-standard notation. Reviewer 2 notes Figure 7 has unclear FID monotonicity (two points with same FID value?).

9. **Figures missing details and inconsistencies**:
   - Figure 1 does not specify the dataset used (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 recommends adding D-FLD and FID to Figure 1.
   - Figure 4 uses linear X-axis, while Figure 5 uses logarithmic; log transformation may oversimplify comparison (Reviewer 4). FLD should also be shown in Figure 4.
   - Figure 8 y-axis label likely should be “FLD”; D-FLD and FID should be added (Reviewer 2).

10. **Need for formal introduction of other metrics**: Reviewer 2 suggests adding a mathematical description of FID and a table comparing properties of different metrics.

11. **Generalization and retraining**: FLD requires training a flow model per dataset, which may limit generalization compared to FID (which uses ImageNet pre-trained Inception). Reviewer 1 questions if the model must be retrained per dataset; Reviewer 4 asks whether hyperparameter selection is dataset-dependent and seeks recommendations.

12. **Minimum dataset size for training the flow**: Reviewer 4 asks what size of real dataset is needed to consistently train the flow model for meaningful FLD results.

13. **Sensitivity to flow architecture**: Reviewer 2 asks how sensitive the metrics are to the architecture used for normalizing flows (ablation missing).

14. **Probabilistic models are already likelihood-based**: Reviewer 3 notes diffusion models can compute likelihood without additional training; the paper should clarify the precision loss when using a smaller 1.9M flow model versus full diffusion likelihood.
