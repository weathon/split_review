- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces *graph reconstructability* as a new measure of GNN expressivity: the ability to recover the input graph’s topology from learned node embeddings. Theoretical results show that when node features are initialized with **identity features**, both GCN and GIN can reconstruct the graph; with **contextual features** (label information plus noise), only GIN succeeds. To reduce the large embedding dimension needed with identity features, the paper proposes **Nearly Orthogonal Random Features (NORF)** and the **Graph Reconstructable Neural Network (GRNN)**. Experiments on synthetic graphs and on the real-world datasets **Pubmed, Actor, and DBLP** evaluate link prediction and community detection. While some reviewers found the perspective novel and the theoretical analysis valuable, others argued the contribution is incremental, the motivation unclear, and the experiments insufficient.

### Strengths
- The question of whether graph topology can be reconstructed from learned node embeddings appears novel in the context of GNNs. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The paper provides novel theoretical results on the ability of GCN and GIN to reconstruct graphs based on identity and contextual features. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- GRNN with NORFs is shown to outperform baseline methods (GCN, GIN, GAT, SGC) in community detection and link prediction tasks on several datasets. (Reviewer 2)
- The theoretical analysis is well structured, proofs are provided in detail in the appendix, and the paper is generally easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The paper studies the expressive power of GNNs from a new perspective, which has some significance in theoretical GNN studies, and the proposed approach differs from existing works. (Reviewers 3, 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution and unclear motivation**: Several reviewers found the contribution incremental and the motivation unclear. The notion of graph reconstructability essentially encodes adjacency-list information into node features, which is fundamentally different from the ability to learn useful graph properties (e.g., cycles, cliques). Adding unique identity features (or NORFs) means the input graph can always be reconstructed trivially, but this does not imply the GNN can extract structural properties relevant for downstream tasks. (Reviewers 3, 4) The motivation “why reconstruct the input graph when it is already given” is not convincingly addressed, and the cost of adding identity/contextual features is high while the benefit for representation learning remains unclear. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing discussion on relationship between reconstructability and expressivity, especially for graph-level tasks**: There is no discussion or evaluation of how graph reconstructability relates to the expressive power of GNNs for graph-level tasks (e.g., graph classification). Although positional encodings and identity features are known to improve expressivity beyond 1-WL, the paper does not provide further theoretical results linking reconstructability and expressivity, nor does it include graph prediction experiments comparing GRNN to state-of-the-art expressive GNN models. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4) The question of whether high reconstructability naturally leads to more expressive graph-level representations remains unanswered. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient and incomplete experiments**:
  - **Link prediction and community detection**: The reported improvements of GRNN over baselines are not consistent across all datasets (e.g., Table 1 shows GRNN best only on the Actor dataset; on other datasets baseline methods are comparable). (Reviewer 2) The experiments lack several well-established benchmarks (e.g., OGB link-prediction datasets ogbl-ppa, ogbl-collab, ogbl-ddi) and comparisons to recent strong labeling tricks such as DRNL in SEAL and Distance Encoding. (Reviewer 3) The community detection task should include at least one more benchmark dataset. (Reviewer 3) The experimental setup for Figure 1 is not fully explained (what is $\epsilon$ in Fig. 1b? which features are used in Fig. 1b-c?). (Reviewer 2)
  - **Node classification**: The main paper focuses on link prediction, while node classification experiments (which are arguably more meaningful for a node-level reconstruction perspective) are relegated to the appendix. (Reviewer 1) Results tables lack bolding for the best results, making them hard to interpret, and the effectiveness of NORF would be clearer if performance changes relative to identity features (IF) were shown. (Reviewer 1)
  - **Baseline tuning and configuration**: Details on how baseline methods (GCN, GIN, GAT, SGC) were tuned are missing, making it difficult to assess the significance of reported improvements. (Reviewer 2) The implementation of CommDGI and SEAL in the link prediction experiments is not described. (Reviewer 3) Plots are very small and difficult to read. (Reviewer 2)
- **Loss of permutation invariance**: Using identity features or NORFs explicitly breaks the permutation invariance of GNNs. This is a critical property for learning structures; two isomorphic graphs with different identity features would become non-isomorphic under such representations. Reviewers note that this makes the graph reconstructability problem trivial and undermines the standard notion of GNN expressiveness. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Theoretical rigor and clarity issues**:
  - Definitions are imprecise or ambiguous. For example, Definition 1 could be misinterpreted as a machine learning model predicting the adjacency matrix from raw node features. (Reviewer 2) The “affliction matrix” in Proposition 7 is not defined (likely a typo for “affiliation matrix”). (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - Several propositions (e.g., Propositions 6 and 7) are stated in natural language without formalization, and the proofs contain unclear notation (e.g., $E$ in the appendix) and imprecise statements (e.g., equating the inner product of embeddings with Jaccard similarity). (Reviewer 2) The “only if” part of Proposition 1 is unclear. (Reviewer 4) Explanations of $\tau_l$, $\tau_u$, and the omission of “-1” in proofs of Propositions 2 and 3 are missing. (Reviewer 4)
  - The definition of contextual features assumes label information is available everywhere; it is unclear whether signal means label information, and what “central node’s identity” means in the context of contextual features. (Reviewer 4)
  - The paper claims that GRNN adds a weight $w_j$ before every $\mathbf{h}_j$ in aggregation, which breaks the injectivity of the sum aggregation that is crucial for GIN’s WL-test analogy; the paper does not discuss whether this matters for reconstructability or expressivity. (Reviewer 4)
  - A sentence about the infeasibility of distinguishing model capability on real-world graphs using GRR (each dataset returns only 1 or 0) is unclear and unexplained. (Reviewer 4)
  - The writing contains grammar/language errors (e.g., “no more powerful than the 1-WL test, i.e. distinguishing…” should be “i.e. in distinguishing…”; “our proof is also held” → “our proof also holds”; “affliction matrix” → “affiliation matrix”; “Pudmed” → “Pubmed”). (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparison with related reconstruction literature**: The paper does not adequately discuss how its approach relates to methods that learn node embeddings with the explicit goal of preserving graph structure (e.g., matrix factorization, random walks) or to the concept of graph reconstruction from subgraphs in subgraph GNN work (e.g., [3]). (Reviewer 2)
