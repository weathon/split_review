## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) from the perspective of spurious correlations and source bias. The authors argue that the source-trained model is biased by spurious correlations, leading to poor performance on target samples dissimilar to the source. They propose a Debiased SFDA setting where a pre-trained, frozen reference model provides source-independent prior knowledge for debiasing. A contrastive learning objective (DeCo) is introduced to align the source model’s classification boundary with a clustering module trained on the reference model’s features, and a diagnostic metric (Hardness Accuracy, HAcc) is proposed to measure the degree of source bias. Experiments show that DeCo generally improves over existing SFDA methods when integrated with AaD and SF(DA)² on standard benchmarks (VisDA, Office-Home, DomainNet). Reviewers agree the problem is important and the paper is well-motivated, but raise concerns about limited novelty, insufficient technical contribution, unclear definition of source bias and HAcc, lack of efficiency analysis, and reliance on an external reference model whose debiasing quality is not analyzed.

### Strengths

- The paper investigates SFDA with a focus on spurious correlations and source bias, a crucial and practical problem in real-world scenarios. (R1, R3, R4)
- The authors discover a consistent performance trade-off on easy vs. hard target samples (based on similarity to the source model’s average representation) through a preliminary experiment – a novel observation. (R2, R3)
- The problem setting is well-motivated and easy to follow; the authors clearly position their work relative to prior SFDA literature. (R1, R3)
- The proposed DeCo method is simple to implement and can be integrated as a plug-and-play component into existing SFDA methods (e.g., AaD, SF(DA)²). (R2, R4)
- The diagnostic metric HAcc, which splits the dataset based on a hardness score following a power-law distribution, provides a quantitative way to evaluate the degree of debiasing under varying source-target domain differences. (R2, R4)
- Extensive experiments and rigorous evaluation are conducted to validate the effectiveness of DeCo, including comparisons on multiple benchmarks. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses

- The concept of source bias and spurious correlation in SFDA is not clearly defined. While the introduction discusses spurious correlations learned by the source model, a comprehensive definition is lacking, and the connection between the observed performance gap on easy/hard splits and spurious correlations is not sufficiently established (e.g., Figure 1 may only reflect distribution shift). R1, R4
- The novelty of the work is limited. The method adds an extra pre-trained model and fine-tunes the source model using a basic contrastive loss; the improvement from introducing external prior knowledge appears predictable. R2, R3, R4
- The technical contribution is insufficient. The approach consists of two steps: (1) unsupervised training of a new clustering head on the reference model’s features (existing method) and (2) alignment of source model and clustering boundaries via contrastive loss. The selection of positive/negative pairs entirely depends on the clustering model, which can be flawed if the reference model is poor. R2, R3, R4
- No analysis is provided of the quality or debiasing extent of the reference model. The reference model itself may be biased, yet there is no discussion on how to guarantee its debiasing properties or how its performance on the target domain affects DeCo. R2, R4
- The metric HAcc is claimed to “assess source bias”, but it actually evaluates the average accuracy under different domain bias scenarios; this wording is inaccurate. Moreover, the definitions of H-score, HAcc, and the debiasing analysis (Figure 4) are confusing: e.g., higher H-scores represent more distinct/challenging samples (yet earlier splits are meant to be easier), and in Figure 4 right it is unclear whether “h” refers to HAcc or the average H-score. R1, R2
- The description of H-score and HAcc is insufficiently detailed, both in the main paper and the appendix. Most HAcc experiments are conducted only on VisDA; more experiments on other datasets are needed to evaluate generality. R2, R3
- Lack of efficiency analysis. DeCo introduces an external feature extractor, an additional network (g), and data augmentations; computational cost, memory usage, and training time are not compared or discussed. R1
- Only two SFDA baselines (AaD and SF(DA)²) are integrated with the proposed debiasing algorithm. Results are not enough to convincingly demonstrate its effectiveness; more methods should be tested. R4
- The class space of the reference pre-training dataset (ImageNet) overlaps with some test domains (e.g., Office-Home, VisDA). The authors state both that classes in T can be novel to f_r and that the pre-training dataset should be related to the SFDA task; this contradiction is not resolved. Ablation studies on different pre-training datasets (including one with no class overlap) are missing. R4
- The objective’s design choices are not fully justified. In Eq. (3), the denominator of contrastive loss includes only negative samples; the advantage of this variant over standard InfoNCE is not explained. R2
- Can the alignment term in Eq. (3) replace the InfoNCE loss in AaD with dot product? The final distributions of positive/negative neighbor counts across datasets are not reported. R1
- Missing theoretical grounding for generalization and debiasing; no connection to invariance principles (e.g., IRM) that could justify DeCo as a transferer of stable non-spurious features. R3
- Ablation of how the accuracy of the large-scale pre-trained reference model on the test domain affects DeCo is needed. In a hypothetical setup, can DeCo match the performance of the open-source model? R3
- Notation issues: “mdoel” typo (line 225); L_{aad} vs L_{AaD} inconsistency (Eqs. (1) and (4)); symbol f_g appears unexplained (line 268); g(x) likely means g(f_r(x)) but is not clarified (lines 277–282). R2, R4
- When the reference model is poor, clustering produces many indistinguishable samples; aligning the source model’s classification boundaries may introduce errors, and the positive/negative pair selection in contrastive loss can exacerbate mistakes. R2
- Entropy curves should be included in analysis alongside H-score to understand under-confidence. R3
- Figure 6 does not explicitly label β1 and β2. R2
- Note: One reviewer (R2, score 3) found the novelty and technical contribution substantially weaker than the other reviewers (R1, R3, R4, scores 5), highlighting that the improvement from external knowledge is predictable and the method is an incremental application. R2
