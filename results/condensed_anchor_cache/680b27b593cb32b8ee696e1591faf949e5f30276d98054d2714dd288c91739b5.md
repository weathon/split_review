## Merged Review

### Summary
FB-Bench introduces a Chinese multi-turn benchmark (734 curated samples, 8 task types, 5 response deficiency types, 9 feedback types) to evaluate LLMs’ responsiveness to human feedback in two scenarios: error correction (fixing warranted criticism) and response maintenance (sticking to a correct answer despite misleading feedback). The evaluation uses a weighted checklist (GPT-4o-generated) with GPT-4o (August) as judge. 31 LLMs are tested; the main finding is that models generally perform better at error correction than at response maintenance, with notable variation across models and regions.

### Strengths
- **Originality & gap-filling**: The focus on error correction vs. response maintenance is novel and less explored in prior benchmarks (R1, R3, R4). The checklist-based LLM-as-judge enables finer-grained evaluation (R1).
- **Well-organized presentation**: The paper is clearly structured with clean figures, making it easy to follow (R1, R4).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: 31 open- and closed-source models are tested, revealing interesting performance differences (R2).
- **Key insight**: The observed disparity between error correction and response maintenance is striking – most models are overly responsive to feedback, which connects to sycophancy and other known issues (R1, R3, R4).
- **Use of human annotators** in constructing the benchmark is appreciated (R3).
- **Timely topic**: Human-AI collaboration and feedback responsiveness are important but under-studied (R4).
- Stronger supporters (R1, R4) rate it 5 and highlight its originality and clarity; others (R2, R3) give 3, citing rigor and clarity concerns – indicating disagreement about overall contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Out-of-distribution first turn**: The first-turn assistant response is not generated by the tested model but is fixed from a less preferred response. This may invalidate real-world applicability – e.g., Claude-3.5-Sonnet’s low response maintenance could differ if it answered first; Phi-3-small-8k-instruct’s high maintenance might be negative if its own answers are often wrong (R1, R3, R4). Missing experiment: comparing results when the model generates its own first turn (R1).
- **Insufficient annotation rigor**: No details on annotator number, background, pay, or inter-annotator agreement (IAA). The human-LLM pipeline is not justified versus pure human construction, and the proportion of agreement with GPT-4 (used to identify response issues) is unreported (R1, R3, R4). “Heuristic” for target data selection is unspecified (R1).
- **Language ambiguity**: The benchmark is Chinese but this is only stated in the appendix; it should be mentioned in the abstract/introduction (R2, R4). Language composition and its effect on model performance (e.g., models trained in China performing better) are not controlled in analyses (R1, R4).
- **LLM judge and checklist unvalidated**: The checklist weights are arbitrary, and no verification is provided that GPT-4o (August) is a reliable judge; ranking changes when using a different judge are unknown (R1, R2, R3). Questions: why this specific model? (R1)
- **Contradiction in data curation**: GPT-4 is used to annotate causes of dissatisfaction – this may introduce the same sycophancy the paper criticizes (R1). The paper criticizes prior benchmarks for using LLM-synthesized data (line 473), yet GPT-4 is also used to synthesize significant portions of FB-Bench (lines 272–275) (R1, R2).
- **Analysis lacks depth**: The claim that the error-correction/response-maintenance gap is “more pronounced among LLMs developed outside of China” (line 379) is not supported with concrete numbers and ignores language as a confound (R1, R4). Error correction vs. maintenance is treated as a fixed property, but it is likely a calibration issue – a model can be forced into either behavior via system prompts, yet no such ablation is done (R1). The analysis does not connect to known literature (e.g., sycophancy papers, cultural training biases) (R4).
- **Clarity and missing details**: The evaluation setup is incompletely described – what exactly is the input? Does the first-turn response come from the tested model? (R3). “Fine-grained” feedback is not analyzed for its actual diversity (R3). Missing citations (e.g., WILDBENCH for checklist-based evaluation) (R2). Typo: “dialugues” line 52 (R2). Figure 6 colors too light (R1). Figure correspondence not aligned (R2). Data sources (online chat service, human preference data) are not cited or detailed (R1, R3).
- **Difficulty filter questionable**: Three similar-sized models are used as difficulty filters – why these three? Filtering rate per model not reported (R4).
- **Checklist specifics absent**: No examples of the checklist (instance-level or task-level) and how weights are decided (R1, R2). Does removing the checklist change rankings? (R1)
- **Overclaimed terminology**: “three-tier hierarchical taxonomy” is more an evaluation framework than a taxonomy (R2).
