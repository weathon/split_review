## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces meta specification as an alternative design principle for open-ended learning systems (OELS), defining systems implicitly through constraints rather than explicit specification of state spaces and evolution functions. Central is the concept of watchmaker functions—stochastic evolution functions coupled with verification routines. The authors present a unified OELS framework and demonstrate an implementation co-evolving robot morphologies and tasks using an LLM as a watchmaker function, with 3D physical simulation and language-based descriptions.

### Strengths
- The paper reads coherently and is well written with a very pretty presentation (R1, R4).
- The proposition of defining constraints instead of target specifications is an interesting approach that could offer novel alternatives for addressing design problems (R1).
- The meta-specification approach presents a unique perspective on OELS, shifting from explicit to implicit design, which could simplify design complexity while expanding the space of possibilities (R2).
- The paper formalizes a unified framework for OELS, creating a common language that facilitates comparison of different OELS approaches (R2).
- The implementation co-evolving robotic agents and tasks showcases the potential of meta specification and watchmaker functions to create emergent behaviors without requiring highly specific design constraints (R2).
- The use of LLM-based watchmaker functions offers a promising approach for larger-scale OELS implementations (R2).
- The paper offers a practical solution to a significant scaling problem in open-ended learning systems (R3).
- Open-endedness is an important subject that deserves more attention (R4).
- The experiments, although minimal, point towards some novelty: co-evolving both tasks and agents in a 3D world with language descriptions and LLM-based evolution operators, hinting at potentially interesting future work (R4). *[Note: reviewer R4 also noted strong resemblance to Faldor et al. 2024 (see Weaknesses).]*

### Weaknesses
- **Plagiarism / Lack of novelty:** Two reviewers (R1, R3) explicitly raise plagiarism of existing ideas (ethics concern). Multiple reviewers (R1, R3, R4) identify a lack of novelty. R4 argues the first 8 pages essentially say: “If we want to explore spaces defined by more flexible representations than fixed-shape tensors, such as language, we need to add some checks to ensure the resulting proposals are feasible” – a concept already present in prior work (e.g., Karl Sims’ virtual creatures, 1994; Lehman et al., arXiv 2206.08896), where tree operations followed by sanity checks are implicitly “watchmaker functions”. The experiments bear strong resemblance to Faldor et al. 2024 (OMNI-EPIC, arXiv:2405.15568), which also uses LLMs and PyBullet; this work is not cited, which is a serious oversight (R4). Even if from the same team, Faldor et al. 2024 must be cited (R4 update).
- **No empirical results / lack of open-ended learning demonstration:** The paper omits any training procedure (due to cost/compute), which is crucial for evolved robots to become increasingly capable (author statement; noted by R1, R2, R3). R2 adds that showing an actual more open-ended learning process would have made the paper more impactful; a control mechanism should be integrated to ensure continuous progress. Figure 5 shows only very low generation numbers; the system was not run for longer (R2 question). R1 questions the costs of omitting training and whether reliance on LLMs is a notable limitation.
- **Unclear contribution / framework confusion:** The contributions are unclear compared to POET – what can the proposed system solve that POET cannot, or is it mostly a generalized formulation of a class of algorithms? (R2). R4 finds the OELS framework confusing: the distinction between “evolution functions” and the “control mechanism” is not obvious; the authors’ own classification of POET’s control mechanism (minimum criterion and novelty check) is inaccurate because those are part of the environment evolution function (original POET paper defines this as “Mutate_envs”). R4 questions why additional formalism, notation, and terminology are needed to describe what has been done for decades.
- **Verification feasibility and design difficulty:** R2 asks: Is it always possible and feasible to run the verification process? How difficult is it to design this part in comparison to traditional explicit specification? R1 asks about potential theoretical benefits of watchmaker functions and requests more tangible examples.
- **Minor errors:** Typo: “representation sapce” in the watchmaker function definition (R2, R4). In line 334, “generalized transformation” should likely read “viability” to match “Stochasticity” (R4).
