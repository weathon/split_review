## Merged Review

### Summary

This work addresses the high sample count required for importance sampling in SDF-based neural implicit surface reconstruction (e.g., VolSDF, NeuS). It proposes Edge-Sampler, a four-step strategy to reduce samples by up to 10× while preserving theoretical error bounds: (1) double-sampling to detect thin surface edge intervals, (2) error-bounded weight sampling with piecewise CDF fitting, (3) sample interpolation to estimate Riemann sum error, and (4) error-guided ray sampling. Experiments on four DTU scenes are reported.

### Strengths

- **Motivation**: The paper is well-motivated, targeting the time-consuming issue of large sample counts on SDF thin edges when using error-bounded sampling (R1).
- **Clarity**: The manuscript is easy to follow and clearly explains all concepts (R2).
- **Method design**: The four-step sampling strategy (double-sampling, piecewise fitting, Gaussian Process, error-guided ray sampling) is reasonable, practical, and introduces several new techniques to the sampling process (R3, R4).
- **Claimed efficiency**: The method is reported to achieve up to 10× reduction in sample counts while still preserving the theoretical error bound, improving both sampling time and geometry reconstruction accuracy on the presented scenes (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Experimental scope and completeness** (raised by R1, R2, R4):
  - Evaluation is limited to only **4 scenes from the DTU dataset**; all are Lambertian surfaces. No results are shown for specular surfaces or for the full DTU benchmark, making it difficult to assess general performance.
  - Missing complete result tables for all DTU scenes (R1), and no table compares sample counts directly against other methods (R1).
  - Visualization differences in Figures 4 and 6 are hard to perceive (R1).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons** (R1, R2, R4):
  - For surface accuracy, only **NeuS** is used as a baseline; no comparisons are made with recent methods such as VolSDF (Yariv et al. 2021), HF-NeuS (Wang et al. 2022), Geo-NeuS (Fu et al. 2022), NeuS2 (Wang et al. 2023), Neuralangelo (Li et al. 2023), or other works (R2).
  - The **speed comparison neglects the Occupancy Grid technique** from Instant NGP (Müller et al. 2022). Moreover, the proposed approach is slower than NeuS unless NeuS’s interpolation technique is employed (R2).
  - There is no comparison that combines the proposed network with the original NeuS sampling (R2).
  - Model configurations of compared methods in Section 5.1 are not clarified (R1).
- **Theoretical limitation and mismatch** (R2, confidence 5, strongly critical):
  - The approach assumes that **narrower sampling around the surface is always beneficial**, but at the start of optimization the surface is unknown; this could cause slower convergence. This limitation is not acknowledged.
  - There is a **mismatch between motivation and implementation**: the introduction emphasizes the Laplace distribution of **VolSDF**, but all experiments appear to use **NeuS’s** density conversion. The paper does not justify this choice.
- **Novelty and significance concerns** (R4, confidence 5):
  - Efficient sampling for neural implicit representations has been studied since NeRF; the reviewer finds the contribution **not exciting** and expects a significant improvement in efficiency/quality for a top-tier venue.
  - Newer representations (e.g., Instant NGP, K-planes, 3D Gaussians) use **different sampling strategies** (e.g., Occupancy Grids) and achieve high quality already, which weakens the contribution of a method that may not transfer to those representations.
- **Ablation study insufficient** (R3, also noted by R1):
  - Only different fitting strategies are ablated (Figure 6); more ablation studies are needed for the other strategies (double-sampling, sample interpolation, error-guided ray sampling).
- **Clarity and writing issues** (R1, R2, R4):
  - **Figure 4 has no caption and is not explained** in the main text (R1, R2, R3).
  - **Time units are missing** in Tables 2 and 3 (R1, R2).
  - **Table 1 is unclear**: it is not obvious which entry is the proposed method (e.g., “Edge32 w/ GP”) and the original NeuS sampling appears to have the best timing (R4).
  - The paper has **too many equations** (equations 1-8 are a recap of prior work and could be simplified) (R4).
  - An **algorithm summary** of the full edge-sampler mechanism is missing (R1).
  - Time comparison fairness is questionable because batch size >1 is used (R1).
  - NeuS’s performance in Table 3 degrades compared to its original paper; no explanation is given (R1).
- **Contrast among reviewers**: One reviewer (R2, confidence 5) is severely critical, identifying a fundamental theoretical flaw and mismatched implementation, while another reviewer (R3, confidence 2) views the method as promising and highlights its practicality. R4 (confidence 5) questions the overall novelty and the relevance of the approach given modern representations.
