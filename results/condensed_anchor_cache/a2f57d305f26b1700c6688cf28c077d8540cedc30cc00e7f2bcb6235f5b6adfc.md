## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AdamE, a variant of Adam that uses step-dependent sequences for the exponential decay rates on the first and second moments of the gradient (β₁ and β₂), rather than the fixed default values (0.9, 0.999). The authors provide theoretical convergence guarantees for both convex and non-convex settings and evaluate AdamE on language modeling, node classification, and graph clustering tasks using DCRN. Reviewers acknowledge the relevance of the topic and the attempt to automate hyperparameter tuning, but they raise substantial concerns about novelty, theoretical correctness (multiple errors and missing steps), insufficient experimental scope (only toy-scale tasks), and suboptimal presentation. Reviewer 3 is notably more critical (score 1) than the other reviewers (scores 3), emphasizing fundamental flaws in the proofs and insufficient benchmarks.

### Strengths
- **Relevance**: The problem of automating exponential decay rates in Adam is well-motivated and aligned with ICLR’s focus on optimization for deep learning (R1).
- **Idea**: Using adaptive decay coefficients (α_t, β_t) to replace fixed hyperparameters is a conceptually novel direction that could improve convergence if correctly implemented (R3).
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides convergence proofs for both convex and non-convex settings, which is a positive aspect of the submission (R2, R4).
- **Empirical results**: AdamE is tested on concrete tasks (graph clustering with DCRN, language modeling, node classification) and shows performance gains over default Adam (R2, R4).
- **Illustration**: The quadratic objective example offers an intuitive understanding of why dynamic rates can help when the gradient vanishes (R4). *(Note: R3 and R4 later argue this example is not convincing for general cases.)*

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty relative to prior work**  
   - R1, R2, R4 note that the core idea—using decreasing β₁ and increasing β₂—has already been studied in AdamNC (Reddi et al. 2019) and in Zou et al. (2019). R1 additionally points out that many experimental observations (e.g., effects of different β₁/β₂ combinations) are standard in the literature and that recent theoretical works (Zhang et al. 2022, Li et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2024) are not cited. R4 states that the convex proof is nearly identical to Kingma & Ba (2014) and the non-convex proof follows Zhou et al. (2018) closely.  
   - R3 found the idea “quite novel” but still gave a score of 1; the majority view is that the technical contribution is incremental.

2. **Theoretical correctness issues**  
   - *Regret bound (Theorem 2.2)*: Both R3 and R4 argue that the claimed O(√T) regret is incorrect. Equation (14) contains a term √T ‖g_{1:T,i}‖₂, and since ‖g_{1:T,i}‖₂ itself grows as O(√T), the term is O(T). In contrast, the bound in Kingma & Ba’s Theorem 10.5 does not have the explicit √T factor (R4).  
   - *Non-convex proof sign flip*: R3 points out an erroneous sign change between equations (42) and (43).  
   - *Missing epsilon in update*: R4 notes that the derivation uses x_{t+1}−x_t = λ_t v_t^{-1/2} m_t, but the stability constant ε is omitted, which affects the convergence analysis (compare with Zhou et al. 2018).  
   - *Convex proof errors*: R2 identifies three specific mistakes:  
     (i) ‖θ_n−θ_m‖₂ ≤ D₂ does not imply ‖θ_t−θ*‖₂ ≤ D₂ without proving convergence to θ*.  
     (ii) Equation (18) should have m_{t,i}^2 / v_{t,i} instead of m_{t,i}^2 / √v_{t,i}.  
     (iii) The reduction from ∑_t √(t v_{t,i}) to √T v_{T,i} is not justified.  
   - *Unverified step*: R4 questions why λ_{t-1} v_{t-1,i} ≥ λ_t v_{t,i} holds (line 356).  
   - *Strong assumptions*: R1 remarks that the convergence analysis relies on a bounded gradient assumption that has been relaxed in more recent work.

3. **Insufficient experiments**  
   - R1, R3, R4 all state that the experiments are limited to toy or small-scale settings (e.g., graph clustering with DCRN, simple language models). R3 explicitly requests comparisons on standard benchmarks such as ResNet on ImageNet and transformers on common language datasets. R4 notes that only graph clustering is shown; broader validation is missing.

4. **Motivation and illustrative example**  
   - R3 finds Section 2 unconvincing: the 1D quadratic example shows that all hyperparameter choices converge quickly, so it does not demonstrate a practical advantage. R4 argues that this example is unsuitable for comparing adaptive methods because gradient descent would also perform well; the observed superiority of AdamE over Adam in that case is expected and does not imply general effectiveness.  
   - R3 adds that AdamE, as t grows, becomes essentially AdaGrad (α_t → 0, β_t ≈ 1/t), but this connection is not discussed.

5. **Presentation and writing**  
   - R4 reports many typos (e.g., “<” should be “>” in formulas (29) and (42)), inconsistent notation (d_q/s_q in the algorithm versus m_t/v_t in the proofs), and a lack of clarity in the proof steps (too many indices without explanation, no separation into lemmas). R2 also notes minor errors in the convex proof.  
   - R4 suggests using bold notation for scalars/vectors/matrices and splitting Theorem 3.1’s proof into lemmas.

6. **Contrast in reviewer opinions**  
   - R3 (score 1) is significantly more critical than R1, R2, R4 (scores 3). R3 emphasizes that the theoretical results contain fundamental flaws (regret bound, sign flip) and that the experiments are far from standard, while the other reviewers, though also raising serious issues, are less severe in their overall assessment.
