## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Hindsight Relabeling using Interactions (HInt), a method that filters trajectories for hindsight relabeling based on a null counterfactual definition of interaction: an object interacts with a target if removing it would change the target’s dynamics. A masked dynamics model (NCII) is learned to infer such interactions, and trajectories lacking a causal path from actions to the goal object are discarded. Experiments in 2D and 3D robotic domains (Robosuite, Robot Air Hockey, Franka Kitchen) show improved sample efficiency over HER and other baselines. Reviewers generally found the motivation and core idea compelling, but raised concerns about scalability, hyperparameter sensitivity, the degree to which the actual implemented algorithm deviates from the presented ideal, and missing comparisons. Scores range from 5 to 8, indicating disagreement on the method’s generality and practical soundness.

### Strengths
- The null counterfactual definition of interaction is intuitive, concise, and causally grounded (R1, R2, R4).
- The problem of hindsight relabeling in object-centric environments is well motivated, and the proposed extension of HER is a practical contribution (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The writing and presentation are clear and well structured (R3, R4).
- The experimental evaluation covers a diverse set of relevant domains and shows that NCII achieves better interaction detection accuracy than heuristic baselines (Table 1) and that HInt yields up to 4× sample efficiency improvements (R1, R4).
- The method details, including the engineering of null state inference and the nulling operation, are practical (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Scalability and domain limitations:** The method is limited to object-centric domains with relatively simple dynamics. The computational complexity of inferring null states is not well addressed: R1 notes that the inference may scale as O(n²) in state dimension, conflicting with the paper’s O(1) claim on line 263. Set-based architectures (e.g., PointNet, GNN) may not generalize to other domains (R2).  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and lack of ablation:** Both the null threshold \(\epsilon_{\text{null}}\) and the interaction-frequency threshold (Appendix D) are critical yet not ablated. R1, R3, and R4 request a sensitivity analysis showing accuracy and episode reward across a range of thresholds for several environments. It is unclear whether a single threshold works across all environments in Table 1 (R3).  
- **Core algorithm not fully presented in main text; simplified implementation used:** Important components—the actual filtering criteria (Appendix D), the reweighing based on log-probability of a transition (Appendix C), and the fact that only interactions between the controlled object and the target object are considered—are moved to the appendix. The main text gives the impression of a more general algorithm (path-from-action-to-target), while the experiments use a simpler engineered criterion combined with additional hyperparameters. R4 argues this weakens the claim that NCII is more general than heuristics (line 87) and suggests that an ablation of different filtering criteria is needed.  
- **Missing baselines:**  
  - HER “future” and “final” goal-selection variants should both be compared in Figure 4, as the current comparison only uses one variant and performance differences are small in some environments (R3).  
  - A comparison to CAI (estimating influence of actions on the target object) is missing; it would isolate the benefit of detecting null‑counterfactual interactions beyond simply checking action effects (R4).  
- **Missing detail in main text:** The reweighing trick (Appendix C) that helps stabilize training is not mentioned in the main text but appears to be important for obtaining the reported results (R4).  
- **Minor presentation issues:**  
  - Equation (2) uses ‘min’ where ‘max’ is intended (R4).  
  - Equation (3) lacks an absolute value when thresholding log-probability differences (R4).  
  - Figure 1 caption appears to have “(left)” and “(right)” switched (R4).  
  - Line 268 contains a duplicate “using using” (R3).  
  - Line 187 should be \(d_{\pi}\) (R1).  
  - The notation in Equation (3) has ambiguous parentheses on the logarithm (R3).  
  - The filtering function in line 303 rejects trajectories whereas Appendix D uses the opposite sign convention (R4).  
- **Questions that reflect missing analysis or unclear reasoning:**  
  - How is the state space defined across environments? Inferring null states in high-dimensional spaces (e.g., images) seems challenging; per-state complexity O(n²) conflicts with stated O(1) (R1).  
  - The handling of trajectories with very few interactions is not addressed (R3).  
  - In Table 1, lower accuracy values are better, which is counterintuitive and could be clarified (R3).
