## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces TacQuad, an aligned multi-modal multi-sensor tactile dataset from four optical-based visuo-tactile sensors (GelSight, DIGIT, DuraGel, and another), and proposes UltraTouch, a unified static-dynamic multi-sensor representation learning framework. UltraTouch employs a multi-level structure: pixel-level masked autoencoding (MAE) on tactile images and videos, semantic-level multi-modal alignment (with vision and language), and cross-sensor matching to learn sensor-agnostic features. The method is evaluated on material classification, grasp stability prediction, and a real-world pouring task. Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty of the dataset and dynamic representation learning, but raise concerns about the exclusive focus on optical sensors, performance degradation when adding more sensor data, insufficient ablation, limited exploitation of fine-grained alignment, and the need for stronger downstream validation on unseen sensors.

### Strengths
- **TacQuad dataset contribution**: The aligned multi-sensor tactile dataset, especially the fine-grained spatio-temporal aligned data collected via a shared platform, is a significant resource for the tactile sensing community. None of the prior works have collected tactile videos with shared speed and depth using multiple sensors (R2). It is open-sourced, helping address the scarcity of tactile data (R4) and improving generalization across sensors (R1).
- **Novelty of static-dynamic representation**: UltraTouch is the first model to learn dynamic tactile representations through pretraining, moving beyond prior works limited to static images. This is a considerable improvement for fine-grained manipulation tasks (R2). Capturing both static and dynamic features provides comprehensive tactile information understanding (R1).
- **Performance on downstream tasks**: UltraTouch outperforms prior pretraining frameworks on material classification and grasp stability prediction in most experiments (R2), and the real-world pouring task demonstrates the value of dynamic representations (R2, R4). The experiments are comprehensive, covering multiple tasks (R4).
- **Clear visualization and presentation**: The t-SNE visualizations clearly show the impact of components on representation space (R1). Fig. 2 serves as a nice abstract of the entire framework (R3). The paper is well-motivated and well-written, with modules designed specifically for tactile sensing differences from vision (R3).
- **Efficient labeling**: Using a language model (GPT-4o) to generate descriptions improves dataset collection efficiency (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited to optical-based tactile sensors**: The dataset and framework exclusively focus on optical-based sensors, neglecting other modalities such as capacitive, resistive, or piezoelectric sensors. This limits generalizability to broader tactile sensing applications (R1, R4). While this may be out of scope, it is a significant limitation (R4).
- **Performance degradation when incorporating more sensor data**: In material classification (Table 2, Sec 5.2), the model using only GelSight data (83.5%) outperforms the full model using all four sensors (80.53%). This raises concerns about the effectiveness of multi-sensor representation learning and questions the necessity of the TacQuad dataset. The fact that the TAG+Feel+YCB-slide+OF 2.0 combination also averages higher than the full model further questions dataset design (R1, R3). The paper does not adequately explain this drop or provide a principled way to choose dataset size/scope (R3). The same issue for grasp prediction is not addressed (R3).
- **Insufficient ablation and component analysis**: The paper lacks a thorough ablation study to demonstrate the contribution of each component (R1). In particular, the effect of the pixel-level MAE stage on downstream tasks is unclear—material classification and grasp prediction may not need such low-level detail to achieve good performance (R4). The source of performance drop when adding sensors (is it lack of some specific dataset?) is not ruled out (R3 question 2).
- **Real-world pouring task evaluation not convincing**: The performance of UltraTouch on the pouring task (mean error) is not clearly superior to existing methods, and the mean error metric alone lacks sufficient detail. Absence of clear baseline comparisons makes it difficult to assess the true contribution (R1). Task details (e.g., what "mean error" means) are only clarified in the appendix (R1 minor).
- **Limited frames for dynamic modeling**: Only 3 frames are used per tactile video clip (Appendix A.4). This appears insufficient to capture fine-grained information like hardness or texture variations during sliding. An ablation experiment with longer video lengths (e.g., 5, 7, 10 frames) is needed to quantify the impact (R2). The computational cost of longer videos is a valid concern (R2 question).
- **Fine-grained spatio-temporal alignment not fully exploited**: The calibration platform collects finely aligned data, yet downstream tasks (except pouring) are static, and even pouring does not directly leverage fine-grained alignment. Potential cross-sensor generation tasks (e.g., generating high-resolution tactile images from low-resolution sensors) are not explored, which could better showcase the platform’s value (R2).
- **No downstream evaluation on unseen sensors**: The claim of sensor-agnostic features transferring to unseen sensors is supported only by qualitative t-SNE visualization. Quantitative downstream task performance on an unseen sensor is needed (R3). The clustering for seen sensors (GelSight, DIGIT) also shows mixing, raising questions about loss weight tuning (R3).
- **Calibration and sensor variability concerns**: The precise localization of contact position across sensors may not fully account for variations in sensor mounting or object geometry, potentially causing misalignment (R4). The paper does not address variations among individual sensors of the same type (e.g., DIGIT pixel-wise differences as reported by Bhirangi et al. [1]), nor the effect of gel degradation over time if gels were replaced (R4 questions).
- **Missing limitations and future work**: The main paper does not include a limitations or future work section (R1).
- **Presentation issues**: The use of many colors for sensor types may hinder readability (R1). Tables (e.g., Table 1-3) are hard to read due to detailed dataset color configuration; the first row in Table 1 (from CLIP) is not explicitly indicated as in Tables 2/3 (R3). Citation parentheses are missing in many places; line 797 missing space (R3 minor, R4 minor).
- **GPT-4o labeling quality not validated**: The paper does not provide experiments proving the effectiveness of the text descriptions (R1 question 6). The gap between GPT-4o outputs for OF-Real vs. sensors without tactile input, and whether that affects training, is unclear (R3 question 1). The number of corrections needed is not reported (R4 question).
