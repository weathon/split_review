## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning strategy for a diffusion model (EDGE) for music-to-dance generation. An imitation policy is first trained in a physical simulator to mimic real-world ground truth motion; this policy is then used to define an imitation reward for fine-tuning the diffusion model via on-policy RL. An anti-freezing reward is also introduced to prevent the model from generating trivial static motions. Experiments on AIST++ show that the method improves physical plausibility (e.g., fewer penetrations, less foot sliding) and avoids freezing. The approach builds on Yuan et al. (2023) and Black et al. (2024).

### Strengths
- Physical plausibility in dance generation is a significant problem that has not yet received much attention (Reviewer 1).
- Demos show that the method successfully avoids penetration and freezing (Reviewer 1).
- The overall idea of training an imitation policy and then using it to fine-tune a diffusion model toward physically plausible motions is innovative (Reviewer 2).
- Feasibility is demonstrated; the method is compared against three state-of-the-art methods (EDGE, FACT, MotionDiffuse) and is shown to be superior in both aesthetics and physical plausibility using quantitative and qualitative measures (Reviewer 2).
- The framework is simple and practical (Reviewer 3).
- Baseline comparisons are thorough and results are analyzed thoroughly (Reviewer 3).
- Related work is sufficiently covered (Reviewer 3; but see weakness about missing Baiiando++ from Reviewer 1).
- The two rewards (imitation and anti-freezing) target physically appealing motion, and the heuristics (e.g., motion magnitude) make sense (Reviewer 4).
- The RL training methodology is well suited for capturing and imitating diverse motion sequences (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to existing physically plausible motion methods:** No comparison to PhysDiff (Reviewer 1). The difference between the proposed RL fine-tuning and PhysDiff is unclear (Reviewer 1). The ablation “EDGE w proj” is described as “similar to PhysDiff” and labeled as post-processing, but PhysDiff embeds the simulator into the diffusion model in the final iterations, which is not equivalent to post-processing (Reviewer 1). Alternative post-processing using optimization-based projection to the AMASS dataset (e.g., Pose-NDF, NRDF) is simpler and more straightforward than the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
- **Unfair comparison setting:** The proposed method trains the imitation policy on AMASS and then fine-tunes on AIST++, while EDGE trains only on AIST++. This makes comparisons between the methods potentially unfair because AMASS contains many realistic human motions that can help the imitation policy without explicit physical constraints (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and notation issues:** The description of the RL fine-tuning process is unclear (Reviewer 1). The link between the imitation policy (Section 3.1) and the reward function (Equation 5) is not explained – is the policy used to produce a sequence, and if it is stochastic, is only the mean used? (Reviewer 2). Notation is confusing: many different symbols for states/poses (\(S_t, s_t, x_t^s, x_{t+1}^A, x_{t+1}^{res}, \bar{x}_t^s, x_t, x_0^s, \bar{x}_0^s\)) are not fully described; \(t\) is used for both time and diffusion step; “residual between current state and next frame of reference motion” is mentioned but no reference motion has been introduced (Reviewer 2). The on-policy sampling detail is ambiguous: “we sample 2,048 motions from the training dataset of AIST++” – since RL fine-tuning should use motions generated by the current policy, sampling from the training set seems to provide a balanced input (positive/negative examples) but this is not clarified (Reviewer 1). Theoretical parts are sloppy and appear to follow derivations from Yuan et al. 2023 and Black et al. 2024 without updating notations to integrate everything (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing evaluation details:** Human Perception Results for the proposed method are marked ‘/’ in Table 1 (Reviewer 3). The 40 participants’ selection criteria are not described (e.g., dance expertise) (Reviewer 2). Why are the numbers exactly 70% and 75% for EDGE in Table 1? How many sequences are evaluated? (Reviewer 2). The reason why freezing sometimes occurs without the anti-freeze reward is not explained (Reviewer 2). What makes the system specific for dance generation beyond general human motion? (Reviewer 2).
- **Dependence on imitation policy and simulation:** Results are highly dependent on the performance of the imitation policy and the simulation settings; analysis in this direction is needed (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). The generated motions from the physical simulator are not the same as the extracted simulated motions, creating a mismatch (Reviewer 4).
- **Contradiction in collision handling:** The paper emphasizes the importance of controlling the character with SMPL skinned mesh because it is a physically-constrained model (L191-193), but later states that disabling collision checks between adjacent joints in both training and inference helps better replicate reference motion under collision constraints (L192-222). This is confusing (Reviewer 4).
- **Anti-freezing reward is heuristic-based:** The anti-freezing reward is a heuristic; the approach seems less principled than simply using a projection step (Reviewer 4).
- **Low diversity explanation insufficient:** In “Other Non-physical Metrics,” the authors claim low diversity is caused by short motion clips, but they do not compare diversity of different methods and ground truth by clipping motions to the same length as the diffusion model (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing related work:** Bailando++ (Siyao et al., 2023) is not cited (Reviewer 1). (Note: Reviewer 3 stated related work is sufficiently covered, but this omission is raised by Reviewer 1.)
- **Heavy reliance on prior work:** The proposed work borrows a lot from Yuan et al. 2023 and Black et al. 2024, both in underlying ideas and formulations (Reviewer 2). (This is raised as a weakness regarding novelty, though Reviewer 3 found the related work sufficient.)
- **Ablation requests:** Ablations are expected for w/wo collision checks in training and inference (Reviewer 4). Ablation for training the imitation policy on only AIST++ vs. the current scheme (AMASS + AIST++) is expected (Reviewer 4).
