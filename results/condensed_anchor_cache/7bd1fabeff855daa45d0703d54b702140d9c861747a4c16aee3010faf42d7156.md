## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Gradient Storm, an extension of the Sleeper Agent backdoor attack that enables multi-trigger, multi-target poisoning by designing perturbations distributed across the model’s parameter space. The method introduces cycle rounds and optimization cycles to partition the dataset and optimize poisons on disjoint subsets. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10 (and one result on GTSRB) using ResNet-18 and other CNNs, with evaluations against eight defenses. The authors claim effectiveness and transferability in black-box settings.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Gradient Storm enables multi-trigger, multi-target backdoor attacks from a single poisoned dataset (Reviewer 1).
- The work reexamines the existing optimization-based poisoning framework and expands it to a multi-trigger setting (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer finds the proposed method performs very well and has good reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- The attack improves upon existing methods and is evaluated across multiple architectures and benchmark datasets (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Sleeper Agent–style backdoors are interesting and warrant further exploration (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution**: The changes relative to Sleeper Agent are seen as minor; the technique is essentially an expansion to a multi-trigger, multi-target setting, and several reviewers find the contribution insufficient (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). One reviewer specifically questions what new methods are actually proposed beyond Sleeper Agent (Reviewer 3). The claimed contribution “Multiple Triggers, Targets, and Attack Types” is not well justified—it is not demonstrated why paired target-trigger combinations matter, and simply implementing more attacks is not a substantial contribution (Reviewer 4). The introduction lacks a clear presentation of the paper’s own contributions and consists mostly of literature review (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation limitations**: Only two datasets are used (CIFAR-10 and one ASR result on GTSRB), and only one trigger type is considered (Reviewers 2, 4). Experiments on GTSRB are nearly absent (Reviewer 2). The experiments in Section 4.4 (multi-trigger attack) do not add much beyond showing that ResNet-18 can learn 2–3 backdoors on CIFAR-10 (Reviewer 2). No baseline comparison with simpler multi-trigger methods is provided to illustrate the advantages of Gradient Storm (Reviewer 1). The trade-off between the number of triggers and stealthiness is not analyzed (Reviewer 1). The effect of key hyperparameters—cycle rounds \(R\) and optimization cycles \(S\)—is not studied; only one setting is used without justification (Reviewer 2). It is unclear whether the increased ASR comes from the partitioning mechanism or from multiple optimization cycles (Reviewer 2). Poison budgets \(B\) are not reported across experiments, and their effect on ASR is not examined (Reviewer 2).
- **Defense evaluation concerns**: Table 3 shows the attack fails against at least three defenses (e.g., I-BAU and MOTH perform well), yet the paper claims robustness (Reviewers 1, 4). The hyperparameters used for each defense are not described, nor is it shown how much clean data is needed to remove the attack under a charitable evaluation (Reviewer 2). No comparison is provided showing how other attacks (e.g., Sleeper Agent) perform against the same defenses (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis and reported results**: Section 4 presents results without commenting on whether they support the claims; the implications of the findings are not discussed (Reviewer 1). Tables 1 and 2 only compare different triggers, not different methods—they do not contrast Gradient Storm against other attacks (Reviewer 3). The paper does not analyze interaction effects between triggers when they are activated simultaneously or sequentially (Reviewer 1). Guidance on tuning parameters (number of triggers, poison budget, gradient matching threshold) is missing (Reviewer 1). The use of 8 retraining periods for Gradient Storm while only 4 for Sleeper Agent is not justified; it is unclear whether using 8 equally spaced retraining periods for Sleeper Agent would change the results (Reviewer 2). The computational cost is very high, requiring retraining a surrogate model for (number of optimization cycles) × (number of cycle rounds) × (number of triggers) times, implicitly assuming significant attacker resources (Reviewer 2).
- **Technical and presentation issues**: There is symbolic ambiguity: in line 135, \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) are unclear (sample vs. feature, and label indexing) (Reviewer 3). There is confusion between \(\delta\) and \(\Delta\) in lines 133 and 144 (Reviewer 3). The definition of \(D_i\) in line 150 needs elaboration (Reviewer 3). Equation (1) imposes a norm constraint on the noise size that seems unnecessary and uncommon (Reviewer 3). Algorithm 1, line 22, contains a typo (Reviewer 2). Standard data augmentation (random crop) is not used, which may reduce the attack’s effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
- **Contrast in reviewer views**: While one reviewer states the method “performs very well” (Reviewer 3), another notes that the results in Table 3 do not clearly show effectiveness (Reviewer 4), and Reviewer 1 observes that I-BAU and MOTH show good performance against the attack.
