## Merged Review

### Summary
InfoBatch is a dynamic data pruning framework that randomly prunes low-loss samples (based on a running mean threshold) and rescales the gradients of remaining samples to approximate the original gradient. An annealing phase uses the full dataset at the end of training. InfoBatch is plug-and-play and architecture-agnostic, achieving lossless training acceleration: 40% cost savings on CIFAR10/100, ImageNet-1K, ADE20K; 24.8% on MAE pretraining; 27% on diffusion models; and 20% on LLaMA instruction fine-tuning. The method is simple, easy to implement, and has low overhead.

### Strengths
- Clear presentation, easy-to-follow writing across all reviewers.
- Simple, pragmatic, and easy to implement method with minimal extra computation.
- Theoretically justified unbiased gradient expectation via rescaling (though one reviewer finds the unbiasedness proof trivial).
- Replaces costly sorting with mean-thresholding, reducing overhead compared to prior dynamic methods.
- Extensive evaluation across many tasks (classification, segmentation, vision pretraining, instruction fine-tuning) and scales (CIFAR, ImageNet, ADE20K, MAE, LLaMA), demonstrating consistent lossless savings.
- Solid ablation studies that explain the effect of each component.
- Practical significance: speeds up training, reduces time and energy costs.

### Weaknesses
- **Apples-to-apples comparison concern (Reviewers 2 & 3, strong):** The annealing period (full dataset at the end) may give InfoBatch an unfair advantage over baselines that lack such fine-tuning. Reviewer 3 suspects that annealing and rescaling are the key ingredients, not the specific selection rule. Reviewer 2 asks whether wall‑clock times in Table 2 include annealing, whether Table 1’s comparisons under equal prune ratio are fair given annealing, and why annealing is used only at the end (not the start). Reviewers 2 & 3 request additional baselines: apply annealing and rescaling to Random*, ε‑greedy, and UCB to test whether selection rule matters; also add a full‑data baseline trained with fewer epochs and higher learning rate commensurate with saved computation.
- **Threshold and pruning criterion not sufficiently justified (Reviewers 2, 3, 4):** In Table 5, a random pruning criterion performs nearly as well as the loss‑based condition, raising doubts about the necessity of using loss values (Reviewer 2). Absent is an ablation on different threshold selections (e.g., mean + std, median, percentile) (Reviewers 3 & 4). Reviewer 4 asks whether pruning probability could depend on sample score. Reviewer 3 questions whether the median or percentile might be preferable over the mean for asymmetric loss distributions, and how to set hyperparameters `r` and `δ` on a new dataset/architecture.
- **Overhead cost savings appear marginal (Reviewers 2 & 4):** The overhead reduction from O(N log N) to O(1) thresholding is claimed as a contribution, but Reviewer 4 deems it residual compared to total training time. Reviewer 2 notes that in Table 2 the wall‑clock and node‑hour savings between UCB and InfoBatch are negligible, and asks how total saved cost was computed in Tables 6 and 7.
- **Theoretical analysis is trivial (Reviewers 3 & 4):** Section 2.3 merely shows unbiasedness, which Reviewer 3 calls “completely obvious” from independent Bernoullis. Reviewer 4 suggests summarizing this section.
- **Missing recent works (Reviewer 1):** The paper omits relevant static data pruning methods [1–3] and the dynamic method [4] (Prioritized Training on Points … ICML 2022).
- **Misleading title and framing (Reviewer 3):** “Lossless” is not theoretically guaranteed (only empirical) and may mislead; the relation to coreset selection is overstated—InfoBatch speeds up a single training run rather than producing a reusable coreset.
- **Dense text and small figures (Reviewer 4):** Figures are too small; Section 2.3 and other parts could be condensed to improve readability.
- **Typos (Reviewer 3):** “constrainT” → “constrained”, “cubersome” → “cumbersome”, missing appendix reference in Section 3.3, “bond” → “bound”.
- **Missing experimental details (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):** How many random seeds used? Is Figure 1 a real plot or illustration? Which dataset is used for Table 4? How does annealing exactly stabilize rescaling when variance is unchanged (Table 4)?
