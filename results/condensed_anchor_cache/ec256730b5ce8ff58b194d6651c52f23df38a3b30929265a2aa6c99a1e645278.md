## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MultiHot Embedding for numerical features in deep learning, which discretizes values into bins and activates multiple neighboring bins to form a multi-hot representation, aiming to reduce information loss and improve training. Evaluations are conducted on six datasets with two architectures (ResNet, MLP). Reviewer assessments vary widely: two reviewers (R1, R3) rate the paper 5 (low confidence 2), praising novelty and organization, while three reviewers (R2, R4, R5) rate it 3 (high confidence 4) finding limited novelty, insufficient experiments, and lack of rigor.

### Strengths
- The method is novel and original, addressing information loss and insufficient training in numerical feature discretization (R1, R5).
- The idea of using multiple neighboring bin activations is intuitive and useful (R2).
- The paper is well-organized, well-written, and easy to read (R1, R2, R3).
- The pros and cons of discretization are analyzed in detail (R3).
- The proposed method is well-motivated and shows effectiveness on several datasets (R1, R4).
- The paper provides some theoretical analysis of the method’s advantages (R1; note: other reviewers disagree on sufficiency).
- A sensitivity analysis provides insights into discretization configurations (R1).
- The work tackles an under-explored space with potential applications (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and triviality**: The idea of letting numerically similar features share embeddings has been used in time-series and word embedding tasks (R2). The method is considered trivial and the problem is not interesting, as modern neural networks handle numerical features well (R4). Highlighting pros/cons of discretization is not a sufficient contribution for a top venue (R5).
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis**: No concrete theoretical results, e.g., for information loss during discretization (R1, R2, R4). Sections 3.2 and 3.3 lack formal derivation (R4). It is unclear if claimed properties generally hold (R2).
- **Weak experimental evaluation**:
  - Only two models (ResNet, MLP) are tested; this is insufficient to demonstrate generality (R3, R5). The reasoning for not using transformers is not detailed (R3).
  - Table 1 is unclear: columns are not labeled properly; RMSE/Accuracy metrics mentioned in caption are not found in the table (R4). The method does not show advantage on some datasets without explanation (R3).
  - No comparisons against published numbers from prior work (e.g., the AutoDis benchmarks); the claim that AutoDis is SOTA is not validated with proper baselines (R5). Improvements may stem from under-tuned baselines rather than the method itself (R5).
  - Missing ablation experiments, e.g., direct comparison between MultiHot and OneHot (R3).
  - Experimental setup details lacking: hyperparameters leading to improvement are not specified; it is unclear if they are the same across benchmarks (R5).
- **Lack of hyperparameter analysis**: The paper reports only a single configuration per dataset; no analysis of the effect of discretization width or number of activated bins (m) on performance (R1, R2, R5). No guidance on choosing optimal values (R1). Parameter m is hard to decide in new problems (R2).
- **Missing formal problem definition**: No clear specification of input, output, objectives, or loss function optimization (R1).
- **Practical concerns**:
  - Training time likely increases; scalability and practicality are not addressed (R3, R4).
  - If the sum over multi-hot embedding is not constant (unlike one-hot), the representation may be unfair across features (R4).
  - Handling of outliers is not evaluated (R4).
  - Edge case example: 11:59 vs 12:00 are numerically close but contextually different – how does the method handle such cases? (R2).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (R1, R3) gave high scores (5) but with low confidence (2), while three reviewers (R2, R4, R5) gave lower scores (3) with high confidence (4), indicating stronger criticisms on novelty, experimental depth, and theoretical grounding.
