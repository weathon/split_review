## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TOPING, an intrinsically interpretable GNN framework that uses topological data analysis (persistent homology) to identify stable rationale subgraphs. It introduces rationale filtration learning and a topological discrepancy loss to separate important substructures from noise, with theoretical guarantees that the loss is optimized by the ground truth under certain conditions. Experiments on multiple synthetic and two real molecular datasets show improvements over state-of-the-art methods (up to 20%). However, several reviewers have concerns about presentation clarity, experimental fairness (the proposed method uses a different backbone than baselines), scalability, and limited evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Novel use of topological data analysis (TDA) for interpretable GNNs.** All reviewers recognize this as a novel and promising direction; the application of persistent homology to rationale extraction is original.
- **Solid theoretical foundation.** The paper proves that the topological discrepancy loss is a lower bound of a Wasserstein distance under Gromov-Hausdorff metric and that the loss uniquely identifies the ground-truth rationale subgraph under certain conditions (R1, R2, R3). This provides a rigorous mathematical basis.
- **Extensive experimental evaluation.** The method is tested on multiple synthetic and real-world datasets (R2, R3). The results show significant accuracy and interpretability improvements (R1, R2, R4).
- **Novel problem framing.** The paper identifies and addresses the limitation that existing methods often assume rationale subgraphs are nearly invariant across instances within the same category (R4). One reviewer noted this is a reasonable and novel aspect, though the experiments supporting it are limited.
- **Introduction of the p-homology functor to XGNN** is a novel technical contribution (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity are major concerns across all reviewers.** Specific issues include:
  - Figure 1 is confusing: the caption is overly long and poorly segmented (R1); the relationship between subgraph \(G_t\) and the filtration parameter \(t\) is unclear—one reviewer noted a discrepancy between text (line 170) and the figure where edges appear to increase as \(t\) decreases (R2); the meaning of \(\beta_1\) is not explained (R3); the row lines are not defined (R4).
  - Figure 2 lacks explanation: the meaning of \(\mathcal{T}_X\) and \(\mathcal{T}_\epsilon\) and how they are concatenated with the GNN output is not clear (R3); the term \(L_{topo}\) is not explained (R4). The overall prediction pipeline remains ambiguous after reading Section 3 (R3).
  - Equation (3) contains an undefined \(\mathcal{R}\) (R3, R4) and is not properly referenced or contextualized (R2).
  - Definitions of topological invariants (e.g., \(\tau\)) are not mathematically rigorous (R2), and the filtration-subgraph construction is confusing (R2).
  - The sets \(\mathcal{G}\) and \(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G})\) defined in rows 267-269 are never used afterward (R3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and prior regularization concerns.** The bimodal Gaussian prior for edge filtration values is empirical, and the hyperparameters (\(\mu\) fixed, \(r\) learned) raise questions about sensitivity (R1). There is risk of trivial solutions where the two learned Gaussians collapse or produce negligible divergence (mode collapse) (R4). More justification for this prior choice and analysis of parameter sensitivity is needed.
- **Unfair comparison and backbone discrepancy.** Reviewer 4 pointed out a critical flaw: baselines use GIN backbone while TOPING uses CINpp backbone (Appendix C.2.1). The reported performance gains could stem from the backbone change rather than the proposed method. The instability of baselines when adapted to CINpp is not explained, making the comparison potentially biased. (R4, score 6, confidence 4)
- **Scalability and efficiency not addressed.** One reviewer (R2, score 5, confidence 4, more negative than others) specifically noted missing time complexity analysis and runtime comparisons with baseline methods. Persistent homology calculations are computationally heavy, and the method requires storing \(|E|\) edge filtration values, which is impractical for large graphs (millions/billions of edges). No discussion of memory requirements or adaptation strategies for large-scale graphs is provided. (R2)
- **Missing experiments and analyses.** 
  - MUTAG results are not impressive in terms of interpretability or accuracy; further analysis is needed (R1).
  - The experimental datasets are limited to synthetic and simple molecular graphs; qualitative visualizations for MUTAG are missing (R3).
  - The paper claims the method handles variable core subgraphs across same-category instances but does not provide sufficient analysis or examples to demonstrate why TOPING overcomes this challenge better than previous methods (R1).
  - No ablation or analysis evaluates whether the topological discrepancy loss considers node/edge features, which would be important for feature-relevant graphs (R3 Q6).
- **Implementation ambiguity.** Reviewer 2 questioned how parameter sharing between \(f_\phi\) (outputs edge scores in \([0,1]^{|E|}\)) and \(h_\phi\) (outputs node representation in \(\mathbb{R}^{|V|}\)) is feasible given their distinct output spaces (R2 Q4). This requires clarification.
