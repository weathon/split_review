## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses limited sample diversity in SGMCMC for Bayesian Neural Networks by proposing a parameter expansion (EP) that decomposes each weight matrix into a product of matrices. Theoretical analysis bounds the expected Euclidean distance between consecutive samples by the depth of the decomposition. Experiments on image classification (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) show improvements in distribution-shift robustness, OOD detection, and sample diversity. The method is simple, easy to implement, and does not increase inference cost, though it introduces additional parameters during training.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important problem of poor sample diversity in SGMCMC, which limits uncertainty estimation.
- Simple and practical: reparameterizes weight matrices via matrix products, easy to adopt for general architectures, and avoids running multiple chains.
- Provides theoretical justification: Theorem 3.2 connects depth of EP to a bound on consecutive-sample distances; gradient-flow analysis offers insights into exploration.
- Extensive empirical evaluation: robustness to distribution shifts, OOD detection, loss surface analyses, comparison with HMC, and validation that improvements hold even with data augmentation.
- Experiments cover multiple CIFAR datasets, VGG-16 architectures, and a range of metrics (accuracy, ECE, OOD AUROC, diversity measures).
- Paper is well-structured and clearly written.
- Includes variants for convolutional layers and theoretically grounded design choices (e.g., using l=4 for experiments).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and theoretical grounding**: It is unclear why parameter expansion specifically improves sample diversity. Theorem 3.2 is not fully convincing: parameters c and d (number of matrices) have an effect similar to step size, but the paper does not explain how this form of implicit preconditioning differs fundamentally from simply using a larger step size, or why this specific preconditioning is beneficial. The theorem should clarify whether standard SGMCMC is a special case (c=d=1?) and why values c,d≥1 are used while experiments refer to c=0,d=0 as standard SGMCMC.
- **Computational/memory overhead**: The expansion increases the number of parameters (for an m×n weight, storing c m×m and d n×n matrices) and thus computational cost. The paper notes this is mitigated for convolutions but does not provide a detailed analysis of overhead across architectures, wall-clock time, or burn-in period. Sample diversity depends on depth, which could be prohibitive for very deep or wide networks. Questions about possible mitigation (e.g., low-rank constraints) are left unanswered.
- **Missing comparisons**: The method is not compared to other SGMCMC techniques designed to improve sample diversity, such as cyclical SGMCMC, or to meta-learning approaches for diversity (Gong et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2024). Comparison with deep ensembles is also absent, despite the paper noting that ensembles outperform SGMCMC due to diversity.
- **Convergence and mode discovery**: Figure 1 shows that EP finds multiple modes but the bottom left region appears over-dense, raising concerns about whether the sampler converges to the target distribution. A more detailed convergence analysis for multi-modal targets is needed.
- **Relation to cyclical SGMCMC**: Figures 3 and 4 use cycle indices, suggesting the method may be implemented on top of cyclical SGMCMC (cSGMCMC), but the paper never explicitly states this. If PX-SGMCMC builds upon cSGMCMC, the choice must be justified and the method clearly disentangled from cSGMCMC’s own diversity improvements.
- **Benchmark performance**: Reported error on CIFAR-100 is ~29%, whereas VGG-16 with standard training typically achieves <20%. The paper should explain this lower baseline and discuss experimental setup limitations.
- **Clarity in Section 3**: The description of preconditioning creating “extraordinary directions of gradient steps” (lines 168-174) is vague and needs more precise elaboration. Variables in Equation 11 are not clearly related to r for the unexpanded network. In Figure 2, the change in condition number (beyond max/min singular values) is not reported, though it would be informative.
- **Novelty relative to prior SGMCMC**: The paper does not clearly articulate how the proposed mechanism (reparameterization) fundamentally differs from existing techniques that also aim to improve exploration (e.g., meta-learning diffusion/curl matrices, cyclical step sizes). A deeper explanation of the unique contribution and its implications on sampling dynamics is needed.
- **Figure interpretation**: The color scale in the toy example (Figure 1) is not explained, making it unclear what the values represent (e.g., density, log-posterior, or sample count).
