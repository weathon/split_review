## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how fine-tuning affects internal mechanisms in language models, using entity tracking as a case study on LLaMA-7B and its fine-tuned variants Vicuna-7B and Goat-7B. The authors first apply Path Patching to extract entity tracking circuits, then use Desiderata-based Component Masking (DCM) to confirm that the circuit components in all three models share the same functionality (Object, Label, Position). Finally, they propose Cross-Model Activation Patching (CMAP) to attribute the performance gain of fine-tuning to improved handling of positional information, specifically through the Value Fetcher and Position Transmitter heads.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a clear explanation of how fine-tuning enhances entity tracking, focusing on attention head circuits.
- The method CMAP is novel and directly attributes performance gains to specific components (Value Fetcher, Position Transmitter).
- Extensive experiments using Path Patching, DCM, and CMAP disclose several interesting findings: (1) all three models share a similar circuit, (2) components in that circuit have the same functionality across models, (3) the performance gain from fine-tuning is primarily due to improved positional information handling.
- The consistency of DCM results (on Position Transmitter and Value Fetcher) with CMAP experiments strengthens the paper’s credibility.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with granular identification of four attention head groups.

### Weaknesses
- The study is limited to a single model architecture (LLaMA-7B) and its fine-tuned variants (Vicuna-7B, Goat-7B) on only an entity tracking dataset. Two reviewers raised concerns that this narrow scope prevents generalizing the conclusions to other architectures (e.g., encoder-only models, different layer sizes) or tasks testing different cognitive mechanisms. One reviewer specifically questioned the high significance of entity tracking for broader language understanding and the justification for using attention mechanisms to study it.
- The claim that the original and fine-tuned models implement entity tracking with the “same” circuit is not fully supported by the evidence: Goat-7B achieves 82% accuracy while the circuit identified in LLaMA-7B reaches only 68%, suggesting the fine-tuned model’s circuit may differ (though with overlap). The authors should extract circuits from Vicuna-7B and Goat-7B via Path Patching and compute the overlap with LLaMA-7B’s circuit.
- The mapping between the three desiderata (Object, Label, Position) and specific circuit functions is insufficiently clear. The exact mechanism by which activation patching distinguishes these roles is not explained in enough detail.
- The CMAP analysis lacks clarity on why patching the QK-circuit of the Value Fetcher and the value vector of the Position Transmitter heads yields the largest performance improvement. The underlying computations in QK and OV circuits and how fine-tuning alters them are not discussed.
- The paper does not report completeness or minimality scores for the extracted circuits (as done in Wang et al., 2022); faithfulness alone is not sufficient to prescribe which circuit explains behavior well.
- The mathematical expression in Section 4.2 (Minimality paragraph) appears incorrect, and the description of filtering heads contributing less than 0.5% of functionality is inconsistent with that expression.
- The motivation for dividing heads into exactly four groups (A, B, C, D) rather than three or five is not justified.
