## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper reframes video inpainting as a conditional generative modeling problem using video diffusion models. It introduces inpainting-specific sampling schemes to capture long-range dependencies and a novel method for conditioning on known pixels in incomplete frames. Evaluated on newly constructed datasets (BDD-Inpainting, Traffic-Scenes), the method is shown to generate diverse, high-quality inpaintings that are spatially, temporally, and semantically consistent, outperforming flow- and attention-based approaches in challenging occluded scenarios.

### Strengths

1. **Novel generative perspective & effectiveness**: The method reframes video inpainting as conditional generation, enabling synthesis of novel, semantically consistent content for large occlusions, going beyond propagation-based methods. It effectively captures long-range temporal and spatial dependencies, producing realistic object behavior even when entire content must be generated. [R1, R2, R3, R4]

2. **Quantitative improvements**: The method shows clear quantitative improvements over state-of-the-art flow- and attention-based video inpainting methods on the constructed datasets, especially in challenging scenarios where occluded input is scarce. [R1, R2, R4]

3. **Extensive evaluation of sampling schemes**: Different inference sampling schemes are evaluated extensively, demonstrating their impact on quality. [R2]

4. **Large-scale dataset construction**: The authors invested significant effort in building a large-scale video dataset (focused on traffic scenes) to train the diffusion model. [R3]

5. **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper is easy to follow; the idea is intuitive and works practically well; the motivation is clear and the techniques are sound. [R2, R4]

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of evaluation on standard benchmarks**: The method is only evaluated on the authors’ own datasets (BDD-Inpainting, Traffic-Scenes), not on widely used video inpainting benchmarks such as YouTube-VOS and DAVIS. This makes it difficult to compare fairly with existing state-of-the-art methods, which are typically trained on those benchmarks. Additionally, the lack of quantitative comparisons on standard datasets undermines the claim of strong experimental results. [R2, R3, R4]

2. **Unfair comparison due to training data discrepancy**: The claim of “strong experimental results” may be biased because the proposed model is tested on datasets it was trained on, while baseline methods were trained on different datasets (YouTube-VOS, DAVIS). A dedicated ablation study should isolate the method’s effect from the dataset’s effect (e.g., train the proposed model on standard datasets and compare). [R1, R3]

3. **Limited diversity and generalizability**: The evaluation is heavily focused on car-centric traffic scenarios. All demonstrations show cars in inpainted regions; no examples with other objects are provided. This raises concerns about generalizability to broader real-world applications and zero-shot inpainting of other objects. The paper lacks discussion of how the model handles highly cluttered or dense environments with multiple simultaneous object occlusions and ambiguities. [R1, R3, R4]

4. **No control over inpainted content**: The model does not offer any mechanism to specify what content should fill the missing regions (e.g., object type, color, text prompts), unlike recent mask-guided video editing techniques that can control inpainted details. [R3]

5. **Missing comparisons with relevant recent methods**: The paper does not compare or discuss recent relevant works, including mask-guided video editing techniques (which can fill gaps with specified cars via text prompts) and other recent video inpainting methods such as [FFF-VDI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11402), [FGT++](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10048), and [SViT](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Lee_Semantic-Aware_Dynamic_Parameter_for_Video_Inpainting_Transformer_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf). [R3, R4]

6. **Unclear architecture and contributions**: The framework lacks a clear illustration of how conditional inputs are processed in the forward pass, the roles of trainable vs. frozen modules, and whether new layers for controllable generation are introduced. The paper should present a complete method graph (not just model inputs) and better highlight innovative contributions in the introduction. [R1, R4]

7. **Lack of inference cost analysis**: The paper does not provide inference costs (e.g., time and peak GPU memory usage) for a specified input size and number of frames, nor does it compare these with other SOTA methods. Additionally, the computational cost of different sampling schemes is not analyzed, which is important for practical applications. [R1, R2, R3]

8. **Training procedure not aligned with inference**: The training procedure (Eq. 3) randomly samples input/output frame combinations, but it is unclear whether training with sampling schemes similar to those used at inference would improve performance. Investigating whether alignment between training and inference sampling matters, or if the current uniform treatment of all combinations is suboptimal, would be valuable. It should also be clarified if the model is trained from scratch. [R2]

9. **Missing limitations and failure cases discussion**: The paper does not discuss potential limitations, failure cases, or how the model resolves ambiguities when multiple objects simultaneously enter and exit masked regions. [R1, R4]
