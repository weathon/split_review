- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper argues that any polynomial basis of the same degree has the same expressive ability and that crafted polynomials yield only linear benefits. It proposes Graph Information Activation (GIA) theory and designs a simple polynomial filter (SimpleNet) based on the normalized Laplacian. Reviewers appreciate the research question and the simplicity of SimpleNet, noting its robust performance and computational trade-off. However, they raise concerns about the novelty and strength of the theoretical results, the validity of the uniform optimization formulation, the outdated and small-scale experimental evaluation, the permutation invariance issue of Equation (7), the computational complexity of dense filters, insufficient comparisons with related filters (Krylov subspace, JKNet), and the lack of analysis on over-smoothing and non-convexity. Two reviewers rate the paper 5 (positive), while two rate it 3 (negative), reflecting split opinions.

### Strengths
- The research question of investigating key differences between graph polynomial filters is interesting and meaningful (Reviewer 3).
- Theoretical results (Theorem 1, Theorem 3.3) show that any polynomial basis of the same degree has the same expressive ability and provide a unifying perspective; these are of interest to the GNN community (Reviewers 1, 3).
- SimpleNet exhibits structural simplicity, robust performance, and a good trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The matrix \(2I - L\) is similar to the GCN filter and yields strong performance (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Empirical evaluation is weak**: Datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, Computers, Photos) are small and outdated. Results in Table 3 show very similar performance across methods; Table 4 shows underperformance on non-homophilic graphs; Table 5 shows computational time is not appealing. Better benchmarks (Dwivedi et al. 2020, Open Graph Benchmark) and more recent heterophilic datasets (e.g., [5]) are recommended (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Theoretical contribution is limited and contested**: Theorems are simple and standard (Reviewer 1). Theorem 3.1 only shows linear representation equivalence, not performance equivalence; K linearly independent components do not warrant downstream performance. The analysis overlooks non-convexity introduced by MLP and activation functions, and the impact of optimization methods (SGD, Adam) (Reviewers 3, 4). Some reviewers argue that the expressive ability analysis is insufficient to reveal essential differences between filters (Reviewer 3).
- **Equation (7) breaks permutation invariance**: The use of node-specific weights \(\alpha_s\) instead of degree-dependent \(\alpha_k\) implies the filter is not permutation equivariant, which is a fundamental requirement for GNNs (Reviewer 1).
- **Claim about GNN optimization formulation (Eq. 4) is unfounded**: Only PPNP and APPNP fit the unified optimization form; the paper's claim that all GNNs can be represented as such is not supported (Reviewer 2).
- **Graph Information Activation (GIA) theory is unclear and lacks novelty**: It is essentially a reintroduction of graph coloring (Reviewer 2). Its motivation and advantages are not clear; the performance gain from positive/proper activation and connection to generalization are not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational complexity is high**: The polynomial filter matrix becomes dense (\(O(n^2)\)) even for sparse graphs, losing sparsity benefits. Complexities discussed by Defferrard et al. (2016) are not adequately addressed; actual computational time in Table 5 is not appealing (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient comparisons with existing filters**: The paper should compare with Krylov subspace filters (Defferrard et al. 2016) and the filter perspective in [ii] (Reviewer 4). FixedMono and learnedMono are variants of JKNet (Xu et al. 2018) and should be clarified and compared accordingly (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing analysis of over-smoothing**: The paper does not analyze SimpleNet's behavior with respect to over-smoothing (Reviewer 2).
- **Heterophilic datasets are out-of-date**: Reliable evaluation requires more recent benchmarks (Reviewer 3; see also Reviewer 2,4).
- **Presentation and proof errors**: Several symbol errors in the proofs (Eq. 16, 17, 19, 39) undermine rigidity, though they do not affect final results (Reviewer 3).
- **Open questions that double as criticisms**: (i) How were experimental numbers in Section 4 obtained? Did the authors run all experiments themselves? (Reviewer 1). (ii) Why add \((2I-L)^i\) and \(L^j\) separately instead of concatenating them? (Reviewer 2). (iii) Can the filter be expanded as \(\sum (a_k I - L)^k + \sum (b_k I + L)^k\) for better control on non-homophilic graphs? (Reviewer 4). (iv) What are the advantages of GIA theory over existing interpretations? (Reviewer 3).
