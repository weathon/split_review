## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces AL4PDE, a benchmark that unifies active learning (AL) with neural PDE solvers in a solver-in-the-loop setting. It provides multiple parametric PDEs, state-of-the-art surrogate models, and batch AL methods (uncertainty-based: top-K, SBAL; feature-based: Core-Set, LCMD with Gaussian sketching). Experiments on 1D and 2D PDEs show that AL reduces average error by up to 71% compared to random sampling, reduces worst-case errors, generates consistent datasets, and produces reusable datasets beneficial for unseen surrogate models.

### Strengths
- Extensive numerical experiments on multiple PDEs are provided, with detailed descriptions of neural models and training procedures for completeness. (R1)
- The benchmark fills a needed gap in computational infrastructure for neural PDE solvers, addressing the central challenge of data efficiency. (R3)
- The paper is well-presented, pedagogical, and easy to follow. (R2, R3)
- The modular framework is novel in extending neural PDE methods with active learning, and includes a variety of batch selection strategies and neural PDE solvers covering recent and classical works. (R2)
- The well-designed modular framework provides a solid foundation for further research on active learning in the context of PDEs. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing references:** Important references on AL for scientific ML are omitted, e.g., [1] Bruna et al. (Neural Galerkin schemes with active learning) and [2] Gajjar et al. (Provable active learning for parametric PDEs). They should be discussed in the introduction. (R1)
- **Limited PDE dimensionality:** The experiments are limited to relatively low-dimensional problems; including high-dimensional PDEs (as in [3] Gao & Wang) would better demonstrate scalability and robustness. (R1)
- **Missing offline comparison:** The paper claims that AL yields "more accurate surrogate models trained in less time" (line 88), but this is not supported. No experiments compare AL to training with the full dataset (“offline” performance); only AL methods are compared to each other. (R2)
- **Novelty concerns:** One reviewer considered the novelty limited, as the framework is a combination of existing AL and neural PDE methods. (R2, minority view; other reviewers found the benchmark fills a needed gap.) (R2)
- **Missing DoE baselines:** The benchmark should include Design of Experiments (DoE) baselines such as Latin Hypercube sampling and more sophisticated DoE methods to bridge AL and DoE communities. (R3)
- **Lack of connection to existing UQ libraries:** The benchmark should broaden UQ methods by connecting to open-source efforts (e.g., UQ 360). Any UQ method providing a confidence interval can serve for AL. (R3)
- **Spatial averaging tradeoffs not discussed:** The implementation takes a spatial average over features to make feature-based AL translation invariant, but the tradeoffs of this significant dimensionality reduction versus the benefits of translational invariance are not explained. (R3)
- **Periodic boundary conditions limit scope:** All implementations use periodic boundary conditions, which restricts applicability. Examples without periodic BC should be added. (R3)
- **Acquisition functions are not PDE-specific:** The implemented acquisition functions are general-domain AL methods, orthogonal to PDE problems. PDE-specific methods (e.g., adaptive sampling [1] mentioned in Related Work) should also be included. (R4)
- **Limited scope:** The scope of surrogate models, acquisition functions, and PDE types studied is limited, impacting practical applicability. (R4)
- **Ambiguity in parameter definition:** The definition of field variable/channel c (line 96-97 and equations (3) and (5)) is unclear and requires more explanation. (R1)
