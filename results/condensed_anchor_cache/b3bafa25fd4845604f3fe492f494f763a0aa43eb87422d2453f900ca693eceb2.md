## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes MuseGNN, a GNN architecture that scales energy-regularized GNNs to very large graphs by incorporating a subgraph sampling strategy into the energy function design. The forward pass iteratively reduces this sampling-based energy, and node embeddings serve dual purposes: as predictive features for downstream tasks and as minimizers of the energy function. The authors provide theoretical convergence guarantees under different values of \(\gamma\) (which controls inter-subgraph dependency) and validate the model on node classification benchmarks, including the largest publicly-available one exceeding 1TB in size.

### Strengths

- **Scalability to large graphs**: All reviewers note that MuseGNN resolves the scalability problem that previous energy-regularized GNNs face on large graphs. It achieves competitive or superior accuracy on node classification datasets up to 1TB, where prior methods fail due to prohibitive computational cost (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Theoretical convergence analysis**: The paper provides a solid analysis of the convergence properties of the proposed sampling-based energy function and the iterative reduction process, with specific treatment of the parameter \(\gamma\) that controls the degree of dependency between subgraphs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Clear problem motivation**: The paper clearly motivates the critical issue of scaling energy regularization to large graphs and explains why previous approaches (e.g., UGNN, UGCN) become intractable (Reviewer 4).
- **Comprehensive discussion of optimization procedure**: The mechanism of the proposed method, including the handling of replicated nodes across subgraphs and a shared weight matrix, is well explained (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Writing and organization**: One reviewer found the paper’s organization and writing excellent (Reviewer 1, score 8), though another reviewer noted room for improvement (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Hyperparameter selection (\(\alpha\), \(\lambda\))**: The paper does not provide a clear methodology for selecting \(\alpha\) and \(\lambda\) for different datasets, nor guidelines on whether they require tuning per dataset. This lack of systematic approach hinders reproducibility and application to new problems (Reviewer 1).
- **Heterogeneous and heterophilic graphs**: The paper primarily focuses on homogeneous graphs and does not address performance on heterogeneous graphs or heterophilic graphs. The energy-related loss appears to heavily rely on the homophilic assumption, and it is unclear how the model would adapt to different graph types (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: The experimental comparison is limited to a few specific GNN architectures. Reviewers specifically request comparison with SGC and its variants (efficiency and accuracy), as well as additional baselines that also use energy regularization without sampling (to strengthen the superiority claim) (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Training speed concerns**: As shown in Table 1, MuseGNN appears slower than GAT, which is itself known to be a slow GNN. This raises questions about the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation studies from main text**: Several important analyses are relegated to the appendix and should be presented in the main body:
    - The ablation study for \(\gamma\) (currently Table 6 in Appendix B2), which shows how inter-subgraph dependency affects training outcomes (Reviewer 3; also asked by Reviewer 4 about the case \(\gamma=0\)).
    - Empirical investigation of different sampling strategies and their impact on the term \(\|Y_s - \mu_s\|\) (Reviewer 3).
- **Placement of Figure 1 and overall organization**: Figure 1 is referenced in both the introduction and the experiment section (ablation study), which is unconventional and confusing. The paper is only 9.5 pages, leaving room for more detailed analyses and better flow. (Reviewer 3; contrast with Reviewer 1’s positive view on organization.)
- **Uniform regularization strength (\(\lambda\)) across subgraphs**: The energy regularizer \(\lambda \mathrm{Tr}(\mathbf Y_s \mathbf L_s \mathbf Y_s)\) uses the same \(\lambda\) for all subgraphs, while subgraphs likely have varying degrees of smoothness. This may lead to suboptimal performance in practice (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of graph-level classification results**: The expressive power discussion focuses on node-level tasks, but GNN expressiveness is often evaluated on graph-level tasks (e.g., graph pooling). Presenting graph-level results would provide a more complete evaluation (Reviewer 3).
- **Confusing annotations in Section 2.1**: The notation could be clarified by adopting distinctions from prior work (e.g., using \(y\) vs. \(y^*\) for base model and energy-optimized embeddings, as in Ahn et al.) and by specifying the UGCN procedure more clearly (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing theoretical insight for \(\gamma=0\)**: When \(\gamma=0\), the model still achieves high accuracy despite node embeddings across subgraphs not being aligned. It is unclear whether subgraph embeddings can capture global graph information, and the paper lacks theoretical explanation for this behavior (Reviewer 4).
- **Sampling strategy not specified**: The exact sampling strategy used to extract subgraphs from the large original graph is not described (Reviewer 4; also related to missing ablation on sampling strategies noted by Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues**: Table 4 is missing from the main text (row 183) and is only in the appendix (Reviewer 3). The introduction could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the advantages of energy regularization for GNNs (Reviewer 4).
