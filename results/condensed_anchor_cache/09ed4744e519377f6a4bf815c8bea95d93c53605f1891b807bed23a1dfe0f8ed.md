## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DUCT (DUal ConsolidaTion) for exemplar-free Domain-Incremental Learning (DIL) using a pre-trained model. It addresses forgetting at both the representation and classifier levels: representation consolidation merges backbones from different stages via weighted task vectors, and classifier consolidation uses optimal transport to align old classifiers with the merged embedding space. Experiments on four cross-domain benchmarks show state-of-the-art accuracy and low forgetting. Reviewers note the paper is well-organized and motivated, but raise concerns about the novelty relative to existing model merging techniques, notation clarity, theoretical justification, missing comparisons, and limited backbone/dataset scope.

### Strengths
- **Problem formulation and motivation**: DUCT jointly tackles feature forgetting and classifier mismatch, which is a fresh and reasonable perspective on DIL. The exemplar-free setting is more challenging and practical. (R1: strength 1; R4: strength 1; R5: strength 1)
- **Technical creativity**: The combination of task vector merging and optimal transport for classifier alignment is a clever application of existing techniques to DIL. (R1: strength 2)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: DUCT is tested on four benchmark datasets with five task orders, showing robust performance and significant accuracy improvements over previous DIL methods. Ablation and analysis experiments (e.g., Fig. 5 visualization) provide deeper insight. (R1: strength 3; R4: strength 2; R5: strength 2)
- **Simplicity and potential impact**: The algorithm is simple, which could make it broadly applicable. (R5: strength 3)
- **Organisation**: The manuscript is well-organized despite the complex methodology. (R1: strength 4; R3: strength 1; R4: strength 1)
- **Novelty**: Most reviewers rate the method as novel (R1, R3, R4), though one reviewer (R4) notes similar prior work on model integration for incremental learning. (Reviewer disagreement: R1 soundness=3, R3 soundness=3, R4 soundness=2, R2 soundness=2)
- **Analysis of forgetting**: The paper clearly analyzes the underlying causes of forgetting in DIL. (R2: strength 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparison with vision-language models**: Missing experiments with CLIP and CLIP-based fine-tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) to show advantages over general zero-shot models. (R1: weakness 1)
- **Missing backbone generalization**: Experiments only use ViT-B/16; should also test on convolutional backbones like ResNet. Additionally, other methods (e.g., S-Prompts) report results on the stronger CLIP backbone – it is unclear if DUCT maintains effectiveness on a stronger backbone. (R1: weakness 3; R2: weakness 4)
- **Theoretical justification**: Weighted summation of model weights (Eqs. 4–5) is uninterpretable; the paper does not provide theoretical analysis of why this combination works or whether it intensifies feature space conflicts. (R2: weakness 1; R3: weakness 2)
- **Novelty concerns**: The main accuracy boost appears to come from task vectors (ablation Table 2), which is an existing technique; the weighting modification provides limited new insight into why it helps DIL. More explanation is needed, e.g., why DUCT places same classes together (Fig. 5). (R5: weakness 1; R4: weakness 1)  
  - R4 notes that integrating models to balance stages has been done in prior work (Zheng et al., 2023, ICCV).
- **Notation and presentation issues** (multiple reviewers):
  - Many notations are not introduced before use, e.g., \(\phi_i^m\), \(\alpha_\phi\) in Eq. 4. (R3: weakness 1; R4: weakness 2)
  - Equations appear in incorrect order: Eq. 5 depends on a value not defined until Eq. 7. (R3: weakness 1)
  - Confusing phrasing: "b" in "b|Y|" unexplained (line 143); "features", "representation", "embedding" used interchangeably (lines 179–185). (R4: weakness 2)
  - Eq. 4 lacks interpretation of maxima value and initial values in summation. (R4: weakness 2)
  - In Eq. 5, \(\phi_i^m\) uses subscript \(i\) which conflicts with the summation index. (R5: weakness 2)
  - \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) should be explained when first introduced (line 300). (R5: weakness 2)
  - Ambiguity about memory: "at most two backbones in memory" (line 257) contradicts the three models listed; integration process conflicted between line 256 and Algorithm 1. (R4: weakness 2)
- **Imbalance between old and new domains**: As domains accumulate, merged models become complex mixtures while new domain models contain only latest data, potentially causing confusion and forgetting. (R2: weakness 3)
- **Insufficient application scope**: Experiments only on classification tasks; claimed applications (autonomous vehicles, face recognition) are not tested. (R2: weakness 2)
- **Task similarity based on all categories**: May over-magnify influence of some categories while ignoring others. (R1: weakness 4)
- **Missing calculations**: What data are used to compute class centers for the pre-trained model? How to ensure consistency with downstream categories? How to reduce overhead from large category sets? (R1: weakness 2)
- **Upper bound missing**: Could provide an upper bound for Table 1 (e.g., finetuning on union of all datasets). (R3: question)
- **Unclear why two-stage merging is needed**: Could linear classifier weights just be absorbed into the backbone and merged with Eq. 5? (R3: question)
- **Impact of not using task vectors**: What is the result if the method does not use task vectors during integration? (R4: question)
- **Proof of domain-specific feature capture**: The claim that DUCT captures domain-specific features of all domains (lines 198–200) needs experimental proof. (R4: question)
- **First domain performance**: Initial performance on the first domain is not optimal (Fig. 2) – needs elaboration. (R5: question)
- **Head‑merge ratio sensitivity**: What if \(\alpha_W = 0\)? (R5: question; Fig. 3(c) shows decent performance with small \(\alpha_W\))
- **Applicability to class‑incremental learning**: Could the method extend to class‑incremental learning, treating new classes as new categories? (R5: question)
