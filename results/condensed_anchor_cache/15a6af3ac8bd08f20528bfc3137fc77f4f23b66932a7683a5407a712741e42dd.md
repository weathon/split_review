## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies score estimation in diffusion models using a two-layer fully connected neural network trained by gradient descent. It introduces a parametric form to formulate denoising score-matching as a regression problem with noisy labels, and connects the training dynamics to kernel regression via neural tangent kernel analysis. The authors derive generalization error bounds, universal approximation, and convergence guarantees, including implications for early-stopping, and categorize errors into coupling, label mismatch, early stopping, and approximation.

### Strengths
- Addresses the significant question of whether a neural network trained via gradient descent can provably learn the score function, with potential for substantial impact on the deep learning community (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Proposes a novel network-based parametrization and a highly nontrivial technical idea that connects GD training of a two-layer NN with kernel regression, separately bounding each component (RKHS approximation, kernel regression vs. NN, label mismatch) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Provides the first end-to-end theoretical framework that gives generalization error bounds and convergence guarantees for score estimation in diffusion models, including implications for early-stopping (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Well-written with rigorous theoretical analysis and well-stated assumptions (Reviewer 2). *Contrast: While most reviewers find the analysis rigorous, Reviewer 4 expresses concerns about novelty (see Weaknesses).*
- Demonstrates equivalence between minimizing the score-matching problem (Eq. 5) and training the neural network (Eq. 8) under overparameterization (Theorem 3.9 and 3.10) (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity**: The paper is dense; dependency of constants and reasoning behind assumptions need clarification. Notation issues exist (e.g., β_x appears to depend on x but should not; the two δ functions in Assumptions 3.7 and 3.8 lack clear relation). The statement that “Assumption 3.11 can be satisfied by an extension of classical early stopping rules for scalar-valued kernel regression” needs elaboration and a concrete example. Specific details are missing: how γ is initialized, what c_1 depends on in Theorem 3.6, and what is meant by 1 − δ(Δ, R) in Assumption 3.6 (Reviewer 1).
- **Tightness and usefulness of bounds**: Upper bounds appear far from tight. Theorem 3.6’s bound can be much larger than O(d) (the scale of the noisy label); the second term in Theorem 3.9 is huge. It is unclear whether these bounds yield meaningful guarantees in specific contexts (Reviewers 1, 2). Lemma 3.3 has an exponential dependence on dimension d, raising concerns about the curse of dimensionality and limiting applicability to low-dimensional settings. The final dependency on dimension in the main bounds should be explicitly discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Choice of parametric form and training setup**: The decision to fix a and only update W lacks convincing justification compared to random feature models (where a is trained) and the NTK regime (where W remains stable). The uniform sampling of time is not justified; non-uniform weight functions are often used in practice, and the impact of the weight function on results is unexplored (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited generality**: The analysis is confined to a two-layer fully connected network trained by gradient descent, raising concerns about generalizability to deeper networks, other architectures (e.g., Transformers), and stochastic optimization. Whether the analysis can extend to more practical settings is unclear (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Novelty concerns**: The main contribution—establishing a connection between the score-matching objective (Eq. 5) and a deterministic regression problem (Eq. 8)—is not entirely new; similar connections have been proposed in prior work (e.g., [1] cited in Reviewer 4). The convergence and generalization results may be considered incremental, relying on standard NTK analysis without introducing fundamentally novel insights (Reviewer 4). *Contrast: Reviewer 1 notes the parametric form is not novel, while Reviewer 3 calls it a novel design.*
- **Bypassing stochasticity**: The analysis bypasses the inherent stochasticity of the score-matching problem by focusing on a deterministic surrogate. If one directly analyzed the stochastic objective (Eq. 5), NTK would encapsulate Brownian motion randomness, likely leading to different results. Thus the current analysis may not fully capture the dynamics of the original problem (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of empirical validation**: The work is purely theoretical and lacks experiments to validate key assumptions (Assumptions 3.2, 3.4, 3.5, 3.7, 3.8, 3.11). For example, bounded support (Assumption 3.2) is often violated due to outliers or heavy-tailed distributions. Without numerical experiments, it is hard to determine which of the four error categories (coupling, label mismatch, early stopping, approximation) dominates, and the bounds offer limited practical guidance (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Assumption 3.5 appears to be a fact rather than an assumption when the target is bounded (Reviewer 1).
