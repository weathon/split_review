## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ID-Booth, a fine-tuning framework for diffusion models (SD-2.1, SD-XL) that adds a triplet identity loss to improve identity-consistent face image generation for synthetic face recognition (FR) training data, addressing privacy concerns. The method builds on PortraitBooth. Reviewers find the novelty very limited (a minor addition), the quantitative results (Tables 1–3) inconclusive with no clear advantage over baselines, the FR accuracy near random-guess levels, and gaps in scalability, ablations, and comparisons.

### Strengths
* The triplet identity training objective is the central contribution (R1).
* The paper identifies a valid and important problem regarding privacy and consent in face data collection (R2, R3, R4).
* The quantitative metrics used (CMMD, CR-FIQA, FID, Precision, Recall) are insightful and correct (R3, R4).
* The Related Works section and methodology background provide sufficient context (R4).
* Overall writing and clarity are strong (R2, R4).
* The authors plan to release code (R2).

### Weaknesses
* **Limited novelty.** The entire contribution is the triplet identity loss (Section 3.4), which is a minor extension of PortraitBooth and a standard loss in face recognition (R2, R3, R4). There is no significant methodological advance (R2: “just a minor extension”; R4: “severely limited”).
* **Inconclusive quantitative results – no significant improvement.** 
  - Table 1: ID-Booth does not outperform DreamBooth or PortraitBooth on FID and CMMD in most settings across both models and datasets (TFD, FFHQ). When it does, improvements are negligible (margins of +0.02 to +0.1) (R4). 
  - Precision/Recall: the largest margin over the next best is 0.008 (R4). 
  - CR-FIQA: ID-Booth is not the best method (R4). 
  - Table 2: similar behavior, though ID-Booth achieves FDR improvements up to ~+5.2 in some cases, but these alone are insufficient (R4). 
  - FMR100 and FMR1000 show minor gains only for synthetic identities with complex prompts on SD-2.1 (R4).
  - Reviewers note no clear advantage of the additional loss term (R2, R3).
* **Weak face recognition performance.** 
  - The FR model trained on ID-Booth synthetic data underperforms previous synthetic-data methods (Arc2Face, DCFace) (R1). 
  - Table 3 accuracies are near 0.5 (random-guess level) on several benchmarks; the claimed 0.5%–2.8% improvement on an average of ~54% accuracy is not substantial (R2, R3). 
  - These low results are not convincing evidence of the method’s efficacy (R3). 
  - The paper uses an ArcFace-based network during fine-tuning, which may inflate results; given that LAION-5B contains millions of faces, much larger gains would be expected (R2).
* **Evaluation metric concerns.** The paper uses InceptionV3 (likely trained on ImageNet) for FID, Recall, and Precision, despite studies showing its inefficacy for face-generation evaluation (R2). The use of CMMD is acknowledged but does not show advantage over DreamBooth or PortraitBooth (R2). 
  - Reviewer questions: Why is Precision for TFD near-zero in Table 1? Why is there only one CR-FIQA column (not separated by dataset)? Adding a scale for metrics (e.g., is 0.8 FDR improvement significant?) would help interpretation (R4).
* **Lack of scalability and speed analysis.** The method fine-tunes the diffusion model for each identity individually, raising concerns about scaling to large datasets (e.g., over a million identities) (R3). Further, the finetune-then-generate procedure slows sampling; a speed analysis showing time spent on fine-tuning vs. generation is missing (R1).
* **Missing ablations.** The paper provides no ablations (R4). It uses older pre-trained models (ResNet-101 for CR-FIQA, ArcFace for cosine similarity) without exploring alternative choices (R4).
* **Missing comparison with state-of-the-art.** The paper does not compare with IDiffFace, which is mentioned in the related work (R3). The FR baselines Arc2Face and DCFace are mentioned but ID-Booth underperforms them (R1).
* **Insufficient qualitative comparison.** Figure 4 is the only qualitative comparison but lacks the specific prompts used for each image, making accuracy to depicted emotions unverifiable (R4). Figure 3 shows close to no difference across methods (R4).
* **Privacy concern with pretrained models.** The paper raises GDPR issues with scraped face datasets, yet builds on SD-2.1/SD-XL trained on LAION-5B, which itself contains millions of face images with the same consent issues. The authors do not show that the fine-tuned model is free from those training identities (R2).
