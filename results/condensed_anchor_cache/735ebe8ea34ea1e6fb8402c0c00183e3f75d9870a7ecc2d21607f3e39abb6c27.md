## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper aims to unify signal-to-noise (S2N) diffusion models from theoretical perspectives, connecting prior works (Kingma et al. 2021, Zhang & Chen 2022, Song et al. 2020) and introducing a generalized backward equation with hyperparameters \(\rho, \gamma, \delta\). Two reviewers (scores 3,3) find the work limited: poor organization, unclear novelty, marginal experimental gains, and heavy reliance on prior work. Two reviewers (scores 5,5) appreciate the unifying perspective, didactic value, and correct mathematical derivations, but also note insufficient experimental validation and a lack of clear benefits from the theoretical contributions.

### Strengths
- **Unifying theoretical perspective:** Attempts to integrate and organize existing diffusion models (Kingma, Zhang & Chen, Song) into a single framework, connecting forward SDEs with VDM, continuous Non-Markovian processes, and an information-theoretic viewpoint. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Mathematical correctness:** Derivations appear correct. (R2)
- **Good writing quality:** English writing is good and the paper is easy to follow (R3); one reviewer notes strong didactic value, summarizing scattered knowledge. (R3)
- **Interesting empirical observation:** Figure 1 shows that existing models may not make optimal sampler choices for Euler-style first-order solvers, with non-standard \(\gamma\) choices yielding lower FID on CIFAR-10. (R2)
- **Performance improvements in some settings:** Achieves higher FID than inference baselines (e.g., AFHQ with \(\gamma=0.026\)). (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal and inconsistent experimental gains:** The reported improvements are minimal in many cases: 3.39 vs. 3.43 on FFHQ, 1.68 vs. 1.70 on conditional ImageNet, 2.36–2.40 on unconditional CIFAR-10. Only AFHQ shows a meaningful difference. (R1, R2, R4) These marginal gains raise concerns about whether the increased complexity is justified. (R4) Confidence intervals or statistical significance tests are lacking. (R1)
- **Limited experimental scope:** Evaluation is restricted to low-resolution datasets (CIFAR-10 32×32, FFHQ 64×64, AFHQv2 64×64, ImageNet 64×64) and only uses FID as a single metric. Higher-resolution data (common in VDM++, etc.) are not tested, limiting generalizability. (R1, R2, R3, R4) Only pretrained models using the EDM formalism are considered; other samplers (beyond Euler’s method) and other pretrained model families are not compared. (R1, R2) Results rely on expensive grid searches for up to three hyperparameters without discussion of dataset/architecture dependence. (R2)
- **Lack of clear novelty and heavy reliance on prior work:** Substantial portions reproduce or trivially extend known results. Theorem 1 is a simple variable change; Theorem 2 is reproduced from Zhang & Chen (2022, Eq. 16) without clear marking; Theorem 3’s generalization (introducing \(\rho\)) is not challenging and yields only minor improvements, with \(\rho=0\) and \(\rho=1\) already covered by prior work. (R1, R2) The overall contribution is heavily built on Kingma et al. (2021) and Song et al. (2020a) with only incremental additions. (R2, R3) Missing citations: important diffusion model references and fundamental SDE/stochastic process literature are omitted (e.g., survey at github.com/chq1155/A-Survey-on-Generative-Diffusion-Model, arxiv 2209.00796). (R1)
- **Poor organization and unclear presentation:** The paper is disjointed; it is often unclear which parts are novel vs. reproduced. Sections 3.4–3.6 (Theorems 4–6) are not used in the experiments and lack connection to the main story. (R2) The SNR-based viewpoint, a key feature of the title, does not appear necessary for Theorems 1–3 and Corollary 1 (the only results used experimentally) and actually increases complexity of expressions rather than reducing it. (R2) Overly extensive derivations (e.g., lines 253–269) clutter the main text. (R2) The definition of “signal-to-noise space” is never given despite being claimed as a contribution. (R1)
- **Mathematical decoration and notational issues:** Redundant notation (both \(\lambda(t)\) and \(SNR(t)\)) adds cognitive load. (R1) The transition function \(\Phi(\tau,t)\) lacks derivation. (R1) \(\beta(s,t)\) is used before it is defined. (R2) The Euler-Maruyama discussion and Theorems are presented with excessive formality for trivial or known content. (R1) Example images in the appendix are not novel for 2025. (R1)
- **Missing experimental details and questionable experimental choices:** Baseline method is Euler’s method, which is clearly weak; higher-order samplers exist and should be compared at similar NFE. (R1) The dataset split for hyperparameter grid search is not clarified (e.g., separate validation set vs. test data used for tuning). (R1) NFE is inconsistent: 35 in Section 4.1 (Figure 1) vs. 511 in Section 4.2 (Figures 2–3) without explanation. (R2) Figure 2 uses a bar chart with a y-axis not starting at 0, visually exaggerating a trivial difference (1.68 vs. 1.70). (R2)
- **Insufficient insight from theoretical contributions:** The paper states theoretical results but does not provide the authors’ own interpretation of their benefits or connection to experimental outcomes. Section 4 merely paraphrases figures without deeper analysis. (R3) The motivation and intuition for hyperparameters \(\gamma, \delta, \rho\) are unclear. (R2) Only \(\rho=0\) and \(\rho=1\) are evaluated; other \(\rho\) values are not explored. (R2)
- **Typos and formatting:** “Non-Markovnian” should be “Non-Markovian” (repeated). Missing equation numbers. SNR should be typeset as a single operator. S2N abbreviation appears before its definition. (R2, R3)
- **Suggestions to improve structure:** Move some theoretical content (e.g., Theorem 2, Theorem 5) to the appendix to balance Sections 3 and 4. (R3) Clearly state which results are reproduced and which are novel, and the purpose of each generalization. (R2)
