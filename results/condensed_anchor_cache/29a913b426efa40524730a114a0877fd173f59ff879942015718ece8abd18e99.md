## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a window-based dynamic attention (WDA) module and a dynamic-reference entropy model (DREM) for learned image compression (LIC). WDA sparsifies the attention matrix within local windows based on entropy, and DREM adaptively selects context symbols for entropy estimation. The method is evaluated on Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick datasets against learned and conventional codecs. Reviewers agree that the paper addresses the challenge of overfitting in vision-transformer-based LIC by dynamically suppressing irrelevant context. However, most reviewers find the technical novelty limited, the experimental comparisons outdated, the performance gains marginal or inconsistent, and the analysis of computational overhead lacking. One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, praising the thorough mathematical formulation and potential impact, while the other four (score 3) considered the contribution insufficient for acceptance.

### Strengths
1. The WDA and DREM modules capture redundancy in latent space by dynamically tuning attention based on entropy, offering a new perspective on addressing overfitting in ViT-based LIC. (Reviewers 1, 5)
2. The method yields consistent rate–distortion improvements on the Kodak dataset across multiple benchmarks. (Reviewers 1, 4)
3. The paper is well organized and easy to follow. (Reviewer 2)
4. The research is thorough, with rigorous mathematical formulations and comprehensive experiments on multiple datasets; core ideas are clearly presented. (Reviewer 3, positive minority)
5. The introduction of covariance-matrix-based sparsification that adjusts attention weights at a fine-grained level based on feature correlations is noted. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty / incremental contribution.** Dynamic attention (sparsifying the attention matrix, e.g., via top‑k masking) is already known in other fields and simply applied to LIC. The proposed modules are variants of existing Transformer structures rather than fundamentally new frameworks or theories. Previous works (e.g., [2] Liu et al., [3] He et al., Entroformer, EulerMormer) have explored similar ideas in LIC or attention masking, and the differences are not adequately highlighted. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
2. **Motivation for dynamic attention in window-based setting is insufficiently justified.** The connection between long-range modeling and overfitting is not clearly explained; the paper should provide experimental evidence (e.g., testing error curves) proving overfitting in ViT-based LICs. The reason why dynamic attention within windows is beneficial over fixed masking strategies (e.g., fixed top‑k) is not established. (Reviewers 1, 3, 5)
3. **Outdated baselines and missing comparisons.** Only methods up to 2023 are compared; the following recent works should be evaluated: *Frequency-Aware Transformer for Learned Image Compression* (ICLR 2024, H. Li et al.), *GroupedMixer: An Entropy Model with Group-wise Token-Mixers* (TCSVT 2024), *Causal Context Adjustment Loss for Learned Image Compression* (NeurIPS 2024), and state‑of‑the‑art dynamic attention methods (e.g., those cited in Sec. 2.2) which could be adapted by swapping modules. (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
4. **Marginal and inconsistent performance gains.** On the Tecnick and CLIC datasets, the proposed method shows degraded performance: PSNR is lower than VVC and the Jiang (ACMMM2023) baseline, and results are inconsistent with the trend on Kodak. The drop is particularly visible at high bitrates. (Reviewers 1? actually 4 and 5; Reviewer 1 did not mention inconsistency, but Reviewer 4 and 5 explicitly)
5. **Lack of computational overhead analysis.** The additional complexity from covariance calculation and dynamic decision‑making is not quantified. The paper omits comparisons of model parameters, FLOPs, encoding/decoding latency, and memory usage; these are critical for assessing practicality, especially for high‑resolution images. (Reviewers 4, 5)
6. **Ablation study missing for the threshold \(t\).** How the threshold that determines sparsity is set (and its sensitivity) is not investigated. (Reviewer 1)
7. **Figures and terminology issues.** Fig. 2 incorrectly depicts \(Q\) and \(K\) as square matrices (4×4) that cannot produce a 16×16 output through matrix multiplication. Terms like “the sequence” (L215) are undefined. There are missing figure references (lines 193, 284) and grammar/spelling errors (lines 288, 291). (Reviewers 1, 3, 5)
8. **Clarity of key sections.** Section 3.3 (DREM) and Equation 12 need reorganization for better coherence. In Sec. 4.3, “Atten denotes plain attention patterns that discards masks” is ambiguous: does it mean plain window‑based attention or full attention across all pixels? (Reviewers 1, 3)
9. **Limited contribution of DREM.** The dynamic-reference entropy model essentially adds a dynamic attention module to an existing entropy model; its novelty is questionable. (Reviewer 5)
