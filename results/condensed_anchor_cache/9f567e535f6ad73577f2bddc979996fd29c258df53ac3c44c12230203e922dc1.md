## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the problem in graph contrastive learning (GCL) of treating misclassified false negative nodes equally regardless of their proximity to positive nodes. It proposes Smoothed Graph Contrastive Learning (SGCL), which leverages geometric structure of augmented graphs to assign smoothed weights to positive/negative pairs in the contrastive loss. The framework also includes a graph batch-generating strategy for scalability. Experimental results are reported on benchmark datasets.

### Strengths
- The problem of equal treatment of misclassified false negatives is well-motivated and interesting.
- The main idea of smoothing positive/negative weights based on graph structure is simple, innovative, and novel.
- The approach has a solid theoretical foundation, and ample background knowledge is provided to assist readers.
- The proposed method performs well in experiments (one reviewer with score 8 found it highly effective; other reviewers had mixed opinions on experimental validity).
- Presentation is clear according to one reviewer, though others note significant writing issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Outdated baselines and missing SOTA**: The newest baseline is from 2022; the paper misses recent state-of-the-art methods. Reported results do not align with original results for some baselines.
- **Insufficient experimental detail**: The experimental section is too brief, dataset splits are not standard (public split for self-supervised learning), and ablation studies are lacking (e.g., effect of the proposed contrastive objective vs. previous contrastive objectives, impact of each component: smoothing, loss, subgraph generation).
- **No computational cost or time analysis**: The paper does not provide cost analysis or runtime comparisons with baselines.
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Many minor mistakes and typos (e.g., "including , including" in Section 2; "Therfore" in Section 3.1; "distinguishe" in Section 4.2.4; Figure 2 caption incorrectly states g(j) is not a positive pair; missing curly braces in Section 3.2 second paragraph "{0,1}" should be "\{0,1\}"; Section 4.2.3: Is $\tilde D_{ii}$ the degree+1?). The structure is impractical: too much background (Section 3) delays introduction of the proposed method, leaving little space for experiments.
- **Incremental contribution**: The smoothing technique, loss function, and subgraph generating strategy appear easily detachable from the framework; the core idea may be seen as trivial or incremental by some reviewers (score 3 each), though one reviewer (score 8) considered it non-trivial.
- **Lack of rationale and clarity on loss function (Eq.4)**:
  - The intuition behind the loss is not provided, and the choice of $\lambda$ is not explained.
  - The cross-correlation matrix $C$ is claimed to be $N\times N$ (Eq.5) but cross-correlation of the embedding matrix would be $F\times F$ (dimension mismatch).
  - In Eq.4, when $i\neq j$ and $\hat{\pi}(i, j) = 1$ (likely false negative), the loss assigns high weight to minimize $c_{ij}$, which is counterintuitive — $c_{ij}$ should be maximized rather than minimized.
- **Smoothing only uses original graph structure**: After augmentation (e.g., node dropping), node relationships may change; the proposal still relies on the original graph, which may be inefficient or invalid in such cases.
- **Missing persuasive demonstration**: The paper does not convincingly show why misaligning negative pairs is harmful.
- **Restricted to node-level tasks?**: One reviewer questions whether the method can be adopted for graph-level tasks; no such extension is discussed.
- **Additional questions from reviewers**: Final embedding dimensions used in experiments are not specified.
- **Contrasting reviewer opinion**: One reviewer (score 8, confidence 2) found no weaknesses and considered the method strong; the other three reviewers (scores 5, 3, 3) had significant concerns regarding novelty, experimental rigor, writing, and theoretical soundness. This strong disagreement in the overall assessment is an important aspect of the review.
