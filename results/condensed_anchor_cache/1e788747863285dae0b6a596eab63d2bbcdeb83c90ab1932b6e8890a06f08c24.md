## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces APPO, a computationally efficient offline PbRL algorithm that uses a two-player game between policy and model to enforce conservatism without explicit confidence sets. The authors provide sample complexity bounds under standard assumptions (function approximation, bounded trajectory concentrability) and demonstrate competitive performance on continuous control tasks (Metaworld).

### Strengths
- **Theoretical soundness with practical viability**: Multiple reviewers highlight that APPO provides rigorous sample complexity bounds without relying on intractable confidence sets (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 notes that combining theory with competitive empirical performance is a significant contribution, unusual for mostly theoretical work.
- **Algorithmic novelty**: The Q function estimation part in Algorithm 2 is interesting and novel (Reviewer 2). The two-player game formulation enforces conservatism in a tractable manner (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Qualified empirical performance**: APPO performs on par with or surpasses SOTA offline PbRL baselines on continuous control tasks (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 2 notes that this empirical evaluation is lacking in many theoretical works.
- **Versatility**: APPO can be integrated with any standard unconstrained reinforcement learning algorithm (Reviewer 3).
- **Well-motivated and organized**: The paper is clearly written, with excellent formalization and embedding into related work (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 suggests adding an overview table of related work (bounds, assumptions, constraints) would further strengthen clarity.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty concerns (minority view)**: Reviewer 2 (score 6, confidence 3) notes that the core idea of using two-player games to avoid confidence sets is very similar to prior standard RL works (e.g., "Adversarially Trained Actor Critic for Offline Reinforcement Learning" by Ching-An Cheng). This reviewer expects the authors to highlight new analysis techniques, which are not clearly distinguished.
- **Computational efficiency is limited**: Reviewer 2 argues that despite avoiding confidence sets, Algorithm 2 is not computationally efficient because Line 4 cannot be solved with least squares or MLE oracles, which is the common standard for computational efficiency.
- **Unclear justification for rejecting Lagrangian approach**: Reviewer 2 is unconvinced by the claim (Line 204-207) that Lagrangian multipliers to remove confidence sets are not applicable here, since such an approach works in standard RL. (Question 1)
- **Sensitivity to the conservatism regularizer coefficient λ**: Reviewer 3 (score 8, confidence 2) notes that improper tuning of λ leads to suboptimal performance and stability issues, requiring additional hyperparameter optimization. (Question 1: impact and selection of λ)
- **Assumptions may limit applicability**: Both Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 4 raise concerns about assumptions. Reviewer 3: theoretical guarantees rely on function approximation and bounded trajectory concentrability; if these fail in certain environments, performance and reliability may be compromised. (Question 3) Reviewer 4: the fixed-length requirement and Markovian reward assumption are correctly stated but not discussed regarding whether they are substantial or just for proof; fixed-length limits variable-length episodes, Markovian reward may not hold in real-world scenarios (rewards may depend on full history). The paper should discuss implications and potential relaxations.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: 
  - Reviewer 1: APPO is only evaluated on a medium-replay dataset from Metaworld; broader datasets are needed to assess generalizability. (Question 1)
  - Reviewer 4: lacks ablation studies, e.g., impact of smaller/larger $D_{traj}$ sets (independent of number of preferences). (Question 5: also asks for description of $D_{traj}$ size used in experiments.)
  - Reviewer 4: Table 1 should include a rank or critical distance plot for direct comparison with SOTA, as current comparison is inconclusive.
  - Reviewer 2: suggests alternative simpler formulation for Algorithm 2 (replace true environment with estimated $\widehat{P}$ and regularizers) (Question 2).
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - Reviewer 1: How does APPO compare to CPL (Hejna et al., 2024), which uses supervised learning and achieves comparable performance with lower computational complexity? (Question 2)
  - Reviewer 3: Comparison to IQL-based algorithms in terms of hyperparameter tuning advantages and disadvantages. (Question 2)
- **Scalability and data sparsity concerns (Reviewer 3)**: Questions about scalability in high-dimensional state/action spaces and related computational complexity (Question 4); how APPO copes with sparse or non-representative preference data (Question 5).
- **Paper is not self-contained**: Reviewer 4 notes repeated references to the appendix; while often sufficient, some details are missing from the main text (e.g., KL regularizer in Algorithm 1 & 2 is only required for Algorithm 3, but not explained). A condensed version of Theorem 4.1 and other key proofs should be in the main paper.
- **Typo**: Reviewer 4 notes that in Table 1, the baseline "Oracle" should likely be "MR" (method from Choi et al., 2024).
