## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FAPrompt, a zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) framework that learns fine-grained abnormality prompts to overcome the coarse-grained semantics of prior prompt-based methods. Two core modules are introduced: Compound Abnormality Prompting (CAP), which learns a set of complementary abnormality prompts composed of shared normal tokens and learnable abnormal tokens, and Data-dependent Abnormality Prior (DAP), which derives sample-wise abnormality features from the query image to ground prompts across datasets. Experiments on 19 industrial/medical datasets report 3%–5% AUC/AP improvements over state-of-the-art at both image and pixel levels.

### Strengths
1. **Clear writing and organization.** Diagrams effectively illustrate the pipeline and key ideas.
2. **Thorough experimental evaluation.** 19 diverse datasets (industrial defects and medical anomalies) are used; results show substantial improvements over current ZSAD methods.
3. **Novel compound prompt design.** The concept of combining shared normal tokens with learnable abnormal tokens is interesting and original.
4. **Reasonable motivation.** Addressing the limitation of coarse-grained abnormality semantics in prior work is well-justified and practically important.

### Weaknesses
1. **Marginal pixel-level improvement and inferiority on some datasets.** In Table 2, the simple AnomalyCLIP achieves comparable or even superior pixel-level results on several industrial datasets, questioning the effectiveness of the proposed fine-grained approach.
2. **Limited novelty of CAP.** The compound abnormality prompt module is essentially a combination of prompt tuning and prototype learning with an orthogonal constraint; this does not fundamentally differ from existing techniques that capture diverse features. The method is not sufficiently distinct from prior prompt ensemble or prototype learning approaches.
3. **DAP module is technically similar to CoCoOp.** The design of the data-dependent abnormality prior closely follows CoCoOp, with the only modification being the use of selected image patch features instead of global features. The description of the learnable layers in DAP is missing.
4. **Averaging abnormality prompts collapses diversity.** The final abnormality embedding is the average of all K abnormality prompt embeddings, reducing them to a single vector prototype. This undermines the claim of “fine-grained” representation and is insufficient when an image contains multiple anomaly types. Alternatives (e.g., selecting the most similar prompt) are not discussed or evaluated.
5. **Insufficient ablation on K (number of abnormality prompts).** Figure 7 shows very limited sensitivity of pixel-level results to K, and results for K=1 are missing. Without a K=1 baseline (with and without CAP/DAP), the necessity of multiple prompts cannot be justified.
6. **Missing required baselines.** (a) AnomalyCLIP with an ensemble of multiple abnormality prompts trained with the orthogonal constraint loss — needed to verify the contribution of fine-grained prompts over a simple ensemble. (b) State-of-the-art prompt tuning methods such as PromptSRC and TCP are not compared, leaving a gap in relative performance assessment.
7. **Sensitivity and design issues of DAP.** The number of selected patches M is fixed across images, which is unreasonable because anomalous regions vary in scale. For pixel-level ZSAD, M is reported to be quite sensitive.
8. **Visualization conflicts and lack of discriminative evidence.** (a) The heatmaps in Figure 1 appear to contradict actual experiment results (no specifics provided). (b) In Figure 3, the fine-grained prompts (different colors) are not well separable, and no comparison with visualizations from baseline models is provided to substantiate that the prompt-wise anomaly scores demonstrate discriminability.
9. **Inconsistent behavior on medical datasets.** In Table 4, the CAP module alone achieves the best image-level results on medical datasets, even outperforming the full FAPrompt. This discrepancy is not explained.
10. **Token design may induce trivial discrimination.** Because abnormality prompts have a different number of tokens than normal prompts, they may be easily distinguishable without learning fine-grained semantics (L087–L089).
11. **Inaccurate claim about human annotations.** The first contribution states no human annotations, but in cross-dataset scenarios the prompts are updated based on human-annotated normal/abnormal categories from the source data.
12. **Missing ablations on architectural choices.** The number of layers with learnable tokens and the token length are not ablated. Also, the effectiveness of the compound prompt (normal+abnormal tokens) is not isolated: results with CAP and DAP applied independently without this combination are lacking.
13. **Minor presentation suggestion.** Tables 3 and 4 should be merged for easier method comparison; Figure 7 should be moved to the main manuscript since pixel-level results are equally important.
