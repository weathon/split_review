## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses novel class discovery in point cloud segmentation. It proposes an adaptive self-labeling strategy using a semi-relaxed optimal transport (OT) problem with an annealing-like regularization to handle imbalanced classes, and a dual-level representation that incorporates regional consistency into point-level classifier learning. The method is evaluated on SemanticKITTI and SemanticPOSS, outperforming the prior work NOPS by a large margin, with particularly impressive results on SemanticPOSS. The work is well-motivated and technically sound, with comprehensive ablation studies.

### Strengths
1. **Technically sound and well-motivated.** The method directly addresses two major limitations of the prior NOPS approach: the equal class-size constraint (unsuitable for imbalanced point clouds) and the lack of spatial context in point-wise clustering. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
2. **Comprehensive experiments and ablation studies.** The experimental evaluation is detailed and meticulous, thoroughly validating the impact of each component. Results on SemanticPOSS are especially remarkable. (Reviewers 1, 2)
3. **Interesting formulation of relaxed optimal transport.** The adaptive regularization strategy effectively anneals the class-balance constraint, supported by both theoretical meaning and empirical evidence. (Reviewer 3)
4. **Promising empirical performance.** The method consistently outperforms previous approaches, with significant gains on both datasets. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
5. **Generally well-written and easy to follow.** (Reviewer 3, though several reviewers note clarity issues – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity and presentation issues.** 
   - Figure 1 is incomplete and confusing: unexplained symbols and colors, missing “Encoder f_theta”, unclear arrows among p and y, and no clear indication of where the novel pseudo-label generation or dual-level representation takes place. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
   - The paper’s organization disrupts flow and obscures the relation between the two main contributions (self-labeling and dual-level representation); contributions feel scattered. (Reviewer 4)
   - Heavy use of unnecessary sub/superscripts (e.g., Sections 3.1 and 3.2) hinders readability. (Reviewer 3)
   - The motivation for “degenerate solutions” in point-cloud segmentation and the need for a semi-relaxed OT definition are not clearly explained. (Reviewer 4)

2. **Missing methodological details.** 
   - DBSCAN clustering parameters (e.g., epsilon, min samples) are not given; visual examples of the resulting regions and analysis of parameter sensitivity are lacking. It is also unclear whether the number of regions K is fixed across scenes and how those regions are generated. (Reviewers 1, 2)
   - The initialization and update of class prototypes are not elaborated. (Reviewer 1)
   - Fig. 1 suggests the method uses two augmented views of the same point cloud, but the role of these augmentations is never explained – whether they are necessary for the method to function, how they interact with other strategies, and how fairness is ensured in comparisons against methods that do not use them. (Reviewer 3)
   - The role of data-dependent annealing in both the self-labeling and dual-level representation is inadequately described in the introduction. (Reviewer 4)

3. **Insufficient analysis of the relaxed optimal transport component.** 
   - The entropy term added to Eq. 4 (to enable fast scaling algorithms) is not explained; its effect on the OT solution and pseudo-label generation is unaddressed. The appendix discusses time complexity but not class-imbalance aspects. (Reviewer 3)
   - More analysis is needed on how the semi-relaxed OT captures class balance/imbalance, and how reducing weight r recovers the original balanced OT. (Reviewer 3)
   - No visualization or theoretical analysis shows how different values of r affect the generated pseudo-labels (e.g., for larger vs. smaller r). (Reviewer 3)
   - The motivation for introducing a semi-relaxed OT formulation is not clearly established from the outset. (Reviewer 4)

4. **Experimental and applicability concerns.** 
   - The region-level design (dual representation) contributes only marginally in Table 4, undermining its claim as a major contribution – the paper appears to address two separate problems without a clear focus. (Reviewer 4)
   - No ablation study is performed on a more challenging split of SemanticKITTI (e.g., splits 6,7 or similar), which would strengthen the findings – current results are only on one split. (Reviewer 1)
   - In Table 4, applying region-level learning lowers the IoU for ‘Building’. Why does this happen? Is the method more beneficial for smaller objects? (Reviewer 2)
   - Applicability to indoor scenarios (with many more novel classes) is not discussed, limiting practical relevance. (Reviewer 1)
   - Regarding Table 3, adding a column with the split index (as in Table 1) would improve clarity. (Reviewer 1)

5. **Problem scope assumption.** The setting assumes the number of novel classes is predetermined; in real open-world scenarios the number is often unknown. (Reviewer 1)
