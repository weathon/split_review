## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a structured universe graph for partial matching to handle point occlusion, and energy-based OOD detection to remove annotation errors. Evaluations on Pascal VOC and Willow Object datasets show SOTA performance, supported by ablation studies. The concept of a universal graph is considered novel and intriguing. However, reviewers raise concerns about clarity (especially Section 3.1), generalization to unseen classes, experimental completeness (missing recent benchmarks, hyperparameter reporting, qualitative results), the ad-hoc nature of the OOD thresholding, and the method’s reliance on class labels. One reviewer (R4, rating 8) was more positive, emphasizing the method’s significance and robustness, while others (R3, R5) were more critical of presentation and experimental scope (ratings 5).

### Strengths
- The proposed latent universal graph is novel, effective, and has the potential to inspire future work. (R1, R5)
- The approach considers both node and edge information, providing a general formulation where edges help the matching. (R3)
- The outlier detection mechanism is needed given the prevalence of outliers in practice. (R3)
- The motivation is good, and the method is easy to follow due to intuitive motivation. (R2)
- The paper is well-written, clear, and full of details (R2, R6); theoretical and experimental analysis is basically complete. (R6)
- SOTA performance with good ablation studies (R2, R6); outperforms classical and learning-based methods, robust to outliers and OOD keypoints. (R4, R6)
- The paper discusses limitations (R4) and includes ablation studies to analyze design choices. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of Section 3.1 is poor**: lacks high-level description; “ground truth universe matching” and X^gt_iu are never properly defined; the paragraph says it uses ground truth pairwise matching X^gt_ij. (R3) The statement that “keypoints from different categories are mapped to the same universe node” appears to cause category confusion (R3, R5). How are node/edge features extracted from image feature maps in Figure 2? Describe step-by-step. (R5)
- **Definitions needed**: EdgeID in Eqn.6 needs definition (R1); what EdgeID(0) and EdgeID(1) represent? (R5) How is Eqn.4 applied for generating ground truth X_iu? (i) if universal graph nodes > N, factorization is not unique—which one to pick? (ii) X^GT is binary, is X_u continuous or discrete? If continuous, using argmax as label in Eqn.6 may affect performance. (R1)
- **Generalization to unseen categories**: The universal graph is built from training data, so the method cannot generalize to unseen classes during inference (R1, R5). The method requires class information, preventing application to other matching problems (e.g., geometric feature matching for 3D reconstruction or stereo matching) (R3). Experiments or discussion on generalization to new object classes are needed (R5). Could the method be applied in a closed-set only? (R1)
- **OOD definition and detection mechanism**: How is OOD data defined in Eqn.9? If OOD data is seen during training, how is it treated as OOD? Does the method generalize to truly unseen OOD? (R1) The outlier detection via thresholding similarity is weak and ad-hoc: it may reject correct matches (e.g., a wheel from a bike confused with a wheel from a car). Why not learn a function to detect outliers, e.g., by learning to turn off similarity entry in the affinity matrix? (R3)
- **Experiments are incomplete**:
  - Missing recent benchmarks, e.g., IMC-PT-SparseGM [A]; only two datasets (Pascal VOC, Willow Object) falls short. (R3)
  - Some baselines are not SOTA; for example, COMMON [B] achieves high accuracy on Willow and metrics differ from those reported in [B]. (R3) Code for COMMON (ICCV23) is available. (R5)
  - No qualitative results: successful cases not solved by baselines, and failure cases to understand the method’s margin. (R2, R6)
  - Lack of visualizations, including for ablation experiments. (R6)
  - Hyperparameter values not reported: what values of temperature, m_in, m_out, and threshold were used for experiments and ablation? (R3, R5)
  - Ablation studies are small; missing threshold value performance, m_in, m_out, temperature T. (R3)
  - Computational complexity not compared. (R6)
  - Should report Accuracy metric in addition to F1 for more comprehensive comparison. (R4)
- **General matching scenario**: Universe Graph Matching decomposes partial matching into two subgraph matching problems, dealing well with occlusion, but is matching accuracy improved in general (non-occlusion) situations? Needs analysis. (R6)
- **Limitations and future work are not discussed** (R6). The paper mentions limitations in the discussion, but this could be expanded. (R4 notes limitations discussion exists, but R6 says it’s missing; note the disagreement.)
- **Writing** could be improved in abstract and introduction for accessibility to non-experts. (R4) Typos: URL paper mis-cited in line 351/352, typo in line 108. (R4)
- **Why not attention-based approach?** It is unclear why an attention-based approach (e.g., LoFTR) could not be used for outlier rejection. (R3)
- **Training details** should be added. (R5)
