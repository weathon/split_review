## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper generalizes previous frameworks for learning diffusion bridges (e.g., Richter & Berner 2024, Vargas et al. 2024) to processes with degenerate noise—where the diffusion coefficient is zero in some dimensions—and specifically proposes *underdamped diffusion bridges* on an extended space for sampling from unnormalized target densities. The method uses splitting‑based numerical integrators (OBAB, BAOAB, OBABO) and claims end‑to‑end hyperparameter tuning. Experiments show state‑of‑the‑art performance on a range of sampling problems, often with fewer discretization steps and minimal tuning.

### Strengths
- **Performance**: Achieves record results in Table 1 and Figure 3, including medium‑dimensional data (d > 1000), outperforming prior sampling methods (Reviewer 1, 4, 5).
- **Novel extension**: Considering underdamped Langevin dynamics as a diffusion bridge with degenerate noise is a novel and well‑motivated extension of existing diffusion‑based sampling algorithms (Reviewer 2, 4, 5). The framework unifies several prior works (Tab. 2) (Reviewer 3, 5).
- **Originality**: The idea of using underdamped dynamics allows accelerated dynamics combined with score‑matching, a significant and fundamental result (Reviewer 5).
- **Presentation quality**: The paper is well‑organized, clear, and intuitive; detailed proofs, experiment details, and code are provided (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). Figure 5 clearly indicates effective hyperparameters (Reviewer 5).
- **Exploration of integrators**: The use of splitting‑based sampling schemes for underdamped diffusions appears under‑explored in the diffusion‑model literature and improves performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Ablation studies**: Robust verification of the method’s effectiveness is provided (Reviewer 5).
- **Minority positive contrast**: Reviewer 3 (score 8) found the reading enjoyable and the unified framework commendable, rating the contribution closer to 7 than 6. Reviewer 5 (score 8) emphasised originality and significance.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear theoretical contribution**: The novelty of extending prior results to degenerate noise is not well clarified. Prior derivations (e.g., Richter & Berner, Vargas et al.) may already handle or trivially extend to this case; specific theorems/lemmas that fail without non‑degeneracy are not pinpointed (Reviewer 1, 3, 5). Therefore, the significance of contribution 1 (extending to degenerate noise) is unclear (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- **Lack of theoretical justification for underdamped dynamics**: The paper does not provide a theoretical explanation of why underdamped bridges outperform overdamped ones, aside from empirical gains (Reviewer 2). The theoretical benefits beyond practical performance remain uncertain (Reviewer 4).
- **ELBO derivation and likelihood connection**: The claim that score‑matching in the underdamped case is shown equivalent to maximizing a likelihood lower bound is already present in prior work (Dockhorn et al. 2021, Sections B.3, F.1.2). The paper does not clearly distinguish what is new (Reviewer 1).
- **Mathematical rigor concerns**:
  - The conditions at the beginning of p. 15 (e.g., globally Lipschitz, missing linear growth condition) are insufficiently justified for Girsanov’s theorem and existence/uniqueness of the Fokker‑Planck PDE solution (Reviewer 3, questions 3–4).
  - The derivation of the equation after “First, we rewrite the problem by observing that” in Lemma A.3 is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3, question 5).
  - The role of the divergence‑based Radon‑Nikodym derivative (Proposition A.4) is not motivated; it is unclear whether it offers an advantage over Proposition 2.3 (Reviewer 3, question 2).
  - The inclusion of a fixed drift \(f(x,t)\) seems unnecessary; it could be absorbed into the control terms \(u,v\) (Reviewer 3, question 1).
- **Missing generative‑modeling experiments**: The paper discusses generative modeling (Section 2.1) but provides no experimental validation of the proposed underdamped dynamics when the target is only accessible via samples (Reviewer 5). This limits the practical impact of the work.
- **End‑to‑end hyperparameter tuning**: The claim that the method enables end‑to‑end tuning is a main selling point, but the mechanism is not explained. It only appears in the experiments; Figure 5 indicates learning hyperparameters is important, but the method is not detailed (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty of splitting‑based samplers**: Splitting‑based integrators have been used before (Monmarché 2021). It is unclear whether the novelty is simply applying them to diffusion bridges for sampling, which has not fully explored the design space (Reviewer 1).
- **Potential additional cost from splitting integrators**: The use of multistep splitting schemes (OBAB, BAOAB, OBABO) may add training/inference cost that could offset the advantage of fewer discretization steps (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing related work**: The simulation‑based loss appears the same as “Guided proposals for simulating multi‑dimensional diffusion bridges” [1] (forward KL, eqs. 9, 11). The approach of [2] (simulation‑free score matching for neural diffusion bridges) is also relevant but not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Notation and clarity issues**: The notation is confusing; explanations should be given when symbols first appear in the main text (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear role of augmented‑space distribution**: The construction on the enlarged space (eq. 16) is not given enough prominence; its role is unclear (Reviewer 3, question 7).
- **Claimed unification not fully accurate**: The statement that the proposed framework “encompasses all these works as special cases” (Tab. 2) is not completely accurate, because the cited works often contain additional considerations or objectives not present in this paper (Reviewer 3).
- **Lemma 2.4 ambiguity**: The text states “the ELBO in Lemma 2.4 does not depend on the target \(\tau\)”, but the displayed ELBO includes \(\mathbb{E}_{Z_0\sim\tau}[\log\tau(Z_0)]\), which depends on \(\tau\). Clarification needed (Reviewer 5).
- **Remark 3.3**: The remark about bounded drift near terminal time is not sufficiently expanded; relevant references are not clearly linked (Reviewer 3, question 6).
