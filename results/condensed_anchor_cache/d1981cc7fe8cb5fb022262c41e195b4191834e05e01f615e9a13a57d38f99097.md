## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses node classification in heterophilic graphs. It defines “structural heterophily” as a ratio of Laplacian Dirichlet energies, and proposes the Diffusion‑Jump GNN, which learns diffusion distances and uses “jumps” (learnable structural filters based on the diffusion distance matrix) to connect distant homologs, then aggregates node embeddings from multiple jumps. The method is evaluated on multiple datasets and compared with SOTA baselines (Tables 1, 2). However, presentation and clarity are major issues: the paper is poorly written, with undefined notations, confusing jargon, and insufficient justification of claims. Reviewer 1 (score 8) is strongly positive, highlighting a fresh perspective and detailed description; Reviewer 2 (score 1) is very negative, stating the paper is not ready for submission; Reviewer 3 (score 3) acknowledges good performance and reproducibility but finds novelty limited and presentation weak.

### Strengths
- Provides a comprehensive investigation into challenges faced by standard GNNs in heterophilic environments, offering a fresh perspective on understanding and quantifying heterophily (Reviewer 1).
- The Diffusion‑Jump GNN is described in great depth, covering both mathematical and conceptual foundations (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments use the same splits as previous studies, ensuring fairness; hyperparameters are provided and early stopping is used, adding reliability (Reviewer 1).
- Performance is good or at least comparable to SOTAs (Tables 1, 2) (Reviewer 3).
- Code is released, ensuring reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- Figure 8 is clear and could be moved to the main content for better illustration (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

**Presentation and Clarity** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The paper is poorly written: important notations are not defined or explained, making it very difficult to judge contributions (Reviewer 2). Examples:
  - Confusion between \(f_\Theta\) and \(f_\theta\) (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - In Eq. (1), what is \(\ell\) and is it the same as \(\ell^*\)? (Reviewer 2).
  - Terms “unstable states”, “expansion”, “sharp step function” are not defined (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - “Exploration by Parallel Jumping” section needs rewriting: “structural filters” are not clearly explained; why \(K+1\) filters? How to choose \(K\)? The authors seem to mean learning a graph filter but introduce confusing jargon (Reviewer 2).
  - Jumps \(J^k\) are presented as a tuple but should be a matrix; unclear representation (Reviewer 3).
- The abstract is too long and does not concisely capture contributions (Reviewer 2).
- The introduction contains too much technical content that is not thoroughly explained and distracts from the core idea; plainer language is needed (Reviewer 3).
- Some mathematical formulas and concepts may be challenging for non‑experts; more simplified explanation or background would help (Reviewer 1).
- The paper seems to have been written in a hurry and not proofread; it is not ready for submission (Reviewer 2).

**Novelty and Theoretical Justification** (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The novelty is not outstanding. The idea of a filter bank (set of filters/propagation matrices) is included in many existing works (e.g., [2‑5]) (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed “structural heterophily” measure is related to edge homophily. Its connection to spectral properties is not clearly established, making its theoretical justification weak (Reviewer 3). The claim that “For \(R > 1\) the graph is heterophilic” implies only \(R=1\) is homophilic, which seems inaccurate (Reviewer 3).
- The claim that Jumps \(J^k\) (Eqs 2 and 4) act as “filters” is problematic: they may not share the same eigenvectors as the original adjacency matrix, and the learned weights \(C_k\) are applied to edges, not spectral components, so the filter interpretation is not sufficiently justified (Reviewer 3).
- In Section 2, the “smoothest labeling \(l^*\)” derived solely from the Laplacian should be a constant vector, contradicting the claim (Reviewer 3).
- The “hidden graph \(G'\)” is mentioned but never clearly used or connected to “Jumps” (Reviewer 3).
- Why parameterize \(\mathbf{U} = f_\theta(\mathbf{A})\) (Eq. 3) rather than setting \(\mathbf{U}\) as a free parameter? The benefit of this design is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- The claim that “our method needs higher frequency eigenfunctions to capture extreme degree variability” (page 9) lacks evidence or references (Reviewer 3).
- The difference from graph rewiring is not addressed (Reviewer 2).

**Computational Complexity and Scalability** (Reviewer 1)
- The Diffusion‑Jump GNN may introduce high computational costs: computing the full diffusion matrix scales quadratically with the number of nodes; eigen‑decomposition is also expensive. This is a concern for large graphs (millions of nodes) and resource‑constrained or real‑time applications (Reviewer 1).

**Interpretability** (Reviewer 1)
- The “jump” mechanism lacks a clear, intuitive explanation in terms of node interactions, making it harder to understand why it works and when it might fail (Reviewer 1).

**Training Stability** (Reviewer 1)
- The interplay between the Dirichlet loss and the classification loss during training is not fully explored; it is unclear how sensitive the model is to hyperparameters related to these losses. Issues like vanishing/exploding gradients are not discussed (Reviewer 1).

**Missing Comparisons / Ablations** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- No ablation study or analysis shows the contribution of each component (e.g., learning the diffusion matrix vs. using a fixed one; the effect of different numbers of jumps \(K\)).
- The method’s versatility for graphs with multiple node/edge types or other intricate structures is not addressed (Reviewer 1).
- Potential extension to graph classification or link prediction is only mentioned in questions, not explored (Reviewer 1).
- No comparison or discussion with graph‑rewiring methods is provided (Reviewer 2).
- The role of the “hidden graph \(G'\)” in relation to Jumps is never clarified (Reviewer 3).
