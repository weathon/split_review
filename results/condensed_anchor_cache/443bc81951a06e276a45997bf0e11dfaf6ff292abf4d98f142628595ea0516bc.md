## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies controllable language generation (CLM) as a bi-objective RL problem (reward and likelihood), proposes Reward Dropout (keeping only top-percentile rewards to guarantee Pareto improvement), and evaluates on five CLM benchmarks with GPT-2. Reviewers agree the problem is interesting and the method simple/effective, but disagree on significance and quality. Two reviewers (R1, R2; scores 5) find the theoretical presentation poor, many key steps unjustified, the link between theory and experiments weak, and experiments uninformative without non-RL baselines or proper comparison to prior work (e.g., aclanthology 2022.naacl-main.57). One reviewer (R3; score 8, raised after rebuttal) considers the theoretical analysis thorough and the problem important, but notes shortcomings in the Pareto analysis and model size.

### Strengths

- The problem of balancing reward and likelihood in CLMs is interesting and important (R3).
- The theoretical analysis from a bi-objective / Pareto optimization perspective is thorough and provides a foundation (R3).
- Reward Dropout is a simple, effective method that improves performance in practice (R1, R2).
- The proofs and experimental results in Sections 4 and 5 are well-written individually (R1).
- Framing CLM as a bi-objective RL problem is a neat connection (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical justification and clarity** (R2, also R1):
  - The equivalence between Eq.3 (constrained optimization) and Eq.4 (KL divergence) is not justified (R2).
  - The policy π and function class Π are never defined (R2).
  - Why λ=1 in Eq.6 gives the optimal policy is arbitrary and not derived (R2).
  - The paper claims CLM and RLM are “intrinsically analogous” but they are different tasks; the relationship needs stronger justification (R2).
  - Footnote 1 on model-based RL is confusing and “dynamics is usually assumed” is unclear (R2).
  - The theory in Section 4 is rather trivial; the paper should show why the observations are significant (R2).
  - The reward function is exponentiated in Eq.4 without justification beyond mathematical convenience (R1 Q1).
  - If the behavior policy already maximizes reward, does the bi-objective problem reduce to single-objective? (R3 Q1).

- **Experimental evaluation and baselines** (R1, R3):
  - Section 5 experiments are not surprising or insightful: takeaways (bigger models better, OOD tasks harder) are standard. The section does not directly test theoretical conditions (e.g., uniform policy condition from 4.2, or Pareto frontier) (R1).
  - The connection between Sections 4 (theory) and 5 (experiments) is weak (R1).
  - The decoding method is expensive (fine-tunes the entire LLM per target objective, e.g., politeness, sentiment); many CLM methods use a single model with control codes. This limitation is not sufficiently highlighted (R1).
  - No non-RL-motivated baseline is included (e.g., FUDGE, Diffusion LM). Random dropout is not a proper baseline; the benefit of the RL framing is not demonstrated (R1).
  - The NLP experiment is strikingly similar to https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.57.pdf, which uses the same RL trick only at decoding time. Differences and comparisons (e.g., does fine-tuning outperform decoding-only?) are needed to motivate the RL framing (R1).
  - Only GPT-2 (a small LM) is used; larger LLMs may reduce the reward-likelihood trade-off, affecting the method’s significance (R3).
  - The paper focuses on a single “balanced” Pareto solution; the motivation for bi-objective formulation is weakened. If reward is the only concern, why not constrained optimization with likelihood as constraint? Evidence of other trade-off solutions (e.g., approximate Pareto front) is missing (R3).
  - For Figure 2, results under a non-normal distribution behavior policy are needed (R3 Q2).
  - The core idea of reward dropout is to relax the reward distribution for easier Pareto improvement, but quantile dropout sharpens the distribution – why does sharpening improve performance? (R3 Q3).

- **Presentation and completeness** (R1, R2):
  - A minimal version of Appendix D.2 (setup) should be in the main text; the paper is hard to follow without it (R1).
  - The setup in Appendix D.2 does not allow modifying the first part of β(τ), which breaks the bi-objective RL spirit (R1).
  - Framing CLM as RL is not novel: previously done implicitly (e.g., https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09492) (R1).
  - Overall presentation is poor (R2 rating 1, R1 rating 2); undefined concepts and unclear derivations.

- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R3) found the work important and the theory thorough, while the other two (R1, R2) found technical writing poor, experiments unconvincing, and the contribution limited. R3 raised their score after rebuttal but the above weaknesses were not fully resolved.
