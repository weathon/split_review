## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes using variational Bayesian last layer (VBLL) neural networks as surrogate models for Bayesian optimization (BO). It connects VBLL training to exact GP conditioning and develops an efficient online training algorithm with recursive updates. Empirical results on single- and multi-objective benchmarks show VBLL outperforms GPs and other BNNs on tasks with complex input correlations while matching well-tuned GPs on standard problems. The method is implemented in BoTorch.

### Strengths

- The method is well-explained and theoretically justified. The connection between optimizing the variational posterior and recursive Bayesian linear regression is clearly laid out.
- The framework offers practical flexibility through feature re-use and sparse full-model retraining, allowing practitioners to balance performance and computational cost.
- The empirical evaluation covers a diverse set of test objectives, including high-dimensional, non-stationary, and real-world problems (Oil Sorbent, Pest Control) where other methods often struggle due to numerical instability.
- VBLL appears robust to hyperparameter choices and can be used as a drop-in surrogate, unlike GPs which require careful kernel selection.
- The combination of Bayesian last-layer training with parametric Bayesian linear regression is elegant, and the use of natural parameterization (key to computational efficiency) is noted.
- Experimental studies are extensive and well-discussed, with strong results on complex input correlations.
- The paper is well-written, with a clear problem statement and extensive connections to related work. The focus on practical aspects (e.g., continual learning, BoTorch implementation) is appreciated.
- Adapting VBLL to BO introduces non-trivial challenges (recursive conditioning, continual learning, multi-objective stability) that are addressed.
- The paper highlights a valuable class of BNN models for BO and introduces efficient online updates.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing runtime/computational cost analysis** (R1, R4): No wall-clock time or computational budget is reported for surrogate fitting and optimization. This is needed to understand the practical advantage over other BNN surrogates (e.g., deep ensembles, LLLA) and to clarify the difference between VBLL and VBLL CL (continual learning should reduce fitting time but this is not shown). The runtime advantage is a primary claimed motivation but is not empirically validated.
- **Missing baselines** (R1, R4): The paper does not compare against DNGO (exact marginal likelihood with neural network features) or deep ensembles. Without these, the practical merit over existing work is unclear. LLLA performs similarly to VBLL in many settings; the claimed advantages (e.g., reduced noise sensitivity) are not supported by experiments or references.
- **LLLA vs. VBLL comparison** (R1, R4): LLLA outperforms VBLL on several non-stationary benchmarks. The paper suggests VBLL may be less flexible than last-layer Laplace approximations, but this is only briefly discussed at the end rather than in Related Work. A clearer elaboration on the differences is needed.
- **Architecture choice not discussed** (R2): There is no guidance on how to select the network architecture for all but the last layer. The complexity of kernel selection for GPs has essentially been shifted to architecture selection for the neural network, which is not addressed in sufficient detail.
- **DKL discussion insufficient** (R3): Deep kernel learning is a natural alternative, but the paper does not explain why DKL might be less ideal (e.g., computational complexity in high-dimensional settings) in the earlier motivation sections.
- **Lack of insight on underperformance** (R3): While dedicated experiments are in the supplement, the main text lacks high-level insights on cases where VBLL is expected to underperform or its sensitivity to hyperparameters.
- **Early stopping claims unsupported** (R4): Section 3.2 claims that early stopping based on training loss allows training "only as long as necessary" and could benefit other BO surrogate models. No experimental results are provided to demonstrate the effect of early stopping on predictive error or BO performance, making this a major omission.
- **Unsuitable GP lengthscale priors** (R4): The chosen lengthscale range [0.005, 4] for high-dimensional GP baselines appears inappropriate. Recent work ("Vanilla Bayesian Optimization Performs Great in High Dimensions", ICML 2024) suggests length scales around sqrt(D) are more effective. Using such priors could dramatically improve GP performance and make the comparison more informative.
- **Claims lack backing** (R4): Several methodological claims (e.g., about Laplace sensitivity to noise, advantages of early stopping) are presented without supporting proofs, experiments, or references. The authors are advised to either provide evidence or temper the claims.
