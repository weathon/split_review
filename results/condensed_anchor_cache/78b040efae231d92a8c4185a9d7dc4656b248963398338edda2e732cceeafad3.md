## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using LLMs to extract supply-demand dependency graphs from company reports and combine them with technical indicators, news sentiment, and volatility features for stock trend prediction. However, all reviewers find major flaws in writing clarity, experimental rigor, and contribution. One reviewer (R3) is slightly more positive, noting some experimental metrics as a strength, but still rates the paper low (3 vs. 1). There is inconsistency in reported accuracy: one reviewer (R2) notes 95% accuracy on options data, while others mention improvements from 0.52 to 0.78 (R1) or 0.72 to 0.73 (R4). No reviewer considers the paper reproducible or sound.

### Strengths
- **Interesting theme and high-level direction**: Using LLMs for investing, specifically combining fundamentally-driven analysis via dependency graphs with technical analysis (R1). The idea of linking LLM outputs with quantitative methods is noted as potentially valuable (R1).
- **Some experimental metrics reported**: The paper includes accuracy numbers (R3), though these conflict across reviewers and lack supporting details.
- **Intriguing high accuracy reported by one reviewer**: 95% on options volume dataset (R2), but this reviewer doubts correctness and notes significant mismatch with typical stock noise.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and structure**: The paper lacks clear sections (related work, method, experiments) (R1, R2, R3, R4). It jumps between concepts with sparse introductions and contains unorthodox phrasing like “We were pleased with the accuracy” (R4). Format errors and poorly made figures are noted (R2). R1, R3, R4 stress the difficulty of parsing the actual contribution.
- **Insufficient literature review**: No dedicated “Related Work” section; only a very limited number of references (R2, R3, R4). R3 cites a missing reference (arXiv:2303.17564). R4 suggests papers on fundamental/technical analysis reviews and neural network techniques.
- **Ambiguous method and missing details**: It is unclear what exact approach is proposed (R4). The use of ChatGPT/LMMs is revealed only at the end (R4). No prompts or procedures for LLM extraction of dependency graphs are described (R3, R1). The link between the established most-effective time period combination and the motivation to use LLM is not explained (R4).
- **Missing experimental details and reproducibility**: No dataset described, no preprocessing steps, no training/validation/test splits, no start/end dates (R2, R4). News and price data alignment is unspecified – possible data leakage (R2). No performance measure defined (R1). The accuracy computation method is not given (R4). R1 states the paper is not reproducible even for an expert.
- **No baselines or comparisons**: Benchmarks against existing methods are absent (R1, R4). R1 notes an impressive apparent improvement (0.52→0.78) but cannot interpret it without benchmarks. R4 mentions the marginal improvement (0.72→0.73) raises questions about necessity of LLM.
- **Inconsistent reported accuracy and questionable results**: R2 reports 95% accuracy but doubts correctness due to lack of ML background. R1 reports 0.52→0.78, R4 reports 0.72→0.73. The results appear misaligned with stated objectives (R4). Section 4 on causal relationships lacks clarity (R4).
- **Poor figures**: Figure 1 lacks y-axis label and caption (R1, R4). Figures 2 and 3 do not offer useful insights (R4).
- **Disconnected sections**: Section 5 on VIX does not connect with the primary methodology or objectives (R4). No clear evidence of LLM advantage (R4).
- **Marginal technical contribution**: The approach is a naive incorporation of LLM (R2). The contribution remains unclear and questionable (R4). Alignment between experiments and motivation is not evident (R4).
