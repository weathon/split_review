## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Decomposed Representation Learning (DRL) for one-class tabular anomaly detection. DRL maps data into a latent space where each normal sample’s representation is enforced to be a weighted linear combination of randomly generated, training‑free orthogonal basis vectors. A separation loss (supported by Proposition 1) maximizes the variance of the inlier weights to increase expected distance from outliers, while a reconstruction loss prevents task‑irrelevant features. Experiments on 40 ADBench datasets against 15 competitors show state‑of‑the‑art performance, with ablation studies and t‑SNE visualizations. Scores were 6, 5, 6, 6; one reviewer (score 5) was notably more critical, and another (score 6 after rebuttal) raised fundamental theoretical and experimental concerns.

### Strengths
- Novel core idea: learning decomposed representations as linear combinations of orthogonal basis vectors, addressing entanglement issues in reconstruction‑based methods and offering explainability potential. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Theoretical support for the separation loss via Proposition 1, providing a mathematical motivation for the design. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Extensive evaluation across 40 tabular datasets and 15 baselines (including recent methods NPT‑AD and MCM), consistently achieving state‑of‑the‑art results. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Well‑motivated and clearly presented, with a logical flow and proper technical illustration. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- Comprehensive ablation studies and t‑SNE visualizations that give insight into the behavior of DRL’s components. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- Use of a statistical significance test to support the main experimental comparison. (Reviewer 4)
- Appendix includes pseudo‑code, runtime analysis, and additional parameter sensitivity studies. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical foundations unsatisfactorily justified**  
  * The core assumption that normal data can be modeled as a linear combination of fixed orthogonal basis vectors is not theoretically or empirically supported; it may fail for complex, high‑dimensional, or non‑linearly associated features. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
  * Proposition 1 only guarantees an increase in the *expected* distance between an inlier and an outlier, not that outliers will be far from the inlier centroid – a scenario where outliers lie at the centroid while inliers are sparse is not ruled out. (Reviewer 4)  
  * The link between the separation loss and the final decomposition‑based anomaly score is not explained: it is unclear how increased latent distance improves the scoring function. (Reviewer 4)  
  * The rationale for choosing orthogonal basis vectors over other relational properties (e.g., non‑orthogonal but linearly independent) is not provided. (Reviewer 2)  

- **Core motivation questioned**  
  One reviewer argued that since reconstruction‑based methods already bias representations toward normal samples, the advantage of additionally decomposing them is unclear; it is not shown why decomposed representations would be more separable than standard latent codes. (Reviewer 2)  

- **Missing baselines and comparisons**  
  * No performance comparison with KNN, a widely effective tabular anomaly detection method. (Reviewer 1)  
  * Runtime comparisons with other baselines are only minimal (Appendix A.5); computational cost on large‑scale, high‑dimensional data is a concern, especially due to the per‑sample weight learner. (Reviewer 3)  

- **Insufficient experimental verification of theoretical claims**  
  * Figure 1 (showing separation) tests only 2 datasets and 1 competitor, not the full 40 datasets and 15 baselines. (Reviewer 4)  
  * Ablation studies are conducted on only 7 out of 40 datasets. (Reviewer 4)  
  * The ablation variant that applies decomposition loss directly to observations (variant B) performed poorly, but results are reported only as average PR‑AUC without full dataset‑wise comparison or identification of the other variants used. (Reviewer 4)  

- **Missing ablation experiments**  
  * No evaluation of using cosine distance for the decomposition loss. (Reviewers 1, 2)  
  * No evaluation of using only the alignment loss as the anomaly score. (Reviewer 2)  
  * No exploration of L1 distance vs. cosine distance. (Reviewer 1)  

- **Reproducibility**  
  Source code was not released, limiting validation. (Reviewers 3, 4)  

- **Loose bound in separation loss derivation**  
  The lower bound is derived by removing all squared expectation terms, leading to a potentially loose bound; alternate derivations or justification are needed. (Reviewer 1)  

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**  
  * The optimal number of basis vectors (and its relationship to input feature dimensionality) is not principled. (Reviewers 1, 2)  
  * Separation loss is computed between samples and may be sensitive to batch size; with highly similar normal samples it may face convergence challenges or overfit to a subset. (Reviewer 2)  
  * No guidelines for tuning loss‑term weights. (Reviewer 1)  

- **Generalizability and limitations**  
  * No discussion of whether DRL can be applied to other data types (e.g., image, time series) or what properties of tabular data make it especially suitable. (Reviewer 2)  
  * The paper lacks a dedicated limitations and future work section. (Reviewers 1, 2)  

- **Minor statistical remark (from Reviewer 4, did not affect score)**  
  The use of p‑values and the Wilcoxon signed‑rank test in a multiple‑comparison setting may be inappropriate; a multiple‑comparison correction (e.g., Conover‑Iman) would be more rigorous.
