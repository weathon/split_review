## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper evaluates existing instruction-tuned table LLMs (e.g., TableLLM, TableBenchLLM) and finds they suffer from over-specialization: significant declines in out-of-domain table understanding, instruction-following, and general capabilities (up to 20% drop on AI2ARC) compared to their base models. The authors then systematically study hyperparameter effects (learning rate, epochs, training sample size) and find that a learning rate of 1e-6, two epochs, and very few training instances (2,600 total samples from 12 tasks, 0.1% of typical datasets) can preserve general capabilities while maintaining table performance. Based on these findings, they train **TAMA** from LLaMA 3.1-8B-Instruct, which matches or surpasses GPT-3.5 and sometimes GPT-4 on table tasks (e.g., 77.4% on FEVEROUS, 3% higher than TableLLM) and achieves 66.9% on MMLU, close to the base model’s score. All three reviewers agree on the paper’s core findings and the utility of TAMA, but they differ on the novelty and depth of the hyperparameter analysis, with reviewers 2 and 3 raising concerns about contribution and generalizability.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive Analysis:** The paper provides a thorough evaluation of existing table LLMs and the often-overlooked impact of hyperparameter tuning (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Empirical Findings on Learning Rate:** The observed effects of learning rate align with practical experience in hyperparameter tuning for LLMs on tables, offering concrete guidance (Reviewer 1, confident 4).
- **Identification of Over-Specialization:** The work reveals a critical trade-off in current table LLMs, where excessive fine-tuning for table tasks compromises generalization, encouraging the community to reconsider performance claims (Reviewer 2, confident 4).
- **Extensive Experiments:** The study spans multiple tasks (FeTaQA, HiTab, TabFact, WikiSQL, TATQA, FEVEROUS, etc.) to evaluate hyperparameter impacts on both table-specific and general capabilities (Reviewer 2).
- **Effective Hyperparameter Settings:** Through systematic exploration, the authors identify an optimal configuration (1e-6 learning rate, 2 epochs) that supports strong table performance while preserving general abilities, establishing a valuable baseline (Reviewer 2).
- **TAMA’s Performance:** The proposed TAMA achieves impressive performance on representative tabular tasks, matching/exceeding GPT-3.5 and sometimes GPT-4, and is a good contribution to the open-source community for follow-up work (Reviewer 1, confident 4; Reviewer 3, confident 4 adds that TAMA is useful, though he questions the paper’s overall contribution).
- **Illuminates Limitations of Existing Table LLMs:** The paper clearly highlights the weaknesses of prior table instruction-tuned models (Reviewer 3, confident 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited Model Scope:** Experiments are only conducted on Llama models; validation on other LLMs (e.g., Qwen, Phi, Mistral) is needed to confirm generalizability of findings (Reviewer 1, confident 4; Reviewer 2, confident 4 adds that extending to Mistral, Qwen would add long-term value).
- **Missing LoRA/QLoRA Analysis:** The reviewer wonders whether the findings hold when using parameter-efficient methods like LoRA or QLoRA (Reviewer 1, question → missing experiment).
- **Limited Reproducibility:** Training/evaluation scripts, data, and TAMA’s model parameters were not open-sourced during review, restricting verification of the training process and performance claims (Reviewer 2, confident 4).
- **Title-Content Discrepancy on Table Modality:** The title suggests a focus on unique table aspects, but the study does not examine whether the observed trade-offs are specific to table data (e.g., heterogeneity, permutation invariance, numeric density) (Reviewer 2, confident 4).
- **Limited Novelty Relative to Zhou et al. (2024):** The approach of using a small set of tuning data to enhance capabilities has already been proposed by Zhou et al. (2024), and this work offers limited additional insights. The paper does not clarify whether improvements stem from pre-trained knowledge or fine-tuning, does not explore whether stronger base models require less tuning data, and does not compare pre-training corpora (e.g., LLaMA 1 vs. LLaMA 2 vs. LLaMA 3.1) to disentangle instruction-tuning gains (Reviewer 2, confident 4).
- **Lack of Validation Across Multiple Base Models and Hyperparameter Guidance:** The hyperparameter baseline is only for LLaMA 3.1-8B; a practical framework with reliable settings for mainstream models (e.g., Mistral, Qwen) would significantly increase the paper’s impact (Reviewer 2, confident 4).
- **Uncertainty about Small-Sample Conclusion for General Capabilities:** A reviewer questions whether the conclusion that small sample sizes suffice holds when tasks require reasoning, long-text answers, or retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), suggesting that in such scenarios, larger-scale fine-tuning might still be needed (Reviewer 2, question → overlaps with criticism on generalizability).
- **Questionable Contribution of the Paper Itself (minority view):** While TAMA is a useful model, the paper’s main takeaway is merely good hyperparameter choice plus multi-task training, which is standard. Section 3 reads as an experimental report without providing fresh insights such as training dynamics, capability analysis, or deeper understanding—thus limiting the paper’s contribution (Reviewer 3, confident 4, more negative than other reviewers).
