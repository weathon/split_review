## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies RL with preference-based feedback (RLHF) in linear MDPs and nonlinear function approximation. It proposes two algorithms: PR-LSVI (Algorithm 1), which combines MLE and randomized exploration, and a Thompson-sampling-based algorithm (Algorithm 2). The paper derives regret bounds and query complexity bounds, characterizing a near-optimal tradeoff between these quantities controlled by a threshold ε (or β). Reviewers agree the problem is interesting and the theory is solid, but diverge on novelty, practical relevance, and presentation.

### Strengths

- **Novel theoretical contributions:** This is the first RL algorithm for linear MDPs with preference-based feedback that simultaneously achieves sublinear worst-case regret and computational efficiency (Reviewer 1, Rating 8). Prior works achieved sublinear regret but were computationally infeasible even in tabular MDPs.
- **Conceptually simple and computationally tractable algorithms:** PR-LSVI (Alg 1) and Algorithm 2 are described as simple in design (Reviewer 3, Rating 6). The use of randomization enables computational tractability in the preference-feedback setting (Reviewer 4, Rating 6).
- **Practical insight:** The algorithm proposes drawing one trajectory from the latest policy and another from an older policy (rather than two from the same policy), which is a departure from conventional practice and enhances practicality (Reviewer 1).
- **Quantitative tradeoff:** Regret and query complexity bounds are derived, and the tradeoff between them is quantitatively characterized in terms of parameter β; this provides insight for tuning ε to achieve desired performance (Reviewer 4).
- **Extension to nonlinear function approximation:** A Thompson-sampling-based algorithm is introduced with Bayesian regret and query complexity bounds, extending results beyond linear MDPs (Reviewer 4).
- **Well-written and comprehensible:** Despite its theoretical nature, the paper is exceptionally readable (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Incremental contribution / lack of novelty (Reviewer 2, Rating 5):** The proposed algorithm (PR-LSVI) is considered fairly standard (RLSVI) and almost identical to previous work; proof techniques are routine. Reviewers 1 and 4 view the work as novel, while Reviewer 2 finds the novelty limited. Reviewer 3 questions the specific technical novelty beyond Zanette et al. (2019) aside from incorporating preferential noise.
- **Missing comparisons with prior work:** No concrete comparison with the large body of theoretical work on linear MDPs using model-free algorithms (Reviewer 2). Questions remain about the connection to Wang et al. (2023) (Reviewer 3) and to Sekhari et al. (2023a) on contextual bandits and imitation learning (Reviewer 1). The regret dependence on \(d\) (dimension) compared to Zanette et al. (2020) is not commented on (Reviewer 4).
- **No experimental or simulation results:** All reviewers note the absence of numerical evidence. Reviewer 1 emphasizes the need to justify the newly proposed algorithm with simulations (e.g., demonstrating superiority over existing RLHF benchmarks). Reviewer 2 states the work is far from practical RLHF use cases (e.g., LLM fine-tuning with Algorithm 2). Reviewer 3 and 4 also request empirical illustrations, especially to observe the regret–query tradeoff and assess computational feasibility.
- **Large volume / presentation issues:** 53 pages is excessively large for a conference; shortening would improve clarity and impact (Reviewer 3). The two algorithms are disjoint (frequentist vs. Bayesian objectives) and not connected (Reviewer 3). Some notations are undefined (e.g., \(\hat{\theta}_{P,t,h}\)), hindering readability (Reviewer 4). Minor typo: Algorithm 1 line 15 "Quey" should be "Query" (Reviewer 3).
- **Suboptimal parameter dependence:** The regret bound has suboptimal dependence on parameters other than \(T\), especially \(H\), making the exploration dimension larger than necessary (Reviewer 3).
- **Questionable relevance of cumulative regret for RLHF:** It is unclear why cumulative regret is the right metric in the context of RLHF for LLM alignment (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational intensity of Algorithm 2:** Reviewer 4 notes Algorithm 2 appears computationally intensive; Reviewer 2 questions its feasibility for practical settings like LLM fine-tuning.
- **Minor writing/notation issues:** Clarification needed on \(\omega\) after equation (1) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 recommends querying existing lemmas from prior works rather than proving everything from scratch.
