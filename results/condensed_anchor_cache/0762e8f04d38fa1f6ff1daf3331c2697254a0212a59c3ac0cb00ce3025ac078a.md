## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MoDGS, a pipeline for novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes from casually captured monocular videos. It leverages single-view depth estimation to guide the learning of a deformation field, proposes a 3D-aware initialization for Gaussians in canonical space, and employs an ordinal depth loss to improve depth consistency across frames. Experiments on DyNeRF, Nvidia, DAVIS, and a self-collected dataset demonstrate that MoDGS outperforms state-of-the-art methods (including dynamic NeRF and 3DGS variants) in rendering quality, particularly when the input camera is static or moves slowly.

### Strengths

1. **Novel depth supervision in dynamic Gaussian Splatting**: Introducing single-view depth priors into dynamic 3DGS for casually captured monocular videos is a distinct and intuitive contribution. The approach is conceptually straightforward yet impactful, filling a key gap where prior methods require rapid camera motion (Reviewers 1, 2).

2. **3D-aware initialization**: This component specifically addresses a common issue in monocular reconstruction by initializing Gaussians in canonical space using per-pixel 3D flow (derived from estimated depth and optical flow), adding consistency instead of relying on random initialization (Reviewers 2, 4).

3. **Ordinal depth loss**: A differentiable order-based loss that maintains depth order across frames rather than relying on absolute values, tackling scale ambiguity and promoting depth coherence in dynamic scenes. The motivation and distinction from other depth loss formulations are clearly described (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).

4. **Comprehensive experiments**: Thorough evaluations across multiple datasets (Nvidia, DyNeRF, DAVIS, self-collected casual videos) with systematic ablations validating the importance of both the 3D-aware initialization and the ordinal depth loss. Results show significant superiority over multi-view camera methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).

5. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written, with logical flow, informative figures/tables, and easy-to-follow technical explanations (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited innovation according to some reviewers**: While Reviewers 1 and 2 view the approach as novel, Reviewers 3 and 4 consider the contributions incremental. The main innovations – a deformable 3DGS initialization that converts 2D optical flow to 3D flow using a depth map, and an ordinal depth loss that replaces Pearson correlation with order correlation – are seen as optimizing existing elements (Deformable 3DGS, 4DGS) rather than proposing a new structural approach (Reviewers 3, 4).

2. **Heavy reliance on single-view depth estimators**: MoDGS depends on pre-trained depth models like GeoWizard, inheriting their limitations in complex scenes (specular surfaces, low-light, occlusions). There is insufficient analysis of how the choice of depth model affects performance, and no formalized knowledge distillation to adaptively refine the model during training. This raises concerns about generalizability and the independence of results (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).

3. **Ordinal depth loss assumptions**: The loss assumes consistent depth order among frames, which may fail in scenes with complex occlusions, reflections, or rapid/erratic movement where objects appear/disappear frequently. The paper could discuss these limitations and potential adaptations (Reviewer 2). Additionally, a comparison with other depth loss formulations (e.g., perceptual depth consistency, self-supervised monocular depth consistency techniques) is missing (Reviewer 2).

4. **Unfair experimental comparisons**: Most quantitative comparisons are against methods designed for multi-view camera input (requiring rapid motion). The paper lacks quantitative and qualitative comparisons with methods under the same "casually captured monocular video" setting. Although RoDynRF (which also uses single-view depth supervision) is mentioned as a baseline, it is only compared in Fig. 6 (Reviewer 3).

5. **Generalization to highly dynamic scenes not thoroughly tested**: The method may struggle with higher motion complexity, cluttered backgrounds, or multiple moving objects. Further evaluation on broader datasets and analysis of performance stability when depth estimator provides inconsistent depth (e.g., due to environmental variations) is needed (Reviewers 2, 4).

6. **Title suggestion**: Consider making the core contribution ("Depth supervised in dynamic GS") more explicit in the title rather than focusing on the usage scenario (Reviewer 1).
