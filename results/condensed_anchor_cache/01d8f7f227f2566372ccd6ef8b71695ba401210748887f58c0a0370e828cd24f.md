## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ContextGNN, a recommendation architecture that classifies items into "familiar" (within a user’s local subgraph) and "exploratory" (distant). For familiar items, it uses pair-wise representations via a GNN on a sampled 2-3 hop user subgraph; for exploratory items, it employs two-tower (embedding-based) representations because GNNs do not learn useful item-side information (item interaction matrix is dense, shallow embeddings are effective, and GNNs scale poorly with large item catalogs). The two learned embedding spaces are fused via an MLP to produce a final ranking. The method is evaluated on the RelBench platform across multiple tasks involving multi‑behavioral and temporal interactions, outperforming baselines.

One reviewer (score 8, confidence 4) found the approach well‑placed in the literature, easy to follow, and supported by a locality score study and discussion of extensions. The other four reviewers (scores 5, 5, 5, 6) rated the paper lower, citing concerns about novelty, experimental breadth, reproducibility, and scalability. All reviewers agreed the paper is clear and the problem is practically important.

### Strengths

+ The problem (overcoming pair‑agnostic two‑tower limitations) is important in industry (R2).
+ The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4, R5).
+ The motivation—using pair‑wise representations only for the user’s local subgraph while keeping two‑tower for exploratory items—is solid and sound (R1, R3).
+ The locality score study conceptually and empirically supports the rationale behind the approach (R1).
+ The discussion of possible extensions in different directions is appreciated (R1).
+ Experimental setting (on RelBench) is adequately extensive for the page limit and tests multiple tasks, which is rare in previous work (R1, R4).
+ Results demonstrate effectiveness of the proposed method (R2, R5).
+ The use of both pairwise and two‑tower representations is interesting (R5).
+ The minority reviewer (score 8, confidence 4) expressed higher confidence and saw strong contributions, especially the soundness of the approach and the adequacy of the experiments.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing recent baselines**: The experimental comparison lacks many advanced GNN‑based recommendation methods, including LightGCN, SGL, UltraGCN, SimGCL, LightGCL, and attention‑based graph aggregation techniques. Contrastive learning methods, which improve robustness, are notably absent. The list of baselines is not sufficiently diversified or updated to the latest advances (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Unclear novelty and technical contribution**: The core idea of leveraging a local interaction graph for representation learning is mature and well‑studied. The paper does not clearly articulate what is novel compared to existing methods, and the related work does not demonstrate this novelty. No theoretical analysis is provided, and the technical challenges being addressed are not well motivated (R2, R3, R4). One reviewer calls the design “quite basic” and lacking innovation (R4).
- **Limited experimental evaluation**: Only MAP is reported; there is no parameter sensitivity analysis, no ablation study, and no analysis of performance when user behavior data is insufficient in type or quantity. The claim that the method is general for recommendation is not supported because experiments are limited to datasets with rich multi‑behavioral and temporal interactions (R3). Datasets are not clearly described, and scalability to large‑scale data is not addressed (R2, R4).
- **Reproducibility and code**: The paper does not release code or datasets, and there are errors in the equations. This undermines reproducibility (R3, R4, R5).
- **Complexity and efficiency**: Time complexity analysis, runtime efficiency comparisons, and scalability experiments are absent, despite the paper mentioning complexity issues of pair‑wise methods (R4). A reviewer explicitly asks for a time complexity analysis and runtime efficiency experiments against baselines (R4).
- **Oversmoothing and long‑range learning**: How can the GNN learn long‑range (ground‑truth) items if the model cannot explore beyond ~3 hops on average? The paper does not address this given the known oversmoothing issues in GNNs (R1, overlapping with weakness about theoretical justification).
- **Insufficient motivation of temporal factor**: The paper considers temporal factors in modeling but does not explain the technical contributions or challenges of incorporating them (R2).
- **GNN backbone design not illustrated for multi‑behavior and temporal graphs**: The paper mentions both multi‑behavior and temporal graphs but does not explain how the GNN backbone is designed to accommodate such graphs (R5).
- **Related work can be enhanced**: The related work section does not sufficiently discuss advanced two‑tower architectures and does not demonstrate novelty (R2).
