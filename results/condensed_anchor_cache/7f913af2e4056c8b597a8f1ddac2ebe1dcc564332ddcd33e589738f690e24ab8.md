## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PowerNet, a novel architecture that parameterizes weight matrices as truncated matrix power series (Laurent polynomials) and proposes the concept of “quasi-equivariance.” The idea is considered interesting and the architecture is novel with very few parameters (R2, R3, R4), but the paper suffers from significant clarity and theoretical issues. There is strong disagreement: two reviewers gave score 3 (R2 with high confidence, R3 with low confidence) and two gave score 1 (R1 low confidence, R4 high confidence). The positive reviewers see potential but note major gaps; the negative reviewers find the experimental evidence insufficient and the contribution unconvincing.

### Strengths
- The idea of quasi-equivariance and the PowerNet architecture are novel and interesting (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- The use of Laurent polynomials and Laurent convolution in the context of equivariant models is new (R4).  
- The architecture requires only a very small number of parameters (R3).  
- The paper includes visualizations (R1) and mentions a software package (R1).  
- The mathematical formulas are clear and accurate (R4).  
- The authors employ tools from Lie Theory and the Whittaker–Shannon interpolation to heuristically discuss theoretical aspects (R4).  
- Potential for efficient learning and flexible structure (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clarity**: The paper is difficult to read; core ideas are obscured by convoluted language (R1, R2). The connection between weight sharing and quasi-equivariance is not well explained (R2). Section 3.3 appears unfinished (R4).  
- **No formal definition of quasi‑equivariance**: The property is never precisely defined; Figure 1 does not clarify it (R2, R4).  
- **Missing mathematical/theoretical analysis**:  
  - No rigorous analysis, proofs, or theorems linking Laurent polynomials and quasi‑equivariance (R2, R3, R4).  
  - The paper does not explain how truncation of the power series affects the equivariance properties (R2).  
  - There is no discussion on convergence of the power series or conditions under which it would lead to strict equivariance (R2).  
  - The implementation of the matrix exponential based on Lie theory and its connection to quasi‑equivariance are unclear (R2).  
  - No intuitive explanation of how PowerNet embodies quasi‑equivariance (R4).  
- **Limited experimental validation**:  
  - Experiments are restricted to MNIST and CIFAR‑10 classification (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
  - The models tested are only very shallow 4‑layer networks (R1); no evaluation on deeper architectures like VGG or ResNet (R1).  
  - Quality of the trained models is not high (R3).  
  - Missing comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art equivariant architectures (e.g., group convolution, Steerable CNNs) (R1, R2, R3).  
  - No comparison of computational complexity with other equivariant methods (R2).  
  - No experiments on more diverse/standard equivariant tasks (e.g., N‑body dataset, MD17 for molecular modeling, image segmentation, object detection) (R2, R3, R4).  
  - Sparse results with few baselines (R1, R3).  
- **Missing code/reproducibility**: The PowerNet library is mentioned but no code or repository link is provided (R3).  
- **Specific unresolved questions that overlap with criticisms**:  
  - Was this approach attempted in traditional neural networks? (R1)  
  - What is the expressive power of the model given its small number of parameters? (R3)  
  - Would the fully‑connected nature of PowerNet be impractical for high‑resolution images? (R3)  
  - Can base matrices be chosen from structured matrices with fast matrix‑vector products? (R3)  
  - How does the choice of base matrix affect equivariance properties? Are there guidelines for selecting it? (R2)  
  - How is the truncation threshold determined? Does it affect the degree of quasi‑equivariance? (R2)  
  - Under what conditions does the series converge, and does convergence imply hard equivariance? (R2)  
  - Can the authors provide a concrete example of constructing a base matrix from Lie theory for a specific transformation (e.g., rotation)? (R2)  
- **Minor typos**: “exponetiated” → “exponentiated”; “Shannon‑Whitakker” → “Whittaker–Shannon” (R4).
