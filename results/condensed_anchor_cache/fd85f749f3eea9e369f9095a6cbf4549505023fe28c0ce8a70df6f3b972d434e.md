## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies lower-level constrained bilevel optimization (LCBO) and proposes a single-loop single-timescale algorithm DMLCBO that uses Gaussian smoothing to approximate hypergradients, double momentum, and adaptive step sizes. Non-asymptotic convergence analysis is provided. Experiments on data hyper-cleaning and training data poisoning attacks demonstrate effectiveness. Reviewers find the motivation well-founded, the algorithm design novel, and the experimental results promising, but raise critical issues regarding restrictive assumptions, a high iteration complexity that is partially obscured, insufficient justification of differentiability, missing comparisons, and experimental limitations. All four reviewers rate the paper 5, indicating borderline rejection, with concerns outweighing contributions.

### Strengths
- The work is well motivated: solving LCBO efficiently is an important problem. (R1)
- The algorithm design is novel, employing Gaussian smoothing to approximate hypergradients, and provides non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. (R1, R4)
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. (R1)
- DMLCBO is a single-loop single-timescale method, avoiding double-loop updates, and is thus more efficient in principle than prior methods. (R2, R4)
- Extensive experiments (data hyper-cleaning and training data poisoning attacks) and ablation studies are conducted, showing competitive performance. (R1, R3)
- The application of Gaussian smoothing offers new insights for solving LCBO. (R4)

### Weaknesses
1. The reported iteration complexity is significantly worse than claimed and very high in practice. From Remark 2, the condition $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d_2}/(\delta K^{1/4}))\le \epsilon$ yields $K = \tilde{O}(d_2^2/(\delta^4\epsilon^4))$. With $\delta = O(\epsilon d_2^{-3/2})$ from (11), the complexity becomes $\tilde{O}(d_2^8/\epsilon^8)$. This polynomial dependence on $d_2$ and $1/\epsilon$ is a severe limitation and should be discussed explicitly, not obscured by retaining $\delta$ in the bound. The authors should also comment on tightness and potential improvements. (R1 W1, R2 W2)
2. Assumption 3 (differentiability of the projection of the optimality condition) is restrictive and not adequately justified. The paper does not provide representative problem classes satisfying it, and counterexamples exist: for $g(x,y)=(y-x)^2/2$ with $\mathcal{Y}=[-1,1]$, the projection $P_{\mathcal{Y}}(z^*)$ is not continuously differentiable at $x=1$ or $x=-1$ (R1 Q1). More generally, the differentiability of $F$ depends on $y^*(x)$ being differentiable, which is not guaranteed under constraints. A concrete LCBO example $\min_{x,y} -xy$ s.t. $y\in\arg\min_{z\in[-1,1]}xz$ yields $F(x)=|x|$, non-differentiable at $x=0$. The paper does not adequately address these issues, undermining the theoretical results including Lemma 5 and (10). (R1 Q1, R2 W1, R4 W1)
3. Assumption 4 (bounded norms of iterates and stochastic gradient estimates) appears to be made for analytical convenience rather than being a natural consequence of the algorithm or problem. Boundedness might be enforced via clipping, but the paper does not state this nor discuss its effect on convergence. A relaxation or more thorough justification is needed. (R1 Q3, R4 W2)
4. The algorithm is not truly single-loop: it contains an inner loop to approximate the matrix inverse via $Q$ steps. This overhead should be acknowledged and its impact on practical efficiency discussed. (R1 W3)
5. Missing comparisons with closely related work, in particular the penalty-based bilevel gradient descent method of Shen and Chen (ICML 2023). (R1 W2)
6. The numerical experiments may not satisfy the strong convexity assumption (Assumption 2(3)) for the lower-level problem. Moreover, the chosen parameters $Q$ and $\eta$ do not align with the theoretical requirements in Theorem 1. (R2 W3)
7. The dependence of the convergence rate on the dimension $d_2$ but not $d_1$ is unexplained. Why is only the lower-level dimension relevant? (R4 Q1)
8. The paper does not clarify whether the lower-level constraints are active at the solutions returned in the experiments. (R4 Q2)
9. Experimental presentation is limited: (a) only early-time results are shown for the proposed method; later-time results would be more informative. (R3 W2) (b) In Table 3, DMLCBO exhibits higher variance on MNIST compared to baselines; the reason and experimental details require explanation. (R3 W3)
10. The double-momentum technique in DMLCBO only achieves $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4})$ convergence, while prior double-momentum methods for unconstrained bilevel optimization (e.g., SUSTAIN, MRBO) achieve $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$. The authors should discuss why DMLCBO does not attain the improved rate and highlight the technical differences. (R3 W1)
11. The paper contains several typographical errors and unclear notations: e.g., minus sign omitted in (4), undefined $F_{\delta}(x)$ in Remark 1, inequality $\|A\|\le 1$ should be $<1$, unused term $u^Q$ in (9), swapped positions of $v_1$ and $w_1$ in Algorithm 1, missing "$\nabla_y$", incorrect $\nabla F_{\delta}$ vs $\nabla F$, missing absolute value in Lemma 5, incorrect sum in max, etc. These should be corrected. (R1 minor comments)
