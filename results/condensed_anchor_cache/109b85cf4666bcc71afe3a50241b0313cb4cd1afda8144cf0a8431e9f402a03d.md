## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Constrained TRPO (C-TRPO), which incorporates safety constraints into the trust region divergence so that each update stays within the set of safe policies. Reviewers agree the idea of embedding safety into trust region geometry is novel and the theoretical analysis is sound. However, opinions diverge on empirical significance: some find results promising (R1, R5), others see limited improvement over baselines like CPO and TRPO-Lag (R2, R3, R4). Concerns center on inadequate baselines (missing IPO, P3O, Ni et al. 2024), lack of ablation isolating the modified divergence from the hysteresis recovery mechanism, reliance on accurate cost value estimates, and the risk of no solution when the policy approaches the constraint boundary. Scores range from 3 to 8, with the most positive reviewer (R5) citing technical solidity and preserved guarantees, while the most negative (R3) questions the core feasibility and impact.

### Strengths
- Investigates an important problem with interesting connections to prior trust region methods (R1).
- Original idea of modifying the Bregman divergence to embed safety, with a simple recovery scheme for infeasible policies (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- Sound theoretical analysis of convergence and update properties, with links to TRPO, NPG, and CPO (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- Well-structured and clearly written; figures (e.g., Figure 2) provide helpful intuition (R1, R2, R4).
- Competitive reward with reduced constraint violations (cost regret) shown in experiments (R1, R5).
- The approach preserves TRPO’s convergence and policy improvement guarantees (R5).

### Weaknesses
**Experimental comparison and baselines**
- Missing comparisons to state-of-the-art safe RL algorithms: IPO (Liu et al. 2020), Ni et al. (2024), P3O, and control-barrier-function-based methods (R1, R3, R5). Without these, the empirical contribution is unclear (R1, R3).
- Experimental results do not convincingly demonstrate superiority: CPO outperforms C-TRPO in several tasks (e.g., safetycar Button1, safetyracecarcircle1), and TRPO-Lag achieves smaller costs at the end of training with similar reward (R2, R3, R4). The improvement over CPO is limited (R2).
- No ablation study isolating the effect of the modified divergence from the hysteresis recovery mechanism. Adding the same hysteresis to CPO could reproduce the gains (R1, R3). Without this control, the source of improvement is confounded (R3).
- Table 1 presentation needs improvement: highlight best cost per row, add standard deviations; no return is bold in the CarButton1 row (R1).

**Theoretical and practical issues**
- The modified divergence \(D_\phi\) (Eq. 15) goes to infinity as the policy approaches the constraint boundary, potentially making Eq. (14) unsatisfiable and leading to no solution. The paper does not address how this is handled in practice (R3).
- The method relies on an accurately estimated cost value function \(V_c\), which must be learned from samples; estimation errors can undermine the safety guarantees. CPO suffers from the same problem, and C-TRPO does not mitigate it (R2).
- Clarify whether C-TRPO can achieve the same return as unconstrained TRPO; the claim “without sacrificing performance” is not supported (R4). Figure 3 shows C-TRPO is even worse than CPO in return (R4).

**Motivation and novelty**
- The motivation for integrating safety into divergence rather than using a Lagrangian barrier is not sufficiently explained. Why does a log-barrier in the Lagrangian (e.g., IPO) introduce more bias than modifying the trust region divergence? (R1, R4).
- Novelty is limited: Figure 1 does not clearly show why C-TRPO’s trust region is better than CPO’s, and the bounds appear similar (R4). The change compared to TRPO/CPO is incremental (R4).
- The discussion of related work is too short, especially for trust region methods given the paper’s focus (R4). The background section focuses on discrete spaces while experiments are continuous (R1).

**Clarity and presentation**
- Compare theoretical bounds to additional baselines besides CPO (e.g., IPO, Ni et al. 2024) (R1).
- Add a brief discussion of Bregman divergences in the appendix (R1).
- Missing description of the environments and constraints used in experiments (R1).
- Line 86–87: “C-TRPO is an approximation of C-TRPO itself” is confusing (R4). Line 142: typo “CMPD” → “CMDP” (R5). Equation (31): \(V_r^\pi\) should be \(V_{c_i}^\pi\) (R1). Equation (40): definition of \(L_\theta\) missing (R1). Line 167: \(D_k\) inconsistent with earlier Bregman divergence definition (R2).
- Proposition 3: when \(\beta = 0\), \(D_C = D_{KL}\) per Eq. (9), but the text says C-TRPO approaches CPO, not TRPO. Clarify (R3).
- Proposition 4: provide more explanation of why the upper bound of C-TRPO is smaller than that of CPO (R3).
- What initial set assumptions are needed for safety during training and convergence? (R5)

**Reproducibility**
- No code is provided, raising reproducibility concerns (R3).

**Minor**

- Fix figure referencing: Figure 1 is introduced too early without enough explanation (R4).
