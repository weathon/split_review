- Decision: Reject

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes using pre-trained Stable Diffusion to generate semantically controlled data augmentations (DA) for image super-resolution, enriching training datasets with content-level variations. It also introduces a diffusion-based data degradation (DD) method to simulate real-world corruptions by modifying image quality via prompts. On FFHQ and Manga109, DA improves PSNR by 0.8 dB and 0.28 dB respectively; DD is claimed to reduce artifacts in real-world SR. Two reviewers rate the paper 3 (weak reject), one rates it 5 (weak accept).

### Strengths

- Leveraging pre-trained diffusion models for data augmentation and degradation in SR is insightful and a rarely studied but crucial point (R1, R2).
- The approach is straightforward, yet in some experiments it achieves surprisingly significant improvements (R3).
- The paper is clearly written; ablation studies are extensive and sufficient to prove effectiveness of the proposed method (R1).
- The motivation is reasonable, and the perspective is novel (R2).

### Weaknesses

**Experimental comparisons & baselines**

- The ablation in Sec. 4.1 and 4.2 uses 10× more images in the diffusion-based setting (e.g., 10,000) than the baseline (1,000), making the comparison unfair because SR models trained on richer distributions perform better (R1, R3; supported by Li et al. LSdir). A fairer design would be: (1) randomly sample 1000 images, (2) use 10000 original images, (3) augment the same 1000 images to 10000 via diffusion (R1).
- Sec. 4.3 on DD lacks comparison with the well-established degradation pipeline of RealESRGAN and BSRT (Zhang et al. ICCV’21) (R1, R2, R3). The authors should evaluate on real-world benchmarks like RealSR, DrealSR, RealSRSet, OST300, using PSNR and LPIPS (R1, R2). Currently only qualitative comparisons are shown (Figure 3); quantitative comparisons are missing (R3).
- The DD method uses a negative prompt (“poor details”) that shares attributes with the positive prompt; the rationale for this design is unclear (R2).
- The experimental setup is unconventional: DA is tested on animeSR, Manga109, and iCartoonFace instead of standard SR benchmarks (Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100, General100) (R3). Only two baselines (Transformation-based and Diffusion-based) are compared; other augmentation methods (e.g., from [1][2] of R3) are omitted (R3).

**Method description & missing details**

- The paper does not define “modification strength” or explain how it controls SD output, nor how appropriate values are determined for different tasks (R2).
- Fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion (line 211) lacks details: which components are fine-tuned (VAE encoder, UNet, VAE decoder) and hyperparameters (R2).
- Parameter α in Figure 2(A) is unexplained (R2).
- The input feeding process in both Figure 2(A) and (B) is not described (e.g., whether/how noise is added in latent space, parameters) (R2).
- It is unclear how a text-to-image model (SD) that generates high-quality images can simulate real-world degradation; the authors should explain the adaptation (R2).
- DA modifies image content at the semantic level, raising the question whether this is still data augmentation or just introducing new images; the paper does not analyze why DA improves performance (R3). In Table 3, adding more images continues to improve performance, suggesting the benefit may simply come from more training data (R3).
- The method appears sensitive to prompts and strength values; “add noise to image” may behave differently from “noisy image” (R3). The prompt selection process (automatic or fixed) and how to determine best prompts per dataset are not discussed (R3).
- It is unclear whether DA and DD are applied per iteration or pre-processed, and how much extra time they add compared to normal training (R3).

**Evaluation metrics & unnatural artifacts**

- In Tables 1, 2, and 5, the proposed method improves fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) but shows little improvement in perception-based metrics (LPIPS, DISTS); no analysis is provided explaining this gap (R1, R2).
- Some augmented samples contain unnatural textures (e.g., corrupted text in Figure 2(A)); the paper lacks filtering methods to remove such samples (R1).
- More augmented samples should be shown to validate that SD actually follows the prompt (e.g., “add noise to the image”) (R1).
- In Figure 2(B), the shape of the tower tip changes after DD, contradicting the claim that DD does not alter image content (R3).

**Related work & missing references**

- The paper lacks references to diffusion-based data augmentation in other fields (DreamDA, Diverse data augmentation with diffusions, Effective data augmentation with diffusion models) and a highly related work DifAugGAN (R1).

**Minor reviewer disagreement**

- While two reviewers score 3 (weak reject), one scores 5 (weak accept) and notes that the method is straightforward yet yields significant improvements, but still raises concerns about experimental setup and lack of insights (R3).
