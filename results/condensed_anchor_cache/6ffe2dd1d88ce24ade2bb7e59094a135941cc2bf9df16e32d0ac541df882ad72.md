## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies scaling laws in a simplified linear associative memory model that captures key components of transformer attention layers (outer products of embeddings). The authors derive precise scaling laws for the model’s generalization error with respect to sample size and memory dimension, considering both finite data and finite capacity. They analyze different memory schemes (constant weights, frequency-based weights) and also study optimization-based learning (SGD, Adam), examining the effects of learning rate, batch size, and other hyperparameters. Experiments on synthetic data with a Zipf-like token distribution validate the theory. The paper is presented as a formal sandbox for understanding scaling in LLMs through the lens of associative memory.

### Strengths
- **Rigorous theoretical analysis** of scaling laws in a tractable model that mirrors inner layers of transformers (Reviewers 1,2,4,5). The analysis covers both finite-data and finite-capacity error regimes and derives explicit expressions.
- **Novel perspective** on scaling laws: the associative memory viewpoint contrasts with prior continuous-input analyses, and the treatment of discrete inputs with a naturalistic (Zipf) distribution is a promising direction (Reviewers 1,3,5).
- **Comprehensive experiments** back the theory, including fine-grained visualizations of stored associations. Code is provided (Reviewers 1,2,5).
- **Well-organized presentation**: notation, definitions, propositions, and theorems are clearly stated and accompanied by clean simulation plots (Reviewer 2).
- **Practical recommendations** emerge from the optimization experiments, such as the effects of learning rate and batch size on memorization error (Reviewer 1).
- **Significance**: the work draws formal connections between foundation models and associative memory, offering theoretical structure to a field driven by empirical results (Reviewers 1,4,5). The focus on token memorization was particularly appreciated (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Simplifications limit realism**: The model treats each input token as an independent query with deterministic one-to-one output associations, neglecting the contextual token–token interactions that attention provides, multiple layers, non-linearities, and softmax attention. These are major departures from practical transformers. It remains unclear how well the insights generalize (Reviewers 1,2,4,5).
- **Hard argmax discards lower-weighted components**: The analysis assumes hard argmax attention, which ignores the potential contributions of lower-weighted attention heads that capture nuanced relationships (Reviewer 4).
- **Discrete input assumption restricts generalization**: Unseen inputs always cause errors because the setting is discrete. The paper does not discuss when similarity-based generalization could occur in a discrete setting, and the realism of this assumption is questioned (Reviewer 5).
- **Motivation misalignment (minority view)**: One reviewer initially found a mismatch between the title/introduction (which implies studying memory capacity) and the actual content (which studies error scaling of a learning problem where adding data improves error indefinitely). After rebuttal clarifications, this reviewer acknowledged the correct context and raised their score, but the original concern about framing remains a point of confusion for readers (Reviewer 3).
- **Optimization advice may not transfer**: The recommendation of small batches and large step sizes appears opposite to what works in large-scale Transformer training (where large batches and small steps are typical). The simplified model may not reflect hardware constraints, distributed training, or the role of advanced optimizers like Adam (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity issues**: Multiple reviewers noted the paper is dense and could benefit from an architectural diagram (Reviewer 1), an expanded related work section positioning the results against prior associative memory and scaling law literature (Reviewer 5), and more space for interpretation (Reviewer 5). Minor typos/omissions: missing subject in abstract, “batch one” should be “batch size one”, log symbol not defined, figures placed far from text, unclear error bars in Figs. 3/4, high variance in Fig. 7 (Reviewers 3,5).
- **Missing extensions**: The paper does not address how the analysis could be extended to probabilistic associations (Reviewer 4), softmax attention (Reviewer 4), or multiple weight matrices with token–token interaction (Reviewer 1). There is no practical guidance on which memory scheme to use for given (T, d) ranges that map to real transformers (Reviewer 2).
- **Single-layer focus**: The model serves as a proxy for one transformer layer; how results scale to the full multi-layer architecture is not addressed (Reviewer 1).
