## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CodeIt, an iterative program synthesis method that uses a pretrained LLM policy (CodeT5) and a DSL to solve ARC tasks. It iterates between sampling programs from the policy, augmenting them via program mutation and hindsight relabeling, and retraining the policy. On the ARC evaluation set CodeIt solves 40/400 tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance. Ablation studies are provided.

### Strengths
- Simple and straightforward method, well-designed with careful details, easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on the challenging ARC full evaluation set (40/400 tasks); many prior works evaluate only on a simpler subset (R1, R4).
- Ablation studies clearly demonstrate the contribution of each component (R1, R2, R4).
- Approach is a natural extension of iterative policy improvement to program synthesis for ARC; the paper is well-written with good related work and discussion (R4). One reviewer (R3) noted it is essentially a simplified version of DreamCoder, which is an intuitive way to approach the problem.
- While some reviewers found novelty limited, one reviewer (R4) considered the approach excellent and exciting, highlighting its potential for further improvement.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty and contribution**: The core ideas (iterative policy improvement, hindsight relabeling, program mutation) are not new and have been explored in prior program synthesis works (R1, R2). The paper lacks deeper analysis of unique challenges or benefits of applying these ideas to the ARC domain (R2). There is disagreement among reviewers: R1 and R2 find novelty low, R3 and R4 consider the contribution solid.
- **Effectiveness beyond a mutation-only baseline is marginal**: In Table 1, full CodeIt solves only 40/400 tasks vs. 36/400 for a baseline that simply mutates the original training programs (R1). This 1% improvement suggests that the DSL, the reference programs, and the mutation procedure are the major drivers of performance (R1, R3). Reviewers request: how many solved tasks are common between the two methods? Are CodeIt’s solutions more succinct? (R1). R3 suggests that with extensive random mutation, the mutation baseline might outperform the policy-guided method.
- **Scalability and reliance on domain expertise**: The approach depends heavily on a manually designed DSL, mutation operators, and a DSL execution engine, all crafted by domain experts. This limits scalability and generalizability to new domains; performance quality relative to these prerequisites is not studied (R2).
- **Insufficient analysis of model capabilities and errors**: The paper does not analyze what the model learns over iterations—e.g., ability to generate syntactically correct code, effectiveness using individual DSL operators, or types of errors (incorrect operator selection, argument usage, DSL limitations) (R4). A breakdown of which tasks are solved and when, along with examples of failures, would increase understanding (R4). The reported 40/400 tasks seems low for the technique used (R4).
- **Missing comparisons and evaluations**:
  - No apple-to-apple comparison with other learning-based methods under a common setting (R2).
  - Only evaluated on ARC; reviewers suggest testing on another abstract reasoning dataset like RAVEN to show generality (R3, also noted by R4 as potentially helpful).
  - No evaluation on the hidden ARC test set (R4).
  - No comparison with brute-force search approaches from the Kaggle competition that reportedly solve more evaluation tasks (R4).
  - The baseline “policy only” (Figure 3) is not defined in the paper (R2, R4).
- **Missing ablation and experimental details**:
  - Missing ablation on number of sampled programs per task; only context length is varied. A performance vs. number of samples curve would be informative (R3).
  - Sensitivity of mutation hyperparameter φ is not studied; the mutation procedure needs more explanation in the pseudocode (R1).
  - Effect of number of training epochs per iteration during continual learning is not explored (R1).
  - Percentage of sampled programs that execute successfully is not reported (R4).
  - How hindsight relabeling works with the mutation baseline (Section 3.1 / Appendix A.2) is unclear (R4).
  - No quantitative analysis of program compression over iterations (e.g., how many correct programs become shorter) (R1).
  - No evidence that the pretrained model is comfortable using the DSL (e.g., generates correct arguments) (R4).
  - The paper does not explore whether a grid array number encoding (like typical T5 input) would perform better than the custom encoding (R4).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - Variables such as n_ρ, n_tasks, and the x-axis of Figure 3 are not defined (R2).
  - Figure 4 is hard to interpret because the task is not shown and function meanings are unclear (R1).
  - Typographical errors: “sampling” missing in Section 3.3; “finetune” should be “pre-train” in Section 2.2 (R1).
  - Use of “test” may be confused with the hidden test set; rename to “eval” (R4).
  - No mention of code release (R4).
  - Overall writing could use minor revisions for clarity (R4).
