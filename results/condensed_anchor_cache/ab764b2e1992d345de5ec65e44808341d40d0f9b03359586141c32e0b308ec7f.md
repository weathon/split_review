## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FinTruthQA, a benchmark dataset of 6,000 manually annotated financial Q&A entries from Chinese stock exchange (Shanghai and Shenzhen) investor interactive platforms. Four evaluation criteria are used: question identification, question relevance, answer readability, and answer relevance. The authors benchmark various NLP techniques—including traditional ML models, pre-trained language models (PLMs), and large language models (LLMs)—and find that models perform well on question-related tasks but struggle with answer readability and relevance. The dataset aims to support auditors, regulators, and analysts in real-time disclosure quality monitoring.

### Strengths
1. **Novel benchmark dataset**: FinTruthQA fills a gap in financial NLP by targeting the underexplored Q&A format of investor-platform disclosures, providing a structured evaluation of both question and answer quality. The dataset is tailored for financial disclosure quality and has real-world applicability for auditors, analysts, and regulators. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
2. **Comprehensive model evaluation**: The paper benchmarks a wide range of techniques—from traditional ML models to PLMs and LLMs—and includes domain-specific models like FinBERT with continued pre-training. The experiments provide insights into performance limitations, especially on answer readability and relevance, and show that domain-specific models perform better on complex tasks. (Reviewer 1, 2, 4)
3. **Thorough annotation process**: The annotation process involves multiple rounds and quality control, uses comprehensive evaluation metrics, and documents data collection and processing steps. (Reviewer 2)
4. **Real-world problem focus**: The work addresses the crucial challenge of evaluating information disclosure quality on interactive platforms, an unstructured form of financial communication distinct from reports or calls. (Reviewer 2, 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited and inconsistent evaluation protocol**: LLMs are evaluated only on the answer relevance task in a zero-shot setting, while ML and PLM models use supervised fine-tuning across tasks. This inconsistency prevents a fair comparison and leaves unexplored how fine-tuned LLMs would perform on question identification, question relevance, and answer readability tasks. The paper does not report experiment details such as temperature. (Reviewer 1, 2)
2. **Copyright and data usage concerns**: The paper lacks clarity on whether legal permissions have been obtained to use and openly release Q&A entries from stock exchange platforms, raising concerns about ethical and legal viability for the research community. (Reviewer 1)
3. **Missing financial domain-specific LLMs**: Financial LLMs (e.g., FinTral, Open-FinLLMs) are not included, which limits the benchmark’s practical applicability and fails to consider models specifically designed for finance that likely outperform general-purpose models. (Reviewer 1, 4)
4. **Class imbalance issues**: Significant imbalance exists—e.g., question identification is majority positive, and answer readability labels 2 and 3 appear in <3% of the data. This skews model bias toward majority classes and degrades minority class performance. Figure 4 shows label 4 frequently misclassified as label 1 but rarely as labels 2 or 3. The paper does not address mitigation techniques. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
5. **Limited generalizability**: The dataset is exclusively from Chinese markets and focuses solely on the Q&A format, excluding other disclosure types (e.g., reports, analytic reports). Lack of testing across different regulatory or cultural contexts restricts its impact as a global benchmark. (Reviewer 2, 4)
6. **Readability definition and baselines**: The definition of readability is narrow (ease of understanding/writing quality) and misses components like structure, clarity, style, and grammar. No established readability baselines (e.g., Gunning fog index) are included; the authors should reference workshops like TSAR. (Reviewer 3)
7. **Subjectivity and annotator bias**: Human annotation for subjective criteria (readability, relevance) may introduce bias, and consistency across annotator groups is not demonstrated. (Reviewer 4)
8. **Lack of in-depth error analysis**: The paper provides only overall performance results without detailed analysis of specific failure cases, which limits understanding of dataset complexity and model improvement directions. (Reviewer 4)
9. **Missing related work**: Several relevant works are not cited: FinBen, FinTral, Large Language Models as Financial Data Annotators, FinCon, Open-FinLLMs. (Reviewer 4)
10. **Title discrepancy**: The title suggests a focus on truthfulness, but the content addresses readability and relevance, not factual accuracy. (Reviewer 3)
11. **Limited technical contribution**: The overall technical novelty to financial Q&A or general domain Q&A is limited. (Reviewer 2)
12. **Traditional metrics question**: The use of traditional evaluation metrics may not be fully appropriate for the financial sector; this is raised as a question. (Reviewer 2)
