## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes using transformer architectures to approximate the mean-field dynamics of interacting particle systems. Leveraging the permutation equivariance of transformers and a universal approximation theorem for transformers (Alberti et al., 2023), the authors prove bounds on the distance between the true mean-field dynamics and the dynamics obtained from a finite-dimensional transformer’s expectation (“expected transformer”). Numerical experiments are performed on the Cucker–Smale flocking model and the mean-field training of two-layer neural networks. While the idea is novel, reviewers agree that the theoretical contribution is limited and mostly a straightforward extension of existing results, the assumptions are too strong and unvalidated, and the experimental evaluation is insufficiently detailed and inconclusive.

### Strengths

*   Using permutation-equivariant transformers to approximate infinite-dimensional mean-field dynamics is an intriguing and novel application (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
*   The paper provides theoretical approximation guarantees grounded in the universal approximation theorem for transformers (Alberti et al., 2023), offering at least qualitative justification (Reviewers 1, 3).
*   Numerical validation on two examples – Cucker–Smale and mean-field two-layer neural network training – demonstrates potential (Reviewers 1, 3).
*   One reviewer (R1, rating 5) considered the mathematical parts correct and the technical contribution sound, though still noting limited novelty and too-strong assumptions.

### Weaknesses

*   **Limited novelty and practical method.**  
    *   The work is largely based on the existing transformer approximation theorem (Alberti et al., 2023) with straightforward modifications; Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 are direct consequences and could be merged (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
    *   No fully practical method is studied: \(\mathcal{T}_n\) involves an expectation and cannot be computed, and \(\mu^{\mathcal{T}_n}\) requires multiple applications of \(\mathcal{T}_n\); the theoretical analysis is not performed on the practical algorithm of Remark 1, leaving key implementation issues unresolved (Reviewer 2).  
    *   A practical method to approximate \(\mu^{\mathcal{F}}\) should be proposed and analyzed, and it is unclear how Theorem 4 would adapt to such a method (Reviewer 2).  
    *   The trade-off in Remark 1 (dividing data into \(B\) batches of size \(n\)) is not discussed: \(B=1\) is best for approximation, but computational cost is quadratic in \(n\) (Reviewer 2).

*   **Unsound or unclear theoretical assumptions.**  
    *   The assumptions in eq. (16) and the inequality above (19) are too strong; they need validation, e.g., for the Cucker–Smale system (Reviewer 1).  
    *   The global Lipschitz and linear growth conditions should be validated for the examples (Reviewer 1).  
    *   Theorem 4 assumes \(\mathcal{F}\) satisfies Assumption 2, but it should refer to \(\mathcal{T}_n\) (not yet defined); Assumption 2 and Theorem 4 lack clarity. The property that (18) has a unique Lagrangian solution for \(\mathcal{T}_n\) should be proven, or at least the difficulties explained (Reviewer 2).  
    *   Many norms are imprecise or incorrect in terms of space (e.g., lines 235, 243, 269, 271, 284, 317, 350) (Reviewer 2).  
    *   The theoretical results do not provide error rates and are not referenced in the numerical section, so there is no connection between theory and experiments (Reviewer 3).  
    *   For Theorem 2, the cited reference does not control the dependency of the number of MLP parameters \(\zeta\) on \(\epsilon\); it is unclear whether a special case is considered (Reviewer 3).  
    *   Uniform-in-time approximation guarantees appear unattainable with the current strategy (Reviewer 4).

*   **Insufficient and poorly presented experiments.**  
    *   The simulation description is too brief to be reproducible (Reviewer 2).  
    *   Figure 1 shows SGD outperforming the transformer, contradicting the claim of “favorable training and test loss performance” (Reviewers 2, 3).  
    *   Only one setting per example (Cucker–Smale with \(N=20\) – very small; no non‑consensus behavior; no higher dimensions; no other models like Cucker–Dong or Hegelsmann–Krause) (Reviewers 2, 4).  
    *   No ablation studies, architecture details, or discussion of computational cost (Reviewers 2, 3).  
    *   No experiment showing reliable estimation of the vector field (only trajectory prediction) (Reviewer 4).  
    *   The meaning of “trained with SGD” vs. transformer training is ambiguous; L505 contradicts the description before Section 5.1 (Reviewer 4).  
    *   Descriptions use vague terms (“quite small”, “quite well”) instead of precise quantitative evaluations (Reviewer 3).  
    *   Figure 3 is not in the correct position; Figure 1 has formatting problems (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
    *   It is unclear how initial velocities are computed in the Cucker–Smale example (Reviewer 4).  
    *   The authors state that the transformer model does not compute any gradients (L505), but earlier it is described as trained – clarification needed (Reviewer 4).

*   **Presentation and clarity issues.**  
    *   The paper’s positioning is unclear: the introduction focuses on “identifying dynamical system models for physical processes”, but the related work shifts to PINNs and Neural Operators; the focus on mean-field dynamics should be aligned (Reviewer 3).  
    *   Many typos, language issues, and imprecisions (Reviewers 2, 4). Specific examples: L029, L042-44 (Ref [19] works on microscopic, not mean-field level), L055 (ref should appear earlier), L061 (add a reference for the continuity equation), L094 (why relevant?), L149 (existence/uniqueness), eq. (5) (no ReLU), L190 (output shape ambiguous), L200, eq. (8) (include parameters), section 3.2 title (“with” instead of “of”), L228, L234 (wrong inequality direction), L261, eq. (12) (formatting), L326, L390 (condition or consequence?), L423, L471, L534 (Reviewer 4).  
    *   Notations are used multiple times for different objects, making the paper difficult to read (Reviewer 2).
