## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper modifies Deep InfoMax (DIM) by injecting independent noise (Gaussian or uniform) into the normalized encoder output while keeping the same InfoMax objective, enabling automatic distribution matching to a prior (e.g., Gaussian, uniform). The theoretical justification relies on maximum entropy under covariance or range constraints. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR demonstrate distribution matching and a trade-off between downstream performance and matching quality. Reviewers appreciated the clarity, intuition, and simplicity, but expressed concerns about the limited scale of experiments, insufficient analysis of noise necessity, and lack of evaluation on larger datasets such as ImageNet. Scores: one reviewer gave 8 (notably more positive); three gave 6 (moderate, with reservations).

### Strengths
- **Intuition and theoretical foundation**: Noise injection is motivated by maximum entropy: Gaussian noise enforces a fixed covariance and thus maximizes entropy under that constraint, driving representations toward Gaussian; uniform noise within a narrow range enforces bounded support, driving representations toward uniform. A theoretical proof supports the effect of both noise types. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Clarity and writing**: The paper is well-written, with robust notation, clear derivations, and rich background that makes the method easy to follow. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Simplicity and novelty**: The modification is simple yet yields non-negligible improvements, particularly in conditional/unconditional sampling. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Empirical demonstration**: Experiments on CIFAR and MNIST show the method’s usefulness for distribution matching and the associated trade-off between representation quality and matching fidelity. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Theoretical and empirical trade-off analysis**: The paper provides both upper bounds on mutual information and empirical characterization of the noise–performance trade-off. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scale of experiments**: All tests use small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR). Results are near-saturated on MNIST (Figure 1). The method’s effectiveness on larger, more diverse datasets (e.g., ImageNet, CIFAR-100) is not verified. This applies to both representation learning (linear probing) and generation/sampling tasks. (Reviewers 2,4; also Reviewer 1 suggests larger experiments)
- **Missing theoretical analysis of noise necessity**: Lemma 3.1 highlights the importance of both additive noise and input augmentation, but the analysis focuses on augmentation and does not clearly explain why additive noise is necessary (e.g., its significance versus other forms of regularization). (Reviewer 3)
- **Efficiency not discussed**: The title claims “efficient” distribution matching, yet no analysis of computational or sample efficiency is provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing empirical validation on other SSRL methods**: The paper claims the same noise-injection approach can be applied to other self-supervised representation learning methods, but no experiments are conducted to demonstrate usefulness or training fragility. (Reviewer 4)
- **Bandwidth limitation concern**: Adding noise limits the bandwidth of the representation, so improvements may vanish when scaling to larger and more diverse datasets. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of explanation for uniform noise**: Before the proof of uniform noise injection, a short intuitive explanation (similar to line 236–239 for Gaussian noise) is missing; the rationale for small epsilon and discrete support is not immediately clear. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unaddressed questions about experimental details and generalization**:  
  * Whether the linear probe is trained on the encoder output or an intermediate layer is not specified. (Reviewer 2)  
  * Transferability of gains from CIFAR to ImageNet is not examined. (Reviewer 2)  
  * Stability of training under large-scale settings is not tested. (Reviewer 4)  
  * Interpretable single directions in the learned representation (for generation) are not shown on more advanced datasets (e.g., FFHQ, ImageNet). (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor formatting issue**: Citation overflow on page 1 breaks reference formatting. (Reviewer 2)

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive about the method’s novelty and empirical results, while the three reviewers at score 6 consistently raised the need for larger-scale experiments, deeper theoretical analysis of noise necessity, and broader validation. This contrast should be considered when weighing the strengths and weaknesses.
