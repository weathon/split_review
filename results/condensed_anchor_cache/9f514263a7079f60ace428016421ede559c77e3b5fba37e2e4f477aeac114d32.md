## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces SynPO, a self-boosting framework that uses synthetic preference data for LLM alignment. SynPO trains a self-prompt generator (controlled by keywords sampled from a large corpus like RefinedWeb) and a response improver that refines model outputs to create chosen responses, pairing them with original model outputs as rejected responses. The pipeline uses only a small amount of seed SFT data; the remaining preference data is synthetic. After four iterations on Llama3-8B and Mistral-7B, the authors report significant improvements in instruction following (22–30% win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and ArenaHard) and general performance (3–5% on the Open LLM Leaderboard). While many reviewers praise the method's novelty and extensive experiments, one reviewer (score 5) strongly questions whether the approach is truly "self-boosting," given the reliance on external keyword selection and seed data, and finds the writing unclear. Another reviewer (score 8) notes that each pipeline component is known but the combination is novel. Concerns about data leakage, out-of-distribution evaluation, lack of insight into keyword effectiveness, and unexplained large performance gains are raised across multiple reviewers.

### Strengths

- **Novel and practical solution** to the critical challenge of scarce high-quality preference data; provides a scalable way to generate synthetic preference data without human annotation (R4).
- **Sound approach to diversity** in self-improvement via keyword-based prompt generation; keywords offer transparency, granularity of control, and ease of analysis (R1, R3, R5).
- **Intuitive pipeline** where each component contributes to final performance; creative use of small seed SFT data to steer the entire process (R2).
- **Extensive and strong empirical results**, including data analysis (Figures 4 and 5) and thorough ablation studies (R2, R3, R4).
- **Demonstrates infinite generation potential** through experiments on performance vs. number of synthetic datasets; validates diversity in keyword list with supporting experiments (R5).
- **Establishes effectiveness in multi-turn dialogue**, which prior work rarely considered (R5).
- **Timely topic** given recent attention to alignment and the high cost of human preference data (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Not fully "self-boosting"** as claimed: requires external knowledge (e.g., seed SFT data, careful keyword selection from a large corpus) to guide learning; the model's generation ability also depends on keyword prompts (R1).
- **Unclear writing in introduction**: it is not well explained which model is the self-prompt generator, which is the response improver, and how they relate (R1, R3). Additional typos: L193 "infinite" should be "huge"; L247 sigma should be beta; L314 and L316 contain errors (R2). Incorrectly claims use of base models (Mistral-base and Llama3-base) when models actually underwent SFT (R3).
- **Missing test for benchmark data leakage** from the synthetic preference data; no checks reported (R2).
- **Insufficient insights on keywords**: no analysis on what types of noise keywords are effective for bad responses, which extraction forms are more beneficial, or how order affects outcomes; needs ablation on excluding noise keywords (L156–157) and experiments varying keyword count or increasing it significantly (R1, R5).
- **Weak validation of out-of-distribution data**: seed data reflects OpenAI GPT preferences, same as AlpacaEval 2.0 and Arena-Hard, so benchmark gains may partly reflect in-distribution bias. Improvement on TruthfulQA (included in UltraFeedback) yet minimal effect on other datasets; concerns that SynPO does not fully resolve in-distribution bias and may be less efficient than CoT-based methods (R3, R5).
- **Unexplained dramatic win rate improvement** from 6.6% to 34.0% over SFT baseline; limited ablation analyzing why synthetic data works so much better (R4).
- **Performance degradation on some tasks**, e.g., Mistral's performance on GSM8K (R4).
- **Knowledge benchmark improvements are suspicious** (e.g., TruthfulQA) because knowledge cannot be gained from self-improvement loops; question whether gains come from GPT-4 data. Suggested baseline: multiple rounds without updating the policy model to isolate the effect (R3).
- **Questions about preference data construction**: why is the rejected response always from the base model instead of from previous iteration models? Does the number of filtered-out preference pairs decrease across iterations? (R2)
- **Need for references** to prior work on word-to-text tasks and augmentation via keyword extraction and contrastive learning (e.g., CommonGen [1], CHEF [2]) (R5).
- **Individual pipeline components are known** (prompt generation, preference pair construction); only the combination as a whole is novel (R2).
