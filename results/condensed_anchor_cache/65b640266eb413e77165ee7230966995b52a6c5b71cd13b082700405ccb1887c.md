## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces T-MARS (Text Masking and Re-Scoring), a data filtering method for large image-text datasets (e.g., LAION). The motivation is the observation that ~40% of LAION images contain text overlapping significantly with the caption, which may incentivize models to rely on OCR instead of learning visual features. T-MARS detects and masks text, then re-scores the masked image against the caption using CLIP, discarding low-score pairs. Experiments on DataComp benchmarks show strong gains: e.g., top-ranked on ImageNet at medium scale, outperforming CLIP filtering by 6.5% on ImageNet and 4.7% on VTAB. The method is simple, scalable, and shows increasing gains with data/compute scaling. Four reviewers rated the paper 6, 6, 6, 8; the eighth reviewer was notably more positive, while others were positive but with specific concerns. One reviewer raised their score after rebuttal.

### Strengths
- **Important and timely problem**: Data curation for web-scale multimodal datasets is a key open challenge in deep learning. The paper makes a significant step forward, with high impact potential for practitioners (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Simple and novel approach**: masking text and re-scoring with CLIP is straightforward, easy to apply, and scalable to large pools (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Strong empirical results**: T-MARS yields large accuracy gains over strong baselines (CLIP filtering by 6.5% on ImageNet, 4.7% on VTAB) and is top-ranked on DataComp’s medium scale (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Gains scale exponentially with data/compute (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Robust evaluation**: Evaluated on multiple baselines (2M to 128M), multiple downstream tasks, and shows no bias in the filtered subset (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Results align with other works emphasizing data quality (e.g., “Textbooks Are All You Need”) (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity**: The overall idea and motivation are clear and easy to understand (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 found the paper very clear and well written; Reviewer 1 and 4 praised the presentation. (Note: Reviewer 2 found the writing somewhat obscure.)
- **Observation novelty**: The key observation that ~40% of LAION images have overlapping text provides a concrete, data-driven motivation (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and theory**: The motivation is somewhat weak and lacks theoretical analysis of why text-only images degrade visual learning compared to, e.g., mislabeled data. A deeper discussion of alternative ways to address text overlap (beyond masking) would give more insight (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). *Minority view*: Reviewer 4 considered the motivation strong and did not raise this concern.
- **Analysis of text overlap**: Chapter 3’s manual analysis of 500 samples lacks statistical rigor—no error bars, no quantification of representativeness of the whole dataset. Larger random samples with confidence intervals are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Reliance on CLIP score**: The method depends on CLIP similarity for filtering, which can be noisy and introduce additional biases. The paper does not tackle this limitation (Reviewer 3).
- **Choice of downstream evaluation tasks**: The paper uses inconsistent subsets of DataComp’s 38 tasks across tables (e.g., 17 in Table 1, 23 in Section 5.3) without clear explanation. Some strong baselines from DataComp are missing in some tables (Reviewer 4). *Also noted by Reviewer 1*: only accuracy is reported, which may be narrow and miss other biases.
- **Missing experiments on other datasets**: Experiments are limited to LAION; would be good to test on other datasets like CC12M to show generalizability (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). [*Ablation/missing comparison*]
- **Lack of large-scale experiments**: The paper does not run the largest DataComp scale (1.28B pairs), but shows scaling trends. Reviewer 4 notes this as a weakness but does not count it against the authors given cost constraints.
- **Potential artifact from masking**: Masking text with average color of surrounding pixels may alter the original image distribution. Could generative inpainting be better? (Reviewer 1). What if we train *with* masked images instead of discarding them? (Reviewer 3). [*Ablation/analysis request*]
- **Unclear filtering threshold**: Why exactly 50% of the pool is filtered? (Reviewer 4). Why different masking percentages? (Reviewer 1). [*Method justification*]
- **Writing clarity**: Reviewer 2 found the paper (especially Chapter 6) hard to follow. Reviewer 3 also noted Chapter 6 needs rewriting for better presentation. (Other reviewers found it clear.)
- **Missing details**: No distribution of CLIP scores before/after filtering provided (Reviewer 1). Whether data augmentation is used and its effect on improvement is unclear (Reviewer 1). [*Missing analysis*]
- **Direct text removal suggestion**: Could simply removing all text from images avoid distraction? (Reviewer 2). [*Alternative approach discussion*]
