## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper extends the identifiability result of Liu et al. (2022) for latent causal models from linear Gaussian to polynomial causal relationships with exponential-family noise. It also discusses partial identifiability when only some coefficients change across environments, proposes an empirical estimation method, and validates on synthetic and real-world data. Scores range from 3 to 6 (four reviewers gave 6, one gave 3). Reviewers generally praise clear writing, tackling a challenging problem, and the theoretical extension to nonlinear/non-Gaussian settings. However, weaknesses cluster around insufficient novelty relative to prior work, ambiguous definitions, overly strong assumptions, limited experimental scale, and a lack of clarity in key technical steps. The minority reviewer (R1) is markedly more negative, viewing the contribution as a Corollary-level extension and questioning the justification of assumptions.

### Strengths

- **Important problem and significant theoretical extension**: Extends latent causal model identifiability from linear Gaussian to polynomial models and exponential family noise, and reduces the number of required environments from quadratic to linear (R2, R3, R4, R5). Tackles an important and challenging problem (R3, R4).
- **Clear writing and exposition**: Well-written with clear assumptions, proof sketches, and intuitive explanations; general presentation is easy to follow and rigorous (R2, R3, R4, R5). The proof sketch and insights paragraphs are particularly appreciated (R4).
- **In-depth discussion of partial identifiability**: Provides useful and insightful treatment of scenarios where only some coefficients change, which is more realistic in practice (R3). Also discusses partial (un)identifiability (R1, R5).
- **Principled approach with experimental support**: The method is principled, and experiments on synthetic and real-world data support the theoretical findings (R2, R5). The empirical validation is reasonably thorough and complements the theory (R5).
- **Self-contained treatise**: Covers both theory and empirics, with transparent communication of assumptions (R1, though with caveat that execution is lacking).
- **Moves beyond restrictive linear Gaussian**: Shows that the identifiability result holds for nonlinear and non-Gaussian cases, which is a significant step (R2, R4, R5).

### Weaknesses

- **Technical novelty and significance**: 
  - The paper heavily relies on the unpublished Liu et al. (2022) and is seen as an incremental improvement; R1 considers it a Corollary-level discussion rather than a significant independent contribution. The specific technical advancements needed to move from linear Gaussian to polynomial/exponential family are not clearly articulated, leaving the impression of mere substitution (R1).
  - Core ideas (exponential family for noise, leveraging distribution changes for permutation/scaling) have been explored in prior works; the novelty mainly lies in applying them to learning latent causal relationships, not just latent variables (R2). For latent identification alone, prior interventional works already provide permutation and scaling guarantees with more general structures (R2).
  - R5 notes that reliance on an unpublished paper affects novelty, though overall novelty is sufficient for acceptance. R1 is the most negative; other reviewers find the contribution significant enough.

- **Ambiguous or missing definitions**:
  - “Identifiability” is not formally defined; it is unclear whether identification is up to permutation, scaling, or other transformations, and what “unidentifiability” means (R4). The paper later notes permutation indeterminacy is circumvented by assuming the correct topological order, which R2 and R3 consider a limitation.
  - “Changes across environments” is ambiguous: does it mean (A) there exist two environments where a coefficient differs, or (B) for each pair of environments the coefficient differs? The text does not clarify (R4).
  - Roles of latent variables z, u, n are not clearly distinguished; the data-generating process is not explained from first principles, which would lead to confusion for readers unfamiliar with the literature (R1).
  - The Kronecker product definition is non-standard and not adequately explained; it is unclear how it differs from the usual definition and why it is necessary (R1).
  - “DAG constraint” is first mentioned in the proof sketch; it should be introduced as an assumption before that point (R4).
  - Equation (6) contains unexplained “= N(…)” notation; zi does not appear on the RHS (R4; also R5 asks where zi is hidden).
  - MPC metric: correlation between what exactly? (R5)
  - Corollary 3.2: the statement does not appear to follow from the preceding theorem, so “corollary” is inappropriate (R4). The φ in its proof is not defined (R4).

- **Assumptions too strong, numerous, or not justified**:
  - The number of assumptions is “absurd” (R1), and some are severe, e.g., the existence of a suitable matrix L is not justified; claims of bridging theory and practice are overestimated (R1). 
  - Additivity of noise and knowledge of the number of latent variables are strong assumptions (R5).
  - The polynomial assumption is chosen without discussion of necessity, expressiveness, or alternatives (e.g., splines, neural networks); overfitting with high-degree polynomials is not addressed (R3).
  - Reliance on the exponential family assumption for noise identification limits applicability in real-world scenarios where noise distributions may deviate (R2).
  - The method assumes the correct topological order to resolve permutation indeterminacy; this is a significant caveat for learning causal relationships (R2, R3).
  - For partial identifiability, the paper does not address whether the equivalence class of latent variables leads to a unique or equivalence class of causal graphs; the identifiability of the latent structure (not just variables) is not discussed (R3).

- **Experiments too limited**:
  - Scale is small: maximum latent dimension is 5; larger dimensions (d=10 or 20) should be tested (R2).
  - Results are only shown for cases where the method works (LinearGamma, LinearBeta, PolyGamma); difficult cases (e.g., inverse Gamma) are omitted, even as failure cases in the appendix (R5).
  - Experiments are somewhat artificial, and real-world relevance is not demonstrated (R5).
  - No comparison against latent identification methods that leverage interventional data (which would be more appropriate baselines than Beta-VAE) (R2).
  - Could plot F1 score alongside SHD for latent causal discovery (R2).

- **Corollary 3.2 issues**:
  - R1: Seems to carry no value because the whole paradigm is based on the complete change assumption; its purpose is unclear.
  - R4 and R5: Several text-level errors (typo u_{2l+1} when u only goes up to 2l; unclear how the condition differs from Theorem 3.1; φ undefined). 
  - R5 asks: What prevented an unchanged coefficient from happening in Theorem 3.1?

- **Theoretical and presentation concerns**:
  - Theorem 3.1(i): “measure zero (i.e., has at most countable number of elements)” is incorrect in dimension ≥2 (e.g., a line in R² has uncountably many points) (R4).
  - Theorem 3.1(iii): “takes up” is ambiguous; does it mean u₀ etc. are possible values of random variable u? (R4)
  - Proof sketch: additive noise models are said to be identifiable, but linear Gaussian additive noise models are not – clarification needed (R5).
  - Start of Section 3: “even if the causal relationships are nonlinear and noise distributions are non-Gaussian” – the linear Gaussian case is the least identifiable, not the most, so the statement is confusing (R4).
  - Prior on lambda: “lower-dimensional” encodes acyclicity; does this require assuming the causal order? (R5)
  - Presentation could benefit from more illustrations/visualizations of the assumptions and coefficient changes (R3).
  - Typographical errors: “identifyable” → “identifiable” (pp. 5, 7), “polynomial propriety” → “the property” (p. 5), Appendix A.4: “support that” → “suppose that”, “proof of corollary A.3” → “proof of corollary 3.2”, “APPENDIX” → “Appendix” (R4).
