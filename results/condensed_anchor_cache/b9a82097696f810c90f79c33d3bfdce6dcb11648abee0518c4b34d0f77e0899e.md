## Merged Review

### Summary
MMTryon introduces a multi-modal, multi-reference virtual try-on framework that accepts text instructions and multiple garment images. It aims to support multiple items, enable style control, and eliminate segmentation dependency via a parsing-free encoder and scalable data pipeline. Reviewers agree that the multi-modality attention mechanism and the parsing-free design are interesting contributions, but they diverge sharply on novelty, completeness, and rigor: Reviewers 1 and 2 find the work strong and superior to prior methods, while Reviewer 3 considers contributions overstated, baseline comparisons incomplete, and texture quality poor. Reviewer 3 is much more critical (score 3 vs. 8 and 10), and these disagreements are preserved below.

### Strengths
- Novel multi-modal, multi-reference attention mechanism that enables multi-garment try-on with fine-grained text guidance (e.g., zipped/unzipped, tuck-in/tuck-out) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Parsing-free garment encoder and scalable data generation pipeline that removes segmentation dependency during training and inference, reducing artifacts and improving efficiency (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Extensive qualitative and quantitative evaluations on high-resolution benchmarks and in-the-wild test sets, with many ablation studies demonstrating impact of each component (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Well-written and well-organized paper (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Ability to combine multiple garments and manipulate fitting style via text commands (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Overstated novelty / unclear task definition:** Several claims are debated. Line 96 states "is the first model to support the multi-modal compositional try-on task," but similar multi-modal capabilities already exist in M&M VTO, AnyFit, and VITON-DiT. The paper does not clearly position against these works, and the motivation for the study is unclear. Reviewer 3 recommends explicit comparison and clarifications about novel advantages. (Reviewer 3; also echoed in Reviewer 2's "Related Work lacks recent studies.")
- **Limited novelty of attention mechanism:** The multi-modal multi-reference attention is described as a simple extension of reference attention (Zhu et al. 2024); it is not clear how details are decoupled and aligned. The garment encoder uses cross-attention without unique design or functional enhancement beyond standard mechanisms. (Reviewer 3)
- **Issues with texture details:** In multiple cases (including appendix), fine textures are not fully recovered – e.g., pants and logos are changed or poorly rendered. This applies to both single- and multi-garment scenarios. Reviewer 3 suggests identifying specific failures and mechanisms to improve texture quality. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Incomplete comparisons:** 
  - Quantitative results for single-garment cases missing for Outfit Anyone, Kolors-Virtual-Try-On, Wear-Any-Way, and MV-VTON. Table 1 should include these metrics. (Reviewer 3)
  - Figure 6 does not clearly display advantages over prior methods. (Reviewer 3)
  - Under multi-modal conditions, Table 7 suggests background is not controllable, but ImageDressing has achieved background control; it should be compared or explicitly stated as out of scope. (Reviewer 3)
- **Fusion without decoupling:** Fusing text and image features directly without decoupling may cause unintended consequences (e.g., modifications to garments not described in prompt); the paper does not analyze this or justify the strategy. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inference process unclear:** How the model prioritizes or combines multiple garment references is not explained; it is not clear how unintended modifications to untargeted garments are avoided. Example: in the first image, pants changed unexpectedly. (Reviewer 1)
- **Confusing implementation details:** Different SD versions are used – SDXL on line 295 and SD 1.5 on line 352 – without explanation of why or implications for outputs. (Reviewer 3)
- **Ablation terminology unclear:** Terms "w/o TQL" and "w/o MRA" in Fig. 8 are not clearly defined; the ablation would benefit from more precise explanation of how each component is removed and what the impact is. (Reviewer 1)
- **Dataset need:** The e-commerce dataset details are missing, and there is no commitment to open-source the dataset or data pipeline. While the data pipeline is a highlight, it is not a contribution if not released. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing challenging cases:** No results on garments with letters, numbers, or diverse textures; these are realistic challenges in VTON. (Reviewer 3)
- **Formatting issues:** In Table 2, "0.7" should be "0.70". Commas at the end of formulas 1 and 2, missing at the end of formulas 3 and 4. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inference time / GPU memory:** Not provided for different tasks. (Reviewer 1, as question; overlapping with missing detail criticism)
