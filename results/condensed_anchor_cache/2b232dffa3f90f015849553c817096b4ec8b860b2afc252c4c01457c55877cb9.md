## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces "late chunking," a method that first embeds all tokens of a long text using a long-context embedding model and then applies chunking just before mean pooling, rather than chunking before encoding. The resulting chunk embeddings retain full contextual information. Evaluations on retrieval tasks (BEIR subset) show consistent improvements over naive chunking across models and chunking strategies (fixed-size, sentence-based, semantic) without requiring additional training. The paper also proposes a dedicated fine-tuning approach. Reviewers find the method intuitive, simple, and beneficial off-the-shelf, but raise concerns about its applicability, computational cost, robustness, and evaluation scope.

### Strengths
- Well-designed experiments, clearly presented and easy to understand.
- Consistently outperforms naive chunking across almost all tasks and models tested.
- Method is intuitive, simple, and can be used off-the-shelf without extra training, benefiting the research community.
- Addresses the important practical problem of chunking for text embeddings (e.g., in RAG).
- Reduces information loss caused by direct chunking by encoding full context before chunking.
- Extensive analytical experiments exploring technical details of late chunking.
- Paper is well organized and presented.

### Weaknesses
- **Applicability and computational overhead**: The method only works when the text to be encoded is longer than the chunk size but still within the model’s limit, so it does not address the core issue of embedding models’ inability to handle very long sequences. It requires encoding the entire document (or a large portion) even when only a small chunk is needed, leading to significant overhead compared to standard independent chunk encoding. This trade-off between performance gains and computational cost is not adequately addressed (Reviewers 1, 4). A sliding-window approach (as in "long late chunking") still loses long-range dependencies beyond the window; this limitation is insufficiently discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Performance regression on fact-based retrieval**: Figure 3 shows that late chunking declines in performance on several datasets: NarrativeQA (chunk size > 128), 2WikiMultiHopQA (chunk size > 16), SummScreenFD (chunk size > 128), QMsum (chunk size > 256), Needle-8192 (chunk size > 4), and Passkey-8192 (chunk size > 32). This suggests fusion of contextual information can hurt precise retrieval when relevant information is sparse, raising robustness concerns (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental setup**: Only a subset of BEIR is used; the choice of datasets seems arbitrary, and a full BEIR evaluation is needed to validate general claims (Reviewer 4). The benchmark may not isolate scenarios where contextual information is necessary; a dedicated dataset (or subset) that requires long-range context would be more convincing (Reviewer 2). Additionally, only retrieval performance (e.g., NDCG) is measured, not downstream task performance (e.g., QA or summarization), making it unclear whether gains translate to practical improvements (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing baseline details**: It is unclear whether the naive chunking baseline follows standard practice (overlapping strides or including meta-information like document title per chunk). If not, comparisons may be unfair (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited chunk sizes**: Experiments only use chunk sizes up to 512 tokens, though the embedding models support 8k context. Gains diminish as chunk size increases, and it is unclear whether late chunking remains effective near the model’s length limit, where the benefit of chunking is largest (Reviewer 3).
- **Section 4.5 is a case study**: Rather than a systematic comparison, it adds little weight to the paper’s central argument; its space could be better used to address other concerns (Reviewer 2).
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with criticisms**:
  - The paper should provide more practical examples showing that current practice overlooks the scenario where chunk size < embedding model length, to motivate the method (Reviewer 1).
  - In Table 2, late chunking helps most on NFCorpus with Jv2 but least on TRECCOVID with Jv2. What causes these differing trends across datasets and models? (Reviewer 2)
  - If chunk boundaries are the same in late and naive chunking, why does late chunking still produce better embeddings? How does late chunking interact with causal attention in LLM retrievers? (Reviewer 4)
