## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Graph Parsing Network (GPN), a novel graph pooling method that adaptively learns a personalized pooling structure for each graph via a graph parsing algorithm. The algorithm infers pooling structures from edge scores, generating discrete assignment matrices for node clustering. GPN is evaluated on graph classification, node classification, graph reconstruction, and efficiency comparisons. Reviewer opinions varied: one reviewer (score 3, confidence 4) found the approach novel but considered the paper ad-hoc and confusing; two reviewers (scores 8, confidences 5 and 3) praised its novelty, clear motivation, and performance; one reviewer (score 5, confidence 3) appreciated the extensive experiments but raised concerns about theoretical claims and ablation significance.

### Strengths
- **Novel and sound approach**: The idea of using a graph parsing algorithm to produce an individual pooling structure for each graph is novel, interesting, and the motivation to adaptively condense graphs based on structural differences is sound (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Clear presentation**: Two reviewers found the paper well-written, with clear motivation and model structure (Reviewers 3,4); one reviewer (R1) disagreed (see Weaknesses).
- **Strong empirical performance**: GPN outperforms state-of-the-art graph pooling methods on graph classification tasks and achieves competitive performance on node classification (Reviewers 1,2,3). It also demonstrates good ability to preserve graph structure and node feature information in reconstruction (Reviewer 3).
- **Memory and time efficiency**: GPN is efficient in memory usage and computational time, especially compared to other assignment-matrix-based pooling methods (Reviewer 2). The graph parsing algorithm has no learnable parameters, contributing to efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- **Extensive evaluation**: The paper includes experiments on multiple tasks (graph classification, node classification, reconstruction) and efficiency tests (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Ad-hoc and confusing presentation**: One reviewer (score 3) found the paper ad-hoc, with undefined jargon and confusing language, making it difficult to follow (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of information-theoretic perspective**: Graph pooling could benefit from fundamental limits analysis (e.g., compression rate-distortion), which the paper does not address (Reviewer 1).
- **Similarity to prior edge-centric pooling**: The work may be similar to [1], which also manipulates edges to generate cluster assignment matrices for pooling. The novelty relative to [1] is not fully clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear motivation for Equation (4)**: It is unclear why edge scores are added to 1 before multiplication with node features, how this operation affects gradient flow during backpropagation, and whether alternative aggregation methods were considered (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing statistical tests**: The main results (Tables 1 and 2) show performance comparable to baselines when considering mean and standard deviations; statistical significance tests (e.g., t-tests) are missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited scalability evaluation**: The method is only tested on small graphs; experiments on larger, more complex graphs (tens of thousands+ nodes) are absent, leaving scalability unproven (Reviewer 3).
- **Over-smoothing risk and tree depth analysis**: No analysis of how the depth of the generated parsing tree affects performance, nor discussion of trade-offs when graphs have varying structural properties requiring deeper parsing (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear training procedure**: The paper does not clearly specify the loss function, optimization algorithm, or how the GNN layers and graph parsing network interact during training (Reviewer 3).
- **Time complexity misstatement**: The claim that each pooling layer has O(n^(k)) time complexity is inaccurate because computing edge scores C still requires O(m^(k)) time (Reviewer 4).
- **Proposition 5 is doubtful**: The argmax operator may not produce unique output when multiple nodes have identical edge scores (e.g., identical feature vectors and shared neighbors), leading to non-deterministic behavior and undermining the theoretical claim (Reviewer 4).
- **Ablation study not significant**: In Table 4, the performance difference between the original GPN and the variant with mean pooling is smaller than the standard deviation, indicating no statistically meaningful gain (Reviewer 4).
- **Non-differentiability of argmax**: The DOM uses argmax, which is not differentiable. It is unclear how the model is trained end-to-end, especially for the graph parsing algorithm (Reviewer 4; also a question from Reviewer 4).
- **Notation and explanation gaps**: 
  - In Equation (10), the superscript on GNN' should be indexed by layer; H^(k) in Eq. (3) and GNN'(X^(k), A^(k)) in Eq. (10) are essentially the same but notation is inconsistent, especially in node classification (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - It is unclear why GPN (which also uses an assignment matrix) is significantly more memory-efficient than other assignment-matrix-based methods, as shown in Figure 5 (Reviewer 2).
