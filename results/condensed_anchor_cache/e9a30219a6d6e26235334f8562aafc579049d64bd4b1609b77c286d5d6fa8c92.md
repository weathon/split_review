## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MMARP (Massively Multi-Agents Role Playing), which uses many simulated LLM agents with buyer/seller roles and binary price judgments (cheap/expensive) to predict stock prices via demand-supply curve intersection. Reviewers agree on some strengths: the paper is well-written with clear visuals (R1, R4), uses token probabilities to efficiently simulate large agent populations (R2, R3), provides ablation studies (R2), analyzes results through economic demand theory (R3), and has strong background/motivation (R4). However, reviewers raise major concerns: limited novelty and oversimplification (binary responses, agents not diverse; R1, R2, R4), lack of clarity in problem definition and motivation (R2, R4), overclaims on numerical understanding (no control experiment, linear transformation used; R1, R3, R4), flawed experimental design (only 10 stocks over 2 years, missing baselines like FinMA, missing metrics like Sharpe Ratio; R1, R2, R3, R4), missing comparisons (efficient simulation vs. regular multi-agent; R3), insufficient reproducibility (R1), and inadequate related work (R2). There is notable disagreement: Reviewers 1 and 2 (score 3) are highly critical, finding the method lacking in novelty and rigor; Reviewer 3 (score 6) considers it innovative and promising despite issues; Reviewer 4 (score 5) appreciates clarity but questions the claims.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and clear presentation**: Paper has logical structure and helpful visuals (R1, R4). R4 adds strong background and motivation.
- **Creative application of LLMs in market simulation**: Uses collective agent behavior to model demand-supply dynamics, showing some economic patterns (R1, R3, R4). R3 specifically finds the analysis through economic demand theory interesting.
- **Efficient simulation via token probabilities**: Using next-token weights to simulate large agent populations reduces computational load (R2, R3). R2 notes this isolates numerical understanding capability.
- **Ablation studies and visualizations**: Conducted many ablation studies and plots (R2). R3 and R4 also find graphs helpful.
- **Promising results on MSE and IC**: Continuous price prediction integrating economic theories yields performance approaching state-of-the-art on the tested stocks (R3). R1 notes the method outperforms deep-learning and financial LLM baselines.
- **Timely and interesting topic**: LLMs in financial forecasting is relevant (R4).

### Weaknesses
**Limited novelty and oversimplification**
- MMARP uses binary “cheap/expensive” and “good/low” responses, which severely oversimplify real market decision-making (R1). More complex trading mechanisms already exist in prior work (R1 cites [1]; R2 cites FinAgent, FinMem, StockAgent, FinCon).
- Agents share identical model, training data, and near-identical inputs, so there is minimal diversity; the simulation is more like a single-agent system (R1, R4). The term “multi-agent” is overclaimed since only one additional seller agent is introduced (R4).
- The demand-supply hypothesis is questionable when all agents derive from the same model (R1). R2 further notes the paper does not discuss how simulating human behavior interacts with numerical understanding as the number of agents increases.

**Unclear problem definition and motivation**
- The paper’s purpose is ambiguous: is it an evaluation framework for LLM numerical understanding, or a method to improve stock prediction? (R2). R2 also states the “Agent-Based Simulation for LLMs” subsection in Background seems unrelated; recent financial LLM agent works are missing.
- The law of large numbers is invoked but not defined with concrete input, output, and convergence conditions (R2, R4). R4 questions why a simulation of 100,000 agents is needed if the token probability distribution is already known, and why LLM logits should follow the law of large numbers.

**Overclaims on numerical understanding**
- The paper claims LLMs can understand numerical values, but the method does not address the fundamental encoding problem for continuous numbers (R3). No alternative encoding strategies or theoretical framework are provided (R3). The linear transformation used to map LLM outputs to actual stock prices suggests the LLM is only a feature extractor, not that it understands numerical dynamics (R3). R1 notes the absence of a control experiment isolating the effect of multiple agents vs. a single model.
- The title is misleading: the paper only tests stock price estimation, not “value” in a general sense (e.g., algebraic problems) (R4). The experiments only show LLMs possess basic financial knowledge and can make context-based judgments, not that they understand values (R4). R1 adds that the method does not address limitations in precise numerical reasoning (e.g., handling small differences).

**Flawed experimental design and evaluation**
- Experiments use only 10 stocks over two years, insufficient to substantiate claims (R1). Key financial LLMs like FinMA and more diverse general-domain models are not included (R1). Evaluation relies primarily on Information Coefficient (IC); MSE is not consistently better than all baselines (R1, R2). Missing standard financial metrics: Sharpe Ratio, Max Drawdown, Annualized Return (R2).
- The choice of price prompt range [-1,1] and the concept of “LLM value space” are not justified and lack theoretical basis (R3). R3 also notes the paper fails to explain extreme abnormal predictions (e.g., 5000% price changes in Figure 6) that are unrealistic for the selected stocks, raising robustness concerns.
- LLM outputs are heavily influenced by provided context information; changing the news summary source or tone could alter results, making conclusions about understanding presumptuous (R4).

**Missing comparisons and reproducibility**
- There is no direct comparison between the proposed efficient simulation (using token distributions) and a regular multi-agent simulation (directly simulating many agents); it is unclear if the approximation alters behavior (R3). R1 also notes the lack of control for multiple agents.
- The paper lacks transparency on exact inputs, outputs, and inference parameters, hindering reproducibility (R1). Specifics on prompt structure, price variation values, and inference settings are missing (R1).

**Inadequate related work**
- The Background section is unclear and the “Agent-Based Simulation for LLMs” subsection appears unrelated (R2). Several recent works on LLM agents for stock trading (FinAgent, FinMem, StockAgent, FinCon) are not cited (R2).
