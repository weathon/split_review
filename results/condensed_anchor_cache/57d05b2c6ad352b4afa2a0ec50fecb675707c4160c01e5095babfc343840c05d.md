## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) that identifies population risk and domain drift as major factors. To address these, it proposes an iterative optimization combining top‑k importance sampling to purify pseudo‑labels and nearest neighbor voting for semantic domain alignment. The method achieves competitive or state‑of‑the‑art results on Office‑Home and DomainNet, but not on VisDA‑C. The approach is easy to follow but is considered relatively complex, and several reviewers noted that its key techniques (reliable set selection, neighbor aggregation) are not novel. There are also concerns about the assumption that pseudo‑labels can be treated as ground truth and about the limited scope of ablation studies.

### Strengths
- Provides a comprehensible theoretical analysis of SFDA that derives a risk bound and motivates the proposed method (R1, R2, R3).
- The two strategies (top‑k importance sampling and nearest neighbor voting) directly address the identified risk bound terms (R2, R3).
- Achieves competitive or improved performance on Office‑Home and DomainNet, with extensive comparisons on Office‑Home and VisDA‑C (R1, R2, R4).
- Includes an ablation study and hyperparameter analysis that demonstrate the impact of different components (R2).
- Well‑written, well‑organized, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns** – Several reviewers found the key ideas not sufficiently novel. Selecting reliable samples for training is a widely used paradigm [R1 cites Ming et al. (POEM), Yang et al. (AUTO), Xu et al. (importance sampling), Tranheden et al. (DACS); R4 lists Source Data‑absent UDA through Hypothesis Transfer, ProxyMix, Divide to Adapt]. The neighbor aggregation strategy is similar to prior work (e.g., Intrinsic Neighborhood Structure, SHOT, nearest neighborhood clustering, Attracting and Dispersing) [R4]. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1 and proof) closely resembles that of "Learning Bounds for Domain Adaptation" [R4]. Sampling strategies and curriculum learning are also not new (R4). Reviewers suggest adding these references to related work (R1) and clearly illustrating novelty (R4).
- **Insufficient and limited ablation studies** – The main ablation experiments only use one source‑target pair (Office‑Home A→C) (R1, R3). The ablation should cover diverse tasks with different source models (R2, R3). There is no ablation isolating C‑sampling and T‑sampling to prove the effectiveness of I‑sampling (R3). Hyperparameter analyses (number of rounds, ratio of reliable data, top‑k for neighbors) are not shown for other dataset combinations (R1, R2). It would also be useful to count the number of samples sampled and analyze common samples across rounds (R1).
- **Assumptions and theoretical issues** – One reviewer (R3, rating 3) strongly questioned the assumption that pseudo‑labels can be treated as ground truth, which is unreasonable for SFDA, and doubted the correctness of Eq. 2 because D_tl and D_tu may not both be i.i.d. from the target domain (R3). The interaction in Eq. 7 (intersection of C‑ and T‑sampling) degrades to single sampling when one set is empty – needs clarification (R3). A sensitivity analysis of the trade‑off parameter ρ in Eq. 13 is missing (R3). Figure 3 (b)(c) shows pseudo‑label accuracy decreasing from 90% during adaptation, which is hard to understand (R3). The method is considered too complicated and does not properly correspond to the theoretical analysis (R3).
- **Missing comparisons and experiments** – The selection strategy should be compared with other well‑known picking strategies (e.g., minimum entropy, nearest distance to classifier weights, max probability) (R4). A comparison of adaptation time with competitors is missing (R2). DomainNet has only a few evaluated approaches (R2). Results on VisDA‑C should be placed more prominently in the experimental section (R3). A typo exists in Table 3 caption ("A->R") (R3).
