## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SpecRaGE, a fusion-based multi-view spectral representation learning framework that integrates graph Laplacian methods with deep learning. It uses neural networks to approximate the joint diagonalization of graph Laplacians, achieving generalization and scalability. A meta-learning module dynamically weights views to handle noise and outliers. Experiments on benchmarks show strong performance, especially on contaminated data.

### Strengths
- The integration of graph Laplacian methods with deep learning for multi-view representation learning is a creative and novel fusion-based approach (R1, R2, R3, R4). The framework effectively combines advantages of SpectralNet (Shaham et al., 2018) and approximate joint diagonalization of Laplacians (Eynard et al., 2012; 2015) (R1).
- The approximate joint diagonalization of Laplacians is addressed mathematically, opening the door to graph embedding from single view to multiple views (R1).
- A meta-learning model (MLP) is developed to weight single-view Laplacians, enabling dynamic fusion for robustness (R1, R2).
- The method solves generalization to new data and scalability to large datasets via a parametric neural mapping and mini-batch training (R1, R2).
- Dynamic fusion technology enhances robustness against outliers and noise in contaminated multi-view data (R2). Experiments on two noise/outlier cases (manually added) demonstrate effectiveness (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- The paper is well organized, easy to follow, with clear problem statement and thorough experimental validation (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). Technical details are well described and analyses insightful (R1).
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard multi-view benchmarks, significantly outperforming existing methods when dealing with outliers and incomplete views (R2). Comparative experiments indicate validity of the proposed framework (R1).
- Experiments demonstrate effectiveness of SpecRaGE in the face of noisy and outlier data (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited innovation (R1, R4, R5; partly contradicted by R2 who praises novelty, and R3 who calls it creative):** The framework is primarily a combination of existing techniques – SpectralNet for single-view out-of-sample embedding and approximate joint diagonalization for multi-view fusion (R1, R4). Similar frameworks have been proposed, e.g., MvSCN (Huang et al., 2019) (R5). All proposed components are not new (R5). Reviewer 2 explicitly praises novelty, but reviewers 1, 4, 5 view the contribution as incremental.
- **Generalization and scalability claimed as contributions but already standard (R4):** Generalization via neural mapping for new samples is common to most deep clustering methods (R4). Scalability through stochastic mini-batch training was introduced in SpectralNet (ICLR 2018) (R4). The paper’s contribution is not clearly differentiated from these established capabilities.
- **Gap between mini-batch embeddings and global embeddings (R1):** The orthogonalization on small mini-batch Laplacian matrices is algebraically not equivalent to orthogonalization on the full (global) matrix. This gap may enlarge in multi-view settings, and the learned embedding could deviate from that obtained by traditional manifold learning. An illustrative example (e.g., teapot dataset with circle embedding) is needed to validate (R1).
- **Approximate joint diagonalization quality (R3):** When Laplacians are non-commutative, cumulative errors may affect feature accuracy. The author should discuss this (R3).
- **Meta-learning fusion module is insufficiently justified and not novel (R1, R4, R5; echoed by R2, R3):** The weight vector w is generated by an MLP from concatenated \(\hat{x}_i\), but the implementation is unclear and lacks detailed explanation (R4). The approach of learning view weights is widely used, and the specific contribution in robust meta-learning fusion is not convincingly demonstrated (R4, R5). The generation of weights is not explained, especially when data quality differences are large; reliability and avoidance of misleading weight allocation need discussion (R3). The definition of weight vector w is missing in the meta-learning section (R3).
- **Robustness approach is weighting, not elimination (R4):** The method assigns weights to low-quality views rather than removing noise. For example, a partially occluded face loses important noise-free features because the whole view gets down-weighted, reducing but not eliminating noise impact. A theoretical explanation for the robustness claim is needed (R4).
- **Robustness experiments only on synthetic corruptions (R1, R2):** Noise/outliers are manually added by algorithm. Real-world contaminated data or multi-modal datasets (e.g., image+text) should be tested to validate effectiveness in practice (R1, R2).
- **Poor baseline selection (R2, R3):** The comparison includes methods from 2013 and 2015, which are outdated. ICLR submissions should compare with more recent algorithms such as APADC, CPSPAN (R3). The chosen baselines lack representative significance (R2).
- **Lack of experimental analysis (R2, R3, R5):** The paper simply presents results without sufficient analysis. For example, performance on InfiniteMNIST (1M scale) is similar to BDGP (2k scale) without explanation (R2). The significance of Gaussian noise value 1.2 (Line 433) is not justified (R2). Ablation experiments are insufficient: it is not shown whether the meta-model weighting is needed, or whether view fusion is necessary (R3, R5). No ablation to verify effectiveness of each module (R5).
- **Performance improvement over SOTA is not significant (R1):** SpecRaGE does not show clear superiority (e.g., over AECoKM and MetaViewer). Advantages in training/inference time, model complexity, or computation complexity should be discussed (R1).
- **Framework is not end-to-end; tricks affect joint embedding quality (R1).** The more important task is joint embedding, not just downstream clustering/classification. Comments or future work needed (R1).
- **Notation and variable choices are non-standard (R3):** Using \(\mathcal{X}\) for eigenmatrix, \(\hat{x}\) for prediction? \(Y\) for view-specific representations, lowercase v for total number of views, lowercase q for index, etc. These are unusual and reduce clarity (R3).
- **Readability of Figure 1 (R2):** Newly introduced input symbols are unexplained, reducing figure readability (R2).
- **Missing visualization of joint diagonalization (R4):** A diagram showing the diagonalization structure would help readers intuitively grasp the concept (R4).
- **Experimental data not from real contaminated scenarios (R2):** The synthetic corruption limits generalizability to real noisy scenarios, weakening effectiveness claims (R2).
