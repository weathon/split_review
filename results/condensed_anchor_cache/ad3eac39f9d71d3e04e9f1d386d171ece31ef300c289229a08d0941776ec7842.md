## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SFi-Former, a graph transformer (GT) that uses sparse flow‑induced attention (SFi‑attention) to capture long‑range dependencies. SFi‑attention is derived by minimizing an energy function based on network flows with ℓ1‑norm regularization. The model aggregates features selectively via sparse attention and is evaluated on GNN Benchmark and Long‑Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB) datasets.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical perspective:** The paper interprets attention through circuit theory and energy‑based optimization, offering a fresh viewpoint for GTs (R2, R3, R4). The theoretical foundation and guarantee are considered strong (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Relevance of the problem:** The work addresses the important challenge of capturing long‑range interactions in graph data, which is crucial for many applications (R1).
- **Empirical performance on long‑range tasks:** Experiments on LRGB datasets show competitive or SOTA results (R1, R4). The model also exhibits smaller generalization gaps, suggesting reduced overfitting (R1, abstract).
- **Extensive experiments:** The method is tested on diverse benchmarks (GNN Benchmark, LRGB) (R1, R5).
- **Disagreement in overall assessment:** While some reviewers (R1, R2, R4) rated the paper higher (score 5) and praised its originality and contribution (R4 gave contribution 4), others (R3, R5) rated it lower (score 3) and questioned the experimental validity (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficiently justified motivation:** The abstract claims the method alleviates weak inductive bias, overfitting, and over‑globalizing, but there is little follow‑up experimentation to demonstrate these effects. Moreover, the observed generalization gap remains 20‑30%, which the authors do not fully explain (R2, R3).
2. **Lack of sparsity verification:** The authors assert that SFi‑attention learns sparser scores, yet no experiments (statistical analysis or case studies) are provided to verify this claim. The role of ℓ1‑norm in inducing sparsity is not well‑explained; sparsity appears to rely on a hard threshold (R2, R3).
3. **Poor clarity in key sections:**  
   - Section 3.2 (introduction of friction) is confusing and the “Soft” function design is unclear (R2, R3).  
   - Section 3.3 does not clearly explain the roles of Z, R(X), F(X), and how Z*(R(X),F(X)) is computed in the attention mechanism (R2, R3).  
   - Overall mathematical exposition is dense and hard to follow (R4, R5). Resistor definition is ambiguous: are resistors defined only on edges or between all node pairs? (R2)  
   - Typos: in line 147‑148, the query and key should be swapped (R2, R3).
4. **Missing comparisons and ablations:**  
   - No ablation excluding the friction parameter to clarify its specific role (R1).  
   - No comparison with simple sparsity regularization techniques (e.g., L1, L2, or entropy regularization) applied to attention coefficients in existing GTs (R1).  
   - No experiment that replaces the original attention in an existing GT (e.g., GraphGPS) with SFi‑attention to isolate its effect (R5).  
   - Ablation study is conducted on only three datasets; components show significant gains on some datasets but little on others, without analysis (R3, R5).  
   - The loss function in Equation (9): is it computed at every attention layer? What is the total loss for the whole transformer? (R2, R3)
5. **Experimental results not fully convincing:**  
   - On the GNN Benchmark (first experiment), performance gains are minimal and within random variation (R1).  
   - In Table 2, the proposed method underperforms a baseline on most datasets; the paper does not clarify why or specify conditions where SFi‑Former is better (R2).  
   - Results are highly sensitive to hyperparameters (Tables 3, 5, 6), requiring very careful tuning of batch size, learning rate, epochs, and warmup. This makes the comparison less convincing; gains may come from parameter tuning rather than model design (R5).  
   - The combination of adjacency matrix and attention matrix in Eq. 11 is questionable: adjacency values can be much larger than attention values when a node has few neighbors, and Fig. 4 shows nodes assign larger attention to themselves than to others (R5).  
   - The SFi‑attention calculation appears unrelated to graph structure; deeper insights into modeling graph structural data are needed (R5).
6. **Related work and theoretical positioning:** The paper claims limited prior research on energy‑based models in GTs but ignores existing work like DIFFormer and SGFormer, which have similar energy‑constrained frameworks (R1). The theoretical explanation also omits the adjacency matrix and message‑passing components, while many recent studies (e.g., elastic GNNs) link energy functions to GNNs (R1).
7. **Missing analysis and outlook:** No analysis of model complexity or computational trade‑offs is provided, even though scalability is acknowledged as beyond scope (R1, R4). The conclusion is brief and does not discuss future directions, limitations, or real‑world applicability (R4).
