## Merged Review

### Summary
RePLan integrates multiple foundation model components into a system for iterative high-level robot planning and low-level reward code generation. It uses an LLM for high-level semantic planning, a VLM for perception and motion error explanation, an LLM for reward code generation, an MPC motion controller, and a verifier. Evaluated on 4 simulated manipulation tasks with up to 4 subtasks, compared against Language2Reward and ablations. Reviewers find the idea topical and clearly presented, but express major concerns about insufficient evaluation, insufficient explanation of the VLM Perceiver, unclear contribution relative to prior closed-loop feedback works, and limited real-world applicability.

### Strengths
- Overall method is interesting and could potentially work for a large variety of planning tasks (R1: score 6, most positive).
- Tasks are more complex than simple pick-and-place, illustrating more complex reasoning (R1).
- Paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2, R3).
- Motivation is topical: feedback and adaptive replanning are important for grounding foundation models (R2).
- Method requires no additional human input beyond the initial instruction compared to the baseline Language2Reward (R2).
- Accompanying videos are helpful for showcasing the method (R4).
- The idea of using a VLM to generate textual feedback for replanning is a good addition to this research direction (R4).
- Literature review is thorough (R3; note: R3 also characterizes it as a laundry list, see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation.** Only 4 tasks with considerable similarities; only 3 trials per condition; horizon length limited to at most 4 subtasks, contradicting the claimed “large-scale and long-horizon kitchen environment” (R2, R3, R4). As a result, quantitative results show high variance and are not convincing. For comparison, the Language2Reward baseline was tested on 17 tasks with 10 reward functions each, each run 50 times through MPC; RePLan without replanning achieves only 1/3 success on the easiest task vs. 3/3 for Language2Reward, suggesting noise (R4). R1, the most positive reviewer, also calls for more tasks and suggests using LLMs to generate additional long-horizon tasks to demonstrate robustness (R1).
- **VLM Perceiver insufficiently explained and potentially unreliable.** No details on which VLM is used, how prompts are constructed, how motion errors are passed to the VLM, how the verifier coordinates with the VLM, or what the VLM’s success rate is (R2). The module requires extended reasoning (e.g., “Block is in the way of opening the door”) from an end-to-end zero-shot VLM, which is challenging with current models (R2). R1 recommends testing the method by replacing the VLM with text descriptions of the scene to isolate its contribution (R1). Failure cases from the VLM in deciding task completion are not discussed (R4).
- **Reliance on simulator ground-truth for low-level control.** The MPC uses state attributes like “block_r_side” and “cabinet_handle” from the simulator, undermining the perception pipeline and raising questions about real-world applicability (R3). This creates a discrepancy: high-level planning uses VLM grounding, but low-level control uses perfect state information; a text-based planner with ground truth could bypass the VLM entirely (R3).
- **Missing experimental details.** Exact prompts for all LLMs and VLMs are not provided in the paper or appendix (R3, R4). Insufficient examples of paired LLM output and environment execution (R3).
- **Core contribution unclear.** It is not clear whether the contribution lies in incorporating LLM/VLM feedback into planning, into code generation, or in the verifier; the method should be compared/discussed against prior works that already use VLM feedback for closed-loop planning (e.g., Inner Monologue, Voyager) and replanning via code generation (R2, R4). The combination of existing components may not be novel, especially given the weak experimental results (R4).
- **No comparison to non-LLM baselines** such as task-and-motion planning or hierarchical RL (R3).
- **Motion planner module not thoroughly tested**; how it is evaluated is not addressed (R1).
- **No cap on replanning iterations** is mentioned; potential failure cases from the Perceiver in deciding task completion are not discussed (R4).
- **Citation format issues**: parentheses are often missing around author names (R3, R4).
