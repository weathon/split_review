## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces an unsupervised method for learning low-dimensional representations of multiple related time series using a monotone variational inequality (VI) framework. It assumes each sequence obeys its own autoregressive (AR) model, with the AR parameters across sequences sharing a low-rank structure. The problem is cast as convex matrix recovery via nuclear norm regularization, enabling efficient computation and provable recovery guarantees. The learned representations are evaluated on UCR time-series classification, clustering for language and genomic sequences, and synthetic recovery experiments.

### Strengths

- **Novel formulation**: Combining low-rank matrix recovery with monotone VI for sequential representation learning is novel and sound (R1, R3). The method obtains theoretical guarantees (computational complexity, parameter recovery) while maintaining convexity (R2, R4).
- **Theoretical rigor**: The framework is well-formulated, analytically justified, and includes provable recovery guarantees under the low-rank assumption (R1, R3, R4). The approach is well-rooted in relevant literature (R3).
- **Clear writing and presentation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3). The introduction, problem statement, methodology, pseudocode, and figures are clear and comprehensive (R1, R3).
- **Empirical performance**: Experiments demonstrate the method’s efficacy in parameter recovery and competitive performance on real-world data (R1, R4). The technique enables efficient computation of latent AR parameters (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing benchmark comparisons**:
    - No comparison against established representation learning methods such as contrastive learning or masked modeling (e.g., maximising mutual information between views, reconstructing masked inputs). This omission makes it difficult to assess relative strengths/weaknesses against current state-of-the-art in representation learning (R4, minority: R4 is most critical on this point).
    - Clustering experiments lack results from other models; the paper shows the proposed method’s representations cluster well, but does not include clustering results for the classifiers listed in Table 2 (R2, minority: R2 is more critical on this point).
    - Given the goal of learning representations, direct comparisons to other low-rank time-series approaches would better highlight the specific advantages of this method (R3). This is a broader issue also raised by R1 (see next point).

- **Linear vs. nonlinear model evaluation**:
    - No direct comparison of the linear and nonlinear monotone AR models (both with low-rank regularization) on real-world data. The shift between linear/nonlinear is confusing: Section 4.3 mentions nonlinear dynamics, but nonlinear appears applied in Section 4.2 as well (R3). The choice of link function $\eta$ is restrictive—it must be monotone and fixed. If $\eta$ is nearly identity, the performance gain over the linear model may be small (R1).
    - Model recovery experiments are only for the linear case. The paper should clarify why (e.g., testing nuclear-ball regularization in a simpler setting) and ideally add parameter recovery for the nonlinear case (R3).
    - The added cost of the nonlinear model (evaluation of $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{A}^*$) may not be justified if the nonlinearity is minimal (R1).

- **Role and tuning of hyperparameters**:
    - The role of the low-rank constraint (via $\lambda$) is under-analyzed: how much does performance improve over the unconstrained case? Can the optimal $\lambda$ reveal dataset complexity? (R3)
    - Is increasing the embedding dimension $d$ the only way to increase model complexity when the link function is too simple for low-constant $C$ and complicated dynamics? Does increasing $d$ always improve recovery effectively? If the link function is inadequate, rank($\mathbf{B}$) may not reduce sufficiently—how should one choose or design $\eta$? (R1)
    - The effect of $\lambda$ on reconstruction error (Fig. 1a) shows a quadratic relation—likely due to underfitting/overfitting (R1). The time to search for $\lambda$ and its scaling with problem dimensionality are not reported (R1). Tuning $\lambda$ is discussed, but other algorithmic aspects (e.g., choice of proximal stepsize) are not (R3).

- **Clarity and exposition**:
    - The method section is mathematically rigorous but hard to follow, especially for readers unfamiliar with the cited literature. It is unclear which parts are novel; a summary of contributions at the end of the introduction or method section would help (R3).
    - Notation is inconsistent: equations (6–7) and (14) use different indexing, creating confusion about the scope of the optimization problem (R4). The paper should clarify the relationship between variables and indices (R4). Using $\mathcal{H}_{i,t}$ explicitly for the history of sequence $i$ up to time $t$ would improve clarity (R4).
    - When introducing the monotone property of the link function (Line 122, 132), the paper should either briefly explain why it is important (referencing Sec. 3.2) or cite the definition. A formal Definition block for monotonicity (from Lines 262–269) would help (R1).
    - The term “geometry” (e.g., “geometry of the subspace”) is awkward because the subspaces studied are linear (flat planes). The paper should state explicitly that all subspaces are linear (Line 136) and either avoid “geometry” or define what it means (R1). Similarly, R3 found the shift between linear and nonlinear models confusing on first read.
    - Pseudocode is given, but the speed difference between recurrence (8) and (15) is not reported (R1).

- **Limitations and future directions**:
    - The paper lacks any discussion of limitations or potential challenges for practitioners (R1, R3). Tuning $\lambda$ is described, but other practical issues (e.g., handling higher-dimensional sequences, scalability, choice of link function) are not addressed (R3).
    - Several open questions are noted: (a) Must the link function be continuous? (b) How widely applicable is the assumption that sequences share a common domain (Line 114)? (c) Can the model separate important low-SNR signal components from noise in a denoising setting? (d) What is the origin of the term “link” function? (R1)

- **Minor presentation issues**:
    - Typos: duplicate “indicates” near page break (pp. 1–2); “thewfirst” (Line 106); “spacewof” (Line 285); missing “by” (Line 115) (R1, R2, R3, R4).
    - Incorrect citation format: use \citep and \citet (e.g., Line 089) (R1).
    - Clarify special notation: what does “vec()” mean? (R1)
    - Table 2: bold the best average in each column (R1).
