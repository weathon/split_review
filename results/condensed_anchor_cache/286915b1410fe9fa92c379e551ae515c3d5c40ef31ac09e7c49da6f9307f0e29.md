## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper re-examines the Knowledge Localization (KL) assumption, which holds that a fact can be localized to specific knowledge neurons (KNs) in LLMs. Through statistical and modification-based analyses, the authors identify facts that do not adhere to this assumption (inconsistent knowledge). They propose the Query Localization (QL) assumption, comprising (1) Query-KN Mapping and (2) Dynamic KN Selection, where the attention module plays a role in selecting KNs for answering a query. Based on QL, they introduce a Consistency-Aware KN modification method for model editing. The claims are supported by 39 experimental setups across multiple architectures and additional visualizations.

### Strengths
- **Originality and novelty**: The paper critically challenges the widely accepted KL assumption by identifying its limitations in both knowledge storage and expression. The introduction of the QL assumption—with Query-KN Mapping and Dynamic KN Selection—offers a creative rethinking that for the first time integrates the attention mechanism into this context (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Extensive empirical validation**: 39 experimental setups are conducted across multiple LLM architectures (e.g., GPT-2, others) to evaluate the prevalence of inconsistent knowledge and validate the QL assumption. This thorough evaluation solidifies the credibility of the findings (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Well-structured and clear**: The paper is logically and structurally clear, with well-reasoned problem descriptions, metric designs, and detailed explanations (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Significant implications**: The work has substantial implications for LLM interpretability, model editing, and knowledge management, offering a valuable foundation for future advances (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Grounded method**: The Consistency-Aware KN modification method is empirically grounded and validated by statistically significant improvements in model editing performance (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and practical significance (minority concern)**: One reviewer (Reviewer 1, score 6) found the motivation for challenging the KL assumption insufficiently elaborated. The paper does not clearly explain why the existing assumption is inadequate for current knowledge management tasks beyond the specific model editing task, and how the QL assumption leads to tangible improvements in downstream applications. The connection to real-world applications needs to be more explicit.
- **Performance improvement minimal (minority concern)**: The same reviewer noted that the performance gap between the proposed Consistency-Aware method and the two baselines appears minimal, especially for consistent facts. They requested statistical significance tests, effect sizes, and exploration of scenarios where the method yields more significant gains. Other reviewers did not raise this issue.
- **Limited diversity of relations**: The experiments use a limited number of relation types, constraining generalizability. The paper should discuss the characteristics of the relations used and include experiments on a more diverse set, including complex or differently-propertied relations (Reviewer 1).
- **Threshold sensitivity**: The identification of consistent vs. inconsistent knowledge relies on Otsu’s threshold. A deeper analysis of how the choice of threshold impacts the absolute quantity of identified inconsistent knowledge and whether the core findings are stable across a range of reasonable threshold values is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational cost and scalability**: The QL assumption introduces additional involvement of the attention module, potentially increasing computational load. The paper does not compare computational costs (e.g., inference time, memory usage) between KL-based and QL-based methods, nor does it discuss scalability for large-scale or sequential edits (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing implementation details**: The prompts/instructions used to obtain results for Figure 1 are not specified (Reviewer 2).
- **Interaction of multiple related knowledge**: The paper does not analyze whether multiple consistent facts activate their respective knowledge neurons simultaneously or additively when a query involves multiple related facts (Reviewer 2).
- **Effect of Consistency-Aware method on model behavior**: A case study or interpretability analysis on how the Consistency-Aware KN modification affects model behavior beyond metrics is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Interpretability of Dynamic KN Selection**: Further details, such as visualization or interpretability analysis, on how the Dynamic KN Selection process works are lacking (Reviewer 3).
- **GPT-2 lower KII ratio**: The paper does not explain why GPT-2 has a lower KII (inconsistent knowledge) ratio compared to other models; a potential factor is its smaller parameter count (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete analysis of experimental metrics**: Table 2 presents metrics like Reliability, Generalization, and Locality, but not all are analyzed in the paper (Reviewer 4).
- **Paragraph transitions and omitted results**: Some paragraphs lack smooth transitions, and certain experimental results are omitted in the analysis (Reviewer 4).
