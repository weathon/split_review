## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes f-DIME, a family of mutual information estimators based on the variational representation of f-divergence and density ratio learning. The key contributions are: using f-divergence for MI estimation, theoretical variance analysis showing lower variance, and a derangement shuffling strategy to avoid fixed points in permutation (as opposed to random permutation). The paper claims improved bias/variance trade-off and lower complexity compared to existing neural MI estimators. Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty of the f-divergence framework and the derangement idea, and appreciate the theoretical analysis. However, they raise strong concerns about insufficient experimental validation (limited to simple Gaussian data), missing baselines (e.g., DIM, FLO, RenyiCL), lack of proof for the claim that derangement enables unbounded estimation, presentation issues in the theoretical sections, and missing analyses of bias and the effect of the f-divergence choice.

### Strengths
- The use of f-divergence for mutual information estimation is novel and provides a general framework that connects many existing MI estimators.
- The paper provides theoretical variance analysis showing that the proposed estimator has lower variance than previous approaches.
- The derangement strategy for sampling from the product of marginals is original, well-motivated, and addresses a practical issue that was often overlooked.
- The two-stage estimation procedure avoids partition function estimation, which contributes to variance reduction.
- The computational complexity analysis (Section 6) as a function of batch size and critic architecture is meaningful.
- The theoretical analysis is solid and supports the main claims.

### Weaknesses
1. **Claim of best bias/variance trade-off not supported by experiments.** In Figs. 1 & 2, some DIME variants (HD-DIME, KL-DIME) show large bias, there is no consistent winner across f-divergences, and NJEE/SMILE often perform comparably or better. No theoretical or empirical analysis of bias is provided; when true MI is small, the bias behavior is not explained (R1, R4).  
2. **Claim of unbounded MI estimation via derangement lacks proof and contradicts known limits.** The paper only shows that the permutation bound is bounded by \(\log N\) but does not formally prove that derangement overcomes this. This contradicts the fundamental limitation of McAllester & Stratos (2020) that any distribution-free high-confidence lower bound on MI from \(N\) samples cannot exceed \(\mathcal{O}(\log N)\). **One reviewer (R2) was particularly critical of this discrepancy** and noted no supporting theory or experiments.  
3. **Ambiguous presentation in the theoretical sections (4 & 5).** Assumptions, notations, and proof sketches are introduced abruptly; results are not organized as theorems/propositions, harming readability. Important theoretical aspects (e.g., Lemma 3) are placed in the appendix. **One reviewer (R2) found this section very difficult to follow** and recommended restructuring, while another (R3) considered the theory solid—this contrast shows presentation is polarizing.  
4. **Experimental evaluation is limited to simple unimodal Gaussian data.** No experiments on benchmark image datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, ImageNet) or in representation learning tasks, which is a standard application of MI estimators. This weakens the claims of higher accuracy and robustness (R3, R5).  
5. **Missing comparisons to closely related methods.** Comparisons to DIM (JSD-based), FLO (Fenchel dual to avoid partition function), and RenyiCL (skewed Renyi divergence) are absent. The differences with SMILE, GAN‑DIME, and JS are not clearly explained, and performance appears hardly distinguishable in the provided experiments (R3, R5).  
6. **Novelty is limited.** The paper builds on well‑established f‑divergence theory; the derangement idea is novel but similar shuffling practices have been used in implementations before (R3).  
7. **Relation to previous estimators is unclear.** It is not stated which existing estimators (MINE, NWJ, etc.) are special cases of f-DIME for specific \(f\) choices; a clearer connection is needed (R4).  
8. **Effectiveness of derangement not ablated.** All experiments use derangement; a comparison without it is necessary to demonstrate its benefit (R4).  
9. **Several inaccurate or misleading statements.** (a) “Monte Carlo sampling renders MINE biased” is incorrect—the bias comes from Jensen’s inequality applied to \(\log\); (b) “NWJ estimator is unbiased” is misleading—both MINE and NWJ can be tight given a sufficiently expressive critic. “NWJ provides a formal lower bound, while MINE provides an estimate that can be either an upper or lower bound depending on the critic” would be more accurate (R5).  
10. **Notation issues.** The permutation function is denoted \(\sigma(y)\), which is non‑standard; \(\pi\) is more common and clearer (R5).  
11. **Missing baseline: FLO.** FLO also applies Fenchel duality to reduce variance and should be compared for a fair evaluation of the two‑stage estimation procedure (R5).  
12. **Several statements are hard to follow.** E.g., “the partition function estimation \(\mathbb{E}_{p_X^N p_Y^N}[\hat{R}]\) represents the major issue”, the definition of derangement, and the notion of “fixed points” after permutation (R5).
