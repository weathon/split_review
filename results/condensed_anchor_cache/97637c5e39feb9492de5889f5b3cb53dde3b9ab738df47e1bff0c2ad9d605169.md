## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviews provide scores 5, 8, 6, 8 (average 6.75). Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 5) is the most critical, questioning novelty, missing discussion of related works, and insufficient explanation of key equations. Reviewers 2 and 4 (scores 8, confidence 3 and 4) are strongly positive, praising the experimental evaluation and technical soundness. Reviewer 3 (score 6, confidence 3) acknowledges methodological innovation but raises concerns about the rigor and necessity of some theoretical proofs. All reviewers agree the paper has clear motivation and theoretical analysis, and that experiments demonstrate effectiveness. Weaknesses focus on incomplete derivations, missing ablations, limited novelty compared to supervised methods, insufficient comparison with multi-view SSL methods, lack of analysis for key parameters, and the need for additional experiments (semi-supervised learning, noisy labels). There is disagreement on writing quality and novelty: Reviewer 4 finds the paper well-written; Reviewer 2 says writing needs improvement. Reviewer 1 considers the contribution incremental; Reviewers 2, 3, 4 find it innovative.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation: The paper provides a deep analysis of the challenges when applying Orthogonal Low-rank Embedding (OLE) to SSL (representational collapse and inability to distinguish positive/negative correlations). All reviewers note the problem is well-discussed.
- Detailed theoretical analysis: Multiple reviewers (1, 3, 4) highlight that the paper supports its approach with rigorous theory. Reviewer 3 specifically mentions the low-rank bias matrix and decoupled constraint mechanism as methodologically novel, addressing representation collapse in unsupervised settings.
- Comprehensive experimentation: Reviewers 1, 2, 4 confirm that sufficient experiments are performed, and results demonstrate the method’s effectiveness. Reviewer 2 commends the evaluation of adaptability and robustness through transfer learning to linear classification tasks, showing superior performance.
- Broad applicability and generalization: Reviewer 3 notes that SSOLE shows good generalization ability across datasets, adapting to different image classification tasks and enhancing feature representation robustness.
- Detailed method description: Reviewer 2 states the method is described in detail, enhancing reproducibility.

### Weaknesses
- Limited novelty and missing related work: Reviewer 1 (score 5) argues that employing low/high-rank enforcement to adjust distances between contrastive sample pairs has been proposed in supervised learning (e.g., LDA), making the contribution incremental. This reviewer also notes that some related works are not discussed. In contrast, Reviewers 2, 3, 4 treat the adaptation to SSL as innovative. (Preserve disagreement.)
- Need for additional explanation and missing derivations:
  - **Eq. (2) decoupling**: Reviewer 1 requests more explanation of how Eq. (2) achieves decoupling of low-rank and high-rank enforcement.
  - **Relationship between limitations, challenges, and method**: Reviewer 1 asks for a clearer discussion on how the three limitations, two challenges, and the proposed method connect.
  - **Probability bound proof**: Reviewer 3 states the derivation for why the probability of \(|\cos(\theta_{ij})| > 1/d\) is larger than \(1/d\) is missing; the current use of Chebyshev inequality is not clearly shown, including variance calculation of cosine similarity.
  - **Row orthogonality and nuclear norm**: Reviewer 3 notes the claim that “Since the rows of \(\tilde{V}\) are nearly orthogonal, the nuclear norm is dominated by the sum of the row norms” needs a more rigorous argument; perturbation bounds on singular values should be provided.
  - **Use of \(\approx\)**: Reviewer 3 points out that the symbol \(\approx\) is used without definition (high-probability vs. numerical approximation), requiring explicit error bounds or concentration inequalities.
  - **Appendix A.4 product of diagonal matrix**: Reviewer 2 asks for a more detailed explanation of why all diagonal elements of \(PP^T\) are either 1 or -1, given that \(P\) is a diagonal matrix.
- Unnecessary proof: Reviewer 3 considers Theorem 3.3 (unitary invariance of nuclear norm) common knowledge and unnecessary without a clear explanation of its specific relevance to the core contribution.
- Missing experiments and ablation studies:
  - **Semi-supervised learning**: Reviewer 2 finds the semi-supervised experiments limited (few datasets, results not striking) and suggests additional datasets to be more convincing.
  - **Adaptability to noisy labels**: Reviewer 2 questions whether the method works with weakly supervised datasets containing noisy labels; this is a missing experiment.
  - **Multi-view comparison**: Reviewer 1 notes that the paper discusses multi-view learning throughout (including experiments) but does not compare with other multi-view SSL methods; the title does not mention multi-view learning, which is inconsistent.
  - **Ablation on deviation matrix**: Reviewer 4 suggests an ablation study to investigate the effectiveness of enforcing intra-class low-rank via the deviation matrix instead of the original feature matrix.
- Parameter analysis: Reviewer 4 recommends analyzing the influence of \(\lambda\), which controls the balance between intra-class compactness and inter-class separability.
- Claim lacking verification: Reviewer 1 states the paper’s claim of achieving competitive performance without large batch sizes, memory banks, or dual-encoder architectures lacks detailed verification or discussion.
- Writing quality: Reviewer 2 (score 8) states the writing needs improvement, while Reviewer 4 (score 8) finds it well-written with clear motivations. (Preserve disagreement.)
