## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers rated the paper 8, 8, 5, 5. Reviewers 1 and 2 are highly positive, praising the novel universal computation model, tight lower bounds, and coverage of both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings. Reviewers 3 and 4 (scores 5) are more critical, with Reviewer 3 arguing insufficient delta over prior work [1] (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2024) and Reviewer 4 questioning practical applicability and missing discussion of communication bottlenecks. All reviewers acknowledge the theoretical thoroughness but raise concerns about presentation clarity, missing experiments, and the model’s realism.

### Strengths

- **Closes significant gaps** by providing tight lower bounds that match convergence rates for many parallel optimization problems (periodic delay patterns, random device outages, etc.) and explicitly connecting to existing upper bounds (R1).
- **Universal computation model** is a realistic extension of previous work, capturing unstable, time-varying, and arbitrary worker speeds; it formalizes real-world scenarios like hardware/network delays and fluctuating computation powers (R1, R2, R4).
- **Exhaustive coverage** of both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings (including federated learning applications) and both convex and non-convex results (R1).
- **Matching lower and upper bounds** are achieved by the optimal Rennala SGD and Malenia SGD methods; previously developed algorithms are shown to remain optimal in this new setting (R2).
- **Clear writing and rigorous definitions**; despite notational heaviness, the paper provides intuitive explanations (R1, R3).
- **Concise introduction** that immediately conveys what results to expect (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient delta from prior work [1] (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2024).** Theorems 6.4 and A.2 of [1] correspond exactly to Examples 5.4 and 6.5; the added generality leads to few new examples (only Example 6.5 for the heterogeneous setting) and the proof techniques (e.g., Theorem 5.1 up to equation (25)) are largely reproduced from [1] with no clear explanation of the new technical difficulties (R3). This makes the contribution appear incremental—more suitable for a journal than a conference (R3). The authors should justify the value added over [1] and incorporate that discussion in the final version (R3).
- **Lack of numerical results or experiments** (R2, R3). Empirical validation would strengthen the paper’s claims.
- **Communication aspects are not addressed.** The model assumes instantaneous broadcast of a computed gradient to all workers, ignoring bandwidth limitations, straggler effects, and possible communication failures (R2, R4). It is unclear whether the universal computation model can capture network congestion, packet loss, or the fact that transmitting a gradient may take longer than computing it (R4, R2).
- **Model limitations insufficiently discussed.** For example, the model assumes computation speeds are independent of data being processed, which may not hold in practice; it is also unclear whether the model can capture stragglers (slow workers that delay overall computation) (R4).
- **Heterogeneous lower bound may be too pessimistic.** The heterogeneity across workers (division of data blocks \(h_j\)’s) appears adversarial; the lower bound (19) cannot recover the homogeneous bound (12), and it is unclear what practical settings produce such heterogeneity. Restricting the adversary to not allow arbitrary data block divisions would lower the bound (R1). The authors should discuss if techniques from the heterogeneous setting can recover homogeneous results under notions of data heterogeneity (e.g., second-order heterogeneity) (R1).
- **Proof sketch (Section 7) is not helpful.** The worst-case function construction is not spelled out (a highly nontrivial step). Line 486 likely means “first coordinate,” and the random variables \(\eta_k\) should be defined earlier. Section 7.2 is unclear, especially the jump between lines 517 and 522 (R3).
- **Presentation organization** could be improved. Examples should be moved forward to immediately follow the long, hard-to-digest statements of Theorem 5.1 and 6.1. A plot for a specific instance (e.g., fixed computation model) would help parse implications (R3). Equations (12), (13), (15) appear redundant because the minimum is always at \(m=1\) (R3).
- **Theoretical results may be too complex for practitioners.** Tight lower bounds are primarily of theoretical interest; the paper lacks actionable guidelines, concrete examples, or case studies showing how the complexities translate into practical method/parameter choices (R4).
- **Missing comparisons and clarifications.** The paper should compare its lower bound proof techniques with those of [3] (arXiv:2305.12387) and highlight which delayed-feedback settings are not captured by that work (R1). A comparison with the graph-oracle framework [4] (arXiv:1805.10222) would help clarify what the proposed model captures that graph oracles cannot (R1). It would also be useful to connect the results to partial participation in federated learning (refs [1],[2]) (R1) and to explain the difference between Theorem 6.1 and 6.2 (randomized vs non-randomized functions) (R1).
