## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces backdoor attacks on semantic segmentation models, formulating the Influencer Backdoor Attack (IBA) that misclassifies all pixels of a victim class when a trigger appears on non-victim pixels. Two improvements are proposed: Nearest-Neighbor Injection (NNI), leveraging the contextual aggregation ability of segmentation models, and Pixel Random Labeling (PRL) for scenarios where the trigger cannot be placed near victim pixels. Experiments on multiple architectures and datasets (e.g., VOC) achieve up to 95% attack success rate after poisoning only 10% of the training set, while preserving clean performance. The attacks are also tested in real-world scenes.

### Strengths
- Backdoor attacks for semantic segmentation are an interesting and underexplored threat model; the paper fills this gap (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The attack scenario has practical relevance, especially for self-driving systems where semantic segmentation is widely used (Reviewer 3, 4).
- The proposed methods (IBA, NNI, PRL) are not trivial extensions of classification attacks; they are designed based on the mechanism of semantic segmentation (Reviewer 1, 4).
- Experimental results are striking: 95% ASR after poisoning 10% of the VOC training set (Reviewer 2); effectiveness is shown on several architectures and datasets, including real-world scenes (Reviewer 1).
- PRL improves the poisoning rate necessary to achieve high success rate (Reviewer 1).
- The paper provides extensive ablation studies on attack parameters to support design choices (Reviewer 1).
- The related work is presented exhaustively, providing historical background (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Claim of being first:** The authors claim to be the first backdoor attack on segmentation, but existing work [1], [2] should be differentiated and compared; the novelty claim is contested (Reviewer 3).  
- **Trigger placement constraint:** The default constraint that triggers must overlap with pixels of a single class is overly restrictive and may not reflect real-world scenarios where triggers could span multiple semantic classes. This limits generalizability and practical relevance (Reviewer 1).  
- **Real-world experiments insufficient:**  
  - One reviewer states the paper did not provide real-world experiments; the trigger may be affected by lighting, viewing direction, and distance (Reviewer 2).  
  - Another reviewer requests physical experiments: stamp the trigger patch, take video with a camera, and feed it to the attacked model (Reviewer 4).  
  - *Contrast:* Other reviewers note that real-world applicability is demonstrated, but the level of detail is considered insufficient (Reviewer 1 suggests discussing real-world results more in the main paper with more images).  
- **Trigger visibility:** The trigger used is sizable, conspicuous, and easy to detect by the naked eye. The effectiveness would be enhanced by using subtler or invisible triggers that blend into the background (Reviewer 2, 3).  
- **High poisoning rate:** The method requires a high poisoning rate to achieve high ASR (e.g., Table 2), raising concerns about practicality (Reviewer 3).  
- **Label alteration detectability:** The method modifies labels in the training set, which could be identified by pre-trained semantic segmentation models that have learned correct labeling patterns, serving as a potential defense (Reviewer 2).  
- **Fine-tuning scenario:** In the common practice of fine-tuning a large pretrained model on a small downstream dataset with few epochs, the limited exposure to poisoned data may not be sufficient to learn the backdoor dependency (Reviewer 2).  
- **Defense resistance:** The paper compares only fine-tuning-based defenses; missing experiments against other defense categories such as data cleansing, model modification, and model validation (Reviewer 3). More results on resistance to defenses, e.g., image pre-processing, are needed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Backbone evaluation:** Testing only on ResNet-50 is limited; evaluating on more recent backbones (transformers, more advanced CNNs) would provide a more comprehensive assessment of robustness (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing details in presentation:**  
  - The pipeline figure should highlight the trigger area (Reviewer 4).  
  - More justification for why only patch-based attacks are considered, with additional real-world scenario details (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing NNI+PRL main results:** The performance of the combined NNI+PRL strategy should be included in the main results, since the goal is to design a strong attack (Reviewer 4).  
- **Discussion of limitations:** The paper should discuss potential limitations of the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
