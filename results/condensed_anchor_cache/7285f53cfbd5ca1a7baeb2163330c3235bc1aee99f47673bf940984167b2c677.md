## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Cut Cross-Entropy (CCE) to reduce the memory footprint of computing the cross-entropy loss during LLM training by never materializing the full logit tensor (batch × sequence length × vocab size). The loss is decomposed into the correct-token logit (a vector dot product) and the log-sum-exp of all logits (accumulated via blockwise partial sums), both computed without storing the full logit matrix. Inspired by FlashAttention, the forward pass uses tiled matrix multiplication over the classifier head (C) and embeddings (E). In the backward pass, CCE recomputes intermediates and introduces gradient filtering (skipping elements with value < 2⁻¹² in bfloat16) and vocabulary sorting to exploit sparsity, reducing compute time with negligible approximation. Experiments on Gemma-2-2B show memory consumption for the loss computation drops from 24 GB to 1 MB and the classifier head from 28 GB to 1 GB, while matching the speed and convergence of strong baselines (e.g., torch.compile).

### Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem**: Reducing the memory footprint of cross-entropy loss in LLMs is important as vocabulary sizes, batch sizes, and sequence lengths continue to grow. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Clever algorithm**: The approach takes inspiration from FlashAttention and elegantly avoids materializing the full logit tensor. (Reviewer 1)
- **Empirical performance**: CCE reduces training memory without degrading quality/convergence or training speed relative to strong baselines (torch.compile). (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Generality**: The method generalizes beyond transformer LLMs. (Reviewer 2)
- **Convergence guarantees**: The paper demonstrates theoretical convergence guarantees compared to standard cross-entropy. (Reviewer 2)
- **Enlightening techniques**: The gradient filtering and vocabulary sorting tricks are insightful. (Reviewer 4)
- **Kernel implementation**: Custom CUDA kernels are provided, with experiments verifying reduced memory and latency. (Reviewer 4)
- **Clarity and deployability**: The solution is presented clearly (color coding for C, E, LSE) and is easy to integrate into realistic training pipelines. (Reviewer 3; note: Reviewer 2 found clarity lacking – see Weaknesses)
- **Identifies a new challenge**: The paper highlights the escalating memory cost of the cross-entropy layer due to growing vocabularies. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity of the backward pass and notation**:  
  - Several reviewers found the exposition of the backward pass and the notation confusing.  
  - Reviewer 1 asked: *What is the “v” index in Algorithm 3? Why doesn’t recomputing CᵀE cause slowdowns? Please provide a granular time breakdown. Explain the “second matmul in main memory” paragraph—does “main memory” mean HBM? There may be dimension mistakes in the backward equations (lines 266‑269); the correct shapes for d/dE and d/dC should be d/dE = C (S ⊙ LSE_grad) [D,V]×[V,L] and d/dC = E (S ⊙ LSE_grad)ᵀ [D,L]×[L,V]. Include an appendix version of Algorithm 3 with the indexed backward pass. Improve notation using V, L, D and block dimensions.*  
  - Reviewer 2 found Section 4 hard to follow without deep GPU‑kernel and LLM‑architecture knowledge; the algorithm appears monolithic and lacks a clear exposition of the key insights. They suggested decoupling theoretical reasoning from GPU implementation or starting from an efficient CE kernel and showing the modifications step by step.  
  - Reviewer 4 noted that symbols on page 4 (Cᵀ, C_{xᵢ}ᵀ, C_X , (CᵀE)_X) are confusing and recommended a symbol table in the appendix.  
  *One reviewer (Reviewer 3) found the writing clear; the other three reviewers raised clarity concerns, with Reviewer 2 being the most critical (score 6, confidence 2).*

- **Missing ablation studies**:  
  - Reviewer 2 argued that the many modifications in Sections 4.1‑4.3 lack ablation to show which components are necessary or most beneficial. They also questioned whether the improvements are GPU‑dependent (i.e., would they hold on a single‑threaded CPU?).  
  - Reviewer 4 asked how randomly initialized weights affect gradient filtering and vocabulary sorting during pretraining from scratch—an ablation that is not provided.

- **Missing experiments and analysis**:  
  - **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Reviewer 1 requested experiments exploring how CCE’s performance changes with different ratios of vocabulary size, sequence length, and hidden dimension, as well as sparsity level in the softmax.  
  - **Speed trade-offs**: Reviewer 1 asked whether there are regimes where CCE is meaningfully slower than torch.compile.  
  - **Sequence parallelism**: Reviewer 1 also requested a discussion of sequence parallelism approaches that can reduce per‑GPU logit memory.  
  - **Memory number justification**: Reviewer 3 asked for a detailed breakdown of the 89% memory‑consumption figure for Gemma 2 (N=80000), including data types and contributions from logits, activations, and other intermediates. Similarly, the numbers in Table 1 (1477 MB, 8000 MB, 4000 MB, 24000 MB) need explanation: the logits alone (8192×256000 in float32) are 8000 MB, so why does memory usage differ so much across implementations?  
  - **Gradient‑checkpointing analysis**: Reviewer 3 noted that the paper’s analysis assumes gradient checkpointing is enabled, and asked for an evaluation of CCE when checkpointing is disabled.  
  - **Vocabulary size and model family variation**: Reviewer 4 pointed out that Gemma‑2‑2B has an unusually large vocabulary (256 k). They requested experiments or discussion on models with smaller vocabularies (e.g., Llama‑3‑8B 128 k, Mistral‑7B 32 k, Phi‑3.5 32 k) and larger hidden sizes (e.g., Gemma‑2‑27B) to understand when CCE’s benefits diminish.

- **Numerical precision explanation**: Reviewer 3 asked for a detailed explanation of the bfloat16 gradient‑filtering threshold of 2⁻¹²: how the 7‑bit mantissa leads to this value and why values below it are effectively ignored in summation or normalization.

- **Theoretical justification**: Reviewer 2 questioned why the same chunking strategy used in CCE cannot be applied to standard cross‑entropy, and asked for a clearer statement of the key insights that enable CCE’s efficiency.

- **Resource dependence**: Reviewer 2 asked whether the improvements are algorithmic or rely on GPU parallelization; i.e., would CCE work in a non‑parallel (CPU) setting?
