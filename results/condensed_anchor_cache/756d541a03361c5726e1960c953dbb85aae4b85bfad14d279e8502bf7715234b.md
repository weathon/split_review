## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes UNIC, a unified framework using neighbor information for both image clustering (unsupervised) and Generalized Category Discovery (GCD, semi-supervised). It first mines positive and negative neighbors from a pre-trained DINO backbone via nearest-neighbor search, then cleans positive neighbors by filtering based on second-order neighborhood overlap. A parametric classifier is then trained with positive and negative classification losses (pulling positives together, pushing negatives apart) in an end-to-end pipeline, and for GCD labeled examples are used as ground-truth neighbors. The method reports state-of-the-art results on STL-10, ImageNet-100/200 for clustering and on ImageNet-100, CUB-200 for GCD. The main technical contributions are the neighbor mining/cleaning strategy and the unified pipeline.

### Strengths
*   **Unified framework:** Bridging clustering and GCD in a single pipeline is interesting and reasonable (Reviewers 2,4). It enhances performance across both tasks.
*   **Simple, effective neighbor cleaning:** The second-order Euclidean distance strategy to exclude noise from positive neighbors is simple and demonstrably effective (Reviewers 1,3).
*   **Substantial gains:** The method provides substantial improvements over existing baselines in both tasks (Reviewer 1: +3% clustering, +0.8%/+5% GCD). Reviewer 2 agrees results are competitive.
*   **Ablation studies:** Hyper-parameter choices (neighborhood size, loss weighting, mining steps) are properly ablated; ablations provide insights into component contributions (Reviewers 1,4).
*   **End-to-end pipeline:** Unlike multi-stage clustering approaches, UNIC trains end-to-end without inefficient self-labeling steps (Reviewer 4).
*   **Broad applicability:** The simplicity suggests easy extension to other partially supervised settings (e.g., standard semi-supervised learning) (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
*   **Limited novelty:** The core idea of mining positive/negative neighbors is already extensively explored in contrastive learning (e.g., [1] ICLR2021, [2] NeurIPS 2020) (Reviewer 4). The implementation details are not significantly innovative over many existing positive/negative mining methods for clustering and GCD (Reviewer 2). It is unclear why UNIC achieves such large improvements beyond prior works.
*   **Insufficient baselines and comparisons:**
    *   Missing direct comparison with other positive/negative mining strategies (Reviewers 2,3). Reviewer 2 asks to clarify the main difference and contribution.
    *   Missing key baselines such as DeepDPM [3] (Reviewer 4).
    *   Many included baselines are not the latest or most competitive (Reviewer 4).
    *   Missing evaluations on larger datasets like ImageNet-1k to strengthen evidence (Reviewer 4).
*   **Limited evaluation breadth (especially fine-grained and long-tail):**
    *   For GCD, common practice is to evaluate on the full SSB suite (including Stanford Cars, FGVCAircraft), but only CUB-200 is used (Reviewers 1,3). This is insufficient to demonstrate generalizability.
    *   No long-tail evaluation (e.g., Herbarium19) to test behavior under imbalanced class distributions (Reviewer 1).
    *   Only one fine-grained dataset (CUB) used; the method's performance on other fine-grained benchmarks (Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft) is needed (Reviewer 3).
*   **Lack of insightful analysis:**
    *   Why does the contrastive loss harm clustering performance in Table 3? (Reviewer 2)
    *   In GCD ablation (Table 4), why are labeled negative neighbors inferior to mined negative neighbors (second vs. third row)? (Reviewer 2)
    *   For clustering, an ablation replacing the proposed positive mining strategy with a simplified or existing one should be shown in Table 3 (Reviewer 3).
    *   For GCD, more ablations are needed in Table 4, e.g., combinations of labeled/mined positive/negative neighbors for labeled and unlabeled sets (Reviewer 3).
    *   Dependence on backbone: With a less powerful backbone (e.g., DINOv1), methods show significant reduction on fine-grained classes. The authors should analyze the bounds on feature extraction capabilities that make UNIC fail (e.g., when basic K-means accuracy drops to 60% or 50%) (Reviewer 3).
*   **Confusing/clarification issues:**
    *   Equation 6 computes entropy between two scalar values, which is non-standard (entropy is defined over a distribution). Is it binary cross-entropy? Needs full formulation (Reviewer 1).
    *   The selection of positive (nearest) and negative (furthest) neighbors is simplistic. Prior works show that hard negatives are more effective; alternative selection strategies should be explored (Reviewer 4).
    *   Explanation needed for the selection of parameter τ₂ on ImageNet, STL, and CUB (Reviewer 3).
    *   Reviewer 1 asks whether the mining strategy was considered on top of existing GCD methods (e.g., contrastive GCD baseline) to isolate gains.
    *   Reviewer 4 asks whether various representation learning methods (MAE, Supervised ViT, iBot) were tried, and which SSL strategy works best.
*   **Presentation issues:**
    *   Table 2 is small and difficult to read; Table 6 is uncentered (Reviewer 1).
    *   Figures referenced in main text placed after summary section disrupts readability (Reviewer 4).
    *   Table 2 has inconsistent decimal places (some two decimals, others one) (Reviewer 4).
    *   Typo: Line 258 “y ̂_i,y ̂_p,y ̂_p” (Reviewer 3).
