## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper tackles unknown rejection — i.e., accepting correctly classified inputs while rejecting misclassified covariate-shifted and all semantic-shifted samples — by proposing TrustLoRA, a framework that acquires failure-specific reliability knowledge via separate Low-Rank Adapter (LoRA) modules fine-tuned on covariate shifts (using AugMix) and semantic shifts (using Outlier Exposure, OE). These LoRA weight increments are then linearly combined at inference via a scaling parameter α to control the trade-off between handling the two shift types. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-200/500 with ResNet and Vision Transformers are presented.

### Strengths

- **Practical relevance**: The paper addresses the real-world need for a model that can reject both misclassified covariate-shifted and semantic-shifted examples while accepting correct ones — a task that prior separate lines of OOD generalization and OOD detection do not jointly cover. Reviewer 2 and 3 highlight this as an important problem.
- **Efficient knowledge integration via LoRA**: Using low-rank adapters to capture shift-specific knowledge is parameter-efficient and computationally lightweight. The modularity allows combining adapters through simple arithmetic operations without retraining the full model. Several reviewers note this advantage (R1: “innovative”, “parameter-efficient”; R2: “efficient integration”; R3: “interesting”, “portable”).
- **Flexibility and controllability**: The α parameter enables dynamic adjustment of the emphasis between covariate and semantic shift rejection without retraining, allowing practitioners to adapt to deployment needs (R1, R2).
- **Comprehensive experimental validation**: The method is tested across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-200/500), architectures (ResNet, ViT), and compared to several baselines, showing strong unknown rejection performance. R2: “comprehensive”, “outperforms several baselines”. R1: “extensive results demonstrating robustness”.
- **Theoretical support**: Proposition 3.1 provides a theoretical perspective on achieving desirable trade-offs in multi-objective optimization (R2). R3 also acknowledges that the study offers insights such as the impact of covariate shifts (Fig. 2) and the trade-off between rejection of different failure types (Fig. 4), which motivate future work.

**Reviewer disagreement on novelty and impact**:  
One reviewer (R4, score 3) strongly criticizes the novelty, arguing that Equations (4) and (5) are a straightforward combination of existing methods (AugMix, OE, LoRA) and that the performance gains are marginal over AugMix alone. In contrast, another reviewer (R2, score 8) finds the approach innovative and practically significant, emphasizing the flexible integration and theoretical support. R1 and R3 are moderately positive, noting the method is interesting but with concerns about missing comparisons and capacity.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons to related prior works on unknown rejection / failure detection** (R1, R3):
  - The paper references [R1] for handling covariate and semantic shifts, but provides no comparative experiments with that work.
  - Several prior works specifically tackle unknown rejection or unified failure detection: Zhu et al. “RCL: Reliable continual learning for unified failure detection” (CVPR 2024); Cen et al. “The devil is in the wrongly-classified samples” (ICLR 2023); Zhu et al. “OpenMix: Exploring outlier samples for misclassification detection” (CVPR 2023); Li et al. “SURE: Survey recipes for building reliable and robust deep networks” (CVPR 2024). These must be discussed and experimentally compared to assess the true novelty and effectiveness of TrustLoRA.
- **Risk of underfitting and LoRA capacity** (R1, R3):
  - When the pretrained model is frozen and only LoRA is fine-tuned, the limited number of added parameters may be insufficient to capture the complexity of both covariate and semantic shift reliability knowledge. R1: “It's unclear how low-rank adaptation can effectively capture the complexity … risk of underfitting”.
  - R3 questions why only the B matrix is trained and whether training both A and B would affect performance. Efficiency arguments alone are insufficient; further advantages (or disadvantages) should be clarified.
- **Dependence on auxiliary data (Outlier Exposure)** (R2):
  - The method requires access to auxiliary outlier data for OE. The quality and representativeness of this data directly affect performance, and it may not be readily available. R2: “This dependency is a significant limitation … performance could be severely compromised if auxiliary data does not adequately cover potential semantic shifts.” R1 also notes that the CIFAR-10/100 experiments do not specify which data was used as D_aux, which hampers reproducibility.
- **Problem setting misalignment with real-world inference** (R3):
  - The main experiments (Tables 1, 2) distinguish only between “correctly classified covariate-shifted” and “misclassified covariate-shifted + semantic shift”. In a realistic open environment, ID samples, covariate-shifted, and semantic-shifted samples coexist. The correct evaluation should distinguish “correctly classified ID + correctly classified covariate-shifted” from “misclassified ID + misclassified covariate-shifted + semantic shift”. Although ID test data is discussed briefly on page 8, including it in the primary experimental setup would better align with real-world requirements.
- **Limited novelty and marginal improvements over strong baselines** (R4):
  - Equations (4) and (5) amount to applying LoRA to existing AugMix and OE objectives. This integration is straightforward and lacks a novel theoretical underpinning or significant algorithmic contribution. R4: “The core idea … does not present a significant methodological advancement.”
  - In many experiments, AugMix itself is the second-best method, only slightly behind TrustLoRA. This raises the possibility that performance gains are largely due to AugMix and auxiliary data rather than the proposed framework. Marginal improvements call the method’s contribution into question.
- **Overly complicated presentation of “knowledge”** (R4):
  - The paper introduces terms like “knowledge acquisition” and “knowledge compression” (around line 180) without a clear formal definition. In practice, “knowledge” refers to LoRA weights, and “compression” is a linear combination of LoRA increments. This terminology can confuse readers and may obscure the method’s simplicity.
- **Missing proof for Proposition 3.1** (R1). Additionally, R3 questions whether the claim that TrustLoRA can approximate a family of mappings f_n is guaranteed solely by linear combination of LoRA weights.
- **Gradient interference analysis in multi-task learning** (R2):
  - A theoretical and empirical analysis comparing how the LoRA-based approach mitigates gradient conflicts (versus standard multi-task learning) for simultaneously optimizing covariate shift adaptation and semantic shift detection is missing. This would strengthen the understanding of the method’s advantages.
- **Need for experiments on larger-scale datasets** (R1):
  - The current experiments are limited to smaller datasets (CIFAR-10/100). Although ImageNet-200/500 are used, full ImageNet-1K experiments would further demonstrate scalability to more complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Minor issues** (R3, R4):
  - Fig. 1: red crosses and green check marks should be specified.
  - Line 47: citation issue “Bai et al., (Bai et al., 2023)” needs correction.
  - Numbering: “(2) Recoverable” should likely be “(3) Recoverable”.
  - Proposition 3.1: missing closing bracket.
  - Line 318: “generally keep equal numbers of misclassified semantic-shifted data” should be “misclassified covariate-shifted data”.
  - Line 485: reference “Narasimhan et al.” missing publication year.
  - Line 491: “Fig. 8” should be “Fig. 7”.
  - Line 239: typo “semantic shifts” should be “semantic shifts” (R2).

**Reviewer disagreement on fundamental novelty**:  
One reviewer (R4) considers the method’s contribution low, describing it as a straightforward combination with marginal gains. Another reviewer (R2) rates it highly innovative and practically valuable. R1 and R3 are more moderate, seeing the approach as interesting but requiring stronger evidence and comparisons.
