## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a virtual experience (VE) method for goal-conditioned RL that augments Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) by constructing virtual goals from diverse trajectories, using a task-probability-density-based curriculum and a self-supervised subgoal planning policy. Experiments on AntMaze, Reacher, and Sawyer show improvements over prior GCRL and hierarchical RL methods.

### Strengths
- The overall idea of using virtual experiences from diverse trajectories to augment HER is elegant and addresses an important problem in goal-conditioned RL.
- The method significantly outperforms prior online GCRL and hierarchical methods on several tasks (AntMaze, Sawyer, Reacher), with a wide performance gap.
- Extensive experimental validation across multiple environments with comprehensive visualizations and a detailed experimental setup.
- One reviewer particularly praised the idea of distilling subgoal-conditioned policies to long-horizon goals, noting it helps propagate easily learned small-scale behavior to noisier long-horizon learning.
- The same reviewer found the results thorough and the different components well-ablated – a contrast to the concerns of other reviewers about missing ablations.
- Visualizations (e.g., Figure 5) were found useful for understanding the method.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation** are major concerns. Section 4.1 (task-probability-density-based curriculum learning) is very hard to grasp: notation (e.g., variable \(j\)), the meaning of “task probability density”, the purpose of the range filter condition, and how state transitions are “lacking connection” are all unclear. Section 4.2 (subgoal planning) is also difficult to follow: subgoal sequence notation is not explained clearly, and it is ambiguous whether the update is A2C-like or AWR-like. Overall, the interaction between the high-level subgoal policy, density model, and imitation regularization is not clearly explained. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Ablation and component contribution** is disputed. Two reviewers note that the three key components (high-level subgoal policy, density model for curriculum, imitation regularization in Eq. 9) are not independently evaluated; the benefit of the \(p\)-norm for subgoal sequences is not isolated; and the effectiveness of Eq. 9 is not shown in the ablation study. One reviewer, however, considered the ablations well-done – indicating reviewer disagreement about adequacy.
- The **curriculum learning component** adds significant complexity (requires training a Flow or RealNVP model, whose details are not discussed) yet provides limited benefit (Fig. 7, middle), and requires sensitive hyperparameters (state density clipping range \((0.8, 1.2)\)).
- The **experimental scope** is limited to relatively simple state-based domains with intrinsic 2D structure (AntMaze, Reacher, Sawyer); most analysis focuses on AntMaze. Generalization to higher-dimensional state spaces (e.g., Franka Kitchen, CALVIN) is unclear, and the high-level policy learning problem becomes more complicated in higher dimensions.
- The **handling of noise** from virtual experiences is insufficiently addressed. The method assumes virtual goals are useful without explicitly accounting for the distributional discrepancy between real and virtual transitions; the implicit curriculum may not be effective in complex real-world settings. There is no clear discussion of how the task probability density is estimated or how sensitive performance is to estimation accuracy.
- **Missing justifications and specific concerns**:
  - No theoretical justification for why imitating the subgoal-generating policy (Eq. 9) is beneficial, nor for why the relabeling approach is superior to HER.
  - The direction of the KL divergence in Eq. 9 is unexplained, and the meaning of \(\pi^{\text{prior}}\) is unclear.
  - In Fig. 12, most proposed subgoals appear very close to the goals, raising questions about their utility.
  - Selecting all virtual goals causes the method to fail (Fig. 7).
  - It is unclear why using only a single subgoal (\(k=1\)) in AntMaze yields the best results.
  - The method section begins by describing differences from Kim et al. 2023 without first introducing that prior work.
  - The use of the entire 31-dimensional state space as the goal space is not justified.
  - The definition of “Uniform smoothing” (line 357) is not provided.
  - The paper does not state whether random goal sampling from the replay buffer (common in HER implementations) is used for baselines (especially SAC+HER).
- **Tuning procedure** is unclear: VE appears to be tuned separately per environment, but it is not explained how tuning was done without evaluating on the target goal-conditioned task.
- **Formula symbols** are often unclear, hindering readability and comprehension.
- **Minor formatting and typographical issues**: misuse of \cite/\citet instead of \citep; green citecolor hard to read; typos “Swayer” instead of “Sawyer” in Figures 3, F.2, etc.; non-abbreviated captions in Figures (e.g., Fig. 7) would be helpful.
- **Open questions** that reflect missing clarification or analysis:
  - Can \(1<p<2\) be used in the \(p\)-norm? When might \(p=\infty\) be suboptimal?
  - How exactly is the task density model used for curriculum learning and how does it fit into Eqs. (5) and (7)?
  - Can the entire framework adapt to different lower-level off-policy algorithms such as HER?
  - What is the main difference from other subgoal works that attain subgoals without learning (midpoint, bisection, graph-based)?
  - Is there any information to deduce from Figure 5?
