## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies total variation distance estimation between two distributions over \(\{0,1\}^n\) using subcube conditioning (SUBCOND) queries. It presents the first polynomial-sample distance estimator in this model, DistEstimate, with query complexity \(\tilde{O}(n^3/\varepsilon^5)\). The proofs are provided and experiments on scalable benchmarks are reported. The reviewers generally acknowledge the theoretical contribution (first such result, exponential improvement over the standard model’s \(\Omega(2^n/n)\) lower bound), but raise serious concerns about the experimental evaluation, clarity of the algorithmic intuition, and the fit for the ICLR audience.

### Strengths
- The problem of total variation estimation is important, and the exponential lower bounds in the standard sampling model motivate the study of stronger models; the SUBCOND model is natural for the Boolean hypercube (R1, R4).
- The paper gives the **first** polynomial sample distance estimator in the SUBCOND model, achieving an exponential improvement over the \(\Omega(2^n/n)\) lower bound for the standard sampling model (R3, R4).
- The techniques are novel and sound: “taming” the distributions by adding noise to ensure non-trivial marginals, and using conditional sampling to obtain multiplicative approximations of probabilities via the chain rule \(\Pr[x=a] = \Pr[x_1=a_1] \Pr[x_2=a_2|x_1=a_1] \cdots\), then plugging into a prior result for importance weight estimation (R1, R2). The proof is mostly self-contained and uses a clever analysis with negative binomial distributions (R2).
- The paper provides clear motivation for why a stronger model is needed and discusses future research directions (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Experiments are insufficient and poorly executed.** R3 (score 1, overall very negative) explicitly states that Figure 1 and Table 1 “make no sense” and that the experimental part is “extremely unsatisfying”. Specific criticisms shared by multiple reviewers: (i) The tested dimensions are not high enough and \(\varepsilon=0.5\) is too large relative to the scale of TV distances (R2, R3). (ii) Figure 1 lacks error bars, at least for small \(n\), to demonstrate stability (R2). (iii) No repeated experiments showing the relationships between \(n\), \(\delta\), and \(\varepsilon\) (standard in the area) (R3). (iv) No comparison with other existing methods to demonstrate significance (R3). (v) The description of the experimental setup is vague – how scalable benchmarks were generated and how real-world circuits are represented by Boolean formulas is unclear, making it difficult to assess practical relevance (R4). (vi) The motivation for applying distance estimation to constrained samplers is not clarified (R4). The minority reviewer (R3) rates the paper very poorly due to experiments, while R1 and R4 rate it highly (8) and R2 (6) is moderately positive; this strong disagreement centers on the experimental deficiencies.
- **Lack of algorithmic/analytic intuition.** The paper does not clearly explain how the tamed distribution is accessed algorithmically using SUBCOND queries; the connection between the theoretical “taming” and practical implementation needs clarification (R4). There is no breakdown of where the \(n^3\) and \(1/\varepsilon^5\) factors come from, making it difficult to understand the bottlenecks of the approach (R4).
- **Fit for ICLR and title overreach.** The applications of subcube conditioning queries feel disconnected from mainstream machine learning interests, so the motivation for studying this particular query model at ICLR is not entirely convincing (R4). The title is too broad: it promises distance estimation for high-dimensional distributions (often thought of as \(\mathbb{R}^n\)), but the domain is the Boolean hypercube; the paper should specify “discrete distributions” and mention the need for conditional samples (R1).
- **Missing intuition and incomplete explanation.** An example illustrating scenarios where SUBCOND queries are available should appear in the introduction, rather than only in the Application section (R2). The notation for distributions is inconsistent (\(P,Q\) vs. \(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\)) (R2). There is a question about whether the definition of \(Z\) in Lemma 1 should be divided by \(m\) (R2).
- **Missing technical details.** Is it possible to provide a high-probability bound with respect to the number of queries? If not, what is the technical hardness? (R3). The paper could also discuss extensions to other domains and what a reasonable analog of SUBCOND might be (R1). Questions about the tradeoff between sample complexity and TVD for the datasets remain unanswered (R4).
