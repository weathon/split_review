## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies the last-iterate convergence of stochastic gradient methods for convex optimization.  Its main claimed novelty is obtaining high-probability last-iterate convergence rates for SGD without a bounded-domain assumption, for convex (and strongly convex) problems.  The results are presented in a unified framework covering composite objectives, Bregman distances, smoothness, Lipschitzness, expectation and high-probability guarantees, and heavy-tailed noise.  The key technique adapts a construction from Zamani & Glineur (2023) of auxiliary points \(z_t\) to exploit convexity.

### Strengths

- The goal of obtaining high-probability last-iterate rates for SGD without bounded domains is interesting and important.  The problem has attracted interest from the ML community because the last iterate is often used in practice.
- The results are general and bypass previous constraints (compact domain, bounded noise), covering a wide range of settings: composite, non-Euclidean norms, Lipschitz/smooth, (strong) convexity, expectation and high-probability bounds.
- For smooth functions, the work provides an \(\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{T})\) last-iterate bound, improving upon the previously known best result of \(O(1/T^{1/3})\) (Reviewer 3 notes this as a solid improvement).
- The idea of constructing the sequence \(z_t\) to make use of convexity is intuitive yet powerful (Reviewer 3 praises this as a clear technical contribution).
- The proofs are comprehensive and mostly carefully written (Reviewer 1 acknowledges this despite readability concerns).  The unified treatment covering many settings is also appreciated.
- Reviewer 3 finds no substantial weakness and rates the work as a solid contribution (score 8), while Reviewer 1 rates it 6 and Reviewer 2 rates it 6, indicating a positive but not uniformly strong assessment.

### Weaknesses

- **Verifiability and proof presentation (Reviewer 1, major concern):** The reviewer could not verify the main proof within a reasonable time due to excessive generality and complicated notation.  The proofs appear to spend too much effort on marginal cases, obscuring the main contribution.  Reviewer 1 recommends focusing on a simplified setting (e.g., constrained problem, Euclidean distances, Lipschitz or smooth convex function) with a self-contained proof in the main text, moving generalizations to the appendix.  Reviewer 2 did not check proofs carefully; Reviewer 3 found the presentation fair (soundness 3 and presentation 3).
- **Oversell and vague novelty claims (Reviewer 1):** The paper repeatedly uses phrases such as “entirely new,” “brand new analysis,” and “first unified inequality” in a way that feels like oversell.  It is often unclear which parts are from prior work (Zamani & Glineur 2023, Liu et al. 2023) and what the new technical difficulties are.  The claim that composite/Bregman/smooth extensions are novel is questionable because Orabona (2020) left those as exercises; the paper does not explain why Orabona’s approach would not generalize.  Such claims mislead readers and detract from the genuine high-probability contribution.
- **Incremental nature and fit (Reviewer 2, shared by Reviewer 1):** The work is seen as combining existing techniques (Orabona 2020 for expectation; Liu et al. 2023 for high probability; Zamani & Glineur 2023 for the last-iterate argument) without major technical novelty.  Reviewer 2 suggests the content is more suited for a mathematics/optimization journal than ICLR.
- **Bounded variance assumption contradicts strong convexity (Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2):** Strong convexity and a uniformly bounded (sub-)gradient cannot both hold on unbounded domains.  Reviewer 2 provides an explicit counterexample.  The paper should explain the significance of this case and handle it carefully, separating it from the main contribution.
- **Step-size depends on \(x^*\) (Reviewer 1):** Theorem 3.1 uses a step-size choice that depends on \(\|x_1 - x^*\|\).  The paper later claims it works with any step-size independent of \(x^*\), but that should be stated in the theorem itself.  A step-size depending on the unknown optimum is not desirable.
- **Missing technical explanations and readability issues (Reviewer 1):**
    - No explanation of why combining Zamani & Glineur (2023) and Liu et al. (2023) might create additional difficulties.  Even if none arise, this should be stated clearly.
    - The notation is unnecessarily convoluted (e.g., \(\eta_{t\in[T]} \le \frac{1}{2L\vee \mu_f}\), \(v_{t\in\{0\}\cup[T]}>0\), \(t\vee 1\) inside sums).  Standard, more readable alternatives exist.
    - The paper should explain why Orabona (2020)’s analysis does not extend to high probability (i.e., why the new approach is needed).
    - The claim that Harvey et al. (2019a)’s bounded-noise result “extends to sub-Gaussian” is handled via a footnote that seems to imply doubt.  If the authors are not convinced, they should verify with the authors of Harvey et al. or provide a clear argument.
    - “Smoothness with respect to optimum” (page 3) is ambiguous.
    - The discussion in Section 2.1 is unnecessary and should be moved to the appendix.  The remark “We also would like to emphasize that…” is awkward.
    - Footnote/book references to (Lan, 2020) are vague; specific locations should be given.
    - In the proof of Lemma 2.1, the fifth line of an equation chain is unexplained.
    - In Lemma 4.1: the requirement on \(v_k\) and the proof that \(z_t\in\mathcal{X}\) should be justified (convex combination of points in \(\mathcal{X}\)).
    - The representation of \(z_t\) after equation (7) is unclear; the paper should explicitly write it as a convex combination and note that the \(v_s\) are monotone and sum to 1 (as done by Zamani & Glineur).
    - Many steps (page 15 last inequality, page 17 estimation, page 18 application of Lemma 2.1, page 19 definition of \(w_1\) and bound) lack justification and should be expanded.
- **Lack of proof sketch or intuition in main text (Reviewer 3):** The paper would benefit from a simple example of the \(z_t\) construction and an explanation of how it is used to exploit convexity.  This would greatly improve readability.  Reviewer 3 suggests making the results presentation more compact to free space for such discussion.
- **Marginal claim about composite objective (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2):** The abstract criticizes prior work for being “limited to a single objective,” but composite optimization still involves a single objective (the sum of two functions).  This phrasing is misleading.
- **Terminology error (Reviewer 1):** “last-iterative convergence” is grammatically incorrect and should be “last-iterate convergence” throughout the paper.
- **Potential overclaiming about smooth improvements (Reviewer 1):** The paper claims the smooth result is the first improvement over Moulines & Bach (2011), but Orabona (2020) left the smooth extension as an exercise.  If Orabona’s analysis can be extended, the claim is not new; the authors should explain why it cannot be applied.
- **Strong convexity with bounded gradient (Reviewer 1):** This combination is contradictory on unbounded domains; the significance should be explained and separated from the main contribution.

**Additional questions from reviewers that overlap with criticisms or missing analyses:**
- (Reviewer 1) What are the technical novelties beyond Liu et al. (2023)?  Section 1.2 should clarify this.
- (Reviewer 1) Does the combination of Zamani & Glineur (2023) and Liu et al. (2023) create additional difficulties?  The paper should state yes or no.
- (Reviewer 1) Why is the analysis of Orabona (2020) not sufficient for high-probability guarantees?  Why does the paper build on Zamani & Glineur instead of Orabona?
- (Reviewer 1) How does Lemma 4.2 improve over Orabona (2020) when the Bregman and smooth extensions are left as exercises?
- (Reviewer 2) Can the techniques be extended to non-convex objectives?  (This request for discussion is a missing experiment/analysis.)
- (Reviewer 1) The step-size in Theorem 3.1 choice depends on \(x^*\); is the recovery of \(L/T\) rate in the noiseless case only with that specific step-size?  Clarify.

**Minority positive contrast:** Reviewer 3 does not find any substantial weakness and rates the work 8, considering it a solid contribution with good presentation, while Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2, despite positive scores (both 6), raise significant concerns about writing, oversell, incrementality, and verification difficulty.  This disagreement should be considered in evaluating the paper.
