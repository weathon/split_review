## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GAIA, a two-stage data-driven talking avatar generation method: first, a VAE disentangles video frames into motion and appearance latent codes; second, a diffusion model generates motion sequences conditioned on speech and a reference portrait. The method also introduces a large-scale filtered talking avatar dataset (AVENA). Reviewers acknowledge impressive visual quality, scalability, and flexibility, but raise concerns about missing disentanglement evaluation, unfair comparisons (models trained on different datasets), insufficient baselines from end-to-end trainable models, lack of inference time analysis, limited generation examples, and ambiguous “zero-shot” naming. After the rebuttal, Reviewer 1 (score 8) finds the results genuinely impressive and has addressed concerns; other reviewers rate 6 and remain more cautious.

### Strengths
- Impressive quality of results in terms of both lip-sync and visual quality; the method achieves superior naturalness, diversity, lip-sync, and visual quality (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Model design is conceptually simple, straightforward, and yet evidently effective; it requires very few pretrained components (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The framework is scalable—larger models yield better performance—and as a self-supervised method it can utilize readily available training data at scale (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- The paper proposes a new large-scale talking avatar dataset (AVENA) with 8.2K hours and 16.9K unique speakers (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- The ablation study is rich and increases overall quality (Reviewer 2).
- Writing is supported by equations and well-drawn figures that make the explanation clear (Reviewer 2).
- Evaluation includes a user study, which is good for addressing output quality (Reviewer 3).
- The method is highly flexible and supports many related applications, e.g., controllable talking avatar generation and text-instructed avatar generation (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Minority more-positive view:** After the rebuttal, Reviewer 1 (score 8) states that the results “look genuinely impressive” and that their concerns have been addressed, indicating strong confidence in the method’s quality.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing disentanglement evaluation:** The paper lacks a quantitative assessment of how well the appearance and motion latent spaces are separated. Specifically, cross-reenactment experiments (swapping motion latents between different identities and measuring quality/identity preservation) are not provided. This is crucial for generalizing to new identities and poses (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient discussion of related end-to-end trainable models:** The limitations of 3DMM-based methods are addressed, but other end-to-end trainable talking face synthesis models (e.g., PC-AVS, PD-FGC) are not discussed or compared. The work would benefit from benchmarking against these (Reviewer 2). Also, a recent video-driven SOTA (arxiv.org/pdf/2012.08261.pdf) that claims improvements over face-vid2vid and FOMM is critically missing as a comparison (Reviewer 3).
- **Unfair comparison due to different training data:** The proposed method and the baselines were trained on different datasets. It is impossible to isolate performance gains due to the model architecture from gains due to data preprocessing/filtering. A fair comparison requires training all models on the same data, including unprocessed datasets used in prior works (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing inference time comparison:** No analysis of inference speed is provided. Some baselines (e.g., SadTalker) could be improved given the computational budget of running a diffusion model for every time step; a computationally fair comparison should be discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Ambiguous “zero-shot” naming:** The model requires one portrait image of an unseen speaker, which contradicts typical zero-shot definitions. The authors should clarify this terminology (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited generation examples:** The paper does not provide a large number of visual results. Missing examples include long videos, different reference frames with the same driving speech, and different driving speech with the same reference frame (Reviewer 4). A related missing analysis: the effect of randomness from the diffusion model on generations (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation on training data size:** It would be interesting to see whether scaling data further brings benefits beyond model size scaling (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing flow issues:** The manuscript is hard to follow due to frequent cross-references and the placement of the Discussion section at the end of Section 4 (Model) rather than after experiments (Reviewer 2). Ethical considerations are relegated to the Appendix; they should appear in the main manuscript (Reviewer 2).
- **Additional questions raised by reviewers:** (a) Will the filtered dataset and pre-trained models be released? (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). (b) Can the method drive cartoon characters (humanoid or non-humanoid)? (Reviewer 4). (c) Clarify the dataset collection: the paper states “we collect High Definition Talking Face Dataset (HDTF) … and Casual Conversation datasets …” but it is unclear whether these datasets’ samples are included in AVENA or used separately (Reviewer 2).
