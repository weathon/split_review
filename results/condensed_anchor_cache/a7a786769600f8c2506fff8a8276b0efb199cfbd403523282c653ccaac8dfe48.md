## Merged Review

### Summary
Thought Propagation (TP) is a training-free framework that prompts LLMs to propose and solve analogous problems, then reuses those solutions to improve reasoning on the input problem. It is plug-and-play with existing prompting methods and achieves substantial performance gains on shortest-path reasoning, creative writing, and LLM-Agent planning. Reviewers find the idea interesting and the results promising, but express concerns about computational overhead, token-cost comparison methodology, and the relevance of the shortest-path testbed.

### Strengths
- **Originality and novelty:** TP introduces a generalized analogical reasoning framework for LLMs, extending the idea of neighborhood propagation from GNNs to problem-solving. It is plug-and-play with existing methods and does not require task-specific prompt engineering. (Reviewer 1)
- **Clarity:** The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. The methodology section clearly explains the framework, and the experimental description facilitates implementation and reproducibility. (Reviewer 1)
- **Extensive evaluation:** Experiments across three challenging tasks with multiple LLM backbones demonstrate substantial performance improvements. (Reviewer 1)
- **Significance:** The modular design is broadly applicable and likely to benefit diverse research directions. (Reviewer 1)
- **Counterintuitive and interesting finding:** TP improves the input problem even without ground-truth validation of the analogies’ correctness, which is surprising given that methods like ToT spend compute directly on the input problem. (Reviewer 3)
- **Important topic with careful motivation and detailed analysis:** The work addresses an important research direction and provides thorough result analysis. (Reviewer 3)
- **Training-free and efficient:** TP avoids sophisticated training or careful dataset design, is environment-friendly, and generalizes to various LLMs. (Reviewer 2)
- **Significant performance gains:** Consistent improvements are observed across tasks. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Token cost comparison is insufficient:** Token-cost analysis is provided only for the shortest-path task (Figure 5), which is a problematic testbed. The shortest-path task is contrived—one would not typically use an LLM for it—and performance is likely highly sensitive to the natural language representation of the graph. No token costs are reported for the creative writing or planning tasks. This casts doubt on the central finding that TP outperforms ToT efficiently. (Reviewer 3; note: this is a strong criticism from Reviewer 3, contrasting with the more positive views of Reviewers 1 and 2)
- **Missing comparison with self-refined reasoning methods:** Since TP also refines solutions in the aggregation module, it should be compared with methods like self-refined LLM reasoning (e.g., [1,2]). (Reviewer 1)
- **Potential solution deterioration in shortest-path:** It is not evaluated whether TP sometimes worsens solutions on certain test instances instead of improving them. (Reviewer 1)
- **Higher computational cost and complexity:** TP requires extra steps to propose, solve, and aggregate analogous problems, making it more computationally costly and complex than methods like Chain-of-Thought. (Reviewer 2)
- **Typos:** Page 9, last sentence of section 6 uses incorrect quotation marks. (Reviewer 2)
