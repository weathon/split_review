## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Relay Diffusion Model (RDM), a cascaded framework that unifies diffusion processes across resolutions via blurring diffusion and block noise. The key insight is that the same noise level at higher resolution yields a higher SNR in the frequency domain, causing a mismatch. RDM avoids conditioning on upsampled low-resolution images by transferring a low-resolution image/noise into an equivalent high-resolution one, thus continuing diffusion without restarting from pure noise. It achieves state-of-the-art FID on CelebA-HQ 256×256 and sFID on ImageNet 256×256, surpassing ADM, LDM, and DiT. The framework follows EDM and cascaded diffusion, but introduces locally correlated noise for higher-resolution images. Some reviewers praised the analysis and clarity, while others noted that the resolution tested (256×256) is only medium, questioned the fairness of training efficiency claims, and found the SOTA claims weakened by use of class-balanced FID and stronger baselines (e.g., MDT) in some metrics.

### Strengths
- **Originality and insight.** The paper combines a low-resolution ordinary diffusion model with a high-resolution blurring diffusion model for the first time, solving the noise-schedule problem in high-resolution diffusion. The analysis of the noise gap between resolutions via the frequency domain (SNRs across frequencies) is interesting and may inspire future work. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Clear exposition.** The writing is well-structured, the problem is presented straightforwardly, and the derivation of the block-wise correlated noise sampler is explained clearly. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **State-of-the-art results.** RDM sets new SOTA on ImageNet 256×256 for constant CFG (sFID 3.97 vs. 4.57 for MDT-XL/2-G) and on CelebA-HQ 256×256, with large margins over ADM, LDM, and DiT. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Reduced training and sampling dependency.** The design avoids the need for conditioning on low-resolution images during the high-resolution stage, enabling the low-resolution model to be pre-trained (e.g., EDM checkpoints) and reducing total training and sampling steps. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Comprehensive experimental comparison.** The paper provides extensive quantitative and ablative experiments on two datasets. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Resolution and terminology.** Several reviewers noted that 256×256 is not “high-resolution” by current standards; models like simple diffusion and VDM++ fit 512×512 end-to-end. It is unclear whether RDM scales to 512×2, 1024×2, or higher without re-tuning hyperparameters. (Reviewers 2, 3) One reviewer expected experiments at least at 512 resolution. (Reviewer 3)
- **Training efficiency claims are questionable.** The paper claims faster convergence and reduced training steps, but the high-resolution stage uses a pre-trained low-resolution model (EDM), so it is not trained from scratch. Comparing its convergence curve (Figure 1) with from-scratch baselines is misleading. (Reviewer 2) The training cost in wall-clock time is not reported, making it hard to assess practical cost. (Reviewer 2) The model still requires training on the highest resolution (256×256), so memory/compute savings over standard cascaded methods are not fully justified. (Reviewer 1, as a question)
- **SOTA claims are undermined.** The paper uses class-balanced FID (FID-CB) interchangeably with standard FID, but prior works may not have used this trick, making the “SOTA FID” claims potentially unfair. (Reviewer 2) On ImageNet 256, RDM only slightly improves sFID over MDT-XL/2-G (3.97 vs. 4.57, not “a large margin” as stated in the abstract), while lagging behind on FID, IS, and Recall. (Reviewer 4) The paper lacks evidence that sFID is an “improved version” of FID. (Reviewer 2)
- **Incomplete comparisons and missing experiments.** No comparison is provided for an end-to-end model that simply introduces block noise in early diffusion steps; such an ablation would isolate the impact of block noise. (Reviewer 1) No evaluation of RDM trained from scratch (without pre-trained low-resolution model) is given, so the claim of simplicity over CDMs is unsupported. (Reviewer 2) The paper does not compare inference cost (steps/time) with baselines in their optimal regimes (e.g., CFG tuned). (Reviewer 4) The related work f-DM (Gu et al., ICLR’22) is a “relay” diffusion but is not mentioned; the differences should be described. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear technical explanations.** The transition from Section 3.1 to 3.2 (why combining block noise and blurring diffusion in the second stage) is hard to follow. The role of the patch-wise blurring operation D_T^p in connecting low- and high-resolution stages is not clearly articulated. (Reviewer 1) It is not explained how blurring aligns with the high-resolution noise schedule and how block noise interacts with blurring to achieve the desired effect. (Reviewer 1) The text below Equation (11) states that blurring and block noise corruptions can be considered independently, but no formal proof is given. (Reviewer 1) Figure 2 does not specify which noise schedule (linear or cosine) is used, making the relationship to 32×32 and 64×64 confusing. (Reviewer 4)
- **Qualitative evidence is missing.** The paper claims that artifacts in low-resolution images can be corrected in the high-resolution stage, but no qualitative examples are shown to support this. (Reviewer 3)
- **Specific presentation issues.** Figure 1 should clarify that the model is not trained from scratch. (Reviewer 2) In Table 3, the grouping of methods is unexplained, and the meaning of “trained images” in Table 4 (e.g., 2500M for ImageNet64, 820M for CelebA-HQ) is ambiguous—likely training iterations, but the notation is misleading. (Reviewer 4) Minor errors: double “the” on page 4; denominator of Equation (6) should be s or s²; in Equation (8) ϵ’ should be under the expectation symbol. (Reviewer 1) Algorithm A.4 does not include the low-resolution diffusion model process. (Reviewer 1) Questions about how to choose D_T^p to ensure distributional matching, and whether the reduced sampling steps are due to the second-order sampler rather than the method (e.g., MDT uses DDPM sampler), were raised. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Disagreement among reviewers.** While some reviewers considered the contributions strong and the analysis insightful (scores 8), others found the resolution limitation, comparison fairness, and incomplete ablations significant enough to rate the paper lower (scores 6). One reviewer initially had concerns but later raised their score after the rebuttal, noting that most concerns were addressed. (Reviewer 1)
