## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Branch-GAN, a method that improves text generation in Transformers via adversarial training with branching sequences. For each training sample, multiple sequences are branched from different starting points, providing dense reward and loss signals to the generator and discriminator. The approach uses a combination of MLE and adversarial losses. Human evaluation (10k+ annotations) shows that Branch-GAN produces higher-quality texts than larger baselines (including models 16× the parameters) while using less than 0.01% of the original training data. The method is evaluated on two domains (Wikipedia and Fictional Stories) and includes robustness analysis under noisy contexts. The paper also provides a dataset of manual text quality rankings and analyzes the correlation of automatic metrics with human judgments.

### Strengths
- **Novel method**: Branching for efficient discrimination in a GAN setup with Transformers; uses three prediction heads that implicitly incorporate scheduled sampling benefits. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Data efficiency**: Achieves significant quality improvements using <0.01% of the original training data. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Stable training dynamics**: Dense signals from multiple branches overcome sparsity problems of previous language GANs (e.g., SeqGAN, RankGAN). (Reviewer 3)
- **Human evaluation**: Large-scale study (10k+ annotations) demonstrates clear quality improvements over original texts and baselines (including Pythia 6.9B). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Robustness analysis**: Stable TTR and perplexity under noise. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential broader applicability**: Batching technique may generalize to RLHF or other reward-based training. (Reviewer 1)
- **Valuable released resources**: Dataset of human quality rankings and analysis of automatic metric correlation are of interest to practitioners. (Reviewer 1)
- **Well written and evaluated for the text-continuation setting**. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of diversity / mode collapse detection** (Reviewer 3, high confidence): The paper does not measure whether Branch-GAN sacrifices diversity/coverage for quality/coherence. Previous language GANs (e.g., in “Language GANs Falling Short”) were later found to trade quality for diversity and show no improvement when both are considered. Branch-GAN’s remarkably increased perplexity and generation quality resemble those overclaimed results. This issue is flagged as **dangerously needing attention** (minority strong critique).
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Only two domains (Wikipedia, Fiction); generalization to summarization, dialogue, QA, or prompting tasks is not tested. (Reviewers 1, 2) Reviewer 4 asks if the method was tuned separately per task and whether multi-task tuning is supported.
- **Lack of comparison with other language GANs that use pre-trained Transformers**: Without direct comparison (e.g., to cooperative GANs or other adversarial approaches with pre-trained backbones), it is unclear whether gains come from the branching mechanism or simply from combining a GAN with a large pre-trained model. (Reviewer 2)
- **Loss function motivation unclear**: Equation (1) is poorly motivated; why use this variant instead of REINFORCE/PPO with baseline/advantage? More details from Appendix A.1 should be moved to the main text. (Reviewer 1)
- **Confusing illustration**: Figure 1 omits technical details of the discriminator and how dense adversarial rewards are constructed, which is the most novel part. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational cost**: Both generator and discriminator must be tuned; memory and compute demands are high for large LLMs. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing ablation/questions about branching details** (Reviewer 4): 
  - Starting point sampling: if a branch starts at position N-1, is it easier to discriminate than those starting earlier? Should the range be [1, N-d] rather than [1, N-1]?
  - If a termination token is generated, is the branch still forced to length d?
  - Training complexity with respect to branch number and depth.
  - Robustness analysis: how exactly are random characters selected? Example? Is the effect sensitive to position?
- **Unclear Table 4**: Which model is being evaluated? Why compare “vs. Branch-GAN 1B*”? (Reviewer 1)
- **Win-rate between models**: The paper only shows comparisons to original texts, not head-to-head comparisons (e.g., Branch-GAN vs. Pythia 6.9B). (Reviewer 1)
