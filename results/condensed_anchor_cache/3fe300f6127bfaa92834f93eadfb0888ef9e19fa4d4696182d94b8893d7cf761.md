## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Boltzmann Priors for Implicit Transfer Operators (BoPITO), combining Boltzmann Generators (BG) with Implicit Transfer Operators (ITO) to accelerate molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. ITOs learn time-coarsened transition densities from MD data but require extensive unbiased MD data. BoPITO improves sample efficiency by (1) using a pretrained BG to sample equilibrium starting points, (2) separating the score function into a fixed equilibrium part (the BG) and a learned time-dependent component, and (3) interpolating between ITO models trained on off-equilibrium data and the unbiased equilibrium distribution to approximate rare dynamics. Experiments are conducted on the 1D Prinz potential and alanine dipeptide.

### Strengths

- The method addresses a highly relevant problem: temporal coarse-graining of molecular dynamics to enable simulation over much longer timescales.
- Incorporating Boltzmann priors into ITO provides inductive bias for long-term dynamics, improves data efficiency (one order of magnitude claimed), and guarantees asymptotically unbiased equilibrium statistics.
- The use of cheaply obtainable off-equilibrium data is a promising approach to train more scalable models.
- The paper is clearly written and self-contained, with extensive background sections on MD, BGs, ITOs, and diffusion models.
- Numerical results are convincing in showing advantages over ITOs (PRO) for the tested systems.
- The method seems sound and well-founded in physical/statistical principles (spectral decomposition of the transfer operator, score-based diffusion models).
- The authors address limitations and envision future work;
- Code is provided, properly formatted, and runs out-of-the-box.
- Making use of Boltzmann generators to capture modes with very small probability mass in the Boltzmann distribution is a key contribution.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of comparison to other ML-based methods:** The paper does not compare BoPITO to other machine learning approaches for accelerated MD, such as machine learning potentials (e.g., neural network potentials), Timewarp, Flow Annealed Importance Sampling (FAB), or other methods that learn time-coarsened dynamics. Additionally, quantitative comparison of stationary and dynamic observables to naive MD is missing for alanine dipeptide, whereas ITO originally provided such comparisons for Chignolin, Trp-Cage, BBA, and Villin.
- **Limited molecular systems and scalability concerns:** Experiments are conducted only on the 1D Prinz potential and alanine dipeptide, which are very low-dimensional. The paper lacks results on more complex systems (e.g., larger proteins, Chignolin, Trp-Cage, BBA, Villin, polymers, electrolytes) that would demonstrate practical applicability and scalability. While the authors mention favorable scaling, no evidence is provided.  
- **Missing ground truth transition densities:** For alanine dipeptide (Figure 5), the conditional transition probability densities are shown for BoPITO and ITO (unbiased), but no ground truth is provided. Even if computing a full ground truth is infeasible, a comparison with a Markov State Model (MSM) or another approximation would help assess accuracy. Ground truth is also missing in other plots where feasible.
- **Unsupported data efficiency claim:** The paper claims BoPITO improves data efficiency, but no direct experimental comparison (e.g., plots of performance vs. amount of training data for ITO vs. BoPITO) is provided. The current results do not sufficiently demonstrate that BoPITO requires less data to achieve comparable performance.
- **Pre-trained Boltzmann generator (BG) assumption:** The method assumes a pre-trained BG, but the impact on applicability, computational cost, and data requirements for training the BG is not discussed. It is unclear whether a BG for complex systems (e.g., Chignolin) is readily available or easily trained. The paper should address the scalability of training BGs for larger systems and the potential impact on overall efficiency.  
- **Missing clarification of definitions:** Terms such as “off-equilibrium,” “biased,” “unbiased” are used without precise definitions. For example: Is off-equilibrium equivalent to non-ergodic? Are biased simulations generated by enhanced sampling? A clear taxonomy would avoid confusion.
- **Technical inconsistencies and unclear explanations:**  
  - In lines 139–142, it is stated that eigenvalues satisfy \(-1 < \lambda_i < 1\) and also \(\lambda_i = \exp(-\tau \kappa_i)\) – but an exponential cannot be negative. This contradiction needs resolution.  
  - The notation \(t_{diff}\) (used from line 191) is not defined.  
  - The interpolator section is confusing: \(N_{\text{int}} > N_{\text{max}}\) (training lag) is used for sampling, yet it is not explained why this does not cause out-of-distribution issues or how the model implies an interpolator. The role of \(N_{\text{int}}\) as an annealing parameter is unclear.  
  - The derivation of the score function ansatz and its connection to the spectral decomposition of the transfer operator needs a more detailed, intuitive explanation; the current version is too concise for readers unfamiliar with the formalism.  
- **Unclear writing and missing pseudocode:** The paper assumes familiarity with the ITO framework. More extensive background, a detailed derivation of the score parameterization, and pseudocode (even in appendix) would improve readability.
- **Energy conservation and physical dynamics:** The method does not guarantee energy conservation, and it is not clear how one can guarantee physically realistic dynamics. This point requires explicit discussion.
- **Questions about asymptotic unbiasedness:** Where do the asymptotic guarantees come from? The paper mentions asymptotically unbiased equilibrium statistics by construction (line 60), but it is not clear whether reweighting (e.g., as in [Nicoli et al. 2020]) is enforced. Also, the role of importance resampling in the BG context is not explained.
- **Minor issues:**  
  - The paper claims “improve ITO learning in three ways” (line 209) but then lists four bullet points.  
  - Figure 3 shows that BoPITO and ITO perform similarly on short and medium timescales, with BoPITO mainly outperforming on long timescales; it would be helpful to discuss the practical relevance of medium timescales.  
  - The definition of the “crystal” baseline in Figure 2 is not given.  
  - It would be beneficial to move the discussion section to earlier as related work to provide context.  
  - The eigenfunctions in the spectral decomposition are used only to justify the score function ansatz, but it is unclear if they appear in any training objective – this should be clarified.
