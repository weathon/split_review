## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) to replace vector quantization in VQ-VAEs. FSQ projects the latent representation to few dimensions (typically <10), quantizes each dimension coordinate-wise to a fixed set of scalar values (e.g., via tanh bounding then rounding), and forms an implicit codebook as the product of these sets. It avoids codebook collapse and eliminates the need for auxiliary losses (commitment loss, codebook reseeding, entropy penalties). FSQ is evaluated on image generation (MaskGIT) and dense prediction tasks (depth, colorization, panoptic segmentation via UViM), achieving competitive performance with VQ-VAE despite its simpler design.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and ease of implementation:** FSQ is straightforward, requires fewer hyperparameters, and removes complex training tricks needed in VQ-VAE. All reviewers commend this.
- **Effective performance:** FSQ achieves competitive results across multiple tasks and codebook sizes, often matching or outperforming VQ with larger codebooks (>2^10). Experiments are extensive with detailed comparisons (R1, R2, R4).
- **Avoids codebook collapse and under-utilization:** The fixed grid structure guarantees full utilization, eliminating a key VQ issue (R1, R4).
- **Low model capacity/complexity:** No learned codebook is needed, reducing parameters and computational cost (R1, R3).
- **Writing clarity and easy access:** The method is clearly described and easy to follow (R2, R3, R4).  
*One reviewer (R2, score 8) is notably more positive, emphasizing the method’s effectiveness, extensive experiments, and clear writing; others are more critical about novelty and performance gaps.*

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and technical contribution:** Several reviewers question originality. R3 notes FSQ resembles compressive autoencoders (e.g., [1,2]) applied to generative tasks, with minimal technical/theoretical contributions. R1 also raises novelty concerns.
- **Performance gap vs. VQ:** FSQ results are often slightly worse than VQ, especially with small codebooks (R3, R4). R4 adds that FSQ’s expressive capability is slightly weaker.
- **Missing architectural and training details:** The paper lacks specifics on the FSQ-VAE encoder/decoder architecture, training hyperparameters (epochs, size), and whether FSQ is trained from scratch or adapted from a pretrained VQ-VAE (R2). This hinders full assessment.
- **No design principles for quantization hyperparameters:** The choice of dimensions and number of quantization levels per channel (uniformly >5) lacks justification; no theoretical/empirical analysis of optimality or relationship between levels and representational capacity (R2). R4 also notes difficulty in tuning these hyperparameters.
- **Lack of speedup and compression analysis:** R4 requests numerical comparisons of inference speedup and compression rate vs. VQ. R3 points out that indices map to a fixed grid (not a learned codebook), limiting the method’s compression potential.
- **Practicality of large codebooks:** R4 questions cost-effectiveness: FSQ shows advantages only for codebook sizes >2^10, but such large sizes may hurt the second-stage trade-off between parameter size and FID, reducing practicality.
- **Missing comparison to Lookup Free Quantization (LFQ):** R2 asks for comparison to concurrent work (arxiv 2310.05737) that also compresses the codebook.
- **Unexplored design choices:** R1 asks how the choice of tanh bounding affects performance. R3 questions why increasing model capacity (Sec 3.3) does not improve results given FSQ still underperforms VQ.
- **Quality of representations not analyzed:** R3 asks for analysis beyond optimized metrics (e.g., whether same-class samples are closer in FSQ representations).
