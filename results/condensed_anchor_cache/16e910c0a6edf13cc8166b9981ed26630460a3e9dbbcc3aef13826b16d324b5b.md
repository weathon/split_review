## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multi-objective alignment method using ORPO that combines a log-odds alignment objective with a self-judgment classification objective (predicting the preferred response) while reusing existing alignment data without sampling new data. Tested on Mistral 7B on Alpaca Eval, it explores scalarization, conjoint, and conditional optimization strategies. Results show mixed to slight improvements over the ORPO baseline. One reviewer (score 5) found the integration innovative and writing clear; four reviewers (scores 3) judged the experiments weak, motivation unclear, and baselines insufficient, with no significant gains demonstrated.

### Strengths
1. The direction of self-rewarding (making the model judge its own outputs) is interesting. (R1)
2. Reducing dependency on high-quality data is attractive. (R1)
3. Integrating ORPO with a multi-objective optimization framework to simultaneously address alignment and self-judgment is innovative and promises efficiency improvements in LLM fine-tuning. (R2)
4. Introduction, related work, and methodology sections are clearly written, providing a solid theoretical background and operational framework. (R2)
5. Clear explanation of the approach and its connection to previous works; novel contributions are clearly explained. (R3)
6. The self-judgment data collection addresses various types of biases by creating about 50 prompts to get the correct judgment as per the model. (R3)
7. Exploring techniques for combining strengths of different learning paradigms through multi-objective optimization is an interesting avenue. (R4)
8. Methods to exploit LLMs as judges are interesting. (R4)
9. The ablation study appears thorough: systematic comparison of various strategies for combining the two objectives (conditional, epoch-by-epoch, and task-by-task) with detailed results reported. (R5)

### Weaknesses
1. **Weak experimental validation and insufficient baselines.**  
   - Only one model (Mistral 7B) and one evaluation dataset (Alpaca Eval) are used, limiting generalizability. Testing on other models (2B–13B) is needed to determine if issues like verbosity are method-specific. (R2)  
   - Missing key baselines: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) model, pretrained model, and established alignment methods (DPO, PPO, other conventional techniques). (R4, R5)  
   - No direct comparison to DPO, despite using ORPO; the choice of ORPO over DPO is not justified. (R1, R4)  
   - The framing as an alternative to iterative fine-tuning (line 97) is not tested; a direct comparison with iterative fine-tuning methods showing similar/better performance with comparable or less data is needed. (R5)

2. **Motivation and framing not well supported.**  
   - The argument that sampling from the model may not be necessary because LLMs are used to judge their own outputs is unclear; verification and generation may differ fundamentally, and the hypothesis is not empirically tested. (R5)  
   - The paper does not clearly explain why the two objectives (alignment performance and self-judgment performance) may be contrary. Providing the loss for self-judgment data on the baseline model would help; if they are always aligned, multi-objective optimization is unnecessary. (R3)  
   - The claimed self-judgment data is not truly self-generated (responses are not from the same LLM), making the “self-judgment” label misleading. (R5)  
   - The CONJOINED method optimizes against the self-judgment objective, which contradicts the multi-objective optimization argument. (R3)

3. **Optimization and training concerns.**  
   - The Pareto frontier in Figure 3 (right) does not smoothly move across scalar weights; solutions are scattered, indicating poor optimization or high random effects (batch order, etc.). Multiple runs or longer training are needed to validate results. (R3)  
   - Numerical stability issues may arise from computing odds ratios with very small probabilities. (R3, Q2)  
   - Why log-sigmoid is used in equation 3 is not explained. (R3, Q1)  
   - It is unclear why some training runs use 2 epochs while others use 3. (R2, Q6)  
   - Equation 8 referenced in line 283 is missing; likely equation 7 for the Conjoined gradient is intended. (R2, R5)  
   - The y-axis label in Figure 3 is missing. (R5)  
   - In equation 6, the frozen weights $\theta'$ are from the same model, but it is unclear whether a different prompt should be used for the self-judgment task versus the alignment task (both use $x$). (R5)

4. **Presentation and clarity issues.**  
   - The paper is hard to follow; the clarity of the presentation and overall pitch needs improvement. (R4)  
   - The experiment and results section reads rushed and lacks examples (e.g., verbosity, overfitting) that could be included in an appendix. (R2)  
   - Verbosity is identified as a significant issue without an applicable solution; more testing on prompt engineering or response format control (e.g., asking for brief responses) could help mitigate the bias. (R2)  
   - The method relies on annotated alignment data; using a small LLM (Mistral-7B) to re-annotate the same data and treat it as separate ground truth is questionable unless done at large scale unsupervised (like Self-Rewarding Language Models). (R3)  
   - Little insight is provided on which factors work and why. (R1)  
   - More baseline methods are needed to demonstrate the choices of the proposed method. (R1)  
   - Multi-objective optimization techniques should support more than two objectives; limiting to two is not justified. (R4)  
   - It is not clear how longer context length might be an issue, given the self-judgment dataset wraps a brief prompt around the longer alignment data. (R2, Q5)  
   - The separation of alignment data into non-overlapping subsets for alignment and self-judgment could mitigate overfitting but is not tested. (R2, Q3)  
   - Pure self-judgment training (w1=0, w2=1) is not tested, which would help contrast with the alignment-only case. (R2, Q4)

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 2 (score 5, positive overall) found the writing clear and the methodology innovative, but still noted several of the above weaknesses. The other four reviewers (score 3) were more critical, especially regarding experimental rigor, motivation, and presentation clarity.
