## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends identifiability results from self-supervised learning (nonlinear ICA) to supervised classification with cross-entropy loss, under assumptions including a von-Mises-Fisher clustering model for the data generating process and uniform instance labels. The authors prove that under these assumptions, the learned representation recovers ground-truth latent factors up to a linear transformation. Empirical validation includes synthetic data (R^2 >98% in many settings), the DisLib disentanglement benchmark (strong Pearson correlations), and ImageNet-X (weaker but positive correlations).

### Strengths
- Presents a new and interesting theoretical result: under Assumptions 1C (Appendix A.2) cross-entropy training yields linearly identifiable representations, extending identifiability from self-supervised learning to general supervised classification.
- The paper is clear and well-written; efforts to make the theory approachable by drawing from practical observations (e.g., linear representation hypothesis, superposition) are appreciated.
- The experiments are appropriate and demonstrate that the theoretical results can hold beyond the restrictive assumptions about the data generating process.
- The empirical framework for validating the hypothesis is clever; the synthetic experiments show that when Assumptions 1 hold, DIET successfully recovers cluster vectors with high probability.

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaiming the generality of the result.** The article (title, abstract, lines 255-256, 304-305, 310-311, 521-525, 534-536) asserts that cross-entropy alone yields linearly identifiable representations, without sufficiently emphasizing that the proof requires strong additional assumptions (Assumptions 1C in Appendix A.2). This claim is too strong and may mislead readers. [Reviewer 3]
- **Empirical validation lacks falsification tests.** The experiments only show success when assumptions hold; the paper does not test what happens when assumptions are violated. Proposed experiments: (1) randomly swap a percentage of samples between two clusters after generation and report recovery statistics; (2) increase the swap percentage to observe a trend; (3) repeat with more clusters involved. Such tests would demonstrate the hypothesis can be falsified and should be easy to run with the current setup. [Reviewer 1]
- **High-dimensional latents cause substantial identifiability degradation even in well-specified settings.** In Table 2/3, when dimensionality exceeds 10 (e.g., d=20), R^2 drops markedly despite all assumptions being met. This is a major practical issue since true latents are often high-dimensional and models are misspecified; additional discussion is needed. [Reviewer 2]
- **Synthetic misspecification examples are not convincing.** Replacing vMF with Laplace already degrades performance substantially, yet this is a mild misspecification. Stronger tests like mixtures of Gaussians with varying numbers of components would better probe the theory’s robustness. [Reviewer 2]
- **Assumption of uniform instance labels is important but underemphasized.** Many real classification tasks have unbalanced labels, yet the paper does not clearly state how this assumption limits applicability. [Reviewer 3]
- **Inconsistency between claim that assumptions can be relaxed and observed low R^2 when assumptions are met.** The paper states (lines 485, 518-519) that linear identifiability seems to hold even when some assumptions are violated, but Tables 2 and 3 show surprisingly low R^2 values for some conditions that should satisfy all assumptions (e.g., N=10³, d=20, 100 classes, vMF κ=10; N=10³, d=5, 100 classes, vMF κ=50). The authors attribute this to “content-style partitioning” and insufficient augmentation overlap, but the explanation is not fully clarified. [Reviewer 3]
- **Weak results on ImageNet-X lack thorough discussion.** Most factors of variation show correlations near chance in Figure 3, and the few that are above chance are still weak. The paper should explain why violations of assumptions cause such substantial reduction in identifiability. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3]
- **Low Pearson correlations in DisLib (Table 4) need interpretation.** Some correlations are below 0.8, and several below 0.6; the paper does not discuss what these values imply for the theory’s practical validity. [Reviewer 3]
- **Missing experimental details for DisLib and ImageNet-X.** The paper should include hyperparameters, data preprocessing steps, evaluation metrics, and any modifications to standard implementations—either in the appendix or via an open GitHub repository—to allow reproducibility and comparison. [Reviewer 1]
- **Unaddressed questions about technical details.** (a) What is the impact of repeated elements in the training data when using the sample-index classification head? (b) Why is a high concentration parameter (κ) so detrimental to identifiability, and does high κ violate an assumption (it is not marked with a red X in the tables)? [Reviewer 2]
- **Typos and formatting issues.** Line 71: extraneous comma after hyphen; Line 457: extra “training” (should read “underlying training classification task was solved”); Line 527: “Conlusion” should be “Conclusion”. [Reviewer 2]
