## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents BrainPy, a differentiable brain simulator built on JAX and XLA, designed to bridge brain simulation and brain-inspired computing (BIC) by providing flexible, efficient, and scalable simulation with automatic differentiation. It introduces sparse and event-driven operators, an abstraction for synaptic computations, modular multi-scale model construction, and object-oriented JIT compilation for memory-intensive dynamics. Benchmarks show efficiency and scalability, and a differentiable biologically plausible spiking model is demonstrated. Reviewers generally find the work interesting and potentially impactful, but opinions on its novelty and fit for ICLR vary significantly (scores: 6, 6, 8, 1, 6). One reviewer (score 8) strongly endorses the work; another (score 1) writes that it is a valuable contribution but appears to mark it down on suitability; the remaining three (scores 6) are positive but note several shortcomings.

### Strengths
- Addresses a clear need for a modern framework that combines biologically realistic brain simulation with differentiability for training (reviewers 2, 3, 5).
- Leverages JAX and XLA to achieve efficient, scalable simulation on CPUs, GPUs, and TPUs with JIT compilation (reviewers 2, 3).
- Demonstrates improvements in speed and memory efficiency over existing brain simulators (e.g., NEURON, NEST) on benchmark tasks (reviewers 1, 3, 5). One reviewer specifically notes gains from exploiting sparsity and event-driven computation (reviewer 4).
- Provides a user-friendly interface and supports multi-scale model construction; the library is in an advanced stage of development (reviewer 2, 5).
- Clear presentation and comprehensive comparisons with existing tools (reviewer 2, 3).
- One reviewer (score 1) states: "This is a very valuable contribution to the world of brain simulation, which seems likely to find a lot of users due to its speed and differentiability" – though this reviewer also assigned the lowest score, implying reservations about conference fit rather than technical merit.

### Weaknesses
- **Fit to ICLR and novelty**: Multiple reviewers question the paper’s relevance to ICLR, citing a lack of original theories or new empirical evidence; the contribution is primarily engineering/system design (reviewers 1, 2). One reviewer (score 1) also appears to share this concern despite high praise.
- **Missing comparisons with BIC libraries**: Quantitative comparisons with deep learning/BIC frameworks such as SpikingJelly are not provided; the unique advantages over existing SNN tools need clarification (reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 2 specifically asks: "How does BrainPy's speed and scalability compare to CARLsim?"
- **Missing large-scale/distributed simulation demonstration**: No demonstration of distributed simulation/training on a CPU or GPU cluster; scaling plots use a linear scale (not orders of magnitude), which does not convincingly show scalability (reviewer 5). Reviewer 5 also notes that the E/I network with 80 synapses per neuron is much sparser than typical biological local connectivity.
- **Insufficient discussion of deep learning integration**: Despite the stated goal of bridging brain simulators and DL frameworks, the paper lacks concrete discussion or examples of how BrainPy connects to modern deep learning models (reviewer 2).
- **Engineering quality and documentation**: One reviewer finds the package more like a collection of course scripts than a proper Python package and recommends improvements in engineering quality and documentation (reviewer 1).
- **Compilation cost not evaluated**: The paper does not measure the cost of JIT compilation versus runtime, how it scales with neuron count, or whether it is a disadvantage compared to tools like NEURON; also unclear if compilation cost depends on CPU vs. GPU (reviewer 3).
- **Memory efficiency claim**: The claimed memory efficiency (section 4.2) appears to assume low-rank connectivity; the paper does not clarify whether gains generalize beyond that assumption. For 86 billion neurons, how exactly is storage handled? (reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also asks whether the JIT weight generation works only for random weights, not for learned plasticity.
- **Differentiability details unclear**: Equation (28) is insufficiently defined: neither *x* nor *spike'* are defined, and it is unclear if *width* is the same as *V_th*. Additionally, do the computational benefits of sparse activity extend to gradients? For example, the derivative w.r.t. a zero weight need not be zero (reviewer 4).
- **Inaccurate claim about hierarchical composition**: The statement that hierarchical composition is not shared by other simulators is false; NetPyNE supports it (reviewer 3).
- **JIT credit unclear**: Section 4.5 reads as if the authors implemented JIT themselves, but all JIT capabilities come from JAX (reviewer 3). This should be clarified.
- **Missing experimental details**: The NEST version used is 2.20 (not the more recent 3.x), and the code producing Fig. 4C was not found in the supplementary materials (reviewer 5).
