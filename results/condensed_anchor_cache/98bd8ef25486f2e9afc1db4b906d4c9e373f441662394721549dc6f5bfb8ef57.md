## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SelfPrompt, a prompt-tuning approach for vision-language models (VLMs) in a semi-supervised learning setup. SelfPrompt introduces three components: (a) weakly-supervised sampling to select a diverse and representative labelled set, (b) cluster-guided pseudo-labelling, and (c) a confidence-aware semi-supervised learning module. Evaluations are conducted on 13 datasets with reported gains of 6.23% in standard semi-supervised learning, 6.25% in active semi-supervised learning, and 4.9% in base-to-novel generalization (2-shot). One reviewer found the performance gains significant and the sampling issue insightful, while other reviewers raised serious concerns about novelty, problem setting clarity, reproducibility, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The method achieves significant performance gains over prior baselines.
- Identifying the sampling issue in existing CLIP few-shot benchmarks is insightful.
- Experiments are extensive, covering 13 datasets, and include thorough ablation studies that help understand design choices.
- The paper is well written and easy to follow.
- Qualitative examples and failure cases are helpful for understanding model behavior and limitations.
- Tuning VLMs in the semi-supervised setting is an important and practical problem.
- Using a limited budget to label important samples is a practical and relevant problem.

### Weaknesses
- **Technical novelty is limited**: The method largely relies on pseudo-label thresholding and selection, which has been well-studied in semi-supervised learning for years.
- **Missing baselines**: Two recent baselines are omitted and should be compared against to claim state-of-the-art: PLOT (Prompt Learning with Optimal Transport) and AWT (Augmentation, Weighting, and Transportation). Additional baselines from base-to-novel generalization are also missing.
- **Problem setting is unclear and potentially mismatched**: The paper states that existing methods “randomly select samples” as a limitation, but in standard SSL the labeled set is given and not controllable—random selection is an experimental choice, not a method limitation. The paper’s setting (selecting which unlabeled samples to label) is different from standard SSL, making direct comparisons to methods designed for standard SSL improper. The authors should clarify the setting and ensure fair comparisons.
- **Motivation for discarding high-confidence samples is questionable**: The claim that “highly confident samples offer minimal information gain” contradicts common practice in SSL, where thresholds are used to select high-confidence samples for training (e.g., [1], [2]). This motivation is not supported by references.
- **Cluster-guided pseudo-labeling still relies on VLM**: The claim that the method “does not rely on the VLM to generate pseudo-labels” (line 253) appears incorrect. VLM probabilities are used to determine samples for clustering (line 253: “does not rely on the VLM to generate pseudo‐labels” – but VLM probabilities are used for sample selection). Moreover, cluster embeddings are derived from VLM pre‐trained features, so bias in VLM pseudo-labels may carry over.
- **Contradiction in handling confidence**: After discarding high-confidence samples as uninformative, the confidence-aware SSL module later uses confident samples for supervised learning. This inconsistency is not explained.
- **k-means with N clusters (n × c) is unexplained**: Clustering into N = number of labeled samples (not C classes) lacks semantic meaning, and the motivation is unclear.
- **Hyperparameters not reported**: The values of hyperparameters p, q, τ, and λ are not provided in the experiment section, harming reproducibility.
- **Many undefined or unclear variables and notation issues**:  
  - In line 264, $(x_{j1}, y_j)$ – what is j?  
  - $X_{\text{weak}}$ is not clearly defined (only stated as $x_i \notin X^+$, but origin of $x_i$ is ambiguous).  
  - $L_{\text{weak}}$ is not defined.  
  - The function $\text{top}_t(\cdot)$ is not defined.  
  - $f$ (or $\theta$) is not clearly introduced; $\lambda$ as a hyperparameter is not defined.  
  - Subscripts are confusing: $s_i$ for a vector but $s_c$ used for a scalar.  
  - Variance in mathematical notations (e.g., $Q_k$ inconsistent across lines 236/238; $P_j$ inconsistent with definition; $X_p$ in Eq. 4 inconsistent with line 264; $X_L$ in Eq. 5 inconsistent).  
  - Input $x_i$ should be in bold; $p_{ic}$ undefined.  
  - $\mathbf{z}^*_j$ and $\| \cdot \|^2$ not defined in line 262.  
  - SOTA is not spelled out initially.
- **Loss function and training process are unclear**: It is not explicitly stated what the loss function in line 282 optimizes. The prompt-tuning pipeline is not formally defined in preliminaries. The reviewer asks: how do the three datasets ($X_L$, $X_+$, $X_{\text{weak}}$) change during tuning? A pseudo-code would improve clarity.
- **Unexplained empirical observations**:  
  - Why does CPT fall short against SelfPrompt under 4-shot experiments (Table 7c)?  
  - If the number of samples is increased beyond 50, performance continues to improve – what are the limits?  
  - Dropping both high and low confidence samples could remove useful information and slow convergence; what is the effect of dropping only one subset?
- **Weakly-supervised learning (WSL) literature is not discussed**: Given that WSL is a key component, related WSL papers should be referenced in the related work.
- **Similarity to previous work**: The confidence-aware SSL module appears similar to [3]; the authors should discuss differences.
- **Realism of setting**: It is unrealistic to assume that we can selectively choose which samples from an unlabeled set to obtain labels for in practice.
- **“Gather the labels of the selected samples” (line 249)**: It is unclear whether these labels are pseudo-labels or ground truth labels.
