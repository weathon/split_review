## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers evaluated the paper proposing SimuDy, a method to reconstruct training data from a DNN's parameters by simulating non-linear training dynamics via nested optimization of a dummy dataset. All reviewers acknowledged the method outperforms prior linear-dynamics baselines and works on deeper models (ResNet-18). However, they expressed significant concerns about the restrictive assumptions (knowledge of initial weights, hyperparameters), high computational cost, limited evaluation scope, and unclear practical implications. One reviewer (score 8) was very positive about the simplicity and impressive results; others were more reserved (scores 5–6), questioning generality and setting validity.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and extension to non-linear dynamics**: The method is the first to reconstruct training data from deep networks like ResNet by simulating non-linear training dynamics, moving beyond linear-regime methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- **Clear writing and simple implementation**: The paper is clearly written and the method is straightforward to implement (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
- **Strong quantitative and qualitative performance**: SimuDy outperforms previous methods (especially Loo et al.) in terms of SSIM and visual quality (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
- **Robustness to unknown hyperparameters**: The method works even when exact training hyperparameters are unknown (Reviewer 1).  
- **Thorough experiments in chosen setting**: Extensive evaluations across dataset sizes, initializations, and dummy dataset configurations were conducted (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic assumptions / lack of threat model**: The method requires prior knowledge of initial parameters, architecture, and hyperparameters (e.g., training steps, batch size). Reviewers 2 and 4 noted this is a restrictive setting for a data reconstruction attack; Reviewer 4 specifically asked for a defined threat model and an explanation of why this particular setting was chosen.  
- **Computational and memory burden**: The method stores the entire training computation graph and backpropagates through multiple steps, leading to high memory and time costs. Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 requested a breakdown of actual reconstruction time and memory requirements; Reviewer 3 highlighted scalability issues for larger models or high-resolution images.  
- **Limited comparison to relevant baselines**: Only Loo et al. was compared. Reviewers 2 and 4 asked for comparisons with Buzaglo et al. and Haim et al. (the first successful data-reconstruction-from-parameters method). Reviewer 4 also suggested comparing with model inversion attacks (or clarifying why they are not comparable).  
- **Missing experiments on diverse datasets and architectures**: Experiments were limited to CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18. Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 requested tests on larger datasets (e.g., full CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, MNIST, ImageNet) and other architectures (other ResNets, Vision Transformers) to assess generalizability. Reviewer 1 also asked about reconstruction with multiple objects in an image.  
- **Unclear influence of regularization and initialization**: Reviewer 2 noted the effect of the TV regularization coefficient on reconstruction quality is not quantitatively analyzed. Reviewer 1 found the dummy dataset initialization (noise / natural images) arbitrary and suggested a systematic study of different initialization strategies.  
- **Overfitted toy setting**: Reviewer 4 questioned whether the success depends on the extreme model-to-data ratio (ResNet-18 trained on only 120 images); this raises doubts about whether memorization or overfitting is a necessary condition. An experiment with a larger training set (e.g., full CIFAR-10/100) was requested, even if only partial reconstruction is achieved.  
- **Limited discussion of practical implications and broader usefulness**: Reviewer 1 noted the lack of discussion on privacy/security or federated learning implications. Reviewer 3 questioned how SimuDy contributes to understanding training dynamics or improving training/interpretability, and felt its usefulness beyond verifying memorization is unclear.  
- **Missing related works**: Reviewer 2 pointed out that Zhao et al. (gradient/trajectory matching via cosine symmetry) and Chijiwa (optimizing cosine similarity of training dynamics) are strongly related but not cited.
