## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Advantage-Aware Policy Optimization (A2PO) for offline RL on mixed-quality datasets. A2PO first trains a CVAE to model action distributions conditioned on both state and the (continuously updated) advantage value, thereby disentangling behavior policies of different quality. The agent then performs latent-space policy optimization (essentially TD3+BC) towards high-advantage actions. Experiments on D4RL single- and mixed-quality datasets show improved performance over baselines like CQL, LAPO, and MOPO. Ablations demonstrate that the policy is responsive to advantage conditioning.

### Strengths

- The paper is easy to read and well-presented; one reviewer rated it in the top 5% for readability and cleanliness. (R1, R2, R4, R5)
- The empirical results on D4RL navigation and locomotion tasks are strong, outperforming several standard baselines. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- A detailed ablation analysis is provided, including a study showing the policy is highly responsive to the given advantage value. (R2, R3)
- The method can sample different actions depending on the specified advantage, enabling intuitive control over policy quality. (R1)
- The handling of the underestimation issue common in offline RL is clean and elegant. (R2)
- The problem of learning from datasets with mixed-quality behavior policies is interesting and relevant. (R4)
- *Minority contrast*: Most reviewers found the results convincing, but Reviewer 5 was far more critical, arguing that the method is heavy, unstable, and not competitive with recent approaches (score 3 vs. 5-6).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons with diffusion-based policies.** Reviewer 1 notes that strong diffusion-based offline RL methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy, IDQL) should be compared. Reviewer 5 adds that recent diffusion policies outperform A2PO, making the heavy CVAE approach less impressive.
- **Missing experiments on manipulation tasks.** Reviewer 2 points out that many baselines show strong performance on FrankaKitchen and Adroit (D4RL); these are omitted and it is unclear if A2PO transfers beyond locomotion/navigation. Reviewer 1 additionally asks about Meta-World and ManiSkill.
- **Unfair comparison with LAPO.** Reviewer 3 notes that the policy optimization step in A2PO differs from LAPO: A2PO uses a TD3+BC-style loss with a behavior cloning (BC) term, while LAPO does not. This introduces a confounding factor; it is unclear whether gains come from advantage conditioning or the additional BC regularization. A direct comparison with LAPO under the same policy optimization framework is missing.
- **CVAE training instability and sensitivity.** Reviewer 5 stresses that the advantage values used as CVAE conditioning are non-stationary (value functions update during training), causing the CVAE to fluctuate. The paper acknowledges that the number of training steps \(K\) needs careful tuning, and Figure 5 shows the method can fail badly with improper \(K\) (Reviewer 1 also raises this issue). Reviewer 1 asks how to determine the optimal \(K\) before training and whether this stage is time-consuming.
- **Limited novelty.** The idea of conditioning on advantage is not new; Reviewer 2 requests a citation to reward-conditioned supervised learning (Brandfonbrener et al., 2023). Reviewer 3 calls the contribution incremental, noting it essentially moves advantage from a loss weight (as in LAPO) to a conditional variable.
- **Omitted recent baselines.** Reviewer 4 points out missing baselines BEAR, AWAC, and SPOT [1]. Reviewer 5 adds that the paper lacks comparisons with in-sample learning methods XQL, SQL, and other 2023 offline RL algorithms, and suggests comparing with representation-learning baselines. Reviewer 5 also criticizes the inclusion of BPPO, which is known to cheat by using online evaluation during offline training.
- **Methodology details unclear.** Reviewer 4 asks how \(\xi^*\) is selected in Equation (7) and what the optimal selected action \(\alpha_\xi^*\) means. Since \(\xi\) is normalized to \([0,1]\), the value \(\xi=1\) may correspond to a single data point in the mixed dataset; it is unclear how the CVAE learns an action distribution from that. Reviewer 4 also finds the pseudocode in Algorithm 1 too limited and requests more important details.
- **Hyperparameter \(\lambda\) omitted and tuning unclear.** Reviewer 5 observes that the real implementation is TD3+BC in latent space, but Equation (8) omits the trade-off parameter \(\lambda\) between max Q and BC penalty. The paper does not discuss how \(\lambda\) is tuned; if carefully tuned, even original TD3+BC could be strong. Reviewer 2 also asks what hyperparameters were tuned for A2PO vs. baselines, given narrow margins.
- **Questionable results on some antmaze tasks.** Reviewer 5 notes that CQL and IQL scores are reported as zero on antmaze-medium-diverse and antmaze-large-diverse, whereas other papers report reasonable numbers. A2PO’s own performance on these tasks looks bad. This discrepancy is not addressed.
- **Mixed-dataset construction not detailed.** Reviewer 4 asks for a clear description of how single-quality datasets were combined to form mixed datasets, and for an ablation on the proportion of each quality type. Reviewer 5 comments that mixing in random data is not practically meaningful.
- **Minor typo.** Reviewer 3 notes that on Page 8, the reference to Figure 4 should be Figure 5.
