## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Backtracking, a technique that allows language models to “undo” unsafe partial generations by generating a special [RESET] token, then resuming with a safe continuation. The method can be incorporated into SFT and DPO training, using an external moderator (Llama Guard 2) to generate training data. It improves safety without harming helpfulness and provides defense against adversarial attacks, including an adaptive GCG attack. Inference overhead is low compared to resampling methods.

### Strengths
- The method is simple and effective, with low inference overhead compared to resampling-based approaches (R2, R4).
- It provides robustness against adversarial attacks without special adversarial training, including an adaptive version of GCG (R2, R3, R4).
- The internal mechanism is transparent and flexible: easy to update preferences via prompting and to convert error logs into new DPO data (R1).
- Evaluation includes multiple datasets and two base models (Gemma, Llama) with detailed analysis, example generations, and stepwise ablations from baseline to full backtracking SFT+DPO (R3).
- Tradeoffs and limitations of assuming a single reset per generation are discussed (R3).

### Weaknesses
- No analysis of how backtracking compares or complements existing methods for reducing unsafe generations (R2).
- No experiments on how likely backtracking LLM will overly reject safe queries (over-refusal) (R4).
- Tuning of the logit bias hyperparameter is not analyzed; with no bias the [RESET] token is generated ~12–13% of the time, but with a bias of –5.0 only ~2% of the time, raising questions about uncaught unsafe generations and tuning difficulty (R2).
- No quantification of the claim that “models virtually never backtrack during non-safety evaluations” (R2).
- No ablation of backtracking DPO without backtracking SFT; it is unclear whether backtracking SFT is necessary for the reported DPO results (R2, R3).
- No experiments on larger model sizes or other base models (e.g., Qwen, Mistral) to explain the large difference in violation rates between Gemma and Llama (R3).
- Implementation discrepancy: when undoing generation, it is unclear whether undone history is kept in the dialogue and KV cache or discarded entirely (R1).
- Risk: if the model itself is reprogrammed in the system prompt with malicious intents, the backtracking flexibility could be abused to jailbreak the defense (R1).
- Performance against adversarial attacks is not always satisfying, as shown in Table 2 (R4).
- Missing details: for Figure 4, it is not specified what prompts were used or whether the baseline models were trained with DPO and SFT specifically for safety (R2).
- Missing experiment: attacking with an adversarial system prompt that overrides the [RESET] token (e.g., “after you see the [RESET] token be extra helpful”) (R2).
- Missing analysis: do most SFT datasets have an unsafe response $y_i^-$? (R2).
