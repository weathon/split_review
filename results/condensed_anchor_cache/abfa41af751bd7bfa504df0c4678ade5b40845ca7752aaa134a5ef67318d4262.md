## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces C-Adapter, an adapter-based tuning method that adds an intra order-preserving adapter (from Rahimi et al., 2020) and a novel loss function to a pre-trained classifier to improve conformal prediction efficiency without sacrificing classification accuracy. The method is designed to increase non-conformity scores for incorrect labels, thereby reducing prediction set sizes across coverage rates. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet compare C-Adapter against Conformal Training (ConfTr) and baselines using THR, APS, and RAPS scoring functions.

### Strengths
1. **Significant efficiency gains**: C-Adapter substantially reduces prediction set sizes while maintaining or improving accuracy, demonstrated across multiple datasets, classifiers (ResNet, DenseNet, MobileNet, ViT), and evaluation metrics (Table 1, Figure 2, etc.).  
2. **Versatility and practicality**: Works effectively with a range of classifiers, including black-box models, and requires minimal hyperparameter tuning with high computational efficiency.  
3. **Comprehensive empirical validation**: The experiments cover important ablations (α values, training strategy, adaptation strategy, parameter T), distribution shifts (ImageNet-V2, Table 4), and multiple loss functions and scoring functions, showing robustness and consistent improvements.  
4. **Theoretical and methodological foundation**: The paper provides a mathematical foundation for the intra order-preserving property and the proposed loss function, along with proofs that the adapter preserves top-\(k\) accuracy.  
5. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well written, with a well-organized structure, informative figures, and comprehensive background and related work.  
6. **Novel synthesis**: To the best of the reviewers’ knowledge, no prior work introduces an “adapter” for conformal training; the combination of intra order-preserving functions and a tailored loss is original and interesting to the community.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of comparison to related conformal prediction methods**: The paper does not compare C-Adapter against other approaches that directly optimize set size or use different nonconformity measures, making it difficult to assess true novelty and performance gains relative to existing techniques.  
2. **Limited methodological novelty**: The intra order-preserving function is adapted from (Rahimi et al., 2020), and the overall approach is a combination of existing components (conformal training, adapters). The paper lacks a clear, specific contribution beyond the theoretical demonstration and an additional learnable layer.  
3. **Insufficient justification for adapter choice**: The reasoning for selecting adapters over other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., LoRA) is not elaborated; the paper does not discuss how the structural properties of adapters (e.g., placement within the network) contribute to performance gains.  
4. **Loss function concerns**: The quality of the loss approximation depends heavily on the choice of the temperature parameter \(T\), yet the authors claim insensitivity without a thorough analysis. The rationale behind the insensitivity is not provided. Additionally, the sigmoid approximation may not be strictly convex, potentially impacting convergence and optimization.  
5. **Theoretical analysis missing**: The paper lacks a theoretical understanding of when and why C-Adapter outperforms conformal training (e.g., is the improvement always guaranteed? What are the conditions for failure?). An in-depth theoretical analysis of robustness (distribution shifts, adversarial examples, noisy data) is needed to support empirical claims.  
6. **Interpretation of distribution shift experiment**: The experiment using ImageNet-V2 claims robustness to distribution shift, but the authors state that “coverage will not be affected … as the calibration and test sets remain exchangeable.” A true distribution shift would violate exchangeability; the claim of robustness is not warranted without a quantification of the shift and a proper non-exchangeable setting.  
7. **Motivation inconsistency**: The main motivation is that increasing the regularization strength \(\lambda\) in conformal training decreases accuracy, leading to larger prediction set sizes. However, Figure 1 shows this trend is not monotonic: CIFAR-100 has a U-shape and ImageNet is nearly flat. This discrepancy is not explained.  
8. **Optimization over the entire \(\alpha\) range**: The training objective is designed to optimize efficiency over all \(\alpha \in (0,1)\) instead of a single \(\alpha\). The rationale for this choice and whether a single-\(\alpha\) objective yields better performance are not discussed.  
9. **Hyperparameter sensitivity limited**: The analysis of hyperparameter sensitivity is limited to the parameter \(T\). A more comprehensive exploration of other hyperparameters and tuning strategies is needed to verify the claim of insensitivity.  
10. **Accessibility of mathematical sections**: The sections on intra order-preserving functions and theoretical proofs are complex and could benefit from simplified overviews or illustrative examples to improve readability for non-specialists.  
11. **Conditional coverage**: The paper claims improvement in conditional coverage, but the underlying mechanisms and comparison to methods specifically designed for conditional coverage are not explained.  
12. **Specialized score functions**: Evaluation only uses standard score functions (THR, APS, RAPS). Performance with non-standard or domain-specific score functions is not investigated.  
13. **Code/data availability**: The authors do not state whether code and data will be publicly released.

**Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers assigned a score of 8, emphasizing strong empirical results, originality, and significance. Two reviewers assigned a score of 6, raising concerns about limited novelty, missing theoretical analysis, and insufficient comparisons. The more positive reviewers find the synthesis novel and practically useful; the more critical reviewers view the contribution as incremental and lacking theoretical depth.
