## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates the conformal isometry hypothesis for grid cells, presenting numerical and theoretical evidence that hexagonal periodic patterns emerge when a minimal recurrent neural network optimizes a conformal isometry loss function. The model is agnostic to transformation models (ReLU, GELU, Tanh) and requires fewer assumptions than prior work. The authors provide theoretical analysis showing that the hexagonal flat torus minimizes deviation from local conformal isometry, demonstrate conformal isometry in real neural data (Gardner et al.), and extend the framework to produce multiple grid modules with approximately √2 spacing.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, self-contained, and clearly explains concepts; the math is thorough.
- The framework is novel and reduces the number of assumptions required for hexagonal patterns compared to prior models (e.g., Gao et al. 2021, Xu et al. 2022).
- The minimal model is simple yet sufficient to produce striking grid cell firing patterns.
- Numerical experiments are convincing, especially the ablation experiments in Fig. 3e–h.
- The core theoretical insight in Section 4.1 is interesting; the theoretical analysis in Sections 4.3, 4.4, and 5.2 is technically sound.
- Robustness of grid cells emerging from optimizing the conformal isometry loss is demonstrated across activation functions.
- The connection between grid scale and the scalar \(s\) in the conformal isometry is explicitly shown.
- Analytical results explain why the hexagonal torus is optimal among flat tori.
- Real neural data (Gardner et al.) are shown to satisfy conformal isometry (Fig. 5, up to \(|\Delta x|=0.2\)).
- The framework is expanded to produce multiple grid modules (Fig. 6, Table 1), yielding organized spacings.
- The RNN approach provides a foundation for further normative models of grid cells.
- Table 1 reports valid rates for different models and shows improved hexagonality.

### Weaknesses

- **Normative explanation**: There is no functional benefit or normative reason why the neural manifold *ought* to be a conformally isometric embedding of physical space (e.g., energy efficiency, decoding accuracy). This makes it hard to interpret the significance of the results—is the match to biology a coincidence or a deeper principle? (R3, R1)
- **Missing comparisons to alternative theories**: The paper should more thoroughly compare the conformal isometry hypothesis with other major theories of grid cell patterns (e.g., oscillatory interference, attractor networks) to position the unique contributions and limitations. Currently only the introduction discusses prior work; a deeper discussion is needed in the main text. (R1, R2)
- **Clarity of optimality argument**: While it is clear why the hexagonal torus is locally isotropic, the claim that it is optimal because it “achieves the least deviation from local flatness by distributing extrinsic curvature evenly” needs more rigorous explanation. A derivation or explicit connection to Cauchy–Schwarz inequality would clarify why other flat tori do not distribute curvature evenly. (R4)
- **Theoretical sections 4.2–4.4**: These sections are confusing and appear to list three separate results when they actually all support the same conclusion (hexagonal flat torus minimizes deviation). Suggest merging these subsections and presenting the main result as a formal proposition, with Section 4.3 as a lemma. The motivation could be clearer. (R1, R3)
- **Multiple modules and conformal isometry**: The extension to multiple modules feels tacked on; the connection to conformal isometry is lost. More discussion is needed on how multiple modules (and the need to decode place responses) affect the representation and how scaling factors relate to isometry. The relationship between multiple modules and decoding at different resolutions should be explicit. (R4)
- **Normalization robustness**: Figure 3f shows that removing normalization (Assumption 3) destroys hexagonal patterns. Real biological networks are likely normalized imperfectly. The paper should investigate robustness under imperfect normalization, e.g., \(1-\varepsilon \leq \|v(x)\| \leq 1+\varepsilon\) for \(\varepsilon>0\), and report how large \(\varepsilon\) can be before grids fail. (R1, R3)
- **Limited activation functions and missing network details**: The study only tests ReLU, GELU, and Tanh. Results with non-monotonic or differently saturating activation functions would assess robustness. The number of network layers is not reported, and the rationale for choosing these specific activation functions is not explained. This makes it difficult to assess model complexity and generality. (R2)
- **Missing ablation on parameters breaking hexagonal patterns**: The paper should include an ablation study where an assumption is violated or a parameter is changed to identify conditions that break hexagonality (e.g., varying \(\lambda\), removing constraints). (R1)
- **Unclear design choices**: 
  - The choice of 1.25 and 3 in Section 3.3 is not explained. (R1)
  - The construction of Fig. 2b(ii) and (iii) needs clarification. (R1)
  - “\(\Delta x\) is constrained to be smaller than 3 grids” – does “grids” refer to the underlying space discretization? Use a different term to avoid confusion with grid cells. (R4)
  - What is “valid rate” in Table 1? (R4)
- **Scale discrepancy between real data and model**: Real neural data (Gardner et al.) satisfy conformal isometry up to \(|\Delta x|=0.2\) (Fig. 5), while the model only shows isometry up to \(|\Delta x|=0.08\) (Fig. 4). The paper should discuss whether this is due to variability in grid properties (Redman et al., 2024) not captured by the model, or other factors. (R4)
- **Neural data preprocessing**: Which module from the Gardner et al. dataset was analyzed in Figs. 5 and 11? None of the modules reportedly had 93 grid cells; was screening performed to select cells? (R4)
- **Minor presentation issues**:
  - Some sentences in the abstract are repeated verbatim in the introduction. (R1)
  - Lines 76 and 86: “our loss function” should be clarified at first mention. (R1)
  - Line 126: “D is the 2D Euclidean domain” need not be restated (already stated on page 2). (R1)
- **Venue concern (Reviewer 3)**: ICLR may not be the optimal venue, as this work primarily targets experimental/theoretical neuroscientists and has little cross-over to machine learning or AI. (R3)
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**:
  - How does the conformal model compare to standard grid cell models (advantages/limitations)? (R2)
  - How does having multiple modules affect the conformal isometry? (R4)
  - What is the impact of \(\lambda\) on the results? (R1)
  - Is the finding on the proportion of pattern scale to parameter \(s\) unique and new? (R1)
  - Could the hypothesis be experimentally tested in biology or inspire new ML architectures? (R2)
