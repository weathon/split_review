## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes WaveToken, a wavelet-based tokenizer for time series forecasting that first normalizes, then decomposes the input via a maximally decimated Discrete Wavelet Transform (Biorthogonal-2.2 family) into approximation and detail coefficients, quantizes them using Freedman-Diaconis binning into a 1024-token vocabulary, and feeds the tokenized coefficients into a T5 encoder-decoder architecture trained with next-token prediction. During inference, it autoregressively generates future wavelet coefficients and inverse-transforms them to obtain forecasts. The model is evaluated on 15 in-distribution and 27 zero-shot datasets, showing competitive performance with a smaller vocabulary than prior methods.

### Strengths

- Introduces a wavelet-based tokenizer that bridges classical signal processing with modern deep learning.
- Achieves comparable or better performance than recent foundation models (e.g., Chronos) using a much smaller vocabulary (1024 tokens vs. 4096 tokens), demonstrating better information density.
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation on multiple datasets (15 in-distribution, 27 zero-shot) across three complementary metrics.
- Includes qualitative analysis using attention maps and ablation studies to provide insights.
- The paper is well-structured, clearly written, and the mathematical formulations and methodological steps are sound.
- The problem of tokenizing continuous, real-valued time series for LLM-style models is well-motivated and important.
- The model is pre-trained on a large corpus of diverse datasets and shows effectiveness in capturing local and global patterns.
- Reviewer 2 is strongly positive (score 8), emphasizing the soundness of the formulations and the importance of the problem.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty / technical contribution:** The core idea of wavelet-based tokenization for large models has been explored before (e.g., Wave-ViT, Zhu et al. 2024), albeit in different domains, and wavelet features are already well-studied in time series analysis. The work combines wavelet-based extraction with an existing model (Chronos) for a specific task (univariate forecasting), offering limited incremental contribution.
- **Marginal performance improvement / overclaims:** 
  - Several reviewers find the performance gains over Chronos marginal (Tables 3, 4). Reviewer 4 notes that wavelet-based features do not improve WQL or MASE in either in-domain or zero-shot settings; in-domain performance even hurts compared to the adapted Chronos. Only the newly introduced VRSE metric shows incremental improvements.
  - The paper claims "excellent forecasting accuracy" and "superior generalization capabilities," but the results do not uniformly support this.
  - Calling it a "foundation model" may be an overclaim given the fixed context length limitation (a pronounced defect for long-term forecasting).
- **Questionable design choices / insufficient justification:**
  - **Thresholding:** Extensive thresholding theory (Section 3.2) contradicts the empirical finding that no thresholding works best in the 8-GPU case. One reviewer questions how aggressive thresholding could obscure subtle patterns and whether this limits robustness across diverse datasets.
  - **Single-level decomposition:** The finding that single-level decomposition is optimal seems to contradict the motivation for using wavelets. Does this undermine the wavelet rationale?
  - **Cross-entropy loss:** Using cross-entropy for next-token prediction on continuous-valued targets is unconventional. How does it guide learning for continuous variables? Comparison with MSE is missing.
  - **Learning autoregressive models on wavelet coefficients:** Wavelet coefficients do not preserve temporal transitions, raising irrationality concerns. The literature suggests learning a diffusion process on decomposition-based coefficients might be more reasonable; no attempt in this direction.
  - **Vocabulary advantage:** The ability to use a smaller vocabulary (1024 vs. 4096) is not strongly justified; more evidence is needed that the wavelet-based vocabulary can be extremely small.
  - **Architecture generality:** Only analyzed for autoregressive models (Chronos); results for other forecasting paradigms (non-autoregressive, patch-based models like TimesFM) or other time series tasks (classification, regression, imputation) are not explored.
- **Missing experiments and ablation:**
  - No ablation comparing against simpler tokenization approaches (e.g., direct quantizing without wavelets) to justify the added complexity.
  - No results reported across multiple random seeds for robustness.
  - No explanation of why the last H observations are used as a test set even for zero-shot benchmarking, instead of a rolling window evaluation.
  - No sensitivity analysis for different batch sizes / computing configurations (single-GPU vs. 8-GPU discrepancy noted).
- **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  - Main text lacks a clear mathematical formulation of the problem setup and wavelet decomposition.
  - Shape information for approximation (\(a_k\)) and detail (\(d_k\)) coefficients is unclear (Line 225).
  - Explanation of length preservation in DWT (Line 227) is unclear.
  - Insufficient explanation of unexpected results for other normalization choices (Line 221).
  - Terminology issues: "language model" is used for a model trained from scratch on time series; "forecasting accuracy" vs. "performance" is used inconsistently; "time-localized frequencies" without brief explanation for broader audience.
  - Grammar: "Both Chronos, TimesFM, and Moirai" (Line 415) should be "Chronos, TimesFM, and Moirai".
- **Reproducibility and limitations:**
  - Hyperparameter tuning configurations (including final settings) are not provided.
  - Code is not released; one reviewer explicitly asks whether it will be released.
  - No explicit limitations section.
- **Other questions from reviewers (included as criticisms):**
  - How does the wavelet-based tokenizer fundamentally differ from prior wavelet tokenizers in handling temporal vs. spatial data (e.g., Wave-ViT)?
  - How does the model perform on long-term forecasting (potentially suffering from multi-step error accumulation like Chronos)?
  - What are the advantages of discrete vocabularies over continuous embeddings (e.g., TimesFM, Moirai) for time series forecasting?
  - Have the authors analyzed the learned vocabulary for patterns that could improve tokenization?
  - What is the ground for the claim that existing models focus more on recent history or uniformly over all steps (lines 78-79), given transformers' attention-weighted focus?
