## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DOI (Diverse Offline Imitation), an offline algorithm for unsupervised skill discovery that maximizes diversity via mutual information between states and skills, while constraining each skill’s state-visitation distribution to be close to expert state demonstrations via a relaxed KL-divergence bound (≤ε). The method combines a variational lower bound (as in DIAYN), DICE-based optimization for the KL constraint, and Lagrangian duality. Experiments are conducted on D4RL benchmarks (Walker2D, HalfCheetah, etc.) and on a 12-DOF quadruped robot (SOLO12) in simulation and in reality, showing a trade-off between skill diversity and task performance. One reviewer (score 5) finds the approach principled and effective; two reviewers (score 3) raise concerns about limited novelty, evaluation, and presentation.

### Strengths
- Proposes a principled offline algorithm for unsupervised skill discovery that jointly maximizes diversity and imitates state-only expert demonstrations, controlled via a KL constraint level ε (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- Connects Fenchel duality, reinforcement learning, and skill discovery to solve the combined objective; uses off-policy algorithms (variational lower bound, DICE, Lagrangian) from prior work to realize the method (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrates the method on standard offline benchmarks (D4RL) and on a custom offline dataset from a 12-DOF quadruped robot, with policies transferred to the real robot (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Real robot deployment videos are made available (Reviewer 3).
- Figure 1 is helpful; the main-text writing is mostly clear and the math is step-by-step (Reviewer 3).
- Empirically shows the diversity-performance trade-off and metrics such as ℓ₂ distance of expected successor features, ℓ₁ distance of importance ratios, and expected difference of importance ratio (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty compared to SMODICE**: The main differences are learning multiple skill-conditioned policies instead of a single policy and relaxing the KL constraint from 0 to ε; this is seen as an incremental change (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 notes that the theoretical lemmas are minor variations over previous DICE works and should be compared more thoroughly.
- **Missing baselines**: No comparison with other state-of-the-art unsupervised skill discovery methods (e.g., DIAYN, CIC). For a fair offline comparison, prior methods could be combined with offline RL (e.g., DIAYN + IQL) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation insufficient**:
  - Metrics are only computed on the offline dataset; online rollouts of learned skills to measure actual visitation distributions are missing (Reviewer 2).
  - No qualitative analysis of the actual behaviors of the learned skills, or demonstration that skills are meaningful and useful (Reviewer 2).
  - Performance comparison with SMODICE (Figure 4b, Tables S2, S3) shows that DOI (ε>0) does not outperform SMODICE (ε=0); experiments testing generalization/robustness where higher diversity could be beneficial are absent (Reviewer 3).
  - Real robot results are not discussed in the experimental section (Section 6) (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation and formulation issues**:
  - The purpose of staying close to expert is not well motivated; explanations with examples would help (Reviewer 2).
  - Using KL divergence penalizes any deviation from the expert distribution, even if that leads to a valid diverse skill – this is especially problematic in offline settings where expert data may not cover all states (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper claims “skill discovery” but assumes a finite set of skills Z that appears predefined, unlike continuous skill spaces in CIC; a clearer definition is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing details and unclear presentation**:
  - Computational complexity of the algorithm is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
  - Definition and initialization of the skill space Z (e.g., is z a one-hot or continuous? what is |Z| for each environment?) are not given (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
  - Algorithm 1 introduces a discriminator c* not mentioned in the main text; Phase 2 describes training π_z* but Section 3.2.1 suggests the policy is trained in Phase 1 (Reviewer 3).
  - Related work is a laundry list with no comparison to DOI (Reviewer 3).
  - Tables S2 (Walker2D) and S3 (HalfCheetah) contain identical entries (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 3a x-axis label (“data”) and color-coding are unclear (Reviewer 3).
  - Several technical questions are posed: convergence analysis? what if algorithm does not converge? (Reviewer 1); how is the reward based on c* used to compute importance sampling ratios? (Reviewer 3); how does DOI differ from Domino on the SOLO12 dataset given the data was generated by a skill-based algorithm? (Reviewer 2).
