## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes STAC, a framework for long-term spatio-temporal dynamics modeling. It combines a frequency-enhanced spatial module (FSM) using FNO and Vision Transformer, an ODE-enhanced temporal module (OTM), and channel attention fusion (IFTM). To preserve long-term information, a cache-based recurrent propagator (CRP) stores previous feature maps. Training uses teacher forcing with Mixup and semi-supervised adversarial learning (SSAL). The authors introduce a new fire dynamics benchmark (FIRE) and evaluate on 14 datasets, reporting superior performance over state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important problem of long-term system dynamics modeling with applications in fluid dynamics, astrophysics, and earth science.
- Introduces a new fire dynamics benchmark (FIRE) that could benefit the community.
- The cache-based recurrent propagator offers a novel perspective on enhancing long-term dependencies by storing historical feature maps.
- Integrates complementary spatial and temporal modeling (ViT, FNO, Neural ODE) with channel attention fusion, and employs effective optimization strategies (teacher forcing, Mixup, adversarial learning).
- Extensive experiments on 14 diverse benchmarks demonstrate effectiveness and generalizability across fluid dynamics, video prediction, and PDE solving.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The framework largely combines existing components: frequency-based spatial modeling (FNO, ViT), an ODE-enhanced temporal module (similar to SimVP v2 but with an ODE solver), and a cache memory mechanism (analogous to the recall gate in E3D-LSTM or other well-developed memory approaches). Reviewers noted that only CRP and IFTM are relatively novel, and the overall contribution is incremental. No clear advantage is shown over using only Transformer or FNO (e.g., FNet showed feedforward layers can perform like FFT; why not use one?).
- **Insufficient ablation study.** Only RMSE on one dataset (Spherical Shallow Water) is reported. A much more detailed ablation is needed to understand each component’s contribution, including: removing FNO or Transformer from FSM; replacing OTM with ConvLSTM or PredRNN; replacing CRP with E3D-LSTM’s recall gate; ablating teacher forcing/Mixup, channel attention, and SSAL individually across multiple metrics and datasets.
- **Missing efficiency comparisons.** No reporting of parameters, FLOPs, running time, or GPU memory for STAC or baselines. SSAL may increase training instability and time complexity, which is not discussed.
- **Incomplete baseline comparisons.** Advanced neural operators such as LSM and U-NO are not included. It is also unclear whether all baselines were trained with the same loss function, which is essential for fair comparison.
- **Weak visual evidence.** Figures 3 and 4 do not show a significant visual improvement of STAC over other methods.
- **Unclear parameter and design choices.** The parameter α in CRP is not explained (is it learnable?). CRP’s similarity to standard RNNs raises questions about parallelization. IFTM separates temporal and spatial processing artificially and merges channels without learnable factors, limiting interpretability and scalability. FSM applies different treatments to the same data and uses direct fully-connected mapping, potentially losing spatial information.
- **Confusing notations.** In Section 4.3, the definitions of input, feature map, and output are hard to match with the subsequent description.
- **Concerns about OTM’s purpose.** The ODE solver is equivalent to a simple rk4 solver; the datasets are temporally regular, so the claim of learning continuous dynamics is not justified.
- **Small gains from auxiliary losses.** The improvements from teacher forcing/Mixup, channel attention, and SSAL are marginal; SSAL may destabilize training.
- **Missing standard deviations.** Experimental results lack reported standard deviations across runs.
