## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends Slot Attention and Invariant Slot Attention to explicitly disentangle shape and texture in object-centric representations. It uses a two-stream architecture: one stream processes a Sobel-filtered (edge-detected) image to encode shape, and the other processes the original image to encode texture, conditioned on shape information. The approach is evaluated on simple synthetic benchmarks, achieving good reconstruction quality and some improvements in object discovery. Reviewers generally find the motivation clear and the disentanglement interesting, but they raise significant concerns about the ad-hoc design, limited novelty, insufficient comparisons with baselines, lack of testing on realistic data, and reliance on a simple edge filter. One reviewer (score 6, high confidence) is substantially more positive, praising the thorough evaluation and the fact that disentanglement improves performance; the other three (score 5, moderate confidence) are more critical, particularly about generalizability and missing related work.

### Strengths
- The motivation to explicitly disentangle shape and texture in object-centric learning is promising and well-articulated (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Using a Sobel filter to remove texture information for the shape encoder is a unique approach (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The experimental evaluation is thorough, with multiple tests that confirm the intended disentanglement; notably, the decomposition appears to improve results over baselines, which is rare for disentanglement methods (Reviewer 4).
- Strong quantitative results on image reconstruction, good results on scene understanding, and qualitative demonstrations of texture transfer (Reviewer 1).
- The latent representations are more interpretable (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is generally clearly written and the figures are good (Reviewers 2, 3).
- It is interesting that weak equivariant constraints still work well here (Reviewer 3).
- The experiment section contains dense comparisons with several baselines (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Ad-hoc architecture:** The method is explicitly hand-designed for shape and texture and cannot be easily extended to other latent dimensions, which limits its significance (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 acknowledges that the approach only *biases* the disentanglement, not enforces it.
- **Texture decoder uses shape information**, reducing applicability outside the current setting (Reviewer 1).
- **Shape decoder relies on a Sobel filter** to remove texture; some benefits may stem from the filter itself rather than the full architecture (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing downstream comparisons:** The paper does not compare with Slot Attention (SA) or Invariant Slot Attention (ISA) on downstream tasks (e.g., the tasks in Figure 2 or benchmarks like the one in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11281.pdf), which is crucial for validating practical utility (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty claim is false:** The statement “no research has been carried out on explicit disentanglement of texture and shape in object-centric learning” is incorrect. D3DP (arXiv:2011.03367) achieves the same goal using Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN); this related work should be discussed and compared (Reviewer 2).
- **Sobel filter analysis lacking:** The paper does not explain how the Sobel filter removes texture, its mathematical basis, or its limitations. It is unclear whether the filter generalizes to real-world textures (e.g., COCO) or high-frequency patterns (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 4 notes that the reliance on simple edge detection is a significant limitation for realistic data (e.g., DINOSAUR features).
- **Limited to simple datasets:** All experiments are on synthetic datasets with smooth, near-monochrome textures (e.g., CLEVR). Harder benchmarks such as ObjectsRoom, ClevrTex, or WaymoOpen are not tested, which is necessary to demonstrate real-world applicability (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 echoes this concern.
- **Disentanglement degrading on CLEVR:** At the end of Section 5.2, the reported disentanglement already degrades on the slightly more complex CLEVR scenes, raising robustness concerns (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation: how crucial is the edge filter?** No experiment shows performance without the Sobel filter or with varying filter quality (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work omission:** Lorenz et al. (unsupervised landmark detection) uses a nearly identical two-stream information bottleneck and should be more thoroughly discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Rotation invariance removed:** Why was rotation invariance (present in ISA) removed from the experiments? This decision needs justification (Reviewer 1).
- **Position/scale disentanglement not analyzed:** Are the shape and texture dimensions truly disentangled from position and scale? To what extent were position and scale varied in the experiments? (Reviewer 1)
- **ISA necessity unclear:** It is not clear whether basing the approach on ISA is necessary or simply a modeling choice; an ablation would help (Reviewer 4).
- **Generative experiments incomplete:** What happens when sampling shapes instead of textures? (Reviewer 4) – this missing experiment limits the demonstration of controlled generation.
- **Variance regularization formulation unclear:** Is variance computed across feature dimensions (like LayerNorm) or across batch elements (like BatchNorm)? The paper should clarify (Reviewer 3).
