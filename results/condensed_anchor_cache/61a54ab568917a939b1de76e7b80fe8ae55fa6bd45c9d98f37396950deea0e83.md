## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers assessed the paper, which reinterprets caching methods like Tip‑Adapter from a kernel perspective and proposes a training‑free method ProKeR (Proximal Kernel ridge Regression) that introduces proximal regularization in an RKHS. All reviewers acknowledge the novel theoretical angle and competitive few‑shot results on 11 datasets. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, emphasizing the theoretical grounding and computational efficiency; the other three (scores 5) raise concerns about theoretical rigor, writing clarity, missing comparisons and experiments, and practical trade‑offs. The reviewers differ on presentation quality (one finds it clear, another confusing) and resource efficiency (one says memory/computation‑efficient, another points to 3× inference time vs. GDA).

### Strengths
- **Theoretical foundation**: Provides a kernel‑based reinterpretation of Tip‑Adapter as a modified Nadaraya‑Watson estimator, offering a new understanding of training‑free caching methods (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The derivation (e.g., connection to kernel ridge regression) is detailed (Reviewer 2).
- **Incorporation of global information**: ProKeR addresses biases present in local nonparametric methods like Tip‑Adapter by introducing a global proximal regularizer in RKHS, balancing local adaptability and global regularization to prevent overfitting (Reviewers 1, 4). This is a key insight (Reviewer 3).
- **Competitive performance**: ProKeR achieves state‑of‑the‑art results on standard few‑shot classification benchmarks across 11 datasets under the training‑free setting (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 notes “competitive performance on most few‑shot datasets”; Reviewer 4 confirms “state‑of‑the‑art for training‑free few‑shot adaptation under two settings.”
- **Efficiency claim**: Reviewer 1 highlights that ProKeR is memory‑ and computation‑efficient, leveraging Random Fourier Features and a closed‑form solution in kernel ridge regression to reduce resource demands. (Note: Reviewer 4 counters with a 3× inference time vs. GDA – see Weaknesses.)
- **Clear presentation (minority view)**: Reviewer 4 states the overall presentation is clear and easy to follow. (Reviewer 2 strongly disagrees – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Theory rigor and justification**:
  - The claim that predictions close to the zero‑shot predictor are beneficial (lines 201, 277–279) lacks theoretical evidence. Reviewer 2 contrasts with AMU‑Tuning, which does not enforce proximity but achieves ~5% higher accuracy on ImageNet before fine‑tuning, suggesting CLIP’s zero‑shot prediction may be sub‑optimal. The paper does not resolve this contradiction.
  - Approximations (e.g., discretization from eq. (6) to eq. (7)) are not accompanied by error analysis, which may be critical in low‑data regimes (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 finds the relation between eq. (2) and eq. (3) unclear (missing denominator in eq. (2)).
- **Missing comparisons and experiments**:
  - No comparison with recent related methods: DMN (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024) is mentioned by Reviewers 1 and 3; AMU‑Tuning (Reviewer 2) is also absent.
  - ProKeR is only evaluated for training‑free few‑shot adaptation. It is not tested for zero‑shot adaptation (Reviewers 1 and 4). Reviewer 1 asks whether the method can operate without few‑shot samples; Reviewer 4 asks for extension to fine‑tuning (like Tip‑Adapter‑F).
  - No base‑to‑new generalization results (as done in GDA) to assess CLIP’s generalization ability after adaptation (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 5 omits results for GDA, making direct comparison incomplete (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and clarity**:
  - The paper introduces many components (RBF, LLR, RKHS, RFF, Polynomial, etc.) without a high‑level overview or clear pipeline; core contributions are obscured (Reviewer 2). Some terms like RKHS lack introduction or contextual support (Reviewer 2). The citation format uses numbers, not author‑name as required by ICLR style (Reviewer 3).
  - The distinction between “global” and “local” is confusing: Tip‑Adapter already uses all training samples, which seems global; the paper’s explicit definitions are unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Reproducibility and implementation details**:
  - Hyperparameter values (e.g., λ) are not reported in the implementation section; the method of hyperparameter selection on ImageNet (which has no validation set) is not clarified (Reviewer 4). “Validation shots” is ambiguous – is it a validation set or shots from it? (Reviewer 4).
  - It is unclear whether experiments were run 3 times (following CoOp’s protocol) and how data augmentation was applied; no code is provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Performance and practical trade‑offs**:
  - ProKeR surpasses GDA by only a small margin on CoOp’s datasets but requires nearly 3× inference time (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 notes the mechanism is complex and resource consumption is non‑competitive, yet performance advantages over SOTAs are not obvious.
  - ProKeR appears sensitive to kernel choice; Reviewer 4 asks for discussion on why specific kernels work better.
  - A comprehensive component ablation (e.g., effects of RFF, global regularizer, kernel types) is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The analysis of λ is placed in the appendix; Reviewer 4 recommends moving it to the main paper and using actual λ values instead of just λ labels in the plot.
- **Minority disagreement on efficiency**: While Reviewer 1 claims efficiency, Reviewer 4 reports 3× inference time vs. GDA, and Reviewer 2 describes resource consumption as non‑competitive. This contrast is noted.
