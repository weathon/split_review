## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates the impact of atypical video data (sci-fi, animation, unintentional actions, anomalies) on representation learning for open-world discovery, specifically out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. The authors collect a new dataset of ~5,486 videos across four atypical categories, then fine-tune a ResNet3D-50 (pre-trained on UCF-101) using an outlier exposure loss (Hendrycks et al.) to encourage uniform predictions on atypical samples. OOD test performance is evaluated on HMDB-51 and MiT-v2 using maximum softmax probability thresholding. Results indicate that combining Oops (unintentional actions) with either sci-fi or animation data marginally improves OOD detection over Kinetics400 as an outlier source, and increasing category diversity further boosts performance.

### Strengths

- Well-written and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 3).
- First paper to introduce a dataset containing atypical videos in sci-fi and animation categories (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments show that combining Oops data with sci-fi/animation gives better OOD results than Kinetics400 as an outlier source (Reviewer 4).
- Ablation studies on how different outlier sources individually contribute to accuracy (Reviewer 3).
- Tests a known strategy (outlier exposure) from text/image domains on video classification, demonstrating it also works with the new dataset (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and narrow contribution**: The method applies known techniques (Hendrycks outlier exposure) to existing/public datasets; the proposed “atypical” dataset is mostly a combination of public data (sci-fi/animation videos collected from YouTube/Hollywood, but cannot be redistributed by third parties). The contribution is minimal—only showing OOD detection in a very specific setting (ResNet3D-50, UCF as in-distribution, HMDB/MiT as OOD test). Does not rise to ICLR novelty level (Reviewers 3, 4). Overclaimed: paper claims contribution to “visual representation learning in the open world” but only shows OOD results (Reviewer 4). Missing comparison to prior “known unknown” work (Bendale & Boult, CVPR 2016) (Reviewer 3).

- **Insufficient experiments**: Only fine-tune vanilla ResNet3D-50 with one in-distribution dataset (UCF-101). Need extensive experiments on stronger models (e.g., transformers) and more in-distribution datasets (Kinetics400, etc.) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). No evaluation on open-set recognition (OSR) or trade-off on known-class accuracy (Reviewer 3). Does not explore advanced OOD detection methods beyond maximum softmax probability (Reviewer 2). Figures 4 and 5 shown as redundant (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing quantitative rigor**:  
    - All combinations of atypical categories (4 choose 2, 3, etc.) are not reported; only some random pairs/groups are shown, making results not meaningful (Reviewer 2).  
    - Mean results across different test datasets (HMDB, MiT) are averaged despite different distributions—invalid (Reviewer 2).  
    - Table 3, 4, 5 and Figures 4, 5 often lack baseline performance, causing severe readability issues (Reviewer 2).  
    - No individual data source evaluation in Table 3—only pairs (Reviewer 4).  
    - Dataset sizes are not uniform across comparisons; Oops is much larger than other categories, so performance difference could be due to scale, not content (Reviewer 4).  
    - Gaussian noise dataset is not described: which original dataset was used, how noise amount was determined (Reviewers 2, 4).  
    - Diving48 outlier introduced inside experiments section without prior explanation (Reviewer 4).  
    - Effect of fine-tuning with Gaussian noise, Diving48, and K400 is poorly explained; Diving48 alone already outperforms the proposed atypical dataset, invalidating its effectiveness (Reviewer 2).  
    - No convergence analysis; results in Fig. 4, 5 show increasing categories improves performance, but no rationale for stopping at 4 categories or exploring larger sets (Reviewer 1).  
    - Training on more data (atypical + UCF) is an unfair comparison with methods that use only UCF (Reviewer 3).

- **Lack of insight into core atypical data**:  
    - No clear definition of quality (distribution, quantity, categories, attributes) for atypical data; no experimental conclusions on this (Reviewer 1).  
    - Sci-fi and animation are non-existent in real-world scenarios; claiming such data generalizes open-world OOD is unsupported by quantitative evaluation—fine-tuning on only these categories gives *worse* performance than baseline (Figure 4, Table 4) (Reviewer 2).  
    - Dataset statistics incomprehensive: missing explanation of how unintentional and abnormal videos were selected from existing datasets, how frames were sampled, why unintentional category has many more videos/longer duration (Reviewer 2).  
    - Dataset is too small (<6k videos) to support representation learning claims (Reviewers 1, 4).  
    - No protocol for automatically selecting useful OOD training data; cannot generalize to other scenarios (Reviewer 4).  
    - No exploration of how to avoid overlapping activities between in-distribution and atypical datasets (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing important details**:  
    - Equation 2 (L_OE) has undefined elements; missing citation to Lee et al. (ICLR 2018) who introduced the loss (Reviewer 3).  
    - Notation incomplete/inconsistent; table captions do not define symbols (Reviewer 2, 4).  
    - Not clear how outlier samples are combined with in-distribution samples during fine-tuning (e.g., sample balancing, ratio) (Reviewer 4).  
    - How “atypical-n” categories (n=2,3) are selected—any motivation for chosen combinations? (Reviewer 2).  
    - No discussion of whether OOD class frequency in test set matters (Reviewer 3).  
    - The paper is video-specific but methods are agnostic; why focus solely on video? (Reviewer 1).  
    - Figure 4 missing horizontal lines (Reviewer 3).  
    - Writing quality low with grammatical mistakes, convoluted phrasing, and low presentation (organization, caption completeness) (Reviewer 4).  
    - Overview of existing video datasets misses large-scale efforts like WebVid (Reviewer 4).  
    - Line 269 claim that something is “difficult” but then suggest it is possible—unclear for real-world application (Reviewer 3).

- **Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (R1, R2) gave rating 3 and acknowledged potential; two (R3, R4) gave rating 1, strongly questioning novelty, experimental rigor, and dataset contribution. The more positive reviewers still noted insufficient experiments and lack of insight. No reviewer defended acceptance.
