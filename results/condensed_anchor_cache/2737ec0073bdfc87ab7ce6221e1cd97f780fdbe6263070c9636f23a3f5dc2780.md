## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies stochastic contextual bandits with general reward function spaces and time-varying graph feedback. It proposes the FALCON.G algorithm that uses inverse gap weighting on a carefully selected exploration set (dominating set) to achieve improved regret bounds. The authors claim both problem-independent upper bounds of \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\delta T})\) (where \(\delta\) is the averaged expected domination number) and problem-dependent bounds, along with a matching lower bound \(\Omega(\sqrt{\delta T})\). Two options for constructing the exploration set are presented: Option 1 uses an independence set, Option 2 uses a dominating set. Experiments compare against FALCON and show regret scaling with graph parameters rather than action set size.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and motivation is clearly stated. The extension of contextual bandits with general function spaces and graph feedback is a well-motivated and interesting question (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The paper provides a thorough comparison with three closely related prior works, and the authors have made a clear effort to improve upon existing results (R1, R2).
- The algorithm is theoretically analyzed: matching upper and lower bounds are proven (under Option 1 for the independence number \(\alpha\)), and the dependence on the graph parameter \(\delta_f\) in both bounds adds strength to the tightness argument (R1). 
- The paper explores both Option 1 (independence set) and Option 2 (dominating set) for constructing the exploration set, analyzing the resulting exploration–exploitation trade-off, which is an interesting structural contribution (R1, R2).
- The writing is clear and accessible, with a good balance of intuition and formal proofs (R1).  
  *However, two reviewers (R2, R3) found the paper not well-structured, with notation scattered and the flow difficult to follow; this disagreement on presentation is noted.*
- The problem considered is important and the motivation is clearly stated (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Correctness of the \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\delta T})\) claim.** One reviewer (R3) strongly contests the main claimed result of an \(O(\sqrt{\delta T})\) regret upper bound for Option 2. The reviewer argues that this claim is incorrect because it breaks known lower bounds even in the non-contextual setting (e.g., the star graph yields \(\Omega(\min(\sqrt{KT}, T^{2/3}))\) but the paper asserts \(O(\sqrt{T})\)). The reviewer points to a specific technical error: Lemma B.2 and B.3 consider the policy on \(\Psi(S_t)\), but the regret benchmark may not lie in this set, which invalidates the analysis. Furthermore, the detailed proof for the dominating-number-based bound is omitted from the appendix; only a brief explanation is given on page 6. Other reviewers (R1, R2) also flagged that the notion \(\delta_f\) (fractional dominating number) is not properly defined and the proof of the \(\delta_f\) bound is not provided. This is a severe weakness: the central claimed improvement (switching from independence number to dominating number) lacks rigorous support.
- **Novelty and relation to prior work.** The upper bound for Option 1 (independence number) is not new; it essentially follows from Proposition 4 of Zhang et al. (2023) for self-aware undirected graphs (R3). The idea of applying inverse gap weighting to a selected exploration set is described as quite natural/obvious, and it is unclear where the novelty lies beyond the proof aspects (R2). The paper should more clearly highlight the new algorithmic ideas and their necessity.
- **Missing experiments and comparisons.** The experiments only compare against FALCON, not against Zhang et al. (2023) or other graph-feedback contextual bandit algorithms. The authors should explain why the latter are omitted (R2). Reviewer 1 asks whether real-world or high-dimensional datasets were tested and whether there are scalability issues. The simulations could be strengthened by including baselines from the related works (R1 Q4).
- **Clarity and structure.** The paper is poorly structured (R2, R3). Table 1 and Table 2 use notation that is not introduced in the main text, forcing readers to reconstruct definitions. Section 1.2 discusses “first and second options” before they are defined. A simplified summary of the theorem before the comparison would help (R2). Additionally, Theorem 3.1’s phrasing is confusing: it says both “expectation of regret is taken w.r.t. all kinds of randomness” and “with high probability” – the specific randomness of the high-probability event and how it differs from the expectation needs clarification (R1 Q1).
- **Need for justification of “two options.”** Presenting two separate options for ConstructExplorationSet seems unnecessary. Option 2 is recommended and superior in both theory (improvement from independence to dominating number) and experiments, but the authors do not explain why Option 1 is retained or why they cannot be merged into one procedure (R2). The necessity of forced exploration (as opposed to a UCB-type approach) is also not justified; the paper should discuss why constructing valid confidence bounds is challenging in this setting (R1).
- **Questions about the offline oracle.** The regression oracle used in FALCON.G resembles batch regression rather than true offline regression. The paper should clarify whether the oracle requires access to data from the current round (R1). The computational complexity of solving the regression problem and how it scales with the function class size and number of arms should be discussed (R1).
- **Fundamental nature of \(\delta_f\).** The graph parameter \(\delta_f\) (fractional dominating number) is central to the claimed improvement, but it is unclear whether it is an inherent property of the problem or merely an artifact of the algorithm’s design and proof methodology. The paper should provide more intuition and discuss whether other algorithms could achieve similar performance without relying on this specific parameter (R1). 
- **Minor issues and missing details.** The definition of the set \(S_t\) is missing (R2 Q5). The paper uses undirected feedback graphs; can the results be extended to directed graphs, as considered in Zhang et al. (2023)? (R2 Q4). The authors prefer high-probability bounds, but most prior works give expected regret; it would be useful to discuss whether expected regret can be easily derived from the high-probability result (R2 Q3). The reviewer also notes that for Wang et al. (2021), the upper bound can be expressed in terms of the independence number \(\alpha(G_t)\) and only requires knowing an upper bound on \(\alpha\), which mitigates the “criticism” on page 4 (R2 Q1).
