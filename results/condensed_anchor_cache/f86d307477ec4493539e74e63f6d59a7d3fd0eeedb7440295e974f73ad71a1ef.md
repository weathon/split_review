## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes State Transformer (STR), a causal transformer that reframes motion prediction and planning for autonomous driving as a unified sequence modeling task. The model processes context (map elements, past trajectories), proposals (e.g., intention points), key points (locations at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 seconds), and future states (full 10 Hz, 8‑second trajectory) via dataset‑specific encoders (ResNet18 for NuPlan, MTR vector encoder for WOMD) and an autoregressive decoder with diffusion for key points. Evaluations on NuPlan and WOMD show that larger variants follow scaling laws with dataset and model size, but WOMD performance is below SOTA. Most reviewers agree on strong novelty in scaling analysis but find the writing unclear, the model overly complex, the experiments incomplete, and the framing as pure sequence modeling unconvincing.

### Strengths
- Motion prediction and planning are important problems; explicitly connecting the two tasks is valuable for the community (R1, R3).
- First attempt to scale trajectory forecasting to significantly larger models (e.g., 125M, 1.5B parameters) and one of the first demonstrations of an autoregressive transformer for motion forecasting (R2).
- Scaling plots (model size vs. validation loss, dataset size vs. loss) are novel and provide useful guidance for future data/compute scaling (R1, R2, R4).
- Demonstrates the usefulness of the NuPlan dataset for larger‑scale research; outperforms all baselines on NuPlan (R2, R3, R4).
- Experiments convincingly show improved performance with increasing dataset size and model size, indicating potential for further scaling (R2).
- Sufficient originality and significance; story captures popular trends; generic and scalable architecture (R4).
- Achieves commendable results on large public datasets (R4).
- Able to conduct motion planning and prediction simultaneously, capturing complex semantic information (R3).
- Leverages temporal information by formulating motion tasks as sequential prediction problems (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and organization:** The model description is too general, often listing what *could* be implemented rather than what was actually used (e.g., “Proposals can be intention points, goal points, or endpoint heat maps”; “STR is compatible with GMM or diffusion decoders”) (R2, R3). This makes it hard to understand the exact architecture. Notation mismatches exist (e.g., between \(s^F\) and \(s^T\) in §3.1) (R1). The adaptation from MTR is not clearly stated; the training pipeline (e.g., training an autoregressive regression model, then freezing the encoder and training a diffusion‑based keypoint decoder, then training an upsampling head) should be explicitly described (R1). The introduction of conditional diffusion in §3.2 lacks a tight logical connection to §4 where diffusion is barely mentioned (R3). The “decoders” section (§5.2) is hard to follow – terms like “stacked MLPs”, “additional MSE loss”, “distance regression loss”, and “classification scores” are undefined (R1). The models for NuPlan and WOMD should be described separately (R2). Several reviewers recommend removing the diffusion component to simplify the paper and isolate the scaling result (R2, R1).
- **Motivation for design choices:** Using a causal mask for the historical trajectory encoder is not motivated; full attention across time (as in Wayformer) should be more parameter‑efficient, especially when history is only partially observed (R1). Similarly, the autoregressive diffusion process (generating key points one‑by‑one) is not justified – diffusion models normally predict all future points simultaneously (R1). The use of a causal transformer throughout is questioned because there is no sampling step except during keypoint generation (R2). If the goal is to improve stability of diffusion, the paper should emphasize that rather than framing the work primarily as “sequence modeling” (R1). The independence approximation for multi‑agent future trajectories is not acknowledged (§3.1) (R1).
- **Missing ablations:** No ablation quantifies the benefit of the keypoints module (R4). No comparison of GMM vs. diffusion decoder for keypoints is provided (R4, R1). The scaling plot (Figure 2) does not compare against a baseline with a full‑attention encoder and all‑at‑once diffusion, which would test whether the design choices improve scalability (R1). For planning, closed‑loop evaluation is absent – this is critical for a planning component and its omission is a severe gap (R4, also alluded to by R1).
- **Experimental limitations and metric issues:** Performance on WOMD is well below state‑of‑the‑art: Table 2 shows minADE and minFDE significantly worse than MTR/MTR++ (R1, R4). The reported WOMD metrics for SceneTransformer are incorrect – the paper averages over t=3,5,8 seconds, while SceneTransformer reports only at t=8 seconds, making the comparison unfair (R4). Only MTR‑e2e is compared on WOMD, missing comparisons to MTR and MTR++ which achieve mAP >0.40 (R1). On NuPlan, the 125M model is best; scaling to 1.5B does not yield significant improvement, which weakens the scaling claim (R2). No scaling analysis is shown for WOMD motion (R4). Qualitative results show larger models produce better trajectories, but quantitative trends are weak (R2). The abstract claims the model “extend[s] beyond the horizon of 8 seconds” but no such experiment is presented (R1).
- **Novelty concerns:** Applying transformer and diffusion to motion prediction/planning is not new; the encoder‑decoder architecture that takes context and predicts future states is similar to MPNet and other prior work (R3). The core novelty appears to lie in improving diffusion stability rather than in the sequence‑modeling framework itself (R1). The framing as a “large trajectory model” is undercut by the heavy reliance on dataset‑specific encoders and a separate diffusion decoder, which differ from the simple token‑level approach of LLMs (R2).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R4) is the most critical, downgrading their assessment to “weak reject” due to the weak experimental support (inflated metrics, missing closed‑loop planning, missing WOMD scaling) and urges the authors to correct the SceneTransformer numbers. A minority of reviewers (R2, R3) rate contribution lower (2) while R1 rates it higher (3). All reviewers have fair to good confidence (3).
