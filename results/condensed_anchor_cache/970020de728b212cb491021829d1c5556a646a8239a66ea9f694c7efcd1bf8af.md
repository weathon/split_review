## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MD3DQN, an end-to-end reinforcement learning model for traffic signal control that takes raw surveillance video (from CARLA simulator) and outputs phase-change decisions using a multi-agent turn-based module with an Entropy Attention mechanism. The system is evaluated on a single four-approach intersection under clear, rain, and fog conditions. It reports cumulative reward improvements of 85.2% over Fixed-time 40 and 54.4% over DQN-VTP, with a 41.8% ablation gain from the entropy mechanism. The work aims to reduce the sim-to-real gap by using raw camera images rather than structured traffic statistics.

### Strengths
- Novel end-to-end pipeline that processes raw images directly for traffic signal control, addressing the sim-to-real gap (reviewers 1,2,3,4,5).  
- Integration of computer vision and reinforcement learning for a practical real-time application (reviewer 2).  
- Entropy Attention mechanism improves decision-making by leveraging uncertainty and phase timing delays, showing clear ablation gains (reviewers 1,3,5).  
- Multi-agent decision module per traffic phase provides a structured control design (reviewer 5).  
- Problem formulation is clear and well-structured (reviewers 1,2,4).  
- The use of video feeds (even simulated) aligns with real-world surveillance setups, facilitating transfer (reviewer 2,5).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evaluation scope:**  
  – All results are from a single run; multiple trials with error bounds are needed (reviewers 3,4,5).  
  – Only one simple single intersection is tested; complex scenarios (different structures, phase settings, multiple intersections, varying traffic densities, real-world datasets) are missing (reviewers 2,3,4,5).  
  – Only two baselines (Fixed-time 40 and DQN-VTP) are compared; SOTA RL methods (FRAP, MPLight, Max Pressure) and non-RL methods like Max Pressure are absent (reviewers 2,3,4,5).  
  – Evaluation relies solely on reward; objective metrics (queue length, stop time, stability) are lacking, and Table 2 shows high variance under weather conditions (reviewer 3).  
  – Only weather conditions are varied; different traffic flow patterns and densities are not considered (reviewer 5).

- **Incomplete and missing ablations:**  
  – The visual processing module is not ablated against a non-visual solution using statistical inputs; the advantage over existing methods that use density or pressure is not shown (reviewers 1,5).  
  – Only the entropy attention is ablated; no ablation of the bridge module, multi-agent design, or image feature extractor choice (reviewers 1,5).  
  – The impact of the end-to-end gradient flow (whether decision module gradients update the image processor) is unclear (reviewer 1).

- **Methodological clarity and detail deficits:**  
  – Image features from the perception module are not specified (hidden vector or BEV map?) (reviewer 1).  
  – The relation between ResNet, YOLO, and ViT in Section 4.3 is unclear: do they produce the same feature format? How do α_sp, α_tmp relate to F_sp and F_tmp? (reviewer 1).  
  – Subscript j in the phase agent notation is undefined (reviewer 1).  
  – “Phase timing distance” is not clearly defined; is it always 5 seconds? (reviewer 1).  
  – The design of f_Q in line 301 is not explained (reviewer 1).  
  – Sequence phase delay and the reward weight definitions (r1–r4) are missing (reviewer 4).  
  – It is not explained how to draw the specific conclusion from Figure 8 (MD3DQN-res with entropy under rain vs without entropy under fog) (reviewer 4).  
  – Pretraining without images (Section 5.1): does the bridge module or entropy attention still apply? (reviewer 1).  
  – The difference between the proposed method and the DQN-VTP baseline is not explicitly stated (reviewer 1).  
  – No report of computational/storage costs or real-time latency, critical for deployment (reviewer 5).

- **Limited novelty perception (minority contrast):**  
  – Reviewer 3 (score 1) views the components (advantage DQN, attention mechanisms) as all previously proposed, with little incremental innovation. Other reviewers (1,2,4,5) found the end-to-end vision approach original.  
  – The model’s action space (only 4 phases, 2 actions) and fixed 5-second green durations are simplistic and may not handle real-world complexity (reviewers 2,5).  
  – Control is restricted to a single intersection; multi-intersection coordination is not addressed (reviewer 3).  
  – The paper does not convincingly justify why an end-to-end system is superior to a modular pipeline (e.g., estimate density/visibility first, then decide) (reviewer 1).

- **Presentation and reproducibility issues:**  
  – Figure quality is poor (reviewer 4).  
  – Writing and organization need improvement (reviewer 4).  
  – Code is not provided (reviewer 4).  
  – More related work on TSC problem formulations and existing RL methods is needed (reviewer 4).  
  – Only SUMO is used; advanced simulators could provide richer insights (reviewer 2).  
  – Raw images come from CARLA, not real sensors; this limits direct validation of the sim-to-real claim (reviewer 4).
