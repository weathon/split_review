## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RelitLRM, a feed-forward transformer model that generates relightable 3D Gaussian splatting representations from 4–8 sparse posed images captured under unknown static lighting. Geometry is predicted per-pixel by a transformer, then a diffusion-based appearance generator combines geometry features with a target environment map (HDR) and noisy target views via self-attention; after denoising, only appearance tokens are kept and decoded into spherical harmonics for the Gaussians. The model is trained end-to-end on synthetic multi-view renderings under varied known illuminations. Rasterization yields novel views under arbitrary lighting.

### Strengths
- Pseudocode in A.1 clarifies the pipeline effectively.
- The diffusion-based approach is exceptionally fast compared to per-scene optimization methods.
- Trained on a rich dataset with a huge variety of lighting conditions.
- Lighting input is a simple environment map, making relighting straightforward.
- The end-to-end transformer architecture for relightable reconstruction is original.
- The method models geometry and rendering independently (like NeRF) and is practical, requiring only 4–8 views without per-scene optimization.
- On both synthetic and real-world datasets (Stanford-ORB, Objects-with-Lighting, TensoIR), it offers competitive relighting results with far fewer input views than optimization-based baselines.
- The task is important for downstream applications (AR/VR, robotics).

### Weaknesses
- **Ambiguity/decomposition claim overblown:** The paper suggests it resolves the ambiguity between lighting and shading, but it does **not** produce an explicit reflectance decomposition. The method estimates the product of light and reflectance (view‑dependent SH), not intrinsic material properties. This should be clarified in the related work and introduction. (R1)
- **Architecture efficiency and design:** The model uses self‑attention across all modalities (geometry tokens, environment map tokens, noisy image tokens) and then discards most tokens after the final transformer block. The reasoning for this design (versus cross‑attention) is not justified. This inefficiency wastes model capacity and may limit feature extraction for the environment map. (R2)
- **Lack of attention analysis:** No visualization or analysis of the transformer’s attention maps is provided. It is unclear whether the model attends to relevant regions in the environment map (e.g., for specular effects) or how it processes the different modalities. (R2)
- **Qualitative issues and shadow inaccuracies:** In the results, the Pepsi can exhibits incorrect color and blurry text compared to NVDiffRec-MC and ground truth. For the Lego/hotdog examples, the predicted shadows differ from ground truth, while TensoIR and InvRender correctly capture the shadows. These issues suggest the model struggles with fine details and accurate shadow casting. The authors do not systematically analyze why this happens (e.g., whether increasing views to 16 would help, as they claim performance saturates around 16). (R2)
- **Missing important baselines:** The paper omits comparison with IllumiNeRF and “A Diffusion Approach to Radiance Field Relighting” (EGSR 2024). IllumiNeRF directly tackles the same task and outperforms RelitLRM on both Stanford-ORB and TensoIR benchmarks (e.g., PSNR-H/PSNR-L/SSIM/LPIPS: 24.67/31.52/0.969/0.032 vs. 25.42/32.62/0.976/0.027 on Stanford-ORB; 29.05/0.936/0.082 vs. 29.709/0.947/0.072 on TensoIR). (R2)
- **Novelty claim overstated:** The first contribution (“regression-based geometry reconstructor followed by diffusion-based appearance synthesizer”) is similar to IllumiNeRF, which already proposed that idea. The paper should better differentiate its contribution. (R2, R3)
- **Limited improvement with more views:** In Table 5, increasing from 8 to 16 views yields only marginal gains, suggesting a bottleneck in the model’s ability to leverage additional information (e.g., attention mechanism or Gaussian merging method). (R3)
- **No geometry evaluation:** The paper separates geometry and rendering but does not provide quantitative evaluation or visual results of the reconstructed geometry. It is unclear if geometry is accurate or if the rendering compensates for geometric errors. (R3)
- **Missing training details:** Hardware setup, number of GPUs, training time (hours/days), and memory requirements are not provided. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Benchmark description error:** The claim (L356) that Stanford-ORB has “60 training views and 10 test views per lighting setup” is incorrect. (R2)
- **Rendering speed:** The method takes 2–3 seconds per rendering, which is not real‑time. Moreover, the output is Gaussian splats, not triangle meshes and textures, limiting its adoption for 3D digital assets. (R3)
- **Number of Gaussians:** It is not discussed whether the predicted number of Gaussians is sufficient for diverse object topologies (e.g., hair, fur). (R1, R3)
- **Object insertion and indirect lighting:** For scene insertion (Fig. 1c), it is not explained how the environment map is captured at the object’s position or how indirect light from other objects is handled. (R3)
- **Typos:** L290: “is involves” → “involves”. (R2)
