## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SinCro, a framework for learning 3D-aware representations from single-view RGB input for reinforcement learning. It uses a masked ViT encoder and a latent-conditioned NeRF decoder trained with cross-view completion and time contrastive loss. The key claim is that the representation can be obtained during deployment without multi-view images or camera calibration, relying instead on a pre-trained autoencoder that reconstructs 3D structure from a single view.

### Strengths
- The problem of learning viewpoint-invariant representations from single-view input for RL is well-motivated and addresses a practical limitation of prior methods that require multi-view or calibrated cameras during deployment (R1, R4, R5).
- The approach bypasses the time-consuming optimization of classical NeRF and can potentially predict occluded regions thanks to the autoencoder architecture (R2).
- Extensive experiments demonstrate superior performance over prior 3D representation-based methods (e.g., DrM) in complex tasks, achieving comparable results to multi-view baselines with only single-view input at test time (R2, R3, R4).
- The evaluation includes thorough ablation studies and qualitative analysis, clearly showing viewpoint invariance and robustness to camera perturbations (R1, R5).
- The writing and presentation are generally clear and well-structured (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to NeRF-based representation learning and viewpoint-aware policy methods.** The paper does not compare against methods such as NeRF-MAE, ZeroNVS, PixelNeRF, RegNeRF, GS-LRM, or approaches that explicitly use novel view synthesis for policy learning (e.g., [1], [2], [3] mentioned by R1). These comparisons are essential to assess novelty and advantage (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- **No real-world experiments.** Evaluation is limited to synthetic MetaWorld environments. Prior works [2,3] show real-world results, so the lack of real-robot evaluation raises questions about practical applicability under sensor noise, lighting variations, and dynamic objects (R1, R2, R4).
- **Camera calibration claim is misleading.** The paper claims “without requiring camera calibration,” but pre-training requires posed multi-view images with known intrinsics and extrinsics. The claim should be clarified as applying only during deployment. Additionally, the reliance on fixed camera poses may cause the encoder to learn a rigid RGB-to-pose mapping, limiting generalization (R2, R3).
- **Time contrastive loss can harm representation learning for static scenes.** Equation (3) repulses state features at different timesteps, which is problematic when the agent is stationary – it may push apart representations of the same scene state, hindering consistent state encoding (R2).
- **Generalization and overfitting concerns.** The encoder-decoder is trained on multi-view images; it is unclear how it generalizes when inference inputs are from the same viewpoint (as in line 291). The latent code effectively encodes a fixed mapping (t, action, state, pose, intrinsics, object), suggesting overfitting. For example, recovering a red annotation not visible in view V3 (Fig. 3) is unlikely without overfitting (R2, R3). The impact of pretraining data mix on out-of-distribution generalization (sim2sim, sim2real) is not discussed (R1).
- **Limited environment complexity.** All RL experiments are on MetaWorld tasks, which are relatively simple by 2024 standards. More complex, cluttered environments (e.g., RLBench) would better demonstrate robustness and generalizability to real-world manipulation (R2, R4).
- **Writing quality issues.** Some sections are unclear, e.g., line 300 uses ambiguous phrasing (“randomize viewpoint” vs. “random pick a viewpoint”), and the flow around data shuffling is disjointed. The explanation of camera calibration and the ray (x,d) computation is insufficient (R3).
- **Table 1 is misleading.** It should separate training and deployment requirements for the proposed method and baselines, since pre-training still requires posed multi-view data (R2, R5).
- **PSNR metric contradiction in Figure 3.** NeRF-RL achieves higher PSNR yet produces blurrier reconstructions than the proposed method. This needs explanation; PSNR may not capture perceptual quality or utility for control, and sharper edges in non-salient areas may inflate it (R4).
- **Missing explicit 3D representation baselines for RL.** The paper should compare against methods using explicit 3D representations (e.g., depth maps, 3D volumes) as in recent conference proceedings (R3).
- **Should compare to large-scale pre-trained embeddings like DinoV2.** Since the proposed representation requires synchronized multiview data for training (limiting data scale), comparing with DinoV2 – which learns view-invariant features from large data – would be informative (R5).
- **Overemphasis on reconstruction metrics.** The paper focuses heavily on few/single-view reconstruction PSNR, which is not the primary goal. The core contribution is 3D-aware representations for RL, so reconstruction evaluations should be deemphasized or better justified (R5).
- **Figure quality.** Figure 2 is a PNG instead of vector graphics, reducing presentation quality (R4).
- **Missing discussion of concurrent works.** The paper does not mention concurrent methods that leverage generative models for view-invariant RL policies, such as Dreamitate, VISTA, and RoVi-AUG (R5).
