## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Extended Flow Matching (EFM), an extension of flow matching for conditional generation. EFM learns a matrix field that defines a continuous map from the joint space of time and condition to the data distribution, enabling smooth shifts between conditions. The authors introduce Multi-Marginal Optimal Transport (MMOT-EFM) as a variant that aims to minimize the Dirichlet energy. Experiments are conducted on a 2D toy model and conditional molecular generation (ZINC-250k with a binary label). Overall, reviewers find the motivation and theoretical framework interesting and rigorous, but express major concerns about insufficient experimental validation, limited practical applicability, unclear advantages over simpler baselines, and lack of clarity on how the method generalizes to unseen conditions. Reviewer ratings range from 3 to 6, with one reviewer much more negative on soundness, presentation, and contribution.

### Strengths
- The problem of extending flow matching to sparse or unobserved conditions is important, particularly in scientific applications.
- The theoretical framework integrating time and condition in a joint flow is highly innovative and well-structured, with clear progression from FM to OT-CFM to EFM and MMOT-EFM, and Table 1 effectively highlights differences.
- The motivation is clear: when posteriors are known for several conditions (e.g., from MCMC), EFM provides a way to interpolate along generalized geodesics. The authors provide rigorous theorems and derivations.
- Figure 1 provides an intuitive illustration of the conditional components.
- The paper identifies an interesting direction: using optimal transport to impose smoothness across conditions, with potential to guide generalization to unseen conditions.

### Weaknesses

**Experimental Scope and Validation:**
- The experiments are limited to low-dimensional settings (2D toy and molecular generation with a binary bond-count label) and do not demonstrate scalability to higher-dimensional data or continuous condition spaces. The molecular generation setup is overly simplistic and does not reflect typical complexity; the use of a binary label does not assess diverse molecular properties.
- No experiments are conducted on image datasets (e.g., MNIST, inpainting) or style transfer, despite style transfer being a main motivation.
- The paper lacks direct comparisons to established OT-CFM and conditional flow matching methods (e.g., COT-FM, Bayesian-FM) and to other conditional molecular generation methods. This makes it difficult to assess the advantages of MMOT-EFM over simpler alternatives.
- One reviewer notes that a conditional FM network that receives both source and target conditions should suffice for style transfer, and the paper does not provide a clear example where EFM is either necessary or significantly outperforms such baselines.
- The extrapolation capability to unseen conditions is not convincingly demonstrated; the toy example (Sec. 7.1) is considered meaningless by one reviewer because one can construct an inverse problem that aligns with in-distribution posteriors arbitrarily on OOD data, and it is unclear why minimizing Dirichlet energy yields theoretically sound posteriors (possibly computing Wasserstein barycenters).
- No run times are provided for baselines; only that EFM completes within 4 hours, but baselines may train much faster.
- Scaling in N_c and condition dimension appears poor; experiments only reach 32 dimensions at most.

**Theoretical and Clarity Concerns:**
- The core theoretical contributions are largely direct extensions of existing flow matching theory; the paper does not clearly delineate novel theoretical results or analyze the implications of the extensions (e.g., on the use of finite-sample optimal transport plans).
- It is unclear how imposing smoothness via the matrix field enables extrapolation to unseen conditions. The assumptions behind the smoothness constraint (e.g., piecewise continuity of p(x|c)) are not sufficiently discussed, nor when they hold in practice.
- The claim that no impartial metric exists for conditions on digits is countered by the inverse problem perspective (e.g., observations y = Ax + ε).
- The effect of using batched/mini-batch optimal transport plans on the learned field is not discussed.
- The claim of minimizing an upper bound on Dirichlet energy is questionable when using finite-sample approximations of the transport plan.
- The intuition behind the regression in MMOT (Eq. 3.4) is not explained.
- Comparison to related work: The paper should cite Albergo et al. (2023) for multi-marginal flows. The description of Wildberger et al. (2023) is inaccurate: they do not require knowledge of p(c) and their method can be easily adapted to the sparse-condition setting by training on joint samples (x_{i,j}, c_i).

**Presentation and Completeness:**
- Several figures (especially Fig. 4) lack sufficient explanation, and source points are nearly invisible (grey on grey).
- Notation is introduced without definitions (e.g., p_c vs. p_{0,c}, GG-EFM not defined, "ConvHull" not explained).
- Appendices D5 and F are empty or almost empty.
- Typos: "focus ton he" (line 311), "Shrödinger" for "Schrödinger".
- No code is provided; the algorithm description is not perfectly clear.
- The paper does not discuss limitations, such as scaling with large batch sizes and dimensions.

**Note:** One reviewer (score 3) is significantly more negative than the others, questioning the overall soundness, presentation, and contribution, while other reviewers (scores 6, 6, 5, 5) find the theory promising but experimental validation lacking.
