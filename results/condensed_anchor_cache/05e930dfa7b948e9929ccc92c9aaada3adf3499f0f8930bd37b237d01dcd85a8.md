## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BLDA to address class imbalance in unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation. It assesses prediction bias by analyzing predicted logits distributions, aligns positive and negative logits across classes using anchor distributions and cumulative density functions, and couples Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) to estimate logits online for unbiased pseudo-label generation during self-training. A regression head predicts cumulative density as domain-shared structural knowledge. Experiments are conducted on GTAV→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes benchmarks, integrated into older baselines (e.g., Tables 1,2,4). Reviewers generally agree the motivation is clear and the method shows improvements, but raise concerns about computational overhead, outdated comparisons, limited novelty, insufficient explanation of anchor selection, and lack of label noise handling.

### Strengths
1. Clear motivation with thorough statistical analysis of class bias in UDA semantic segmentation (Figures 1 and 2) [R1, R2].
2. The paper is well-written, well-structured, and easy to follow; figures and formulas precisely describe the method [R2, R3].
3. The proposed module can be easily integrated into existing UDA methods, potentially having broad applicability [R1].
4. The method is generally effective, showing performance improvements on most classes in the two benchmarks (GTAV/SYNTHIA→Cityscapes) [R1, R3, R4]. One reviewer notes that each module (GMM, anchor distribution alignment) is simple yet effective [R2].
5. The approach of analyzing and aligning logits distributions with anchor distributions to achieve balanced prediction is considered novel and addresses an important issue [R1 (new way to measure class distribution changes via logits), R4 (novel method to align logits distributions)].
6. Comprehensive experiments covering three transfer tasks for segmentation, an additional image classification task (supplementary), and extensive qualitative analyses [R2]. (Minority: Reviewer 3 considers the benchmark small-scale and only GTAV/SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, suggesting Vistas, IDDA, BDD100k, VIPER for further validation.)
7. Visual aids effectively demonstrate the distribution trends and the efficacy of the proposed module [R1, R3].

### Weaknesses
1. **Computational overhead**: The method is computationally heavy due to an additional regression head, extra training objectives, and GMM updates via EM algorithm, incurring significantly more computation time and memory usage than baseline methods. This may limit scalability to larger datasets or more complex models [R1, R2]. (Reviewer 2 requests a theoretical complexity analysis and reporting of actual inference time, training time, and memory usage compared to baseline without BLDA.)
2. **Outdated baselines**: In Tables 1, 2, and 4, all existing methods equipped with BLDA are from two years ago. It is questionable whether current state-of-the-art UDA methods (2023-2024) already address prediction bias issues; lack of evaluation on recent SOTA methods makes it difficult to assess the true impact and relevance of BLDA [R1, R2, R3]. (Reviewer 2 requests integrating BLDA into recent methods [A] Focus on Your Target, [B] CDAC, [C] Diffusion-based Image Translation with Label Guidance, [D] Learning Pseudo-Relations. Reviewer 3 suggests adding more datasets: Vistas, IDDA, BDD100k, VIPER.)
3. **Limited novelty**: The data distribution problem is not new, and the use of anchor distributions for alignment and GMM for unbiased generation has been explored in previous works (e.g., [a] Multi-Anchor Active Domain Adaptation, [b] Category Anchor-Guided UDA, [c] ProtoGMM, [d] Uncertainty-aware Pseudo Label Refinery). The authors should provide more discussion distinguishing their method from these [R3]. (Minority: Two reviewers [R1, R4] view the method as novel; R4 specifically considers the alignment of logits distributions with anchor distributions a novel contribution. This contrast should be noted.)
4. **Insufficient explanation of anchor distribution selection**: The paper lacks sufficient explanation regarding the selection criteria for the two anchor distributions (P_p for positive predictions, P_n for negative predictions), raising questions about the method's validity and soundness [R4].
5. **Alignment may not address label noise**: The proposed alignment to anchor distributions as part of post-hoc class balancing may not effectively address label noise, which is a crucial aspect of self-training where pseudo-label denoising is often central to performance. Recent studies [1] Domain Adaptive Semantic Segmentation without Source Data and [2] A Curriculum-style Self-training Approach for Source-Free Semantic Segmentation show that negative pseudo-labeling can enhance robustness and reduce noise. The authors should clarify the rationale for their alignment-based approach in comparison to negative pseudo-labeling methods [R4].
6. **Figure 3 issues**: The figure lacks labeled x- and y-axes, making it challenging to interpret the logit distributions for positive and negative samples. There is also no clear explanation of the direction of reweighting and resampling applied to the logit distributions, which obscures the intended insights and the method's balancing mechanism [R4].
7. **Missing citations and typo**: The paper fails to cite several highly relevant works ([A]-[D]) in Section 2.1 Related Work [R2]. A minor grammatical error: "Discusiion" in L307 [R4].
