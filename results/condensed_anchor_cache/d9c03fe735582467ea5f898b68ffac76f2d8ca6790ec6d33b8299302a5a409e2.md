## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Long-LRM, a feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction model that processes up to 32 input images (960×540) in 1.3s on a single A100 GPU. It uses a hybrid Mamba2+transformer architecture with token merging and Gaussian pruning to handle long token sequences and memory constraints. Evaluations on DL3DV-140 and Tanks and Temples show competitive quality to optimization-based 3DGS while being 600× faster. All reviewers note the extension to longer input sequences, but raise concerns about novelty, incomplete comparisons, insufficient justification for design choices, and missing discussion of prior Mamba-based 3D reconstruction works.

### Strengths
1. **Extension to longer input sequences.** The model scales feed-forward 3D reconstruction to many more input views (e.g., 32) than prior works that handle only 1–4 images (R1, R2, R3).
2. **Sound hybrid architecture for long sequences.** Combining Mamba2 with transformer blocks is a reasonable choice for processing extreme token lengths, and the design is backed by ablations (R1, R2, R3). Reviewer 2 notes the justification in Table 2, though the comparison with GS-LRM is considered somewhat unfair.
3. **Practical memory‑efficiency techniques.** Token merging and Gaussian pruning are effective for scaling to high resolution (960×540) where other variants fail; the ablation study comprehensively demonstrates their contributions with clear metrics (R1, R2).
4. **Speed advantage over optimization‑based methods.** The single feed‑forward pass is orders of magnitude faster (600×) than per‑scene optimization, with comparable quality on large datasets (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and technical contribution.** The core architecture is seen as a straightforward combination of Mamba2 and transformer blocks – a setup already proposed in the Mamba v2 paper and used in many Vision Mamba works. The main contribution is applying Mamba2 for long‑sequence modeling in 3DGS, which all three reviewers consider insufficient. Nearly all components have been used in prior feed‑forward 3D object reconstruction (R1, R2, R3).
2. **Missing discussion of related Mamba‑based 3D reconstruction works.** The paper does not cite or contextualize Hamba, Gamba, and MVGamba, which have been publicly available for over six months and also use Mamba for 3D reconstruction. This omission weakens the motivation and the perceived novelty (R1, R2).
3. **Insufficient justification for the choice of Mamba over alternatives.** 
   - The comparison with GS‑LRM in Table 2 uses the same patch size for both the proposed 7M1T and GS‑LRM architectures, which is an unfair comparison. A more rigorous analysis of GS‑LRM’s limitations in handling long input sequences (beyond fixed patch size) is needed (R2).
   - The dismissal of cost‑volume methods (e.g., MVSplat) due to OOM issues is not well‑founded: these are often engineering problems solvable with FlashAttention or lightweight designs. The authors should acknowledge efficient cost‑volume implementations and provide a deeper analysis of why they are unsuitable here (R2). Reviewer 2 warns this may mislead the feed‑forward 3D scene reconstruction community.
4. **Incomplete comparisons.** 
   - Only naive 3DGS is used as a comparison; recent large‑scale or regularized 3DGS/NeRF variants such as Zip‑NeRF, Scaffold‑GS, and Mip‑Splatting are missing (R3).
   - No comparison with generalizable feed‑forward methods (MVSplat, pixelSplat) under sparse‑view settings is provided (R3).
   - It is unclear how the model performs on city‑scale scenes that require hundreds of images, or how Gaussians from separate chunks can be seamlessly concatenated. Comparisons with CityGaussian and Octree‑GS (designed for very large scenes) are requested (R3).
5. **Lack of regularizations for quality issues.** 
   - The videos reveal visible floaters without any further regularization (R3).
   - No regularization for aliasing effects is proposed, despite the model operating at high resolution (R3).
6. **No surface reconstruction results.** While the paper shows novel‑view synthesis, it does not evaluate on surface reconstruction, despite recent advances (2DGS, High‑quality Surface Reconstruction using Gaussian Surfels) that would strengthen the contribution (R3).
7. **Justification of venue fit (ICLR vs. CV).** Reviewer 1 questions whether the engineering optimizations (token merging, pruning) align with ICLR’s focus on fundamental ML methodology rather than a computer vision conference.
