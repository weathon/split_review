## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MeanSparse, a post-training method that improves the robustness of adversarially trained convolutional and attention-based neural networks by sparsifying mean-centered feature vectors. The technique cascades activation functions with operators that project features near their precomputed mean, reducing feature variations. Applied to top RobustBench models, MeanSparse raises AutoAttack accuracy from 73.71% to 75.28% on CIFAR-10, from 42.67% to 44.78% on CIFAR-100, and from 59.56% to 62.12% on ImageNet, with negligible clean accuracy loss. The method is efficient, requires no additional training, and incurs limited inference cost. Three of four reviewers rated the paper 3–5, one rated 6; confidence ranged from 3 to 5.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea** – applying sparsification to mean-centered features is an innovative way to target non-robust features. (R1, R4)
- **Efficiency** – the method is post-training, applicable to any pre-trained model without retraining, and adds minimal inference cost. (R2)
- **Extensive experiments** – results span CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet; multiple architectures (convolutional and attention-based) and threat models are tested, including adaptive attacks in Appendix A.4. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Meaningful robustness improvements** on SOTA adversarially trained models (e.g., +1.57% on CIFAR-10, +2.11% on CIFAR-100, +2.56% on ImageNet AutoAttack). (R3)
- **Clear intuition and helpful visualizations** – Figure 1 effectively conveys how MeanSparse operates. (R1, R3)
- **Well-organized paper** – motivation, methodology, and results are clearly described. (R4)
- **Limitations discussion** included. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Potential gradient masking** – the MeanSparse operator induces zero gradients for projected features, applied at multiple layers. This may severely limit gradient information for white-box attacks. Black-box tests may not be sufficient because the gap between white- and black-box attacks for the base model could be of similar magnitude to the reported improvements (1–3%). A simple adaptive attack (removing projection in the backward pass, akin to BPDA) and a rigorous analysis of gradient norms across layers with/without MeanSparse are missing. (R2, high confidence) *One reviewer (R3) noted the method is robust to the adaptive attacks tested, but R2’s concern about gradient masking remains a key weakness.*
- **Ambiguity in describing the method** – the term “feature” is never explicitly defined (input features? final-layer activations? internal-layer activations?). The introduction alternates inconsistently between “design of activation functions” and “sparsifying features” without linking the two. The “sparsification operation” is not clearly explained; a concise mathematical definition or a simple example would help. (R1, R3)
- **Missing experimental and reproducibility details** – only an NVIDIA A100 GPU is mentioned. Software versions, hyperparameters, data preprocessing, and other configuration details are absent. (R1)
- **Insufficient baselines** – the paper does not compare MeanSparse with other sparsity-based methods. (R4) *Also, the choice between two candidate approaches in Sec. 3 (page 6) is not justified with a comparative advantage analysis.* (R1)
- **Scalability to smaller models not shown** – experiments only use large networks (e.g., WRS-70). Performance on smaller architectures like ResNet-18 or Swin-Small should be reported. (R4)
- **Generalization across attention-based models** – only certain attention-based models are tested; it is unclear whether the method works on others (e.g., ViT). (R4)
- **Concern about statistics computed on test data** – after examining the code, one reviewer notes that MeanSparse calculates running mean and variance from input data. This is viewed as a potential misuse of test data to adjust model parameters. (R4)
- **Intuition about the feature mean being a high-probability point is not justified** – in multimodal distributions the feature mean is often unlikely. The method assumes blocking minor variations around the mean removes non-robust features, but this may not hold in practice. (R3)
- **Threat model scope limited** – only ℓ∞ (and ℓ2 in Appendix A.2) attacks are evaluated. Robustness to other threat models (e.g., unbounded ℓ_p attacks) is not studied; the paper should explicitly state its limitation to ℓ_p bounded attacks if that is the case. (R3)
- **No analysis of effectiveness versus attack strength** – e.g., robustness at perturbation size 16/255. (R4)
- **Sensitivity to class imbalance** – feature means may shift when classes are imbalanced, potentially affecting MeanSparse’s performance; this is not discussed. (R3)
- **Data used for mean/std calculation unclear** – are statistics computed on benign or adversarially perturbed data? (R3)
- **Discussion in Sec. 3.1 appears disconnected from the final approach** – the connection is not made clear, and the variable \(z_{k-1}\) in Eq. (7) is not defined. (R2)
- **Writing error** – Figure 2 title contains the extraneous text “equation 3”. (R1)
