## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper uses epistemic uncertainty from Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) to guide mixed-precision quantization. Two algorithms are proposed: QuantileInform (allocates bit-width per layer based on uncertainty) and RandomBits (stochastic gradient optimization to maximize quantization likelihood). Experiments are conducted on MNIST and CIFAR-10, showing reduced average bit-width while maintaining accuracy (VGG-16: 3.05 bits, 90.5% vs. 91.9% full-precision on CIFAR-10; LeNet: 3.16 bits, 99.0% vs. 99.2% on MNIST).

### Strengths
- The integration of epistemic uncertainty analysis into quantization is a novel perspective (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 specifically praised the innovative interpretation of bits as random discrete variables, enabling gradient optimization.
- The paper provides a mathematically rigorous foundation with clear derivations and well-justified methodological choices; presentation is strong with clear notation and well-designed figures (Reviewer 1).
- The mixed-precision algorithms achieve comparable performance to standard Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) (Reviewer 2) and allow flexible bit-width allocation (Reviewer 3).
- Promising results on MNIST and CIFAR-10 demonstrate practical utility in reducing model size while retaining accuracy (Reviewer 1). Author acknowledgment of limitations is noted as scientific rigor (Reviewer 1).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 3) was substantially more positive on novelty and presentation, while Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 3, confidence 5 and 3) were more critical, especially on experimental scope and novelty. This contrast is reflected in weaknesses below.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope (all reviewers):** Testing is confined to MNIST and CIFAR-10. Reviewer 1 states that larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) are needed to demonstrate scalability and robustness. Reviewer 3 adds the absence of Transformer experiments and larger datasets. Reviewer 2 notes insufficient evaluation on modern models (e.g., LLMs) and diverse datasets.
- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art methods (all reviewers):** Reviewer 1 requests comparisons with AMC and HAWQ. Reviewer 3 mentions Adabin as a missing baseline. Reviewer 2 calls for comparison with the latest quantization algorithms. No benchmarking against recent mixed-precision approaches is provided.
- **Novelty concerns (Reviewers 2, 3):** Reviewer 2 argues the approach builds directly on prior work [1] (Blundell et al., 2015) on weight importance for pruning, reducing originality; the core idea remains closely tied to pruning methods despite extension to quantization. Reviewers 2 and 3 rated contribution low (2 each), while Reviewer 1 considered it innovative (score 3). This sharp contrast in evaluation of novelty is noted.
- **Missing computational complexity and acceleration analysis (Reviewers 1, 3):** Reviewer 1 notes that claims about computational efficiency and linear memory requirements lack rigorous proof or experimental validation. No theoretical or practical runtime complexity is provided. Reviewer 3 observes that actual acceleration (latency/throughput) is not shown. The computational cost of Bayesian inference could be a bottleneck for larger models (Reviewer 1).
- **Reliance on BNN training (Reviewer 2):** The method requires models to be trained as Bayesian neural networks, making it unsuitable for pre-trained deterministic models and restricting practical applicability.
- **Uncertainty motivation not fully justified (Reviewer 3):** Reviewer 3 questions why uncertainty is introduced and what advantages it offers; the paper could better explain the rationale.
- **Scope of quantization unclear (Reviewer 3):** Reviewer 3 asks whether only weights are quantized (not activations). The paper focuses on weight quantization but does not explicitly state this.
- **Hardware feasibility not addressed (Reviewer 3):** Reviewer 3 questions how the algorithm can be implemented on low-bitwidth hardware; this is not discussed.
- **Clarity of derivations (Reviewer 1):** Some complex derivations, particularly in the appendix, could benefit from additional simplification.
- **Limited architecture validation (Reviewer 2):** No evaluation on state-of-the-art models beyond VGG-16 and LeNet; more complex architectures are needed.
