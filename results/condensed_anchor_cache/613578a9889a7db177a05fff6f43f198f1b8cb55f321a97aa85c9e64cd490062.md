## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Compose, a zero-shot approach for depth-aware scene editing using text-to-image diffusion models. It introduces a multiplane representation that divides scenes into fixed-depth planes and a Multiplane Feature Guidance (MPG) technique that aligns source latents with target edits at each denoising step. The method is validated on scene composition and depth-aware object insertion, outperforming task-specific baselines. Reviewers note the problem novelty, elegant integration of classic multiplane imaging into generative frameworks, and impressive qualitative results. However, they also raise concerns about incomplete comparisons, missing implementation details, reliance on depth inputs, and certain performance inconsistencies.

### Strengths
- The studied problem (depth-aware compositional scene editing) is novel and applicable to visual effects, game design, advertising.
- The proposed Multiplane Feature Guidance incorporates classic multiplane imaging into diffusion models in an elegant, intuitive way, enabling zero-shot editing without additional training.
- The method is the first to perform depth-aware object placement with a single exemplar object/background image; the baselines defined for this new task are reasonable.
- Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments demonstrate that Diffusion Compose outperforms existing methods in depth-aware object placement and scene composition, with especially high-fidelity scene composition results (foreground objects relighted/blended with background lighting).
- The ablation study provides clear visual and quantitative analysis of each module’s contribution.
- The paper is overall well-written; the intuition snippets in Section 3.3 aid readability.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient discussion of related work**: The paper does not adequately discuss or compare with layered compositing methods like LayerDiffusion (Li et al., 2023) and LayerDiff (Huang et al., 2024), which also handle layer-wise generation.
- **Limited quantitative ablation**: The ablation study lacks quantitative metrics. For object placement, DINO-sim, KID, delta_depth, and Clip-sim should be reported (as in Table 1). For scene composition, FID and LPIPS should be reported (as in Table 2).
- **Marginal performance advantage**: In Table 1, the proposed method only outperforms Image MPI+H on CLIP score and is worse on other metrics. In Table 2, there is no significant advantage over Image MPI+H. The visualization of MPI+H’s copy-paste issue (Figure 5) is insufficient.
- **Heavy reliance on depth information**: The method depends on depth maps from off-the-shelf monocular depth estimators, which produce relative depth. It is unclear how this affects composition when objects at very different absolute distances are combined. The mask generation also uses a Sobel filter (Anydoor), requiring additional information. The impact of depth estimation quality on final editing is not analyzed.
- **Inconsistent object identity preservation**: Object identity is well preserved in scene composition but degrades in object insertion. In Figure 1, the inserted lamp changes color across frames, indicating inconsistency in visual attributes. Reviewer 4 explicitly contrasts the two tasks: scene composition results are high fidelity, but object insertion identity is not well preserved. The paper does not address this discrepancy.
- **Misleading claims about applicability**: The paper claims the method applies to text-to-image diffusion models, but the implementations rely on Stable Diffusion v2-depth and Anydoor, which are trained to accept depth map inputs. The method may not transfer to models without depth conditioning.
- **Missing implementation details**:
  - The images used for editing (foreground/background) are generated images; the intermediate latents used to generate them must be saved. This should be explicitly stated.
  - How prompts are curated given a background image A and object image B is not explained (e.g., what edit text prompt is used).
  - The monocular depth estimation model used to extract multiplane representations is not specified. A diagram showing how multiplane representations are extracted from a depth map (next to Figure 2) would clarify the method.
  - It is unclear how `x^{depth}` is obtained (Line 192) – it appears to come from an off-the-shelf depth model, but this is not stated.
  - The user must provide a specified relative depth at runtime; this is unfriendly for practical use.
- **Rationale for design choices**:
  - Equation 5 uses loss-based optimization to close the feature gap; why not simply use a linear combination? The effect of different numbers of optimization iterations (e.g., 5 vs. 8) per timestep is not explored.
  - The diffusion timesteps and optimization layers are selected empirically without theoretical motivation or analysis of generalizability.
  - The role of alpha blending (Equation 3) is unclear – why not directly use a typical 2D mask (e.g., segmentation mask) without opacity?
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Figure 2 is placed below Figure 3; Figure 2 should be above Figure 3. The caption of Figure 3 lacks sufficient explanation (labels (a) and (b) should be marked) and does not explain Latent MPI.
  - Citations lack parentheses; the paper should use \citet{} and \citep{} as per formatting instructions.
  - The term “object insertion” and “object placement” are used interchangeably; a single term should be settled.
  - In Section 3.3, color is defined as `c_i` and opacity as `\alpha_i`, but later `c^i` and `\alpha^i` is used.
  - Tables should use bold/italics to distinguish best/second-best metrics.
  - In Figure 4 caption, there is an extraneous "the" before "t".
  - Questions about whether parameter α in Equation 5 should filter optimization steps, and whether the multiplane mask could be applied to Anydoor to enhance performance, overlap with the missing ablation and dependence-on-depth criticisms.
