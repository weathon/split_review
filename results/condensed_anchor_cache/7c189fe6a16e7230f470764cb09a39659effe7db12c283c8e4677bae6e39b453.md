## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Active Learning, which integrates a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model with active learning to adaptively select the most informative projection angles in limited-angle CT. During inference, the diffusion model generates posterior samples conditioned on current measurements; uncertainty (posterior variance) is used to choose the next angle. Experiments on simulated parallel-beam CT datasets demonstrate gains in PSNR over uniform sampling and other generative model baselines, using fewer measurements.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important and under-explored problem: reducing CT radiation dose via adaptive angle selection. (R1, R3)
- The solution is well-motivated: selecting angles with highest posterior variance promises to yield the most informative measurements. (R4)
- Novel combination of diffusion models with active learning for experimental design in CT. (R3)
- Clear, straightforward presentation; easy to read. Open about limitations (e.g., hallucination disclaimer L078, thorough discussion). (R1)
- Existing work, results, and limitations are well discussed. (R3)
- Demonstrates notable gains in PSNR over uniform sampling; uses half or fewer measurements to achieve the same performance as comparison methods. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution clarity**:  
  * The paper’s contributions are not explicitly clear. Diffusion sampling is based on Song et al. 2023; active sampling is based on existing uncertainty sampling. Difficult to see where the novelty lies. (R4) The method is essentially a combination of Hard Data Consistency and uncertainty sampling, but no ablation is provided to isolate the effect of the “soft” data consistency modification. (R1)  
  * Significant overlap with Elata et al. ECCV 2024 (contemporaneous). The acquisition function differs, but the covariance-based acquisition function benchmarked in the Appendix performs identically. Technical differences should be enumerated. (R1)  
  * Disagreement: Reviewer 3 considers the combination novel. (R3)
- **Limited experimental validation**:  
  * Missing active CT baselines: No comparison against existing active learning methods for CT (e.g., arXiv:2006.02420, arXiv:2211.01670, ACM 3503161.3548204). (R1) Reviewer 2 also asks for comparison with an unnamed active learning method on reconstruction and inference time. (R2)  
  * Only parallel-beam CT experiments; not tested with fan-beam or 3D cone beam, which would verify effectiveness. (R2) Experiments use only simulated projection data (parallel Radon transform), incurring the “inverse crime” that can produce overly optimistic results. Real projection datasets exist (e.g., LDCT, Nature datasets) but are not used. (R1, R2)  
  * No low-dose or sparse-view baselines: The paper is motivated by dose reduction but does not benchmark against popular low-dose CT methods (e.g., total variation regularization). Only comparisons against other sampling-based methods constructed for this submission are provided. (R1) Could TV be used as a regularizer in the proposed setup? (R1)  
  * Need to compare with DPS or the proposed method without an active loop, using uniform sparse angles (e.g., 27, 15, 18 angles), to isolate the benefit of the active learning component from the diffusion model itself. (R2)  
  * Only CT experiments are conducted; the method is stated to be generic, but MRI (with more active learning studies) is not studied and could provide benchmarks. (R1)
- **Computational cost and practical advantage**:  
  * No runtime or computational cost is reported. Diffusion models are computationally heavy; the active selection requires n rounds of k posterior sampling and full-view projection, leading to significant overhead. (R1, R2, R4) The trade-off between computational cost and reconstruction quality should be analyzed; if choosing the next angle takes x times longer than uniform sampling, the method must collect at least x times fewer measurements to be advantageous. (R4, R2) Potential mitigations should be discussed. (R2)
- **Dependence on pre-trained diffusion model and generalizability**:  
  * Pre-training a diffusion model is computationally substantial and limits applicability for small or frequently changing datasets. (R3) The model is highly dependent on the training data; generalizability to significantly different distributions is questionable, with potential performance degradation. (R3) The pre-trained model may introduce undesirable biases leading to systematic errors, which need evaluation and mitigation. (R3) Improvement may come from the diffusion model rather than the active learning component. (R2)
- **Technical details insufficient**:  
  * How the posterior distribution is obtained for active learning is not sufficiently detailed; steps, approximations, and assumptions should be explained for reproducibility. (R3)  
  * The acquisition function should be compared with Elata et al.; the covariance-based acquisition in the Appendix performs identically. (R1)  
  * No ablation comparing soft vs. hard data consistency (the primary technical delta). (R1)  
  * Eq 3: why take the mean of posterior samples first and then apply the forward operator? Would it be better to apply the forward operator first and then take the mean? (R4)  
  * Variable t in line 300-301 could be confused with diffusion time steps. (R4)  
  * Notation x* in Algorithm 1 is not defined. (R2)  
  * Projection geometry setting must be given. (R2)  
  * Plot distribution of selected angles per dataset; shape of object influences selected angles. (R2)  
  * Why are smaller images cropped then rescaled? Rescaling changes the distribution. (R3)
- **Presentation issues**:  
  * Experimental section 4.2 lacks context; methods (SWAG, Bootstrap, etc.) are introduced without explaining where they are used. A “Comparison Methods” subsection title would be clearer. (R4) The paragraph at lines 468-473 lacks clarity about which Table/Figure it discusses. (R4)  
  * Typographical error: L462 “on pair” → “on par”. (R1)
- **Minor points**:  
  * The paper states medical images are not suited because they are acquired fast/sparse, but the goal is to have fewer measurements with high resolution; clarification requested. (R3)  
  * How would the model handle samples destroyed by high dose/long measurement? Would it automatically reduce distortions? (R3)
