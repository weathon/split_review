## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates prompting Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for semantic segmentation using either text or visual prompts on the MESS dataset. It introduces few-shot prompted semantic segmentation, finds that text and visual prompts are complementary, and proposes PromptMatcher—a training‑free combination of SoftMatcher+ and LISA—to exploit both modalities. The method achieves state‑of‑the‑art results among training‑free approaches.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (all reviewers).
- The observation that text and visual prompts are complementary is of practical significance (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; though Reviewer 1 notes lack of novelty).
- The systematic evaluation and introduction of few‑shot prompted semantic segmentation provide a structured analysis of VLM capabilities (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; but Reviewer 1 disputes the novelty of the benchmarking task—see Weaknesses).
- Detailed analysis of model behavior in some sections (Reviewers 1, 3).
- PromptMatcher achieves state‑of‑the‑art results among training‑free methods on the MESS dataset, validated by experiments (Reviewers 2, 4).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 4 rated the paper 5, while Reviewers 1–3 rated it 3. The weaknesses noted by Reviewer 4 overlap substantially with those from the other reviewers; the more positive assessment does not challenge the core criticisms below.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and methodological contribution.** PromptMatcher is a simple combination of existing methods (SoftMatcher+ and LISA) without novel methodology (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The complementary nature of text and visual prompts has already been explored in prior work such as T‑Rex2 (Reviewer 2). No ablation study validates the contribution of individual components (e.g., point proposals, rejection models, influence of different text prompts) (Reviewers 1, 3). The approach is an engineering combination rather than a principled integration (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental scope and validation.** Experiments are limited to the MESS dataset; more general datasets (COCO, PASCAL, RefCOCO) are needed (Reviewer 3). The method is only tested on semantic segmentation, not on panoptic or instance segmentation, limiting generalizability (Reviewer 2). The paper does not sufficiently discuss recent LLM‑based segmentation works (e.g., LLaFS) that achieve superior performance, making the claim that “VLMs lag far behind custom models” debatable (Reviewer 4).
- **Modest performance improvement and misleading claims.** PromptMatcher improves only 2.5% over the previous SOTA (LISA), and the gap to the Oracle Ensemble+ remains large (45.3 vs. 53.8) (Reviewers 2, 4). The claim of an 11% improvement from anticipating prompt modality is misleading because it refers to an oracle upper bound, not the actual method (Reviewer 1). LISA outperforms PromptMatcher on some tasks (e.g., the Earth task in Table 4) (Reviewer 4).
- **Issues with claimed contributions and presentation.** The paper claims a novel benchmarking task, but it is essentially identical to the MESS benchmark, which already evaluates more models (Reviewer 1). The title suggests exploring prompting for VLMs, but the method mainly connects VLMs with other models (Reviewer 1). Table 1 caption is inconsistent with the table structure (text‑prompt vs. visual‑prompt blocks reversed) (Reviewer 1). Numerical discrepancies exist: SoftMatcher+ results differ between Table 1 (41.6) and Table 2 (41.8), and CAT‑Seg results differ from the original MESS paper (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions about text prompt refinement.** Given that varying text prompts can significantly impact performance, it is unclear whether more precise text prompt engineering could further improve PromptMatcher without relying on visual prompts (Reviewer 2).
