## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers address imbalanced regression using ConR, a contrastive regularizer that models global and local label similarities in feature space. The method is orthogonal to existing approaches, easy to integrate, and shows performance gains on four benchmarks (AgeDB-DIR, IMDB-WIKI-DIR, NYUD2-DIR, and the authors’ new MPIIGaze-DIR with 2D labels). The core idea—using InfoNCE-based loss with label-similarity‑based pushing weights for negatives, and including negative pairs defined by similar predictions but dissimilar true labels—is considered novel. Empirical results indicate improvement in most cases, but several weaknesses are identified.

### Strengths
- Imbalanced regression is a practical and important problem. The paper addresses fairness and real‑world label imbalance.
- The adaptation of supervised contrastive loss to continuous labels is novel, especially the pushing weight based on label similarity and density for negative samples.
- The regularizer is designed to pull positive pairs together and relatively repel negative pairs, modeling both global and local label relations.
- ConR can be seamlessly integrated with existing methods and generally boosts performance across benchmarks.
- A new dataset, MPIIGaze‑DIR, with a 2‑dimensional label space is provided, which may benefit the imbalanced regression community.
- The paper is well‑written and easy to understand.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis / guarantees:** No theoretical insights or generalization bounds supporting why ConR reduces prediction error. Empirical results alone are considered insufficient by multiple reviewers.
- **Missing “ConR‑only” baseline:** All experiments combine ConR with existing methods; its standalone performance should be reported as a baseline to isolate the regularizer’s effect.
- **Insufficient comparisons:**  
  - No comparison with other contrastive regression methods (e.g., [a]).  
  - Missing direct comparison of learned feature representations against Balanced MSE and RankSim (e.g., in Figure 4).  
- **Connection to imbalance unclear:** The method appears to apply contrastive learning to regression without a clear mechanism for handling minority under‑representation or avoiding majority bias in the contrastive sampling process.
- **Failure cases and performance drops:** Adding ConR sometimes hurts *Few‑shot* or *All* performance (Tables 1–3). Reviewers request explanations for these drops and for why the baseline ordering changes (e.g., LDS+FDS+RankSim underperforms FDS+RankSim, yet adding ConR reverses this on AgeDB but not on IMDB‑WIKI). A detailed analysis of these phenomena is needed.
- **Inconsistent results:** Figure 3 and Table 3 report contradictory *Few*‑shot results for Balanced MSE. Clarification required.
- **Complexity vs. reweighting:** The augmentation step may increase memory and time overhead. Why not use simpler reweighting methods that achieve comparable results without this cost?
- **Loss function justification:**  
  - The objective in Eq. (4) and the reason for combining with the ordinary regression loss are introduced hastily; more detailed justification and definition are needed.  
  - Eq. (1): The outer sum implies instances with more positive samples contribute more. Is this desired? Provide reasoning.  
  - The standard regression loss ℓ_R is applied to original instances, while ConR uses augmentations. Why are original instances not directly involved in ConR?
- **Hyperparameter ω (similarity threshold):** ω is a crucial parameter, but its setting is application‑dependent and may require trial‑and‑error. More insights or guidelines are needed.
- **Augmentation method not described:** Section 3.2.1 does not explain how instances are augmented; this should be included either in the main paper or appendix.
- **Anchor selection:** If an instance has no negative samples it is not used as an anchor. What is the reasoning? Could methods without negatives (BYOL, SimSiam) be considered?
- **Continuous pushing weight:** The inverse frequency discrete weighting is used; how is the continuous weight determined? Is kernel density estimation required?
- **Empirical density‑based weight computation:** The method requires the empirical label distribution to compute pushing weights. Clarify how continuous weights are derived from discrete frequencies.
- **Missing details:**  
  - No deviation measure in results.  
  - Function f_S (Appendix) could be more fully described.  
  - Some references are incomplete (e.g., page 16, duplicate entries: Kang et al. 21, Khosla et al. 20).  
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - Eq. (3): 1/2N assumes all augmented instances are anchors, but some may not be.  
  - Figure 5: Uses 1/ω instead of ω; “higher similarity threshold” appears contradictory when 1/ω is larger.  
  - p9: Phrase “sharing feature statistics to an extent…” is unclear and needs elaboration.  
- **Dataset and metric documentation:** All four datasets (including the new MPIIGaze‑DIR) should be described in the appendix. For NYUD2‑DIR, the rationale for selecting only two of its many metrics needs justification.
