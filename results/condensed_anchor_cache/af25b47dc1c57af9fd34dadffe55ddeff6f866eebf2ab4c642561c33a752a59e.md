- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 5, 8, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Matrix Function Neural Networks (MFNs), a novel GNN architecture that parameterizes non-local interactions via analytic matrix equivariant functions using resolvent expansions. Reviewers acknowledge the method's novelty and strong empirical performance on ZINC, TU datasets, and quantum systems. However, significant concerns about presentation clarity, experimental fairness, computational cost documentation, and theoretical justification were raised. Reviewer 2 was substantially more positive (rating 8, confidence 4), commending the clarity and elegance, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (ratings 5 and 6, confidence 2 and 3) faulted the exposition as poor and found key sections difficult to follow.

### Strengths

- Strong empirical results; MFN achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on ZINC, TU, and quantum force-field benchmarks. (R1, R2, R3)
- Original and novel architecture that pulls from resolvent calculus and electronic structure methods to design an expressive and efficient class of GNNs. (R1, R2)
- Matrix-variate formulation with efficient parameterization via resolvent expansions is unique and well-motivated. (R1, R2)
- The method is elegant and the architecture is sound; the approach extends spectral convolutional methods in a principled way. (R2, R3)
- Reviewer 2 found the paper clear and well-written overall, with full experimental details in the supplementary. (R2; note: this is a minority view – R1 and R3 found presentation poor.)

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and clarity are major issues.** Multiple incomplete or undefined notations: (R1) \(A^*\) not defined (presumably adjoint), \(f_\theta(H)\) called "scalar-to-scalar" but appears matrix-to-matrix; Eq. (10) lacks clear description of how \(g\) acts on \(H\); proof of Eq. (10) missing. (R2) Domain/codomain of \(s\) above Eq. (1) not defined; \(\mathfrak{Z}z\) in Sec. 4.3 undefined; Shaw et al. citation for positional encodings already in original Vaswani et al. (R3) Eq. (3) uses \(\mathcal{G}\) inconsistently; Eq. (4) \(\Phi \odot g\) confusing; \(\rho\) called orthogonal when unitary; many more notation issues. (R1, R3) Strongly criticize the writing quality; R1 rates presentation 1, R3 rates presentation 1. (R2 rates presentation 3, stating it is good with minor issues, highlighting disagreement.)
- **Method description in Section 4 is hard to follow.** (R3) It is unclear what functions \(\phi_m\) do – input/output spaces not specified; Eq. (7) indexing is confusing: \(H_{cij, m_1, m_2}\) is inconsistent with \(bn \times bn\) matrix; Eq. (7) seems to use fixed basis functions but they may be learnable; indexing over union of neighbors should be intersection; examples of \(\phi_m\) promised but not given (is Eq. (21) an example?). (R2) The choice of basis (Sec. 4.1) is unclear: when can we automatically learn these functions? Any practical limitations? (R1) No proof given for Eq. (10).
- **Lack of code, making results unverifiable.** (R3) No code shared; zip file only contains dataset and training details, not a runnable implementation. (R2) Cites that open-source implementation would alleviate adoption difficulty.
- **Experimental fairness and parameter counting concerns.** (R3) It appears complex numbers are counted as one parameter each, but a fair comparison should count two (real and complex parts). (R3) The sentence "initial edge features are obtained by concatenating PDF descriptors used for ZINC" raises concern: are these features outputs of a trained PDF model? That would be unfair for comparison. (R1) Unclear how initial edge features are extracted for all experiments – this is also noted by R2 as a general weakness, since in all experiments models are not end-to-end MFNs (they use a few MFN layers followed by standard layers, and initial edge features come from other architectures).
- **Computational cost is not adequately characterized.** (R1) Cost depends on graph dimension \(d\) and choice of update equations (11)–(13); no wall-clock time or memory usage comparison; need clear evidence of superiority over Transformers. (R2) The small number of MFN layers in all experiments suggests a computational restriction – is that confirmed? (R3) Runtime comparisons missing, especially given reliance on spectral decompositions or their approximations.
- **Section 4.3 is confusing and lacks rigor.** (R3) "Combe-Thomas theorem" is not cited; the bound before Eq. (16) likely requires dependence on weight norm to reach Eq. (16); geometric expressiveness claims are stated without proof – what does equivalence to infinite-layer MPNN mean? Does the matrix channel require an infinitely sized pole expansion? What norm for approximation? What are "two-body entries"?
- **Constraints on learnable weights and poles.** (R2) Do the learnable weights and poles in Sec. 4.2 need to satisfy any constraints for the resolvent calculus definition? Can learning produce invalid curves or undesirable behavior? (R2) Also, is the small number of layers purely a computational choice?
- **Expressiveness limitations.** (R3) The parameterization manipulates eigenvalues of Laplacian/adjacency, which from WL/expressiveness results are insufficient to encode all graph information – what are the practical limitations?
- **Transformer scaling comment is outdated.** (R2) Citing quadratic scaling of transformers ignores recent efficient variants like PerceiverIO that achieve linear cost.

**Reviewer disagreement on overall assessment:** Reviewer 2 (score 8, confidence 4) found the paper clear and contributions significant, while Reviewers 1 (score 5, confidence 2) and 3 (score 6, confidence 3) rated it lower due to presentation, experimental concerns, and lack of rigor.
