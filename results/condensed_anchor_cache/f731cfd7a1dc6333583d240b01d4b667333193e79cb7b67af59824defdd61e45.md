## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes B-PDF, a memory-efficient fine-tuning method that integrates block coordinate descent (BCD) with a Hessian-informed zeroth-order optimizer (HiZOO). Model layers are treated as blocks; only a subset of layers is updated per iteration, and diagonal Hessian information is stored only for active layers. Evaluated on OPT-1.3B and LLaMA-2-7B, the method claims up to 39% memory reduction over HiZOO while maintaining accuracy comparable to MeZO. Reviewers acknowledge the memory savings vs. HiZOO and the clear motivation, but they raise significant concerns about limited novelty, insufficient evaluation (small models, missing ablations), overclaimed advantage over MeZO, and baselines that lack standard engineering (gradient checkpointing, LOMO/GaLORE). Reviewer scores are split: two (R2, R3) rated 5; two (R1, R4) rated 3.

### Strengths
- Achieves up to 39% memory reduction compared to the prior Hessian-informed method HiZOO (validated across Tables 1, 2, 4) and keeps memory comparable to MeZO (R1, R3, R4).
- The motivation is well‑established: extending HiZOO with BCD reduces the memory overhead of storing the diagonal Hessian for all layers (R2, R3).
- Multiple BCD update rules are proposed (Eq. 7), offering different ways to select active blocks (R3).
- The paper is well‑structured and easy to follow (R2).
- The approach absorbs benefits from both HiZOO (Hessian acceleration) and BCD (block‑wise, low‑memory updates) (R2).
- On OPT-1.3B, B‑PDF yields better performance than HiZOO while using substantially less memory (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and marginal gains**  
   - The combination of HiZOO and BCD is straightforward and offers little new insight (R2).  
   - The performance improvement over the baseline MeZO is minimal: average scores 70.2 vs. 70.0 (R1).  
   - Convergence rate (Fig. 3) is nearly identical to MeZO, contradicting the claim of a convergence‑enhanced alternative (R1).

2. **Insufficient evaluation scope**  
   - Experiments are limited to OPT-1.3B and LLaMA-2-7B. In contrast, the main baseline HiZOO was evaluated on larger models (13B–66B), making scaling unclear (R2, R3).  
   - Only a few datasets are used (R2).  
   - No ablation on block size or update interval to understand the trade‑off between memory and performance (R3).  
   - The effect of different BCD rules (Eq. 7) on memory, runtime, and accuracy is not analyzed (R2).

3. **Missing comparisons**  
   - What is the performance of MeZO augmented with BCD? (R2)  
   - How does B‑PDF compare to a properly engineered first‑order BCD that uses gradient checkpointing and weight‑update‑during‑backward (LOMO, GaLORE)? (R4) – Table 3 shows that first‑order BCD, when it does not OOM, clearly outperforms B‑PDF (R4).  
   - Can reducing the LoRA rank achieve the same memory as zeroth‑order methods, and how would that affect performance? (R2)  
   - Reducing batch size in first‑order optimizers could also reduce memory; is a fair memory‑vs‑runtime comparison made? (R2)  

4. **Weakly engineered baselines / overstated memory advantage**  
   - The paper underestimates activation storage: gradient checkpointing (`model.gradient_checkpointing_enable()`) and LOMO‑style updates are not used. With these techniques, Llama‑2‑7B can be fine‑tuned on 24 GB GPUs, yet the paper claims OOM on SST2 (short sequences) on a 48 GB GPU (R4).  
   - For a model with *D* blocks, embedding size *N*, parameters scale as *O*(*DN²*) while activations (with flash attention) scale as *O*(*BLDN*). As models grow, activation storage becomes less dominant, further weakening the paper’s memory motivation (R4).  

5. **Clarity issues**  
   - The definition of a “block” is ambiguous: sometimes it clearly means a layer, other times a subset of layers. The granularity used in all experiments is never stated (R3).  
   - Hyperparameters for BCD (update interval, block granularity) are not explained; how are they determined? (R3)

6. **Runtime concerns**  
   - The method is >50× slower than LoRA (Table 2), raising practical doubts for larger models and larger fine‑tuning datasets (R2).

7. **Reviewer disagreement**  
   - Two reviewers (R2, R3) rated 5, stressing good motivation and memory reduction.  
   - Two reviewers (R1, R4) rated 3, arguing that gains over MeZO are negligible and that the baselines are not fairly engineered to reflect existing memory‑saving techniques.  
   - The overall evidence does not convincingly show that B‑PDF is a practical improvement over either MeZO or well‑engineered first‑order BCD.
