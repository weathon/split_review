## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates whether video diffusion models, like image-based ones, can serve as effective feature extractors for video understanding tasks. The authors introduce a unified probing framework that freezes pre-trained models and trains lightweight task heads on their internal features, evaluating six models (SVD, ModelScope, Open-Sora, SD, SDV, DINOv2, VJEPA) across action recognition, object discovery, scene understanding, and label propagation on datasets including UCF101, HMDB, MOVi-C, MOVi-E MOT, DAVIS, and JHMDB. Results show video diffusion models rank among the top performers, especially for temporal dynamics and scene structure. However, reviewers are split (scores 3,3 vs 5,5) on whether the work provides sufficient novelty, insights, or rigor relative to existing work (especially DIFT) and whether the evidence convincingly demonstrates advantages over simpler self-supervised or image-diffusion baselines.

### Strengths

- The topic is timely and of great interest; analyzing diffusion models for video understanding is important and meaningful for the community.
- The proposed probing framework is technically sound, and the experiments are comprehensive across six models and four tasks.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow; motivation is clearly presented.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty relative to DIFT** (R2, R4): The method essentially applies the diffusion feature extraction approach of DIFT to videos and freezes backbones. It provides little new methodological insight and reads more like a technical report.
- **Results do not show convincing advantage over baselines** (R2, R4): In action recognition, SVD performs similarly to VJEPA. In object discovery, DINOv2 is much better. In scene segmentation, DINOv2 and ModelScope are best on two metrics. In label propagation, SVD/ModelScope perform similar to SD. Given that SVD extends SD 2.1 and has enormous training cost, the minor performance gap is not promising. Overall, good results are not achieved for each individual task.
- **Underwhelming comparison to state-of-the-art and related work** (R1, R4): Table 3 results are below the original DIFT paper (e.g., DIFT reports PCK@0.1=61.1 for JHMDB, while the paper reports 60.48 and 60.52 for SD and SDV; even larger deviations on DAVIS). Missing comparisons with other video models (VideoMAEv1/v2, InternVid) and critical citations (e.g., GenRec, which applies video diffusion to action recognition on K400 and SSv2).
- **Insufficient temporal evaluation** (R3, R4): UCF101 and HMDB do not require strong temporal/dynamic modeling for recognition. Experiments should be conducted on datasets that require effective temporal dynamics (e.g., Something-Something v2, Kinetics). The paper's conclusion that video diffusion models capture motion patterns is a well-known consensus and does not provide new insights.
- **Superficial description of video probing methodology** (R1, R4): Only ~20 lines are spent on the most novel part. Details are missing on implementation, parameter settings, and how individual frames are processed. Feature extraction is not self-contained; it is unclear whether the block index (line 299-303) was optimized per model per task as in Table 4. The paper should explore features from different layers and consider fine-tuning more backbone parameters (R4).
