## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes training a small language model (Arithmetic-GPT) on arithmetic tasks simulating expected value (EV) and present value (PV) calculations, using both uniform and ecologically valid synthetic data. Without any natural language training, the model predicts human risky and intertemporal choices, outperforming traditional cognitive models and large LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-3-70B) in explained variance. The authors argue that ecologically valid arithmetic training is sufficient to produce human-like decision-making patterns.

### Strengths
- Creative and surprising approach: offers a minimal explanation for why LLMs replicate human cognitive biases via next-token prediction on mathematical data, without language supervision (R1).
- Novel method and strong results for a small model trained on relatively little data (R2).
- Demonstrates ability to create a good cognitive model for the target task; deduction of implicit values from Arithmetic-GPT is interesting and insightful (R2).
- Comprehensive benchmarking across multiple models (behavioral, neural, LLMs) and several datasets; systematic comparison strengthens results (R2, R3, R4).
- Clear and well-written paper with informative figures (R2, R3, R4).
- Original test environment to evaluate LLMs as cognitive models; ecological pre-training is a simple yet effective approach (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental depth**: The paper is lean on experiments; lacks ablation on model size and data quantity (R2). The absence of a performance drop when using smaller datasets suggests possible overfitting to the target data distribution rather than generalization from underlying computational principles (R2).
- **Marginal improvement over baselines**: Arithmetic-GPT only marginally outperforms LLaMA-3-70B (Table 3), yet the paper emphasizes its superiority; LLaMA already captures much of the variance without specialized training (R3). A trade-off discussion between a general model (LLaMA) that can act as a cognitive model across many tasks vs. a small dedicated model is missing (R2).
- **Generality claimed but only one task tested**: The approach is claimed to be general, but only expected value calculations are used. Training on other math tasks not relating to expectation (R1) or on language-oriented tasks (R2) is needed to support generality. The connection between the chosen computational task and meaningful cognitive hypotheses is unclear, limiting the approach’s generalizability (R2).
- **Missing analysis of computational abilities**: The rationale for using ecological distribution is that computational errors underlie human deviations from rational choice, but the models’ EV calculation abilities are not benchmarked in- and out-of-distribution (R2). It is unclear whether 15K examples are sufficient to master the arithmetic or whether a model that excels at EV calculation also serves as a better cognitive model (R2).
- **Uncertainty about genuine cognitive modeling**: It is not clear whether Arithmetic-GPT’s human-like behavior reflects actual human decision processes or merely general statistical tendencies that happen to deviate from optimal choice; the task may be too simple for sophisticated claims about LLMs as cognitive models (R3).
- **Unclear preprocessing decisions**: Section 3.4 contains many specific preprocessing choices (e.g., annual discount factor set to 0.85, special token for ambiguous gambles) whose need and impact on results are not explained or justified (R1).
- **Missing comparisons**: Why not also train LLaMA or Arithmetic-GPT on the task features as the MLP does (R3)? Could a strong model trained on human data provide a better upper limit (R2)? The impact of using open models with open data (e.g., LLM360, OLMo) on the LLM experiments is unexplored (R2).
- **Presentation issues**: Typographical errors (“fitted” should be “fit” – line 376; Figure A2 appears under Appendix F instead of E) (R3, R4). LLaMA3 performs slightly better than Arithmetic-GPT on intertemporal choices, but the text does not reflect this, and the top results from LLaMA3 are not bolded in the table (R2).
- **Minority contrast**: While two reviewers (R1, R4) are highly positive (ratings 8) and see no major weaknesses, two others (R2, R3) are more critical (ratings 6), raising concerns about overinterpretation, experimental depth, and task simplicity. The reported scores reflect this disagreement.
