## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the generalization mechanisms of diffusion models, comparing inductive biases of UNet-based denoisers (which exhibit geometry-adaptive harmonic bases) and transformer-based DiTs. The authors find that the harmonic basis analysis does not explain DiT generalization. Instead, they propose that DiT generalization emerges from the locality of self-attention maps. They empirically confirm this by visualizing attention maps showing local patterns (pixels mainly attend to neighbors) that strengthen with more training data. They then modify DiT by injecting local attention windows (masking out pixels outside a neighborhood) to enforce locality. This improves generalization (measured by PSNR gap and FID) when training data is limited, demonstrated on CelebA, ImageNet, and LSUN datasets. The paper also studies the placement and effective size of these windows.

### Strengths
- The problem of understanding generalization in diffusion models is interesting and highly-relevant. (R1, R3)
- The paper is well-presented and easy to follow. (R1)
- Extensive experiments on CelebA, ImageNet, and LSUN show relative improvements in FID and PSNR gap under limited training data. (R1, R3)
- The analysis connecting attention map locality to generalization is coherent and provides an interesting perspective. (R1, R2, R4)
- The “theoretical” analysis—comparing UNet/DiT spectral properties and discovering local attention patterns—is engaging. (R3)
- One reviewer (R3, score 8, low confidence 2) finds the experimental results sufficient for confirming the main hypothesis. (R3)
- Controlling generalization of DiT via local attention windows is a novel approach. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Disconnect in logic**: The first part of the paper on harmonic bases (comparison of UNet vs. DiT eigenvectors) seems disconnected from the proposed method and experimental results. It is unclear how altering attention window size relates to differences in the harmonic basis patterns, and whether the harmonic basis patterns change after modification. (R1)
- **Limited novelty**: The analysis perspective and method are similar to prior work [1]. Local attention windows have been widely used in previous studies. The paper lacks a theoretical explanation for why local attention windows improve generalization in DiTs. (R2, R4)
- **Clarity of Jacobian analysis**: The paper is not self-contained. The Jacobian of the denoising network is crucial for the spectral analysis but is never properly introduced. It is unclear whether the Jacobian is w.r.t. the input image, parameters, or something else; how its eigendecomposition is performed given high dimensionality; and how its spectral properties connect to generalization behavior. Specific questions from reviewers: In Section 2.2, what exactly is the Jacobian? Is it the derivative of the denoising NN w.r.t. its parameters? How is it computed as in [1]? Is it diagonal? Dimensionality (HW × M)? Are eigenvalues or singular values computed? (R2, R3)
- **Concerning results when scaling up**: The FID score increases (worsens) with local attention constraint when training data is large (e.g., full CelebA, ImageNet), which contradicts the claim that locality enhances generalization. Improvements appear limited to small-data regimes, suggesting the benefit may be due to other factors or only helpful when data is scarce. (R1)
- **Confounding by model complexity**: Using local attention may simply reduce model size/complexity. Improved generalization with limited data is a known phenomenon—an appropriate ratio of model size to data. The key reason may be decreased complexity rather than locality per se. (R4)
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: The paper relies heavily on FID. Expanding to other metrics such as CLIP Score would provide a more comprehensive assessment of model capabilities. (R4)
- **Limited dataset and task exploration**: Improvements focus on limited training data scenarios (CelebA, ImageNet subsets, LSUN). Impact on other datasets (e.g., MS COCO, PartiPrompts) or high-resolution tasks is not explored. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
  * Include simplified UNet and UNets from improved diffusion models for comparison. (R2)
  * Provide qualitative visual comparisons (e.g., generated samples) demonstrating generalization improvement of DiT with local attention. (R4)
  * Show impact of local attention window size on performance. (R2)
  * Report results from experiments conducted in latent space. (R1)
  * For Figure 4, show results with different colormap thresholds. (R1)
- **Minor presentation issues**: Grammar/spelling errors (e.g., Section 1: “Their training involves approximates a distribution…” should be “approximating”). (R2) Typos: Line 190 – σ_t undefined; Line 202 – “PNSR” should be “PSNR”. (R1)
- **Open questions indicating missing analysis**:
  * Equation 4: What is \(\hat{x}_0^k\)? What is the intuition behind setting \(K=300\)? (R1)
  * What is the training time for different training sizes \(N\)? Does the norm of attention maps change with training time? (R1)
  * Line 308 states the autoencoder is removed—how do you then go from \(H \times W\) to \((HW) \times d\) token dimension? (R3)
  * Purpose of black boxes over faces in Figure 1? Not an inpainting task? (R3)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R3, score 8, low confidence 2) was highly positive, finding the analysis interesting and the experimental results sufficient. In contrast, reviewers R1 and R4 (score 3 each) raised major concerns about disconnect, limited novelty, confounding factors, and insufficient evaluation. Reviewer R2 (score 6) appreciated the quality and significance but pointed to lack of clarity and novelty.
