## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper defines Federated Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FFSCIL), where each client holds very few samples for new classes. It proposes Unified Optimized Prototype Prompt (UOPP), which combines task-wise prompt learning, unified static-dynamic prototypes rectified via Neural Ordinary Differential Equations, adaptive dual heads, and weighted aggregation. UOPP addresses catastrophic forgetting, overfitting, and prototype bias. Experiments on three datasets show UOPP significantly outperforms existing FCIL, FSCIL, and CIL methods (up to 76% gain in average accuracy and 67% in balance mean accuracy). The method exhibits robustness to varying numbers of clients and global rounds, low communication costs, and moderate running time. Theoretical analysis on convergence and generalization is provided, and source code is publicly available.

### Strengths
- Novel problem definition: Federated Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FFSCIL) is interesting, practical, and has significant real-world deployment value. (R1, R4, R5)
- Effective approach: UOPP integrates prompt learning, dynamic prototype rectification via Neural ODEs, and adaptive dual heads to jointly handle task interference, overfitting, and prototype bias. The ODE-based rectification is a significant technical contribution. (R1, R3, R5)
- Comprehensive experiments: UOPP achieves state-of-the-art performance across three benchmark datasets, with clear gains over baselines from FCIL, FSCIL, and CIL. Extensive ablation studies and robustness analysis (varying clients, global rounds) confirm effectiveness. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
- Strong theoretical support: The paper provides theoretical analysis on convergence and generalization, as well as a deeper stability-plasticity trade-off analysis. (R1, R2, R4, R5)
- Thorough resource analysis: Complexity, running time, parameter count, and communication costs are reported, demonstrating efficiency. (R2)
- Practical design: The method respects data privacy, optimizes communication, and considers computational constraints. (R1)
- Clear implementation details: Algorithm pseudocode and an anonymous GitHub repository facilitate reproducibility. (R2, R5)
- Open source code: Provided to enhance transparency and support further research. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- Good illustrations and analysis of prototype rectification. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and problem statement are unclear**: The introduction fails to clearly establish the need for FFSCIL, why exemplars cannot be saved, and why synthetic samples are avoided. The novelty relative to existing prototype-based and prompt-based continual learning works is insufficiently emphasized. (R2, R4)  (One reviewer (R5, score 8) was strongly positive about the problem’s value, while others (R2, R4) found motivation weak.)
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: Several relevant methods are omitted:
  - PILoRA (ECCV 2024), a recent federated class-incremental learning work using LoRA, should be compared to contrast prompt learning vs. LoRA and prototype usage. (R2)
  - Prototype calibration works for FSCIL: TEEN (ECCV 2024), statistics calibration (ICCV 2023), and exemplar-free drift compensation (ECCV 2024) are directly relevant for prototype rectification; the paper should compare and discuss differences between class-similarity-based rectification and Neural ODE-based rectification. (R2, R4)
  - FedNCM, a federated learning method that uses prototype-based NCM classifiers without training, is a missing baseline that could show performance without further fine-tuning. (R4)
- **Inappropriate baselines**: Many compared methods are designed for either federated CIL or few-shot CIL, but not both, making it hard to isolate the gains specifically from the FFSCIL setting. (R3)
- **Data heterogeneity and non-i.i.d. analysis insufficient**: The paper lacks rigorous evaluation under varying degrees of data heterogeneity and class imbalance across clients. The prototype rectification may produce biased prototypes when local data distribution differs from global, especially with few samples. (R1, R3)
- **Data privacy concerns**: The support set S is generated from Gaussian distributions centered on prototypes, which acts as pseudo-rehearsal. This technique (also used in HiDe-Prompt) may violate data privacy principles. (R2)
- **Computational cost of Neural ODEs**: The use of Neural ODEs may demand significant resources; the paper does not provide a detailed breakdown of time/memory requirements for training and inference, nor a comparison with simpler alternatives for prototype rectification. (R1)
- **Unclear experimental details**:
  - The few-shot constraint (T>0) is vague: Is it at the client level (random subset of whole training set) or task level (fixed subset)? If client-level, global aggregation leverages more data; if task-level, global rectification is limited. (R5)
  - How is the query set Q constructed from prototypes Z_l? (R5)
  - What loss is used to train GradNet? (R5)
  - How are client weights ω_l determined in server-side aggregation? (R5)
  - The implementation of parameter D in Table 4 is not explained. (R5)
- **Long-term evaluation missing**: The paper does not analyze how performance degrades over extended periods and increasing numbers of tasks, including quantitative measurement of catastrophic forgetting and knowledge retention of base classes. (R1)
- **Scalability questions**: No analysis of how the model handles a significant increase in clients or data volume, or continuous addition of new categories without large increases in training time/resources. (R1)
- **Balance mean accuracy unclear**: It is not defined; recent FSCIL papers use harmonic mean accuracy to evaluate old and new class performance. (R4)
- **Pre-trained weights not stated**: Whether the ViT model uses pre-trained weights is not mentioned; fair comparison requires all methods to start from the same weights. (R4)
- **Running time concern**: Although claims of low resource consumption, UOPP’s running time (6.05 hours) is longer than most other methods. (R2)
- **Typos**: line 270 “parameter Ψ” should be Φ; line 362 “10 round per class” should be “10 round per task”. (R5) Table captions should be placed at the top per ICLR template. (R2)
- **Specific technical queries that overlap with criticisms**:
  - How many iterations are used for prototype rectification? Is there an early stopping mechanism to prevent overfitting? (R1)
  - Can Neural ODEs be replaced by simpler strategies? What is the impact without ODE? (R1)
  - Why does larger global round lead to worse performance (Table 3)? (R5)
  - Is there an error in equation (4)? (R5)
  - Explore additional few-shot settings between one-shot and five-shot, and more than five shots. (R2)
