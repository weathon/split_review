## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sequence Location Masking (SLM), a data augmentation technique for pseudoknot-inclusive RNA secondary structure prediction (SSP) using masked language modeling and uncertainty quantification. The authors extend three genomic foundation models, fine-tune them on augmented datasets, and report improved F1-scores (e.g., >89% on RNAStrAlign, >66% on bpRNA). One reviewer rated the paper 5, highlighting the thoughtful design and reported improvements; two reviewers rated it 3, citing major weaknesses in comparison, dataset splits, and novelty.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and challenging problem: RNA secondary structure prediction with pseudoknots (all reviewers).
- The paper is clear and well written (Reviewer 2; also noted by Reviewer 3).
- The data augmentation technique appears thoughtfully designed, targeting key limitations like data scarcity and class imbalance, and shows benefits for the specific models used (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3).
- Reported improvements in F1-score and MCC across datasets illustrate impact on imbalanced class representation, particularly for pseudoknot-inclusive structures (Reviewer 1).
- Leveraging advances in genomic foundation models (GFMs) for this task is a sensible approach (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison with state-of-the-art methods.** The paper does not compare against UFold, SPOT-RNA, SPOT-RNA2, RNAformer, or Probabilistic Transformer (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The authors argue that these methods use a different data format (adjacency matrix or BPSEQ), but conversion to dot-bracket is possible (Reviewer 2). Additionally, IPKnot and PKiss are not considered SOTA; PKiss can only predict a subset of pseudoknots – this limitation is not mentioned (Reviewer 3). The authors claim SOTA performance, but only compare to outdated baselines.
- **New, non-comparable dataset split.** Instead of using existing evaluation splits (e.g., PK-Test from Gong et al., TS0-3, TS-hard), the authors introduce a custom split for RNAStrAlign and BpRNA-1m, making results incomparable with prior work (Reviewer 3). No experiments on ArchiveII (Reviewer 1) or high-quality structures from the PDB (Reviewer 2; >6000 structures) are included.
- **Homology/train-test leakage not properly addressed.** Data are split using CD-Hit based only on sequence similarity, but structural similarity should also be considered to avoid homologous training and test sets. This is a known issue (Flamm et al. 2022; Szikszai et al. 2022; Qiu et al. 2023) (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Methodology clarity and details.** The steps for Sequence Location Masking (SLM) are unclear; a diagram would help (Reviewer 1). The abbreviation UQ is not introduced (Reviewer 3). In Table 2, “random mutation” is not well-defined, and it is unclear why SpliceBERT performs so poorly with augmentation (Reviewer 1). The exact changes made to the GFMs for secondary structure prediction are not described, even in the appendix (Reviewer 3).
- **Timeliness and scope of the approach.** The augmentation only works for secondary structures represented in dot-bracket notation, so recent deep learning methods that use adjacency matrices cannot benefit – this limitation is not discussed (Reviewer 3). Similarly, the method is limited to single-nucleotide tokenization; the paper sells other tokenizations (e.g., k-mer, BPE) as limitations of other foundation models without acknowledging this restriction (Reviewer 3). The work is very specific and does not clearly align with the ICLR audience (Reviewer 3).
- **Inaccuracies in claims.** Line 55/56 incorrectly states that RNABench does not consider pseudoknots; it does (Reviewer 3). Line 67-70 contains a contradiction: calling SSP NP-Hard, but then saying dynamic programming methods require polynomial time (Reviewer 3). Line 79 cites a paper for data augmentation [Runge et al.] that does not actually use data augmentation (Reviewer 3).
- **BpRNA pseudoknot handling.** BpRNA reports pseudoknots up to page number 7, but the authors only consider up to page 4. Rare pseudoknots (stronger class imbalance) would be a more interesting test case (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure 3 clarity.** It is unclear how F1-score is calculated separately for each base-pair type (Reviewer 1).
- **No code released for reproducibility** (Reviewers 2, 3).
