## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DebiasDiff, a plug-and-play debiasing method that learns attribute latent directions (gender, race) via self-discovered 1-D adapters and a distribution indicator, without requiring additional data or model retraining. Reviewers agree on advantages: no extra annotation, computational efficiency during inference, strong debiasing results, transferability across diffusion model variants, and informative ablation studies. However, significant concerns are raised: novelty is limited due to close similarity to Concept Sliders [1] (nearly identical loss formulation), missing comparisons with prior self-discovering methods (ITI-Gen, Self-discovering interpretable diffusion latent directions, Concept Sliders) and omission of the standard Winobias dataset. Evaluation is restricted to only gender/race biases, single-person/occupation prompts, and fixed bias ratios (no ablations with varying ratios). The intersectional debiasing approach uses orthogonal loss that prevents reuse of individually trained adapters for multi-attribute debiasing. One reviewer (score 3) was notably critical of novelty and reusability, while the other two reviewers (scores 5 and 6) were more positive but noted missing comparisons and limited evaluation scope.

### Strengths
- Training attribute-specific latent directions via adapter learning does not require additional annotated data or reference datasets. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Applying the distribution indicator during inference is computationally cheaper and faster than alternatives. (Reviewer 1)
- Strong debiasing results on single and multiple attributes (gender, race) across various datasets. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- The technique transfers across multiple diffusion model variants without retraining. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of each component (e.g., orthogonal loss) for final performance. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. (Reviewer 3)
- Comparisons with relevant baselines show improvement over previous state-of-the-art. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 also notes improvement)

### Weaknesses
- (Major) Novelty concern: The optimization process for learning adapters closely resembles Concept Sliders [1]. The loss $\epsilon L = \epsilon\theta(X, c_t, t) + \eta(\epsilon\theta(X, c_+, t) - \epsilon\theta(X, c_-, t))$ becomes identical when the negative prompt $c_-$ is replaced by the neutral prompt $c_t$. The modification to 1-D adapters (as in [2]) applied only to cross-attention layers is seen as incremental. One reviewer (score 3) found the novelty low, while others (scores 5 and 6) noted similarity but acknowledged contributions. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- Missing comparisons with prior self-discovering debiasing methods: ITI-Gen [a], Self-discovering interpretable diffusion latent directions [2], and Concept Sliders [1]. The omission of the standard Winobias dataset [3] from comparisons is not justified, and comparison protocols differ from prior works (e.g., FID vs. BRISQUE). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- Limited evaluation: Only gender and racial biases tested; no results for other attributes (e.g., hair color, eyeglasses). (Reviewer 1)
- Evaluation restricted to single-person/single-occupation prompts; no results for images with multiple persons or scene-based images. (Reviewer 1)
- Fixed bias ratio (e.g., 50:50 gender) in evaluations; missing ablations for varying ratios (e.g., 0:100 to 100:0) as shown in ITI-Gen. (Reviewer 1)
- Intersectional debiasing uses an orthogonal loss between attributes during training, which prevents reuse of individually trained adapters for multi-attribute debiasing without retraining, limiting reusability. (Reviewer 3)
- FD numbers reported are higher than those in Balancing act [3], likely due to using stable diffusion vs. unconditional models; this discrepancy and the choice of BRISQUE over FID need clarification. (Reviewer 3)
- Equation error in Figure 4: $\epsilon\theta(X_t, g_t, t) + \eta(\epsilon\theta(X_t, \textbf{d}, t) - \epsilon\theta(X_t, g_t, t))$ should be corrected. (Reviewer 3)
- (Minor) Lack of justification for why attribute adapters are necessary instead of simply using the attribute token internally in the conditioning. (Reviewer 2)
