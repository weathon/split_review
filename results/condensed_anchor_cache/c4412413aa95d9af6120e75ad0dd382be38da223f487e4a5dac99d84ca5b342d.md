## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Many-objective Multi-solution Transport (MosT), a bi-level optimization framework that uses optimal transport (OT) to assign subsets of many objectives (tasks/clients) to a smaller set of solutions (models), with the goal of finding diverse Pareto-stationary solutions. The lower level solves the OT matching, and the upper level trains each solution with MGDA on its assigned weighted objectives. MosT is evaluated on federated learning, multi-task learning, and mixture-of-prompt learning, outperforming baselines like FedAvg, FedProx, MGDA, linear scalarization, and TAG.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and clearly presents the idea.
- The combination of optimal transport and MGDA is a natural and novel approach for many-objective multi-solution optimization.
- The paper addresses an under-explored but important setting where the number of objectives can exceed the number of solutions.
- MosT achieves good performance across synthetic problems and multiple real-world applications (federated learning, multi-task learning, mixture-of-prompt learning for LLMs).
- The framework naturally yields specialized models (experts) for complementary subsets of objectives due to the sparse OT solution.
- The paper provides both theoretical convergence guarantees and comprehensive empirical validation.
- Experiments are extensive and cover several diverse domains.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing strong baselines:** The experimental comparisons use mostly trivial baselines (e.g., FedAvg, FedProx, MGDA, linear scalarization). The paper does not compare against state-of-the-art methods for many-objective optimization with multiple solutions, such as sum-of-minimum optimization [1, ICML 2024], personalized federated learning via mixture-of-distributions (e.g., EM-based methods), or bi-level mask learning for multi-task decision transformers (e.g., HarmoDT). These omissions make it hard to judge the novelty and practical advantage of MosT.

- **Evaluation metrics are insufficient:** The primary metric is the mean of average performance across all objectives and solutions. This metric can mask poor performance on individual objectives and does not capture solution diversity or Pareto front coverage. Hypervolume is the established metric in MOO; the paper claims it is computationally infeasible for many objectives but does not estimate its complexity or provide an alternative justification. The average metric alone cannot validate the claimed diversity of the Pareto set.

- **Theoretical concerns:**
    - **Hypergradient ignoring:** In the bi-level formulation, the OT weights \(\Gamma\) depend on the solution parameters \(\theta\) through the constraint; the gradient of the upper-level objective should include a hypergradient term \(d\Gamma^*(\theta)/d\theta\). The paper ignores this dependence and treats \(\Gamma\) as a constant when computing gradients. The authors provide no analysis of this approximation’s effect on convergence or solution quality.
    - **Guarantee not stronger than MGDA:** The lower-level problem is solved with MGDA, which can reach any Pareto optimal solution of the weighted problem—the same Pareto set as the original multi-objective problem. The paper does not provide any theoretical guarantee that MosT finds *diverse* Pareto solutions, only that each solution is Pareto stationary. The mechanism by which the OT-based weights ensure diversity lacks formal support.
    - **Concave regions:** MGDA and linear scalarization cannot reach concave parts of the Pareto front. The paper does not discuss whether MosT overcomes this limitation or provide theoretical/empirical evidence that it covers the full front including concave regions.

- **Motivation and clarity issues:**
    - The terms “diversity” and “complementary subsets” are not clearly defined or motivated. The informal definition in Section 4 is insufficient. The paper does not connect the OT weight matrix to a concrete diversity metric or explain what constitutes a complementary subset.
    - **Balanced weight matrix:** The preference for a balanced OT matrix (no model dominating most objectives) is not justified. If many objectives share similar structure, a single solution may be optimal for all; forcing balanced assignments could degrade performance.
    - **Usefulness for \(n \ll m\):** The paper claims MosT works when solutions outnumber objectives, but it is unclear why optimizing separate models for each objective (trivially giving \(m=n\) solutions) would not be strictly better. The advantage over per-objective optimization is not explained.
    - **Diversity in federated learning:** Figure 3 shows client-model diversity throughout training, but in standard FL, models are aggregated and redistributed, leading to consensus. The paper does not clarify whether MosT uses aggregation or keeps separate client models, nor why diversity among clients is desirable (the goal is usually a single global model).

- **Experimental issues and missing analysis:**
    - **Runtime anomaly:** In Table 4, linear scalarization takes longer than MGDA, which is counterintuitive. The computational overhead of linear scalarization over MGDA is not explained.
    - **TAG failure in MTL:** Reviewer 1 doubts the reported failure of TAG in the MTL setting; TAG groups tasks and trains groups individually, which should not fail. The paper needs a detailed analysis of why TAG underperforms.
    - **Ablation studies are insufficient:** The ablation for “diversity-encouragement regularization” provides only a weak explanation (“enhances performance and fairness”) without granular analysis (e.g., different regularization strengths, per-objective performance). More rigorous ablations are needed.
    - **MGDA weights on bias only:** The rationale for computing MGDA weights using only the final-layer bias weights is not justified.
    - **Figure 1 clarity:** Figure 1 should indicate that 5 solutions are shown and the text should explain how one solution is selected for a given objective.
    - **Mean loss description opposite to figure:** Lines 349-351 state “the mean loss increases and then decreases,” but Figure 2 appears to show the opposite.

- **Baselines and related work are outdated:** The paper cites only one reference from 2023–2024. The baselines used (FedAvg, FedProx, MGDA) are well-established but not state-of-the-art. Recent advances in MGDA variants, personalized FL, and multi-task optimization are not discussed or compared.

- **Notation and minor errors:**
    - The simplex notation should be \(\Delta^{n-1}\) and \(\Delta^{m-1}\), not \(\Delta\).
    - Norms: \(||a||\) and \(||a||_2\) are both used without definition.
    - Compilation errors: “\(\tau=0\)” in Line 280, inconsistent dash styles in lines 74 and 86.
    - Repeated “Eq. equation *” errors appear in lines 196, 206.
    - Equation (45-46) to (47) requires \(L_i\) to be bounded; this assumption is not stated.
    - Lines 190-215 are a standard description of MGDA and could be placed in the preliminaries section.

- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (Reviewer 2) found no noticeable weakness and rated the paper 8, while other reviewers (scores 5-6) raised multiple significant concerns, particularly regarding theoretical rigor, baseline selection, and evaluation metrics.

**Questions from reviewers (overlap with weaknesses):**
- Can the authors provide theoretical or empirical evidence that MosT overcomes the concave-front limitation of MGDA and linearization?
- Why is MosT preferable to directly optimizing separate parameters for each objective when \(n \ll m\)?
- In FL, how and when are client models aggregated? Why is client diversity maintained and desirable?
- What is the time complexity of hypervolume for \(M\) solutions and \(N\) objectives? Why is it not used?
- In the average accuracy metric, what exact average is computed (over all model-objective pairs)?
- How does the OT weight matrix relate to a concrete diversity metric? Does the optimization guarantee diversity?
- Can the authors compare MosT quantitatively/qualitatively with the sum-of-minimum method [1] and recent personalized FL/multi-task methods?
