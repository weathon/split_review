## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TNCN, a temporal version of Neural Common Neighbor (NCN) for link prediction in temporal graphs. It combines a memory-based backbone (resembling TGN) with a temporal common neighbor extractor that dynamically updates a neighbor dictionary and uses multi-hop common neighbors between source and target nodes. The model is evaluated on five datasets from the Temporal Graph Benchmark (TGB), achieving state-of-the-art results on three of them and showing speedups of up to 6.4× over GNN baselines. While the idea of extracting common neighbors in dynamic graphs is not new and some components mirror existing methods (CAWN, KDD2024 work), the engineering integration of memory and CN techniques is noted. Reviewers disagree: two rate it 6, one 5, and one 3, with the latter strongly criticizing novelty, theoretical claims, and experimental gains.

### Strengths
- **Extensive and well-conducted experiments.** Evaluated against 9 baselines on 5 TGB datasets; best on 3/5 datasets (R1, R2, R3). Sufficient experimental results demonstrating effectiveness and efficiency (R4).
- **Interesting and compelling motivation.** The idea of explicitly extracting common neighbor features for temporal link prediction is well-motivated (R3). Addresses a cutting-edge research direction (R4).
- **Clear presentation overall.** The method section is clearly described with formulas and definitions (R1). Paper is well-organized (R3), with proofs in the appendix that enhance professionalism (R1). Writing logic is clear (R4).
- **Engineering contribution.** The combination of common neighbor techniques with memory-based methods is a practical engineering approach (R2). 
- **Theoretical analysis provided** supporting the method, though one reviewer considers it derivative (R3, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns.** 
  - The core components rely heavily on established techniques: the memory module closely resembles TGN with no clear innovations (R2); the common neighbor extractor uses multi-hop methods similar to CAWN (R2) and closely resembles a KDD2024 work [1] (R3). 
  - The combination of these components is not a significant conceptual leap (R2). 
  - The paper does not clearly articulate the specific differences between TNCN and its static predecessor NCN, nor why the temporal extension constitutes a core contribution (R4).
  - One reviewer (R3) notes that the CN extractor includes no specific improvements for temporal graphs; simply extracting "monotone k-hop events" does not substantiate temporal adaptation.
- **Theoretical claims presented as novel are already established.** The O(|E|) memory complexity for event aggregation and the upper/lower bounds are known from prior work (Caen, 1998) and do not represent new insights (R2). 
- **Experimental limitations and minor performance gains.** 
  - Performance improvements over baselines are minimal; results in Table 5 (metric unclear—possibly AUC or AP) show only limited gains, not substantial enough to justify the model’s complexity (R2). 
  - On the *comment* dataset, TNCN with 0–2-hop common neighbors performs worse than with 0–1-hop, deviating from the trend on other datasets (R1 Q2). This inconsistency is not explained.
  - Important baselines are missing: since TNCN extends static NCN, static link prediction methods should have been included (R3). 
  - The impact of batch size on the strategy is not discussed, though the model operates in batch processing mode (R4). 
  - No parameter analysis is provided for hyperparameters such as multi-hop settings (R1, R3) or the surprise threshold (R1 Q1), which would help validate robustness.
  - No case studies are provided to illustrate practical application (R3).
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** 
  - The abstract implies that common neighbor methods are used mainly in static graphs, overlooking their established role in dynamic graph modeling (R2). 
  - The motivation for combining memory and neighbor techniques is only briefly mentioned at the end of the introduction (R2). 
  - Notation is ambiguous: *emb* in Equation 2 is not explained (R3). 
  - The method constructs a common neighbor neighborhood from source–destination pairs, possibly losing information from non-common nodes (R3). 
  - The main text dedicates significant space to proofs, making the method and experiment sections less substantial and some information less clear (R4). 
  - The paper should more strongly emphasize its contributions and logical arrangement (R4). 
  - A pseudo-code would make the relatively complex process easier to follow (R1).
- **Missing computational complexity discussion.** The paper should compare computational complexity with similar methods (both static and temporal) (R4).
- **Reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (R2, score 3) is significantly more critical, finding the novelty limited, performance gains marginal, and theoretical results derivative; other reviewers (scores 5–6) acknowledge the contributions but raise substantial concerns.
