## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a unified dual RL framework based on Lagrange duality of regularized policy learning, casting several offline RL and offline IL methods as instances of dual-Q or dual-V problems. The authors propose new algorithms: ReCOIL for offline IL (removing coverage assumptions and discriminator) and f-DVL for offline RL (addressing XQL’s training instability). Experiments on locomotion, manipulation, and navigation benchmarks show competitive performance. Reviewers praise the conceptual unification and thorough derivations but identify multiple technical bugs, presentation issues, and overclaimed results. Scores range from 1 to 8, with significant disagreement on the severity of theoretical errors and empirical strength.

### Strengths
- The dual RL formulation provides a clean, unifying lens that connects many recent offline RL/IL methods (IQLearn, XQL, OPOLO, SMODICE, CQL/ATAC) and reveals shared structure.
- The analysis is self-contained, with detailed derivations (Appendix C) that are easy to follow given basic convex optimization knowledge.
- For offline IL, ReCOIL relaxes restrictive coverage assumptions, avoids discriminator instability, and achieves strong imitation results from arbitrary off-policy data.
- For offline RL, f-DVL replaces XQL’s Gumbel regression loss with alternative f-divergences, improving training stability.
- The framework’s adaptability spans offline IL, offline RL, and online RL settings.
- The theoretical contribution (showing that pessimistic value learning like CQL emerges naturally from dual RL) is considered a potentially impactful insight for future research.

### Weaknesses
- **Technical errors and missing assumptions**  
  - Proposition 4: The derived objective (Eq. 70, Appendix D.1) corresponds to ATAC (Cheng et al., 2022), not CQL; the connection should be updated to ATAC.  
  - Theorem 1 proof (Appendix C): The max_d is incorrectly moved inside min_Q after Eq. (108); strong duality must be applied first.  
  - Eq. (10) derivation implicitly assumes d₀ = d_E (initial state distribution matches expert), which is a strong assumption not commonly made in offline/off-policy RL; this should be highlighted, or Eq. (140) presented as the main result. Also, a factor 1/4 is missing in the square loss term.  
  - Proposition 3 proof contains multiple issues: (a) f′ and (f′)⁻¹ are only non-decreasing, not strictly increasing; (b) the step from Eq. (79) to (80) uses invalid implications (e.g., claiming (f′)⁻¹(t) > 0 ⇒ t > 0, which fails for f(t) = |t‑1|); this invalidates the remaining derivation.  
  - Typo in Eq. (35): second term inside f* should be γ Σ_{s′,a′} p(s′|s,a) π(a′|s′) Q(s′,a′), not π(a|s′).  

- **Empirical claims and experimental design**  
  - f-DVL’s gains over existing offline RL methods (especially IQL) are marginal in many tasks; the performance improvement is not as significant as claimed across the full benchmark.  
  - The claim that “f-DVL leads to noticeable improvements even in online RL benchmarks” is overstated: baselines are too few (only SAC), and performance is not noticeably better in ~75% of the tasks. Additional baselines (PPO, TD3, AlgeaDICE, RRS, BAC, REDQ) and more tasks are needed to support the claim.  
  - The offline IL experiment design is questionable: the suboptimal dataset contains 200 expert demonstrations (though unlabeled). A more convincing evaluation would remove expert trajectories entirely. Table 2 lists “1000 expert transitions” – is this one expert trajectory?  
  - The claim in Sec. 7.1 that SAC is “brittle to off-policy data” is not fair; filling the replay buffer arbitrarily with expert data at the start is not a proper test of an ADP-style algorithm.  
  - Several crucial experimental results (Appendix H) should be moved to the main paper for coherence.  

- **Presentation and clarity**  
  - Figure 1(a) is difficult to interpret: axes, labels, colors, and the connection between the policy visualization and distribution gap need a clearer caption.  
  - Table 2 highlighting is inconsistent (e.g., hopper medium-few-expert: RCE has highest mean but is not highlighted in blue). Table 3 highlighting criteria are unclear and inconsistently applied.  
  - The paper lacks standardized metrics and confidence intervals in several result tables.  
  - Wording issues: Sec. 4.1 last paragraph – “This insight also allows us to cast IL method OPOLO …” should reference “this objective function” instead (since the objective originates from SMODICE). Sec. 4.2 first paragraph – “Unfortunately” is inappropriate for describing XQL’s lack of policy regularization. The sentence about “unfortunately … has evaded connections … Proposition 2 shows, perhaps surprisingly, that XQL can be cast…” is confusing.  
  - The connection between the sentence “To prevent extrapolation error … we rely on an implicit maximizer …” and Eq. (12) is unclear.  
  - Typos: page 36 – “We base the implementation of SAC off pytorch_sac …”.  

- **Unaddressed technical clarifications**  
  - In Eq. (10), is the reward assumed zero in the Bellman consistency term?  
  - How does ReCOIL estimate policy visitation and reward for the results in Sec. 7.1? Clarify if Fig. 1 and Fig. 10 use OPOLO or SMODICE for comparison.  
  - What do the distributions D and dᴼ refer to in Eq. (12) and (13)? Are they both d_mixᴱ,ˢ?  

- **Title and scope**  
  - Claiming “New” in the title is unnecessary; “A unified framework” is more appropriate.  
  - XQL’s heavy hyperparameter sensitivity should be acknowledged; IQL is a more general example of implicit policy improvement.  

- **Disagreement among reviewers**  
  - One reviewer (score 8) finds the discriminator removal and framework adaptability highly commendable, while another (score 1) considers the theoretical errors critical enough to compromise the paper’s contributions. The majority (scores 6,6,8) recognize value but agree that technical issues and overclaimed empirical results need correction.
