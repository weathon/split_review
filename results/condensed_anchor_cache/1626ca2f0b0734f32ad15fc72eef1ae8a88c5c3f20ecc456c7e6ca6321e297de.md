## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Interpolate, a plasticity-injection method that periodically resets the most active (high-utility) neurons by interpolating the network parameters with a functionally equivalent random permutation (model merging). The method is evaluated on three types of distribution shifts (shuffle, rotation, noise) on CIFAR-10 with small MLP and CNN architectures. The results show that resetting active parameters can be competitive with existing methods (ReDo, CBP), challenging the common belief that only dormant-neuron resetting works.

### Strengths
- The paper presents a new and intriguing empirical finding: resetting the most active neurons (not just dormant ones) can help maintain plasticity and improve generalization, running counter to conventional expectations. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- The use of model merging (via permutation invariance) as a resetting mechanism is novel in the context of plasticity loss. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper provides a clear background, making the problem accessible. (Reviewer 1)
- Section 4.1’s comparison of interpolation vs. random noise resetting is a useful ablation. (Reviewer 3)
- Section 4.2’s comparison of different utility functions (including active selection) reveals that the choice of which neurons to reset matters, even if no clear winner emerges. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper is generally well written and clearly states its contributions. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient empirical validation across datasets and architectures.** Experiments are limited to CIFAR-10 with simple MLP (3 hidden layers) and CNN (2 conv layers). Testing on broader datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100, noisy datasets, Permuted MNIST, Continual ImageNet), more complex architectures (ResNet, high-capacity networks), and other problem domains (deep RL) is needed to establish robustness. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **No clear demonstration of plasticity loss in the baseline.** For Permuted CIFAR-10 and Noisy CIFAR-10 in Section 4.4, SGD actually increases accuracy over tasks, meaning the setting may not induce plasticity loss. This casts doubt on whether Interpolate is truly mitigating the phenomenon. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of explanation for why resetting active neurons works.** The paper does not provide intuitive motivation, underlying mechanism, or theoretical justification. Why would unlearning the most active neurons help? Is the dormant-neuron phenomenon from ReDo also present? No analysis of loss landscape, activations/representations before/after reset, or changes in dormancy metrics is given. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Implementation choices not justified.** Multiple design dimensions are fixed arbitrarily without ablation:
  - Number of functionally equivalent parameters averaged: why two? Not exploring more than two. (Reviewer 2)
  - Mixing ratio: only 0.5/0.5; other convex combinations (\(\lambda\) and \(\sum\lambda_i\)) not tested. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
  - Selection of \(\theta_{\text{perm}}\): why uniformly random? Could other criteria (e.g., gradient alignment, sharpness) yield better resets? (Reviewer 2)
  - Random noise vs. interpolation: only one “reset function” beyond random noise is compared; other perturbations (small noise, only blending with functionally equivalent parameters, re-randomization, re-initialization, checkpoint-based resetting) are not explored. (Reviewers 2,3)
- **Insufficient comparisons with other resetting methods.** Missing comparisons with Shrink & Perturb, resetting last few layers, DASH, and other state-of-the-art resetting techniques. (Reviewer 2)
- **Hyperparameter and implementation details missing or inconsistent.** Details needed: exact model architecture (use of normalization), batch size, running time, number of runs for each experiment, definitions (“reset with random noise”, “dormancy” in Fig. 2, “jump in training loss” in Fig. 1). Hyperparameters for each method in Figs. 2 and 3 are not reported. The optimal hyperparameters found in Sec. 4.4 are not used in Fig. 2, reducing interpretability. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and insufficient tuning.** In Fig. 2, no hyperparameter search is performed; the default may be fortuitously tailored to “Interpolate (active: 20%)”. Ablation on resetting ratios (only 5% and 20%) is too narrow. In Fig. 3, the optimal combination ratio changes drastically with number of epochs (10 epochs → 10%/90% optimal; more epochs → performance drops that combination to worst), suggesting high sensitivity to training setup. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Statistical significance and presentation of results.** Only averages over 5 seeds; no medians, interquartile means, or confidence intervals. Section 4.1 appears to show results from a single seed, making the correlation weak. The x-axis in Fig. 3 differs between subplots without explanation. Fig. 3 also lacks the 100%/0% and 0%/100% anchor points. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Missing exploration of key hypotheses.** The claim in lines 197–199 (that the merged model provides an appropriate reset location) lacks empirical support. No experiments comparing \(\theta\), \(\theta_{\text{perm}}\), \(\theta_{\text{reset}}\), \(\theta_{\text{reset}}+\epsilon\), or averaging more than two permuted models are provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Terminology is misleading.** The title, abstract, and introduction claim “resetting active neurons,” but Interpolate is an interpolation (0.5/0.5 mix) with a functionally equivalent permutation, not a full reset. This is more a model-merging technique. The term “model merging” itself is also misleading, as it commonly refers to ensembling separate models rather than resetting. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Notation and writing errors.** In Eq. (2) and surrounding text: definition of \(\phi_k\) in \(\mathbb{R}^k\) unclear; typo \(pi_k\) should be \(\pi_k\) (line 188). Algorithm 1: \(D\) repurposed; loop variable mismatch. \(k\) changes from meaning number to proportion between sections. Caption typo (“Apply” → “Applying”). Appendix A double full stop. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Missing experiments in reinforcement learning** – a domain where plasticity loss is heavily studied. (Reviewer 3)
- **Dormancy appears to increase without plateauing** in Fig. 2; showing evolution over more tasks would clarify long-term behavior. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of results with Adam optimizer.** (Reviewer 4)
