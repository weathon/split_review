## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a mixture-of-experts (MoE) framework for dataset distillation (DD) to mitigate cross-architecture performance degradation. Multiple expert models each distill a distinct subset of the data, with a distance correlation minimization strategy encouraging diverse representations. During evaluation, a mixup-based fusion combines synthetic data from experts. Experiments on small datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) and architectures (ConvNet-3, ResNet18, VGG‑11, AlexNet) show improved cross-architecture performance, especially in low-data regimes.

### Strengths
- Novel application of mixture-of-experts to dataset distillation, addressing the relevant problem of cross-architecture performance degradation. The combination of multiple experts with distance correlation minimization is an innovative approach to promote diversity in distilled data.
- Extensive experimental validation across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) and architectures (ConvNet-3, ResNet18, VGG‑11, AlexNet). Results consistently demonstrate advantages, particularly under low‑data settings.
- Mixup‑based fusion strategy is thoughtfully designed and implemented to exploit complementary information across experts.
- Methodology is well‑documented with clear mathematical formulations. Ablation studies justify the inclusion of distance correlation minimization and mixup fusion.
- The proposed method is compatible with most existing dataset distillation methods (each expert can use any distillation technique). Detailed implementation is provided.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical justification.** Why distance correlation minimization specifically enhances cross‑architecture generalizability is not explained. A stronger theoretical or empirical analysis connecting this metric to transferability is needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Lack of substantive novelty.** The approach primarily combines existing techniques (MoE, distance correlation, Mixup) without major methodological advance. The “MoE” is a simple ensemble of experts rather than a true sparsely activated mixture‑of‑experts with adaptive routing (Reviewer 3).
- **Outdated baselines and datasets.** Experiments are confined to small datasets (CIFAR‑10/100) and older architectures (ConvNet‑3, VGG‑11). No evaluation on modern large‑scale tasks (e.g., ImageNet) or architectures (e.g., ViT, EfficientNet), limiting demonstrated scalability and practical relevance (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing computational trade‑offs.** The paper does not compare training time, memory usage, or resource consumption of the multi‑expert setup versus single‑expert distillation or direct training on the full dataset. Current DD methods often require significantly more computation to create distilled datasets than training on the full data, undermining the efficiency goal (Reviewers 2, 3). Concrete metrics on training time and memory, especially as the number of experts or dataset size increases, are absent (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete ablation to validate the MoE framework.** The setting “mixup‑based fusion without distance correlation” is not reported, so the pure effect of multiple experts is not isolated (Reviewer 4). Further, an ablation comparing one expert with IPC=20 to two experts each with IPC=10 fused into IPC=10 would clarify benefits (Reviewer 4).
- **Same‑architecture performance not reported.** Results focus on cross‑architecture experiments; why the MoE framework improves cross‑architecture but not necessarily same‑architecture performance is unexplained. The methodology has no direct connection to cross‑architecture experiments (Reviewer 4).
- **Assumption of equal storage budget per expert** may not be practical. Impact of imbalanced storage distributions (e.g., 70‑30 split) is not explored (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited discussion of limitations** and scenarios where the multi‑expert approach may not yield improvements (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparison to alternative diversity‑promoting methods** (e.g., ensemble regularization, other diversity metrics) to demonstrate the uniqueness of distance correlation (Reviewer 2).
- **No experiments beyond classification** (e.g., object detection) to verify generalizability (Reviewer 1).
- **Mixup parameter choice not justified.** How Beta distribution parameters were selected and their impact on performance is not analyzed (Reviewer 1).
- **Limit on number of experts not investigated.** Whether diminishing returns occur with many experts is unexplored (Reviewer 1).
- **One reviewer notes missing CIFAR‑100 experiments** (Reviewer 4 states they are missing; Reviewer 2 claims they are present – inconsistency in the paper).
- **Minor typos** (e.g., Line 508.5 quote error) (Reviewer 4).
- **Figure 2 low resolution** – difficult to see diversity in synthetic images (Reviewer 4).
