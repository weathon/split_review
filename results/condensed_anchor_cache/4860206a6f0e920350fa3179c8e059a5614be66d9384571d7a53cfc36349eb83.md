## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ADDP, a framework to align generative diffusion denoising with discriminative perception objectives. It identifies three issues: (1) unequal contribution of denoising timesteps to perception quality, (2) training-denoising distribution shift causing unexpected quality drops at later steps, and (3) the interactive potential of the generative process. Corresponding solutions are contribution-aware timestep sampling/loss weighting, diffusion-tailored data augmentation (applied to ground truth), and an interactive user interface via classifier-free guidance. Evaluated on depth estimation (Marigold), referring image segmentation (RIS; InstructPix2Pix), and generalist perception (InstructCV), the method improves performance without architectural changes.

### Strengths
- **Novel and meaningful problem discovery.** The paper addresses a previously unexplored gap between generative diffusion and discriminative perception. The key insights—contribution-aware timestep sampling, diffusion-tailored data augmentation, and interactive interface—are non-trivial and stem from logical analysis of the denoising process (R1, R2, R3). Reviewer 2 highlights that the innovations originate from meaningful problem discovery and are logical explorations of the original problem.
- **Exceptional clarity and thoroughness.** The methodology and motivation are clearly presented, with comprehensive consideration of variants (different augmentation approaches, alternative definitions of \(c_t^2\)). The experimental section is thoroughly comprehensive, including validation experiments, performance across multiple tasks, and detailed ablation studies (R1, R2). The paper is well-written with clear explanations aided by diagrams (R1).
- **Substantial performance improvements without architectural changes.** The proposed methods provide significant improvements over standard diffusion-based models on depth estimation, referring image segmentation, and generalist perception tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance (R1, R3, R4).
- **Interactive denoising as a new capability.** The paper demonstrates that the generative denoising process can be leveraged as a controllable user interface, enabling user correctional prompts and multi-round interaction—a feature that distinguishes it from traditional discriminative models (R1, R3). Reviewer 1 notes significant implications for human-in-the-loop correction.
- **Deep exploration of the denoising procedure.** The work includes a detailed timestep-contribution analysis and investigates distribution shifts during the denoising process for perception tasks (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete explanation of core methodology details.** Several critical details are missing from the main text or are obscured.
  - The derivation and adaptation of contribution factors \(c_t^2\) per timestep and per task is insufficiently explained. It is unclear whether \(c_t^2\) is used as a loss weight, a timestep sampling weight, or both (Eq. (2) vs. Fig. 2), and how this choice impacts training dynamics (R1, R4). Reviewer 1 asks for specific insights into how \(c_t^2\) is estimated and why it differs between tasks like depth estimation and RIS. Reviewer 4 questions why linear regression is used to estimate \(c_t\) instead of direct determination.
  - The analysis in Fig. 1 lacks details on how mIoU scores are computed during sampling. If intermediate estimated \(\hat{x}_0\) values are used, these do not reflect final generated outputs, making the conclusions insufficient. The stochastic nature of the SDE introduces variability; the authors should clarify if IoU is averaged over multiple runs and how this impacts the analysis (R4).
  - Key discussions are relegated to the supplementary material: the revised objective for augmented ground truth (Sec. B.3.2) and the intensity of augmentations across timesteps (Sec. B.3.1). These are fundamental for understanding and reproducibility but are absent from the main paper, harming reproducibility (R2).
- **Limited novelty of data augmentation and missing comparisons.** 
  - The augmentation techniques (color, shape, location changes for masks; blur for depth maps) are standard for perception tasks. Applying them to ground truth instead of input is not thoroughly justified, and it is unclear if this provides a significant advantage over traditional input-side augmentation (R3).
  - Several prior works address loss-weighting and timestep-sampling strategies in diffusion models (e.g., [1–5] in the review) and timestep-dependent data augmentation (e.g., [6]) or reducing exposure bias via augmentation (e.g., [7]). Without direct comparisons to these established methods, it is difficult to assess the distinct advantage of the proposed approaches (R4). The paper needs baseline comparisons to validate that the technical contributions are not trivial extensions of existing ideas (R4).
- **User interface section feels disconnected and shows marginal gains.**
  - The interactive correctional guidance and agentic workflows appear disconnected from the core timestep-aware training contributions. The performance improvements in Table 3 (particularly for RIS) are marginal, and the connection between the two parts is not clearly established. The interface feels like an add-on rather than an integral contribution, and its practical value is questionable (R2). Reviewer 2 also raises clarity on how correctional prompts are formulated and whether they require manual input (R1). One reviewer questions how effective the system is without human-generated prompts (R1).
- **Concerns about baseline fairness in RIS.** It is unclear whether the InstructPix2Pix baseline underwent equivalent training (e.g., fine-tuning on specific RIS datasets). Comparing a general image-editing model with a task-specific fine-tuned model may be unfair. Without specification of the training procedure, the reported improvements may be misleading (R2).
- **Generalizability and robustness are not fully validated.**
  - The methodology is demonstrated on only a few tasks (depth, RIS, generalist). It remains unclear how well it generalizes to a broader range of perception tasks such as pose estimation, 3D reconstruction, or to other diffusion architectures (conditional/latent models with varying noise schedules) (R1, R3). Further analysis under real-world conditions (lighting, weather, occlusions) is needed to assess robustness (R3).
  - The augmentation strategy’s ability to simulate distribution shifts for tasks where shifts are not well-represented by data corruption is not discussed, and the effectiveness of specific augmentation types is not analyzed (R1).
- **Manual setup required.** The proposed methods require manual configuration for data augmentation and \(c_t\) settings, reducing automation (R4).
