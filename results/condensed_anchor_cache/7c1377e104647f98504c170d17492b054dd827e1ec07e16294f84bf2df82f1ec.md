## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RefPlan, a doubly Bayesian approach for offline model-based planning that combines Bayesian uncertainty modeling (via a VariBAD-style VAE) with model-based planning under a control-as-inference framework. It aims to improve offline-learned policies at deployment time under epistemic uncertainty. Evaluations are on three D4RL tasks (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d). Most reviewers find the formulation novel and mathematically sound, but raise concerns about background clarity, insufficient experimental scope, missing baselines, mixed performance, and one reviewer (R3) strongly questions innovation and generalization claims.

### Strengths
- The problem of improving offline RL policies under epistemic uncertainty at deployment time is important and well-motivated (R1, R2, R5).
- The proposed combination of Bayesian inference over dynamics (model uncertainty) with control-as-inference (planning under uncertainty) is non-trivial and novel to the best of most reviewers' knowledge (R1, R2, R4, R5). R5 notes it is the first work to do so under the variational inference framework.
- The mathematical formulation in Sections 4.1 and 4.2 appears sound and free of errors (R1, R2).
- The writing is generally clear and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4). Figure 2 provides a clear depiction of the proposal (R4).
- The empirical results are promising and verify the adaptivity of the method on several tasks (R5). The number and types of environments are adequate to rank algorithms in aggregation (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Background clarity and missing definitions:** The paper lacks explanations of key concepts used in the method, such as Bayes-adaptive MDPs (BAMDPs), epistemic POMDPs, and the control-as-inference framework, making it hard to discern novelty versus known background (R1, R2). A fuller description, especially of how these components are integrated, would improve clarity (R1).
- **Limited experiments and missing baselines:**
  - Evaluated on only three tasks (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d), which is insufficiently persuasive (R3).
  - Missing comparison with APE-V (Ghosh et al., 2022), a relevant adaptive offline algorithm (R5).
  - Does not compare with VariBad itself, and an ablation replacing VariBad with a non-meta-learning model-based approach is absent (R2, R3).
  - No comparison with the deterministic and stochastic path in PlaNet/Dreamer models, and RefPlan may have higher variance due to outer sampling over the model m (R4).
  - Lack of online learning experiments: the experiments only show initial performance; it is unclear whether the same performance could have been reached offline without online planning (R2).
  - The distinction between research questions RQ1, RQ3, and RQ4 is unclear; RQ1 appears to be a superset of RQ3 and RQ4. The specific sources of epistemic uncertainty in each setting are not sufficiently explained (R1).
- **Performance concerns:**
  - Performance is mixed: improvements are not obvious on some tasks (e.g., Hopper), where model-free CQL still performs well even under OOD settings without extra planning or uncertainty modeling (R4).
  - The gap to LOOP, which also uses extra computation, is not significant (R3, R4).
  - The results show only marginal improvements over LOOP (R3).
- **Theoretical support and evidence for Bayes-optimality:**
  - There is no strong evidence that the agent has learned a near-Bayes-optimal policy. Theoretical support or additional experiments (e.g., a navigation task with behavior visualizations) are recommended (R3).
  - The VariBAD meta-learning setting assumes multiple tasks, but the experiments use a single environment; the encoder may collapse, and the Bayesian argument may not hold as all trajectories come from the same environment (R2).
- **Novelty concerns:** One reviewer (R3) strongly questions novelty, stating that offline model-based planning as probabilistic inference is common (citing [1]) and the algorithm is a minor modification of VariBAD. Related work in offline meta-RL (citing [2][3]) addresses multi-task adaptation, which is more valuable than the single-task generalization in this paper. Other reviewers (R1, R2, R5) consider the combination novel.
- **Methodological issues and unfair comparisons:**
  - RefPlan only fine-tunes a baseline policy; it is unclear why the learned model cannot be used to optimize a new policy directly. This also makes the comparison unfair due to the extra computation needed for test-time planning (R4).
  - Model-based baselines discard their learned models during RefPlan’s fine-tuning stage, which is an unfair advantage (R4).
  - The policy and model are decoupled; the paper should compare with a version that computes optimal policies directly from model-based policy optimization (R4).
  - Figure 1 is misleading: it could be interpreted as a graphical model being input to the encoder (R4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and computational concerns:**
  - The hyperparameters likely need careful tuning per task, limiting usability (R5).
  - RefPlan adds a VAE network, increasing computational burden in training and execution; its computational efficiency should be studied (R5).
  - Ablations are needed on the sampling step to understand the trade-off between variance, performance, and computation (R4).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Small errors and missing details: Algorithm 2 references a line number without lines in Algorithm 1; error bars in experiments are not defined; bold vs. underline in Table 1 is not explained; H-step is not defined (R1).
  - The effect of each component in the experiment in Section 5.1 is hard to understand; a visualization of uncertain regions or how the policy is selected would help (R4).
  - Should include the related work: Guez et al. 2012 (Efficient Bayes-Adaptive RL using Sample-Based Search) (R4).
