## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper extends the Vector-HaSH model (Chandra et al., 2023) of the hippocampal–entorhinal circuit by adding an MLP to model sensory neocortex and an RNN for action selection, then trains the full system with reinforcement learning on the accumulating tower task (Nieh et al., 2021). Five model variants are compared to test how conjunctive encoding of position and evidence in medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) and non-grid sensory input to hippocampus affect learning and neural representations. The authors conclude that conjunctive MEC coding and active EC-to-hippocampal pathways are necessary for the co-tuned place cell representations seen in experiment and for rapid task learning.

### Strengths

- **Novel multi‑region architecture that bridges hand‑designed circuit models with RNNs.** The combination of a Hebbian‑learning Vector‑HaSH module with a gradient‑trained RNN provides a unique testbed for studying interactions between hippocampus, entorhinal cortex, and neocortex in a decision‑making task (R4; echoed by R2).
- **Rigorous testing of multiple alternative hypotheses.** The five model configurations (Table 1) serve as biologically interpretable ablation studies that make the conclusions more robust (R3). Experiments systematically vary grid‑cell coding (position‑only, disjoint pos+evi, joint pos+evi) and whether sensory evidence enters the hippocampus directly (R3, R5).
- **Faithful replication of experimental neural phenomena.** Conjunctive tuning curves (Fig 3 & 7) and place‑cell location biases are convincing and closely match findings from Nieh et al. (2021) (R1, R3, R5). The clear connection to a concrete experiment is a strength (R3).
- **Clear rationale for model development and hypothesis testing.** The paper explains why different grid‑place interactions are considered and shows how behavior and neural representations differ across models (R5).

### Weaknesses

- **Weak justification for the Vector‑HaSH base model and lack of comparison to alternatives.** The introduction (lines 122‑123) provides insufficient reasoning for choosing Vector‑HaSH over other grid‑cell models, especially given that the proposed architecture is only a small modification of that previous work (R1). A fair comparison to a standalone RNN (without Vector‑HaSH) that receives both velocity and sensory evidence, with matched neuron count, is needed to clarify the role of the Vector‑HaSH module (R4).
- **Oversimplification of the hippocampal circuit.** The model reduces the hippocampus to an intermediary connecting MEC and LEC, omitting internal recurrent connections (e.g., CA3). This simplification may critically affect how co‑tuned place cells emerge and whether the findings are artifacts of the simplified architecture (R4). Replacing the hippocampus with a standard RNN that receives MEC spatial input and LEC evidence input could test whether co‑tuning would still appear even with disjoint or evidence‑free MEC encoding (R4).
- **Unfair comparison across models due to differing input pathways.** The neocortex is modeled as a single MLP that is the only channel for sensory evidence into the EC‑HPC circuit. Models 0‑2 lack this MLP, making it unclear whether performance differences stem from architecture or from the presence/absence of a learned sensory preprocessing module (R2).
- **Missing implementation details and ambiguous claims about grid‑cell coding schemes.** It is unclear how the joint vs. disjoint grid‑cell codes are implemented computationally and mathematically (R2, R3). The claim on line 309 that joint coding emerged in M4 conflicts with Table 2; the authors likely mean M5 (R2, R3). Additional clarification of how each model variant differs architecturally, in external inputs, and in parameters is required (R2).
- **Lack of mechanistic insight for observed performance differences.** M5 yields faster learning, but no intuition or supporting analysis explains why joint MEC encoding and non‑grid EC input improve learning speed (R2). Similarly, M0 outperforms M3 in steps per episode (Fig 2B) even though M3 has additional spatial information – this counter‑intuitive result is not discussed (R4).
- **Qualitative rather than quantitative comparison with biological data.** PCA clusters are assessed visually (e.g., Fig 5) without statistical tests for cluster separability in high‑dimensional space (R2). Only one experimental dataset (Nieh et al., 2021) is replicated; recapitulating additional neural phenomena (e.g., representational drift) or task variants would increase generality (R5).
- **Claim of “rapid learning” is not justified.** Agents require close to 1000 episodes to converge, while “rapid learning” in the literature typically refers to zero‑ or few‑shot learning (R5). The term should be re‑evaluated or explicitly defined.
- **Several unclear or contradictory statements and missing analyses.** (i) In Model 2, sensory evidence is bound to the hippocampus, yet the network fails to learn – why does coupling make it hard for the downstream RNN to decouple the inputs? (R4). (ii) M3 and M5 show similar success rates (Fig 2A) even though M3 lacks evidence from EC – an explanation is needed (R5). (iii) M0 and M5 show similar decreases in steps per episode (Fig 2B) despite different success-rate curves (R5). (iv) Representational analyses (Figs 3‑5) are not shown for M0 and M3, which would help interpret their behavior (R5). (v) Section 5.3.1 is confusing: the title states “only joint integration model exhibits…” but both M3 and M5 are joint integration models; the correct claim is “only joint integration with activated EC pathway” (R3). (vi) In line 431 the reference to Fig 5 A1/A2 should be B1/B2 (R3). (vii) Figure 5 caption should clarify which row (A/B) corresponds to which model (M3, M4, M5) and what each column shows (R3). (viii) Line 467 mentions no separability in accumulated evidence but provides no example (R3). (ix) The CAN operation in Equation (1) is not defined in the main text or appendix (R4). (x) It is unclear how position is represented and input to the grid‑cell module (R3). (xi) The MLP module receives sensory inputs from non‑grid EC; the biological plausibility and correspondence of this pathway (e.g., direct sensory regions?) should be discussed (R3).
- **Limited implications for representation learning or general ML.** The paper focuses on replicating specific neurophysiology without deriving generalizable principles (R1). Section 2.3 on grid cells in ML feels like an aside that is never linked back to the model or findings (R1). One reviewer (R3) found the paper to be a solid computational neuroscience study with no significant weaknesses, while the other four reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5, 5) raised substantial concerns about methodology, clarity, and over-claiming.
