## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IV-VAE, a video VAE with a Keyframe-based Temporal Compression (KTC) architecture and a Group Causal Convolution (GCConv) module. KTC splits latent space into two branches: one half inherits a low-dimension image VAE prior for keyframes, the other half performs temporal compression via 3D GCConv. GCConv uses standard convolution within frame groups (ensuring inter-frame equivalence) and causal padding between groups. The paper also introduces a MotionHD dataset. All three reviewers find the work well-motivated and showing clear improvements over existing video VAEs (e.g., OD-VAE, OS-VAE) in reconstruction metrics and generation FVD. However, reviewers disagree on the contribution’s significance: Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more positive, noting the observations are interesting and the generation experiments are a plus, but still considers the contribution marginal; Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 5) raise concerns about missing comparisons, insufficient evaluation of impact on video generation, clarity issues, and technical ambiguities.

### Strengths
- The KTC and GCConv modules improve spatiotemporal compression quality, address frame flickering caused by forward-only causal convolution, and accelerate convergence compared to standard 3D-causal VAEs.
- The proposed method shows clear improvement over existing video VAEs (e.g., OD-VAE, OS-VAE, CogX-VAE) in FVD, PSNR, SSIM on multiple datasets (Kinetics-600, MotionHD) with fewer parameters.
- Introduction of the MotionHD dataset provides a valuable benchmark for high-resolution, high-motion video VAE evaluation.
- The cache-based mechanism enables processing long videos in segments with lower hardware overhead (though quantitative quality evaluation is lacking – see Weaknesses).
- The paper demonstrates VAE effectiveness in downstream video generation (training Latte with different VAEs on Kinetics-600 and Skytimelapse), not just reconstruction.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evaluation of VAE impact on video generation:** The only generation metric used is FVD on Latte; the paper lacks quantitative evaluation of how the VAE affects training/inference time, memory, or generation quality of the diffusion model. Ablation experiments (Table 4) show TMPE provides limited improvement with extra computation, but it is unclear if the gain stems from the algorithm or simply increased compute.
- **Missing comparisons with recent related works:** The paper does not cite or compare with Video Probabilistic Diffusion Models (CVPR 2024) or Hybrid Video Diffusion Models (ECCV 2024), both of which focus on improved video autoencoders.
- **Marginal contribution despite improvements:** The proposed method does not achieve higher temporal compression (still 4×) and still suffers from memory/computation burdens fundamental to video generation. The encoding/decoding time comparison is only with CogX-VAE (Table 6); a more extensive efficiency analysis across baselines is needed.
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Figure 1 is hard to interpret: dashed lines (especially the green arrow) and their significance are not explained.
  - The fifth paragraph of the Introduction is difficult to follow and Figure 1(b) does not align with the text (e.g., why CogX-VAE’s third frame outperforms others, or why OS-VAE’s second frame is better than the third).
  - Model names (OD-VAE, OS-VAE, CogX-VAE) are not clearly linked to citations until Section 4.3, hindering readability.
  - Notation in Section 3.1: `[0, 0, W_{2D}]` for `W_{3D}` is ambiguous—are these zero matrices or vectors? Clarify for reproducibility.
  - Section 3.2: Statement “the number of frames in the frame group is not fixed during the forward pass” contradicts the `t_c=4` setup. Please explain.
  - Section 3.3: The first frame as a keyframe is only mentioned in a figure, not in the text. Also, is `Z/2 = C_{out}`? The mechanism for assigning weights to temporal up/downsampling layers for independent processing of two frames per group needs clarification.
  - Section 3.3: Why maintain two branches after converting GCConv3D to 2D after two temporal downsampling stages?
- **Missing spatio-temporal metric:** The paper does not include STREAM (ICLR 2024) which evaluates both spatial and temporal coherence.
- **Poor qualitative comparison:** Visualizations show frames stacked as images (which makes it hard to see differences) rather than video files or better examples. More examples and proper video files are needed.
- **Missing hyperparameters:** Details for the diffusion model (Latte) experiments are not provided.
- **Reproducibility:** It is unclear if the MotionHD dataset and VAE checkpoint/code will be publicly released.
