## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally praise the paper for tackling the overlooked problem of pluralistic alignment, proposing two novel and clearly motivated reward modeling approaches (PAL-A and PAL-B) that capture both shared and personalized preferences. They highlight the method’s strong empirical performance: e.g., on Reddit TL;DR, PAL is 1.7% more accurate for seen users and 36% more accurate for unseen users than the previous best method, with 100× fewer parameters; on Pick-a-Pic v2, it achieves 2.5% higher accuracy with 156× fewer learned parameters. The ability to train on a single consumer GPU while baselines require multiple A100s is also noted. However, all reviewers raise concerns about the evaluation. Two reviewers (scores 6, confidence 3) question whether the benchmarks truly reflect real-world heterogeneity and note that the preference modeling (e.g., focusing on summary length) may be too narrow. A third reviewer (score 8, confidence 2) is more positive about the novelty and efficiency but finds the Pick-a-Filter experiments unconvincing due to missing baselines (PickScore), unclear results on the filter examples (possible collapse to a color classifier), and insufficient documentation of experimental details (e.g., base model for Table 2). Presentation clarity issues (Figure 1 confusing; Figure 4 hard to read) are also noted. All reviewers agree that the paper makes a solid contribution but requires stronger experimental validation.

### Strengths
- Addresses pluralistic alignment, a commonly overlooked assumption in alignment literature (R1, R2); the two proposed approaches (PAL-A and PAL-B) are novel and clearly differentiate from existing works (e.g., KTO) that treat individual differences as noise (R2).
- Modular design naturally combines commonalities and personalized preferences, enabling flexible trade-offs and complementing existing alignment methods (R1).
- Strong empirical results: on Reddit TL;DR Summary, 1.7% more accurate for seen users and 36% more accurate for unseen users than the previous best method, with 100× fewer parameters; on Pick-a-Pic v2, 2.5% more accurate than the best method with 156× fewer learned parameters (R1, R2, R3).
- Can be trained on a single consumer-grade GPU, whereas baselines require multiple A100s (R2).
- Few-shot localization of preferences for new users; converges quickly (R1).
- Rigorous experimental setup with clear visualizations and algorithms (R1).
- Provides theoretical analysis of sample complexity for reward generalization to new users and unseen samples (R3).

### Weaknesses
**Benchmark and dataset concerns**
- No benchmark reflects real-world use cases that highlight the benefits of a heterogeneous model; both Reddit TL;DR and Pick-a-Filter are semi-synthesized and artificially accentuate diversity, calling into question whether pluralistic alignment is a valuable problem to solve (R1).
- Datasets may lack sufficient diversity, limiting generalization to broader scenarios (R3).
- Preference modeling mainly focuses on summary length, which may not cover a broader range of personalized preferences; the preference distribution for seen/unseen users is limited to length (R3).

**Missing baselines and experiment details (Pick-a-Filter)**
- The PickScore baseline is missing. The authors claim they cannot compare PickScore because its training set overlaps with the Pick-a-Filter validation set, but this can be fixed by eliminating overlapping examples (as already done for Table 3) or constructing a custom validation split from Pick-a-Pic v2 that does not overlap with PickScore’s training data. The reviewer requests either including PickScore after fixing the overlap, or justifying why these options are not possible (R2).
- For Pick-v1 results (Table 2), the base model is not documented (CLIP-H or PickScore?). Without this information, the claim about parameter efficiency is hard to evaluate. Presentation would be improved by adding Table 2 as an extra column in Table 3 (R2).

**Effectiveness of filter examples and potential color classifier collapse**
- The red and blue filter examples seem too rival; a naive color classifier may already achieve high accuracy in identifying preference. It is unclear whether PAL offers any benefit over such a classifier. Figure 4 needs improvement: in the low-β region, it is hard to see differences. The authors should discuss if PAL is simply collapsing to a color classifier and compare against a naive color classifier. A proper PickScore comparison (which would likely be a flat line like CLIP and HPSv2) would clarify whether PAL provides any advantage in the low-β region, which may be more representative of actual human preference discrepancies (R2).

**Missing experiments and comparisons**
- In Section D.3.2, the choice of foundation model greatly impacts performance; an ablation study investigating whether this effect is observed with other alignment methods would be valuable (R1).
- When should one opt for PAL-A versus PAL‑B? (R1)
- Why did the Reddit experiments not include results of PAL-A? (R2)

**Presentation clarity**
- Figure 1, as the first graph, is confusing: pasted images and formulas are unclear, and there is no clear caption or explanation of the symbols or viewing order (R3).
- Figure 4 is hard to read; it is difficult to distinguish which line is higher (R2).
- Some experiment details are lossy; e.g., base model for Table 2 is not documented, weakening claims (R2).
