## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SWYCC, a VAE trained with a diffusion loss that uses a U‑Net refinement decoder on top of an initial MSE+LPIPS reconstruction. It aims to improve reconstruction quality over GAN‑based autoencoders. Reviewers acknowledge the direction is promising and the method shows some quantitative improvements on ImageNet, but raise major concerns about novelty (the approach is very similar to Diffusion Autoencoders and diffusion‑based super‑resolution), insufficient comparisons and metrics, limited datasets (only ImageNet, no CelebA/CIFAR/LSUN), unclear training procedure, high computational cost (especially with classifier‑free guidance), and a lack of justification for the initial decoder’s role. One reviewer strongly supports the work (score 6), while another considers it trivial and unfinished (score 1); the remaining three are moderately negative (score 3).

### Strengths
- The use of a diffusion loss for autoencoder training is an important and promising direction to explore. This is one of the first works to show that it can lead to better reconstruction than GAN‑based losses (Reviewer 1).
- The method outperforms the prior GAN‑based autoencoder on ImageNet with common metrics CMMD and FID, and the advantage holds across different compression rates (Reviewer 1).
- The variance visualization provides interesting insights into what information is sampled (Reviewer 1).
- Full explanation of technical details, including architecture details and training process (Reviewer 2).
- Wide and well‑explained ablation studies on the number of denoising steps and classifier‑free guidance scales help understand the importance of these parameters (Reviewer 2).
- The inclusion of a classifier‑free guidance experiment introduces a new aspect to the diffusion‑based decoder, although it doubles sampling time (Reviewer 4).
- The approach is conceptually simple and effective (Reviewer 5).
- Several ablation studies are conducted (Reviewers 1,2,5).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and prior art:** The method is conceptually similar to Diffusion Autoencoders (Preechakul et al., CVPR 2022) and to diffusion models used for super‑resolution, where a coarse initial image is iteratively refined. The paper does not sufficiently highlight the differences or novelty. Claims of being “first” to jointly train an encoder and decoder under a diffusion loss are incorrect; prior works such as DiffusionAE and DiVAE also exist (Reviewers 3,4,5).
- **Missing comparisons:** No comparisons are made with several relevant baselines: DC‑VAE, VAEBM, Stable Diffusion XL’s VAE, DiffusionAE, DiVAE, and DiffuseVAE. The only baseline is a GAN‑based autoencoder, and it is unclear whether that baseline was retrained under the same settings. Comparisons on simpler datasets (CelebA, CIFAR, LSUN, CelebA‑HQ‑256) are absent, so the method’s general applicability is unknown (Reviewers 1,2,4,5).
- **Insufficient metrics:** The paper relies heavily on FID and CMMD but omits widely used reconstruction metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, Inception Score, and rFID. No evaluation is performed on the rate‑perception‑distortion plane (Blau & Michaeli, ICML 2021), which is standard for perceptual compression methods (Reviewers 2,3,4,5).
- **Training procedure unclear:** It is not specified whether the encoder and initial decoder are trained jointly with the refinement U‑Net or if they are pre‑trained and frozen. The text claims that the impact of the initial decoder is investigated in Table 1, but that table does not contain any such analysis (Reviewers 1,3,4,5).
- **2‑stage pipeline undermines claimed contribution:** The autoencoder still relies on MSE+LPIPS loss for the initial reconstruction; the diffusion loss acts only as a refinement step. The paper does not provide experiments or theory showing that the same representation would be learned without the LPIPS loss, nor does it justify that the diffusion decoder alone would not require a coarse reconstruction (Reviewer 1).
- **Qualitative comparisons are insufficient:** Reconstruction results are shown without corresponding raw inputs, making it difficult to assess visual quality. More visual examples across different compression rates and diverse samples are needed (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- **Low resolution only:** All experiments are performed on 256×256 ImageNet images. No results on higher resolutions are shown, and it is unclear whether the method scales to high‑resolution images (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Model size and computational cost:** The decoder consists of two parts, including an additional U‑Net. The paper does not address whether the improvement is simply due to having a larger model. The classifier‑free guidance doubles sampling time, and the overall training and inference cost compared to GAN‑based methods is not discussed. The limitations section only mentions inference steps, ignoring model size, training time, and dataset requirements (Reviewers 2,4,5).
- **Presentation issues:** The footnote in the paper leaks author information. Some reviewers found the paper unfinished and the method trivial (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 6) strongly supports the approach and the direction, finding the improvements over GAN‑based methods convincing. In contrast, Reviewer 3 (score 1) considers the method trivial and the paper not meeting conference standards, and points out the missing raw inputs and the discrepancy about Table 1. The remaining reviewers (scores 3) are moderately critical, citing insufficient novelty, evaluation, and analysis.
