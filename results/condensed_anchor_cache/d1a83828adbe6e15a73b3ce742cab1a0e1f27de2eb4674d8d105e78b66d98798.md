## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper targets text-driven whole-body motion generation (facial expressions, hand gestures, body motions). The framework includes a Holistic Hierarchical VQ-VAE (H²VQ) for body/hand motion reconstruction using two structured codebooks, a Hierarchical-GPT for autoregressive generation, a conditional VAE for facial motion, and a pre-trained text-motion alignment model (based on TMR-style contrastive learning) that provides text embeddings and alignment supervision. Experiments are on Motion-X, GRAB, and HumanML3D. The paper is the first to tackle the whole-body task; reviewers agree on its novelty in task definition but differ on technical novelty (Reviewer 4 scores 8, others 5–6).

### Strengths
- **First work on text-driven whole-body motion generation**, addressing facial expressions, hand gestures, and body motions simultaneously (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Promising and thoughtful solution**: H²VQ compresses whole-body motion into hierarchical discrete codes; Hierarchical-GPT predicts body and hand tokens in an autoregressive manner (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Pre-trained text-motion alignment model** (TMR-inspired contrastive learning) used as a prior to replace CLIP embeddings, with additional alignment supervision in the generation stage – this bypasses the CLIP semantic gap and is considered a meaningful contribution to the text-to-motion community if released (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Quantitative advantages**: clear performance gains over SOTA motion generation methods in common metrics (except multi-modality and diversity) (Reviewer 4). Introduced new evaluation metrics: TMR-R-Precision(256) and TMR-Matching Score (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing is fluent** (Reviewer 1). Presentation rated excellent by Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 4.
- **Disagreement on overall assessment**: While three reviewers (1,2,3) found technical novelty limited, Reviewer 4 rated the paper an 8, highlighting clear advantages and the value of the retrieval-based alignment approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty** – the pipeline closely follows T2M-GPT: VQ-VAE for motion reconstruction and GPT for generation. H²VQ simply extends RVQ by hierarchically quantizing hand and body rather than directly quantizing the whole body; Hierarchical-GPT predicts body and hand tokens alternately instead of a single token sequence. These modifications are straightforward (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Pretraining a motion encoder/text encoder via contrastive alignment (like TMR) is not new**, and using it to replace CLIP in the prediction stage is a natural extension (Reviewer 2). Text-motion alignment supervision has also been used in prior works such as TEMOS (Reviewer 3).
- **Facial expression module (cVAE) lacks evaluation and integration analysis:**
    - No quantitative evaluation of facial expression generation against any baseline (Reviewer 4). The paper does not provide experiments analyzing this module (Reviewer 1).
    - Generated faces appear static in visualizations despite textual descriptions of emotions (e.g., “happily”, “angrily”) – emotion dynamics are not demonstrated (Reviewer 2).
    - It is unclear how facial expressions are synchronized with body and hand motions; this is crucial for coherence (Reviewers 2, 4).
    - Regarding datasets: existing datasets may lack diversity and realism in hand poses and facial expressions; the generated hand poses have minor distinctions but are not highly realistic (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient comparisons**:
    - No comparison of solely body generation results with previous text-to-body works (Reviewer 1). Too few methods compared overall (Reviewer 1).
    - Missing comparison with a simple baseline that directly combines SOTA models for facial expression, hand pose, and body pose generation (Reviewer 3).
- **Physically implausible artifacts** (e.g., foot sliding) are visible in results – no discussion provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with missing analyses or experiments:**
    - For H²VQ: what are the codebook sizes used? (Table 2) Are body and hand modeled separately and then combined? How does performance scale when codebook size increases from 1024 to 4096? (Reviewer 2)
    - What text embedding is used for facial generation – CLIP or the proposed alignment model? (Reviewer 2)
    - How is coherence maintained between separately modeled body/hand and face motions? (Reviewer 2)
    - Can the method generalize to out-of-distribution text descriptions? (Reviewer 4)
    - Why are hands encoded before body in H²VQ, but body tokens predicted before hand tokens in Hierarchical-GPT? Is it due to empirical performance? (Reviewer 4)
    - In the supplementary video, T2M-GPT examples show characters moving backward – please confirm correct rendering (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor issues** (notation/typos):
    - Section 2.1: “$F$” should be “$L$” for frame length (Reviewers 3, 4).
    - Section 2.2: the sentence “In the encoding phase, we input hand and body motions, yielding hand and body tokens…” is repeated (Reviewers 3, 4).
    - Appendix C.1, Algorithm 1 line 4: “$\hat{z}^B$” should be “$z^B$” (Reviewer 4).
    - Section title “3.1.2 Evaluation” is misleading – suggest “Evaluation Details” (Reviewer 1).
