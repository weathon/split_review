## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a statistical method to determine the minimum accumulation precision for Block Floating Point (BFP) matrix multiplication, analyzing fixed-point intra-block accumulation and floating-point inter-block accumulation. The method uses Laplace-distribution assumptions and three-standard-deviation estimates to set accumulator bitwidth. Experiments on MMLU Llama2-7B, SQuAD-v1.1 BERT-Large/BERT-Base, and CIFAR-10 ResNet-50 report performance close to FP32 baselines, with hardware area savings of 13.7%–28.7% and power savings of 13.7%–28.7% over high-precision accumulation, and 10.3× area and 11.0× power reduction vs. BFP16 implementations. Reviewer opinions are sharply divided: one reviewer (score 6) found the theoretical framework solid and hardware validation convincing; another (score 1) considered the work lacking in detail, novelty, and theoretical rigor; the remaining reviewers (scores 5, 3) raised significant concerns about experimental setup, assumptions, and presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- The motivation to address accumulation as a hardware bottleneck in low-precision BFP MAC is valid and interesting (Reviewer 1, though with reservations).
- A theoretical framework is presented that analyzes the effect of accumulation precision in quantized GEMM, taking both data statistics and floating-point swamping (FnRR) into account (Reviewer 2). This is seen as providing a solid foundation for further research.
- The method is validated across multiple models (Llama2-7B, BERT-Large, BERT-Base, ResNet-50) and demonstrates actual hardware benefits (area and power savings) with a complete synthesized design (Reviewer 2).
- Figure 1 clearly illustrates the target of improvement and the bottleneck of the accumulator in hardware (Reviewer 3).
- The paper clearly describes its strategy: using three standard deviations to estimate the largest output magnitude of the accumulator (Reviewer 3).
- The paper makes a contribution to arithmetic for low-cost inference (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of low-precision floating-point format specification.** The paper does not state the number of exponent and mantissa bits for FP numbers with fewer than 16 bits (e.g., FP8 can have 4-bit exponent+3-bit mantissa or 5-bit exponent+2-bit mantissa) (Reviewer 1, Question 1).
- **Insufficient hardware implementation details.** The hardware architecture is not described, bitwidths for each component are not specified, and the synthesis environment (tools, libraries, target technology) is missing, undermining reproducibility (Reviewers 1, 4; Questions 2 and 4).
- **Unclear derivation and weak theoretical foundation.**  
  - The connection between the statistical analysis in Sections 4.1/4.2 and the optimized accumulator bitwidth is not clearly established; the optimization appears empirical (Reviewer 1).  
  - Equation (2) relating partial-sum range to \(2^{A_{width}}\) and \(2^{W_{width}}\) is not fully explained – it is unclear whether the bitwidth refers to exponent or mantissa, and the derivation of the term \(log(k) + A_{width} + W_{width}\) is not justified (Reviewer 1, Question 3).  
  - The assumption that weights and activations follow a symmetric distribution centered at zero (Assumption 1 in Proof of Theorem 1) is questionable, especially after ReLU or batch normalization which introduce skew. No robust justification or reference is provided (Reviewer 4).  
  - The paper claims inter-block accumulation precision can be reduced to 2–3 bits for BFP8 Seg (Table 3) – this seems overly aggressive and raises concern that the FP32 baseline used for comparison is unnecessarily high, skewing evaluation (Reviewer 1).  
- **No clear advantage over simpler methods.** The intra-block partial sum analysis and FnRR-based analysis are not shown to offer any significant benefit over simple methods like min/max or 3-sigma-based truncation (Reviewer 1).
- **Figure 5 is inadequately described.** The x- and y-axes are not clearly labeled (e.g., is the y-axis accuracy? what do “scores” mean?), line descriptions are missing, the floating-point baseline (dashed lines?) is ambiguous, and the block size used is not stated. This makes it difficult to interpret the results and assess how closely the theoretical prediction matches the lower bound of required bitwidth (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; Questions 5).
- **Experiments mix inference and training.** Lines 385–388 suggest the evaluation includes both inference-only and training procedures. The accumulator precision required may differ significantly between inference and training (e.g., overflow can act as clipping during training, allowing some quality recovery). These two setups must be separated and ablated (Reviewer 3).
- **Choice of benchmark: ResNet50 on CIFAR-10 may be inappropriate.** CIFAR-10 is a small dataset for ResNet-50, potentially leading to overfitting. The paper should justify this choice or consider more appropriate benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-1K) (Reviewer 4).
- **Practicality concerns.** The method predicts different accumulation precisions for different models, but real hardware typically runs many models (and possibly training) on the same chip. Specializing precision per model is impractical, and training requires larger data ranges than inference, so the approach may not be useful in real-world hardware design (Reviewer 2). This criticism overlaps with the request to evaluate on training.
- **Presentation clarity issues.** Many italic/non-italic terms are mixed (e.g., \(n\) vs. \(n\)), symbols and definitions are inconsistent (e.g., \(I_e\)/\(W_e\) vs. \(I\)/\(W\), sigma in line 316 vs. symbol sigma), and there are numerous typos. These issues obscure the mathematical derivations (Reviewer 4).  
- **The main idea and key metric (FnRR) are not explicitly illustrated.** The paper should better highlight the central idea and introduce FnRR visually or in early sections, rather than relying heavily on equations without clear motivation (Reviewer 4).
