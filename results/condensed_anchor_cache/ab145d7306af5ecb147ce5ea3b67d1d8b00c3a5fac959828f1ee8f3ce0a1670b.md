## Merged Review

### Summary
Habitat 3.0 is a simulation platform for studying collaborative human-robot tasks in home environments. It provides accurate humanoid simulation with diverse appearance and motion, a human-in-the-loop (HITL) infrastructure supporting mouse/keyboard and VR control, and two collaborative tasks: Social Navigation (locate and follow humanoid avatars) and Social Rearrangement (collaborative scene rearrangement). The platform evaluates learned heuristic and end-to-end RL baselines, demonstrating emergent behaviors (e.g., robot yielding space) and showing that automated evaluation with humanoids can indicate relative policy ordering when tested with real human partners. HITL experiments involving human participants evaluate coordination and efficiency.

### Strengths
- Complements existing simulators (e.g., Habitat 1.0, VirtualHome) by focusing on human-robot interaction (HRI) and including human models, human-object/robot interactions, and human-in-the-loop evaluation.
- Provides efficient simulation that reduces RL training time and offers a validation environment, lowering risks for real-world deployment.
- Introduces a Human-in-the-Loop platform enabling real‑time human control of avatars via mouse, keyboard, or VR devices; supports data collection and reproducibility.
- Addresses realistic human and robot simulation, including shape/motion diversity (e.g., using SMPL‑X for humanoids, diverse robot models).
- Designs two benchmark HRI tasks (Social Navigation and Social Rearrangement) with multiple baselines (heuristic experts, end‑to‑end RL policies) and evaluates them in automated and HITL settings.
- One reviewer (score 8, high confidence) particularly emphasized the realism of simulation and the platform’s potential for advancing embodied AI; another (score 6) noted the overall quality and contribution despite reservations.

### Weaknesses
- **Humanoid motion realism**: One reviewer (score 6) noted unrealistic, jerky movements (rigid rotation to waypoints then straight‑line path) lacking fluidity and momentum, limiting transferability and accurate HRI analysis. Another reviewer (score 8) considered the simulation realistic, indicating disagreement on this point.
- **Baseline results not encouraging**: In social navigation, no baseline achieved useful following rate (F) or feasible collision rate (CR). In social rearrangement, none generalized to assist partners effectively; relative efficiency (RE) improvements were marginal even in solo vs. collaborative comparisons. Even HITL results were not strong, making it difficult to derive robust insights from the evaluations (Reviewer 2, score 6).
- **Task diversity limited**: Only two tasks (social navigation, social rearrangement) are studied, with no support for more complex collaborative interactions (e.g., physical object manipulation, joint problem‑solving) or other embodied AI tasks like visual‑language navigation (Reviewers 1, 2; one reviewer (score 8) also noted potential for extension).
- **Robot agent diversity not addressed**: The platform does not evaluate different robot forms, limiting generalizability to real‑world HRI (Reviewer 2).
- **Collaboration opportunities may be insufficient**: RE metrics show only marginal efficiency gains when agents collaborate versus working alone, suggesting tasks may not fully leverage collaboration (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited sensor suite**: Robots currently use only depth camera, human detector, and GPS. Lack of LiDAR (accurate 3D point clouds) and sound sensors limits obstacle avoidance, robust mapping, and response to auditory cues in dynamic HRI (Reviewer 3, score 8).
- **Hand motion and skin deformation unrealistic**: Fixed hand poses (no grasping animation) and lack of skin deformation reduce visual and ecological validity, preventing study of complex manipulation tasks (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and clarifications**: The social navigation task resembles visual tracking (e.g., End‑to‑end Active Object Tracking via RL, ICML 2018); the paper does not discuss this connection (Reviewer 1). Motivation for population‑based approaches in Social Rearrangement is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Object interaction simplicity**: Objects are kinematically attached/detached upon hand contact, simplifying them as point particles; no geometry, weight, or material properties are modeled. The paper does not discuss how to incorporate more complex freeform grasping or physical interaction (Reviewer 1).
- **Integration and usability questions**: It is unclear how easy it is to import motion‑capture data (e.g., AMASS dataset via VPoser) or how the reliance on VPoser and simple interpolation handles complex tasks (Reviewer 2). Physical collaboration scenarios (partners acting on same object) are not supported; steps required are unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of roadmap for future extensions**: The paper does not discuss how to scale to more tasks, incorporate physics simulation, fine‑grained object interactions, sim‑to‑real deployment, or more motion variety (e.g., wiping, knocking) (Reviewers 1, 2). Standardization and open‑sourcing of HITL tools were not addressed (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient HITL analysis**: The HITL evaluation is interesting but lacks detailed analysis and insights beyond basic metrics; one reviewer (score 6) considered this a missed opportunity (Reviewer 2).
