## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies in-context learning (ICL) by separating two abilities: context-scaling (improvement with more in-context examples) and task-scaling (improvement with more pre-training tasks). The authors introduce Simplified GPT (SGPT), where key, query, and value matrices are set to identity and linear attention with L1 normalization is used. On synthetic ICL tasks (linear regression with fixed/multi noise, two-layer ReLU networks, decision trees, sparse linear regression), SGPT matches GPT-2 performance, motivating a kernel smoothing interpretation. The paper then shows that standard MLPs with vectorized input only task-scale, while MLPs with kernel smoother features context-scale but not task-scale; combining both inputs yields both abilities. The work aims to isolate the architectural components responsible for each scaling behavior.

### Strengths
- The introduction clearly motivates the question of context-scaling vs. task-scaling (R1, R4).
- A wide range of synthetic tasks from the ICL literature is considered (R1).
- The SGPT construction is novel and surprisingly achieves performance comparable to full GPT-2 on the tested tasks (R2, R4).
- The connection between ICL and kernel smoothing is clearly presented and insightful (R2).
- Separating context-scaling and task-scaling via kernel features and vectorized input provides a novel way to understand their individual impacts (R2, R4).
- The paper is well-written, easy to read, and addresses an important topic (R3, R4).
- The contrast between Transformers and MLPs sparks a fascinating architectural discussion (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work** (R1, R4): The improvement of Transformer ICL with more examples is already known (Bai et al. 2023; von Oswald et al. 2023). Reviewers 1 and 4 argue that the kernel smoothing connection and one-step gradient descent equivalence (Section 5.1) were already established by von Oswald et al. It is unclear how SGPT provides a novel insight beyond these prior constructions, especially since von Oswald et al. also draw the same connection using simple KQV matrices (R1). The claim that previous theoretical results only apply to fixed context length (l.157-159) is not justified (R1). Reviewer 4 further notes that existing works (e.g., Bayes-optimal predictor) already explain context-scaling; the paper's claim that they fail to do so is unclear.

- **Lack of theoretical justification** (R2, R3): How SGPT implements the Hilbert estimate (via the activation function in attention) is not straightforward (R2). The manuscript does not contain a derivation of the theoretical connection between identity KQV matrices and the Hilbert kernel (R3). Reviewer 2 asks for theoretical justification of the task-scaling ability of both Transformers and MLPs.

- **MLP context-scaling claims** (R3, R4): The paper claims MLPs do not context-scale, but prior work by Tong & Pehlevan (2024, arXiv:2405.15618) shows that MLP-Mixers do context-scale on both regression and classification tasks; standard MLPs also context-scale when measured by excess MSE (Fig. 1d, 1i, Fig. 6b in that work). This suggests MLP context-scaling may be bottlenecked by insufficient data or long inputs rather than architectural inability (R3). Reviewer 4 notes that given sufficient capacity, an MLP could implement ridge regression (e.g., the Bayes-optimal solution for linear regression) and thus should be able to context-scale in principle; failure may be due to optimization, not expressivity. Additionally, Fig. 7B in the paper shows that unmodified MLPs exhibit some context-scaling for 5M pretraining tasks, contradicting the claim (R3). The paper also missed the reference arXiv:2311.18194 on MLPs and context-scaling (R4).

- **Triviality of kernel feature results** (R4): The kernel mapping (Eq. 11) is guaranteed to be consistent in the infinite-context limit, so experiments in Fig. 5 are not surprising. MLPs with such features will naturally context-scale, but this does not provide new insight (R4). Similarly, the claim that combining kernel features with vectorized input yields both scalings is expected given the properties of each component (R1, R4).

- **Need for ablations and clarity on SGPT** (R2, R3, R4): It is unclear why the KQV matrices are set to identity; if they were learnable, could they also give task-scaling? (R2). The impact of depth on SGPT’s performance is not studied—if multiple SGPT layers are required to match GPT-2 at short contexts, then the role of kernel smoothing alone is diminished (R4). The effect of using linear attention without L1 normalization is not explored (R4). The SGPT equation (Eq. 13) appears more specific than the generic feature map ψ (Eq. 9); the connection should be clarified (R1). The right panel of Fig. 3 and Fig. 4(B) (2-layer NN, SGD) need explanation (R2).

- **Scalability of kernel smoothing interpretation** (R3): Kernel smoothers suffer from the curse of dimensionality and require dataset size exponential in input dimension, yet modern Transformers handle embeddings with tens of thousands of dimensions. It seems implausible that the kernel smoother view applies to real-world Transformers (R3). This point was raised as a conceptual weakness.

- **Missing experiment** (R2): What is the task-scaling performance of a single-layer SGPT (to match Fig. 5 for context-scaling)? (R2).

- **Contrast with prior work on MLPs** (R1): Tong & Pehlevan (2024) also show that MLPs cannot context-scale (Fig. 1d), but this is not reflected in the related work (l.143-146). The authors should clarify how their findings relate (R1).
