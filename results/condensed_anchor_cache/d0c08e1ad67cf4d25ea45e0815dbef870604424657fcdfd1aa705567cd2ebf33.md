## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an amortized Bayesian posterior inference method that uses permutation-invariant set-based networks (DeepSets or Transformers) to map a dataset of any cardinality directly to a posterior distribution over model parameters. Training uses a reverse KL (ELBO) objective, avoiding ground truth parameters, and is applied to linear/non-linear regression, classification, and Gaussian Mixture Models. Experiments compare architectural choices (DeepSets vs Transformer, Gaussian vs Normalizing Flow), evaluate against non-amortized baselines (MLE, MCMC, random) and forward-KL amortized inference, and test robustness under model misspecification.

### Strengths
- **Versatility of application** (R1): The method handles both fixed- and variable-dimension parameter spaces via masking.
- **Model robustness** (R1, R2): Using reverse KL gives resilience to model misspecification, as demonstrated on real-world data.
- **Method soundness** (R2): The overall approach is technically sound.
- **Significance of the problem** (R2): Building amortized Bayesian inference that accommodates datasets of varying cardinality and is robust to model specification is an important goal.
- **Extensive experimental evaluation** (R3): The paper provides detailed architectural comparisons (Gaussian vs Normalizing Flow, DeepSet vs Transformer) with enough details for reproducibility.
- **Interesting architectural comparisons** (R3): The ablation studies on network backbones and posterior parameterizations are a notable contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns** (R1, R2, R3):
  - The paper omits relevant work on amortized GP hyperparameters [1][2][3] that also employs permutation-equivariant architectures (e.g., Transformers) for set-based posterior prediction, reducing the novelty of the architectural choice (R1).
  - Dataset-level amortized inference is already studied in meta-learning and Neural Processes (NPs) [1]; the only difference is inferring decoder parameters directly rather than optimizing free parameters for predictions. The authors claim their method provides uncertainty about model parameters, but experiments focus on predictive performance; Table 4 and Fig. 4(c,d) lack comparisons against non-amortized baselines to demonstrate this supposed advantage (R3).
  - The paper’s contributions are not clearly delineated from prior work; it is hard to assess what is claimed as novel (R2).
- **Variable dimension handling is limited** (R1): Masking wastes GPU memory and is not equivariant w.r.t. dimensions; alternative approaches (e.g., the method in [1]) may be beneficial when each dimension has its own parameters (linear regression, GMM) (R1).
- **Unclear handling of GMM with variable number of mixtures** (R1): The strategy for managing variable output dimensions and determining the number of mixtures is not explained.
- **Insufficient clarity and presentation** (R2):
  - Figures are placed throughout the paper (e.g., experimental figures in the introduction) rather than grouped in Section 4.
  - Equation (8) closely resembles Equation (9) from prior work; the explanation is diluted and does not build clearly on that baseline.
  - Section 4.3 mixes methodological content into the experiments section; all methodological elements should be grouped together.
  - The description of how reverse KL training uses “unpaired data” (Section 4.3) is insufficiently detailed.
- **Missing related work discussion** (R2): Geffner et al. (2023) on compositional score modeling for simulation-based inference should be discussed.
- **Evaluation metrics are poorly defined and potentially flawed** (R2, R3):
  - The “expected L2 loss” and “expected accuracy loss” are not mathematically defined. The L2 metric is unsuited for multimodal posteriors: an approximation placing mass halfway between two modes yields a lower L2 than one putting half the mass in each mode (R2).
  - Predictive log marginal likelihood, which better measures epistemic uncertainty, is not evaluated instead of L2 (R3).
  - No confidence intervals are provided; the significance of experimental results cannot be judged (R3).
- **Missing baselines and ablations** (R1, R3):
  - A non-amortized version of the proposed method should be included to quantify the amortization gap (R3).
  - Ablation study should sweep dimensionality from 1D to 100D to test extrapolation beyond training dimensions (R1).
  - Comparison to recent natural-gradient VI methods [2][3] that prevent mode collapse in expressive posterior approximations is missing (R3).
  - For DeepSets vs. Transformers, the paper should compare against Bayesian aggregation [4] which addresses the same limitation of naive pooling (R3).
- **Minor weaknesses** (R1):
  - The choice of Langevin over HMC/NUTS [4] is not justified; HMC (e.g., NUTS) might perform better given moderate dataset sizes.
  - Figure positions are not specified in the manuscript.
  - The paper does not explore alternative variable-dimension handling beyond masking (e.g., [1]).
