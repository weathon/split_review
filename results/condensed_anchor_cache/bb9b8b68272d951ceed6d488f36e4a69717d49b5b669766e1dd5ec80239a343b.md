## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper compares average-reward reinforcement learning (R-learning) to dopamine transients measured in rat ventral striatum across three behavioral tasks (two Pavlovian, one operant). Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) trained with average-reward RL are compared to photometry data from Mohebi et al. (2019, 2024). The model reproduces the experimental trends; however, the difference between average-reward RL and temporal discounting with a very large discount factor is minor. Two reviewers (scores 5) find the work significant, well-written, and using up-to-date data; one reviewer (score 3) sees limited novelty and insufficient differentiation from classical RL.

### Strengths
- Uses recent experimental data from fluorescent dopamine sensors (Mohebi et al., 2019, 2024), modeling tasks with up-to-date reinforcement learning algorithms.
- Premises are laid out concisely; related work (neuroscience and RL literature) is adequately addressed.
- The basic idea is interesting and the chosen route of investigation is original: investigating R-learning as an alternative explanation for dopamine signaling using new tasks, new data, and RNNs.
- The paper is remarkably well-written, with detailed explanations of the models, high-quality vector figures, and accessibility to the broad ICLR community.
- Multi-faceted investigation establishes that, in the considered tasks, R-learning qualitatively explains trends observed in the dopamine data.

### Weaknesses
- The difference between average-reward RL and a large-discount-factor model is not clearly established; the paper should explicitly state why the discounting framework should be rejected rather than vaguely writing “in some respects.”
- Lack of novelty: no novel RL technique is introduced; an existing algorithm is used to model very simple tasks and partially confirm prior findings. The paper fails to communicate the significance of the results and to place them in the bigger picture of current RL research.
- Similarity between classical RL models (e.g., V-learning/discounted TD) and dopamine signaling has been claimed in prior work, but this paper does not rule out such alternatives. No new arguments are added that favor R-learning over standard value-based RL.
- Several methodological questions and missing comparisons/ablations are not addressed:
  - Why do responses to relayed reward cues decrease with average-reward RL in Figure 3? Which parameter specifies this reduction?
  - How does average-reward RL compare to advantage learning (where state-value subtraction can amplify action differences despite large baselines)?
  - How does the parameter α for the update of average reward affect model performance?
  - How could the long-term RPE of ventral striatum be combined with discounted short-term RPEs in other striatal regions in an Actor-Critic framework?
  - How does the library from Golub & Sussillo facilitate confirming a stable fixed point?
  - The wording “with content” on Line 99 is unclear.
  - The paper uses average-reward for batch-level training of RNNs rather than as an online, time-varying learning target. Does this hamper significance? How would results compare with a batch size of 1 (online learning)?
