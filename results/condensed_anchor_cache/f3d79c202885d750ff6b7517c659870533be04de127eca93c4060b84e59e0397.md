## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates mathematical capabilities in LLaMA-2 7B, showing that with large numbers of random generations (up to 1024), the model can achieve very high “oracle” accuracy (97.7% on GSM8K, 72.0% on MATH). The authors argue the main issue is inconsistent elicitation of these capabilities, and that scaling supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) data—especially using GPT‑4 Turbo‑synthesized examples—can improve reliability. Training on synthetic data up to ~1M samples yields 82.6% on GSM8K and 40.6% on MATH, surpassing prior 7B models. However, multiple reviewers challenge the novelty, methodology, and generalizability of these claims, and question whether the results reflect genuine mathematical reasoning or overfitting and pattern matching.

### Strengths
- **Uncovering an interesting phenomenon (Reviewer 1, 3):** The finding that small models (LLaMA‑2 7B) can achieve very high pass@k accuracy (e.g., 97.6% GSM8K, ~70% MATH) is an interesting and less‑explored observation.
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation (Reviewer 1, 2):** The paper conducts extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks, includes various data scaling scenarios, and compares against many prior models.
- **Well‑structured and clearly written for the most part (Reviewer 1, 3, 4):** The presentation is logically organized, methods are detailed for reproducibility, and arguments are presented in an easy‑to‑follow manner.
- **Challenging the assumption that only large models excel at complex reasoning (Reviewer 2):** The scaling approach with synthetic data demonstrates that smaller models can be boosted to perform at a level competitive with much larger or closed‑source models, highlighting the importance of data scaling.
- **Clear demonstration of practical performance gains (Reviewer 2):** The final model achieves high benchmark scores, suggesting that synthetic data can be a viable substitute for real data in this context.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of significant novelty (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4):** The core ideas—using multiple generations to pick a correct answer (pass@k) and distilling from a strong model via synthetic fine‑tuning—have been explored in prior work. Prior studies have shown that 7B‑scale models improve with math‑related pre‑training or distillation. The paper does not introduce surprising or fundamentally new insights.
- **Methodological concerns with pass@k as a measure of “capability” (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 3):** Using pass@1024 to claim the model “already possesses strong math abilities” is questioned. Reviewer 4 provides a strong analogy: generating many random paths in a maze and finding one that works does not mean the program is capable of solving mazes. High pass@k may simply reflect the number of tries, not underlying reasoning competence. Reviewer 3 notes this motivating observation is underexplored.
- **Risk of overfitting to GSM8K and MATH (Reviewer 1, 2, 4):** The synthetic data is generated from GPT‑4 using prompts with seed questions that are very similar to or derivative of the GSM8K and MATH benchmarks (Reviewer 4 asks whether seed questions come from train or test set). This raises concerns that the model may overfit to evaluation distributions rather than gaining general math reasoning. Reviewer 4 notes that training on synthetic MATH questions yields far higher accuracy than training on synthetic GSM8K, suggesting exposure to similar questions matters more than reasoning.
- **Limited benchmark selection (Reviewer 2, 3, 4):** Evaluation is restricted to GSM8K and MATH. Reviewer 2 calls for testing on more diverse or difficult benchmarks (MMLU college‑level, OlympiadBench) to demonstrate robustness. Reviewer 3 notes the lack of evaluation on llama3 is a glaring omission. Reviewer 4 points out that the “out‑of‑distribution” Hungarian exam is still very similar to MATH.
- **No evidence that improvements reflect genuine reasoning (Reviewer 2, 3, 4):** Recent work (Mirzadeh et al. 2024, Zhang et al. 2024) suggests models rely on pattern matching and memorization on these benchmarks. The experiments do not provide evidence for improved reasoning ability; stepwise or process‑level analysis is absent (Reviewer 3). Figure 8 in the appendix shows reasoning mistakes increase with more synthetic data, further undermining the reasoning claim.
- **Reliance on GPT‑4 as judge and generator without validation (Reviewer 2):** Using GPT‑4 to evaluate generated responses without demonstrating correlation with human judgment weakens credibility. A small‑scale human evaluation or citation of existing alignment studies is needed.
- **Synthetic data generation risks not discussed (Reviewer 2):** The paper does not address “model collapse” from synthetic data, an issue documented in the literature. While GPT‑4 might mitigate this, the lack of analysis is a weakness.
- **Impractical data requirements (Reviewer 1, 2):** Generating 1024 responses per question or up to 76 million synthetic examples is computationally inefficient for real‑world applications, limiting practical usefulness.
- **Claims of generality overly broad (Reviewer 2):** The title and text imply findings apply to “common 7B LMs,” but experiments only involve LLaMA‑2 7B. Without testing on other 7B models (e.g., llama3, Mistral), such generalizations are premature.
- **Unsupported power‑law scaling claim (Reviewer 2):** The paper claims “power law scaling” without fitting a functional form or providing rigorous evidence. The observed trend should be called a scaling trend.
- **Writing and presentation issues (Reviewer 2, 3):** The abstract and introduction contain vague statements (e.g., “significant improvement” without numbers), confusing phrasing on emergence theory, and grammatical errors. Captions and figures lack sufficient context and summaries. Some sentences like “Table 2 shows the results.” are too terse.
- **Lack of depth in key analysis (Reviewer 3):** The initial observation that most generations are incorrect is not deeply studied theoretically or empirically. Figure 5 / Section 4.3 would be stronger with step‑wise grading (LLM, humans, or PRM800k).
- **Minority positive reviewer (Reviewer 1) agrees with several weaknesses but is more forgiving; their overall rating (5/5) is notably higher than the other reviewers (3/5 each), indicating strong disagreement about the overall merit.**
