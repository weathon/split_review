## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a novel task, dance accompaniment (generating a follower’s responsive dance conditioned on a leader and music), along with a high-quality mocap dataset (DD100: 117–115.4 minutes, 10 genres, 20 cameras, 120 FPS, SMPL-X). The proposed method, Duolando, tokenizes motion via VQ-VAE, uses a GPT-like autoregressive model conditioned on music, leader motion, and past follower motion, and refines outputs with off-policy RL. Evaluation includes three kinematic metrics and a user study. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: two reviewers rated the paper 8 (strong novelty, dataset, clear writing), one gave 6 (solid work but limited impact and output quality issues), and one gave 3 (missing ablations, unclear music representation, artifacts, doubt about professional quality). Confidence is moderate (mostly 4, one 3). Reviewers agree on the novelty of the task and dataset value but disagree on method contribution and clarity.

### Strengths
- **Novel task and high-quality dataset**: The dance accompaniment task is new and well-motivated for VR/AR. The DD100 dataset is a major contribution (114–117 minutes, 10 duet dance genres, collected with 20 optical cameras and meta gloves at 120 FPS, fitted with SMPL-X). Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 highlight its quality and potential impact; Reviewer 3 agrees the dataset is a strength but is less positive overall.
- **Method design**: Duolando’s approach is straightforward and reasonable: tokenizing motion with VQ-VAE and autoregressively generating follower tokens with a GPT conditioned on music and leader motion. The off-policy RL stage to handle out-of-distribution conditions is a notable novelty (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Thorough evaluation**: The paper proposes three separate interaction metrics (inspired by beat alignment) and conducts a user study. Ablations (including the RL component) are performed. Reviewers 1 and 2 praise the evaluation; Reviewer 1 notes results are less impressive than ground truth but effective for baselines.
- **Clear writing and figures**: The paper is well-written, with clear notation, tables (e.g., Table 1 summarizing dataset uniqueness), and figures (e.g., Figure 4 explaining the autoregressive generator). Reviewers 1 and 2 appreciate the clarity; Reviewer 4 notes some ambiguity.

### Weaknesses
- **Output quality and artifacts**: Generated motions are rigid and lifelike compared to ground truth (Reviewer 1: “a lifeless mannequin”). Multiple reviewers note artifacts: floor-skating, body penetration, flickering frames, exaggerated hand motions (Reviewers 2, 3). The RL only addresses lower-/full-body misalignment, leaving other artifacts unmitigated; Reviewer 2 asks why other artifacts were not tackled and suggests adding metrics for this improvement. Reviewer 3 doubts some generated samples are beat-aligned and questions dataset professionalism.
- **Missing ablations and experiments**:
    - *Music input*: No ablation without music input – the leader may already convey rhythm (Reviewer 3).
    - *RL design*: No comparison against on-policy RL, and no ablation on the ad-hoc reward design (Reviewer 3).
    - *Signal dominance*: No experiment swapping music and leader motion between test cases to understand which modality dominates. Also no analysis of music genre impact (Reviewer 4).
    - *Pre-training*: Why not pre-train on noisier dance data before fine-tuning on the small mocap dataset? (Reviewer 1).
- **Dataset and copyright concerns**: The dataset uses copyrighted music (e.g., “Charlie Puth – LA Girls”) without clear copyright details; this may compromise long-term dataset release and value (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity and missing details**:
    - *Music representation*: Not specified (MIDI, spectrogram, raw waveform?) (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 notes no details on music tokenization.
    - *Equation vs figure discrepancy*: Equation 1 states both `p` and `M` as VQ-VAE inputs, but Figure 3 shows only `p`; need clarification on separate/joint encoding and decoding (Reviewer 4).
    - *Data statistics*: Missing per-style sequence counts, joint velocity distributions, movement-type histograms (Reviewer 4).
    - *Terminology*: Ambiguity between “duet dance generation” and “dance accompaniment”; dataset might support full duet generation from music alone, which is underexplored (Reviewer 4).
- **User study and evaluation details**:
    - User study used 2D skeletons rather than 3D-rendered motions; why? (Reviewer 2).
    - Need training time per stage (Reviewer 2).
    - Mocap quality evaluation: observed flickering frames and exaggerated hand motions in the supplementary video (Reviewer 2).
- **Reusability and impact**: Limited insights transferable outside dance generation; the RL refinement for GPT is domain-specific (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 gives a contribution rating of 1 (“poor”).
