## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a hybrid approach for Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) combining a Decision Transformer (DT) trained offline on expert trajectories from ODrM* with GPT-4o for real-time centralized guidance via a chain-of-thought prompt. Agents switch to LLM-generated actions for up to 5 steps when conflicts or goal changes occur. Experiments are conducted in static and dynamic (goal-changing) environments on custom low-obstacle-density maps, comparing against PRIMAL and DCC. The authors claim reduced training time, lower collision rates, and improved adaptability.

### Strengths
- Builds on the recent trend of exploring transformer architectures (DT+GPT-4o) for planning in MAPF.
- DT is trained offline per agent in a decentralized manner on expert data.
- The combination of DT and LLM creates a flexible system that can handle both static and dynamic environments, adapting quickly to shifted goals.
- The framework could potentially address real-world MAPF challenges (partial observability, dynamic goal adjustments) in logistics or swarm robotics.
- The paper is overall clearly written and discusses some limitations of using LLMs for planning.
- Among the first works to combine a Decision Transformer with an LLM specifically for MAPF with changing goals.
- Well-designed visual aids effectively illustrate experimental setups and outcomes.

### Weaknesses
- **Inadequate baseline comparisons and missing state-of-the-art methods:**  
  - The main baseline PRIMAL is 5 years old; SCRIMP and CACTUS (which the authors mention) are known to perform better and should have been included.  
  - For the dynamic setting, the formulation is similar to life-long MAPF, which has dedicated methods (e.g., PRIMAL2) that are not compared.  
  - Centralized planners like LaCaM scale to 1K agents, yet the paper only evaluates up to 64 agents.  
  - In static experiments, DCC outperforms the proposed framework for larger team sizes (Fig. 4); the paper only shows advantages in smaller settings.  
  - Tables 3 and 4 do not clearly demonstrate that DT+GPT-4o outperforms baselines overall.  
- **Violation of decentralized and partially observable assumptions:**  
  - The LLM prompt encodes the full map and information about all other agents, making the system partially centralized. This contradicts the stated decentralized/partially observable problem statement (Dec-POMDP) and is not adequately discussed.  
  - Multiple reviewers note this issue; the paper should compare against a version where only each agent's local observations are passed to the LLM.  
- **Experimental setup is too simplistic and lacks rigor:**  
  - Only custom maps with low obstacle density are used; standard benchmarks (MovingAI, POGEMA) are available and should be employed for fair comparison.  
  - The scale (up to 64 agents) is small; real-world MAPF often involves larger numbers.  
  - Results are reported without standard error or variance across trials.  
  - The paper only uses simulated environments; real-world validation (addressing sensor noise, communication delays, unpredictable behaviors) is missing.  
- **Training time claims are overemphasized and not properly justified:**  
  - Training on demonstrations (expert data) is inherently easier and converges faster; this does not prove superiority of the architecture.  
  - The authors do not account for model parameter count when comparing training time with PRIMAL or DCC.  
  - Collecting expert trajectories itself requires a computational cost that is not accounted for in the “few hours” claim.  
- **Prompt engineering limitations and LLM latency concerns:**  
  - The reliance on manually designed prompt templates may limit generalization across different environments.  
  - Using GPT-4o for real-time guidance could introduce latency issues, especially as the number of agents increases.  
- **Lack of clarity in key design choices:**  
  - The credit assignment mechanism for the joint reward (collision penalty) is not explained; it is unclear how individual agents learn from a shared reward in a decentralized manner.  
  - How the variable number of agents is handled for tokenizing actions and observations in the DT is not addressed.  
- **Missing ablations and comparisons:**  
  - No ablation testing the effect of using only GPT-4o (without DT) for the overall task.  
  - No comparison of DT+LLM with a simple alternative like DT+ODrM* or DT+DCC (to isolate the LLM contribution).  
  - The authors use only expert trajectories in DT training; the effect of including suboptimal trajectories (to test robustness) is not explored.  
  - Automated prompt generation methods to improve efficiency are not considered.  
- **Unclear metric definition:**  
  - The paper defines “number of collisions … in a successful episode” but does not clarify whether an episode with collisions is still considered successful.  
- **Disagreement on novelty and motivation:** While some reviewers found the combination of DT and LLM to be a novel extension, another reviewer found the motivation insufficient and the integration arbitrary; the synergy between the two components is not clearly justified.
