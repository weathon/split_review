## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates how data diversity (via data augmentation and synthetic data) shapes the weight landscape of neural networks, compared with traditional regularization (dropout, weight decay). Using Random Matrix Theory (RMT) to analyze eigenvalue distributions of pre-trained CLIP ViT models fine-tuned on CIFAR-10/100, the authors observe that diverse data influences the weight landscape similarly to dropout, and that synthetic data can enhance out-of-distribution generalization but also cause model collapse if overused.

### Strengths
- The topic of data augmentation’s impact on the weight landscape is interesting, and the use of RMT is straightforward.
- The observation that dropout and data augmentation exhibit similarities in affecting the weight space is intriguing and seems expected/reasonable.
- The final discussion section makes several good points about the impact of regularization and data augmentation.
- Originality: The specific application of spectral analysis to compare data augmentation approaches with other regularizers is new.
- The paper is easy to read, well-organized, and provides good insights into the impact of diversity and synthetic data on generalization with respect to the network landscape.
- Leveraging RMT provides a valuable perspective for examining more complex methods.
- The study includes multiple experiments investigating the effects of various regularization and augmentation strategies.
- The diversity per class analysis for different augmentation strategies is motivating.

### Weaknesses
- **Methodology not convincing.** The paper states “since the heavy-tailed nature of pre-trained models… we focus on the trend” but this is unclear. Weight differences (ΔM) between pre-trained and fine-tuned models are naturally large due to different training data/objectives; it is not adequately explained why comparing these differences across fine-tuning methods isolates the effect of each technique. The claim that techniques falling in the same quadrant have the same effect is not sufficiently justified. The analysis needs to clarify how quadrant analysis isolates technique-specific effects from inherent pre-trained/fine-tuned differences.
- **Analysis lacks clarity.** ESD plots (Figure 3) are hard to interpret – are features like spread, tail, or other properties being analyzed? The same issue arises for Figure 4. Table 2 merely lists numbers without a clear takeaway; the meaning of those numbers and how they support claims should be explained. Class order in Figure 1 likely impacts Vendi Score results, but this is not considered; the paper must demonstrate that class order does not affect conclusions.
- **Limited scope: only one model and small datasets.** Experiments use only CLIP ViT on CIFAR-10/100. The findings’ generality is unclear. Additional models (e.g., ResNet18/34, convolutional vs. transformer layers) and larger/realistic datasets (e.g., ImageNet or subsets like imagenette) are needed. CIFAR-10/100 are small and may not capture real-world complexities; moreover, CLIP pre-trained models are typically trained on higher resolution images – fine-tuning on 32×32 may be problematic.
- **Synthetic data experiments feel disconnected.** It is not clear why synthetic data experiments belong in this paper; the connection between spectral analysis of weight matrices and synthetic data generation is not well-articulated. The paper does not establish how observed spectral changes relate to specific synthetic data characteristics (diversity, realism). Additionally, the claim that synthetic data can hurt generalization lacks in-depth analysis – e.g., a graph of synthetic data amount vs. performance or final model diversity.
- **Lacks theoretical analysis.** The paper is primarily an experimental report. Findings could be strengthened by theoretical explanation of why these phenomena occur, rather than only empirical observations.
- **Missing baselines and visualizations.** No combination baselines like Dropout+Weight Decay (from Fig. 3) or Fine-tuning with Very Large Dropout (Zhang & Bottou) are included. There is no loss landscape visualization, which would complement the current spectral analysis to show behavior of data augmentation/synthetic data.
- **Unclear Vendi Score usage.** Different spaces (raw pixel vs. feature space) yield different observations (Figure 1), but it is not clarified how VS is calculated in each space and why they are not directly comparable. Why is CLIP used instead of Inception? The definition of VS(K) is unclear – what does K represent? The paper states VS was not used to measure diversity in feature space but then uses it to evaluate diversity introduced by augmentation/synthetic data – this distinction is ambiguous.
- **Reproducibility not addressed.** No mention of open-source code or reproducibility details.
- **Typos.** L203: “Since the…” should be “Due to the…”? L210-211: “properties of the spectral” should be “properties of the spectrum” or “spectral properties of the weight matrices”?
- **Pre-training interaction not explored.** It is unknown whether starting from a zero-shot pre-trained model vs. training from scratch changes the effects of diversity or augmentation. Does the pre-training already include augmentations that the base model is invariant to? How does pre-training model choice interact with data diversity effects? (Reviewer 2 asks if base model trained with augmentations already; Reviewer 4 asks about generalization to other architectures.)
- **Lack of real-world relevance motivation.** Data diversity and generalization is a hot topic (e.g., Hemmat et al. 2024), but the paper does not sufficiently motivate why the analysis is important or actionable. Could findings optimize application of regularization/augmentation? Could a diversity metric guide augmentation during training, balancing synthetic vs. real data to prevent collapse? These are not discussed.
