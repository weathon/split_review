## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LOVECon, a training-free text-driven long video editing pipeline built upon ControlNet. It introduces cross-window attention for global style consistency across windows, a latent fusion mechanism (using source video DDIM inversion and attention maps) for accurate control, and a frame interpolation model to reduce flickering. Experiments on 30 videos using CLIP-based metrics show edited videos that preserve background structure while modifying target regions. All three reviewers rated the paper 5 (weak reject) with contributions rated 2 (fair).

### Strengths
- The work tackles the challenging task of training-free long video editing. [R1,R2,R3]
- The paper is overall well written and easy to follow. [R1,R3]
- The method provides a simple, systematic solution that maintains structural fidelity and consistency. [R3]
- Visual results demonstrate editing on the target area while keeping other regions intact, with quantitative evidence of improved video quality and temporal consistency over compared baselines. [R1]
- Rich experimental results and code/supplementary are provided. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty – each component is borrowed or incremental:** The cross-window attention is a naive variant of ControlVideo’s attention and has been widely used in prior long video generation/editing works [R1,R2,R3]. The latent fusion module based on attention maps and the frame interpolation model are also not new [R1]. The overall approach is a combination of existing methods without significant technical innovation. [R1,R3]
- **Insufficient evaluation and weak metrics:** Experiments use only 30 videos, which is far from enough to demonstrate robustness. [R1] CLIP-based metrics may capture only semantic similarity, not overall video quality (temporal consistency, sharpness, artifacts); IS and FVD (on a larger dataset) would be needed. [R1]  
- **Lack of ablation to isolate contributions:** Since the main visual benefit (editing only target region) is likely from the latent fusion module, it is impossible to evaluate the effectiveness of cross-window attention without an ablation study (e.g., Fig. 5a without latent fusion). [R1]  
- **Doubts about temporal consistency:** The method still fails to establish true long-range temporal dependencies. Video actions are locally coherent but overall appearance is strange; e.g., in Fig. 5(a), the method blurs the background and changes the target subject over time (car window turns red), whereas fully cross-frame attention (ControlVideo) maintains both background loyalty and temporal consistency of the subject. [R3]  
- **Limited editing capability:** Because the model heavily reuses existing components, it cannot perform shape transformations, object additions or deletions; outputs are restricted to color or style changes. [R3]  
- **Unanswered reviewer questions that reflect missing experiments/comparisons:**  
  * How do the authors ensure consistency *within* a window, when the cross-window mechanism addresses only inter-window consistency? [R3]  
  * In Fig. 3, why does ControlVideo-II fail to achieve fine-grained control (e.g., hair color) that it reportedly achieves in its original paper? [R2]  
- **Minority disagreement:** Reviewer 3 finds the experimental results “rich and excellent” despite the limited scope, while Reviewer 1 considers 30 videos insufficient; this contrast is preserved. [R1 vs R3]
