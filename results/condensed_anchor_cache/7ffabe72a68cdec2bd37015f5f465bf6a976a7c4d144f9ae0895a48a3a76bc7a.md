## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper rethinks complex query answering on knowledge graphs, focusing on EFO1 queries. It identifies gaps in the tree-form queries studied by prior work, introduces a new dataset (Real-EFO1) covering ten previously unstudied query types, and proposes a fuzzy-logic-based method (FIT) that extends QTO to handle cycles in query graphs and trains link predictors end-to-end. The empirical results show consistent improvements over baselines on both the new dataset and existing benchmarks. Reviewer opinions are split: two rate the paper 8/8, praising its theoretical analysis and contributions, while two rate it 6/6, raising concerns about definitions, baselines, presentation, and omitted comparisons.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a clear theoretical characterization of the query families previously investigated, identifying the gap between tree-form queries and full EFO1 queries, and offers a complexity analysis showing that tree-form EFO1 reasoning is linear in the number of variables.
- A new dataset, Real-EFO1, is introduced with ten new query types that were not covered by past work, providing a rigorous benchmark for evaluating complex query execution methods.
- The proposed FIT method shows consistent and significant improvements over baseline approaches in both the new dataset and existing datasets, with proper ablation studies demonstrating that the extensions to QTO (cycle handling, sparsification, end-to-end training) each contribute positively.
- One reviewer highlights that FIT’s message-passing over query graph nodes and edges stays faithful to the KG’s allowed relations and combinations, and uses back-propagation to update probability values from the actual answers.
- Another reviewer notes that the analysis rethinks traditional claims (e.g., exponential growth in reasoning time) and firmly supports that the tree-form query family is not even a subset of EFO1, strengthening the motivation for the new dataset and method.

### Weaknesses
- **Definition mismatch:** A key criticism (from one reviewer) is that the paper uses a broader definition of tree-form queries than the one employed by prior work (e.g., Ren & Leskovec 2020), which explicitly avoided universal quantification. This disagreement undercuts the paper’s claim that prior work does not cover all EFO1 queries, according to that reviewer. Two other reviewers, however, accept the paper’s analysis without raising this objection.
- **Missing comparisons:**
    - The paper does not compare against GNN-based query graph execution methods, which would be a natural baseline given the message-passing nature of FIT. This omission is cited as significant by one reviewer.
    - The key difference between FIT and FuzzQE (a fuzzy-logic-based prior method) is not clearly discussed, and FuzzQE is not included in the experimental comparisons (Table 2). This is noted by one reviewer as a weakness.
- **Presentation issues:**
    - The computation of the crucial quantity Cu(node) is relegated to the appendix rather than appearing in the main paper. Several reviewers request that key definitions and explanations be moved to the main text for clarity.
    - The paper lacks a dedicated Related Work section, making it difficult for readers unfamiliar with the area to assess the claims. This absence is especially problematic given the paper’s critique of existing methods.
    - A reviewer points out that the definition of EFO1 is missing from Definition 6, and an intuitive explanation of the variable *k* in Equation (4) would be helpful.
- **Performance concerns:** One reviewer notes that the MRR on two datasets is still low (~30), and it is unclear whether this reflects inherent difficulty or limitations of the approach.
- **Proof soundness:** One reviewer questions the proof that tree-form queries cannot represent all FOL queries, arguing that existential leaves might be representable in a different tree structure; another reviewer, however, fully supports the paper’s theoretical analysis.
- **Missing definition:** The paper does not clearly define the new query families used in Real-EFO1 in the main text, requiring readers to consult the appendix for essential context.
