## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ToVE (Transfer of Vision Experts), a framework that enhances vision-language (VL) learning by transferring knowledge from a hub of pre-trained vision experts to a frozen CLIP image encoder. A token-aware gating network dynamically routes expert knowledge to vision tokens, and a "residual knowledge transfer" strategy preserves generalizability while allowing selective expert detachment for efficiency. The authors also explore merging expert knowledge into a single CLIP encoder to avoid expert inference at deployment. Experiments show competitive performance across VL tasks using significantly less training data (two orders of magnitude fewer). The method is compared to models like BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, and Prismer.

### Strengths
- Novel approach: introducing a hub of pre-trained vision experts and a token-aware gating network for efficient VL learning (R1, R2, R3). Architecture design relating knowledge fusion to mixture-of-experts is novel (R3). Fusing multiple experts into the vision encoder is a good idea (R3).
- Competitive performance: achieves strong results across multiple VL tasks (NoCaps, VQAv2, VSR, POPE) using substantially less training data than prior works (R1, R2). Experiments demonstrate the superiority of using multiple vision encoders (R2). Better results than Prismer, a related approach (R3).
- Interpretability: visualizations of gating network routing decisions illustrate how expert knowledge is allocated across image regions, enhancing interpretability (R1).
- Insightful ablations: ablations on types of knowledge merging and expert detachment provide useful insights (R3).
- Conceptual accessibility: overall idea and method are easy to understand (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Computational complexity and fairness of comparison:**
  - Routing expert knowledge and the token-aware gating network add significant complexity; initial setup and training are computationally intensive, with high memory/processing demands (R1, R4). Using multiple encoders multiplies parameter count and computation, making comparisons with other base-sized VLMs unfair unless parameter counts are reported (R2, R3). Reviewer 2 further notes that after distillation into a single encoder (ToVE-lite), performance does not clearly beat simply using EVA alone: NoCaps 104.1 vs. 109.1, VQAv2 74.0 vs. 74.4, VSR 65.9 vs. 63.8, POPE-R 86.6 vs. 85.7, POPE-C 81.9 vs. 80.8 (average 82.50 vs. 82.76); decreases on NoCaps and VQAv2 with only marginal improvements otherwise, raising robustness concerns (R2). Reviewer 4 similarly questions whether gains come from the stronger backbone (EVA) rather than each expert contributing, and requests re-running a baseline with EVA as the sole visual feature extractor to clarify the contribution of other experts (R4).

- **Load-balancing loss and expert usage:**
  - The load-balancing loss (L_aux) is introduced but its effectiveness is not extensively validated; no ablation on loss parameters or analysis of how it prevents over-reliance on a subset of experts (R1). The paper cites the concept without explaining its application within ToVE (R4).

- **Domain mismatch and generalization:**
  - The paper lacks discussion on handling potential domain mismatches between vision experts (trained on natural images) and specialized downstream tasks (e.g., medical images, satellite imagery). No strategies for adaptation are suggested (R1).

- **Missing comparisons and experimental scope:**
  - Missing comparisons with relevant methods: Dou et al. (CVPR 2022) and ViLT (ICML 2021) (R4). The setting is considered somewhat outdated; reviewers recommend implementing ToVE within the LLaVA framework to demonstrate effectiveness in broader general-purpose multimodal LLM scenarios (R2). Datasets are limited; suggestions include adding GQA, TextVQA, VizWiz, and reporting CIDEr for COCO in Table 3 (R2).

- **Presentation of results:**
  - In Tables 1 and 2, ToVE results are bolded but are not state-of-the-art (e.g., BLIPv2 has higher CIDEr on COCO, InstructBLIP has higher POPE-R). Tables need to show number of training parameters for fair comparison and may require redesign (R3). The paper leaves the true contribution difficult to assess without specifying pre-training datasets for individual vision encoders (DINO, Depth, etc.) and the language model (R2).

- **Methodological clarity and writing issues:**
  - Numerous grammatical errors and sentence-construction problems (R1, R3, R4). Notation inconsistencies: the mapping function from d_k to d_lang is described as changing token lengths rather than feature dimensions (R3). After Eq. 6, t_clip and t_fuse are referenced but do not appear in the equation (R3). The claim that MoE commonly uses top-1 routing is incorrect (original MoE did not; Switch Transformer uses top-1, sparse MoE uses top-k) (R3). The method requires token-specific output from each expert (Eq. 1); it is not described how tokens are obtained from low-level vision experts (e.g., depth, edge) (R3, also R3 asks in questions). The explanation of why setting weights to -inf in formula 3 improves efficiency and how the model avoids activating all experts is insufficient (R4). The concept of L_aux is cited without explanation of its application in this method (R4). The organization of the methods section is confusing (R4).
