## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a realistic continual learning setting with a restricted computational budget (FLOPs) per timestep and sparse labels. It introduces DietCL, a semi-supervised method that combines self-supervised learning (MAE) on unlabeled data with supervised replay on labeled data, along with a budget allocation mechanism. Experiments on large-scale datasets (CLOC, ImageNet10K, CGLM) show DietCL outperforms existing supervised and semi-supervised CL methods by 2–4%.

### Strengths

- The problem setting – continual learning under simultaneously limited compute (FLOPs) and sparse labels – is important, realistic, and under-explored. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The method is simple and performs well across many ablations and datasets. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Defining compute budget by FLOPs allows considering scenarios where a full epoch is infeasible, which is challenging but reflects real deployment constraints. (Reviewer 4)
- Section 3.2 effectively motivates the approach by diagnosing why existing methods fail under low budget (overfitting, insufficient compute). (Reviewer 3)
- DietCL is well-designed with sufficient details: using unlabeled data as a regularizer to eliminate overfitting and the stability gap, and a budget allocation mechanism. (Reviewer 3)
- The evaluation of prior methods seems fair and complete. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and presentation:** The paper is poorly organized – the related work discussion continues until page 5, challenges are reiterated multiple times, and DietCL itself gets less than a page of coverage. (Reviewer 1, strong criticism) Minor presentation issues: confused notation between Appendices (l,u,m) and main text (r,m,b); typo in Figure 6. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
- **Novelty and exposition:** The core idea (MAE + masking logits of unseen classes + a budget loss term) is not well articulated. The connection between the method and the budget constraint is unclear beyond adding a budget loss term. (Reviewer 1, strong criticism; Reviewer 2 notes the method is simple, which warrants more ablation)  
- **Loss balancing:** Equation (4) adds three loss terms (reconstruction, masking, budget) without any hyperparameters to scale them (e.g., α_r, α_m, α_b). A reconstruction loss (Euclidean distance) added to cross-entropy losses will inevitably have a scaling issue. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Missing ablations:**  
  - No ablation with alternative self-supervised methods (e.g., contrastive learning) instead of MAE. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The impact of the order in the ablation study (Replay → L_m → Balanced Buffer → L_r) is not tested; other orders may yield different observations. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The buffer threshold is learned via cross-validation, but the computational cost and number of timesteps needed are not reported. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Missing comparisons:**  
  - Comparison with Prabhu et al. (2023), a supervised continual learning method, is needed. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Supervised baselines (ER, ER-ACE) are almost as good as DietCL in Table 1; the paper lacks ablations varying budget and task complexity against these methods to understand DietCL’s limitations. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Overfitting claim overstatement:** Figure 2 shows DietCL still overfits, just less severely than others. The paper’s text claiming it does not suffer from overfitting is an overstatement. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Buffer design:** The task-balanced buffer includes only data from the current and previous timestep. It is unclear why earlier timesteps are excluded – including them could improve diversity and prevent forgetting, if the budget allows. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Real-world connection:** The paper should quantify how far the low-budget setting is from a real scenario (e.g., a Snapchat-like application with typical hardware). (Reviewer 3)  
- **Missing empirical details:** No report of number of runs or variance for results (Figures 2–6). Does example ordering impact learning, and are some methods more sensitive? (Reviewer 4)  
- **Notation error:** Overloading of t on page 6 in definitions of A(t) and A. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Unexplained behavior:** The non-monotonic behavior of DietCL in Figure 4 is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
