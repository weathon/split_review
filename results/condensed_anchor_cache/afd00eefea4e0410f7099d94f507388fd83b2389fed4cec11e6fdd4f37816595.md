## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an extension of DreamerV2 for Hidden-parameter MDPs (HIP-MDPs) by adding a recurrent module that explicitly estimates hidden environmental parameters (e.g., friction, mass) from history. The estimated parameters condition the world model, actor, and critic. The method is evaluated on 5 continuous control tasks (3 DMControl, 2 custom MuJoCo). Reviews are mixed: one reviewer rated it 5 and found it intuitive and relevant, while three reviewers rated it 3, raising major concerns about task difficulty, baselines, novelty, and evaluation rigor. All reviewers note that the core idea is simple and grounded, but question the strength of the evidence.

### Strengths
- The method is intuitive, conceptually simple, and can be combined with other model-based RL approaches beyond Dreamer (R1, R2, R4).
- The addition of a simple LSTM module for hidden parameter estimation improves performance in 3–4 of the 5 tasks (R3).
- The approach is grounded in the HIP-MDP theoretical framework (R3).
- The paper is accessibly written and provides all information for reproducibility (R3); ablation studies are included (R3).
- An interesting experimental result: in one task (throwing), model-free SAC without explicit parameter estimation performs best, suggesting that for some HIP-MDPs explicit estimation is too hard and a robust policy is better (R1).
- Hidden parameter estimates can be reused for other tasks on the same environment (R2).
- One reviewer (score 5) described the method as “intuitive and relevant” and found the work appealing (R4). This more positive assessment contrasts with the other three reviewers who found the novelty limited and evaluation insufficient.

### Weaknesses
- **Toy, low-dimensional tasks**: The environments are 2D, low-dimensional (e.g., walker, pendulum) and only one parameter is randomized per task. Harder tasks (e.g., Humanoid, Ant, SlimHumanoid) used in related work like CaDM are missing. There is little evidence the method scales to more complex or multiple hidden parameters (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Weak/ill-fitting baselines**: 
  - PPO is used but is not sample-efficient for the low-data regime (2M steps). Sample-efficient model-free baselines such as RedQ/DroQ are missing (R1).
  - DreamerV2 is compared, but DreamerV3 would be a more competitive baseline (R1).
  - The domain adaptation baseline RMA uses sample-inefficient PPO and is not allowed sufficient training steps; it should be run longer (or compared to sample-efficient domain adaptation methods like CaDM [Lee 2020] or VariBAD [Zintgraf 2019] that estimate hidden parameters in a similar model-based framework) (R1).
  - There is little discussion of how the proposed method improves upon RMA, the most important baseline (R4).
- **Hidden parameter values used in the loss function** (R3): The log-likelihood terms in the loss appear to assume knowledge of the true hidden parameter values (e.g., for computing reconstruction loss of omega). This makes the comparison with DreamerV2 inappropriate, as the privileged information is provided during training. A hidden-parameter-informed Dreamer should be included as an upper bound (R3).
- **Performance is mediocre**: The method outperforms baselines in only 2 of the 5 tasks (R4). In the throwing task, the claim of outperforming all baselines is false (SAC does best) (R3). Improvements over baselines are not strong overall (R3). In one task, SAC even outperforms (R1).
- **Benchmark not suited for evaluating hidden parameter methods**: RMA itself only outperforms other baselines in 1 of the 5 tasks, suggesting that estimating hidden parameters may not be important for these tasks, or that better baselines are needed (R4). The benchmark is too simple to justify the approach (R1, R2, R4).
- **Evaluation details are insufficient**:
  - Only three random seeds are used (R2).
  - Implementation details of baselines (especially RMA) are incomplete; hyperparameters like the learning rate of 0.004 are not justified (R2).
  - Performance margins between the proposed method and baselines are narrow for some tasks (R2).
  - The cutoff of 2 million steps for evaluation seems arbitrary; the authors should test on more environments (R2).
- **Statistical and reporting inconsistencies**:
  - For the throwing environment, Table 1 reports the hidden parameter range as [0.2–1.0], but Figure 6 shows a real value of 0.042, which is out of range (R2).
  - The text says “our agent finds near-correct hidden parameters … with a few environmental steps,” yet SAC outperforms in throwing because the policy “doesn’t have a lot of steps to estimate hidden parameters” (contradiction) (R2).
  - RMA is missing from Figure 6’s parameter estimation plot (R2).
- **Limited novelty**: The core idea (explicitly estimating hidden parameters via a privileged network and conditioning the policy) is well-known from RMA and similar works; the contribution is largely an implementation of this strategy into DreamerV2 (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer (score 5) also called it incremental (R4).
- **Missing analyses and ablations**:
  - No analysis of which inputs are best for the parameter estimation module (R3).
  - No investigation of how the estimated parameter omega should be fed into subsequent modules; an embedding layer could be beneficial (R3).
  - The paper does not analyze the effect of having an explicit parameter estimation module feeding into RSSM but not the Decoder or ConditionedNet (R2).
  - The motivation for estimating omega twice (head vs. tilde) is unclear; the reconstruction error computation (Figure 5) is not clearly defined (R3).
  - It remains unclear how the method would learn hidden parameters from scratch without being given their true values during training (R3) – a major open challenge.
- **Related work is terse**: The paper does not adequately compare or contrast with prior approaches that learn context vectors to capture extrinsics (e.g., CaDM, VariBAD) (R2).
- **Motivation for reward-dependent hidden parameters is weak**: Real-world examples that fit the HIP-MDP model (where hidden parameters affect rewards) are not convincingly provided (R2).
