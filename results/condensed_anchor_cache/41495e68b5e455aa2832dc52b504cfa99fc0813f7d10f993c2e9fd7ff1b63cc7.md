## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the trade-off between safety and helpfulness in LLM alignment. It distinguishes between safety knowledge and safety values, proposes a data categorization (EHD, IHD, MHD), an adaptive message-wise alignment training (masking gradients based on reward), and a harmful token filtering mechanism at inference (via search or RAG). Experiments evaluate each component, claiming improved safety and helpfulness. Reviewers agree the topic is important but have mixed assessments: one reviewer (rating 6) finds the empirical results insightful and the adaptive approach effective; three reviewers (ratings 3, 3, 5) find the paper lacks clarity, details, and coherence, with limited contribution.

### Strengths
- The topic of LLM alignment balancing safety and helpfulness is important and interesting.
- The paper introduces novel notions of safety knowledge vs. safety value, which are conceptually interesting.
- The categorization of harmful data (EHD, IHD, MHD) and empirical investigation of their impact on alignment dynamics (e.g., saturation of safety, drop in helpfulness with more safety data) are insightful.
- The adaptive training approach (highlighting segments based on reward) shows a significant increase in safety while maintaining helpfulness compared to baselines.
- The risk token filtering at inference is an interesting approach for adversarial attacks where specific words trigger unsafe behavior.
- The paper proposes a comprehensive pipeline covering pre-processing, alignment, and post-processing, which could act as a mature solution for industry.
- The structure of the paper is clear (one reviewer notes).
- Extensive experiments are conducted to verify the three steps.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and clarity**: The paper is not clearly written, making it hard to follow. Missing references to supplementary materials, typos (e.g., Figure 2: “Number of safety training data” ambiguous; line 394: “More data does not means no safe”; line 387 reference to nonexistent Figure 5). The concepts of safety knowledge and safety value are not clearly defined; examples in appendix do not help distinguish EHD vs. IHD (e.g., both Political categories). Concrete examples in the main manuscript are needed.
- **Missing details**: Lots of experimental details are missing: Figure 2 settings (what are datasets A and B?), definition of safety score, what “real-world data” refers to, how the 260k data were collected, how forbidden words are defined in Algorithm 1, details of Section 4.3.
- **Limited contribution and coherence**: The paper reads like three separate tricks (data categorization, adaptive masking, token filtering) combined without clear correlation or mutual improvement. The connection between data categorization and the other two components is unclear. The adaptive masking part may just reflect better quality data points rather than the masking itself. The harmful token filtering part feels off-topic and should be cut (one reviewer strongly suggests).
- **Unconvincing claims**: Several claims are not well supported: “This highlights ... restrict their safety” (line 238), “RAG ... assess contextual harm” (line 356), “This indicates ... the knowledge it possesses” (line 391). The claim that “More data does not means no safe” and its connection to analysis unclear.
- **Results and evaluation issues**: Safety score on explicit harmful data remains low even with adaptive approaches; reference to known models of similar size (e.g., Llama-3-8B) is missing. Token filtering results are only reported as average safety and precision without clarifying what constitutes a true/false positive, making it hard to assess over-filtering trade-offs. Figures 2 and 3 do not clearly show how different safety data distributions affect helpfulness.
- **Missing related work**: Additional works on balancing helpfulness and harmlessness (e.g., [1,2,3]) are not cited.
- **Questions left unanswered**: How is Equation (13) connected to other content? How is RAG used in Section 3.3 (Equation 14 unclear)? How are 'anti-risk-intent' and 'anti-risk-fact' defined (line 382)? Is there a way to improve safety on explicit harmful data?
- **Presentation quality**: One reviewer rates presentation as 1 (needs major rewrite); others note 2. Paper requires substantial rewriting to improve clarity, rigor, and focus.
