## Merged Review

### Summary
Kosmos-G aligns the output space of a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with the CLIP text encoder embedding space via an AlignerNet, then feeds the aligned embeddings into Stable Diffusion. Score distillation instruction tuning on a newly curated compositional dataset enables zero-shot subject-driven generation and image editing from interleaved multi-image and text input without modifying the diffusion model.

### Strengths
- The problem of image generation from generalized vision-language inputs, especially involving multiple images, is interesting, underexplored, and the proposed approach shows promising results.
- The idea of aligning the MLLM output space with the CLIP text space and directly leveraging Stable Diffusion is a valid and clean design.
- Zero-shot multi-entity subject-driven generation and image editing are achieved without training the diffusion model, highlighting potential for seamless integration with existing U‑Net techniques.
- The newly collected compositional image generation dataset should be useful to the community.
- Qualitative results demonstrate good capabilities of the method.

### Weaknesses
- The paper claims zero-shot multi-entity generation and interleaved input handling, but the presented cases focus mostly on image editing, show at most two input images, and are limited to image-text-image scenarios. More complex/rich interleaved multi-image and text examples (e.g., >2 images, varied interleaving orders) are needed to fully illustrate the claimed capability.
- Section 2.3 on “Score distillation instruction tuning” lacks clarity: the entities used in KL divergence calculation, the precise mathematical formulas, and whether the KL loss is necessary (or if the diffusion model loss could be applied directly) are not clearly explained.
- The ablation study is not comprehensive. For the aligner design, other options to achieve the alignment should have been compared; more justification is needed for why the current AlignerNet (encoder‑decoder Transformers) is superior to simpler alternatives (e.g., GlueNet, whose only difference is an MLP but uses the same loss and domains).
- Novelty of AlignerNet is limited—it essentially replaces the MLP in GlueNet with an encoder‑decoder Transformer under the same loss and alignment domains.
- Missing comparisons on relevant benchmarks: since training data includes InstructPix2Pix, a direct comparison on image editing benchmarks is expected. Likewise, a comparison with GILL on visual storytelling is missing.
- In AlignerNet, both MSE and REC losses are used, yet no ablation is performed to isolate their individual contributions.
- Table 2 shows that end-to-end fine-tuning fails; however, recent works (BLIP‑Diffusion, EMU, MGIE) successfully connect MLLM to SD via end-to-end fine-tuning without explicit alignment. The paper does not explain why Kosmos‑G behaves differently and requires extra alignment.
- The possibility of further enhancing subject-driven generation by also training the diffusion model is not explored.
- While constructing the compositional generation dataset, it is unclear how multiple objects of the same class in one image are handled and whether their segmentation masks would cover multiple instances under the same mask.
