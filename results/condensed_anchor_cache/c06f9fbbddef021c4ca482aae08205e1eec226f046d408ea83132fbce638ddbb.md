## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Online Weight Approximation (OWA), a continual learning scheme that models weight dynamics across tasks to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in task-incremental, domain-incremental, and class-incremental settings (with known task IDs at test time). The method is evaluated on several datasets (e.g., CIFAR10, other simple benchmarks) and compared only to a single replay baseline. Reviewers note the method is novel and theoretically motivated, but they raise major concerns about weak empirical evaluation, missing comparisons with state-of-the-art continual learning methods, scalability issues, and the fact that many existing task-incremental methods already achieve forgetting-free performance, making OWA’s contribution limited.

### Strengths
- Focuses on the capacity problem in continual learning, using weight sampling motivated by online function approximation (R1).
- Solid theoretical foundation with rigorous theorems (R1).
- The problem setups (domain-/class-incremental) are challenging and interesting (R2).
- The idea is novel (R3).
- The paper is well written and the problem setup is mostly clear (R2). (But multiple reviewers (R1, R3, R4) note clarity shortcomings; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Weak empirical evaluation**:
  - Only compared with a single replay method; “Vanilla” is not a real continual learning baseline (R1, R4).
  - No comparisons with recent state-of-the-art: EWC, MAS, GDumb, REMIND, DER++, CLS‑ER (R2); also missing Serra et al. (ICML‑18), Wortsman et al. (NeurIPS‑20), Ke et al. (NeurIPS‑21), Lin et al. (ICLR‑22, NeurIP‑22), Kim et al. (NeurIPS‑22) (R3).
  - The replay baseline performance is low, making it hard to believe OWA is state‑of‑the‑art (R1).
  - The comparison to rehearsal methods is unfair because the number of replay samples is reduced; better to compare with regularization‑based methods like EWC or MAS (R2).
  - Experiments lack diversity and repeatedly yield similar conclusions (R1); datasets are simple and not large‑scale; need ImageNet‑1k or ImageNet‑100 (R2).
  - Networks used are shallow (ResNet‑10/18); scalability to deeper networks (ResNet‑34/50, Vgg, WideResNet) is not demonstrated (R2).
- **Scalability and complexity**:
  - O(n²) training time per parameter is prohibitive for large networks; need detailed computational analysis (time and memory) and empirical training time comparison with baselines (R2).
  - Unclear whether the memory budget is constant and whether the number of Euler steps S is fixed or grows (R1).
- **Task‑incremental assumption and weak positioning**:
  - The method assumes known task IDs at test time and solves task‑incremental learning (TIL), not true domain‑ or class‑incremental as claimed. Many TIL methods already achieve forgetting‑free, and OWA is weaker than some existing ones (R3).
  - Section 4.2 claims class‑incremental but no task‑ID prediction is made, so it remains TIL; the evaluation metric (average incremental accuracy) is weak – last accuracy after all tasks would be much lower (R3).
  - The first set of experiments (Sec 4.1) should involve knowledge transfer, but OWA by nature cannot transfer knowledge across tasks (R3).
- **Clarity and writing**:
  - Abstract does not clearly state the specific problem and limitations in CL literature; motivation for applying OWA to CL is unclear (R4).
  - Related work section lacks discussion of limitations and relation to proposed method (R4).
  - Method description is not clearly connected to continual learning (R4); there is a lack of insight into how OWA mitigates forgetting (R4).
  - Some reviewers found the paper well‑written (R2), but multiple other reviewers rated presentation as poor/fair (R1: 1; R3: 2; R4: 2).
- **Technical clarity**:
  - What data is used to train Eq. 7? The approach appears very similar to SupSup (Wortsman et al.) (R3).
  - The size of C^j for each task potentially spans the full network (R3).
  - What does w^i represent? What is N in Eq. 3? Why are different ResNet depths used for different datasets? (R3).
- **Missing citations and discussion**: The paper seriously lacks citations of and discussion with related literature (R3).
