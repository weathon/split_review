## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Lagrangian Flow Networks (LFlows) for modeling fluid densities and velocities while satisfying the continuity equation by design. The core insight is expressing solutions to the continuity equation as time-dependent density transformations via differentiable and invertible maps (Lagrangian flows). LFlows transform a base density with parameterized diffeomorphisms conditioned on time, avoiding numerical ODE solvers or penalty terms. The method is validated on 2D and 3D problems, including a real-world bird migration dataset, and compared against PINN, DFNN, and SLDA baselines. The paper aims at data-assimilation problems where initial/boundary conditions and the PDE itself may be partially unknown.

### Strengths
- The paper contributes to neural PDE methods by enforcing the continuity equation directly through network design, marrying neural networks with conditioning normalizing flows.
- The proposed LFlows are computationally efficient compared to divergence-free neural networks (Richter-Powell et al.) because they avoid higher-order automatic differentiation.
- LFlows outperform competing models (PINN, DFNN, SLDA) in predictive accuracy across several 2D/3D density modeling tasks, while also being computationally efficient.
- The method is validated on both simulated and real-world data (bird migration from weather radar), which could serve as a standard benchmark for similar problems.
- The approach tackles data-assimilation-style problems where many system parameters are unknown (e.g., unknown PDE, initial/boundary conditions), which is relevant for biomedical imaging and other applications.
- The writing is clear (noted by Reviewer 3). The paper provides complete proof for the theoretical insights (noted by Reviewer 2).
- Extensive experiments covering both simulated and real-world settings (Reviewer 2). The method performs well in both performance and efficiency (Reviewers 1,2).
- The two application settings (sparse data on density/velocity; only density known but additional equations for velocity) are interesting and demonstrate the flexibility of LFlows (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (minority Reviewer 3 is more negative):** The idea of using the Lagrangian view of the continuity equation to parameterize both velocity and density appears in prior work (TIPF parameterization in [1]), with the only difference being that LFlows uses \(\Phi_t\) instead of its inverse (as in TIPF). Since TIPF uses RealNVP which has ready access to the inverse flow map, the advantage of avoiding the inverse is not clearly explained. Reviewers 1 and 2 did not raise this novelty concern.
- **Limitations of normalizing flows / bijective layers:** The method inherits difficulties with handling discontinuities (sharp transitions or shocks), a significant limitation for many physical phenomena (Reviewer 1). The reliance on bijective transformations restricts the ability to model non-invertible dynamics, potentially limiting applicability (Reviewer 1).
- **Computational cost:** The computational cost of training the normalizing flow itself, especially with complex architectures, is not thoroughly discussed (Reviewer 1). More broadly, the complexity of LFlows compared to existing methods for high-dimensional hydrodynamic flows is not detailed (Reviewer 1, Question). Scaling to large problems (e.g., turbulent flows) is not addressed (Reviewer 1, Question).
- **Higher-dimensional performance:** Reviewer 3 notes that all experiments appear to be in 2D (though the paper mentions 3D synthetic examples; the reviewer’s point stands that higher-dimensional experiments (\(d \geq 10\)), such as those in [Richter-Powell 2022], are missing). It is suspected that LFlows may perform poorly in higher dimensions because invertible pushforward networks can be inexpressive or hard to train, as found for TIPF (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation and clarity:** 
  - Section 3.1 uses theorems informally without exact formalization (Theorem 1, 2, existence/uniqueness of Lagrangian flows). The full definitions should be in the main text, with proofs in the appendix (Reviewer 2).
  - Section 4 does not describe the implementation of LFlows well; details currently in Appendix A.3 (model architecture) should be moved to the main text. Baseline implementation details should be deferred to the appendix (Reviewer 2).
- **Sparse data handling:** More details are needed on how LFlows processes spatially sparse data (Reviewer 2, Question). This is a core setting in the paper.
- **Noise sensitivity:** Since LFlows enforces the continuity equation strictly, it may face problems when input data is noisy or contains errors. This should be discussed (Reviewer 2, Question).
- **Error accumulation over long time horizons:** The potential for error accumulation when propagating densities over long times is not explored (Reviewer 1), a critical aspect for practical applications.
- **Evaluation concerns:**
  - In Section 5.2, the gold standard \(W_2^2\) is computed using discrete estimates and then averaged. Discrete estimates of \(W_2\) are known to be biased; the accuracy of the reported values (red bars in Figure 3) is questionable. Would more samples shift the bars? (Reviewer 3, Question)
  - In Equation (15), the double integral appears to require uniform samples from \(\Omega\), which could be very inefficient if the flow concentrates on a small portion of the domain (Reviewer 3, Question).
- **Assumptions about data:** In setting (ii) (density known for all time), it might be possible to reconstruct velocity without additional equations by solving the continuity equation directly. The need for extra equations is not justified (Reviewer 3, Question).
