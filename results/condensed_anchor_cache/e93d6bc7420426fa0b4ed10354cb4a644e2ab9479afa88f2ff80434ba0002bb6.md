## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper derives Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) for two learned optimizer (LO) architectures and proposes a meta-training recipe for μ-parameterized LOs (μLOs). Experiments show that μLOs improve meta-generalization to wider, deeper, and longer-horizon tasks compared to LOs trained under standard parametrization (SP), and can match/exceed VeLO (trained with 4000 TPU-months) at a fraction of the compute (≤250 GPU-hours per μLO). The core modification is adding a 1/FAN_IN coefficient to the weight update (Equation 2) for hidden-layer parameters.

### Strengths

- **Empirical results compelling and significant across many settings.** The proposed μLOs show strong meta-generalization, especially to wider networks, deeper networks (5× meta-training depth), and longer training horizons (25× meta-training steps). Results in Figures 4, 5, and 6 demonstrate that μLOs often match or exceed VeLO-4000 on large-width models despite using orders of magnitude less meta-training compute. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Innovative application of Maximal Update Parametrization to learned optimizers.** Applying μP theory to transfer optimizer behavior rather than just hyperparameters is a clever extension. The method provides a practical solution to the costly hyperparameter optimization that usually accompanies scaling. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Effective identification of learned-optimizer limitations.** The paper clearly pinpoints poor meta-generalization (to larger models, longer training) as a key weakness of existing LOs and addresses it directly. (Reviewer 2)
- **Stable performance in long-horizon settings.** The μLOs exhibit notably stable loss decrease over extended training trajectories compared to SP baselines. (Reviewer 3)
- **Potential to dramatically reduce meta-training cost.** The results indicate that with μP, LOs can be trained affordably yet generalize well, making them more accessible. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited technical novelty; straightforward application of μP.** Most of the core ideas are directly from the original μP work [1]. The modification (adding a 1/FAN_IN coefficient on the update term in Equation (2)) is conceptually small, and the paper does not provide a deeper theoretical justification for why this specific scaling yields such significant improvements. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Missing follow-up μP theory for depth generalization.** The paper does not account for the extension of μP to depth (Tensor Program VI, arXiv:2310.02244). Hence, the observed depth generalization is more consequential than intended and could be improved with an explicit maximal update parameterization for depth. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons.** 
    - VeLO-4000 is missing from Tables 5 and 6 (Reviewer 1). 
    - There is no comparison to **per-task tuned Adam/μAdam** to determine whether μLO obviates the need for task-specific tuning. (Reviewer 1)
    - Results on **ResNet and Plain ResNet** (to test loss‑landscape smoothness) are absent. Reviewer 3 notes that prior work shows ResNet has a smoother landscape; testing on Plain ResNet would better demonstrate robustness.
    - **Downstream evaluation metrics** (e.g., validation accuracy, test performance) are not reported; only training loss is shown. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of computational cost and overhead details.** 
    - The paper does not break down the resources needed for hyperparameter tuning of μ-based methods, or the total meta-training cost (except that pretraining an LO with tuned hyperparameters costs "16 TPU-months", but it is unclear whether this is for a single LO or multiple). (Reviewers 1, 2)
    - The **per-step overhead** of using the LO (relative to Adam/SGD) is not quantified. Reviewers 1 and 2 ask how much slower training becomes when using the neural-network-based optimizer.
    - Given such high pre-training cost (16 TPU-months per LO), retraining is infeasible for most practitioners, making comparison to SOTA methods like VeLO even more critical. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** 
    - **Figure 4 legend is confusing:** dashed/solid lines are not distinguished for VeLO vs LO and μVeLO vs μLO (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). 
    - The primary metric (training loss) lacks definition: it is unclear if it is the loss exactly at the given iteration, if error bars (e.g., Figure 2) represent standard deviation across seeds, if any smoothing is applied, and whether it is computed on the full training set or a subset. (Reviewer 2)
    - Section 4.1.1 states 1,000 training steps were collected, but plots (Figure 3 + appendix) show only 50% of that range. The number of seeds used per experiment is not reported. (Reviewer 2)
    - It is ambiguous whether “μAdam” refers to μAdam or μAdamW. (Reviewer 2)
- **Inconsistent performance across tasks and instability.** 
    - In Figure 5a, μLO diverges; across Figures 5a and 5b the ranking between μLO and μVeLO changes, making it hard for practitioners to choose. (Reviewer 2)
    - On the most out-of-distribution tasks (Transformer language modeling, ViT image classification), μLO does **not** achieve best performance, diluting the claimed contribution. (Reviewer 3)
    - Contrary to the statement in Line 516 (“muVeLO_M stably decreases training loss over time for each task”), Figure 6a shows the loss beginning to diverge. (Reviewer 2)
    - μVeLO exhibits **high variance** relative to other methods, raising concerns about its reliability. (Reviewer 2)
    - In Figures 4a and 4b, muLO and μAdam converge to similar final loss values, so the main benefit appears to be improved anytime performance; without accounting for the LO’s overhead, the practical significance is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
    - In Figure 4c, μAdam’s loss trajectory has an unexplained erratic shape. (Reviewer 2)
- **Questions about comparative performance.** Reviewer 1 asks why μLO is better than μVeLO on average despite VeLO being a more powerful model; this lacks explanation.
- **The paper is not self-contained.** Important terminology and derivations rely heavily on the original μP paper [1], making it difficult to follow without external reading. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing theoretical justification for depth and trajectory scaling.** Reviewers 1 and 4 ask why using μP for width also enables generalization to greater depth and longer optimization trajectories, and request a theoretical discussion or clarification.
