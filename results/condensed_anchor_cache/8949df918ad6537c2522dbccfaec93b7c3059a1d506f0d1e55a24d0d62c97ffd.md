## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EGALA, a scalable graph adversarial attack that approximates the gradient of the loss with respect to the adjacency matrix as a matrix product, enabling the use of Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search (ANNS) to efficiently identify entries with dominant gradients. The method is designed for two-layer linear GCNs (SGC) and evaluated in a transfer attack setting. Experiments on large-scale graphs show improved efficiency and competitive attack strength compared to PRBCD and GRBCD.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical contribution**: The derivation of the gradient approximation is convincing, elegant, and provides solid theoretical analysis (R1, R2). The idea is technically sound and novel (R3).
- **Scalability and efficiency**: EGALA does not rely on random candidate edges, instead using ANNS to focus on important edges, which improves efficiency (R1). It imposes minimal computational burden, is memory-saving, and avoids instability of random block sampling (R3). On the large products graph, EGALA is 3.5× faster than PRBCD and 1.5× faster than GRBCD, with ~30% lower memory cost (R1).
- **Attack performance**: In the evaluated transfer setting, EGALA consistently outperforms PRBCD and GRBCD in attack strength, though differences are often small (R1).
- **Implementation and broader applicability**: The method is easy to implement (R3). It addresses an important problem (scalability) and is evaluated on large-scale datasets (R4). The paper is well-written (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Model-specificity and limited applicability**  
   - EGALA is exclusively based on two-layer linear SGC as the surrogate model; it cannot be straightforwardly extended to other GNNs (e.g., GAT, GPRGNN) (R2, R3, R4). This is a critical issue; the authors mention it but do not address it, and without such generality the contribution may not be significant (R4).  
   - The attack is model-specific, making adaptive attacks for other GNNs difficult (R1).  
   - The method is limited to structural perturbations only, further restricting its scope (R3).  
   - In experiments, using SGC as the surrogate for baselines may reduce their attack capability, raising fairness concerns (R3).

2. **Transfer attack setting and lack of adaptive attacks**  
   - The evaluation is only in a grey-box transfer setting (attacking different victim models via an SGC surrogate). Transfer attacks are known to be much weaker than adaptive attacks, and robustness evaluated under transfer attacks gives a false sense of security (R1, R2).  
   - Authors should have prominently placed disclaimers and a comprehensive discussion on the purpose and limitations of such an attack (R1).  
   - Missing comparisons: transfer EGALA should be compared with adaptive versions of PRBCD and GRBCD (R1). Attacks should be conducted on defenses like Jaccard GCN or SVG GCN (R1). Only the scalable defense Soft Median is attacked; other types (e.g., graph purification) should also be considered (R4).  
   - One reviewer (R2) noted that the advantages over PRBCD/GRBCD are not convincing, as PRBCD/GRBCD would provide much stronger adaptive attacks, but no such study is presented.

3. **Insufficient empirical evaluation and ablation studies**  
   - **Ablation components missing**: No ablation on clustering method, number of clusters, number of closest vector pairs, period of cluster update, or Δ_t in the algorithm (R2, R3). These hyperparameters strongly affect accuracy and computational cost.  
   - **Missing comparisons**:  
     - No time-complexity comparison of EGALA vs EGALA-N, despite Table 3 showing identical time/memory for both, which raises concerns about the ANNS advantage (R2).  
     - The cost of EGALA on PubMed is higher than baselines without explanation (R2).  
     - Performance comparison with PGD topology attack on small datasets is absent (R3).  
     - No evaluation of local attacks on larger graphs like Papers100M (R1).  
   - **Gradient validation**: No visualization or comparison of approximated gradients with exact gradients on small graphs to verify that EGALA indeed identifies the largest entries (R1, R4).  
   - The reported time/cost comparison between EGALA and baselines is less convincing because hyperparameters for baselines (e.g., block size, number of clusters) are not discussed or ablated (R2).

4. **Lack of theoretical guarantees on approximation errors**  
   - Multiple approximations are used (Equation 16, nearest neighbor search), but no theoretical analysis of approximation errors is provided, making it unclear whether EGALA reliably identifies the highest-gradient edges (R4).  
   - The necessity of approximating d a_ij / d e_ij for scalability is also questioned (R1).

5. **Questions on asymptotic complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity**  
   - Exact asymptotic complexity is not stated (R1).  
   - How computational cost is affected by hyperparameters (e.g., number of clusters) is unclear (R1).  
   - How hyperparameter tuning was performed across all GNN models is not described (R4).  
   - The impact of mini-batch training on large graphs and its effect on gradient approximation is unclear (R4).

6. **Minor issues**  
   - References from Sec. 3.3 to eq. 11 could be improved (R1).
