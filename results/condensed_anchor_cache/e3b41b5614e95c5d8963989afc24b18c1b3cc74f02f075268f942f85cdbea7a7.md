## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a hybrid online and offline reinforcement learning (RL) framework for variable selection in mixed integer programming (MIP). An online RL agent dynamically selects between an expert (strong branching) and a learned policy for sample generation; an offline “RL” agent fits a value function to filter high-return samples. The approach is iterated and evaluated on several MIP instances against state-of-the-art IL methods, SCIP, and Gurobi.

### Strengths
- **Originality and relevance**: A novel framework that combines IL and RL for MIP branching, addressing an important research direction (Reviewer 1, 3). The improved training time and performance are significant (Reviewer 1).
- **Strong empirical results**: Comprehensive evaluations across multiple MIP problems show the method outperforms state-of-the-art learning-based models, SCIP, and often Gurobi (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). Ablation studies confirm the importance of the proposed subcomponents (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity**: Overall writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, 3). Related work is discussed well (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes presentation issues (see Weaknesses).
- **Technical soundness**: The employment of RL in the branching task is technically sound (Reviewer 3). Baselines appear relevant and appropriate (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation**: The paper does not sufficiently justify why a complex RL approach is needed when strong branching (FSB) is already a good expert and the bottleneck is IL accuracy or input representation, not demonstration quality (Reviewer 3). This concern is not adequately addressed.
- **Complexity and fragility**: RL methods are sensitive to hyperparameters and the deadly triad. The paper does not demonstrate robustness or provide justification for the added complexity; the combined RL-CO pipeline may be fragile in practice (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete specification of the online RL agent**: It is unclear which specific actor-critic algorithm is used (e.g., SAC, DQN, REINFORCE) and how exactly the online agent decides between expert and learned policy (action space, Q-learning type) (Reviewer 1, 2). This hinders reproducibility. Reviewer 1 asks for a concrete explanation, e.g., “two actions, one for expert, one for learned policy”.
- **Offline RL description is misleading**: The “offline RL agent” is actually a filtration mechanism that fits a value function to returns and thresholds trajectories; it is not an offline RL policy (Reviewer 2). Clarification needed.
- **Missing ablations and hyperparameter impact**: The paper does not explore sensitivity of key choices: lambda (Eq. 4), frequency of online training (Freq=5), and trajectory sub-sampling parameter z (Reviewer 2). Without analysis, robustness is unclear.
- **Limited improvement over ML4CO-KIDA**: In Table 7, the gains of HRL-Aug over ML4CO-KIDA appear marginal, raising questions about practical significance (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing wall-clock time comparison**: No wall-clock time comparison between the proposed method and strong branching or baselines is provided (Reviewer 1). This is important for practical applicability.
- **Lack of analysis explaining lower IL accuracy but higher end-to-end performance**: The paper does not explain what multi-step information the RL agent captures that IL misses, which could guide better input design (Reviewer 3).
- **Necessity of online RL training**: Intuition suggests iterative offline data collection and policy update might suffice; the paper does not justify why online updating during data collection is necessary (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues**: Tables are ordered confusingly (e.g., main results in Tables 7/8 appear after ablations in Tables 3/4) (Reviewer 2). Typos: “Framework” capitalized in Section header (p.4), “off-policy” should be “offline” (Section 3.3), “We” incorrectly capitalized in dataset description (Section 4.1), a sentence thanking reviewers appears prematurely (Section 4.4) (Reviewer 2). Also, Table 6 lacks best-method highlighting (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty concerns**: The proposed method may apply only to MIP, and it is unclear how much technical novelty the framework contributes beyond existing work (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Missing details on dual bound handling**: In SCIP, infeasible child nodes receive a dynamic large FSB score; the paper does not specify how this case is handled in the proposed approach (Reviewer 3).
