## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how the sparsity and rank of recurrent connectivity affect robustness in closed-loop control tasks. It theoretically links sparsity and rank at initialization to spectral properties (spectral radius, spectral norm) and empirically studies these effects across RNNs, LSTMs, and closed-form continuous-time networks (CfCs). Experiments on tasks such as Seaquest and HalfCheetah under distribution shifts show that low-rank, sparse CfCs can outperform their full-rank, fully-connected counterparts, suggesting that modulating connectivity induces an interpretable prior beneficial for closed-loop generalization.

### Strengths
- The problem setting (analyzing rank and sparsity of recurrent connectivity for closed-loop robustness) is novel. (R1)
- The theoretical analysis linking sparsity/rank to spectral radius and norm is sound and extends to broader settings. (R2, R4)
- The empirical investigation is thorough, covering many axes (sparsity, rank, architecture, online vs. distribution shift) and combining tools from dynamical systems, computational neuroscience, and deep learning. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The analysis decomposing recurrent-state dynamics into recurrent-driven and input-driven components is novel. (R2)
- The methods are described well enough for reproducibility, and code availability is planned. (R2)
- The paper is well written and the theoretical results are simple and insightful. (R4 – minority reviewer, score 8, contrasts with readability concerns from others)

### Weaknesses
- **Paper readability and clarity of contributions**: Several reviewers found the paper difficult to follow. Section 3 presents the parameterization and theoretical results without connecting to empirical findings; the main takeaways from experiments (e.g., which sparsity/rank/architecture combination is most effective) are not clearly distilled. (R1, R3) The proof of Theorem 4.1 (now Theorem 1) is unclear, especially the statements for rank *r* under both initializations. (R3)
- **Missing baselines**: Standard architectures such as convolutional or fully connected networks (without temporal connections) are not included for the tasks (Seaquest, HalfCheetah). This would help gauge the significance of the robustness gains. (R1, R2)
- **Robustness metrics and validation**: The proxies for robustness (spectral properties, reward ranking) are only partially justified. Missing analyses include: loss as a function of noise level, Lyapunov exponents for dynamics, and assessment of the recurrent Jacobian (not just the connectivity matrix) to compare architectures. (R2, R4) The “vanishing gradient” analysis is a misnomer without studying learning dynamics; Lyapunov exponents would be more appropriate. (R2)
- **Insufficient statistical evidence**: Only 3 random seeds are used per setting, and averaging over 5 perturbations seems insufficient given the high-dimensional task spaces; it is unclear whether favorable sparse masks are ruled out. (R2)
- **Reward ranking and distribution shift analysis**: *Ranking* alone is an unclear proxy; normalized return or expert performance baselines would be more informative. It is not shown what reward decrease each distribution shift causes, or whether all networks perform poorly (making “robustness” claims weak). Performance per perturbation type is missing. Actual average ranks (numbers) would be clearer than color-coded figures. (R2, R3)
- **Theoretical limitations**: Theorem 1 does not cover the case where the recurrent matrix is not fully connected initially, i.e., the influence of sparsity *s* on the spectrum in that context. The last claim of Theorem 1 is only proved for orthogonal initialization. (R2)
- **Figure and analysis details**: 
    - The scale of subplots in Figures 4, 5, 6 is inconsistent. (R3)
    - Figure 5’s use of top-5 PCs’ explained variance is insufficient to compare effective dimensionality across methods. (R3)
    - The meaning of colored regions in Figures 5, 6, 7 is not specified (variance? confidence interval?). (R2)
    - ΔW in Figure 7 is not clearly defined (should be “change in weights during training” rather than ambiguous phrasing). (R2)
    - The paper does not specify which distribution shifts are applied in the experiments. (R4)
- **Conceptual contradictions and open questions**: 
    - Seeming contradiction: Section 4.3 claims low-rank improves robustness, but higher sparsity (which increases effective rank) also improves robustness; this needs clarification. (R2)
    - It is claimed that higher dimensional dynamics lead to robustness (Fig. 16), but networks with higher spectral norm (RNNs) show lower dimensionality; this discrepancy is unexplained. (R2)
- **Parameter count and architecture details**: The number of parameters for each architecture is not reported; CfC has two vanilla RNNs and a gating mechanism, but how *F* is parameterized and how parameter count relates to performance is unclear. Optimizer hyperparameters (e.g., Adam defaults) are not given. (R2)
- **Minor weaknesses**: Reference to echo state networks should cite Jaeger/Maass instead of Goodfellow et al. (R4)
