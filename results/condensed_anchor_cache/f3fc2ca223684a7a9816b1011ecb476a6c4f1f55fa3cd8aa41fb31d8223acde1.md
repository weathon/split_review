## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces KOWCPI, a conformal prediction method for time-series that uses the Reweighted Nadaraya-Watson (RNW) estimator for quantile regression to learn optimal data-adaptive weights, addressing non-exchangeability in time series. The authors provide theoretical conditional coverage guarantees under strong mixing conditions and demonstrate empirically that KOWCPI produces narrower intervals while maintaining coverage on real datasets. Reviewers note the method’s independence from specific time-series models and the optimal beta selection for interval width. However, some find the innovation limited relative to existing methods like SPCI, and several concerns are raised about experimental completeness, sensitivity to parameters, adaptation to non-stationarity, and differentiation from baselines.

### Strengths
1. Provides a conditional coverage guarantee (more meaningful than marginal) with proofs and clearly stated assumptions (R1).
2. The method is independent of specific time series models, using RNW estimator for broad applicability (R2).
3. The optimal beta search (minimizing width while maintaining coverage) yields more efficient intervals (R2).
4. Conditional coverage guarantee only requires strong mixing on residuals, not full stationarity (R2).
5. Strong coverage and efficiency results on intervals from real datasets (R1, R2).
6. Paper is well-written and easy to follow (R3, R4).
7. Problem is important and well-motivated (R3).
8. The algorithm leveraging the RNW estimator is innovative (R4). (Contrast: R2 finds innovation limited; see Weaknesses.)
9. Theory section is correct and well-argued (R4).
10. Experiment comparison is comprehensive, including almost all baselines in the space (R4). (Contrast: R3 thinks baseline list is incomplete; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation issues:** Grammatical errors, dense notation without plain-language accompaniment. Algorithm 1 missing pieces (variables *n*, *w* unexplained; line may need $(X_t, Y_t)$, $t=1,2,\dots,T$). Table 1 confusing: bold only indicates authors’ method, not best performance (R1).
- **Innovation limited:** The approach is a natural integration of conformal prediction with RNW; innovation is not particularly strong, and theoretical results focus on asymptotic accuracy (R2). (Contrast with R4, who finds the algorithm innovative.)
- **Insufficient differentiation from SPCI:** Algorithm and guarantees feel very similar to SPCI; KOWCPI does not significantly outperform SPCI in parts of Figure 2(c). Need explanation of differences in theoretical guarantees, assumptions, and performance to guide method choice (R4).
- **Theoretical assumptions and sensitivity:** Bound heavily depends on weight estimation and strong mixing. Need sensitivity analysis of theoretical bounds and discussion of robustness to violations of the strong mixing condition (R1, R3). Provide diagnostics to assess assumption plausibility (R1). Also consider theoretical guarantees for other dependent structures beyond strong mixing (R3).
- **Issues with strong dependence and large window length *w*:** When dependence is strong, dimension of $\tilde{X}$ (including *w* lags) may be large, making kernel estimates unreliable for limited data; large *w* also leads to low coverage convergence rate. Need experimental results showing coverage and interval width as *w* increases for different sample sizes and datasets with varying temporal dependence (R2). Is it possible to train a function to compress $\tilde{X}$ for dimensionality reduction? (R2) Can a more precise theoretical choice of *w* or *h* be given for ARMA-GARCH models? (R2)
- **Non-stationarity:** Method assumes stationarity; need discussion of extension to local stationarity, and techniques like differencing or detrending. Include experimental results on non-stationary time series (R2, R3).
- **Missing synthetic experiments:** Only real datasets are used without known dependency structures. Synthetic data with varying autocorrelation, heteroscedasticity, and non-stationarity should be added to assess robustness more comprehensively (R3).
- **Incomplete baseline comparison:** Comparison methods should include conformal PID (Angelopoulos et al., 2023), BCI (Yang et al., 2024), and decay-OGD (Angelopoulos et al., 2024) (R3). Provide a table comparing key features of KOWCPI and each baseline method (R3).
- **Missing experimental details and analysis:** Add standard deviations to Table 1; explain rationale for highlighting only the first row, and highlight other methods achieving same coverage with lower width; add experiments for additional $\alpha$ values; provide qualitative examples explaining why KOWCPI confidence bands are better; elaborate on Figure 2(d) with further analysis of weights (R4). Include sensitivity analysis for parameters $T$ (number of past residuals), bandwidth $h$, and window length $w$ (R2, R3). Consider designing an adaptive window length strategy using change point detection or online learning (R3).
- **Potential methodological issues:** Quantile crossing in $\hat{Q}_\beta$? Could both quantile estimates be positive/negative leading to invalid intervals? Are there discontinuities over time in quantile estimators? (R1)
