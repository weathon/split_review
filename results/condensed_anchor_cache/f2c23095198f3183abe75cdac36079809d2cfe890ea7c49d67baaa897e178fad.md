## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multilingual federated prompt tuning paradigm for low-resource languages, combining federated learning (FL) with parameter-efficient prompt tuning to preserve user privacy and improve cross-lingual transfer. Evaluated on the XNLI and NC classification tasks using a single global encoder with aggregated prompts, the method reportedly achieves a 6.9% higher accuracy over local cross-lingual transfer, reduces trainable parameters by over 99% (278M total, ~1.2M trainable), and includes a “language distance” analysis. Reviewer scores are highly divergent: 8 (very positive), 5 (moderately positive), 3 (negative), and 1 (strong negative). Disagreement centers on the novelty of the combination, the meaningfulness of privacy claims, and the adequacy of the evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Practical and efficient method**: The approach is simple, efficient, and appropriate for multilingual scenarios, leveraging parameter-efficient fine-tuning to reduce computational cost and avoid overfitting on small data (reviewer 1, 3, 4). The reduction of trainable parameters by >99% is noted.
- **Well-designed experiments with extensive analysis**: Experiments are convincing with thorough ablation on data efficiency, language distance, and communication cost (reviewer 1, 4). The language distance concept is used to highlight strengths of the paradigm, though some view it as not novel (reviewer 2).
- **Innovative combination of FL, multilingual low-resource LLMs, and PEFT**: The paper mashes these areas into a coherent storyline (reviewer 2). Some reviewers find this combination innovative and a good application of FL (reviewer 3, 4), while others consider it trivial—this contrast is preserved below.
- **Demonstrated performance improvement**: On the NC and XNLI tasks, the method outperforms baselines (reviewer 1, 3). The results show the paradigm bolsters data efficiency and mutual enhancement across languages, especially low-resource ones (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: Multiple reviewers consider the proposed method a trivial combination of existing, established techniques (Federated Learning and Prompt Tuning) with no modification or adjustment (reviewer 1: “further investigation of the multilingual setting”; reviewer 2: “conflating existing metrics with innovation”; reviewer 3: “very trivial combination… no novelty”). One reviewer (3) is emphatic: “fundamental problems with originality, novelty… unrelated existing and non-novel method designed for a different problem.”
- **Privacy claims are unsupported or unwarranted**: The paper claims privacy preservation without any evidence (e.g., whether the model memorizes local data) (reviewer 2, 3). Reviewers note that FL inherently does not guarantee privacy (e.g., transferring prompts, not data, does not imply less information leakage; reviewer 3). The problem of a low-resource community wanting to shut down internet and borders is called “unfounded” (reviewer 3). The justification for using FL for privacy in multilingual fine-tuning is missing (reviewer 2, 3).
- **Conceptual weaknesses**: 
  - The baseline is contrived (no FL) and thus gives the proposed approach an edge (reviewer 2).
  - What the paper calls “prompts” are classifier model inputs, not decoders-style LLM prompts as commonly acknowledged (reviewer 2).
  - The real problem is improving low-resource languages with limited data; the paper does not suggest a better solution than trivial FL + prompt tuning (reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation weaknesses**:
  - Only two tasks (NC and XNLI) are used; both are primarily on high-resource languages, contradicting the title’s “low-resource” focus (reviewer 2, 4). Suggested additional low-resource datasets: MasakhaNEWS, MasakhaNER, MasakhaPOS, IndicNLP tasks (reviewer 4).
  - No comparison with better parameter-efficient methods like LoRA/QLoRA (reviewer 3).
  - Prompt tuning results are much worse than full-federated tuning, casting doubt on the cost-saving trade-off (reviewer 3).
  - No generative or knowledge-based tasks (QA, translation, summarization) are performed (reviewer 3).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Poor citation formatting: citations not separable from main text, e.g., “Abc, et al., 2023” without parentheses; \citep{} should be used (reviewer 2, 3).
  - Abbreviations (NLI, PFL, PLM) not defined in advance (reviewer 2).
  - Missing figure/table references: “As depicted in Figure ,” and “shown in 3” (reviewer 1).
  - Typo: “Finetuning accuracy across different lanugages” (reviewer 1).
  - Grammatical errors: “Throughout the fine-tuning…” (reviewer 3).
  - No code release, making claims unverifiable (reviewer 2).
- **Specific questions that remain unanswered and overlap with criticisms**:
  - Why is multilingual centralized learning far worse than federated learning in Table 2? (reviewer 1)
  - How were training and parameter averaging tuned? (reviewer 1)
  - Which part of the model is fine-tuned (since trainable parameters are ~1.2M out of 278M)? (reviewer 2)
  - Aggregation formula is confusing: is it \(h_{global, t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{m} h_{k, t}\)? The \(t+1\) on the last term seems erroneous (reviewer 4).
- **Other reviewer concerns**: 
  - Author responses to criticism (reviewer 3) were “wordy, unnecessary, filled with politically correct things to avoid the scientific issue” and did not resolve the outlined problems. 
  - The paper claims to be the first in certain aspects, which is inaccurate given prior work (reviewer 3).
