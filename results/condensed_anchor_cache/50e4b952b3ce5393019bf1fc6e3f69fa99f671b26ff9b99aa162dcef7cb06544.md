## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper systematically studies the effect of $\gamma$, the scale of the output layer, on neural network optimization and generalization. Using empirical sweeps across datasets (MNIST, CIFAR, TinyImageNet) and architectures (MLPs, CNNs, Vision Transformers), the authors identify scaling laws between $\gamma$ and the learning rate $\eta$: $\eta \propto \gamma^2$ in the lazy regime ($\gamma \ll 1$) and $\eta \propto \gamma^{2/L}$ in the rich regime ($\gamma \gg 1$) for a feed-forward network of depth $L$. These scalings are confirmed theoretically using a deep linear network with a single univariate sample, for both MSE and cross-entropy loss. Using optimal scaling, the paper explores the ultra-rich regime ($\gamma \gg 1$), observing consistent training dynamics across large $\gamma$ (with a time reparameterization) and finding that optimal online performance often occurs at large $\gamma$, sometimes exceeding the $\gamma=1$ baseline. Various dynamical phenomena (catapult effect, silent alignment, progressive sharpening, step-wise loss drops) are cataloged across the $\gamma$-$\eta$ plane.

### Strengths
- Extensive empirical study across diverse datasets (MNIST, CIFAR, TinyImageNet) and architectures (MLP, CNN, Vision Transformer), with log-spaced sweeps over $\gamma = 10^{-5}$ to $10^5$ and $\eta = 10^{-12}$ to $10^{12}$. (R1, R3, R4)
- Identification of non-trivial scaling laws $\eta \propto \gamma^2$ (lazy) and $\eta \propto \gamma^{2/L}$ (rich), confirmed across models and theoretically derived for deep linear networks with both MSE and cross-entropy loss. Phase portraits from theory (Fig. 6) match empirical sweeps. (R1, R2, R3)
- Observation that for $\gamma \geq 1$, generalization performance is similar or better across different $\gamma$ when the learning rate is appropriately chosen, and that large $\gamma$ can match or exceed $\gamma=1$ performance after sufficient training. (R2, R3)
- Interesting dynamical phenomena reported: catapult effect, silent alignment, progressive sharpening, step-wise loss drops. The connection between silent alignment and edge of stability is noted as worth further investigation. (R1, R2)
- Consistency of training dynamics for large $\gamma$ under a time rescaling. (R3)
- Clear writing, well-organized, and effective figures. (R2, R4)
- Optimal scaling offers practical hyperparameter insights for empirical studies. (R4)
- Originality in investigating the scaling relationship across lazy, rich, and ultra-rich regimes. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Significance concerns**: Two reviewers (R2, R3) question practical applicability, noting that the parameterization (large $\gamma$ with centered model) and constant learning rate are not commonly used in practice, making it unclear how insights translate to real-world training. (R2, R3)
- **Novelty**: R2 argues that many phenomena (lazy training, catapult phase, silent alignment) have been extensively studied, and the ultra-rich regime is not particularly interesting since performance ends up similar to $\gamma=1$. In contrast, R1 sees potential for performance improvement and novel scaling laws. (R2, R1 disagreement)
- **Insufficient evidence of feature learning**: The title emphasizes "Feature Learning," but there is insufficient empirical evidence about *how* feature learning occurs when $\gamma \gg 1$. R4 requests a more granular analysis of weight matrices beyond the final layer (e.g., singular value decomposition of weights in different layers). (R4)
- **Oversimplified theoretical model**: The theoretical framework uses a deep linear network with a constant target. R4 suggests analyzing at least simple single-index models (e.g., $f(x)=\langle a,x\rangle^p$) to better understand feature learning. (R4)
- **Missing experimental details and controls**: 
  - No information is provided on the number of repetitions for each empirical result. (R1)
  - Scaling results in the main text do not always explicitly state whether they apply to MSE or cross-entropy loss, causing confusion (e.g., Fig. 2d shows $\eta\sim\gamma$ for cross-entropy while text claims $\eta\sim\gamma^2$ for lazy networks). (R1)
  - R2 asks how robust the optimal scaling exponent $\gamma^{2/L}$ is for deep nonlinear networks: does performance degrade significantly if the exponent is $3/L$ or $2/(L+1)$? (R2)
  - R2 asks whether the optimal learning rate changes if learning rate decay (commonly used in practice) is included. (R2)
- **Incomplete cross-phenomenon analysis**: R4 asks whether the saddle-point phenomenon observed in Fig. 4(b) (MLP, MNIST) also occurs under the conditions of Fig. 4(a) (CNN, CIFAR) and vice versa, and whether differences in first few layer weights exist across $(\gamma,\eta)$ combinations beyond the final layer shown in Fig. 5. (R4)
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper flow could be improved by presenting key theoretical and empirical results earlier and emphasizing them, rather than leaving theoretical results at the end. Key contribution points are too long and could be hyperlinked to sections/figures. (R1) The paper would benefit from a more detailed mathematical formulation of the network structure and parameterization. (R3)
