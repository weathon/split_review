## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Mix-LN, a hybrid normalization that applies Post-LN to early layers and Pre-LN to deeper layers, aiming to address gradient vanishing in early layers (Post-LN) and diminished gradient norms in deeper layers (Pre-LN) of LLMs. The authors provide theoretical and empirical analysis of gradient norm trends and evaluate Mix-LN on models up to 1B parameters, showing improvements in perplexity and accuracy over Pre-LN and Post-LN in pretraining and supervised finetuning. A minority reviewer (score 8) finds the paper well-organized, insightful, and comprehensive, while the majority of reviewers (scores 5–6) consider the contribution incremental, with limited theoretical grounding and concerns about scalability and practical applicability.

### Strengths
- The observation that Pre-LN leads to diminishing gradient norms in deeper layers and Post-LN causes vanishing gradients in early layers is clear, well-motivated, and supported by both theoretical analysis and experiments (R1,R3,R4,R5).
- Mix-LN is a straightforward solution that can be easily applied to existing models without architectural changes (R5).
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation across multiple model sizes (71M to 1B) and tasks (pretraining and SFT), using diverse metrics such as Angular Distance, Performance Drop, and Gradient Norm (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5).
- Consistent empirical gains, especially in mid-sized models, with improved training stability compared to Post-LN (R2).
- Well-structured presentation with clear diagrams and mathematical formulations (R3,R4).
- Ablation studies and analysis on both open-weight and in-house models provide solid evidence for the method’s effectiveness (R1,R4,R5).
- Realistic and likely correct empirical setup (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical foundation**: The paper lacks rigorous mathematical analysis of Mix-LN’s properties, including formal proofs for convergence, optimal mixing ratio selection, and understanding of the optimization landscape (R2,R4). No theoretical boundaries are provided for vanishing/exploding gradient conditions relevant to Eqs. (3) and (4) (R1). Given the well-explored field, such incremental changes require stronger theoretical backing (R2).
- **Scalability concerns**: Gains from Mix-LN diminish for larger models (e.g., LLaMA-1B), and the improvement over Pre-LN is larger for the smaller 71M model (12 layers) than the deeper 1B model (32 layers), contradicting the claim that Mix-LN particularly benefits deeper models (R1,R2,R5). Experiments are limited to models up to 1B parameters and LLaMA-based architectures, leaving scalability to production-scale models (7B–175B) and generalizability to other architectures untested (R4). Reviewer 5 suggests testing with slim deep architectures if larger models are infeasible.
- **Insufficient comparison with recent normalization techniques**: No direct comparison with other normalization methods that address deep-layer inefficiencies, such as Admin, Sandwich LN, or group normalization (R1,R2). Without these, it is difficult to assert Mix-LN’s advantage over other innovations (R2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The success of Mix-LN heavily depends on the mixing ratio α; for example, on LLaMA-1B BoolQ, only Pre-LN performs better (R1). The paper does not thoroughly explore α sensitivity, and the optimal α likely varies across architectures and scales, with no clear methodology for selecting it (R2,R5). This limits practical usability.
- **Limited applicability**: Mix-LN is evaluated only on LLMs; its effectiveness on non-language tasks (e.g., image classification with Transformers on ImageNet-1k) is not tested (R1,R2).
- **Incremental innovation**: The proposed hybrid normalization is a straightforward combination of existing techniques rather than a fundamental advancement in normalization methodology (R4).
- **Missing metrics and clarity**: The paper does not report performance drop when using Mix-LN (R1). Section 4 lacks an introduction for perplexity (R3). No mention of computational overhead during training or inference (R2).
- **Figure and presentation issues**: Figures of angular distance are non-intuitive; the term “block size” is confusing and only described at line 402–403 (R3). Typo conflict: Figure 3-c (main body) vs. Figure 3-e (caption) (R3). Figure 4 a–b shows seemingly opposite results compared to Figures 2/3, requiring clarification (R3). Reviewer 3 also questions the triangle-like yellow areas in Figure 2-a and the dark-blue line in Figure 2-b.
- **Missing asymptotic comparison**: It is unclear how Mix-LN’s final performance compares to Post-LN when both are trained to complete convergence under the same hyperparameters; a controlled study would distinguish whether benefits extend beyond computational efficiency (R4).
