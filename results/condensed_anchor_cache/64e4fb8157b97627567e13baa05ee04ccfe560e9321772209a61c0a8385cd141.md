## Merged Review

### Summary
This work extends deep kernel machines (DKMs) to convolutional architectures by introducing convolutional kernels and inter-domain inducing points on image patches, building on the DKM framework of Yang et al. (2023). The model trains in ~77 GPU hours and achieves strong performance on standard benchmarks (e.g., 92% on CIFAR-10, with further results on MNIST and CIFAR-100 reported in the abstract), outperforming previous convolutional GP methods and NNGP/NTKs. The main technical novelty lies in the efficient sparse convolutional DKM formulation, including a novel inter-domain inducing point approximation. The paper includes a comprehensive ablation study of architectures and hyperparameters.

### Strengths
- Strong performance on classification benchmarks: 92% test accuracy on CIFAR-10, which is impressive for a kernel-based method (Reviewers 1,2).
- The inter-domain inducing point approach is novel, well-suited to the convolutional setting, and computationally efficient (Reviewers 1,2,4).  
- The technical development (model formulation, training algorithm) is sound and sensible (Reviewer 1).
- Comprehensive study of different architectures, hyperparameters, and design choices (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is clearly written and well organized (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 also found the content clearly presented relative to the title.
- One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 8) was notably more positive, highlighting the novelty of the inducing point method and the thorough experimental evaluation, while other reviewers (scores 5) acknowledged the soundness but found the contribution more incremental.

### Weaknesses
- The contribution is seen as incremental by some reviewers: the method is a combination of existing DKMs with inter-domain inducing points from convolutional (D)GPs, without a fundamentally new theoretical insight (Reviewer 1).  
- The derivation of the objective as the ELBO for an infinite-width DGP is mentioned but not proved; the first principle underlying the NNGP-prior regularized objective remains unclear (Reviewer 1).  
- The transition from finite-width feature calculations to the infinite-width kernel limit is not rigorously justified. The kernel in Section 4 is computed with a finite number of channels (N_l), which appears to contradict the infinite-width assumption, and the specific steps to take the limit are not transparent (Reviewer 4).  
- The definition of the inducing-point variable C^ℓ (Eq. 18) is confusing and unintuitive: it sums over all inducing points from the previous layer, which is not part of any standard convolution. This design choice is not adequately motivated or explained (Reviewer 2).  
- It is not clearly established whether the convolutional kernel matrix K_Conv is positive-definite under the learnable parameterization using C^ℓ (or at least a priori). More explanation is needed beyond the claim that it is a covariance of a random vector (Reviewer 4).  
- The experimental evaluation is limited to accuracy and negative log-likelihood. A key expected benefit of Bayesian/GP models is uncertainty quantification (calibration, out-of-distribution detection), which is not evaluated. Demonstrating these properties would strengthen the paper (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
- The paper does not discuss how the method contributes to explaining the learning mechanism of Bayesian convolutional NNs, which is a primary motivation for kernel-based analyses (e.g., NNGP, NTK) (Reviewer 4).  
- The practical value and classical kernel benefits (interpretation via RKHS, primal-dual representation, analytical results) are not articulated. The paper does not explore alternative architectures (e.g., simpler than ResNet-20) or show flexibility in architecture design (Reviewer 3).  
- The related work section is incomplete: it omits other lines of deep kernel machines (e.g., Mairal et al., Suykens et al.), which could help distinguish the unique contributions of the proposed method (Reviewer 3).  
- Writing quality needs improvement: some sentences are disjoint, and there are typos (e.g., the second paragraph under Table 3) (Reviewers 2,3).  
- Comparisons in Table 3 are only on CIFAR-10, not CIFAR-100; this limits the completeness of the benchmark evaluation (Reviewer 4).  
- The ablation study in Table 1 is extensive but many variations yield similar performance, making it difficult to extract the most important design choices. A clear default recommended setting would be beneficial (Reviewer 3).  
- The empirical effect of varying the number of inducing points (Table 2) is not discussed in terms of efficiency or accuracy trade-offs (Reviewer 3).  
- The choice of nonlinear activation functions in the provided code is not clearly described or justified (Reviewer 3).  
- Some reviewers noted that comparison against a ResNet with the same number of parameters would help assess whether the DKM regularization provides an advantage over a standard neural network of similar capacity (Reviewer 1).  
- Reviewer 4 observes that the scope is somewhat limited, as kernel methods are primarily valued for explaining learning dynamics rather than achieving SOTA performance.
