## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies high-probability convergence for composite and distributed stochastic minimization and variational inequalities under heavy-tailed noise with bounded central α-th moment. It proposes new clipping methods (Prox-clipped-SGD-shift, Prox-clipped-SSTM-shift) that clip the difference between stochastic gradients and adaptively updated shift vectors, instead of clipping gradients directly. Similar ideas are applied to variational inequalities. The paper provides convergence guarantees for convex, quasi-strongly convex, and monotone settings, and includes a comparison table with prior work. All three reviewers rate the paper 5 (weak reject), citing limited technical novelty and presentation concerns.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and well-organized** (R1, R3). The problem setting is clearly described, and the paper is technically sound with proof sketches (R3).
- **Comprehensive coverage** of multiple problem templates (convex/quasi-strongly convex, composite, distributed) and variational inequalities under different assumptions (star-cocoercivity, quasi-strong monotonicity, monotonicity) (R2).
- **Important problem setting**: heavy-tailed noise and high-probability guarantees are relevant for theory and practice (R2).
- **Motivation for new technique** is well-illustrated by first presenting naïve or non-implementable approaches (R2).
- **Comparison table** helps put contributions in context with existing works (R2).
- **Proposed methods** (Prox-clipped-SGD-shift, Prox-clipped-SSTM-shift, and extensions to VI) are clearly identified (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution** (R1, R2, R3). The paper combines gradient clipping (a widely used technique for α-th moment noise) with existing tools for composite/distributed problems and VI, without major innovation. One reviewer notes that after establishing martingale difference sequences, Freedman’s inequality suffices for high-probability bounds, making the analysis routine (R1).
- **Paper is excessively long** (115 pages), making it unsuitable for a conference review cycle (R1, R2). Both reviewers suggest splitting into shorter papers or an arXiv version.
- **Presentation is overly complex** (R2). While the paper attempts to be comprehensive, the large number of variants and theorems (e.g., Theorem 2.2) is difficult to unpack. Simpler results and a clearer explanation of how clipping differences helps the analysis would improve readability. The proof organization (e.g., Theorem D.1 with a 9-page proof) is not reader-friendly.
- **Confusing comparison with Nguyen et al. (2023)** (R2). Several points need clarification:
  - The table uses $R^2$ vs. $V$; the paper states $R^2 \leq V$, suggesting Nguyen et al.’s constant may be better. This should be stated explicitly.
  - Nguyen et al. do not assume a unique solution, while this paper does (footnote p.3 says “for simplicity”). Whether the uniqueness assumption can be removed and what effect that would have is unclear.
  - For constrained settings, Nguyen et al. require $\nabla f(x^*)=0$; this paper assumes unique solution. It is unclear which is more restrictive, and both are rather restrictive in the convex case.
  - The paper’s bound (page 24) has an additional $\log T$ factor from a union bound, leading to worse dependence on the probability parameter compared to Nguyen et al. Clarification on the log dependencies is needed.
- **No experiments** (R3). The paper introduces new optimization methods but does not validate them empirically against relevant baselines for either optimization or VI problems.
- **Lack of explanation for distributed extension** (R2). The paper claims to handle distributed composite optimization, but does not clearly explain why previous works (e.g., Nguyen et al. 2023) could not be straightforwardly extended, nor what new tool enables this paper’s success.
- **Motivation for combining optimization and VI is weak** (R3). The two problems appear independent and the contribution seems incremental.
- **Missing discussion of bounds in context** (R3). A comparison of the derived bounds with existing clipping bounds (e.g., after each theorem) would enhance significance.
- **Assumption 1 should specify** that eqs. (4) and (5) hold for all $i \in [n]$ (R3).
