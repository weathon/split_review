## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Dist Loss, a new loss function for imbalanced regression that minimizes the distribution distance between predictions and target labels using kernel density estimation and pseudo-labels. It is designed to improve performance in few-shot (low-density) regions. Evaluations on IMDB-WIKI-DIR, AgeDB-DIR, and ECG-Ka-DIR image datasets show few-shot improvements, and the loss is compatible with existing methods. Reviewers appreciate the problem’s importance and the novel idea, but raise concerns about presentation clarity, experimental comparisons, performance gains, and generalizability to non-image data.

### Strengths
1. **Novel approach for imbalanced regression**: Dist Loss is a new loss function specifically designed to align prediction and label distributions, targeting the under-studied problem of imbalanced regression (Reviewer 2). The idea of using pseudo-labels and sorting techniques to make the distribution distance trainable is interesting (Reviewer 2).

2. **Easy integration**: The loss is easy to incorporate with existing techniques and enhances their performance in few-shot areas, making it a practical addition to current approaches (Reviewer 1). It works both as a standalone loss and combined with existing losses (Reviewer 2).

3. **Evaluated on multiple datasets**: Experiments cover three datasets from computer vision and healthcare: IMDB-WIKI-DIR, AgeDB-DIR, and ECG-Ka-DIR (Reviewer 1, 3).

4. **Good performance and efficiency**: The method shows significant improvements over baselines in some settings (Reviewer 2). Running time is as fast as mean squared error and significantly faster than other baselines (Reviewer 2; note: Reviewer 1 reports Balanced MSE is faster in Table 3—minority disagreement preserved).

5. **Clear illustrations**: The paper is clearly written with elucidatory illustrations for easier understanding (Reviewer 3; note: Reviewer 2 found the presentation lacking details—minority contrast preserved).

### Weaknesses
1. **Discretization contradicts claims about classification methods**: The loss discretizes continuous labels into fixed bins (Figure 1), which seems to make classification-based imbalanced methods applicable. The authors claim in Section 2.2 that such methods cannot be well adapted to regression, but this is not sufficiently explained given the discretization. More justification is needed (Reviewer 1, Questions 1).

2. **Limited performance gain over Balanced MSE**: In Table I, Dist Loss underperforms Balanced MSE on IMDB-WIKI-DIR using the GM metric, and the overall improvement over Balanced MSE is not promising. The authors should explain why Dist Loss struggles in these cases and where it provides advantages (Reviewer 1, Weakness 2 and Question 2).

3. **Missing comparisons with recent related works**: The paper does not compare with Wang & Wang (2024, Variational imbalanced regression) and Keramati et al. (2023, ConR). For AgeDB DIR in the few-shot setting, ConR combined with LDS achieves MAE of 9.62 and 6.87, but Dist Loss appears to underperform. The authors should also compare with all existing works in Table 4 (Reviewer 1, Weaknesses 3 and 5, Questions 3 and 6). Additionally, results for *all*, *many*, *medium*, and *few* shot settings should be reported (as done in those works), not just few-shot, to assess generalizability (Reviewer 1, Weakness 3).

4. **Reproducibility and experimental settings**: The reported performance of ConR differs from its original paper on AgeDB DIR. The authors must clarify whether they follow the same experimental settings as ConR and other works, and explain any differences that might impact results (Reviewer 1, Weakness 3, Question 4).

5. **Training time efficiency trade-off**: Table 3 shows Balanced MSE is more efficient in training time than Dist Loss. The authors should discuss the trade-off between time cost and performance gain, and highlight the practical advantages of using Dist Loss despite this cost (Reviewer 1, Weakness 4, Question 5).

6. **Lack of detail in methodology and presentation (Reviewer 2)**:
   - Figure 2: The roles of “Labels” and “Predictions” inside Dist Loss calculation are unclear when the loss already receives Pseudo-labels and Sorted Prediction (Weakness i).
   - Section 3.2.1 jumps into computing pseudo-labels/predictions without explaining *why* pseudo-labels are needed to address imbalance or how KDE is applied (batch vs. whole dataset). No background on KDE is provided (Weakness ii).
   - The standard regression formulation in Section 3.1 discretizes labels into B bins, but it is unclear how the model is trained with these discretized labels (Weakness ii).
   - Equations (1) and (2) and the definition of \(N_{L'}\) are hard to follow without an illustrative figure (Weakness iii).
   - Overall, the methodology section is not well presented, with information presented sporadically (Reviewer 2).

7. **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper provides no theoretical analysis of the algorithm (Reviewer 3).

8. **Limited evaluation scope**: All datasets are image datasets and all models are ResNet-like. The method has not been evaluated on text data or tabular data (Reviewer 3). Also, the imbalance rates are fixed; the paper could manually tweak data to generate scenarios from mildly to extremely heavy-tailed distributions to investigate robustness (Reviewer 3).

9. **Missing evaluation metrics**: The discretization effectively transforms the regression problem into a classification problem. The authors should report classification metrics (overall accuracy, few-shot accuracy) and correlation metrics (Pearson’s or Spearman’s) alongside regression metrics, as well as actual-by-predicted correlation plots (Reviewer 3, Question).

10. **Lack of method details (Reviewer 2, 3 questions)**:
    - The exact form of the loss function \(L(\cdot)\) in Figure 2 is not clear from text or figure; for sequences [1,3,3,4,4,4,6] and [1,2,2,3,5,6,7], how is the loss computed? (Reviewer 3)
    - How is the number of bins \(B\) determined? (Reviewer 3)
    - What is *probability-based inversely* (INV) in the ablation study (Table 4), and why do INV-L1 and INV-L2 show benefits? (Reviewer 2, Question c)
    - How does training remain stable and avoid exploding gradients given that Dist Loss works with discrete sorted orders and potentially large values? (Reviewer 2, Question d)
    - Is KDE used only during training or also during testing? What is its input? (Reviewer 2, Question a)
