## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Equivariant Masked Position Prediction (EMPP), a self-supervised learning approach for molecular GNNs. Instead of masking atom attributes or denoising with Gaussian mixtures, EMPP masks 3D atom positions and predicts distributions over distances and angles to neighboring atoms. EMPP can be used both as a pre-training task and as an auxiliary loss during supervised training. Evaluations on QM9 and MD17 show improvements over baseline models. One reviewer was markedly more positive, emphasizing the originality and significance of the method; the other three reviewers were more reserved, raising concerns about methodological clarity, fairness of comparisons, computational overhead, and whether the claimed advantages over denoising methods are fully substantiated.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of prediction target**: EMPP predicts distributions over relative atomic positions (distance and angle) rather than directly regressing coordinates or denoising with a Gaussian mixture. This is a clean conceptual evolution over prior attribute masking and denoising approaches. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Strong empirical results**: EMPP achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on QM9 and MD17 benchmarks, both as a pre-training task and as an auxiliary loss. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Versatility**: The method works both as a standalone pre-training objective and as an auxiliary task during supervised learning, increasing its potential utility. (R1)
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-organized, with clear motivation and explanations of the method. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Code provided**: Anonymous code is released to support reproducibility. (R4)
- **Simple yet effective**: Despite its novelty, the approach is conceptually simple and gets state-of-the-art results. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparisons in Section 5.2**: When EMPP is used as an auxiliary task, it relies on the Equiformer backbone, but it is compared against other architectures that lack this backbone. This confounds architectural improvements with the EMPP objective. (R2, R3)
- **Unclear pre-training generalization**: On some QM9 properties (HOMO, LUMO, G, H), EMPP pre-training leads to performance declines compared to the auxiliary-task setting (Table 1 vs. Table 3). The paper does not analyze whether this stems from the pre-training task, the number of pre-training epochs, or the choice of backbone. (R1)
- **Limited benchmark scope**: QM9 and MD17 consist of small, simple molecules. The paper does not evaluate on larger or more complex systems (e.g., proteins, drug-like molecules), leaving doubts about generalization to realistic scenarios. (R1)
- **Modest gains on MD17**: On MD17, EMPP’s improvements over the baseline Equiformer are relatively small, raising questions about whether the added complexity is justified. (R3)
- **Computational overhead not addressed**: EMPP requires computing distributions over a spherical grid (100² points) for each masked atom. No analysis of training time, memory, or comparison to simpler methods is provided. (R3). Also missing empirical results on the effect of averaging over multiple masked atoms (Eq. 8) on efficiency and possible trade-offs between performance and cost. (R2)
- **Inconsistent and irrelevant content**: The paper begins with a discussion of large language models, which is irrelevant since EMPP is not related to sequence or SMILES language models. The main ablation study (Section 5.4) studies denoising baselines, not the proposed method; the actual ablation for EMPP is relegated to Appendix C. The pre-training objective is presented inconsistently: Figure 1 and Section 3.2.1 suggest a force-prediction network, while Figure 2 and Section 3.2.2 regress the displacement vector \( r_{ij} \). The end-to-end training objective for QM9/MD17 (energy and force prediction) is not clearly specified; it is unclear whether the masking auxiliary loss is used during fine-tuning. (R4)
- **Gaussian mixture issue claim is questionable**: The paper claims EMPP avoids Gaussian mixture approximations, but the loss function (Eq. 14) relies on a Gaussian assumption for the KL divergence, and the direction loss (Eq. 16) uses a softmax-based form. The only difference is that the Gaussian is defined over radii and angles rather than Euclidean coordinates. Moreover, iterative denoising (diffusion models) or invariant noise-scale prediction can handle the variable-variance landscape, making the claimed distinction unclear. (R4)
- **Lack of innovation in force prediction**: The idea that predicting positions is equivalent to learning the potential energy surface and force field has been well established in prior work (references given). The paper does not clearly differentiate its “force prediction” framing from existing denoising pre-training. (R4)
- **Inconsistent pre-training target across neighboring atoms**: The ground truth for a masked atom’s position is expressed relative to multiple neighboring atoms. Since the distance and angle distributions are constructed independently for each neighbor, they are inconsistent unless the variance is zero (i.e., a Dirac delta). The paper does not address this inconsistency. (R4)
- **Missing ablations on key parameters**: The impact of the temperature \(\tau\) in Eq. 13 and the variance \(\sigma\) in Eq. 14 on performance is not tested, despite these parameters controlling the sharpness of the target distributions. (R4)
- **Missing ablation on separate distance/angle modeling**: The paper attributes improved performance partly to modeling distance and angle separately, but does not provide an ablation comparing this to directly predicting the displacement vector in Euclidean coordinates. (R1)
- **Missing baseline with modified Equiformer**: Without applying the same architectural modifications used in EMPP to the baseline Equiformer (from TorchMD-Net), it is impossible to isolate the improvement due to the masking task. (R3; note: the citation of the Equiformer architecture is also wrong)
- **Sensitivity to spherical grid sampling**: The effect of the number of sampling points on performance and computational cost is not examined. (R3)
