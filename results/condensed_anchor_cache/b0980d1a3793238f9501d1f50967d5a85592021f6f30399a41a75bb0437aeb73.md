## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents DISCO, a diffusion-based solver for large-scale combinatorial optimization (CO) problems, including Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) and Maximal Independent Set (MIS). DISCO constrains the sampling space using solution residues and employs an analytically solvable denoising process to accelerate inference. Experiments on TSP-10000 and MIS benchmarks show strong solution quality and speed, with generalization via a divide-and-conquer strategy. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and the approach effective, but raise concerns about novelty, insufficient analysis of key components, and unaddressed questions about the graph-splitting method and baseline comparisons. One reviewer (score 5) views the contribution as incremental and the performance improvement over DIFUSCO as marginal, while others (score 6) find the method solid but note limitations.

### Strengths
- DISCO significantly reduces inference time via analytically solvable denoising and constrained sampling space (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The paper is well-structured, clear, and easy to read (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on large-scale TSP-10000 instances and challenging MIS benchmarks, demonstrating superior solution quality and speed (Reviewer 2).
- The divide-and-conquer strategy generalizes DISCO to solve large-scale instances off the shelf, sometimes surpassing models trained specifically for those scales (Reviewer 2).
- Empirically extended experiments include: illustration of generalization ability, comparison of time consumption and computational workload, and addition of strong baseline T2T (NeurIPS 2023) for comparison (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of using a feasible solution as residue constraint to guide generation is novel and reasonable (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results show DISCO is efficient and effective on large TSP problems (Reviewer 3).
- Proposed method demonstrates improvements on TSP and MIS (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The approach appears incremental. Solution residue is from Residual Denoising Diffusion Models (Liu et al., CVPR 2024) and multi-modal search is from Fu et al. (AAAI 2021); prior work on CO has explored conditioning on initial solutions. The technical methodology closely follows conventional DDPM and the fast sampling process is similar to existing DDM techniques with limited innovation beyond adding residues. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Insufficient analysis of multi-modal graph search:** The contribution of this module is not thoroughly analyzed. Experiments should include performance metrics with and without the module and detail the computational overhead. The claim of multi-modal property enhancing solution quality is unclear without additional evidence. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Unclear why residue term leads to better solutions:** Figure 1 only provides intuition; more supporting analysis and evidence are needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Graph-splitting risks for global problems:** TSP and MIS are inherently global; how does the graph-splitting approach avoid sub-optimal outcomes? Clarification needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Scalability concerns:** Graph search may lead to exponential growth in trial variance; scalability might remain an issue, as noted by the authors in limitations. (Reviewer 2)
- **DIFUSCO baseline with DDM sampler:** What is the performance (solution quality and sampling time) of the DIFUSCO baseline when using DDM as sampler? This comparison is missing. (Reviewer 4)
- **Performance improvement over DIFUSCO is marginal.** (Reviewer 4) [Note: minority view]
- **MCTS vs. sampling:** Authors select sampling over MCTS for testing, citing that MCTS relies on TSP-specific heuristics (Fu et al., 2021) and is less suited to other problem types. However, Table 1 considers only TSP, making this choice questionable. (Reviewer 3)
- **Role of degraded solution X_d:** X_d is obtained by connecting vertices sequentially to form a tour; this may have a huge impact on solutions. How is the correct search direction ensured when X_d differs between training and testing? (Reviewer 3)
- **Unaddressed scale generalization:** Why are TSP-5000, 8000, and 10000 chosen? Does the method work on smaller scales like TSP-100, 500, 1000? (Reviewer 3)
- **Inconsistent baseline performance:** In Table 3, ATT-GCN performs very poorly on TSP-5000 and 8000 but much better and faster on TSP-10000. Explanation is needed. (Reviewer 3)
