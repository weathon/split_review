## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LC-PLM, a protein language model based on a bidirectional Mamba-style architecture (BiMamba-S) with shared projection layers, and LC-PLM-G, a graph-contextual variant that incorporates protein-protein interaction (PPI) graphs via tokenized random walks. The authors compare against ESM-2 and report improvements on several downstream tasks. Reviewers are split: one reviewer (score 8) finds the topic relevant, the empirical results strong, and the graph integration innovative, noting the paper is well-written. The other three reviewers (scores 3) are critical, arguing that the approach largely overlaps with existing work (ProtMamba), that comparisons to relevant baselines and SOTA methods are missing, and that key claims about long-context necessity and Mamba’s advantages are unsupported. The graph-contextual extension is seen as ad-hoc and not clearly beneficial. The minority positive reviewer acknowledges the paper’s merits but the majority see insufficient novelty, weak justification, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and relevant topic (long-context protein language modeling).
- Empirical results show that LC-PLM outperforms ESM-2 on the pre-training MLM objective and on several downstream tasks, with reported improvements of 7% to 34%.
- Experiments cover a wide range of model sizes (130M to 1400M parameters) and diverse downstream tasks (e.g., contact prediction, structure prediction, ProteinGym, TAPE).
- The introduction of LC-PLM-G, which integrates PPI graph information via tokenized random walks with EDGE and NO_EDGE tokens, is seen by some as a novel direction for incorporating relational information between proteins.
- The paper is generally well-written and presents the material clearly.

### Weaknesses
- **Little novelty and missing baseline comparisons**:
  * Protein modeling with the Mamba backbone was already proposed in ProtMamba [1]; bidirectional Mamba blocks with parameter sharing have been done previously [2].
  * The authors do not compare to ProtMamba [1] or discuss differences/benefits, nor do they compare to other Transformer-based architectures beyond ESM-2 (e.g., flash-attention variants). For experiments such as ProteinGym (Table 4), true state-of-the-art methods and performances are missing.
  * It is unclear whether gains come from increased context, graph pretraining, architecture, or other components; the paper only compares LC-PLM/LC-PLM-G to ESM-2, not to a Mamba-based model without long context or to a Transformer with comparable context.
- **Weak justification for SSMs over Transformers**:
  * Protein sequence modeling does not face the latency/bandwidth constraints of real-time LLMs; the quadratic complexity argument is weakened by efficient Transformers (e.g., flash attention). No concrete examples are provided where Mamba succeeds where Transformers fail in protein tasks.
  * The claim that long contexts are inherently beneficial lacks biological or empirical evidence—functional motifs often reside locally, and the benefit of extremely long contexts for protein understanding is not demonstrated.
- **Preliminary or unsupported claims**:
  * “Such protein LMs cannot extrapolate to longer proteins” is empirically and theoretically unsupported; Transformer-based pLMs can handle long contexts (e.g., 16k).
  * “Universal amino acid token-level representation” is undefined and unsupported.
  * The paper assumes long context is essential without clear empirical rationale (e.g., missing to show that extended context length improves downstream tasks beyond presented results).
- **Graph-contextual modeling (LC-PLM-G) is ad-hoc and shows limited benefit**:
  * Forcing graph structure into tokenized text to apply a language model seems counterintuitive compared to using a GNN directly.
  * The homology-based context assembly used in ProtMamba is better justified.
  * In Table 3 (TAPE benchmarks) and Table 4 (ProteinGym), LC-PLM-G vs. LC-PLM and ESM-2-G vs. ESM-2 show improvements within standard deviation or even degradations (e.g., ESM-2-G hurt performance), weakening the necessity claim for multi-sequence PPI contexts.
- **Technical errors and missing details**:
  * Figure 2 has a mistake regarding the orientation of the third linear projection block.
  * Table 1 and Table 3 show one digit too many.
  * Line 101 fails to cite Hochreiter (1991) for vanishing gradients.
  * In Figure 6, using “value × 2^1” as ticks looks strange.
- **Missing experiments and clarifications**:
  * TAPE evaluation for Contact Prediction, Fluorescence, and Stability are not reported.
  * Criteria for “universal” in Table 1 are not specified (e.g., when is a method considered universal).
  * The context size and hyperparameters used for ESM-2 are not reported; it is unclear if ESM-2’s context could have been increased to match memory use.
  * For RQ3 (UniRef50 training, UniRef90 evaluation): why this mismatch?
  * In Figure 7, the criterion for “LC-PLM-G captures graph topology much better” is not defined.
  * The indication that long-range dependencies benefit structure prediction (lines 439-440) is not clearly supported.
  * For the folding test (RQ4), how were structures obtained from ESM-2? Alignment of folding trunk and distribution differences are not addressed; downsampling to 1.5% may cause high variance; robustness and error bars are missing.
  * RQ1: how were the 250k test sequences held out? Was ESM-2 retrained for Figure 4? What exactly is “Evaluation Loss” on the y-axis?
  * The claim “7% to 34% improvement” should specify which task corresponds to 7% and which to 34%.
  * The authors should state clearly which models were trained in this work and which were taken as pretrained versions.
