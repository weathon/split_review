## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EquiPocket, an E(3)-equivariant GNN for ligand binding site prediction. It consists of three modules: local geometric modeling, global structure modeling (chemical and spatial graphs), and surface message passing (surface-EGNN). A dense attention output layer aggregates features across layers to handle variable protein size. Experiments show strong empirical performance against existing methods. Reviewers agree the paper is well-written and the method addresses known limitations, but raise concerns about missing comparisons, limited novelty, and unclear problem framing.

### Strengths
- The method addresses several limitations of prior approaches (voxelization, rotation sensitivity, surface characterization, protein size shift) and demonstrates clear superior empirical performance on benchmarks (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Modeling surface features via surface graphs and adapted EGNN is effective and insightful (Reviewer 1).
- The dense attention output layer for variable protein size is well-motivated and validated via ablation studies (Reviewer 1, 4 appreciated the discussion of protein size shift).  
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; code is provided (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- The idea of using an E(3)-equivariant GNN for binding site prediction is interesting for the bioinformatics domain (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unawareness of ligand structure**: The method does not consider the properties or structure of the ligand molecule. Binding sites are known to depend on the ligand, and there is a significant correlation with small molecule properties, not just spatial relationships (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Missing baselines**: The authors did not compare against P2rank (widely used, e.g., in TANKBIND, E3BIND) (Reviewer 1), ScanNet (2022, Nature Methods) (Reviewer 3), or protein-ligand docking methods that also predict binding sites (Reviewer 4). These omissions may diminish the claimed superiority.  
- **Limited significance vs. related tasks**: Reviewer 2 notes that given many existing ligand docking and structure-based drug design models, the significance of this standalone prediction task is limited; showing downstream task improvement would be beneficial. However, Reviewer 3 found the method not lacking significance, noting it is “relative better than previous methods, which is not lack of significance.”
- **Missing ablation**: Ablation of the relative direction prediction loss is needed (Reviewer 2). A more informative ablation would independently remove the local (L) and global (R) feature extractors (i.e., EquiPocket/L, EquiPocket/R, EquiPocket/LR) (Reviewer 4).
- **Dense attention role**: Reviewer 1 found the dense attention module crucial per ablation, but Reviewer 3 states its role in reducing the negative impact of protein size shift is limited.  
- **Novelty concerns**: E(3)-equivariant GNNs have already been applied to very related tasks (e.g., protein-ligand docking), diminishing the novelty of introducing them here (Reviewer 4). (Reviewer 3 considered the idea “of interest.”)  
- **Unclear problem setting**: Proteins can have multiple binding sites and can bind to different ligands. How are ground-truth labels computed, and does one protein with multiple label sets affect training/prediction? (Reviewer 2, 3).  
- **Clarity needed for baseline graphs**: For baseline EGNN and SchNet, what kind of graph is used? Are surface atom graphs used? (Reviewer 4).  
- **Inference speed**: Message passing on the full protein atom graph may be slow; a comparison of inference speed across methods is missing (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minor questions**: Rationale behind swapping MLP and pooling order in Eq.3 (Reviewer 1); adaptability to protein-protein binding site prediction (Reviewer 1).
