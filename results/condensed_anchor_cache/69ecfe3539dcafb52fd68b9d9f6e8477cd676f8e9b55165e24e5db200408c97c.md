## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DynSDPB, a model-agnostic and task-agnostic self-distillation method for fine-tuning language models. It uses soft labels (logits) from the previous mini-batch to supervise the current batch (inspired by the DLB method from CV), dynamically adjusts the temperature and the CE/KL scale, and introduces a vocabulary map matching step for decoder-only models. Experiments on encoder-only (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa) and decoder-only (LLaMA) models across NLU and NLG tasks show improved performance over standard fine-tuning and several KD baselines.

### Strengths
1. Self-distillation in NLP remains under-explored; this work presents a low-cost alternative to teacher-based knowledge distillation, which requires large or commercial teachers. (R1)
2. The dynamic adaptation of distillation influence and temperature is a reasonable addition to handle early-stage prediction inaccuracies. (R1, R2)
3. The method can be integrated as a plug-in technique with existing self-correction and self-training methods. (R2)
4. Empirical results demonstrate effectiveness on a wide range of tasks (NLU and NLG) and model families (encoder-only and decoder-only). (R1, R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty.** The core idea of self-distillation from the previous mini-batch is taken from the CVPR’22 DLB method. The only addition is dynamic adaptation of hyper-parameters (temperature and CE/KL scale), which makes the contribution incremental. (R1, R2, R3)  
   – Claiming “first to propose” is inappropriate; at most the technique is applied to NLP for the first time. (R3)
2. **Lack of analysis on the dynamic hyper-parameters.** There is no explanation of how temperature and scale evolve during training or why this dynamic adjustment yields improvement. (R1)  
   – Similarly, missing analysis of training dynamics: only final numbers are shown; the paper should illustrate how the model gradually improves through self-distillation. (R3)
3. **Missing critical comparisons.**  
   – For decoder-only models, no comparison is made to recent soft-label based distillation methods. (R1, R2)  
   – The paper should compare with a baseline that applies self-distillation only after an initial stable training phase (delayed self-distillation) to justify the early-stage dynamic adjustments and discuss the trade-off. (R2)
4. **Experimental setup concerns.**  
   – For encoder-only NLU tasks, full fine-tuning on smaller models (1B/3B) should be used instead of LoRA, because LoRA uses fewer compute and makes any advantage of the proposed method less meaningful. (R1)  
   – Several baseline results (RoBERTa, DeBERTa) are lower than those reported in the original papers; e.g., RoBERTa_base originally achieves 87.6 MNLI, 92.8 QNLI, 91.9 QQP, 78.7 RTE, 94.8 SST, 90.2 MRPC, 63.6 CoLA; DeBERTa_large achieves 91.1 MNLI, 95.3 QNLI, etc. The discrepancies must be explained. (R2)  
   – The paper only evaluates on classification tasks; it should discuss how to adapt the method to regression tasks in NLU (e.g., STS-B). (R2)
5. **Output Mismatch Alignment for decoder-only models.** If the inputs are the same between the two mini-batches, why is alignment needed? Provide concrete examples where output mismatch occurs during fine-tuning of decoder-only models. (R2)
6. **Poor presentation.** Related work is placed in the appendix instead of the main body. Multiple typos exist, especially line 62: “taking inspiration inspiration from DLB”. (R3; also noted by R1, R2)
7. **Missing aggregated metric.** An overall benchmark score (e.g., average across tasks) should be reported for each method to make overall improvement easy to assess. (R1)
