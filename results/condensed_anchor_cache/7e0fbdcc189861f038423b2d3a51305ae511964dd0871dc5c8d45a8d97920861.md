## Merged Review

### Summary
DrugFlow is a generative model for structure-based drug design combining continuous flow matching with discrete Markov bridges. It provides uncertainty estimates for OOD detection, adaptive ligand size selection, joint preference alignment for property optimization, and an extension (FlexFlow) that samples side chain angles jointly. The paper is well-written with clear figures and thorough methodology.

### Strengths
- Excellent presentation and clarity (Reviewer 1: paper articulated clearly; Reviewer 3: top 10% presentation).
- Figures and tables effectively present complex data and comparisons (Reviewer 1).
- Methodology detailed thoroughly, supporting reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- Good domain knowledge: acknowledges that training for distribution matching then evaluating for optimization is inconsistent (Reviewer 3).
- Side-chain flexibility is modeled (FlexFlow), which is critical in docking/design and has been overlooked in previous SBDD methods (Reviewer 2).
- Uncertainty estimation is a practical and novel addition, common in structure prediction but absent in prior SBDD generation (Reviewer 2: increases practicality). Reviewer 3 agrees the idea is nice.
- Uncertainty visualization (Figure 2) is informative: unrealistic structures (e.g., long carbon chains with bifurcation) are assigned high uncertainty, matching intuition (Reviewer 2).
- Adaptive ligand size modification (adding/deleting atoms during generation) is a useful feature (Reviewer 3).
- Preference alignment for property optimization is well motivated and increases model value (Reviewer 2); experiments in Sections 3.2–3.5 are clearly organized and justify techniques (Reviewer 2).
- Wide range of tasks tested in experiments (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Pocket representation and flexibility**: The paper does not clarify whether the pocket is generated jointly or used as fixed context, nor whether its geometry undergoes any transformation during generation. This makes it difficult to assess the model’s ability to handle diverse protein environments (Reviewer 1, weakness 1).
- **Uncertainty estimation justification**:
  - The assumption that the error is normally distributed (line 133) is not justified; empirical evidence or literature support is needed (Reviewer 1).
  - In line 143, \(\dot{x}_t\) is incorrectly called a “ground truth” vector field; it should be a conditional vector field given \(x_0\) (Reviewer 1).
  - In line 987, \(\max\) is used instead of \(\arg\max\); maximizing Equation 30 is not equivalent to minimizing Equation 31 because the loss surfaces differ, even if minima coincide (Reviewer 1).
  - The technique resembles regularization, but it remains unclear how the variance of the predicted vector field translates into an atom-level uncertainty score (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 further notes that the practical significance of the OOD detection is questionable because the uncertainty estimates vary only by a tiny amount (color bar range of 0.008 in Figure 2B), suggesting the model may not be well-calibrated. (Note: Reviewer 2 found the uncertainty visualization informative, indicating disagreement on calibration quality.)
- **Virtual node explanation lacking**: Whether virtual bonds exist when virtual nodes are incorporated is not specified; the connection to the molecular graph and how these bonds are treated during generation need clarification (Reviewer 1, weakness 3).
- **Side-chain flexibility not used in main evaluation**: The main evaluation in Section 3.1 does not use side-chain flexibility (only the FlexFlow variant does). It is unclear why joint sampling of ligand and side-chain angles was not applied, and whether pocket data from pre- vs post-binding is available; without that, the approach may not differ from treating the pocket as context (Reviewers 1 and 2).
- **Metric and evaluation scope issues**:
  - Metrics reported (e.g., Wasserstein distances, JS divergences) are computed only on marginal (1D) distributions of properties like QED, logP, SA. Matching marginals does not imply matching joint distributions; Figure 4 is the only exception (Reviewer 3).
  - QED, logP, and SA are 1D quantities that do not depend on 3D structure, so matching them does not demonstrate that the 3D model captures structural features (Reviewer 3).
  - No measures of statistical variation (standard deviations, confidence intervals, significance tests) are provided for the finite-sample estimates in any tables, making performance claims poorly supported (Reviewer 3).
- **Baseline and comparison concerns**:
  - It is not stated whether baseline models were re-trained by the authors or used from pre-trained checkpoints, nor whether they were trained to the same extent (Reviewer 3).
  - Simpler 2D baselines (e.g., random SELFIES perturbation, RNN on SMILES with force-field 3D generation) are missing; they would help contextualize the model’s performance (Reviewer 3).
  - No baselines are provided for the preference optimization experiment (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation metric discrepancy**: The reported Wasserstein distances for QED, SA, and logP differ from those in Table 1 of [3]; the evaluation of bonds uses Wasserstein distance whereas prior works [1,2] use KL and Jensen-Shannon divergences. Clarification is needed (Reviewer 1, questions 5 and 6).
- **Preference pair synthesis**: How preference pairs are synthesized (line 206) is not described (Reviewer 1, question 4).
