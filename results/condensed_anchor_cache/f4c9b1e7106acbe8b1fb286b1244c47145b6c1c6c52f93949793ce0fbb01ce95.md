## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes LoftQ, an iterative method that jointly quantizes a pre-trained LLM’s weight matrix and initializes its LoRA adapters to minimize the Frobenius norm difference between the full-precision and quantized+LoRA representation. The approach addresses a shortcoming in QLoRA, which quantizes weights but uses the default LoRA initialization (which assumes non-quantized weights). Experiments on encoder models (classification), encoder-decoder models (summarization), and decoder models (math reasoning, language modeling) across 2‑bit, 4‑bit, and mixed‑precision settings show that LoftQ consistently outperforms QLoRA and, in some cases, even surpasses full‑precision LoRA.

### Strengths
- **Well‑motivated and simple solution**: The lack of proper initialization for quantized LoRA is a clear problem; the proposed iterative joint minimization is a simple yet effective fix.
- **Extensive experiments**: The method is evaluated over multiple architectures (e.g., LLaMA, BART, RoBERTa), datasets (e.g., XSUM, GSM8k, MNLI, QQP, etc.), and quantization regimes (2‑bit, 4‑bit, 2/4‑bit mixed), clearly demonstrating improvements over QLoRA.
- **Thorough ablation studies**: The paper investigates the impact of the number of iterations and provides analysis that clarifies the effectiveness of the approach.
- **Well‑written**: The structure is clear, the motivation is grounded in existing limitations, and the presentation is easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Concerns about the full‑precision LoRA baseline**: Reviewer 1 noted that LoftQ sometimes outperforms full‑precision LoRA (e.g., on XSUM and GSM8k). It is unclear whether the FP LoRA baseline was well‑tuned; quantization may act as an implicit regularizer, giving LoftQ an unfair advantage. The authors should report training epochs and steps for both methods, and consider adding explicit regularization to the FP LoRA baseline to ensure a fair comparison. *(R1, Q1 & Q2)*
- **Missing baseline: quantization‑aware fine‑tuning without LoRA**: To establish an upper bound on LoftQ’s accuracy, a baseline that fine‑tunes the quantized model without LoRA (i.e., quantization‑aware training) should be included. This would help separate the benefit of the initialization from the LoRA approach itself. *(R1, Q3; R2, Q2—related request for “full quantized fine‑tuning”)*
- **Clarify whether LoRA adapters A and B are quantized in LoftQ**: Reviewer 1 was not fully clear on whether the adapters themselves are quantized; this should be explicitly stated. *(R1, Q4)*
- **Limited generative‑task focus**: Reviewer 2 noted that most experiments on encoder‑only models (classification) show that quantized LoRA (with or without LoftQ) still performs too poorly for practical use. The paper could prioritize more experiments on decoder‑based generative tasks, where quantization is more critical. *(R2, Weakness 1)*
- **Missing analysis of memory footprint**: Reviewer 2 asked to show the memory footprint for 2‑bit quantized models during training to better assess practical benefits. *(R2, Q1)*
- **Ablations on the LoRA initialization itself**: 
    - Reviewer 3 suggested an ablation that discards the learned $A_T$, $B_T$ from LoftQ and uses default LoRA initialization instead, to isolate the contribution of the improved quantization vs. the improved initialization. *(R3, Q1)*
    - Reviewer 3 also asks whether the improved initialization might allow using LoRA adapters on fewer layers than the “every‑linear‑layer” rule recommended by QLoRA. *(R3, Q2)*
- **Deeper understanding of LoRA initialization**: The paper does not analyze whether the insights from the LoftQ initialization could be transferred to non‑quantized LoRA fine‑tuning, which would broaden the contribution. *(R3, Q3)*
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R1, score 6) raised multiple concerns about baseline comparisons and missing ablations, while the other two (R2, R3, both score 8) found no serious weaknesses and were more positive about the contribution. The contrast should be noted: R1 was less confident in the method’s demonstrated advantage over full‑precision LoRA, whereas R2 and R3 considered the experiments sufficient.
