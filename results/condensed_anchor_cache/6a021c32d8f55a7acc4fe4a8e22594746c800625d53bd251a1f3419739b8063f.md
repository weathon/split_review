## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper empirically investigates the relationship between pre-training and fine-tuning in LLMs by fine-tuning multiple intermediate checkpoints of a single base model (OLMo-1B) and evaluating on 18 datasets. The authors report four main findings: (i) continual pre-training improves models in ways only revealed after fine-tuning; (ii) fine-tuning benefits tasks not mastered during pre-training more than those already learned; (iii) fine-tuning causes forgetting of previously known domain knowledge and unseen tasks; (iv) fine-tuned models exhibit prompt sensitivity that can be mitigated by more pre-training. Reviewers had mixed assessments: two reviewers (scores 3,3) found the conclusions superficial, lacking novelty and theoretical depth, while three reviewers (scores 5,5,5) valued the problem’s importance and the empirical observations. All reviewers, however, raised significant concerns about the limited model scale and insufficient experiments, with many finding the paper’s contributions insufficient for a top-tier venue.

### Strengths
- The problem addressed – understanding how pre-training and fine-tuning interact – is important and valuable. Questions such as “Does more pre-training hinder better fine-tuning?” and “What is learned and forgotten during each stage?” are straightforward and practically relevant.
- The paper provides a series of experimental analyses and observations (on 18 datasets across various tasks) that can offer guiding insights, especially for researchers new to the field.
- Some findings inform potentially efficient training strategies (e.g., early stopping in pre-training when the downstream task is not yet learned).
- The paper is well written, clearly clarifying the problem each section addresses, and honestly states its limitations.
- The work is among the few to empirically study intermediate pre-training checkpoints, an under-explored area.

### Weaknesses
- **Single, small model severely limits generalization.** All experiments use only OLMo-1B (1B parameters). The findings may not transfer to larger models (e.g., 7B, 13B), different architectures (e.g., OpenELM), or other models with available checkpoints (e.g., Baichuan2-7B-Intermediate-Checkpoints). This is a critical concern for validity and applicability.
- **Lack of novelty; conclusions align with known perspectives or industry consensus.** Several observations (e.g., fine-tuning causes forgetting; tasks not learned in pre-training benefit more from fine-tuning) are considered relatively easy to obtain and have limited impact on the literature. Multiple reviewers noted that the paper does not meet the bar of an ICLR paper in terms of insight.
- **No analysis of model internals or theoretical foundations.** The paper only examines downstream performance without exploring parameter changes, loss landscapes, weight-matrix evolution, or activation patterns. This prevents a mechanistic understanding of why the observed phenomena occur and limits the depth of the contribution.
- **Benchmark datasets are too simple and outdated.** The evaluation uses flan-style datasets that do not reflect modern LLM capabilities. More challenging and comprehensive benchmarks (e.g., MT-bench, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard) are needed to assess prompt sensitivity, instruction following, and complex reasoning. Moreover, the paper does not test in general settings (e.g., AlpacaEval, MT-Bench), making it unclear if the conclusions hold there.
- **Limited SFT task coverage.** Only four supervised fine-tuning tasks are used (summarization, question generation, NLI, paraphrase detection), which restricts the generalization of the results.
- **Some conclusions are not rigorously justified.** For instance, the claim that “some tasks are learned during pre-training while others are not” (lines 300-303) may simply reflect differences in pretraining data coverage (e.g., similar task types present vs. absent). The authors should examine the pretraining data to rule out such trivial explanations before drawing conclusions.
- **Practical guidance is weak or contradictory.** The suggestion to use early stopping in pre-training when fine-tuning yields better results is unrealistic because practitioners typically start from a well-pretrained model, not from scratch. Additionally, the paper appears to contain a contradiction: Section 5 states “the benefits of fine-tuning an LLM could exceed the benefits of continued pretraining,” while Section 7 argues “pre-training can improve models in unseen ways.” The authors should clarify how these two viewpoints reconcile.
- **Insufficient demonstration of how findings inform LLM development.** No promising experiments are provided that translate the empirical observations into actionable design choices or concrete training protocols.
- **Missing related work section.** The paper would benefit from a clear summarization of how this work differs from previous studies on pre-training/fine-tuning dynamics.
- **Layout issues.** Some figures/tables and their corresponding text are not on the same page, which significantly harms readability.
- **Typo:** line 299: “pre-trining” should be “pre-training.”
