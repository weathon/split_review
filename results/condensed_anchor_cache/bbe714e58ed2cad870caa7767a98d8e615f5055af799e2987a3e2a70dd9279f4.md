## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies online learning in loop-free (episodic) constrained MDPs under bandit feedback, aiming for optimal *strong* regret and *strong* cumulative constraint violation (no cancellation of positive and negative terms). It proposes CPD-PO, a primal-dual policy optimization algorithm that leverages an existing adversarial MDP oracle (PO-DB) for the primal update and a binary (UCB-like) dual update. The algorithm achieves \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})\) strong regret and violation, improving the prior best \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{0.93})\) bound for computationally efficient methods and matching the optimal rate.

### Strengths
- The strong regret/violation metric is well motivated and more relevant for safe-critical applications than conventional weak metrics, as it does not allow policy cancellation that obscures individual constraint violations.
- The paper provides the first *computationally efficient* (policy optimization, not LP over occupancy measures) algorithm with optimal \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})\) strong regret/violation, improving the previous suboptimal \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{0.93})\) bound. This is a significant theoretical advance.
- The algorithm design is interesting and novel: it combines a binary dual update, a synthetic loss to convert constraint violations into a loss for the primal, and an off-the-shelf adversarial MDP optimization oracle. This combination is different from prior primal-dual methods that relied on regularization.
- The proof is checked to be correct, and Section 5.2 comparing the proposed algorithm against known algorithms is appreciated.
- The writing is clear, and the discussion of prior work is sufficient (though some related references are missing – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experimental validation**: The paper provides no empirical results. Although theoretical, simulation results like those in Müller et al. (2024) would help assess practical relevance, especially given the dependence on the Slater constant \(\rho\) and the bandit feedback assumption. The paper would benefit from experiments comparing against baselines such as Efroni et al. (2020), OptPess-LP, and model-free methods.
- **Dependence on Slater’s condition and knowledge of \(\rho\)**: The algorithm requires exact knowledge of the Slater constant \(\rho\), and the regret bound contains an \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\rho)\) factor. This is a significant limitation in practice, as \(\rho\) is typically unknown and can be arbitrarily small, leading to degraded performance. The paper does not discuss how to address this (e.g., adaptive estimation or working with only bounds on \(\rho\)), nor does it acknowledge the severity of this dependence. This issue is noted by multiple reviewers.
- **Restrictive setting (bandit feedback)**: The paper assumes bandit feedback (only observed cost/reward of the visited action in each state), which is more restrictive than the full-information or known transition settings used in prior work (e.g., Efroni et al., 2020; Müller et al., 2024). Direct comparisons with those algorithms are therefore not entirely fair, as the information limitation can impact achievable performance. The paper should clarify this difference.
- **Algorithmic contribution is limited**: One reviewer noted that CPD-PO largely builds on the existing PO-DB algorithm, adding only a simple binary dual update. The core techniques are not new, and the novelty lies mainly in the combination. (Contrast: other reviewers praised the improvement from \(T^{0.93}\) to \(\sqrt{T}\) as a huge step, so there is disagreement on the level of algorithmic novelty.)
- **Missing related references**: The paper does not discuss OptPess-LP (which can achieve *zero* strong constraint violation under certain conditions) or a recent model-free method that achieves \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})\) strong violation. A detailed comparison highlighting differences and computational trade-offs is needed. The paper should clarify how its primal-dual approach compares to the LP-based method and the model-free method.
- **Unclear motivation for key algorithmic choices**: The paper does not adequately explain why a binary dual update and a synthetic loss are used, nor how these choices together avoid the oscillation issues that cause linear strong regret/violation in standard primal-dual methods. The connection between these techniques and the avoidance of oscillations needs to be explicitly clarified.
- **Using an adversarial oracle for a stochastic environment**: The algorithm employs an adversarial MDP policy optimization oracle, yet the CMDP has stochastic rewards, costs, and fixed transitions. The rationale for this choice and potential bias/instability introduced by the adversarial oracle are not discussed.
- **Missing discussion of limitations and future work**: The paper lacks a conclusion/discussion section. It does not address limitations such as the requirement for exact \(\rho\), the tabular-only assumption, or the need to extend beyond finite horizon. Open questions (e.g., whether \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(L^5)\) dependency on horizon \(L\) is optimal, lower bound related to number of constraints \(m\)) are not discussed.
- **Writing and presentation issues**: (i) The definition of the loop-free MDP is relegated to a footnote, but it is crucial for the paper’s scope and should appear in the main text. (ii) Explanations after Lemma 3 are vague (e.g., “aforementioned parameters” unclear; why eq. (2) holds not justified). (iii) Constants in Lemma 1 & 2 appear inconsistent with those in Lemma 6 (up to a numerical factor). (iv) Typos: line 5 of Algorithm 2 should use \(L\) instead of \(K\); line 904 missing \(i\) in \(i \in [m]\). (v) Use of \nicefrac makes fractions hard to read when expressions contain sums.
