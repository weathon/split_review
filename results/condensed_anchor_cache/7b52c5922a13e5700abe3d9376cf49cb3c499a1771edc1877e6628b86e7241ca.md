## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DBGMS (Dual-Branch Generative Adversarial Network with Multi-Task Self-Supervised Enhancement) for EEG-based auditory attention decoding. The framework combines temporal and frequency branches with attention mechanisms and graph convolutions, branch-specific GANs for data augmentation, and a multi-task self-supervised learning strategy (temporal order prediction, frequency band reconstruction, time-frequency consistency). Evaluated on the KUL and DTU datasets, DBGMS reports improved detection accuracy, especially for short decision windows, compared to several baselines. Scores are mixed (6, 3, 5, 3, 3), indicating substantial disagreement about novelty, clarity, and experimental sufficiency.

### Strengths
- **Dual-branch GAN architecture** is novel in leveraging both temporal and spectral features with GAN-based augmentation in each domain; this helps mitigate the data scarcity problem in small EEG datasets (KUL, DTU) and prevents overfitting (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Multi-task self-supervised learning** (temporal order prediction, frequency band reconstruction, time-frequency consistency) enhances generalization across subjects and listening conditions (Reviewer 1).
- **Comprehensive evaluation** on multiple datasets (KUL, DTU) and across varying decision window lengths; DBGMS shows strong performance improvements (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
- **Extensive ablation studies** (Table 4) confirm the necessity of all main components; many comparison baselines are included (Table 3) (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Effective combination of techniques** (GANs, graph convolution, attention, dual-branch) into a unified framework that addresses data scarcity, subject variability, and short decision windows – a gap in prior work (Reviewer 5).
- **Diverse appendix experiments** cover cross-subject, cross-dataset, cross-decision window, training convergence, and EEG masking strategies, demonstrating generalization and robustness (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and incremental contribution**: The dual-branch temporal-frequency architecture is argued by some to be incremental – similar temporal-frequency transformers (e.g., TFF-Former [Li et al., 2022]) already exist for EEG decoding, and DBGMS appears to be a straightforward combination of such branches with graph learning and GAN augmentation. The motivation lacks concrete hypotheses (e.g., targeting auditory cortex areas) and reads as a toolkit combination of common EEG techniques (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 1 finds the architecture innovative, while others see it as incremental – this disagreement should be explicitly addressed.
- **Clarity and readability**: The manuscript is extremely hard to read and understand. Figures 1 and 2 have illegible fonts (too small, poor style – Arial recommended). The text is often long and imprecise (e.g., description of adjacency matrix entries without specifying actual formula; vague terms like “multi-dimensional” used unnecessarily). Important details are missing: which time-frequency transform (Fourier?); whether the model is an adversarial autoencoder (the term “adversarial autoencoder” is never cited nor used); notation \( \mathcal{L}_i \) is not clearly explained (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Self-supervised learning terminology and implementation**: The SSL losses are used as auxiliary losses, not for pre-training a robust encoder followed by fine-tuning – this is a mislabeling since self-supervision typically denotes pre-training. The implementation of multi-task learning (Section 2.4, Eq. 27) is confusing; the rationale for selecting these specific tasks and how each individually contributes to generalization is not provided. No empirical ablation isolates the impact of each SSL task (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Motivation for GAN data augmentation**: The GAN essentially replaces the decoder of a masked autoencoder (MAE [He et al., 2022]), yet no reconstruction visualizations are provided to verify that generated samples accurately represent underlying EEG signal distributions. Measures to ensure fidelity of synthetic data are absent (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Experimental sufficiency and robustness analysis**: While some cross-subject and noise robustness experiments are in the appendix, the main text only reports classification and ablation results. Additional experiments are needed: robustness to different noise types (e.g., Gaussian noise, physiological noise EMG/EOG), visualization of learned representations (t-SNE, activation maps), and cross-subject/few-shot/zero-shot performance. Figure 3 and Table 3 present identical information – one should be removed. Figure 4 is confusing: which dataset (KUL or both?); what decision window length?; why are strong baselines from Table 3 not included? (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- **Missing ablations and hyperparameter sensitivity**: The loss function includes hyperparameter λ with no discussion of its setting or sensitivity; no table of optimal hyperparameter values is provided. The sensitivity of GAN augmentation and SSL tasks to hyperparameters is not explored (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Reproducibility and methodological concerns**: 
  - The training and evaluation split during different stages (unlabeled GAN training, self-supervised learning) is unclear – are evaluation sets used already? (Reviewer 2).
  - 5-fold cross-validation is used; it is unclear which results in Table 3 are reproduced vs. taken from original papers; the risk of overly optimistic results from 5-fold CV is not addressed. The meaning of “ST-GCN” in Table 3 is ambiguous (no spatio-temporal setting in the original ST-GCN paper). ST-GCN results for KUL1s are missing. In Appendix A.3, the ST-GCN citation (ICASSP 2024) shows a large discrepancy from the original paper; the experimental setup (e.g., LOSO) is not clarified. ST-GCN results are absent from cross-dataset evaluation (Table 5) – raising concerns about reliability (Reviewer 5).
  - The discriminator loss term is omitted from the total loss (Eq. 30); the full training procedure for the adversarial components is not described (Reviewer 3).
  - No open-source code is provided (Reviewer 5).
- **Real-world applicability and efficiency**: The high complexity (dual-branch, GANs, SSL tasks) raises concerns about computational efficiency for real-time applications – no training/inference times or parameter/complexity comparisons with baselines are provided. Generalization to more diverse, noisy real-world environments is not tested beyond the KUL and DTU datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- **Minor writing and formatting issues**: Abstract lists “three key innovations” but enumerates four; repeated references (lines 570, 573); missing dataset references in Section 3.1; incorrect citation format (should use \citep rather than \cite); author name “Shuxin Cai” in SSF-CNN, STAnet, SGCN, ST-GCN is incorrect – should be Siqi Cai (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).
