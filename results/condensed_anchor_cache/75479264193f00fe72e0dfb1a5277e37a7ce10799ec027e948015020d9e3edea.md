## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses offline imitation learning (OIL) without relying on high-quality auxiliary behavior data. It proposes Behavioral Cloning with Dynamic Programming (BCDP), which applies behavioral cloning on the expert dataset and dynamic programming (Q-learning with a binary reward: 1 for expert state-action pairs, 0 otherwise) on the union of expert and unlabeled offline data. The goal is to maximize the probability of transitioning to expert-observed states. Experiments on D4RL benchmarks show that BCDP outperforms existing offline IL methods, especially when the offline data is purely random. Reviewers generally found the approach simple and the results strong on low-quality data, but several raised concerns about novelty (similarity to TD3+BC), motivation, missing baselines and ablations, evaluation of out-of-distribution performance, and insufficient experimental details.

### Strengths
- **Fresh perspective and timely problem:** The paper challenges the conventional assumption that offline IL requires high-quality auxiliary data, and shows that low-quality (even random) data can be leveraged. This is a practically relevant question (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Simple and sound idea:** BCDP combines behavioral cloning with dynamic programming in a straightforward manner. The approach is intuitive and supported by a theoretical lower bound on performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Strong experimental results:** BCDP achieves state-of-the-art performance on 14 D4RL tasks, with notable gains on random and low-quality datasets (often >40% improvement). Performance curves are solid, especially in sparse-reward domains (Reviewers 1, 2). The experimental setup covers multiple domains and data qualities (Reviewers 2, 3: sufficient).
- **Clear writing:** The paper is well-organized and easy to follow, with lucid explanations of the method and reasoning (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 2 noted the graphs are unusual but relevant.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and clarity of problem setup:** The motivation for the specific dataset split (expert vs. unlabeled) and the task setting in Figure 1 are unclear. Details on the number of trajectories, samples per trajectory, ratio of expert to non-expert data, and task complexity are missing. This makes it hard to interpret the conclusions drawn from Figure 1 (Reviewer 3). Also, the paper does not adequately explain why BCDP performs less effectively on non-random (medium/expert) datasets, and more analysis on dataset quality is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty concerns and similarity to offline RL:** BCDP is essentially offline RL applied on a different data split with a binary reward, and its core mechanism (Bellman updates) is the same as standard off-policy RL. The differences from TD3+BC are minimal: TD3+BC uses a reward function derived from the dataset, while BCDP uses a handcrafted binary reward. The paper does not clearly articulate what unique advantages BCDP offers over TD3+BC or other offline RL methods (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (Score 8) considered the approach solid and did not view the similarity as a major weakness, but another (Score 5) questioned novelty.
- **Missing baseline comparisons and ablation:** BCDP is not compared to TD3+BC, CQL, BEAR, BRAC, or IQL, despite the paper stating that TD3+BC is the most similar offline RL algorithm and could serve as an ablation. No ablation study is provided to isolate the effect of the proposed components. Comparison to model-based imitation learning methods (e.g., [1,2,3] from Reviewer 4) is also missing (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation is insufficient:** The DRG metric (difference in state occupancy) does not quantify test-time performance on unobserved states; a positive DRG only indicates evasion of unobserved states, not successful performance. The paper lacks a clear definition of "unobserved state" and does not evaluate on heldout states, random seeds, or measure action confidence in those states. Standard OOD evaluation practices are missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Reward design is too strict:** The binary reward (1 for expert state-action pairs, 0 otherwise) is problematic in continuous spaces: it is unlikely that any state-action pair in the auxiliary dataset obtains reward 1, so the method relies solely on critic smoothing. The reward does not explicitly encourage reaching unseen expert states and provides no gradient for generalization. An ablation with a discriminator-based reward (as in DWBC, ORIL, DemoDICE) is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing hyperparameters and learning curves:** Important hyperparameters (weight for Q-maximization, network architecture, learning rate, update frequency, number of steps) are not specified. Learning curves are not provided, making it difficult to assess training dynamics, stability, convergence, and reproducibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation issues:** There are grammatical errors and incomplete sentences. The second-best result in Table 1 is not underlined (Reviewers 1, 3). The paper does not discuss its limitations (Reviewer 4).
