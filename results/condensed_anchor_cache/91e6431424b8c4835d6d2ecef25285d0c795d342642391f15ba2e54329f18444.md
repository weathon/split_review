## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces XLand-100B, a large-scale dataset for in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) based on XLand-MiniGrid. It contains 100B transitions, 2.5B episodes, and nearly 30,000 tasks (28,000 after filtering), collected using a PPO policy that was first pretrained on 65K tasks and then finetuned on the task subset. The authors provide utilities to reproduce or expand the dataset, and benchmark two ICRL algorithms (Algorithm Distillation and Decision-Pretrained Transformer). AD shows emergent in-context learning but only limited improvement; DPT performs poorly, which the authors attribute to its lack of positional encoding in a POMDP setting. The work aims to democratize ICRL research by providing a standardized, large-scale benchmark.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly motivates the need for a large-scale, open-source dataset in ICRL, an important gap that is hindering progress. This contribution may significantly boost research in the field.
- The dataset is substantially larger and more diverse than prior ICRL datasets (e.g., NetHack, Procgen, GATO), and includes complete learning histories instead of just unordered or optimal trajectories.
- The authors spent significant effort on engineering: tuning compression levels and HDF5 chunk sizes to balance dataset size and sampling throughput, and providing estimated optimal actions needed by methods like DPT.
- Including a lighter version (XLand-Trivial-20B) makes the benchmark more accessible to resource-limited researchers.
- The paper is well-structured, well-written, and provides comprehensive details on data collection, dataset format, and implementation methodology. Reproducibility is taken seriously (code open-source under Apache 2).
- The work addresses a genuine limitation of current ICRL research, which often uses simple environments and small datasets. Even though the environment itself is a gridworld, the scale and diversity of tasks represent a step forward.
- The design decisions (e.g., storage format, chunking, compression) are sound and practical.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited environment diversity (disagreement among reviewers):** The dataset uses only one type of environment (XLand-MiniGrid, a 5×5 single-room grid). Two reviewers (R1, R4) view this as a critical limitation that reduces generalizability to continuous action spaces, high-dimensional observations (robotics), or other complex domains. They give lower scores (6) partly for this reason. The other two reviewers (R2, R3) consider the dataset itself sound and do not see this as a significant weakness, though they note it as a limitation acknowledged in the paper.
- **Lack of a clear benchmark split and evaluation protocol:** The paper does not specify which tasks should be used for training vs. evaluation (e.g., by number of training tasks, by number of rules). This makes it unclear for users how to start using the dataset. An ablation study varying the number of training tasks (e.g., 100/1000/10000/all) to assess the effect on ICRL abilities is missing (R4).
- **Data collection uses only PPO without justification:** The dataset is generated solely by PPO (with GRU), with no theoretical or empirical explanation for why PPO is especially suited for ICRL compared to other RL algorithms (e.g., those with different exploration or convergence behavior). This may bias learning histories toward a specific style and limit the dataset's representativeness (R3).
- **Filtering by final returns does not guarantee informative learning histories:** Tasks with low final returns are filtered out, but high returns do not ensure that the learning trajectories demonstrate generalizable problem-solving strategies. Circuitous routes or exploitation of environmental quirks may still be present. Conversely, low-return trajectories that contain valuable exploration or adaptation patterns may be discarded (R3).
- **Approximate expert actions may compromise fidelity:** The dataset includes estimated optimal actions from the PPO policy, which is only an approximation of the true optimal policy. There is no measure of approximation error, so the impact on models relying on expert demonstrations (like DPT) is unknown (R3).
- **DPT baseline is likely unfair:** DPT’s poor performance is attributed to the lack of positional encoding in its transformer, preventing it from using historical information from the current episode in the POMDP setting. A simple modification—adding positional encoding just to transitions from the ongoing episode—could remove this limitation and might yield different results. The current comparison may not reflect the method’s true potential (R2).
- **Little in-context improvement observed for either method:** AD shows only slight in-context improvement (especially for 1-rule tasks), and DPT shows none. This differs from prior ICRL works and raises the question of whether the issue lies in the algorithms or in the dataset itself. The learning curves in Figures 12-13 show agents reaching optimal performance within a few hundred steps of training (with 8K-15K episodes total), leaving little learning progress for the rest of the data. This could explain the lack of in-context improvement (R4).
- **AD performance degrades with longer context length:** In Figure 8, AD's performance strictly decreases as context length increases from 1024 to 4096. This is counterintuitive and not well explained. Possible causes (e.g., difficulty in distinguishing current vs. previous episode data) are not explored (R2, R4). Additionally, the median episode length and how often the context spans multiple episodes are not reported, making it hard to interpret the results (R2).
- **Need for more visualizations:** The abstract description of tasks (rules 1–9) would benefit from concrete illustrations showing how the task tree changes with increasing rules, example trajectories for complex tasks, and a visual of the actual 5×5 grid used (Figure 1 appears to show a multi-room layout, which is not the evaluation environment) (R1, R4). This would help researchers understand exploration challenges and task diversity.
- **Dataset novelty is limited:** While the dataset is a practical contribution, the idea of collecting a large-scale ICRL dataset is not novel per se (R1).
- **Optimal action labeling:** The paper states that obtaining true optimal actions is unlikely for real-world problems, but prior work (e.g., D4RL) has used fully finetuned RL policies to collect “expert” data. The authors should clarify why this approach is not applicable to their tasks (R1, line 350).
- **Evaluation procedure clarification needed:** Evaluation uses 512 episodes per task across 1024 unseen tasks. It should be confirmed whether this means 1024 separate single-task evaluations (each 500 episodes) where the transformer context never mixes data from different tasks (R2).
