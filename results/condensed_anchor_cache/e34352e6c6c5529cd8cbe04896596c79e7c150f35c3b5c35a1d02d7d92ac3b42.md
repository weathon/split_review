## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SciPIP, a literature retrieval and scientific idea generation system. It uses a multi-pronged retrieval strategy combining semantic, entity, and citation co-occurrence, along with clustering, to fetch relevant papers. The idea generation module employs a dual-path method: one path augments the LLM with retrieved literature, the other prompts the LLM to brainstorm independently, then filters and refines the results. Experiments on NLP conference papers (ACL 2024) show that SciPIP retrieves citations similar to those of existing top papers and generates ideas that outperform baselines like AI Scientist (in idea proposal) and SciMON (in retrieval). All four reviewers agree the topic is interesting, but three (score 3 each) find significant limitations in evaluation, originality, and scope; one reviewer (score 5, confidence 2) is more positive but still raises concerns about evaluation methods and practical applicability.

### Strengths
- The paper is generally well-written, easy to follow, and uses clear visual aids (Figures 1 and 3) to explain the methods.
- The creation of a new annotated dataset of 50k recent AI/ML papers, where each paper is annotated with entities such as main ideas, backgrounds, and summaries, is a useful resource.
- The multi-path proposal strategy (augmented + brainstorming) and the ensemble retrieval method (semantic, entity, co-citation, clustering) are novel integrations. The system demonstrably improves over vanilla LLM prompting and over baselines like AI Scientist and SciMON.
- SciPIP goes beyond single-embedding paper representation by creating separate embeddings for Background, Summary, and Main Ideas, which are used differently across retrieval stages.
- The system includes idea generation, filtering, and refinement pipelines, and combines literature-based and brainstorming paths to balance feasibility and novelty. (Minority reviewer 4 specifically highlights the combination of retrieval and brainstorming as a strength.)
- The topic is interesting and important for understanding LLM capabilities in scientific discovery.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation relies heavily on LLM judgments without adequate human verification.** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) criticize that novelty and feasibility are scored by GPT-4o with no clear criteria. For instance, the paper does not verify inter-annotator agreement between LLM and human experts, nor does it justify why an LLM can judge feasibility (e.g., resource, time, and execution) when generated ideas lack experimental details (R1). R3 and R4 also question whether semantic similarity alone (from an LLM) captures true innovation, logic, or technical feasibility. A high similarity score could simply reflect copying from training data or minor tweaks rather than genuine novelty (R4).
- **The provided example undermines the claimed evaluation quality.** In Figure 4, SciPIP generates an idea about multimodal contrastive learning that uses a widely accepted approach (CLIP, 2021) but omits the ground-truth’s specific data augmentation strategy; yet the LLM assigns a similarity score of 4 (R1, R3). This raises doubts about the reliability of the LLM as an evaluator.
- **Data contamination risk is not addressed.** R1 notes that GPT-4o has a knowledge cutoff of Oct 2023 and that ACL 2024 papers may have had preprint versions before that date, potentially inflating similarity scores. This contamination is not discussed.
- **The related work section is insufficient.** Many important works are missing: large-scale literature databases (arXiv, PubMed, Semantic Scholar), modern neural IR models (OpenAI-v3, Grit-LM, RankLlama, RankGPT), and existing IR benchmarks (DORIS-MAE, SciDocs, SciRepEval). Also missing is Citation-Enhanced Generation (Li et al., 2024) as suggested by R4. The retrieval method is compared only with two other strategies, not with these modern baselines or on standard benchmarks.
- **Scope and generalizability are narrowly limited.** The system is built and evaluated only on AI/ML/NLP papers, but the paper’s title and framing claim a “scientific paper idea proposer”. There is no discussion of applicability to physics, biomedicine, or mathematics (R1). The pipeline is likely tailored to NLP prompts and may not transfer to other domains without major modifications; this limitation is not openly acknowledged (R1, R4 suggests it may be more suitable as a demo paper).
- **Originality of the technical contributions is low.** R1 and R2 point out that the retrieval strategy is an ensemble of existing techniques (entity matching, semantic search, co-citation, clustering). The idea proposal pipeline is an implementation of known approaches. The most original aspect—the annotated database—lacks quantitative quality control in its creation. R3 adds that SciMON already uses semantic, entity, and citation signals; SciPIP’s enhancements are minor and come with extra computational cost.
- **Ablation studies are missing.** The contributions of individual submodules (e.g., the three embedding types, dual-path generation, filtering/refinement) are not evaluated. Without ablation, it is unclear which components drive the reported improvements (R3).
- **Missing baselines and standard metrics.** The retrieval experiment uses only recall, not NDCG or precision, and does not compare against existing scientific IR benchmarks. The idea generation evaluation compares only with AI Scientist; other LLM-based proposal systems or standalone LLMs are not included (R1).
- **Reliability of SentenceBERT is questioned.** R2 notes that SentenceBERT has known limitations in capturing nuanced semantics, and the paper uses precomputed models that may not be optimal for entity-text similarity. R1 similarly observes that the chosen embedding model (sentence-bert) is out-of-date with a short context window.
- **Presentation lacks clarity in several spots.** R2 finds Table 1 and its explanation confusing; R1 notes the limitations section is too short and insufficient. Some details about the database creation and quality control are missing (R1).
- **Practical applicability is unverified.** Without user studies or expert reviews, it is unclear whether SciPIP’s outputs are truly useful for researchers (R4). R4 suggests the paper would be better positioned as a demo paper or could be reoriented toward HCI with user studies.

### Reviewer Disagreement
Reviewer 4 (score 5) is notably more positive, emphasizing the practical utility and the combination of retrieval and brainstorming as valuable innovations, and rating contribution 3 (vs. 1 or 2 from others). However, even this reviewer lists overlapping weaknesses—evaluation via similarity scores, potential copying, need for expert review—and questions whether the paper is more suitable as a demo paper. The three lower-scoring reviewers (scores 3) are more critical of originality, evaluation rigor, and scope, with two rating contribution as 1.
