## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents ED-NeRF, a text-guided 3D NeRF editing method that embeds real-world scenes into the latent space of a latent diffusion model via a refinement layer, and uses a DDS distillation loss with a binary mask to guide editing. The approach aims to improve efficiency and output quality. Reviewers found the method straightforward, integrating existing techniques (latent NeRF, DDS), and achieving good results on color/texture edits. However, concerns about novelty, validation of efficiency claims, fairness of comparisons, limited scope of experiments, and missing view-consistency evidence were raised.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- DDS distillation loss with binary mask is effective for editing (R1, R2, R3).
- The method preserves the integrity of the original 3D scene while enabling desired modifications (R2).
- Embedding NeRF training in latent space reduces time and computation cost; this is a significant and smart move (R3).
- The refinement layer is simple yet effective, overcoming quality drifting in latent-space rendering (R3).
- The adaptation of DDS to 3D is novel and reasonable (R2, R3).
- Qualitative results demonstrate efficient modification of specific objects while preserving the surrounding environment, outperforming baselines (R2, R3, R4).
- Code is provided for reproducibility (R2).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is incremental; the method combines existing latent space and DDS techniques without a fundamental breakthrough or strong theoretical justification (R1). *Contrast:* R3 considered the approach original.
- Efficiency claims are insufficiently validated: no formal training-time comparison appears in the main paper; the time analysis in the supplement only reports inference time, ignoring time for generating source images/latent feature maps (R1, R2, R3). The method is not compared against existing fast NeRF training/fine-tuning techniques (R1).
- Refinement layers are straightforward post-processing layers derived from SD VAE; design justification is lacking (R1).
- Experiments are limited to color changes and local texture edits; more complex tasks (style transfer, object replacement, object removal, object insertion) are not evaluated (R1, R3).
- The comparison with Instruct-NeRF2NeRF may be unfair because the proposed method uses an object mask while the baseline does not; it is unclear whether improvements come from the mask rather than the DDS loss. Mask acquisition and sensitivity to segmentation accuracy are not addressed (R4). This is a major concern, and the submission may be considered incomplete without resolution (R4).
- No free-viewpoint video results are provided, making view-consistency of edits difficult to assess (R2). This is a primary concern for some reviewers (R2).
- Missing ablation: combining SDS and DDS losses is not tested; Equation 9 item order may be incorrect (R3).
- Writing is casual with mistakes: the relation between Eq.12 and Eq.13 is unclear; there is a typo in Fig.2(c) and minor typos elsewhere (R1, R3).
- Rendered depth maps appear coarse (R2).
