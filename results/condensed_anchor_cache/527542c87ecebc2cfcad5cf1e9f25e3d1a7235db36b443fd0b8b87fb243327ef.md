## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Predictive Prior, a regularization for object-centric learning (OCL) inspired by human gestalt perception: features belonging to the same object can predict each other. The prior is implemented as an external loss that guides slot-feature assignment. Experiments on MOVi-C, Super-CLEVR, and PTR show improvements over prior OCL methods in object discovery, compositional generation, and VQA.

### Strengths
- The idea is novel and well-motivated by human perception.  
- The method is straightforward to implement.  
- Results are strong across multiple datasets (Super-CLEVR, MOVi-C, PTR) and outperform recent OCL baselines.  
- The writing and figures are clear and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ablations and analysis:**  
  - The multiple loss functions (reconstruction, prior) are not ablated. Reviewer 1 asks to ablate reconstruction vs. the proposed objective and examine how switching either affects accuracy.  
  - The choice of L1 over L2 for reconstruction is unexplained; an ablation comparing the two is requested (Reviewer 4).  
  - The role of the pre-trained DINO backbone is not isolated – Reviewer 1 asks what happens if training from scratch with only the new objective.  
- **Missing comparisons with relevant methods:**  
  - CuTLER (DINOSAUR-based segmentation) is not compared or discussed (Reviewer 1).  
  - Diffusion-based approaches (SlotDiffuzr, SysBinder) are missing (Reviewer 2).  
  - Methods that perform well on complex OCL datasets (LSD, DINOSAUR on CLEVRTEX, MOVi-E, COCO) are not included (Reviewer 4).  
- **Limited dataset scope and scalability concerns:**  
  - Results are only on synthetic-scene datasets (CLEVR-variants, MOVi-C, PTR). Reviewers 2 and 4 request experiments on real-world data (COCO, OpenImages) and more complex/higher-resolution scenes (CLEVRTEX, MOVi-E). Reviewer 4 explicitly questions scalability and suggests discussing limitations.  
- **Conceptual and technical questions about Predictive Prior:**  
  - The method’s alignment with the title is disputed: Reviewer 4 notes the predictor only sees one part and recognizes another, not whole-object completion; Figure 1 is misleading. Clarification is requested on whether the method can actually complete whole objects from parts.  
  - Reviewer 3 raises the gestalt interpretation: are parts predicted from appearance/structure or from a semantic prior? Any proof?  
  - Semantic co-occurrence (e.g., keyboard and mouse) could produce high inter-predictability across different objects; how is this avoided? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Use of absolute position is argued to be wrong; relative position should be used to capture structural information (Reviewer 3).  
  - Training the similarity prediction network appears to require supervision, making the method less fair vs. fully unsupervised approaches (Reviewer 3).  
  - Risk of a trivial solution where the predictor outputs high similarity for any feature pair (Reviewer 3).  
- **Implementation and presentation issues:**  
  - Figure 2 omits the learnable segmentation mask M (Reviewer 4).  
  - Figures are not in PDF format; expressions like “clamp(a, b)” are informal (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 4 asks about the decoder used for MOVi-C (transformer-based) and how it generates the alpha masks needed for the prior loss.  
  - Reviewer 4 notes that the BOQSA visualization on PTR (Figure 3) appears better than the original paper’s; asks if any techniques were used.  
- **Computational cost** of the added predictor and modules is not reported (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing discussion** of part-whole hierarchy representation (e.g., Hinton’s work) is noted (Reviewer 2).  

**Reviewer rating contrast:** Reviewer 1 (score 6) was the most positive, highlighting strong empirical results, while Reviewer 4 (score 3) was the most critical, questioning the conceptual grounding, scalability, and presentation clarity. Both concerns are preserved above.
