## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that the paper tackles an underexplored and valuable problem—integrating distributed tactile sensing into dynamic hand-object reconstruction—and that the proposed DF-Field representation, force-aware optimization, and the HOT dataset are promising contributions. However, opinions are divided: two reviewers (scores 6) are positive about the novelty and results, while two (scores 3) find the presentation confusing, the ablation incomplete, and the justification for tactile information insufficient. Common concerns across all reviewers include missing comparisons to contact-based refinement methods (ContactOpt, TOCH, S2Contact, VTacOH), unclear ablation studies, and uneven comparisons with methods that use only RGB input.

### Strengths
1. **Novel integration of tactile sensing**: The combination of visual reconstruction with distributed tactile data via DF-Field directly models contact fidelity, overcoming a key limitation of purely visual methods (Reviewer 1).
2. **Innovative DF-Field and force-aware optimization (FO)**: The energy-based contact representation is intuitive and leads to state-of-the-art results on both DexYCB and HOT datasets, especially for deformable objects (Reviewer 1, 4).
3. **HOT dataset**: A simulated dataset containing 600 sequences of hand-object interactions with both rigid and deformable objects, including tactile readings, is a valuable resource for the community (Reviewer 1, 2).
4. **Qualitative illustrations**: Figures (e.g., Fig. 1, 3, 4) effectively connect the method to practical scenarios and support the quantitative comparisons (Reviewer 2).
5. **Focus on deformable objects**: The work attempts to model generic hand-object interaction that includes deformable objects, which is currently underexplored and important (Reviewer 2, 3).
6. **Readability**: The proposed method is easy to understand and well-structured (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing comparisons to contact-based hand pose refinement methods**: The paper does not compare its force-aware optimization (FO) with existing contact-based refinement methods such as ContactOpt (Grady et al., CVPR 2021), TOCH (Zhou et al., ECCV 2022), S2Contact (Tse et al., ECCV 2022), and VTacOH (Xu et al., CVPR 2023). Such comparisons would clarify the advantage of using tactile data over geometric constraints alone (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
2. **Unclear necessity of tactile information**: 
   - In Table 3, using a uniform force reading (M_j=0.5) across all contact regions still improves results, raising the question of whether tactile sensors are truly needed or whether the same gain could be obtained from geometric constraints (Reviewer 1, 3).
   - The force-aware optimization loss (Eq. 9) is dominated by geometric terms (Lr, Lo). When the initial hand pose prediction is far from the object, the tactile-related energy (E) vanishes, leaving no driving force to correct deep penetration or missed contact (Reviewer 3, Question 1).
   - Benefits over simple distance-based contact modeling are not demonstrated (Reviewer 4).
3. **Unfair comparison with gSDF**: 
   - VDT-Net uses full 3D point cloud input, while gSDF uses monocular RGB. This makes the comparison unequal; reconstructing from RGB is inherently harder (Reviewer 3, 4).
   - The gSDF baseline is re-implemented with point cloud input, but details of this re-implementation are missing (Reviewer 1, Question 1; Reviewer 4, Question 4).
   - The Chamfer Distance values reported for gSDF differ from those in the original gSDF paper; the discrepancy needs explanation (Reviewer 1, Question 1; Reviewer 4, Question 7).
4. **Insufficient ablation studies**: 
   - Table 2 only ablates simple tactile feature fusion and provides only object-reconstruction metrics; hand pose metrics (MPJPE, PD, CIoU) are needed to compare with Table 1 (Reviewer 1, Question 4).
   - The role of DF-Field itself is not ablated; it is unclear which component contributes most to the improvement (Reviewer 2, 3).
   - Ablation of the design choices (e.g., why energy-based modeling is preferred) is missing (Reviewer 2).
5. **Presentation and writing issues**: 
   - Excessive and abrupt use of acronyms (DF-Field, ViTaM-D, VDT-Net, FO) without proper introduction; some acronyms are used only a few times (Reviewer 2).
   - The paper focuses on *what* is done but lacks explanation of *why* design choices were made and *how* they are motivated (Reviewer 2).
   - Minor mistakes: duplicate citation of “Learning joint reconstruction…” and improper capitalization in L305 (Reviewer 4).
6. **Real-world applicability unclear**: The paper does not explain the practical scenarios where both point cloud data and distributed tactile sensors would be available, nor does it discuss extension to real tactile sensors (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4 Question 10).
7. **Limitations of tactile representation**: 
   - The definition of DF-Field is introduced without theoretical justification from physical laws (Reviewer 3).
   - The tactile representation covers only the inner (palmar) side of the hand, neglecting contact on the dorsum side (Reviewer 4).
8. **Miscellaneous concerns**: 
   - The reconstructed object mesh in Fig. 4 appears coarser than gSDF/HOT objects despite similar SDF resolution; visual quality discrepancy needs explanation (Reviewer 1, Question 3).
   - The method predicts flow from time t-1 to t; if point clouds for time t are available, direct geometry prediction might be more intuitive (Reviewer 4, Question 2).
   - VDT-Net is fine-tuned per object category, while gSDF is not (Reviewer 4, Question 6); this could affect fairness.
   - Ground truth contact maps on the object surface are used for evaluation; it is unclear how these are obtained and whether predicted tactile distributions on the hand are evaluated (Reviewer 3, Question 2).
