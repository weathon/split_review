## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes constraint-conditioned actor-critic (CCAC) for offline safe RL. CCAC combines a constraint-conditioned variational autoencoder (CVAE) and a constraint-conditioned binary classifier to distinguish out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs, using the constraint budget as a conditioning label. The OOD detector \(v(s,a|\kappa)\) is then integrated into actor-critic updates via an overestimated cost Q-function to penalize unsafe OOD actions and enforce safety. The method is designed to handle varying constraint thresholds in a zero-shot manner. Empirical evaluations on the DSRL benchmark show CCAC achieves high rewards with low costs, outperforming existing methods. The paper includes theoretical justification (Theorem 4.1) and extensive ablation studies.

### Strengths

- **Well-written and clear**: All four reviewers agree the paper is well-written, easy to follow, and clearly motivates the problem and contributions (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- **Novel and effective method**: CCAC is intuitively straightforward and easy to implement (R4). It achieves state-of-the-art performance on several DSRL environments (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Strong adaptability and robustness**: The method demonstrates zero-shot adaptation to different cost budgets and robustness to distribution shifts and varying data quantities (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Comprehensive ablation studies**: Ablations clearly show the value of each component, validate constraint-aware behavior, and confirm robustness to OOD data and overestimated cost (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Good visualization and justification**: Section 5.3 provides experimental justification for using CVAE and clear visualizations of the overestimated cost Q-function (R2). The offline dataset visualizations are intuitive (R1).  
- **Interesting idea and technical clarity**: The core idea is interesting and technical details are easy to follow (R3). The paper provides theoretical analysis (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited evaluation environments**: Only 9 out of 30+ environments in the DSRL benchmark are used (R1, R4). More complex tasks (e.g., humanoid) are missing (R1).  
- **Insufficient seeds**: Only three random seeds are used; at least five are recommended for reliability (R1).  
- **Sample efficiency concern**: Figure 9 suggests the algorithm’s sample efficiency is not good (R1).  
- **Unclear OOD generation mechanism**: The description in lines 203–204 is insufficient. The paper should explain that \((s,a)\) pairs are generated via CVAE with various Gaussian noise vectors \(z\) and those with \(h<0.5\) are designated OOD (R2).  
- **Figure 1 requires elaboration**: The overview figure does not clearly describe inputs/outputs of each module, learning flow, or arrow meanings; the caption should include full descriptions (R2).  
- **Missing comparison with TREBI**: TREBI [2] also handles varying cost budgets in offline safe RL but is not compared (R2).  
- **Motivation for varying threshold is incomplete**: The claim that existing methods overlook varying thresholds (lines 46–53) is not fully justified; several prior works [1,2] address this, and the paper should explain their shortcomings (R2).  
- **Toned-down related work claim**: Line 98 states “these methods overlook the dependence of actions on the constraints,” but the cited works are offline RL (not safe RL); this statement should be softened (R2).  
- **Fundamental concern about OOD vs. out-of-budget (OOB) blending** (R3): The paper blurs the boundary between OOD and OOB. Labeling all OOD data as unsafe (cost lower bounded by \(\varepsilon\)) risks preventing generalization to unseen safe state-action pairs, which contradicts offline RL’s goal of improving beyond the dataset. The OOD label \(h(s,a|\kappa)\) is determined solely by whether cumulative cost exceeds the threshold; a label of 0 does not guarantee the sample is in-distribution, only that it is not out-of-budget. Consequently, the agent lacks value information for low-risk unseen pairs, reintroducing the original OOD problem. The approach may work only when high-risk actions also have low value, but its behavior on high-value, low-risk OOD actions is unclear. Thus, Theorem 4.1 (a CQL-like derivation) may not be meaningful (R3).  
- **Assumption of smooth reward/cost functions**: Using the trained CVAE to augment data and annotate with original cost/reward values introduces errors if the reward/cost functions are not smooth over the spatial space (R4).  
- **Misattribution of FISOR’s conservatism**: Line 323 attributes FISOR’s overconservative behavior to the use of hard constraints, but it is actually due to using the largest feasible region, which should reduce overconservatism (R4).  
- **Question on policy loss and penalty**: The policy is trained via Equation 9 using solely the overestimated \(Q_C\); the reviewer asks how this avoids overestimating actions beyond the dataset without a separate penalty term (R2).  
- **COptiDICE discrepancy**: Table 1 shows COptiDICE with significant constraint violations, contradicting its original paper’s results on similar tasks. The explanation in line 323 (biased estimation) is unconvincing because COptiDICE itself overestimates cost for OOD data (R2).  
- **Optional continuation of CVAE training**: Why not continue training the CVAE during policy training? The current separate training is not justified (R1).  
- **Intuition for zero-shot adaptation**: The paper provides empirical evidence but lacks an intuitive explanation for why CCAC can zero-shot adapt to different cost budgets (R1).  
- **Minority contrast**: Reviewer 3 is more critical than the others, raising the fundamental OOD/OOB concern and questioning the significance of the theoretical result. In contrast, Reviewer 4 is the most positive (score 8), praising the concept and results, but also noting the limited environment coverage and assumption smoothness issue.
