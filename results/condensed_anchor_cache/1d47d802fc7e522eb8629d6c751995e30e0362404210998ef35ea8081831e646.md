## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the MM1.5 family of multimodal LLMs (1B–30B, including 1B/3B MoE variants) built on MM1 by focusing on data curation across three training stages: large-scale pretraining, high-resolution continual pretraining with OCR/synthetic captions, and supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The work provides extensive empirical ablations on data mixtures, dynamic high-resolution image processing, and SFT dataset ratios, offering actionable insights for MLLM training. It also presents MM1.5-Video and MM1.5-UI variants. Reviewers agree that the paper is well-written and presents thorough experiments, but two reviewers (scores 6) note it lacks novel methodology or theoretical depth, resembling a technical report, while two (scores 8) find the findings novel, valuable, and impactful for the community. Concerns include selective comparisons, reproducibility (no open-source release), limited transferability of data-specific hyperparameters, and insufficient analysis of observed phenomena.

### Strengths
- Clarity and organization: well written, easy to follow (R1, R2, R4)
- Significance to a trending topic: data-centric analysis provides valuable empirical guidance for future MLLM research (R1, R2, R3)
- Thorough experiments: systematic exploration of data mixture ratios, training strategies, and model architecture across pretraining, continual pretraining, and SFT (R2, R3, R4)
- Specific actionable findings: e.g., optimal pretraining data ratio 50:10:40 (image-text, interleaved image-text, text-only) highlighting importance of text understanding; importance of high-resolution OCR data (R2, R4)
- Strong scaling and architecture insights: models from 1B to 30B, MoE variants (1B, 3B) show performance scaling and MoE outperforming dense counterparts, especially at 1B (R3, R4)
- Significant performance improvements over MM1 and several current SOTA models, particularly on text-rich, refer & ground, multi-image, and in-context learning tasks (R1, R3, R4)
- Specialized SFT variants (MM1.5-Video, MM1.5-UI) show strong results on video and UI benchmarks (R3)
- Detailed release of datasets, data mixture ratios, hyperparameters, and processing strategies (dynamic splitting, positional indicators, overview image placement) despite backbones not being publicly available (R4)

### Weaknesses
- Lack of novel methodology, tasks, or benchmarks; the work is a comprehensive ablation report using known techniques (MoE, Dynamic Res) without strong new insights compared to previous works (R1, R2, R3)
- No theoretical analysis or in-depth explanation for observed phenomena; simple assumptions/hypotheses are insufficient—e.g., why the optimal ratio of text-only data changes between pretraining and SFT, and if there is a theoretical guideline for determining optimal mixture between multimodal and text-only data (R2, R3)
- Data-specific hyperparameters from ablations may not be practical for other researchers due to dependence on specific dataset, model size, architecture, and training setup (R3)
- No open-sourcing of model weights, code, or data—limits reproducibility and practicality (R3)
- Selective comparisons in Tables 4, 7–11: results shown only against weaker models (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B, InternVL2-2B, Cambrian-34B) while their stronger family members and Qwen2-VL are omitted; this is not conducive to understanding field progress (R3)
- In Figure 2, Refer&Ground data appears to downgrade other performance categories; similar issues in Figure 5—raises question of whether MMBase average score is a fair metric across categories (R3)
- Ablation on skipping the second stage completely not provided (R1)
- Refer&Ground data requires 2× general purpose data; how to make it more data-efficient? (R1)
- Table 1 interpretation unclear: dynamic image splitting does not consistently outperform static splitting in comparable setups (n=10, 1440 tokens); static splitting works best overall, so why claim dynamic is better? (R1, R4)
- Final recipe uses positional indicators for sub-images even though Table 3 shows separators work better overall (R4)
- Presentation issues: rephrase “slight increase” vs “significantly improve” in section 2.2.1 (R4); bold best results in tables (R4); section ordering (continual pretraining before SFT would follow the recipe logically) (R4)
- Questions: confirm data ratio sampling rule for 1:2 general:science (R4); is dynamic splitting used in both continual pretraining and SFT? (R4); why are 7B models not compared with similarly sized open-source models like LLaVA 1.5 7B? (R4)
