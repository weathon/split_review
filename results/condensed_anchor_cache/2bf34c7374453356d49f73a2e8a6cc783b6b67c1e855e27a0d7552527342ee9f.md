## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Offline Decoupled Prioritized Resampling (ODPR), a plug-and-play method that resamples offline dataset transitions according to advantages (ODPR-A) or trajectory returns (ODPR-R) before applying a policy-constrained offline RL algorithm. The goal is to mitigate the equal treatment of good and poor actions under uniform sampling. Theoretical analysis claims the resampled distribution induces an improved behavior policy. Experiments on BC, TD3+BC, Onestep RL, CQL, and IQL across D4RL show performance gains for both ODPR variants.

### Strengths
- Clear presentation and easy-to-follow motivation (R1, R4).
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation across five algorithms and multiple D4RL domains (R1, R2, R3).
- The method is algorithm-agnostic, making it a widely applicable plug-in component (R1, R2).
- Introduces a novel perspective: prioritizing transitions to improve the behavior policy before constraint, with decoupled resampling for stability (R2).
- Provides theoretical analysis that attempts to justify the improvement in behavior policy (R2).
- Two practical implementations (advantage-based ODPR-A and return-based ODPR-R) trade off computation and performance (R2, R3).
- The insight that policy evaluation does not need to be prioritized is interesting (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient motivation and theoretical concerns** (R4, also echoed by R3):  
   - The core idea of resampling β to β' without any constraint can exacerbate distribution shift: β' and π may query many OOD states, and value approximation error on those states can be arbitrarily large (R4).  
   - The logic that prioritized resampling should lead to the best action (maximizing A(s,a)) is flawed because the distribution shift prevents using the pure strategy; the real problem is how to improve β without straying far from β, which is exactly what offline RL already tries to do (R4).  
   - The analysis in Section 3.2 assumes d_β'(s)=d_β(s), but the unconstrained transform makes this assumption invalid, so the guaranteed improvement over β is not established (R4).  
   - Consequently, the iterative resampling scheme (Section 3.4) appears unmotivated (R4).  
   - R3 questions whether the theoretical justification for iterative resampling is meaningful: importance sampling could achieve the same effect as explicit resampling, and the benefit of explicit resampling is unclear (R3).

2. **Lack of comparison against key related methods** (R1, R2, R3):  
   - Several existing reweighting/resampling baselines are missing: AW/RW (trajectory weighting), Top-k% sampling, BAIL (filtering data), UWAC (uncertainty weighting), and ReDS (heteroskedasticity support constraints) (R1, R2).  
   - AWAC also assigns higher weights to good transitions; no comparison is made and it is unclear why ODPR’s advantage-based reweighting would be better (R2).  
   - “Beyond Uniform Sampling” (cited by R3) proposes a very similar idea (importance sampling vs. explicit resampling) and already reports superior performance over ODPR-like schemes; ODPR should be compared directly to it (R3).  
   - Behavior-policy-independent methods (e.g., stabilizing off-policy Q-learning via BEAR, adversarially trained actor-critic) are not included; comparative results would help assess ODPR’s unique advantage (R1).

3. **Fairness of experimental comparisons** (R1, R2):  
   - In Table 3, AW/RW scores are taken directly from their original paper, but differences in codebase, hyperparameters, and offline RL algorithm settings make the comparison unfair (R1, R2).  
   - AW uses an advantage estimator of initial states, which is different from ODPR-R and RW; comparing ODPR-R with RW is essential (R2).  
   - ODPR-A hyperparameters (e.g., p_base, number of iterations) are tuned per domain, but AW/RW are not similarly optimized; performance gains may depend on tuning (R2).  
   - Need to rerun all baseline resampling methods in the same codebase with identical hyperparameter search effort to isolate ODPR’s benefit (R1, R2).

4. **Concerns about significance and practicality** (R1, R3, R4):  
   - The added complexity of learning an advantage function and weighting samples may not be justified by the observed performance gains; experiments on more realistic (non-D4RL) datasets are needed (R1).  
   - The method has many tunable components (weight function, p_base, iterations for ODPR-A) and poorly chosen hyperparameters can degrade performance; if online evaluation is expensive, this limits practicality (R3).  
   - Several reviewers suspect that careful hyperparameter tuning of the underlying offline RL algorithm (e.g., BC loss weight in TD3+BC, pessimism level in CQL) or using larger model sizes could match ODPR’s performance without resampling (R1, R4, citing Tarasov et al. 2023).  
   - Eliminating suboptimal transitions may hurt generalization; results on generalization are not provided (R4).

5. **Missing analyses and ablations** (R1, R2, R3, R4):  
   - The effect of return-based resampling on state distribution is not analyzed; it may reduce effective state coverage – the effective dataset size after resampling should be reported (R3).  
   - How does ODPR behave when the offline behavior policy has uniformly similar and poor performance? (R2)  
   - Why is another resampling/weighting procedure needed when regularized offline RL (e.g., AWR) already implicitly does the same thing? (R4)  
   - Aggregate results are reported as sum of scores; more reliable metrics (IQM, probability of improvement) should be used to confirm that the performance gain holds (R1).

6. **Contribution novelty** (R3, R1):  
   - Resampling methods similar to ODPR have already been proposed in existing literature (e.g., “Beyond Uniform Sampling”), limiting the novelty of this work (R3).  
   - The overall contribution is rated fair or poor by three reviewers (R1, R3, R4) due to lack of compelling evidence and unresolved theoretical issues.
