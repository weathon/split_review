## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes 3DAxisPrompt, a visual prompting method that adds 3D coordinate axes and SAM-generated masks to multi-view renderings of point clouds, enabling GPT-4o to perform 3D grounding/reasoning tasks (indoor/outdoor localization, route planning, robot action prediction). Evaluations are conducted on 20 scenes each from ShapeNet, ScanNet, FMB, and nuScene. Reviewers diverge: one rates the work highly (6), finding the method novel, effective, and well-evaluated; three others rate it low (3), citing limited contribution, missing baselines, and weak scientific validation.

### Strengths
- The proposed visual prompting method (3DAxisPrompt) is novel and enhances the 3D understanding capabilities of MLLMs without additional training, directly adapting to localization, route planning, and action prediction (Reviewer 4).
- The paper explores a range of visual prompt formats (coordinate axes, masks, bounding boxes, color highlights, marks) for 3D grounding and reasoning, tested on several self-designed tasks (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments across multiple datasets (ShapeNet, ScanNet, FMB, nuScene) demonstrate the method’s effectiveness (Reviewer 4).
- The methodology is clearly stated and reproducible (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is well-written and lucidly presented, with a comprehensive related work section (Reviewer 2).
- The prompting method is straightforward and easy to apply, and efficacy is shown on a limited set of scenarios (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited contribution and lack of scientific analysis:** The method is simple, but the paper does not provide an in-depth investigation into *why* MLLMs can perform 3D grounding with this prompting; it reads more like a technical report than a research contribution (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The term “3D grounding ability” is overstated given the limited scope of tasks (Reviewer 3). The primary task reduces to asking the model to output coordinates of a point given a Cartesian system, which may not demonstrate genuine spatial understanding (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons on standard benchmarks:** There are no baseline methods in Tables 1 and 2, making the numbers hard to interpret (Reviewer 2). The paper lacks comparisons with existing 3D grounding methods (e.g., LLM-Grounder [1], PIVOT) and does not evaluate on widely accepted benchmarks such as ScanRefer, Referit3D, or 3D QA datasets (Reviewers 1, 3). Without such comparisons, the work fails to demonstrate an advantage over state-of-the-art approaches (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation issues:**
    - No quantitative ablation of the 3D axis component itself (Reviewer 2).
    - Only tested on a single MLLM (GPT-4o); generalization to other models (e.g., LLaVA-Next) is unknown (Reviewers 1, 2).
    - Evaluation is limited to 20 scenes per dataset, which is insufficient for scientific significance (Reviewer 2).
    - The pipeline requires external models (SAM) to generate masks, further complicating the assessment of MLLM’s innate 3D ability (Reviewer 4).
- **Axis placement and view selection not systematically studied:** It is unclear how the 3D axes are set (e.g., fixed origin, alignment with scene boundaries). In real-world settings, axes may not be perfectly aligned with the point cloud; no study of this effect or optimal view selection is provided. For example, Figure 3-3 shows Y=7.08, which appears inaccurate compared to other aligned outputs (Reviewers 3, 4). Could zooming or adjusting viewpoints improve accuracy? (Reviewer 3)
- **Practical shortcomings:** Runtime is not efficient enough for practical applications in planning and localization (Reviewer 4). The method would benefit from demonstrations in real-life scenarios (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited scope of coordinate systems:** Only Cartesian axes are tested; the paper does not explore other coordinate systems (polar, cylindrical, log-scaled) that could provide deeper insight into the model’s spatial reasoning (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing in-depth analysis and additional visualizations:** The authors should provide more visualizations (Reviewer 1) and restate the innovativeness and necessity of the method, explaining how it differs from similar approaches (Reviewer 1).
- **Typo:** Line 349: "rend" should be "trend" (Reviewer 2).
