## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers agree the paper tackles unsupervised sequential disentanglement, a practical and challenging problem, by extending diffusion autoencoders (DiffAE) to video with an elegant probabilistic graph model that splits latents into static (time-invariant) and dynamic (time-variant) factors. The method shows strong quantitative results on reconstruction and swapping on chosen benchmarks, and the reliance on model structure rather than auxiliary losses is noted as interesting (R1, R2, R3). However, there is substantial disagreement: R1 (score 6) finds the approach novel and effective, while R2, R3, R4 (score 5 or 5+5) raise major concerns about limited technical novelty, insufficient evaluation scope, and missing comparisons. R4 is the most critical, questioning the problem definition, overclaiming, and implicit factorization assumptions. All reviewers note issues with presentation and missing details.

### Strengths

- The probabilistic graph model factorization is natural and elegant, and the network design is simple but effective (R1, R2).
- Strong empirical results on reconstruction, swapping, and generation are demonstrated on three practical datasets (face/motion datasets) (R1, R2, R3).
- The approach obtains disentangled representations by relying on model structure rather than auxiliary losses, which is interesting and contrasts with prior work (R2).
- The method extends diffusion-based disentanglement to sequential video data, enabling video editing applications; this is an interesting application scenario (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Technical novelty is limited.** The disentanglement function is decoupled from the diffusion module and instead extracted via LSTM in the semantic encoder—a technique well-studied in prior works (e.g., disentangled sequential VAE, S3VAE). This effectively makes the contribution a conditional diffusion with post-hoc disentanglement rather than a fundamentally new unsupervised disentanglement framework. The paper overclaims its contribution (R3, R4).
- **Evaluation scope is too narrow and lacks generalization evidence.** Experiments only use short, single-person, structured video datasets (face/motion) where static/dynamic factors are already well-defined. Generalization to longer sequences, multi-person interactions, dynamic backgrounds, varying viewpoints, or other modalities (e.g., audio, multi-object video) is not shown. Standard benchmark datasets from prior sequential disentanglement works are missing (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Missing crucial comparisons and ablations.** (1) It is unclear whether disentanglement comes from the factorization or from the size difference between s (large) and d (small) (R1). (2) No comparison to DiffAE applied frame-by-frame for swapping (R3). (3) No reconstruction results with only one latent factor (e.g., keep dynamic, set static to zero) to demonstrate disentanglement (R3). (4) No complexity or time-cost analysis (e.g., real-time factor) to support claims of fast sampling (R3). (5) Lack of comparison to recent diffusion works that demonstrate intrinsic disentangled features (e.g., [a,b,c]; R4); relevant animation-generation works (Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate) and image2video methods with similar application scenarios are not discussed (R3).
- **Problem definition, formulation, and assumptions are problematic.** The paper lacks a clear problem definition: it begins with high-level representation disentanglement but shifts to generative modeling without explaining the formulation. Examples in Figure 1 (gender modification) are misaligned with the claimed disentanglement goal and are inappropriate (R4). The formulation in Section 4.1 assumes static and dynamic factors are independent without justification; Eq. (4) is unclear (V is mentioned in Line 185 but never defined) (R4).
- **Presentation issues.** The Introduction gives a skewed picture of VAE/GAN image generation by citing outdated works (2016, 2018) and ignoring progress; it should focus on limitations for sequential disentanglement rather than general VAE/GAN flaws (R2). A citation error is present (line 651 incorrect author name) (R2). Derivation of the training objective from score matching is not fully included, though it would be beneficial (R1).
