## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MultiRC, a framework for joint anomaly prediction and detection in time series. It integrates reconstructive and contrastive learning using a multi-scale structure with an adaptive dominant period mask to handle variable reaction times, and generates negative samples to maintain training momentum. Evaluation on seven benchmark datasets reports state-of-the-art performance on both tasks.

### Strengths
- The problem of joint anomaly prediction and detection, especially with variable reaction times, is interesting and offers a new perspective to the community (R2, R4).
- The multi-scale structure with adaptive dominant period mask and the generative strategy for creating negative samples are novel components (R4).
- Good numerical results on anomaly detection tasks (R1).
- Experiments are conducted on seven datasets (R2).
- Thorough experimental design includes sensitivity, scale, and ablation studies; the codebase is well-structured and accessible (R4).
- Writing is clear and easy to understand; figures and tables are beautiful and clear (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Problem definition and task distinction**:  
  * The definitions of “anomaly” and “precursor” are vague; the anomaly prediction task may be fundamentally identical to standard anomaly detection if anomaly labels are accurate (R5). A formal definition is needed, and the reaction time setting is confusing (R1).  
  * The difference between precursor and real anomaly is not clear (R2).  
  * There is a potential error in the definition of the antecedent range (t-r: r) in the anomaly prediction task (R3).  
  * It is suggested to reformulate the problem as a pattern prediction problem (R1) and to use the UCR dataset with accurately labeled anomalies (R5).  
  * The paper lacks dataset analysis and visualized examples to support the research (R5).

- **Missing related work**:  
  Several important works on anomaly precursor detection and early anomaly detection are not cited: “Unsupervised Time Series Anomaly Prediction with Importance-based Generative Contrastive Learning” (Kai Zhao et al.), “dCNN/dCAM: anomaly precursors discovery in multivariate time series with deep convolutional neural networks” (Paul Boniol et al.), “Deep Multi-Instance Contrastive Learning with Dual Attention for Anomaly Precursor Detection” (Dongkuan Xu et al.), and “Early anomaly detection in time series: a hierarchical approach for predicting critical health episodes” (Vitor Cerqueira et al.) (R2). R5 also notes insufficient investigation of related work and limited methodology innovation.

- **Writing and presentation issues**:  
  * Full metric names are not provided (e.g., Aff-F1, Aff-P, Aff-R) (R2).  
  * Figure 2 is too complex and not self-contained (R2).  
  * Table 2 does not highlight the best results (R2).  
  * The anomaly prediction module is not distinctly outlined within the framework (R3).  
  * Prior work is inaccurately represented: the description of Anomaly Transformer overlooks “prior-association” and “series-association”; the description of DCdetector omits its multi-scale sampling structure and approach (R3).  
  * Many details in formulas and algorithms are unexplained or contain errors: the subscript range in x_{t-h-1:t-h-1} is incorrect; it is unclear how patch x_p maps multiple scales to different timesteps; Formula 6 lacks formal definitions for c and i; the generation of x_{n,p} is unclear, and definitions of n and p are ambiguous; the settings for lambda_Con and lambda_Rec in (11) are not explained; variables h and f are introduced but not utilized in the model section; the specific differences between anomaly prediction during training/testing and anomaly detection are not explained (R5). Overall, the writing quality is unsatisfactory (R5).

- **Experimental and evaluation concerns**:  
  * Results on all metrics (F1, Precision, Recall, AUC) should be shown for all datasets (R2).  
  * More showcases with different types of precursors are needed to demonstrate effectiveness (R2).  
  * Suspicious performance data: the PAD method’s F1 score on the SWaT dataset (0.562) is lower than both its Precision (0.671) and Recall (0.557), which is counterintuitive; verification is requested (R3).  
  * The source code uses window sizes of 64, which is much shorter than the actual abnormal window size (>1000) (R5).  
  * More details are needed on the construction operations for anomaly samples (e.g., whether different construction methods and intensities were used for different datasets) (R3).  
  * Instance normalization may produce false negative results when abnormal time series segments are shifted from the original range (R5).

- **Model complexity and computational costs**:  
  * The paper does not address the computational time and resource demands added by components such as negative sample generation (R4).  
  * The dual-branch, multi-scale structure with asymmetric encoder-decoder networks and generative strategies may be overly complex, leading to difficulties in model training, interpretability, and implementation (R4).  
  * With channel independence, the computational cost for multivariate time series may be high; it is unclear whether dealing with multivariate data is much slower (R5).

- **Anonymous URL issue**: The code repository’s Google Drive link displays the owner’s photo and username, which may violate the anonymous submission policy (R2).

**Minority contrast**: While R4 praises the novelty of the multi-scale structure and negative sample generation, R5 finds the methodology limited in innovation and the related work investigation insufficient. R3 points out inaccuracies in prior work description but acknowledges clear writing. Scores are split: two reviewers gave 3, one gave 5, and two gave 6.
