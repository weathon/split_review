## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a unified framework to examine the trade-off between diversity and consistency in LLM ensembles, introducing a consistency score for gating (Gated MoA / GMoA) and a mixture refinement algorithm. These insights are used to design the Dynamic Mixture of Agents (DMoA) inference-time ensembling strategy, which achieves state-of-the-art results on the Big Bench Hard (BBH) benchmark. The work also introduces the EigenDivergence metric, based on EigenScore, for measuring semantic divergence across model outputs. The core hypothesis is that different tasks require different levels of ensemble diversity/consistency.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses a compelling and nuanced problem—balancing consistency and diversity within LLM ensembles—which is fundamental as different tasks often prioritize one over the other (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The paper is well structured with clear, precise definitions of both DMoA and GMoA frameworks, enabling a thorough understanding of the distinctions and innovations (Reviewer 1).
- DMoA achieves state-of-the-art results on the Big Bench Hard (BBH) benchmark, demonstrating efficacy across challenging tasks (Reviewer 1).
- The analysis is comprehensive and provides useful intuitions on what may or may not work in a mixture-of-models framework (Reviewer 2).
- The introduction and analysis of the EigenDivergence metric, based on hallucination-detection methods and using external embeddings, is a valuable contribution (Reviewer 3).
- The tasks evaluated are comprehensive, covering a wide range of abilities (Reviewer 2).
- The takeaways on diversity and consistency are clear and well presented (Reviewer 3).
- The unified dynamic mixture-of-agent framework effectively addresses the diversity–consistency trade-off across tasks (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Computational cost analysis is missing.** No breakdown is provided for the number of forward passes per model, overhead of the consistency scoring mechanism, cost of dynamic model selection, or latency implications. This is essential for a balanced view of performance gains vs. computational expense, especially compared to other ensemble strategies and CoT approaches (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **The purpose and value of the first experiment (Section 4.1 / divergent filtering / GMoA) are questionable.** The result is nuanced: divergent filtering only slightly improves four out of five reasoning tasks and decreases performance on three tasks. One reviewer argues that the insights drawn from this experiment are weak:
  - Insight 1 (“LLM ensembles outperform individual models regardless of divergence filtering”) does not add value because it holds “regardless” of filtering.
  - Insight 2 (“removing information can improve task-specific performance”) is based on an improvement in four tasks but a decrease in three.
  - Insight 3 (“performance varies when semantic diversity is altered”) is trivial and does not reveal a meaningful pattern (Reviewer 2). Another reviewer (Reviewer 3) liked the EigenDivergence analysis, so there is disagreement on the value of this part.
- **A key claim in Section 4.3 about maximizing diversity is problematic.** Removing the two most consistent outputs does not necessarily maximize diversity (it could remove two similar outputs that are still distinctive from others) and also removes outputs that may be more correct, biasing the result. This undermines Insight 1 in Section 4.4 (that maximizing semantic diversity harms performance) (Reviewer 2).
- **Sections 3.3 and 4.4 are unclear and appear disconnected** from the main contribution. These sections require significant rewriting to clarify how they tie into the overall paper (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **The design of mixture optimization leads to domination by a single model** (Fig. 3, left) because the greedy algorithm replaces the model with the lowest delta with the one having the highest delta. This may sacrifice diversity for marginal per-task performance gains, raising concerns about generalizability (Reviewer 4).
- **EigenScore extension to multiple models has inherent limitations.** The sentence-embedding spaces of different models are not aligned during pre-training/fine-tuning, so they do not inhabit the same representational space. The effectiveness of any projection and potential information loss need more thorough examination (Reviewer 4).
- **DMoA requires individual search/optimization of MoA structures per task**, using divergence filtering and mixture optimization. This is costly, requires additional task-specific datasets for evaluation, and is heavily dependent on the final aggregation model: performance drops substantially when Claude-3.5-Sonnet is not used as the aggregator (90.20 vs. 83.63 normalized accuracy on BBH) (Reviewer 4). This dependency raises questions about practical applicability in resource-constrained settings (Reviewer 4).
- **DMoA is not tested on the seven tasks used in the first two experiments (AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, GSM8K, MATH, CSQA, ARC-C, ARC-E).** This would be a natural progression and is missed (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **No critical focus on examining intermediate reasoning.** While semantic diversity is a focus, a deeper look at how semantic diversity manifests in reasoning (e.g., examples where correct reasonings from multiple models are still judged semantically diverse) is missing. Appendix H is a good start but a controlled experiment is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **The analysis of multiple sentence embeddings in Appendix F.2 is insufficient** (the 0.78 number is unclear without context about variance or embedding model choice) (Reviewer 3).
- **No justification is given for not using closed-source models**, especially since EigenDivergence does not require model weights (Reviewer 3).
