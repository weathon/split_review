## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a gradient-based bilevel optimization method for hyperparameter optimization. It accumulates short-horizon gradients from each inner optimization step to approximate hypergradients, aiming to address memory and convergence limitations of existing approaches such as reverse-mode differentiation and implicit differentiation. The authors provide theoretical analysis showing that the proposed hypergradient is a sufficient descent direction under Lipschitz smoothness conditions, and they validate the method empirically on few-shot meta-learning and data hyper-cleaning tasks.

### Strengths
- **Problem significance:** Bilevel optimization for hyperparameter tuning, meta-learning, and reinforcement learning is an important and active area (Reviewer 1).
- **Originality:** The method offers a novel approximation strategy by aggregating greedy (short-horizon) gradients from each inner step and leveraging the Lipschitz smooth parameter for stability (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewers 2 and 3 specifically highlight that accumulating these gradients is a new way to balance memory and accuracy.
- **Theoretical contribution:** The paper proves that the hypergradient is a sufficient descent direction and provides convergence guarantees maintaining Lipschitz continuity (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Empirical validation:** Experiments on few-shot meta-learning and data hyper-cleaning show that the proposed method improves performance over baselines (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 notes that the empirical section is clear and metrics/comparisons are transparent.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing approximation error bounds:** No precise error bound is given between the proposed hypergradient (Eq. 6) and the exact hypergradient (Eq. 5). The only bound provided is a rough upper bound on the Hessian. Reviewers 2 and 3 explicitly request this bound; Reviewer 1 also raises concerns about error accumulation in high-dimensional settings and asks for more detailed analysis of how the approximation affects optimization performance.
- **Scalability and cost analysis:** The algorithm still requires T Jacobian-vector products, scaling with the number of inner steps, which likely limits large-scale applicability (Reviewer 2). The paper claims large-scale capability but only uses small datasets (e.g., FashionMNIST) (Reviewers 2, 3). Missing runtime and memory comparisons with baselines (Reviewer 1, 2). Reviewer 3 requests experiments with different T to demonstrate the choice of γ and more large-scale datasets.
- **Outdated and narrow baselines:** Compared methods are mostly from before 2020 (Reviewer 2). The paper should compare with more recent methods such as SOBA (Dagréou et al., 2022) and FDS (Micaelli & Storkey, 2021) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also notes that the connection to existing work needs clearer delineation.
- **Restrictive assumptions:** The upper-level objective is assumed to be strongly convex, whereas in many bilevel optimization settings it is nonconvex (Reviewer 3). This limits the method’s applicability.
- **Limited novelty and theoretical depth:** One reviewer (R1) found the novelty moderate and the theoretical results less robust compared to influential prior work. Another reviewer (R3) noted that the Hessian approximation technique appears in Lee et al. (2021), reducing the contribution. Both R1 and R3 suggest that a more direct comparison with established theories is needed to demonstrate added value.
- **Presentation issues:** Citation format needs checking; Equation (1) has a typo (should be \(\alpha^* =\) not \(\alpha^*-\)); line 200 is missing a figure number (Reviewer 3).
