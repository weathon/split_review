## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper identifies exposure bias in diffusion models—caused by score estimation and discretization errors—and proposes a prompt learning framework that trains a lightweight prompt prediction model to compensate for this bias at each sampling step. The model is trained using VE SDE to simulate exposure bias and includes a time-dependent weighting function. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CelebA, and ImageNet 256×256 across various diffusion model architectures show improvements in FID and IS with only ~5% additional sampling overhead.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-motivated, well-articulated, and easy to follow; the insight is interesting and the method is simple, effective, and can be easily integrated with various diffusion model architectures.
- The authors provide theoretical analysis supporting the approach, making the paper more believable and theoretically sound.
- The method addresses a challenging problem (exposure bias) with wide application scenarios; the idea of revisiting the training-inference score function gap and proposing a fine-tuning framework is novel.
- Extensive experiments are conducted on multiple traditional benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet 256×256) across diverse diffusion models (including ADM and Latent Diffusion), demonstrating significant improvements in FID and IS.
- The prompt prediction model introduces negligible time overhead (5%), showing practical value.

### Weaknesses

- **Visual improvements are subtle and require more comparisons.** Several reviewers noted that improvements in Figures 4, 5, 6 (supplementary) and Fig. 7 are not pronounced; the magnitude of improvement relative to added complexity warrants further investigation. More images for intuitive comparison (beyond Fig. 7) and visualization to illustrate the gap between the true Gaussian kernel and its modeling counterpart are needed. (R1, R2/R3, R4, R5)
- **Architecture and naming of the prompt prediction model are unclear.** The architecture of the prompt learning network is not described; please include module architecture or network detail in supplementary materials. The title "prompt learning" appears more like noise learning; the relationship between the proposed method and prompt learning needs clarification. (R1, R2/R3)
- **Training data and computational overhead are not fully justified.** It is unclear how the training data for the prompt learning module is generated and whether it is distinct from the diffusion model training data, affecting generalizability. With batch size 1024 and 150k iterations for EDM (Tab. 8), ~100M data samples are used; for a lightweight prompt model with fixed DM parameters, why are so many samples needed? Would generating 1024×1024 images require even more data and resources? Further analysis of the effect of the number of parameters of the Anti-Bias Prompt Model on inference performance is needed. Did the authors try using LoRA to minimize the difference in Eq. 12, which could reduce overhead? (R1, R2/R3, R5)
- **Missing comparisons with related methods and modern T2I models.** The selection of baseline models is relatively simple; would exposure bias become less significant with updated model architectures and larger training data? The paper uses VE SDE to get x_{t+1} to simulate exposure bias, but hat{x}_t from the prompt model is not Gaussian noise; this approach is similar to methods like [1] (Common Diffusion Noise Schedules and Sample Steps are Flawed) that mitigate training-sampling discrepancy—any comparisons? Experiments are limited to traditional benchmarks; without popular T2I models (e.g., SDXL), the practical value of this paper is limited. (R1, R2/R3)
- **Equation 7’s representation of exposure bias is incomplete.** The authors claim Φ(hat{x}_t, x_t) represents exposure bias, but only discretization error is found, not the score estimation error—please clarify. (R2/R3)
- **Missing details on NEFs.** The paper lacks references or calculation methods for NEFs, making it hard to reproduce results and compare with other works. (R1)
- **Table 9 should be moved to the main paper.** This table is important for illustrating the efficiency of the algorithm. (R5)
- **Code is not provided.** Code, even a simple version for CIFAR-10, is important for reproducibility. (R5)
- **Table 4 needs clarification.** Numbers 10, 20, 50 in Table 4 are unclear; what metrics do they reflect? Modify the table or provide more details in the caption. (R2/R3)
- **Diversity of generations could be affected.** (R1—question/weakness overlap)
