## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes G‑SFormer, a 2D‑to‑3D human pose lifting method that combines a Part‑based Adaptive Graph Neural Network (spatial encoding) with a Frameset‑based Skipped Transformer (temporal encoding/decoding). It also introduces parameter‑free data‑completion strategies. On Human3.6M, MPI‑INF‑3DHP and HumanEva, G‑SFormer achieves competitive MPJPE while using substantially fewer FLOPs (total) and parameters than many prior works. Ablations verify that the skipped attention and adaptive GNN are beneficial. However, several reviewers raise serious concerns about the fairness of the efficiency comparison, the lack of quantitative robustness evidence, incremental novelty, missing comparisons with recent efficient methods, and insufficient exploration of design choices.

### Strengths

- Well‑structured, clearly written presentation (R1, R3, R4, R6).
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies on three widely‑used datasets (Human3.6M, MPI‑INF‑3DHP, HumanEva) (R1, R5).
- G‑SFormer achieves competitive or near‑state‑of‑the‑art accuracy while having a fraction of the parameters and total FLOPs of many existing methods (R1, R2, R4, R6).
- Multiple model sizes (G‑SFormer‑B/S/T) allow accuracy‑efficiency trade‑offs (R2).
- Ablations confirm the benefits of the skipped transformer and the adaptive GNN (R2).
- The related‑work section is comprehensive and includes comparisons with latest works (R2).

### Weaknesses

1. **Unfair efficiency comparison / overclaim.**  
   G‑SFormer uses a **seq‑2‑frame** pipeline – it estimates the 3D pose of only the center frame from a 2D sequence. In contrast, many compared methods (MixSTE, MotionAGFormer, KTPFormer, etc.) are **seq‑2‑seq** and estimate the full 3D sequence. The paper’s claim of “around 1% computational cost” is therefore misleading. When computing **FLOPs/Frame** (a standard metric, e.g. used by MotionAGFormer), G‑SFormer is *less* efficient than those seq‑2‑seq methods. A concrete comparison from Reviewer 6:

   | Method          | Pipeline  | FLOPs (M) | FLOPs/Frame (M) | MPJPE |
   |-----------------|-----------|-----------|-----------------|-------|
   | MixSTE          | seq2seq   | 278076    | 1144            | 40.9  |
   | STCFormer       | seq2seq   | 156392    | 643             | 40.5  |
   | MotionBERT      | seq2seq   | 349434    | 1438            | 38.2  |
   | MotionAGFormer  | seq2seq   | 156492    | 644             | 38.4  |
   | KTPFormer       | seq2seq   | 278119    | 1144            | 40.1  |
   | **G‑SFormer**   | seq2frame | 2366      | **2366**        | 40.5  |

   Moreover, to estimate a continuous block of 243 frames, seq‑2‑seq methods process exactly those 243 frames, while seq‑2‑frame methods additionally require 121 frames before and after the block (R6). The paper should report FLOPs/Frame and discuss this trade‑off (R5, R6). The inference time comparison (wall‑clock) is also missing (R2, R4).

2. **Robustness claim is not substantiated.**  
   The title and abstract claim robustness to noisy 2D joints, but no quantitative experiment is provided. Multiple reviewers (R1, R4) request a controlled noise injection (e.g., adding Gaussian noise to input keypoints, as in PoseFormerV2) and a comparison of performance degradation with other methods. The motivation from the architecture alone is insufficient (R1). Visual results in the appendix are qualitative only (R1, R4).

3. **Limited novelty and incremental contribution.**  
   - Combining GNN and Transformer for 3D HPE is already done by MotionAGFormer, which outperforms G‑SFormer in both accuracy (38.4 vs 40.5 MPJPE) and efficiency (R5, R6).  
   - Part‑based body modeling is explored in prior works (e.g., MTF‑Transformer, references [7‑9]) (R3, R5, R6).  
   - Skipped self‑attention (reducing token count) resembles earlier efficient methods: Deciwatch (R3), HoT (R6), and PoseFormerV2 (R1). The statement that “none of them cut the quadratic overhead of self‑attention” (L78‑80) is contradicted by HoT and Deciwatch (R3, R6).  
   - The contribution is largely incremental and limited to the 2D‑to‑3D lifting community (R2).

4. **Missing comparisons with recent efficient methods.**  
   The paper does not compare against MotionAGFormer, HoT, flash‑attention, or state‑space models that also target efficient 3D pose estimation (R4, R5). Efficient lifting methods (PoseFormerV2, Strided Transformer, Uplift & Upsample) are not discussed in Related Work (R1, R3).

5. **Data completion and positional encoding yield marginal gains.**  
   - The data‑completion strategies improve MPJPE by only ~0.2 mm in Table 5, which may be noise and may not generalize (R1, R2).  
   - Sinusoidal positional encoding (SPE) also shows tiny gains in Table 3 (R2).  
   - Table 5 lacks the ablation “w/o SPE + w/o DR” and a comparison with raw data expansion (Fig. 4c) (R3).  
   - Questions remain about the advantage of SPE over learnable PE or rotary PE (R3).

6. **Only studio datasets evaluated.**  
   Experiments are limited to Human3.6M, MPI‑INF‑3DHP, and HumanEva – all controlled settings. The method should be tested on in‑the‑wild datasets such as 3DPW or EMDB to demonstrate generalization (R2).

7. **Presentation and formatting issues.**  
   - Figures have **unreadably small font sizes**; vector graphics (PDF) are recommended (R2, R3, R6).  
   - Figure 1(a) should annotate the number of frames used by each model (R3).  
   - Typos: L102 “sate of the art”, L262 “.:”, L350 “matrics → metrics” (R2).  
   - Tables 3 and 4 are hard to read (R2).

8. **Part‑based GNN claimed to avoid predefined skeleton, but uses human priors.**  
   Splitting the body into parts (e.g., trunk, arms) inherently encodes skeletal prior. To support the claim of not relying on predefined topology, the authors should test with a random partition of joints (R3). The impact of different partition schemes (e.g., each joint as a separate part, overlapping joints) is not explored (R3).

9. **Insufficient ablations for design choices.**  
   - Table 6 lacks experiments with **m=1** using Spatial‑MLP and joint‑wise GCN to isolate the effect of skip attention (R3). A pure‑transformer joint‑modeling baseline is also needed (R3).  
   - The method of partitioning the body is not varied (R3).  
   - AMASS pre‑training yields only moderate improvement and its usage is not explained clearly; the paper should analyze why (R3).

10. **Missing analysis of skipped transformer generalization.**  
    Would replacing the naive transformer in prior methods (e.g., PoseFormer) with the skipped transformer also improve efficiency? This experiment is not conducted (R1).  
    - The comparison of attention maps in Appendix A.4 uses P‑STMO; more recent methods (e.g., MotionAGFormer) should be used instead (R6).  
    - Data padding: is the maximum padding T/2 frames? The paper should clarify (R3).
