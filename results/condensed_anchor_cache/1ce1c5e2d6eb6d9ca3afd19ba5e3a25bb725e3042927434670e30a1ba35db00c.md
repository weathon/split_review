## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes QA-LoRA, a modification to QLoRA that uses group-wise quantization to allow low-rank matrices to be added directly in low-bit tensors, eliminating the need for post-training quantization or conversion to FP16 during inference. It is claimed to be simple to implement (few lines of code) and achieves better memory efficiency and accuracy compared to QLoRA with post-training quantization. Experiments are conducted on LLaMA and LLaMA2 families.

### Strengths
- Addresses a significant issue in QLoRA: eliminates the need for separate post-training quantization (which drops accuracy) and avoids converting to FP16 during inference, resolving the mismatch between fine-tuning and inference (R1, R2).
- Simple implementation: only two lines of code modification required (R1).
- Thorough evaluation: covers competitive methods and diverse model architectures (LLaMA, LLaMA2) across multiple scenarios; experiments are convincing and show memory efficiency and accuracy gains (R1, R2).
- Enhances memory efficiency over state-of-the-art while preserving accuracy (R2).
- Good paper organization, presentation, and references (R3).
- Proposed method has sufficient novelty (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Reasoning behind the method is unclear: why all \(c_{ij}\) should be equal (the main motivation for group-wise quantization) is not well explained (R1). One reviewer (score 5, R1) found the explanation insufficient, while the other reviewers (scores 8, 6) considered the method novel but also noted missing details.
- The terms “degree of freedom of quantization” and “degree of freedom of adaptation” are new and undefined; they should be explicitly defined before claiming they are increased or managed by the proposed method (R1).
- In Algorithm 1, the function `merge_with_quantization` is defined but never used (R1).
- Page 6 is a table of results with little interpretation; the presentation is hard to read—consider a graph instead (R1).
- Table 2 shows QA-LoRA has nearly 2× fewer parameters than QLoRA, which seems like an unfair comparison. The time in hours for fine-tuning with similar parameter counts should be reported (R1).
- Section 3.3’s explanation of why rank degenerates to 1 is not comprehensive (R1).
- QA-LoRA introduces a hyper-parameter \(L\) (group size) that requires additional optimization; it is unclear if it can be selected without tuning (R2).
- Larger models (30B–60B) where the method would be most impactful are not discussed (R2).
- Figure 3 legend has a typo: “A-LoRA” should be “QA-LoRA” (R2).
- Parameter offsets in experiments are not properly accounted for:
  - Group-wise quantization adds additional scaling factors whose size should be explicitly stated (e.g., in MB/GB), as they contribute to overall memory footprint (R3).
  - The reduction in low-rank matrix size should be quantified in number of parameters, not just described qualitatively (R3).
- The ablation study only examines group size; \(D_{\text{int}}\) (internal dimension) is also part of the accuracy–model-size tradeoff and should be varied to find the lowest viable \(D_{\text{int}}\) compared to vanilla LoRA (R3).
