## Merged Review

### Summary
MetaDD is a plugin framework designed to enhance the cross-architecture generalization of dataset distillation (DD). It partitions features into meta features (common across architectures) and heterogeneous features (architecture-specific), then uses an architecture-invariant loss with GradCAM-based feature alignment to increase meta features and reduce heterogeneous ones. MetaDD is low-memory, seamlessly integrable into existing DD methods, and achieves improved performance with reduced memory consumption.

### Strengths
- MetaDD addresses the critical, rarely explored limitation of cross-architecture generalization in dataset distillation, opening a new direction.
- The key innovation is integrating class activation maps (CAMs) into DD algorithms to analyze and align feature distributions across architectures, enhancing interpretability and generalizability.
- MetaDD is a modular, plug-in component with low memory consumption that can be seamlessly integrated into existing DD methods without altering their core mechanisms.
- The method is supported by robust empirical evidence and thorough analysis; the analysis of meta and heterogeneous features provides strong motivation.
- MetaDD improves transferability of distilled data, achieving higher performance on unseen architectures (e.g., on Distilled Tiny-Imagenet with Sre2L (50 IPC), cross-architecture accuracy reaches 30.1%, surpassing GLaD by 1.7%).
- The use of CAMs visually demonstrates feature preferences of different architectures, making the approach intuitive.
- The paper is well-organized, with clear methodology enabling reproducibility.
- Despite introducing multiple auxiliary networks, the method maintains memory efficiency.
- The approach increases meta features and reduces heterogeneous features, balancing the feature set for model training.
- The authors present a balanced assessment, acknowledging both strengths and potential limitations.

### Weaknesses
- **Performance gains are modest and sometimes within variance**: One reviewer (R1) finds improvements significant, but other reviewers (R2, R3, R4) note that improvements are often marginal and could be due to variance. In several groups the improvement is negligible. For example, in Table 1(a), *Sre2L + MetaDD* performs worse than *Sre2L+GLaD* on GoogleNet and ViT-B-16, yet it is incorrectly bolded. The ablation study shows similar trends when introducing \(L_{ai}\).
- **Inappropriate comparison selection**: The paper compares MetaDD with Dream, which is not designed for cross-architecture generalization. More persuasive comparisons would be with algorithms that share similar objectives.
- **Inadequate explanation of loss function components**: The individual roles of each component (e.g., cross-entropy and KL divergence terms) in the architecture-invariant loss are not thoroughly explained. The design rationale is unclear; the contribution of each component to optimization should be detailed.
- **Unclear relationship between meta-features and heterogeneous features**: The paper does not clearly explain why increasing meta-features causes a reduction in heterogeneous features. Additional theoretical or empirical evidence is needed.
- **Lack of quantitative evidence for meta-feature proportion**: The authors claim MetaDD increases meta features and reduces heterogeneous features, but no quantitative data on the proportion of area occupied by meta features is provided.
- **Selection of auxiliary networks not discussed**: The method introduces multiple auxiliary neural networks to enhance generalization, but the paper does not provide guidance on how to select the appropriate combination of auxiliary networks.
- **Impact on training time for large datasets**: The introduction of multiple auxiliary networks and the architecture-invariant loss may significantly increase training time, especially for large datasets. This is not addressed.
- **Reliance on pre-trained NNs**: MetaDD’s dependence on pre-trained neural networks could limit flexibility in scenarios where pre-trained models are unavailable or unsuitable.
- **Limited experimental scope**: The number of experiments on different datasets is limited. Validating on a broader range of datasets would strengthen conclusions.
- **Insufficient ablation study**: The ablation study is not comprehensive. It would be beneficial to explore different combinations of losses (e.g., varying the weights of loss terms) and analyze their individual and collective impacts on generalization.
- **Missing hyperparameter details**: The table of hyperparameters does not clarify the proportions of the individual losses (e.g., are they set to 1:1:1?). This could undermine the optimization benefits originally intended.
- **Technical errors and presentation issues**:
  - Numerous typographical errors and incorrect formulas. Example: lines 158-159 typo: “improve the vision transformer CAMZhu et al. (2023);…”.
  - Incorrect subindices (i, j, u, v) in line 176.
  - Table 1(a): *Sre2L + MetaDD* is bolded incorrectly when GLaD should be bolded for GoogleNet and ViT-B-16.
  - Lines 478-480: “$2x$” should be “$2 \times$”.
  - Equation (7): Uses \(W_{head}\), but the text says Q and K matrices; authors should clarify.
  - Table 5 column headers are unclear: “component” likely means number of auxiliary models, “Quantity” likely means method.
  - Equation (9): Missing summation over \(m\) in the loss function.
  - Table 2: Does not specify which network was used for experiments.
  - Table 4: Does not indicate the type of GPU used for timing experiments.
- **Figure and table layout**: The layout of various figures and tables is not aesthetically pleasing. Paper may exceed the 10-page limit.
- **Potential issue with Fig 1**: In Fig 1, the CAM visualizations for cross-arch and same-arch should be identical since the original dataset hasn't undergone the DD process and is directly tested on a single architecture; the distinction is unclear.
- **Bold formatting in tables**: Bold formatting is applied to the authors' method even when it is not the best result; should bold the best result under the same experimental setup.
