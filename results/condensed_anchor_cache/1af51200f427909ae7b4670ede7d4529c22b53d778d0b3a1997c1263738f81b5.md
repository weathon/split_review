## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates multi-turn human red-teaming on Llama3-8b-Instruct, demonstrating over 70% ASR on HarmBench against defenses that report single-digit ASRs under automated single-turn attacks, and also tests on WMDP-Bio for unlearning. The authors release the MHJ dataset (2,912 prompts). Reviewers agree the multi-turn focus and dataset are valuable, but raise concerns about limited model evaluation (only Llama3-8b-Instruct), missing automated baselines (e.g., CodeAttack, PAP), unclear unlearning evaluation metrics, lack of ablation isolating multi-turn from human-crafting effects, and incomplete dataset release. There is notable disagreement: one reviewer (rating 3) finds the evaluation insufficient, while another (rating 8) praises the dataset’s high quality and the experiments’ informativeness.

### Strengths
- Evaluates LLM jailbreak robustness from a realistic, multi-turn perspective that better reflects real-world misuse (Reviewer 1).
- Publishes the Multi-Turn Human Jailbreaks (MHJ) dataset of 2,912 prompts, a valuable open-source resource for the community (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- First multi-turn human red-teaming study, revealing significant safety vulnerabilities that bypass current state-of-the-art defenses (Reviewer 3).
- Human-driven attacks are manually curated, high-quality (in contrast to synthetic datasets like hackaprompt), and multi-turn data is scarce (Reviewer 4).
- Analysis across several defenses is useful, showing that SOTA defenses can be broken in roughly 15 minutes on a large subset of questions, often faster than algorithmic attack runtime (Reviewer 4).
- Results expose that multi-turn human jailbreaks outperform current automated attacks, highlighting additional safety vulnerabilities (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model evaluation.** Only Llama3-8b-Instruct is tested; stronger models like ChatGPT are not included, limiting generality (Reviewers 1, 2). The human red-teaming data collected on Llama could have been examined on other LLMs (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing automated attack baselines.** Black-box attacks using similar tactics, such as CodeAttack and PAP, are not compared. Without these, it is unclear whether human superiority stems from inherent advantages or simply missing baselines (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unclear evaluation metric for model unlearning.** The manual review process lacks a clear definition of success (e.g., whether model output includes content from the forget set, and whether evaluation uses semantic similarity or string matching), hindering reproducibility and assessment of true effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
- **No isolation of multi-turn vs. human-crafting effect.** Figure 3 compares human multi-turn attacks to automated single-turn attacks, but lacks a human single-turn condition, making it impossible to attribute performance differences to multi-turn capabilities versus human crafting skill (Reviewer 4).
- **Variability in red teamer skill.** The paper admits skill and experience vary, but provides no baseline for red teamer ability, undermining reproducibility and comparison of defenses against human-based attacks (Reviewer 1).
- **No discussion of plausible defenses for multi-turn human-based attacks** (Reviewer 1).
- **Cygent defense results not reproduced under original paper’s setup.** Although Llama is open-source and the defense is published, the evaluation deviates from the original setup, reducing reproducibility and clarity (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete dataset release.** Only HarmBench data is released; the WMDP-Bio data (dual-use biosecurity) is not included, despite being a core contribution (Reviewer 4).
- **Non-successful jailbreak attempts not released.** Releasing these could aid training/fine-tuning or further analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis of tactic effectiveness.** The “Direct Request” tactic shows unexpectedly high ASR (Figure 8) but receives no analysis to explain why it bypasses safety defenses (Reviewer 3). More generally, the effectiveness of different tactics is not analyzed in depth (Reviewer 2).
- **No identification of factors enabling human-led outperformance.** Many tactics (e.g., Injection, Obfuscation, Output Format, Request Framing) are also used by automated attacks; the paper does not isolate what drives human-led jailbreaks to succeed where similar automated tactics fail (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing practical details.** Monetary cost of the human-based attack is not mentioned (Reviewer 1). Implementation details of automated attacks for WMDP-bio questions are missing (Reviewer 1). Statistics on red-team member count and per-member sample distribution are absent (Reviewer 4).
