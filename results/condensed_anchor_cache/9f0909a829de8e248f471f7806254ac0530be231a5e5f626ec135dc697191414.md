## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a geometric perspective on phase transitions in diffusion models based on the concept of tubular neighborhoods and injectivity radius of the data manifold. It provides theoretical characterizations (Theorem 3.7) of the injectivity radius, analyzes the score vector field at the boundary, and conducts experiments on synthetic datasets (spheres, ellipses, tori) and real datasets (MNIST, FMNIST) using late initialization to detect phase transitions. The reviews are mixed: two reviewers (scores 3,3) find the contribution weak due to unclear definitions, missing explanations, and questionable experimental validity; one reviewer (score 5) appreciates the perspective but criticizes lack of clarity; one reviewer (score 8) is highly positive, praising the theoretical rigor, intuitive figures, and successful extension of prior work (Raya 2023), while noting missing connections and desire for further analysis. There is significant disagreement on the quality and contribution.

### Strengths
- Novel perspective connecting geometric characteristics of data distribution (tubular neighborhoods, injectivity radius) to phase transitions in diffusion models (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Rigorous mathematical definitions and characterizations: tubular neighborhoods, injectivity radius as minimum of first and second injectivity radii, Theorem 3.7 for computing radii, and proof that score vector fields at the boundary point inward, attracting trajectories into the neighborhood (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Algorithmic tools for estimating the first injectivity radius (Reviewer 1).
- Clear, intuitive figures that visually explain geometric ideas (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental validation on a variety of synthetic geometries (unit spheres, ellipses, tori) with controlled confounds, and on real datasets (MNIST, FMNIST) via embedding into S^d (Reviewers 1, 4). The experiments demonstrate correlation between increase in Wasserstein distance and proportion of particles within the tubular neighborhood (Reviewer 3).
- Successfully addresses limitations of prior work (Raya 2023) by going beyond highly symmetric cases, providing a more general tool for analyzing symmetry breaking in manifolds with non-constant curvature (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing definition and mechanism of phase transitions**: The paper does not clearly define what constitutes a phase transition, specify an order parameter, or explain the mechanism that makes the tubular neighborhood boundary special compared to other neighborhood radii (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The timing of the increase in Wasserstein distance does not precisely align with the 0.99 threshold, and the observed behavior may be coincidental with the exponential diffusion of singularities in the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (Reviewer 3).
- **Wasserstein distance not defined**: It is never specified between which two distributions the Wasserstein distance is measured. This makes the experimental results (tables, figures) uninterpretable (Reviewers 1, 3). Additionally, the observed increase in Wasserstein distance as t → 0 contradicts typical assessment where distance should decrease; this requires explanation (Reviewer 2).
- **Oversimplified geometry assumption**: The paper assumes a single injectivity radius for all points on the manifold, ignoring that real data may not satisfy the manifold hypothesis in full generality (see Brown et al. ICLR 2023) and that intrinsic dimensions and curvatures vary drastically, making “entering the tubular neighborhood” ambiguous (Reviewer 2). This assumption is particularly problematic for the MNIST/FMNIST experiments, where embedding into S^d gives a fake injectivity radius of 1 and ignores the datasets' true intrinsic geometry (Reviewer 2).
- **Experiments on toy models do not probe injectivity radius**: The synthetic experiments use unit spheres, ellipses, and tori, but the injectivity radius is critically determined by the origin (sphere) or the short axis (ellipse), while most trajectories approach from the outside and never “see” these critical points. The connection between injectivity radius and the observed phase transition is therefore not empirically supported (Reviewer 2). The overall scope remains confined to toy models, and several experiments deviate from the conjectured behavior (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing details in algorithms and notation**:
  - The algorithm exploiting Theorem 3.7 to estimate R1 is not clearly stated (Reviewers 1, 3). Algorithm 1 requires revision (Reviewer 3).
  - Proposition 3.4 refers to “tubular neighborhood” before it is formally defined; the definition (Def C.10) should be moved to the main text (Reviewer 1).
  - Equation 9 likely has a typo: “s” should be “t” (Reviewer 1).
  - Proposition 4.2 uses inconsistent notation f(t,x) instead of f_t(x) (Reviewer 1).
  - The estimation of R2(M) is described as “by definition” instead of “following the definition” (Reviewer 1).
  - Table 1’s caption is incomprehensible; column names appear inconsistent between proportions and initialization times (Reviewer 1).
  - Figures 4 and similar: the vertical line is not explained; the x-axis refers to late initialization time (T=1000 being standard) but this is not stated in captions (Reviewer 1).
- **No connection to fixed-point analysis from prior work**: The paper does not relate its geometric singularities to the fixed-point potential energy analysis of Raya (2023), nor to the language of statistical physics. Understanding how the disappearance of singularities corresponds to bifurcations of stable fixed points would strengthen the theory (Reviewer 4). 
- **Lack of analysis for multiple critical times**: The paper focuses on the first symmetry breaking event (at highest curvature), but does not experimentally verify subsequent transitions at regions with lower curvature, which are also genuine phase transitions (Reviewer 4).
- **Need for stronger empirical validation**: To support the conjecture, additional experiments are needed: statistical/quantitative correlation analysis between Wasserstein distance and proportion within tubular neighborhood; experiments on a family of datasets with varying injectivity radii to demonstrate dependence; reparameterization of the inference process to extend the final portion for more precise observation of the phase transition (Reviewer 3).  

Note: One reviewer (Reviewer 4) is significantly more positive about the paper’s contribution and theoretical quality, assigning a score 8 while the other three reviewers (scores 5,3,3) are critical of clarity, experimental validity, and conceptual framing. This contrast is preserved in the above summary and weaknesses.
