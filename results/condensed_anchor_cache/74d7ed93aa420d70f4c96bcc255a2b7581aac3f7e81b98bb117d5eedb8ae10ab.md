## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ProgSyn, a framework for programmable tabular data generation that fine-tunes a pre-trained generative model using differentiable relaxations of fairness, privacy, and logical constraints. Experiments across four datasets demonstrate that compound constraints can be satisfied while maintaining high downstream accuracy. Reviewers find the problem timely and the presentation clear, but raise concerns about novelty, fairness generality, programmability claims, and the depth of validation.

### Strengths
- **Timely and relevant topic.** The paper addresses a real issue: generating high-quality synthetic tabular data with customizable constraints (R1, R3).
- **Extensive experiments.** The framework is evaluated on a wide variety of constraints and compared against state‑of‑the‑art specialized methods (R1). Results are promising, particularly for compound constraints (R3).
- **Clear and focused presentation.** The paper is easy to digest (R1, R3); the overall architecture and methodology are straightforward to understand (R1).
- **Careful handling of differentiability.** The approach supports differential privacy, logical constraints, and statistical constraints while ensuring differentiability, notably via a novel differential computation for binary masks to handle non‑differentiable hard logical constraints and counts (R2).
- **Well‑written code.** The provided code is standardized and aligns closely with the paper’s core ideas, making it a good resource for readers (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited fairness support.** Only demographic parity is considered; many other fairness definitions (equalized odds, equal opportunity, predictive parity, equal error rates, individual fairness, causal fairness) are ignored, and no discussion is given on how the framework could be extended (R3).
- **Exaggerated “programmability”.** The term is misleading: Figure 3 resembles a conventional config file rather than a program, and specifying epsilon/delta for DP is not typically called programming (R3).
- **Lack of adaptability.** The approach cannot change the constraint set without retuning the model; there is no discussion of how to generate a variety of constraint specifications efficiently (R1, R1’s question).
- **Missing baselines for combined constraints.** It is unclear why DP and fairness must be optimized together; a two‑step baseline (generate a fair dataset then add random noise for DP) should be empirically compared (R3).
- **Insufficient stress testing.** Current experiments show almost no accuracy reduction after applying DP (Table 3), suggesting the method has not been tested in truly challenging scenarios where constraints conflict and accuracy must drop (R3).
- **Weak statistical validation of generated data.** Authenticity is assessed mainly via downstream classification accuracy (XGBoost), without rigorous statistical hypothesis testing comparing the generated data’s distribution to the original. Higher‑order statistical properties may be overlooked (R2). More reliable authenticity verification approaches beyond loss‑function control are needed (R2’s question).
- **No analysis of privacy‑utility trade‑off.** The paper mentions differential privacy but does not delve into how privacy parameters impact utility, nor does it present a detailed trade‑off analysis for complex tabular data (R2).
- **Narrow downstream validation.** Experiments rely heavily on XGBoost; using additional models (e.g., ONN, xDeepFM, SVM, GBDT) would strengthen the validation, especially for practical applications like recommendation systems (R2’s question).
- **Lack of justification for generation over filtering.** For tabular data, simple rule‑based filtering is cheaper than generation; the paper should clarify whether ProgSyn fills missing distributions in the real data, rather than simply applying conditional filtering (R2’s question).
- **No rigorous formulation of statistical control.** The paper lacks detailed proofs or derivations for the statistical constraint formulations (R2’s question).
- **Writing and formatting issues.** The introduction mixes background with methodology overview; a clearer logical flow (introduction, related work, formulation) is recommended. There are non‑standard writing elements: lengthy formulas (p6), pseudocode (p5), page breaks (pp. 15, 20, 25), formatting inconsistencies (pp. 10‑12), and improper use of color in tables (R2).
- **Code compatibility and reproducibility.** The code fails to run on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 (CUDA capability sm_89) and lacks clear instructions for environment setup and dependency management (R2). Updated version requirements are needed (R2).
- **Related work omission.** A relevant paper on constrained time‑series generation is noted but not discussed in the submission (R1’s reference).
