## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GCGQ, a generalized graph auto-encoder that introduces quaternion operations for attributed graph clustering. The method uses Four-View Projection (FVP) to project attributes into four quaternion components, Quaternion Graph Encoders (QGE) with Hamiltonian product to incorporate structure, and a clustering-friendly loss that does not require pre-specifying the number of clusters k. The model aims to overcome over-smoothing and over-dominating issues in GCNs. The reviews diverge sharply: Reviewers 1 and 2 find the motivation weak, novelty insufficient, and technical gaps significant; Reviewer 2 specifically asserts the method closely mirrors the accepted KDD2024 paper “QGRL: Quaternion Graph Representation Learning for Heterogeneous Feature Data Clustering” by Chen and Cheung, with similar diagrams and methodology. Reviewer 3 is positive, praising the first application of quaternion convolution in unsupervised graph learning and the flexible clustering objective. Reviewer 4 echoes concerns about motivation and missing experiments while acknowledging the innovation.

### Strengths
- The introduction of quaternion operations into attributed graph clustering is novel, being the first application in unsupervised graph learning (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- Observation of the “over-dominating” phenomenon (Reviewer 1).
- Strong performance across multiple datasets and metrics, with comprehensive experimental validation on six datasets using NMI, ARI, Accuracy (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Inclusion of the Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Test and average performance ranks adds rigor (Reviewer 2).
- Complexity analysis is a good addition (Reviewer 2).
- Visualizations are well made (Reviewer 2).
- The generalized clustering objective adapts to different cluster counts k, avoiding repeated training (Reviewer 3, 4).
- The shallow multi-view projection and quaternion operations preserve graph attribute information while overcoming over-smoothing (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for quaternion operations is unclear.** The paper does not sufficiently explain why quaternions are necessary or superior to conventional techniques like concatenation or attention mechanisms for handling node attributes (Reviewer 1, 4). The benefits for the clustering task are not empirically validated (Reviewer 1, Q3).
- **Insufficient theoretical depth on quaternion learning.** The impact of the non-commutative nature of quaternion multiplication on information flow and embedding quality is not discussed (Reviewer 1, 4). The rationale for choosing average as the quaternion fusion operator (versus min/max or other pooling) is not justified (Reviewer 2).
- **No evidence that over-smoothing is addressed.** The paper claims GCGQ mitigates over-smoothing but provides no quantitative analysis comparing embedding discriminability (e.g., at higher GCN layers) versus standard GCNs. Visualizations of node embeddings would help (Reviewer 1, 4, Q1). The method also stacks graph convolutional layers and may itself suffer from over-smoothing (Reviewer 1, Q1).
- **Over-dominating effect is not distinguished from over-smoothing.** The paper treats them separately, but “embeddings of topology-adjacent but attribute-dissimilar nodes become similar” is essentially over-smoothing (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.** The trade-off parameters α and β are not explored via plots or tables showing performance variation; guidance for choosing values on different datasets is absent (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Scalability concerns not addressed.** The method’s computational complexity is ~O(n² d̂²), which may be impractical for large graphs with millions of nodes. Memory requirements are not discussed (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). No experiments on large graph datasets are included (Reviewer 2). Some recent methods (e.g., S3GC, cited [4]) have lower complexity O(n²k + n d̂k) and superior performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Baselines are outdated.** Many recent methods from 2022–2024 using contrastive learning (e.g., Mrabah et al., IJCAI2022; Liu et al., AAAI2023; Devvrit et al., NeurIPS2022; Mrabah et al., TKDE2022) achieve higher performance on Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed and should be compared (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). The omission of newer contrastive graph clustering methods weakens the claim of superiority (Reviewer 2).
- **Significant overlap with prior accepted work.** The method closely mirrors the KDD2024 paper “QGRL: Quaternion Graph Representation Learning for Heterogeneous Feature Data Clustering” by Chen and Cheung, including similar diagrams and training specifics. The paper does not clarify how it diverges from or improves upon that work (Reviewer 2, strongly negative). This overlap challenges the technical novelty (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of ablation for clustering-oriented loss.** The loss L_sc is critical, but there is no ablation study omitting it. If ineffective, the paper’s distinction from conventional graph representation learning (which also does not require k) diminishes (Reviewer 1, Q4). Similarly, he effectiveness of quaternion convolution on feature interaction is not demonstrated with clear experiments (Reviewer 3, Q1).
- **Notational inconsistencies and errors.** L is defined as I+A in Section 3.1 but as the Laplacian in Section 3.3 (Reviewer 2). Method is called both GCGQ and GQRL (including appendix) (Reviewer 2). Typos: “convolusion” in Figure 1; “W^Q ∈ H^{n×(4d̂)}” should be “4d × 4d̂” (line 228); “quarternion fusion” misspelled (eqn 5); line 285 incorrectly says “node attributes indicated by Â” (Reviewer 2). Citation format using \citet needs correction (Reviewer 1, Q5).
- **Mathematical derivations are complex and hard to read.** More visual illustrations and examples are needed to explain the quaternion convolution module and clustering process (Reviewer 3). Limitations of quaternion representation regarding computational resources or time overhead are not discussed (Reviewer 3). Quaternion operations may incur high time and memory overhead on large-scale graphs compared to other methods (Reviewer 3, Q2).
- **Experimental scope limited.** No results on large graphs; the used datasets have only a few thousand nodes (Reviewer 2). The advantages of GCGQ over existing methods with lower complexity are not addressed (Reviewer 2).
