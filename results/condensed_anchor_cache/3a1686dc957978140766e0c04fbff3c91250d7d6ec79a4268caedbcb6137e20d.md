## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces OccProphet, a camera-only occupancy forecasting framework with an Observer-Forecaster-Refiner pipeline that reduces computational cost by 58%–78% and achieves 2.6× speedup over Cam4DOcc while maintaining or improving accuracy. The key idea is early reduction of spatial resolution combined with tripling-attention fusion and temporal refinement. Reviewers generally found the paper well-written and the experiments extensive, but raised concerns about design clarity, potential information loss from reduced resolution, and whether the performance improvement stems from underfitting of the baseline. Two reviewers were more positive (score 8), while two were more cautious (score 6), highlighting a contrast in confidence about the claimed effectiveness.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Figure 2 effectively illustrates the effect of OccProphet (Reviewer 1).
- Ablation studies demonstrate the benefits of each module (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive experiments on nuScenes, Lyft-Level5, and nuScenes-Occupancy support effectiveness (Reviewer 1).
- OccProphet addresses the computational limitations of prior methods, crucial for real-time edge deployment (Reviewer 2).
- Provides a good balance between efficiency and effectiveness in occupancy forecasting (Reviewer 3).
- Significant reduction in computational requirements (58%–78%) with relative accuracy improvement of 4%–18% over state-of-the-art (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Many design details are unclear: symbols, use of 6-DoF parameters, how `C+6` becomes `C`, colon symbol in line 313 (Reviewer 1).
- The paper lacks detailed explanation for the three modules’ design (Reviewer 4).
- The distinction from traditional encoder-decoder architecture should be more clearly highlighted (Reviewer 2).
- The tripling-attention and reduced-resolution feature aggregation may sacrifice granularity in dense scenarios (Reviewer 2).
- Lack of analysis on performance over varying forecasting time horizons, especially extended timeframes (Reviewer 2).
- Lack of failure scenario analysis (minor concern) (Reviewer 1).
- Concern: performance improvement may stem from OCFNet (Cam4DOcc) being underfit and OccProphet converging faster; reviewers request experiments with longer training for both methods (Reviewer 3).
- Concern: since OccProphet reduces spatial resolution early, there is inherent information loss; temporal-spatial interaction is also used in OCFNet, so why does OccProphet perform better? Need analysis or explanation (Reviewer 3).
- High computational cost of Cam4DOcc not fully analyzed: what contributes to its cost, memory usage, training GPU days? (Reviewer 1).
- How does the method handle environmental dynamics in multi-frame 3D occupancy? The pipeline learns dynamics in latent space without physical constraints; could predictions fail in rare/unseen scenarios? Examples of such failures? (Reviewer 1).
- Handling of occlusions: have the 4D labels been completed for occlusions? Can the method handle situations where any frame from the historical RGB images loses an object? (Reviewer 1).
- The E4A module (Figure 4) resembles a 4D UNet; why does FLOPs increase significantly while parameters decrease? Is this due to upsampling? (Reviewer 1).
- In TAF, after global average pooling (3D, 2D, 1D), temporal attention is applied on same-scale features; would cross-attention between different scales yield better results? (Reviewer 1).
- In the Forecaster, is the condition for prediction learned from past voxels? (Reviewer 1).
- Comparisons could be more thorough: follow output of a 4D BEV method like BEVDet4D with a 2D-to-3D detection head for comparison (Reviewer 1).
- Current baselines lack sufficient comparison methods and evaluation metrics to demonstrate superiority (Reviewer 2).
- What is the actual frames-per-second of the model? Is it sufficient for deployment? (Reviewer 4).
