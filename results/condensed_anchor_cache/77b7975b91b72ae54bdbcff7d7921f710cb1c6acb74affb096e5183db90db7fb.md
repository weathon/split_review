## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a blockchain-based framework (BC-UCB) for robust multi-agent multi-armed bandits in the presence of malicious participants. It combines MAB with smart contracts and validators to simultaneously address optimality, security, and privacy. The authors provide theoretical regret bounds (log T) under various assumptions (e.g., number of malicious participants, cost definitions). No numerical experiments are included.

### Strengths
- The idea is very novel: applying blockchain technology to multi-agent MAB to simultaneously address optimality, security, and privacy.
- Detailed theoretical analysis with provable regret bounds (log T) under various settings (e.g., number of malicious participants, cost definitions), including proofs for different problem variations.
- The paper showcases significant effort and careful consideration of multiple scenarios.

### Weaknesses
- No experimental or numerical results are provided. The paper validates claims only theoretically, making it difficult to assess the algorithm’s effectiveness. Reviewers suggest comparisons with other Byzantine-robust algorithms as baselines.
- Insufficient motivation and unclear real-world setting. The described applications are not compelling (e.g., agents may be self-interested rather than malicious), and the combination of multi-agent MAB with blockchains lacks a clear practical or theoretical scenario. Basic context is undefined: what do arms represent? what is the utility for honest participants? is the mechanism centralized or decentralized?
- Poor readability and organization. The introduction is excessively long (~2.5–3 pages) yet does not clearly present contributions until late. No dedicated related work section. Dense references without context. Undefined abbreviations (e.g., UCB), redundant notations, and questionable assumptions (e.g., B=T in line 169, fixed number of blocks, probability of valid block). Theorem 1 mentions “existential forgery, universal composability” defined only in the appendix. Figure 1 caption overlaps text. Paper ends abruptly at the page limit (conclusions in appendix).
- Unclear description of malicious behaviors. The types of attacks (e.g., Byzantine) are not clearly defined, yet the framework relies on assumptions about their structure.
- Missing comparisons with existing work. The abstract references existing robust multi-agent MAB with log T regret but gives no concrete citations or comparisons. No theoretical or numerical comparison to other Byzantine-robust algorithms is provided.
- Missing lower bound analysis. The paper proves an upper bound but does not derive a regret lower bound, leaving optimality unclear.
- Missing computational complexity analysis. The purpose, complexity, and whether the algorithm is centralized or decentralized are not discussed.
- Missing citations for standard notations (lines 164–165), existing Byzantine-resilient multi-agent MAB (line 224), and a common blockchain assumption (line 230).
- The paper feels like a collage of pre-existing techniques without offering independent technical insights from either MAB or blockchain sides.
- Lack of intuition in method description. Section 3 presents lengthy details without explaining the purpose or insight of sub-techniques, making it easy to lose track.
- Unclear applicability to specific blockchain platforms (e.g., any blockchain vs. Ethereum with validators).
