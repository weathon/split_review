## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses robust ego-motion estimation and photorealistic 3D reconstruction under realistic noise. It provides three contributions: a scalable synthetic noise data-generation pipeline, the Robust-Ego3D benchmark, and a novel method, Correspondence-guided Gaussian Splatting (CorrGS). Reviewers unanimously agree the work fills a crucial gap in Neural SLAM robustness evaluation and that the experiments are extensive across synthetic and real datasets. They praise the pipeline’s customizability and CorrGS’s internal clean representation. However, they identify gaps in depth-noise evaluation, synthetic-to-real transfer, lack of theoretical depth, readability issues, and limited real-world testing. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive about the breadth and novelty of the contributions but also criticizes the noise realism and paper structure.

### Strengths
- First systematic robustness benchmark for Neural SLAM systems that produce photorealistic renderings, going beyond traditional SLAM benchmarks. (R1, R4)
- Novel customizable data-synthesis pipeline that generates large-scale, variably-perturbed RGB-D datasets simulating realistic sensor imperfections, motion, and synchronization errors—a major step toward real-world evaluation. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- CorrGS’s correspondence-guided approach (maintaining a clean internal 3D map and aligning noisy observations) is innovative and consistently outperforms prior SOTA methods (iMap, NICE-SLAM, SplaTAM, ORB-SLAM3) on both synthetic and real-world sequences, especially under fast motion and dynamic illumination. (R1, R3, R4)
- Extensive, methodologically sound experiments: evaluations on ATE, PSNR, Depth L1 loss, with thorough ablation studies (including PQV and CPL) and theoretical perturbation analyses that justify each component. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Figures and tables are of high quality; the perturbation taxonomy (Fig. 1) and key results (e.g., Table 1, Fig. 4-6) clearly illustrate the impact of noise and CorrGS’s robustness. (R1, R2, R4)
- The problem formulation systematically decouples noise factors (motion perturbations, sensor brightness, motion blur, etc.) and studies their separate effects, providing a structured foundation for future robustness research. (R3, R4)
- The paper clearly defines the challenges posed by real-world noise, addressing a gap that existing models often overlook. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Depth noise evaluation is insufficient** compared to RGB. The paper does not thoroughly explore depth noise characteristics (e.g., systematic ToF plane-to-curve artifacts, varying reliability with distance, structured vs. Gaussian noise) or examine strategies for depth inpainting/outlier removal. Additional experiments with severe depth noise or missing depth data would strengthen robustness claims. (R1, R3)  
  *Reviewer 1 questions: How does PQV perform under varied noise levels? Were PQV thresholds tested? Does CorrGS handle high-frequency dynamic changes? Can CorrGS be adjusted for specific sensor noise profiles (motion blur, depth range limits)?*  
  *Reviewer 3 questions: How does CorrGS perform when the initial internal model is significantly misaligned or inaccurate? How sensitive is CPL to extremely bad initial poses?*

- **Synthetic noise may not capture real-world complexities.** The additive Gaussian depth noise and other perturbations (e.g., independent brightness changes) do not authentically replicate systematic artifacts from real ToF cameras or interactions between noise sources. Real-world validation is limited to one small chunk of video; applying CorrGS to larger real datasets like ScanNet++ is needed. (R1, R3, R4)  
  *Reviewer 1 asks about outdoor applicability and sensor-specific adjustments.*

- **Paper organization and readability suffer.** There is heavy cross-referencing to the appendix in the main text (especially Section 3’s problem formulation), which interrupts the flow. The paper reads more like a technical report than a focused research article, with extensive experimental details overwhelming the narrative of the novel method. Several reviewers suggest restructuring to prioritize the core contributions—e.g., shortening or removing the problem formulation section (Sec. 3) and moving less critical details to the appendix. (R2, R4)  
  *Reviewer 2 questions the necessity of the problem formulation section as a separate entity.*

- **Lack of theoretical or interpretive analysis.** While experiments are thorough, the paper rarely explains why CorrGS works—e.g., how correspondence-guided learning handles occlusions or large viewpoint changes under noise. A deeper mechanism-level discussion is absent. Similarly, the paper does not analyze specific model vulnerabilities per noise type (Gaussian vs. motion blur vs. salt-and-pepper), which would make the benchmark more actionable. (R2)  
  *Reviewer 2 also notes the paper transitions too quickly from stating the noise problem to proposing the solution, missing a richer discussion of why noise-free models fail (e.g., effects on feature extraction, bundle adjustment).*

- **Benchmark scope is limited.** Robust-Ego3D is based on the Replica dataset (indoor scenes only); the authors should investigate more diverse settings, including outdoor scenes and highly-dynamic environments, to improve utility. (R4)

- **No new evaluation metrics are introduced.** The benchmark relies on existing metrics (ATE, PSNR, Depth L1). Introducing noise-specific metrics (e.g., quantifying pose robustness stability) could strengthen the contribution, though some reviewers consider this minor. (R3)

- **Initialization sensitivity is underexplored.** CorrGS assumes a rough initial model; the paper does not show how performance degrades with poor initial poses or model misalignment. (R3)  
  *Reviewer 3 questions: Can CorrGS recover from far-off initial poses? When does CPL fail despite PQV?*

- **Limited comparison to classical non-neural SLAM.** The paper compares to a few dense Neural SLAM methods and ORB-SLAM3, but further comparisons with classical methods (e.g., DVO-SLAM, KinectFusion) would better highlight CorrGS’s robustness improvements. (R1)
