## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies a critical distribution-shift problem in existing fidelity metrics (Fid_+, Fid_–, Fid_Δ) for GNN explainability and proposes robust metrics that transform explanation subgraphs to approximate the training distribution. Reviewers strongly agree the topic is significant and the theoretical analysis is valuable. However, there is substantial disagreement: one reviewer (rating 3) finds the presentation poor and experiments too limited, while two reviewers (ratings 8) praise the theoretical grounding and potential impact. Another reviewer (rating 5) questions whether the proposed solution actually measures fidelity of the original subgraphs and suggests an alternative approach. Several weaknesses regarding clarity, scope, and computational cost are raised across reviews.

### Strengths
- **Significant research problem.** The paper addresses a fundamental, overlooked issue in GNN explainability—distribution shift when evaluating explanation subgraphs—which could set new standards for evaluation (R2, R3, R4).
- **Strong theoretical analysis.** Thorough information-theoretic framework showing how existing fidelity metrics misalign with a formal definition of explainability (R1, R2, R3, R4). Both R1 and R2 note the theoretical analysis is a core contribution.
- **Innovative and well-founded solution.** The proposal to sample edges to bring subgraphs closer to the training distribution is simple yet effective, with solid theoretical foundation (R2, R3). R3 calls it “simple and effective with solid theoretical foundation.”
- **Empirical verification.** The paper provides empirical analysis on synthetic and real datasets (4 datasets, 2 tasks) showing the proposed metrics are more consistent with ground-truth explanations (R3, R4).
- **Potential to guide the field.** Several reviewers highlight that the problem is timely given the increasing number of papers on explainable GNNs; the proposed metrics could play an important role (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor structure and presentation (minority reviewer, R1).** The paper devotes excessive space to theoretical derivations that could be moved to the appendix, while motivation for the proposed fidelity measures and experimental results are too limited. R1 rates presentation as “1 poor.” Other reviewers (R3, R4) rate presentation as “3 good,” indicating disagreement.
- **Limited experiments.** R1 and R4 note that only small-scale datasets are used; R4 asks whether the metrics scale to large real-world graphs and whether there are computational complexity concerns. R3 notes the proposed method is non-deterministic and more time-consuming than original fidelity.
- **Questions about the solution’s validity.** 
  - R2 raises three fundamental concerns: (1) Should explanation subgraphs be OOD by nature? Is the field moving toward IID explanations? (2) Because the metric samples (down/up-samples) the explanation subgraph, it measures fidelity of a transformed version, not the original subgraph itself. (3) An alternative is to compare explanations at the same sparsity level (citing NeurIPS 2021 paper); what are the authors’ thoughts?
  - R3 asks why the proposed method fails with GIN on Tree-grid, and how to select α₁ and α₂.
  - R4 asks: (1) What does the convergence ratio of 1/2 mean in practice, and does the proposed metric provide a better rate? (2) How is the method applied to node classification (two datasets are node classification)? (3) Is the OOD problem unique to graphs or does it also affect other domains (e.g., images, language)? 
- **Lack of clarity on motivation and relation to existing methods.** R1 states the paper does not sufficiently justify why the proposed metrics are ideal or discuss differences/relations to existing explainable GNN methods. R4 notes the proposed metrics are not easy to understand until reading the algorithm in the appendix.
- **Generality of theoretical results.** R1 questions why the theoretical results are special for graphs—many definitions seem to apply to general machine learning. R4 similarly asks if the OOD issue affects non-graph domains. (R1’s score 3 suggests this is a notable limitation; other reviewers do not raise it.)
- **Minor typos** (R3): In introduction, “nodes presenting atoms” → “nodes representing atoms”; “subgraph of the input which satisfies” → “subgraph of the input that satisfies”; missing articles in Section 3 (e.g., “low error probability” → “a low error probability”).
