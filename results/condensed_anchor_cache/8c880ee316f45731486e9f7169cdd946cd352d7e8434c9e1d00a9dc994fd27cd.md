## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SafeDPO, a direct preference optimization (DPO) variant that incorporates safety constraints for LLM alignment. The key idea is to set reward to negative infinity for unsafe responses, swap preference labels based on harmfulness, and add a hyperparameter Δ to the loss. The authors claim this avoids training separate reward and cost models, reducing computational overhead while achieving competitive safety alignment.

### Strengths
- The motivation to simplify safety-alignment fine-tuning by avoiding explicit reward/cost model training is good (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- Several reviewers found the approach novel: one reviewer (4, rating 8) highlights that directly using safety indicators rather than harmless preference annotations is novel, and the method dominates SOTA PPO-λ in both model-based and GPT-4 evaluations. Another reviewer (3) considers proving single-stage safety optimization under mild assumptions and the offset Δ as novel contributions, with rigorous proofs in the appendix.
- The paper provides a good set of experiments comparing against Safe RLHF and three DPO variants, building on prior experimental setups (Reviewer 1).
- SafeDPO requires only single-stage training compared to multi-stage alternatives, which is a practical advantage (Reviewer 3).
- The paper covers both theoretical analysis and empirical results (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution**: Several reviewers (1, 2) consider the technical modifications minor. Reviewer 2 points out that Section 3.1 only defines a piecewise reward function, Section 3.2 swaps labels confusingly, and Section 3.3’s hyperparameter Δ shows trivial or even negative effect (Fig. 4: large Δ yields no harmless ratio improvement while helpfulness degenerates). Reviewer 1 notes improvements are marginal and asks for explicit innovations over Safe RLHF and DPO. In contrast, Reviewer 4 found the approach novel and dominating SOTA.
- **No efficiency evidence**: The paper repeatedly claims efficiency in computation, memory, and data but provides no complexity analysis, training time, memory usage, or sample efficiency comparisons with baselines. This is a major limitation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The central claim of reduced overhead is unsupported.
- **Unconvincing assumptions**: The “mild assumptions” behind Propositions 3.1 and 3.3 are not clearly justified. Reviewers (1, 2) request elaboration on why they are considered mild. Proposition 3.2 uses confusing phrasing (“... can be regarded as sampled from ...”) that obscures the derivation (Reviewer 2).
- **Weak hyperparameter analysis**: The effect of Δ (Equation 17) is not practically meaningful; Fig. 4 shows no clear benefit and helpfulness degrades as Δ increases (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 asks for a practical guide on selecting Δ. While Reviewer 3 found Δ novel, the experimental results do not demonstrate its value.
- **Evaluation dataset inconsistency**: The evaluation dataset differs from the one used in the original Safe-RLHF paper, making comparisons with PPO-λ less straightforward. Reviewer 4 recommends using the original evaluation sets or explaining the discrepancy.
- **Suspicious baseline results**: DPO-HELPFUL shows unexpectedly low helpfulness, raising concerns about whether DPO baselines were fully and appropriately tuned (Reviewer 4). No analysis or explanation is provided.
- **Missing comparisons and analyses**:
  - Reviewer 3 suggests comparing with Multi-objective DPO (Beyond One-Preference-Fits-All Alignment) theoretically or empirically.
  - Reviewer 1 asks where the reward r(x,y) and cost c(x,y) in Equation 10 come from (same as standard DPO?).
  - Figure 2 lacks error bars or standard deviations; Reviewer 3 requests both avg and std for stability.
  - The harmless ratio definitions differ between model-based and GPT-4 evaluation, and helpfulness conclusions vary substantially between the two methods (Reviewer 5). The paper does not reconcile or explain this discrepancy.
  - No breakdown of harmlessness vs. helpfulness across the 14 harm categories of the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K / BEAVERTAILS subset (Reviewer 5).
  - The bias claim regarding intractable/tractable distribution replacement (Section 3.2) is insufficiently justified (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation**: The paper is hard to follow due to excessive notation (Reviewer 2). Minor: the related work section should be moved earlier (Reviewer 3).
