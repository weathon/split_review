## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates temporal reasoning in Video LLMs. Diagnostic probing experiments reveal that visual representations contain sufficient information for perfect temporal classification (e.g., an LSTM on visual features achieves 100% accuracy on the ordering task), strongly suggesting the bottleneck is the LLM’s own difficulty with temporal concepts (the LLM alone scores near-random on the same textual questions). To address this, the authors propose Textual Temporal Reasoning Transfer (T3), which fine-tunes the LLM component on purely text-based temporal reasoning tasks synthesized from existing image-text datasets using GPT-4o, without any video data. On LongVA-7B, T3 yields a 5.3 absolute accuracy gain on TempCompass, outperforming ShareGPT4Video-8B trained on 28K video samples. The model also achieves 49.7 accuracy on the Video-MME temporal reasoning task, surpassing InternVL-Chat-V1.5-20B and VILA1.5-40B. A strong correlation between textual and video temporal task performance is reported.

### Strengths
- Novel diagnostic analysis demonstrating that the primary bottleneck in Video LLM temporal reasoning is the LLM’s own temporal capability, not the visual encoder. Probing experiments show visual features are sufficient for perfect classification by small classifiers, while the LLM alone struggles on textual temporal QA.
- The T3 approach is innovative and scalable: it uses only text data generated automatically via GPT-4o from existing image-text datasets, requiring no video annotations. This yields significant performance improvements on challenging benchmarks (e.g., +5.3% on TempCompass for LongVA-7B).
- The enhanced LongVA-7B model achieves competitive performance on comprehensive video benchmarks, surpassing larger models such as InternVL-Chat-V1.5-20B and VILA1.5-40B on Video-MME temporal reasoning.
- The paper is well-written, clearly motivated, and contains informative figures and extensive experiments. The analysis is thorough, including probing studies and correlation analysis.
- The finding of a strong correlation between textual and video temporal task performance validates the transferability of temporal reasoning abilities from text to video domains.

### Weaknesses
- The temporal concepts covered by T3 are limited (order, attribute change, temporal referring, grounding). Important aspects such as causal relationships, durations, counting events, relative velocity, and rotation are not addressed. Some of these (e.g., number of rotations of a diver, relative speed) may be difficult to represent as discrete text sentences.
- The synthesized tasks are template-based and may lack the subtle temporal cues present in real-world video descriptions, potentially limiting generalization to downstream video tasks. The use of GPT-4o for caption generation also introduces potential bias; the impact of caption quality (vs. human annotations) is not fully explored.
- The choice of LongVA as the base model is questioned because it was trained only on images and text, making video evaluation zero-shot. It is unclear whether similar gains would hold on backbones that have seen video training (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision, phi-3.5-vision, Qwen2-VL). Claims about the LLM bottleneck would be stronger if validated on such models.
- The probing setup may be unfair: the visual encoder was fine-tuned for the probe, while the LLM was not. Given limited task diversity, overfitting could explain the perfect visual-feature accuracy. One reviewer requested testing an LSTM on LLM features to see if it can also overfit.
- The approach shows minimal improvement on larger LLMs (mentioned at line 538). Its effectiveness is mainly demonstrated on a relatively small 7B model; results on larger models (e.g., Qwen2-72B) are missing, raising doubts about scalability and long-term utility (large LLMs already handle ordering well).
- It is insufficiently demonstrated that the performance gain stems from enhanced temporal reasoning rather than improved instruction following (e.g., learning to output “Answer the option only”). The paper lacks analysis of mis-formatted or invalid response rates across experiments, and the ablation study using image-based transfer does not fully isolate temporal reasoning from instruction following.
- Evaluations are limited to a few benchmarks (TempCompass, Video-MME). Standard temporal reasoning benchmarks such as NextQA and ActivityNet are not included, and ordering is only a subset of temporal reasoning.
- The claim that the LLM is the sole bottleneck is not fully convincing. Temporal reasoning may also depend on text-image alignment capabilities. Extensive prompting experiments on more models and datasets are needed to support this strong claim, and probing should be conducted on real video datasets rather than only synthetic ones.
- Several specific questions and missing experiments: (1) Are the generated temporal questions in-distribution or out-of-distribution relative to benchmark answer choices? (2) What is the performance of the fine-tuned LLM on the text-only part of video questions? (3) Is there an analysis of performance gain vs. number of training data points? (4) Can probing be done on real datasets? (5) How does the method explain GPT-4o’s strong temporal reasoning? (6) A minor note: LLaVA-ReCap-558K is derived from LCS-558K, not BLIP558K as stated (lines 861-862).
