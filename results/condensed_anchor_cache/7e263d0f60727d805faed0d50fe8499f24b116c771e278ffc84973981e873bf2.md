## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ITIT (InTegrating Image Text), a training paradigm that leverages cycle consistency to train vision–language generative models on unpaired image and text data. A joint encoder with separate decoders performs bidirectional image-to-text (I2T) and text-to-image (T2I) generation. A small set of paired data provides initial alignment, while larger unpaired sets are used by enforcing consistency between original samples and cycle-generated counterparts. Experiments show that ITIT with only 3 M paired data (plus 398 M unpaired) achieves performance comparable to models trained on orders-of-magnitude more paired data (e.g., 398 M). Reviewers generally find the motivation clear, the method simple yet effective, and the evaluation thorough, but raise concerns about the practical effectiveness of the cycle consistency, computational overhead, generalization, and the novelty of the contribution.

### Strengths
- **Clear and solid motivation:** Using unpaired data for vision–language generation addresses a significant gap in the literature and reduces reliance on expensive paired datasets. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Innovative yet simple cycle‑consistency paradigm:** The framework bridges I2T and T2I generation within a single model, enabling training on unpaired images and texts. (R2, R4)
- **Competitive performance with far fewer paired data:** ITIT shows scaling behavior similar to high‑quality paired data; with only 3 M paired + 398 M unpaired it matches the performance of models trained on 398 M paired data. (R1, R2, R4) R2 notes this may be the first demonstration of unpaired pre‑training achieving comparable results.
- **Plausible computational efficiency for unpaired training:** The framework provides a way to learn from unpaired data without prohibitive cost. (R1)
- **Well‑presented ablation studies:** Ablations support the claim that unpaired data contributes positively. (R1)
- **Clear writing and organized figures/tables.** (R2, R3)
- **Cycle consistency improves image–text alignment:** The full cycle loss enhances generation quality; without it the model fails on generation consistency. (R4)
- **Paves the way for more economical training** of vision–language models. (R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Claimed “paired data gap closed” (Fig 4) vs. actual performance gap:** Figure 1 shows a significant gap in FID and CIDEr against methods like CM3Leon, Muse, and GIT. The gap may be small only on Shutterstock-like data; on more diverse datasets the claim may not hold. (R1)
2. **Generalization to new domains:** No experiments on, e.g., medical imaging where paired data is scarce. How does ITIT perform when only domain‑specific unpaired images and texts are available? (R1)
3. **Compatibility with other generative models:** Unclear whether the framework works with diffusion/flow/consistency models as the I2T stage, or how much it depends on the unified token‑based architecture. (R1) Similarly, can diffusion be used for the T2I side? (R2, R3)
4. **Alternative data‑cleaning methods:** Would a simpler approach like CLIP‑filtering confident pseudo‑labels (semi‑supervised discriminative learning) achieve similar gains? Such methods might preserve information lost in the ITIT cycle (e.g., the “blue” signal in Fig 6). (R1)
5. **Substantial computational overhead:**  
   - Training time is 2× that of non‑cycle baselines despite 24 decoding steps per cycle. Quantitative metrics for the one‑step back‑propagation efficiency savings are missing. (R1, R4)  
   - The total compute of 4 M paired + 398 M unpaired is *larger* than traditional training on 398 M paired data; a direct efficiency comparison is needed. (R4)  
   - Careful batching and TPU utilization for pseudo‑pair generation are not analyzed. (R1)  
   - Time consumption is a disadvantage with no justification provided. (R3)
6. **Dependence on scaling unpaired data:** The paper’s value hinges on being able to collect 10× more high‑quality images/texts (but unpaired) than all existing paired datasets combined; this feasibility is not demonstrated. (R1)
7. **Marginal gain of full cycle over half cycle (Table 2):** The improvement from full cycle (I2T2I & T2I2T) over half cycle (pseudo‑targets only) is very small. This raises doubts about whether cycle consistency is actually needed; half cycle is computationally cheaper and could easily be combined with off‑the‑shelf models like BLIP v2 or Stable Diffusion. (R2)
8. **Frequent failure cases from synthetic data:** Early‑stage generated captions and images are noisy, especially on datasets like CC3M, which already contain noisy pairs. The paper does not share such failure cases. (R2, R4)
9. **Innovation considered engineering‑oriented:** All core network structures already exist; the novelty lies mainly in memory‑reduction tricks (e.g., generating from 0→1 without gradient, then returning to 0.9 and re‑doing 0.9→1). This is seen as a contribution of engineering rather than a new concept. (R3)
10. **Reconstruction correctness in I2T2I:** The T2I model can produce many plausible images from a caption; why must the second I2T reconstruction necessarily be correct? The cycle consistency assumption is not theoretically justified. (R3)
11. **Missing comparison with embedding‑space cycle consistency:** Related works (Equivariant Similarity, ICCV 2023; CyCLIP, NeurIPS 2022) enforce cycle consistency in latent embedding space for an image–text encoder. A direct comparison between embedding‑cycle and generation‑cycle would clarify the advantage of the proposed approach. (R3)
12. **Gradient estimation details:** In T2I2T, T2I is performed twice with a stop‑gradient in the middle – why not run T2I once? (R4) Also, why ViT was chosen as the joint encoder and how it extracts text features. (R4)
13. **Initialization and two‑stage training:** It is unclear whether ITIT is trained from scratch or uses pre‑trained weights. Also, a two‑stage scheme (first pre‑train on paired data, then add unpaired) is suggested but not evaluated. (R2)

*Note:* Reviewers disagree on the importance of the cycle consistency: R2 finds its effect marginal, while R4 emphasizes that removing it harms generation consistency. This contrast is preserved in points 7 and 4 above.
