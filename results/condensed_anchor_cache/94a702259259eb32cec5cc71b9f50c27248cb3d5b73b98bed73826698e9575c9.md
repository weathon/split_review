## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Masked Mamba, a self-supervised learning framework for histopathological image classification. It combines masked autoencoder (MAE) pretraining with a Mamba vision backbone. Key components include Patch Ghosting for subpatch merging (capturing local features), a MixedMamba Block (later renamed Directional Mamba vision backbone), and modifications to convolution operations (replacing causal convolutions with a blend of deep separable and regular convolutions). The model is trained with 75% masking to reconstruct input patches. Evaluations on four patch-level classification datasets (including TCGA COAD, LaC) compare against MAE, ViT, Swin Transformer, and other baselines. The reported improvements are modest but consistent across tasks.

### Strengths
- The concept of combining Mamba with masked autoencoding for pathology image feature learning is novel and of interest to the SSL community (R1, R2, R3).  
- The proposed Patch Ghosting module effectively enhances local multi-scale feature extraction (R4).  
- Extending Mamba from 1D sequence modeling to 2D image modeling is a valuable and well-motivated direction (R4).  
- The masked autoencoder training helps exploit Mamba’s representational capacity (R3).  
- Experimental results show modest but consistent improvements over several baselines (R1, R3).  
- The masked Mamba approach is novel relative to standard vision backbones like ViT and Swin Transformer (R1).  

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: While R1 finds the concept novel, R4 notes that the combination of Vision-Mamba and MAE shows limited novelty, and R3 points out that listing “using SSM” as a contribution is redundant because SSM is already integral to Mamba. The claim that “a blend of deep separable and regular convolutions” reinvents spatial feature extraction is overstated—such combinations are well known (e.g., Inception) (R3).  
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparisons with more successful self-supervised methods like DINO (R1), nor with current pathology-specific models such as CTransPath, PLIP, or other foundation models (R3). The Patch Ghosting module should also be compared with the commonly used Local Perception Unit (R4).  
- **No whole slide image (WSI) experiments**: The evaluation is limited to patch-level classification; feature extractors are most useful for WSI tasks, and experiments on WSI classification would strengthen the paper (R1).  
- **Efficiency claims unsubstantiated**: The paper claims efficiency but provides no table of parameters or FLOPs to support this (R4).  
- **Dataset description is insufficient**:  
  - The reference for TCGA COAD cites a review paper (Couture 2022), not the dataset itself (R3).  
  - No tasks, labels, or class-wise statistics are given for any dataset (R3).  
  - It is unclear whether the data split is published or performed by the authors. If the latter, the split ratio is given but no cross-validation is reported (R3).  
  - Original patch magnification is missing, only that patches are resized to 224×224 (R3).  
- **Scalability to high-resolution images not shown**: Resizing all patches to 224×224 does not convincingly demonstrate that Mamba is applicable to high-resolution pathological images (R4).  
- **Performance significance and ablation**: Improvements are mostly fractional, and it is unclear whether they are statistically significant (R3). Ablation studies show no significant improvement from Patch Ghosting in 3 out of 4 tasks (R3).  
- **Unclear or unsupported claims**:  
  - The claim that Patch Ghosting encourages the model to capture diverse feature representations of similar cells is not supported by experiments (R1, same as question).  
  - The intuition behind Patch Ghosting is unclear, and the name (“ghosting”) is unexplained; its superiority over other patch merging operations is not justified (R3).  
  - The statement “the resolution in pathological images is often influenced by staining and sectioning” needs clarification (R3).  
- **Equations lack detail and contain errors**:  
  - Equation 1 defines matrices A, B, C with dimensions only, without explaining their significance (R2).  
  - Equation 6 uses double arrows without explanation of their meaning (R3).  
  - Equation 11 has an incorrect sign: “(1+yn)” (R3).  
  - Line 161: “The 1 is transformed into a discrete function”—unclear what “1” refers to (R2).  
  - Line 288 mentions “SSD” without prior introduction (R3).  
- **Supervised classification label misuse**: The title states “self-supervised” but later calls it “unsupervised.” Training the classification head still requires labels; the method should be described as self-supervised feature representation learning followed by supervised classification (R2).  
- **Writing quality issues**:  
  - Parts of the text appear to be written with an LLM, using hyperbolic phrasing like “…manifested the most exemplary classification proficiency” (R1).  
  - The contributions listed in the abstract and introduction are inconsistent (R4).  
  - General presentation quality is poor (R2, R3).  
- **LaC dataset performance**: Accuracy and F1 scores are exceptionally high on LaC, which is known to be very difficult. The authors need to explain how their method effectively learns such a discriminative embedding space (R2).
