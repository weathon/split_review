## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents DIFF2, a backdoor attack targeting security-centric diffusion models used for adversarial purification and robustness certification. The attack superimposes a malicious diffusion-denoising process that guides trigger-embedded inputs toward an adversary-defined distribution while preserving normal behavior for other inputs. Evaluations on multiple benchmarks and diffusion models show significant reductions in both post-purification and certified accuracy. The work highlights security risks of using pre-trained diffusion models as defensive tools and explores potential countermeasures.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The attempt to backdoor diffusion models is novel; it demonstrates a possible threat in using pre-trained diffusion models for defense (Reviewer 2). This is a trailblazing effort in backdooring security-centric diffusion models and addresses a pertinent, timely topic (Reviewer 4).
- Comprehensive experiments show attack effectiveness and utility across different datasets and diffusion models (Reviewer 1). The experiments are relatively solid and technically sound (Reviewer 3).
- Transferability of the proposed attack is provided (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Practicality and threat model realism:** The attack requires very high privileges, including access to the victim network, arbitrary addition of dirty training data, and modifications to the loss function. This is straightforward and lacks subtlety, limiting its usefulness (Reviewer 2). The assumption that surrogate and target classifiers are identical in main experiments is unrealistic; deeper exploration of transferability is imperative, and the experiments in Section 5.4 appear insufficient (Reviewer 4).
- **Stealthiness issues:** Post-purification images (Figure 3, rows 3/4) exhibit visible distortions and the trigger clearly remains. The perturbation budget of L_inf=16/255 is larger than the typically sufficient 8/255 for CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18; this choice is not justified and raises concerns about practical relevance (Reviewer 2). The visible artifacts increase the probability of detection by human inspection or backdoor detection methods (e.g., frequency-based methods) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 suggests using x_a instead of x_a* in Algorithm 1 to improve stealthiness. The trigger being largely preserved after adding noise suggests the method relies heavily on this characteristic; analysis is needed on whether the attack would still work if the trigger was less prominent after diffusion (Reviewer 2).
- **Trigger design:** The fixed 5x5 trigger in the lower-right corner appears arbitrary. The authors should consider alternative designs (e.g., triggers with L_inf=8/255 distributed across the whole picture) and justify the choice, discussing trade-offs between stealthiness and effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
- **Significant clean accuracy drop:** DIFF2 reduces clean accuracy from 86.4% to 70.5% (Table 2), which largely impacts usability (Reviewer 3). Robust accuracy also drops heavily (e.g., SDE from 85.6% to 60.3%, a -25.3% reduction), demonstrating increased vulnerability (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited dataset scale:** All experiments are on small datasets; further experiments on larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) are needed to illustrate generality (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete related work review:** The paper primarily relies on BadNets (Gu et al., 2017) and does not discuss contemporary or more innovative backdoor attack techniques. The trigger mechanism inherent to BadNets is susceptible to detection, as shown by Chen et al. (AAAI 2019) (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical analysis clarity:** In Theorem 1, the relationship between KL(q_T, p_T) and KL(q_T, diff(x_c, T)) when T is large is not well explained. The authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the behavior of these KL divergences and their implications (Reviewer 2).
- **Inconsistency in results:** Table 4 shows very low ASR (sub-40%) while the accompanying text mentions an ASR of 77.1%; this discrepancy needs clarification (Reviewer 4).
- **Sampler dependency:** It is unclear whether the attack works consistently with different samplers (e.g., DDIM) (Reviewer 1).
- **Defense evaluation missing:** It is unclear whether existing backdoor defenses (e.g., ANP [2]) can defend DIFF2; the authors should evaluate this, potentially following methodology from [3] (Reviewer 3).
