## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a contrastive guidance method for protein sequence-structure co-generation using dual flow models. A high-quality (HQ) model is fine-tuned on designable synthetic proteins and a low-quality (LQ) model on the broader synthetic set from MultiFlow. During sampling, the vector field is steered away from the LQ predictions and toward the HQ predictions. Experiments on lengths 400 and 500 show improved designability (scRMSD, scTM) over base MultiFlow, but with reduced diversity. Reviewers are divided: some find the idea novel and well-motivated (R1, R3, score 5 and 6), while others critique the novelty relative to prior work (autoguidance), missing comparisons, limitations of evaluation metrics, and unclear trade-offs (R2, R4, scores 5 and 3).

### Strengths
- The contrastive guidance approach is well-motivated, conceptually sound, and novel in the context of protein co-generation (R1). It tackles sampling-time guidance, a key future direction for protein generative models, and the dual-model contrast is a clever alternative to a classifier when intermediate-step designability is hard to compute (R4).
- The method is clearly motivated and easy to follow; the authors provide comprehensive benchmarks with error bars for lengths 400 and 500, including comparisons across evaluation protocols (1 vs 8 ProteinMPNN sequences) (R2).
- Empirical results show meaningful improvements in designability metrics (scRMSD, scTM) over base MultiFlow, especially for long proteins, and the framework has potential generalizability beyond protein design (R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written and the results are adequately presented (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing critical baselines and comparisons**  
  (R1) No comparison with the simpler pipeline of backbone design + inverse folding (e.g., ProteinMPNN) is given; co-design must outperform this to be justified. (R4) No comparison with Proteus (ICML 2024), which reportedly achieves ~0.9 designability for length-400 and ~0.75 for length-500 (vs. 80% and 37% here). (R2) Baseline performance for FrameFlow, etc., is far worse than reported in public benchmarks (ScaffoldLab, ProteinBench); e.g., FrameFlow scRMSD is 6.12 vs. 2.12 in ProteinBench for length-400/500 with 8 ProteinMPNN samples, and the large error bars (±4.94) overlap with stronger numbers. The authors should provide a side-by-side comparison, check evaluation protocol differences, and re-run under public benchmark conditions.

- **Designability metric is flawed and leads to biased evaluation**  
  (R1) Designability relies on ESMFold refolding, which has many false negatives and bias toward alpha helices. The Flow-HQ dataset only includes structures foldable by ESMFold. A secondary structure analysis is needed (Figure 4 appears dominated by alpha helices). Novelty metric is inadequate: it should measure similarity to synthetic data, not just PDB, to show how close generated samples are to the training distribution.

- **Limited evaluation lengths raise confounds**  
  (R1, R2, R4) Only lengths 400 and 500 are evaluated; most baseline methods were trained on proteins up to length 256–384, so the improvement may come from additional training on long sequences rather than contrastive guidance. Shorter lengths (50–300) must be tested. (R2) This is especially important because the fine-tuning is done exclusively on length-400 samples.

- **Not fully demonstrated that improvement comes from contrastive guidance**  
  (R1) MultiFlow already used distillation; fine-tuning on designable samples alone nearly matches contrastive guidance at epoch 3 (Figure 3 right). (R2, R4) The method uses two models; a fair comparison would generate 2× more samples for baselines and evaluate the top half. (R4) Ablation: why not contrast baseline MultiFlow with Flow-LQ instead of training a separate Flow-HQ? How does that perform?

- **Designability-diversity trade-off not clearly established**  
  (R1) The method reduces diversity and novelty compared to baseline. (R2) The authors should present designability and diversity at different temperatures for all methods and show whether the Pareto frontier is advanced. (R4) The pairwise TM diversity metric is difficult to interpret (de-duplication and random selection of 50 proteins). More standard diversity metrics (number of clusters, as in MultiFlow, Proteus, Genie2) should be used. Known trade-offs exist; generating similar proteins could inflate designability without true gain.

- **Novelty concerns relative to existing methods**  
  (R2) The core idea is nearly identical to autoguidance (Karras et al. 2024), which combines high- and low-quality model predictions with CFG-like scaling; the paper does not cite or discuss this, nor address theoretical analysis (e.g., autoguidance avoids dropping parts of the data distribution). (Minority: R1 and R3 found the approach novel in the protein co-generation context; R4 was not persuaded that the observed gains are attributable to guidance rather than training on longer sequences or increased sampling cost.)

- **Lack of clarity and missing technical details**  
  (R3) The explanation that contrastive guidance “gently steers away from non-designable regions” is vague; the LQ model likely affects all regions uniformly, and since it is trained on synthetic data containing many designable proteins, its vector field may not always be detrimental. (R4) Error bars and ± notation in figures/tables are not explained. (R1) scRMSD for fine-tuning on designable samples is unstable across epochs—why? (R2) In Figure 3b, the efficacy of contrastive guidance appears to decrease with more training—why?

- **Questions that overlap with missing experiments**  
   - How does the model perform on shorter proteins (50–300)? (R1)  
   - For the benchmarks in Table 2 and 3, are the same sampling temperatures used for MultiFlow and the proposed method? Numbers under varied temperatures are needed to assess the designability-diversity frontier. (R2)  
   - Could a classifier predicting designability be used instead of dual models? (R3)  
   - Is contrastive guidance generalizable to other domains, and what challenges would arise? (R3)  
   - What are the setting details for Figure 3 (right) — length, sample count, and why is baseline MultiFlow not shown as a line? (R4)  
   - Is there evidence that residue frame representation is the best? (R2)
