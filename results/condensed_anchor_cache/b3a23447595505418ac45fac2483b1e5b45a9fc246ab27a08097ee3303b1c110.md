## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the intrinsic dimensionality of complex networks using metric node embeddings (L2 distance model) rather than vector-based inner product embeddings (Logistic PCA). The authors prove that metric embeddings can yield dimensions up to two lower than LPCA while preserving perfect reconstruction. They develop an efficient logarithmic search procedure (binary search with warm starts) to identify the exact embedding dimension, with O(N log N) complexity via KD-trees for large networks. A two-phase optimization combines the Hierarchical Block Distance Model (HBDM) with hinge-loss optimization. Empirically, they find lower embedding dimensions than previously reported on ten real-world networks and demonstrate lossless embeddings for graphs up to one million nodes. The work has theoretical, methodological, and empirical contributions. Reviewers disagree on the significance of the improvement and the thoroughness of experimental validation.

### Strengths
- **Original idea and significance**: The question of intrinsic dimensionality is relevant and the metric embedding approach is novel. (R1,R4)
- **Strong theoretical foundation**: Proves that metric embeddings (L2) can achieve dimensions ≤ D_LPCA - 2, providing a tighter upper bound. (R2,R3)
- **Efficient scalable algorithm**: O(N log N) complexity using KD-trees, binary search, and warm starts; demonstrated on networks up to 1M nodes. (R2,R4,R5)
- **Comprehensive empirical validation**: Tests on 10 diverse real-world networks (varying size, type), 5 searches per dataset, ablation studies, comparisons with baseline methods (LPCA, Eigenmodel). (R2,R4)
- **Clear practical implications**: Lower-dimensional representations benefit tasks like node classification, community detection, visualization, compression. (R1,R2,R4)
- **Reproducible research**: Code provided, detailed hyperparameter settings. (R2)
- **Well-written presentation**: Generally clear motivation and results. (R2,R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing tests on synthetic benchmarks**: No experiments on artificial network models generated in a geometric space of known dimension (e.g., hyperbolic or Euclidean), making it difficult to interpret how well the method recovers the true embedding dimension. (R1) – A minority reviewer (R1) strongly criticizes this omission and suggests specific references (Kovács et al., Almagro et al., Krioukov et al., Thomas et al., Muscoloni et al.).  
- **Limited exploration of alternative metric spaces**: The method uses Euclidean metric embeddings; hyperbolic geometry is not tested despite being more natural for many real networks (e.g., internet, social). The related work discussion fails to cite several foundational hyperbolic embedding algorithms (e.g., Boguñá et al. 2010, Papadopoulos et al. 2015, Muscoloni et al. 2017). (R1,R2)  
- **No downstream task evaluation**: The paper claims relevance for node classification, link prediction, etc., but never measures performance on these tasks after embedding. It is unclear whether the exact low-dimensional embeddings improve or maintain utility compared to popular approximate embeddings (DeepWalk, node2vec, LINE). (R3,R4)  
- **Unclear significance of the improved upper bound**: Reducing from D_LPCA to D_LPCA−1 or −2 appears small; the paper does not contextualize what this reduction means for practical interpretability or downstream tasks. (R3) – Reviewer 3 questions whether this theoretical advancement is incremental relative to prior work (Chanpuriya et al. 2020, 2023).  
- **Insufficient justification of the efficient search algorithm**: No runtime complexity analysis is provided for Algorithm 1 (logarithmic search), and no convergence or termination guarantee is given. The choice of initial lower/upper bounds (lb, ub) seems arbitrary without justification from graph properties. (R3,R4)  
- **HBDM description is inadequate**: Equation 6 and the connection between hierarchical clustering and log-likelihood approximation are not clearly explained. The role of cluster centroid distances and how they approximate pairwise distances is vague. (R5)  
- **Optimization sensitivity**: The method may not find the absolute minimal dimension; it is sensitive to initialization and hyperparameters. No Monte Carlo analysis is provided to assess convergence probability or average D. (R2,R3)  
- **Memory inefficiency**: Preserving the full adjacency matrix A in memory is not efficient; the paper relies on sampling methods to handle large matrices. (R2)  
- **Limited to unweighted networks**: The analysis only considers presence/absence of links; extension to weighted networks is not discussed or addressed. (R4)  
- **Unclear how the KD-tree-based exactness check applies to LPCA/Eigenmodel**: The paper does not clarify whether the scalable check is exclusive to the L2 model or can be adapted to other models. (R5)  
- **Surprising result for Roadnet-PA**: A road network, inherently 2D, requires 16 dimensions for exact embedding; the paper does not explain this discrepancy. (R4)  
- **CC sampling slower than RN sampling**: Figure 3 shows connected component (CC) sampling takes longer to produce exact representations than random node (RN) sampling, but the reason is not explained. (R5)  
- **Vague claims**: Terms like “relatively small number of dyads misclassified” (L.357) and “efficient” (Algorithm 1) are not precisely quantified. The paper does not explain why the active set update procedure (Eqs. 6 & 7) is guaranteed to terminate. (R4)  
- **Missing related work**: Several relevant references in sign rank (Razborov & Sherstov 2010) and hyperbolic embedding are omitted. (R1,R5)  
- **Minor presentation issues**: Typos (e.g., “20.000” → “20,000”, “course” → “coarse”, “missclassified”), inconsistent Y-axis ranges in Figures 3 and 5, and unclear interpretation of Figure 1 text. (R2,R4,R5)
