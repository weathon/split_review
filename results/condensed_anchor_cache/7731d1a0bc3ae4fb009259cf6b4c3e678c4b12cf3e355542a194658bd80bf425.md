## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IC-NPDE, a transformer-based hypernetwork that generates parameters for a small neural ODE-like solver from past states, enabling next-state prediction for dynamical systems with unknown temporal PDEs. It aims to decouple parameter estimation from prediction for improved interpretability and in-context learning. Experiments are conducted on multiple 1D/2D PDE datasets. Four reviewers rated 3, one rated 5 (low confidence 1). Most found the work well-written but lacking novelty and thorough evaluation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and easy to follow; the logic and motivations are clearly articulated. [R1, R2, R3, R5]
- The task is of high practical importance: handling multiple unknown physics (different equations or coefficients) in a single model, rather than many trajectories from one fixed PDE. [R2, R3]
- The idea of combining in-context learning (ICL) with a differentiable neural ODE solver is a promising direction. [R4]
- The forecaster is more interpretable than black-box models, using a simple CNN as the vector field and connecting to classical numerical methods. [R3]
- The ability to train directly on multiple different PDEs showcases versatility and robustness. [R5]
- One reviewer (R4, score 5) was more positive about the direction, though with low confidence; the other four gave score 3 with moderate-to-high confidence.

### Weaknesses
**Novelty & Contribution**  
- The technical contribution is low. Stacking well-known neural modules (transformers, hypernetworks, neural ODEs) without new architectural insight; adaptating neural ODE solvers via hypernetworks has been explored before (e.g., [1] from R3). [R1, R3, R5]  
- The method does not substantially differ from existing meta-learning strategies (e.g., [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] from R3); these should be compared as baselines. [R3]  
- The application of ICL to continuous vectors from physical data is not properly motivated – unlike quantized tokens in text, the adaptation mechanism is not well explained. [R3]  
- The claim that the method works for “different physics” is not adequately supported: the varying physical quantities (parameters, external forces, boundary conditions) are not sufficiently emphasized in dataset descriptions (Table 1). [R2]  
- The title is misleading: the network requires fine-tuning on new datasets, contradicting the “in-context” notion of zero-shot adaptation. [R3]  
- The paper claims that methods like [3,4] lack inductive bias for physics, but provides no experiments to substantiate that claim. [R2]

**Experimental Evaluation**  
- Baselines are outdated or insufficient. Only AViT is compared; missing comparisons with recent neural PDE solvers (e.g., pre-trained PDE solvers), meta-learning approaches, and methods for multiple physics or varying parameters. [R1, R2, R3, R5]  
- No comparison with models trained on single PDEs in Table 3 and Table 5 to highlight generalization vs. specialization trade-offs. [R5]  
- All experiments are limited to 1D and 2D PDEs; the authors mention spatial dimension up to 3 but provide no 3D results (e.g., 3D Navier-Stokes, 3D Reaction-Diffusion). [R1, R5]  
- The prediction horizon (e.g., 32 steps) is too short; long-term rollout accuracy is not assessed. [R1]  
- Spatiotemporally coarser datasets should be tested. [R1]  
- Only simulated data are used; real-world applications (e.g., climate science) are needed to demonstrate robustness. [R5]  
- The NRMSE results are reported at specific time steps without clarification – is it the error for that frame or an average from time 0 to T? An average over the full trajectory would be informative. [R3]  
- The representation analysis (Figure 5) is too simple: distinguishing viscosity 0.1 vs 0.01 is trivial; more challenging and diverse settings (as in [1,2]) are needed. [R2]  
- UMAP visualization shows points already well clustered at epoch 0; explanation is missing. [R3]  
- The effect of fine-tuning on new PDEs is unclear: how many different physics are present during training, and are test coefficients new or seen? [R3]

**Methodological & Technical Concerns**  
- The “inductive bias for physical systems” is not clearly defined; it is unclear how it is integrated beyond using rollouts with a small step size. [R2]  
- Only one numerical scheme (Runge-Kutta 4) is used; stiff systems may require implicit methods, and the framework’s performance across different discretizations is unexplored. [R4]  
- No analysis of computational complexity (transformer hypernetwork + neural ODE solver) or robustness to initial conditions, noise, or time integration length. [R4]  
- The phrase “much smaller neural PDE solver” is imprecise; IC-NPDE uses a Neural ODE architecture where the network defines the vector field and a standard solver integrates. [R5]  
- The claim that Transformers are used as hypernetworks due to their auto-regressive properties is inaccurate – vanilla softmax Transformers are not inherently auto-regressive (requires modification like [7]). [R5]  
- The choice of fixed-step RK4 over adaptive-step solvers (e.g., Dopri5) is not justified and makes the step-size ablation potentially unnecessary. [R5]  
- Can the method handle arbitrary-sized input contexts (N past states varying at inference)? The ICL analogy suggests yes, but this is not demonstrated. [R3]
