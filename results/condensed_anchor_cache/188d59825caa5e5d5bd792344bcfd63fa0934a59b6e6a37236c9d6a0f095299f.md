## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the sample complexity of learning an optimal policy in average-reward Markov decision processes (AMDPs) under uniform ergodicity with a generative model. The proposed algorithm achieves a sample complexity of \(\widetilde O(|S||A| t_{\text{mix}} \epsilon^{-2})\), matching the known lower bound and closing the gap left by previous upper bounds. The approach combines a reduction from average-reward to discounted-reward MDP (Jin & Sidford 2021) with optimal warm-up (Li et al. 2020) and an analysis of mixing discounted MDPs (Wang et al. 2023). The result provides the first minimax optimal guarantee for AMDPs in this setting and, as a byproduct, yields optimal rates for uniformly ergodic discounted MDPs.

### Strengths
- First minimax optimal sample complexity guarantees for AMDPs under a generative model; closes the gap between upper and lower bounds (explicitly by a factor of \(t_{\text{mix}}\)).
- As a byproduct, provides optimal sample complexity for uniformly ergodic discounted MDPs.
- Algorithm is computationally feasible and conceptually simple.
- Paper is well-written with clear background and context; notations and proofs are rigorous and solid (R3, R4).
- Addresses an important problem in reinforcement learning theory (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- Limited algorithmic novelty: the algorithm is primarily a synthesis of existing techniques from (Jin & Sidford 2021), (Li et al. 2020), and (Wang et al. 2023). R2 notes that all main instruments have already been introduced in those papers; R1, R3, and R4 echo this concern. (R2, despite rating the paper 8, also acknowledges this as a weakness.)
- The main paper does not sufficiently explain the critical technical point that enables the improved bound (e.g., Proposition A.1, the key concentration inequality). More discussion on the difference from previous analyses and on the specific challenges overcome is needed (R1, R3).
- Notation issues: \(\alpha\) in (2.6) appears to be a vector in \(\mathbb{R}^{|S|}\), yet \(\bar\alpha\) later selects a maximum, which seems inconsistent; \(\eta\) in (2.4) is introduced without definition (R3).
- No numerical experiments are provided to verify the predicted optimal sample complexity scaling; multiple reviewers (R1, R3, R4) request such validation.
- No discussion of whether the choice of the distribution of \(Z(s,a)\) matters (e.g., could a non-uniform distribution affect performance?) (R3).
- The paper does not address barriers to obtaining an algorithm with dependence on the span of the optimal value function instead of mixing time (R2).
- No discussion of whether the reduction to discounted setting can be avoided (R2).
- No analysis or extension to exploration-based (online) settings, e.g., like Ortner (2020) (R2).
- No discussion of implications for settings where uniform ergodicity does not hold, such as weakly communicating AMDPs (R4).
