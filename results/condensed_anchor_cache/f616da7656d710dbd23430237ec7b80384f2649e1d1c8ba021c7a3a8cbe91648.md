## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies distortion-free watermarks for LLMs, showing that key collisions in pseudo-random sampling cause distribution bias toward the original LM distribution. It proves that a perfect distortion-free watermark is impossible, introduces a hierarchy of distortion-freeness (step-wise, weakly, strongly), and proposes beta-watermarks to trade off watermark strength and distribution bias. Experiments on benchmark datasets show beta-watermarks reduce bias under key collisions.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and its contributions are clearly stated. It clarifies what “distortion-free” can mean for LLM watermarks and the implications. (R1)
- The mathematical proofs (e.g., impossibility of strongly distortion-free watermarks) appeared correct. (R1) The theoretical proofs align well with experimental results. (R3)
- The problem studied is important and timely – key collisions are inevitable, so addressing them is an important problem. (R2)
- Exploiting true randomness within the usage of pseudorandom keys is a good insight for designing watermarks. (R2)
- Investigating pseudo- versus true-random sampling offers a valuable contribution to the field. (R3)
- The proposed trade-off method (beta-watermark) demonstrates practical flexibility and applicability in specific scenarios. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and technical merit**: Many claims are straightforward to prove (e.g., Theorem 4.13: a model with detectable watermarks and the same output distribution as the original does not exist). (R2) The beta-watermark (0<β<0.5) is similar to the permute-reweight baseline (β=0); novelty is limited and performance gains in Table 2 are marginal. (R2)
- **Similarity to prior work**: The beta-watermark shares considerable similarities with DiPmark (Wu et al., “DiPmark”), and the algorithms in Appendix A are nearly identical to Algorithms 1 and 2 of DiPmark. A comparison with DiPmark is missing. (R3)
- **Unclear motivation for some claims**: The importance of strongly distortion-free watermarks is unclear. The paper suggests that a non-distortion-free watermark would cause repetitive generations, but this also occurs with greedy decoding without watermarking. Moreover, Aaranson et al. use random decoding on top of the modified distribution (not just greedy decoding), so repeatedly prompting with the same input does not necessarily produce identical outputs. (R1) Is this understanding correct?
- **Confusing phrasing**: “exhibit a significant distribution bias toward the original LM distribution” – what exactly does “toward the original LM distribution” mean? (R1)
- **Poorly motivated detection task**: Theorem 4.12 (detecting watermarked models) makes little sense – a service provider already knows which models are watermarked; the real task is detecting watermarked texts, not watermarked models. (R2)
- **Limited experiments**:
  - Baselines are restricted to distortion-free methods; statistical watermarks are not included. (R2)
  - No comparison with other watermarking techniques mentioned in the paper (e.g., DiPmark). (R3)
  - No examples comparing watermark text effects under different parameters. (R3)
  - No analysis of detection time, algorithmic complexity, or system performance overhead. (R3)
  - Data only for δ>1; no results for δ<1. Would the proposed method still show an advantage under δ<1? (R3)
  - Figure 3 does not clearly show which watermark performs better; is there any direct measurement of distortion-freeness? (R2) Figures 4 and 6 show minimal differences and should be combined. (R3)
- **Missing motivation for distortion-freeness**: The paper does not show that distortion leads to worse performance in watermarked models, so the importance of distortion-freeness is not well-motivated. (R2)
- **Problem positioning unclear**: The paper analyzes watermark behavior under key collisions, reflecting information capacity issues, but typical text watermarking applications have low demands on key space. The problem setting lacks clear positioning. (R3)
- **Detection algorithm lacking evaluation**: The black-box distortion-free detection algorithm in Section 4.3 relies on comparisons with the original unwatermarked model, but no comparative experiments are provided. What advantages does it offer? Would it improve efficiency? (R3)
- **Minor**: Typo in line 60 – “In particular” should not be capitalized. (R2)
