## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EATQA (Evidence-Enhanced Triplet Generation Framework), a unified triplet generation approach that trains LLMs on three tasks (evidence generation, question answering, question restoration) using gold evidence annotations. The goal is to reduce hallucination in generative question answering by encouraging the model to understand logical relationships among Question, Evidence, and Answer. Evaluated on MultiRC and QASPER, EATQA applied to LLama achieves state-of-the-art results.

### Strengths
- The method is well-motivated and the paper is easy to follow, with clear exposition of the architecture, figures, and equations.
- The experimental setup is comprehensive: EATQA outperforms prior state-of-the-art LLMs and hallucination mitigation methods on **MultiRC** and **QASPER**, with detailed comparisons and ablation studies showing the importance of each component.  
- The proposed triplet generation framework yields significant improvements on both benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited innovation**: The three-training-loss multi-task approach lacks technical depth and novelty. Evidence generation (extractive sentence selection) and query restoration are well-explored; the method does not provide new theoretical insights or a departure from existing multi-task learning paradigms.  
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: Missing recent retrieval-augmented models and methods using structured knowledge bases, knowledge graphs, or external sources. No comparison with prior related work on hallucination alleviation.  
- **Limited generalizability**: Evaluated only on MultiRC and QASPER. Not tested on more diverse or complex QA datasets such as NQ, TQ, StrategyQA, or MusiQA, which would better assess robustness.  
- **Gold evidence dependency**: Training requires gold evidence annotations, limiting applicability to datasets without such annotations.  
- **Modest improvement margins**: On some baselines (e.g., CAD, RHO) the gains are relatively modest.  
- **Missing computational cost and inference time comparison** against baselines.  
- **Hallucination evaluation not thorough**: The analysis in Sec. 5.4 does not directly measure how well hallucination is alleviated. No explicit comparison with related hallucination mitigation methods is provided.  
- **Analysis in Sec. 5.3 is incomplete**: No explanation of how reference evidences were obtained when datasets lack evidence annotations, nor how the scores were calculated.  
- **Sec. 5.2 analysis adds limited insight**: Since document length is proportional to number of sentences, Tables 4 and 5 alone might suffice.  
- **Hyperparameters not clearly described**: In particular, the value of the KL-divergence weight \(\alpha_{kl}\) and how it was fixed is not specified.  
- **Writing and formatting issues**: The paper contains incorrect LaTeX cite commands (making reading difficult), non-vector images, and requires a native English language check. Reviewers noted content is partly hard to understand.
