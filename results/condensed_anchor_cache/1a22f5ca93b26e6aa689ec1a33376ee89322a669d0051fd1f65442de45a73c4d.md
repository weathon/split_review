## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies substructure counting power of subgraph GNNs and proposes ESC-GNN, which precomputes structural embeddings (degree, node-level distance, edge-level distance) for rooted subgraphs around each edge, then applies a standard GNN on the augmented graph. The authors theoretically relate subgraph GNNs to the WL hierarchy and claim that their encoding method provides efficient substructure counting without brute-force subgraph enumeration.

### Strengths
- The investigation of GNNs' substructure counting abilities is important for understanding expressive power (all reviewers).
- The generalization of subgraph GNNs to leverage higher-order GNNs as backbones is a sensible theoretical extension (R1).
- Augmenting the input graph with precomputed subgraph information is a well-justified, reasonable data augmentation / efficiency technique (R1, R2, R4). The precomputation strategy mitigates the complexity of running MPNNs over subgraphs (R4).
- The theoretical results (e.g., linking counting power to WL hierarchy) complement the proposed method (R1, R4). The presentation of theory is clear, and Figure 1 helps understanding (R4).
- The handcrafted structural embeddings allow a standard GNN to count small substructures (4-cycles, 4-cliques, 3-paths) without expensive subgraph enhancement (R2, R4).
- The architecture is simple but expressive for substructure counting (R3).
- The synthetic experiments demonstrate value, especially in light of efficiency gains (R4). The analysis of QM9 results, including cases where ESC-GNN performs less well, is thorough (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theory–method mismatch**: The bulk of the paper (Section 4) advocates higher-order GNNs, but the proposed ESC-GNN uses a standard GNN on an augmented graph. The connection between Sections 4 and 5 is unclear (R1, also R1 Q1). One reviewer questions the theoretical justifications of the encoding method and what guarantees it provides (R1, R1 Q2).
- **Lack of novelty relative to prior work**: The approach is closely related to Bouritsas et al. and Barceló et al., which also inject subgraph isomorphism/homomorphism counts into GNNs and use handcrafted features (R1, R1 Q3).
- **Scalability claim is overstated**: Not all subgraph-enhanced GNNs suffer scalability issues; there exist efficient methods that learn which subgraphs to use (e.g., Qian 2022). The paper does not compare to such methods (R2, R2 Q1). A minority reviewer (R2) finds that the theoretical counting results have limited practical relevance because they only cover very small substructures (≤4 nodes) and drop sharply in performance for larger ones.
- **Permutation equivariance violation** (major concern): The node-level distance encodings in \(s_{uv}\) are concatenated in an arbitrary order (u first vs. v first). For undirected edges, this choice (based on node ID) breaks permutation equivariance (R3).
- **Proposition 4.5 is not properly justified**: The claim that a subgraph GNN rooted at k-tuples with backbone m-WL can be implemented by (m+k)-IGN does not follow from cited work; previous results only cover (k=1,m=2) or (k arbitrary, m=1). The proposition is not proven and needs clarification (R3, also R3 Q1). A related claim that ESC-GNN is strictly less powerful than subgraph MPNNs on 2-tuples is contradicted by Proposition 5.1, which says it can be as powerful (R3 Q2).
- **Insufficient and poorly presented experiments**:
    - Theoretical results focus on graph-level counting, yet node-level tasks are tested. The reasoning for this choice is unclear (R3).
    - Table 1 shows good performance for small substructures (3-cycles, 4-cycles, etc.) but errors increase severely for 5-cycles and 6-cycles, indicating poor generalization (R2). The MAE cutoff of 0.01 for the successful bracket is not explained (R2 Q3).
    - Real-world results on QM9 are strong on some tasks but substantially worse on others (R4). Results on OGBG-HIV and ZINC (reported only in appendix) are not strong; the paper reports time on these datasets but omits main results, and the paper lacks a clear use case where ESC-GNN would be the obvious best choice (R4, R3 Q4b).
    - ZINC counting experiments are limited to cycles; results do not report standard deviation or multiple seeds (R3 Q4c). The backbone used is a graph transformer, and results with a standard GNN are not shown (R3 Q4c).
- **Limited scope**: The counting framework is specialized to small common patterns (paths, cycles, cliques up to size 4). The paper does not discuss more general substructure detection or provide case studies on real datasets to motivate why these patterns matter in practice (R4, R2 Q5). One reviewer notes the hand-crafted nature and ad-hoc theoretical arguments may only be useful in extremely specific instances (R2).
- **Clarification needed on Theorem 4.4**: It is not specified whether it applies to induced or non-induced substructures (R3 Q3). The values of k and m used for experiments are not provided (R2 Q4). The definition of “globally expressive models” is unclear (R2 Q2).
