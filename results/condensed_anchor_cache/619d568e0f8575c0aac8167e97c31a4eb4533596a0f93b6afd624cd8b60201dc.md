## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes mDP-DPG, a momentum-based discrete prompt learning method with doubly policy gradient for black-box LLMs in imbalanced binary classification. It integrates pairwise AUC loss into policy gradient optimization to handle class imbalance and introduces two variance reduction techniques: an unbiased variance-reduced doubly policy gradient estimator (VR-DPGE) and the STORM technique. Theoretical convergence guarantees are provided. Experiments on benchmark datasets (CoLA, MRPC) and real-world imbalanced datasets (Amazon reviews) show mDP-DPG outperforms baselines.

### Strengths
- First paper to address the challenge of data imbalance in discrete prompt learning for black-box LLMs.
- Integrates pairwise AUC loss into policy gradient optimization for prompts, targeting imbalanced classification.
- Introduces dual variance reduction (VR-DPGE and STORM) to stabilize optimization and accelerate convergence.
- Provides rigorous theoretical convergence guarantees within standard frameworks.
- Experimental validation on diverse imbalanced text classification datasets demonstrates clear improvements over baselines.
- Potential to serve as a fine-tuning method for commercial black-box models.

### Weaknesses
- Technical novelty is limited; the method largely combines existing techniques (AUC loss, policy gradient, STORM).
- The paper only supports binary classification. No extension to multi-class classification is provided (e.g., whether it requires multiple prompts). This limits practical applicability since binary classification is rare in real-world LLM use.
- The scenario of using black-box LLMs (API-only) for simple classification tasks was questioned by one reviewer (score 5, lower than others). Manually designed prompts can already achieve strong performance on GLUE-like tasks, reducing the appeal of the proposed method to the ICLR audience. Other reviewers did not raise this concern.
- Experiments lack comparisons with widely used API-only LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5, GPT-4) and with other existing black-box prompt tuning methods such as APE, EvoPrompt, and ZOPO. One reviewer noted that the backbone models used (RoBERTa-large, GPT2-XL from 2019; Llama 3 8B) are outdated or too small relative to commonly deployed models (e.g., Llama 3 70B, GPT-4).
- The assumption that tokens in the prompt are sampled independently from their marginal distribution is questionable; tokens in a meaningful prompt should have relations. The paper does not justify this assumption. Relatedly, no discussion is given on whether the learned distribution converges to a delta distribution over a single optimal prompt, or how to obtain a stable discrete prompt when probabilities spread across many tokens.
- Insufficient explanation of key technical components: no adequate references for VR-DPGE; Equation (8) is not explained in terms of how it reduces variance; notation \(T^{(k)}\) is not clearly defined; the role of momentum in the STORM strategy is not discussed; the \(\text{proj}_{\mathcal{C}}\) function is not defined. The theoretical results lack interpretation of what the convergence guarantees imply about the quality of the resulting prompts.
- Several AUC values in Tables 1 and 2 fall below 0.5 (worse than random). The paper does not explain possible reasons for this.
