## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a two-pronged adversarial defense called Distributional-Discrepancy-based Adversarial Defense (DDAD). It builds on Statistical Adversarial Data Detection (SADD) by detecting adversarial examples (AEs) using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and then, instead of discarding detected AEs, passes them through a denoiser before classification. The training phase optimizes MMD statistics to obtain MMD-OPT and trains a denoiser by minimizing MMD-OPT between clean examples (CEs) and AEs. During inference, CEs go directly to the classifier, while AEs are denoised. The paper provides theoretical motivation linking adversarial risk to distributional discrepancy. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K show improved clean and robust accuracy compared to SOTA defenses, including against unseen transfer attacks. Reviewers generally found the idea interesting and the theoretical motivation a strength, but raised concerns about the practical reliance on batch-wise inference, evaluation fairness, reproducibility, and the handling of mixed batches or out-of-distribution inputs.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-motivated and clearly explains the idea, connecting adversarial risk to distributional discrepancy via Theorem 1, which bounds classifier error on AEs by CE error plus variation distance (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- The proposed DDAD combines statistical detection (SADD) with denoising in a two-pronged manner, avoiding information loss from discarding detected AEs—a limitation of prior SADD methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5).
- Without relying on large additional data, DDAD significantly improves both clean and robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K across multiple backbone architectures, and it shows scalability to unseen transfer attacks in a gray-box setting (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The approach is computationally less intensive than purely denoiser-based defenses (Reviewer 5).
- One reviewer (score 8) found the method novel and leading to significantly better results, while another (score 5) raised fundamental issues with the batch-based formulation (minority contrast preserved).
- The paper provides theoretical proofs and algorithms, and the first three sections are well written (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Impractical batch-wise assumption:** The method requires batch-wise processing to compute MMD, but standard DNN inference accepts single inputs. The problem formulation is thus impractical for real-time or single-instance applications. Even if batch inference is assumed, the attack surface changes and the original robustness problem (single-input AE detection) is not solved. The discussion in Section 7 does not convincingly justify this shift (Reviewer 4; also noted by Reviewer 5).
- **Lack of unified evaluation:** Baseline methods are evaluated with AutoAttack while DDAD uses adaptive white-box attacks, making fair comparison difficult. Results under the same attack (e.g., AutoAttack or PGD+EOT) should be reported (Reviewer 1).
- **Threshold and sensitivity:** The detection threshold for MMD-OPT is set to 0.05 without clear justification. A sensitivity analysis showing the impact on clean and robust accuracy is missing. Adaptive or learned thresholds should be explored (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Theoretical gaps:** Assumptions 1/2 about the existence of a ground-truth labeling function $f_A$ invariant to adversarial perturbations need rigorous justification and discussion of conditions. Theorem 1 uses variation distance while the method uses MMD; the mathematical or empirical relationship between these discrepancies is not established (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Reproducibility and complexity:** The paper does not provide open-source code or detailed replication instructions. The multi-stage training (MMD optimization, denoiser training, threshold tuning) adds complexity, making reproduction challenging (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Inference reference batch selection:** It is unclear how the batch of clean validation data used as reference is chosen (e.g., uniform per class or empirical distribution). If an attacker supplies only one class along with a few AEs, the whole batch might be misclassified as clean. The method is sensitive to reference batch composition (Reviewer 3).
- **Out-of-distribution robustness:** No evaluation is done on distribution-shifted data (e.g., CIFAR-10.2, CIFAR-C, CIFAR-P). Such samples might be incorrectly flagged as adversarial and unnecessarily processed by the denoiser (Reviewer 3).
- **Mixture of CEs and AEs:** Performance degrades when batches contain a mixture of clean and adversarial examples. MMD-based detection is not robust to variations in the proportion of AEs, which is a significant limitation for real-world unknown mixtures (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Limited domain evaluation:** Experiments are only on image classification (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-1K). The method needs validation on diverse domains such as medical imagery, autonomous driving, or NLP to assess generalizability (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing related work:** Nayak et al. (2023, DE-CROP) also uses an MMD-based loss to train a denoiser for adversarial defense and should be discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear details in Figure 1 and Table 3:** Figure 1 does not clarify whether the two classifiers are the same, how AEs are generated (specifically for that classifier), how test data are attacked, and what the detection probability is for attacked test data. Table 3’s description of “unseen transfer attacks” in a gray-box setting needs clearer explanation tied to the figure (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete extension of assumptions:** Equations 4–6 and the associated assumptions are not explicitly extended to components like Batch Normalization layers, pooling layers, or transformer structures; a more comprehensive explanation is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Alternative discrepancy measures not explored:** The paper does not compare MMD with other statistical measures (e.g., Wasserstein distance, energy distance) or discuss drawbacks (Reviewer 5).
- **Denoiser architecture impact:** The impact of different denoising DNN architectures (e.g., DUNET vs. others) on MMD-OPT and overall performance is not investigated (Reviewer 4).
- **Equation (5) detail:** It is unclear whether $m = n$ in the equation; the variable $m$ appears undefined (Reviewer 4).
