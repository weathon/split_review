## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Low-Rank Correction (LRC) for post-training quantization (PTQ) of LLMs to 4-bit weights and activations (W4A4). LRC adds full-precision low-rank weight matrices that act on the unquantized activations to correct errors induced by quantizing both weights and activations. The method jointly optimizes the quantized weight matrix and the low-rank matrices using alternating minimization, leveraging GPTQ as a subroutine for weight quantization. On Llama-2, Llama-3, Phi-3, and Mixtral models, LRC achieves competitive perplexity and zero-shot task accuracy, reducing the gap with the full-precision model; using rank equivalent to 30% of the original weight matrix, the accuracy gap is reportedly closed on Phi-3.

### Strengths
- The studied problem of efficient LLM deployment via compression is important and timely.
- The method is novel in introducing low-rank correction jointly optimized with quantization for both weights and activations (W4A4), differentiating it from prior work that primarily addresses weight-only quantization.
- The formulation is intuitive and well-explained, with clear derivations making the paper easy to follow.
- The method is modular: it can use any weight quantization technique as a subroutine (GPTQ in the paper), and the low-rank correction can be plugged into other frameworks.
- Sensible ablations identify the individual contributions of weight and activation quantization and demonstrate when low-rank correction is beneficial.
- Achieves state-of-the-art results for W4A4 quantization, substantially reducing the accuracy gap compared to QuaRot; with rank 30%, the gap is closed on Phi-3 (though this claim is contested by one reviewer).
- One iteration of alternating minimization suffices for good performance (noted by Reviewer 4).
- The approach has practical potential for deploying compressed LLMs.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution**: Several reviewers (R1, R5) consider the contribution limited, noting high overlap with prior works (Zhang et al. 2024, Ou et al. 2024, and the joint optimization technique is already known from Saha et al. 2024). Reviewer 4 additionally points out similarity to LoRA and QLoRA and recommends citing them. Other reviewers (R2, R3, R4) acknowledge novelty and effectiveness, creating disagreement. The paper does not clearly articulate how it significantly advances beyond existing low-rank correction techniques for simultaneous weight and activation quantization (R5).
- **Missing theoretical justification**: The paper contains several propositions without formal proofs (R4). There is no error analysis for the approximation methods; it does not demonstrate that the iterative optimization of low-rank corrections converges to a solution minimizing Equation 2 (R5). The assumptions of full rank in Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 are questionable since activations in LLMs often exhibit low-rank or ill-conditioned structure (R5). The theoretical analysis is overly simplified and does not address practical scenarios (R5).
- **Lack of computational efficiency analysis**: The paper does not analyze the computational cost and memory footprint introduced by the low-rank correction matrices. Multiple reviewers (R2, R3, R5) request detailed analysis of inference speed, memory usage, FLOPs, and memory accesses, and how these scale with rank size and model size. The authors leave concrete implementation strategies for future work, limiting practical adoption (R3). The additional memory cost of the low-rank matrices (e.g., bits per weight or average bit precision) should be explicitly reported to allow fair comparison (R1).
- **Insufficient baselines and experimental comparisons**: The paper compares only against QuaRot (R1). It should also compare to Quip, Quip#, LQER, and other contemporary PTQ methods (R1). Generative tasks such as GSM8K are missing; these are typically harder for quantized models than multiple-choice tasks (R4). The claim that rank 30% closes the accuracy gap seems to hold only for Phi-3, not for all models, and the corresponding results are not clearly located in the evaluation section (R1). There is an unexplained anomaly: for Phi-3, LRC(1) outperforms LRC(5) in Table 1 (R1). The last row (LRC(5)) for Mixtral is missing in both Table 1 and Table 5 (R2). In Table 3, LRC fails to beat SVD on average score, which requires explanation (R2).
- **Sensitivity to calibration data not explored**: The choice of calibration dataset X is not analyzed. The paper lacks a systematic investigation of how dataset size, domain, and diversity affect LRC performance (R2, R3). Robustness and generalizability of the method are therefore unclear.
- **Presentation and completeness issues**: The paper has several typos (lines 57, 191, 353, 405, 421, 455) (R4). The subsection "on the effect of rank" (L399–407) does not reference the relevant table/figure (R1). The phrase "10% additional ranks" (L431) is not meaningful (R1). Best results are not bolded in tables (R1). The paper assumes familiarity with GPTQ and Cholesky; an introduction in the appendix would aid broader audience (R2).
- **Activation quantization scheme not advanced**: The method relies on standard round-to-nearest for activation quantization and does not propose any improved activation quantization technique (R3). While the authors note that better activation quantization could improve results, they do not explore alternatives or analyze the limitations of round-to-nearest in the LRC context (R3).
