## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GMValuator, the first training-free and model-agnostic data valuation method for generative models. It formulates data valuation as a similarity-matching problem and incorporates image quality assessment (IQA) to calibrate contributions. The authors introduce four evaluation criteria (C1–C4) and evaluate on multiple datasets and generative architectures. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty and efficiency of the approach, but note limitations in evaluation scope, theoretical rigor, presentation, and missing ablations. One reviewer is notably more positive (score 8, well-written) while others are more critical of the writing and scope.

### Strengths
- First model-agnostic and retraining-free data valuation method for generative models – a novel and intuitive idea. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Efficient: no retraining or Hessian computation required. (R1, R2)
- Provides detailed theoretical justification for the similarity-matching formulation, with empirical validation that makes the approach reasonable. (R1)  
- Well-designed experiments covering multiple datasets (benchmark and high-resolution large-scale), generative models, distance functions, and encoders; results are promising and significantly outperform baselines on the proposed metrics. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Open-source implementation, increasing credibility and reproducibility. (R1)
- Paper covers related literature well. (R3)
- One reviewer finds the paper well-written and easy to follow (R3); another strongly disagrees on presentation (R1 – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation / Motivation:** The introduction lacks a clear definition of the data valuation problem (input, objective) and does not highlight the urgent need for such methods in generative models. The principle of GMValuator is not explained before stating goals. (R1)  
  *Contrast:* Reviewer 3 finds the paper well-written and easy to follow. (R3)
- **Scope limited to images:** The method is only evaluated on image samples, yet the paper claims “for generative models.” The statement that it can be adapted to NLP is not sufficiently justified; how to apply to text or multimodal data is unclear. (R1; R1 Q1)
- **Lack of results on SOTA text-to-image models:** For models like Stable Diffusion, the wide image domain likely requires many generated images and generation is slow. More results and ablation on such models (e.g., diffusion models) would strengthen the evaluation. (R2; R3 Q3)
- **Evaluation metrics are coarse-grained:** The four proposed criteria (C1–C4) may not fully reflect the effectiveness of data valuation; they may be too coarse to capture nuanced contributions. (R2) Only four criteria are used; why these four? Missing aspects such as cost-benefit analysis (trade-offs between data acquisition/processing costs and performance gains) with formulas and examples. (R4)
- **Missing ablation on quantization step:** The effect of the quantization step in the recall phase is not explored. (R3; R3 Q1)  
- **Theoretical depth:** While the motivation is intuitive, the framework would benefit from a more rigorous theoretical foundation to strengthen its reliability across applications. (R3)  
  *Contrast:* Reviewer 1 considers the theoretical justification detailed and empirical validation sufficient. (R1)
- **Accuracy and complexity not discussed:** The paper does not discuss how accurately the framework captures contributions of individual data points in various scenarios, nor its computational complexity. (R4)
- **Scalability not addressed:** The method is not tested on progressively larger datasets, and processing times for different data sizes are not reported, leaving real-time applicability unclear. (R4)
- **Missing comparison with other model-agnostic approaches:** No comparison with Information-Theoretic Measures (Akhilan Boopathy et al., ICML 2023) or examination of whether such approaches are applicable to generative models in a model-agnostic, retraining-free manner. (R4 Q)
- **Question on practical limitations:** If data valuation is not used in training generative models, what are the limitations? Can the proposed method or evaluation metrics measure those limitations? (R1 Q2)
- **Question on relation to nearest neighbor search:** How different is the proposed score from a simple nearest neighbor search with a rerank metric in the embedding space? (R3 Q2)
