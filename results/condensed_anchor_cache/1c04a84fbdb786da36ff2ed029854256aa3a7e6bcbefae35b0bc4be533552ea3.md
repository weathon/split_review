## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MUTUD (Multimodal Training and Unimodal Deployment), a framework that leverages audiovisual training to improve inference with only one modality (audio). It introduces a Temporally Aligned Modality feature Estimation (TAME) module with codebooks to estimate missing visual features from audio. The method is evaluated on three audiovisual speech tasks: speech recognition, active speaker detection, and speech enhancement. Results show performance gains over unimodal baselines while reducing model parameters and MACs by ~80% compared to full multimodal models. Reviewers 1 and 2 were more positive, highlighting clear presentation and practical efficiency; Reviewers 3 and 4 were more critical, citing limited novelty, insufficient experiments, and weak baselines.

### Strengths
- Clear presentation; the TAME design is simple and easy to understand (R1, R3). The overall structure is well-organized (R4).
- The motivation of using multimodal training for efficient unimodal deployment is innovative and appears to be the first adaptation to audiovisual speech tasks (R1, R2). The approach reduces model size and compute compared to multimodal models (R2, R4).
- The method is parameter- and compute-efficient: 80% fewer parameters than full multimodal systems, with reduced MACs (R1, R2, R4).
- Experiments are sufficiently extensive across three tasks and include different noise conditions and SNR levels (R1, R4). Ablation studies on codebook sizes and architectural choices are provided (R4).
- The codebook-based modality alignment is a useful technique for multimodal-to-unimodal distillation, and the interleaving method for aligning audio and visual dimensions works effectively (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution**: The core idea of using one modality to inform another through a learned association is not new (e.g., "Multi-modality Associative Bridging through Memory: Speech Sound Recollected from Face Video"). The paper does not clearly articulate what unique innovations TAME brings beyond existing modality bridging techniques (R3, R4). R4 notes that the methods section is extremely short and largely defines standard codebook concepts without citing the original work; loss functions are also uncited. The overall technique is a simple, direct application of others’ work, insufficient for ICLR (R4). R1 and R2 found the approach novel, but R3 and R4 strongly disagree on the level of innovation.
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: Only two baselines are compared: an adapted audio method from 2019 (GCRN) and an adapted audiovisual method from 2023 (R4). Older models are insufficient to support claims (R3, R4). The paper lacks comparison with contemporary state-of-the-art models on each of the three tasks (at least 3 audiovisual and 3 audio-only methods suggested (R4)). Missing baseline: using multiple modalities at training only to improve unimodal performance has been studied before (e.g., Abavisani et al.) and should be compared (R2). R3 emphasizes the need for comparison with recent works in AVSE, AVSR, and Active Speaker Detection.
- **Lack of independent validation on out-of-domain/unimodal datasets**: No evaluations on standard audio-only datasets (e.g., for speech enhancement, ASR, speaker detection) to check whether the model genuinely learns audio-to-video projection or merely overfits to biases in the audiovisual dataset (R3). R4 adds that there is no empirical justification that estimating visual features from audio is better than using audio features alone; the codebook methodology should be tested in audio-only, audiovisual, and audiovisual-with-audio-only settings to isolate improvements.
- **Limited performance gains**: The reported improvements over audio-only models are marginal, raising questions about practical utility given added complexity (R3). R1 also asks for a detailed analysis of trade-offs between performance gains and the practical costs of deploying audiovisual systems (e.g., sensor requirements, synchronization). R1 requests concrete industry scenarios where visual data acquisition is justified by significant enough gains.
- **Generality and modality scope**: Only the audiovisual case is studied, and within that only audio for inference (not video-only) (R4). It is unclear if MUTUD generalizes to other settings (e.g., text as an additional modality) or other visual modalities (face vs lip) (R1, R2, R4). Extending to a more general multimodal setting would strengthen the work (R2). R1 also notes that the listed multimodal challenges (lines 45-54) apply specifically to audiovisual, not speech-text, and that multimodal input can be noisy/confusing – a missing challenge.
- **Temporal alignment and robustness**: The paper does not discuss the impact of temporal misalignment between audio and video streams on TAME performance, nor how the model handles unsynchronized data common in real-world scenarios (R1). This could lead to error accumulation (R1).
- **Evaluation metrics and inference cost**: Parameters and MACs are reported but inference times are missing, which is more relevant for real-world deployment (R4). The breakdown of how parameters/MACs are allocated across codebooks and modules is not provided (R4). Codebooks can be slow unless augmented with transformers (e.g., https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13438) – inference times should be included (R4).
- **Methodological issues**: The claim that visual modality is the most widely used in speech tasks (line 36) lacks evidence (R1). The tables are not well labeled; it is often unclear which task or dataset each table corresponds to (R4). There is a typo on line 058 (R2). The variable ‘D’ on lines 195-196 should be defined (R2). The related work section is missing the ReVISE paper (R1). R3 and R4 note that experiments are not tested on more advanced backbone architectures (only older ones like GCRN 2019); validation on recent backbones is needed (R3).
- **Disagreement on experimental sufficiency**: R1 states experiments are sufficiently extensive, while R3 and R4 strongly argue they are insufficient (R3: “lacks persuasiveness”, R4: “do not support the authors’ claims”). This contrast is maintained.
