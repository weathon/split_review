## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a diffusion-based portrait animation method using facial parsing maps as motion guidance, a random scaling technique to prevent identity leakage, foreground-background separation, and a cyclic overlap strategy for temporal consistency in long videos. The method is trained on the VFHQ dataset and compared against several state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods. One reviewer (score 8) finds it novel and thorough, while three (score 3) see it as an incremental modification of existing work (e.g., AnimateAnyone) and question the rationale and advantages of face parsing maps over keypoints.

### Strengths

- The paper is very well-written and the figures make the method easy to follow.
- The use of facial parsing maps as a portrait-driven modality is a novel proposal compared to sparse landmarks.
- The method shows SOTA performance on expression metrics (8% improvement) and outperforms prior methods quantitatively and qualitatively in the presented comparisons.
- An ablation study is conducted verifying the positive effect of random scaling, foreground-background separation, and cyclic overlap, with supporting visualizations in the appendix.
- The authors provide detailed code to facilitate reproducibility.
- Each component of the method is ablated.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: The overall architecture is very similar to AnimateAnyone (ReferenceNet + PoseGuided diffusion) with only small modifications (stronger augmentations and sampling tricks). The paper does not further understanding of the problem or solve a superset (e.g., relighting, hair animation). This view is held by three reviewers despite one reviewer praising sufficient novelty.
- **Rationale of facial parsing maps vs. landmarks is unclear**: The paper lacks a fair ablation study comparing face representations (parsing maps, landmarks, meshes, reference images) to justify the choice. Parsing maps have inherent robustness weaknesses (susceptible to occlusions like partial eye or mouth obstructions) and cannot capture eyelid or mouth movements. Denser keypoint sets exist (e.g., InsightFace 203, MediaPipe 478).
- **Temporal consistency artifacts**: Videos in the supplementary material show notable jitter and inter-frame inconsistency, especially in hair and the neck area (e.g., 001.mp4, 002.mp4). The cyclic overlap technique does not fully resolve these issues.
- **Missing video comparisons with SOTA**: The supplementary videos do not include comparisons with previous methods, so temporal stability cannot be qualitatively compared. It is possible that prior SOTA methods achieve better temporal consistency (omission concern from Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparisons with relevant methods**: The paper does not compare with prior GAN/motion flow face reenactment methods such as Face-vid2vid and DA-GAN, nor with the recent LivePortrait (portrait animation with stitching and retargeting control).
- **Random scaling does not fully solve identity-skin coupling**: While random scaling alleviates correlation between parsing map and facial shape for the face area, other areas like nose shape and size remain correlated with the person's identity.
- **Driving results not always accurate**: Figure 2 shows that head pose and expressions sometimes differ from the driving images, and in the last two rows the proposed method performs worse than some compared methods.
- **Cross-reenactment identity performance**: Table 1 shows poor identity performance in cross-reenactment; the reason is not clearly explained (possibly affected by driving person shape).
- **Unclear explanation**: Lines 306-308 are not easy to understand and need a more intuitive explanation.
- **Multiple reviewers**: The proposed improvements (random scaling, separation, cyclic overlap) are considered training/inference tricks; overall visual quality is not impressive to some reviewers.
