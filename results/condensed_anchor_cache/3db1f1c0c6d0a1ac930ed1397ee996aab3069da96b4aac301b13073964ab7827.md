## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes VFair, a method to train fair classifiers without access to sensitive attributes by minimizing the variance of per-sample losses while keeping the average loss from increasing. The optimization uses a dynamic harmless update approach (inspired by dynamic barrier gradient descent) that modifies gradients only when they align with the primary objective. Experiments on four benchmark datasets compare against several baselines and report accuracy disparities and other metrics. Reviewers note the idea is novel and interesting, but raise concerns about weak theoretical justification, modest empirical gains, missing comparisons, and lack of statistical significance reporting.

### Strengths
- The idea of minimizing the variance of losses as a proxy for worst-group fairness is novel and interesting, and addresses a real-world need for fair classification without demographic information (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The paper is well written and reader-friendly, with clear explanations of the optimization method and the harmless update intuition (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Pointing out the relationship between MAD and variance (Reviewer 2).
- Proposition 2 (losses must be independent of protected groups) is highlighted as a strong motivator; one reviewer suggests it should be in the main body (Reviewer 4).
- The harmless update design shows thoughtful heuristics to avoid degrading utility (Reviewer 4).
- The experiments are comprehensive in terms of number of datasets, baselines, and metrics (Reviewer 4). One reviewer found that VFair may outperform existing methods (Reviewer 3), while others found the gains modest (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Weak theoretical foundation**: Proposition 1 is criticized for being a very loose upper bound (inflating the right-hand side by a factor of n) and therefore unpersuasive. The bound is not tight and the reliance on this inequality to motivate variance minimization is problematic (Reviewer 4).
- **Underwhelming and inconclusive results**: The reported metric values in Table 1 (and Table 2) are very close across methods, making it hard to draw conclusions. This is a major concern across reviewers (1, 2, 3, 4). No confidence intervals, standard deviations, or statistical significance tests are provided (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The differences to unconstrained ERM are small. One reviewer notes that only variance (the metric VFair explicitly optimizes) improves, while other fairness metrics do not improve over baselines, raising doubts about practical utility (Reviewer 4). Another reviewer sees VFair outperforming existing methods (Reviewer 3), which contradicts the weaker assessments—this discrepancy is unresolved.
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison to standard constrained optimization approaches (e.g., Lagrangian dual ascent as in Cotter et al. 2019) or to methods that have access to sensitive attributes (to contextualize performance) (Reviewer 1). The paper does not explain why standard constrained optimization was not attempted (Reviewer 1). The claim that the Lagrangian uses a fixed multiplier is incorrect (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing fairness metrics**: The paper only considers accuracy disparities; major fairness metrics like equalized odds and statistical parity are not reported. Two of the metrics in Table 1 should be replaced with such metrics (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental details**: The ML model (type, architecture), loss function used (e.g., BCE?), optimizer, learning rate decay strategy, hyperparameter epsilon robustness, and stability are not clearly specified (Reviewers 1, 2). The definition of \(\hat{\sigma}\) in Appendix B.2 conflicts with that in the main body (Reviewer 2). The robustness of \(\epsilon\) is not studied (Reviewer 2). Code is not shared (Reviewer 1). Some rows in Table 2 (greyed out) always produce a constant/uniform classifier, which needs explanation (Reviewer 1).
- **Related work gaps**: The paper does not discuss prior work on variance regularization for robustness or label noise, and how the fairness objective differs (Reviewer 2). Connections to the Rashomon effect literature and flat loss landscapes (Reviewers 3, 4) should be discussed.
- **Questions on optimization details**: How does equation (6) lead to \(\lambda = 0\) in certain cases? (Reviewer 2). Does “sample will be filtered out” mean it is not used for training that iteration? (Reviewer 2). The method may take longer to train, which could be an unfair advantage if runtime is not reported (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority positive view**: One reviewer strongly endorses the idea and finds the results promising (Reviewer 3, score 6), while another doubts the practical contribution (Reviewer 4, score 6 but with strong criticism of results). Reviewer 1 (score 3) is very critical of the empirical evaluation and theoretical justification.
