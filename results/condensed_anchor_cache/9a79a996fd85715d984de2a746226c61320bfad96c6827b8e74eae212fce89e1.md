## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates whether LLMs linearly represent the truth or falsehood of factual statements. Using LLaMA-13B, the authors introduce a mass-mean probing method (connecting centroids of true/false activations without training), curated datasets of simple declarative sentences, and provide three lines of evidence: visualizations show linear separation, probes transfer across datasets, and causal interventions flip the model’s output. One reviewer (score 8) finds the analysis detailed and the methods effective, while the other three reviewers (scores 3,5,5) raise significant concerns about novelty, generalizability, arbitrary experimental choices, and unclear practical relevance.

### Strengths

- The motivation is timely and important – understanding LLM truthfulness aligns with trustworthy AI and truth-conditional semantics.
- Mass-mean probing is a training‑free alternative to logistic regression; it overcomes the problem that logistic regression may confound the truth direction with independent features.
- The mass-mean probe shows strong causal efficacy: intervening with its direction changes model outputs from true to false and vice‑versa.
- Visualization (PCA) of true/false statement representations reveals clear linear structure.
- Detailed analysis of within‑task and cross‑task generalization, using both synthetic and natural data.
- Curated controlled datasets (e.g., declarative sentences with unambiguous truth labels) are a useful contribution for future work.
- The causal intervention demonstrates that the truth vectors are “active” – not just correlational – highlighting the value of working with open models.

### Weaknesses

1. **Claims are not sufficiently novel.** Linear separability of truth representations and causal intervention have already been demonstrated (Li et al., Burns et al.). The paper does not clearly differentiate its findings from this prior work. *(Reviewer 1)*

2. **Only one model (LLaMA‑13B) is tested.** It is unclear whether the linear structure, transfer, and causal effects generalize to other architectures, sizes, or pretraining objectives. All four reviewers note this; Reviewer 3 calls the verification “lacking” given the paper’s title, and Reviewer 1 asks whether the truth direction holds in other LLMs. *(Reviewers 1,2,3,4)*

3. **Mass‑mean probing does not improve generalization accuracy over existing methods.** While it offers causal advantages, its predictive performance is not clearly superior, raising questions about practical utility. *(Reviewer 1; contrast: Reviewer 2 sees mass‑mean as effective without training)*

4. **Experimental settings appear arbitrary and may be cherry‑picked.** Results are reported only for: a specific layer (layer 10 for intervention, while layer 12 was used earlier), a specific part of the network (after residual stream, at the period token), and specific configurations (PCA, centroids). Many plausible alternative choices are unexplored, making it hard to rule out selection bias. *(Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 also questions the choice of layer 10)*

5. **Issues with Table 2 and intervention details.**  
   - The False→True direction’s success rate is far worse for logistic regression than for mass‑mean; no intuition is provided. *(Reviewer 2)*  
   - The α values used in interventions are extremely large (so the resulting vector almost equals αθ). It is unclear why values are so high and whether a baseline that simply uses αθ alone would behave similarly. *(Reviewer 2)*

6. **Practical takeaway is unclear.** The linear separability may be an artifact of the curated dataset’s simplicity; real‑world statements are messier. It remains unclear how to leverage the findings — e.g., could a loss be injected during training to improve factuality? *(Reviewer 4)*

7. **Causal intervention is at the hidden level, not at the word/token/text level.** Most probes can flip binary output with a hidden‑level intervention; a more interpretable demonstration would be to also make the change visible in the generated text (e.g., inserting a negation word). *(Reviewer 4)*

8. **Correspondence between the truth vector perturbation and natural language operations is not studied.** For example, does moving from true to false along the vector correspond to adding “not” to the sentence? *(Reviewer 3)*

9. **Paper would benefit from a richer discussion** of how to interpret the many experimental results as a whole. *(Reviewer 2)*

10. **Dataset details should be in the main text** instead of relegated to appendices. *(Reviewer 2)*

11. **Generalization of mass‑mean probing to multi‑class tasks** beyond binary is not addressed. *(Reviewer 2, as a question)*

12. **Styling issue:** Figure on page 17 is disproportionate. *(Reviewer 1)*
