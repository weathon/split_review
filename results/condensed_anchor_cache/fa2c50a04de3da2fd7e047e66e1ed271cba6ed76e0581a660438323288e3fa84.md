## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper benchmarks different hyperparameter optimization (HPO) frameworks for continual learning (CL), focusing on how to assemble validation sets in a streaming setting. It compares several realistic HPO strategies (e.g., first-task, current-task, seen-tasks) using replay-based CL methods on standard benchmarks (split CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet). The empirical finding is that none of the HPO frameworks consistently outperforms the others, leading the authors to recommend selecting an HPO framework based on computational efficiency. The paper concludes that current CL benchmarks may not be sufficient to discriminate between HPO approaches.

### Strengths
- Correctly recognizes that HPO is a largely overlooked but important problem in CL.
- Well-motivated: clearly highlights why standard “end-of-training” HPO is unrealistic in practice.
- Addresses a significant research question and is, to the reviewers’ knowledge, the first structured benchmarking of different validation-set construction strategies for HPO in CL.
- Clear presentation and understandable writing; ideas are easy to follow.
- Technically correct (within the chosen restrictions) and main conclusions are intuitive.
- Benchmarking is fairly robust within its scope: multiple replay strategies, varying task complexity, and baseline performance with default hyperparameters are reported.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of HPO strategies:** The only HPO method considered is grid search. The paper does not benchmark any actual continual HPO methods (e.g., approaches that adapt hyperparameters across tasks with positive transfer). This limits the claimed “realism” and the paper’s relevance to the broader HPO-in-CL problem.
- **Limited set of CL methods:** Only 5 replay-based methods are used; no regularization-based or architectural CL methods are tested. These other families are likely more sensitive to hyperparameter choices (e.g., regularization weight heavily impacts forgetting; architectural approaches may make current-task HPO meaningless). The paper does not justify why only replay methods were selected.
- **Insufficient statistical power:** Only 3 random runs are used, which is too few for reliable comparisons.
- **Missing pros/cons discussion of individual HPO strategies:**  
  *First-task HPO* cannot tune hyperparameters that only matter in multi-task settings (e.g., DER++’s memory-related parameters).  
  *Current-task HPO* cannot measure forgetting, which is critical for CL.  
  *Seen-tasks HPO* increases memory overhead and may be better spent using the held-out data for training. These drawbacks are not empirically observed or discussed.
- **Assumption of partitioned classes:** The experimental setup uses disjoint class splits, which is highly unrealistic and contradicts the paper’s goal of “realistic” HPO.
- **Focus on end-of-training accuracy only:** The paper ignores “anytime performance” – performance evolution over the stream is not shown, despite being a common and informative plot in CL. Metrics like forgetting are also omitted; some HPO strategies would be expected to differ on those.
- **Ungrounded recommendation:** The paper recommends choosing the computationally cheapest HPO (first-task HPO) but does not clarify under what conditions this choice is justified. The recommendation is seen as premature.
- **No discussion of limitations:** The paper lacks a limitations section. For instance, online CL settings, settings without crisp task boundaries, only one data modality (images), and only one network architecture are used.
- **Questionable novelty:** One reviewer notes that the work is essentially a benchmark/survey; its main contribution is the code. Another reviewer acknowledges originality but within a narrow setting. (Disagreement on novelty between reviewers.)
- **Unrealistic assumption on validation splits:** Using validation sets from previous tasks is unrealistic not only because of the data access assumption, but also because that data could be used for training instead of simply for validation.
- **Lack of analysis on hyperparameter sensitivity:** The paper examines very few hyperparameters and does not analyze how different HPO strategies affect which values are selected per hyperparameter, nor which hyperparameters are most impactful across tasks.
- **Missing considerations of task ordering and class imbalance:** The effect of varying task ordering on variance is not studied. For “seen-tasks HPO (Mem)”, the validation set may be class-imbalanced, but no adjustment is discussed.
- **Terminology issue:** The term “end-of-training HPO” is potentially confusing, as it implies the HPO happens at the end rather than repeated training over the full stream. A clearer term (e.g., “full-stream repeated HPO”) would be better.
- **Cost explanation lacking:** The paper says the heterogeneous task setting is more computationally costly (Line 297) but does not explain why.
- **Bold threshold not justified:** The 0.5% threshold for highlighting table entries is arbitrary and unexplained.
