## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper establishes a connection between linear deviations and a new class of untimed communication (UTC) deviations in extensive-form games, proving they are equivalent (Theorem 4.1). This allows existing algorithms for UTC regret (e.g., CFR) to minimize linear-swap regret, leading to algorithms with improved per-iteration complexity and strong empirical performance. The paper presents theoretical guarantees (O(√T d²) regret) and experiments showing orders-of-magnitude speedups over Farina & Pipis (2023).

### Strengths

- **Technically sound and rigorous.** All reviewers agree that the theoretical results are correct and the analysis is clean. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Comprehensive and persuasive empirical evaluation.** Experiments across several game types demonstrate significant runtime improvements (orders of magnitude) and lower average regret compared to Farina & Pipis (2023). (R1, R2, R4) R3 also notes the empirical performance greatly speeds up convergence.
- **Interesting and novel conceptual connection.** The equivalence between linear deviations and UTC deviations draws a novel parallel with sequential deviation sets, opening up potential for studying untimed deviation mappings and corresponding equilibrium concepts. (R3)
- **Intuitive example.** Section 5 provides an intuitive understanding of UTC deviations. (R3, R4) R4 further notes the example clearly demonstrates UTC deviations need not be communication deviations.
- **Bridges decision problems and online learning.** The reduction enables the use of algorithms like CFR for online optimization to minimize linear swap regrets. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and presentation issues.** Several reviewers found parts of the paper difficult to follow. R1 notes Section 5 is hard to understand despite effort, and suggests reorganizing Figure 1 into smaller figures with shorter captions. R2 finds Section 3 (communication deviations and UTC decision problem) abstract and lacking intuition (e.g., why a state is needed). R4 rates presentation as poor, citing undefined notations (e.g., “co X”) and the absence of a formal definition of linear deviations (given its central role). R1 also suggests adding a table of existing algorithms for linear-swap regret with their computational complexity for easier comparison.
- **Contribution perceived as limited by some.** R1 cannot clearly identify the key contribution and expects a reduction process (similar to Theorem 2.3) showing exactly how CFR is applied to linear deviations. R3 finds the paper not very substantial beyond introducing UTC; the faster convergence relies on an existing algorithm (CFR) and other results are not technically significant. R1 and R3 both rate contribution 2, while R2 and R4 rate it 3 – a noted contrast in perceived novelty.
- **Missing theoretical discussions and comparisons.**
    - R1 asks why the UTC-linear equivalence is significant compared to potential connections with other deviation types.
    - R1 notes the per-iteration complexity of CFR depends on solving a fixed-point problem, and asks how this compares with the complexity of Farina & Pipis (2023) – a formal comparison is needed.
    - R4 requests a formal discussion of computational efficiency (currently only informally sketched in the introduction; should be in Section 6).
    - R4 asks whether the O(√T d²) upper bound is tight (can a lower bound be shown?), and asks for elaboration on how to use the algorithm from Cohen et al. (2021) to achieve Õ(d^w) complexity (what linear program must be solved?).
- **Unexplained regret improvement.** R4 notes that although Figure 2 shows CFR achieving better regret than Farina & Pipis (2023), no explanation is provided for this improvement. (R2 also notes significant empirical improvement but does not request explanation.)
- **Typographical and minor errors.** R2 points out: in Section 5, “must immediately its first mediator query” is missing a verb; in the paragraph above Section 7, “this significant improves” should be “significantly”; Theorem 2.3 has “where x^{(t)} ∈ X” – likely should be Φ, and the utility vector u^{(t)} is undefined; the notion of “deterministic” external regret is also undefined.
- **Request for additional content.** R3 wishes for more discussion on the nature of untimed communication equilibria (noting the appendix already covers connections to standard communication equilibria). R4 asks for elaboration on the algorithm from Cohen et al. (2021) to improve readability.
