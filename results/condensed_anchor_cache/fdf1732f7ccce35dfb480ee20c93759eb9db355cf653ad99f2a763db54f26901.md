## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper develops an entropic regularization formulation and a Sinkhorn-type algorithm for optimal transport problems under equality and inequality constraints. The authors provide theoretical bounds on approximation error (exponential decay with regularization parameter) and sublinear convergence rate via a Lyapunov function, and propose acceleration techniques. The algorithm is validated on toy numerical experiments.

### Strengths

- Extends Sinkhorn methodology to a general class of constrained OT problems with both equality and inequality constraints; this is a natural and rarely explored direction that could benefit applications like domain adaptation and fairness (R1, R2, R3).
- The use of a Lyapunov function to characterize the dual optimization and perform constraint updates is novel and enables convergence analysis (R1).
- Provides convergence analysis (sublinear rate) and approximation error bounds (Theorem 1, Theorem 2) supported by detailed proofs; results are significant and likely to be referenced (R1, R3).
- The algorithm is accompanied by acceleration mechanisms (dynamic regularization scheduling, second-order acceleration) and sparsity considerations for the Newton step (R2 mentions this positively, R3 notes it is an extension).
- Decent survey of related literature on OT, ML, and efficient solvers (R1).

### Weaknesses

- The motivation for studying constrained OT is insufficient across all reviews. The paper does not convincingly convey why this problem is important for the ML community, nor does it discuss specific practical scenarios (e.g., fairness-aware learning, structured domain adaptation) where constrained OT is more suitable than unconstrained OT (R1, R2, R3, R4). 
- The justification for using entropy regularization for the additional inequality constraints is unclear. Since the algorithm requires Newton's method for the third block (unlike standard Sinkhorn which has exact block coordinate descent), alternative barrier methods (e.g., logarithmic barrier with self-concordance) could be more natural and better suited for the doubling strategy; the paper does not address why entropic regularization is preferable (R2, echoed as a question by R2).
- The numerical experiments are limited to toy scenarios with small size and unrealistic cost matrix distributions (uniform for random assignment, Rademacher for ranking). The experiments do not demonstrate scalability to large, high-dimensional, or real-world ML problems (e.g., MNIST with at least thousands of points, domain adaptation tasks). This undermines claims of usability and efficiency (R1, R2). 
- The theoretical results have unclear practical implications: (i) Theorem 1's "exponential decay" effectively requires the regularization parameter to grow inversely with the duality gap, which could be extreme for large problems; (ii) Theorem 2 appears dimension-free but actually depends on the regularization parameter eta, and thus on dimension; the dependency on eta should be made explicit (R2). 
- The novelty of the algorithmic contribution is limited; aside from Algorithm 1, the analysis largely extends previous works (Weed 2018, Altschuler et al. 2017) and the acceleration techniques are straightforward extensions of existing approaches (R2, R3, R4). A more thorough discussion of how the specific combination yields novelty is needed (R3).
- The paper is hard to follow in several places: there is insufficient explanation of key quantities (e.g., function L in line 202, interpretation of Theorem 2, purpose of Section 3.1), frequent reference to the appendix, and the contributions list is not clearly organized (R4 strongly; R3 notes the partial optimal transport is relegated to appendix, possibly to bypass page limits). 
- The computational complexity of the proposed algorithm is not analyzed in a way comparable to standard Sinkhorn (which has O(n^2/epsilon^2)); only polynomial-time proof is given, which is not attractive enough (R4). 
- Several specific questions highlight missing analyses: 
  - How does the algorithm behave when the cost matrix does not follow simple distributions (e.g., sparse or clustered costs)? (R1)
  - Can the approach support backpropagation in neural networks like standard Sinkhorn? (R1)
  - What is n in Theorem 1? (R3)
  - Why is f a Lyapunov function? (R3, need more explanation)
  - What does "depending only on the LP" mean in line 187? (R4)
  - Are the parameter settings (K=O(1), K+L=O(1)) justified beyond simplifying analysis? (R4)
  - Why is APDAGD claimed to not outperform? (R4)
  - The paper mentions partial optimal transport only briefly; this should either be included with full treatment or omitted (R3).
- Minor: line 166/167 has repeated "the following"; consider citing combinatorial OT solvers for partial OT (R1).
