## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces the setting of "blind unlearning," where the unlearning algorithm does not have access to the forget set. The authors propose RELOAD, an approximate unlearning method that estimates the gradient of the forget set by subtracting the gradient of the retain set from cached gradient checkpoints of the full dataset, then performs a single gradient ascent step, selectively reinitializes unimportant parameters based on a novel knowledge value (ratio of L2 norms of forget gradient to full gradient), and fine-tunes on the retain set. The method is extended to "remedial learning." One reviewer (score 6) considers the method a significant algorithmic contribution with impressive empirical results; the other four reviewers (scores 3 each) raise serious concerns about motivation, theoretical grounding, experimental comparisons, and privacy risks.

### Strengths

- The problem of unlearning without explicit access to the forget set is well-motivated and has attracted significant attention.
- The concept of blind unlearning and the remedial learning setting are interesting.
- The paper combines existing ideas (checkpointing, gradient ascent, sparsity) in a clean way.
- The new method for calculating knowledge values (ratio of L2 norm of forget dataset gradient to L2 norm of full dataset gradient on a well-optimized model) is novel.
- The empirical results cover multiple datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, SVHN) and models (ResNet18, VGG), with evaluation on several metrics and baseline algorithms; the detailed description of metrics in the appendix is well-written.
- The technique and experiment part is easy to understand.
- The paper is well-written (one reviewer).

### Weaknesses

1. **Theoretical Issues**  
   - The definition of *recoverability*: claiming f injective implies all datasets are recoverable does not address non-injective cases; exact recoverability is an unrealistically high bar.  
   - The gradient approximation $\nabla_\theta L(D_f) = \nabla_\theta L(D) - \nabla_\theta L(D_{retain})$ is only valid at the checkpoint parameters; the paper does not argue why this approximation holds after multiple gradient ascent steps.  
   - The derivation from Eq. (4) to Eq. (8) lacks novelty—it is simply linearity of differentiation. The connection to practical effectiveness is insufficiently justified.  
   - Equations (5)–(7) fail when applied to average or mini-batch gradients (scalars 1/b, 1/n are needed); the equality is not properly derived under mini-batch settings.  
   - The "single-based" ascent update (Line 241) is unclear.  
   - Several reviewers note the lack of theoretical guarantees or empirical intuition explaining why RELOAD outperforms methods that use the forget set.

2. **Insufficient Experimental Comparisons and Setting Diversity**  
   - Missing comparisons with baselines from Pawelczyk et al. (IC test, targeted/indiscriminate/gaussian poisoning attacks) and Goel et al. (unlearning tests).  
   - Missing comparisons with existing weight importance methods (e.g., SalUn [A] – Fan et al. ICLR 2024) and gradient-based input saliency methods [5,6]. The paper claims superiority but does not report ablation results comparing different knowledge value formulations.  
   - Missing comparisons with existing zero-shot unlearning methods that require neither forget nor retain set ([C–F] Golatkar et al., Tarun et al., Kodge et al.; [H] Chundawat et al.).  
   - Experiments only on ResNet18/VGG with CIFAR10 and CIFAR100, which are insufficient for generalizable empirical claims.  
   - Only two forget-set sizes evaluated: 10% random and 100 samples class unlearning. More settings are needed.  
   - SSD results on CIFAR100 show 1% accuracy, which is the trivial accuracy; previous SSD results have shown much better performance, suggesting poor hyperparameter tuning.

3. **Unclear Motivation and Practicality**  
   - The setting is weaker than zero-shot unlearning, which requires no access to any training data.  
   - It is unclear when an organization has access to a large retain set (90%), multiple gradient checkpoints, but not the forget set—this seems memory-intensive and selective.  
   - The forget set is typically much smaller than the retain set (e.g., 10% vs 90%). The paper aims to reduce retaining user data, but requires retaining 90% of the data; other works reduce usage of remaining data and require only 20% total data during unlearning.  
   - The title "blind unlearning" is misleading because the method still requires the retain set and the full gradient; "Partially Blinded Unlearning" would be more appropriate.

4. **Privacy and Gradient Leakage Risks**  
   - Storing $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D})$ (full gradient) is risky: prior work [B] (Geiping et al. NeurIPS 2020) shows that gradients can be inverted to reconstruct training data, especially from fully connected layers. The paper does not discuss or mitigate this risk.  
   - The setting allows access to gradients of the entire dataset, which can leak membership information; privacy-preserving mechanisms like DP-SGD are typically needed.

5. **Algorithm and Implementation Concerns**  
   - Selective re-initialization on each element (Line 8, Algo 1) could increase computational burden.  
   - Hyperparameter $\alpha$ (threshold for re-initialization) is uniform across all parameters; no guidance on tuning or sensitivity analysis.  
   - The computation and normalization of knowledge values for comparison with $\alpha$ is unclear.  
   - The impact of Laplace smoothing constant $\varepsilon$ in Eq. (8) is not discussed.  
   - The contribution of step (1–3) (one-step gradient ascent) is unclear; steps (4) and (5) alone may be sufficient. Ablation studies are missing for both the gradient ascent step and the parameter selection component.

6. **Writing and Clarity Issues**  
   - The remedial learning setting is introduced late and without sufficient background; references should be given if not new.  
   - Confusing forward references: e.g., Line 211 mentions "Recall from the relationship...in Section 3.2"; Line 213 references the numerator in Eq. (8) before that equation is presented.  
   - Typos: Line 183 "instanced", Line 209 "\\sum_{i=1}^N", Line 147.  
   - Some notations are unclear (e.g., whether Eq. (8) uses accumulated or averaged loss).  
   - Evaluation metrics NSKL and FSKL are unclear and may be unnecessary for evaluating unlearning performance.

7. **Reproducibility**  
   - The paper lacks hyperparameter details for both the proposed method and comparison methods, as well as fine-tuning details. The provided links contain no verifiable procedures.
