## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Cooperative Coevolution QD (CCQD), a framework to improve sample efficiency in Quality-Diversity (QD) reinforcement learning by decomposing policy networks into representation (early layers) and decision (later layers) components. Two coevolved subpopulations (a small representation list and a larger decision grid) are evolved with independent selection, variation operators (evolutionary + actor-critic critic per representation), and MAP-Elites style survival. Experiments on QDax suite and Atari Pong claim ~200% sample efficiency improvement over baselines (PGA-ME, QDPG, ME, PBT-ME (SAC)). All three reviewers gave a rating of 8, but with differing confidence and substantial concerns: reviewer 2 strongly doubts the experimental validity due to premature stopping and mismatched asymptotic results; reviewer 3 questions novelty due to prior work on cooperative coevolution in RL; reviewer 1 notes missing asymptotic analysis.

### Strengths

- Clear, well-motivated idea: partitioning neural network parameters into representation and decision layers to reduce the search space and improve sample efficiency in QD for RL.
- The method is simple, elegant, and easy to understand; the paper is well written and places the work nicely in context with a good literature overview.
- The framework is general and can be instantiated with different selection/variation operators.
- Ablation studies (e.g., qualitative impact of representation vs. decision parts) are informative and answer key questions.
- Introduction of Atari Pong environment shows versatility beyond the standard QDax suite.
- Overall the approach addresses an interesting and important problem in QDRL; using coevolution for representation/decision decoupling is novel and elegant.

### Weaknesses

- **Major experimental concerns (reviewer 2, strong confidence):** The main benchmark results on QDax do not match those reported in recent works (e.g., PBT-ME at ICLR 2023). For instance, on Ant-Uni the maximum fitness reported is ~1500, while asymptotic values are 6000–7000; on HalfCheetah ~1500 vs. 6000–7000; on Walker2d-Uni ~800 vs. 4000. This suggests all runs were stopped far before convergence, invalidating conclusions about sample efficiency. The authors’ claim that PBT-ME needs more computational resources due to hyperparameter tuning contradicts the original paper’s demonstration of tuning within the same budget. The sample efficiency gains over PGA-ME appear limited and may be due to minor changes (parameterized universe critic update, one critic per representation) rather than the coevolution strategy itself.
- **Missing asymptotic analysis (reviewer 1):** The paper emphasizes sample efficiency but does not confirm that CCQD reaches the same or better asymptotic performance as baselines. Without this, the practical advantage is unclear.
- **Limited novelty due to prior work (reviewer 3):** Cooperative coevolution has been applied to reinforcement learning before, even in the context of quality-diversity‑like evolutionary algorithms: "Evolutionary reinforcement learning via cooperative coevolutionary negatively correlated search." The paper should revise claims of being the first and discuss the distinction; otherwise, the contribution may be limited.
- **Missing clarity on diversity criteria (reviewer 1):** The description of how diversity (behavior descriptors) is used to separate symbols/cells in the QD algorithm is insufficient.
- **Unanswered questions overlapping with missing analysis/ablation:**
    - What is the motivation for using both an evolutionary operator and a learned actor‑critic update? Could this be ablation-studied?
    - Is there a principled way to decompose the neural network into factors for QD? How does the split affect parameter robustness (e.g., sensitivity to noisy inputs)?
    - How exactly does addressing the large‑scale search space via cooperative coevolution improve sample efficiency? A more explicit explanation is needed.
- **Minor issues (reviewer 2):** There is a citation error: (manon et al.) should be (flageat et al.) on page 7. More discussion on coevolution design choices (e.g., split motivation) would strengthen the paper; some technical details common to QDRL could be moved to supplementary. A relevant reference: "The Quality-Diversity Transformer: Generating Behavior-Conditioned Trajectories with Decision Transformers" (GECCO).

**Contrast among reviewers:** Reviewer 2 is highly critical of the experimental setup and considers the work unconvincing in its current form, while reviewers 1 and 3 are more positive overall but share concerns about asymptotic performance and novelty, respectively. Reviewer 3 questions the contribution given prior work, whereas reviewer 2 thinks the idea is original but poorly validated.
