## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FILLER (Framework for Integrating Layer-Level Edge-shift Recovery), a post-processing method to enhance GNN robustness against edge distribution shifts (edge addition/removal) at test time. It inserts an edge-shift recovery (ER) layer between aggregation and update in each GNN layer. The method is evaluated on node classification across 10 datasets and 5 GNN architectures, with theoretical analysis and ablation studies. Reviewers agree it addresses an important problem, is well-organized and intuitive, and the evaluation is comprehensive relative to vanilla GNNs. However, comparisons with related literature are highly insufficient, limiting assessment of practical advantages.

### Strengths
- The issue of edge distribution shift (graph structure varying between training and test) is significant and timely for GNNs.
- The proposed method (FILLER) is intuitive, simple to understand, and can be added as a plug-in to many existing GNN architectures.
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow.
- The evaluation spans 10 graphs and 5 architectures, demonstrating broad applicability.
- Thorough ablation studies and empirical verification for most statements are provided.
- Some theoretical analysis is provided to justify how FILLER mitigates the representation gap caused by edge shifts.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with related literature:** The paper only shows FILLER can enhance traditional (vanilla) GNNs but does not compare against any relevant baseline for handling graph distribution shifts.  
  - Missing comparisons with graph OOD methods, graph test-time adaptation (e.g., GraphTTA, Empowering Graph Representation Learning with Test-Time Graph Transformation – ICLR 2022), continual learning, graph domain adaptation, and robust graph ML approaches.  
  - Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 all raise this as a major limitation. Reviewer 4 is most critical, noting the comparison is “highly insufficient” and that the paper does not clearly discuss how FILLER differs from existing work.  
  - Without such comparisons, it is unclear whether FILLER is more effective than existing alternatives or why a user would prefer it (e.g., over continual learning or domain adaptation). Even a proof-of-concept or justification for the absence is considered necessary by Reviewer 2.

- **Unclear real-world applicability:** It is not clear what real-world scenarios (beyond benchmark datasets like Cora) involve edge addition/removal at test time where FILLER would be useful. Reviewer 2 asks for a concrete use-case example.

- **Technical clarity issues:**  
  - Some formulas are missing labels (e.g., ER-Advanced Layer in lines 249-251).  
  - Symbols in critical equations are hard to discern (e.g., g in Eq1, g’ in Eq2, g_B in Eq4, h_D in lines 249-251).  
  - The rationale for setting φ(A)=O is unclear; it is not explained whether this is meant to characterize differences between neighboring nodes and central nodes. Reviewer 1 suggests random perturbations per epoch might be more beneficial.  
  - The paper does not mathematically define the “edge distribution shift” problem precisely (Reviewer 3).

- **Methodological concerns:**  
  - The use of the matrix pseudoinverse (Eq5) to compute W may be a marginal trick if performance gain is small; overcomplicating the model could hinder adoption (Reviewer 1).  
  - Figure 1 lacks an experimental detail: are the results for random edge deletions? Also, why does performance increase at very high sparsity ( > 0.9) (Reviewer 1)?  
  - It is unclear whether the proposed method follows the homophily assumption. If the node transformation g(H) is designed to capture differences in neighborhood changes (H_N – H̃_N), does it assume the node is similar to its neighbors? Also, can FILLER be applied to heterophilic graphs? (Reviewers 1 and 3).

- **Limited scope of edge shifts considered:** The paper only considers random edge removal/addition. Can the method handle other types of edge distribution shifts? (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing related work discussions:** The paper briefly reviews graph distribution shift methods (Sec 4) but does not discuss how FILLER differs from graph structure learning (e.g., A Survey on Graph Structure Learning) or robust graph ML methods. Reviewer 4 requests more discussion and comparisons.
