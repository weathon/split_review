## Merged Review

### Summary
Interpreter is a two-stage unsupervised video object segmentation method. Stage 1 uses slot attention on images to extract frame-level object-centric slots. Stage 2 uses another slot attention on the set of frame-level slots to produce a compact set of video-level slots, trained with Sinkhorn divergence. Segmentation is obtained by attention propagation from video-level to frame-level slots. Evaluated on YTVIS-19 and MOVi-E, it shows strong results on YTVIS-19 mIoU and MOVi-E FG-ARI, but performance is mixed across metrics and datasets.

### Strengths
- The hierarchical slot attention (per-frame then video-level) is a novel and elegant design that avoids chunking and allows handling temporal dependencies across the full video without frame matching.
- Use of Sinkhorn divergence as a relaxed optimal transport objective for matching unordered sets of slot tokens is a technically sound contribution. One reviewer (Rating 5) particularly appreciated this contribution.
- The paper includes significant qualitative results, including failure cases, which aid understanding.
- Figure 1 provides a clear overview of the approach, helping readers follow the technical details.
- The approach achieves remarkable performance on the real-world YTVIS-19 dataset, demonstrating its practical potential.
- The authors make a good effort to analyze and explain the mixed quantitative results, rather than stopping at numbers.
- The overall design is straightforward and effectively applies slot attention to video-level temporal aggregation.

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental scope and evaluation gaps:**
  - Despite claiming to address video object segmentation (VOS), the paper evaluates on video instance segmentation (YTVIS-19) and video semantic segmentation (MOVi-E). It is not evaluated on standard VOS benchmarks such as DAVIS, and no comparison is made with state-of-the-art VOS methods (e.g., STM). This undermines the claimed task and the ability to assess the contribution.
  - The claim of handling long videos is not convincingly demonstrated; the datasets used contain videos on the order of seconds, not minutes. No definition of “long” is provided and the experimental setup does not reflect long-video scenarios.
  - Results are contradictory across datasets: on YTVIS-19 mIoU is high but FG-ARI is low, while on MOVi-E the opposite trend occurs. The analysis in Section 4.3 relies on a limited number of examples; a statistical investigation is needed to explain this contradiction and verify general applicability.
  - No results are provided on DAVIS-17-unsupervised, which is a standard benchmark for unsupervised video segmentation.
  - Only two ablation studies are presented (number of slots and clustering distance threshold). Critical ablations are missing, including the effect of varying the second-level slot count \(K\), end-to-end fine-tuning of Stage 2, the impact of using Hungarian matching with L1/L2 loss instead of Sinkhorn, and the importance of the DINOv2 backbone (e.g., comparing with a ViT-s/8 backbone as used by some baselines).
  - Qualitative results are shown only for Interpreter; comparisons with competitive methods are absent, making it hard to judge relative segmentation quality.

- **Insufficient technical exposition:**
  - Mathematical details are missing: the Sinkhorn divergence is never formally defined; the function “SH” in Eq. (2) is not explained; the final loss function is not given. This prevents a complete understanding of the method.
  - The architecture of encoders and decoders in both stages of training is not described.
  - The technical section (Sec. 3.2) feels congested, while Figures 2–4 occupy two full pages and appear to be space-fillers rather than conveying necessary information.

- **Limited architectural novelty:**
  - The hierarchical design (video-level queries aggregating temporal context from frame-level queries) is very similar to the proposed architecture of VITA (Heo et al., 2022). Beyond differences in target task (video instance segmentation vs. unsupervised segmentation) and objective function (Sinkhorn vs. cross-entropy), the architectural contribution appears incremental.

- **Efficiency claim not supported:**
  - The title and text promise efficiency, but no experiments or comparisons demonstrate it. No runtime or memory measurements are provided, and comparisons to STEVE, BA, or VideoSAUR on efficiency grounds are absent. When normalizing for pre-trained backbone (Interpreter uses ViT-B/14, BA uses ViT-s/8), the efficiency advantage is unclear.

- **Writing and readability issues:**
  - The experimental section combines main results, qualitative analysis, and ablations in a way that makes it hard to understand the purpose of each experiment.
  - Figures 3 and 4 show segmentation results without the original input frames, complicating interpretation.
  - Several phrases are informal and out of place (e.g., “The last row shows a cute cat.”, “unexpectedly well”, “surprising” without justification).
  - Related work discussion omits key VOS methods such as STM (space-time memory networks), making the motivation for slot attention unclear.

- **Contradictions and undeveloped discussions:**
  - The paper states that the second-level slot number stays under ten (8 for YTVIS-19). Reviewers argue this contradicts the claimed ability to handle extensive temporal context (L101) and question why different slot counts are used for YTVIS-19 and MOVi-E.
  - The significant discrepancy between FG-ARI and mIoU is not adequately explained. If object segmentation is accurate over time, both metrics should be high; the opposite trends suggest a systematic issue.
  - Alternative approaches such as using the same slot index per frame (instead of video-level slot attention) or Hungarian matching with L1/L2 are not explored or compared.
