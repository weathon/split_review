## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RDEIC for extreme image compression using compressed feature initialization and relay residual diffusion. Reviewers generally find the paper clear and the experiments comprehensive, but the novelty is considered limited by three reviewers (1,2,4) as a combination of existing techniques (GLC, deffeic, ResShift, SeeSR, DiffBIR, etc.). Reviewer 3 is more positive, finding the framework innovative, addressing fidelity issues, and providing strong results. Weaknesses include insufficient comparisons (e.g., with GLC, broader baselines), lack of clarity in motivation and technical details, mixed fidelity metric performance (PSNR, MS-SSIM, SSIM gaps), potential visual result artifacts (chroma deviations, bitrate differences), ambiguity in experimental reporting (model versions, BD-rate definitions, guidance scale), entropy model control issues, missing computational complexity and overfitting analysis, and several formulaic and formatting concerns.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, with detailed and comprehensive experiments (R1, R2).
- Reduces computational complexity by reducing denoising steps, valuable for resource-constrained environments (R1).
- Experimental arrangement is reasonable; ablation studies prove effectiveness of proposed strategies (R2).
- Introduces an innovative framework (RDEIC) that improves image compression efficiency (R3) [minority positive].
- Effectively addresses fidelity issues in existing diffusion-based methods (R3).
- Provides strong experimental results demonstrating advantages (R3).
- Fast diffusion sampling as a practical research approach holds value and positively impacts the community (R4).
- Balance between smoothness and sharpness provides practical insights into mapping sampling step N to reconstruction quality (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Limited innovation: Pipeline is essentially a combination of GLC’s codec framework and deffeic’s generative model (R1, R2, R4). Adding noise to latent features is a common operation (R2 cites SeeSR CVPR23, Pixel-Aware SD ECCV24; R4 cites DiffBIR, ExposureDiffusion ICCV23, Plug-and-Play Medical MICCAI23). Residual diffusion is similar to ResShift (R2, NIPS23). No comparison with GLC (R1). No ablation to show whether using RRD alone (replacing stable diffusion) would improve DiffEIC-50 (R1).
- Motivation unclear: How residual diffusion and pre-trained stable diffusion resolve randomness from pure noise and improve fidelity is not clearly explained (R2). Why pre-trained stable diffusion (unseen task inputs) produces expected results, and why choose it over directly using CFG (R2).
- Visual result issues: Fig.1 and Fig.5 show significant chroma deviations in Text-Sketch; possible RGB/BGR mix-up or baseline bias; PerCo-20 brightness bias (R4). In Fig.1, RDEIC has 7.5% higher bitrate than deffeic despite subjective improvement; similar in Fig.5 (R1). Comparisons may not be rigorous.
- Fidelity metrics inconsistent with motivation: Fig.4 shows RDEIC does not achieve best results on PSNR, MS-SSIM, SSIM, with significant gaps (R2). Why RDEIC is worse than MS-ILLM on NIQE metric is not analyzed (R1).
- Ambiguity in experimental reporting: Not specified which of the five trained models (different λ_r) is used in comparisons, nor the guidance scale values (R2). In Tab.3, “2/5” in DS column unclear whether performance refers to 2-step or 5-step; BD-rate columns labeled “Distortion” and “Perception” not defined (which metrics?) (R2). Should use detailed metrics like PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS instead of BD-rate (R2). In Tab.2, BD-rate of RDEIC with 2 DS is 0; unclear which method is the anchor (R2). Also two implementation versions both report BD-rate 0, causing confusion (R4).
- Potential issue with entropy model control: Uses unusual entropy models (VQ-E, VQ-D) without adequate control or explanation; may influence comparison results and not reflect primary contribution (R4).
- Baseline selection ambiguity: Using “Ours” as baseline in Table 1 leads to row of zeros; should use a recognized baseline (e.g., JPEG, BPG, or state-of-the-art) for BD-rate comparison (R4).
- Formula clarity issues: Derivation from Eq.2 to Eq.4 not clear (R4). Interpretation of ε_sd(z_n, n) as low-frequency component in Eq.11 lacks theoretical support (R4). l_p in Eq.9 undefined (R4).
- Limited discussion on computational complexity of the new method (R3). Potential overfitting concerns not addressed (R3). Insufficient comparison with a broader range of existing compression techniques (R3).
- Definition of “extremely low bitrates” lacks precision; threshold may vary with content (R4). Codebook learning details (initialization, interaction with l_p) needed (R4). Would placing diffusion in encoding or hyperprior change complexity conclusions? (R4) Is diffusion effective mainly as post-processing for perceptual quality or also for compact representation? (R4)
- Minor formatting: missing commas in lines 100, 220, 229, 238 (R4).
