## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the implicit policy-finding problem in offline RL by formulating it as a constrained optimization problem, leading to the AlignIQL and AlignIQL-hard algorithms. The method extracts policies from IQL-learned Q-functions using a regularization function and derives a closed-form solution. Experiments on D4RL benchmarks show competitive performance, especially on sparse-reward tasks like AntMaze and Adroit.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical Rigor**: The method is derived from a principled constrained optimization formulation, providing a fresh and theoretically sound perspective on implicit policy extraction (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Empirical Performance**: Competitive or superior results are achieved on D4RL benchmarks, notably excelling in challenging sparse-reward tasks (AntMaze, Adroit). The alignment constraints contribute to stable and reliable policies (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Theoretical Insights**: The paper offers valuable analysis into why weighted regression can be effective for policy extraction in IQL, deepening understanding of the underlying mechanisms (Reviewer 3).
- **Robustness**: The method shows increased robustness to hyperparameter variations compared to existing methods, a practical advantage for deployment (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Generalization of Theoretical Results**: The derivation relies on $f(x)=\log(x)$; it is unclear if the results hold for other choices of $f$ and how the choice would impact the policy (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear Advantage Selection Mechanism**: Remark 5.7 is hand-wavy; the loss function does not clearly show how actions with positive advantage are selected, and the connection between Eq. 15 and high Q-values relative to V is insufficiently explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Unfair Comparison in Table 1**: The proposed method uses a diffusion policy architecture while the IQL (AWR) baseline uses an MLP; performance gains may be due to the architecture rather than the core algorithm. Standard deviations are missing, weakening the evidence (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear Experiment in Section 6.2**: The purpose of the experiments is unclear; no baseline is defined and the comparisons are not well-specified (Reviewer 1).
- **Increased Complexity and Scalability Concerns**: AlignIQL requires additional multiplier networks and diffusion models, raising computational cost and hyperparameter sensitivity. Scalability to image-based tasks is not addressed (Reviewer 2). The complexity may deter practitioners and limit large-scale deployment (Reviewer 3). The use of diffusion modeling is not explained in the methods section (Reviewer 2).
- **Performance Concerns**:
    - On MuJoCo tasks (already saturated), AlignIQL is considerably worse than Diffusion QL and worse than IQL in 4 out of 9 tasks, despite higher computational cost (Reviewer 2).
    - There is a significant performance difference between the authors’ results and the original IDQL paper, leaving uncertainty about improvements (Reviewer 2).
    - Inconsistent hyperparameters are used across results, and the ablation study and sensitivity analysis are under-explored (Reviewer 2).
    - The claim that AlignIQL is insensitive to the number of action samples $N$ (Figure 2) is not obvious; reporting mean and standard deviation would be more convincing (Reviewer 2).
    - The optimization may risk overfitting in AntMaze sparse-reward environments, reducing generalization; more testing on sparse-reward tasks is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Terminology Issue**: “Policy alignment” is used differently from its common meaning in language models; a formal definition or a rename is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited Scope of Evaluation**:
    - Evaluation is restricted to D4RL continuous control; generalizability to other domains, discrete action spaces, hierarchical tasks, or real-world noisy datasets is not established (Reviewers 2,3). Questions about compatibility with transformer architectures remain (Reviewer 3).
    - A more comprehensive comparison against a broader range of state-of-the-art algorithms is needed to fully contextualize contributions (Reviewer 3).
    - Performance may depend heavily on dataset quality; robustness in real-world scenarios with noisy or incomplete data is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing Analysis**: The paper does not analyze key factors affecting alignment between Q-values and the learned policy, nor does it provide a computational complexity comparison to other methods. Applicability to discrete action spaces and advanced architectures (e.g., transformers) is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor Issues**: Notation errors in Equation 1 (missing $\pi(a|s)$), Equation 2 (undefined $Q_\theta$), Equation 7 (overloaded $a$) (Reviewer 1). Duplicate references (lines 568-573) and editing errors (lines 916-917) (Reviewer 2).
