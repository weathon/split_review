## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RoboShot, a fully zero-shot method that improves the robustness of pretrained model embeddings by leveraging language models (LMs) to obtain insights from task descriptions. These insights are used to remove harmful and boost useful components in embeddings without any supervision. A theoretical analysis characterizes conditions for improvement. Empirical evaluation across nine image and NLP classification tasks (Waterbirds, CelebA, PACS, VLCS, CXR14, CivilComments, HateXplain, Amazon, Gender Bias) and multiple model backbones (CLIP ViT-B-32, CLIP ViT-L-14, ALIGN, AltCLIP, BERT, ADA) shows an average improvement of 15.98% over zero-shot baselines. The method is also compatible with a zero-shot label-free adaptation variant.

### Strengths
- Novel setting and methodology: improving robustness of zero-shot models without fine-tuning or extra data, preserving out-of-the-box usability. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Interesting theoretical analysis that characterizes conditions under which RoboShot can outperform existing zero-shot methods. (R1, R3)
- Extensive and rigorous experimental evaluation across diverse datasets covering image and text classification, with varied model architectures and language models. (R1, R3, R4)
- Consistently strong empirical results: RoboShot outperforms several zero-shot baselines, with the biggest gains in worst-group accuracy. (R1, R2)
- Simplicity and cheap computational cost, making it easily adoptable by practitioners; source code provided. (R3)
- Clear and well-written paper with detailed explanations of the algorithm and framework. (R1, R3, R4)
- Method is compatible with a variety of pretrained models and LMs, and offers a label-free adaptation variant when many unlabeled examples are available. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Core assumption of orthonormal concept embeddings is not well motivated.** Reviewer 3 considers this a core criticity and finds the experiment in Appendix F.5 inconclusive. Reviewer 2 also requests clarification on how the basis vectors (\(z\)) are identified in experiments, given that the decomposition of insight vectors depends on that. (R3, R2)
- **Missing evaluation on large-scale datasets like ImageNet.** Requested by Reviewers 1 and 2. Reviewer 2 further notes that in many standard classification tasks, removing spurious features might hurt accuracy; while one can choose not to remove them, results on ImageNet would be informative. (R1, R2)
- **The abstract’s claim of 15.98% improvement is potentially misleading:** it refers only to Worst-Group accuracy, not overall average accuracy. Reviewer 3 points out that the framework often fails to maintain average accuracy, making it advisable only when worst-group accuracy is the metric of interest; a fallback method that reverts to the baseline when the approach is detrimental would be desirable. (R3)
- **Dependence on language model insights is a critical vulnerability.** Reviewer 4 (minority reviewer, score 6) emphasizes that if the LM fails to anticipate failure modes (e.g., spurious correlations like background, texture, lighting), the method cannot remedy them. This is concerning for complex real-world scenarios where spurious correlations are subtle and hard to articulate. (R4)
- **Not truly zero-shot because the pretrained model (e.g., ChatGPT) has been trained on internet data containing information about the classes.** Reviewer 1 suggests evaluating on rare or unseen classes to test generalization. (R1)
- **Benchmarks are limited to zero-shot classification; harder tasks like zero-shot semantic/instance segmentation are not addressed.** (R1)
- **Missing details and ablations:**
    - \(X_{proj}\) is not defined in the LFA (label-free adaptation) section. (R2)
    - No ablation on the number of insight vectors (\(m\)) needed; how to choose \(m\) is unclear. (R2)
    - Qualitative assessment of \(u^k\) (Figure 2) lacks quantitative metrics such as class separability (e.g., ratio of inter-class to average intra-class distance). (R3)
    - Figure 1 and Figure 2 captions need improvement: Figure 1 lacks a textual description of the process; Figure 2 does not define \(Y_0\) and \(Y_1\). (R3)
- **Table 1: Why does RoboShot perform much worse on Waterbirds except when using CLIP-B?** (R3, question)
- **Figure 2: What are \(Y_0\) and \(Y_1\)?** (R3, question)
