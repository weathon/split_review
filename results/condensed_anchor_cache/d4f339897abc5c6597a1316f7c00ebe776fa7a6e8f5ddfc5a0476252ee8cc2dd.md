## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies and addresses the problem of infinite Lipschitz constants in the noise prediction network $\epsilon_\theta = -\sigma_t\nabla\log q_t(x)$ near $t=0$ in diffusion models. The authors provide theoretical proof (Theorem 3.1) and empirical confirmation of the exploding Lipschitz constant and propose a simple fix, E-TSDM, which applies a time transformation (quantization) to tie together small timesteps. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements, including over 33% FID reduction for DDIM and DPM-Solver and state-of-the-art results on FFHQ 256×256. The paper is generally well-received, with most reviewers praising its novelty, theoretical grounding, and practical impact. However, one reviewer notes that the observation of infinite Lipschitz constants is not entirely novel (Song et al., 2021a; Vahdat et al., 2021) and suggests toning down originality claims, while also pointing out minor language issues and the lack of open-source code. There is general agreement on the effectiveness of E-TSDM but calls for expanded discussion of alternative methods and clearer exposition of which Lipschitz constant matters and why.

### Strengths

- **Discovery of a fundamental issue:** The paper reveals that $\epsilon_\theta = -\sigma_t\nabla\log q_t(x)$ has an infinite Lipschitz constant as $\sigma_t\to 0$, which explains instabilities in training and sampling of diffusion models. While one reviewer argues this observation is known (Song et al., 2021a; Vahdat et al., 2021), others consider it a previously unexplored challenge. The paper provides rigorous theoretical proof (Theorem 3.1) and empirical validation.
- **Simple and effective solution:** E-TSDM uses a time-transformation (quantization of timesteps near zero) that is easy to implement and yields clear benefits. This is highlighted by multiple reviewers.
- **Strong empirical results:** The method achieves a 33% reduction in FID for DDIM and DPM-Solver and sets a new state-of-the-art on FFHQ 256×256. Results are demonstrated across diverse datasets in Figures 3 and 4.
- **Discussion of alternative approaches:** The paper considers several other potential fixes (including methods from the appendix) and explains why they are less effective, providing useful context.
- **Potential impact on the community:** The analysis of Lipschitz singularities provides insights for designing more stable diffusion models and acceleration methods. Reviewers consider this an excellent contribution.
- **Presentation:** The paper is generally well-written and clear, with one reviewer commending the "very well carried out" presentation. Theoretical and practical aspects are balanced.

### Weaknesses

- **Originality concern (minority reviewer):** One reviewer (score 6) states that the presence of infinite Lipschitz constants in diffusion models was previously noted by Song et al. (2021a) and Vahdat et al. (2021), and recommends that the authors tone down their claims of being the first to observe it. This reviewer suggests the paper could make the observation more original by linking it to non-uniqueness of strong solutions of the SDE (Øksendal, 2003). Other reviewers (scores 8) found the observation novel and commendable.
- **Clarity about the Lipschitz constant:** The abstract and introduction do not explicitly specify *which* function has high Lipschitz constant (*i.e.*, $\epsilon_\theta$) and *why* it matters (neural networks struggle to learn high-Lipschitz functions, and discretization of reverse SDE becomes inaccurate). This should be clarified (noted by Reviewer 3).
- **Expanded discussion of alternative methods:** The "Remp" method (Appendix D.3.3) is not mentioned in the main text. A more thorough quantitative comparison of all alternative fixes (including those in Section 4.4) would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 3). Limited space is acknowledged, but the authors are encouraged to expand.
- **Missing open-source code:** No code is provided, which would help reproducibility and adoption (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor language issues:** "vexing propensity of diffusion models" and "Recently, there have been massive variants" are awkward (Reviewer 4).
- **Additional questions/suggestions that indicate missing experiments or clarifications:**
  - Avoid saying "$t$ is small" when the issue is $\sigma_t$ being small; the noise schedule rather than timestep index is key (Reviewer 1).
  - Define $\beta_t$ and $\eta_t$ in Equation 10 (Reviewer 1).
  - Could the authors show that directly learning $\nabla\log q_t(x)$ with standard least squares is prohibitive (Reviewer 1)?
  - Explore alternative methods: (i) learning $\sigma_t^2\|\nabla\log q_t(x) - \frac{1}{\sigma_t} I\|^2$ (weighted least squares), (ii) learning only for warped timesteps $f_T(t)$ and using only those steps for sampling, (iii) inputting $\sigma_t$ directly into the network to adjust Lipschitz automatically (Reviewer 1). These are requests for empirical or theoretical scrutiny.
  - How does the proposed E-TSDM differ from the observations in Song et al. (2021a) and Vahdat et al. (2021)? A clearer distinction would help (Reviewer 4).
