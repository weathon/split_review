## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies the convergence of Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD) in the population limit, proposing an \(( \epsilon, \delta )\)-approximate gradient flow framework to prove sub-linear convergence in KL divergence under the log-Sobolev inequality and assumptions on the kernel. This is the first KL-divergence convergence result for the SVGD population limit. Numerical experiments with an RBF kernel are provided to support the theory.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, extensively reviews previous works, and clearly discusses how the present work fits the existing literature (Reviewer 1).
- The core theoretical contribution has sufficient novelty: it combines existing results in SVGD and RKHS to prove the first convergence result of the SVGD population limit in KL divergence assuming LSI (Reviewer 2; one reviewer finds the contribution not significant enough – see Weaknesses).
- The idea of approximate gradient flow is useful for analysis of other sampling algorithms based on Wasserstein gradient flow; the RKHS proof techniques can be used to study other variants of SVGD (Reviewer 2).
- The authors demonstrate sublinear convergence of SVGD (Theorem 1), the main result (Reviewer 1).
- The AGF framework can include most existing analyses of approximate function gradient flow and is promising for obtaining better results for SVGD (Reviewer 3).
- Numerical experiments seem to support the theoretical results (Reviewer 3).
- The convergence of SVGD under KL divergence has been an open question, making this topic of great interest for theoretical researchers (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Assumption 6 is too strong / unclear / likely contradictory.** Multiple reviewers criticize Assumption 6. Reviewer 4 states it essentially assumes properties of the \(\nabla\log p_t\) gradient directly, bypassing the core challenge of analyzing how the particle distribution evolves and how its gradient relates to the target; this significantly limits applicability. Reviewer 3 notes that Assumption 6 requires \(\{\lambda_i\}\) to have positive lower bounds independent of \(t\), but Assumption 5 implies the kernel has finite trace and \(\sum_i \lambda_i < \infty\), which seems contradictory. The assumption is also hard to verify, and the authors do not adequately address the discrete, iterative nature of SVGD where \(\nabla\log p_t\) depends on iterations (Reviewer 4; see also next point). One reviewer (Reviewer 2) does not mention this issue, while others consider it critical.

- **The AGF framework is not actually used since \(\delta\) is always 0** (Reviewer 3). The framework is presented but then effectively unused in the analysis of SVGD.

- **The proof has gaps or mistakes.** Reviewer 3 questions the claim in the proof of Theorem 1 that "\(m\) satisfying Eq. (65) does not monotonically increase w.r.t. the iteration \(t\)" can imply the existence of a largest \(m'\) independent of \(t\); this reasoning appears invalid. Reviewer 2 has a related question about the proof argument between (66) and (67): why is monotonic non-increase enough? Reviewer 1 found the proof outline (Section 4.2) dense and hard to grasp, lacking intuitive discussion.

- **The constant \(c_0\) in Theorem 1 is not characterized.** Reviewer 4 emphasizes that \(c_0\) is the most crucial constant but its relationship to problem parameters (target distribution, kernel) is unclear, limiting practical implications. No estimation or bound is provided.

- **Contribution significance is disputed.** While Reviewer 2 finds sufficient novelty, Reviewer 3 argues the method essentially proves \(I_{\mathrm{stein}}(\mu_t|\pi) \ge c_0 \|\nabla\log(\mu_t/\pi)\|^2_{L^2(\mu_t)}\) and then applies LSI; the contribution is not significant enough for an ICLR paper. Reviewer 3 asks what difference exists from results invalidated by Duncan et al. (2023) regarding eq (7).

- **No conclusion section** – a substantial lack preventing summary of main findings (Reviewer 1).

- **Numerical experiments are insufficient and uninformative.** Reviewer 1 finds the experiments (Section 5, Appendix D) lacking; they do not illustrate any substantial benefit of the framework. The take-away message is unclear. The y-axis labels in figures are too small, and plots would benefit from shared y-axis ranges for easier comparison (Reviewer 1). Additionally, Figure 2 shows a non-monotonic decrease of KL, which Reviewer 4 asks to be explained.

- **Missing practical impact discussion.** Reviewer 1 notes the paper misses a discussion of how the results can be used in practice for sampling tasks or approximating unnormalized target densities. The residual bias from finite particles (since the theory assumes infinite particles) is not discussed; Reviewer 1 asks whether this bias could be estimated.

- **Missing comparisons and references.** Reviewer 2 states that convergence results for finite-particle SVGD should have been introduced in Section 1, citing [Shi & Mackey 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09721) and [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14076). Reviewer 1 asks how SVGD compares to neural importance sampling (Müller et al. 2019) and whether the analysis can be extended to other kernels beyond the RBF kernel.

- **Proof details need clarification.** Various reviewers struggle with specific steps: obtaining equation (13) from (9) (Reviewer 1), the sum below equation (16) having a mis-index (Reviewer 1), and the proof between (66) and (67) (Reviewers 2 and 3).

- **Minor presentation issues:** Missing footnote explanation of the Radon-Nikodym derivative (Reviewer 1), ambiguous pronoun “this study” on page 7 (Reviewer 1), typo “ker” before Section 4.2 (Reviewer 1), “kernelsatisfies” -> “kernel satisfies” in Section 4.3 (Reviewer 1).
