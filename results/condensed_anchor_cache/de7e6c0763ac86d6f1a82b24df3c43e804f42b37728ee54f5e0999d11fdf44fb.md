## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents SnapMem, a snapshot-based 3D scene memory representation for embodied exploration and reasoning. It uses clusters of co-visible objects in images, along with frontier-based exploration, to support lifelong autonomy. SnapMem is evaluated on three benchmarks for embodied question answering and navigation, showing performance improvements over object-centric graph baselines. Reviewers generally agree the idea is justified, but raise significant concerns about clarity of exposition, incremental contribution, reliance on known poses/coordinates, rule-based design with many hyperparameters, missing ablations and baselines, and lack of analysis on failure cases and scene complexity.

### Strengths
- The core idea—preserving informative snapshot images based on object co‑visibility to capture rich spatial and semantic information—is sound and reasonably justified (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- VLM is appropriately used to leverage the visual information in the snapshots (Reviewer 1).
- The frontier-based exploration component is considered reasonable and effective for navigation (Reviewer 1).
- SnapMem demonstrates consistent performance improvements over traditional scene representations across three different benchmarks (LLM‑Match, EM‑EQA, etc.), illustrating generalization ability (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The memory retrieval (prefiltering) technique reduces computational load, contributing to efficiency (Reviewer 2).
- SnapMem is memory‑efficient and presented as a promising approach for lifelong autonomy (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer found the paper writing clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 3, score 6), though other reviewers disagree on clarity (see Weaknesses).
- Detailed prompts are provided in the appendix for reproducibility (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution and missing differentiation**: The object‑image relations and frontier ideas are common in related spatial memory and scene representation work; the authors do not clearly articulate the key differences and novel contributions (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 2 and 4 also question whether SnapMem is simply a better interface for VLMs rather than a fundamentally new representation, especially since it relies on known poses and coordinates—information already sufficient to build a 3D scene graph (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and completeness of method explanation**:
  - The manuscript does not fully deliver explanations; the pipeline is based on ConceptGraph without necessary background (Reviewer 1).
  - Notations in Algorithm 1 are unclear (e.g., `||O||`, `F(I) = ||O_I||`); the definition of “confidence” (line 220) is missing (Reviewers 1, 4).
  - It is unclear how frontier snapshots (which may lack object detections) are integrated with the object‑detection‑based clustering (Reviewer 1).
  - The navigation pipeline at test time is not described: frequency of capturing images, frequency of running the SnapMem update and VLM inference, average memory size, action space (discrete/continuous), and navigation policy are all unspecified (Reviewer 1).
  - The meaning of “same observation” (line 226) needs clarification (Reviewer 4).
- **Rule‑based design and hyperparameter sensitivity**:
  - Clustering relies on a basic rule‑based co‑visibility approach; more sophisticated clustering/segmentation methods are not considered (Reviewer 2).
  - The system has many predefined rules and hyperparameters (e.g., `max_dist`, `K` in prefiltering). It is unclear how these generalize beyond the three tuned tasks (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing evaluations and ablations**:
  - No ablation is provided for the prefiltering step: there is no information on the number of images before/after prefiltering, nor a comparison of performance with and without it (Reviewer 2).
  - The experiments do not analyze failure cases; metrics for different question types would help understand when SnapMem fails (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 also asks whether the bottleneck is prefiltering or VLM reasoning.
  - SnapMem is not compared against 3D point‑cloud‑based representations (e.g., 3D‑LLM, LEO) on the EM‑EQA task (Reviewer 3).
  - Only GPT‑4o is tested; using a closed model hinders reproducibility. Testing open‑source VLMs is suggested (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 2’s `Avg. frames` column is unclear; an efficiency column (number of images stored) is missing from Table 1 (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing related work**: The paper discusses 3D scene representations but omits 2D scene representations and topological mapping (e.g., Topomap, Robohop, Neural Topological SLAM, Topological Semantic Graph Memory). Discussions on why SnapMem is better for exploration and embodied QA than these 2D methods are lacking (Reviewer 4).
- **Scoping concerns**: SnapMem builds memory based on detected objects; areas with no detectable objects are not captured, though they may be important to an embodied robot (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also notes that since SnapMem relies on object co‑visibility (largely 2D), building the pipeline purely in 2D (without poses) might be more practical.
- **Presentation issues**: Missing citations (e.g., ConceptGraph in Sec. 3.1) and a typo (line 219, `I*` missing a symbol) (Reviewers 1, 2). One reviewer (score 6) found the paper clear, contrasting with others; this is noted as a reviewer disagreement.

*Reviewer 3 (score 6) is notably more positive than Reviewers 2 and 4 (both score 3), particularly on the clarity and overall merit of the method, while Reviewers 2 and 4 emphasize incremental contribution, missing details, and evaluation gaps.*
