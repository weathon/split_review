## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BrainMixer, an unsupervised framework that learns representations of brain activity by jointly modeling voxel-level time series and functional connectivity. The approach adapts MLP-Mixer for the voxel time series (with TimeMixer and VoxelMixer) and introduces a Temporal Graph Mixer for the functional connectivity graph. The two encoders are pre-trained via contrastive learning using an InfoNCE objective between global and local embeddings of the two views. Experiments are conducted on multiple datasets (fMRI, EEG, MEG) for classification and anomaly detection tasks, with reported improvements over 13 baselines. The problem is well motivated, but reviews diverge: while two reviewers (scores 6 and 6) find the performance encouraging and the approach well tailored, one reviewer (score 3) finds the paper unclear in its contributions, insufficiently justified for either machine learning or neuroimaging, and lacking in fair comparisons.

### Strengths
- The problem of spatiotemporal representation learning for brain data (fMRI, EEG, MEG) is important to neuroscience and brain imaging (R2, R3).
- The paper demonstrates an interesting application of contrastive pre-training to multi-view (voxel activity + functional connectivity) neuroimaging data (R2).
- Experiments cover multiple datasets and modalities (fMRI, EEG, MEG) and compare the proposed model to many baselines (R1). The reported performance improvements are large and encourage further investigation (R3).
- The paper discusses numerous related works and proposes tailored processing steps for brain data, e.g., temporal graph patching (R3).

### Weaknesses
**Clarity and Description of Methodology** (all reviewers, especially R2 and R3)
- The voxel-mixer description is confusing: the “dimension reduction” step using \(W^{(s)}_{\text{segment}}\) seems to reduce time, not space, but the text suggests it groups voxels into patches. Notation is inconsistent (subscript \(i\) appears only on some matrices, dimensions are missing). The roles of softmax and “flat” in equation (2) are unclear (R2, R3).
- The functional connectivity encoder lacks crucial details: the source of the connectivity graph \(\mathcal{G}_F\) is not defined, how patches are created from temporal random walks is vague, and the mixing process is poorly explained (R2, R3).
- Terms such as “temporal graph,” “functional patching,” and “token” are not defined or used in a non-standard way. “Temporal graph” is used to describe connections across time points, which is not standard (R3). “Functional patching” (mapping patches onto continuous coordinates via linear interpolation) is not explained for EEG/MEG where voxel/channel locations are not directly available (R3).
- Figure 1 organizes the components but the level of detail is insufficient to see how dimensions change through the pipeline (R3).
- The objective function uses variables \(Z_V\) and \(Z_F\) that are not defined; the description says mutual information between functional connectivity and voxel activity is maximized, but the objective uses \(H_{\text{voxel}}\) (voxel activity) and \(Z_V\) together, while \(H_{\text{time}}\) is not in the loss (R3). Notation \(\tilde{T}\) is undefined (R2, R3).
- Grammar and phrasing issues: “toward revealing the understanding” (R2), “minuetes” for “minutes” (R2, R3). The first paragraph of the introduction is awkwardly written (R2). The abstract statement “we bridge this gap” is vague (R2).

**Experimental Design and Fairness** (R1, R2, R3)
- The experimental design is poorly described: it is unclear whether all data (including test) is used for unsupervised pre-training (which could leak information), whether splits avoid having the same subject in both training and test sets, and how cross-validation is performed (R1).
- Table 2 comparisons are potentially unfair: the type, capacity, and pre-training usage of baselines are not described. A parameter count analysis is missing – the proposed model effectively doubles parameters by using two encoders (voxel-mixer + connectivity encoder) compared to single-view models like BNT, so improved performance may simply be due to more parameters (R2). The paper does not compare to a simple logistic regression on FNC (R2).
- Hyper-parameter selection is not discussed for either BrainMixer or the baselines. Data augmentation parameters (e.g., random walk length) are said to be dataset-dependent, yet no valid hyper-parameter selection procedure (without test set access) is described. It is not reported whether the same augmentation was applied to all methods (R3). The augmented data can itself account for performance differences if baselines are trained without it (R3).
- For the anomaly detection task (Table 2), AUC PR is missing (many “normal” cases, few anomalies). AUC-ROC is insufficient. Calibration (Brier score or calibration curves) is missing for all classification tasks (R1). The synthetic anomaly detection task is described as a toy problem with questionable relevance to neuroimaging; it is borrowed from a single workshop paper (R2).

**Rigor and Statistical Analysis** (R1)
- Table 1 reports accuracy without providing the class distribution; accuracy is unjustified without balance information. Confusion matrices are needed (R1).
- No statistical comparisons (e.g., best model vs. others with p-value correction) are performed for Tables 2 and 3. Normality assumptions and appropriate tests (e.g., Wilcoxon) are not checked (R1).

**Unclear Contributions and Missing Analysis** (all reviewers, especially R2)
- It is unclear whether performance gains come from the proposed encoder architectures, the functional patching strategy, the unsupervised contrastive pre-training, or simply from concatenating fMRI and FNC embeddings. An ablation study separating these components is missing (R1). The paper does not show which contribution is essential.
- The paper claims to operate at the voxel level but does not visualize learned voxel groupings or spatial maps, unlike prior voxel-level work it contrasts itself with. This is a significant omission for a neuroimaging contribution (R2).
- The proposed patching approach (functional patching) is not ablated against alternatives. Related work (Geenjaar et al., 2022) already applied MLP-Mixer to fMRI with a spectral clustering patching strategy – no comparison or ablation is provided (R1).
- The claim that most studies focus on either voxel-level activity or functional connectivity is inaccurate; many approaches (e.g., ICA, RBM, NMF) extract intrinsic networks from voxel data before computing connectivity (R2). Similarly, MEG/EEG do not provide ready localization to brain parts without solving an ill-posed inverse problem (R2).
- The section “How Does Brain Detect GAN Generated Images?” has a cramped discussion and unclear experimental design. GAN performance is not reported, distributions are compared only visually without numerical values (R1). Figure 3 shows “distribution of detected abnormalities” but the method to obtain these maps, color bar, p-values, and FDR correction are missing (R1). Additionally, the ROIs in Figure 3 appear too precisely carved, which the described methods would not produce (R2).
- Missing related work: the Glacier model (Mahmood et al., ICASSP 2023) and a 2022 paper by the same authors achieve similar goals (R2). The appendix/supplementary material is empty (all reviewers).
