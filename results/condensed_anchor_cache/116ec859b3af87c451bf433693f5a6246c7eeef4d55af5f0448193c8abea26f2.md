## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel black-box membership inference attack (MIA) for diffusion models that requires only access to the image-to-image variation API, not the internal U-Net. The method is based on the hypothesis that training images yield smaller reconstruction errors. Experiments on DDIM, Stable Diffusion, and Diffusion Transformers show performance improvements over prior methods, with additional ablation studies and a real-world application on DALL-E.

### Strengths
- Novel black-box MIA method that uses only the variation API, making it more practical than white-box alternatives.
- Intuitive and theoretically justified approach, with clear demonstration of effectiveness across DDIM, Stable Diffusion, and Diffusion Transformers.
- Comprehensive experiments: multiple datasets, full ablation studies on key hyper-parameters, and a real-world application on DALL-E’s API.
- Significant performance gains over prior MIA methods (noted by reviewers 1–3).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing, motivation, and organization**: The Introduction’s motivation is not solid enough (reviewer 1). The paper does not clearly articulate why MIA without U-Net access is difficult, what specific challenges exist, and how they are addressed (reviewer 4). Equation numbering is chaotic and Algorithm 1 formatting lacks indentation (reviewer 4). The abstract is too brief and should include more method insights and key results (reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient analysis of experimental results**: In Table 1, the proposed method achieves only ~1–3% improvement over SOTA on most metrics and performs worse than PIAN on the TP metric for CIFAR‑10 (reviewers 1,4). No explanation is provided for the lower TP of REDOFFUSE on CIFAR‑10 (reviewer 1). The effect of longer diffusion steps on degrading performance is not analyzed, and potential solutions are missing (reviewer 1). Only one case study is given in Sec. 6; more member/non-member comparison examples are needed (reviewer 1).
- **Practicality of the attack scenario**: The attack assumes the variation API allows intermediate denoising queries, but most real-world APIs (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3) only provide final generated images, not denoising outputs (reviewer 3). The paper should specify which specific types of variation APIs enable this attack and discuss their practical availability.
- **Lack of computational requirement analysis**: The proposed method may require many denoising queries compared to baselines, yet no analysis of the number of queries, time complexity, or memory requirements is provided (reviewer 3).
- **Unclear algorithm explanation**: The intuition in Sec. 4.2 regarding the “bias” term and why a well-trained model satisfies ∇_θ L(θ)=0 is ambiguous; a more precise definition and derivation are needed (reviewer 3).
- **Vague evaluation in Table 4**: It is unclear how L1 and L2 distances are interpreted as membership inference accuracy (reviewer 2).
- **One-sided ablation metric**: Ablation experiments use only AUC; additional metrics would make the results more convincing (reviewer 4). (Reviewer 4 notes that extra content in the appendix exceeds page limits.)
- **Biased application experiment**: The DALL‑E test uses only 30 famous paintings and 30 generated paintings, which is too small to be convincing (reviewer 4).
- **Missing experiments and analysis**: No results for DDPM are provided (reviewer 3). Generalization to fine-tuned diffusion models is not addressed (reviewer 3). No discussion on how to provide confidence levels for membership predictions to avoid false accusations (reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient connection to prior work**: The paper does not adequately discuss whether similar ideas (e.g., reconstruction-based MIA) have been proposed in other MIA domains (reviewer 2).
- **Disagreement among reviewers on significance**: Some reviewers (1–3) found the performance improvements significant, while reviewer 4 noted that improvements are only marginal (1–3%) and that the method can be worse on some metrics (TP on CIFAR‑10).
