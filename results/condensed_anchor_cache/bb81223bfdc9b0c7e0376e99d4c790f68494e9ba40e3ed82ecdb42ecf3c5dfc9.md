## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes S2GNN, a Spectral Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Network that unifies short-term spatio-temporal forecasting (STF) and long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) within a single framework. It uses a spectral GNN with learnable filters for global feature extraction, scale-adaptive node embeddings, cross-correlation embeddings, and an adaptive graph structure to handle varying sequence lengths and improve scalability. Experiments on eight public datasets (covering both STF and LTSF) claim consistent state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses an important gap between STF and LTSF, which previously had incompatible model capabilities (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5). Reviewer 4 calls the problem “important”; Reviewer 2 says it is “significant and practical.”
- Novel model components: spectral GNN with learnable filters, scale-adaptive node embeddings, cross-correlation embeddings, and adaptive graph structure (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5). Reviewer 4 finds the scale-adaptive node embeddings “interesting,” though others consider the overall contribution insufficient (see Weaknesses).
- Extensive experiments across multiple datasets, including both STF and LTSF benchmarks, demonstrating consistent outperformance (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5). Ablation and efficiency studies are included (Reviewer 4).
- Use of spectral GNN and an MLP-based sequential model makes the architecture scalable and efficient for longer inputs and larger datasets (Reviewer 2). This directly addresses the computational inefficiency of existing STGNNs on long sequences.
- Learnable filters enhance interpretability, directly showing that temporal data exhibit both homophily and heterophily (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 5 notes interpretability is still limited (see Weaknesses).
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 5). Reviewer 2 praises “comprehensive and clear problem definition.”
- The adaptive graph structure and learnable filters allow flexible handling of different input lengths (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Motivation unclear.** The claimed rationale for addressing “sample indistinguishability” to improve model efficiency is not explained (Reviewers 3, 4). Why this problem arises, how the embeddings specifically reduce computational cost, and why an adaptive graph is needed for efficiency remain ambiguous (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 3 asks: “what is the connection between sample indistinguishability and model efficiency or size?” Reviewer 4 states: “I don’t see how [cross-correlation embedding] can help to achieve more efficient learning to process both short and long term dependencies.”
- **Insufficient contribution and lack of novelty.** The main novelties are the two embedding methods. But the distinction from existing temporal/positional embeddings is not clear. The cross-correlation embedding may simply be a typical temporal embedding with an added node ID (Reviewer 3). The adaptive adjacent matrix is not considered a contribution—it is just a trainable weight per node (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 judges the contribution “not enough for ICLR,” while Reviewer 2 (score 8) found strong contribution. Reviewer 3 (score 3) finds the method lacks innovation, and Reviewer 1 notes the ablation does not isolate LTSF contributions.
- **Missing key baseline comparisons for LTSF.** Several reviewers note the absence of recent strong LTSF models, such as iTransformer (Reviewers 2, 3), xLSTM (Reviewer 1), and ForecastGrapher (Reviewer 3). Additionally, no comparison with models designed for large-scale spatio-temporal datasets like BigST (Reviewer 2). The selected baselines are not state-of-the-art, limiting credibility (Reviewer 3).
- **Ablation studies do not isolate LTSF-specific contributions.** It remains unclear which component (spectral GNN, scale-adaptive embeddings, cross-correlation embeddings, or adaptive graph) drives long-term forecasting performance (Reviewer 1). No ablation is tailored to clarify this (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 questions: “Which part (ablation study) is responsible for LTSF?”
- **Visual evidence for long-term superiority is weak.** Fig. 8(b) shows only two baseline models and does not visually suggest strong advantage (Reviewer 1). The claim of “smaller fluctuations around the diagonal” is not convincingly supported (Reviewer 1).
- **Poor writing quality and unclear figures.** There are errors: in Equation 2 the text says “constant constant” (Reviewer 3). The figures, especially Figure 2, are too small and the font is hard to read (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Labels and captions lack clarity (Reviewer 3). The manuscript’s logic is criticized: the authors discuss pros/cons of spatial vs. spectral GNNs and then adopt spectral without clear reasoning (Reviewer 4).
- **No detailed scalability analysis.** Although efficiency is claimed, computational complexity for larger node counts and sequence lengths is not analyzed (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 2 requested evaluation on large-scale spatio-temporal datasets (e.g., BigST), which is missing.
- **Limited interpretability.** Despite learnable filters, the paper does not explain which features the model prioritizes or how the adaptive graph evolves during training (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 2 finds the filters interpretable (minority positive view); Reviewer 5 finds the spectral operations lack intuitive explanation.
- **Model handling of node count changes is unclear.** The paper claims existing methods cannot adapt to node changes, but does not explain if S2GNN is inductive or how it handles missing/additional nodes in testing (Reviewer 3). The statement should be clarified with illustrations (Reviewer 3).
- **Training requires many epochs.** Most TSF benchmarks converge within 10 epochs, but Figure 2 shows S2GNN needs many epochs, yet the graph structure appears stable and lacks variation—possibly just model convergence (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of components explicitly designed for both short- and long-term forecasting.** Reviewer 3 asks: “I don’t see any components designed specifically to optimize both short- and long-term TSF tasks,” while other reviewers assume the architecture unifies them. This discrepancy suggests the claim needs stronger justification.
