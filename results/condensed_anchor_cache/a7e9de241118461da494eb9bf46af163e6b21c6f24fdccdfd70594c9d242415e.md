## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UCAN, a technique to extend randomized smoothing to anisotropic noise, providing theoretical certification guarantees for three noise parameter generators (fixed pattern, universal, input-dependent). Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet report improved certified accuracy (up to 182.6% at large radii). Reviewers find the paper well-written and the approach novel, but raise major concerns about evaluation fairness (use of scaled radius), the validity of input-dependent certificates, limited technical novelty of the main theorem, missing baselines, and the practical relevance of the Lebesgue measure as a robustness metric.

### Strengths
- Novelty of extending randomized smoothing from isotropic to anisotropic noise (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Comprehensive theoretical certification guarantees for anisotropic noise, with analyses that transform existing isotropic RS methods (Reviewer 1, 3).
- Three noise parameter generators (fixed pattern, universal, input-dependent); the input-dependent component is particularly interesting (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Well-written, clearly structured, and easy to read (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- Thorough experiments on high-dimensional datasets (e.g., ImageNet) with a wide range of prior methods (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Effective visualisations that distinguish different noise schemes (Reviewer 2).
- Simplicity of the approach is considered a strength, not a weakness (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation fairness**: The certified accuracy (e.g., 70% on CIFAR-10 at l₂ radius 1.75) appears inconsistent with a standard Lp norm (l₂ norm from l∞ budget 8/255 is ≈1.74), suggesting the use of a scaled radius rather than an absolute Lp norm. This makes comparisons with isotropic RS methods potentially misleading. The headline 182.6% improvement over SOTA is not an apples-to-apples comparison. Results vs. `min σᵢ * R` should be presented for a fair comparison (Corollary 3.3). (Reviewer 1, 2, 4)
- **Input‑dependent noise breaks certificates**: The highest-performing input-dependent approach (Section 4.3) re-optimizes μ,σ at the perturbed input x+δ, violating the requirement that the smoothing distribution be fixed within the certified ball. Theorem 3.2 does not hold for this scheme. This issue is known in prior work (Eiras et al. 2022). The paper must either fix this (e.g., with a memory‑based approach) or remove the input-dependent component. (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Limited novelty of the main theorem**: Theorem 3.2 is essentially a direct corollary of prior results obtained by per‑dimension scaling (a simple affine transformation); it adds little beyond existing theory. (Reviewer 1, 4; also described as straightforward by Reviewer 3)
- **Missing baselines**: No comparisons with existing certified methods for l₁ and l∞ robustness (Reviewer 1 suggests references [1,2,3]).
- **Practical relevance of the proposed metric**: The Lebesgue measure of the certified region is not well‑justified as a measure of adversarial risk. A larger certified area does not guarantee a larger minimum adversarial distance (the established risk measure). The certified distances to the nearest adversarial example are unchanged by this work, and the paper fails to show that the proposed metric outperforms standard certified radius. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing details and clarity issues**:
   - The paper states the theory relies on “assumptions” with “limited universality” but does not specify what these assumptions are or why they are stringent. (Reviewer 3)
   - Definition 3.1 should be presented as a theorem, not a definition. (Reviewer 3)
   - The definition/review of the alternative Lebesgue measure should be moved to Section 3.1, where Table 1 first uses it. (Reviewer 3)
   - In Section 4.2, the loss function should be written as ℒ(θ_g) to clarify the optimization variable, and σ(θ_g), μ(θ_g) should be used in the smoothing loss expression. (Reviewer 3)
   - The paper uses a binary formulation for Cohen et al., which gives poorer results; this should be clarified. (Reviewer 4) The definition of Acc in the Metrics section contains an error: V'_S appears on the LHS but not on the RHS. (Reviewer 4)
   - Why a 5‑layer NN for the noise parameter generators? No ablation study or motivation is provided. (Reviewer 1)
   - Training details are unclear: are the variance loss and smoothing loss optimized alternately or simultaneously? (Reviewer 1)
   - At small radii, isotropic SOTA methods outperform UCAN; some discussion of this would be welcome. (Reviewer 4)
- **Overall contribution seen as incremental**: The core idea is a straightforward per‑dimension scaling of existing RS techniques. While the simplicity is a strength, the improvement over SOTA is not fundamentally new (Reviewer 2, 4). One reviewer (Reviewer 2) is particularly critical of the contribution (score 1) and of the metric choice.
