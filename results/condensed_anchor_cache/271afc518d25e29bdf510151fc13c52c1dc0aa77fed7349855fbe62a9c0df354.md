## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SkillTron, a visual object navigation system that combines learning-based and classical map-based planning via a skill fusion decider, and uses an interactive semantic map (built on SegmATRon) for online segmentation improvement. Experiments are conducted in Habitat on HM3D. Reviewer scores are 3, 1, 1, 3, indicating strong disagreement: two reviewers see a reasonable system with useful ablations and some improvement over baselines, while two reviewers find severe experimental errors, weak contributions, and poor writing that make the work unacceptably incomplete for publication.

### Strengths

- Proposes a complete architecture that combines both learning-based (RL) and classic map-based planning methods (exploration and goal-reaching) using a decision module, leveraging the strengths of each.
- Exploits SegmATRon to achieve more reliable segmentation via online modification of segmentation model weights during inference using a buffer of frames.
- Performs useful and reasonable ablations of their own method, showing the relative contribution of individual components (e.g., number of frames, agent turn angle).
- Diagrams of the proposed pipeline are well made and clear; the description of the approach is presented clearly and the motivation and details explained well in parts.
- Approach is robust to segmentation noise through image dilation/erosion and temporal confidence accumulation.
- Experiments show some improvement over baselines, with the proposed approach outperforming the Habitat 2023 challenge winner on validation scenes (Reviewer 4), though improvement under OneFormer setting is marginal.

### Weaknesses

- **Experimental errors and invalid comparisons**: The method (SkillTron+SegmATRon) is evaluated on a manually selected subset of HM3D validation episodes, while comparison numbers from the Habitat Challenge use a completely different (secret) test set. No 1‑1 comparison on a shared test set is provided. The paper should either evaluate on the full public HM3D test set or submit to the challenge for a fair comparison (Reviewer 2).
- **No statistical significance reported**: The claimed “significant improvement” over SkillTron+OneFormer in ablations shows a change in success rate, SPL, and SoftSPL of ≤0.02, and no standard error metrics are given, making the improvement statistically unsubstantiated (Reviewer 2).
- **Heavily based on SkillFusion without clear differentiation**: The architecture appears to be a direct extension of SkillFusion (Staroverov et al.) with SegmATRon added, but the differences and novel contributions are not clearly stated. Several reviewers ask for a detailed comparison with SkillFusion (Reviewers 1, 4). The contributions over prior work appear minor.
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Writing quality, English, and flow need significant improvement; many sentences are hard to understand (Reviewer 3). The paper suffers from a tight page limit, leaving many crucial details about architecture and parameter choices missing, making the work very hard to reproduce (Reviewer 1). The paper reads like an experiment report for the Habitat ObjectGoalNav challenge, with problem formulation very specific to that challenge and unclear generality (Reviewer 3). The submission uses the camera‑ready template (author names deleted) instead of the anonymized submission template (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing motivation and vague design choices**:
  - Why are all four combinations (classical exploration, classical goal‑reaching, RL exploration, RL goal‑reaching) necessary? What is the benefit over having just classical or just RL? (Reviewer 4)
  - The definition of L_{fusion} is not clearly given (Reviewer 4).
  - When is RL‑based exploration actually used? Mentioned in Fig. 2 but not in Sec. 4.1 (Skill Fusion Decider) (Reviewer 4).
  - The motivation for “Navigation Image Sequences” (Sec. 4.2) is unclear; how does it relate to the offline segmentation model? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why not use highly performant semantic SLAM methods for building a 2D semantic map? (Reviewer 2)
- **Weak baselines and insufficient related‑work comparison**: Experiments only compare against 2 team submissions (Reviewer 3). The paper makes conjectures stated as facts (e.g., “such approaches are not correctly linked to the algorithms for planning map trajectories” regarding Ramakrishnan et al. (2022) – why is the RL approach “incorrect”?) (Reviewer 2). A comprehensive comparison with all corresponding SOTA methods (including those used as components) is missing, and the relative improvements are not clarified (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing ablations**: No ablation is provided for the effect of erosion/dilation/accumulation/fading (Sec. 4.3) (Reviewer 4).
- **Sensor noise assumption**: It is unclear what kind of sensor noise occurs at test time – does the agent use ground truth odometry and depth? (Reviewer 4).
