## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces DPLM-2, a multimodal diffusion protein language model that extends the discrete diffusion protein language model (DPLM) to jointly model sequences and 3D structures. It converts 3D backbone coordinates into discrete structure tokens via lookup-free quantization (LFQ), trains on experimental (PDB) and synthetic structures, and employs a warm-up strategy leveraging pre-trained sequence-based PLMs. The model is evaluated on unconditional generation, folding, inverse folding, motif-scaffolding, and representation learning. Reviewer 1 (score 3, confidence 5) is critical, citing mode collapse, weak baselines, and evaluation gaps. Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 8 and 8, confidence 4 and 5) are positive, highlighting originality, strong empirical performance across tasks, and clear presentation, though they note missing details, evaluation concerns, and analysis gaps.

### Strengths

- **Originality and novelty**: DPLM-2 is the first multimodal diffusion protein language model, contributing a framework combining sequence and structure tokens with LFQ-VAE, and sequence pretraining followed by LoRA fine-tuning (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). This extension of DPLM to multimodal scenarios is a meaningful research question (Reviewer 3).
- **Clear writing and logical structure**: The paper is clearly written, generally easy to follow, and logically structured (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Simultaneous sequence-structure generation**: The model achieves joint generation of sequences and structures without requiring a two-stage approach (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Strong empirical performance across diverse tasks**: DPLM-2 demonstrates competitive performance in unconditional generation, folding, inverse folding, and motif-scaffolding, with comprehensive analysis (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Valuable ablation studies on sequence pretraining and data augmentation provide insights (Reviewer 2).
- **Experiments on structure tokenization**: The investigation of structure tokenization is valuable for the community (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Mode collapse and catastrophic forgetting**: Reviewer 1 notes that DPLM-2 is initialized from DPLM weights, leading to severe mode collapse (poor diversity in Table 2, high AAR in Table 5). Training from scratch with thorough ablation is recommended. Reviewer 3 questions whether the performance decline in Table 7 is due to catastrophic forgetting, as DPLM-2 in that table may use structural pretraining, introducing two variables (architecture + structural data) vs. DPLM. The paper should disentangle these effects and address mode collapse.
- **Limited baselines and comparisons**: Reviewer 1 criticizes that unconditional sequence generation compares only with EvoDiff; more baselines are needed (AR transformers, CARP, continuous diffusion, flow-matching). For inverse folding, existing methods are weak baselines; recognized IF models should be included (Reviewer 1). For representation learning, broader baselines (e.g., GNN-based models) would be helpful (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 notes that MultiFlow (retrained) lacks evaluation on Novelty and Diversity.
- **Evaluation gaps and metric concerns**:
    - Sequence-based metrics are missing; diversity should be assessed via sequence clustering (Reviewer 1). The designability metric only measures consistency with ESMFold predictions, not true protein quality (Reviewer 1). Using pdb-TM (TM-score against PDB) is questionable given training on synthetic data (Reviewer 1).
    - Unconditional generation shows contradictory results: high scTM but significantly worse scRMSD (mean and variance). This discrepancy is unusual and warrants investigation (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 also notes that pLDDT trends (Figure 3) are not aligned with scTM/scRMSD trends, suggesting pLDDT may be unreliable for generated proteins. Further analysis of generated structures (secondary structure content, physically unrealistic features, distribution comparisons with native proteins) is needed (Reviewer 2).
    - In inverse folding (Table 5), DPLM-2 has higher AAR but lower scTM compared to ESM3; this may be due to ESMFold bias or sequence distribution shift (Reviewer 3). Clarify if RMSD is over all residues or aligned regions (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of structure tokens**: Explanation of tokenizer training and token combination should be moved from appendix to main text (Reviewer 2). Lack of interpretability: do tokens correspond to local structural environments or are they abstract? (Reviewer 1). Structure tokens are concentrated in alpha helices and beta sheets, with low density in loop regions; since loops are flexible, this suggests tokens may not be well-learned there, leading to worse designability in loop-rich regions (Figure 4B) (Reviewer 3). Vocabulary usage rate for VQ-VAE at size 1024 may explain performance differences with LFQ (Reviewer 3, question 6). Reviewer 2 asks about trade-offs in structural invariance when using discrete tokens vs. 3D coordinates, and Reviewer 3 notes discrete tokens limit ability to capture finer structural details.
- **Insufficient details on training and noise schedule**:
    - How sequence and structure tokens are combined and how the structure tokenizer is trained needs clearer explanation (Reviewer 2).
    - Distinct noise levels per modality: how noise schedules are determined and how they affect learning joint/conditional/marginal distributions is underdeveloped (Reviewer 2).
    - Section 4.2 mentions supervised fine-tuning with a folding objective, but the loss function, learning rate schedule, and number of training steps are missing (Reviewer 2).
    - Reviewer 1 asks: why does argmax decoding work now (page 9, line 442) when it did not in the original DPLM? Also, the model does not learn protein length distribution – have the authors attempted to address this? (Reviewer 1 question 1). Ablation results in Table 3 are controversial; please clarify the procedure and number of samples (Reviewer 1 question 2). The DeepLoc experiment is insufficient – why only one tiny dataset? Provide experiments showing catastrophic forgetting (Reviewer 1 question 4).
    - Clarify the meaning of DPLM-2 (seq→structure) and (structure→seq) in Table 2 (Reviewer 2).
    - Reviewer 3 notes minor errors: equation on line 197 missing right parenthesis; \(b_i(t)\) in equation (1) is undefined.
- **Co-generation consistency claim**: The paper claims consistency between structure and sequence, but line 223 assumes conditional independence. A rigorous mathematical proof is needed (Reviewer 1 question 5).
- **Limited dataset scale**: The model is trained on a relatively small protein structure dataset; exploration on larger-scale structural data could unlock further potential (Reviewer 3).
- **Repetition issue**: Diffusion protein language models (including DPLM) tend to generate repetitive amino acids; DPLM used a resampling scheme. Does DPLM-2 suffer from similar repetition, and if so, how is it resolved? (Reviewer 3 question 5).
- **Consistency in presentations**: Inconsistent bolding of best results in Tables 2 and 6; clarify presentation of mean and median values (Reviewer 2). In Table 4, DPLM-2 zero-shot folding has high RMSD compared to ESM3, PVQD, ESMFold; competitive only after SFT (Reviewer 2).
