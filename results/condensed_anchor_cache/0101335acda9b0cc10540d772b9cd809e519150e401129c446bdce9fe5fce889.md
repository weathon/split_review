## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a framework for visualizing features learned by vision transformers using an optimization-based approach (similar to Olah et al. 2017). It compares ViTs and CNNs, finding that ViTs make better use of background information and depend less on high-frequency texture. It also finds that ViTs preserve spatial information except in the last layer, where patch tokens become similar. The analysis extends to CLIP-trained ViTs, revealing that neurons activate for semantic concepts rather than visual features. The method is applied to multiple ViT variants (DeiT, CoaT, ConViT, PiT, Swin, Twin).

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and largely neglected problem: understanding how vision transformers work and shedding light on these black boxes, which is helpful to the vision community.
- Well organized and written, with adequate mention of prior research.
- Findings that ViTs make better use of background and foreground information compared to CNNs, and that their predictions depend far less on high-frequency information, are interesting. (Note: one reviewer disputes the fairness of the comparison behind this finding—see Weaknesses.)
- Finding that ViTs maintain spatial information despite lacking CNN inductive bias, but this property disappears in the last layer, is a key observation. (Another reviewer notes this behavior was already reported in DeepViT 2021—see Weaknesses.)
- Analysis of text-guided ViTs (CLIP) is a different approach than existing work and an important contribution for understanding future vision-language models.
- Substantial number of experiments providing comprehensive visual analysis across multiple ViT variants (DeiT, CoaT, ConViT, PiT, Swin, Twin).

### Weaknesses
- The visualization method lacks novelty; it mainly borrows from Olah et al. 2017 with some engineering tweaks.
- Some observations are not new. The finding that ViTs maintain spatial information except the last layer, where patch tokens become very similar, has already been pointed out by DeepViT 2021. The last layer behavior is expected and not surprising.
- The comparison in Table 2 (ViTs vs CNNs on background/foreground usage) is unfair: the ViTs used are more powerful than the CNNs (more parameters, more computations) and have consistently better classification accuracies. Thus the conclusion that “ViTs are better at using background information” is not convincing.
- Results in Table 1 and Section 4 fail to yield significant or novel insights. The performance gap between “Natural Accuracy” and “Isolating CLS” could likely be closed by fine-tuning only the linear classification head, reinforcing that the CLS token aggregates information in later layers—this has been indicated by prior work (Touvron et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2021). The findings lack substantial significance.
- The paper does not get into the meat of what transformers really do: it does not explore what key, query, and value operations learn, why they are more effective, or how they relate to convolution. Visualizations of these features could give good insights.
- No exploration of why transformers use foreground/background more effectively (if the comparison were fair).
- The key finding that the last layer loses spatial information (Section 4, page 5) lacks theory and/or experiment-based proof. The authors seem unsure why this happens. More justification is needed.
- The findings in Section 5 and Table 2 are interesting but limited to evaluating only the basic ViT architecture.
- Currently the presented findings appear somewhat isolated; the paper would benefit from more in-depth analysis and insight into the interrelationship between these findings.
- Missing or incomplete references: Page 3 “Recent work ?”, Section 2.1, Section 2.2, Section 3 (“augmentation ensembling ?”).
- Minor clarity issues: The term “Normalized Top-5 ImageNet Accuracy” is not defined. The choice of the fifth layer in Section 6 is not well-justified.
- Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms: What really explains higher performance of ViTs vs CNNs in the architecture—can that be visualized? How much of these results apply to MLP-Mixer or its variants? What is the effective receptive field of neurons (not just full receptive field)? Can the paper show how much and where in the image a neuron is getting its input from? For researchers who believed CNNs model human vision, does diverging structure of ViTs mean we are giving up on a unified vision model?
