## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MaskInversion, a method that leverages explainability maps from pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) to generate context-aware embeddings for image regions specified by a mask at test time. Starting with an initialised embedding token, it iteratively refines the token to minimise the discrepancy between the token’s explainability map (derived via LeGrad) and the query mask. Only the embedding vector is updated; the foundation model remains frozen. A gradient-decomposition strategy is proposed to speed up multi-mask optimisation. The region embeddings are evaluated on open-vocabulary retrieval, referring expression comprehension, localised captioning, and image generation across PascalVOC, MSCOCO, RefCOCO, and OpenImagesV7.

### Strengths
1. **Motivation and intuition** (R1, R3). The problem of poor localisation in CLIP is clearly identified, and the method – using explainability maps to retain global context while focusing on a region – is intuitive and novel (R3: unlike clipping/blurring/masking, the model retains access to the full image).
2. **Good performance** (R1, R2, R3). MaskInversion achieves superior results on a wide range of vision‑language benchmarks, with comprehensive quantitative and qualitative experiments (R2).
3. **Zero-shot setting** (R2). The method operates without task‑specific training, leveraging pre‑trained knowledge.
4. **Clear presentation** (R2, R3). The paper is overall well‑written and clearly outlines the method and its validation across downstream tasks.

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing important baselines** (R1). The paper discusses MaskCLIP but does not compare to simple training‑free attention modifications (MaskCLIP’s removal of Q/K, SCLIP’s Q‑to‑Q and K‑to‑K attention, CLIPSurgery’s V‑to‑V attention). Without these comparisons it is unclear whether the performance gains justify MaskInversion’s complexity. After the rebuttal, the authors added experiments showing that MaskInversion outperforms these baselines in most cases, but the new results were not integrated into the revised paper, which could mislead readers.
2. **Scalability concerns** (R1 after rebuttal). On OpenImagesV7 (more complex, more masks per image), MaskInversion underperforms compared to CLIPSurgery, raising questions about its scalability to dense‑mask scenarios.
3. **Limited novelty** (R2). The method essentially applies the LeGrad explainability map to improve feature extraction; a substantial part of the method section reviews LeGrad, reinforcing the perception of limited innovation.
4. **No ablation on the regularization hyperparameter $\alpha$** (R2). The regularization loss is described as important to avoid trivial solutions, yet no ablation study is provided across different values.
5. **Dependence on mask quality** (R2, R3). The method assumes high‑quality input masks; in practice obtaining such masks is challenging, and the paper lacks analysis of failure cases or scenarios where MaskInversion underperforms (e.g., multi‑object scenes, complex captions in MSCOCO).
6. **Lack of multi‑object analysis** (R3). Experiments focus on single‑object settings; no analysis is provided for multiple objects or their interactions, which limits the evaluation of the method’s effectiveness.
7. **Unexplored nature of global context** (R3). While the method aims to retain global information, there is no visualization or analysis (e.g., attention maps) showing how the global context modulates the localized embeddings across different scenarios. One reviewer (R3) specifically asks: “What type of global image context does this method capture? Provide visualizations.”
8. **Underperformance in one referring expression task** (R3). MaskInversion with ViT‑B/16 underperforms the simple Masked Crop baseline on RefCOCO+. No sufficient analysis is given for why this discrepancy occurs, especially given the method’s supposed advantage of leveraging global context. This raises doubts about robustness in appearance‑based descriptions.
9. **Limited practical benefit of gradient decomposition** (R1). The speed improvement is clear only when >10 masks per image. The paper does not report the typical number of masks in the evaluated tasks, so the practical advantage may be limited to niche scenarios.
10. **Minor typos** (R1, R3). Line 481 incorrectly references “Table 4.5”; in the related work section “maks” should be “masks”.

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (score 5) was more critical, especially regarding missing baselines and scalability, and noted that after rebuttal the added experiments were not discussed in the paper. Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 6) acknowledged the method’s strengths but raised concerns about novelty, ablation, and analysis depth.
