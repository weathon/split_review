## Merged Review

### Summary

The authors propose a hypernetwork-based approximate Bayesian inference framework for neural networks. They factor model parameters into deterministic components and low-dimensional implicit neural representations (INRs) that output multiplicative perturbations. An approximate posterior is placed over the hypernetwork weights using Laplace approximation, SWA-Gaussian, or normalizing flows (RealNVP). The method is evaluated on UCI regression, UCI gap, and CIFAR-10/100 out-of-distribution detection, showing competitive performance compared to baselines such as Dropout, ensembles, and full-weight-space Bayesian methods.

### Strengths

- Novel combination of approximate Bayesian inference with implicit neural representations as hypernetworks for BNNs, not explored in prior work according to Reviewer 2; Reviewers 3 and 4 also find the idea interesting and relevant.
- Good exploration of multiple posterior parameterizations: Laplace, SWA, and normalizing flows (Reviewer 1).
- Valuable practical insights: multiplicative perturbations consistently outperform additive ones; CNNs benefit from shared hypernetwork representations (Reviewer 2).
- Thorough OOD detection evaluation on CIFAR-10 with detailed analysis (Reviewer 2).
- Competitive empirical performance across multiple datasets (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Clear writing and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 rates presentation as excellent.
- Comprehensive citation of related work (Reviewer 1).
- Interesting observation that predictive uncertainty exhibits a stationary, distance-dependent structure similar to a GP, unlike Dropout or Deep Ensembles (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- Novelty concerns: The core ideas (shared hypernetworks, Bayesian inference over them, blending deterministic and stochastic weights) have all been explored before (Karaletsos et al. 2018, etc.). Reviewer 1 characterizes the paper as primarily an engineering exercise that lacks substantial methodological novelty and theoretical justification for the specific combination. Reviewer 4 is more positive (rates contribution 3/good) and Reviewer 2 also rates contribution as good, but Reviewer 1 rates contribution as fair and Reviewer 3 rates it as fair.  
- Clarity issues in Section 3.1: The description of the INR model with SIREN is difficult to parse; many notations are introduced without a supporting figure. A step-by-step algorithm would help readers (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4, however, finds presentation excellent.  
- Laplace approximation derivation is confusing: Equations (4)–(5) derive a full Hessian Laplace, but the closed-form posterior in Equation (7) corresponds to linearized Laplace with the generalized Gauss-Newton (GGN) approximation. The text does not state which is used in experiments, nor whether additional approximations (e.g., Kronecker factorization) are employed (Reviewer 2).  
- Missing and outdated baselines: The paper does not compare to other subspace inference methods (e.g., Kristiadi, Dusenberry, Daxberger) or to a block-diagonal KFAC Laplace as a full-network alternative; only last-layer Laplace is included (Reviewers 2, 3). Baselines like Dropout, BbH, and Ensembles are relatively old; comparisons to modern Laplace approximations directly on main network weights are missing (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 suggests including the simple baseline of making only the last few layers probabilistic.  
- Inconsistent baseline reporting across figures: Figure 1 is missing Laplace; Figure 2 lacks SWAG; Figure 3 omits SWAG and INR-SWAG variants (Reviewer 3).  
- Scalability and computational cost are not discussed: No runtime or memory analysis is provided. It is unclear how weight coordinates are batched during hypernetwork forward passes and whether a single pass with all coordinates is feasible. Applicability to larger networks (e.g., ImageNet) is not addressed (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- Suitability of SIREN activation is not well justified; ReLU hypernetworks are not tested. RealNVP may struggle with high-frequency perturbations compared to SIREN (Reviewer 2). The effect of integer coordinate inputs and whether they are normalized is unclear (Reviewer 4).  
- The paper does not explain why multiplicative perturbations are more successful than additive perturbations (Reviewer 2).  
- Different INR variants (SWAG vs. Laplace) are used in different experiments without stated rationale (Reviewer 2).  
- The influence of INR network size on performance is only briefly studied (Figure 1 for CIFAR-10), with the surprising result that a 350-parameter network outperforms larger 4k and 10k versions. Error bars are missing for UCI datasets, and analysis on MLPs is absent (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- The trade-off between distribution expressiveness and inference accuracy in subspace approaches is not discussed; it is unclear why an INR subspace is preferable to simpler alternatives like making only the last layers probabilistic (Reviewer 3).  
- (Minor) Typographical errors: line on page 3 (“wf”) and Eq. (5) subscript (Reviewer 4).  
- Reviewer 1 would like more empirical evidence to differentiate the method from prior hierarchical weight models like Dusenberry et al.
