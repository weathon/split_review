## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an accelerated stochastic second-order method (and a tensor generalization) that is robust to both gradient and Hessian inexactness. The method achieves the first optimal convergence rates in this setting, matching derived lower bounds. Extensions cover tensor methods and a restart scheme for strongly convex functions. Experimental results confirm superiority over Extra-Newton and SGD.

### Strengths
- Addresses a valuable and naturally arising question: optimal handling of gradient and Hessian inexactness in stochastic second-order methods.
- The paper follows a clear, layered progression from problem setup to lower bounds, algorithm design, and extensions (tensor methods, strongly convex case).
- Proof techniques appear solid and sound.
- Under the assumption of unbiased gradients, the paper establishes a lower bound on convergence rate for inexact Hessian & gradient, and proposes a method that achieves this lower bound except for the last term.
- A relatively thorough analysis is provided, extending to tensor methods and the strongly convex setting.
- The flow of introduction and problem setup is good.
- Table 1 shows a faster convergence rate than state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Agafonov et al., Extra-Newton, SGD).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of intuitive explanation:** The rationale behind the algorithm’s design and parameter choices (e.g., $\alpha_t$, the model $\omega_x^{M,\bar{\delta}}$, the estimating sequence technique) is not clearly motivated. Several reviewers (R1, R3) request more intuition.  
  - R1 asks: why is the additional quadratic term $\frac{\bar{\delta}}{2}\|x-y\|^2$ included in the subproblem?  
  - R1 asks for insights on the aggregation of stochastic linear models in Algorithm 1.  
  - R3 notes that parameters’ impact on performance and convergence is not explained.  
- **Practical implementation concerns (R2, score 1 — a strongly negative minority opinion):** Step 4 of the algorithm involves solving another optimization problem, whose computational overhead, complexity, and feasibility are not addressed. This raises serious doubts about practical efficiency and scalability. The same reviewer finds no strengths.  
  - R2’s question: how does step 4 work in practice?  
- **Comparison issues:**  
  - Table 1 compares to Agafonov et al.’s rate, but that baseline does not assume unbiased gradients while the proposed method does; this may make the comparison unfair.  
  - R3 asks: is the proposed algorithm better than existing methods in terms of computational time? (not addressed)  
- **Assumption nesting and clarity (R1):** Assumptions 2.2 (stochasticity) and 2.3 (inexactness) are highly related and lead to similar convergence rates, but their high-level relationship is not clearly set forth.  
- **Missing citations and references:**  
  - Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 5.2 appear similar to lemmas in Agafonov et al. 2020 but are not cited there.  
  - R4 notes: *\citet* is wrongly used instead of *\citep* in many places.  
- **Missing local convergence analysis:** R1 asks whether the method enjoys superlinear local convergence (as typical for accelerated cubic Newton type methods) and whether this is worth investigating under stochastic/in-exact settings.  
- **Unclear or vague descriptions:**  
  - Section 4 (“Lower Bound”) has a generic title and a vague first sentence.  
  - Function $\psi_t(x)$ above Algorithm 1 is not defined (though appears in the algorithm).  
  - Section 7 (page 8) notation $f(x)=E[f(x,\xi)]$ is an abuse of notation.  
  - The definition of $\Phi_x$ at the beginning of Section 2 and under Assumption 5.1 may be unused.  
- **Minor presentation issues:**  
  - Page 2 line 2: “$O(1/T^2)$” should be “$\Omega(1/T^2)$”.  
  - “bounded variance stochastic Hessian” should be “stochastic Hessian with bounded variance”.  
  - Formatting issues in algorithm list (sequence numbers).  
  - Figures 1 and 2 are too small (R3, R4).  
  - A large gap in the appendix after equation (26).  
- **Open question about trade-off and practical relevance (R1, R4):**  
  - R1 asks: what is the optimal trade-off between inexactness in gradients and Hessians? The paper only implicitly investigates this.  
  - R4 asks: does it make sense to analyze Hessian inexactness when gradient inexactness dominates the rate as $T\to\infty$ (the $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ term)?  
- **Restart mechanism (R1):** The specific mechanism and necessity of restart in Algorithm 3 are not elaborated; when should a restart be performed?  
- **Precision strategy (R4):** How was the dynamic strategy for the precision level $\tau_t = c / t^{3/2}$ derived?  
- **Contributions 1 and 3 (R4):** Could be integrated for conciseness.
