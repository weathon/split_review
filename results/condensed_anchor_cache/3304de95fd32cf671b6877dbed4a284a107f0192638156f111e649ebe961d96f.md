## Merged Review

### Summary
SteerDiff proposes a lightweight adapter module that sits between user input and the diffusion model, identifying unsafe concepts in the text embedding space via an MLP classifier trained on unsafe-safe prompt pairs (constructed using an LLM) and then applying a linear transformation (with steering parameter ϵ) to shift those embeddings toward safe regions. The method is evaluated on SD1.4 using I2P and COCO-30K, and tested against red-teaming attacks (P4D, SneakyPrompt). Reviewers are divided: two (R1, R2) rate 3 and criticize clarity, novelty, and experimental depth; two (R3, R4) rate 5 and highlight effectiveness and robustness while also raising concerns about generalization, theoretical grounding, and missing baselines.

### Strengths
- **Practical significance and timeliness** (R1, R3, R4). The problem of generating inappropriate content from T2I models is important.
- **Lightweight and efficient** (R2, R4). SteerDiff does not require intensive computation or full model fine-tuning; it operates as an intermediary module.
- **Effective removal of NSFW content** (R2, R3, R4). Experiments on I2P and COCO-30K show reduced inappropriate generation while maintaining image quality (FID reported) and preserving intended semantics for safe prompts.
- **Robustness against adversarial attacks** (R4). Under white-box attack P4D and black-box attack SneakyPrompt, SteerDiff achieves low attack success rates. (R3 noted that other attacks such as Ring-A-Bell, MMA, POSI were not discussed.)
- **Good visualizations** (R3). The paper includes clear generated image examples.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing, formatting, and clarity** (R1, R2). Specific issues: Equation in Sec 2.1 is an unconditional diffusion equation, not Stable Diffusion; parameter W is introduced in Eq 3 but defined only in Eq 4; Figure 1 does not explain the meaning of blue blocks; Figure 2(a) uses trapezoidal frames for Text Encoder and Diffusion Model, which can be mistaken for U-Net; Figure 2(b) does not differentiate blue and red points; text embeddings are denoted as ‘E’ instead of the more standard ‘e’; the lower part of Figure 3 lacks explanation. These issues make the paper hard to follow.
- **Limited technical novelty** (R1, R2). R1 states that the approach is similar to prior works on guiding models via vector shifts (citing Schramowski et al. “Safe Latent Diffusion” and Yang et al. “MMA-Diffusion”). R2 notes the paper focuses on optimizing prompt embeddings without adequate technical depth. R3 also observes strong similarity to Latent Guard (Liu et al.), which uses LLM-constructed unsafe-safe pairs and detection in embedding space, and questions whether steering malicious prompts is genuinely superior to rejection—suggesting that the transformation mechanism could introduce new vulnerabilities. (R3 and R4 rated the paper higher overall, but the novelty concern is shared across reviewers.)
- **Insufficient experimental details and missing ablations** (R1, R2, R3, R4). Specific points:
  - The MLP identifier is critical but its structure, training details, and accuracy are not reported (R1). Why was an MLP chosen? Would fine-tuning a larger model improve results? (R1’s question).
  - Figure 5 (concept forgetting) only shows qualitative examples; quantitative results (e.g., accuracy, FID for removed concepts) are needed to support the claim that other content is unaffected (R1).
  - The paper does not specify which classifier (e.g., NudeNet) was used for NSFW detection and why only five metrics are reported (R2).
  - For the Erased Stable Diffusion (ESD) baseline, the version (ESD-u or ESD-x) is not specified, and results on other NSFW detection tasks from ESD are missing (R2).
  - On COCO-30K (safe prompts), SteerDiff achieves better FID than baseline SD1.4. Since the method is not supposed to rewrite safe prompts, this improvement needs explanation (R2).
- **Limited scope of evaluated models, concepts, and baselines** (R3, R4).
  - Experiments are conducted only on SD1.4; application to SDXL is not attempted (R4).
  - Only harmful content removal is considered, not style or copyright unlearning (R4 notes that ESD includes such experiments).
  - Comparison baselines are limited. Missing methods include SPM (CVPR’24), AdvUnlearn, and RECE (ECCV’24) (R4). For adversarial attacks, Ring-A-Bell, MMA, and POSI should be discussed (R3).
- **Concerns about generalization and theoretical grounding** (R2, R3).
  - The LLM is used to enumerate unsafe and safe terms. If the list is not exhaustive, the classifier may fail on unfamiliar concepts (R2). Would retraining the identifier and matrix W be required for new unsafe concepts? (R3)
  - Safe prompts can still produce inappropriate content due to ambiguous wording; the method does not address this (R2).
  - Treating unsafe/safe terms as direct opposites using antonyms risks altering prompt semantics (R2). Why not let the LLM rewrite the entire prompt directly? (R2)
  - No theoretical proof is given that unsafe embeddings can be transformed into safe ones via a linear transformation (R3).
  - The steering parameter ϵ controls intensity, but it is fixed. If ϵ is increased, does the probability of inappropriate content decrease? Might a learned optimal steering degree offer more precise control? (R3)
- **Missing human evaluation** (R4). Quantitative metrics alone do not fully capture image quality or harmfulness reduction; human assessment would strengthen the claims.
