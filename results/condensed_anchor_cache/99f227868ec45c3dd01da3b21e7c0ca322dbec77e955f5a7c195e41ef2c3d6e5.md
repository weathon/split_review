## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a prompt-tuning approach using adaptive inclusive tokens to mitigate stereotypical biases (gender, race, age) in text-to-image (T2I) models, without requiring explicit attribute specification or prior bias knowledge. The method employs a lightweight adaptive mapping network and an anchor loss. Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty and importance of addressing bias, but express mixed opinions on the method’s effectiveness and raise substantial concerns about experimental validation, metric validity, theoretical foundation, implementation details, and practical trade-offs between fairness and factual accuracy.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem for AI ethics and fairness by targeting gender, race, and age biases in T2I models (R1, R3).
- Introduces a novel approach using adaptive inclusive tokens that does not require explicit attribute specification or prior knowledge of bias distribution (R1, R2, R3).
- The method is model-agnostic and simple in design, potentially easy to adopt into existing systems (R1).
- Extensive experiments evaluate effectiveness across multiple attributes (R1).
- The approach generalizes to unseen concepts and can handle multiple attributes simultaneously (R2, R3).
- The paper is well-written and clearly structured (R2, R4).
- Learning an adaptive mapping network to customize tokens for specific occupations is reasonable and justified (R4).
- Experimental results demonstrate improvement over previous bias mitigation techniques in scenarios without attribute specification (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited effectiveness compared to baselines**: Quantitative results (Table 1) and qualitative examples show no significant improvement over the FM method. One reviewer questions whether the model-agnostic nature can actually enhance other methods (R1). Another notes that qualitative examples in the supplementary material still generate predominantly single-gender images, creating inconsistency with quantitative claims and casting doubt on overall effectiveness (R3). In contrast, one reviewer finds the experiments effective (R4).
- **Conflict between fairness and factual accuracy**: The method may introduce factual errors for non-neutral concepts (e.g., generating a female U.S. president), but the paper does not discuss or evaluate this trade-off between accuracy and fairness (R2).
- **Lack of theoretical foundation**: The adaptive mapping network and anchor loss lack clear motivation and theoretical discussion, making it difficult to understand the underlying principles (R2). Another reviewer notes the approach is straightforward (training to shift biased concepts to less ambiguous representations) and questions whether such a simple method can be truly effective (R3).
- **Implementation details are vague**: The specific structure and training process of the adaptive mapping network are insufficiently described; the provided figures are unclear and fail to convey the authors’ intentions (R2). The ablation study (Table 2) is confusing: the setting without the adaptive mapping network is not explained, nor how the anchor loss (Eq. 2) is calculated without it (R1). Experimental settings for Figure 1(a) are missing (R1).
- **Training time contradicts “lightweight” claim**: The training time of nearly one hour raises concerns about practicality and efficiency, especially for rapid deployment or real-time adjustments (R3).
- **Metric validity concerns**:
  - The CLIP zero-shot classifier used for evaluation may itself be trained on biased data, affecting the accuracy of attribute classification (R1).
  - The D_kl metric shows SD1.5 outperforming SD2.1 and SDXL (SD1.5 > SD2.1 > SDXL), which is inconsistent with qualitative results, suggesting the metric may be problematic (R3).
  - The reported FID scores are 200–300, whereas typical FID values are on the order of 10^1; this may indicate insufficient statistical sampling, and ImageReward or PickScore might be fairer metrics here (R4).
- **Dependence on the text encoder**: The method focuses on text-encoder biases and does not examine biases inherent to the diffusion model itself. If the text encoder is biased or manipulated (e.g., through adversarial attacks), the mitigation may become ineffective. The paper does not explore different text encoders, limiting generalizability (R4).
- **Anchor loss requires human pre-determination**: The loss needs predefined non-dominant bias attributes for each occupation in the training data, necessitating human input and potentially introducing its own biases (R4).
- **Limited experimental scope**: The setup is limited in complex scenarios and across different datasets, restricting understanding of robustness and applicability (R2). The impact of prompts, models, and data volume on the final model is not adequately addressed (R2). Lack of discussion on practical application value or how to ensure both fairness and factual accuracy in real-world use (R2).
- **Unexplained token performance**: Why a single token performs better than multiple tokens (which have higher complexity) is not explained (R3).
