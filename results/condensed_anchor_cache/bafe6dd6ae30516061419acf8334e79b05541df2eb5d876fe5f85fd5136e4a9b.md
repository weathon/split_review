## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a principled approach to assess the interpretability properties of Capsule Networks (CapsNets), focusing on the encoding of part‑whole relationships. The authors conduct a systematic investigation using multiple capsule‑based architectures on MNIST, SVHN, PASCAL‑part, and CelebA datasets. Their findings suggest that the representations learned by CapsNets may not be as disentangled or as strictly related to part‑whole relationships as commonly claimed in the literature. The work is presented as one of the first attempts to perform such an analysis and challenges prevailing assumptions about CapsNet interpretability.

### Strengths
- The paper conducts a systematic and principled study to evaluate interpretability in CapsNets, with a clear motivation and a sound setting.  
- The analysis uses multiple datasets (MNIST, SVHN, PASCAL‑part, CelebA) and various capsule‑based architectures, providing a comprehensive evaluation.  
- The proposed permutation‑based analysis and relevant unit selection are reasonable, and the results support the paper’s conclusions.  
- To the authors’ knowledge, this is one of the first attempts towards analyzing CapsNets regarding disentanglement and part‑whole relationships.  
- The approach introduces an appropriate interval for perturbation analysis via first‑order statistics and considers a variety of standard benchmark architectures and different settings.

### Weaknesses
- The experiments are mostly conducted on small‑scale, low‑resolution datasets (MNIST, SVHN, PASCAL‑part, CelebA), which limits the generalizability of the findings about interpretability. One reviewer (score 5, confidence 3) particularly stresses that this makes the results unconvincing and the visual differences between baselines non‑obvious; the other reviewers (score 6) also agree that larger, more complex datasets (e.g., ImageNet, datasets with occlusions or many classes) should be considered.  
- There is a question about whether the conclusions hold for transformer‑based networks, as all experiments are based on ConvNet‑like architectures. (Raised by the minority reviewer, score 5).  
- The discussion of related work references mostly works from before 2020; many recent capsule network publications are not covered. (Raised by the minority reviewer, score 5).  
- Notation inconsistencies and missing definitions hamper readability:  
  - \(A_c = [a_{1,c}^l, a_{2,c}^l, \dots, a_{s,c}^l]\) is introduced without specifying the layer superscript, and \(S'\) and \(A'\) are never defined (is \(S'\) the set of examples for class \(c\)? is \(A' = w\times h\times d\)?).  
  - The first‑order statistics are said to be in \(\mathbb{R}^{S'\times A'}\), but the dimensionality should change after a reduction.  
  - There is a typo \(A_1^M\) in \(A_{all} = [A_1;A_2;\dots ; A_1^M]\) (likely should be \(A_M\)).  
  - The matrix \(A_{all}\) is layer‑specific but omits the \(l\) superscript that appears later in the \(\alpha\) definition.  
- Concern about perturbation magnitude: a large change in a vector entry (relative to the original magnitudes) can lead to misleading decoding results, and it is not clear whether the current formulation captures the subtle differences in activations between examples.  
- Visualizations are not sufficiently clear:  
  - Fig. 2 lacks a description of what the colors represent.  
  - Fig. 4 caption does not explain legends such as \(D_0\!-\!D_9\) (presumably the digit classes).  
  - Fig. 6 needs a more detailed caption to avoid requiring the reader to repeatedly consult the text.  
- The Relevance Mass Accuracy (RMA) metric is not clearly justified as a proxy for measuring part‑whole relationships. It measures spatial overlap between ground‑truth masks and a single‑channel heatmap, but how this relates to heatmaps from spatially rearranged capsule responses is unclear. The statement that “the mean of RMA is lower than anticipated” is not justified with a prior expectation, and the authors themselves admit that the low overlap may have other sources. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) considers this analysis inadequate and calls for further investigation.  
- The analysis does not explore behavior on datasets with more complex object hierarchies, occlusions, or a larger number of classes (e.g., ImageNet), raising concerns about the scalability of the observed interpretability properties.
