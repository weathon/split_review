## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores integrating mixture-of-experts (MoE) into Vision Transformers (ViT), introducing ViMoE. It studies image classification (and semantic segmentation, though experiments focus on classification) using ImageNet1k (one reviewer mistakenly states only CIFAR). The authors find that MoE layer configuration heavily affects performance due to unreliable routing and propose adding a shared expert to stabilize training. They also analyze expert routing behavior to prune redundant layers for efficiency. While the paper is clearly written and offers a thorough ablation study, reviewers raise major concerns: (1) confounding design choice of per-image expert assignment invalidates key conclusions about optimal layer placement; (2) limited novelty and unclear contributions (shared expert is not new, no comparison with state-of-the-art soft MoE); (3) unfair and incomplete comparisons (should mark as DINOv2+ViMoE, compare with fine-tuning methods like LoRA, VPT); (4) limited evaluation scope (only one dataset, only classification task, no practical VLM/LLM scenarios); (5) marginal performance gains and lack of theoretical depth.

### Strengths
- Clear writing, easy to read, well-structured (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Addresses an important research question: optimal MoE configuration in ViTs (Reviewer 4).
- Simple method (replacing FFNs with sparse MoE layers and fine-tuning) improves classification performance over DINOv2 (Reviewer 2).
- Provides a relatively thorough ablation study on number/placement of MoE layers, expert sharing, and routing strategy (Reviewers 2,4).
- Visualizes and evaluates expert loadings and assignments, enabling systematic study (Reviewer 4).
- ImageNet1k is a suitable large-scale diverse dataset for MoE study, with ground-truth labels to quantify routing choices (Reviewer 4).
- Sufficient analysis overall (Reviewer 3); easy to implement (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Confounding design choice (per-image expert assignment)**  
   - The major conclusion that early MoE layers hurt performance is derived from experiments where expert assignment is shared across *all tokens in an image* (image-level). This is problematic: shallow features are not class-separable, so forcing image-level assignments at early layers is inherently ineffective. Existing work (e.g., Oldfield et al. NeurIPS 2024, Mustafa et al. NeurIPS 2022) uses per-token assignment and finds useful specialization to low-level patterns. The authors’ small study in Table 4 is insufficient; a larger ablation without the image-level constraint is needed to validate whether early MoEs are truly detrimental. Without this control, the paper’s core conclusions are not supported (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
   - The authors acknowledge on L080 that early layers cannot route images of the same class to the same expert, which is expected—so the design choice itself creates the studied problem (Reviewer 4).

2. **Unclear contributions and limited novelty**  
   - The shared expert is not a new contribution; it simply confirms previous works’ ideas (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).  
   - The overall technical novelty is limited: adding sparse MoE to pre-trained ViTs via fine-tuning is straightforward (Reviewers 2,3).  
   - The paper fails to clarify what novel insights it adds beyond existing knowledge, e.g., whether the pruning/analysis results are unique to this paper (Reviewer 4).  
   - No comparison with the current published SOTA for ViT MoEs: “From Sparse to Soft Mixtures of Experts” (Puigcerver et al., ICLR 2024). The authors must discuss how their design relates (can they be combined? is one dominant?) (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).

3. **Unfair and incomplete comparisons**  
   - The method should be labeled as DINOv2+ViMoE, not presented as a standalone new method in Figure 1 and Table 2 (Reviewer 2).  
   - Other reported methods should be ensured to undergo 200-epoch fine-tuning on ImageNet, and results of ViMoE applied to other backbones (ViMoE+X) should be provided (Reviewer 2).  
   - Missing comparisons with other fine-tuning/adaptation techniques: e.g., SupCon, VPT, LoRA (Reviewer 2).  
   - Missing comparison with feature clustering visualizations to demonstrate how the model organizes visual features (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).

4. **Limited evaluation scope**  
   - Experiments are restricted to image classification only on ImageNet1k (Reviewers 2,3). One reviewer asserts the dataset scale is limited to CIFAR, which other reviewers refute—nonetheless, the evaluation lacks diversity (Reviewer 1, confidence 3 vs. Reviewer 2/3/4 indicating ImageNet).  
   - No validation in practical application scenarios such as VLM or LLM domains, where MoE is most prominent (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).  
   - Request to test on harder/more diverse datasets: SUN, Places (Reviewer 3, confidence 5) or additional generalization tasks (Reviewer 2).  
   - Reviewer 3 also notes that ImageNet1k has many bad annotations; better datasets are suggested.

5. **Marginal performance improvement and lack of theoretical depth**  
   - The performance gains achieved by the proposed scheme are extremely marginal (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).  
   - The paper lacks in-depth theoretical or intuitive analysis to justify design decisions (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).  
   - Missing technical details on the shared expert (e.g., L160 says more details needed) (Reviewer 2, confidence 4).

6. **Minor issues**  
   - L414: formatting error – “3e^{-4}” should be written as $3\times10^{-4}$ or 3e-4 (Reviewer 2).  
   - L044: “subsets” should be “subset”? (Reviewer 4).
