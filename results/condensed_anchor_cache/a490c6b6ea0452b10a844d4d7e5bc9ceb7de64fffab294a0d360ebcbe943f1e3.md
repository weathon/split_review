## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces M^3PC, a test-time Model Predictive Control method built on top of a pretrained bidirectional trajectory model (BTM) trained with masked autoencoding. By varying mask patterns, the BTM acts as both a policy and a world model. At inference M^3PC uses MPC with uncertainty-aware action sampling to guide decisions without further training. Experiments on D4RL (simulated motion control) and RoboMimic (real robot manipulation) demonstrate improved performance in offline RL, offline-to-online RL, and goal-reaching tasks. Two reviewers rated the paper 8 (high confidence); two gave 6 (moderate confidence), indicating a split on novelty and thoroughness without detracting from the experimental evidence.

### Strengths

- **Novel framework** combining a pretrained Masked Trajectory Model with test-time MPC. Action reconstruction with uncertainty awareness improves robustness under stochastic settings. The four masking strategies (forward, backward, policy, goal-reaching) are cleverly designed to enable multiple functionalities from a single unified model.
- **Thorough evaluations** across simulated benchmarks (D4RL, RoboMimic) and a real robot manipulation task, covering offline, offline-to-online, and goal-conditioned settings. The method shows substantial improvement over DT, BTM, and ODT.
- **Ablation studies** validate key design choices (e.g., unified vs. separated models, utility function variants).
- **Clear presentation** with informative figures; the paper is well-written and easy to follow. Reviewer 4 noted nice figures; Reviewer 3 praised the simplicity and conceptual foundation.

### Weaknesses

- **Computational cost and real-time feasibility.** Multiple reviewers request a detailed analysis of the overhead introduced by MPC at each interaction step, including time for forward/backward prediction and scaling with sequence length, model size, and planning horizon. Reviewer 4 specifically asks to report computation time and add test-time planning baselines to Table 1. Given that only the first action of each plan is executed, this bottleneck could be significant in real-time applications. (*Severity: high; confidence: moderate*)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.** 
  - No direct comparison with Trajectory Transformer (TT), which also performs sequence-based planning (with return heuristics). Such a comparison would clarify relative performance and efficiency, especially since both use sequence models for decision-making. (Reviewer 1)
  - Missing model-based offline planning methods: MBOP, MOPP, GOPlan. MOPP and GOPlan also handle uncertainty by pruning trajectories. (Reviewer 4)
  - For offline-to-online, Reviewer 3 suggests including or replacing IQL with a conservative Q-learning approach such as Cal-QL for better context.
- **Long-horizon goal-reaching limitations.** When the number of required steps exceeds the planning horizon/time budget (e.g., AntMaze-Large), backward MPC may fail. The paper does not discuss strategies like hierarchical planning or iterative refinement. Reviewer 1 asks for quantitative results on such long-horizon tasks. (Reviewer 1, echoed by Reviewer 2)
- **Novelty concerns.** Some design choices are reminiscent of Dreamer, MTM, TT, UniMASK, and MaskDP. The paper lacks a detailed comparison highlighting the unique contributions: e.g., how M^3PC’s sampling process differs from TT’s, how the [PI] mask differs from MaskDP's goal-reaching mask, and how the return heuristics relate to prior work. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4: "fewer novel contributions")
- **Clarity and detail gaps.**
  - Figure 7: which task is shown? The legend is unclear; methods (a)–(d) should be linked to curves. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
  - Acronyms MPC-M and MPC-Q appear without prior introduction. (Reviewer 3)
  - Table 3 missing standard deviations. (Reviewer 3)
  - Typo in Figure 2 caption: "show have" -> "show/have". (Reviewer 4)
  - Line 294: reference to Figure 2 should be Figure 3(b). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 1)
  - Goal-reaching: in the main text, it seems only final position is needed, but appendix uses entity goal trajectories; clarify. (Reviewer 3)
  - Shaded areas in reward curves are not explained. (Reviewer 3)
  - How are actions selected from inverse dynamics during backward planning: sampling or mean of Gaussian head? (Reviewer 3)
  - Motivation for trajectory-level entropy constraint (Eq. 2) is unclear; it may push out-of-distribution actions in offline RL; ablate without it. (Reviewer 4)
- **Ablation improvements.** The unified model outperforms separate models, but a comparison with a well-known world model like RSSM would strengthen the claim. (Reviewer 4)
