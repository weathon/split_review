## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Bender's Oracle Optimization (BOO), which frames Bender's decomposition as a sequential decision-making process and uses RL to generate Bender's cuts. The method aims to provide safety and feasibility guarantees during training and deployment under the assumption of access to a (possibly inaccurate) representation of the feasible set. Empirical results show a 2% optimality gap and ~800–850× speedup on a nonconvex packing problem relative to a global MINLP solver, and a 17% improvement on a stochastic job-shop scheduling problem compared to an MILP solver. While the combination is novel, reviewers disagree on its positioning: some see it as a safe RL contribution, others argue it belongs to the learning-to-optimize literature. One reviewer (score 5) found the idea excellent and impactful; the other four (score 3) considered the contribution incremental, the experiments insufficient, and the theoretical claims unsupported.

### Strengths
- Novel framing of Bender's decomposition as a sequential decision-making problem, where RL is used to efficiently generate cuts (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). This is seen as a clever and potentially impactful idea, particularly for safe RL and hierarchical RL (Reviewer 3).
- Provides a general framework for handling constraints in RL by enforcing hard constraints during training and inference (Reviewer 1).
- Achieves significant empirical improvements: a 2% optimality gap and 850× speedup (abstract: 800×) compared to a global MINLP solver on a nonconvex, nonlinear packing problem; 17% improvement over an MILP solver on a stochastic job-shop scheduling problem (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The ability to handle stochasticity in scheduling is a notable strength (Reviewer 2).
- Two computational examples illustrate the potential benefits of the method (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Mismatch with safe RL framing / missing related work and baselines**: The paper is framed as safe RL, but the problems solved are offline optimization; claims about avoiding catastrophic failures during training/execution seem out of place (Reviewer 2). No comparisons are made with learning-to-optimize approaches (e.g., Lan et al. 2023) or with standard safe RL methods such as CPO, action masking, safety-critics, safety projection (e.g., control barrier functions) (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). The related work focuses on safe RL but omits the learning-to-optimize literature entirely, making it hard to gauge the contribution (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees despite "provably safe" title**: The paper contains no proofs of safety (regret bounds, sample complexity, convergence). The safety guarantee relies on the (strong) assumption that constraints are known and enforced at each iteration, not on any property of the proposed method (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). One reviewer notes that this assumption is a strong prerequisite and makes comparisons with model-free approaches like CPO unfair (Reviewer 5). Another reviewer questions how newly added cuts could cause infeasibility in sub- or master problems (Reviewer 5).
- **Incremental contribution**: The application of Bender's decomposition to constrained MDPs is straightforward; modifications to existing methods are not clearly stated, and all modules appear off-the-shelf (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Missing ablation and comparisons**: No comparison is provided against pure Bender's decomposition without RL, so the contribution of RL to the reported improvements is unclear (Reviewer 2). No comparison with existing learning-to-optimize methods that also use RL (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Incomplete discussion of assumptions and limitations**: The method considers only optimality cuts, not feasibility cuts (line 170), and only linear constraints (line 213) – the paper claims broader applicability but does not justify it (Reviewer 2). Access to a constrained world model is a strong assumption; scenarios where such a model is available are not discussed (Reviewer 1). The authors mention real-world applications like autonomous driving but provide no evidence of performance on safe RL benchmarks (Reviewer 3). It is also unclear whether the problem becomes an MDP or POMDP after decomposition (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental validation**: Only two problems are considered; more diverse environments and standard safe RL benchmarks are needed (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). The reward vs. global maximum difference at the start of training requires more interpretation (Reviewer 3). No statistics over seeds are reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of implementation details**: The training algorithm, inference step, and details of the GNN policy (how state $s$ is input, message passing, output of weights $\omega$ and bias $b$) are not sufficiently elaborated (Reviewers 1, 5). Typesetting errors: Equations (8) have flipped variables compared to (5)–(7); $y^*$ in lines 185–186 is misformatted; line 196 is missing $\mathcal{A}\in$; Figure 2 x-axis label “$5.4\times10^4$” is unclear (Reviewer 2).
