## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Low-Rank Quantization Adaptation (LoQA), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for quantized LLMs, building on QA-LoRA. LoQA comprises two components: (1) Holistic Quantization Low-Rank Adaptation (HQ-LoRA), which reparameterizes and fine-tunes both the quantization scale and zero-point parameters for broader optimization while preserving fully quantized inference, and (2) Quantized Bit-Aware Scaling (QBAS), which adjusts the LoRA scaling factor based on the target bit-width (e.g., $maxq = 2^{N-1}$). The method is tested on LLaMA models at 4/3/2-bit precision, achieving consistent performance gains over QA-LoRA, QLoRA, and IR-QLoRA, especially in ultra-low-bit regimes. The authors claim broad applicability to post-training quantization (PTQ) techniques, but only GPTQ is evaluated. All five reviewers rated the paper 5, reflecting incremental contributions and notable but limited experiments.

### Strengths
- **Better representation power than QA-LoRA**: Tuning both scale and zero points (vs. only zero points) gives greater flexibility and consistently improves generalization across models and bit budgets (R1, R3, R4, R5).
- **Strong performance at low bit-widths**: LoQA outperforms previous methods at 2–4 bits, and is competitive with IR-QLoRA in the 4-bit regime (R1, R2, R3).
- **Inference efficiency**: Unlike QLoRA, LoQA does not retain 16-bit LoRA parameters during inference, enabling fully quantized inference (R1, R3). However, no actual speed or memory benchmarks are reported (R3).
- **Simplicity and effectiveness**: The method is straightforward, well-motivated, and yields promising empirical results (R4, R5).
- **Code provided**: Source code is available for reproducibility (R5).
- **Addresses deployment relevance**: Quantization-aware tuning is a key challenge for LLM deployment (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty**: The work is a direct extension of QA-LoRA (R1, R3, R4, R5). The core idea (reparameterizing both scale and zero-point) is similar to QA-LoRA but with an extra LoRA component (R5). Two LoRA variants are used compared to QA-LoRA’s single variant, raising concerns that improvements may come from higher parameter count (R1, R5). The formulation in Sec 3.2 is unclear (e.g., “two LoRA variants” on line 249) (R3, R5).
- **Poor presentation and experimental clarity**:
  - Experiment details are underspecified (R1). The LoRA rank used in compared methods is not reported (R1, R5). The “4+16 bit” setting is not formally defined (R5). “Post-fine-tuning” on line 269 is incomplete (R5). Naming is inconsistent (“Llama-7b” vs. “Llama-7B”) (R5). Table captions are not self-contained (R1).
  - Figure 2 is overly simplified and unclear: it lacks annotations for QA-LoRA and LoQA, and does not visually distinguish the two methods (R3, R4, R5). The concept of Holistic LoRA is not well conveyed (R4).
  - In tables (e.g., Tables 4, 7, 8), best results are not consistently bolded (R5).
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - Only the LLaMA model family is evaluated; LLaMA-3 results show only training loss, not test/validation performance (R3, R5). Improvements on LLaMA-2 are not clear (R5).
  - Important baselines (IR-QLoRA, QLoRA) are missing from Tables 5, 7, and 9 (R5). Only GPTQ is tested, despite claims of broad PTQ applicability (R5). The method only supports group-wise quantization (R4).
  - Evaluation is dominated by MMLU after fine-tuning on Alpaca/Flan; the only commonsense reasoning benchmark (Table 5) shows performance close to QA-LoRA, while LoQA uses ~2× training complexity (R1). Lack of newer datasets (e.g., SlimPajama) or domain-specific tasks (GSM8K, OASST1) limits generalizability (R2, R3).
  - No inference time/memory benchmarks are provided, despite fully quantized inference being a stated goal (R3, R4). Section 4.3 only mentions potential kernel optimizations (R3).
- **Technical concerns**:
  - The scaling hyperparameter $\alpha$ in QBAS: is it the same across bit-widths? (R1). The choice $maxq = 2^{N-1}$ in Sec 3.3 is not sufficiently justified (R5).
  - LoQA requires more training time and memory than baselines, and production deployment may involve significant complexity (R2).
