## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis for sub-domain alignment in unsupervised domain adaptation, establishing a generalization bound that is at least as strong as full-domain bounds. Based on this theory, the authors propose DARSA (Domain Adaptation via Rebalanced Sub-domain Alignment) to handle shifted marginal sub-domain weights. Experiments on Digits and TST benchmarks show effectiveness in label shift scenarios. Two reviewers rated the paper 6, one rated 5; opinions diverge on the novelty of the theory, the innovation of the algorithm, and the sufficiency of the experimental evaluation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and reader-friendly.
- It provides a theoretical foundation for sub-domain alignment methods, with a bound (Theorem 4.5) that is at least as strong as full-domain bounds (Theorem 4.10). The theory is considered thorough and insightful by some reviewers, and the method is clearly grounded in this theory.
- The DARSA algorithm is simple yet effective in addressing shifted marginal sub-domain weights, a problem that adversely impacts existing sub-domain methods.
- Empirical results on Digits and TST benchmarks demonstrate the method’s competitiveness in label shift scenarios.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical novelty and rigor**: Several reviewers question the novelty of the theoretical analysis. The bound in Theorem 4.5 is seen as similar to existing bounds (e.g., [1,2]), offering little new perspective. The analysis in Theorem 4.10 overlooks the impact of finite sample sizes within sub-domains (which have fewer samples than full domains), potentially overstating the benefits of sub-domain alignment. One reviewer (score 5) rates soundness as 2 (fair), while the other two rate it 3 (good), indicating disagreement on technical confidence.
- **Connection to prior sub-domain methods**: The paper does not clearly link its theoretical framework and algorithm to existing sub-domain alignment works. It lacks explicit discussion of the limitations of prior sub-domain methods and how the proposed work enhances their understanding or robustness, both in theory and experiments.
- **Algorithmic novelty**: One reviewer notes that the practical application simply uses class labels to segment subdomains, essentially merging class importance weighting with the W1 distance. The method is considered not exceptionally innovative, and the paper does not explore alternative sub-domain segmentation strategies.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
  - Only two datasets (Digits and TST) are used, which is too limited to demonstrate generalizability. Reviewers recommend experiments on large-scale datasets.
  - Most compared UDA baselines were published before 2020; more recent SOTA methods should be included. Notably, no baselines specifically designed for label shift (e.g., the method in [1]) are compared, while many baselines (e.g., DANN, ADDA) target covariate shift.
  - The paper lacks insightful analyses such as visualization of the subdomain rebalancing weights. Contribution ratings reflect this: two reviewers rate contribution as 2 (fair), one as 3 (good).
