## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the loss landscape of two-layer ReLU networks, analyzing activation regions defined by the activation patterns of ReLU neurons over training data. It focuses on differentiable critical points. Under mild overparameterization, it shows that most activation regions have the property that every differentiable critical point of the squared error loss (with nonzero second-layer weights) is a global minimum. For one-dimensional input data, stronger results are proved: most non-empty activation regions contain a high-dimensional set of global minima and no bad local minima. Experiments confirm a phase transition in Jacobian rank as overparameterization varies.

### Strengths
- The theoretical results are rigorous and provide a novel perspective on the optimization landscape, using geometric/combinatoric arguments and bounding the number of activation regions with no bad local minima. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The analysis is independent of the specific choice of initialization or dataset distribution. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper provides a rigorous definition for generic points using the fact that algebraic subsets of ℝⁿ have measure zero; this method may be useful in other areas. (Reviewer 2)
- The proof techniques, particularly the use of random sign matrices, appear new. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to digest. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Numerical experiments (Figures 2, 3) validate the theoretical findings. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Restrictive setting with fixed last-layer weights.** The analysis assumes the last layer weights *v* are fixed, unlike typical networks where all weights are optimized. It is unclear whether including *v* in the parameter space changes the conclusions of Theorem 5 (Reviewer 2), and this assumption may significantly alter the optimization landscape and limit practical applicability (Reviewer 1).
- **Only differentiable critical points are considered.** The paper ignores non-differentiable local minima, which are known to exist in generic deep ReLU networks with cross-entropy loss [1]. Laurent & von Brecht (2018) show for hinge loss that local minima can only occur at non-differentiable boundaries except flat regions (Reviewer 3). The paper's treatment of non-differentiable points is limited to the one-dimensional input case (Section 5); behavior in higher dimensions remains unaddressed. It is unclear whether such points are local minimizers and whether they cause gradient descent to get stuck (Reviewer 2). Thus, it remains uncertain whether "most of the landscape is favorable to optimization" (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of existence guarantees for critical points.** The theorems only state that in most activation regions, *if* differentiable critical points exist, they are global minima. They do not guarantee the existence of such points in those regions (Reviewer 3). A pathological scenario is possible: critical points do not exist in most regions (by volume), while the few bad ones that occupy negligible volume yet dominate parameter space contain bad local minima (Reviewers 2, 3). The paper's attempt to address existence (e.g., affine set of global minima) is limited to one-dimensional input (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited scope and scalability.** The results are restricted to a single-hidden-layer network where only the first layer *W* is trained; given an activation region, the prediction is linear in *W*, making the Jacobian constant and simplifying proofs (Reviewer 3). Extension to deeper networks would require the last hidden layer to be at least as wide as the number of data points *n*, a much stronger condition than the *n/d₀* requirement in Theorem 5 (Reviewer 3). Mild overparameterization is achieved only for one-dimensional input data; for higher-dimensional inputs the counting method appears intractable (Reviewer 4). Consequently, the applicability to practical deep architectures and high-dimensional settings is questionable (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Missing discussion of training implications.** The paper does not formalize how the property that most activation regions have no bad local minima translates into gradient descent's success (Reviewer 2). Furthermore, Figure 9(b) shows that for mildly overparameterized networks, gradient descent does *not* converge to good local minima despite the Jacobian being full rank at initialization, which may contradict the theoretical findings and requires explanation (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing relevant citations.** Some directly relevant works are omitted: [A, B, C] on existence of bad local minima; [D] and others on partitioning parameter space by sign of pre-activations (Reviewer 3). Also, the citation to Safran & Shamir (2016) should be corrected to Safran & Shamir (2018) (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear experimental details.** It is not specified how the Jacobian's full rank is determined, and the experimental setting for Figure 9 is not clearly described (Reviewer 1). The constants in the bounds are not discussed, nor whether they can be quantified in experiments (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and justification questions that constitute weaknesses:**
  - The logic around Corollary 8 is unclear: if under general position and *d > n* most activation regions are non-empty, why does the corollary still refer to "non-empty activation regions"? (Reviewer 4)
  - The paper should compare with Liu (2021), which appears to provide stronger results (Reviewer 4).
  - When *d₀ = 1*, Theorem 5 already provides strong results, so the motivation for deriving Theorem 10 is unclear (Reviewer 4).
  - The equivalence between a ReLU with one-dimensional input plus bias and a two-dimensional input without bias (Section 6) is not straightforward and requires additional explanation (Reviewer 2).

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 3) is the most critical, particularly emphasizing the limited scope, lack of existence guarantees, and missing citations; other reviewers (scores 6) view the contribution more positively but share several of these concerns.*
