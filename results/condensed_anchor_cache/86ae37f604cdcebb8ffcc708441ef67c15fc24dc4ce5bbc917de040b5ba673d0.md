## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel brain decoding framework combining surface vision transformers (SiTs) with tri-modal CLIP alignment (audio, video, fMRI) to generalize across subjects and unseen stimuli. Using 7T HCP movie-watching data from 174 subjects, it achieves up to 80% top-1 accuracy in retrieving stimuli from held-out subjects’ fMRI. Reviewers agree the multimodal alignment and cross-subject generalization are innovative and impactful, but raise significant concerns about missing comparisons, evaluation depth, clarity of results, limited dataset diversity, computational cost, and basic retrieval tasks vs. state-of-the-art reconstruction.

### Strengths
- **Novel tri-modal CLIP alignment**: Extends prior unimodal/bimodal approaches by jointly embedding fMRI, audio, and video in a shared space (R1, R2, R4). R2 notes visual reconstruction improves through tri-modal integration.
- **Generalization across unseen subjects and stimuli**: Demonstrated for held-out subjects and previously unseen movie clips, a crucial step for universal decoding (R1, R2, R4). R2 explicitly highlights evaluation on (i) unseen subjects, (ii) unseen stimuli, (iii) both combined.
- **Surface vision transformers (SiTs)**: Effectively capture long-range spatial structure of cortical surface topography, well-grounded and technically solid (R1, R3). R3 cites innovative combination of ViT on cortical patches with contrastive learning.
- **Attention map interpretability**: Reveals patterns linked to semantic and visual systems, providing insight into neural underpinnings (R2, R4).
- **Potential impact**: Applicable to BCI, neurofeedback, and personalized brain simulations (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison against alternative architectures (3D CNNs, etc.) (R1, R3). No quantitative comparison to state-of-the-art decoding/reconstruction methods (R1, R2). Ablation lacking to isolate contribution of ViT vs. CLIP (R3). Missing within-subject baselines and chance-level metrics to assess significance (R3). No comparison to other identification/classification approaches in the field (R3).
- **Evaluation depth and task simplicity**: Retrieval tests are easier than reconstruction; should discuss use of powerful generative models (R1). Decoding problem is essentially nearest-neighbor classification, a basic form (R3). Only tested on 3-second clips; longer temporal dynamics not captured (R2). Fixed 6-second hemodynamic lag ignores regional variability (R2).
- **Dataset and stimulus limitations**: Only validated on HCP 7T movie-watching data; no evaluation on other datasets or more diverse stimuli (abstract, silent films, non-narrative) (R3, R4). Generalizability within same dataset vs. broader population unclear (R4). Limited diversity in movie stimuli used for training (R2).
- **Results interpretation and reporting issues**: Table 1 confusing – “inter vs. intra movie” columns need clarification (R3). Counterintuitive higher inter-movie accuracy demands explanation (R3, R4). High variance (e.g., fMRI→audio: 70.9±20.3, fMRI→video: 80.3±18.0) not explained (R4). Figure 4 captions unclear; brain regions not labeled (Broca’s, auditory cortex, etc.) and maps not quantitatively validated against known functional networks (Yeo) (R2, R4). No plots with uncertainty estimates (R3). Cryptic writing with excessive notation; grammatical/syntactical errors (R3). Poor figure quality (Fig. 1) (R3). Ambiguous use of “movie” vs. “video” (R3). Main results (Table 1) conflated with ablation studies (R3). vsMAE pre-training benefit not explained (R4). Sine-cosine positional embeddings compared to learned alternatives not evaluated (R4).
- **Computational and practical concerns**: High resource requirements (multiple A100 GPUs) (R2, R4). Self-attention mechanisms limit scalability to higher resolution data (R4). Subcortical structures (e.g., LGN) not included despite sensory role (R4). Computation time not reported (R4).
- **Missing discussion**: Ethical/privacy implications of reconstructing visual experiences from brain data not addressed (R2).
- **Originality hard to evaluate**: Need better situating relative to prior works (Dahan et al., Tong et al., Feichtenhofer et al.) (R3).
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with weaknesses**: Why not use full test set for retrieval? Set size affects performance (R1). Biological plausibility of wav2vec vs. cochleograms/spectrograms (R1). Constraints of 3-second windows on capturing longer cognitive processes (R2). Application to another dataset recommended (R3). How to handle subcortical noise with CIFTI format? (R4).
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: One reviewer (R2, score 8) is notably more positive, emphasizing strong interpretability and reconstruction improvement, but still acknowledges the above weaknesses. Other reviewers (R1, R3, R4, all score 6) are more critical, particularly about missing comparisons, evaluation rigor, and clarity.
