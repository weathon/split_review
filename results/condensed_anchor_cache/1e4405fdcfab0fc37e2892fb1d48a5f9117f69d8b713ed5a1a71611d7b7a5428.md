## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SP-Mamba, a multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework combining Mamba with self-predictive representation learning for decentralized policy optimization. The method uses an MLP-VAE to encode observations, a Mamba model to process sequential latent states, and a self-predictive loss that predicts future encoder outputs (rather than reconstructing observations) to improve feature learning. Experiments are conducted on six SMAC tasks, showing improvements over RNN-based policies and a naive Mamba baseline. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the ablations thoughtful, but raise major concerns about limited evaluation scope, baseline performance not matching prior literature, missing comparisons (especially Multi-Agent Transformer and direct MAPPO), and insufficient justification for claims of substantiality, scalability, and generality.

### Strengths
- The combination of Mamba with self-predictive RL is a novel approach to stabilizing Mamba in MARL and is well-motivated and well-placed in the literature (Reviewer 1, 2).
- The self-predictive loss focuses on reward maximization rather than reconstruction, which is an insightful departure from typical world model training (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Ablation studies are thorough and well-considered—e.g., comparing loss functions (showing reconstruction can harm performance), different VAE architectures (MLP, categorical, SimNorm), and combination of objectives (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- The paper is well-organized and generally easy to follow; error bars are included in results (Reviewer 1, 3).
- The idea of letting the decoder predict the next encoder output (instead of reconstructing raw observations) is interesting and worth further investigation (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope**: The method is evaluated on only 6 tasks from a single environment suite (SMAC v1). This is not “substantial” as claimed; it is average or below for a MARL paper, and multiple reviewers note that SMAC v1 is saturated, overfit, and known to be trivial (e.g., near 100% win rates achievable by MAPPO alone). No experiments on SMAC v2, which corrects these issues, are included. Only 4 seeds are used, whereas recommended practice is at least 10 seeds (Reviewer 2, 3; citing Gorsane et al. 2022, Ellis et al. 2024, Agarwal et al. 2021).
- **Baseline performance discrepancies**: The reported win rates for baselines (MAPPO, QMIX, RODE) are significantly lower than those documented in prior work (e.g., Yu et al. 2022, Rashid et al. 2020). Specifically, in 4 of 6 tasks, SP-Mamba’s performance does not match MAPPO’s reported performance in the original paper (which the code extends), and in 2 tasks the results overlap with MAPPO. The QMIX baselines are also worse than those in Yu et al. 2022. This undermines the claim that SP-Mamba outperforms strong baselines (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- **Missing key comparisons**: 
  - Multi-Agent Transformer (MAT, Wen et al. 2022) is cited as state-of-the-art but not used as a baseline, even though it is a transformer-based policy similar to Mamba and has been tested on the same SMAC tasks, where it significantly outperforms the results reported here (Reviewer 2).
  - Additional baselines (FT-QMIX, Qplex, MAPPO directly) are needed to strengthen evaluation. Since SP-Mamba builds on MAPPO, a direct comparison with MAPPO under identical settings is critical (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of sample efficiency reporting**: Win rate is reported only against training time, not number of environment steps. Without the latter, it is impossible to assess sample efficiency, which is crucial in RL. The caption of Table 1 references runtime data in Appendix B, but that data was not found by Reviewer 1 (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Unclear experimental settings and hyperparameter choices**: Training steps and total training time are not clearly specified. The paper claims lack of hyperparameter tuning as a strength, but it is not reasonable to expect hyperparameters that work for SP-Mamba to also work well for MAPPO/PPO. No empirical evidence or justification for hyperparameter choices (e.g., for VAEs and the Mamba model) is provided (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Claims not supported by evidence**: 
  - “Substantial experiments” – only 6 tasks from one suite, contradicting the claim (Reviewer 2).
  - “Scalable and efficient” – no analysis of scalability (number of agents, timesteps, model size) or complexity (Reviewer 2).
  - “Complex multi-agent environments” – not demonstrated; tasks are from a suite considered saturated and insufficiently challenging (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Technical concerns and missing details**:
  - Why is the critic trained with a GRU instead of Mamba? This is not discussed despite the paper being about replacing GRU with Mamba (Reviewer 2).
  - Why maintain a hidden state *and* cache a sequence of observations for inference? Does the cached sequence impact memory requirements? (Reviewer 3)
  - When shuffling all data in the buffer into a single batch, is the time ordering within sequences maintained? (Reviewer 3)
  - The choice of VAE hyperparameters (e.g., why 32×32 categorical variables and not smaller sizes?) and why “a” appears instead of “u” on line 92 (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper should discuss or compare with S5, an SSM used for partially observable single-agent RL (Reviewer 2).
  - Question Q3 (line 301–302) is vague: “effective in terms of what metrics and compared to which baseline?” (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty of VAE component**: Using an MLP as the VAE is not considered novel (Reviewer 3).
