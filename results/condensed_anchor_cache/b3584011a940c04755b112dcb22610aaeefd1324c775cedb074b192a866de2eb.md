## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces HHD-Ethiopic, a new OCR dataset of ~80,000 annotated text-line images from 18th–20th century historical handwritten Ethiopic manuscripts. Two test splits are provided: an IID set (~6,000 images) and an OOD set (~16,000 images from 18th century documents only). Human-level recognition performance is measured (multiple annotators, expert-supervised) and compared with state-of-the-art OCR models (CRNN, ASTER, SVTR, CTC-based, attention-based, transformer-based) using CER and NED. The best model (HPopt-Attn-CTC) outperforms humans on the IID set and performs comparably on the OOD set. The dataset and code are publicly available. Reviewers are split: two rate the paper 5 (valuable dataset, good baselines), and two rate it 3 (limited methodological contribution, insufficient experiments, missing comparisons). All reviewers acknowledge the value of the new dataset for an under-resourced script.

### Strengths
- Presents a large, publicly available dataset (HHD-Ethiopic) for historical handwritten Ethiopic script, a low-resource and under-studied domain. This is valuable for research inclusion and archival processing. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- The dataset includes two test splits (IID and OOD), which provides a more realistic evaluation scenario. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Human-level performance is established as a baseline, with multiple annotators and expert supervision. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- Baseline evaluations of several SOTA handwriting recognition models (CRNN, ASTER, SVTR, HPopt-CTC, HPopt-Attn-CTC, HPopt-Transformer) are provided. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Code is available to reproduce experiments. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)
- The high human error rate makes the dataset challenging and potentially useful for assisting archivists. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **No new technical method or algorithmic contribution.** The paper is purely a dataset paper without proposing any novel recognition approach or adaptation to the specific challenges of Ethiopic script. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Incomplete and potentially misleading baselines.** Several models (CRNN, ASTER, SVTR) were not trained to convergence due to resource constraints, making their poor performance confusing and undermining their value as baselines. (Reviewer 1, Appendix C)
- **Error in Table 2 and Figure 5.** The table incorrectly labels Test-set-II (OOD) as Annot-VI instead of the correct designation; as a result, the claim that HPopt-Attn-CTC surpasses human performance on Test-set-II is wrong. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparisons with existing resources and models.**
  - No reference to the Transkribus Ethiopic model (https://readcoop.eu/model/ethiopic-classical-ethiopic-scripts-from-ethiopia-and-eritrea/). (Reviewer 3)
  - No mention or test of Tesseract, which supports Ethiopic and could be a simple baseline since the script is not cursive. (Reviewer 3)
  - No reference to the article at https://journals.openedition.org/jtei/4109. (Reviewer 3)
  - No comparison with standard HTR frameworks (Transkribus, pylaia, trOCR) or with prior models evaluated on other Ethiopic datasets (e.g., Abdurhaman et al. report <2% CER on their dataset). (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The paper claims in Section 2 that no sizable Ethiopic dataset exists, yet Appendix B lists existing datasets; these should be discussed in the main text. (Reviewer 1)
- **No full-page images provided.** This prevents evaluation of end-to-end full-page models, which are currently the best-performing approach compared to line detection + HTR cascades. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient analysis of data and annotation quality.**
  - No inter-annotator agreement measure is reported, despite the high human error rates (over 26% CER) raising questions about ground truth reliability. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - No details on manuscript provenance, selection criteria, or number of manuscripts. (Reviewer 3)
  - No analysis of whether humans and models make similar or different errors. (Reviewer 1)
  - Human performance was measured without access to reference material; it is unclear how this compares to reading with resources. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing metrics.** Recognition accuracy (a standard metric in OCR) is not used; only CER and NED are reported. (Reviewer 2)
- **Risk of writer/doc leakage.** The paper does not specify whether the same writer or document appears in both training and IID test sets. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor issues.** The format of citations needs fixing; the "remark" column in Table 1 is unnecessary; grammar error in Sec. 3.1 ("we have generate"); CTC, attention, and transformers are incorrectly listed in parallel (they are loss, mechanism, model respectively). (Reviewer 1)
- **The academic challenge of the dataset is not clearly demonstrated**, and the potential impact of the benchmark seems limited without a new solution. (Reviewer 4)
