## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses stability and faithfulness issues in Label-free Concept Bottleneck Models (LCBMs) by introducing Faithful Vision-Language Concept (FVLC) models. The authors formalize four properties for faithful concepts (overlap with ground truth, robustness to noise, correctness of predictions, stability of output layer) and incorporate them into a min-max objective with an algorithm to optimize it. Experiments on four vision-language datasets show improved stability over LCBM baselines with minimal accuracy loss. Reviewers generally agree the problem is important and timely, but differ on presentation clarity, completeness of related work, and experimental rigor.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important and timely problem of instability and unfaithfulness in concept-based explanations, particularly for label-free CBMs (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Provides a formal, clear definition of the faithful concept bottleneck model and four critical properties, with a well-defined objective function incorporating stability conditions (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Conducts extensive experiments with numerical stability comparisons across multiple datasets, including ablation studies showing all terms are needed (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- The method is simple and effective, outperforming PCBM and LCBM baselines in stability and accuracy; paves the way for language-based CBMs with faithfulness guarantees (Reviewers 1,4).
- The effect of input perturbations on concept explanations is fairly unexplored, making this a novel contribution (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation clarity**: One reviewer found the writing poor and hard to follow (Reviewer 2), while others found it generally clear but with room for improvement (Reviewers 1,3,4). Specific issues:
  - The flow of data through the network (image encoder → concept layer → classifier) is unclear; need a walkthrough with a concrete example (Reviewer 1).
  - Key terms like “concept bottleneck” and “stability conditions” should be introduced and defined in one place early in the section (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 2’s objective is unclear; the noise (black/white dots) is not explained in the caption (Reviewers 2,3).
  - Figure 3 text is too tiny to read concepts (Reviewer 3).
  - The notation \(\tilde c\) vs \(c\) and maps \(\tilde g\) vs \(g\) is confusing; concepts should be written as \(g(x)\) to show input-dependence (Reviewer 4).
  - The mathematical formulations of stability conditions need better motivation and explanation (Reviewer 1).
  - In Section 3 (Label-free CBM), “we propose” should be replaced with “they propose” since the method is by Oikarinen et al. (Reviewer 2).
  - The statement that \(\tilde c = c\) when \(\alpha_1 \to 0\) is not always true; it depends on the loss function chosen (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete related work and baselines**: The related work misses several important papers, including Concept Embedding Models (Zarlenga et al., NeurIPS 2022), Interpretable Neural-Symbolic Concept Reasoning (Barberio et al., ICML 2023), Entropy-based Logic Explanations (Barberio et al., AAAI 2021), Addressing Leakage in CBMs (Havasi et al., NeurIPS 2022), Route, Interpret, Repeat (Ghosh et al., ICML 2023), Distilling BlackBox to Interpretable (Ghosh et al., MICCAI 2023), Language in a Bottle/LaBo (Yang et al., CVPR 2023), and A Framework for Learning Ante-hoc Explainable Models via Concepts (Sarkar et al., CVPR 2022) (Reviewer 2).
  - Only PCBM and LCBM baselines are used; comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods like Post-hoc CBMs (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023) and LaBo (Yang et al., CVPR 2023) are needed (Reviewers 2,4).
  - For datasets with ground-truth concepts, basic CBM and other methods should be compared (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental details and dataset choices**:
  - Table 1 lacks variances, best/highlighted values, and best configuration of the proposed method (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 2 has no bold/highlighted numbers, making it hard to see which are higher (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing ablation study with various perturbations and configurations (Reviewer 2).
  - The image model used for “Standard (No interpretability)” in Table 1 is not stated (Reviewer 1).
  - Harder medical datasets (e.g., HAM10k, MIMIC-CXR) should be tested to strengthen claims about trustworthiness (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalizability**: The method is only evaluated on vision data; applicability to NLP or tabular classification tasks is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Definition and evaluation of faithfulness/interpretability**:
  - The difference between “faithful” and “original” concept in Definition 1 is not explained (Reviewer 2).
  - The claim that matching top-\(k\) components of \(\tilde c\) to \(c\) yields equivalent interpretability rests on the assumption that \(c\) is already interpretable, which must be verified (e.g., via concept accuracy) even for LCBMs (Reviewer 4).
  - How interpretability of vision-language models is evaluated is not addressed (Reviewer 4, question).
- **Stability metric design**: The metric is not \(\epsilon\)-invariant; ideally perturbation \(\epsilon\) should be in the denominator to avoid counter-intuitive scaling. Similar metrics in the literature (e.g., Alvarez Melis & Jaakkola, NeurIPS 2018) put input perturbation in the denominator. The authors should justify their choice or reference alternative designs (Reviewer 3).
- **Optimization and loss function clarity**:
  - The terms in the loss function (page 6) are unclear: what is \(D\)? How do constraints \(\delta\) and \(\rho\) affect the loss and optimization? Their connection to Figure 2 is unresolved (Reviewer 2).
  - The alignment loss might have been done directly with CLIP-extracted concepts without first fitting an LCBM, which introduces noise (Reviewer 4).
- **Architecture choice**: The choice of text and image encoder architecture and its impact on accuracy should be explained (Reviewer 1).
