## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Robust Representation Consistency Model (rRCM), which reformulates denoising along diffusion PF-ODE trajectories from a generative task into a discriminative task in latent space. It uses instance discrimination to enforce consistent representations across time steps, enabling implicit one-step denoising-then-classification. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet claim state-of-the-art certified accuracy (e.g., 5.3% average improvement and up to 11.6% at larger radii on ImageNet) with greatly reduced inference cost (up to 85× average reduction). Reviewers diverge: two find the work novel and strong (scores 8), one rates it adequate (6), and one rates it weak (3), citing motivation, presentation, and fairness concerns.

### Strengths
- Novel integration of consistency models with robust representation learning: reformulating generative diffusion denoising into a discriminative consistency task in latent space, with a theoretical basis in PF-ODE trajectories.
- Thorough experimental evaluation against multiple state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Carlini et al. (2022), SmoothAdv, Gaussian baseline) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, reporting certified accuracy and inference time.
- Significant inference-time savings over diffusion-based randomized smoothing methods (e.g., 46× faster than Carlini et al. on ImageNet, average 85× across settings).
- Strong certified accuracy improvements, particularly at larger perturbation radii (e.g., 11.6% at r=0.5 on ImageNet, 5.3% average across radii).
- The approach is novel and could inspire future work in adversarial defense (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- One reviewer found the paper structure and writing well-organized and clear (Reviewer 2), though others raised clarity issues.

### Weaknesses
**Motivation and Conceptual Clarity**  
- The benefit of the diffusion-based formulation over simply training a classifier on noisy images is not clearly justified. If a classifier must be trained from scratch anyway, why not train it directly with noise augmentation? (Reviewer 1, echoed by Reviewer 2’s question about why consistency models can recover clean data.)  
- The purpose of the image generation experiment (Sec. 4.5) is unclear: a diffusion model trained on real images will produce realistic images regardless of the conditioning encoder’s quality. The paper does not explain what insight this experiment provides (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).  
- The method’s theoretical framing at t_N is problematic: at the final noise level the PF-ODE marginal is standard Gaussian, so forcing representations to be consistent at that step would remove all discriminative power (Reviewer 3).  

**Methodological and Presentation Gaps**  
- The interplay between the consistency loss and contrastive loss is not explained: no complete loss function formula is given, nor how the two losses are balanced (e.g., weighting coefficients). The similarity metric for instance discrimination and the negative sampling strategy are unspecified (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
- Algorithm 1 uses undefined variables: `z_0`, `z_1` (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4), and `z_2` (Reviewer 4). It is also unclear whether `mlp(g( , ))` refers to the linear head or the non-linear projector. The loss names `ct_loss` and `cnsis_loss` are not defined, and there is a typo (“cnsis” on next line) (Reviewer 3).  
- The role of the teacher model in the claimed teacher-student training is unclear: only EMA weights are used, which is a standard practice even in SimCLR and is not normally called knowledge distillation (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).  
- The claim that adding a trainable MLP is a data augmentation method and that it “ensures” meaningful representations is not supported by evidence or citation (Reviewer 4).  
- The paper cites SimCLR for statements it does not make (line 247-253) (Reviewer 3).  
- The method section lacks a dedicated subsection on fine-tuning (Reviewer 3).  
- Several writing issues: “Line 42”, “Line 305” incomplete sentences; Table 1 caption should be on top; figures 3,4,5 have “Classification accuracy” on y-axis but likely refer to certified accuracy; Figure 3 is never mentioned in the text; figure legends/dataset labels missing; line 466 typo (Figure 4 instead of Figure 3) (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).

**Experimental Fairness and Claims**  
- The 46× speed improvement is overclaimed: it holds only for a specific configuration; on average the speedup over Gaussian baseline or Carlini et al. is more modest (Reviewer 1).  
- Latency numbers are reported without specifying hardware configuration and batch size (Reviewer 3).  
- The Gaussian baseline’s certified accuracy at radius 0 is reported as 41% on ImageNet, which seems surprisingly low and raises questions about whether the comparison is fair (e.g., different resolution, architecture, or training details) (Reviewer 1).  
- MoCo-V3 accuracy in Figure 3 is significantly lower than the original paper’s results at radius 0; this raises concerns about whether the implementation or the comparison is correct (Reviewer 4).  
- In Figure 1, baseline methods use different model architectures (e.g., number of parameters), so it is unclear whether latency reductions are due to the proposed method, a smaller model, or a high-performance toolkit (Reviewer 4). The authors should report adversarial accuracy of baselines when using the same model as rRCM (Reviewer 4).  
- MoCo-V3 is compared in the appendix but not included in the main certified accuracy tables; it is unclear why this comparison is emphasized but omitted from primary results (Reviewer 3).  
- Improvements in certified accuracy (Table 1) are marginal in some settings, and a more thorough analysis across radii and datasets, including comparison with more recent consistency-model baselines, is needed (Reviewer 2).  

**Additional Reviewer Questions (included as weaknesses when overlapping with criticisms)**  
- Why can a consistency model recover perturbed data to the original? Explain the denoising capabilities of the model (Reviewer 2).  
- What is the upper limit of this method, and has it been reached? (Reviewer 2)  
- What are the “raw” classification accuracies of rRCM-B-Deep on ImageNet at various radii? (Reviewer 3)  
- Hardware configuration and batch size for latency numbers? (Reviewer 3)  
- Does the approach generalize easily to other models and datasets? (Reviewer 1)  

**Note on Presentation Improvement**  
- Reviewer 3 stated after rebuttal that most clarity concerns were addressed, suggesting the paper improved; however, other reviewers still raised unresolved clarity issues (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). The contrast between the positive assessment of one reviewer and the critical assessments of others is preserved.
