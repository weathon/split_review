## Merged Review

### Summary
RepCodec is a speech representation codec for semantic speech tokenization, using VQVAE to reconstruct speech representations from pretrained encoders like HuBERT or data2vec instead of raw audio. It outperforms k-means clustering in ASR and speech resynthesis across various encoders and languages, with comprehensive experiments demonstrating effectiveness.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-structured, and easy to understand. The motivation and description of the proposed method are very clear.
- The approach is simple yet effective, showing promising results in both ASR and unit-to-speech resynthesis compared to the clustering method (k-means).
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of RepCodec across different speech encoders and languages, affirming robustness.
- The authors analyze the issue with the quality measure of semantic tokens based on their similarity to ground truth phonemes, illustrating that the reconstruction loss of RepCodec exhibits a higher correlation with performance (intriguing discovery that PMNI can deviate from performance).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics and missing core downstream tasks**: Evaluation relies predominantly on WER. To convincingly show superiority, other metrics are needed: speaker similarity, F0 error, and mean-opinion score (MOS) in speech resynthesis. The lack of speaker similarity metrics makes it hard to assess speaker identity preservation, and absence of F0 error leaves prosody quality unclear; WER only measures content accuracy and neglects other aspects of speech quality. Experiments are limited to ASR and speech resynthesis; they lack empirical investigations into language modeling of audio, text-to-speech (including zero-shot TTS like Vall-E), speech-to-speech translation, or conditional modeling of acoustic tokens given semantic tokens. The absence of language modeling experiments makes it difficult to assess utility for capturing sequential dependencies, and omitted TTS experiments leave open the question of how well tokens can generate speech from text.
- **Novelty concerns**: Compared to SoundStream, RepCodec simply replaces the input from raw waveform with SSL representations, limiting novelty.
- **Need for comparison with SpeechTokenizer**: RepCodec shares similarities with SpeechTokenizer (SpeechTokenizer: Unified Speech Tokenizer for Speech Large Language Models). The differences should be elaborated, and adding baseline numbers using the SpeechTokenizer repo (https://github.com/ZhangXInFD/SpeechTokenizer) would add value.
- **Clarification needed on definitions and details**:
  - The term “semantic quality” in the introduction should be defined.
  - In Figure 1, the different bar heights in the encoder and decoder parts are confusing since neither sampling nor dimension reduction is applied; explanation needed.
  - Equation (5) lacks sufficient explanation; symbols (Ñ_k and e_i) are inadequately defined.
  - Equation (7) appears to be incorrect: it should be \( F^* = \arg\max_F p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{s}) = \arg\max_F \prod_{i=1}^{m} p(y_i|y_{<i}, \mathbf{s}) \).
- **Speaker timbre information in semantic tokens**: Since previous works (VALLE, AudioLM, PolySpeech) use k-means tokens, and for speech generation tasks (TTS, VC) speaker timbre can be harmful in semantic tokens, it would be valuable to verify whether encoded features retain speaker information. Speaker similarity is not evaluated.
- **Weight analysis for reconstruction and quantization loss**: A more in-depth analysis of the weights (\(\lambda_r, \lambda_q\)) would be beneficial.
- **Requests for clarification on specifics**:
  - The claim that k-means discards plenty of information needs concrete examples for readers to understand limitations.
  - In AudioLM, the statement “WER is dramatically increased from 2.5% to 6.0% by using discrete tokens of k-means from w2v-BERT XL” – clarify what the 2.5% baseline refers to.
  - Why does the performance gap change among different speech encoders after VQ and k-means quantization compared to original representations (especially Whisper)? I.e., what representations are suitable for clustering?
- **Lack of speech resynthesis samples**: Providing samples would help assess quality.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: Some reviewers found the paper strong with no significant weaknesses beyond suggestions for further validation, while others considered novelty limited and evaluation insufficient for the core claims. This contrast affects overall assessment.
