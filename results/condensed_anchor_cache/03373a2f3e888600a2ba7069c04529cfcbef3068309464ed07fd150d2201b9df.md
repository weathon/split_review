## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an LLM-based agent for long-form video understanding that uses query-adaptive frame sampling and self-reflective verbal reinforcement (reflexion-like) to dynamically select relevant frames and refine its reasoning. The framework includes a policy generator, planner, frame sampler, evaluator, refiner, and long-/short-term memory. Experiments are performed on EgoSchema, MovieChat, NextQA, and Ego4D-NLQ, reporting gains in accuracy while accessing fewer frames than prior methods like VideoAgent. Reviewer opinions diverge strongly: one reviewer (rating 8, confidence 4) finds the work technically sound and comprehensively evaluated, with only minor concerns; three reviewers (ratings 3, 3, 3; confidence 4-5) raise substantial issues about novelty, missing/fair comparisons, and the efficiency assumption; one reviewer (rating 5, confidence 5) sees some novelty but also notes insufficient baselines and overhead concerns.

### Strengths
- The manuscript is clear, well-written, and addresses a topic of interest to the community (R1).
- The proposed improvements over VideoAgent (reflexion-like self-debug, dynamic memory, new tools including a sampler) are technically sound and well-motivated (R1).
- Evaluation is comprehensive and impressive, with significant gains on challenging benchmarks and a useful metric on the number of frames needed (R1, R4, R5).
- The query-adaptive frame sampling method is novel and reduces computational overhead by dynamically selecting relevant frames (R3, R4).
- The dynamic memory idea is interesting and makes sense for long-form video QA (R2).
- The ablation study is useful (R2). The paper provides concrete examples on Ego4D, step-by-step reasoning traces, and full prompts for easier reproduction (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution articulation are unclear.** The paper does not clearly distinguish its novelty from existing works that use reasoning, memory, and tools (e.g., VideoAgent, MMCTAgent, VideoTree, DoremonGPT) (R2). The query-adaptive sampler’s uniqueness and how the LLM performs it efficiently are not explained (R2). Several reviewers consider the integration of multiple tools and memory to offer limited novelty (R2, R3).
- **Insufficient and unfair comparisons.** Missing comparisons with relevant recent methods: MMCTAgent (Kumar et al., 2024), VideoTree (Wang et al., 2024), and VideoAgent (Wang et al., 2024) – and for Table 7, VideoTree results are omitted even though described as stronger (R2). Also missing comparisons with Qwen2-VL, CogVLM2-Video, InternVL2 (R3). Baselines on NextQA and EgoSchema are insufficient (R4). The paper uses Claude-Sonnet while many baselines use GPT models, making comparisons unfair (R2). Some baseline results are incorrect – e.g., LifelongMemory’s EgoSchema score is on the full dataset, not the subset used (R5). LifelongMemory and LLoVi only have access to the caption tool, so comparisons are not controlled (R5).
- **Efficiency metric is questionable.** Accessing fewer frames does not guarantee efficiency because the agent requires costly reasoning from the planner and sampler; total input/output token count or wall-clock inference time should also be reported (R5). The dynamic memory may increase response time (R1). Scalability and latency with increasing video length/complexity are not analyzed (R3, R4).
- **Lack of code release.** No code or detailed reproduction instructions are provided, hampering verification and adoption (R3).
- **Narrow benchmarking.** Evaluations are limited to few benchmarks; missing diverse benchmarks like MVBench, Video-MME, and LVBench (R3).
- **Generalizability concerns.** If the toolkit must be pre-designed and coded, this limits the method’s generalizability to new tasks (R4).
- **Ablation design issue.** In Table 8, it is unclear how “evaluator only” or “refiner only” ablations are possible because the evaluator and refiner are both essential components of the reflexion framework (R1).
- **Clarify LLM choice.** The paper should elaborate on why Claude-Sonnet was chosen (R1).
- **Presentation issues.** Font size in almost all graphics is too small (R1).
- **Qualitative analysis is missing.** Common failure modes and frequencies of each tool being invoked are not discussed (R5). The example in Figure 3 appears counterintuitive – the sampler jumps from frame 7200 to frame 0, and at frame 7200 it uses ask_video_agent instead of image QA or object tracking to check for a chopping board (R5).
