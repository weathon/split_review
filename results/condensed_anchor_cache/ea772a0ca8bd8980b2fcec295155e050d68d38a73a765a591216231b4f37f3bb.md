## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes improving LLM reasoning at inference time by deriving a control vector from residual stream activations using contrastive prompt pairs (positive: correct reasoning; negative: incorrect outputs or random strings). PCA selects the dominant component, and the vector is added to the residual stream scaled by α. Experiments on inductive (IOI) and deductive (bAbI) reasoning with Pythia models (up to 7B) and Mistral-7B-Instruct show moderate accuracy gains (e.g., 8% on bAbI), alongside KL divergence and entropy analyses. The authors claim reasoning is controllable similarly to other information-processing tasks.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: Applying representation engineering / control vectors to improve reasoning is novel (R1, R2, R3, R4). It extends prior work on style/truthfulness modification to a cognitive capability, and it is “counterintuitive that reasoning is a direction in representation space” (R4).
- **Methodological clarity**: Steps for deriving control vectors are clearly explained (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The paper is “well-structured and self contained” (R4), with a “meticulous introduction” that reads as a mini‑tutorial (R5).
- **Comprehensive evaluation metrics**: Accuracy, KL divergence, and entropy together provide a multi‑faceted analysis (R1, R3, R4). The use of auxiliary metrics strengthens the assessment.
- **Significance of the problem**: The question whether reasoning requires distinct treatment from other LLM tasks is important (R1, R3). The claim that reasoning may be an emergent general processing ability is a useful contribution (R3).
- **Training‑free intervention**: The method improves performance without additional fine‑training (R4).
- **Specific result highlight**: An 8% improvement on bAbI over baseline is a validating result (R4).
- **Model‑scale analysis**: Testing across multiple Pythia scales and Mistral is acknowledged (R1), though see weaknesses.

### Weaknesses
1. **Task simplicity and generality of reasoning claims** (multiple reviewers, with strongest criticism from R5):
   - IOI and bAbI are too simple; IOI is “out of the question” for claiming broad reasoning, and bAbI are toy tasks known to be hackable by statistical regularities (R1, R2, R3, R5). R5 states: “The claim that this is about ‘Reasoning’ is too heavy for the experimental foundations.”
   - The paper’s conclusion that “there is no intrinsic difference between reasoning and other information‑processing tasks” is not substantiated: it either becomes a tautology or would require direct comparison with other tasks such as retrieval (R2). With only two simple tasks, extrapolation to a broad reasoning faculty is unsupported (R2, R5).
   - **Missing generality test**: To support claims about reasoning as a broad faculty, the authors should demonstrate that boosting bAbI/IOI performance transfers to other reasoning domains (e.g., GSM8K, MATH, ARC) (R1, R5). R5 argues that without such evidence, the technique only shows “a capacity to do bAbI” (or IOI), comparable to prior work improving chess/Othello by residual stream modification – undermining conceptual novelty.
   - R5 adds: the paper avoids characterizing what reasoning is (lines 38, 438), making the central claim hard to evaluate; philosophical commitments are unclear.

2. **Limited model scale and number**:
   - Only models up to 7B parameters tested; larger models (e.g., Pythia‑12B) may capture reasoning dynamics differently and should be included (R1, R2, R3). R2: “Very few models, very small models.”
   - Pythia results are reported only for IOI; bAbI results for Pythia‑1.4B and Pythia‑2.8B are missing (R1). R1 requests these experiments.

3. **Contrastive pair construction arbitrary and insufficiently analyzed** (R1, R3, R4, R5):
   - Using random 75‑character strings as negative samples is “arbitrary” and “unnatural” (R1, R3, R4).
   - The paper mentions three negative‑scheme options (incorrect answer, model‑mismatched output, random string) but does not explain how they were combined or compared in the experiments. An ablation study is needed (R4).
   - Without analyzing the types of errors reduced, it is unclear whether the intervention improves genuine reasoning or merely pushes the model toward distributional in‑vocabulary tokens (e.g., selecting a name from the story rather than the random string). Measuring probability of “natural incorrect” tokens (like the other name in IOI) would help (R4).
   - R1 suggests testing the contrastive approach on math tasks (GSM8K/MATH) where answer ambiguity is minimized, to separate reasoning from semantic correctness.

4. **Hyperparameter α selection not systematic** (R1, R2):
   - The choice of α dominates outcomes, but no general procedure is given for selecting α beforehand. A principled method for setting α is needed (R1, R2).
   - R2 asks: How can we find the best α in a new deployment situation?

5. **Effectiveness concerns from logit‑based accuracy results**:
   - Pythia‑2.8B shows a decrease for B and BL conditions and almost no improvement for A and AL conditions in logit‑based accuracy (R1). This raises doubts about the method’s robustness and suggests statistical‑learning bias rather than genuine reasoning improvement (R1). R1 considers this point a major concern.
   - Results are only slightly better for some α ranges (R2). The best improvements are moderate (R2, R4).

6. **Entropy and KL divergence results require clarification**:
   - Entropy is expected to decrease as the model concentrates probability mass on the correct token. However, for bAbI on Mistral (Figure 4), entropy appears positively correlated with probability mass (R4). The authors should explain this discrepancy and discuss the correlation (R4).
   - R3 notes that probability‑mass results “disagree a little” with entropy/KL divergence.
   - KL divergence is reported to rise linearly with α, but plots (Figures 3,4) show it rises with magnitude of α (i.e., quadratic in α) (R4). (R4 considers this a minor point.)

7. **Lack of comparison to baselines**:
   - No comparison to fine‑tuning or chain‑of‑thought prompting (R2, R3). A “fair baseline” would be fine‑tuning (R3). R2 asks whether similar reasoning cues could be provided contextually.
   - R2 also asks: why must contrastive pairs be prompt‑based rather than instance‑based (“good and bad reasoning instance”)?

8. **Control vector construction details missing**:
   - How are activations aggregated across tokens? The paper does not explain whether the last token is used or another design choice; pseudocode or a diagram would help (R4).
   - The evaluation from logits is not explained: how were correct/incorrect token probabilities selected? (R4)
   - R2 and R3 both question how to find the best α and whether performance is sensitive to layer choice (R3).

9. **Minor issues**:
   - “illustrated in 1” → “illustrated in Figure 1” (R2).
   - R5: The paper seems to want to be about a software framework but chose “reasoning” as more attractive; the software is not described despite being used.
