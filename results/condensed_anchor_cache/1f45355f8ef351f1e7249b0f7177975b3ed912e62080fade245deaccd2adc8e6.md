## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces VidEgoThink, a benchmark for evaluating egocentric video understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), motivated by Embodied AI. It builds on Ego4D and GPT-4o to generate data across four tasks: video question-answering, hierarchy planning, visual grounding, and reward modeling. Three human annotators filter the data. Experiments evaluate API-based (e.g., GPT-4o) and open-source image/video MLLMs. Key findings indicate that all current MLLMs perform poorly on these tasks, highlighting the need for further advancement in first-person vision for Embodied AI.

### Strengths
- **Timely and relevant topic** – Addressing MLLMs for Embodied AI is an important and under-explored area. The benchmark is built on real-world egocentric videos (Ego4D), providing a realistic evaluation environment.
- **Comprehensive task design aligned with Embodied AI** – The four tasks (video QA, hierarchical planning, visual grounding, reward modeling) cover a broad spectrum from scene understanding to low-level control, reflecting thoughtful consideration of embodied requirements. One reviewer (R4) emphasized this is the first benchmark to integrate all four tasks, showcasing deep understanding of low-level control needs.
- **Clear evaluation dimensions and metrics** – For each task, the paper defines specific sub-dimensions (e.g., object/action/scene for VQA, last-frame-based object grounding, reward modeling) and corresponding metrics, providing a usable assessment framework.
- **Efficient data construction** – The pipeline leverages existing Ego4D annotations and GPT-4o for label generation, combined with human filtering, enabling scalable benchmark creation.
- **Comprehensive model evaluation** – The paper tests 14 MLLMs, including API-based, open-source image-based, and video-based models, providing a broad view of current capabilities.
- **Reveals limitations and points to future research** – Consistently poor performance across all tasks highlights significant gaps in MLLMs for egocentric video understanding, offering valuable directions for improvement. Most reviewers found this insight useful, though one (R2) criticized the depth of analysis.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty in benchmark design** – Several reviewers noted that the tasks largely combine existing benchmarks: Video QA overlaps with EgoTaskQA and other egocentric QA frameworks; hierarchical planning borrows from prior goal-step parsing (Ego4D Goal-Step, ALFRED); visual grounding follows standard formats (object detection, temporal grounding). Two reviewers (R2, R3) questioned how the approach distinguishes itself from prior work that also processes Ego4D annotations with LLMs (e.g., NAQ, EgoSchema, [2,5,6]). Table 3’s “task types” are seen as primarily existing benchmarks, lacking persuasive evidence of novelty.
- **Data quality and scale concerns** –  
  - **Small video coverage** – Many tasks use fewer than 50 original Ego4D videos (out of 9,611 total). For example, the Object subtask in VQA covers only 29 videos across 9 scenes (~3 per scene); Object grounding yields fewer than 2 videos per scene for 25 scenes. This limits the benchmark’s usefulness for the research community (R1).  
  - **Insufficient quality analysis** – The human filtering process lacks detail: criteria, annotation instructions, percentage filtered, annotator background/salary (R5). The statistical analysis in the appendix only covers question/answer length and scene types, not task difficulty, activity density, or scene-level challenges (R4).  
  - **Reliance on GPT-4o raises fairness issues** – The same model (GPT-4o) is used to generate ground truth and (in open-ended evaluation) as a judge. This may bias evaluation toward language style similarity to GPT-4o rather than pure correctness, and it risks unfair comparisons with other models (R1, R5). Additionally, one reviewer (R1) noted that the implementation details (e.g., function g(·) in Eq.1) are unclear and may involve GPT-4o in the evaluation pipeline.
- **Misalignment with Embodied AI claims** –  
  - The evaluation presents complete videos and questions, unlike streaming processing typical of embodied agents (R3). Pre-recorded videos capture only one path for a goal, making planning evaluation biased, especially with few videos (R1).  
  - The title claims “for Embodied AI” but only human egocentric data is used; tasks are inspired by embodied AI but the benchmark remains an egocentric video understanding benchmark (R5).  
  - The introduction emphasizes relative positions to ego (e.g., “microwave to my right”), but example annotations (Figure 2) use absolute locations (e.g., “oven is in the kitchen”), contradicting the claimed motivation (R1).
- **Experimental analysis provides limited insight** – The analysis section is only one page, focusing mainly on GPT-4o without deep comparisons across other models. Missing are: performance variations under different conditions (e.g., video length, action density), which model architectures perform better/worse, and actionable future directions for MLLM design from an Embodied AI perspective (R2). One reviewer (R3) specifically asks for suggested enhancements to address the poor planning/grounding results and discussion of how improvements would translate to practice.
- **Ambiguities and contradictions in task design** –  
  - **Video QA dimensions** – The rationale for including object prediction but not action prediction is unclear (R3).  
  - **Mid-to-low planning task** – Several issues: (1) The task may be solvable with LLM knowledge alone; GPT-4o performs better with 8 frames than 32 frames, yet the “only-qa” and “captions” baselines show lower performance, creating inconsistency (R3). (2) The “function-like” format (verb-noun pairs) is described as closer to embodied AI but is essentially text rewriting; not all models follow the format, harming evaluation fairness (R5). (3) Near-zero accuracy for non-GPT-4o models may indicate flawed benchmark design or evaluation errors (R2, R5).  
  - **Object grounding prompt** – The prompt only says “Please give out the bounding box coordinates of the object,” lacking necessary context about the video or last frame (R5, citing L1231).  
  - **Sparse sampling undermines validity** – With average video length ~270.74 seconds (30 fps), using only 32 frames means sampling ~1 frame per 250 frames. This sparsity, combined with complex egocentric scenes (rapid camera motion, many objects), likely cripples MLLMs’ video understanding, making the poor performance less informative (R4).  
  - **Frame grounding metric** – MSE is listed as the metric but contradicts Equation 3 (R3).  
  - **Model selection omission** – Despite noting GPT-4o lacks video input, the paper does not evaluate API models with video input (e.g., Gemini 1.5) (R3).  
- **Reviewer disagreement** – While one reviewer (R4) praised the benchmark as robust and well-defined, two others (R3, R5) gave very low scores (3,3) citing limited novelty, insufficient analysis, and design flaws. Another (R2, score 5) found the benchmark thoughtful but the experimental analysis too shallow. The positive reviewer’s (R4, score 6) viewpoint is represented in the strengths above but contrasted with the critical points that raise fundamental concerns about the benchmark’s validity and impact.
