## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a scalable, simulation-free algorithm (SF-EUOT) for solving the Entropic Unbalanced Optimal Transport (EUOT) problem. The authors derive a dynamic formulation of EUOT, connect it to the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) problem and stochastic optimal control (SOC), and obtain a dual formulation with optimality conditions including a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) constraint. The method alternates (or jointly) optimizes a value function network V and a one-step generator T_θ, avoiding SDE/ODE simulations during training and requiring only one neural function evaluation (NFE) at inference. Experiments are conducted on 2D toy data, Gaussian benchmarks, CIFAR-10 image generation (Table 1), and unpaired image-to-image translation on CelebA Male→Female and Wild→Cat (Table 2, Figure 8). The results show improved performance over previous SB/EOT solvers and competitive results with diffusion/flow models, though not state-of-the-art among recent generative models.

### Strengths
1. **Novel dual formulation and theoretical connections** – The paper derives a dynamic dual of EUOT and relates it to SB, SOC, and action matching, providing a principled framework that unifies these problems. The coordinate descent between the value function and distribution dynamics (including an HJB regularizer) is novel and has not been successfully optimized in a PINN-like fashion for OT before.
2. **Simulation-free and one-step generation** – Thanks to the reciprocal property and a static generator parameterization, SF-EUOT avoids SDE/ODE simulations during training and requires only 1 NFE for sampling, a significant advantage over multi-step SB methods. It is the first EOT model that does not require pretraining.
3. **Good empirical performance on image tasks** – On CIFAR-10, SF-EUOT achieves competitive FID compared to diffusion/flow models (e.g., 2.97 from Choi et al., 2024, is noted as a related baseline) with only one inference step. On image-to-image translation, it outperforms OT-based counterparts (e.g., NOT, DSBM) in terms of FID, and beats CycleGAN/DiscoGAN.
4. **Sound and well-connected theory** – The mathematical derivations are accurate and clearly connect to broader OT and SOC theory, with the problem clearly stated as dynamic EUOT.

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity of the dual formulation (Eq. 23 vs. Eq. 25) and role of the HJB constraint** – Reviewers found it unclear why the dual (Eq. 25) depends on the entire time-dependent V_t when the primal (Eq. 23) only involves V_1. It is not explained how the optimal control u* is implicitly captured, nor why time-dependent V is necessary. Additionally, the impact of the HJB regularizer (Eq. 26) when V is not optimal is not well-defined; the behavior of the algorithm under non-optimal V and its effect on convergence requires clarification.
2. **Optimization procedure and algorithm details** – The paper simultaneously updates V and T_θ (lines 229–230), but the motivation and order of updates are not specified. Reviewers questioned whether an alternating scheme is intended and whether simultaneous updates are stable. The full procedure is not clearly described; an explicit algorithm pseudocode is needed. Reviewer 4 also noted a potential bug in line 366 (discrete-time approximation of the time derivative instead of partial derivative).
3. **Limited comparisons and outdated baselines** – In Table 1, the diffusion baselines are from 2021–2022 and are now outperformed by one-step distillation methods (e.g., CTM, GDD with FID ~1.6–1.7). In image-to-image translation (Table 2), the comparisons are limited: missing newer one-step GANs (CUT, StarGAN-v2, ITTR) and other SB methods (e.g., NSB). The Wild→Cat experiment omits DiscoGAN, CycleGAN, and NOT that were used in Male→Female. Reviewers requested more consistent and modern baselines.
4. **Empirical effectiveness and mode collapse concerns** – Figure 3 shows that SF-EUOT does not fully recover a precise optimal transport plan. On Table 3, SF-EUOT performs slightly worse than some benchmarks on synthetic datasets. Figure 8 (Wild→Cat) exhibits significant mode collapse. Only FID is reported; reviewers emphasized the need for a faithfulness/similarity metric (e.g., LPIPS, MSE) to assess the tradeoff between realism and transport cost.
5. **Theoretical novelty is incremental** – Adding an f-divergence penalty to relax marginal constraints (from EOT to EUOT) is a standard technique. The paper does not provide a strong argument for why this specific choice is a significant advancement beyond existing unbalanced OT methods.
6. **Missing baselines, references, and experiments** – Missing baseline: Choi et al. (2024) solving UOT with FID 2.97 on CIFAR-10 should be compared. Missing references for generative modeling with unbalanced OT (Lübeck et al., 2022; Dao et al., 2023). The claim that there is no closed-form for EUOT is incorrect: Janati et al. (2020) provide closed forms for unbalanced Gaussians, so the Gaussian experiment (Sec. 5.2) should also be run in the unbalanced case. Robustness to outliers is not discussed or tested.
7. **Terminology and presentation issues** – The term “stochastic map” is inaccurate; “conditional probability” or “probability kernel” would be better. Notation switches between u_t(x) and u(t,x). Abbreviations (NFE, IPF, IMF, HJB) are not defined upon first use. Figure 3 legends are too small. Many equations in Sec. 4.1 could be moved to the appendix. Incorrect citation: (Jordan et al., 1998) is not about generative modeling. Typos: “Dynamcial” (line 175), “Conducted same benchmark” (line 513).
8. **Computational cost and scalability** – The Laplace operator (via Hutchinson trace estimator) requires Hessian-vector products, which is more expensive than Jacobian-vector products. The paper claims superior scalability (line 458) but does not provide any comparison of computational cost (e.g., training time, model size) or converge guarantees. The term NFE is not defined or explained in relation to the method. The method was not tested in the ODE scenario (σ=0), which could reduce training cost.
9. **Figure 3 shows deterministic maps** – Despite training a stochastic map, only one-to-one mappings are visualized; it is unclear whether the generator is deterministic or if stochasticity is not displayed.

*Note: A minority of reviewers (R4) were more critical of the baselines and mode collapse, while others (R1, R5) were more positive about the method’s novelty and performance. Overall, the weaknesses reflect a consensus on missing comparisons, clarity issues, and the need for a more rigorous evaluation.*
