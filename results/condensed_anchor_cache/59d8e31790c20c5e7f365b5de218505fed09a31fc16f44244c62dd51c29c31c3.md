## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes iFormer, a family of mobile hybrid vision networks combining convolutional and transformer blocks. It evolves from ConvNeXt by lightweighting the architecture and introducing Single-Head Modulated Attention (SHMA) to replace multi-head attention in later stages. Experiments on ImageNet-1k, COCO, and ADE20K show iFormer achieves strong accuracy-latency trade-offs (e.g., 80.4% top-1 at 1.10 ms on iPhone 13) and outperforms recent mobile networks like MobileNetV4. Five reviewers rated the paper (four scores of 6, one score of 8). The majority are positive about the clarity and comprehensiveness, but raise concerns about novelty, design justification, ablation evidence, and missing comparisons. The high-scoring reviewer (R5, score 8) is notably more enthusiastic, praising the novelty of SHMA and the mobile optimization techniques.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4). The logic of starting from ConvNeXt, lightweighting it, then exploring attention is reasonable (R3).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluations on ImageNet-1k, COCO object detection/instance segmentation, and ADE20K semantic segmentation are thorough (R1, R3, R4). Knowledge distillation results are also reported, which is suitable for mobile networks (R3).
- **Main contribution SHMA**: SHMA provides a new approach to designing efficient attention and transformer blocks, outperforming SOTA mobile baselines with stronger performance and lower latency (R1). R5 specifically highlights SHMA as novel, including the single-head modulation mechanism, parallel feature extraction branch for dynamic information exchange, and redesigned reshaping operations to minimize memory costs.
- **Practical mobile design**: A series of novel techniques, such as stacked overlapping convolutions in early layers (instead of non-overlapping patches) and four-stage structure with fast conv in early stages and self-attention in low-resolution stages, are considered well-motivated for mobile latency (R5). The focus on latency (not FLOPs/params) is appropriate (R5).
- **Supporting analysis**: The analysis of cosine similarity between multiples supports the use of single-head attention (R3). R5 notes the model achieves >80% top-1 accuracy with 1.1ms latency on iPhone 13, a great contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Single-head self-attention already used in SHViT; alternative self-attention in GhostNetV2; modulation in token mixer used in Conv2Former. The paper references these methods but does not sufficiently differentiate its contribution, impacting novelty (R3). R5, however, considers the combined SHMA design as a novel contribution (disagreement).
- **Design justification and ablation gaps**:
  - The motivation for substituting half the conv blocks at stage 3 and all blocks at stage 4 with transformer blocks is unclear. In Figure 2, this change yields only 0.4% accuracy gain but adds 0.12 ms (≈10% slower) latency (R1). An ablation study on choice of stages or different conv/transformer ratios is needed.
  - The evolution from ConvNeXt to lightweight iFormer may not generalize to larger models, and some steps show very minimal improvements (R3). The largest model (iFormer-L ~15M) is small; no results for larger iFormer (≈100M) are provided (R3, question).
- **Missing evidence for speed claims**:
  - R1 questions the claim that extra reshaping operations in MHA are the main cause of slower inference vs. SHA. Other factors (split/concat operations, reduced self-attention channels) also affect runtime. No ablation or empirical evidence isolates the impact of reshaping alone. Also, the SHA definition in Fig. 4 (full-channel QKV projection) does not align with the SHA in SHViT; clarification is needed (R1). R5 asks for runtime complexity (e.g., FLOPS) and peak memory consumption on iPhone (R5).
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - Comparison with NAS methods (e.g., MobileNetV3 via NAS) is not included (R4).
  - Performance on other mobile devices (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Nano, Raspberry Pi) is not reported (R4). R5 also asks about battery life at certain FPS (R5).
  - Some relevant studies are not discussed in related work or comparisons (R2 cites [1,2] unnamed). Specifically, iFormer-S has same latency as RepViT-M1.0 with fewer params, but larger variants have lower latency with substantially more params – reason not explained (R2).
- **Missing experimental detail**: R1 asks for performance and latency of MHA as a missing intermediate step between “kernel sz.” and “SHA” in Figure 2.
