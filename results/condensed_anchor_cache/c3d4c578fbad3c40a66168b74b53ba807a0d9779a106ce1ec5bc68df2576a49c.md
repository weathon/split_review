## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a two-phase framework (IHAC) that combines hierarchical reinforcement learning with large language models. Phase I uses an LLM to guide high-level option selection via imitation learning with an annealing strategy, gradually reducing reliance on the LLM. Phase II applies a standard RL algorithm (PPO) to refine the policy. The method is evaluated on MiniGrid, NetHack, and Crafter, and it outperforms baselines (LLMxHRL, LLM4Teach) in both performance and token efficiency.

### Strengths
- Addresses a key problem: using LLMs as intrinsic reward generators to enhance RL sample efficiency (R1, R4, R2).
- The algorithm is straightforward, with clear writing and experiments supporting the claims (R1).
- Comprehensive evaluation across MiniGrid, NetHack, and Crafter; consistent improvements over baselines in performance and token efficiency (R1, R2, R4).
- Well-motivated: the LLM policy is informative for both exploration and exploitation via policy mixing and KL regularization (R4).
- Novel idea: converting common knowledge from LLMs into options to guide sparse-reward policy learning (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and technical contribution.** One reviewer (score 3) argues the work has nothing to do with hierarchical RL—high-level actions are pre-defined, the agent does not learn low-level control, and the setting degenerates into standard single-layer RL (R3). Another reviewer (score 6) notes that grounding an LLM as a policy planner in HRL and using regularized methods have been discussed in prior work (e.g., arXiv:2310.10645, arXiv:2402.16181), making the main contribution just the use of LLM during exploration (R4). Reviewer 2 (score 5) finds the technical contribution limited to a KL-regularized pre-training phase (R2).
- **Algorithmic and theoretical issues.** Phase I learns a value function from imitation learning with LLM-generated actions, but Phase II runs PPO, which learns its own value function from scratch based on its own policy. The mismatch is not addressed, and the paper lacks theoretical justification (R3). Additionally, PPO learns V(s) not Q(s,a), which may be incompatible with the proposed method if Q-values are needed (R3). The training in Phase I is offline-policy, while Phase II uses an online algorithm (PPO), mixing regimes without justification (R3).
- **Token efficiency argument is unconvincing.** Table 3 shows a 10% pre-training percentage, and Figure 8’s last row indicates that success rates for 3 of 4 tasks saturate after 10% of training, suggesting the two-stage algorithm may effectively be one stage (R1). The claimed 90-95% token reduction is because the LLM only generates guidance in the first phase; if the second phase does not improve the policy, the efficiency gain is less compelling (R1). No training curve showing the phase transition is provided (R1).
- **Insufficient evidence of the RL phase’s impact.** Only 1 out of 4 MiniGrid scenarios and 1 out of 2 NetHack scenarios demonstrate a clear improvement from the second phase (R1). The ablation study shows that PPO alone already performed well in many cases; the authors should show scenarios where PPO would fail and the new algorithm succeeds (R2).
- **Unfair comparison / limited baselines.** The paper uses ActionNet to translate options into low-level actions via a pre-defined mapping, but it is unclear if baseline algorithms (LLMxHRL, LLM4Teach) also use ActionNet. If not, this creates an unfair advantage (R1). The baseline selection is limited; relevant works such as GLAM, TWOSOME, SayCan, DigiRL, and ArCHer are not discussed or compared (R3).
- **Experiments use toy examples.** The chosen environments are academic scenarios (e.g., MiniGrid, not full Crafter). Testing on more complex environments like the full Crafter game would better highlight the method’s impact (R1).
- **Paper is poorly written.** Incorrect use of “hierarchical RL” is confusing; the related work section unnecessarily discusses HRL and imitation learning, and the LLM Agent section is unrelated to the topic (R3). All equations lack detailed explanation and analysis (R3). Minor issues: Algorithm 1 missing caption (R3), line 239 contains an unreplaced placeholder (R3), line 274 typo “importantbt” (R3), and equation in line 248 has no label (R3). The authors have a very limited study of prior work using LLMs to facilitate RL training (R3).
- **Extra hyperparameters are introduced** (α, λ_t, number of Phase I iterations) without sufficient analysis of their sensitivity (R2).
- **Unclear details.** How does the LLM calculate the probability of a high-level action in the equation at line 248? (R3). What is the second KL divergence used for in Equation (1), and why is the first KL divergence inverted compared to the second? (R3). What does it mean in lines 318–319: “For all baselines, we did not train them until they converged”? (R3). How effective is the LLM policy in exploration versus exploitation separately? (R4).
