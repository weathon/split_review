## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes MAA (Meticulous Adversarial Attack) to improve the transferability of adversarial examples across vision-language pre-trained (VLP) models. MAA consists of (1) a Resizing and Sliding Crop (RScrop) technique that enriches initial adversarial examples by generating diverse, zoomed-in views of an image, and (2) a Multi-Granularity Similarity Disruption (MGSD) strategy that maximizes embedding distances between adversarial and clean examples at multiple layers/components. Evaluated on image-text retrieval, visual grounding, and image captioning across several VLP models (CLIP, ALBEF, BLIP, etc.), MAA achieves state-of-the-art transferability. Reviewers note that while the method shows significant gains, weak points include insufficient justification/analysis of RScrop’s claimed benefits, missing comparisons to other image augmentation techniques, reproducibility issues, limited source-model diversity, and higher computational cost. One reviewer was notably more positive (rating 8); the other four gave 5 or 6.

### Strengths

- The transferability improvement compared to SOTA attacks is significant (R1, R3, R5). The method consistently outperforms existing approaches across multiple models, datasets, and tasks.
- The combination of RScrop and MGSD is a simple yet effective idea that builds on existing techniques (image augmentations and multi‑granularity similarity disruption) to achieve strong results (R3, R5).
- The experimental setup is comprehensive: multiple VLP models (CLIP, ALBEF, BLIP, etc.), benchmark datasets, and downstream tasks; ablation studies and parameter analysis provide insights into the method’s components (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (R4, R5), and the presented results (tables, figures) facilitate understanding (R5).
- The work addresses an important issue – adversarial transferability in VLP models – and the findings can guide robustness evaluation (R5).

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient justification and analysis of RScrop**  
   - The claim that RScrop helps the model “attend to intricate local details” and “previously overlooked boundary regions of adjacent patches” is not verified. No concrete evidence (e.g., Grad‑CAM visualizations, quantitative fine‑grained tasks) is provided to show that RScrop extracts finer‑grained contextual features (R1, R3).  
   - No comparison is made against other input augmentation techniques used in prior work (e.g., random resize and padding [DIM], translation [TI‑DIM], scale & translation [SI‑NI‑TI‑DIM], and various augmentations from SIA). Table 6 should include such baselines to demonstrate the advantage of the specific RScrop design (R3).  
   - The effect of unzooming (scale factor < 1.0) or random scale (e.g., 0.5–2.0) is not studied. This would test whether the claimed benefit is due to zoomin‑g alone (R3).  
   - The connection between RScrop and MGSD is unclear: does the effectiveness of each augmentation technique differ when MGSD is applied or not? (R3)

2. **Missing details and reproducibility concerns**  
   - The description of how sliding crops are performed and how multi‑granularity similarity disruption is achieved is insufficiently detailed (R4).  
   - The provided code is chaotic, cannot be easily reproduced, and appears to be adapted from the SGA repository rather than built independently. The code structure does not clearly correspond to the method section (R4).  
   - No error bars or results with different random seeds are reported, raising doubts about the validity and stability of the experimental outcomes (R4).

3. **Limited source‑model and task diversity**  
   - The source model is fixed to CLIP ViT‑B/16. Following existing work, results with other source models (e.g., ALBEF, BLIP) should be reported for completeness (R3).  
   - Performance on BLIP is not as strong as on other models (R2).  
   - The method is only evaluated on smaller VLP models; performance on large models like LLaMA‑3.2 and MiniGPT‑4 is weaker, and the explanation for this degradation is vague (R2). Attack success on flagship black‑box models (GPT‑4, Claude, GPT‑4o) is not demonstrated (R1, R2).  
   - Text perturbations are not explored as thoroughly as image perturbations, despite being an important aspect of multi‑modal attacks (R5).

4. **High computational cost**  
   - The method uses many augmentations (K) and iterations (60), requiring significantly more computation. This increases attack delay, making it less suitable for real‑time scenarios and requiring more powerful hardware. The trade‑off between attack success rate and computational overhead is not discussed (R2, R3, R1).

5. **Lack of deep analysis and theoretical grounding**  
   - The paper lacks formula analysis and illustrative figures to explain the proposed RScrop and MGSD, and does not provide an in‑depth understanding of why MAA outperforms other methods (R4, R5).  
   - The claim that VLP models rely on model‑specific features and regions (line 78) and that ViTs lose crucial contextual information (line 101) need citations and mechanistic explanation (e.g., modality‑specific biases, fixed patch size, lack of spatial inductive biases) (R1).  
   - Data augmentation via scaling and joint attacks on shallow features are existing techniques; the novelty of applying them to VLP models is limited (R4).  
   - MGSD resembles the existing VLAttack, and no significant conceptual departure is demonstrated (R3, R4).

6. **Presentation errors and minor issues**  
   - Formula (1) has a symbolic error: $f_{img}^{i}(t)$ should be $f_{img}^{i}(x_k)$ (R2, R3, R4).  
   - The description of formula (1) does not match the equation (R2).  
   - Citation style is incorrect (use \citet and \citep properly) (R3).  
   - Some questions from reviewers overlap with weaknesses and remain unanswered:  
     * Why is BLIP excluded from I2T and T2I retrieval experiments, visual grounding only with ALBEF, and image captioning only with BLIP? (R2)  
     * Can the method be extended to other multi‑modal tasks or domains, and what are its limitations for very large or complex images? (R5)  
     * Suggestions to ensemble multiple source models or attack larger black‑box models (GPT‑4) are made but not implemented or discussed (R1).
