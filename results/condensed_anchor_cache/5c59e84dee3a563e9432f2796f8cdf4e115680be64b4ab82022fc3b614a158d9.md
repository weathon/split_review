## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers agree that this paper provides a new global convergence analysis for policy gradient (PG) in tabular average-reward MDPs. The main result is a sublinear convergence rate of O(1/T) for projected PG, achieved without the smoothness assumption needed in discounted-reward analyses. A novel smoothness lemma for the average-reward objective is proved. Table 1 summarizing MDP-dependent constants and the discussion relating these results to discounted-reward bounds are appreciated. However, reviewers raise significant concerns: the paper’s regret-based framing is not connected to the per-step error analysis; comparisons to related work are incomplete (missing linear convergence from policy mirror descent, ignoring ε-factors in rates, and not separating planning from learning); the restriction to unichain MDPs (Assumption 1) is a major limitation; the MDP-dependent constant C_PL could be prohibitively large; the simulation study fails to isolate eﬀects of individual constants; and the presentation contains many typos and undefined notations.

### Strengths

1.  **First comprehensive global convergence proof for PG in average-reward MDPs.**  
    The paper provides the first finite-time global convergence analysis of projected policy gradient for infinite-horizon average-reward MDPs, with a sublinear O(1/T) rate and without the smoothness assumption required in discounted-reward analyses (Reviewers 2,3,4). *Reviewer 3 specifically calls it the “first proof of global convergence of Project Policy Gradient for average reward MDPs.”*

2.  **Novel smoothness result.**  
    A key technical contribution is establishing the smoothness of the value function for ergodic MDPs, which is of independent interest and enables the proof (Reviewer 4). *Reviewer 1: “The technical contributions are solid, rigorous, and clearly articulated … The proofs are checked to be correct and are largely self-contained.”*

3.  **Useful exposition and organization.**  
    The paper is overall well-written and readable, especially the problem motivation, literature review, and the high-level presentation of results (Reviewers 1,2,4). *Reviewer 1: “the flow is friendly to first-time readers.”* The relation of the new results to discounted-reward bounds is discussed (Reviewer 1). *Reviewer 2: “The paper admits a good organization. Its technical part is written mostly clearly and precisely … delivers a good and accurate review of related literature, to my best knowledge.”*

4.  **Table 1 and instance-specific constants.**  
    The summary of instance-related constants in Table 1 gives a clear idea of the bound’s complexity (Reviewer 1). *Reviewer 4: “New state-of-the-art convergence rate … new smoothness result.”*

5.  **Numerical results on toy problems.**  
    Reporting simulations, albeit on toy MDPs, provides empirical validation (Reviewers 1,2). *Reviewer 1 likes the discussion linking instance-specific bounds to potential improvements.*

### Weaknesses

1.  **Unclear or missing connection between regret and per-step error.**  
    The abstract and introduction claim a regret bound of O(log T), yet the paper’s core analysis only deals with per-step error measures (e.g., suboptimality gap). The connection between the O(1/k) convergence rate and the claimed O(log T) regret is not explained, and a precise definition of the regret considered is never given. *Reviewer 2: “Please clarify. The connection … is not made explicit … A more detailed explanation … is needed.”* *Reviewer 4 argues that regret is a learning concept and not appropriate for a planning problem with exact gradients: “the author should solely stick to the convergence rate result.”* This point is a major weakness.

2.  **Incomplete and misleading comparisons to related work.**  
    - **Omitted ε-dependence in rate statements.** Equation (1) and other comparisons omit the ε factor, making rates appear better than they are (Reviewers 3,4). *Reviewer 3: “why was the dependence of ε omitted?”* *Reviewer 4: “equation (1) does not show any dependence on ε … I think it makes the comparison quite confusing.”*  
    - **Failure to mention linear convergence results.** The work of Xiao (2022b) establishes a linear convergence rate for policy mirror descent (which generalizes PG) in the discounted setting; this is not cited or discussed (Reviewer 2’s comment that PG is an instance of PMD is echoed by Reviewer 3 regarding [Liu 2024]). *Reviewer 4: “I am surprised that the authors failed to mention the linear convergence rate.”*  
    - **Missing comparison to Liu et al. (2024).** This work improves the rate for softmax PG and shows linear convergence for PMD (Reviewer 3).  
    - **Outdated state-of-the-art.** Bai et al. (2023) is no longer the only work establishing regret bounds for average-reward MDPs; a recent paper supersedes it (Reviewer 4).  
    - These omissions make the paper’s contributions less clear and its positioning weaker.

3.  **Restrictive MDP assumption (unichain / ergodic) and limited discussion of extensions.**  
    The analysis relies heavily on Assumption 1 (unichain MDPs), without which Lemma 1 (closed-form value function) would not hold. The paper does not discuss how restrictive this assumption is for real-world problems, nor does it explore potential ways to extend the analysis to communicating MDPs or infinite state-action spaces. *Reviewer 2: “One key limitation … the restriction to the class of unichain MDPs … renders unlikely … that the technical tools could be used beyond the restricted class.”* *Reviewer 1 asks about extension to parametric policies and infinite spaces; Reviewer 4 asks whether smoothness extends beyond ergodic MDPs.* This is a severe limitation that affects the practical relevance and generality of the results.

4.  **MDP-dependent constant C_PL could be very large, and its analysis is missing.**  
    The constants in the convergence bound (e.g., C_PL) may depend exponentially on |S|, making the O(1/k) rate less meaningful in practice. The paper provides no analysis or discussion of how large these constants can be and what practical problems may have prohibitively large constants. *Reviewer 2: “A key question is how bad the MDP-dependent constant C_PL could be … I expect it could be exponentially large in the size of state-space |S|. The paper should provide a more detailed analysis … examples … practical implications.”*

5.  **Weak simulation study.**  
    The experiments do not control for individual MDP constants. Varying the proportion of actions with reward -1 may change multiple constants simultaneously; the effect of the target constant is not isolated. *Reviewer 1: “It is not sufficient to only vary the proportion of actions yielding a reward of -1, as this may not fully isolate the effect of C_r from other constants. … careful construction of MDP families is needed.”* *Reviewer 1 also notes the simulations are on very simple (toy) problems.* *Reviewer 2 acknowledges numerical results are reported but only on toy problems.*

6.  **Presentation issues: undefined notations, formatting errors, and typos.**  
    Multiple reviewers found the writing in need of polishing. Specific issues include:  
    - $\pi_k$ used before definition (Reviewer 2, line 50).  
    - Precise definition of $d^\pi(s)$ never given (Reviewer 3).  
    - Step‑size used in Theorem 1 not specified (Reviewer 3).  
    - Equation (8) and its reference or proof missing (Reviewer 3).  
    - Inconsistent notation: $d_\mu(\pi^*)$ vs $d_{\mu,\gamma}^{\pi^*}$ (Reviewer 2).  
    - Many citation formatting errors: misuse of \cite vs \citep/\citet, missing spaces (Bertsekas), repeated citation (Xiao 2022) (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
    - Typos: line 82 “is , Bai”, line 198 missing parenthesis, line 251 “Further is the function is”, line 269 “euclidean norm”, line 346 “Lemma below”, line 384 using S instead of |S|, line 398 “By L, did you mean L_2^Π?” (Reviewer 2).  
    - Line 133: use $\Delta(\mathcal A)$ for readability; Table 1 should specify the norms used for $C_p$ and $C_m$ (Reviewer 2).  
    - Line 155/181/202: equation references incorrect (Reviewers 3).  
    - Remark after Theorem 1 is unclear: “the bound $\frac{\sigma}{k^p}$ less meaningful for initial k” – please clarify (Reviewer 3).  
    *While Reviewer 2 and 4 note the paper is “nicely written” overall, the prevalence of these errors reduces clarity and professionalism.*

7.  **Related works should better separate planning vs. learning contexts.**  
    The first paragraph of Section 1 cites learning papers (that estimate from data) when discussing planning algorithms (assuming model access). The paper itself assumes exact gradient access, which is a planning setting; this distinction should be made explicit in the abstract and introduction. *Reviewer 4: “The authors should rewrite the related works … separate … planning (exact gradients) … from learning (gradient estimates) … The work of the authors falls into the first group. This should be highlighted.”*
