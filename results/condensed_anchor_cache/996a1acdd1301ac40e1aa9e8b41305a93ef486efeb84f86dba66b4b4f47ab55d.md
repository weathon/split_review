## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes GRACE, a semi-supervised method for multimodal single-cell matching using geometric graph construction and non-parametric label generation. The method maps data into a shared embedding space via ZINB distribution maximization and aligns distributions using mutual nearest neighbors. Experiments on benchmark datasets show average performance improvements of 6.71% (R2A) and 14.17% (A2R) over the second-best method. One reviewer (rating 5/confidence 3) praised the technique combination and empirical validation; two reviewers (both rating 3/confidence 4 and 2) raised major concerns about fairness, realism, and methodological justification, leading to a polarized evaluation.

### Strengths
- GRACE combines joint representation learning, semantic alignment, and nonparametric semi-supervised learning to address multimodal matching.
- Empirical study demonstrates effectiveness; ablation studies and parameter analyses validate the rationality and stability of each component.
- The paper is well-formatted with clear figures and easy to read.

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing and clarity**: Notations are complex and unclear; Figure 1 notations are described as gibberish. The overall training process is not clearly described and needs further elaboration.
2. **Unconvincing design choices**: The use of ZINB distribution is not justified for scATAC-seq data—no evidence that it fits that distribution. The difference between Eq. 5 and standard classification cross-entropy is unexplained and lacks an ablation study. Consistency learning motivation is also unconvincing.
3. **Unrealistic problem setting**: The scenario with partly annotated scRNA-seq and partly annotated scATAC-seq is uncommon; more realistic would be labeled scRNA-seq and unlabeled scATAC-seq. Using only 50–200 labels in evaluation is not realistic. In the more realistic setting (Table 5), GRACE’s improvements over baselines are marginal, limiting the significance of the work.
4. **Unfair comparisons**: GRACE uses paired multi-omics data as input (a strong prior), while several baselines do not require pairing. The advantage may come from this additional information. Key baselines (GLUE, MOFA+) are missing from Table 1. Classification results could be obtained by KNN on integrated embeddings for fairer comparison.
5. **Missing computational complexity analysis**: No time complexity analysis is provided, and the method requires building a neighborhood graph. The paper lacks parameter counts vs. baselines, training/inference times on standard hardware, memory requirements for different dataset sizes, and analysis of scaling with dataset size or number of cell types.
6. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Many hyperparameters make the method less practical. No automatic tuning method is explored; selecting optimal parameters for new datasets remains an open question. Sensitivity analysis covers only a narrow range; stability across broader parameter values is not shown.
7. **Reliability of non-parametric label generation**: The support representations from a small amount of labeled data may not cover all cell types, leading to inaccurate label distributions that degrade downstream performance. The paper does not discuss the impact of limited label distribution on matching or cite related literature on this issue.
8. **Insufficient evaluation scope**: Performance should be tested using labeled data sampled from different subsets of cell types, and as a function of labeled dataset size. The impact of the distribution of limited labeled data on matching quality should be analyzed.
9. **Weak related work**: The related work section lacks a thorough introduction to the problem and does not sufficiently analyze the advantages and disadvantages of the proposed model structure, leaving the motivation unclear.

*Note:* One reviewer (rating 5) was significantly more positive, emphasizing the effectiveness of the technical contributions and empirical results. The other two reviewers (rating 3) were critical of the fairness, realism, and significance, creating a clear contrast in assessments.
