## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty of the theoretical framework—introducing distributionally diverse (DD) risk and proving optimality of uniform training under an entropy-based uncertainty set—and appreciate the practical remedies (gentle finetuning, rebalancing). However, they raise significant concerns about insufficient justification for why DD risk is superior to other DRO formulations, questionable experimental results (density estimation issues, lack of clear improvement over ERM, inconsistent use of tricks, missing baselines), and theoretical assumptions (noiseless classification, ineffective bounds for empirical measures, over‑claimed LLM implications). A minority reviewer (Reviewer 3) is strongly critical of the core claim that DD risk is a good surrogate for worst‑case risk, while others share many of the same concerns but are more positive about the framework.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical framework**: The paper introduces the distributionally diverse (DD) risk and demonstrates the optimality of training on a uniform distribution under an entropy‑based uncertainty set (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Generalization bounds and risk gap characterizations are provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear logical organization and thoughtful presentation**: The paper is well‑structured, with a clear progression from worst‑case risk to DD risk to the uniform distribution surrogate, and it explicitly acknowledges limitations (Reviewer 3). It is well written overall (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical remedies**: Gentle finetuning and rebalancing strategies offer actionable methods to handle non‑uniform training data, which are relevant for real‑world applications and large‑scale model training. Reviewer 2 specifically highlights the potential impact on foundation model design and training optimization.
- **Relevance to large models**: The emphasis on uniform training provides new ideas for training large models, and the fine‑tuning/rebalancing strategies have application potential in basic model design (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient justification of the DD risk formulation**: Multiple reviewers question why the entropy‑based uncertainty set is superior to other DRO approaches (e.g., Wasserstein, f‑divergences) and why it is a good surrogate for worst‑case risk. Reviewer 3 strongly argues that convergence to worst‑case risk only holds as γ→∞ (an asymptotic property shared by many uncertainty sets) and that Theorem 3.2 shows DD risk scales with γ, preventing estimation by uniform risk in that regime. The paper does not provide a compelling argument beyond the theoretical result that uniform distribution is optimal under this specific constraint; the practical meaning of entropy as a measure of diversity and its connection to minimizing worst‑case error are unclear (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). A reviewer also questions whether uniform distribution is truly beneficial when image data lie on low‑dimensional manifolds (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental concerns**:
    * *Density estimation*: The learning of the density estimator is not clearly detailed. Density estimation itself is as hard as the original task, and the paper lacks metrics to evaluate its accuracy or analyze how errors propagate and potentially hurt model performance (Reviewer 1).
    * *Practical effectiveness and inconsistency*: The rebalancing strategy does not show significant improvement over ERM in Tables 2 and 3. Gains appear to come from tricks (UMAP, label conditioning, WDL2) applied inconsistently across datasets; no clear ablation study isolates the core rebalancing contribution. Different methods are used per dataset (e.g., WDL2 for ColoredMNIST, UMAP for PovertyMap) with no guidance on general method selection (Reviewers 2, 4).
    * *Missing baselines*: Tables 1 and 2 omit many relevant baselines such as ARM, VREx, RSC (Reviewer 4). For PovertyMap, the state‑of‑the‑art C‑Mixup achieves 0.53 compared to the paper’s best 0.47 (Reviewer 3).
    * *ColoredMNIST dataset*: This dataset exhibits concept shift rather than covariate shift, contradicting the paper’s claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical issues and assumptions**:
    * The theory assumes 0‑1 loss and an existing ground truth model f∗(x), implying a noiseless classification setting that is unrealistic (e.g., mixture of Gaussians) (Reviewer 3).
    * The risk gap is characterized via the ℓ1 distance δ(u,p) between continuous measure u and empirical measure p, which always equals 2 when p is discrete, making Equation (8) ineffective. The bounds lack sample‑size dependence and cannot reflect non‑asymptotic behavior (Reviewer 3).
    * Claims about implications for LLMs and foundation models are over‑claimed; importance weighting is known to be ineffective for over‑parameterized models, and Theorem 3.1 does not account for the inductive bias of optimizers (Reviewer 3).
- **Over‑pessimism concern**: Like other DRO methods, the proposed approach may assign high weights to noisy data or outliers, potentially harming learning (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation gaps**: Figure 3 mentions “WDL2” and Table 2 references “UMAP” without explanation in captions or main text (Reviewer 2). The choice of α that minimizes the upper bound in Theorem 3.2 is not discussed (Reviewer 3). No universal criterion is provided for method selection across different distribution shifts (Reviewer 4).

*Note*: Reviewer 3 is notably more critical of the theoretical foundation, especially the claim that DD risk is a good surrogate for worst‑case risk, while other reviewers raise similar concerns but are generally more positive about the framework. This disagreement is preserved.
