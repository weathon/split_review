## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HASS, a speculative sampling method improving upon EAGLE by addressing two issues: objective misalignment (training objective focusing on top-K tokens instead of full distribution) and context inconsistency (training uses target model hidden states while inference uses predicted states). HASS introduces harmonized objective distillation and harmonized context alignment (progressive training using the model's own predictions across multiple draft tokens) to adapt the draft model to actual decoding scenarios. Experiments on LLaMA models (2.81x–4.05x wall-clock speedup averaging across three datasets) surpass EAGLE-2 by 8%–20% in acceptance length with no additional inference overhead.

### Strengths
- Motivation is clear and well-founded, addressing two specific shortcomings of EAGLE (ability to predict top-K tokens and number of tokens successfully predicted) with practical solutions (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Experiments are well-detailed with clear metrics, comprehensive across multiple LLaMA architectures and three datasets (dialogue, code, math), comparing against baselines including EAGLE, EAGLE-2, and others (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Empirical results are convincing: HASS achieves 2.81x–4.05x wall-clock speedup over vanilla decoding and improves acceptance length by 8%–16% over EAGLE-2 (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Extensive ablation studies provide insights: e.g., reweight factors in Table 5, and clear demonstration of the impact of the two proposed improvements (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The method introduces no extra inference overhead and only acceptable additional training cost (3–4 extra alignment steps), making it nearly a 'free lunch' (Reviewer 3).
- The paper addresses highly relevant LLM latency reduction (Reviewer 4) and makes sense from the perspective of typical speculative decoding acceptance process (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Training efficiency and overhead**: HASS requires higher training overhead than EAGLE. During additional training steps, each token's KV matrix differs, making it infeasible to compute all attention scores via a single matrix multiplication (Reviewer 3). The paper states "n times the training overhead of EAGLE," but HASS steps (except step 0) have larger KV caches and attention scores; detailed reports on computational FLOPs, memory access bytes, GPU memory usage, actual execution time, and peak GPU memory for each step are missing (Reviewers 3, 4). *Note: Reviewer 3 views the training cost as acceptable, while Reviewers 1 and 4 consider it insufficiently analyzed.*  
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: The paper lacks comparisons with vanilla speculative sampling and Medusa (Reviewer 4). Additionally, training the draft model on Model-Generated data could mitigate context misalignment; a comparison to EAGLE-2 trained on such data (e.g., in Table 8) is requested (Reviewer 2). Generalization issues when tested on data different from the training distribution are not addressed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient analysis of self-distillation dataset**: The construction and configuration of the self-distillation dataset are not sufficiently explored. The quality and configuration may be more crucial than the framework itself; a deeper discussion and analysis are needed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing analysis across token counts and task types**: The paper lacks experiments analyzing how performance varies with different token counts (scalability) and across diverse task types to assess robustness (Reviewer 1).  
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:  
  - Section 3.2 (Harmonized Context Alignment) is overly brief and lacks rigorous mathematical presentation of the loss function; it is not easy to parse what the input and prediction are at each step (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - Figure 3 is confusing: Training Step 2 should have \(f^{(l)}_{t-2}\) and \(f^{(l)}_{t-1}\) in the bottom row and only use superscript \((s_1)\) in the bottom right entry (similarly for steps 3 and 4) (Reviewer 2).  
  - The light green text in Figure 3 is difficult to read (Reviewer 4).  
  - Table 1 uses “-” marks for PLD and Lookahead Temperature=1 without explanation (Reviewer 4).  
  - The vertical axes in Figures 5 and 6 are not clearly labeled and the ranges do not appear to correspond to acceptance rate (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing specification of training data**: The paper does not specify what data was used to train the draft model in the experiments (Reviewer 2).
