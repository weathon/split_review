## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces and characterizes "tokenization bias"—the phenomenon where tokenized and statistically equivalent byte-level language models produce different predictive distributions over the next byte. The authors propose the Byte-Token Representation Lemma, which maps between token and byte distributions, and use it to derive a next-byte sampling algorithm that converts any tokenized LM into a statistically equivalent token-free model without additional training. They demonstrate practical benefits on fill-in-the-middle (FIM) coding tasks (≈18% improvement on benchmarks) and model ensembling (up to 3.7% improvement over individual models), outperforming the standard token healing fix.

### Strengths
- First to systematically define and formally characterize the tokenization bias phenomenon, with illustrative examples (e.g., a 3rd-order Markov chain) that are interesting and useful to the community.
- Provides a solid theoretical foundation (Byte-Token Representation Lemma) and an efficient, training-free algorithm for exact byte-level probabilities.
- Evaluates the method on two practically relevant tasks: FIM code completion and model ensembling, showing significant gains (e.g., ≈18% on FIM benchmarks, up to 3.7% improvement over individual models).
- The method is broadly applicable: it can combine predictions from any LLMs regardless of vocabulary differences, reminiscent of older ensemble approaches now enabled for modern tokenized models.
- The paper is well-written and studies an important, under-explored problem.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Only two tasks (FIM coding and model ensembling) are covered. Multiple reviewers request experiments on other tasks, including generic code completion without FIM, natural language completion (e.g., story completion), computer use scenarios, and multi-step/long-context tasks where tokenization bias may compound over reasoning chains or multi-turn dialogue.
- **Comparison to prior work:** The method is very similar to the ACL 2024 paper "Token Alignment via Character Matching for Subword Completion", which addresses the same issue and proposes more evaluation scenarios. The paper does not compare with this method. One reviewer (rating 5) considers this a major omission and questions whether the proposed method offers meaningful gains beyond token healing (e.g., in the sampling/pass@10 setting, improvement is very minor: from 84.0 to 84.1 in one case).
- **Ensembling experiments lack baselines:** It is unclear whether the proposed byte-level ensembling outperforms existing methods for ensembling models with distinct vocabularies (e.g., consensus voting, ranking over diverse samples). The gains reported (e.g., Table 1 and Table 2) are described as "not-so-strong", and the ensembling strategy (how models are combined) is not clearly explained.
- **Computational overhead and efficiency:** The paper discusses computational overhead but does not thoroughly analyze latency impact in a practical setting. Reviewers request decoding efficiency metrics for the methods in Figure 6, especially since efficiency is cited as a motivation.
- **Interaction with sampling techniques:** The interaction of the proposed byte-level prediction method with popular sampling strategies (top-p, top-k) has not been explored, particularly in the ensemble context.
- **Vocabulary diversity quantification:** The practical impact depends on how much tokenization vocabularies vary across popular LLMs; no quantitative analysis of vocabulary overlap or distribution is provided. If tokenization schemes are converging, the advantages may be limited.
- **Figure clarity and potential errors:** The meaning of the right two sub-figures in Figure 6 is unclear. The caption of Figure 2 may have a typo: the probability of sampling "A" on the left-hand side is stated as α, but one reviewer believes it should be (1-α).
- **Generalizability concerns:** It is unclear if the method applies to non-deterministic tokenization or mixed token vocabularies, or to tokenization algorithms beyond BPE and MPE (e.g., WordPiece, Unigram).
- **Missing explanation of FIM setup:** Reviewers question why a prefix would stop mid-token at inference time, why new tokens arise, and why the study is restricted to two prompting formats. A clearer explanation of the evaluation setting and its realism is needed.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (rating 8) is highly positive, emphasizing the novelty and strong practical results. Another reviewer (rating 5) is more critical, highlighting the strong similarity to prior work and the very minor gains in certain settings. Other reviewers (ratings 6 and 6) are interested but request broader evaluation, more baselines, and additional analyses.
