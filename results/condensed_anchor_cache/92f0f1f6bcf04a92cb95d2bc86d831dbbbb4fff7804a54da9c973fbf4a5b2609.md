## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents PlaSma, a framework to equip small language models with procedural knowledge and planning capabilities via distillation from large language models (LLMs), a human-curated dataset (CoPlan), and a verifier-guided step-wise beam search decoding. It also introduces a replanning task. Experiments show that smaller models (770M–11B) can compete with or exceed the teacher LLM in planning and replanning, and in the embodied environment VirtualHome.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and well-structured** (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Important direction**: cost, carbon footprint, and accessibility benefits of using smaller models for planning (Reviewer 2); sensible to scale and cost (Reviewer 3); interesting practical problem (Reviewer 3).
- **Good description of methodology** in all aspects, especially data generation/curation and the combination of distillation, dataset, and verifier-guided decoding (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Sensible task complexity**: goals, conditions, verification (Reviewer 3).
- **Secondary model for correctness** (the verifier) is a good idea, especially for lower-capacity models (Reviewer 3).
- **Cost-effective human-in-loop data curation** (CoPlan) is novel and useful (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Guided decoding with a step verifier** (RoBERTa-based) helps regulate step validity (Reviewer 1).
- **Results are interesting**, showing smaller models can match or surpass larger teachers (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Motivation/justification of the teacher-student paradigm is weak** (Reviewer 1). The paper does not clearly articulate whether the key benefit is cost, performance specialization, controllability, or better integration with downstream tasks. If the goal is to specialize commonsense knowledge for planning, the same approach could be used with same-sized or larger LMs. Reviewer 2 also questions: if small models can be paired with human input or external verifiers, why is distillation from a larger model necessary?  
2. **Novelty concerns** – Reviewer 4 notes that training a small LM to imitate an LLM via distillation is not novel (citing [A] for distillation on complex reasoning tasks); the main contribution is limited to extending this to planning. The beam-search planning idea is similar to [B] (though recency was discounted). In contrast, Reviewer 3 found the contributions more significant (score 8).  
3. **Missing key experiment**: comparison with the original (undistilled) smaller model is absent, making it impossible to gauge the performance gain from distillation alone (Reviewer 4).  
4. **Evaluation in domains with hard executability conditions** is lacking – the human evaluation uses loose executability conditions. A comparison with domains like those in the International Planning Competitions would provide objectively determinable correctness (Reviewer 2).  
5. **Need for comparison with GPT-4** in addition to GPT-3 (Reviewer 2).  
6. **Potential increased bias from distillation** is acknowledged but still a concern (Reviewer 2).  
7. **Poor explanation of the ProScript dataset** – its instruction complexity and task diversity are unclear, making results in Table 2 hard to interpret (Reviewer 3).  
8. **Abuse of terms**:
    - “Step verifier” is actually adding a bias, not verifying; a better name would be “quality bias” or “bias term”. With a large α, the aggregation in Eq. (3) cannot prevent catastrophic errors that the LLM finds appealing (Reviewer 3).  
    - “Symbolic procedural knowledge distillation” is overloaded (AI traditionally associates “symbolic” with logic/graphical models). Suggested “instruction distillation” (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also asks what is “symbolic” here.  
9. **Claim that human-written data is better for evaluation is not justified** – human-written data may lack diversity due to annotator incentives. The dataset description and analysis are crucial to prove the point, otherwise results are not convincing (Reviewer 3).  
10. **Need for formal analysis of the step verifier** – the perturbations (ordering, completeness, topicality, fluency) need detailed discussion of intuition and formal properties. How does a single RoBERTa classifier score reflect all these criteria? (Reviewer 1)  
11. **Missing formal loss function definitions** for teacher-student distillation and verifier training (Reviewer 1).  
12. **Evaluation metrics need more detail**: the AMT human evaluation and its criteria (coverage, order, overall quality) – are these complete? Need correlation analysis between human eval, BLEU/ROUGE, and embodied environment success rate. Any proposal for automatic plan evaluation? (Reviewer 1)  
13. **Clarity issues**:
    - What does “PlaSma” mean across different tasks? The 6 models in Table 1 vs. Fig. 4 need clarification (Reviewer 3).  
    - Section 2.2 uses “task” ambiguously (Reviewer 4).  
    - The tone of “we introduce the task of counterfactual planning” should be reduced; prior work on plan revision exists (e.g., Ow & Smith, AAAI’88; Williams & Burdick, ICRA’06) (Reviewer 3).  
14. **Questions about overfitting**: what factors (model size, data amount) influence when distillation leads to overfitting? This would help motivate generating a dataset larger than ProScript (Reviewer 4).  
15. **How is CoPlan different from ProScript?** Besides size, is the novelty only the counterfactual/replanning subset? (Reviewer 4; also relates to Reviewer 3’s request for other possible datasets).  

*Note:* There is minor disagreement on the significance of the contributions: Reviewer 3 (score 8) found the approach novel and well-described, while Reviewer 4 (score 6) considered novelty limited. The above weaknesses reflect concerns raised by at least one reviewer.
