## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes multiple techniques to improve Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) for text-to-3D generation: denoising in both latent and image spaces of the diffusion model, a timestep annealing strategy that progressively reduces the sampled noise level, a z-coordinate variance regularization for single-stage optimization, and kernel smoothing for coarse-to-fine importance sampling to reduce texture flickering. All three reviewers agree that the presented qualitative results and ablations demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed techniques. However, the contributions are viewed as discrete and the experimental evaluation is criticized for lacking quantitative metrics and more thorough comparisons.

### Strengths

- The strategy of performing denoising in both image and latent space is useful for improving detail.
- The proposed z-variance loss alleviates cloudy artifacts and performs better than the distortion loss.
- Single-stage optimization is useful and the proposed solution is impressive; it produces highly detailed and view-consistent 3D assets.
- Compared to DreamFusion, Magic3D, and Fantasia3D, rendered images exhibit enhanced photo-realism, improved texture details, and more natural lighting.
- Ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of each individual technique.

### Weaknesses

- The contributions of the paper are discrete and fragmented, comprising several small contribution points without a clear cohesive thread or central theme. It is unclear how the individual components (timestep annealing, z-coordinate variance regularization, kernel smoothing) synergistically contribute to the final result, making it difficult to grasp the core innovation. (All reviewers note this; Reviewer 2 additionally notes that the introduction could better explain why previous works were unable to achieve single-stage optimization, e.g., the trade-off between geometric accuracy and texture quality.)
- All experimental results are qualitative. A quantitative evaluation (e.g., CLIP similarity between text prompts and generated content) is needed to avoid potential cherry-picking of good prompts. (Reviewers 1 and 3)
- A user study should be conducted to quantitatively evaluate the method. (Reviewer 1)
- The generalization of the proposed strategies to other baseline methods (e.g., ProlificDreamer) is not shown. (Reviewer 1)
- The results from Fantasia3D in Figures 4 and 14 are also very impressive in terms of texture quality and geometry. More comparisons are needed to demonstrate superiority. Reviewer 3 specifically requests comparisons on these DreamFusion prompts: 1) an orangutan making a clay bowl on a throwing wheel; 2) a raccoon astronaut holding his helmet; 3) a blue jay standing on a large basket of rainbow macarons; 4) a corgi taking a selfi; 5) a table with dim sum on it; 6) a lion reading the newspaper; 7) a tiger dressed as a doctor; 8) a chimpanzee dressed like Henry VIII king of England; 9) an all-utility vehicle driving across a stream; 10) a squirrel gesturing in front of an easel showing colorful pie charts.
- The kernel smoothing technique is not explained clearly. Only the kernel [1, 1, 1] was tested; have other choices been tried? The method lacks a clear equation describing how it works; Figure 3 only shows results with/without kernel smoothing, not the process. (Reviewer 3; note that Reviewer 2 found the technical aspects clear, indicating disagreement on this point.)
