## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PISA, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for large language models based on compressive sensing. It treats the weight update matrix as a k-sparse approximation in a spatial domain, uses a measurement matrix and block-structured approximation to reduce error bounds, and claims competitive performance with as few as 500 learnable parameters. Theoretical error-bound analysis is provided, and experiments are conducted on NLP and vision tasks.

### Strengths
- The problem of parameter-efficient fine-tuning is important and timely (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The compressive sensing perspective is novel and interesting (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The work includes both theoretical contributions (error bounds, block-size analysis, expressive power) and empirical evaluations (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The method achieves competitive accuracy with a very small number of trainable parameters (e.g., 500) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Enhancements such as matrix subdivision, Kronecker product, and subsampled FFT matrix are used to improve performance and memory reduction (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer (Reviewer 3) is more positive overall (score 5), finding the idea novel, the presentation clear, and the combination of theory and practice valuable. The other reviewers are more critical (scores 3, 3, 3), but acknowledge the new perspective.
- Experiment design appears sufficient (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation is not well-stated or convincing.** The connection from the limitations of low-rank LoRA to the compressive sensing view is unclear; sparsity in weight updates is empirical, not directly motivated by low-rank structure (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The claim that LoRA plugins accumulate to unignorable cost is unconvincing because LoRA is already very small (Reviewer 3).
- **Method description is hard to comprehend.** Key implementation details are missing: what “sparsity” refers to, which parameters are learnable and how they are optimized, and how the compressed representation is translated back to weight space (e.g., using \( \text{vec}^{-1}(A^\dagger b) \)?) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). An algorithm block is recommended (Reviewer 1). The concluding step after Equation (13) is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Technical novelty appears limited.** The core innovation is the compressive sensing view, but it is not well motivated; the rest of the method applies existing compressive sensing techniques, and the connection between the theoretical analysis and LLM fine-tuning is weak, with assumptions not clearly stated (Reviewer 1).  
- **Theoretical gaps in Lemma 1 (key motivation).**  
  - The lemma is not found in the cited references (Reviewer 4).  
  - It refers to “certain conditions” without specifying them (Reviewer 4).  
  - Compressed sensing classically uses basis pursuit, not the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse; the cited reference provides guarantees only for basis pursuit (Reviewer 4).  
  - The probability of failure \(\delta\) appears without explanation of its source (Reviewer 4).  
  - The lemma is proven for a single fixed signal, not for all signals in the optimization space; thus it does not guarantee the inequality during training (Reviewer 4).  
  - Theorems should be backed by empirical analysis demonstrating the main results (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational and memory complexity analysis is flawed or incomplete.**  
  - Table 1’s complexity does not match the paper’s conclusions; the condition for PISA to be more memory-efficient than LoRA is \(k < 2rb^2/d\), contrary to the paper (Reviewer 2).  
  - Section 4.5’s efficiency claim appears incorrect: \(O(d^2 + (d/b)^2 + d^2\log b) > O(d^2 + 2dr)\) for any \(d\) (Reviewer 3).  
  - The FFT step requires \(d^2 \log d\) operations, which is larger than any term in Table 1 and is not accounted for (Reviewer 2).  
  - No practical runtime or memory comparison with LoRA and BitFit is provided; the full memory and computational requirements are not compared (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Inference time is not compared (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing recent baselines**, e.g., LoRA-XS, LoRA-FA, VeRA, for RoBERTa backbones (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing parameter counts** in Table 4 (Reviewer 3).  
- **Multiple writing and presentation issues.**  
  - Typos: missing spaces before citations (lines 38-40), missing spaces in lines 111 and 116, wrong citation format (line 124, section 5) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Notation is messy and inconsistent: same symbols (\(A\), \(B\)) used in different contexts; math symbols not rendered as math (lines 153, 155); dimensions of \(m_1, m_2, m\) unexplained; “measurement matrix” confusing in the learning context; L1 minimization written inconsistently in the same paragraph; Hermitian transpose on \(A\) in Equation (10) used without justification while absent in Equation (5); transition in Equation (10) unclear (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - Figure 1 caption missing space and punctuation; Table 5 missing title (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - The term \(m\) is inconsistent in Section 5.4 (Reviewer 2).  
  - Suggest defining 2D FFT before the property in Equation (11) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Suggest presenting block_size relative to the original matrix dimension (e.g., 16 out of 1024) rather than absolute numbers (Reviewer 3).  
- **Other missing details.**  
  - Why isn’t #params affected by block_size in Table 4? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Which matrices are updated? Only attention? Which specific matrices? (Reviewer 3)  
- **Minor contrast:** One reviewer (Reviewer 4) describes the paper as well-written, but the majority (Reviewers 1, 2, 3) find it poorly written and difficult to follow.
