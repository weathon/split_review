## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Riemannian Diffusion Mixture (RDM), a framework for generative modeling on Riemannian manifolds. It constructs a diffusion process as a mixture of endpoint-conditioned bridge processes (Brownian bridges on manifolds). The authors develop two bridge types: the Logarithmic bridge, which uses the tangent vector that minimizes the geodesic to the target endpoint, and the Spectral bridge, which uses a spectral distance derived from the Laplace–Beltrami operator. The training objective is divergence-free and can be time-rescaled. The method is tested on Sphere, Torii, mesh datasets, and a 2D hyperboloid, with comparisons to CNFM and other baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses key limitations of prior generative models on manifolds: expensive divergence (implicit score matching) and reliance on heat kernel approximations.
- The approach is natural and elegant: it extends Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, 2023) to Brownian bridge SDEs.
- The two-way bridge matching technique reduces computation, and the divergence-free training objective with time-rescaling aids numerical stability.
- The framework is applicable to general manifolds with non-trivial curvature, including synthetic distributions on triangular meshes and a 2D hyperboloid.
- Experiments show better or comparable results to CNFM and other baselines.
- The idea of guiding a diffusion process by mixing tangent directions to the data distribution is new and intuitively appealing.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation imprecision:**  
  - The statement that the score function “does not explicitly take into account the underlying geometry” is misleading: the score on a Riemannian manifold uses the Riemannian gradient, which depends on the metric. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
  - The paper says “compact manifolds” but actually requires compact, connected, orientable manifolds (otherwise the volume form and inverse of exp map may not be well defined; e.g., hyperbolic spaces do not fit). (Reviewer 2)  
  - Notation is not introduced: boldface/capitalized notation and \(U\) in Equation 1 are not defined. (Reviewer 2)  
  - “Inverse of the exponential map” is not globally well-defined (e.g., at the cut locus, or in \(SL_2(\mathbb{R})\) the exponential map is not surjective). (Reviewer 2)

- **Originality and novelty concerns:**  
  - The class of processes in Equation 3 is not new; it corresponds to “Fermi bridges” in stochastic analysis (arXiv:1604.05182, 2105.13190, 2104.03193), though they have not been used for generative modeling. (Reviewer 3)

- **Computational efficiency claims are questionable:**  
  - The Riemannian divergence required for implicit score matching scales poorly to high-dimensional manifolds. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The claim that the approach is much cheaper because it avoids two-sided simulation and divergence computation is misleading: reusing the same samples in forward and backward matching trades off variance for simulation cost, and the high variance likely requires multiple simulations. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Training equation (11) still requires many logarithm map evaluations, which are expensive on general manifolds. The spectral bridge can speed this up, but finding eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on high-dimensional manifolds is likely infeasible. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The time-rescaled proposal distribution \(q(t)\) is essentially the same idea as in Huang et al. 2022, but with a cruder constant approximation. (Reviewer 2)

- **Experimental evaluation is insufficient:**  
  - Table 1 results are problematic: the earth science datasets have known train/test split sensitivity, and the baseline numbers appear copied from other papers without re-implementation on the same splits. This makes the table non-comparable. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The experimental validation may not cover all scenarios; details of the setup and results are not easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Training time is not extensively discussed or compared. (Reviewer 1)

- **Claims not fully supported by experiments:**  
  - The method is claimed to start from any prior distribution on the manifold, but this capability is not exploited in any experiment. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The claim of scaling to high dimensions is overstated: the highest-dimensional experiment is a Torus of dimension \(10^3\), which is a product of 1D circles (\(S^1\)) and does not test the challenges of true high-dimensional manifolds. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The reviewer asks: is the method tested on higher-dimensional manifolds? (Reviewer 1) – This question is reflected in the lack of high-dimensional experiments beyond product tori.

- **Missing comparisons and ablations:**  
  - No comparison to OT-Flow Matching (Tong et al., 2023) for Euclidean geometries; the reviewer asks whether optimal transport paths could improve numerical stability. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The approach of caching training samples (simulating the SDE once per sample) could also be applied to Riemannian Flow Matching, raising the need for a fair comparison to substantiate faster training claims. (Reviewer 2)

- **Overall benefit unclear:**  
  - The benefit over denoising diffusion models is not convincingly argued: the distance-based loss (replacing score matching) also requires expensive log map computations, and the claim of “superior performance on diverse manifolds with dramatically reduced in-training simulation steps” is not fully validated. (Reviewer 3)
