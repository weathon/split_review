## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PGMF, a teacher-student distillation framework for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis (MSA). The teacher (PGMF-Teacher) uses MLLM-generated prompts to bias attention maps via conditional alignment modules (visual-to-language, audio-to-language) and achieves state-of-the-art performance on CH-SIMS and competitive results on CMU-MOSI. The student (PGMF-Student) distills this knowledge and performs well without prompt guidance at inference, using fewer parameters.

### Strengths
- Leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for MSA is a promising and worthwhile direction.
- Embedding the teacher-student paradigm into MSA is a well-considered approach.
- The specific method of prompt-guided attention transfer from MLLMs is original and has not been seen in other papers (Reviewer 3); it could be utilized for more tasks by the research community.
- Ablations are included regarding the effect of prompt guidance, loss weight hyperparameters, and extension to different architectures (though some are delegated to the appendix).
- Multiple-run averages along with standard deviations are reported, improving evaluation rigor.

### Weaknesses
- The conditional alignment module appears to simply take a dot product of two attention maps after applying attention mechanisms; one reviewer questioned its theoretical or empirical novelty beyond existing methods. Another reviewer noted that the attention scores in Figure 3 range from -0.0004 to 0.0006 (very close to zero), which raises concerns about model soundness—possibly due to the Hadamard product operation making scores extremely sparse or near zero.
- The multimodal fusion module concatenates features from different modalities and feeds them into a transformer encoder, which is a common and widely adopted design.
- Overall, one reviewer (score 5) considered the implementation simplistic and lacking sufficient innovation to substantiate a significant contribution, while another (score 6) noted that distilling from large models to small models is a common practice and the idea is relatively straightforward.
- The selection of baselines is limited: no baseline from 2024 is included. Given the rapidly evolving field, more recent baselines are strongly recommended.
- The "Effect of Each Component" analysis appears superficial and lacks depth; one reviewer raised the concern that it may have been generated by AI without sufficient refinement.
- Since the goal is to train a smaller student model, a comparison of parameter counts with other baselines is missing and would help substantiate efficiency claims.
- The evaluation could include the larger and more recent CMU-MOSEI dataset.
- The rows labeled “Searched best seed” in Tables 1 and 2 should be removed, because the seed is not a tunable hyperparameter.
- The “Related work” section has been delegated to the appendix; it should be part of the main paper to clearly position the work.
- Sections 2.4 and 2.5 are verbose, and some equations (especially Equation 11) are uninformative about the actual fusion operation; these sections need revision for concreteness and clarity.
