## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PackNets, a neural encoder-decoder architecture for 2D circle packing in arbitrary shapes, inspired by variational autoencoders. An encoder maps each circle index to a point; a constraint block ensures the point lies within the shape (parameterized by a radial function b(θ)); a perturbation block adds scheduled noise sampled from a Beta distribution to push circles apart; a decoder predicts the circle index from the perturbed point. Training minimizes cross-entropy so that circles become separable. The method is tested on packing congruent and non‑congruent circles into circles, squares, pentagons, and one arbitrary shape. Packing density is compared against the DCCP method and best‑known solutions from Packomania. The authors claim the first solution for packing circles in arbitrary shapes and report competitive densities, outperforming DCCP in some non‑congruent cases.

### Strengths
- **Original approach.** Reframes packing as an encoder‑decoder task, drawing an analogy to VAEs and potentially leveraging the VAE literature (Reviewers 1,2,4).  
- **Arbitrary‑shape support.** The method extends prior work (Jose et al. 2024) from packing in a circle to general outer shapes (Reviewer 1).  
- **Handles both congruent and non‑congruent circles.** This is a significant advance over many traditional methods that are restricted to equal sizes (Reviewer 2).  
- **Perturbation scheduling.** The gradual, scheduled Beta‑distribution noise helps separate circles without extra computational overhead (Reviewers 1,2).  
- **Competitive packing density.** Reported densities approach best‑known solutions on Packomania and outperform DCCP in non‑congruent settings, albeit marginally in some cases (Reviewers 1,2).  
- **Clear writing and figures.** The architecture and components are explained well; Figure 2 is especially appreciated (Reviewers 1,3,4).  
- **Well‑motivated application.** Circle packing is a practically relevant problem (Reviewer 3).  
- **Text is largely free of syntactic errors** (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Constraint violation undermines comparisons.** The method does not enforce a strict non‑overlap constraint; circles often overlap significantly. The reported metric (packing density) is only the objective, ignoring feasibility. Comparisons with methods that produce strictly non‑overlapping packings are therefore unfair and the results misleading (Reviewers 1,3,4).  
- **No justification for using neural networks.** It is unclear why centers cannot be optimized directly, or why a decoder/cross‑entropy loss is needed instead of a geometric loss on circles. Ablation studies are completely absent (Reviewer 3).  
- **Scope claim overblown.** (a) The claim of “arbitrary shapes” is contradicted by the radial‑function parameterization b(θ), which only describes star‑shaped domains and cannot capture shapes with holes (e.g., an annulus) (Reviewers 3,4). (b) The method is evaluated only on simple convex polygons and circles; adaptation to highly irregular boundaries is unclear (Reviewer 2).  
- **Sub‑optimality is poorly motivated and explained.** The paper focuses on sub‑optimal solutions without clarifying why, which constraints are relaxed (overlap allowed below a threshold?), or the practical utility of such a relaxation (Reviewer 1).  
- **Lack of statistical reporting.** The reported densities come from individual runs; the method has multiple stochastic components (random initialization, Beta noise, scheduled perturbation). No averages, standard deviations, or number of runs are given, making rigorous comparison impossible (Reviewers 1,4).  
- **No runtime comparison.** Optimization methods trade off solution quality and runtime; runtimes are needed for a fair assessment (Reviewer 3).  
- **Sensitivity to perturbation schedule.** The method’s success depends on careful tuning of the Beta distribution parameters. It is not discussed how to set them or how sensitive performance is (Reviewer 2).  
- **Computational cost.** The process may become expensive as the number of circles grows (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing analysis of strict non‑overlap.** The paper does not test or compare results when the non‑overlap constraint is enforced (e.g., zero tolerance) (Reviewer 1).  
- **Limited to 2D circle packing.** The approach is not demonstrated or extended to higher‑dimensional packing or covering problems (Reviewer 4).  
- **Poor presentation details.** Table 1 is hard to read (too many digits, poorly formatted); references to visual agreement with Packomania solutions are made but those solutions are never shown (Reviewers 3,4).  
- **Limitations are not discussed.** The paper lacks a section acknowledging the above weaknesses (Reviewer 3).  
- **The analogy to VAEs is interesting but superficial** – the perturbation differs in method and purpose from VAE noise injection (Reviewer 3).
