## Merged Review

### Summary
CameraCtrl introduces a plug-and-play camera pose control module for text-to-video diffusion models. It uses Plücker embeddings to encode camera trajectories and injects them via cross-attention into frozen video backbones (AnimateDiff, Stable Video Diffusion). Training combines static-scene datasets (RealEstate10K, Objaverse, MVImgNet) with synthetic data. Extensive ablations validate design choices (camera representation, injection location, training data). The method outperforms MotionCtrl on camera reconstruction and image quality.

### Strengths
- **High practical significance**: Camera control is crucial for video generation, 3D/4D generation, and downstream tasks. Several reviewers note this as a core strength.
- **Clear motivation and simple design**: The paper is clearly written, easy to follow, and the approach is technically straightforward. Code is available. The plug-and-play nature (no fine-tuning of the base model) is appreciated.
- **Thorough and informative evaluation**: Ablations cover every critical design dimension (Plücker vs. alternative embeddings, injection architecture, training data recipe). The method is demonstrated on multiple backbones (AnimateDiff, SVD). Qualitative results show limitations honestly.
- **Solid experimental results**: Performance is better than close competitor MotionCtrl. Results appear promising and solid. The method successfully combines with other control types.
- **Minority reviewer (score 8)**: Emphasizes the paper’s strong contribution and simplicity without bells and whistles, noting that the evaluation is solid and informative, and the method’s components are well motivated.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (R1, R4)**: Using a ControlNet-like network for control signals is not particularly novel; similar problem considered in MotionCtrl. The iteration improves camera encoding, injection, and data but conceptual innovation is limited. R4 notes that the approach is technically incremental.
- **Static-scene training bias (R1, R2)**: RealEstate10K and other static datasets may introduce bias against dynamic object motion. R1: foreground objects in examples show limited movement. R2: generated videos show reduced ability to generate dynamic motions compared to the original baseline (e.g., ODD), though better than other methods. R2 questions why general video datasets (e.g., with estimated camera trajectories from particleSFM) were not used.
- **Object-centric / 360° capability (R2)**: In object-centric generation, object movement is minimal relative to background. R2 asks whether the model can handle 360° camera trajectories (e.g., from front to back of a person) when trained on Objaverse or MVImgNet.
- **Problem formulation concerns (R4) – scale ambiguity**: Camera motion can only be interpreted relative to generated content; translation scale is undefined because scene scale is arbitrary. The model likely does not learn a canonical metric scale. Training data (COLMAP, Objaverse) have inconsistent or arbitrary scales. This undermines the premise of precise camera control.
- **Problem formulation concerns (R4) – framing and alignment**: The relationship between camera motion and content is not specified (e.g., whether a turn-table trajectory places the object at the pivot point). Camera motion is only useful when content and framing are coherent.
- **Problem formulation concerns (R4) – intrinsic parameters**: It is unclear how focal length (intrinsics) is handled. Plücker rays can encode any camera model, but it is not shown whether the model accurately captures focal length, which affects object size and scale interpretation.
- **Evaluation bias (R4)**: The paper manually discards generated videos where COLMAP fails (line 983). This introduces selection bias favoring the method. R4 suggests measuring success rate curves (percentage of reconstructions at various accuracy levels) for fair comparison.
- **Evaluation metrics (R4)**: A new scale-invariant metric is proposed (line 990) instead of using existing scale-invariant metrics from the literature. This is a minor concern but noted.
- **Camera representation discussion (R3, minor)**: The choice of Plücker embeddings could be better motivated. The property of constant embedding along a ray obscures camera origin, requiring the model to solve a system of equations. R3 asks whether representations like (o, d) or (normalized_o, d) would be more directly useful (camera origin readily available). A figure to illustrate would strengthen discussion.
- **Missing comparison (R1)**: Comparison and discussion with recent camera control method VD3D are needed.
- **Applicability to DiT-based methods (R2, question)**: Can the approach be applied to DiT-based architectures that do not separate temporal and spatial aggregation?
- **Writing issues (R1, R4)**: R4 notes a broken sentence structure at line 195. Line 460: unclear whether COLMAP failure is due to generation quality or COLMAP limitations.
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: R3 (score 8) found only a minor weakness (representation discussion missing figure) and a question about Plücker vs. alternative representations. This contrasts with the more fundamental concerns raised by R1, R2, and especially R4 about problem formulation and evaluation.
