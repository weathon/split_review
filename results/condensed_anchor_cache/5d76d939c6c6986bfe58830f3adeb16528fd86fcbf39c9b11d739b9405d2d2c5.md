## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes VLB-IRL, a variational lower bound for inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) derived from a probabilistic graphical model with reward and optimality nodes. The method simultaneously learns a reward function and policy by maximizing the lower bound. Evaluated on MuJoCo and AssistiveGym environments, it shows performance comparable to or better than GAIL and IQ-Learn, particularly in sparse reward settings. Reviewers are sharply divided: one reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) finds the approach novel, elegant, and potentially high-impact, while three reviewers (scores 3, 1, 1; one with confidence 5 who checked math carefully) identify fundamental flaws in the graphical model, incorrect equations, missing justifications, and insufficient evaluation. The stronger negative assessments dominate due to the severity of the technical errors.

### Strengths
- The variational inference approach to IRL is creative and novel; the derivation from the graphical model is elegant and could form a basis for new IRL methods beyond the maximum-entropy formulation (Reviewer 2).
- The approximation of the optimality distribution is discussed in sufficient detail, and the method shows promising results, especially in sparse-reward settings (Reviewer 2).
- Using the RL value function for unshaping the GAIL reward and a KL loss to learn a second reward model appears novel (Reviewer 3).
- The experimental protocol is well described (Reviewer 1).
- The method’s empirical performance is promising (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Graphical model and theoretical foundation are flawed / unconvincing**  
   - The graphical model is unclear: the representation of reward variables and the definition of V<sub>t</sub> (cumulative future reward) are not adequately reflected (Reviewer 1).  
   - The conditional independence of O<sub>t</sub> from s<sub>t</sub>,a<sub>t</sub> given r<sub>t</sub> is not justified; r<sub>t</sub> and r<sub>t'</sub> should be related via reward function parameters, but the graph does not show this (Reviewers 1, 4).  
   - The ELBO in Eq. (4) directly contains the KL term KL[ q(O<sub>t</sub>|r<sub>t</sub>) || p(O<sub>t</sub>|s<sub>t</sub>,a<sub>t</sub>) ], which is non-standard in variational inference. Moreover, the claim that all terms except the KL are constant w.r.t. r<sub>t</sub> makes the minimization nonsensical because p(O<sub>t</sub>|s<sub>t</sub>,a<sub>t</sub>) is arbitrary (Reviewer 4).  
   - The derivation relies on hidden, unjustified assumptions: expert actions are independent of state, and the probability of optimality is conditionally independent of s<sub>t</sub>,a<sub>t</sub> given the immediate reward (Reviewer 3).  
   - It is unclear why reward information alone can indicate optimality; no justification is given for approximating optimality from r (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
   - The overall graphical model design appears flawed, undermining all theoretical contributions (Reviewers 1, 4).

2. **Mathematical errors and incorrect derivations**  
   - Equation 9 erroneously states A(s,a) = σ(A(s,a)), a fundamental mistake; Eq. 10 uses σ(exp(A(s,a))), which is also incorrect (Reviewer 3). Additionally, Eq. 9 should read exp(A(s,a)) instead of A(s,a), and the equal sign should be proportional (Reviewer 2).  
   - Equation 6 mislabels the discriminator output as the *true* distribution p(O|s,a), whereas it is an approximation. Consequently Theorem 2 is wrong: it does not bound the actual approximation error. The proof step Eq. (15) is unjustified (Reviewer 3).  
   - Missing expectation over s<sub>t+1</sub> in Eq. 10 implies deterministic dynamics are assumed without mention (Reviewer 3).

3. **Unclear method details**  
   - C<sub>θ</sub>(s,a) is not explicitly defined; it is unclear whether it outputs logits or probabilities (Reviewer 3).  
   - The reward network’s output type is ambiguous: the paper illustrates with a Gaussian distribution but does not state whether a Gaussian is actually used in experiments, nor how the distribution affects the variational bound and policy update (Reviewers 2, 3).  
   - The need to model reward as a random variable conditioned on state-action is not justified; nothing prevents q from conditioning directly on (s,a), making the reward node redundant (Reviewer 2).  
   - It is unclear whether the classifier must be completely retrained each iteration and what the computational cost is (Reviewer 2).

4. **Evaluation shortcomings**  
   - The claimed statistical significance via a t-test is not properly described: no details on the type of t-test, handling of multiple comparisons, or appropriateness given non-Gaussian distributions and unequal variances (Reviewer 3).  
   - Performance is reported using the best policy among 5 seeds, which can favor unstable methods; average performance should be reported instead (Reviewer 3).  
   - No learning curves are provided (Reviewer 3).  
   - For noisy-demonstration experiments, comparisons to methods specifically designed for that setting (e.g., [1]) are missing (Reviewer 3).  
   - Ablations are missing: no analysis of the hyperparameter λ and no ablation to isolate the effect of unshaping the reward with the value function (Reviewer 3).  
   - The paper does not explain the sources of performance improvement over other IRL methods (Reviewer 2).

5. **Presentation and clarity**  
   - Formatting is inconsistent: bold font for s<sub>t</sub>,a<sub>t</sub> is not maintained throughout; O<sub>t</sub> has a bold subscript t (Reviewer 2). There are extraneous periods (Reviewer 2).  
   - The claim that IRL lacks “control as inference” modeling is overstated: maximum-entropy IRL also stems from this model. The paper should acknowledge original work predating Levine et al. (Reviewer 2).  
   - The limitations and future work section is too brief (Reviewer 2).
