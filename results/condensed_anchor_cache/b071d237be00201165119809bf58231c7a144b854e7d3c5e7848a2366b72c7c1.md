## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a characteristic-based neural network (CBNN) for solving one‑dimensional hyperbolic conservation laws. It combines the method of characteristics with the Rankine‑Hugoniot condition: characteristic lines are used for smooth regions and an ODE solver for shock waves. The loss function is motivated by the representation (5) and justified by Lemma 1. Numerical experiments are performed only on Burgers’ equation, covering a single shock, rarefaction waves, and shock‑wave interaction. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (weak reject) and one reviewer rated it 1 (strong reject); all agree that the writing is poor, the experimental evaluation is insufficient, and novelty is indistinguishable from existing work.

### Strengths
1. **Novel combination of neural networks with classical methods.** Using the method of characteristics and the R‑H condition within a neural network framework is a poorly explored idea with potential (R1,R2,R3,R4).  
2. **Theoretical component.** Lemma 1 provides a practical upper bound on the neural‑network approximation error in terms of the loss (R1,R4).  
3. **Numerical coverage of multiple discontinuity types.** Experiments include a single shock, rarefaction waves, and shock‑wave interaction (R3,R4).  
4. **Domain decomposition.** The space‑time region is divided into subdomains based on initial‑value influence, allowing independent solution approximation (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Poor writing and presentation.** The paper has many grammatical mistakes, unclear math, wrong punctuation and capitalization, and is very sparse/short (R1,R3,R4). Missing details make it difficult to understand how the method extends beyond the classical case (R4). One reviewer (score 1) essentially copied the abstract as the “Strengths” section, indicating no original strong points were identified (R2).  
2. **Insufficient numerical experiments.** Only Burgers’ equation is tested. No comparisons with existing methods (PINNs, NDNN of Lorin & Novruzi 2024, Liu et al. 2023) are provided, so the claimed significance is not demonstrated (R1,R3,R4). The experiments do not show that CBNN outperforms or addresses the convergence issues attributed to other methods (R4).  
3. **Unclear role of the neural network and overlapping contribution.** Much of the solution is built with the method of characteristics, shock formation, and the R‑H condition; the neural network’s added value is unclear (R1). The approach appears to overlap significantly with NDNN (Lorin & Novruzi), yet no detailed differentiation or comparison is given (R3). The necessity of developing yet another neural‑network method for this PDE class is not convincingly motivated (R4).  
4. **Theoretical concerns.** Lemma 1 requires λ’(x)>0, but the authors do not justify why this can be assumed without loss of generality (R1,R4). The bound is proved under Assumption 1, which demands a differentiable (hence continuous) initial condition, yet the method targets discontinuous initial conditions (R4). The paper claims theoretical results guarantee accuracy for multiple shock waves, but no supporting evidence is provided (R4).  
5. **Misunderstanding of “shock”.** A discontinuity in the initial condition is a contact discontinuity, not a shock; a shock arises when characteristic lines converge. The paper conflates these (R1).  
6. **Missing implementation details.** No information is given on: neural‑network architecture, training algorithm, number/sampling of data points, how exact solutions were computed (Section 4.2), whether reported MSE is the loss or distance to the true solution, and the grid for L² error (R4). The effect of architecture and hyperparameters on performance is not discussed (R3).  
7. **Limited scope and generalizability.** The method is formulated only for one‑dimensional problems (R2,R4). Extension to higher dimensions faces computational complexity and difficulty managing discontinuities (R2). Heavy reliance on characteristic lines may limit applicability to different hyperbolic equations or boundary conditions (R2).  
8. **Lack of ablation study.** No component‑wise analysis is performed to assess the contribution of each part of the CBNN framework (R3).  
9. **Unanswered questions that expose missing experiments or analyses.**  
   - How does CBNN handle multiple interacting shocks or rarefaction waves? Were specific tests conducted? (R2).  
   - Could PINNs be applied directly on each subdomain? (R4).  
   - Provide evidence for the claim that Lorin & Novruzi (2024) and Liu et al. (2023) have convergence issues under complex circumstances, and show that CBNN does not face the same issue (R4).  
   - How does the choice of neural‑network architecture/hyperparameters affect performance? (R3).  
   - Why is the method formulated only in 1D? What are the main barriers to higher dimensions? (R4).  
   - The claim that accuracy and training efficiency are guaranteed even as the number of shocks increases is unsupported; the proof appears to exclude shocks (Assumption 1) (R4).  
   - Several cross‑references are incorrect (Sections 2.4‑2.6 refer to Sections 3.1‑3.3); Lemma 1 is sometimes called a theorem (R4).  
   - Figures lack axis labels (R4).
