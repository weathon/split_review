## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RECAST, a task incremental learning method that reparameterizes model weights into a set of shared weight templates and module-specific scaling coefficients via a Neural Mimicry pipeline. This dramatically reduces the number of learnable parameters (to fewer than 50 per task). The method is architecture-agnostic and is shown to improve accuracy over several benchmarks when combined with existing incremental learning methods. Reviewers found the idea novel and interesting, and the results on parameter efficiency and accuracy are promising, but raised concerns about experimental scale, missing analyses, unclear writing, and insufficient justification of the method’s incremental learning contributions.

### Strengths
- The idea of incorporating Neural Mimicry into continual learning is novel and interesting, introducing a new pipeline that significantly reduces the number of learnable parameters by decomposing weights into frozen template banks and learning only coefficients.
- The method is architecture-agnostic, with experiments on both CNNs (ResNet-34) and ViT-Small demonstrating effectiveness.
- RECAST reduces the number of learnable parameters to fewer than 50 per task, several orders of magnitude less than competing methods like LoRA.
- The presentation of results focuses on both parameter efficiency and storage alongside accuracy, which is clear and informative.
- RECAST enhances existing adapter-based methods (e.g., Piggyback, CLR), making it compatible and valuable as a supplementary tool.
- The setting is flexible, allowing adjustment of groups *G* and templates *n* to achieve the desired level of model compression.
- The method shows better accuracy with fewer trainable parameters when combined with previous incremental learning methods (e.g., up to 3% improvement over SOTA).

### Weaknesses
- **Scale and applicability to large models:** One reviewer found the experiments too small-scale (models only about 21M parameters) and argued that scalability to large models (e.g., LLMs, ViT-Base/Large on ImageNet) is not demonstrated. The claimed “< 1% parameters” in Fig. 1 is actually a ratio (2×10⁻⁶), not absolute parameters, and it is not shown that this ratio translates faithfully to larger models. Other reviewers also noted that effectiveness on larger models and datasets has not been adequately addressed.
- **Missing core CL metrics:** No forgetting values are reported, which are critical for evaluating continual learning setups.
- **Writing and organization:** Multiple reviewers found the paper poorly organized and unclear. Specific issues include: Section 2 mixes the proposed method with related work; Subsection 2.1 introduces concepts from later sections; the opening paragraphs of Subsections 2.1 and 2.2 are largely redundant; the overview of RECAST (Fig. 2) appears too late (Subsection 2.2 instead of at the start of Section 2); pseudo-code lacks notation explanation; details on obtaining pretrained model weights for Neural Mimicry are insufficient.
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:**
    - No rehearsal-based baselines (e.g., ER, ER-ACE, DER++) are included, which is unfair when claiming strong performance in settings that could use a memory buffer.
    - RECAST appears to be a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method rather than a dedicated incremental learning method; comparisons with other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., prompt-tuning, LoRA, DoRA) are needed.
    - In Figure 3 (LoRA comparison), it is unclear what “CF” stands for. The evaluation should compare LoRA vs. RECAST on the same backbone, but the figure is not clearly described. The parameter range is not fully explored for both methods, and the comparison at the same number of parameters is only partial.
- **Storage scaling questions:** For Table 1, why does the total storage for RECAST + Piggyback/CLR increase linearly with tasks? For CNN-based models, if learnable task parameters are already minimized from *P* to 4×10⁻³ *P*, why can’t storage be further reduced? In Table 2, why does total storage remain unchanged when tasks are added? It should increase with *M + T × 6/8 × 10⁻⁴ Pr*.
- **Training efficiency and complexity:** Wall-clock training time and GPU memory are not reported (Tables 1, 2). The reduced parameter count suggests efficiency gains, but empirical evidence is needed. The training complexity of the Neural Mimicry process itself should also be reported.
- **Weakness of EWC, LWF, and GDUMB in Table 1:** The accuracies for these methods are much worse than ResNet-34; the reason is not explained.
- **Integration with existing methods:** It is unclear how RECAST integrates with previous incremental learning methods (as shown in Tables 1 and 2). The paper does not clarify the integration mechanism.
- **Plasticity vs. stability:** The method does not directly address the balance between plasticity and stability in incremental learning; learning only coefficients with frozen templates may be insufficient if the pretrained data distribution differs from downstream tasks.
- **Difference from Template Mixing:** Technical differences between RECAST and previous Template Mixing methods are not clearly explained, making it difficult to assess novelty (e.g., design of template banks and coefficients, how RECAST supports incremental learning with fewer parameters).
- **Task-order analysis:** It is unclear whether task order is fixed as listed or randomized across runs. Per-task accuracy is not reported, so it is unknown if accuracy varies between early and late tasks. The impact of task order on weight adaptation would be an interesting study.
- **Class Incremental Setting:** The paper exclusively focuses on the Task Incremental Setting without justification for not exploring the more challenging Class Incremental Setting.
