## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CIFAR-10-Warehouse, a dataset of 180 subsets (300–8,000 images each) built by querying multiple search engines (Google, Bing, Baidu, 360, Sogou, Pexe, Flickr) and prompting Stable Diffusion 2.1 with color and style variations of the 10 CIFAR-10 classes. Subsets cover natural images, cartoons, and unrealistic object–context combinations, resized from 224×224 to 32×32 for experiments. The testbed supports two tasks: model accuracy prediction (AutoEval) and domain generalization (DG). Extensive benchmarking over many classifiers and methods is provided, along with cleaned annotations. The paper also discusses potential uses for denoising, unsupervised domain adaptation, and OOD detection.

### Strengths
- **[Critical – R1, R2, R3, R4]** The dataset is a substantial, realistic contribution: it has many more domains (180) than prior testbeds, includes both real-world web images and synthetic generative images (Stable Diffusion), avoids synthetic corruptions, and provides cleaned annotations.
- **[Critical – R2]** The evaluation is comprehensive: many models were tested across different subsets on both tasks, and the appendix is detailed.
- **[Important – R1, R2, R3, R4]** The DG setup is thoughtful (e.g., using alternative sources like Yandex) and the experiments reveal interesting insights, including limits of current state-of-the-art accuracy prediction and domain adaptation methods.
- **[Important – R1, R4]** The paper is well-written, clean, and easy to follow; the dataset creation process is clearly explained and respects privacy/licenses.
- **[R1 – minority positive]** Reviewer 1 (score 8) was highly positive, emphasizing that the dataset is thorough and high-quality, and that the benchmarking points out failure modes that can inspire future method improvements. The other three reviewers gave score 6.

### Weaknesses
- **[Critical – R2]** The use of MAE of prediction scores as the main metric in Table 2 is problematic: Figures 2 and 9 show no correlation between prediction scores and accuracy, while Figure 7 shows high Spearman rank-order correlation. Hence MAE reveals little about predictiveness.
- **[Important – R2]** Conclusions from Task 1 need improvement:
  - Claiming the benchmark is “harder” is weak because corruptions can be made arbitrarily harder by increasing magnitude; the value should be in highlighting what aspects networks fail to capture.
  - The claim that predictions are more consistent across classifiers may be an artifact of limited variation (only color changes) rather than diversity; cartoons and SD images do show classifier variation.
- **[Important – R2, R3, R4]** There is little analysis of the dataset itself. Specific missing analyses:
  - How many visually distinct domains are there? Possibly only 36 (12 color palettes × [real, cartoon, SD]), not 180, as images across search engines look similar (R2). The performance drop on Baidu/360 may be due to fewer images, not domain gap (R2).
  - More justification is needed for dividing domains by color and cartoon/no-cartoon rather than other styles (R3).
  - Empirical comparison with existing datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10-Cs) is needed: are performance rankings aligned? Are there contradicted conclusions? (R3)
  - Potential biases from search engine data collection are not discussed: query design for balanced class representation, geographical/cultural biases (R4).
- **[Important – R4]** CIFAR-10 is small (low resolution, 10 classes); findings may not generalize to larger datasets like ImageNet or WILDs or to tasks beyond image classification (object detection, segmentation, video analysis).
- **[Important – R2, R4]** Some missing release details: will scraping code, classifier code, and trained models/features be released? (R2). A concrete roadmap for future versions is not provided (R4).
- **[Important – R4]** Additional state-of-the-art baselines (e.g., GVRT, VNE) could be included for a more comprehensive comparison.
- **[Minor – R1]** An unfinished sentence at the last line of page 6 (typo).
