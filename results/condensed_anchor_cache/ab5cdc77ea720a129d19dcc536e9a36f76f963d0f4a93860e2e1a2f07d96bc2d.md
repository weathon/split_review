## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a generative learning framework for non-convex optimization problems with multi-valued input-solution mappings, using a rectified flow (RectFlow) model to learn input-dependent solution distributions instead of a single-valued mapping. The authors provide theoretical characterization of optimality loss and runtime complexity, and empirically demonstrate improvements over recent NN schemes, GANs, and Diffusion models on synthetic and combinatorial optimization problems.

### Strengths
- **Novel application and concept.** Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 highlight the novel application of the RectFlow model to this problem and the innovative idea of learning input-to-solution distributions rather than input-output mappings. Reviewer 2, however, considered the application of RectFlow too straightforward and lowering novelty (see Weaknesses).
- **Clear motivation and background.** Multiple reviewers (2, 3, 4) praise the clarity of the problem formulation, motivation for solution generation, and the writing overall.
- **Theoretical contribution.** Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 note the value of the theoretical analysis, including the theorem bounding the optimality gap in terms of dataset quality, vector field approximation error, ODE discretization steps, and number of samples, as well as the runtime complexity tradeoff.
- **Strong empirical evidence.** Reviewers 1 and 4 find the experiments convincing, showing that the proposed method significantly outperforms baseline NN schemes in optimality while preserving speedup.
- **Important problem.** Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 emphasize the significance of solving multi-valued non-convex optimization problems with applications in engineering and science. Reviewer 4 adds that the paper could pave the way for future research.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficiently diverse or complex experiments.**  
  - Reviewer 1 states the experiments are demonstrated only on two toy problems, lacking evidence on real engineering problems.  
  - Reviewer 2 argues the first simple function estimation is oversimplified, and the second combinatorial optimization uses three problems with simple constraints, not showcasing constraint handling under complex scenarios. They recommend adding more complex problems with multiple constraints.  
  - Reviewer 4 notes the experiments could benefit from including applied examples (e.g., AC optimal power flow, real-time scheduling, wireless communication), though still considers the evidence sufficient.
- **Missing comparisons to other generative models for optimization.** Reviewer 2 points out that many existing generative models for optimization also learn solution distributions than single-valued mappings (e.g., GNN-guided predict-and-search, DIFUSCO, SurCo) and should have been compared.
- **Novelty concern (minority reviewer).** Reviewer 2 (score 5) argued the method is too straightforward, lowering novelty, and that the empirical results do not support the theory for complex problems with multiple constraints. In contrast, reviewers 1, 3, and 4 found the concept novel and the contribution significant.
- **Insufficient clarity in motivation and adoption framework.** Reviewer 1 notes the motivation for using a generative framework is not clearly explained (benefits over alternatives) and calls for a practitioner’s guide on requirements for adoption.
- **Incomplete explanation of experimental details and results.**  
  - Reviewer 1 asks for probabilities of samples in Figure 3 and clarifies that Figure 3 would be clearer with a heatmap.  
  - Reviewer 3 asks what the “speedup” column in Table 3 is compared against and whether the solutions from Section 4.3 were used in Section 6.  
  - Reviewer 3 requests a more expanded and clarified explanation of experimental results overall.
- **Lack of ablation study and sampling improvement.** Reviewer 3 suggests expanding Section 4.3 (inexact NN vector field and ODE discretization errors) with empirical solutions and an ablation study if multiple solutions exist. Reviewer 3 also asks whether the proposed solutions in Section 4.3 were actually used to generate results.
- **Unclear parts in theoretical proof.** Reviewer 4 points out that the derivation from equation 27 to 28 and from 31 to 32 is unclear, and that Lipschitz assumptions should be made explicit in the theorem statement. Reviewers 1 and 3 also note the theoretical parts could be better explained.
- **Missing experiments on larger or complex problems.** Reviewer 2 asks for empirical insights on scalability and constraint handling, and whether the method was tried on large problems. Reviewer 1 similarly notes the lack of real-world problem demonstration.
- **Questions about the framing of multi-valued mappings.** Reviewer 2 questions the authors’ claim that existing work focuses on single-valued mappings, noting that considerable research estimates solution distributions with diversity techniques. This overlaps with the missing comparison weakness.
- **Question about non-convex vs convex multi-solution mappings.** Reviewer 2 asks if convex problems (e.g., multi-solution TSP) also have many one-to-many mappings, seeking clarification on the problem focus.
