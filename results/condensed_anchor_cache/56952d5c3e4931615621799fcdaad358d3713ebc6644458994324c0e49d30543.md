## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a training method (Duet) to improve clean accuracy for L2-norm certified defense models. It uses a pre-trained vanilla model as a guide, applies SVD to weight matrices, and transfers knowledge by aligning rotation matrices. The method is tested on CIFAR-10 with a single epsilon and three baselines. All four reviewers rate the paper 3 (Reject) with similar overall assessments: the idea is interesting and the topic is important, but the experimental evaluation is far too limited, the presentation is poor, and claims are not well supported. Reviewers consistently note missing baselines, datasets, and comparisons; unclear algorithmic details; and grammatical/technical errors. There is no major disagreement among reviewers; all point to insufficient evidence for the claimed improvements.

### Strengths

- The paper studies the important and open problem of improving clean accuracy for certified defense models, contributing to the tradeoff between clean and certified accuracy (R1, R2).
- The insight that rotation matrices from SVD can be changed while preserving certification guarantees is novel (R1). The idea of using a pre-trained vanilla model as a guide is novel and could help bridge standard and certified training (R2, R3).
- The statistics in Table 1 on rotation matrix similarity between naturally trained and certified defense models are informative (R1).
- The method shows an improvement in clean accuracy against the chosen baselines, though at the cost of a small reduction in certified accuracy (R1, R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Highly limited experiments**: All results use only a single dataset (CIFAR-10), a single epsilon, a single model, and only three relatively old baselines (LMT, BCP, LocalBCP). Many important baselines are missing, including: IBP, CROWN-IBP, COAP, GloroNet/TRADES-like tradeoff, simple combined objectives (standard + certified training), and Randomized Smoothing (R1, R2, R3, R4). The paper does not test on MNIST or TinyImagenet (R3). Existing work [Hu et al., 2023] provides much more comprehensive experiments (R1, R2). The core results in Tables 2 and 3 come from a single model only (R1).
- **Unsupported claims in Section 4.2**: The section “Why does certified robustness training lead to a decrease in clean accuracy?” is not backed by any experiment or theory (R2, R4). Reviewers question how these claims can be empirically verified (R2).
- **Missing related work**: Several works studying the accuracy gap in certified training (e.g., Lee et al., 2021; De Bartolomeis et al., 2023; Jovanović et al., 2022) are not mentioned. This gap is not well motivated in the introduction (R3).
- **Poor presentation and unclear writing**: The draft is rough; many sentences are hard to understand. Examples include:
  - “It causes the clean accuracy of the main inference task because the DNNs model is too regularization” (R3, R4).
  - Repeated sentence in Section 4.4 (R1, R4).
  - Seven-line ungrammatical sentence on page 6 (R1).
  - Algorithm 1 is written informally and contains multiple errors: missing initialization of `Lip_cer` (line 8 does not work first time), `U_cer` and `V_cer` used before being computed (line 11), `z*_cer` computed but never used (line 13) (R4). Variables like “matrix A”, “z_hat”, and “z_{y_0}” are undefined or have typos (R4).
- **Method unclear on key details**: 
  - It is not explained how `U_cert` and `V_cert` are obtained—does the algorithm actually run SVD and differentiate through it? (R1, R4).
  - The paper targets L2-norm certified defense only, but this is not stated early (R1).
  - No details on how convolutional layers are handled (R1).
  - How the approach applies to 1-Lipschitz layers (e.g., Cayley, SLL) is unclear (R1).
- **Mixed empirical results**: The improvement over baselines is modest and comes at the cost of reduced certified accuracy (R4). LocalBCP outperforms the proposed method in some settings (e.g., on clean accuracy), and while LocalBCP requires more time/memory, the proposed method also adds overhead compared to vanilla BCP (R4). This makes the claimed advantage arguable (R4).
- **Additional presentation and conceptual issues**:
  - The description of randomized smoothing is inaccurate (“adding a smooth classifier to the input” instead of adding noise) and its dismissal as “probabilistic” ignores that the threshold can be set as required (R4).
  - There appears to be a misunderstanding of certified robustness: “inputs satisfying certified robustness may still be adversarial examples” is incorrect; certification guarantees no adversarial example within the epsilon ball (R4).
  - In Figure 2, clean test accuracy is higher than clean training accuracy, which is unusual and unexplained (R4).
