## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces AGFN, combining Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNet) with adversarial training and a hybrid decoding strategy (similar to epsilon-greedy) for solving TSP and CVRP. GFlowNet generates diverse solution trajectories; a discriminator distinguishes raw outputs from locally-optimized trajectories; training alternates to improve solution quality. Experiments on instances of size 200, 500, and 1000 nodes show strong performance and generalization on synthetic and real-world benchmarks, outperforming popular construction-based neural solvers, especially on larger instances.

### Strengths

- Novel combination of GFlowNet with adversarial training for VRPs, addressing scalability and solution diversity. *One reviewer (R3) considers this a creative extension; two reviewers (R1, R2) note that the integration of existing components (GFlowNet, adversarial training, epsilon-greedy) is not significantly novel but acknowledge the application.*
- Hybrid decoding strategy is well-suited for generating diverse trajectories and helps avoid local optima (R1, R2).
- Good performance and scalability, especially on larger instances (200, 500, 1000 nodes) compared to baselines (R1).
- Detailed experiments with thorough explanations of results, aiding future reference (R2).
- Well-written paper with clear explanations of the AGFN framework (R3).

### Weaknesses

- Insufficient innovation beyond integrating existing components; no significant improvements to the adversarial learning framework for generating diverse samples (R1, R2). One reviewer (R3) disagrees on novelty.
- Missing key technical details: how to generate the sparse edge set (R1); explanation of the reward function and the meaning of the loss function (R2).
- Lack of practical guidelines for hyperparameter selection, e.g., how to pick optimal value of 𝒫 for hybrid decoding (R1); general hyperparameter sensitivity analysis missing (R3).
- No experiments on larger scales (e.g., 5000, 10000 nodes) to assess scalability limits and potential failure (R2).
- Unclear advantage of using GFlowNet for problems like CVRP where the optimal solution is unique; diversity may be less critical (R2).
- Need theoretical or experimental evidence that adversarial training is superior to other diversity-generation methods, or a demonstration of specific improvements to the adversarial framework (R2).
- Code not open-source, hindering reproducibility evaluation (R2).
- More analysis on computational efficiency (e.g., time, memory) and scalability is needed (R3).
