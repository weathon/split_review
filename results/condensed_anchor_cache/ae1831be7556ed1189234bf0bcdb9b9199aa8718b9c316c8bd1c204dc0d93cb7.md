## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a two-stage method to improve chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in vision-language models (VLMs): (1) distill rationales from GPT-4o into a 193 k CoT dataset, then supervised fine-tune (SFT) a VLM (LLaMA3-LLaVA-NeXT-8B); (2) apply Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using correct/incorrect reasoning pairs derived from the model’s own outputs. Evaluations on nine datasets show some accuracy gains. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) was generally positive; the other three (scores 3, 3, 5; confidences 4, 3, 4) were more critical, especially of novelty and the size of improvements.

### Strengths

*   The release of the GPT-4o-generated CoT dataset is likely to be useful for future VLM research (reviewers 1, 3).
*   The two-stage design (SFT then DPO) is conceptually clear and the method is easy to understand (reviewers 2, 3, 4).
*   Results show improved accuracy on several ImageQA benchmarks under both direct and CoT prompting, including marginal gains from the DPO stage (reviewers 1, 4). One reviewer notes that these improvements, while small, are still positive (reviewer 1).
*   Helpful ablations on dataset composition, training, and evaluation methods are provided (reviewer 1).
*   The failure analysis is insightful and potentially useful for other VLM research (reviewer 3).
*   The paper is well-written and the motivation (equipping VLMs with CoT capability) is interesting (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

*   **Novelty is limited.** The distillation of rationales from a large teacher model (GPT-4) and the two-stage SFT+RL pipeline have been widely adopted in prior LLM and VLM work (e.g., STaR for correctness filtering, DPO on correct/incorrect chains). The approach closely follows existing recipes without a technical innovation that distinguishes it (reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 specifically notes that the claimed contributions (dataset, verifying SFT, applying DPO) make the paper read more like a resource paper than a methodological advance.
*   **Improvements are small and of uncertain significance.** Gains from DPO (Tables 6, 7, Figure 7) are quite small, and it is not clear whether they are statistically significant. Improvements from CoT fine-tuning on non-math and chart datasets appear inconsistent. Overall, it is hard to tell whether gains come from the method itself or simply from having more GPT-4o-generated data (reviewers 1, 2).
*   **Scalability concerns.** The method is bottlenecked by the teacher model (GPT-4o) and requires ground-truth answers from existing supervised datasets for the pairwise preference construction. This makes it less suitable for open-domain or complex problems where such annotations are not readily available (reviewers 1, 2).
*   **Lack of discussion on VLM’s missing CoT ability.** Even though the LLM backbone (llama3-8b) may already possess CoT capabilities from its massive text-only pretraining, the paper does not explain why the VLM does not retain this ability or why the CoT was not improved directly in the language backbone (reviewer 4).
*   **Missing related work on language-based CoT reasoning.** The paper would benefit from discussing how LLM research tackles similar CoT challenges in pure language tasks, especially since VLMs use a vision encoder that projects visual information into the language space and may share characteristics with the underlying LLM (reviewer 3).
*   **Insufficient detail in related work.** Prior VLM CoT methods are not discussed enough; for instance, Shao et al. (2024) is described as focusing on object localization, but their CoTs may involve visual regions and are applied to VQA tasks. The differences between this paper’s method and prior VLM CoT fine‑tuning approaches are not clearly articulated (reviewer 1).
*   **Causal attribution in DPO stage.** It is unclear how the method ensures that a correct answer is due to the generated rationale rather than the model’s parametric knowledge. The paper should report what proportion of correct answers are accompanied by an incorrect rationale (reviewer 4).
*   **Unclear experimental setup.** The meaning of “format alignment only” vs. “direct responses only” is ambiguous, and it is not specified which datasets are used to train the “format (1)” model in Tables 3 and 4 (reviewer 1).
*   **Technical contribution vs. resource contribution.** The paper’s main deliverable appears to be the dataset; the technical novelty of the two-stage approach is not convincingly demonstrated (reviewer 3).
