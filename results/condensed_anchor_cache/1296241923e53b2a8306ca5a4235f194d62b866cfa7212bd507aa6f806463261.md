## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses task-level zero-shot adaptation in meta-RL using a Coarse-to-Fine Policy Refinement (CFO) combined with Holistic-Local Contrastive Representations (HLR). CFO leverages language instructions to select skill-specific expert modules (coarse policy) and then refines it via a hypernetwork-generated fine policy. HLR applies contrastive learning from holistic and local perspectives to produce robust task representations. Experiments on Meta-World ML-10 and ML-45 show significant performance gains over baselines (e.g., ~42.3% and ~45.4% higher success rate). While the topic is important and the approach is novel, several reviewers noted missing analyses, limited benchmarks, and insufficient justification for design choices.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a very important problem: task-level zero-shot adaptation in meta-RL.
- The coarse-to-fine policy refinement idea is novel—first capturing general skills via language-guided MoE selection, then adapting with a hypernetwork-based fine policy.
- The contrastive representations (HLR) embed task information in a robust, interpretable way (Fig. 3b), showing hierarchical characteristics of non-parametric task variability.
- Combined with CFO, the method achieves strong zero-shot adaptation to unseen tasks within just two episodes.
- The two insights—(1) effective adaptation requires general skill understanding, and (2) robust task representations are key for task-aware policy—have potential to motivate future research.
- The paper is clear and well-written (one reviewer noted this, though another found motivation unclear).
- Experimental results on Meta-World show significant improvements in success rate and return over baselines.

### Weaknesses
- The approach requires task category labels and task IDs during training (loss ℒ_LCR uses category index i and task index j), limiting applicability when such metadata is unavailable.
- The method depends on pre-trained expert modules and HyperNetworks, which are absent in baselines like VariBAD, LDM, and SDVT. Comparisons of training time, parameter count, and memory usage are needed for fairness.
- The framework is complicated, and the motivation for combining CFO and HLR is not clearly explained. One reviewer found the paper clear, but another noted insufficient rationale for why these components synergize (beyond ablation).
- The expert policy training procedure is unclear: is it jointly trained? If so, how are unused experts prevented from learning nothing? Visualization and quality evaluation of expert policies are missing.
- Missing comparisons with well-known meta-RL methods MAML and PEARL; the paper only compares with VariBAD, LDM, and SDVT.
- Experiments are limited to Meta-World and only two fixed train/test splits (ML-10, ML-45). Concerns about overfitting and lack of generalizability testing (e.g., swapping train/test tasks) are raised.
- Several necessary analyses are absent:
  - Visualization and comparison of HLR representations against previous methods.
  - Analysis of MoE activation patterns (which experts are selected for which instructions) to understand behaviour.
  - Ablation study details are insufficient: what replaces CFO or HLR when removed? (e.g., removing HyperNetwork, what policy is used?).
  - Does the method also improve variation-level adaptation (though easier)? No results provided.
- Notation issues confuse derivation (e.g., definition of x_ijk^- in Eq. 7 and Eq. 8, relation between c_i^n and x_ij, dependence of HCR/LCR losses only on φ). These affect clarity.
- Figure 4 and Figure 5 share the same x-axis unit (1e8 steps) but represent different processes (evaluation vs. training); the rationale is unexplained.
- Return discrepancy: Table 2 (“Ours” on ML-45 Test tasks, 2911.7±105.1) is larger than Table 1 Episode 2 (2893.3±107.5), yet success rates match; this inconsistency is not explained.
- Appendix D.1 is incomplete.
- Code is not provided, which would help validate the claimed large improvements.
- A discussion of limitations is missing from the paper.
