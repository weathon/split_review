## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a minimax game formulation for Bayesian neural networks, pairing a deterministic network f(x, μ) with a stochastic perturbation f(x, μ) + r ξ(ρ), which is likened to a Brownian motion of f. The method is applied to supervised and representation learning settings. Experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 examine how embedding dimension, batch normalization, bias, and noise perturbation influence a learned radius r, interpreted as a bias-variance tradeoff and used for out-of-distribution detection. The work is seen as an innovative application of minimax to BNNs (reviewer 2, rating 3), but the majority of reviewers (1,3,4; ratings 1,1,1) found the writing and presentation far below publishable standard, with many undefined terms, unclear formulations, and insufficient baseline comparisons, making it difficult to assess contributions or validate claims.

### Strengths
- **Innovative use of minimax in BNNs**: Applying the minimax framework to Bayesian neural networks is an interesting approach that extends beyond deterministic setups (adversarial training, GANs) (reviewer 2; similar sentiment in reviewer 4, citing Buening et al. 2023).
- **Broad robustness evaluation**: The paper covers a range of robustness aspects – embedding dimensions, noise impacts, bias, batch normalization – across several datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10) (reviewer 2).
- **Clear problem motivation**: The motivation for the work is clearly stated (reviewer 2).
- **Experimental validation for some effects**: Results on the impact of batch normalization and bias on robustness provide empirical support for the theoretical framework (reviewer 2).
- **Number of numerical experiments**: A substantial set of numerical experiments is presented (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and presentation**: The paper reads like a draft, not a finished manuscript (reviewers 1,3,4). Specific issues: leftover yellow highlighting in abstract (reviewers 1,3); leftover “Contributions” and “Acknowledgments” sections (reviewer 1); truncated Appendix (reviewer 1); unchanged LaTeX templates (reviewer 4); inconsistent cross‑references (e.g., “Fig. IV” vs “Figure 4”, “Table VII” vs “Table 4”) (reviewer 3); unlabelled axes in figures (e.g., Fig. 3) (reviewer 4); underexplained captions (Fig. 1, 4) (reviewer 4); numbering misalignments in figures (reviewer 4). English is often confusing and below publishable standard (reviewer 1).
- **Undefined or late-defined notations**: The method is not clearly presented. Equation (1) uses “pre()” which is never defined (reviewers 1,3,4). Variables μ, ρ, h, r are introduced only after Equation (2), almost a page later (reviewers 1,3,4). The definition of ρ as “the randomness or variance shape of ξ” is too vague (reviewer 4). The summand in Equation (2) contains nothing indexed by i (reviewer 4). MCR (minimax coding rate) and ΔR are not properly explained (reviewers 3,4). “Brownian motion” in this context is undefined (reviewer 3).
- **Missing or weak problem formulation**: The minimax nature of Equation (1) is not explicit – it does not look like a minimax problem (reviewer 3). The roles of the two players and their rewards are not described (reviewer 3). The “restriction condition” (line 90) is unexplained (reviewer 3). The term “case 1” and “case 2” for training r (golden search for every new ξ vs only in minimum process) is not explained (reviewers 1,3). “pre()” and constraint unclear (reviewer 1).
- **Lack of baseline comparisons**: There are no thorough comparisons with traditional BNNs, adversarial training, or other related methods (reviewer 2). The only comparison is to LDR in Table 1, where the proposed method (“NetD”) performs worse, and the authors remark that accuracy “is not the main focus” (reviewer 4). No ablation or deterministic counterpart is shown to demonstrate robustness improvement (reviewer 4). Tables 2 and 3 list values of r for different datasets but without any comparative method (reviewer 3).
- **Limited experiment scope**: Testing on more complex datasets beyond MNIST/Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10 would strengthen generalizability (reviewer 2).
- **Superficial discussion of out-of-distribution detection**: The OOD detection section merely lists results without exploring why performance behaves as it does or discussing implications (reviewer 2).
- **Bias and batch normalization effects not explained**: The claim that bias and batch norm undermine robustness is stated without intuitive or numerical insights (reviewer 4); reviewer 2 asked why certain configurations disrupt robustness and how that insight can be used.
- **Unclear contributions**: The contributions are difficult to parse – e.g., “enough embedding dimension” is vague (reviewer 3); “a well-trained neural network can use stochastic sampling to find suitable radius” is unclear (reviewer 1). The phrase “uncorrelated representation” appears only in the introduction, not properly defined (reviewer 1). The paragraph relating to rate-distortion loss (Yu et al. 2020) is confusing (reviewer 1).
- **Multiple confusing sentences and claims**: Example: claiming VAE can be framed as a minimax game is unwarranted (reviewer 1). Lines 154–157 contradict themselves on whether MinMax BNN is worse or better than LDR (reviewer 3). Line 113 and line 179 use unclear phrasing (“figure of the minimum process of radius”, “is given in Fig. IV”) (reviewer 3). Histogram in Fig. 4B has a weird shape (reviewer 3); line 232 “Histogram of trained models” – histogram of what? (reviewer 3); “We design” should be “We design” (reviewer 3).
- **Soundness concerns**: Given the presentation issues, reviewers rated Soundness 1 or 2, Presentation all 1s except another 1, Contribution all 1s except reviewer 2 gave 2 (reviewer 2), but overall majority found the work not publishable (reviewers 1,3,4). Reviewer 1 rated Confidence 4, reviewer 3 rated Confidence 5 (very confident in assessment of low quality).
- **Unanswered method details**: “How does the proposed minimax BNN approach compare to adversarial training in robustness performance?” (reviewer 2). “What are the computational implications of using minimax optimization for BNNs?” (reviewer 2). “What is ‘golden search’ for case 1?” (reviewers 1,4). “Is NetD the result of the proposed method? If so, it performs worse than LDR” (reviewer 3).
