## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces two structural encodings, GKSE and MKSE, derived from the Green and Martin kernels (long-term random walk behavior). These are integrated into GRIT and evaluated on graph classification/regression, achieving state-of-the-art on 7/8 benchmark datasets (molecules, DAGs, images). The paper provides theoretical expressiveness analysis and shows MKSE has unique power beyond RRWP.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated extension of RRWP by considering steady-state behavior of random walks (R1, R2, R3). The approach is intuitive, simple, and a natural extension (R2).
- Theoretical analysis demonstrates expressiveness: MKSE is shown to be uniquely expressive beyond RRWP (R4, R1). Also proven more powerful than shortest-path encoding (R2).
- Empirical evaluation covers diverse graph types – molecular (with non-aperiodic substructures like benzene rings), directed acyclic circuits, and image graphs (R3, R4). GRIT+GKSE/MKSE achieve SOTA on 7/8 datasets (R4, R1).
- Paper is well-written, self-contained, with good combination of mathematical reasoning and intuitive figures (R1, R2, R3, R4). Presentation is clear despite minor typos.

### Weaknesses
- Performance gains over GRIT+RRWP are minimal and often within one standard deviation; statistical significance is not established (R3: “most numbers within standard deviation of next best existing model”; R4: “improvement over GRIT+RRWP appears to be minor”).
- The explanation for why GKSE/MKSE are superior (lines 479-509) is unconvincing. Reviewer 1 provides alternative interpretations where RRWP is preferable: (1) RRWP yields decoupled representations (e.g., multi-hop neighbor separation) while GKSE/MKSE are more uniform; (2) RRWP’s oscillation on non-aperiodic substructures helps identification, whereas GKSE/MKSE are less sensitive; (3) on DAGs RRWP gives sparse representations (allows K tuning) while GKSE/MKSE produce dense redundant information. These interpretations contradict the paper’s claims and are not adequately refuted (R1). This reviewer does not reject the contributions but asks for clarification and additional evidence (R1).
- Experiments only test GKSE/MKSE with GRIT. No evaluation on other graph transformers (GPS, NAGphormer, etc.) to demonstrate broad applicability (R2, R3, R4). Also missing appropriate baseline DAG Transformer on DAG datasets (R3). Table 3 lacks a +RRWP baseline (R3).
- No ablation on combining GKSE and MKSE (concatenation) or combining with RRWP (R2, R3). This is a missing experiment to assess potential complementary benefits.
- Efficiency concerns: Table 10 shows GKSE/MKSE are computationally more expensive than RRWP (R4). No analysis of memory usage or training time impact, a key practical issue (R2, R4). Given similar performance, motivation for preferring GKSE/MKSE over RRWP is weak (R4).
- Missing details: (a) description of GRIT architecture (to make paper self-contained) (R4); (b) how encodings are integrated – GKSE/MKSE are defined over node pairs, so likely used as edge-level structural encodings, but this is not clarified (R4). Minor typos (e.g., “matrixwith” l.126, incorrect “GKSE” in Thm 3 part 2 l.290) and citation format confusion (e.g., (13) vs [13]) (R2, R4).
- Connection to hitting times and commute time distances is not discussed; these are closely related and could provide further insight (R4).
- Reviewer confidence differs: R3 (confidence 5) is very confident about minimal improvements; R1 and R2 (confidence 3) are less certain. The two reviewers scoring 8 are positive, while the two scoring 5 are critical of the significance and generalization of the results. This disagreement is noted.
