- Decision: Accept

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SVBench, a benchmark for evaluating Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) on streaming video understanding. It comprises 49,979 QA pairs from 1,353 videos, organized into temporal multi-turn QA chains (4-5 contextual questions per video segment) with temporal linkages across segments. A semi-automated annotation pipeline (GPT-4 generation, human verification) creates these chains, covering six relationship types. Evaluation uses dialogue mode (within segment) and streaming mode (across linked segments, 80% chance of jumping to temporally linked questions), scoring on five dimensions via LLMs (METEOR, GPT-4 Score). The authors also propose StreamingChat (built on InternVL2 with InternViT, MLP projector, InternLM2) fine-tuned with LoRA for long-context video. Results: GPT-4o leads (66.29% dialogue, 58.17% streaming); StreamingChat best among open-source models (59.41% dialogue, 53.90% streaming); all models score below 60%, indicating significant room for improvement. Four reviewers rated the paper: scores 6, 8, 8, 8. The minority reviewer (score 6) raised stronger concerns about evaluation methodology, while the other reviewers (score 8) found the benchmark valuable but also noted several missing analyses and comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Novel technical contribution:** The semi-automated annotation pipeline (LLM + human verification) for temporal multi-turn QA chains is methodologically sound and produces large-scale, high-quality data. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Comprehensive empirical validation:** Evaluation spans 14 models (open- and closed-source), with multiple metrics (METEOR, GPT-4 Score) and detailed ablation studies comparing single-instance vs. multi-turn QA performance. (Reviewer 1)
- **Temporal linkage concept:** The design of temporal linkages between QA chains forces models to maintain context across video segments, mimicking real-world streaming scenarios and addressing a real gap in video understanding benchmarks. (Reviewer 1)
- **Practical significance:** Streaming video understanding is a challenging and relevant real-world problem; SVBench fills a clear gap in existing benchmarks that focus on isolated, single-instance text inputs. (Reviewer 2, 3, 4)
- **Well-organized presentation:** The paper provides clear explanations of SVBench, QA chain structure, temporal linkages, and evaluation framework, making it easy to follow. (Reviewer 2, 4)
- **Introduction of StreamingChat:** The proposed model significantly outperforms open-source LVLMs on SVBench and achieves competitive performance on other vision-language benchmarks, serving as a strong baseline and starting point for future research. (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Extensive model comparison:** The study compares 14 models, offering valuable insights into current capabilities and limitations in streaming video understanding. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Complete solution (benchmark + model):** The paper provides both a benchmark and a model, forming a complete evaluation framework. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Heavy reliance on LLMs for evaluation without human validation:** LLMs are used both to generate annotations and to evaluate model outputs, creating a circular dependency. No human validation study is provided to confirm that automated scores align with human judgments. This is a significant methodological concern that could mask biases. (Reviewer 1 – minority reviewer, score 6; also echoed by Reviewer 4). The evaluation also does not explore alternative LLM evaluators, hyperparameter sensitivity (temperature, repetition), or multiple-run variability. (Reviewer 1). Additionally, using GPT-4 Score is costly and not fully trustworthy; open-source language models for semantic evaluation (e.g., computing distances in semantic space or prompting open-source LLMs) should be explored. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing human performance comparison:** The paper does not compare model performance to human annotations, limiting our understanding of the gap between current models and human-level streaming video understanding. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing analysis of model size and input length impact:** The experiments do not systematically analyze how model size (e.g., InternVL2 has 1B–72B versions, Video-LLaMA2 has 72B) or the number of frames processed affect performance. Also, training data quality and quantity across models are not analyzed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Overlooked important long-video models:** Several recent models capable of long-video understanding (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, LongVILA, Long-LLaVA, Oryx) are not included in the evaluation, weakening the comprehensiveness of the comparison. (Reviewer 2) – Reviewer 3 separately requests results on Flash-VStream and Video-online. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing video length distribution statistics:** The paper lacks detailed statistics on video durations (average, median, range), making it difficult to assess suitability for different architectures and computational constraints. Potential biases from a disproportionate number of short or long videos could skew results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient differentiation from prior streaming models (e.g., Video-online):** The training method of StreamingChat is described at a high level (interleaved multi-image training), but a detailed comparison of architectures, training objectives, loss functions, and temporal dependency handling with Video-online is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of latency and inference speed metrics:** For a streaming video understanding benchmark, metrics like FPS, overall latency, and computational cost breakdown (feature extraction, language processing, temporal reasoning) are crucial for assessing real-time applicability but are not provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **No sample data to demonstrate benchmark quality:** While an LLM-based check is used to rank QA chain annotations, no concrete data samples are shown to visually demonstrate the quality and significance of the benchmark. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential data leakage concern:** Videos come from publicly available datasets; the paper does not address how models in the evaluation might have encountered this data during training. (Reviewer 2, Q1)
- **Information sparsity in long videos not addressed:** The mechanism for constructing meaningful QA chains in videos with sparse key events is not described, and there is no discussion of how to handle low-density information scenarios. (Reviewer 2, Q2)
- **Effect of audio not analyzed:** It is unclear whether videos retain audio tracks, and whether audio assists in answering questions in SVBench (reviewer 2, Q3). The maximum video processing time of StreamingChat is not reported. (Reviewer 3, Q1)
- **Missing comparisons with Flash-VStream and Video-online:** Direct comparison with these latest streaming video models on SVBench is absent, which would further highlight the advantages or limitations of the proposed benchmark and model. (Reviewer 3, Q2)
- **Inference speed and resource consumption curves not provided:** Reviewer 3 requests data on how inference speed and resource consumption (e.g., GPU memory usage) vary with video length. (Reviewer 3, Q3)
