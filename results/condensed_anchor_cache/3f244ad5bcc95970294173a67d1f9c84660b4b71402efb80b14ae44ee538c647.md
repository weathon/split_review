## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents EAC, a prompt-tuning framework for continual spatio-temporal graph forecasting guided by expand and compress principles. Three reviewers rated the paper 8, citing its novelty, strong motivation, clear methodology, and impressive experimental results. One reviewer rated it 3 and raised substantial concerns about the experimental design, missing comparisons, and insufficient detail. While all reviewers acknowledged the practical relevance and the clever use of a frozen backbone with a dynamic prompt pool, the critical reviewer questioned the completeness of the evaluation and the validity of some conclusions.

### Strengths
- Novel prompt-tuning paradigm for continual spatio-temporal forecasting, integrating dynamic prompt pool adjustments to handle streaming data while preserving past knowledge.
- Methodology supported by both empirical and theoretical analysis, with clear explanations of the expand and compress principles.
- Efficiency through freezing the base STGNN and tuning only a few prompt parameters, leading to reduced training overhead.
- Universality: EAC can be combined with different STGNN architectures and performs well on various spatio-temporal data types (traffic, weather, energy).
- Clear organization, well-written paper with effective figures and tables that complement the textual explanations.
- Addresses a highly practical problem: dynamic sensor deployment and evolving spatio-temporal patterns.
- Reasonable design that balances knowledge preservation (fixing backbone) with adaptation to new data (updating prompt pool).

### Weaknesses
- Missing comparisons with upper-bound setups: full historical training (retraining on all accumulated data) and separate models trained independently per period. The paper should discuss the computational cost, memory requirements, feasibility under changing graph structures, and the trade-off between reduced performance and computational efficiency. Reviewer 4 further notes that separate training may be more storage-efficient (only latest model) and potentially more accurate, so the justification for the trade-off is needed.
- Missing comparisons with other recent continual learning techniques: reinforcement learning (Xiao et al., 2022), data augmentation (Miao et al., 2024), and similar prompt-based methods [1,2,3] mentioned in related work. The paper should provide reasons for these omissions and a detailed discussion of how EAC differs from those approaches, especially regarding handling dynamic graph structures, node-level prompts, and mitigating parameter inflation.
- Need for analysis of domain differences in results (Section 5.2). The discussion should cover the unique characteristics of weather, traffic, and energy datasets (e.g., smoothness of traffic vs. volatility of energy) that might influence model performance.
- Efficiency and scalability concerns: the performance of EAC is heavily influenced by dataset scale (Section 5.4). Deeper analysis is required on how the number of nodes and temporal length affect training time, memory usage, and prediction accuracy, including explicit scalability comparisons with other methods. Parameter bloat from the prompt pool is a related issue that needs discussion. Long-term effectiveness is also questioned: Figure 5 (few-shot) shows RMSE increasing from 24 to 28 (over 10% degradation), and a similar trend is suspected in non-few-shot scenarios. The core paper should discuss trade-offs between performance and parameter growth, computational resources for large-scale deployments, and performance degradation over extended time spans.
- Many experimental details are missing. Specifically: hyperparameter settings, network architectures, and training procedures for baselines; data preprocessing steps, feature selection methods, and statistical properties of each dataset. More recent baselines from 2023-2024 should also be considered.
- Design of the prompt pool is not clearly explained. It is unclear what the prompt pool contains, how exactly prompts are utilized within the model, and how the system handles the incorporation of new sensors in dynamic environments.
- The choice of pre-training backbone is stated to be crucial; it is not discussed whether larger-scale STGNN backbones yield better effectiveness.
- The paper does not consider scenarios where the graph shrinks (e.g., removal of sensors or monitoring stations). Why was this scenario not included in comparisons? The model’s adaptation in such cases should be addressed.
