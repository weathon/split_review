## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes EG-SAT, an Equivariant Graph Self-Attention Transformer that integrates attention mechanisms with Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions (ACSFs) to capture higher-order geometric interactions (radial and angular) in 3D molecules. The model aims to improve scalability and maintain roto-translational invariance. Experiments are conducted on QM9 and MD17 datasets. While the integration of attention with ACSFs is a potentially interesting direction, the reviewers unanimously find critical shortcomings: lack of novelty (equivariance and graph transformer are already common), outdated and incomplete baseline comparisons (missing many recent methods), insufficient ablation and complexity analyses (with unsupported scalability claims and an inconsistency regarding angular triplet complexity), and writing/presentation issues. The paper’s contributions are not well-supported, and its experimental validation is weak.

### Strengths
- The integration of attention mechanisms with Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions (ACSFs) is interesting and represents a potentially promising direction for future research.
- The paper provides a detailed theoretical foundation on symmetry, group representations, and equivariance, with supporting proofs in the appendix.
- The framework is applicable to multiple molecular property prediction tasks (QM9, MD17) and the implementation details (hyperparameters, architectural specifications) are clearly documented, aiding reproducibility.
- The paper is well-organized and written in a clear, accessible manner, providing comprehensive background knowledge on equivariant graph neural networks and molecular learning.
- The model attempts to address the scalability limitations of traditional ACSFs through attention-based mechanisms.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient Novelty and Contributions:** Multiple reviewers note that the claimed contributions are not novel. Encoding geometric information to achieve equivariance/invariance is already common in models like SchNet, DimeNet, and SphereNet; introducing irreps is unnecessary for an invariant GNN. Using a graph transformer architecture is also widely done. Scalability via attention is already present in graph transformers; the necessity of the specific gating/GRU mechanism is not justified. Overall, the paper lacks significant novelty and its contribution claims are not supported.
- **Outdated and Incomplete Baseline Comparisons:** All five reviewers criticize the baseline selection. Methods such as SphereNet, Equiformer (V1 and V2), LEFTNet, SaVeNet, Geoformer, and Molformer are missing from QM9 and MD17 comparisons, even though some (e.g., Equiformer) are cited. This makes the reported “state-of-the-art” claims invalid. Additionally, ACSFs themselves are not included as a baseline. The selection criteria for baselines are unclear, and the comparison methods differ between datasets without justification.
- **Missing Ablation Studies:** No ablation studies are conducted to evaluate the contribution of individual components (e.g., angular information, attention mechanism, gating). Reviewers specifically request ablations showing the benefits of angular information integration.
- **Computational Complexity Analysis is Flawed and Unsupported:** The paper claims linear complexity with respect to edges, but this is inconsistent with the use of angular information (β_ijk) which inherently involves all possible triplets (O(n³) with respect to nodes). No justification is given for how the model maintains linear complexity. Moreover, there is no empirical comparison of computational cost (runtime, memory footprint) with baseline methods, despite claims of improved scalability and suitability for high-throughput screening.
- **Motivation and Clarity of the Research Problem:** The specific research problem is unclearly defined. The limitations of ACSFs are mentioned but not analyzed in sufficient detail. Excessive background (e.g., irreps, symmetry groups) is included without clear connection to the proposed method, obscuring the main contributions. The paper reads more like a review, with the method only presented on page 7.
- **Equivariance and Chemical Validity Not Verified:** There is no proof or visualization that the attention mechanism preserves chemical validity (e.g., physical plausibility of predictions). Reviewers request visualizations or case studies demonstrating that the model achieves equivariance.
- **Questionable Experimental Validity and Limited Scope:** The experiments are only on small molecules (QM9, MD17). There is no demonstration of generalizability to larger molecular systems or different chemical structures. The paper’s title and introduction emphasize scalability, but no such experiments are provided.
- **Writing and Formatting Deficiencies:** Multiple presentation issues are noted: the size of Figure 1 and font of Figure 2 need adjustment; table captions should be above the table; Section 4 has a redundant subsection label “4.1”; the abstract uses “their” without a clear referent; and there is an error in Equation 5 where d_ij should be d_jk. These issues make the paper difficult to read and not of top-conference quality.
- **Lack of Sensitivity and Justification Analyses:** No hyperparameter sensitivity analysis is provided. The gating mechanism and its improvement over standard ACSFs are not explained. The challenges and improvements from integrating self-attention with Transformers are not discussed.
