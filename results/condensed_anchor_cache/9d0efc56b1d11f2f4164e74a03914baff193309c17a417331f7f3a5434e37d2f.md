## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) and proposes that a signed mask (binary sparsity mask with parameter sign information) can transfer generalization potential to a randomly initialized network. It introduces AWS, a variant of learning rate rewinding (LRR) that encourages low error barriers via interpolation of normalization layer parameters. AWS eliminates the need to retain trained normalization parameters. Experiments on CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet with various architectures (MobileNetV2, MLP-Mixer, ResNet, DenseNet) show that the signed mask alone (with random magnitude initialization) can approach dense network performance.

### Strengths
- **Pioneering focus on signed mask**: The paper is the first to systematically study the role of signed masks in LTH. Instead of requiring weight rewinding or specific initialization, it shows that a signed mask combined with random initialization can yield winning tickets (Reviewer 4).
- **Addresses normalization layer dependence**: AWS removes the reliance on trained normalization layer parameters—a major limitation in prior LRR-based methods—by encouraging linear mode connectivity between the AWS subnetwork and its counterpart with initialized normalization parameters (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Empirical breadth**: The method is evaluated across multiple datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet) and architectures (MobileNetV2, MLP-Mixer, ResNet, DenseNet) with detailed experimental setup, supporting the claim that signed masks transfer generalization (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Clear exposition**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear motivation, a good overview of LTH issues, and a simple AWS variant (Reviewer 2).
- **Practical relevance**: Finding a signed mask may be easier in practice than finding ad-hoc initialization, making LTH more applicable (Reviewer 4).
- **Valuable problem**: The search for winning tickets is an open and important topic in sparse network training (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Two reviewers (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3) consider the change over standard LRR trivial, with insufficient research novelty. The core insight is specific to normalization issues; the AWS modification is a slight interpolation variant (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). In contrast, Reviewer 4 views it as pioneering.
- **Restricted scope of ImageNet experiments**: Only MobileNetV2 and MLP-Mixer are evaluated on ImageNet, both with accuracy below 70%. It is unclear if the method generalizes to more powerful networks (e.g., ResNet-50, Vision Transformers). Following the LRR baseline (Renda et al., 2020), experiments with ResNet-50 on ImageNet are expected (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation on sparsity**: Table 1 lacks sparsity ratios; it is unclear which sparsity levels correspond to the remaining parameter ratios in Figure 3. Additional sparsity ratios and behavior at different levels (especially for ImageNet) are needed for comparison with standard LRR (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete empirical support**: 
  - The CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet experiments lack a table analogous to Table 1 that clearly states sparsity levels and performance (Reviewer 2).
  - Training epochs (100 for CIFAR, 120 for ImageNet) may be insufficient for full convergence (Reviewer 3).
  - More comprehensive empirical studies with larger networks and more sparsity ratios are required to support the main claims (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Figures unclear**: In the main results figures, it is ambiguous which line represents the proposed method versus baselines and how the proposed method outperforms others (Reviewer 3).
- **Notation inconsistencies**: In Line 184, the LRR subnetwork should be denoted as \(\mathcal{A}(\theta_T^{LRR}, m_T^{LRR})\) (as in Line 436) rather than \(\theta_0^{LRR}\). Also, it is unclear whether \(\theta_0\) (Line 172) is the same as \(\theta_{init}\) (Reviewer 4).
- **Assumes presence of normalization layers**: The method explicitly handles normalization parameters, which limits generalization to networks without such layers (e.g., LeNet, VGG16 without batch normalization). Testing on such architectures is recommended (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability concerns**: The method requires careful handling and interpolation of normalization parameters, which may complicate scaling to very large models or datasets (Reviewer 1).
- **Dependence on LRR pre-training**: Although the claim is “signed mask is all we need,” the signed mask itself is obtained through multiple iterations of LRR. Ideally, future work could derive the mask directly from a pre-trained dense network (Reviewer 4).
