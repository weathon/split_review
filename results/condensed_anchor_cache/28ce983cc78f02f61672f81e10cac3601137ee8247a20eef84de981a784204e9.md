## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LOQA, a decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithm for general-sum games. LOQA belongs to the LOLA family and assumes that opponents sample actions proportionally to their Q-values. The method aims to foster cooperation without computing meta-game solutions. Experiments on the Iterated Prisoner’s Dilemma (IPD) and the Coin Game show improved computational efficiency compared to POLA, with state-of-the-art performance at reduced cost. Reviewers acknowledge the relevance and clarity of the work but raise concerns about limited novelty, restrictive assumptions, insufficient evaluation, and missing comparisons. Ratings span 3, 3, 3, 5, and 6; one reviewer (rating 6) is notably more positive about the contribution to scaling LOLA, while others (ratings 3) find the contribution incremental and question the assumptions and generality.

### Strengths
1. **Computational efficiency**: LOQA reduces the computational cost of LOLA-based methods, avoiding meta-game solutions and achieving faster wall-clock time than POLA (demonstrated in Figs. 3 and 4) – a step toward scaling this line of research (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
2. **Clear exposition**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and the method is clearly motivated with a plausible underlying assumption (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
3. **Detailed related work**: The related work section covers LOLA and meta-learning approaches to social dilemmas thoroughly (Reviewer 4).
4. **Sound experiments**: The experimental validation includes IPD (Fig. 1) and Coin Game, with statistically significant results; the analysis of training dynamics and scaling behavior is informative (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).
5. **Empirical validation of tit-for-tat**: LOQA is shown to learn tit-for-tat-like cooperative strategies in IPD (Fig. 1) (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited baseline comparisons and environments**: 
   - Only one baseline (POLA) is compared on the Coin Game; M-FOS, which also addresses LOLA’s computational cost, is omitted despite being effective on the same environment. Additional environments (full-history IPD, Chicken) are needed to assess generalizability (Reviewers 2, 4).
   - The scaling evaluation only enlarges the Coin Game grid; more complex and realistic environments (e.g., Meltingpot 2.0 with diverse opponent policies) would strengthen claims of scalability (Reviewers 2, 4). Application to zero-sum games or high-dimensional tasks like StarCraft II is not addressed (Reviewer 5).

2. **Strong assumptions and limited applicability**:
   - LOQA assumes opponents sample actions proportionally to their Q-values and uses discrete actions; it cannot shape opponents that do not follow this assumption and cannot handle continuous action spaces (Reviewer 5).
   - The algorithm requires access to opponents’ Q-values (or their estimates), which typically requires common observation or reward information – an assumption often unrealistic in real-world settings (Reviewer 1).
   - The method is currently only applicable to two-agent environments; this limitation is not acknowledged in the paper (Reviewer 3).

3. **Insufficient novelty and contribution depth**:
   - Using opponent rewards to shape one’s own policy is not novel; the paper does not clearly articulate how LOQA differs from prior approaches that use a similar idea (Reviewer 1).
   - The contribution is incremental: modifying an existing algorithm to improve speed rather than performance, and the resulting method is quite complex. Some reviewers consider it insufficient for a top conference (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). In contrast, one reviewer (rating 6) finds the efficiency improvement a useful contribution toward scaling LOLA.

4. **Missing theoretical analysis and solution concept**:
   - No quantification or bound on suboptimality for each agent under the modified objective (Reviewer 1).
   - No reasoning about the solution concept achieved (e.g., Nash equilibrium of the modified game) or proof that it yields a socially optimal solution of the original game (Reviewer 1).
   - Unclear whether the analysis extends to cases where all agents use LOQA (Reviewer 1).
   - Why does M-FOS fail on the Coin Game? No intuition provided (Reviewer 2).

5. **High variance and instability concerns**:
   - LOQA shows significant variance across seeds (Fig. 4); the number of seeds (3) is insufficient to assess robustness and should be increased (Reviewer 4).

6. **Missing discussion of alternative approaches**:
   - The related work omits other MARL methods for prosocial equilibria such as reward redistribution, mediation, contracts, and similarity-based equilibria (Reviewer 3).

7. **Technical details and implementation questions**:
   - The approximation of the opponent’s Q-value as an empirical return (Section 5.1) is questionable; a 1-step or n-step TD estimate could yield better bias-variance tradeoff (Reviewer 3).
   - The information structure assumed (e.g., whether each agent observes the full state and opponent actions) is not explicitly defined, affecting the optimality of the resulting policy (Reviewer 1).
   - A log-scale y-axis in Fig. 3 would improve clarity of scaling trends (Reviewer 4).
