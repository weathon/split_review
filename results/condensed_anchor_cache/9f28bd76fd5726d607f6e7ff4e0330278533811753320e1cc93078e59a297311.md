## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a Bayesian Neighborhood Adaptation (BNA) framework that models the number of hops in GNN message passing as a beta process, jointly inferring the plausible neighborhood scope with GNN parameter optimization. The method is evaluated on homophilic and heterophilic benchmarks, showing competitive performance with several GNN variants. However, reviewers disagree sharply on the significance and efficiency of the contribution, with one reviewer (score 1) finding the performance improvement modest compared to the computational overhead.

### Strengths

- **Novel idea**: Framing neighborhood scope selection as a Bayesian inference problem using a beta process prior is original and differs from typical HPO/NAS approaches.
- **Thorough empirical analysis**: Evaluation covers homophilic and heterophilic graphs, small and large datasets, over-smoothing analysis, and computation time (Table 5).
- **Competitive performance**: BNA improves or matches state-of-the-art GNN variants (e.g., ResGCN, GAT, JK-Net) across multiple aspects; theoretical analysis of over-smoothing supports its expressivity.
- **Rigorous theory**: Mathematical proofs for expressivity improvements are provided; variational inference handles the intractable beta process prior.
- **Adaptability**: Method can be applied as a plug-in to different base GNN models to capture long-range relationships.
- **Clarity**: Methodology and figures clearly explain the model formulation; tables comprehensively compare baselines.

### Weaknesses

1. **Modest performance gain vs. high computational cost** (severe criticism from Reviewer 2; also noted by Reviewer 1).  
   BNA requires 3–5× more training time (Table 5). The claim that this is cheaper than cross-validation over model depths is not validated under the same time budget. One reviewer found the improvement insufficient relative to the overhead.

2. **Missing baselines and comparisons**.  
   - No comparison with Bayesian GNN methods (e.g., those discussed in Section 2.3) in the experiments.  
   - No comparison with prior works that achieve adaptive depth efficiently (e.g., differentiable methods).  
   - Results in Table 1 report best/second-best across all architectures, but per-architecture comparison (baseline vs. +BNA) is needed; e.g., ResGCN is competitive with ResGCN+BNA given standard deviations.

3. **Weak evidence for neighborhood scope determination**.  
   There is no synthetic dataset with known ground-truth scope to demonstrate that BNA correctly infers the neighborhood scope. All empirical benefits are indirect.

4. **Insufficient modelling intuition for the beta process prior**.  
   The choice of prior is not justified regarding its strengths/weaknesses; only a decay assumption (low ν_j reduces weight of distant nodes) is presented.

5. **Unclear definition and potential technical issues**.  
   - It is unclear whether π_l represents probability of messages from nodes *up to* l hops or *exactly* l hops (due to augmented adjacency matrix with self-loops).  
   - The symbol ⊗ (line 204) is not defined; vector/matrix dimensions are missing (e.g., \(\mathbf{H}_l \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times O}\)).  
   - Potential over-squashing: Theorem 2’s derivation (line 854) suggests feature sampling reduces message passing, which may exacerbate over-squashing for long-range tasks.  
   - Potential over-fitting: PubMed requires 6-hop aggregation but is typically a short-range task; no analysis is provided.

6. **Need for ablation on residual connections**.  
   The theoretical analysis (Res+BNA vs. Res alone) does not compare NoRes+BNA with NoRes; residual connections are not required in all models (e.g., GAT, JK-Net were tested without them).

7. **Weak literature review** (Section 2).  
   Descriptions of prior works list algorithms without discussing their relevance, strengths/weaknesses relative to BNA. Exception: Section 2.3.

8. **Scalability and robustness questions** (raised by Reviewer 3).  
   - How does BNA scale to larger datasets (e.g., ogbn-mag, ogbn-papers100M)? Memory and computational cost are not evaluated for such sizes.  
   - How robust is the beta process to noisy or missing edges?  
   - Extension to dynamic graphs is not discussed; adaptation to evolving structures remains open.

9. **Minor errors/typos** (listed by Reviewer 1):  
   - Use of `\cite{...}` inside parentheses instead of `\citep{...}`; space missing after “beta process” (line 178); “right-hand” instead of “left” (line 239/240); Equation (9) uses \(\mathbf{H}_0\) instead of \(\dots\); “AU-ROC” vs. “AUC-ROC” (line 488); “with a graph convolution” (line 108).  
   - Bold face missing for beta process samples in Equations (6) and (8).
