## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies how instruction diversity enables LLM generalization. Through controlled experiments (string replacement, abstraction rewriting, code generation) and fine-tuning of specialist and generalist models, the authors argue that cross-domain diversity—not data volume alone—is decisive for instruction-following performance. Reviewers acknowledge the well-designed experimental framework and the practical focus on dataset collation, but raise concerns about novelty, the separation of instruction-following from reasoning, the validity of small-scale toy experiments (GPT-2), limited model sizes (≤7B), missing ablations and quantitative guidance, and contradictory results in Table 1. Two reviewers (scores 5, 5) are more positive; two (scores 3, 3) are more critical.

### Strengths
- The focus on trade-offs and dynamics between specialists and generalists is relevant and compelling (R1).
- The experimental design is cleverly constructed for controlled study of instruction-following, using string replacement, abstraction, and code generation tasks (R1, R4).
- A substantial number of controlled experiments are conducted with clear, detailed setups (R4).
- The paper challenges the assumption that simply expanding data volume improves performance, highlighting the superior benefits of semantic diversity (R3).
- Practical guidance on dataset collation is offered, contributing actionable insights for building robust, generalizable models (R3).
- The string-based toy experiments (Section 2.2) are an interesting way to decouple instruction-following from other capabilities (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear connection between research gap and findings:** The introduction discusses "decomposition of reasoning and knowledge retrieval in LLMs" (Line 51) but then shifts to data diversity for specialists and generalists (Lines 80–88); the link is not clarified (R1).
- **Paper structure is hard to follow:** No brief overview of the experimental setup at the end of the introduction; Figure 1 appears on Page 1 but is not referenced until Page 8 (R1).
- **Missing quantitative guidance:** For specialist models, how much general data is needed to enhance them effectively for a given task? (R1). For generalist models, no systematic recipe for domain, length, use case, or persona distribution is provided (R2).
- **Confounding domain scaling with data size:** In Section 5, it is unclear how the authors controlled for the impact of domain scaling versus data size when claiming greater domain diversity benefits generalists (R1).
- **Novelty concern:** The viewpoint that data diversity benefits instruction tuning is not new—foundation model tech reports already use diverse instruction-tuning data (R2). The coding experiments in Section 4.2 yield findings very similar to prior works (e.g., Bukharin & Zhao 2023; Zhang et al. 2024) without offering new insights (R3).
- **Validity of toy experiments with GPT-2:** Section 2.2 trains GPT-2 (a pre-LLM-era model) on simple string tasks; it is unclear whether observations scale to larger models (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct) or newer architectures, making conclusions unreliable (R2, R3).
- **Limited model scale and scope:** Experiments are mostly on 7B models; larger models trained on larger and more diverse corpora may not show the same sensitivity to instruction-tuning diversity (R2). The base LLM for Section 5 (fine-tuning generalist LLMs) is not specified (R2).
- **Conceptual issue with isolating instruction-following from reasoning:** The paper isolates IF from reasoning, but these are joint capabilities—IF can be part of reasoning and is a general capability that includes coding, math, etc. (R3).
- **Contradictory results in Table 1:** Some diverse mixtures underperform less diverse ones (e.g., Base Budget 60 + OSS(14) + Alpaca(0.5) + CoT(0.5) is lower than + OSS(14) + Alpaca(1) + CoT(0); Base Budget 60 + OSS(0) + Alpaca(7.5) + CoT(7.5) is lower than + OSS(0) + Alpaca(15) + CoT(0)), contradicting the claim that diversity improves performance (R4).
- **Missing ablation:** Results for combining all three domains (e.g., +UI(6) + OO(6) + AL(4)) are omitted; exploring this setting would provide interesting insights (R4).
- **Incomplete coverage for CodeQwen (Figure 6):** The impact of scaling mixtures of specific and general data is not comprehensively explored; additional findings with more diverse data are needed (R1).
- **Missing relevant citations:** References on training specialist and generalist models that emphasize mixing instructions from general and specific domains (e.g., Yuan et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b) are not included (R1).
- **Minor typo:** Line 50 has incorrect citation format (should be (Dong et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024b)) (R1).
