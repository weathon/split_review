## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Refine-PPI, a framework for predicting changes in binding free energy (ddG) upon mutation in protein–protein interactions. It introduces a masked mutation modeling (MMM) strategy to hallucinate mutant structures from wild-type denoising pretraining, and a probability density cloud network (PDC-Net) that extends EGNN to sets of Gaussian distributions to capture atomic uncertainty and dynamics. The method is evaluated on SKEMPI.v2. The core ideas—hallucination and PDC-Net—are considered interesting, but the presentation, motivation, experimental validation, and clarity of contributions receive substantial criticism.

### Strengths

- **PDC-Net is a novel and relevant extension of EGNN**: Generalizing EGNN to sets of Gaussians provides innate uncertainty estimates and appears to be a new contribution (not previously seen by reviewers). Results in Section 4.3.3 support the approach.
- **Simultaneous prediction of mutant structure and ddG**: Considering both mutant geometry and dynamics/flexibility during binding is a promising direction.
- **Chain cluster split in the benchmark**: Splitting by protein chains rather than structures is a good practice that avoids data leakage (Reviewer 2).
- **Clear methodology description**: The paper provides a step-by-step breakdown of the Refine-PPI framework, making the motivation and technical details easy to follow (Reviewer 3).
- **Potential significance**: The ability to hallucinate mutant structures and model atomic uncertainty could be important for applications where mutant structures are sparse (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of clear theme and integration**: The paper jumps between hallucination, MMM, and PDC-Net without clearly defining the main contribution or how components integrate. The application area (PPI, conformation change, free energy) remains obscure (Reviewer 4).
- **Motivation for MMM is missing**: Why is learning to denoise wild-type structures advantageous for ddG prediction? The reasoning is not provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Notation and presentation are confusing**:
  - Algorithm 1 symbols ($\mathcal G_t^{\text{WT/MT}}$, $\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_t^{\text{WT/MT}}$) are undefined; link to Eq. (1) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - Variables in Sections 2 and 3 are poorly defined; a notation table is needed (Reviewer 3).
  - “Masking” and “denoising” are used interchangeably, but Algorithm 1 does not show a conventional masking operation (Reviewer 2).
  - Coordinate updates in Algorithm 1 appear unused; their relationship to the hallucinated mutant structure is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - The confusing sentence “the resulting $x^{\text{WT}}$ does not carry gradients with no backpropagation at this phase” is unexplained (Reviewer 2).
- **PDC-Net derivation and implementation are incomplete**:
  - Eq. (5) and (6) treat mean and variance updates as uncoupled via independently learned $\phi_\mu$ and $\phi_\sigma$; Appendix B.2 partially corrects this, but a full derivation of equivariant updates for Gaussian distributions is missing (Reviewer 1).
  - It is unclear whether Gaussians are treated as probability densities or rigid bodies (Reviewer 1).
  - The equivariance proof (Eq. 11) contains questionable equalities; if $\Sigma$ is always diagonal, the notation could be simplified (Reviewer 2).
  - Huber loss in Eq. (2) is not defined for vectors—is it applied component-wise? Its use may break equivariance; a theoretical guarantee is needed (Reviewer 4).
  - Proof in Appendix D only covers the main-text variant (Reviewer 1).
- **Integration of PDC-Net with MMM is poorly explained**: How does PDC-Net fit into the overall workflow (Algorithm 1)? This is not clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Empirical evaluation lacks rigor**:
  - No error bars, confidence intervals, or multiple runs in Table 1 (Reviewer 2, 4). RMSE/MAE variance across predictions should be reported; cluster-cross-validation variability is needed (Reviewer 2).
  - Pearson correlation is the main metric, but it is unclear why a linear relationship between predictions and ground truth is assumed (Reviewer 2).
  - Statistical tests for superiority over baselines are missing (Reviewer 2).
  - Difference to other methods could arise by chance (Reviewer 4).
  - Results only on SKEMPI.v2; method should be tested on additional datasets (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablations and analyses**:
  - Contribution of PDC-Net alone is unclear: substitute EGNN with standard distance/angle encoding and compare (Reviewer 1).
  - No validation of how hallucinated mutant structures influence ddG accuracy (Reviewer 3).
  - Ablation study strategies (initializing/corrupting masked regions) lack justification (Reviewer 3).
  - Running time comparison is needed to assess computational cost of including mutation structure (Reviewer 3).
- **Citation issues**: Luo et al. (2023) is improperly cited; large parts of Table 1 appear to be copied from that work (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear derivations and details**:
  - Eq. (3) requires a derivation or reference (Reviewer 2).
  - Whether the primary variable is the squared or non-squared norm is ambiguous (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 6 caption: “darker color corresponds to more flexible regions” but WT 5F4E shows the core in dark; the shading is unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - Table 6 needs a precise description (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 9 terms (“seed-PDB”, “acceptable prediction error”) are undefined (Reviewer 2).
  - Chapter A.3 (algorithm) is empty (Reviewer 2).
- **Formatting problems**: Overlapping text near Figure 4 and its paragraph (Reviewer 3).
- **Inaccurate analogy**: The quantum-mechanical analogy (lines 238–245) is flawed and should be removed (Reviewer 1).
- **Strong reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (score 5) considered the ideas sound despite the above issues; one reviewer (score 1) found the paper’s presentation and validation severely lacking; one (score 3) had moderate concerns. All concerns are reflected above.
