## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes VPNs, a data-model co-design approach that introduces learnable visual prompts into the pruning pipeline. The method jointly optimizes weight masks and visual prompts while keeping pretrained weights fixed, then fine-tunes both weights and prompts after pruning. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with several pruning baselines, showing performance gains. Reviewers find the idea interesting and the writing clear, but raise major concerns about fairness of comparisons, insufficient experiments (no ImageNet-1k results, limited structured pruning evaluation), lack of latency measurements, missing baselines (e.g., lower-resolution input, learning other parameters without prompts), unclear generality beyond classification, and heavy dependence on pre-trained weights.

### Strengths
- Good writing, presentation, and compelling graphics (clear logic, well-crafted figures).
- Interesting and effective idea: introducing visual prompts into the pruning pipeline to identify better subnets, with joint optimization of masks and prompts.
- Effectiveness validated on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across several pruning methods, showing substantial improvements under the same sparsity.
- Transferability demonstrated by applying VPNs to other pruning methods and showing performance gains.
- Only a small number of learned parameters are needed to improve model performance after pruning.
- The direction of data-model co-design for sparsification is novel and promising.

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparison concern**: The proposed method uses models augmented with visual prompts, while baselines do not. A fairer comparison would apply both the proposed and baseline methods to a model that has already been fine-tuned with visual prompts, to isolate the effect of the joint optimization.
- **Insufficient experiments on large-scale data**: No results on ImageNet-1k, which is the standard benchmark for pruning. This is particularly important because the method uses ImageNet-1k pre-trained weights; its effectiveness on the same dataset used for pre-training should be demonstrated. Experiments only on CIFAR-10/100 (32×32 resolution) do not show whether gains transfer to higher-resolution images. The gains observed by padding CIFAR data to 224×224 may partly come from the pre-trained weights rather than the method itself.
- **Lack of structured pruning evaluation**: Structured pruning is more practical for acceleration, but the paper focuses mostly on unstructured sparsity (which does not accelerate in practice). For structured pruning, speedup should be measured by latency on real hardware, not theoretical FLOPs. The limited structured pruning experiments on CIFAR-100 are insufficient, and comparisons with more recent structured pruning methods are missing.
- **No latency benchmarks for unstructured sparsity**: The paper claims unstructured sparsity benefits non-GPU hardware, but does not report latency for unstructured sparse models on CPU. Such experiments (e.g., on lightweight networks) would make the results more convincing.
- **Missing baselines to isolate visual prompting’s role**: (a) How about learning additional parameters without using visual prompts – would the method still work? (b) How is visual prompting different from data augmentation? (c) Since visual prompting effectively uses lower-resolution input, a baseline with the same lower resolution (no prompt) and less pruning to match the FLOPs of VPNs is necessary to show that the gains come from the prompt, not just from reduced input size.
- **Generality and scope limitations**: The method is demonstrated only on classification tasks. It is unclear whether it can be applied to detection, segmentation, or other tasks. Moreover, visual prompting may deteriorate applications sensitive to resolution (e.g., object detection).
- **Dependence on pre-trained weights**: The method requires pre-trained weights from a larger dataset (ImageNet). Its performance without pre-trained weights, or when trained from scratch, is not explored, limiting generalizability to scenarios where such pre-trained models are unavailable.
- **Need for evaluation on smaller models**: Experiments on resource-constrained models (e.g., MobileNet) are missing. Sparsification is particularly relevant for such architectures, and the method’s effectiveness there would be more informative.
- **Unclear presentation of key concepts**: The emphasis on “data-model co-design” is not concretely explained early in the paper, making it hard to follow. The paper could better clarify the novelty relative to simply adding parameters.
- **Confusing figure labeling**: The use of “our best” curves in figures is unnecessary (the information is already in the VPNs curve) and may cause confusion (e.g., a straight line labeled “ours”).
- **One reviewer (score 3, absolutely certain) is significantly more negative**: This reviewer emphasizes that unstructured pruning does not provide practical acceleration, argues that structured pruning should be the focus with latency-based speedup, demands the lower-resolution baseline, and warns that the method may fail on resolution-sensitive tasks. This contrasts with the other reviewers (all score 5, confidence 4) who find the idea interesting but agree on many of the same weaknesses.
