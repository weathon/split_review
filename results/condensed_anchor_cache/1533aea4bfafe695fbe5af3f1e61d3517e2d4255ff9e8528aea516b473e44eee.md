## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a framework for analyzing neural networks using dynamic and static neurons, defines neuron entropy as a measure of network expressiveness, and proposes the HENP algorithm that combines entropy with norm-based pruning to remove unnecessary parameters during training. The method is evaluated on VGG16/ResNet-18 with CIFAR-10.

### Strengths
- Offers new insights into neural network behavior and expressiveness, with a clear progression from theoretical foundations to practical applications.
- The proposed HENP-1 and HENP-2 approaches outperform other structured pruning methods across a range of sparsity levels, demonstrating effectiveness and robustness.
- Structured pruning approaches are important for improving inference-time computational complexity; the overall idea is sound.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited empirical evaluation**: Experiments focus only on CIFAR-10 with VGG16/ResNet-18; results not shown on larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet-1k) or more recent architectures (e.g., transformers like Swin). Reviewer 3 notes this severely limits generalizability.
- **Inconsistencies between claims and figures**: Section 4.2.1 claims “higher neuron entropy correlates with improved accuracy” in deeper layers (layer 11, Figure 2c), but Figure 2c shows the best-performing model (batch size 64, red line) has the lowest neuron entropy. The claim that the best model “exhibits the lowest variance across different layers” is not shown in the figure. Reviewer 2 adds that Figure 2 is based on a single trial, so randomness is not ruled out.
- **Unclear writing and missing details**: 
  - The transition between Sections 3 and 4 is unclear; Section 3 lacks relevance for later sections.
  - Equation (1) in Section 4.1 has undefined symbols; the peak in entropy for many rectified zones is described as obvious.
  - Use of “pruning frequency” instead of “pruning interval” in Section 5.3 is technically incorrect.
  - Practical implementation details are insufficient: how are the weights \(w_c\) in weighted entropy determined in practice?
- **Limited computational overhead analysis**: No quantitative analysis of the cost of calculating neuron entropy during training (e.g., comparison of training times with/without the calculation, scaling with model size and batch size).
- **Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art methods**: Comparisons are limited to a few structured pruning methods; missing recent entropy-based pruning approaches [A–C] (Spadaro et al. 2023, Liao et al. 2023, Quétu et al. 2024) and other recent pruning techniques [D–G] (DepGraph, Dynamic Structure Pruning, Fluctuation-based, Structural Alignment).
- **Narrow activation analysis**: Only ReLU and Leaky ReLU activations are considered; performance with activations that have more complex regions is untested.
- **Hyperparameter analysis omitted**: The role of hyperparameters in the proposed method is not discussed.
- **Theoretical gaps**: The claim linking higher capacity for capturing complex zones to improved model performance lacks rigorous proof; the 50% calculation in Lines 214–215 is not explained; the type of “stability” referred to in Lines 244–245 is ambiguous (lower variance? constant entropy throughout training?). Reviewer 1 also notes that the claim about best model having lowest variance appears contradicted by Figure 2.
- **Technical novelty concern** (Reviewer 3): Using entropy to guide pruning is not new (references [A–C]), limiting the novelty. This criticism is not raised by other reviewers but is explicitly stated.

*Note: Reviewer 2 is more positive about the empirical results (HENP outperforms structured pruning), while Reviewer 3 is more critical of novelty, writing, and evaluation depth. Reviewer 1 highlights interesting insights but points out contradictions and missing analysis.*
