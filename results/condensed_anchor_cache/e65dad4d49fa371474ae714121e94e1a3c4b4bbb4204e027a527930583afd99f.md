## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents GCML (Grounding Complex Motions using Large Language Model), a framework that generates full-body human motions in 3D scenes from natural language and scene inputs. GCML uses an LLM-based task planner to decompose complex user prompts into simpler subtasks, a 3D Visual Grounding Model (OpenScene) to identify interaction targets and obstacles, and a pretrained motion generation model (OmniControl) to synthesize motion sequences conditioned on subtask descriptions and spatial control information. The method is training-free and leverages off-the-shelf modules. Experiments on the HUMANISE dataset show competitive performance for simple actions, and a new Complex Motion Evaluation Set demonstrates advantages over two baseline methods.

### Strengths

- The framework addresses an important and challenging research question: generating complex, long-term human motions in realistic 3D scenes from text and scene inputs, with potential benefits for animation, gaming, and robotics (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The LLM-based decomposition of complex motions into simpler subtasks is a sensible and effective approach that makes the overall task manageable (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The method can take ambiguous prompts (e.g., "a person feels hungry") and generate a sequence of plausible motions, which is impressive (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed Complex Motion Evaluation Set is a valuable addition to advance research in this area (Reviewer 2).
- The approach is training-free and can directly use readily available pretrained modules (GPT-4, OpenScene, OmniControl) (Reviewer 3).
- The subtask executor considers human-scene interaction by using a target map and avoidance map to reach goals while avoiding obstacles (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments show the method outperforms two baseline methods for scene-text conditioned motion generation (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written with high-quality, easy-to-understand figures (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Motion quality is poor**: Generated motions remain unnatural, with noticeable disjointed sub-motion clip junctions, sudden jitter, foot skating, human-scene penetrations (e.g., "sit on the toilet" sequence), and weird, twisted bodies. Quality is far below that of TRUMANS, Object Motion Guided Human Motion Synthesis, and Human-Object Interaction from Human-Level Instructions (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). [Reviewer 3 is particularly critical, rating the paper 3; Reviewer 1 (score 6) and Reviewer 2 (score 5) also note the jitter and unnaturalness.]

- **No real human-scene interaction**: The presented examples (mopping floor, brushing teeth, watering plants, washing dishes) do not show actual contact between the human and scene objects; e.g., in "washing dishes" the person waves hands in the air without touching the dishes (Reviewer 3).

- **Limited novelty in components**: The visual grounding and motion generation parts are similar to Cen et al. (CVPR 2024), and the subtask planning via LLM is common. The overall structure closely resembles VoxPoser (Huang et al., 2023b) in using an LLM-based planner, vision language model for scene grounding, and python programs to build voxel value maps for trajectory synthesis. The distinction between GCML and VoxPoser is not clearly clarified (Reviewers 2, 3).

- **Missing critical technical details**: The paper lacks specifics needed for reproducibility, including: the prompts used for the LLM task planner; how the initial human location is determined; the code examples provided to GPT for the Language Model Program (LMP); how the target map and avoidance map are built; how the N-frame 22-joint trajectory (L306) is obtained from the LMP; how the minimization in Equation 2 is solved and what variable 'd' represents; and whether the output is a single joint trajectory or full-body 22-joint trajectory (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).

- **OmniControl is scene-agnostic**: The motion generation module does not use scene information, which causes artifacts like scene penetration after the subtask executor produces target trajectories (Reviewer 3).

- **Visualization choices hide artifacts**: The top-down view in video results and occlusion by furniture make it difficult to assess motion quality; side-view visualizations would be more informative (Reviewers 2, 3).

- **Weakness in experiment analysis**: The paper does not discuss failure cases or conditions under which the approach would fail. In Table 2, the method sometimes performs worse than the baseline (Afford-motion), with no explanation. No analysis of robustness to sensor noise or ambiguous language goals is provided (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing related works**: Several relevant prior works are not cited or discussed, including "Synthesizing Long-Term 3D Human Motion and Interaction in 3D" (CVPR 2021), "GOAL: Generating 4D Whole-Body Motion for Hand-Object Grasping" (CVPR 2022), "Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners" (ICML 2022), Points2Plans (2024), and Text2motion (2023) (Reviewers 1, 4).

- **Notation issues**: Inconsistent use of symbol 'N' in Lines 236-237 to represent both 'N points' and 'N frames' (Reviewer 1).

- **No discussion of limitations**: The paper lacks a limitations section, e.g., whether the approach can be applied to real-world robots or its robustness to sensor noise (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing timing analysis**: No information is given about the generation time per sequence or how time is distributed across modules; this would be valuable for assessing practical use in creative workflows (Reviewer 1).

- **Cost map resolution concern**: The cost map resolution of 100×100×100 may be too coarse for indoor rooms (which are typically larger than the tabletop grasping scenario in VoxPoser), potentially contributing to human-scene penetrations (Reviewer 3).
