## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a data-centric test-time method, TT-GREB, to improve GNN generalization under distribution shifts without retraining. It decomposes the test graph into environment-invariant (prototype) and environment-varying (spurious) components via prototype extractor and environment refiner modules, then reassembles them into a "reborn" test graph. A dual contrastive learning objective with iterative optimization is used to refine these components. Experiments on five datasets with four GNN backbones show improvement over existing methods, including data-centric baselines.

### Strengths

- The research question is critical and important: improving GNN OOD generalization for real-world deployment.
- The approach is novel; it focuses on test-time graph manipulation rather than modifying architectures or training strategies. The motivation is clear.
- The idea of separately extracting environment-invariant features and refining environment-varying features is reasonable.
- The dual contrastive learning framework for refining test-time graphs is well-designed.
- The method is practical as it does not require retraining or modifications to the pre-trained GNN.
- Experiments are conducted on diverse real-world datasets and multiple backbone networks (GCN, GAT, GPR-GNN, SAGE), showing that TT-GREB can outperform existing methods.
- Test-time adaptation is more realistic and general than architecture-based OOD methods because it can use hints from test data.

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical concerns about the decomposition:**  
  - The assumption that a graph can be explicitly decomposed into environment-invariant and environment-varying parts (Proposition 1) lacks rigorous justification, support, or proof. The discrepancy metric used to bound environment-varying variables is not formally defined.  
  - There is no guarantee that the proposed Test-time Graph Rebirth (Definition 3.1) actually improves OOD generalization; the definition does not inherently imply any performance benefit. A theoretical analysis or more detailed explanation is needed.  
  - One reviewer (score 3) found the problem setup contrived and the decomposition unrealistic, while other reviewers (score 5) found the idea reasonable. This minority reviewer expresses stronger doubt about the theoretical foundation.

- **Unclear design choices:**  
  - Since Proposition 1 claims the invariant and spurious subgraphs are fully complementary, using two separate modules to extract them seems redundant. The paper does not explain why a single module cannot extract both subgraphs, given their complementarity.  
  - If \( G_{te}' \) is constructed by combining the invariant and spurious subgraphs from \( G_{te} \), it should be identical to \( G_{te} \). The paper does not clarify how \( G_{te}' \) is modified or enhanced beyond a simple recombination.  
  - The rationale for pushing the embedding of \( G_{te}' \) (which contains the spurious subgraph) closer to the embedding of the invariant subgraph is unclear. Why is this alignment beneficial for OOD generalization if \( G_{te}' \) still contains spurious information?

- **Marginal improvement over data-centric baseline:**  
  - The improvements over GTRANS (another data-centric method) are small. On most datasets the gains are marginal, with notable improvements only on the Elliptic dataset (5.5% and 7.1% F1 increases with GPR and SAGE). This raises concerns about the method's general effectiveness and its practical significance given its complexity.  
  - The method appears less effective on the large-scale arXiv dataset. It is unclear whether it can scale to even larger graphs (e.g., millions of nodes). The edge reweighting scales quadratically with the number of nodes, and the iterative optimization requires multiple forward/backward passes through the GNN, leading to high computational and memory costs. The reviewer requests a runtime comparison and discussion of scalability to larger datasets.

- **Method generality and graph-specific challenges:**  
  - The approach seems not inherently limited to graph data. The paper does not adequately address what unique challenges for graph data are being tackled beyond general feature disentanglement.  
  - Unsupervised decoupled learning of invariant features at test time is difficult; the paper does not convincingly show why the method can succeed in this unsupervised setting.

- **Presentation quality issues:**  
  - Figures have small captions and ticks, making them hard to read (multiple reviewers). Figure 4 has inconsistent font sizes. Figure 5 is particularly difficult to observe (reviewer 4).  
  - Figure 1(a) is misleading: in the general graph OOD setting, the test graph is not seen during training, but the figure suggests a domain adaptation setting.  
  - The compared baselines are not the most recent (latest is ICLR 2023). The reviewer requests newer comparisons, e.g., including more recent test-time adaptation or OOD methods.

- **Baseline inconsistency:**  
  - EERM, a method designed to improve GNN OOD generalization, underperforms most baselines in Table 1. The paper does not explain why this happens.

- **Other reviewer questions (incorporated as weaknesses):**  
  - Can the method be extended to other architectures like Graph Transformers?  
  - The performance seems highly dataset-dependent; the method's generalizability is unclear.  
  - The iterative optimization strategy may be computationally intensive for large graphs; can the authors provide more detailed runtime comparisons?
