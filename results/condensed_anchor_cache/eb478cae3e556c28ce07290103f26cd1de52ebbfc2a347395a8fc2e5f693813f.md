## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proves an inverse approximation theorem (Bernstein-type) for nonlinear RNNs, showing that if a sequence-to-sequence relationship can be stably approximated by a nonlinear RNN, then it must have an exponentially decaying memory – extending the “curse of memory” from linear RNNs to the nonlinear setting. The paper also proposes a reparameterization method to overcome this limitation. Reviewers generally found the work well written and the extension valuable, but raised concerns about the clarity of the memory definition for nonlinear RNNs, the convincingness and explanation of the reparameterization, the interpretation of the experiments (especially Fig. 3), and the narrow scope relative to the ICLR audience.

### Strengths

- Well-written and pleasant to read (R1, R2, R3).
- Extends previous results from linear RNNs to nonlinear RNNs, which is interesting and brings added value (R1, R2, R4).
- First paper to study the inverse approximation theorem for nonlinear RNNs (R4).
- Introduces formally useful concepts: a memory function for nonlinear functionals that generalizes the linear case, and a notion of stable approximation that serves as a proxy for being learnable via gradient descent (R2, R3).
- Proof techniques are novel and could potentially be applied to other architectures (R2).
- Experiments support the theoretical findings (R2).
- Inverse approximation theorems address the efficiency of approximation, a practically relevant aspect less studied than existence results (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Minority view on scope and impact (Reviewer 1, score 5, low confidence):** The manuscript is too narrow for ICLR; it is unclear what insight it provides for a broader class of RNNs or for deep learning in general. The stable parameterization section is not convincing and needs a concrete example with a dynamical system or real data.
- **Memory definition for nonlinear RNNs (Reviewer 4, also noted by Reviewer 2):** The derivative-based motivation (“queried memory”) is too mathematical and lacks intuitive explanation. The connection between decaying memory speed and the target’s dependence on early inputs is not explicit; a more direct link to standard notions of influence of past inputs on current outputs is needed. The paragraph above Definition 3.2 is hard to follow (R2).
- **Reparameterization explanation (Reviewer 4, echoed by Reviewer 1):** The claim that a negative real part of eigenvalues leads to stability is not explained; the logic in Section 3.4 is incomplete. Why the proposed reparameterization allows eigenvalues to approach zero without causing instability is unclear, and its connection to learning long-term dependencies is not firmly established.
- **Experiment interpretation (Reviewer 2, high confidence):** Fig. 3’s filtering part is unclear. If teacher models are expected to be exponentially decaying, why is filtering required? Was filtering also applied to the left-side plot? The takeaway is ambiguous: does the experiment show “stable approximation ⇒ exponential decay” or “exponential decay ⇒ stable approximation”?
- **Heaviside input choice (Reviewer 3):** The choice at the end of p.4 is non-intuitive; one would expect the network to receive input until time 0 and then evolve autonomously. The authors should elaborate on their choice.
- **Minor presentation issues (Reviewer 2, moderate confidence):**
    - Proof sketch on p.7 is hard to follow and could be moved to the appendix.
    - Several typos: “In order” → “in order”, “approaching 0” → “approaching 0 on the negative side”, “Take exponential…” → “Taking exponential”, “acivations” → “activations”.
    - Avoid using “+” and “→” inside text; replace with “and” and “implies”.
- **Missing connection to related architectures (Reviewer 3):** The reparameterization resembles the one used in LRU (Orvieto et al., ICML 2023) and deep state-space models; this should be mentioned.
- **Extension to LSTMs/GRUs (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3):** The paper could provide insight on whether the proof techniques could conceptually be applied to gated architectures.
- **Limitation of reparameterization (Reviewer 3):** Even with the reparameterization, RNNs still might only be able to learn targets with decaying memory; the paper should clarify what the theoretical results actually imply for the reparameterization.
