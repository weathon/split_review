## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of token dynamics in the Mamba selective state space model, deriving the continuous-time limit and characterizing asymptotic behavior (convergence to zero or divergence to infinity). Based on these insights, it proposes two refinements: excluding the convergent scenario and reordering tokens by importance, with empirical validation on language modeling and image classification. Reviewers found the work original and valuable for advancing theoretical understanding of SSMs, but raised concerns about the restrictive assumptions (1D, parameter sharing, omitted components), limited experimental scope, lack of controlled ablations bridging theory and practice, and insufficient discussion of practical trade-offs. There was disagreement on the strength of empirical results: some reviewers found them modest while others considered them significant.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: The first theoretical analysis of how tokens evolve across depth in deep selective SSMs, filling a gap in understanding these models. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Theoretical rigor**: Provides clear mathematical criteria for token convergence/divergence based on model parameters, with proofs that are sound. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Practical implications**: The refinements derived from the theory—removing convergence and reordering tokens—lead to measurable performance improvements on ImageNet and Wikitext-103. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well-structured, with helpful figures (1–3) and accessible explanations of complex mathematical content. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Significance**: The work has potential to influence both theoretical understanding and practical design of SSMs, with insights that could extend to other models. (Reviewer 2)
- **Empirical significance**: Reviewers 1 and 4 described the empirical improvements as significant; Reviewer 4 noted the results are motivating despite some concerns. (Reviewers 1, 4; note disagreement from Reviewer 3, who found them modest—see Weaknesses)

### Weaknesses
- **Simplified theoretical assumptions**: The analysis relies on strong assumptions that deviate from practical Mamba: time-independent parameters (weights shared across layers), exclusion of layer normalization, short convolutions, MLPs, restriction to 1D, and no complex eigenvalues. These assumptions are necessary for tractability but severely limit direct applicability of the conclusions. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Narrow theoretical scope**: Only the one-dimensional case is analyzed. Higher-dimensional token dynamics, bidirectional (non-causal) settings, and comparisons to other architectures (transformers, gated RNNs like LSTMs/GRUs) are not addressed. This reduces the generality and novelty of the theoretical contribution. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Limited experimental diversity**: Validation is restricted to ImageNet classification and Wikitext-103 language modeling. Broader tasks (e.g., larger language datasets, reinforcement learning, long-range benchmarks) and comparisons with other state-of-the-art SSMs (e.g., GSS, HiPPO) and transformers are missing. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Lack of controlled ablations from theory to practice**: No experiments systematically bridge the gap between the simplified theoretical model (1D, no normalization/convs/MLPs, weight sharing) and the full Mamba architecture. Such ablations would clarify how much each assumption affects the predicted token dynamics and the effectiveness of the proposed refinements. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient analysis of practical trade-offs**: The computational overhead of token reordering and the cost of checking convergence scenarios during training are not quantified. The sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, number of layers) and potential stability issues are not explored. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Learning rate tuning not reported**: Reviewer 4 expressed suspicion about the experimental results because no learning rate tuning was reported; without such tuning, the results are hard to trust. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited novelty of the dynamical analysis**: The main theoretical result (convergence/divergence depending on parameters) builds on standard dynamical systems concepts and may not be surprising. Exploring more complex behaviors (oscillations, chaos, noise effects) or providing deeper comparisons with transformers could increase originality. (Reviewer 2)
- **Confusion between depth and sequence directions**: The paper uses “time” to refer to the depth direction, which can be misleading given that SSMs traditionally evolve along the sequence length. This should be clarified throughout. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor presentation error**: Line 075 mislabels the input, output, and state matrices. (Reviewer 3)
- **Discussion of limitations is brief**: The paper does not thoroughly address the limitations of the analysis or the practical challenges of implementing the refinements. (Reviewer 1)
- **Disagreement on empirical strength**: One reviewer (Reviewer 3) found the empirical results modest and unclear how much the refinements contribute beyond the simplified theory, whereas another (Reviewer 4) considered them significant. This disagreement underscores the need for more comprehensive and controlled experiments. (Reviewers 3, 4)
