## Merged Review

### Summary
BinaryDM proposes a weight binarization approach for diffusion models, introducing two key techniques: Evolvable-Basis Binarizer (EBB) and Low-rank Representation Mimicking (LRM). EBB starts with multiple binary bases for richer representation and gradually regularizes to a single binary basis for efficient inference, applied only to the first and last six layers. LRM aligns low-rank representations of the binarized model with a full-precision counterpart to mitigate optimization ambiguity. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance at W1A4 (FID 7.74 vs baseline 10.87) and efficiency savings of 15.2× OPs and 29.2× model size. It is the first dedicated binarization method for diffusion models.

### Strengths
- Novel and well-motivated methods (EBB, LRM) tailored to the challenges of binarizing diffusion models: EBB enables smooth transition from multi-basis (enhanced initial representation) to efficient single-basis binarization; LRM addresses optimization direction ambiguity via low-rank alignment. (R1, R3)  
- EBB exploits residual connections to provide a favorable initial binarization state without altering network structure at inference (a benefit noted by the more positive reviewer R2).  
- Thorough experimental evaluation across multiple datasets and models; BinaryDM outperforms existing binarization and low-bit quantization methods (LSQ, Q-Diffusion) under ultra-low bit-widths, achieving W1A4 FID 7.74 and significant efficiency gains (15.2× OPs, 29.2× model size). Sufficient performance in the challenging binarization task. (R1, R2, R3)  
- Figure 2 shows EBB provides richer representation (higher parameter variance) during early training, smoothing the optimization process. (R3)  
- The paper is generally well-written with clear motivation, technical depth, and effective figures/tables. (R1; R3 had concerns about presentation, but this strength reflects the majority view).  
- Important practical impact for edge deployment due to large reductions in model size and computational operations. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited application of EBB**: Applied only to the first and last six layers. The paper lacks thorough justification (theoretical or empirical) for this selection; it is unclear whether the same configuration would generalize to other architectures without exhaustive search. Middle layers, left with vanilla binarization, may also benefit, but no ablation studies explore different layer choices. The method’s computational intensity (covariance matrix computation) further hinders such exploration. (R1, R3)  
- **Missing analysis of training stability and convergence**: Binarized models are notoriously difficult to optimize (gradient mismatch, poor local minima), but the paper does not investigate how EBB and LRM specifically improve training dynamics. (R1)  
- **Incomplete comparison against state-of-the-art**: BinaryDM is compared only to baseline binarization and low-bit quantization (LSQ, Q-Diffusion). It does not benchmark against recent advanced binarization/quantization for diffusion models or other generative models, nor against adapted binarization methods from other domains (e.g., ReActNet, InstaBNN). Given the limited existing work on DM quantization, the current results may not fully prove true superiority. (R1; R2, despite being more positive overall, also notes this concern.)  
- **Efficiency analysis is estimation-based, not hardware-validated**: The paper claims existing multi-basis binarization introduces hardware complexity but provides no evidence that the proposed single-basis inference avoids such overhead in practice. The reported OPs savings are estimates; hardware feasibility is not discussed. (R3)  
- **Technical details are unclear**:  
  - For LRM: how low-rank projection matrices are computed and fixed, hyperparameter choices, and stability considerations. (R1)  
  - For EBB Equation 5: why use sign in the second term (`σ_II sign(w - σ_1 sign(w))`) instead of a linear residual (`σ_II (w - σ_1 sign(w))`)? One reviewer (R2) argues the latter may give a better initial state.  
  - Notation issues: Equation 6 ambiguity (channel-wise?); σ₁ domain undefined (can it change polarity?); σ₁ usage at inference unclear; same symbols in Eq. 9 and Eq. 2 for different parameters; ε_t undefined. (R3)  
  - Training details missing: number of iterations, learning rate/scheduler are not explicitly stated to match existing works, potentially affecting comparability. (R2)  
- **Ablation study only for W1A32, not for the target W1A4**: The most impressive result is W1A4, yet the ablation evaluating EBB and LRM components is performed only on W1A32. An ablation for W1A4 is strongly recommended to validate the methods at the actual low precision. (R3)  
- **Lack of theoretical analysis** explaining why the proposed methods improve binarization performance. (R1)
