## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Legendre-KAN, a variant of Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) that replaces cubic B-spline basis functions with parameterized Legendre polynomials, adds normalization layers, and uses smaller initialization to improve accuracy and parameter efficiency. Experiments on symbolic regression and fitting simple nonlinear functions show Legendre-KAN achieves lower test losses and 10–100× accuracy improvement over standard KAN.

### Strengths
- Replacing B-splines with Legendre polynomials is a well-motivated novel approach (R2, R3, R4).
- Legendre-KAN achieves lower test set losses on a set of symbolic expressions compared to standard KAN (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper provides an extensive overview of the method (R1).
- Reviewer 4 (more positive, score 5 with confidence 4) highlights specific technical contributions: (1) Legendre polynomials offer global approximation, higher-order pattern capture, and numerical stability via orthogonalization; (2) a normalization layer to unify input activation to [0,1] and smaller initialization parameters to prevent gradient explosion; (3) experiments on both symbolic and non-symbolizable functions show 10–100× accuracy improvement.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope** (R1, R2, R3, R4). The experiments are extremely small-scale and use only simple polynomial functions; no one uses neural networks to approximate such functions (R2). The symbolic regression evaluation is confined to a small set of equations; well-established benchmarks like SRBench and SRSD should be used (R1). No noise analysis is performed (R1).
- **Missing comparisons** (R2, R1, R3). No comparisons to other KAN alternatives (Fourier KANs, Wavelet KANs, RBF KANs) (R2). If presented as a symbolic regression contribution, the paper should compare against strong baselines such as Operon, DSR, and Neural Symbolic Regressors, not only KAN (R1). If the focus is only KAN comparison, the choice of symbolic regression task and its implications need justification (R1).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis** (R3). The paper provides no deeper justification why Legendre polynomials outperform B-splines; the choice appears arbitrary without analysis of approximation properties or convergence rates in the KAN context (R3, also echoed in R4’s confusion on degrees of freedom).
- **Poor writing and presentation** (R3, R4). Grammatical errors and incomplete sentences, notably in Section 3.1, impede comprehension (R3). The paper is disorganized: experimental results appear in sections typically reserved for background and methodology (R3). Spelling errors (e.g., “wrose” in line 257) and punctuation mistakes (e.g., sentence ending with a comma in line 274) (R4). Figures and tables lack clear captions and explanations; Figure 4b labels appear reversed, Figure 6 is unclear (R3, R4).
- **Architecture details incomplete or inconsistent** (R4). It is not clearly specified which components are inherited from KAN and which are modified (e.g., use of SiLU is claimed as combined with Legendre basis, but it is unclear whether SiLU was already in KAN). Figures have undefined symbols: in Figure 2(a), symbol $B$ with subscript is never defined. A theorem quoted in line 113 has undefined symbols, and the same symbols are reused with different meanings in Section 3.2.
- **Contradictory evidence in motivation** (R4). Section 2.2 uses Figure 3 and Figure 4 to argue that B-splines have localized limitations, but the figures contradict each other: Figure 3(b) shows Legendre fits better than B-spline, while Figure 4(b) shows the opposite; Figure 3(c) shows B-spline fits well, inconsistent with Figure 4(c). The term “certain areas” is vague, and the connection between localized fitting, degrees of freedom, and approximation space is not rigorous (R4).
- **Unclear scientific contribution** (R1, R3). The paper claims applicability to PDEs, GNNs, and Transformers in the abstract and related work, but provides no experiments in non-symbolic tasks (R1, R3). Since prior work shows KAN outperforms MLP only on symbolic representation but is inferior on ML, CV, NLP, and audio, testing Legendre-KAN on diverse tasks is necessary to demonstrate general applicability (R3).
- **Missing clarity on table labels** (R1). Terms like “time ratio”, “Equal params”, “best k/grid”, and “best k” in tables are not explained.

*Note: Reviewer 4 (score 5) was significantly more positive than the other three (each score 3), emphasizing the technical contributions (normalization, initialization) and the 10–100× accuracy gain, but still identified serious weaknesses in motivation, explanation, and writing. The other reviewers found the contribution limited by the narrow evaluation scope, lack of comparisons and theoretical grounding, and poor presentation.*
