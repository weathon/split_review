## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces TAG-EQA, a benchmark for Embodied Question Answering (EQA) and Episodic Memory Question Answering (EM‑EQA) that addresses question ambiguity by incorporating spatial and temporal grounding. It presents a new dataset designed for ambiguous grounded episodic memory QA with spatial reasoning, temporal conditions, and diverse visual features, along with a structured approach to resolve ambiguity in egocentric data.

### Strengths

- **Novelty and focus**: The benchmark targets spatial, visual, and temporal ambiguities in questions (R3, R4) – a practically valuable but often overlooked aspect of EQA (R4).  
- **Structured answers**: Introducing structured answers to prevent information loss and resolve ambiguity is a well-thought methodological choice (R1, R4).  
- **Data synthesis method**: The paper proposes a theoretically sound approach for synthesizing QA pairs using condition combinations and a filtering strategy, avoiding reliance on multimodal large language models (R4).  
- **Evaluation results**: Evaluation results of baseline models are provided (R2).  
- **Dataset relevance**: The dataset incorporates spatial reasoning, temporal conditions, and visual features, making it relevant for developing capable EQA systems (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and completeness**: The paper is difficult to read and follow; Tables 2–4 lack top/bottom rules, and Appendices A.1–A.3 are missing (R1). The conclusion and appendix lack textual descriptions (R2). Paper structure should be optimized; Section 3.1 appears misplaced (R3). The paper seems incomplete and may have been submitted prematurely (R2).  
- **Terminology and positioning**: Confusing terminology is used that does not follow precedent studies (R2). The concept of “passive EQA” is introduced without clear grounding in existing work (e.g., Das et al. 2018) and it is unclear how it differs from VideoQA (R2). The experimental setting is not clearly positioned among EQA, VideoQA, or EM‑EQA branches (R2). The claim about “spurious correlations” in 3D-VQA vs. EQA is not clarified or cited (R2).  
- **Dataset size, diversity, and statistics**: It is unclear if the dataset is large and diverse enough to effectively evaluate EQA models (R1). No exact data statistics are provided (R3). The number of query types is too few (R3). The “multi‑factor” claim lacks details on novelty compared to existing datasets; no statistics or qualitative comparisons of predicate diversity are given (R2, R4). A breakdown of predicate types and their frequencies, and more examples of complex multi-condition questions, are needed to demonstrate diversity (R4).  
- **Evaluation limitations**: The selection of baseline models is limited, and insufficient discussion of their specific limitations when handling ambiguous questions in 3D environments (R1, R4). The experiments are not solid enough (R3). The evaluation only tests on LLMs combined with image-to-text methods, not on multimodal LLMs such as GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, or mPLUG-Owl3 – which are needed to reveal end-to-end challenges (R3, R4).  
- **Temporal reasoning oversimplification**: The benchmark improves temporal reasoning but overly simplifies temporal ambiguities by using counters for repeated actions (R3).  
- **Scalability concerns**: The structured approach and detailed evaluation may be difficult to scale due to the complexity of processing multiple ambiguity layers (R3).  
- **Data annotation accuracy**: The method heavily depends on GPT-4o for extracting object properties; no manual checks or additional filtering are described to ensure correctness. Incorrect annotations could cascade errors in the combined and filtered samples (R4).  
- **Missing qualitative analysis**: Lack of dataset examples with images or figures to illustrate the tasks and settings (R2, R3 asks for Figure 4 optimization).  
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: While R2 lists “evaluation results of baselines supplied” as a strength, R3 notes the experiments are not solid enough, indicating a divergence in assessment of the experimental rigor.
