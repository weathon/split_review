## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates mixture policies in entropy-regularized actor-critic reinforcement learning, focusing on Soft Actor-Critic. It provides theoretical results (bandit setting) showing mixture policies can achieve higher regularized and unregularized objectives compared to unimodal Gaussian policies. To address the lack of reparameterization gradients for mixture policies, the authors derive a half-reparameterization (unbiased) and a full Gumbel-softmax-based reparameterization (biased) gradient estimator. Experiments on classic control (Pendulum, MountainCar, Acrobot), MuJoCo (Hopper, Walker, Ant, Humanoid), DeepMind Control Suite, and continuous bandits compare SAC with mixture policies against vanilla SAC. Results indicate improved exploration in sparse/unshaped reward tasks, comparable performance on shaped reward tasks, and robustness to multimodal critic surfaces.

### Strengths
- Novel reparameterization gradient estimators for mixture policies (half-reparameterization with unbiased gradients, full reparameterization using Gumbel-softmax), filling a gap in the literature where prior work (e.g., SACM) used stochastic gradients without reparameterization.
- Solid theoretical analysis (bandit setting) demonstrating that mixture policies achieve at least as high a regularized objective as their component policies, and that any first-order stationary point of component policies corresponds to a stationary point of the mixture policy.
- Experimental evidence shows improved exploration efficiency in sparse-reward environments (Pendulum, MountainCar, Acrobot, DMC environments) and comparable performance to base SAC in shaped-reward environments (MuJoCo tasks), with some gains on unshaped MuJoCo (e.g., Hopper, Walker) and DMC fish–swim.
- The work is timely, addressing multimodal entropy-regularized RL and providing a practical approach to use mixture policies with variance-reduced gradient estimators.
- Well-structured presentation with clear motivation, theoretical foundations, and effective visualizations (Figures 1 and 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited and inconclusive experimental results**: Performance improvements on sparse-reward tasks are often marginal, and no gains are shown on complex shaped-reward environments (Hopper, Walker, Ant, Humanoid) where mixture policies perform comparably but not better. The sparse-reward environments (Pendulum, MountainCar, Acrobot) are toy domains; missing evaluations on more realistic sparse-reward domains (e.g., Fetch, Adroit) limits scalability claims. [R1/R2/R3/R5]
- **Missing key comparisons**: No comparison with other exploration-oriented methods (e.g., heavy-tailed policies, Kobayashi 2019, Bedi 2024), and no comparison with existing mixture-policy approaches (SACM, Daniel 2012, Celik 2022, Nematollahi 2022, Seyde 2022, Hou 2020, Baram 2021). Comparisons with multimodal policies such as diffusion models (mentioned in introduction) are absent. Also missing discussion on why mixture policies are preferred over mixture-of-critics approaches (D4PG, PMOE). [R1/R2/R3/R5]
- **Theoretical contributions are limited to bandit settings and lack technical depth**: Proofs are straightforward with no major innovation. Proposition 3.2 (bounds for entropy-constrained problems) is not connected to the rest of the paper. Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 do not convincingly show that mixture policies have stationary points when component policies do not, weakening the claim that mixtures provide better solution quality. The assertion that mixture policies are “better” than base policies lacks rigorous theoretical backing—the counterexample does not prove the general case. [R2/R4]
- **Poorly motivated reparameterization gradient estimators**: The paper does not demonstrate why reparameterization is needed over the simpler likelihood-ratio gradient estimator for mixture policies, nor does it provide variance reduction guarantees or theoretical/empirical comparisons of variance. The admitted bias in Gumbel reparameterization is not analyzed quantitatively (e.g., effect of temperature on bias, convergence, stability). [R2/R4]
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and robustness concerns**: Figure 4 (third row) shows larger variation in returns for mixture policies when the entropy scale α is varied compared to Rep-Squashed, indicating worse hyperparameter sensitivity, not robustness. Testing robustness by adding observation noise is missing. No sensitivity analysis is provided for the number of mixture components N, which may be critical in complex environments. [R3/R5]
- **Computational overhead not addressed**: No analysis of wall-clock training time, network forward/backward time, or the trade-off between sample complexity and runtime. Maintaining multiple policy components and learning weighting functions incurs computational cost that is not discussed. [R1/R5]
- **Significant limitation**: Default hyperparameters tuned for base SAC were used for mixture policies in MuJoCo experiments, which may penalize mixture performance and undermines validity. Additional limitations (theoretical scope, environment breadth, hyperparameter sensitivity) need discussion. [R4]  
- **Generalization concerns**: The algorithm appears effective only in specially constructed sparse-reward environments; its advantage in general continuous-control tasks is not established. [R5]
- **Minority disagreement**: One reviewer finds the experimental results inconclusive and the performance improvements marginal, while others acknowledge benefits but note limitations. Two reviewers gave low scores (3) highlighting these issues. [R2/R3]
