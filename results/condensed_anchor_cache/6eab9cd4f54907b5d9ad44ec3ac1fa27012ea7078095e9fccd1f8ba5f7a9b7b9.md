## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Graph Bayesian Flow Networks (GraphBFN), which adapts Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) to graph generation. It uses a continuous latent variable derived from infinite categorical sampling to smoothly interpolate between noise and graph structure. The framework incorporates spectral topological information and introduces an Adaptive Flow Back sampling strategy. Experiments on generic and molecular graph generation are performed. Reviewers note novelty and promising efficiency, but raise substantial concerns about clarity, justification, baseline comparisons, and generalization.

### Strengths
- **Novel adaptation of Bayesian Flow Networks to graph generation** – the framework is a principled and natural way to model discrete graphs through continuous latent interpolation (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Comprehensive experiments with ablation** – shows the effectiveness of the Adaptive Flow Back technique and the overall model produces competitive results (R1). The model exhibits good performance across all benchmarks if results are statistically significant (R3).
- **Better sampling efficiency than graph diffusion models** – significant speed improvements (R1, R3).
- **Detailed theoretical explanation** – the paper provides a thorough theoretical introduction supporting the method (R4). One reviewer (R1) found the theoretical introduction strong despite clarity gaps.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear justification for Bayesian flow networks over spectral methods** – the paper claims graph topology can be captured via spectral features but does not adequately explain why a flow-based approach is superior to other generative methods, nor how spectral features are actually incorporated into the model (R2, R4). The relationship between spectral theory and topological modeling is not clearly explained (R2, R4). R2 asks where figures 1 and 6 are.
- **Missing comparisons with the most closely related works** – no comparison or discussion of differences from GeoBFN and MolCRAFT, nor is there a performance gap evaluation for 2D molecular graphs generated by those models (R2). R3 also notes missing error bars (“You should provide error bars for all your experiments”) and that limited dataset size (e.g., SPECTRE) may cause high variance, casting doubt on some baseline results. R4 says the experiment selects too few datasets with different topological properties; supplemental synthetic datasets (community, ego, BA) are needed to demonstrate generation on networks with various topological properties. The model’s benefit on graphs that do not exhibit community patterns is not shown (R3).
- **Multiple clarity issues in method description** (R1, R3):
    - Loss derivation: the assumptions needed to derive Eq. 4 from Eq. 3 (factorized posterior \(q(y_1,\dots,y_n|x)=\prod q(y_i|x)\)) are not stated or justified. The role of \(q_{noise}\) in Eq. 4 is unclear. Omission of \(\log p(x\mid\theta_n)\) from the final loss (Eq. 11) lacks justification beyond efficiency (R1).
    - Derivation of Eq. 9 from Eq. 6 is not elaborated (not even in appendix) (R1).
    - Definitions of \(y\) and \(\theta\) are vague – whether they represent noisy graphs, node/edge features, or a continuous parameterization of graph distributions is unclear (R1).
    - The role of \(p_\phi\) is hard to understand: what is \(y_i\)? It seems to estimate both \(q(y_t\mid\text{previous})\) and \(p(x\mid y_{1:t})\) (R3). R3 also points out \(p_\theta\) on line 131 is undefined.
    - The “noisy distribution” concept is not clearly presented (R3). Sender and receiver distributions should be introduced in section 2.2 to help connection with section 3.2 (R3).
    - Time direction: the axis goes from noise to data, which is opposite to diffusion; this is not mentioned (R3).
    - Adaptive Flow Back: the paragraph (especially lines 309–312) is poorly written. The motivation behind the condition \(\|\phi(G^{\theta_t},t)-\phi(G^{\theta_{t-1}},t-1)\|^2 \geq \epsilon\) is not immediate, and how it relates to variance of \(\theta\) needs more explanation (R1, R3).
    - Typos: Missing negative sign before KL in Eq. 3; \(\log p_\phi(x\mid\theta)\) should be expectation; latent notation inconsistency \(z_t\) vs \(y_t\) in Eq. 4; line 172 categories definition misses “K categories”; line 272 “W the following notations” (R1).
- **Concern about direct model supervision** – diffusion models directly supervise the data, while GraphBFN regresses against the noisy latent \(y_t\), which is a downside (R3).
- **Missing explanation of scheduling** – the rationale behind the time scheduling in Eq. 13 is not given (R2).
- **One reviewer (R1) is more positive** (score 6, confidence 3) and stresses the novelty and comprehensive experiments, but still raises the same clarity and justification concerns. All other reviewers scored 5 (confidence 3–4) and emphasize the missed comparisons and insufficient clarity.
