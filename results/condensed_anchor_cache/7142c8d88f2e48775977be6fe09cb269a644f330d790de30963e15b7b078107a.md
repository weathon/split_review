## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FedTMOS, a one-shot federated learning (FL) framework that replaces DNNs with Tsetlin Machines (TMs). FedTMOS clusters and reassigns class-specific weights to generate a server model without additional training or public data, achieving large reductions in upload communication costs and server latency. Experiments on MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR10 with 20–80 clients show accuracy improvements over ensemble and state-of-the-art one-shot FL baselines. Reviewers agreed on the novelty of applying TMs to OFL, but opinions differed: one reviewer (score 8) emphasized the comprehensive efficiency evaluations and data-free property, while two reviewers (score 6) found the motivation not fully justified, the evaluation limited to simple datasets, and the approach restricted to a single model type.

### Strengths

- Novel application of Tsetlin Machine to one-shot federated learning, offering an alternative to knowledge-distillation-based methods. (All reviewers)
- Data-free: eliminates reliance on a public dataset. (R1, R2)
- Comprehensive evaluations of communication and compute efficiency alongside accuracy: at least 2.3× reduction in upload communication costs and 75× reduction in server latency compared to methods requiring server-side training. (R1)
- Significant reduction in upload communication costs. (R3)
- Evaluation over varying client numbers (20, 50, 80), a critical factor in one-shot FL. (R2)
- Paper provides a self-contained background on Tsetlin Machine, making the paper accessible. (R2)

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear source of performance gain:** It is not obvious whether the accuracy improvements in Table 1 come from the performance gap between CNNs and CTM rather than the federated aggregation. The centralized (non-federated) performance of both CNN and CTM should be reported to clarify. (R1, R3)
- **Limited applicability:** The method is restricted to Tsetlin Machine and cannot be used with general DNNs. Given that the task is well-established image classification, this severely limits practical deployment. (R1, R3) (Both R2 and R3 explicitly echoed this as a main concern.)
- **Unclear motivation:** Why Tsetlin Machine (an automaton) rather than a general reinforcement learning scheme with reward/penalty? How does this mechanism resolve the key bottlenecks of one-shot FL (e.g., public data requirement, server-side training)? The paper reads like converting a conventional problem into an automaton without explaining the benefit. (R2)
- **Poorly justified design choices:** Using the Gini index to measure data distribution is neither straightforward nor trivial compared to common alternatives (e.g., Gaussian modeling, hard/soft label uploading). How was this choice reached, and what advantage does it bring? (R2)
- **Evaluation on overly simple datasets:** Only MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR10 are used. For datasets like MNIST, few epochs and communication rounds are needed even without one-shot FL. More complex datasets (e.g., Tiny-ImageNet) are needed to demonstrate true effectiveness in convergence and accuracy. (R2)
- **Scalability concerns:** Can FedTMOS achieve comparable accuracy when baseline methods use stronger networks (e.g., ResNet) on challenging datasets like Tiny-ImageNet? (R3)
- **Readability issues:** The notation in line 146 (definition of \(L_j\) from \(L\)) is unclear. (R1, R3)
- **Acronyms:** “OFL” and “TM” are unconventional; “TM” is usually associated with Turing Machine. (R2)
- **Formatting:** Tables 1 and 4 are out of bounds. (R2)
- **Potentially unfair latency comparison:** Server-side latency for DNN methods was measured on a standard compute node; if that node was not GPU-equipped, the comparison is unfair. (R1)
