## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DLA-EF-JA, a multi-view clustering method that introduces dual-level affinity (anchor-sample and anchor-anchor) via self-expression and topology learning, a joint-alignment permutation mechanism to align anchors across views without a baseline view, and an embedding-free discrete cluster label construction. Reviewers acknowledge the paper’s structure, extensive experiments, and certain design choices, but raise significant concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, experimental inconsistencies, methodological clarity, and hyperparameter sensitivity.

### Strengths
- Well-structured with a comprehensive literature review (Reviewer 1, confidence 4).
- Easy to read and follow (Reviewer 4, confidence 5).
- Dual-level affinity mechanism effectively captures both anchor-sample and anchor-anchor relationships, enhancing clustering accuracy (Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, confidence 5).
- Flexible joint-alignment method addresses anchor misalignment without requiring a fixed baseline view, making the model versatile for different data sources (Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, confidence 5).
- The method has linear complexity for the loss function (Reviewer 3, confidence 5).
- Extensive experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate effectiveness and efficiency (Reviewer 1, confidence 4; Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 4, confidence 5). Note: Reviewer 2 is notably more positive (score 6) than others (scores 3, 3, 5) regarding overall contribution.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The core ideas (anchor permutation matrix, self-expression affinity learning, graph Laplacian regularization) are widely used in existing anchor-learning, subspace clustering, and spectral clustering works. The paper primarily applies these components to anchor data without clear theoretical justification.  
   - Reviewer 1, confidence 4: “The anchor permutation matrix idea has been widely adopted by previous works, novelty might not be sufficient to be published.”  
   - Reviewer 3, confidence 5: “The novelty is limited since the involved components have been widely used for anchor learning and spectral clustering; the authors only perform these components on the anchor data.”  
   - Reviewer 4, confidence 5: “Novelty is incremental. It remains unclear why the anchor self-expression enhances the quality of anchors. The connection between anchor self-expression and improved clustering performance is not well-established theoretically or empirically.”

2. **Missing comparisons with related works**:  
   - Reviewer 1, confidence 4: “The comparison methods lack some latest works. Specifically, the reference Liu 2024 (line 581) includes anchor alignment but is not compared with the proposed work.”  
   - Reviewer 3, confidence 5: “The authors do not compare the proposed method with popular deep learning ones.”

3. **Unconvincing experimental results**: Several baseline methods exhibit extremely poor performance on certain datasets, which raises questions about data integrity or experimental setup.  
   - Reviewer 1, confidence 4: “In Table 1, PMSC on Cora and AMGL on DeRMATO perform extremely poorly. The authors should explain possible reasons.”  
   - Reviewer 3, confidence 5: “The authors should check the data because methods like OrthNTF and GSC show very poor performance.”  
   - Reviewer 4, confidence 5: “Results are not convincing. For instance, OrthNTF achieves 69.4% Acc and 68.6% NMI on Reuters, but this paper reports only 28.67% Acc and 3.07% NMI.”

4. **Methodology clarity and missing details**:  
   - Reviewer 1, confidence 4: “Table 5 does not include all symbols. The Methodology section is too brief; each component’s design rationale should be explained.”  
   - Reviewer 2, confidence 3: “The transition from Equation 2 to Equation 3 is unclear. More detailed explanation of each mathematical step would enhance understanding.”  
   - Reviewer 4, confidence 5: “Why learn an anchor affinity matrix \(S_p\) for each view separately instead of a consensus anchor affinity matrix? This overlooks inter-view interactions and may be suboptimal.”

5. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and robustness issues**:  
   - Reviewer 2, confidence 3: “The model’s performance is sensitive to carefully tuned hyperparameters λ and β. How does the model handle scenarios where anchor quality varies across views, especially with noisy anchors? Provide insights on robustness and anchor noise mitigation. How does performance vary with different hyperparameter settings, and how to optimally select them for noisy or high-dimensional data?”

6. **Limited learning of cross-view complementarity**:  
   - Reviewer 2, confidence 3: “The model lacks complex constraints like the Schatten p-norm that could capture deeper cross-view complementarities. How does it handle views with highly distinct features or dimensions?”

7. **Justification for anchor alignment**:  
   - Reviewer 2, confidence 3: “The necessity of anchor alignment is not fully justified. Some recent methods avoid alignment through feature space fusion or shared representations. Elaborate on the essential role of alignment and under what conditions it could be relaxed or simplified.”

8. **Anchor count selection**:  
   - Reviewer 4, confidence 5: “How is the number of anchors \(k\) set? What is its influence on performance?”

9. **Difference from existing alignment methods**:  
   - Reviewer 1, confidence 4 (question): “What is the difference between the anchor alignment module and those of existing works?” (This overlaps with the novelty criticism.)
