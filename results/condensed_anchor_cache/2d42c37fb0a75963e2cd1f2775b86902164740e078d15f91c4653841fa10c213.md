## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents BrainOOD, a framework for out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in brain network analysis using GNNs. It consists of a feature selector and a structure extractor with auxiliary losses including an improved Graph Information Bottleneck. Evaluated on ABIDE and ADNI datasets against 16 baselines, it shows improved OOD performance and interpretability validated by neuroscience literature. The paper also introduces an OOD benchmark for brain networks.

### Strengths
- Novel combined approach: simultaneously identifying informative node features and extracting causal subgraphs via a feature selector and structure extractor, with a batch-level loss to enforce consistent connection selection across subjects. [R1, R3]
- Addresses a critical gap in brain network analysis by focusing on OOD generalization and interpretability, essential for real-world clinical deployment. [R2]
- Thorough evaluation across two major multi-site datasets (ABIDE, ADNI) with 16 baselines, including brain-specific networks, demonstrating competitive performance. [R1, R2]
- Interpretability validation: identified brain patterns align with known neuroscience literature. [R1, R2]
- Ablation study confirms the necessity of each loss component. [R2]

### Weaknesses
- **Overstated benchmark novelty.** The claim of introducing the first OOD brain network benchmark is not well justified. ABIDE and ADNI are existing multi-site datasets with inherent distribution shifts; the site-specific split does not create a fundamentally new challenge. The paper does not demonstrate why this specific OOD split is more difficult or representative than existing multi-site evaluations. [R1, R2]
- **Marginal technical innovation (noted as a contrast: one reviewer [score 6] found stronger novelty).** Many components (auxiliary losses, discrete sampling) are borrowed from prior work. The core technical contribution beyond existing GIB-based methods is limited, and the adaptation to brain networks lacks substantial novelty. [R2]
- **Unclear link between method and OOD generalization.** The paper does not clearly explain how feature selection and structure extraction specifically mitigate distribution shifts (e.g., via invariant feature learning). The motivation appears to be noise reduction rather than domain adaptation. Visualizing top connections on both ID and OOD sets would help demonstrate generalizability. [R1]
- **Feature selection mechanism poorly justified.** The use of covariance of latent representation \(\hat{H}\) to derive \(\hat{X}\) and the tanh activation are not motivated. Why covariance is a suitable proxy for feature importance in brain networks is unclear; other scaling methods might be more appropriate. [R2]
- **OOD problem may not be challenging.** Table 2 shows insignificant performance differences between ID and OOD even for ERM, suggesting the distribution shift is moderate. Comparisons with other brain-specific models (e.g., BrainNetCNN, BrainGNN) under the same OOD condition are missing, making it hard to assess the true impact. [R2]
- **ADNI classification setting problematic.** Using a 6-class setting (CN, SMC, MCI, EMCI, LMCI, AD) is confusing because EMCI/LMCI overlap with MCI (MCI in ADNI1 is LMCI). A 5-class setting (CN, SMC, EMCI, LMCI, AD) is more standard and reasonable and may affect evaluation results. [R3]
- **Unclear experimental details and missing analyses.**
  (a) How were the four losses balanced? Is there a risk of overfitting given the numerous modules and hyperparameters? [R1]
  (b) In-domain testing procedure is unclear; what is the difference between the 10-fold-CV and the overall test in Tables 2 and 3? [R3]
  (c) Conventional ML baselines (e.g., SVM) should be added. [R3]
  (d) Ablation on configurations \((X', A)\) and \((X, A')\) plus examination of node masks is needed to demonstrate module contributions. [R2]
  (e) For the adjacency matrix, were the top 20% connections based on magnitude including negative correlations? [R3]
  (f) ID and OOD checkpoints in Fig. 3: edge scores are very low (max ~0.08); how many edges are typically included in the extracted subgraph? [R3]
  (g) Other parameters (temperature in Eq. 11, sampling \(k\) for final prediction) need sensitivity analysis. [R3]
  (h) The role of reconstruction loss in feature selection is not clearly described. [R3]
  (i) Why retain original \(A\) instead of using \(A'\) during feature selection? How does HPGNN with multiple hops differ from simply multiplying the graph Laplacian? [R2]
- **Transferability not discussed.** It is unclear how the proposed method could be adapted or transferred to other models. [R1]
- **Appropriateness of fMRI on ADNI.** Since fMRI-derived brain networks on ADNI have lower performance than structural MRI, using it as an OOD benchmark may be questionable. [R1]
- **Paper organization.** Extensive background on GNNs and brain networks could be reduced; placing the related work section after the introduction or before the conclusion would improve flow. [R1]
- **Grammar needs checking.** [R2]
