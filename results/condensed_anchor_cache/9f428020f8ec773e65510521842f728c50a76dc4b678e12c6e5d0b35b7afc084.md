## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a unified information-theoretic framework to analyze generalization in VAEs and diffusion models (DMs), treating both the encoder and generator as randomized mappings. It derives generalization bounds for VAEs (including the generator) and DMs, introduces a trade-off dependent on diffusion time \(T\), and provides estimable bounds using only training data. Reviewers generally find the topic relevant and the bounds novel, and note that the empirical results on synthetic and real datasets (Swiss Roll, MNIST, CIFAR-10) partially validate the theoretical trade-offs. However, several reviewers raise concerns about experimental justification, missing sample complexity, insufficient comparison to existing bounds, and writing clarity. One reviewer (score 8) is markedly more positive about the contributions and experimental validation than a more critical reviewer (score 5), who highlights weaknesses in the claim of tighter bounds and the lack of clear sample‑size dependence.

### Strengths
- Tackles a critical and underexplored problem: generalization in widely‑used generative models (VAEs, DMs). (R1, R4)
- Provides novel unified generalization bounds for both encoder and generator under mild sub‑Gaussian loss assumptions; for DMs, overcomes difficulties posed by the triangle inequality for KL‑divergence. (R1, R2, R4)
- VAE bounds avoid Wasserstein distance, impose milder assumptions (bounded to sub‑Gaussian), and apply to arbitrary generators, distinguishing the work from existing research. (R1, R2)
- For DMs, derives a new trade‑off relationship dependent on diffusion time \(T\); under reasonable assumptions, the bound involving the mutual information of \(G^\theta\) yields a tighter \(m^{-1/2}\) rate. (R1, R2)
- Corollaries 4.2 and 4.3 extend the analysis to Wasserstein distance and KL divergence, offering useful tools for theoretical analysis of generative models. (R2)
- Provides intuitive explanations of the bounds and trade‑offs; the story is easy to follow. (R2, R4)
- Numerical experiments on synthetic and real datasets (Swiss Roll, MNIST, CIFAR‑10) serve as initial validation of the theoretical trade‑offs, and several reviewers acknowledge their relevance. (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing explicit sample complexity:** The main theorems do not state how the bounds scale with the number of samples \(m\) (e.g., \(O(1/m)\) or \(O(1/\sqrt{m})\)). The scaling of the sample‑wise mutual information term with \(m\) is also not clarified, limiting practical applicability. (R1)
- **Insufficient justification of claimed tighter bounds:** The assertion that the encoder bounds are tighter than previous work lacks a direct, quantitative comparison with PAC‑Bayes bounds (e.g., “User‑friendly introduction to PAC‑Bayes bounds” [1]) and mutual information bounds (e.g., “Statistical guarantees for variational autoencoders using pac‑bayesian theory” [2]). Visual aids or a table, as in “Evaluating lossy compression rates of deep generative models” [7], would provide clearer evidence. (R1)
- **Theoretical trade‑off not consistently reflected in experiments:** The predicted trade‑off with diffusion time \(T\) is not clearly observed in the KL and BPD metrics, especially in high‑dimensional few‑shot settings. The explanation that the discrepancy stems from inaccurate KL estimation is insufficient; additional experiments with varying dataset sizes and dimensionalities are needed (R1, R4). Specifically, Figure 3 b and c do not support the claim because too few samples were used for reliable KL estimation; the result is about KL‑divergence, not sample quality, so proper estimation with enough samples is required (R4).
- **Experimental design flaw in diffusion model experiments:** When varying \(T\), the number of generation steps is fixed, which changes the step‑size. This can significantly affect generation quality (cf. Zhang and Chen 2023, “Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models with Exponential Integrator”). A fixed step‑size (e.g., 1/1000) should be used to isolate the effect of \(T\). (R4)
- **Lack of model complexity analysis:** The bounds do not account for the number of parameters or depth of the encoder/generator networks. Including bounds for simple neural networks or linear models would strengthen the work by connecting to deep learning practice. (R2)
- **No comparison to proportional‑limit asymptotic analyses:** The paper does not relate its bounds to sharp theoretical results in proportional‑limit settings (\(\alpha = n/d = \Theta(1)\)) for denoising encoders [1], autoencoders [2], and VAEs [3,4]. A discussion in the related work would help contextualize tightness and applicability. (R2)
- **Incomplete empirical validation:** The few‑shot setting (\(m=16\)) may not reflect behavior on full datasets; evaluating bounds on full MNIST and CIFAR‑10 would be beneficial (R2). Moreover, despite the abstract highlighting VAE analysis, no VAE experiments are presented, leaving the VAE‑specific theoretical claims unvalidated (R3). The writing clarity is further hindered by an imbalance between VAE and DM content; a separate background section on the VAE‑DM relationship (e.g., citing Variational Diffusion Models [1]) is suggested. (R3)
- **Questions and missing details (included as weaknesses because they overlap with criticisms or requests):**
    - How do the empirical and expected generalization terms together affect the expected generalization? A theorem analogous to Theorem 4.1 is requested. (R1)
    - What is the practical benefit of bounding the gap between expected and empirical generalization when the empirical error itself is not measurable (since the true target distribution is unknown)? (R1)
    - How is the term \(\hat{L}_{ESM}\) computed in Theorem 6.2? (R1)
    - How can the terms for the generalization of \(G\) in VAEs be estimated in practice? (R2)
    - What is the definition of \(g\) in line 209? (R2)
    - How would the generalization bounds change if the data lie on a low‑dimensional manifold? (R2)
    - What is the theoretical reason or intuition for why the mutual information follows the upper bound in Theorem 6.3? (R4)
    - How small must the sample size be for \(T_3\) (the generator’s generalization term) to remain non‑zero? If enough samples are used for reliable KL estimation, would \(T_3\) become negligibly small? (R4)
    - The proof in Appendix 1.C is incomplete; the final claim of the theorem is not shown in the last lines. (R4)
