## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies that static baselines used in path attribution methods (e.g., Integrated Gradients) inject harmful biases from three categories—color, texture, frequency—leading to biased, fragile, and manipulable attributions. The authors propose UNI, which computes an adaptive, (un)learnable baseline by perturbing the input along an unlearning direction of steepest ascent. This dynamic baseline erases salient features, reduces curvature, and improves faithfulness and robustness. The method is evaluated on multiple architectures and datasets.

### Strengths
- All reviewers agree the paper is well-motivated, clearly written, and easy to follow. The authors collect and clearly explain a set of desired properties for the integration path, which can benefit future work (R1, R4).
- The core idea of using unlearning to create an adaptive baseline is novel and addresses a known limitation of static baselines (R3).
- The paper provides a well-structured analysis of the overlooked problem of static baseline biases, identifying three distinct categories (color, texture, frequency) with supporting experiments (R2).
- Experiments are comprehensive, spanning CNN-based and transformer-based models on multiple datasets (IN and others), with consistent improvements in:
  - **Faithfulness**: MuFidelity scores show consistent improvements across architectures (R2).
  - **Robustness**: Higher Spearman correlation coefficients under FGSM adversarial attacks (R2).
  - **Stability**: Riemann approximation works even with one step (R2).
- The theoretical foundation is carefully presented, including explicit bounds on Riemann approximation error and proofs of monotonicity and completeness properties (R2). Reviewer 2 particularly praises the theoretical analysis and extensive appendix with 20+ detailed figures.
- Experiments are carefully designed and support the authors’ claims from different perspectives (R4).
- The paper is well-structured and reviews prior work effectively (R1).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** While Reviewer 2 is very positive (score 10, contribution 4), Reviewer 3 finds the contribution of limited impact within explainable AI (score 8, contribution 2) and raises concerns about missing related work and broader implications.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification and insufficient method description:** Multiple reviewers note the method description is oversimplified. The pseudo-code lacks sufficient elaboration on each step (R1, R4). The reason why activation-matching guarantees removal of all salient features is unclear; there is no guarantee of convergence within the epsilon budget (R4). Theoretical analysis is missing for linear or simple functions (R3 M3). The paper would benefit from stronger theoretical guarantees on the optimality of the unlearned baseline (R2).
- **Missing ablation and alternative designs:** The choice of KL divergence is not justified; the authors should report experiments with other divergence measures (R1). A variant using a non-informative activation (e.g., uniform) instead of matching the unlearned model’s activation is not discussed (R4). The method is limited to explanation methods that require a reference baseline, restricting broader applicability (R1).
- **Incomplete evaluation of faithfulness and robustness:**
  - Missing complementary faithfulness metrics such as deletion and insertion (R3 M2). The paper should state how MuFidelity is approximated (number of samples, value of |S|) (R3 M2).
  - The paper does not demonstrate that UNI provides explanations that are not simply grayscale reproductions of the input; example attributions from different models on the same inputs would help show model-specific behavior (R4 Q6).
  - The method is only compared to IG and a few other attribution methods. More recent baselines (e.g., RISE, HSIC, Saliency Strike Back) are omitted (R3 M1, R3 m1). Related work section misses important black-box interpretability methods (RISE, Sobol) and some faithfulness metrics (R3 M1).
- **Unclear details and minor errors:**
  - The reasoning for monotonicity conditions (lines 297-298) needs clarification: how does the model process a single feature \(x_i\) rather than an instance \(x\)? How do the two conditions guarantee consistent sign of derivatives or resolve gradient saturation? (R4 Q3, Q4).
  - The relationship between completeness and path monotonicity is not explained; completeness is path-independent (R4 Q5).
  - Hyperparameters are not sufficiently discussed; a detailed analysis of computational overhead would strengthen claims about efficiency (R2).
  - Practical implications are unclear: how much new knowledge about neural networks does UNI reveal compared to other attribution methods? (R3 M4).
  - The paper does not discuss how UNI would adapt to other domains such as NLP, audio, or multimodal networks (R2, R3).
  - Minor typos (line 468 “simultatenously”) (R3 m2) and formatting issues: different \(\varepsilon\) formats in Algorithm 1, citation/parentheses issues, mislabeling “Figure 3” for “Table 3” (R4).
