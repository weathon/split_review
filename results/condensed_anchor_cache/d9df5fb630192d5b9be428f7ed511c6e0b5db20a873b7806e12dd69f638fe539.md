## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses causal discovery (CD) via continuous optimization under an additive noise model (ANM). It identifies instability in solutions of existing inverse-problem formulations (e.g., NOTEARS) and proposes a variation-negotiation regularizer that does not rely on graph-sparsity or other hypotheses. The regularizer estimates the noise (variation) in two ways and minimizes their disagreement. The authors then show a connection between the regularized optimization objective and the training of a diffusion model, leading to a proposed method called DAG-invariant Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (D³PM). Experiments on synthetic and real datasets are reported, with very high performance claimed.

### Strengths

- **Practically important regularizer**: The variation-negotiation regularizer does not depend on any hypothesis about the true DAG (e.g., sparsity), making it useful in real applications where no such prior is available. (R1)
- **Interesting connection between CD and diffusion models**: Linking regularized causal discovery to the training of a diffusion model is novel, creative, and under‑explored in the literature; this interdisciplinary direction opens avenues for future work. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Thorough technical exposition**: The problem is well‑formulated with clear technical details, objectives are well‑stated, and the overall direction is well‑motivated. (R2)
- **Impressive reported performance**: On the two tested function families (\(f_1,f_2\)), the method (D³PM) outperforms nearly all state‑of‑the‑art baselines by several orders of magnitude (Figs. 1a, 1b). (R1, R4)
- **Extensive experiments**: The paper includes experiments on both simulated and real-world datasets, demonstrating versatility. (R2)

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of theoretical guarantees / identifiability**: No proof that the proposed regularizer resolves instability or that the optimization yields the true underlying DAG. The concern is especially concrete: even if the negotiation term \(\|Z_X-Z_N\|^2\) is perfectly minimized, one can add any noise \(n\) to both \(Z_X\) and \(Z_N\) without changing the objective (Eq. 9), so the method may not identify the true residual. No identifiability discussion or theorem is provided for the ANM setting. (R1, R2, R3)  
  R2 adds: there is no theoretical justification that the regularizer effectively mitigates instability; “instability” itself is not formally defined. R3 notes that no assumptions guaranteeing identifiability (e.g., equal noise variances) are discussed.
- **Unclear how the regularizer addresses instability**: The paper claims Eq. (7) resolves the instability of Eq. (6) but provides no explanation or analysis of why stability improves. (R1, R2, R3)  
  R2: the relationship between the diffusion‑model connection and the instability goal is not clearly articulated. R3: it is unclear what “stability” means in context and why Eq. (6) is unstable in the first place.
- **Restrictive functional causal model**: Eq. (1) applies \(f\) element‑wise to each variable, which is much more restrictive than standard additive noise models and resembles a generalized linear model. The paper does not clarify whether \(f\) is element‑wise. (R3)
- **Experiments are not directly aligned with the instability claim**: The experimental design does not specifically test robustness under varying levels of data perturbation; it is unclear how the results demonstrate that the method solves the stated instability problem. (R2)  
  R4 raises stronger doubts:  
  – The two test functions (\(f_1,f_2\)) are only used in Ng et al. (2019); other well‑known baselines (e.g., Lachapelle, Sanchez, Wang) use Gaussian‑process (GP) generated data.  
  – R4 performed crude experiments with DAGMA on GP data (ER‑1, 100 nodes, 1000 observations) and found DAGMA achieved similar or better performance. This suggests that the reported impressive gains may not hold on more standard benchmarks.  
  – The authors should test on other ANMs (random MLPs, GPs, additive GPs). (R4)
- **Presentation issues**:  
  – The derivation of the variation‑negotiation regularizer is hard to follow (starts general, simplifies, adds terms, manipulates, re‑generalizes); clarity would improve by starting directly from Eq. (10). (R4)  
  – Many parts of the setting, methods, and contributions are unclear. (R3)  
  – Notation issues: use \(\mathrm{tr}\) instead of \(tr\); typo in Eq. (289) (\(X_0\) should be \(X_t\)); formatting of \(10^{-k}\). (R4)  
  – Code credits missing: functions like `betas_for_alpha_bar` are from OpenAI’s ImprovedDiffusion without attribution. (R4)  
  – Some tables in the appendix incorrectly bold D³PM when it does not have the best metric (e.g., Table 2, NOTEARS has lower FPR). (R4)
- **DAGness enforcement is still hypothesis‑based**: The paper criticizes \(\ell_1\) regularization for relying on graph hypotheses but then relies on the NOTEARS acyclicity constraint and iterative pruning (a hypothesis about DAGness). This weakens the claimed “intrinsic relation to CD.” (R4)
- **Novelty of regularized optimization**: Placing CD as a regularized optimization (data consistency + regularization) is already standard in methods like GOLEM, ENCO, NODAGS‑Flow. (R3)
- **Missing comparisons / ablated questions**:  
  – How does the proposed regularizer relate to existing regularizers (e.g., \(\ell_1\))? Can the method incorporate a low‑rank assumption if such prior knowledge is available (see “On Low‑Rank Directed Acyclic Graphs”, TNNLS 2024)? (R1)  
  – Does the least‑squares loss for the data‑consistency term imply that noise variances must be equal? (R3)  
  – What constraints (if any) are placed on the noise variables in the ANM? (R1)  
  – Why do all plots in Figs. 1a/1b show a consistent “dip” pattern (improvement then degradation) not seen in baselines? (R4)

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer was highly positive (score 6, confidence 3), praising the regularizer’s practicality and the interesting diffusion‑model connection. The other three reviewers (scores 1, 3, 3) were critical, emphasizing the absence of theoretical guarantees, insufficiently targeted experiments, and unclear presentation. The positive reviewer was willing to raise their score if the identifiability concern (specifically, the additive‑noise ambiguity in Eq. 9) is addressed.
