## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Personalized Visual Instruction Tuning (PVIT), a framework to overcome “face blindness” in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by enabling personalized dialogues targeting specific individuals. PVIT uses a multi‑phase pipeline (visual experts, image generation models, LLMs) to automatically generate large‑scale personalized training data, and incorporates people as a multimodal prefix via in‑context learning (no additional per‑person test‑time tuning). The authors also release a benchmark, P‑Bench (~1500 samples), to evaluate personalization in MLLMs. Experiments on LLaVA show substantial improvements after fine‑tuning with the curated dataset. Reviewer opinions range from moderate (scores 5–6, two reviewers raised scores after rebuttal) to strong (score 8), with the most positive reviewer highlighting the pipeline and benchmark while others raise concerns about scope, novelty, missing comparisons, and ethics.

### Strengths
1. **In‑context learning for personalization without per‑individual retraining**: PVIT leverages the in‑context ability of MLLMs, so no additional tuning is needed at test time for each new person.
2. **Automatic large‑scale data generation pipeline**: The multi‑phase pipeline (visual experts, image generation, (M)LLMs) produces a rich, diverse set of personalized instruction‑following conversations, which is a clear technical contribution.
3. **P‑Bench benchmark**: A dedicated benchmark (~1500 samples) for evaluating personalization in MLLMs, useful for future research.
4. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well‑written and easy to follow, explaining the PVIT framework, data generation, and experimental setup clearly.
5. **Empirical effectiveness**: Good qualitative and quantitative results on personalization tasks; ablation studies validate the contribution of individual pipeline components.
6. **Demonstration of capability**: Fine‑tuned LLaVA (P‑LLaVA) shows improved accuracy on personalized queries, and the approach works in a general MLLM without architecture changes.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited to human faces, not general personalization**: The paper claims to address general personalization but experiments focus exclusively on human faces (names and facial appearance). Questions about non‑human objects (Reviewer 1) and extending to abstract attributes like character/hobbies/professions (Reviewer 2) remain unanswered, limiting the claimed generalizability.
2. **Missing comparison with state‑of‑the‑art closed‑source models**: No evaluation against GPT‑4o, GPT‑4v, or Gemini on P‑Bench. Reviewer 1 notes that GPT‑4o performs well on examples in Figure 2, and Reviewer 4 asks how frontier models handle the face/personalization issue. Without these comparisons, the true performance of PVIT is unclear.
3. **Missing failure case analysis and data quality concerns**: The pipeline relies on pretrained MLLMs that can produce hallucinations, yet no mechanism to detect or filter such errors is described. Reviewer 3 calls for examples of failed cases and a strategy for filtering unreasonable data. Reviewer 1 also notes the absence of a detailed failure analysis, which would help understand the method’s limitations.
4. **Ethical and privacy issues not addressed**: The paper does not discuss the sourcing of human images used in training/testing, consent, or privacy measures. This is a critical omission, and reviewers 3 and 4 explicitly flag the lack of an ethics discussion and safety evaluation.
5. **Limited novelty of the method**: Several reviewers find the approach incremental:
   - Personalized wrapper tokens are similar to mechanisms already common in prior work.
   - The model training part (fine‑tuning LLaVA) is straightforward.
   - Overall, the method is not considered highly novel (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
6. **Insufficient experiments and missing ablations**:
   - **Zero‑shot ability**: No investigation of how the framework performs on unseen individuals (Reviewer 2).
   - **Temporal changes**: No evaluation on the same person at different ages (Reviewer 2).
   - **Difficult identity cases**: Not tested on twins, two people with similar faces/different names, or similar names/different faces (Reviewer 2).
   - **General benchmark performance**: No evaluation of P‑LLaVA on standard (non‑personalized) multimodal benchmarks (Reviewer 4).
   - **Specific model details missing**: LLaVA version used (Reviewer 3) and models in the dual‑level textual extraction module (Reviewer 3) are not specified.
7. **Technical issues and typos**: Line 191‑192 mentions two special tokens but only one is described (Reviewer 2). Also, the paper lacks a dedicated limitations section (Reviewer 3).
8. **Setting of compared models unclear**: For MLLMs that expect single‑image input, it is not explained how they are adapted to multi‑image settings (Reviewer 1’s question).
