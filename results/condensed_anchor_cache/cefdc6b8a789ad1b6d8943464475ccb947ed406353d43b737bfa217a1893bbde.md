## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Molecular Similarity-Aware Consistency Regularization (MSCR), a plug-and-play method using Matched Molecule Pairs (MMPs) and regularization terms (metric and distribution) to align chemical and latent spaces. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem but raise major concerns about novelty, evaluation completeness, clarity, and theoretical foundations. Scores: 1, 3, 5, 3. One reviewer (rating 5) is more positive, appreciating the motivation, piece-wise loss introduction, and ablation studies; the other three (ratings 1, 3, 3) are more critical, citing limited novelty, marginal improvements, incomplete baselines, and insufficient discussion of similarity definition and related work.

### Strengths
- The issue of inconsistency between chemical and latent spaces in molecular design is important and well-motivated.
- Figures 1 and 2 effectively depict motivation and methodology, enhancing clarity.
- The paper clearly outlines motivation and introduces the loss function piece-wise, helping reader understanding.
- Good evaluation across consistency, reconstruction, validity, and uniqueness metrics; the consistency metric is appreciated.
- Nice ablation statistics in Tables 1 and 2; MSCR ablation studies in Table 5.
- The proposed MSCR method is straightforward and plug-and-play, applicable to different generative models.
- Visualizations highlight molecular inconsistencies, improving interpretability.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and limited technical contribution**: MMPs are not novel; MSCR is not novel—minimizing KL divergence between encoder output and its semantically preserved transformation, and forcing similarity between sample pairs and latent pairs, are common techniques. Using consistency loss to constrain input and embedding space is a classic manifold learning problem; augmenting inputs and imposing consistency loss is not fundamentally different from contrastive learning and graph contrastive learning.
- **Incomplete evaluation and baselines**: Only three generative models (TransVAE, GEOLDM) are tested; evaluation lacks current diffusion-based models (e.g., Pocket2Mol, DiffBP, DecompDiff) and other architectures like MolMIM that already address chemical similarity. Need comparison with state-of-the-art methods. Only one similarity metric (Tanimoto) is used; no post-hoc analysis. Improvements are marginal, especially for GeoLDM on QM9 and diffusion models. Missing ablation on the hyperparameter controlling regularization strength. No scalability discussion or computational complexity analysis.
- **Poor definition and justification of similarity**: "Chemical similarity" is vague; it can range from structural to property similarity. The paper does not discuss how to define similarity; minor structural changes can cause activity cliffs. Tanimoto similarity may not capture true chemical similarity. The method does not address cases where structural similarity does not correlate with functional similarity. Need robust definition and justification (e.g., how is similarity score 0.8 in Figure 1b calculated? Which model is used for Figure 1?).
- **Missing experiments and analyses**: No case studies or visualizations of generated/optimized molecules. No interpolation results or depiction of latent space to demonstrate chemically consistent space. No analysis of how increased consistency enhances Bayesian optimization performance. Two-stage training outperforms one-stage but explanation is lacking; why use KL divergence (not symmetric) instead of Jensen-Shannon? Details on how to execute MMPs are insufficient.
- **Presentation and writing issues**: Introduction is too lengthy and convoluted; should focus on latent space regularization problem and directly state the importance of similarity consistency for optimization. Reproducibility is not ensured. Several typographical errors; presentation falls short of academic standards. Missing citations for related work on semantics in latent space (computer vision) and diffusion-based molecular generation models (Pocket2Mol, DiffBP, DecompDiff). Insufficient discussion of related work.
- **Marginal improvements**: Improvements on generation and reconstruction performance are very marginal, especially on diffusion models.
