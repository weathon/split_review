## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes action mapping (AM) for deep RL in continuous environments with state-wise constraints. The approach decouples learning into two parts: first pretraining a feasibility policy that generates feasible actions for each state, then training an objective policy to select optimal actions from that reduced set. This effectively transforms a state-wise constrained MDP (SCMDP) into an unconstrained MDP. AM is evaluated on a robotic arm end-effector pose task and a spline-based path planning environment, using both PPO and SAC. Results are shown against action replacement, resampling, and projection baselines, reporting higher returns and fewer constraint violations.

### Strengths

- The motivation is clear: converting SCMDP into an MDP improves sample efficiency and addresses constraints by learning a feasible action set.
- The paper provides a step-by-step explanation of related concepts, making it accessible.
- Action and state spaces for the experimental tasks are clearly described; visualizations at the end of the experimental section illustrate the effect of AM.
- AM allows agents to express multi-modal action distributions via a simple Gaussian in latent space, improving exploration.
- The method can work with approximate feasibility models, a notable advantage since perfect models are rarely available in practice (one reviewer highlighted this as a particular strength).
- The feasibility policy training is a novel approach that yields fewer constraint violations and higher returns compared to replacement, resampling, and projection.
- Experiments were conducted in realistic constrained environments (robotic arm, spline-based path planning).
- The approach is straightforward and can be combined with any RL algorithm (demonstrated with PPO and SAC).
- The target problem is of great significance to real-world applications.
- Related works are introduced and discussed satisfactorily.

### Weaknesses

- **No code provided**, making results hard to replicate.
- **Only two environments tested**, which limits the persuasive power and generality of the claims; comparisons in more diverse environments (e.g., Safety Gym, other constrained RL benchmarks) are missing.
- **Method is not end-to-end**: it requires pretraining with trajectory data (cost function and feasibility model), and the additional cost of this pretraining is not adequately discussed.
- **The assumption that the feasibility policy can be pretrained is strict**: it requires prior knowledge of the cost function $C^\tau(s;\pi)$ and the feasibility model $G(s,a)$, which may be difficult to obtain in general; in many real-world scenarios, the cost function must be learned through interaction, not known a priori.
- **Constraint violation plots for the path planning task are omitted**, making it impossible to verify claims about performance with approximate feasibility models.
- **Lack of analysis and ablations of the feasibility model**: key details are missing—how is state space sampling performed during training? What metrics determine sufficient training? How does the quality/approximation level of the feasibility model impact overall performance? Without this, the method’s robustness and applicability are unclear.
- **Baseline comparisons are limited to action mapping variants (resampling, projection, replacement). No comparisons with established constrained RL methods (e.g., Lagrangian-based approaches, Safe RL algorithms)**. This makes it difficult to judge relative performance.
- **One reviewer questioned novelty**, noting that content in Section 4 overlaps substantially with Theile et al. (2024); e.g., Equation 16 is nearly identical to the JS loss in Table 2 of that work.
- **Training process is not clearly described**: missing pseudocode, vague details on feasibility model training, and many implementation details absent (network structures, hyperparameters).
- **Several experimental details are missing**: number of seeds/trials in Figure 4 is not stated; why is SAC+Replacement not included in Figure 4(c)? Where is the constraint violation plot for SAC? The sudden increase of AM-SAC at 7500 steps in Figure 4(c) is unexplained.
- **PPO+Replacement strictly ensures constraint satisfaction during training, but AM does not guarantee this**; whether the authors propose any method to address this limitation is not discussed.
- **The definitions in Equations 5–7 seem unnecessary**, as only the function $g$ is assumed in Section 4 and experiments; the role of those equations needs clarification.
- **Minor issues**: typo on line 406 (“actiosn”); symbol $J$ in Equations 1–2 and 3–4 are inconsistent; legends in Figure 4 are too small.

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (rating 6) was quite positive, especially noting the multi-modality and approximate model capability as significant contributions. The other three reviewers were less positive (ratings 3, 3, 5), with two reviewers (ratings 3) raising serious concerns about strict assumptions, limited baselines, lack of novelty, and missing analyses.
