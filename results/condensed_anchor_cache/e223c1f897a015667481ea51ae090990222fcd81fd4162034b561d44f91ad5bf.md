## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a causal-inspired foreground-background feature discrepancy (FD) loss to improve medical image segmentation under data scarcity. The loss encourages separation of foreground and background embeddings at multiple layers. It introduces a new ultrasound dataset for triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC; 15 patients) and evaluates the loss across five datasets and three architectures, also showing robustness to distribution shifts when pooling multi-site data.

### Strengths

- The paper introduces a novel causal perspective on segmentation with scarce labeled data, noting why semi-supervised and augmentation methods are inadequate (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- A mathematical proof is provided establishing that the FD loss improves the upper bound of the Dice coefficient (Reviewer 1).  
- The loss is intuitive and architecture-agnostic; it can be applied at multiple layers of a U-Net with a trainable hyperparameter α to regulate per-layer importance (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- Consistent performance improvements over baselines without the loss across multiple datasets (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- The extension to handling distribution shifts when integrating multi-site data is an interesting benefit (Reviewer 3).  
- The new TNBC ultrasound dataset adds value for the community (Reviewers 1, 2, 4; Reviewer 2 asked about release).  
- One reviewer (score 5) found the loss design practical and the dataset valuable, though the other three (score 3) considered the contribution incremental and the evaluation insufficient.

### Weaknesses

- **Causal connection is not clearly established.** The link between the FD loss and causal mediation is not explained; the only connection is that segmentation depends on latent representations, which is widely recognized. Propositions on functional relationships and conditional independence appear irrelevant to the proposed method (Reviewers 1, 2). Visual or quantitative analysis of the mediator variable Z is missing to support the causal claim (Reviewer 1). Further theoretical analysis showing how the loss leads to causal relevance or irrelevance is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **The method is incremental and similar to prior work.** Adding a loss to increase foreground-background feature distance is conceptually similar to entropy minimization (Grandvalet & Bengio 2004), pixel-level contrastive losses (Chaitanya et al. NeurIPS 2020, MedIA 2023; He et al. AAAI 2021), deep supervision (Dou et al. MICCAI 2016), and learning-to-rank (Gong et al. ISBI 2024). The paper does not sufficiently discuss these relations (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 states the design is trivial, as it merely adds a loss to increase distance, and notes that the conclusion that imposing label supervision on intermediate features helps has already been validated (Isensee et al. Nature Methods 2021).
- **Insufficient evaluation for data scarcity.** Despite the title and framing, experiments only compare networks trained with vs. without the loss, not against other loss functions or methods designed for limited labeled data. More evidence is needed: varying training size, low-shot setup, comparison with data augmentation, and performance of Segment Anything (SAM) (Reviewer 4). The loss’s effectiveness specifically in data-scarce settings is not demonstrated (Reviewers 3, 4). The evaluation does not give a sense of how impactful the loss is relative to other approaches (Reviewer 3).
- **Methodology ambiguities and errors (Reviewer 2):**  
  - Why is the predicted mask \(\tilde{y}\) used instead of the ground truth in Definition 4? How is the label \(y\) defined for feature patches containing both foreground and background pixels?  
  - What is \(k\) in Definition 4—channel or depth? The use of 2D images but later mentioning \((i,j,k)\) is confusing.  
  - The formula for \(B_g\) appears incorrect (should it sum over \(k\)?).  
  - Symbol \(k\) should be replaced in Lemma 5 to avoid confusion with Definition 4.  
  - The proof of Lemma 5 does not account for size differences between feature maps and input images.  
  - It is unclear if the TNBC dataset will be released (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing extension to multi-class segmentation:** The loss is only formulated for binary foreground/background. It is not clear how it generalizes to multi-class settings (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues:** The loss is called "feature discrepancy loss" in the title and "feature distance loss" in the main text; Figure 6 lacks a legend; citations incorrectly use \citet instead of \citep; the framing focuses too much on breast cancer while the contributions are more general (Reviewer 3).
