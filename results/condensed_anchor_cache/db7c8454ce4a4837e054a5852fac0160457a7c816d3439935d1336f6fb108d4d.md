## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Cross-Query Residual Transformer (CResT) for object goal navigation on AI2-THOR. It adds residual connections to a Transformer for one-stage training and uses random masking for data augmentation. Experiments claim state-of-the-art performance, but reviewers find the contributions incremental, the experiments insufficient, and the novelty low.

### Strengths
- The research problem is interesting and holds broad potential value given the importance of visual representations in embodied tasks. (R1)
- CResT excels at extracting visual features from RGB images and achieves state-of-the-art performance on the AI2-THOR dataset. (R2) *(Note: this claim is questioned by other reviewers based on outdated comparisons.)*
- The proposed network can be trained in one stage rather than two, making the training process more concise and efficient. (R2)
- The Random Mask module augments both Global and Local features, improving generalization and reducing overfitting. (R2) *(However, R3 and R4 consider Random Mask a standard technique with little novelty; R3 questions if it is simply dropout.)*
- The submission clearly presents the ObjectNav task. (R3)
- The paper attempts to utilize information at different scales (global and local). (R3)
- The proposed residual connections might help training deeper architectures, though further investigation is needed. (R4; this is a very tentative strength)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution:**
    - The technique is an incremental improvement over VTNet by adding more shortcut connections and random masking. (R1, R3, R4)
    - The overall idea merely merges global and object features from VTNet in a slightly modified form; performance improvement over VTNet is not significant. (R3, R1) Table 2 shows a 1-layer Transformer achieving 0.7312 SR and a 6-layer only 0.7322 SR. (R1)
    - Residual connections have already been well-explored in Transformer literature (e.g., RealFormer [A], ResiDual [B]), but these works are not cited. The paper provides no justification for why its specific residual design is needed or novel. (R4, R3)
    - Random Mask is a common data augmentation technique; claiming it as a contribution is overstated. (R4, R2, R3) No comparison with other augmentation methods is provided to justify its effectiveness in this task. (R2)
    - The work appears more like a general visual extractor and is not specifically tailored to the ObjectNav task. (R3)

- **Insufficient explanation and justification:**
    - The paper claims the Residual Transformer solves the gradient vanishing problem, but no theoretical, visualization, or metric analysis supports this claim. (R1, R2, R3)
    - There is no evidence that gradient vanishing uniquely manifests in ObjectNav; experimental results or training curves showing this phenomenon are absent. (R3)
    - The motivation for the cross-query design is unclear: why is it implemented only in the decoder and not the encoder? No evidence or theoretical justification is provided. (R3)
    - The paper states that overfitting occurs when a larger network trains on a limited dataset, but no experiments or visualizations demonstrate overfitting in the ObjectNav context. (R3)
    - The intro does not properly explain the limitations of prior work or why the proposed visual representation is better—it reads like a related work section. (R4) Statements such as “we propose this because it is better” are uninformative. (R4)
    - Writing needs substantial improvements; there are minor language issues (e.g., “navigating” → “navigation”). (R4)

- **Insufficient experiments:**
    - Validation is only on the AI2-THOR dataset. Other benchmarks such as Habitat or ProcTHOR should be used. (R1, R4) If the goal is to enhance visual features, a more realistic environment like Habitat is more appropriate. (R4)
    - Comparisons with recent methods are missing. The paper compares with outdated works; several 2022–2023 AI2-THOR baselines are not included, e.g., CVPR 2023 papers (Object-Goal via Relations, Layout-Based Causal Inference) and ICCV 2023 (Dual Adaptive Thinking). (R1, R3)
    - **Experimental discrepancies:**
        - The performance reported for VTNet in this paper is lower than in the original VTNet paper. If the experimental setup differs, this should be clearly noted. (R4)
        - In the “No connections / All connections” ablation, the vanilla Transformer achieves only 14.6% SR, which is implausibly low given that Transformers (including VTNet) are known to work well for ObjectNav. This result demands further investigation. (R4)
    - The experimental section is insufficient to substantiate the method’s effectiveness and necessity. (R3)

- **Applicability concerns:**
    - The advantage of end-to-end ObjectNav is questionable when recent work shows such methods fail to transfer to the real world compared to modular approaches. (R1)
    - The reliance on the synthetic AI2-THOR environment limits the demonstration of real-world visual feature enhancement. (R4)
