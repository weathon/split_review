## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers evaluated this paper on a dual-branch HNSW with skip bridges and LID-driven optimization. One reviewer (score 1, confidence 5) strongly criticized the experimental setup as completely wrong and the method as not promising, while two reviewers (scores 5 and 5, confidence 3 and 4) found the contributions novel and partially effective but raised significant experimental concerns, especially regarding implementation language, baselines, and clarity. There is substantial disagreement between the minority negative reviewer and the majority positive reviewers.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and motivation (R2, R3):** The dual-branch structure and LID-based insertion mechanism are well-motivated and novel. The enhancements address key limitations of HNSW: local optima, cluster disconnections, and inference speed.
- **Important problem (R1):** The work targets an important problem for practical application.
- **Diverse benchmarks (R3):** Evaluation across CV, DL, and NLP datasets shows some clear performance gains with reported improvements (2.5–35% accuracy, 12% speed).
- **Readability (R3):** The paper is well-written with clear explanations of complex concepts.
- **Partial empirical evidence (R2):** Experiments have partially shown effectiveness of the proposed method.

### Weaknesses
- **Flawed experimental setup and weak baselines (R1, R2, R3):** The experimental setup does not follow the ANN-benchmark standard (R1). Baselines are weak: only own variations are compared without comparison to a real HNSW implementation in wall-clock time (R1); no advanced methods like IVFPQ from faiss are included (R2); and algorithmic comparisons are insufficient, using only GLOVE for NLP diminishes persuasiveness (R3). Authors should justify baseline choices.
- **Python implementation unsuitable for speed claims (R1, R2):** The algorithm is implemented only in Python, contradicting typical ANN practice where HNSW is in C. Additional operations, especially branching, may increase memory read cost and may not accelerate in C (R1). Python implementation is not a good language model for speed comparison; authors should provide a theoretical time complexity analysis to complement empirical results (R2).
- **Inference speed improvements not convincing (R1, R3):** The reported query time (Fig. 10) shows only modest gains, casting doubt on practical significance (R3). The method adds more operations in top layers but does not numerically show how many distance calculations or IP operations are saved in layer‑0; computational cost may increase (R1). One reviewer (R1) states the method is not promising based on Fig. 10.
- **Lack of clarity and missing details (R1, R3):** The paper lacks a running example; it is unclear what “exclude_set” contains (R1). The definition of LID(x) is unclear—LID reads as query-dependent but multiple high-LID nodes appear in figures; the meaning is ambiguous (R1). Pseudocode for the dual-branch structure, LID-based insertion, and bridge-building techniques would enhance understanding (R3).
- **Insufficient related work (R3):** The related work section is overly concise and lacks a comprehensive review of current research, limiting contextual understanding.
- **Minority reviewer strongly negative (R1):** One reviewer (score 1) finds the experiments “completely nonsense” and the method “not a really working method,” in direct contrast to the two positive reviews. This reviewer has prior top-conference HNSW publication experience.
