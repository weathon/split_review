## Merged Review

### Summary
Paper proposes Diffusion-based MORL (DiffMORL), a diffusion planning framework for offline multi-objective reinforcement learning. It conditions trajectory generation on preference weights and expected return, using classifier-free guidance and an offline data mixup technique to handle out-of-distribution (OOD) preferences. Experiments on the D4MORL benchmark show performance gains over baselines, with detailed ablations.

### Strengths
- Well written and motivated; the approach is clear and easy to understand.
- Applying diffusion modeling to offline MORL tasks is somewhat new and underexplored; it leverages the expressiveness of generative models to capture complex trajectory distributions.
- Strong empirical results on D4MORL: reported numbers clearly outperform baselines that do not use diffusion policy (e.g., MORvS, MODT).
- The mixup-based data augmentation is a reasonable strategy to address the challenge of limited preference coverage and OOD generalization.
- Detailed ablation studies investigate the contributions of each component; code is provided.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty across reviewers**: The paper is essentially a combination of known techniques (Diffuser, Decision Transformer, mixup) applied to MORL; no theoretical challenges or novel algorithmic insights beyond implementation and experimentation.  
  - Minority positive reviewer (score 5) acknowledges that the application is meaningful but still considers novelty below the bar of top conferences.
- **Data mixup concerns**: Linear interpolation of state and action trajectories is questionable. Interpolating between two trajectories (e.g., a HalfCheetah running at 2 m/s and 10 m/s) would likely generate incoherent, physically implausible behavior rather than a valid intermediate trajectory. The paper lacks an in-depth analysis of why this works and whether it produces meaningful synthetic trajectories. No evidence is provided that the technique would generalize beyond this specific setting (e.g., text-to-image generation would fail with a similar approach).
- **Comparison with prior diffusion baseline MODULI**: In Table 1, DiffMORL performs nearly similarly to MODULI, the existing diffusion-based method. The paper does not clearly discuss the differences and improvements over MODULI.
- **Missing analysis of key components**:
  - Independent Preference Encoding (IPE) is described as a natural design (no different from conditioning in standard diffusion models) and lacks deeper analysis showing exactly how IPE vs. preference concatenation (PC) affects trajectory generation; the paper only reports IPE scores higher than PC on one task (MO-HalfCheetah).
  - The role of episodic return as a conditioning criterion (set to a high value during inference) is not justified; the reasons for not using standard episodic return as a metric are not explained.
- **Insufficient justification for OOD handling**: The paper does not convincingly explain why mixup is preferred over existing offline data synthesis approaches using diffusion models (e.g., [1] Diffusion Planner and Data Synthesizer, [2] Synthetic Experience Replay).  
- **Minor citation issue**: When discussing classifier-free guidance (CFG) in Line 166, the paper should cite the original CFG paper rather than Song et al.
- **Practical concerns raised**: DiffMORL incurs high inference cost (10 hours for 2500 steps) and uses a large UNet backbone, whereas lightweight baselines like MORvS (small MLP) remain highly competitive. Could engineering improvements to MORvS yield even larger performance gains? This contrast is not addressed.
- **Missing theoretical clarity**: The paper should clarify why multi-objective RL cannot be reduced to scalarized single-objective RL by adding different rewards together, and why a vector reward formulation is necessary.
