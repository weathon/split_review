## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Cond-FiP, an amortized method for zero-shot inference of structural causal models (SCMs) from observations and causal graphs. It adapts the Fixed-Point Approach (FiP) to condition on dataset embeddings learned by a transformer encoder that predicts functional evaluations given noise samples. The method is evaluated on synthetic data and one real-world benchmark (Sachs, in appendix). It performs comparably to baselines (DoWhy, DECI, FiP) on noise prediction, observational sample generation, and interventional sample generation, both in-distribution and out-of-distribution. The problem is novel and well-motivated, but concerns remain about assumptions, evaluation scope, scalability, and clarity.

### Strengths

- **Novel problem and motivation**: One of the first works to generalize learning functional mechanisms of SCMs from arbitrary datasets and causal graphs, toward causally-aware foundation models (Reviewers 1,2,4). The zero-shot learning of causal models is highly motivated, especially due to limitations of per-dataset training (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear exposition**: The paper is well-written with clear intuitions and relations to similar work (e.g., FiP) (Reviewer 1). The method description is detailed and well-structured (Reviewer 2). Structured presentation (Reviewer 4).
- **Interesting empirical results**: Convincing performance, especially sample generation under distribution shifts and scaling up to 50- and 100-node graphs (trained on 20 nodes) (Reviewer 1). Comparable to SoTA baselines on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings (Reviewer 1).
- **Important task**: Zero-shot learning of causal models is an important task (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Strong assumptions limit practical applicability**:
  - **Additive noise model (ANM) assumption**: The method relies on ANM, which does not capture non-additive noise or more complex functional dependencies. This limits generalizability to real-world scenarios where ANM is violated (Reviewer 1). While ANMs are common, this is a strong constraint (Reviewer 1).
  - **Known causal graphs**: Assumption that the causal graph is known for all datasets is a significant limitation. In real-world settings, causal structure must be inferred, introducing additional challenges and uncertainties not explored here (Reviewer 1). While synthetic evaluation is a good proof-of-concept, this is a strong assumption (Reviewers 1, 2?).
  - **Noise samples required for training dataset embedding model**: Using noise samples as supervision for the dataset embedding model (Section 3.1) is unrealistic for most real-world datasets. Obtaining noise samples is not feasible in practice, limiting applicability despite theoretical appeal (Reviewer 1). The evaluation on Sachs (appendix) fits a Gaussian but lacks interventional results and does not resolve this limitation (Reviewer 1).

- **Limited experimental evaluation**:
  - **Insufficient baselines**: The paper claims matching SoTA but only compares against a small set of methods (DoWhy, DECI, FiP). Missing comparisons to cited works (Javaloy et al., 2023; Khemakhem et al., 2021) and architectures from causal discovery literature (e.g., normalizing flows, other deep generative models for functional mechanism inference) (Reviewer 2). More comparisons are needed, e.g., varying node numbers and generative mechanisms on DoWhy dataset (Reviewer 3).
  - **Lack of ablation studies**: No ablation of important modules (e.g., encoder) is presented, despite being an important factor for results (Reviewer 3). The role of DAG attention in dataset embedding is unclear; what would happen with standard dot-product attention? (Reviewer 2)
  - **Scalability limitation not in main text**: The method does not generalize well to larger datasets (as stated in Appendix C) (Reviewer 2). This limitation should be explicitly stated in the main text; lack of scalability restricts real-world applicability (Reviewer 2).
  - **Reproducibility concerns**: Details of train and test dataset generation are not provided: distribution of graph structures, parameterization of functional relationships, noise models used in training/evaluation (Reviewer 2). Graph density, differences in parameterization between P_in and P_out are missing (Reviewer 2). The regressor used as causal mechanism in DoWhy and DECI architecture are unspecified (Reviewer 2).
  - **Missing comparisons on interventional and sample generation**: Interventional and sample generation could be more comprehensive. A demo example showing how the method differs from others would help; the choice of which node to manipulate likely has a huge impact on results (Reviewer 3). Also, no evaluation on counterfactual inference, which is important in many applications (Reviewer 1).

- **Overclaiming results and need for theoretical justification**:
  - **Misleading claim of zero-shot SCM inference**: The paper claims "first time SCMs are inferred in a zero-shot manner from observations", but the method predicts samples and noise, not explicitly the SCM itself. The ability to infer the SCM (graph and functional relationships) is not demonstrated or evaluated (Reviewer 2). The claim is therefore misleading without direct evaluation of the inferred SCM (Reviewer 2).
  - **Weak theoretical foundation**: The theoretical justification for why zero-shot learning of causal models works is insufficient given the complexity and cross-domain SCM differences (Reviewer 3). There may be problems such as incapability of observation-based learning with conditional FiP (Reviewer 3). The paper should give more explicit explanation of why modeling noise helps zero-shot ability (Reviewer 4).

- **Clarity issues in method explanation**:
  - Ambiguity in notation: The symbol \(\mathcal{T}\) seems to have different meanings in the "Projection" paragraph and "Generation with Cond-FiP" paragraph (notational error?) (Reviewer 2). The relation between Eq. (5) and the equation in "Adaptive Transformer Block" is unclear; \(\mathcal{T}\) might denote the whole generation process, but it's not clear (Reviewer 2).
  - Interventional predictions explanation unclear: How exactly do you "modify in place the SCM obtained by Cond-FiP"? More elaboration needed (Reviewer 2). The mechanism for interventions is not made clear; presumably one feeds an intervened causal graph (with mutilations) and uses the corresponding dataset embedding for conditional generation, but this is not explicitly stated (Reviewer 1).
  - Background on causal learning insufficient: How to compute H and Jac_x? d_n and M are not defined (Reviewer 4). It is not explained how to use noise samples in training: Eq. (1) treats them as residuals, but Section 3.1 says they play the role of target variable (Reviewer 4).
  - Special requirements on training data: Do they need to cover as many domains? (Reviewer 4)

- **Performance inconsistencies**: In Tables 2 and 3, Cond-FiP sometimes has worse performance than FiP; this needs explanation (Reviewer 4). Also, why does performance of baselines on RFF OUT dataset degrade significantly compared to RFF IN? (Reviewer 2) Why does Cond-FiP perform similarly to baselines in noise prediction and sample generation when node scale is same or smaller than training? How is the FiP baseline implemented? In the original paper, FiP recovers topological ordering; is the FiP baseline here aware of the causal structure? (Reviewer 1)
