- Decision: Reject

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes V-Former, an offline RL method that extends implicit V-learning (IVL) to consider temporally extended actions (action chunks) using a Transformer-based policy trained with advantage-weighted regression. It aims to handle narrow, multi-quality data and varying control frequencies, evaluating on Robomimic tasks and Franka Kitchen. Reviewers note that the approach is clearly presented and the motivation is well described. However, opinions diverge: one reviewer found the results impressive and the method intuitive (rating 6), while three others rated it lower (3, 3, 5), citing limited contribution, insufficient baselines, and weak experimental evidence.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- Extending implicit V-learning to n-steps (multi-timestep value learning) is intuitive and well motivated (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
- The combination of IVL with a Transformer policy for open-loop action chunks is novel (Reviewer 4).  
- Results on increasingly suboptimal datasets are impressive (Reviewer 3).  
- The ablation study in Table 4 is appreciated (Reviewer 3).  
- The approach should be simple to implement and adopt (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution**: The main extension (value function over multiple timesteps) is straightforward and the insight that modeling multiple steps helps multimodality is already known in the literature (Reviewer 1). Another reviewer agrees that contribution appears modest (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient baselines**: Experiments mostly compare against ablations of V-Former (VF, VF w/o adv weight, BC, etc.) and do not include established offline RL methods such as IQL, BC-RNN, BCQ, CQL, Decision Transformer, Trajectory Transformer, or offline hierarchical RL works (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The absence of these baselines makes it hard to judge significance (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- **Narrow task/dataset scope**:  
  - Evaluations are limited to Robomimic (expert, suboptimal) and Franka Kitchen (multi-frequency). One reviewer notes the expert+random datasets seem artificial and suggests using the existing multi-human Robomimic datasets instead (Reviewer 4).  
  - The optimal action chunk size is around 3 in Robomimic tasks, which makes it difficult to distinguish the effect of temporally extended actions; the method should be tested on tasks requiring longer horizon control (e.g., D4RL *antmaze* and *kitchen*) (Reviewer 2).  
- **Lack of confidence intervals and low number of seeds**: Many results lack confidence intervals, and only 3 random seeds were run, which is very low (Reviewer 3).  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**:  
  - Action chunk length (N, k) is a task-dependent hyperparameter. No general way to derive it from the offline dataset is provided (Reviewer 1).  
  - The choice of N in Equation 9 (during learning) is not discussed (Reviewer 3).  
  - Different weight functions f(x) were used for Robomimic vs. FrankaKitchen, but no ablation for f(x) across both environments is shown (Reviewer 4).  
- **Methodology concerns**:  
  - It is unclear what value V_ψ(s) is modeling in value learning (Eq. 5-9). The n-step Bellman equation seems to approximate the optimal single-step policy, yet V_ψ(s) is proposed to model values for arbitrary action frequencies/lengths, which is confusing (Reviewer 2).  
  - The advantage of the proposed method over previous offline hierarchical RL works is not discussed (Reviewer 2).  
  - Action chunking as a fixed hyperparameter is restrictive; learning dynamic chunking (e.g., options) might be better (Reviewer 3).  
- **Experimental inconsistencies**:  
  - In Section 5.1 and 5.3, open-loop control achieves best performance, but Section 5.2 uses a close-loop VF for evaluation – no intuition is given for this choice (Reviewer 2).  
  - The success rates in Figure 3 are not normalized; some values exceed 1 (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing comparisons/ablations**:  
  - No evaluation on tasks with discrete action spaces (e.g., maze) (Reviewer 4).  
  - Limited range of action chunk sizes tested (Reviewer 4).  
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (3) finds the method intuitive and results impressive, while others (1, 2, 4) consider the contribution limited and the experiments insufficient, raising the concerns above.
