## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MONet, a polynomial network that uses only multilinear operators (Mu-layers) to capture multiplicative interactions, eliminating the need for activation functions. The architecture stacks Poly-blocks of Mu-layers to achieve high-degree interactions. MONet is evaluated on image recognition (CIFAR-10, ImageNet, ImageNet-C, MedMNIST) and scientific computing (Neural ODE solving), outperforming prior polynomial networks and achieving results on par with modern CNNs and Transformers. While reviewers agree the approach yields impressive performance and efficiency, they raise concerns about motivation, presentation, baseline fairness, capacity analysis, and practical memory usage.

### Strengths
- The Mu-layer design is simple, well-explained, and the underlying theory is well-proven (R1). It simplifies prior polynomial networks by stacking low-degree layers to enable high-order interactions (R3).
- MONet achieves impressive results on ImageNet1K compared to existing polynomial networks and baselines like MLPMixer and ViTs, particularly in Top-5 accuracy (R2). Reviewer 3 highlights that MONet-S matches state-of-the-art performance without any activations and shows strong robustness to image corruptions (e.g., weather, digital categories on ImageNet-C) (R2, R3).
- The model is competitive across multiple domains: image classification, medical image classification (MedMNIST), and scientific computing (ODEs). The ODE experiments demonstrate recovery of polynomial-like right-hand sides (R1, R2, R3). R3 notes the ODE performance appears strong in the presented cases.
- FLOPs and parameter analysis is provided and appears reasonable; memory usage (parameters/FLOPs) is drastically reduced compared to some baselines (R1). R2 acknowledges that FLOPs/params are reasonable in Table 1.
- An ablation analysis is performed on architectural choices (R2).
- R2 notes potential interpretability benefit for ODEs with a known polynomial structure, though this is niche.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and framing issues (R2):** The paper provides virtually no motivation for removing activation functions or for why polynomial networks should be preferred. The claim about interpretability/encryption is unsupported with no analysis. “Getting closer to SOTA alone is not enough motivation if there are no tangible benefits over existing methods.” The statement in the conclusion that the model “avoids the requirement for activation functions or other non-linear mappings” is misleading because multilinear operations themselves are nonlinear (polynomial of degree 2).
- **Poor organization and insufficient background (R2):** The paper is poorly written and organized; the background section is too short and does not place the work in context of existing polynomial network literature. It does not explain what specifically is different from prior polynomial networks, assuming the reader already knows.
- **Lack of generalization/capacity analysis (R1):** It is unclear why MONet generalizes when each Poly-block only captures up to fourth-degree interactions. The authors appeal to composition but provide no rigorous analysis of effective degree, capacity, overfitting, or relation to network depth. The specific choice of fourth-degree is not justified.
- **Baseline comparisons may be inaccurate or unfair (R1):** The reported P-Net numbers are not representative. With >11M parameters, P-Nets achieve 94.5% on CIFAR-10 and ~77% Top-1/94% Top-5 on ImageNet, closely matching or exceeding MONet with fewer parameters. The authors compared against a restricted version of P-Nets without activation functions, despite the original paper [1] emphasizing their importance. Similarly, ResNet-50 can be fine-tuned to 78.8%/94.5% Top1/5 on ImageNet, but Table 2 shows lower numbers. Justification for these choices is required.
- **High practical VRAM usage (R2):** Training MONet requires 4 A100 GPUs for a batch size of 448 (112 per GPU), whereas ViT B/16 and ResNet-50 fit batch sizes of 256 and 512 per single A100 respectively. This is a significant practical bottleneck not captured by FLOPs alone. (It is unclear if A100s are 40GB or 80GB.)
- **Misleading formatting (R2):** Bold figures in captions are misleading; in Table 2, bold does not always indicate the best result.
- **ODE experiments lack details and comparisons (R3):** (i) The paper should provide detailed quantitative results and compare with other state-of-the-art methods such as NeuralODE. (ii) It is unclear whether patch embeddings are necessary for the ODE task; more details on data processing and experimental settings are needed. (iii) The authors should describe how the model recovers the right-hand side of the Lotka-Volterra formula.
- **Low-rank matrix multiplication not explored (R3):** Could low-rank matrices be used for A in the mu-layer? How would this alternative perform?
