## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces $\delta$-Conservative Search ($\delta$-CS), an off-policy method for training GFlowNets that injects noise into high-scoring offline sequences (via random token masking with Bernoulli parameter $\delta$) and then denoises them using a GFlowNet policy. The goal is to improve robustness against proxy reward model misspecification in biological sequence design. The method includes an adaptive version that adjusts $\delta$ based on proxy uncertainty (via $\lambda$ and $\delta_{\text{const}}$). Experiments on DNA (TF-Bind-8, RNA-A), protein (GFP, AAV), and peptide design tasks compare against DyNA PPO, Bayesian optimization, and other GFlowNet-based active learning baselines. Reviewers had mixed opinions: one (score 8) found the exposition clear and the method effective, another (score 6) saw utility but questioned novelty and oracle reliability, while a third (score 3) was critical of experimental evidence and missing baselines, and a fourth (score 5) noted implementation complexity and the reliance on proxy uncertainty. All reviewers agreed on the value of balancing novelty and conservativeness, but raised concerns about the experimental demonstration, comparison scope, and justification of algorithmic choices.

### Strengths
*   The exposition is very clear (Reviewer 1).
*   The idea of $\delta$-CS—injecting noise into high-score sequences and then denoising with GFlowNet to constrain search—is neat (Reviewer 1).
*   The method balances novelty and conservativeness, addressing a key challenge in RL for scientific domains (Reviewers 2, 3).
*   The adaptive variant of $\delta$-CS adjusts conservativeness dynamically, which may be useful in practice (Reviewer 3).
*   The paper provides comprehensive empirical validation across diverse biological sequence design tasks: DNA, RNA, protein (GFP, AAV), and peptide, with comparisons to several baselines (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
*   It consistently outperforms existing methods (Reviewer 1), especially in large-scale scenarios (Reviewer 4).
*   The method mitigates proxy unreliability for out-of-distribution inputs by generating off-policy trajectories denoised from noise-injected high-reward sequences; the $\delta$ parameter controls how novel the denoised sequences are relative to existing ones (e.g., $\delta=1$ corresponds to full on-policy search) (Reviewer 4).
*   The paper leverages GFlowNets for sequential generation, enabling efficient diversity and high-reward sequence generation (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
*   **Adaptivity of $\delta$ is unclear**: The adaptive method sets $\delta$ based on proxy uncertainty $\sigma$, but because $\lambda \sigma \approx 1/L$, it reduces to $\delta \approx \delta_{\text{const}} - 1/L$, which depends only on sequence length $L$, not on $\sigma$. The name “adaptive” may be misleading. It would be helpful to see results if $\lambda$ were chosen as a constant (Reviewer 1).
*   **Choice and robustness of $\delta$**: The evaluation of $\delta$ in Appendix B.1 is for only one dataset; it should be extended to multiple tasks to understand what $\delta$ ranges outperform the strongest baseline and to assess robustness of $\delta$-CS to different $\delta$ values (Reviewer 1). All experiments in Table 1 should report whether the same $\delta$ was used across tasks (Reviewer 2).
*   **Novelty concerns**: The method draws from evolutionary search and existing GFlowNet frameworks, combining aspects rather than developing a unique algorithmic structure. The conservativeness parameter $\delta$ based on a Hamming ball is similar to existing techniques like uncertainty-based exploration in Bayesian optimization and UCB, and the paper does not demonstrate a significant advantage over such methods (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 (score 3) echoed that the novelty is limited, especially since existing off-policy GFlowNet methods (e.g., [7,8] from 2023) use local neighborhood exploration or Thompson sampling; these should have been direct baselines to show the effectiveness of $\delta$-conservativeness against local search and sampling from the same initial misspecified proxy model (Reviewer 3).
*   **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Several important baselines are omitted: trust-region methods for biological sequence design [1,2,3,4] and Bayesian optimization with generative models [5,6] would be more competitive and appropriate given the trust-region intuition of $\delta$-CS (Reviewer 3). Also, the paper does not explore whether $\delta$-CS would work with other search methods beyond GFlowNets (Reviewer 4).
*   **Proxy model uncertainty and reliability**: The method relies on the proxy model to provide reliable uncertainty estimates, but the paper itself notes the proxy is unreliable for out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs. It is questionable to trust the proxy for uncertainty while not trusting its output for search (Reviewer 4). Appendix B shows the adaptation is effective, but further justification is needed. Additionally, the accuracy and generalizability of the oracle function $f$ are critical; the paper does not discuss potential inaccuracy or bias when extrapolating to sequences far from the training data (Reviewer 2). There is no proxy model ablation—how does $\delta$-CS perform under different proxy models (e.g., from poor to high quality) (Reviewer 3)?
*   **Experimental evidence for robustness and actual benefit**: The experiments do not convincingly demonstrate the “actual benefit” of $\delta$-CS over related methods, especially regarding robustness to proxy misspecification. The paper should vary proxy model initializations (from poor to high quality) and evaluate performance, rather than only including one hard task with a small search space (TF-Bind-8). The hypothesis that GFlowNets struggle on long sequences due to proxy misspecification is not quantitatively validated when the search space is scaled up (Reviewer 3).
*   **Implementation complexity and hyperparameters**: The method involves many hyperparameters: $\delta$ (or $\lambda$, $\delta_{\text{const}}$), rank-based reweighted prior with hyperparameter $k$, and alternatives for selecting high-reward sequences and adapting $\delta$. It is unclear whether the specific “three steps” (Lines 187–189) provide a general improvement or if the paper cherry-picked one favorable combination (Reviewer 4). The performance gain of GFN-AL + $\delta$-CS over GFN-AL in Tables 1 and 2 is not substantial compared to the gain of GFN-AL itself, suggesting the main improvement may come from GFlowNets rather than from the search method. It might be worth investigating whether replacing the GFlowNet in GFN-AL with a modified version yields even better performance than modifying the search (Reviewer 4).
*   **Missing analyses and ablations**: There is no quantitative analysis of how the level of conservativeness/noise injection changes across active learning rounds, which would validate the balancing capability of $\delta$-CS (Reviewer 3). Also missing are: batch size ablations (Reviewer 3), analysis of convergence and diversity across rounds (Reviewer 3), and a discussion of how $\delta$ improves exploration, sample complexity, or convergence theoretically (Reviewer 3). The initial dataset sizes (e.g., 50% of whole search space for TF-Bind-8, very large for GFP) are much larger than typical active learning setups; the effect of initial dataset size should be studied, especially in large search spaces (Reviewer 3). Diversity/novelty calculation and how it evolves across rounds should be clarified (Reviewer 3).
*   **Unclear motivation for RL approach**: It is not clear why training a reward model + policy network (GFlowNet) is beneficial over directly using the proxy model for querying in an active learning framework, given that the proxy is already available and inexpensive (Reviewer 3).
*   **Limitations and trade-offs**: The paper lacks a clear discussion of limitations and potential drawbacks (Reviewer 3). The trade-off between exploration (to recover from local optima and collect diverse points) and over-exploration (yielding unreliable results) could be argued more clearly (Reviewer 3).
*   **Minor writing issues**: Line 77 explanation of search space size is unclear; the transition between RL and active learning terminology could be smoother (e.g., “trajectories” not defined). Appendix B.1, line 832 has a misspelling: “conservatives” (Reviewer 3). Line 478 phrase “prone to generating low rewards” might be misworded (Reviewer 3).
*   **Additional questions raised by reviewers that overlap with missing experiments or ablations**: 
    *   Are all experiments in Table 1 based on the same $\delta$? (Reviewer 2)
    *   What is the initial dataset $D_0$, how is it initialized, and what is the cost to acquire rewards for $D_t$? (Reviewer 2)
    *   Can $\delta$-CS be used with any other proxy model? (Reviewer 3)
    *   For baseline methods that require a surrogate (e.g., BO), is the same proxy model of $\delta$-CS used with the same initial dataset? (Reviewer 3)
    *   How is diversity/novelty calculated? Is it the diversity of the last top-k batches? (Reviewer 3)
    *   In Figure 5 (AAV task), why is there a substantial diversity difference between $\delta$-CS and baselines? (Reviewer 3)
    *   How does performance vary with respect to initial dataset size, particularly in large search spaces? (Reviewer 3)
    *   How does noise injection/level of conservativeness differ across active learning rounds; was behavior analyzed with respect to proxy uncertainty? (Reviewer 3)
