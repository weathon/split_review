## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes AutoGenDA, an automated generative data augmentation method for imbalanced classification. It uses BLIP2 to caption images, then transfers caption components (”class-agnostic” parts) to minority classes via StableDiffusion (SD1.4) to generate new samples. An automated search strategy (Gumbel Softmax) optimizes per-class mixing of identity, local-caption, and transfer-caption augmentations. Experiments on Pascal VOC, Caltech101, MS-COCO, and LVIS claim up to 4.9% improvement over standard baselines. Reviewers generally find the idea natural and well-described, but raise major concerns about the underlying assumptions, missing baselines, insufficient datasets, limited novelty, lack of statistical rigor in ablation studies, and high computational cost.

### Strengths

- The idea of using image captions (format “a photo of <y> that …”) to capture class-specific and class-agnostic variance is straightforward; captions are easy to obtain and transfer between classes. (R1)
- The automated search mechanism adaptively selects between identity, local-caption, and transfer-caption strategies per class based on data distribution. (R1)
- Results on four object classification datasets (Pascal VOC, Caltech101, MS-COCO, LVIS) show improvement in most imbalanced and low-shot settings. (R1, R2)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (R2)
- The experimental section is elaborate, including ablation studies on a search-free version, non class-agnostic, and non class-specific augmentations (Table 2). (R2)
- The method outperforms the DAFusion baseline. (R3)
- Preliminary experiments that did not work are described in the appendix, showing transparency. (R3)
- The problem of learning from long-tailed/imbalanced data is important in practice. (R4)
- The overall idea is natural. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Assumption about generative model limitations may be wrong.** The paper claims generative models struggle to produce diverse variations for data-deficient classes (line 044), yet it uses StableDiffusion 1.4 pre-trained on LAION-5B (billions of images). Such a model likely already generalizes well to many classes, weakening the premise. (R2, high confidence 5)  
- **Class-specific vs. class-agnostic differentiation is unclear and risky.** The method truncates captions after “a photo of <y> that …” and treats the remainder as class-agnostic. However, captions often contain class-specific attributes (e.g., “thick whiskers” for a cat). Transferring these to another class (e.g., cow) can generate unnatural images. (R2)  
- **Caption transfer can cause semantic mismatch.** Nearest‑neighbor visual similarity does not guarantee caption compatibility. Example: the caption “sitting on top of the books” is transferred to an elephant (Figure 5), which may be semantically inappropriate. The class‑filtering via m neighbors lacks ablation studies on m and needs more robust semantic verification. (R1)  
- **Missing critical baselines.**  
  - Vanilla text‑to‑image StableDiffusion 1.4 (no caption transfer) is not compared, making it impossible to isolate the effect of the proposed pipeline. (R2)  
  - Several closely related generative augmentation methods are omitted: e.g., Fill‑Up, Diffuse‑Mix (R3), and baselines from references [*1, *2] (R4).  
  - No comparison of computational throughput or time‑per‑image against any baseline. AutoGenDA relies on BLIP2 (large VLM) and per‑dataset textual inversion fine‑tuning of SD1.4, both expensive at inference time. (R2)  
- **Insufficient and non‑standard datasets.** Evaluations are limited to Pascal VOC, Caltech101, MS‑COCO, and LVIS. Reviewers consistently request standard long‑tailed benchmarks: ImageNet‑LT (R2, R3, R4), iNaturalist (R4), CIFAR‑10/100‑LT, Places‑LT, iNat18, and NICO++ (R3). Without these, the results are hard to contextualize. (R2, R3, R4)  
- **Ablation study is weak.**  
  - The search‑free baseline uses only uniform sampling of augmentation types; a stronger baseline would dynamically adjust probabilities based on class sample size. (R1)  
  - The ablation in Table 2 shows only ~0.5% accuracy difference between AutoGenDA and a “local‑caption only” variant, yet no variance or statistical significance is reported. Given Table 1 reports standard deviations up to 3%+, these differences may not be significant. (R3)  
  - The dataset used for Table 2 is not specified. (R1)  
  - Trends of the learned augmentation probability α with respect to the number of shots are not shown or explained. (R1)  
- **Limited qualitative evaluation.** Only 2 generated examples are shown (Figure 5). More examples with different learned α values would help intuition. (R2)  
- **Use of only dataset captions restricts diversity.** The method does not exploit LLMs to generate plausible alternative captions, which could introduce richer class‑agnostic variations. (R1)  
- **Computational cost and overhead are not addressed.** The method requires fine‑tuning a diffusion model via textual inversion and running a large VLM at inference time. No memory or time overheads are reported; these should be included in the limitations. (R2; also raised as a question by R1)  
- **Lack of novelty and related‑work awareness.** The paper claims to be the first to study automated generative data augmentation, but there is an existing research field on this topic (e.g., Fill‑Up, Diffuse‑Mix, and others). Missing related work weakens the contribution claim. (R3)  
- **Basic assumption may be wrong: the text-guided image generator should itself be trained on long-tailed data to properly handle minority classes.** (R4)  
- **Minor issues:** Typo in paragraph title at line 359. (R3)
