## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces UniVIEDM, a unified diffusion model for Visual Information Extraction (VIE) that integrates three subtasks—line grouping, Semantic Entity Recognition (SER), and Relation Extraction (RE)—into a single framework. It represents document layout as a plane graph and casts subtasks as plane graph generation problems. A jump-diffusion process is used in pre-training to learn valid bounding box sets and line segments, while a continuous-time Markov chain diffusion model predicts labels during fine-tuning. Reviewers have mixed opinions: some praise the unified formulation, solid writing, and the non-trivial application of graph diffusion, while others criticize limited novelty, missing empirical evidence for computational complexity claims, insufficient comparisons with joint models (e.g., UniVIE, Doctr), lack of ablation studies, and over-reliance on the appendix. One reviewer (score 5) is notably more positive about the core contribution, while another (score 1) finds the paper unconvincing.

### Strengths
- Well-organized introduction and related work that clearly articulate contributions and target issues.
- Thorough experimental settings, facilitating reproducibility.
- Use of jump-diffusion and continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) to address varying data dimensions and insensitivity to generation order.
- Generative modeling on graphs using diffusion is non-trivial and innovative (minority reviewer's view), as it handles variable graph size (N nodes, N edges) and provides a starting point for document/form generation with graph diffusion models.
- Solid writing and neat correspondence between equations (Eqn 1–6) and model components.
- Unification of three mainstream VIE tasks into one model, which may reduce computational complexity compared to separate models.
- Competitive performance across multiple public datasets (SER F1, Pair F1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of empirical demonstration for claimed computational complexity reduction.** The paper claims O(N^2) reduction for entity relationship extraction, but experiments only compare performance (e.g., F1 scores) without measuring training time, inference steps, or number of relationship combinations compared to a fixed iteration baseline. The claimed efficiency is not supported; in fact, GCN-based discriminative models require one forward pass, whereas the diffusion model requires multiple denoising steps, making the efficiency gains dubious. *Request: report runtime comparisons between the proposed diffusion model and a GCN-based counterpart using the same architectural backbone, and specify the number of diffusion steps. Show training/iteration complexity directly.*
- **Missing RE F1 metrics in Tables 1 and 2.** For the proposed algorithm, only SER F1 and Pair F1 are reported; RE F1 is left blank. The same cells for baseline methods are also empty, making it impossible to compare overall VIE performance. *Request: fill all metric cells to enable fair, transparent comparison.*
- **Insufficient comparison with joint models.** Table 1 includes only UniVIE among joint approaches; Table 2 contains no joint models at all. This limits a fair evaluation against prior unified VIE systems. Prior work such as Doctr (Liao et al. 2023) achieves higher performance on CORD than UniVIEDM but is neither discussed nor compared. *Request: add Doctr and other joint models to the comparison tables and discuss differences.*
- **Missing ablation studies.** The experiments section contains no analysis of the contribution of each component (e.g., using or not using jump-diffusion, removing stage 1 or 2, replacing diffusion with GCN features). Without ablation, it is impossible to understand why and how the proposed approach functions as it does. *Request: provide ablation studies on each stage, including a version that uses pretrained GCN instead of diffusion for stages 1 and 2, then trains only the label-prediction stage with diffusion.*
- **Novelty concerns.** The core graph diffusion method is not original (it follows Campbell et al. 2024). The paper applies diffusion models without strong motivation or proven benefits in efficiency or metric gains over simpler discriminative GCNs (e.g., ROPE). The plain graph formulation is also not new (see beta-skeleton graph in ROPE: “ROPE: Reading Order Equivariant Positional Encoding for Graph-based Document Information Extraction”). The generative power of diffusion is not realized—the model only generates labels on existing layouts, not novel documents. *Request: discuss ROPE and compare graph formulations; justify why DM is needed beyond discriminative alternatives.*
- **Missing details and references.** Important information (e.g., network architecture) is relegated to the appendix, but many reviewers could not find the appendix. Lines 272-273 refer to appendix; lines 205-206 use “2+ types of edge labels” without explanation. *Request: include key design details in the main body and clarify the ambiguous phrase.*
- **Scaling issue impact unclear.** The paper motivates the approach by the scaling issue (quadratic complexity) but does not detail its practical impact on training or inference. If the complexity does not significantly hinder training, the contribution may be minimal. *Request: demonstrate the practical effect of the scaling issue in existing joint models and show how the proposed method alleviates it in terms of training time, memory, or accuracy.*
- **Limited rationale for diffusion model in VIE.** There is no convincing argument for treating SER and RE as generative problems rather than discriminative ones. The paper does not show that the generative formulation provides any advantage over standard GCNs, which are simpler and cheaper. *Request: provide an analysis (e.g., performance vs. runtime trade-off) comparing the diffusion approach to discriminative baselines.*
