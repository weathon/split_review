## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an unsupervised low-light image enhancement method (RLPCM) that integrates a latent consistency model (LCM) with Retinex-based range-null space decomposition. It uses a global illumination adjustment on the range-space component and a natural language guidance module to refine the null-space component. The authors also contribute a new UAV low-light dataset (UAV-LL, 300 pairs). Experiments on benchmarks are reported. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: one reviewer (score 6) finds the approach valid and the modifications appropriate for low-light, while another (score 1) considers the core idea an over-claim and the comparisons unfair.

### Strengths
- **Integration of recent techniques:** The paper explores the combination of consistency models, range-null space decomposition, and language prompts for low-light enhancement, which is a creative perspective (all reviewers).
- **Algorithmic clarity:** Algorithm 1 provides a clear, step-by-step guide to the RLPCM process, aiding reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- **Natural language guidance as a null‑space component:** Using language prompts to learn the null‑space component is a creative application (Reviewer 1).
- **Ablation studies confirm design choices:** Ablation experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed components (Reviewer 2).
- **Quantitative results are strong:** Table 1 shows impressive quantitative results on benchmarks (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Manuscript structure:** The paper is logically structured, easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **One reviewer (scoring 6) specifically notes that combining diffusion‑based null‑space models with low‑light enhancement is valid and that the modifications fit the low‑light degradation well** – a more positive assessment of the technical direction than other reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair training of the lighting decoder:** The lighting decoder is trained on additional normal‑light images from a benchmark dataset, giving RLPCM an unfair advantage over prior methods that adhere to stricter protocols (multiple reviewers: 1, 2, 4, 5). Reviewers question whether training on out‑of‑domain data like COCO or ImageNet would be feasible (Reviewer 2).
- **Null‑space refinement degrades for full‑rank A:** If matrix A is assumed full‑rank, its null space contains only {x=0}, so the “null‑space refinement” term reduces to a scalar factor (1 – γ) and becomes equivalent to classifier‑free guidance. This undermines both the technical novelty and the claim of null‑space manipulation (Reviewers 2, 5; one reviewer (score 1) calls it an over‑claim).
- **Limited novelty:** The application of range‑null space decomposition in a diffusion‑based framework (following Wang et al. 2023b) and the use of text prompts for classifier‑free guidance are established in low‑level vision; replacing the diffusion model with a latent consistency model (Luo et al. 2023) is seen as incremental (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Motivation and justification of the UAV‑LL dataset is weak:** The paper does not clearly explain why a new UAV‑captured low‑light dataset is needed given the abundance of existing datasets. Details on scene types, illumination conditions, and key advantages are missing (Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 5).
- **Language guidance unclear:** The motivation for using contrasted language prompts to refine the null space is not well explained; it is not obvious how CFG (Ho & Salimans 2022) inspires this. The guidance‑scale parameters and how the prompts were obtained are unspecified (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Visual comparisons are not convincing:** Qualitative results (Figs. 5–12) mainly show low‑contrast night scenes; improvements over methods like Zero‑IG, QuadPrior, and LightenDiffusion are not clearly visible (Reviewer 3). The ground‑truth images in the UAV‑LL dataset are themselves quite dark, making the proposed method’s darker outputs appear to be favoured by the metric (Reviewer 4). Zoom‑in windows and evaluations on more complex scenes (e.g., DARKFACE) are missing (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unsupported claims:** The paper claims the global illumination prior “physically guarantees the reliability of range‑space content” and that the consistency model is “more effective for generating null‑space content,” but provides no analysis or evidence for either claim (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation studies:** The ablation table (Table 3) does not include results for removing the self‑attention swapping mechanism, using different language prompts, or varying guidance scales (Reviewers 4, 5). The impact of noise removal (e.g., high‑ISO noise) is not considered (Reviewer 1).
- **Computational efficiency not assessed:** No comparisons of model parameter count, inference speed, FPS, or training time against related methods (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues:** Several typos and formatting errors (e.g., line 70 reference should be Luo et al. 2023, line 086 capitalisation, line 398‑399 grammar, line 462 “RNLP” should be “RLPCM”); unclear how many GPUs were used; no commitment to release code or training data, which hinders reproducibility (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). Related work section should be placed in the main paper given a 10‑page limit (Reviewer 5).
