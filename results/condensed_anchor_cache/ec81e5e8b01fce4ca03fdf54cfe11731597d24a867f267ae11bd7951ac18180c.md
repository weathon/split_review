## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Distorting-based Learning Complexity (DLC), a training-free hardness score that estimates sample difficulty by averaging classification loss over several randomly masked pre-trained models, using Monte Carlo integration. A flexible under-sampling strategy, FlexRand, is introduced to replace top-K pruning and mitigate distribution shift by sampling from easy and hard groups. Experiments on downstream image and instruction datasets show state-of-the-art performance with a 35× reduction in pruning time. Reviewers assigned ratings of 6, 6, and 8; the third reviewer was notably more positive, strongly emphasizing the novelty of the random-mask design and the thorough evaluation on both image and instruction tasks, while the first two reviewers were more cautious regarding robustness claims and practical constraints.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency and significance**: DLC is a training-free scoring method that drastically reduces pruning time (35× on image benchmarks) while maintaining performance, directly addressing the computational burden of fine-tuning large pre-trained models.
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The paper is structured clearly and logically.
- **Adaptive under-sampling**: FlexRand adds flexibility by dividing data into groups based on DLC scores and sampling randomly from each, which avoids the severe distribution shift of top-K strategies and adapts across different data regimes.
- **Novel design of random masks**: The use of randomly masked networks to produce classifiers with different capabilities (without fine-tuning) is interesting and practical; the prediction head is simply an averaged feature, eliminating the need for downstream classifier training.
- **Comprehensive experimental validation**: The method is evaluated on both image and instruction datasets, demonstrating consistent performance improvements across tasks. Detailed figures and baselines are provided.
- **Concrete empirical results**: DLC reduces pruning time by 35× in images pruning benchmark.

### Weaknesses
- **Sensitivity to pre-trained models**: The claim that DLC is not sensitive to the quality of pre-trained models requires stronger evidence. Additional experiments needed across a wider range of model sizes (very small to very large), different pre-training strategies (self-supervised vs. supervised), and weakly versus fully supervised models—including raw performance comparisons of those models. The correlation between DLC scores and actual pruning gains should also be explicitly analyzed.
- **Memory constraints of storing masked models**: The method requires storing multiple masked copies of the model, which may be prohibitive under limited memory. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of memory footprint (number of models, size per model, impact of masking strategy) and potential mitigations (e.g., efficient data structures).
- **Scenarios of underperformance and hyperparameter sensitivity**: The paper should discuss cases where DLC might fail—e.g., when downstream distribution differs greatly from pre-training data, for different data types (image vs. text), and with varying hyperparameters (number of masks, approximation of loss integration). The splitting hyperparameter γ requires tuning, and its tuning time should be included in the pruning time comparison of Figure 1.
- **Lack of explicit use of “learning path”**: The paper argues that easy samples are correctly classified early in learning, but DLC averages loss over random masks—this definition of a “learning path” does not directly appear in the method.
- **Applicability to prior methods is not novel**: The strategy of using a pre-trained model with averaged features as a prediction head can be applied to existing methods like Herding. The paper should test whether this simple substitution also improves those baselines, to isolate the benefit of the DLC scoring itself.
- **Similarity to Dataset Quantization**: The grouping and random-sampling idea closely resembles Dataset Quantization (DQ). The authors need to discuss the differences and advantages over DQ explicitly.
- **Missing details and clarifications**:
  - Exactly how is masking applied to network parameters? (e.g., random weight zeroing? dropout-like?) This is asked by multiple reviewers.
  - How is loss integration implemented? In integration figures, the loss upper bound is 1.0—is it normalized to (0,1)?
  - In Figure 4(a), the MMD value for the Random baseline is missing; it should be shown for completeness.
  - In Figure(d), clarify what the dotted lines represent for data fractions 10%, 20%, 30% (blue, green, orange).
  - Minor errors: Line 021 missing “strategy” after “under-sampling with randomness”; unify “pretrain”/“pre-train” usage; unify notation for sample number (N vs. |D|).
