## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HyperSurv (also called Tri-MoME), a multimodal survival prediction framework integrating Whole Slide Images (WSIs), genomic data, and pathology reports. It extends an existing MoME architecture with hyperbolic geometry constraints (Lorentz manifold) to model hierarchical relationships between pathology terms and image regions. Two losses are introduced: Feature-pooled Entailment Constraint (FEC) and Text Genericity Constraint (TGC). Experiments are conducted on four TCGA cancer cohorts. One reviewer (score 5) finds the problem well-defined, the integration of three modalities effective, the hyperbolic innovation promising, and the experiments comprehensive. Three reviewers (scores 3,3,3) have major concerns about the evaluation framework, missing comparisons, lack of empirical validation of claimed relationships, and data contamination of the CTransPath encoder.

### Strengths
- **Relevant and under-explored problem**: Integration of pathology reports with WSI and genomics for survival prediction is timely and clinically valuable. (R2, R4)
- **Innovative use of hyperbolic space**: The application of hyperbolic geometry to model hierarchical entailment between pathology terms and image regions is novel and conceptually aligned with the data structure. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Clear presentation and helpful visualizations**: The paper is well-written, logically organized, and figures effectively communicate the framework. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Comprehensive experiments on multiple datasets**: Results are presented on four TCGA cancer cohorts with both c-index and Kaplan-Meier curves. (R2, R3)
- **Ablation study and comparisons to existing methods**: The paper includes ablation experiments and comparisons to prior work. (R4, but noted as well-conducted)  
- **Various validation approaches**: Use of KM curves in addition to c-index. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing recent baselines and unfair comparisons**:
  - The paper omits comparisons with state-of-the-art early fusion methods from the past year: SurvPath (CVPR'24), PIBD (ICLR'24), and MMP (ICML'24). SurvPath also uses gene groupings and should be included. (R1, R3)
  - All comparison methods use only two modalities (WSI+genomics); it is unclear whether gains come from adding a third modality (text) or from the proposed architecture. Without controlled bimodal baselines (e.g., pathology+genomics, genomics+text, pathology+text using the MoME backbone with hyperbolic constraints), the contribution of text cannot be isolated. (R1)
  - No comparison with a model that integrates clinical metadata (age, sex, tumor grade, stage) instead of pathology reports; TCGA pathology reports mostly contain such tabular attributes, which could be introduced via late fusion. (R1)
- **No empirical evidence for claimed pathology-text hierarchy**: The core claim—that pathology terms map to multiple image regions and convey more generic concepts—is not experimentally or visually validated. No attention maps, cross-attention visualizations, or examples of captured hierarchies are provided. (R2, R4)
- **Confounds in evaluation framework**:
  - **No site-stratified cross-validation**: The 5-fold CV ignores tissue source sites, which can artificially inflate performance due to demographic confounding (Kather et al., Howard et al., Vaidya et al.). Site-stratified splits are necessary. (R3)
  - **Data contamination of CTransPath**: CTransPath is pre-trained on TCGA, so the same slides are used for pre-training and testing. This is increasingly considered unethical given growing awareness of data leakage. The authors should use a non-TCGA foundation model (PLIP, CONCH, UNI, Virchow, GigaPath). (R1, R3)
  - **Hyperparameter tuning on validation set**: Best hyperparameters (Table 3) are selected on the validation set; evaluation should be done on an independent test cohort such as CPTAC-LUAD or CPTAC-UCEC. (R3)
- **Marginal gains and lack of statistical significance**: Performance improvements over baselines are often within standard deviation; no statistical tests (e.g., permutation test) are provided to confirm significance. (R3)
- **Incomplete multimodal relationship modeling**: The method models only text-image relationships. It does not capture text-gene relationships (e.g., links between genes and pathology terms) or image-gene relationships, which could further improve performance. (R2)
- **Insufficient ablation of architecture components**:
  - No comparison of MoE design with a conventional Transformer that can handle token integration across long contexts; recent works (PIBD, MMP) reduce WSI to bag of prototypes making cross-attention straightforward. (R1)
  - The hyperbolic loss is only validated within MoME; its general usefulness should be tested on a different backbone (e.g., CGM). (R4)
- **Lack of implementation and preprocessing details**: Code is not provided, and preprocessing steps for pathology reports are not described. This hinders reproducibility. (R2)
- **Ambiguous motivation for MoME and hyperbolic representation**: The reason for choosing MoME over a simpler backbone like CGM (which performs better per Table 1) is unclear. The hyperbolic space motivation relies on MERU (Desai et al., 2023) and is not self-contained for general readers. (R4)
- **Efficiency claim unsubstantiated**: The claim of "enhancing efficiency" is not supported; no runtime or parameter comparisons with existing methods are given. (R4)
- **Minor writing issues**: Typos in lines 98 and 115; incorrect statement that UNI and CONCH are different architectures from ViT (they are ViT variants); missing references for genomic survival architectures (e.g., Chen et al. for SNN). (R3)

**Minority positive opinion**: One reviewer (score 5) found the integration effective and the experiments comprehensive, while the three other reviewers (scores 3,3,3) had strong reservations about evaluation soundness, missing evidence, and reproducibility. (R2 vs. R1,R3,R4)
