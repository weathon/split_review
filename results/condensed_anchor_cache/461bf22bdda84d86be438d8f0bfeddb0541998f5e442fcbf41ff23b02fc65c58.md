## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the interplay between sparsity and quantization for model compression. The authors mathematically prove that these operations are not orthogonal: the order of application significantly impacts combined error, with sparsity before quantization (S→Q) being optimal. They introduce an “orthogonality bound” metric to estimate the performance of sparse-quantized models without retraining. Empirical studies on OPT, LLaMA (125M–8B), ViT, and ResNet, measuring perplexity, cross-entropy loss, and ImageNet1k accuracy, largely confirm the theoretical predictions.

### Strengths
- Timely and important topic: the interaction between two widely used compression methods is often overlooked, and the paper provides both theoretical and practical guidance.
- Clear theoretical contribution: proves non-orthogonality of sparsity and quantization, derives optimal order (S→Q), and establishes error bounds (Theorems 3.5–3.9). The proofs are rigorous and presented in an intuitive manner.
- Introduces the “orthogonality bound” metric, a practical tool for practitioners to estimate combined compression impact without full retraining.
- Extensive empirical validation across diverse model families (OPT, LLaMA, ViT, ResNet) and model sizes (125M–8B), confirming theoretical findings.
- Comprehensive literature summary; well-structured paper with clear notation, definitions, and informative tables and appendices.
- Empirically evaluates both LLMs (WikiText2 perplexity) and vision models (ImageNet1k accuracy), with supplementary analysis of several leading LLM pruning/quantization methods (SparseGPT, WANDA).
- Challenges conventional assumptions that sparsity and quantization are orthogonal or order-insensitive.

### Weaknesses
- **Metrics for LLMs**: Only perplexity and cross-entropy loss are reported. Reviewers note that PPL can be misleading for quantized/sparse models and downstream task accuracy (e.g., OpenLLM v1 leaderboard) would strengthen the findings. One reviewer requires that at least one task beyond language modeling be considered.
- **Dataset diversity**: Experiments use only WikiText2 and ImageNet1k; larger or more challenging datasets would bolster generality.
- **Sparsity patterns and quantization schemes**: Only magnitude-based sparsity (unstructured, 2:4, 4:8) and block-wise quantization are studied. The impact of other sparsity patterns (e.g., block-wise) or advanced quantization methods (e.g., GPTQ, NF4) is not explored. One reviewer questions whether the theoretical findings extend to those settings, and notes that magnitude pruning is now less common for LLMs compared to methods like SparseGPT or WANDA.
- **Experimental design / fine‑tuning concerns**: 
  - In the S→Q case, full‑precision activations are used; in the Q→S case, quantized activations are used, making the comparison unfair. A reviewer stresses that to isolate the order effect, both cases should use quantized activations.
  - The fine‑tuning procedure is not fully detailed: Are weight updates performed in full or quantized precision? Are optimizer states kept in fp32? This lack of clarity makes it difficult to assess whether performance differences are due to compression order or numerical precision during fine‑tuning.
  - The theoretical derivations assume no fine‑tuning, but all experiments include fine‑tuning. This disconnect between theory and experiment is noted as a significant concern by one reviewer.
- **Statistical reporting**: Results may be single runs; standard deviations are not provided. Reviewers request averages and error bars.
- **Presentation issues**: 
  - The discussion after Theorem 3.9 is dense and hard to digest; the essential message (in italics) should be kept in the main text and details moved to the appendix.
  - The definition of “orthogonality” (Definition 3.4) departs from the usual mathematical meaning and could be renamed to avoid confusion. The generalization in Definition 3.8 is unclear (coordinate‑wise application, composition allowed only within one coordinate?).
  - Related work should remain in the main body rather than being relegated to the appendix.
- **Theoretical limitations**:
  - Theorems 3.5 and 3.6 are proven only for the L1 norm; generalization to other norms is not established. Empirical evaluation uses the L2 norm for error measurements.
  - The theory is confined to magnitude‑based pruning and max‑scaled block‑wise quantization. One reviewer finds the contribution limited because it does not cover more modern pruning methods (e.g., SparseGPT, WANDA). The appendix itself notes that for WANDA/SparseGPT the order had less impact.
- **Minority more critical view**: One reviewer (score 6) argues that the theoretical claims are not surprising (pruning before quantization is intuitive), and that the experiments are orthogonal to the theory because fine‑tuning invalidates the assumption of no fine‑tuning used in the proofs. This reviewer also questions the novelty and the generality of the definitions and proofs.
- **Specific questions that overlap with criticisms or missing information**: 
  - In Table 2, explain what bold numbers indicate; also show delta to 0% sparsity (e.g., in appendix).
  - Clarify “block” in the quantization context (Does it refer to CNN filters, M:N blocks, or something else?).
  - Why consider block‑wise quantization in the theoretical section? Why not tensor‑level?
  - Is max‑scaled block‑wise quantization standard in practice?
  - Explain the purpose of Theorem 3.7 (analyzing the suboptimal order).
  - Rename “orthogonality bound” to “orthogonality lower bound” or “upper bound” as appropriate (Equation 12).
  - Clarify the sentence: “If the compression methods are non‑orthogonal, and the evaluation metric indicates better model performance with lower values, we expect the compressed model’s evaluation metric to exceed the orthogonality bound.” What does “lower values” refer to?
  - Cite the fine‑tuning method used for sparse fine‑tuning on dense models (L312). Cite the quantization method (one‑shot quantization to sparse fine‑tuned models).
  - Provide a summary table of experimental setup to ease digestibility.
  - In Figure 1, error accumulates across layers; contrast this with a prior work showing attenuation. Consider using relative error instead of absolute.
  - Define “effective bitwidth” and “TOPS/mm²”.
  - For LLMs, specify whether pretrained models are base or instruct‑tuned variants.
  - Confirm that all linear layers were compressed, including the lm‑head but not the embedding layer.
  - Table 10: 1:4 sparsity results contradict typical intuition that higher sparsity worsens performance ─ confirm it is 1:4 and not 3:4.
  - Provide empirical proportion of weights that become equal after quantization (when quantized before sparsity).
