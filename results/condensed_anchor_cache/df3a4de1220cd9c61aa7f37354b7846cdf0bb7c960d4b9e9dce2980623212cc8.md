## Merged Review

### Summary
Adam-mini reduces the memory footprint of AdamW by approximately 50% by replacing per-parameter learning rates with block-wise learning rates. The blocks are derived from a Hessian‑structure analysis of Transformers, leading to a grouping that assigns a single learning rate to each parameter block. The method achieves on‑par or better performance than AdamW in pre‑training, supervised fine‑tuning, and RLHF for language models (39M–13B). On Llama 2‑7B with two A800‑80GB GPUs, it yields 49.6% higher throughput and 33% shorter wall‑clock training time. The paper is generally well‑written, with extensive experiments on GPT‑2 and Llama series, plus smaller non‑LLM tasks.

### Strengths
1. **Significant memory reduction with maintained performance**: Adam‑mini cuts the memory of the second‑order momentum (`v`) by up to 50% while achieving performance on par with or better than AdamW across LLM pre‑training (GPT‑2, Llama), SFT, and RLHF.  
2. **Large throughput and wall‑clock gains**: For Llama 2‑7B on 2× A800‑80GB GPUs, Adam‑mini improves throughput by 49.6% and reduces training time by 33%, attributed to lower communication overhead among GPUs.  
3. **Novel, well‑motivated idea**: The Hessian‑based parameter partitioning is a novel approach that exploits the near‑block‑diagonal structure of the Hessian in Transformers. The paper provides a clear motivation and a simple principle for grouping.  
4. **Broad experiment suite**: The method is evaluated on LLM pre‑training (GPT‑2, Llama series), SFT, RLHF, and a variety of smaller non‑transformer tasks (Section C.2). Results confirm memory savings and performance retention over AdamW and several memory‑efficient Adam variants.  
5. **Potential impact**: Given the high memory demands of large language models, the method offers a practical way to reduce resource requirements without sacrificing quality, making it attractive for memory‑constrained LLM training.

### Weaknesses
1. **Primarily validated on Transformer architectures**: Most experiments focus on GPT‑2 and Llama series. The method’s effectiveness on other architectures (CNNs, RNNs, GNNs, BERT‑like models, vision Transformers, diffusion Transformers) is not sufficiently demonstrated. A few reviewers noted that Adam‑mini’s Hessian‑driven partitioning might not generalize to architectures where the Hessian is less structured.  
2. **Insufficient theoretical justification for block‑wise learning rates**: The paper shows that different blocks have different Hessian patterns, but the central claim—that a single learning rate for all parameters in a block works well—relies on a toy quadratic minimization example. Reviewers question whether this conclusion holds for neural network training in general; more rigorous analysis is needed.  
3. **Lack of thorough comparison with strong memory‑efficient baselines**: Comparisons with Lion and Sophia are brief. Reviewers noted that proper hyper‑parameter tuning (e.g., Lion often requires a very different learning rate) may be missing, and recent benchmarks show that memory‑efficient optimizers can achieve similar results. A more comprehensive, fairly‑tuned comparison is required.  
4. **Generality and scalability concerns**: The method introduces multiple components (Hessian analysis, manual partition specification for large models, computation of average `√v` per block). Some reviewers found the approach “tricky” and questioned its automatic applicability, especially for models not already studied. Integration into existing frameworks appears non‑trivial (e.g., Algorithm 2 requires manual partitions; no automated PyTorch solution is provided).  
5. **Missing evaluations on convergence and downstream tasks**: Comparisons after model convergence are absent; training loss alone may not reflect final generalization. Several reviewers request results on downstream task metrics (e.g., perplexity, accuracy on benchmarks) and convergence behavior.  
6. **Hyper‑parameter sensitivity not explored**: The paper does not study how to adapt hyper‑parameters when switching from AdamW to Adam‑mini, nor does it investigate stability over long training runs or sensitivity to block‑level learning rates.  
7. **Minor clarity and referencing errors**:

   - Line 483: “Figure 12” should be “Figure 12 (a)”.

   - Figure 19 in Section D.2 appears unreferenced.

   - Lines 449–450: “This might because” should be “This might be because”.  
8. **Lack of mathematical depth**: The grouping principle has limited theoretical support. Reviewers noted that adding a stronger mathematical foundation would strengthen the work and could enable further improvements.  
9. **Confidence divergence**: One reviewer (Score 6, Confidence 4) questioned the generality and solidity of the results, rating soundness as 2; another (Score 8, Confidence 4) gave soundness 3, while two others (Scores 8,6) gave soundness 3 and 4, respectively. The more critical reviewer found the method “tricky” and believed that carefully tuned memory‑efficient optimizers (e.g., Lion) could reach similar performance.
