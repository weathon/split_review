## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MolTextQA, a question-answering dataset of approximately 500,000 multiple-choice QA pairs for about 240,000 unique small molecules from PubChem. The QA pairs cover chemical, physical, biological, and application categories. Questions and answers are generated using multiple LLMs (Llama3, ChatGPT) with a human-annotated subset for validation. The authors benchmark several architectural classes (multi-modal models, LLMs) on molecule QA and molecule retrieval tasks, reporting that Galactica and MoleculeSTM (or BioT5, depending on section discrepancy) are top performers, achieving about 70% accuracy. Curation and model details are provided in the paper and repository.

### Strengths
- **Large scale and diversity**: The dataset contains ~500,000 QA pairs covering ~240,000 molecules across diverse property categories. (R1, R3)
- **Structured multiple-choice design**: The use of targeted multiple-choice answers increases evaluation specificity and precision compared to free-form generation. (R2, R3)
- **Cross-architecture benchmarking**: Evaluation of several model types (multi-modal, LLMs) enables direct comparisons in molecular QA and retrieval. (R2, R4—though R4 notes only initial benchmarks were provided)
- **Dual generation with human validation**: Combining LLM-generated content with a human-annotated subset aims to improve dataset quality and reliability. (R1, R3)
- **Thorough curation documentation**: The data curation process, schema, and validation steps are clearly described in the paper and the repository README. (R3)
- **Attempt to address LLM factuality**: The validation steps (e.g., achieving ~96% accuracy) show awareness of and effort to mitigate non-factual content from LLM generation. (R3)
- **Novel task focus**: The dataset specifically targets molecule structure-based questions and textual description-based molecule retrieval, providing a new resource for the community. (R4)
- **Presentation clarity (minority view)**: One reviewer found the paper generally well-written with well-organized figures and tables, in contrast to significant writing concerns from another reviewer. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited question depth**: The dataset is restricted to sentence-level multiple-choice questions, which may not test deeper cross-modal reasoning between molecular structures and text. Many examples appear to involve only simple references to the molecule in the text. (R1)
- **Unclear real-world impact**: The paper does not convincingly justify how progress on this benchmark would translate into meaningful improvements in molecular property prediction or other practical molecular applications. (R1)
- **LLM factuality concerns**: The heavy reliance on LLMs for QA generation may introduce non-factual content if validation is insufficient. Although validation efforts show ~96% accuracy, the remaining 4% error rate could complicate model training. (R2, R3, R4—extra note: R3 acknowledges the problem is addressed but persists)
- **Long-term scalability of validation**: The need for manual validation raises questions about long-term scalability, especially as the dataset grows. (R3)
- **Redundant / trivial questions**: Some questions (e.g., on polarity) may be too straightforward or redundant, reducing task difficulty. (R3)
- **Serious writing and clarity issues (minority view)**: The paper requires significant improvement. Acronyms (e.g., “QA,” “LLMs”) are defined multiple times. Many statements are vague or lack justification: for example, “deep learning methods” (line 042) is unclear; references to “a surge in the development of models” (line 053) are unspecified; and the mention of BLEU score being unreliable is irrelevant because BLEU is not typically used for molecule/text retrieval tasks. (R4)
- **Missing motivation in introduction**: The motivation for a new QA dataset should be explicitly stated in the introduction. The current text references other datasets (line 073) but does not name them, and the justification for a QA approach over retrieval or generation is absent. (R4)
- **Incomplete baselines**: The benchmarking omits several recent strong methods, including BioT5, BioT5+, and MolecularGPT. (R4)
- **Lack of details on human annotation process**: The paper claims a human-annotated subset but provides no information on how annotations were collected (e.g., annotator expertise, annotation protocol, inter-annotator agreement). (R4—question: “How were the human annotations conducted?”)
- **Unsubstantiated insights**: The paper claims to reveal “valuable insights into the advantages and limitations of current models,” but these insights are not clearly presented or supported. (R4—question: “What specific advantages and limitations of current models does the analysis reveal?”)
- **Validation process opacity**: The validation procedure is not clearly explained, raising concerns about overall dataset quality. (R1; echoed by R2 and R4)
