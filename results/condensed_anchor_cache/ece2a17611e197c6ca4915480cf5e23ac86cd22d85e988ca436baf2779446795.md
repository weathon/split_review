## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a hierarchical symbolic music generation system for full pop songs. It defines four levels of music representation—form, counterpoint, lead sheet, accompaniment—and generates them coarse-to-fine via cascaded conditional diffusion models. The model is trained on POP909 (909 songs) and evaluated with objective metrics and a subjective listening test. Overall, reviewers find the results impressive and the approach clever, but raise concerns about potential overfitting, limited generalizability, clarity of diffusion model details, and missing ablations. One reviewer (score 5) is significantly more critical, citing lack of diffusion details, narrow scope for ICLR, and incomplete presentation; the other three reviewers (scores 8,8,8) are very positive.

### Strengths
- **Timely and significant contribution**: First deep music generation model to generate a full structural song given high-level structural hints (R1).
- **Result quality**: Generated samples are convincing, capture the style of POP909, and perform well in objective metrics and user study compared to strong baselines. The hierarchical approach’s contribution is immediately apparent (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Simple yet effective modeling**: Despite complex hierarchical design, each stage uses the same basic diffusion setup, with well-motivated mechanisms (e.g., autoregressive component for local-global consistency) (R4).
- **Controllability**: The hierarchy enables flexible, interpretable control over music flow—accompaniment texture, rhythmic patterns, phrase harmonic structures—beyond from-scratch generation (R3, R4, R2 agrees). Demo page has many interesting examples (R2).
- **Clear writing**: Exceptionally well-written relative to typical symbolic music papers; explains musical concepts and the system clearly, minimizing required expertise (R4). R1 also applauds clarity of Figure 4.
- **Well-engineered system**: The generated pop songs sound convincing and capture style well (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Potential overfitting / copying**: The model is trained on only 909 songs, yet results are very good. Reviewers suspect verbatim copying or near-copies. Authors should provide nearest neighbor analysis, n-gram overlap, or percentage of exact matches to training data (R1, R4). R1 explicitly asks for such analysis in the demo page.
- **Missing limitations and scope discussion**:
  - The musical compositional hierarchy (form, phrase, cadence) is specifically designed for pop music; generalization to other genres (e.g., classical, jazz) is unclear and not discussed (R1, R4).
  - High-level form and structures must still be provided as input; the system cannot discover global structure from scratch (R1).
  - The approach relies on rich annotations (chords, melody, key, phrase boundaries), some human-labeled, which limits applicability to datasets like POP909 and prevents scaling to larger datasets (e.g., Lakh) (R4). The custom Tonal Reduction Algorithm is very close to Polyffusion (R2). R3 questions applicability to general MIDI-like datasets.
- **Lack of details and clarity on diffusion model and sampling**:
  - The diffusion modeling process is non-standard (discrete? continuous? real-valued or binary pianoroll?). It is not accessible to readers familiar with standard diffusion model literature (R2). R1 also asks: “continuous” meaning? Are you using binary or real-valued piano rolls?
  - The autoregressive chunk-wise generation procedure is unclear; R2 asks about sampling time for a whole song.
  - Several unclear descriptions: similarity metric for selecting relevant segments (R1: “Is this X^k or X^<k?”), segmentation into 8-bar segments with 1-bar hop (what is this for?), why 13 times downsampling (not 11), how pre-trained VAEs from Yang et al. (2019) and Wang et al. (2020) are used for autoregressive controls (R1, R2).
  - R2 notes the term “counterpoint” is inappropriate; “draft” or “sketch” would be better. R1 also finds “counterpoint” confusing (seems more like a reduced lead sheet).
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation on model architecture (e.g., removing one hierarchy level) or on the efficacy of the control inputs (R3). R1 also asks: how were melody reduction and simplified chord progression achieved? No pointer to appendix.
- **Evaluation limited to 32 measures**: Despite claiming “whole-song” generation, evaluation only generates 32 measures, which is far from a full song (R1). The subjective study does not have an “overall structure” rating so users cannot judge long-form structure (R4). R1 suggests including ground truth for 32-measure generation.
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Section ordering: qualitative analysis appears before dataset introduction, making it hard to know what to expect (R1). R1 suggests moving Figure 4 to main text instead of Figure 2.
  - Figure 3 would benefit from also including ground truth for 32-measure generation (R1).
  - Table 1 should define M, γ, δ in caption; a visual of the languages and their relationships would help (R4).
  - Demo page lacks qualitative examples from baselines (R4). R1 asks for nearest neighbor analysis on demo page.
  - R2 rates presentation as fair (2) due to missing model details; R1 and R4 rate presentation good/excellent.
- **Minor issues**:
  - “phrase” lacks a strict definition (R1).
  - “more or less the same” is vague (R1).
  - Test input selection for evaluation not explained (R1).
  - Song length M seems to be an input, but not listed in Algorithm 1 (R1).
  - R2: Results may not be of broad interest to the ICLR community (mostly symbolic music generation in pianoroll format).
