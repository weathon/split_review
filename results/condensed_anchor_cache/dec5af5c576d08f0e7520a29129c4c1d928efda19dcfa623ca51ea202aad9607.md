## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to augment test-time adaptation (TTA) by adding an auxiliary distillation loss on unlabeled source data from the training distribution to the standard entropy minimization on target data. The method is built on top of EATA and is evaluated on ImageNet-C and ImageNet-3DCC for ResNet18, ResNet50, and ViT. In episodic and continual settings, the authors report 1.5% and 6% average improvement over EATA on ImageNet-C, and a 6% improvement in a federated TTA setting. The paper also introduces a lookahead analysis that correlates entropy on clean source data with target batch entropy. Reviewers agree that the idea is simple and effective, but sharply disagree on its novelty and positioning relative to unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), with one reviewer (score 3) arguing the method is essentially UDA and the paper misrepresents the TTA setting, while others (scores 6 and 5) find the auxiliary task novel but note significant missing ablations, unrealistic assumptions, and insufficient validation.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and simplicity**: The method is easy to understand and well explained. The execution of experiments and story are clear (R1, R2, R3).
- **Effectiveness**: The proposed auxiliary task shows consistent performance improvements over prior TTA methods (EATA) across ImageNet-C and ImageNet-3DCC (R2, R3). Improvements of 1.5% (episodic) and 6% (continual) on ImageNet-C, and 6% in federated settings are noted (R2).
- **Novelty of auxiliary task**: The combination of source-data distillation with TTA is a simple but novel extension (R2). The adaptive weighting of source and target losses (Eq. 4) is highlighted as a potentially novel contribution (R1).
- **Thoroughness (limited scope)**: Results are well evaluated within the chosen models and datasets, and some hyperparameter analysis is included (R1). The method is placed well among existing works (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Misrepresentation of TTA vs. UDA**: The paper essentially re-invents unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) by using source data during test-time adaptation, yet it never mentions UDA or benchmarks against UDA methods. Classical UDA combines entropy minimization/pseudo-labeling with cross-entropy on source data (e.g., French et al., 2017). The improvement may be obvious: using source data with a TTA method yields better performance than pure TTA (R1, high confidence). The paper’s narrative as a TTA solution is misleading (R1, R3). An extensive review and fair experimental comparison to state-of-the-art UDA methods is missing (R1). The new experiments showing labeled vs. unlabeled source data further weaken the narrative (R3).
- **Memory burden**: Storing the full ImageNet training set adds ~160GB of storage (ResNet50 weights are ~97.8 MB). This is a severe limitation – with that storage, much larger models (e.g., EffNet-L2) could be used instead, which would reduce errors more drastically. The paper does not discuss memory requirements or whether a subsample of the source data was used (R1).
- **Limited model and dataset breadth**: Only ResNet18, ResNet50, and ViT are tested; larger models (e.g., larger ResNets) are absent. Datasets are limited to ImageNet-C and ImageNet-3DCC; natural corruptions datasets like ImageNet-R, -D, and ObjectNet are not considered (R1). Only batch sizes ≥8 are ablated; batch size 1 behavior (possible via MEMO’s batchnorm handling) is not studied (R3). The choice of models should be noted in table/figure captions (R2).
- **No error bars / stochasticity**: The paper uses “significantly” throughout but provides no error bars. Statistical significance cannot be assessed (R1). The results are not reported with confidence intervals (R1).
- **Unclear implementation details**:
    - The two-step update in Eq. (3) is not intuitive: θ_{t+1} is obtained by updating θ_t^c using gradients w.r.t. θ at θ_t^c, but the motivation and necessity of this intermediate step are unclear (R2).
    - The use of training set samples D_s (which the pretrained network has already seen) for the auxiliary task may introduce bias and hinder adaptation to unseen distributions (R2). Using unseen unlabeled source data would be more appropriate (R2).
    - Trainable parameters are not specified: is only normalization adapted or the whole network? Effects are not studied (R3).
    - The importance of the E_0 parameter in data filtration is not ablated; prior work proposed it but a study in this context is missing (R3).
- **Missing ablations**:
    - No ablation on the size of the unlabeled source dataset D_s; the optimal amount and sensitivity to size are unknown (R2).
    - No analysis of the source data batch size effect (R3).
    - How were ε and E_0 (weighting of losses) tuned? An analysis table varying these on a hold-out set is missing (R1).
    - The effect of class balance in D_s is not considered (R2).
- **Federated evaluation unrealistic**: The federated TTA setting assumes all clients know they are adapting to similar category-level domain shifts, which is controlled and not representative of real-world scenarios where clients may face diverse, unrelated shifts (R2). The justification for federated evaluation is not convincing (R3).
- **Lookahead analysis insufficient**: The lookahead tool only measures correlation between entropy on source and target, not test accuracy. Prior works show correlation between entropy and accuracy, but this cannot be taken for granted. The method does not show when adding source distillation is not useful or harmful (R3).
- **Additional weaknesses**: The abstract claims that “limited data” is a key TTA challenge but provides no references. Claims about “several adaptation steps leading to overfitting” are not cited or experimentally shown. Realistic scenarios with insufficient data streams are not exemplified (R1). The notation overloads simple classifier concepts (R1). The paper title suggests multiple auxiliary tasks, but only one is used (R1).
