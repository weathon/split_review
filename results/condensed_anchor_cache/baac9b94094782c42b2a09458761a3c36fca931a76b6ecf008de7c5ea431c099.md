## Merged Review

### Summary
CyberHost is a one-stage audio-driven talking body diffusion model, addressing hand integrity, identity consistency, and natural motion. It uses a Region Attention Module (RAM) and human-prior-guided conditions. It supports zero-shot generation and multiple driving modalities. Experiments show improvements over previous methods (e.g., VLOGGER, DiffGesture). The paper is well-written and the qualitative results are impressive.

### Strengths
- First one-stage approach for audio-driven talking body generation, avoiding multi-stage complexity and limitations of pose/mesh detectors.
- RAM effectively enhances critical regions (hands, face), improving local details and identity consistency.
- Human-prior-guided conditions (motion constraints, structural priors) reduce motion uncertainty, yielding stable and natural animations.
- Superior qualitative results compared to VLOGGER and other baselines; high rendering quality and natural motion.
- Supports multi-modality driving (audio, video, hybrid) with open-set generalization; experiments include audio-to-body, video-to-video, and audio-to-face comparisons.
- Comprehensive experiments with multiple baselines and metrics; ablation studies verify component contributions.
- Paper is well-organized and clearly written.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing failure analysis:** No discussion of failure cases (e.g., extreme poses, occlusions, background noise, singing). Also unclear how model handles audio with significant noise or singing.
- **Scalability not addressed:** Paper focuses on half-body; no discussion of challenges or limitations for full-body animation including legs/feet.
- **No user study:** Subjective evaluation (e.g., naturalness, quality) is missing; current metrics may not capture perceptual quality.
- **Insufficient facial clarity and detail:** Reviewer 2 notes noticeable identity discrepancy between generated face and reference image.
- **Codebook (latent bank) lacks supervisory signals:** Unlike VQ-VAE, the codebook has no explicit supervision; unclear how it guides hand/facial features correctly.
- **Missing ablations for key components:** No ablation showing impact of latent bank size, effect of removing spatial latents, or not decoupling spatial/temporal latents. Also no visualization/analysis of what spatial vs. temporal latents capture. Reviewer 3 and 5 request ablation for motion codebook and ID Descriptor (pose-aligned ref?).
- **Missing comparison with concurrent one-stage audio-driven half-body method [1]:** Paper does not discuss or compare with this method.
- **Missing comparison on standard dataset [2] and beat consistency metric [3]:** Not used.
- **Hand clarity score issues:** Use of Laplacian operator for hand clarity score lacks justification; its necessity and control over hand clarity during inference not demonstrated. Reviewer 2/4/5 question its sufficiency and contribution.
- **Pose-aligned reference feature explanation unconvincing:** Ablation shows lower HKC but not tested on challenging initial poses; how skeleton map contributes to hand quality is not explained.
- **Missing implementation details:**
    - Which specific Wav2Vec layers used? (line 191)
    - How to constrain basis vectors of latent bank to be orthogonal? (line 242)
    - Loss description for regional mask predictor missing. (line 260)
    - How hand heatmap estimator trained? Jointly with Eq.6? Pretrained? Weights shared across timesteps?
    - Are Pose Encoder for Body Movement Map and Pose-Aligned Reference Feature shared? If shared, rationale?
    - Diffusion model initialization: pretrained (extending AnimateDiff's temporal module) or trained from scratch?
    - Dimensions of L_spa and L_temp in latent bank.
- **Hand position detection from noisy latent space during inference not explained** (Reviewer 2).
- **Typos:** line 313 "feference" → "reference"; also noted by Reviewer 4.
- **Note of reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 10, confidence 5) was highly positive, raising rating after rebuttal, praising novelty and effectiveness, while others (scores 6 and 8) expressed more criticisms on clarity, comparisons, and missing analyses.
