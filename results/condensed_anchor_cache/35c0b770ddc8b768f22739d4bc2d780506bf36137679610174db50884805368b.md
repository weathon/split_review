## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedCOG, a federated learning algorithm that mitigates data heterogeneity by generating complementary data at each client. The local training phase first generates task- and client-specific synthetic data by optimizing a learnable input to maximize disagreement between the frozen global model and the previous local model, then uses knowledge distillation on that synthetic data to train the local model. FedCOG is designed as a plug-and-play module compatible with standard FL protocols and requires no server-side changes. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100, Fashion-MNIST, and the real-world multilabel dataset FLAIR show improved accuracy over several baselines.

### Strengths
- Addresses the critical challenge of data heterogeneity from a novel perspective: generating data to complement local datasets rather than correcting models (Reviewers 1,2,4). The idea of capturing residual knowledge between global and client models for data generation is novel (Reviewer 2).
- The method is plug-and-play, with low computational overhead on each client, and maintains compatibility with standard FL protocols such as Secure Aggregation (Reviewers 1,3).
- Sensitivity analysis is well-designed and conducted (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is clearly written, well-organized, and easy to follow (Reviewers 2,3,4). The related work is comprehensive (Reviewer 2).
- Extensive experiments on multiple datasets, including the real-world multilabel dataset FLAIR, which is rarely used in the FL literature (Reviewers 3,4). The use of task-specific and client-specific generation is practical (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and advantages over model-correction methods**: The rationale for using data generation instead of model correction (e.g., FedProx, FedCurv) is not clearly justified. In particular, when the training dataset is very large, generating a sufficient amount of data to achieve alignment could be costly, making model correction a better choice. The unique advantages of data generation over model correction remain ambiguous (Reviewer 1).
- **Computational overhead**: Data generation on the client side adds extra computation compared to classic FL or methods that generate data on the server. The cost may scale poorly with dataset size (Reviewers 1,2). The overhead for different generated dataset sizes should be documented more thoroughly (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of theoretical and convergence analysis**: No formal convergence guarantees, theoretical derivation, or convergence graph (accuracy vs. communication rounds) is provided. Proofs for convergence and, if possible, a security analysis are requested (Reviewers 2,3). The network may not have converged in the experiments; a convergence plot is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - The reported accuracies in Table 1 are notably lower than state-of-the-art FL methods, raising concerns about inappropriate model architecture (5-layer CNN for CIFAR-10), communication rounds, or optimizer settings (Reviewer 2). Consider using more complex networks for CIFAR-10 (Reviewer 3).
  - The improvement over baselines is modest; all results should be repeated with mean and standard error to confirm significance (Reviewer 3).
  - The client number is limited for standard datasets (e.g., 10 clients for CIFAR-10/100) (Reviewer 4).
  - No comparison with synthetic-data-based methods such as FedGen or DynaFed, even though the paper cites them (Reviewer 4).
  - The number of synthetic samples generated per client is not stated (Reviewer 4).
  - Further experiments with Dirichlet-distributed heterogeneity (varying β) are needed to validate the effects on different heterogeneity levels (Reviewer 2).
  - Experiments on high-resolution images and with varying synthetic dataset sizes, along with overhead documentation, are desirable (Reviewer 3).
- **Privacy and security concerns**: Generating data that captures discrepancy between the global and local model may leak information about other clients' data distributions, making an attack easier (Reviewer 3).
- **Data generator details**: The structure of the learnable data generator and whether its weights are updated during training are not clearly explained (Reviewer 4).
- **Applicability to other modalities**: The method is presented purely for vision tasks; applicability to text or other input modalities is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarifications needed**: 
  - Is generating new data for each client simply amplifying the global weight update direction? (Reviewer 3)
  - How would FedCOG harmonize with FedProx when using KD-based training? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why does FedProx exhibit longer local training time than FedCOG in Table 4? (Reviewer 4)
