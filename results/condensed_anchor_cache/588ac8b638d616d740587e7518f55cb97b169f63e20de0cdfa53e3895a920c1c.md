## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies Neural Tangent Kernels (NTKs) in the context of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). It introduces the concept of "alignment" between NTK eigenvectors and data, showing that optimizing alignment translates to optimizing the graph shift operator. The analysis for a two-layer GNN yields theoretical guarantees that the optimal graph shift operator is a function of the cross-covariance between input and output data. Experiments on a multivariate time series prediction task (HCP-YA dataset) demonstrate that GNNs with cross-covariance as the graph shift operator outperform those using the covariance matrix from only the input data.

### Strengths
- Generalizing NTK theory to GNNs expands the theoretical foundation of this area.
- The suggestion of using a cross-covariance matrix introduces a graph that includes both input and output variables, which is not common in current GNNs. The theory could be useful when the graph must be constructed as an affinity matrix from data.
- The paper is well-organized, with a clear delineation of NTK, alignment, and their relevance in GNNs.
- The exploration of NTK constancy in the infinite-width limit and layer-specific training implications enhances interpretability and scientific understanding of GNN learning dynamics.
- The findings have the potential to advance the understanding and analysis of GNNs, providing theoretical insights valuable to the community.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical novelty**: The theoretical framework primarily relies on existing NTK results regarding optimization and generalization, appearing as a direct application to a specific GNN architecture rather than a significant advancement in NTK theory itself. A more distinct and innovative theoretical contribution beyond the current NTK theories would enhance impact. (R2)
- **Missing related work**: The paper does not discuss Huang, W. et al. (2021) "Towards deepening graph neural networks: A GNTK-based optimization perspective," ICLR 2022. Including this work would strengthen the literature review and provide a more comprehensive perspective. (R2)
- **Limited experimental scope**: The experiments are restricted to a single dataset (HCP-YA) and a multivariate time series prediction task. The paper lacks validation on standard GNN benchmarks for node classification or graph classification, making it difficult to assess the practical relevance and effectiveness of the proposed theories. (R2, R3)
- **Missing comparisons to modern GNN architectures**: There is no comparison to GCNs, Graphormers, or other common graph construction methods (e.g., Gaussian kernels, k-nearest neighbors). The current experiments do not provide sufficient evidence for the general applicability or superiority of the cross-covariance graph. (R1)
- **No verification of theoretical guarantees**: The paper does not include experiments specifically verifying the proposed theorems (e.g., Theorem 2 and Theorem 3), leaving the link between theory and empirical results unclear. (R2)
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters**: The experiments do not explore the sensitivity of the method to hyperparameter choices in the optimization used to derive the graph shift operator, which could significantly impact performance. (R1)
- **Computational cost**: The computational cost of constructing the cross-covariance matrix is not addressed; it could be prohibitive for large graphs. (R1)
- **Practical interpretability and guidance**: While strong in theoretical analysis, the paper does not provide extensive insight into practical applications. The implications of NTK constancy and alignment for optimization strategies or architectural choices in real-world GNN applications remain abstract, lacking concrete examples. (R3)
- **Generalizability concerns**: Experiments are conducted under a narrow set of conditions (single dataset, specific task), raising questions about robustness and generalizability to diverse, real-world challenges. The choice of HCP-YA dataset is not justified in terms of alignment with study objectives, especially given that common node/graph classification tasks are typical in related work. (R2, R3)
- **Dimensionality inconsistency**: The size of y_i is stated as ℝ^{n×1} while x_i is also ℝ^{n×1}. The rationale behind this dimensionality choice is not explained. (R2)
- **Extension to deeper GNNs**: The theoretical analysis focuses on a two-layer GNN; it is unclear whether the analysis can be extended to deeper GNNs with aggregation in internal layers. (R3)
- **Potential empirical limitations of covariance-based graphs**: In practice, VNNs and graphs based on covariance matrices have fared worse than those using non-linear affinities. This raises the question of whether the NTK theory fully explains empirical behavior. (R1)
