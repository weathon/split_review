## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the Deepfake Detector Assessment Platform (DAP), intended to evaluate deepfake detectors across six dimensions: benchmark performance, forgery algorithm generalization, image distortion robustness, adversarial attack resilience, forgery localization accuracy, and attribute bias. Extensive experiments are conducted on multiple public and self-built databases with many self-generated fake images, and code is open-sourced. Two reviewers (scores 5) judge the framework comprehensive, rigorous, and useful; three other reviewers (scores 3) consider it insufficient as a platform, lacking insights, and poorly motivated.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive evaluation framework** covering six key dimensions: benchmark performance, forgery generalization, distortion robustness, adversarial resilience, localization precision, and attribute bias. (All reviewers)
- **Extensive experiments** on multiple public and self-built databases, with a large number (5,976,145) of self-generated fake images, providing rich experimental data and result analysis. (Reviewers 1,2,4,5)
- **Open-source platform/tool** (GitHub) that can support future research and consolidate existing evaluations. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Introduction of adversarial attack resilience and attribute bias evaluation**, which are important for practical applications and often missing from existing benchmarks. (Reviewers 2,5)
- Emphasis on generalization and robustness, filling gaps compared to prior evaluation work. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of platform architecture description.** The paper does not discuss the system architecture, how the platform can be utilized, or its components (e.g., Docker, backend-API); it reads as a benchmark paper rather than a platform proposal. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis and method design.** Many choices (e.g., selection of 10 detectors, the nine distortion types, five attributes) lack justification or citation. The paper does not explain why these particular settings are appropriate or how they relate to real-world conditions. (Reviewers 2,3,4,5)
- **Missing video detection.** The evaluation focuses only on still images; video-based deep forgery detection (including temporal effects) is equally important but not addressed. (Reviewers 2,5)
- **Limited adversarial attack and localization experiments.** Only two adversarial attacks (GANprintR, StyleAttack) are applied on only two baseline detectors; forgery localization is evaluated with only one detector. Such limited attempts cannot support robust conclusions and may cause bias. (Reviewer 4,5) Furthermore, the representativeness of StyleAttack is not justified. (Reviewer 5)
- **Evaluation experiments not sufficiently up-to-date or representative.** The detectors evaluated are not the newest; e.g., FaceX-ray (Li et al., 2020a) and Frequency-aware method (Tan et al., 2024) are missing. Half of the reviewers questioned whether the detector set covers SOTA or diverse types (data-driven, spatial artifact, frequency artifact). (Reviewers 4,5)
- **Lack of meaningful conclusions and findings.** The paper merely reports numbers (e.g., accuracies 80.42% and 36.52%) without analyzing why some detectors perform well or badly, what characteristics cause differences, or what future directions should be taken. There is no “Findings and Future Directions” section. (Reviewers 3,4,5)
- **Poor writing quality and unclear motivation.** The motivation for a new platform vs. extending existing benchmarks (Table 1) is not articulated. Arguments are presented without justification (e.g., line 67-68 criticizes “exclusive dependence on public databases” without substantiation). Figures and tables (Table 1, Figure 1) are not referred to in the main text. Logical issues in phrasing (e.g., “Large Multimodal Model” as a deepfake type; line 215 claim that 5M images simulate real-world forgery is unjustified). (Reviewer 3)
- **Distortion evaluation treats perturbations independently**, not as realistic combinations (e.g., compression+noise+blur). A combined distortion experiment is missing. (Reviewer 1)
- **No evaluation on wild/real-world data** (face images/videos not from standard datasets) to test bias and real-world performance. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient research on compressed image detection**, common in social media. (Reviewer 2)
- **Platform extensibility unclear** – no details on how other detectors or new attack/forgery methods can be added. (Reviewer 3)
- **Forgery localization as a unified metric is not reasonable** because not all detection models can localize forgery regions. (Reviewer 5)
- **Attribute bias evaluation lacks valuable conclusions** – it merely reports metrics but does not discuss how the bias affects detector design or what improvements are suggested. (Reviewer 5)
- **Large Multimodal Models used as generation methods are not discussed** – the changes and differences brought by this strategy are ignored. (Reviewer 5)
- **Overclaimed conclusions** – e.g., “this paper analyzes two issues” and “identified potential causes” are not supported by the actual analysis. (Reviewer 3)
- **Paper resembles a research report** rather than a scientific contribution, lacking in-depth experimental analysis and theoretical hypotheses. (Reviewer 5)
