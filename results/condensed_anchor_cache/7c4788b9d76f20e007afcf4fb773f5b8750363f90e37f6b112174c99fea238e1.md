## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LVLM-CL, which uses task-specific prompts and a memory bank to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during continual fine-tuning of large vision-language models, and introduces a new continual instruction tuning dataset derived from TDIUC. Reviewers agree the topic is timely and underexplored, and some praise the dataset and baseline improvements. However, major weaknesses are consistently raised: insufficient comparison with existing continual learning methods (e.g., LwF, EWC, DualPrompt), incomplete ablation and method clarity, writing and formatting issues, and contested dataset novelty (one reviewer points to the existing CoIN benchmark). Reviewer confidence and scores vary: three reviewers rate the paper a 3 (confidence 2-4) but note significant flaws, while one reviewer (score 1, confidence 4) is strongly critical, particularly of the dataset claim and evaluation rigor.

### Strengths
- **Timely and underexplored problem**: All reviewers agree that continual learning specifically for LVLMs is an important and largely unaddressed area, with practical relevance for real-world fine-tuning (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Dataset contribution**: Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 highlight the construction of a new continual instruction tuning benchmark tailored to LVLMs, derived from TDIUC (R1, R2, R4). Reviewer 3 notes it as a new training setup for continual finetuning. (However, Reviewer 4 later questions its novelty – see Weaknesses.)
- **Performance improvement over baseline**: Reviewers 2 and 3 observe that LVLM-CL outperforms the baseline model on multiple VQA datasets; Reviewer 3 adds that the baselines used appear state-of-the-art and the evaluation is detailed across several multimodal datasets.
- **Novel approach design**: Reviewers 1 and 4 consider the integration of task-specific prompts and a memory bank as a novel contribution for the LVLM setting. Reviewer 2 calls it an innovative methodology for an underexplored area, and Reviewer 3 describes it as a first step toward continual finetuning of auto-regressive VLMs (evaluated on LLaVA).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with existing CL methods**: Multiple reviewers note the paper does not compare against established continual learning techniques that could be applied to LVLMs, such as LwF, EWC, and prompt-based approaches like DualPrompt (R1, R2). Current comparisons are limited to zero-shot models, which diminishes the impact of the performance claims (R1). A direct comparison with other CL methods is necessary to assess real improvement (R2). Reviewer 4 adds that the effectiveness is not deeply evaluated, as the authors state “LoRA-finetune is not good enough, so we don’t show it”.
- **Unclear or insufficient ablation studies**: The paper lacks thorough ablation on key components. Reviewer 1 finds the analysis of Task-specific vs. Task-invariant features incomplete. Reviewer 2 notes no clear ablation on how the memory buffer and concentration learning module perform independently. Reviewer 3 points out the lack of proper ablation on the time/memory scaling with the number of tokens produced by the concentration module, and finds the ablation on task order redundant. Reviewer 4 criticizes the lack of a formal definition of forgetting evaluation.
- **Method description and terminology issues**: Several aspects of the method are unclear. Reviewer 1 asks which parameters are explicitly trained in the task-specific features. Reviewers 3 and 4 note incoherent definitions: Eq. (1) uses the same encoding function for E^q and E^v, but Fig. 2 suggests two separate encoders; E_t is called expectation in Line 317 but textual embedding in Line 323 (R3). The naming “concentration learning module” is confusing because it appears to learn task-invariant features (R3). Reviewer 4 notes the lack of definition for E^q and E^v in Eq. (1). The justification for using the hyperbolic tangent function in Eq. (5) is missing (R3).
- **Dataset novelty disputed**: Reviewer 4 strongly contests the claim that there is no readily available dataset, noting that CoIN (Continual Instruction Tuning for Multimodal Large Language Models) already exists. The authors must clarify the differences and justify the novelty. While other reviewers view the dataset as a strength, this criticism indicates a fundamental disagreement.
- **Arbitrary design choices**: Reviewer 3 points out that removing the “absurd” question type (where questions cannot be answered) lacks justification; analysis of how answers compare to other types is missing. The motivation for the concentration module itself is unclear (R3). Reviewer 4 raises concerns about data quality and the rationality of the generated questions.
- **Writing, formatting, and presentation issues**: All but Reviewer 1 note pervasive grammatical errors, awkward phrasing, and inconsistent terminology (R2, R3, R4). Citations are often incorrectly formatted (e.g., “Zhang et al. (2023b)Zhang et al. (2023c)”) (R2). References to tables and figures in text are frequently incorrect (e.g., wrong table numbers for ablation studies) (R2, R4). Reviewer 3 provides a list of minor corrections for the abstract and recommends using a spell-checker. Reviewer 4 notes long-tailed sentences and extra spaces.
- **Lack of discussion on limitations and future work**: Reviewer 4 points out the paper does not include a limitations section or discuss future directions, which is a standard omission.
- **Generality to other LVLM architectures**: Reviewer 1 questions whether the approach (evaluated only on LLaVA) can be extended to other LVLM architectures and what adaptations would be needed. This is not addressed.
- **Parameter cost and component analysis**: Reviewer 3 asks to see the effect of removing the vision projection layer from Eq. (2) and the additional parameter cost. This overlaps with the need for better ablation.
