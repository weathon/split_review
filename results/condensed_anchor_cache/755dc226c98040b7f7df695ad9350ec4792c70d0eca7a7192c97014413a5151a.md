## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a weakly supervised visual grounding method that combines CNN and transformer architectures, introduces a CAM encoder leveraging Grad-CAM, and uses an attention mining loss to encourage focus on entire objects. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on 4 out of 5 evaluated datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, ReferItGame, Flickr30K). However, multiple reviewers identify significant weaknesses: the claimed novelty of integrating Grad-CAM is directly contradicted by prior work (AMC [7] and enhanced X-VLM [8]), the attention mining loss closely resembles GAIN [9] without clear differentiation, the related work section omits several recent methods (VLTVG, QRNet, LUNA, VG-LAW, CPL, WSVG), and the motivation for the weakly-supervised setting is questioned due to annotation cost arguments. The architectural novelty is overstated because existing transformer methods already incorporate CNN backbones (e.g., TransVG, QRNet). Technical concerns exist about Grad-CAM’s faithfulness for transformers, and the paper lacks several key experiments, comparisons to recent methods, and visualizations. Writing and figure clarity also need improvement.

### Strengths
- **Clear architecture and writing**: The design of each module (visual encoder, CAM encoder, multi-modality fusion) is clearly stated and easy to understand.  
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluated on five standard datasets: RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, ReferItGame, and Flickr30K.  
- **State-of-the-art performance**: Achieves top results on 4 out of the 5 datasets.  
- **Ablation studies**: Demonstrate the effectiveness of the CAM encoder and attention mining loss.

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete literature review and overclaim of novelty**:
  - Missing references to recent fully supervised methods: VLTVG [1], QRNet [2], LUNA [3], VG-LAW [4]; and weakly supervised methods: CPL [5], WSVG [6], AMC [7], enhanced X-VLM [8] (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - The claim “no previous attempts have been made to integrate Grad-CAM with existing weakly supervised visual grounding methods” is false. Grad-CAM has been used in AMC [7] (Yang et al., CVPR 2023) and enhanced X-VLM [8] (Pham & Mishima, ICASSP 2023). The paper does not clarify how its use of Grad-CAM differs from or improves upon these works (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
  - The attention mining loss was first proposed in GAIN [9]; the authors do not explain how their loss differs from or improves GAIN in formulation or application (Reviewers 1, 3).

- **Questionable motivation for weakly supervised setting**:
  - In human labeling, bounding box annotation may not add significant extra effort beyond writing region-level descriptions (e.g., “a man in a white shirt next to the door”). The paper does not provide a strict comparison of annotation cost (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - In automatic labeling, both fully supervised (detector + pseudo captions) and weakly supervised pipelines rely on region-level captioning; the paper does not compare their errors or justify the necessity of the weakly-supervised approach under this consideration (Reviewers 2, 4).

- **Overclaim about structural novelty**:
  - The paper claims transformer-based methods are less efficient than their CNN+transformer combination, but existing methods (TransVG, QRNet, VLTVG, VG-LAW, SegVG) all use a ResNet + Transformer (DETR encoder), so there is no clear technical difference. No training cost or speed comparison is provided (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 3 further questions how the combination reduces computation costs.

- **Technical concerns about Grad-CAM**:
  - Grad-CAM is not state-of-the-art for vision transformers; better alternatives exist (e.g., “Token Transformation Matters: Towards Faithful Post-hoc Explanation for Vision Transformer”). Supervising Grad-CAM may be meaningless if it does not faithfully represent the model’s decision-making (Reviewers 2, 4).

- **Missing experiments and ablations**:
  - Comparisons are only with methods published before 2023; should compare with recent works [5–8], especially AMC [7] and enhanced X-VLM [8] which also use Grad-CAM (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - No visualizations of CAM features before/after attention mining loss to qualitatively show improved focus on target objects (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
  - No detailed formula for attention mining loss; specifically, how lambda is computed and how it ensures focus on the whole object (Reviewer 2).
  - No evaluation using a different post-hoc explanation method to test generalizability (Reviewer 4).
  - No ablation or mitigation strategy for non-target object nouns in referring text (e.g., “sofa” in “a white dog lying on the sofa”) affecting CAM features (Reviewer 3).
  - No analysis of generalization to objects outside ImageNet classes, given Grad-CAM is generated from a ResNet pre-trained on ImageNet (Reviewer 2).

- **Unclear writing and figures**:
  - Figure 4 text is too small and hard to read (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - Tables on page 7 are unclear (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - Figure on page 5 is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - Discrepancy between figure and description of visual encoder sequence; need clarification on the order of CNN and transformer modules and how self-attention captures local information (Reviewer 2).
