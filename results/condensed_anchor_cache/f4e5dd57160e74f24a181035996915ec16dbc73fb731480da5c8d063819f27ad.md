- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 8, 5, 8, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Forgetting Transformer, which adds a data-dependent forget gate to standard causal attention by applying a multiplicative decay to unnormalized attention scores. The method is derived from a recurrent formulation, results in a simple additive bias, and requires no positional embeddings. Pre-training a 760M model on 16B tokens, the authors report that the Forgetting Transformer outperforms the Transformer on long-context language modeling, length extrapolation, and short-context downstream tasks, while matching it on long-context downstream tasks. It also achieves strong needle-in-the-haystack results compared to recurrent models (Mamba-2, HGRN2, DeltaNet) and is compatible with Flash Attention. Reviewers unanimously praised the simplicity, clear writing, and motivation, but raised significant concerns about the sufficiency of the empirical evaluation, the small scale of training (16B tokens, 760M parameter model), missing baselines, and the lack of robustness checks. While two reviewers rated the paper 8, one gave 6, and a fourth gave 5, reflecting disagreement over the strength of the evidence.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and clarity**: The idea is simple to implement and understand; the paper is well-written and flows naturally (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Thorough empirical evaluation on many benchmarks**: The paper evaluates on 14 benchmarks, achieving best results on 9. It includes comprehensive ablation studies (e.g., comparison with data-independent and fixed positional biases in Figure 4) and a needle-in-the-haystack test (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Compelling length generalization**: Results on length extrapolation are notably better than those of the Transformer with RoPE (Reviewer 3).
- **Compatibility with Flash Attention** and the ability to replace positional encodings (Reviewer 4).
- **Careful ablations** including comparisons with learnable and fixed decay rates (Reviewer 1) and analysis of the forget-gate dynamics (Figure 2, partially) (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient scale of training**: The model is pre-trained on only 16B tokens with 760M parameters; the authors acknowledge this limitation. Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4 both noted that the scale is small compared to standard practice, raising questions about whether results will generalize to larger models (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Lack of baseline without any positional encoding**: The Transformer baseline always uses RoPE; results on length extrapolation and the needle test without any position encoding are missing. Reviewer 1 specifically asked how the Transformer without positional encoding would perform.
- **Missing important baselines**: Several related methods that also incorporate recency bias or forget-like mechanisms are not compared: Transformer with sliding window attention, chunk-wise attention with moving average (e.g., Megalodon) (Reviewer 2); also Mega, COPE, Selective Transformer (Section 3.3), and LAS Attention. The differences and relative performance against these are not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Evaluation based on a single instance**: Only one pre-trained model is used, without multiple seeds or training curves showing convergence behavior. This makes it unclear whether the reported gains are robust or anecdotal (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 suggested including training/validation loss curves for the variants in Figure 1.
- **Missing comparisons on standard datasets**: Pre-training from scratch on well-established benchmarks (e.g., Wikitext-103, the Pile, RedPajama) is not performed, making it hard to compare directly with results from original papers (e.g., HGRN, Mamba, RWKV) (Reviewer 4).
- **Low Transformer perplexity on Wikipedia (Table 1)**: The Transformer baseline appears to underperform significantly compared to all other methods, which Reviewer 1 questioned as a possible artifact of the long-context training setup, undertuning, or hyperparameter choices. Reviewer 1 also asked whether the Transformer was undertrained or the hyperparameters insufficiently tuned.
- **Unclear role of QK-Norm**: The method often uses QK-norm, and performance varies across tasks. Reviewer 3 asked for an investigation of why QK-norm is important (it was introduced for Vision Transformers), and for results with/without QK-norm on the standard Transformer baseline. Reviewer 2 similarly asked why the impact of QK-norm varies on different tasks.
- **Missing implementation details**: The initialization of the forget-gate parameters \(w_f\) and \(b_f\) is not specified (Reviewer 2). The number of parameters, FLOPs, and latency are not reported in tables, making computational efficiency difficult to assess (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of robustness checks**: Results are not shown across multiple seeds, varying model sizes, different hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size), or other modalities (e.g., image, audio) (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions that highlight missing analysis**:
  - What is the impact of sharing \(w_f\) across heads? (Reviewer 4)
  - How do different heads focus on different scopes? (Reviewer 4) – a more dedicated evaluation of the forget values \(F_{ij}\) across heads, layers, and inputs is missing beyond Figure 2.
  - What are the limitations or failure cases of the Forgetting Transformer? Negative results could be informative (Reviewer 4).
