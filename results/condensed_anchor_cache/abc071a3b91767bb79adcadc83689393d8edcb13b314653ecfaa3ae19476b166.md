## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers evaluated the paper. Two rated 6, one rated 5, and one rated 3. The paper proposes Bi-directional Discrete Process Matching (Bi-DPM), a flow-based method for bi-modality medical image synthesis that uses both forward and backward ODE flows and enforces consistency at discrete intermediate steps. Strengths include novelty, flexible loss handling of paired/unpaired data, and empirical improvements over CFM and RF on MRI T1/T2 and CT/MRI. Weaknesses focus on insufficient theoretical motivation and justification, missing comparisons with diffusion-based methods and more recent GANs, lack of ablation studies and implementation details, concerns about anatomical consistency especially in CT-MRI (SSIM < 0.70), small datasets, readability issues, and the need for efficiency and generalization experiments. One reviewer raised strong doubts about the fundamental applicability of the method to clinically meaningful translation.

### Strengths

- **Bi-directional flow-matching**: The method uses both forward and backward ODE flows, constraining consistency between intermediate states at discrete time steps. This enhances paired information preservation and allows non-linear translation, as shown in toy results (Fig. 2, 3) that convincingly preserve bi-directional relationships even with few paired data. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Flexible loss function**: The loss handles fully paired, partially paired, and unpaired data using LPIPS for perceptual similarity and MMD for unpaired portions, increasing applicability. [R1]
- **Strong empirical performance**: Outperforms Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) and Rectified Flow (RF) on all three datasets (MRI T1/T2, CT/MRI) in SSIM, PSNR, and FID metric. Physician evaluation and a Turing test indicate high realism of synthesized images. [R1, R3]
- **Consideration of 3D and varied data settings**: The paper addresses 3D image synthesis and both paired and unpaired scenarios, demonstrating breadth of applicability. [R1, R4]
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The methodological framework is presented in a structured manner. [R4]

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient motivation and theoretical grounding for bidirectional flow**: The paper does not clearly articulate why enforcing consistency between forward and backward flows at intermediate steps is crucial for maintaining paired information, nor how it connects to velocity field smoothness, stability, or convergence. Remark 1 and the choice of ODE forms lack rigorous analysis of conditions ensuring consistent mapping. Some deeper mathematical derivations are insufficiently explained. [R1, R3]
- **Missing comparisons with diffusion-based and advanced GAN methods**: The claim that diffusion models cannot be directly used for image-to-image translation is unjustified; several works exist (e.g., Graf et al. 2023, Ozbey et al. 2023, Kim et al. 2023, latent diffusion). The only GAN compared is CycleGAN (2017), which is outdated. Comparisons with more recent GANs, Schrödinger Bridge methods, and other unpaired/partially paired translation SOTA are absent. [R2, R3, R4]
- **Lack of ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis**: No ablation on the weight of the unpaired MMD loss, the impact of weight parameters \(w_n\) on training stability, or sensitivity to ODE step size. Only 1-step and 2-step results are provided without systematic exploration of step-size trade-offs between efficiency and image quality. The partially paired data setting shows 2-step Bi-DPM performing poorer than 1-step when the paired ratio is low, without explanation. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Concerns about anatomical consistency and diagnostic utility** (raised strongly by one reviewer): The MMD in Eq. (8) is a weak constraint for anatomical consistency in unpaired data. For CT-MRI, SSIM below 0.70 indicates that much diagnostic-crucial information is lost, and the algorithm may make random guesses in many areas. It is questionable whether the translated image conveys the same information as a real scan; the physician evaluation asked about realism, not whether the same clinical details are preserved. Theoretical grounds for recovering T1/T2 properties from CT Hounsfield units are unclear. [R2]
- **Insufficient implementation and reproducibility details**: The specific architecture of the neural network parameterizing the velocity field, the optimization algorithm, and the loss function are not described in sufficient detail to allow replication. [R3]
- **Missing efficiency and speed evidence**: The claim that Bi-DPM allows faster transfer is not supported by any experiment comparing training/inference times, memory usage, or number of parameters. Comparisons of computational complexity against baselines are absent. [R1, R3]
- **3D extension insufficiently addressed**: The slicing approach in Section 3.2.4 is described as a straightforward extension without addressing potential inter-slice inconsistencies or how spatial coherence across slices is ensured. The performance and limitations on 3D volumes are not explicitly discussed. [R1]
- **Small datasets and insufficient evaluation scope**: The datasets used are too small for a solid evaluation. Experiments on unseen data (e.g., EGD dataset) to test generalization are not provided. Performance on completely unpaired data is not evaluated. [R1, R2, R4]
- **Poor figure readability**: Figures 5 and 7 have fonts that are too small to read, and some figures are generally hard to interpret. [R2, R3, R4]
- **Minor issues**: Notation inconsistencies (line 064/065 should be \(X_1 \sim q(z)\), line 132 “to measures”). The training procedure mentions equal proportion of unpaired and paired data (line 366), but experiments vary paired ratios; the data augmentation method to balance proportions is not specified. [R2]
