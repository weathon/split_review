## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates four knowledge manipulation tasks (retrieval, classification, comparison, inverse search) using synthetic biography datasets (bioS, bioR) in controlled experiments with models including GPT-2, LLaMA, Mistral, and GPT-4o. Results show that while LLMs excel at knowledge retrieval, they struggle with classification and comparison unless Chain of Thought (CoT) prompting is used, and inverse search performance is near 0%. Across architectures and model sizes, including modern LLMs, these fundamental limitations persist.

### Strengths
- Well-controlled synthetic experiments minimize confounds, providing numerous insights (Results 1–8) and standing out from most LLM knowledge research. (R1)
- Thorough evaluation across multiple models (GPT-2, LLaMA, Mistral, GPT-4o, LLaMA-3-405B) and both open- and closed-source LLMs. (R2, R3)
- Highlights fundamental and significant flaws in knowledge manipulation; the testbed and methodology could serve as a benchmark for future LLMs and mark a boundary between AI with and without system II reasoning. (R2, R4)
- Introduces inverse knowledge search as a new evaluation criterion that adds a valuable dimension to LLM evaluation. (R4)
- Synthetic biography datasets and the four well-designed tasks are a valuable asset for future researchers. (R4)
- Paper is well-organized, clearly written, self-contained with illustrative examples, making it easy to follow. (R3, R4)
- Detailed analyses and experimental results contribute meaningfully to understanding LLM limitations. (R4)
- Addresses a crucial issue that warrants broader attention. (R3)
- One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive, emphasizing thoroughness, clarity, and generalizability; the other three reviewers (scores 6) also acknowledge value but note specific limitations and request additional analysis.

### Weaknesses
- Lack of analysis explaining observed results: why full retrieval outperforms partial retrieval, why CoT improves performance, and why performance varies between training setups (bioS vs. bioR). Ablation studies or more granular breakdowns are needed. (R1, R3)
- Missing discussion of overall implications of combining all four tasks; broader conclusions and practical impacts are not drawn. (R1)
- Limited query methods tested: only question forms + fine-tuning. Should also explore cloze-style prompting (closer to pretraining objective), zero-shot, and few-shot prompting to support claims that methods do not work. (R1)
- The CoT format used in training is incomplete (only intermediate answers without the full reasoning chain, L360–362), which may explain why CoT-trained models did not improve; this may have hindered the effect. (R2)
- Fine-tuning on synthetic biography data may cause catastrophic forgetting, affecting models’ understanding of basic concepts like “even” or their general manipulation capabilities. (R2)
- Figures 3, 4, and 6 are difficult to interpret due to color coding and too many lines (e.g., bioS multi5+permute vs. bioS multi5+permute+fullname); reducing the number of results displayed would improve clarity. (R3)
- Should connect to existing literature on LLM reasoning challenges (e.g., [1,2]) and discuss potential solutions based on the findings. (R2, R4)
- Reviewer disagreement: one reviewer (score 8) does not raise the novelty concern, while another reviewer (score 6) notes the result is not entirely new and cites missing references; a third reviewer (score 6) requests analysis of why results occur, which the more positive reviewer also echoes on figure clarity and depth. All reviewers concur that the work is valuable but varies on the need for additional experiments and exposition.
