## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies a new phenomenon of rank collapse in width in softmax-based attention transformers, in addition to the known depth collapse. By modeling attention at initialization as random Markov matrices and using free probability, the authors attribute width collapse to a spectral gap between the largest singular value (outlier at one) and the remaining eigenvalue bulk. They propose a simple fix—subtracting 1/T from each attention matrix element—to close the gap and mitigate rank collapse and exploding gradients. The reviewers generally agree that the theoretical analysis is sound and the solution is convenient, but they raise significant concerns about the limited scope of the model (attention-only, single-layer, single-head) and the lack of empirical validation on real-world tasks and architectures. One reviewer (score 3) was substantially more critical of the practical relevance and experimental completeness than the others (scores 5, 5, 6).

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel concept: rank collapse in width, advancing the understanding of initialization issues beyond the known depth collapse.
- Modeling softmax attention matrices as random Markov matrices and analyzing them via free probability is a sound theoretical approach.
- The explanation linking the spectral gap to rank collapse and exploding gradients is clear and insightful.
- The proposed solution (subtracting 1/T from each element of the attention matrix) is simple, convenient, and easy to implement.
- The theoretical analysis is rigorous, including propositions (e.g., Propositions 5, 6) that analyze the effect of removing the spectral gap.
- The paper is well organized and logically structured, with a clear theoretical narrative.
- Experiments in controlled settings (e.g., gradient norm, rank evolution, loss curves) show that the solution slows rank collapse when the spectrum contains a single outlier (Figure 4, 6 top row, etc.).

### Weaknesses
- **Model simplicity and generality.** The analysis is restricted to an extremely simplified transformer: attention-only, single-head, no skip connections, no layer normalization, and only the first-layer attention matrix is analyzed (Reviewer 4 W1; Reviewer 3 W1). The usefulness is therefore limited. It is unclear if the phenomenon or the fix transfers to common architectural components such as multi-head attention, skip connections, and layer normalization. The paper does not investigate how these components affect the spectral gap or rank collapse.
- **Limited depth validation.** Removing the spectral gap does not benefit optimization in two-layer transformers, as shown in the bottom row of Figure 6 (Reviewer 4 W2). What happens in deeper transformers? The practical relevance of the solution for multi-layer transformers remains unverified.
- **Empirical validation on real tasks is missing.** The experiments are confined to toy metrics (rank, gradient norm, loss curves) and do not include benchmark evaluations on standard NLP (e.g., machine translation) or CV tasks (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). There are no ablation studies on actual Transformer models to measure the impact of the proposed modification on task performance, training speed, or inference speed (Reviewer 1 W3). One reviewer (score 3) strongly emphasized this lack of real-world validation.
- **Initialization assumptions.** The analysis assumes Gaussian initialization, while practitioners often use Xavier or He initialization. It is not shown whether the spectral gap and rank collapse persist under these common schemes (Reviewer 3 W2). Moreover, Figure 8 raises questions about the validity of Xavier initialization, but this analysis is only conducted for a single-layer transformer (line 488) (Reviewer 4 W3).
- **Training dynamics ignored.** The study only considers initialization; the evolution of the spectral gap during training is not analyzed (Reviewer 3 W4). It is unknown whether the gap closes or widens over time and how this affects stability and convergence.
- **Applicability to other attention variants.** The paper does not address whether the observed rank collapse in width exists in other attention mechanisms, such as linear attention (Reviewer 1 Q; Reviewer 2 W1). Similarly, it is unclear if the problem is mitigated by existing tricks used to counter depth collapse (Reviewer 2 W1).
- **Presentation issue.** The introduction combines background and contributions; splitting it into Introduction and Preliminaries would improve clarity (Reviewer 1 W1).
