## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies the local geometry of learned manifolds in generative models (toy models, DDPM, Stable Diffusion 1.4) using three descriptors – scaling (ψ), rank (ν), and complexity (δ) – defined under the Continuous Piecewise-Linear (CPWL) assumption. The authors show correlations between these descriptors and generation quality, diversity, memorization, and propose a reward model trained to approximate ψ to guide sampling. Overall reception is positive but with significant reservations: three reviewers rated 6, while one (Reviewer 4) rated 8 and emphasized the empirical value and impact. All reviewers commend the underlying idea and scope of experiments, but raise concerns about computational cost, missing quantitative evaluations, insufficient reproducibility details, descriptor interpretation, and limited applicability to modern architectures with smooth activations.

### Strengths

- **Novel and interesting idea**: The paper introduces geometric descriptors (scaling, rank, complexity) to understand latent space structure, providing fresh perspective on generative models (R1, R3).

- **Comprehensive experiments across models**: Work covers a range from toy data to DDPM to Stable Diffusion 1.4, exploring multiple phenomena – on/off-manifold, content complexity, corruption, memorization (R1, R2, R3).

- **Practical framework for control**: The reward model trained to approximate local scaling offers a way to influence generation characteristics (e.g., complexity, aesthetics) by guiding latent sampling (R1, R2).

- **Intuitive and well-motivated descriptors**: Based on the CPWL hypothesis, the local geometry is naturally expressed as affine approximations; the three descriptors are theoretically grounded and align with human visual evaluation (R4 – score 8 reviewer).

- **Potential impact**: One reviewer (score 8) particularly highlights that the paper represents a valuable empirical contribution that will significantly impact the study of image manifold geometry (R4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of quantitative evaluation**: The paper relies almost entirely on qualitative visual inspection and lacks quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, IS, human preference scores) to evaluate the proposed geometry-guided sampling or the correlations claimed (R1, R4). This makes it difficult to objectively assess effectiveness (R1).

2. **High computational demands and scalability concerns**:
   - Computing the Jacobian for every linear region (or approximating it) scales poorly with network size; the paper does not report GPU hours per 1k samples or cost for training the reward model (R1, R4).
   - For large models, the cost is likely prohibitive; no analysis of practical feasibility is provided (R1).
   - The method may require unrolling the diffusion sampling process to compute Jacobians across multiple timesteps, further increasing cost (R4).

3. **Insufficient clarity and missing technical details for reproducibility**:
   - The calculation of the three descriptors in high-dimensional spaces (e.g., Stable Diffusion) is not fully specified (R3).
   - The random projection matrix \(B\) used in the Jacobian approximation: how is the number of dimensions \(P\) chosen? Why is a constant projection used for all neighbors around a given latent? How are the columns of \(B\) selected? (R2, R3).
   - The numerical method for computing local complexity (Definition 3) is not included; reference to prior work is insufficient for completeness (R4).
   - The training process for the reward model is vague: data preparation, network architecture, optimization procedure, and the exact reward function definition are missing (R3).
   - The justification for the 0.05 threshold in Section 3.1 for on/off-manifold detection is not provided (R2).
   - The definition of \(S\) in Equation (2) and why local scaling is proportional to the negative log-likelihood when the relationship is injective is not explained (R3).
   - The axes and construction in Figures 3 and 4 are unclear: what are the axes of the local generator? What points are depicted as anchors or pixels? (R2, R3).

4. **Descriptor interpretation and intuition**:
   - Local complexity (\( \delta \)) lacks an intuitive interpretation in high-dimensional latent spaces; the paper reads more as an empirical computation than an in-depth geometric study (R1).
   - The three descriptors appear redundant for many observations: all three (scaling, rank, complexity) increase with visual complexity in Figures 5, 12, 13, and the main applications only use scaling (R2, R4). What distinct properties does each descriptor capture? (R4).

5. **CPWL assumption justification and compatibility with smooth activations**:
   - The paper does not provide empirical justification that the CPWL assumption is a good approximation for DDPM or Stable Diffusion (R2). (One reviewer notes that recent work [3] has verified local linearity, but this is not cited or leveraged.)
   - The approach is only properly defined for piecewise-linear networks; modern architectures commonly use smooth activations (SiLU, GELU, SwiGLU), limiting broader applicability. The paper does not address how to handle or approximate these cases (R1).
   - No experiments are conducted on current state-of-the-art architectures (DiT or MM-DiT-based models such as SD3, Flux) to test cross-architecture compatibility (R1, through question).

6. **Memorization claim is weak for local complexity**: The trend for local complexity is almost indistinguishable between memorized and non-memorized prompts when increasing the guidance scale, undermining the metric’s reliability for memorization detection (R3).

7. **Lack of literature review**: Prior work on out-of-domain detection and uncertainty quantification using local geometry, as well as studies on local dimensionality of diffusion model manifolds (cited as [1–4]), are not discussed. Including these would better contextualize the contribution (R2, R4).

8. **Miscellaneous specific technical questions indicating missing information**:
   - Origin of Equation (3) is not derived or cited (R2).
   - Whether singular values in Equation (2) should be normalized (R3).
   - Whether input space vectors for DDPM/SD are the UNet inputs (noisy latents) or features from intermediate layers (R2).
   - Why at noise scale 0.17T the discrepancy between on- and off-manifold metrics is minimized, and why that indicates highest distinction (contradictory interpretation) (R3).
   - How the reward function is defined and why the Hessian calculation is required initially (R3).
