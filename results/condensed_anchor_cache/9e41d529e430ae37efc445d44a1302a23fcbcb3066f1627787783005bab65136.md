## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses train-test mismatch and suboptimal encoder gradients in vector quantization (VQ) for neural compression. It proposes alternating optimization of the encoder (with differentiable approximation) and decoder (with actual quantization) to avoid mismatch, and a sphere-noise-based stochastic VQ to produce smoother encoder gradients. Experiments on synthetic sources and natural images show improvements over VQ-STE and UQ-AUN. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (moderate) with confidence 4 or 5; one reviewer rated 5 and was more positive, but all reviewers raised significant concerns.

### Strengths
- The problem of train-test mismatch and gradient issues with STE in VQ-based models is relevant and timely (R1, R2, R4).  
- The proposed alternating optimization procedure for encoder, codebook, and decoder is principled and could improve convergence of the rate-distortion loss (R1, R2).  
- The stochastic spherical noise approximation for VQ and the gradient analysis (encoder latent gradient, correspondence between latent and image-space quantization) are clear contributions (R1, R2, R4).  
- The paper is overall well-motivated, well-written, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).  
- Experimental results on a broad range of sources (synthetic and natural images) demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms VQ+STE and UQ+AUN (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient baselines and comparisons** (R1, R2, R3):  
   - Missing comparisons with other VQ approximation methods (e.g., Agustsson et al. 2017, Zhu et al. 2022) and with soft-toward VQ (A2) and probabilistic VQ (A3) using the same Balle et al. 2018 transforms (R1).  
   - BD-rate comparison against these methods is absent (R1).  
   - The baseline UQ-AUN and UQ-STE comparisons are unfair because the entropy model is used in the proposed method but not included in the baselines; BD-rate gain might come from the entropy model rather than the proposed VQ (R2, R3).  
   - R-D performance is evidently worse than current state-of-the-art methods and even worse than BPG444 (R3).

2. **Limited evaluation scope and outdated architecture** (R1, R2, R3, R4):  
   - Experiments on natural images use a single-layer VQ with a low-complexity autoencoder (Balle et al. 2018 transforms, discrete VQVAE entropy model). More recent transforms (Cheng et al. 2020, ELIC, TCM, NVTC, PQ-VAE) are not tested (R1, R2, R4).  
   - The proposed method is only applicable to single-layer quantization and unconditional entropy models, limiting its relevance to state-of-the-art models (R1, R3).  
   - For low-dimensional synthetic sources, the latent dimension equals the source dimension, which is unrealistic for compression (encoder should reduce dimensionality) (R3).  
   - Network architecture details (e.g., encoder layer structure, entropy model) are missing, and some citations for baselines are incorrect (e.g., Table 1 cites hyperprior model for factorized model) (R2).

3. **Theory and assumptions insufficiently justified** (R3, R4):  
   - The claim that a uniform sphere distribution meets the desired gradient property is not theoretically proven; additive uniform noise (UQ-AUN) also approximates the gradient by the difference in RD loss between adjacent centers (Eq.4) (R3, R4).  
   - The advantages of a uniform sphere distribution over conventional additive uniform noise are not clearly explained (R4).  
   - The definition of the hypersphere (set of points with distance ≤ radius vs. = radius) is not given (R4).  
   - The interpretation of neural compression as vector quantization needs clarification relative to prior work (Ballé et al. 2020) (R4).

4. **Alternating optimization strategy is not novel and its superiority unclear** (R2, R3):  
   - Alternating optimization is well-established in VQ literature; prior neural compression works (e.g., Guo et al. 2021, “Soft-then-Hard”) already fine-tune decoder with quantized latents to reduce train-test mismatch, and these citations are missing (R2).  
   - There is no evidence that the proposed alternating optimization is better than mixed quantization (additive uniform noise for entropy model, quantized latent for decoder) (R3).  
   - Questions remain: why the prior is optimized with decoder instead of encoder; how quantization boundaries are defined (nearest-neighbor partitioning); how to guarantee the vector quantizer property ||y−ŷ|| = ||y−e_i|| = ||y−e_j|| (R2, R4).

5. **Presentation and completeness issues** (R1, R2, R4):  
   - Section 3.2 spends too much space on scalar quantization details; moving them to appendix would free space for explaining the proposed method more clearly (R1).  
   - The paper has incomplete sentences, missing citations, and repeated references in the related work (R1, R2, R4).  
   - Several typos: redundant comma after “e.g.”, unnecessary bracket, incorrect citation commands (R4).  
   - Figure 6 (gradient smoothness) should be placed in Section 4 to contrast with Figure 4 (R1).  
   - The implementation of VQ in the latent space is not fully described (R4).  
   - Why the proposed method cannot be applied to architectures like NVTC or PQ-VAE is not explained (R1).

6. **Generalizability questions** (R1, R4):  
   - One reviewer (score 5) still raised concerns about the limited importance of VQ relative to conditional coding; the method should be integrated with SOTA conditional coding models (ELIC, TCM) to evaluate its contribution (R4).  
   - Whether the proposed method is applicable to other NTC models (including those with uniform quantizers) is unclear (R4).
