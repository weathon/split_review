## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the neglect of audio in video large language models by proposing an audio-visual LLM called Dolphin and a large-scale audio-visual instruction-tuning dataset AVU (5.2M video-audio-qa tuples). Dolphin aligns audio and visual modalities both spatially (via a multi-scale adapter with cross-attention) and temporally (via interleaved merging with bi-directional cross-attention). AVU is constructed from existing unimodal datasets using LLMs to generate modality-specific QAs and is partitioned by audio-visual consistency. Experiments show improved performance on audio-visual, audio, and video benchmarks, and reduced hallucination.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and timely: most video LLMs ignore audio information, and this work directly addresses that gap.
- The dataset AVU is large-scale and its curation process—using open-source and commercial LLMs, differentiating consistency levels—is sound and reasonable; it fills a community shortage of audio-visual instruction data.
- The approach to enhancing spatial and temporal alignment in AV-LLMs is innovative, with a multi-scale adapter and interleaved merging.
- Extensive ablations verify each component (spatial/temporal alignment modules) and numerically analyze the impact of the dataset.
- Dolphin outperforms previous audio-visual LLMs on audio, video, and audio-visual benchmarks, including both closed and open-ended audio tasks.
- Training VideoLLaMA with AVU demonstrates the dataset’s usefulness for improving audio-visual understanding.
- The model reduces errors related to audio neglect and shows strong audio-visual comprehension in experiments.

### Weaknesses
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons**: Experiments only compare against VideoLLaMA1, not VideoLLaMA2 or other strong baselines (e.g., FAVOR, video-SALMONN, PandaGPT, OneLLM). More comparisons on specific audio-related tasks and against uni-modal models are needed to demonstrate advantage.
- **Missing hallucination analysis**: The paper claims AVU reduces hallucination but provides no quantitative evaluation (e.g., measuring fabricated/invented responses, especially when audio is crucial). A comparison with other models on such metrics is expected.
- **“Fine-grained spatial modeling” is undefined**: No clear definition or explanation of what this means in the model’s context (e.g., how spatial relationships between audio sources and visual elements are captured). This also applies to the claimed “temporal integration” mechanism.
- **Method novelty is limited and description is vague**: The audio-visual adapters and cross-modal attention have been used in prior work (refs [1–3]); the training pipeline and data generation process are also not novel (refs [4–5]). Architecture details are missing:
  - Notation inconsistency between Figure 2 and text; superscript “i” vs “1” unclear.
  - The connector between encoder and LLM (Q-former or linear) is unspecified and not ablated.
  - No ablation for encoder choices (ViT-L, AST, Vicuna) versus newer alternatives (SigLIP, Beats, Whisper, Qwen, Llama3, etc.).
  - No video encoder used; no ablation for this.
  - How bi-directional cross-attention achieves temporal modeling (e.g., positional encoding, RoPE) is not explained.
  - Table 6 is not explained in the main text.
- **Speech is neglected**: The model uses AST (environmental sound) but does not employ a separate speech encoder, yet it reports results on speech recognition (Table 3). How this is possible is not explained; comparisons with speech-centric models are missing.
- **Dataset reliability concerns** (especially for a proposed benchmark):
  - Prompt templates for constructing meta-information are not provided.
  - In Figure 6, AVU-specific questions differ but answers are identical.
  - In Figure 9, the question asks about frog sound, but the answer discusses color, indicating noise.
  - No human verification of dataset quality; researchers may hesitate to use it as a benchmark.
- **Application scenario limitations**: The dataset is built from at least medium AV-consistency videos; low-consistency videos are used only as negatives. It is unclear how to decide suitability for in-the-wild videos, and what happens if low-consistency videos are used for training.
- **Missing audio-visual task evaluations**: The model is not tested on established audio-visual tasks such as audio-visual caption, audio-visual speech recognition, or audio-visual sound source detection, unlike prior work (e.g., video-SALMONN). No comparison with any-to-any models on such tasks.
- **Unclear handling of uni-modal and misaligned modalities**: How does the model handle missing audio (modality mask?) or semantically irrelevant audio that could introduce negative information? No experiment showing performance gap with uni-modal models.
- **Writing and presentation issues** (Reviewer 4 details, echoed by others’ “vague” comments):
  - Typos: L67 “VideoLlama” repeated, L158 “clap” check, L231 “detrailed” → “detailed”, L414 broken sentence.
  - Figure 1 right subfigure unexplained; Figure 2 right subfigure has typo “his audio.”
  - Inconsistent citations (use /citep).
  - L32: claim that audio is crucial for comprehensive understanding needs substantiation; L72 reason (3) may contradict the introduction’s emphasis on audio’s importance.
- **Minority concerns about experimental rigor**: One reviewer (score 8) noted the need for more experiments on audio-visual datasets; another (score 5) questioned whether using audio–converted-to-text could outperform Dolphin’s audio alignment approach.
- **Questions that imply missing experiments**:
  - What are the effects of using pre-trained models for the data pipeline, and how is hallucination in those models controlled?
  - How do models understand absolute time (e.g., “What time does the train whistle blow?”)?
  - Why not train the smaller encoders directly when the LLM is also trained?
  - How does the model perform when given both video and audio-as-text?
