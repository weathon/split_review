## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies Thompson sampling (TS) under adversarial reward poisoning. It proposes two corruption-robust TS algorithms: one for stochastic Gaussian bandits and one for contextual Gaussian linear bandits. The algorithms use pseudo‑posteriors that are less influenced by attacks and assume either knowledge or ignorance of the attacker’s budget. Regret upper bounds are provided, and experiments are conducted to validate the theoretical claims.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of problem / first TS variants (R2, R3):** This is the first work that provides variants of TS for reward‑poisoning attacks. R2 notes that the algorithms inherit TS’s advantages over optimism‑based methods. R3 states that prior work had not studied TS under reward poisoning and that the proof techniques, while standard, are non‑trivial.  
  *Contrast – R1 does not find the problem or algorithms novel:* R1 views the corruption model as a special case of differentially private online learning and the algorithmic idea (optimistic reshaping of posteriors) as previously used in Hu & Hedge (2022).  
- **Theoretical near‑optimality (R2, R3):** The regret upper bounds are argued to be near‑optimal, relying on lower bounds from prior work.  
- **Clarity and positioning (R3):** The paper is interesting, enjoyable to read, and clearly positions its contributions relative to prior corruption‑robust bandit work.  
- **Experimental support (R3):** The theoretical results are backed by simulations.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited setting and Gaussian assumptions (R4, R1):**  
  – The assumed Gaussian priors and reward distributions are very restrictive. The claim that extensions to general priors are possible is unsupported; the regret proofs (Theorems 4.1, 5.1) rely heavily on Gaussianity for closed‑form posteriors. R4 insists that the scope limitation should be stated in the title, abstract, and introduction.  
  – R1 adds that the corruption model (bounded total corruption) is essentially the same as a privacy‑budget constraint, making the setting a simpler instance of differentially private online learning.  

- **Regret bound issues (R4, R1):**  
  – **Missing sum over arms in Theorem 4.1 (R4):** The final step does not sum over all \(N\) arms, leading to a corrected bound of order \(O(N^{3/2}\sqrt{T\log N} + N^{2}\overline{C} + N^{2})\).  
  – **Impossibly large constant (R4, R1):** The constant \(72(e^{64}+5)\approx 10^{29}\) renders the bound vacuous for any realistic \(N\) and \(T\). R1 suggests that the constant can be improved using results from arXiv:2407.14879.  

- **Presentation and proof clarity (R2, R3, R4):**  
  – Notations are cumbersome, sometimes incoherent (e.g., history \(H_{t-1}\) vs \(F_{t-1}\), mixing \(\theta\) and \(\Theta\)), incomplete (summation missing limits), or undefined (e.g., \(\tau_k\), \(\Delta_i\), \(\overline{E_i^\theta}(t)\), \(c_s\), \(L_i(T)\)).  
  – Proofs consist of long chains of inequalities without justification or references to borrowed lemmas.  
  – Several typos: End of Sections 4 and 5 cite “He et al. (2023)” instead of “He et al. (2022)” (R2). He et al. (2023) is actually about linear MDP, not the same line of work (R3).  
  – Figures 2 and 4 have unreadable axis labels and too‑small fonts (R3, R4).  
  – R4 suspects parts of the paper (e.g., lines 36–47) were written by generative AI due to repetitions and generic phrasing.  

- **Experimental deficiencies (R3, R4):**  
  – **Missing robust baseline for stochastic MAB (R3, R4):** The stochastic bandit experiments do not include any corruption‑robust competitor. For the contextual bandit, the proposed method performs similarly to the robust baseline but often worse (R3).  
  – **Unexplained attack strategies (R4):** The adversarial attack (target arm selection, perturbation magnitudes) is not described.  
  – **Implausible results (R4):**  
    * The regret of TS is almost identical to UCB in Figures 1 and 2.  
    * The left and right plots of Figure 1 appear to show the same data despite different titles.  
    * In the bottom part of Figure 2, the proposed algorithm’s regret *decreases* as the adversarial budget increases, which is counterintuitive and suggests an error.  
    * The robust TS *outperforms* TS and UCB even when the attack budget is zero (a failed sanity check).  
  – **Confidence intervals (R4):** No information about the confidence level is provided.  
  – **Code (R4):** The submitted code lacks a README, comments, or instructions, making reproduction impossible.  

- **Questions that overlap with criticisms (included as weaknesses above):**  
  – (R1) What does “optimality in the face of corruption” (line 65) mean?  
  – (R2) What can be said about Bayesian regret?  
  – (R3) The intuition behind Algorithm 2 and the two remarks (“attacker can apply less influence” and “variance of posterior is limited”) is not explained.  
  – (R4) The missing sum in Theorem 4.1 and the need to verify the bound’s derivation.  
  – (R4) Request for a detailed justification of the “extension to general priors” claim.  

- **Missing comparison table (R2):** The paper lists previous works but does not provide a table that directly compares results, making it hard to assess the contributions.  

- **Some results expected (R3):** Given prior work on corruption robustness, the findings are somewhat anticipated.
