## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers present strongly diverging assessments. One reviewer (score 1, confidence 5) views the work as lacking novelty because kernel-based HDC encoders have been studied since 2021, criticizes the use of only a small synthetic binary image dataset, and identifies several misreferences. Another reviewer (score 3, confidence 4) considers the establishment of a learning/cognition tradeoff for HDC systems to be highly original and theoretically grounded, but finds the experimental validation limited to MNIST and the claims of universal encoding overreaching. A third reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) praises flexibility and theoretical foundation but asks for more experimental detail and larger datasets. Across all reviews, key shared concerns are the narrow scope of evaluation (MNIST only), lack of baselines, missing implementation details, and overclaimed generality.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of the learning/cognition tradeoff (Reviewer 2, score 3):** The paper is highly original in establishing the learning/cognition tradeoff for hyperdimensional systems – this reviewer has not seen prior work do this. The tradeoff is put into theoretically grounded form and used to build a separation metric, an orthogonal class hypervector set, and an encoder that balances correlative learning with structural features.
- **Clear notation and explanation (Reviewer 2):** The notation is clear and concise, methods are clearly explained, and the presentation solidly meets the conference bar, though more detail on the tradeoff and a conceptual example would be appreciated.
- **Flexibility and cost-effectiveness (Reviewer 3, score 5):** The proposed encoder is highly flexible and cost-effective, adapting to diverse scenarios while supporting efficient learning and accurate information retrieval.
- **Solid theoretical foundation (Reviewer 3):** The work is based on a solid theoretical foundation and optimizes performance through parameter adjustment, ensuring effectiveness and accuracy across tasks.
- **Exploration of kernel-based encoders (Reviewer 1, score 1):** The paper explores HDC encoders based on kernel methods for synthetic image classification and recall tasks (though this reviewer considers the approach not novel – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty (Reviewer 1, score 1, confidence 5):** The use of kernel methods to enhance HDC encoding has been investigated since 2021, including theoretical analysis of initialization and kernel shape. Key references: “Vector Function Architectures” (Frady et al., arXiv:2109.03429), “Computing on functions using randomized vector representations (in brief)” (Frady et al., NICE 2022), and “Spatial Semantic Pointers” (Furlong & Eliasmith, Cogn Neurodyn 2023).
- **Insufficient experimental validation (all reviewers):**
    - **Reviewer 1:** The presented results are very weak – only a small synthetic image classification dataset with binary classes, reaching 95% accuracy. Missing experiments on established datasets (CIFAR, ImageNet) and strong baselines. Experiment details are lacking (Reviewer 3).
    - **Reviewer 2:** The scheme is validated only on MNIST, which is considered a prototyping dataset due to its easy class separability and wide class boundaries. Datasets with more nonlinear boundaries (e.g., CIFAR-10) are needed. Recent work (arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09680) has shown ~95% accuracy on MNIST and validated on CIFAR10; the paper should demonstrate the significance of its improved performance over such baselines.
    - **Reviewer 3:** The current experimental results are only on MNIST, which is too simple. Experiments on larger datasets like CIFAR-10 are required.
- **Overclaimed universality (Reviewer 2, score 3):** The paper claims a universal encoding but applies it solely to image data. The Methods section repeatedly references cross-pixel correlation. The authors should walk back claims to image classification/encodings or provide ablation studies on non-image data (e.g., sensor data streams in Kalman filtering [IEEE 10473878], time series [arXiv:2402.01999], symbolic learning [Nature 2023]).
- **No proof of optimality (Reviewer 2):** The cognition/learning tradeoff does not address the claim of “optimal” encoding – the paper provides an elegant proof of two axes along which HD learning proceeds, but not a proof of optimality of the encoding formulation. More general data assumptions are needed.
- **Missing experimental details (Reviewer 3, score 5):** The paper lacks specific experimental steps and hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, training step size).
- **Misreferences (Reviewer 1, score 1):**
    - Holographic Reduced Representations: cited as (Tay et al., 2019) but should be Plate, T. A. (1995), IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks.
    - Multiply-Add-Permute: cited as (Kleyko et al., 2021) but should be Gayler, R. W. (1998).
    - Binary Spatter Codes: cited as (Kleyko et al., 2016) but should be Kanerva, P. (1994), ICANN.
- **Additional questions that overlap with criticisms (Reviewer 2):**
    - For autoregressive data, excessive noise from Gaussian kernel sampling during encoding may harm accuracy, affecting applicability to time series. Can the authors comment on this?
    - The parameter w appears dataset-centric/data-driven, which contradicts the claim of an optimal, universal encoding formulation. Can the authors provide a theoretically grounded value of w?
