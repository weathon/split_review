## Merged Review

### Summary

The five reviewers (scores: 8,8,6,6,6) broadly agree that the paper introduces valuable contributions: AuroraCap (a video captioner using token merging), VDC (a detailed captioning benchmark), and VDCScore (an LLM-assisted evaluation metric). Strengths include good performance, extensive experiments, and a well-written presentation. Weaknesses center on insufficient technical details (pretraining data, training strategy, token merging implementation), potential fairness of baseline comparisons, and concerns about the novelty and stability of the token merging and evaluation approaches, with one minority reviewer (Reviewer 4) explicitly questioning the novelty of both token merging (similar to Chat-UniVi) and the divide-and-conquer evaluation. Additionally, there are requests for more analysis on dataset-dependent performance, GPT version specification, and the metric’s handling of linguistic quality issues.

### Strengths

1. **Effective Token Merging Strategy:** AuroraCap applies bipartite soft matching to merge similar visual tokens, reducing the number of input tokens to 10%–20% of those generated by ViT with only marginal performance loss across benchmarks (Reviewers 1,4,5). The technique is novel and shows potential for efficient video understanding (Reviewer 3). However, one reviewer (Reviewer 4) notes that similar attempts have been made in Chat-UniVi, questioning novelty.

2. **Strong Captioning Performance:** AuroraCap achieves excellent results on both image captioning (e.g., CIDEr 88.9 on Flickr30k, beating GPT-4V’s 55.3 and Gemini-1.5 Pro’s 82.2) and video captioning tasks, using a simple architecture consistent with LLaVA and a three-stage training recipe (Reviewers 1,5). It outperforms state-of-the-art models, including GPT-4V and Gemini-1.5 Pro (Reviewer 5).

3. **VDC Benchmark Contribution:** The proposed VDC benchmark is large-scale and diverse, containing over one thousand carefully annotated structured captions spanning short descriptions, background, main object, camera movements, and detailed temporal descriptions (Reviewers 1,3,4,5). The average caption length exceeds previous datasets, addressing the need for detailed video understanding and generation (Reviewer 1). This is a valuable contribution to an underexplored area (Reviewer 2).

4. **VDCScore Metric:** VDCScore transforms long captions into multiple short question-answer pairs using GPT-4o in a divide-and-conquer strategy, aligning well with human reasoning and avoiding traditional metrics’ difficulties with long text (Reviewers 2,5). It mitigates potential hallucination issues associated with LLM-based evaluation (Reviewer 5). Human Elo ranking confirms better correlation with human judgments (Reviewers 1,5). The metric uses a novel divide-and-conquer approach (Reviewer 3), though one reviewer (Reviewer 4) notes similar approaches have been explored elsewhere.

5. **Extensive Experiments and Analysis:** The paper conducts very extensive experiments across multiple image and video captioning and QA benchmarks, including ablation studies on visual token kept ratios (Reviewers 1,2,5). It explores the impact of token merging ratio on performance, showing that fewer tokens can maintain high accuracy (Reviewer 5).

6. **Clear Presentation:** The paper is well-written, well-structured, and includes intuitive figures (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). It is easy to follow (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient Details on Pretraining Data and Training Strategy:**
   - The 1.3M pretraining dataset for AuroraCap is not described: no specification of sources, data types, preprocessing/filtering steps, or comparison to datasets used by other models (Reviewer 3). This makes it difficult to attribute the source of good performance (Reviewer 3).
   - It is unclear whether the token merging strategy was used during training and how different token merge ratios during training affect inference performance (Reviewer 5). The lack of experimentation on the sensitivity to this hyperparameter raises concerns about robustness (Reviewer 5).
   - The exact training schema and settings (e.g., which configuration from Figure D5/D6) are not clarified, and it is not clear if all compared captioning models were trained under the same schema (Reviewer 1).

2. **Unfair or Incomplete Baseline Comparisons:**
   - The selection of baselines for zero-shot image captioning (Table 2) is limited; stronger models like BLIP2 (CIDEr 121.6 on NoCaps val) and InstructBLIP are omitted (Reviewer 2). A more comprehensive comparison with a wider range of state-of-the-art models is needed to accurately position AuroraCap (Reviewer 2).
   - Similar concerns apply to video captioning and video QA tasks, where the baseline selection appears limited and may not represent the current best performers (Reviewer 2).
   - The low CIDEr score of GPT-4V on Flickr30k (55.3) is questionable; verification is needed on whether prompts and evaluation were fair (Reviewer 4). This result is considered unreasonable (Reviewer 4).

3. **Issues with VDCScore Evaluation:**
   - Reliance on GPT-4o: The exact version of GPT-4o used is not specified, hindering reproducibility and comparability (Reviewer 2). Different GPT versions can heavily affect results, and the potential impact should be investigated and discussed (Reviewer 2).
   - Stability of metric: It is unknown whether a fixed or varying number of question-answer pairs are generated per caption; if varying, how is consistency ensured across captions of different lengths/complexities? Experiments testing stability with varying numbers of QA pairs are missing (Reviewer 3).
   - It is hard to determine which score (VDCScore, VDD, CIDEr, or other metrics like RefCLIP-S, RefPAC-S) is better for evaluating detailed captions (Reviewer 1).
   - The metric may not adequately handle linguistic quality problems such as repetition and grammatical errors. No experiments or examples demonstrate how VDCScore performs on captions with these issues compared to human evaluation (Reviewer 3). Alternative automatic metrics (e.g., n-gram matching on final triplets) should be explored to reduce reliance on GPT (Reviewer 2).

4. **Missing Details on Human Elo Ranking:**
   - The specific dataset used for the Elo Ranking comparison, including its size, composition, and selection criteria, is not provided. Its relation to or difference from the VDC benchmark is unclear (Reviewer 3).

5. **Incomplete Analysis of AuroraCap’s Strengths and Weaknesses:**
   - The paper does not explain why AuroraCap performs well on ANet but poorly on MSVD in video QA tasks. An analysis of question types and visual scene complexity across datasets is needed to understand this discrepancy (Reviewer 2). This discrepancy in performance between captioning and QA suggests the model may not effectively leverage visual understanding for complex reasoning (Reviewer 5).
   - Only spatial token merging is performed; temporal token merging between frames could further improve efficiency, especially for slow-moving scenes or static backgrounds (Reviewer 5).
   - The token merging strategy is static: it applies a fixed kept ratio to all inputs. This may discard crucial tokens in complex scenes while retaining less informative ones in simpler scenes, limiting adaptability (Reviewer 5). The stability of the token merging method on longer video sequence benchmarks (Egoschema, VideoMME) has not been evaluated (Reviewer 4).
   - Why does retaining all visual tokens not lead to the highest VDCScore (Figure 6)? (Reviewer 5)

6. **Presentation and Terminology Issues:**
   - The paper is not very easy to follow due to many tables and figures (Reviewer 1). Minor grammar errors: "BELU" should be "BLEU" (Reviewer 2); line 203 "are only contains" should be "only contain" (Reviewer 2).
   - The model is trained on general tasks but named a captioner; it may be more appropriate to call it a general model while emphasizing captioning capabilities (Reviewer 4).

7. **Missing Practical Details:**
   - The number of GPU hours consumed on this research is not reported (Reviewer 1).
   - While the paper states data, weights, and recipes will be published, it is not clear if the full pretraining data and exact training recipes will be released (Reviewer 1). The community would benefit from open release to facilitate reproducibility (Reviewer 1).

*Note:* One minority reviewer (Reviewer 4) explicitly notes lack of novelty in the token merging strategy (similar to Chat-UniVi) and the divide-and-conquer evaluation method (similar to [2]), while other reviewers view these as novel contributions. This disagreement should be considered when assessing the overall contribution.
