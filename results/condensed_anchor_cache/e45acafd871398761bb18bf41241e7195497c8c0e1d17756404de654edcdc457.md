## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Quadratic Programming Enhanced Model (QPM), a method for globally interpretable image classification. QPM represents each class with a compact binary assignment of very few features (typically 5) selected via discrete quadratic programming from pretrained vision features (e.g., ResNet-50). The binary assignment ensures that features are shared across classes, producing contrastive and generalizable class representations. The features are used to classify by thresholding their average activations. The authors optimize for predictive accuracy and interpretability constraints (diversity, locality, class-independence, contrastiveness) and evaluate on small and large-scale datasets including ImageNet-1K. QPM achieves competitive accuracy and state-of-the-art interpretability metrics, with qualitative results showing easy-to-understand global explanations.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important and challenging problem of global interpretability, providing explanations that are simple for humans to interpret (binary positive weights, few features per class).
- Well-motivated, well-written, and easy to follow; idea intuitive and well-organized.
- Global class representations are compact (typically 5 features per class) and features can be reused across multiple classes, making comparisons easy.
- Good use of discrete optimization with clever relaxation of constraints to select diverse, localized, and predictive features while maintaining accuracy and interpretability.
- Robustness and generalizability demonstrated through evaluation on multiple datasets (CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, Oxford Flowers, ImageNet-1K) and backbone architectures.
- Achieves competitive or state-of-the-art accuracy compared to other interpretable models (e.g., ProtoPNet, ProtoTree, Q-SENN) without sacrificing too much predictive performance.
- Strong structure grounding results on CUB-2011, showing that learned features align with human-understandable bird part annotations.
- Ablation experiments confirm the effectiveness of the \(Z_B\) and \(Z_R\) objectives.
- Qualitative results (e.g., Fig 5-7, Appendix) clearly show the interpretability of the heatmaps and feature assignments.

### Weaknesses
- Fixed choice of five features per class is arbitrary; some classes may need more, others fewer. This fixed number leads to polysemantic features (e.g., Fig 12 shows the green feature highlighting bird beaks on one image and wings on another), which can defeat interpretability. As the number of features increases, how does the computational cost of the quadratic program scale, and what are the practical limits before optimization becomes intractable?
- Explanations are heatmaps without explicit semantic meaning, unlike Concept Bottleneck Models or TCAV that provide human-readable concept labels. The structural grounding evaluation uses only a limited set of pre-defined part annotations on CUB-2011, which may not reflect the full range of features the model actually uses; the model might rely on more nuanced or abstract features not captured by those annotations. Presenting features with human-language labels in figures would be beneficial.
- Standard explanation fidelity metrics (e.g., deletion/insertion) are missing, making it difficult to compare faithfulness. The proposed contrastiveness metric may not fully capture explanation quality. Moreover, QPM shows inferior performance on SID@5, Class-Independence, and Contrastiveness compared to state-of-the-art models (e.g., ProtoPool, ProtoPNet), indicating that the compact binary assignment may limit feature diversity. The authors should discuss the mechanisms behind this trade-off and possible mitigations.
- The method relies on features from pretrained models that may contain biases; no mitigation is proposed for such biases that could affect both accuracy and interpretability.
- The use of Pearson correlation as a similarity measure assumes linear relationships, which may not capture all nuances in complex datasets with significant nonlinear patterns. The authors should clarify how the model handles such cases and what limitations arise.
- Performance gains over Q-SENN are marginal, yet the report Q-SENN’s computational limitations; a comparative analysis (e.g., a table of training/inference speeds) across methods would help justify the trade-off.
- The method does not use negative reasoning (all feature weights are positive), which simplifies interpretation but may be a limitation for explaining the absence of features.
- Experiments on high-stake domains (e.g., medical imaging, autonomous driving) are absent; such evaluation is needed to assess robustness and generalizability in real-world scenarios where interpretability is critical.
- Writing is unclear at several points: L282 (clipping entries between 0 and epsilon), L127-129 (how balanced binary assignment causes general binary concepts), and L372-373 (omission of grounding measurement despite providing results; clarify that it is due to lack of part annotations in other datasets).
- Optimization times reported (4 hours for fine-grained datasets, ~11 hours for ImageNet) but not compared to previous methods, making it hard to assess efficiency. Visual explanations for misclassified samples (e.g., from Appendix Fig 10) should be shown to demonstrate interpretability in failure cases.
