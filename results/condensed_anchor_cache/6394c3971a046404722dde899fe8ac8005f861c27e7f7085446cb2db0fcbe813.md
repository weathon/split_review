## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses point-supervised facial expression spotting (P-FES) with a two-branch framework. It proposes a Gaussian-based Instance-Adaptive Intensity Modeling (GIM) module to assign soft pseudo-labels based on expression intensity distributions, and an Intensity-Aware Contrastive (IAC) loss. Evaluations on SAMM-LV and CAS(ME)^2 show improvements over state-of-the-art P-TAL methods.

### Strengths
- The point-supervised setting is more practical than fully-supervised facial expression spotting (Reviewer 1).  
- The intensity-aware contrastive loss is interesting: it considers intensity differences within same-class and between-class instances, rather than simple pull/push (Reviewer 1).  
- The paper addresses an interesting and well-motivated problem (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3).  
- Ablation study demonstrates contributions of the proposed components (Reviewer 2).  
- Experiments are comprehensive and comparisons with other methods are reasonable (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Soft pseudo-label is not novel enough:** Although well-motivated, soft pseudo-labeling is widely used as a label smoothing technique; the training process is complicated and ad-hoc with three stages, and the improvement over hard-label techniques is minor. The proposed framework cannot outperform F-FES methods (Reviewer 2).  
2. **Intensity score is class-agnostic – unclear handling of closely timed apexes:** The intensity score is shared across all expression classes. What happens when two facial expressions with distinct apexes occur close together in an untrimmed video? Also, the definition of “emotional-class-agnostic” intensity needs clarification (e.g., basic emotions vs. MaE/ME), as intensities of different expressions may vary significantly (Reviewer 1).  
3. **Inference details are insufficient:** How is the outer-inner-contrastive (OIC) score computed and used to determine temporal range and position? The description of multiple thresholds for proposal generation and the discarding of redundant proposals is vague (Reviewer 1).  
4. **Soft pseudo-label update is not explained:** Predicted intensity scores fluctuate during training. How is the pseudo-apex frame and the range for assigning soft pseudo-labels updated based on changing features and intensity output? How does this iterative process converge? (Reviewer 1)  
5. **Duration estimation and Gaussian assumption are not justified:** How is the duration of each expression class chosen? Not all expression instances follow a Gaussian distribution (e.g., a sudden startle). The paper does not address such special cases (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3).  
6. **Neutral frame extraction is challenging for micro-expressions:** Extracting pseudo-neutral frames is difficult for ME classes because micro-expression frames are very similar to neutral frames; how does the framework obtain reliable pseudo-neutral frames and achieve better performance on ME? (Reviewer 2)  
7. **Lack of analysis on pseudo-apex frame evolution:** How do pseudo-apex frames change during different training epochs? (Reviewer 2)  
8. **Random selection strategy for multiple soft pseudo-labels is unconvincing:** The table does not clearly show that “random” is the best approach. No intuition is given for why random selection would outperform accumulated values of MaE and ME (Reviewer 1).  
9. **Figure 4 is unclear:** The legend lacks ground-truth boundary frame marks and the explanation is insufficient (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3).
