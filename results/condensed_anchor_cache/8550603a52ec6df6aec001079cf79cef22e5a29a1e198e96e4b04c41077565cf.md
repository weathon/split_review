## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies CLIP models under a particular type of distribution shift – compositional OOD generalization – where objects and attributes are individually seen during training but their composition is unseen. The authors create a new test dataset, ImageNet‑AO, using DALL‑E with 1000 ImageNet classes and 30 adjectives, validated by humans (~12 000 plausible compositions, ~50 000 images). They verify that the compositions are absent from several CLIP training sets. Key findings: (1) compositional diversity of training data (measured by NMI) improves generalization; (2) image/text representation disentanglement of objects and attributes plays a key role; (3) larger, more diverse datasets lead to better generalization; (4) better disentanglement correlates with better OoD performance. They use NMI, Z‑Diff, DCI‑Informativeness, Explicitness, and DCIMIG metrics to evaluate disentanglement.

### Strengths
- High‑quality test set generated with a text‑to‑image model, providing a simple and straightforward benchmark for compositional generalization (Top‑1 accuracy) – significant for the community.
- Insights from the experiments are interesting and insightful.
- Well‑designed experiments with a large set of results in the supplementary material showing that CLIP models struggle on the proposed dataset.
- The dataset (ImageNet‑AO) is likely to be useful for future research.
- Paper is well‑structured and easy to read.

### Weaknesses
- **Over‑claim on language supervision’s role (R1, R2, R3).**  
  *R1:* The statement that language supervision “positively impacts the model representation quality” (Sec. 3.2) is too strong; prior work (e.g., “Data Determines Distributional Robustness in CLIP”) shows that multiple factors contribute. The authors do not control for image data size/diversity, and comparisons between VLMs and other modalities need more rigorous controls.  
  *R2:* For Fig. 1, image‑only models (DINO‑v2, BEiT‑v2) should be trained on the *same* image datasets as CLIP (ignoring captions) to isolate the effect of language supervision. Without matching the image dataset, the claim remains unsupported.  
  *R3:* Most conclusions (e.g., diversity of captions promotes compositionality) are already prevalent in the literature; the mutual‑information analysis does not provide additional insights.

- **Dataset construction lacks clarity and controls (R2, R3).**  
  - No description or motivation for the selection of the 30 adjectives; how are they guaranteed to be absent or infrequent in training data? (R2, R3)  
  - The “Attribute + Object template” is not described or cited. (R2)  
  - It is not clear whether all 1000 ImageNet classes were used or only a subset; if a subset, how was it chosen? (R2)  
  - Human validation is crucial but lacks detail: how many annotators, instructions, inter‑annotator agreement, and the order of statements in the paper needs correction (R2, R3; note R1 also mentions unclear methodology).  
  - The check for presence of test captions in training data is only claimed without empirical evidence or reference; moreover, the check is not performed for other datasets used (LAION, YFCC15m, CC12m, DataComp) and images with different captions could still appear. (R2, R3)  
  - Human validation only assessed plausibility of noun‑adjective pairs, but DALL‑E may generate images that do not faithfully follow the composition (e.g., confusing attributes or relations between objects) – see [1], [2]. (R3)

- **Limited diversity of models and datasets in experiments (R1, R2).**  
  *R1:* Table 1 should show NMI for a subset of LAION with the same size as other datasets. Table 2 has only 4 data points; adding more CLIP architectures (e.g., ResNet‑based) and dataset variations is needed for statistical significance and generalizability.  
  *R2:* Similar call for more architectures and dataset variants.

- **Unclear methodology for disentanglement metrics and image retrieval (R1, R2).**  
  *R1:* The narrative after Sec. 4 is rushed. Terms like “dimensions” (Sec. 4.1) and “switching dimension” are not explained; the conclusion that higher image retrieval accuracy implies more disentangled embeddings is not well‑justified.  
  *R2:* How are domains defined for NMI based on the captions in the generated dataset? More details on NMI measurement needed.  
  *R2:* Fig. 4 – how are disentanglement metrics computed for the image encoder?  
  *R2:* How were captions created for the OOD set (12 000 classes)? The paper mentions 80 captions for ID set but only single relevant captions for OOD set; examples would help.

- **Contradiction in paper (R2).**  
  The paper states that designing a test set with truly different distribution from the training data is challenging, yet later claims to have verified that the generated compositions are not present in several CLIP training datasets.

- **Minority reviewer more critical (R2, score 5).**  
  R2 rated the paper lower (5 vs. 6) and highlighted additional concerns about the missing image‑only baseline controls, insufficient checks on training data for all datasets, and the lack of a causal link between diverse compositions and disentanglement.

- **Presentation issues (R1, R2).**  
  *R1:* Grammar/formatting errors (e.g., missing spaces in Sec. 4; typo “imrpoved” on page 3).  
  *R2:* Citation format is non‑standard ((x) vs. [x]); inline citations should use “et al.” rather than just [x]; a citation for “compositional OOD generalization” would be useful.

- **Unaddressed models (R3).**  
  DINO‑v2 and BEiT‑v2 are mentioned briefly in the introduction but are not analyzed in the rest of the paper.

- **Open‑sourcing and reproducibility (R2).**  
  Codes, models, and datasets should be released to benefit future research; the authors are asked to comment on release plans.
