## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of suppressing undesired content in text-to-image diffusion models. It first analyzes text embeddings and finds that the EOT (padding) tokens are low-rank and carry prior information, including the unwanted content. Based on this, it proposes two lightweight, training-free methods: **soft-weighted regularization** (via SVD on text embeddings to remove negative target information) and **inference-time text embedding optimization (ITO)** with two losses (negative target prompt suppression loss and positive target prompt preservation loss) that align attention maps to preserve desired content and suppress undesired content. The method requires no fine-tuning of the generator or collection of paired images, works on both pixel-space (DeepFloyd-IF) and latent-space (Stable Diffusion) models, and achieves strong results on most metrics against baselines like negative prompting, P2P, and ESD. Reviewers overall rated the paper 6/6, praising its clear logic, interesting analysis, and practical effectiveness, but noted several weaknesses.

### Strengths

- **Interesting analysis** of the information components within text embeddings (especially [EOT] tokens) provides guidance for future T2I-related research (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Soft-weighted regularization** effectively removes negative target information from text embeddings, improving control over undesired content generation (Reviewer 1).
- The method is **more efficient than previous methods**: no need for fine-tuning the generator and no collection of paired images (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). It is adaptable to many applications (Reviewer 3).
- The **design of the positive preservation and negative suppression losses** is intuitive and clear (Reviewer 4).
- The logic of the work is clear, and the exploratory part of the experiment is plentiful (Reviewer 2). Organization and phrasing are easy to understand (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Outstanding results** on most datasets, with precise suppression of undesired content and outperformance on numerical indicators (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns**: Both improvement points (SVD-based regularization and attention map alignment) are existing methods, thus lacking a certain level of novelty (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 1 and 3 also requested that differences from prior work be further highlighted.
- **Computational and efficiency concerns**: Multiple reviewers noted that the paper does not discuss the additional computational overhead. SVD can be computationally expensive for large matrices, and ITO requires gradient back-propagation during inference, making it less efficient than training-free methods like P2P (Reviewers 1, 2). The paper should **list the time and memory consumption ratios** compared to other methods in the comparative experiments (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete ablation and analysis**: Several experiments are missing or insufficient:
  - Reviewer 2 asks: In the first phase, if instead of SVD, the entire matrix is multiplied by an attenuation factor, how would that affect results? This would test whether SVD is necessary.
  - Reviewer 2 asks: From Table 3, the negative target prompt suppression loss seems most significant. What would be the effect if only that loss is used without the other components?
  - Reviewer 1 notes that in Table 1, the proposed method is outperformed by baselines on some metrics (e.g., IFID in real-image editing by negative prompt baseline). The paper should analyze why this occurs and explore trade-offs, especially compared to ESD in generated-image editing.
  - Reviewer 4 is not convinced that the semantics in padding tokens have significant impact, given that padding tokens typically have very small attention scores. The paper should provide more systematic experiments, e.g., a diagram of the padding token impact w.r.t. prompt length using randomly drawn prompts.
  - Reviewer 4 asks: Why use SVD to extract the main semantic component rather than a simple mean of the padding embeddings? The advantage of SVD over mean needs justification.
- **Limitations in applicability**: 
  - Reviewer 3 asks: Is it possible to change one subject to another in one go (e.g., change “toothbrush” in “Girl holding toothbrush” to “pen”)? The paper only demonstrates removal/addition, not direct replacement.
  - Reviewer 4 questions: Cross-attention consists of attention map and value recombination. Even if attention maps are largely suppressed, undesired semantics may still slip through value recombination. Have the authors addressed this?
- **Evaluation clarity**: Reviewer 4 asks whether the CLIPscores in Table 1 are the similarity between images and negative prompts. The metric interpretation needs clarification.
- **Minority lower confidence**: While all reviewers score 6, Reviewer 4 rates **Contribution** as "fair" (2) and is skeptical about the impact of padding tokens, and Reviewer 2 rates **Soundness** as "fair" (2) due to novelty and efficiency concerns. This contrasts with the other reviewers' higher assessments.
