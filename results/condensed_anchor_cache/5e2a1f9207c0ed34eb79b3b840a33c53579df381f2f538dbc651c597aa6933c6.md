## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a novel metric, τJp (τ-Jacobian product), for weight disentanglement in task arithmetic and introduces a regularization method to minimize τJp during fine-tuning. The authors claim causal relationship between τJp and interference in task addition/negation, show that minimizing τJp eliminates coefficient tuning and improves accuracy, and demonstrate robust performance in incremental learning. The method intends to reduce the need for inference-time hyperparameter tuning and leverages publicly available fine-tuned models.

### Strengths
- Provides a comprehensive theoretical and empirical study of the relationship between τJp and task interference; the metric is novel and well-motivated.
- The proposed regularization method is simple, adaptable to different task arithmetic variants, and eliminates the need for tuning inference-time hyperparameters α, a practical advantage.
- Interesting insights on the link between the proposed regularization and weight disentanglement.
- A more efficient implementation of the method is provided for handling larger numbers of tasks (Equation 11).
- The design of τJp is reasonable and interesting; the paper is well-written and easy to follow; experiments are extensive and results sound.
- Success in incremental learning scenarios where future tasks are unknown aligns with real-world applications and demonstrates scalability.
- Addresses an important and timely topic in foundation model utilization and weight disentanglement.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing theoretical depth and clarity**: The derivation of Equation 7 from the weight disentanglement definition is non-trivial and should be explained more clearly. The paper lacks a thorough explanation of the underlying mechanisms through which minimizing τJp leads to improved orthogonality between task vectors and output gradients; more rigorous analysis is needed. The theoretical justification for why τJp regularization outperforms other methods (especially compared to Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2023), which also fine-tunes in the tangent space) is insufficient. One reviewer notes limited novelty, as the approach fundamentally aligns with that prior work.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: The main tables lack recent task arithmetic baselines (e.g., TIES-Merging, AdaMerging) and standard single-task / MTL baselines (though present in the appendix), making it difficult to understand the existing performance gap. The proposed regularization is not compared against other general regularization techniques (e.g., L2 regularization, elastic net, or other task-specific methods), so it is unclear whether its benefits stem from a general regularization effect rather than the specific τJp objective.
- **Computational and practical concerns**: The method requires access to data from all tasks during the fine-tuning phase, which is often unavailable in realistic task arithmetic scenarios and limits practical applicability. Calculating τJp involves multiple Jacobian-vector products, and the paper does not report runtime or resource requirements, making scalability difficult to assess. The fine-tuning procedure contrasts with directly combining independently obtained task vectors; the paper only briefly mentions efficiency.
- **Incomplete experimental validation**: Experiments are limited to image classification tasks; evaluation in other domains (e.g., language tasks) is missing, which would strengthen generality claims. Task analogies are mentioned in the introduction and background but never evaluated, leaving a gap in validation across all claimed task arithmetic operations.
- **Ambiguity in experimental settings**: The notions of "One-shot" and "fine-tuned" are unclear—whether they refer to tuning coefficients α vs. model parameters θ, and whether "fine-tuned" refers to a specific method (e.g., AdaMerging) or general hyperparameter search. This confusion should be clarified.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The authors mention tuning the regularization strength but do not provide sufficient details on selection criteria or sensitivity analysis. The impact of regularization strength on model plasticity and how it may affect fine-tuning performance is not addressed.
- **Minority contrast**: One reviewer (score 8) considers the method simple and adaptable, while other reviewers (scores 5) emphasize limited novelty (alignment with Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2023)) and practical limitations (need for access to all tasks, high computational cost).
