- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 1, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Restricted Strong Convexity (RSC) theory to analyze the optimization of operator networks trained with variational loss for solving elliptic PDEs. It provides convergence guarantees, studies the role of the condition number of the diffusion coefficient \(A\), and proposes an adaptive weight algorithm to prevent \(q_t\) from vanishing. Reviewers agree the topic is important and the connection to preconditioning is valuable, but they express major concerns about incomplete theoretical proofs, lack of novelty for the condition number effect, poor presentation, and insufficient experimental validation that does not adequately support the theoretical claims.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-motivated: it uses a newly introduced optimization framework (RSC) to provide guarantees on a largely unexplored problem (Reviewer 1).
- Paper organization is suitable for the presentation of the results (Reviewer 1).
- It introduces a new theoretical framework for analyzing the optimization of operator networks with variational losses using RSC, giving a clear picture of the impact of the condition number on convergence, which appears to agree with numerical experiments (Reviewer 2).
- The paper proposes an adaptive preconditioning strategy (Reviewer 2).
- The topic is important; there have not been many works analyzing preconditioning for operator learning techniques (Reviewer 3).
- The authors provide some theory and experiments that demonstrate that preconditioning yields better convergence rates and training stability (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete or unsupported theoretical proofs.**  
  - Lemma 3.2: The non‑emptiness of \(Q_q\cap B^{\text{Spec}}_{\rho,\rho_1}\cap B^{\text{Euc}}_{\rho_2}\) is not proved; without it the existence of \(\theta'\) is void and Theorem 3.4 (the main contribution) is invalid (Reviewer 1).  
  - Theorem 3.4: The proof that \(\delta_t<1\) relies on the unsupported claim that “\(\theta_{t+1}\in Q_{q_t}(\theta_t)\)” by definition of \(Q_{q_t}(\theta_t)\); the RSC set proposed differs from Banerjee et al. 2022, so results from that work cannot be applied immediately (Reviewer 1).  
  - The main convergence result Theorem 3.4 is not useful because the convergence rate \(r_t\) depends on \(q_t\), which itself depends on the gradient; one cannot characterize convergence without control over \(r_t\) (Reviewer 3).  
  - The discussion on preventing \(q_t\) from vanishing (lines 432–464) is speculative, with no proof of a uniform lower bound for \(q_t\) – it is just a consequence of the assumption (Reviewer 3).  

- **Notation, presentation, and clarity problems that make the paper extremely difficult to read and underdeveloped.**  
  - Notation conflicts: \(M\) is used for both input feature dimension (line 100) and sample size (equation (6) and later) (Reviewer 1); \(\omega\) is defined as an input feature but later (line 152) called a member of the “parameter space” (Reviewer 1); in line 99 \(\Omega\) is not clearly defined (Reviewers 2 and 3); \(m_0\) is said to be constant \(M\)? (Reviewer 2).  
  - Definition 3.1: \(\bar{\theta}\) should be in \(\mathbb{R}^p\), not \(\mathbb{R}^m\) (Reviewers 1 and 2).  
  - Theorem 3.4: \(q_t\) is not defined (Reviewer 2); assumptions A1 and A2 are not discussed, and the minimization is given as arginf instead of argmin over compact sets where the loss is lower bounded (Reviewer 2).  
  - Numerous typos: delete “through” in line 031; line 108 should say “of all layers”; equation (1) missing “\((\mathbf{x})\)”; line 234 dimension of \(\bar{\theta}\) should be \(p\); fraction error in lines 1019–1024 (Reviewer 1).  
  - Abstract and introduction introduce \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(q_t\) without any explanation (Reviewer 1).  
  - The paper is not self-contained and very difficult to understand without reading Banerjee et al. 2022 (Reviewer 3). It wastes space on formally introducing neural networks while relevant parts are poorly explained (Reviewer 3). Sections 3 and 4 repeat a lot (Reviewer 2).  
  - Equation (1) is not self‑adjoint due to the \(\nabla u\) term (Reviewer 3). The variational formulation in equation (2) drops the boundary condition (Reviewer 2).  
  - “Uniformly elliptic” is not defined (Reviewer 1). A regularity condition on the coefficient function is needed to guarantee a solution (Reviewer 3). \(d\) is not defined in equation (1) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Assumption 1: typo – should be \(\beta_\phi\) instead of \(\beta_\sigma\) (Reviewer 2). Assumption 2: boldening of \(W\) and \(V\) is missing (Reviewer 2).  
  - Line 305: “closely related to the convergence rate” is vague (Reviewer 2). Line 334 has a broken section reference (Reviewers 2 and 3). Line 375: \(\Omega((NM)^2)\) uses \(\Omega\) as the feature space (Reviewer 2). Line 380 lacks a reference for the claim that controlling this is challenging (Reviewer 2).  
  - Beginning of Section 3 lacks motivation and discussion of prior work (Reviewer 3).  
  - Add the Banerjee et al. 2022 citation at line 071 and update to the published ICLR 2023 version (Reviewer 1).  
  - Clarify the difference between “training efficiency” and “training performance” (Reviewer 1).  
  - Specify that \(\phi\) is applied entry‑wise when its argument is a vector (Reviewer 1).  
  - [Minor: some of these issues affect soundness; Reviewer 2 rates Presentation 2, Reviewer 1 rates Presentation 1.]

- **Limited novelty and insight in the theoretical results.**  
  - The effect of the condition number on optimization is well‑known in numerical PDE solvers; the paper provides almost no new insights beyond that (Reviewer 2; also echoed by Reviewer 3).  
  - The paper confuses physics‑informed neural networks (PINNs) with neural operators in the introduction (Reviewer 3).

- **The adaptive weight algorithm is inconsistent with the theory.**  
  - The algorithm can be interpreted as time‑dependent preconditioning, but once introduced, Theorem 3.4 no longer holds because the algorithm is no longer gradient descent; no new theoretical result is provided for the updated algorithm (Reviewer 2).  
  - Algorithm 1 is not written properly – actual calculations are omitted and the gradient descent step itself is never stated (Reviewer 2).  
  - The preconditionings B and C are not mentioned in the main text (Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient and poorly connected numerical experiments.**  
  - No experiments vary the network width \(m\) to test the convergence rate dependence predicted by Theorem 3.4 (Reviewer 1).  
  - Only one experiment is reported in the main text (the appendix shows adaptive weighting does almost no better than other preconditioning strategies) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Only a single relative L2 error is reported; it is unclear whether this is over a test set or a single solution (Reviewer 2).  
  - Table trial errors do not appear to agree with the graphs; training curves do not agree with the original ULGNet (Choi et al. 2023) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Trial A begins at a much larger loss value; it is unclear whether the plotted losses are always \(L^M\) or \(\hat{L}^M\) for some runs (Reviewer 2).  
  - Trials B–E all operate within the same margin of error, especially for convection‑diffusion; adaptive weighting appears to provide only marginal improvement (Reviewer 2).  
  - The training loss curves for Helmholtz are very different from those in the original ULGNet paper (Reviewer 2).  
  - The approach is only compared to ULGNet, not to standard baseline models such as FNOs or DeepONets (Reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 1 raises the question whether simply tuning a learning rate scheduler could achieve similar improvements (Reviewer 3).  
- **Reviewer 2 is significantly more negative overall (score 1)**, highlighting that the paper is “extremely difficult to read, written sloppily, and appears to be significantly underdeveloped,” in contrast to Reviewers 1 and 3 (both score 3) who acknowledge potential value while demanding major revisions.
