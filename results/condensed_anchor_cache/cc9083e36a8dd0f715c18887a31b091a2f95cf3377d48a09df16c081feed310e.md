## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Erase-and-Awaken Training Strategy (EATS), a cooperative game theory-inspired method to improve generalization in single image super-resolution (SISR). EATS randomly erases a neuron filter, then applies an awakening regularization term to restore predictions, theoretically increasing Shapley values for equitable neuron contributions. Experiments on SRResNet and RRDBNet show improvements over baselines and Dropout. Three reviewers assigned a score of 6 (accept or weak accept) and one reviewer (Reviewer 4) assigned 8 (strong accept), indicating a minority more positive assessment. Reviewers generally praised the novel perspective, theoretical grounding, and simplicity, but raised concerns about limited architectural scope, missing analyses (computational cost, loss balancing, ablation clarity), and specific implementation details.

### Strengths
- **Novel perspective**: The cooperative game theory framing for improving SISR generalization is original and provides a new direction beyond data-driven degradation models (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Simple and general**: EATS is a simple, plug-and-play regularization strategy that can be integrated with existing SR networks (SRResNet, RRDBNet) without architectural changes (Reviewer 1,4).
- **Theoretical foundation**: The paper provides a theoretical proof that EATS improves the Shapley value (contribution metric) of neurons, supporting the claim of equitable contributions (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Empirical effectiveness**: EATS consistently outperforms baselines (SRResNet, RRDBNet) and Dropout on various unseen datasets; extensive analyses (feature visualization, channel salience maps, channel correlations) validate the awakening effect (Reviewers 1,2,4). Reviewer 4 especially highlighted the diversity of analyses and comprehensive experiments.
- **Well-written and clear**: The paper is generally well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewer 1,3,4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited architectural evaluation**: Experiments only test on older SISR architectures (SRResNet, RRDBNet from 2017–2018). Results on more recent attention-based models (e.g., HAN, SAN, SwinIR) are missing, which limits the demonstration of EATS's generalizability across diverse architectural families (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear loss balancing and convergence**: The paper does not explain how the awakening regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{awa}$ is balanced with the primary SR loss during training. Without a dynamic balancing strategy, the stability and convergence of the overall training process are questionable (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: EATS requires an additional forward pass per iteration (two networks: $f_\theta$ and $f_{\theta'}$). The paper lacks a detailed analysis of increased training time, FLOPs, and scalability to larger architectures/datasets (Reviewers 2,3). Additionally, it is unclear whether the framework effectively doubles the parameter count (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear ablation on layer/filter ratios**: In the ablation study on the ratio of layers involved, it is not specified how layers are selected (random, shallow-to-deep, etc.). If shallow-to-deep selection is used, an explanation for why more deep layers yield better performance is needed (Reviewer 3). Furthermore, the effect of the number of filters erased per iteration on generalization is not ablated (Reviewer 4).
- **Comparison with Dropout**: Figure 1 claims EATS alleviates co-adaption and produces consistent channel responses, but it is unclear whether Dropout (a standard regularization that also randomly perturbs neurons) achieves a similar effect (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing deep-layer visualization**: Figure 1 only shows channel responses for a shallow layer (2nd block). Visualizations from shallow to deep layers would provide a more complete picture of how the awakening effect propagates through the network (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor typos**: "Shapely" should be "Shapley" (abstract, page 2); "Managa109" should be "Manga109" (Table 2) (Reviewer 1).
