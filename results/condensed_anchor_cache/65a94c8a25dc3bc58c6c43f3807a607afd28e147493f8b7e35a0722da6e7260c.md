## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data distillation pipeline for tabular data that first maps mixed-type features (continuous and categorical) into a unified latent embedding space via a pretrained transformer-based VAE, then applies dataset distillation strategies (distribution matching, attention matching, gradient matching) in that latent space, and finally decodes back to the original tabular space. The method aims to replace conventional synthetic data generators with a more efficient approach that yields competitive privacy and higher downstream performance at a fraction of the compute cost (reported 5× average speedup). Evaluations are conducted on 7 UCI datasets against generative models and non-synthetic distillation baselines.

### Strengths
- The task is well-motivated: distilling tabular datasets for efficient generation is a timely and natural extension of data distillation, with potential practical impact.
- The idea of distilling on the latent space (after VAE encoding) to handle mixed data types is interesting and methodologically novel, despite additional computation overhead.
- The paper proposes three distinct distillation strategies (DM, AM, GM) adapted from image-domain methods, each achieving strong performance, which demonstrates the quality of the approach.
- Experiments consistently show that the proposed pipeline outperforms baselines (generative models and coreset selection) across multiple datasets, while being computationally more efficient (5× faster synthesis on average).
- The method offers data storage efficiency and cost-effectiveness, which is significant for practical applications with limited compute resources.
- Reviewer 3 (score 5) found the method presented well and easy to understand, and noted that the experiments verify successful retention of performance after distillation.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing, presentation, and formatting issues (multiple reviewers).**  
  - Many table and figure references appear as “??” (e.g., Page 6 Lines 317–322).  
  - Figure/table numbering is often incorrect or missing.  
  - Appendix contains incomplete sentences; the paper is not fully polished.  
  - Misuse of \citet and \citep, broken hyperlinks.  
  - Figure 3 is never mentioned in the text.  
  - L209: \(S_{enc}\) is not defined in Sec. 3-2.  
  - Frequent mentions of image-domain techniques obscure the key contribution to tabular data.  
  - Mathematical notation errors: Eq (1) \([t_0,\ldots, t_N] \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\) is dimensionally inconsistent (should be \(D \times (N+1)\) if each \(t_i\) is a column vector).  
- **Insufficient details on methodology and architecture.**  
  - The details of how the distillation strategies are adapted to tabular data are very difficult to understand; mathematical derivations are missing.  
  - The preliminaries lack a formal mathematical definition of dataset distillation.  
  - The architecture of the pretrained transformer autoencoder is not provided anywhere (not even in the appendix) – needed to assess computational overhead.  
  - The term “DPC” appears inconsistently in sections 2, 4.1, and Figure 4 without clear explanation.  
  - The explanation of the three distillation strategies (DM, AM, GM) is insufficient; it is unclear whether all three are classified as feature matching (reviewer question).  
  - The core-set distillation baseline in Figure 4 is not explained; K-means on VAE latent could serve as an informative baseline.  
  - The initialization of the set \(\mathcal{S}\) is not described.  
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation and missing ablations.**  
  - Only 7 datasets are evaluated; selection criteria are unclear (UCI only). More datasets from OpenML should be included to demonstrate generalizability.  
  - All experiments should be repeated multiple times (\(\sim\)10) with standard errors reported.  
  - The paper claims the method considers class imbalance, but no experimental evidence is provided (no visualizations, no F1/AUC scores; only accuracy reported).  
  - Ablation studies are needed on hyperparameter choices (e.g., embedding dimension \(D\), autoencoder hyperparameters, proxy network size).  
  - The pre-processing method (one-hot encoding + linear transformation for numerical features) is taken from Zhang et al. without investigating alternatives like piecewise linear encoding (Gorishniy et al., 2022).  
  - The choice of MLP over the original proxy networks used for image domains is not justified; insights on why MLP works better for tabular data are missing.  
  - Random sampling should be included as a naïve baseline for all models; from Table 3, “Random” already appears strong on many datasets except Adult – this needs discussion.  
  - The effect of using the method to generate multiple distilled datasets (e.g., 10 times) on privacy risk is not considered; Reviewer 6 specifically argues that DP synthetic generators have post-processing immunity while this method accumulates risk.  
- **Privacy claims are insufficiently supported and overclaimed.**  
  - The only privacy metric used is distance-to-closest-record (DCR), which is not well-defined in the paper (main text gives insufficient information, and it is unclear why the authors report a modification rather than distance to the closest sample).  
  - DCR alone may not capture re-identification risks; other metrics (e.g., membership inference attacks) should be included.  
  - Reviewer 1 requests experimental comparison of privacy loss against generative models.  
  - Reviewer 6 strongly disagrees with the claim that the method provides stronger privacy than DP synthetic generators, because DP has post-processing immunity while repeated use of this method weakens privacy protection.  
- **Computational trade-offs and scalability are not addressed.**  
  - Pretraining the transformer autoencoder introduces additional overhead compared to vanilla dataset distillation; the trade-off between computation efficiency and efficacy is not discussed.  
  - The analysis in L413 is unclear; axes in Figure 5 are not labeled; FLOPs required for pre-training, distillation, and parameter search are not reported.  
  - One claimed application is efficient parameter search, but pre-training may cost more than directly searching on the original dataset.  
  - Scalability to extremely large datasets in terms of memory and latency is not evaluated.  
- **Contribution is incremental.**  
  - The main novelty is adapting existing image-domain distillation methods to tabular data, while other components (VAE encoding) are directly from related work (TabSyn).  
  - The technique of using a unified embedding space for mixed data types is common in the literature; the paper reads more as an experimental validation.  
  - There is no theoretical analysis or results from existing literature to support the effectiveness of gradient/distribution matching for condensation.  
- **Reviewer disagreement on presentation**: Reviewer 3 (score 5) found the method well-presented and easy to understand, while other reviewers (scores 3) found it difficult to follow, with multiple formatting errors and missing details. This contrast should be noted, but even Reviewer 3 raised concerns about missing architectural details and trade-offs.
