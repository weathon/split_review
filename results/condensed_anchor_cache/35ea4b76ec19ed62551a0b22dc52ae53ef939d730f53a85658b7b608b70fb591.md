## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents identifiability results for nonlinear latent hierarchical causal models (LHCMs) under relaxed assumptions (no invertibility requirement) and proposes a differentiable causal discovery algorithm that estimates the structure of such models. The method is evaluated on synthetic data and colored MNIST images. Reviewers generally agree the theoretical contributions are novel and the algorithm is a step forward, but raise concerns about experimental scope, baseline choices, lack of downstream task motivation, clarity of assumptions and causal interpretation, and insufficient evaluation to demonstrate practical benefits.

### Strengths
- First work to provide identifiability results for nonlinear latent hierarchical causal models (LHCMs); the proof technique appears correct (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The rank condition is intuitive and the class of graphs is practical (Reviewer 2).
- Novel differentiable DAG learner for LHCMs, enabling differentiable structure learners in latent variable settings (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Estimating Equation 9 using the Donsker-Varadhan representation is novel (Reviewer 1).
- Paper is clearly written, with good intuition for the theory and method (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Strong empirical results on the datasets tested (Reviewer 2). The evaluation includes a downstream classifier trained on learned representations, a valuable step beyond causal discovery metrics alone (Reviewer 3).
- The image experiment (colored MNIST) demonstrates integration of the algorithm into an autoencoder framework, allowing learning on complex, unstructured data (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Synthetic experiments are too limited.** Only four hand-picked structures (Fig. 3) are used; randomly generated DAGs would be more convincing. Only LeakyReLU activations are tested; other nonlinearities (e.g., tanh, sigmoid) should be explored (Reviewer 1). The ground-truth structure selection process is not justified (Reviewer 4). Baselines perform very poorly in Table 1, but no explanation is given (Reviewer 4).
- **Real-data baselines are inadequate.** The only baselines on colored MNIST are VAE and β-VAE, which do not learn any structure over latent variables. More relevant methods exist, such as causalVAE [3] and other causal representation learning approaches (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewers noted that all compared methods come from the same lab (Reviewer 3). Results are not contextualized in the domain generalization literature (Reviewer 2). Only two digits are used, raising concerns about scalability (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation of downstream benefits is insufficient.** Table 2 uses a very limited setting; performance should be reported as plots with training time on the x-axis to account for complexity (Reviewer 3). The claimed advantage of causal representations for generalization/transfer is not demonstrated; larger-scale experiments and more datasets are needed (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (R3) was particularly critical, noting that the evaluation is severely limited and fails to follow best practices from the deep learning / representation learning community.
- **Missing motivation and unclear practical utility.** It is unclear why learning a hierarchical structure on colored MNIST is useful, as there is no true causal structure for that data. No concrete task or application is given for the learned graph (Reviewer 1). The interpretation of the discovered latent graph (Fig. 4 and Table 3) is not discussed; it is unclear whether the latents have a natural hierarchical meaning (Reviewer 2).
- **Identifiability assumptions and causal interpretation require clarification.** The claimed advantage over existing work (assumptions about measured variables having children) is not clearly articulated as a novel contribution (Reviewer 3). Theorem 3 allows variable permutations; this could change d‑separation statements (e.g., Z₂←Z₁→Z₃ vs. Z₁←Z₃→Z₂) and thus alter causal semantics. The authors must clarify how the result preserves causal interpretability (Reviewer 4). The implication of Condition 3 is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Acyclicity and causal structure constraints are not clearly enforced.** It is unclear how the acyclicity constraint and the overall model structure (Condition 1(ii)) are reflected in the objective (Eq. 10). Without this, the method may learn non-causal structural patterns (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details on interventions.** Section L447-453 mentions interventions but lacks specifics: single vs. multi-node, soft vs. hard, intervention values. This hinders reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- **Related work omissions.** The paper does not discuss the connection to causal representation learning and misses key references [1,2] (Reviewer 1). The cited works on differentiable causal discovery are known to not actually perform causal discovery [1,2] (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor issues:** Typo in Equation 8 (mismatched norms), unclear caption for Figure 2c, and need for clarification of the constraint in Equation 8 (Reviewer 1). These should be corrected.
