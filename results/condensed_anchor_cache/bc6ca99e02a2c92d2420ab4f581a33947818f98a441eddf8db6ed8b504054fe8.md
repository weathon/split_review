## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a mini-batch kernel $k$-means algorithm with a dynamic programming subroutine and truncation to reduce per-iteration cost from $O(n^2)$ to $\widetilde{O}(kb^2)$. Theoretical analysis provides a dimension-independent iteration bound under an early stopping condition, and experiments on several datasets claim a 10–100× speedup with minimal quality loss.

### Strengths
- **Dimension-independent iteration bound** (Reviewer 1): The number of iterations under early stopping is dimension-independent, which is an interesting theoretical result.
- **Well-structured and easy to follow** (Reviewer 3): The paper is clearly written.
- **Natural extension of mini-batch $k$-means to the kernelized setting** (Reviewer 3): The adaptation to infinite-dimensional feature spaces is a sensible step forward.
- **Algorithmic contributions** (Reviewer 2): A simple dynamic programming algorithm reduces the cost of mini-batch Lloyd iterations, and truncation further decreases per-iteration time. Experiments implement the algorithm.
- **Contrast in positivity**: Reviewer 3 (score 6) is notably more positive than the other two reviewers (scores 5), especially about the paper’s structure and the extension being natural.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and similarity to prior work** (Reviewers 1 & 2):  
  – The main technical difference from the analysis of Schwartzman (2023) is Lemma 12 vs. Lemma 10, with nearly identical proofs except the final Cauchy–Schwarz step (using $\gamma^2$ vs. $d$). If no other crucial differences exist, the theoretical contribution is insufficient for a top venue.  
  – The core algorithm and learning rate are identical to prior work; the advance beyond the earlier mini-batch scheme is marginal except for the truncation step.  
- **Potential error in Lemma 15** (Reviewer 2): The definition of $\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}$ on line 379 uses $\alpha_j^i = \sqrt{b_j^i / b}$, where $b_j^i$ depends on the sample $B_i$. The claim that $\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}$ is independent of $B_i$ appears to be incorrect. (Reviewer 2 is willing to update their evaluation after clarification.)
- **Weakness of the iteration bound** (Reviewer 2): The early-stopping criterion (terminate when loss decrease is small) is trivially satisfied by any algorithm that outputs unchanged centers in a single iteration. Thus the iteration bound does not provide meaningful theoretical guarantees about convergence speed.
- **Missing details in the analysis** (Reviewer 2):  
  – Lemma 13: where does the probability come from?  
  – Definition of $\alpha^j_i$ should be highlighted.  
- **Unexplained empirical anomalies** (Reviewer 3):  
  – On `mnist_784`, the mini-batch method achieves higher ARI and NMI than full-batch kernel $k$-means—this is counterintuitive and needs explanation.  
  – On the `letter` dataset, the truncated $\beta$-mini-batch version does not show significant speedup in Figure 1 and appears slower in Figure 9 (for $\tau=300$). An explanation is required.  
- **Insufficient experimental analysis of convergence** (Reviewer 2): All experiments use a fixed 200 iterations; it is not shown whether the full-batch method converges faster or whether the mini-batch gap remains small throughout. A more thorough comparison of per-iteration loss trajectories is needed to understand when a clear gap arises.
- **No practical guidance on batch size** (Reviewer 3): The theory gives termination guarantees for specific batch sizes, but real-world recommendations for choosing $b$ are absent.
