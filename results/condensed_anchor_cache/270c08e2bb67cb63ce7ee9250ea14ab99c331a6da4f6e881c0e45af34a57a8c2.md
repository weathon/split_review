## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes m-PhOeNIX (Multi-Physics Operator Network for In-Context Learning), a neural operator that combines local wavelet experts, context gates, and a memory-based ensembling strategy to simultaneously and sequentially learn solution operators for multiple heterogeneous parametric PDEs while avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The model is evaluated on six time-dependent 1D and 2D PDE systems (advection-diffusion, Nagumo-Burgers, Allen-Cahn, heat, wave, etc.) in both simultaneous and sequential learning settings. While the integration of wavelet neural operators, sequential learning, and local ensembling is original, reviewers raised concerns about theoretical justification, computational cost analysis, missing comparisons, and limited validation on synthetic data only.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles the fundamental problem of catastrophic forgetting in neural operators, which is an important and under-addressed issue.
- The integration of sequential learning, wavelet neural operators, and local ensembling via context gates is clear, concise, and original.
- The idea of using local wavelet experts and context gates to create a flexible framework for capturing multi-scale features across multiple physics systems is interesting.
- Comparative experiments are conducted across a broad range of PDEs with varying dimensions (1D and 2D) in both single-task and joint training settings, enhancing transparency of performance.
- The inclusion of a demonstration showing similarities among each PDE dataset is commendable and highlights the model’s ability to generalize across diverse problem sets.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical foundation is insufficiently developed.** No formal analysis justifies the choice of Daubechies wavelets over other families (e.g., Symlets, Coiflets); the implications of vanishing moments and suitability for the target PDEs are not discussed. Furthermore, the rationale for combining the existing architectures is not clearly motivated. *(Reviewer 1 – minority more critical view; other reviewers did not raise this issue.)*
- **Computational cost and scalability are poorly quantified.** Wavelet-based operations are typically more expensive than FFT, yet no runtime or memory benchmarks are provided. There is no profiling of the computational cost of wavelet transforms and context gating, nor analysis of how cost scales with number of experts and input resolution. Memory overhead from multiple experts and context gates is not analyzed; no discussion of optimization techniques (pruning, quantization) to reduce memory footprint. *(Reviewer 1)*
- **Missing baseline to isolate catastrophic forgetting.** A direct comparison is needed between the proposed method and incrementally training a standard neural operator (e.g., a similarly-sized FNO or WNO) on the same sequence of datasets to show that stability is due to the architecture and not simply sufficient model capacity. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Potential performance degradation observed.** In 1D sequential learning (Fig.4), when training on the Allen-Cahn PDE, performance on the Heat PDE appears slightly worse from row 5 column 2 to column 3. This raises concern that the model may not be entirely immune to forgetting; the authors should investigate if this drop is significant and explain the robustness of the method. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Unclear claim about “small initial trajectory”.** The statement that the model “requires a small initial trajectory to learn the time-dependent solution operators” (l516) is confusing—a single time frame does not provide temporal information. The minimum number of time frames required and how they are used to initialize the model should be clarified. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Comparison against existing multiphysics operators (ICON, AVIT) is ambiguous.** It is unclear whether the reported results are from pre-trained models or fine-tuned on each downstream task. For a fair comparison, fine-tuned results should be included (e.g., MPP outperforms FNO on some PDEs in its paper). *(Reviewer 3)*
- **Only validated on synthetic data.** The model has been tested only on synthetic PDE datasets. Real-world datasets from fluid dynamics or materials science are needed to demonstrate applicability and generalization to more complex, higher-dimensional systems. *(Reviewer 1)*
- **No exploration of varying boundary conditions.** The paper does not address how m-PhOeNIX handles different types of boundary conditions (Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin) across tasks, nor does it experiment with changing BCs, limiting practical applicability. *(Reviewer 1)*
- **Foundation model claim is too strong.** Stating that the model “indicat[es] the characteristic of a foundation model” is premature—only one characteristic (no forgetting) is shown; a foundation model should also demonstrate that training on all datasets is beneficial for each, which the results do not necessarily show. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Distance metric between datasets (l327-328) is questionable.** The proposed metric may not correctly measure distance between operators; e.g., the same advection-diffusion operator with completely different, non-overlapping initial conditions could yield a small distance. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Parameter count may not be the best performance metric.** In transformer models like MPP, reducing spatial downsampling can drastically increase performance without changing the number of parameters, so parameter count alone is not a reliable indicator of model quality. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Missing ablation and clarity issues.** (1) How many local wavelet experts were used? Does changing this number affect performance? (2) Why do most 2D PDE cases exhibit lower relative L2 error than 1D cases? Provide intuition. (3) Include model parameters as a column in Table 1 for all models compared. (4) The term “parameter” is used in multiple contexts—rephrase to avoid confusion; add punctuation to long sentences (e.g., the second-to-last sentence in “Problem-specific comparison”). *(Reviewer 3)*
