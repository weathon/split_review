## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses the conflict between conservative critic updates (to avoid overestimation) and optimistic policy exploration (to reduce regret) by proposing Decoupled Actor-Critic (DAC). DAC learns a conservative actor for temporal-difference targets and an optimistic actor for exploration, using an ensemble of critics to compute lower/upper Q-value bounds and an adaptive mechanism for the optimism level. Experiments on DeepMind Control tasks in low and high replay ratio regimes show favorable comparisons against several baselines. Reviewers find the motivation clear and the method well-structured, but identify substantial weaknesses in theoretical grounding, missing ablations, incomplete baseline comparisons, and presentation quality.

### Strengths

- The core motivation—balancing conservative critic updates with optimistic exploration—is interesting, well-posed, and clearly communicated.
- The paper is well-written, structured, and easy to follow.
- Evaluation on 10 seeds with a multitude of baselines demonstrates strong experimental effort and promotes reproducibility.
- The approach of decoupling actors for exploration and stable learning is neat and practically appealing.
- Adaptive adjustment of the optimism level reduces hyperparameter sensitivity compared to prior work (e.g., Optimistic Actor-Critic).
- The method includes extensive experiments that provide useful heuristics for application-oriented tasks.
- Good reproducibility effort is evident from the experimental details provided.

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical and algorithmic justification:**
  - The algorithm is largely built on heuristics without theoretical analysis; a clear objective function (possibly with constraints) for Algorithm 1 is missing, making it hard to assess what is being optimized (R1).
  - Simultaneously maximizing lower and upper Q-value bounds may still squash all action values higher and lead to overestimation; this risk is not analyzed (R1).
  - There is no discussion of where the algorithm would converge to, nor of the feasibility of the non-linear Q-value approximation in Eq. (6) (R1, R3).
  - The gradient computation in Eq. (7) for the optimistic actor appears to propagate through the first two Q functions, which are interdependent with the actor – this needs clarification (R1).
  - The tandem problem of updating a conservative actor with data from an optimistic actor in off-policy learning (Ostrovski et al., 2021) is not addressed (R4).

- **Missing empirical verification:**
  - The paper does not directly verify that DAC mitigates overestimation (e.g., comparing learned Q-values with Monte Carlo returns) – only evaluation returns are reported, which does not isolate the cause of improvement (R1).
  - The claim that DAC is consistently better than SAC/TD3 is a huge claim; no pitfalls or failure cases are disclosed (R4).
  - Figures 2b and 2c are supposed to illustrate critic disagreement but do not clearly convey this; a better explanation or alternative visualization is needed (R3).

- **Incomplete or insufficient baseline comparisons:**
  - No comparison to ensemble-based methods like REDQ is provided, despite REDQ being highly relevant (R4).
  - Missing comparisons to D4PG or (D)MPO as DMC-native baselines (R2).
  - No comparison to exploration-focused baselines, even though the added optimism is essentially an exploration strategy (R1).
  - The Dreamer experiments omit harder tasks such as Quadruped Run and Humanoid Walk (where Dreamer-v2 excels), and the paper does not extend to visual control (R2).
  - DroQ, a recent alternative to REDQ, is not considered (R2).

- **Missing ablation studies:**
  - The impact of ensemble size (number of critics) is not investigated; an ablation or theoretical analysis is required (R1, R2, R3).
  - The necessity of learning the optimism control parameter and the KL divergence weight is not ablated – could intuitive fixed choices work? (R1)
  - The order of the update rules (lines 10–15) and the choice of learning rates are not justified (R1).
  - The effect of the ensemble on baselines (do they use an ensemble too?) is not controlled, complicating comparison (R1).

- **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  - There are multiple typos, missing articles, and broken references (R2).
  - No text points to Figure 1 or Figure 2; many abbreviations are used early but introduced late (R4). Reviewer 4 rated presentation as poor (score 1).
  - Notation is confusing: \(|\mathcal{A}|\) is used for action dimensionality, whereas standard notation would be \(\text{dim}(\mathcal{A})\) (R3).
  - The caption of Table 2 describes the tasks as “10 HARD DMC tasks”, but some (e.g., Pendulum, Cartpole, Cheetah, Quadruped, Fish) are not generally considered hard (R2).
  - The experimental study of replay ratio appears disconnected from the paper’s primary motivation and should be relegated to the appendix (R1).

- **Specific technical and contextual concerns:**
  - The approach of combining exploration with overestimation mitigation is intuitive; the paper does not articulate a clear advantage over a simple baseline that couples an existing exploration method with an overestimation-avoiding algorithm (R1).
  - The maximum average performance metric in Section F.1 is not clearly defined; is the argmax over time? Why average over tasks? (R2)
  - The evaluation results for Fish and Swimmer (Figures 12 & 13) show extremely low variance, which is suspicious for stochastic tasks with random goal placement – need to confirm whether all runs used the same evaluation seed (e.g., same goal) (R2).
  - The low replay ratio regime uses 3 gradient steps per 1 environment step, which is significantly higher than, e.g., Acme’s MPO default of 1 gradient per 8 environment steps; the impact of such choices on results should be discussed (R2).
  - Harder tasks like Humanoid Walk/Run (Figures 12 & 13) have not converged and should be run longer; recent papers also evaluate on the Dog domain (R2).
  - The paper does not discuss how DAC would generalize to policy parameterizations that do not output mean and variance (e.g., implicit policies, normalizing flows) (R4).
  - It is unclear what the backbone policy distribution is and how variance and mean are explicitly modeled in DAC (R4).
