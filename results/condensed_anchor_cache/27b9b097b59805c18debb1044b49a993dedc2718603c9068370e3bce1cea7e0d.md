## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Mask-aware Scaled Prediction Consistency (MSPC) for automatic backdoor data detection without clean data or a fixed threshold. It extends the existing SPC method by adding a learnable mask and a linear shift to address SPC’s fragility near extreme pixel values, then formulates a bi-level optimization that learns both the mask and a binary variable w_i to flag backdoor samples (those with MSPC loss > 0). Experiments on three datasets and eight attacks show improved AUROC over several baselines.

### Strengths
1. The problem of backdoor sample identification is of sufficient interest to the community.
2. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, 2).  (One reviewer notes it is poorly written; see Weaknesses.)
3. The motivation for adding masks and the linear shift τ is clear and makes an interesting observation about applying SPC to real images.
4. Experimental results are promising and comprehensive, covering a wide range of existing attacks (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
5. The method automatically differentiates backdoor from clean samples via the bi-level optimization, eliminating the need for a manually defined threshold (Reviewer 3).
6. Compared to the previous version (earlier conference), the authors expanded datasets and attack baselines, strengthening the empirical support (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty**: The core idea is closely aligned with the existing Scaled Prediction Consistency (SPC) approach; the refinement via bi-level optimization and a learnable mask is incremental rather than a fundamentally new concept (Reviewer 2, 3). The key novelty of introducing a learnable mask is very similar to Cognitive Distillation (CD) (Huang et al., 2023). After relaxing the mask to continuous values, Eq. 4 differs from CD’s loss mainly by replacing absolute difference with KL divergence (Reviewer 3, 4).
2. **Lack of systematic analysis of SPC failures**: The explanation in Section 4 of why SPC fails in two cases is not supported by quantitative analysis and remains conjectural (Reviewer 3).
3. **“Free of detection threshold” is ill-defined**: The method still uses the loss value as a score and optimizes the threshold through Eq. 5; it is an optimized threshold selection rather than truly threshold-free (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 adds: if all samples with MSPC loss > 0 are removed, many clean samples will be removed when the dataset has an extremely low or high poisoning rate.
4. **Unnecessary binary variable w_i**: Since backdoor samples are simply those with MSPC loss > 0, the introduction of w_i appears redundant (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 also suggests that the automatic search with w_i and SPC loss may actually harm detection (pointed out by the worse AUC on CIFAR-10 compared to CD in Table 1).
5. **Marginal AUROC advantage over STRIP, and sometimes worse**: On CIFAR-10 the proposed method significantly outperforms STRIP only under AdapBlend (γ = 0.3%), and on Tiny ImageNet STRIP surpasses the proposed method (Reviewer 1). This raises questions about the method’s practical advantage.
6. **High computational cost**: The bi-level optimization solves a discrete optimization problem for the binary w, yet STRIP only requires superimposing two images and forwarding through the model, making STRIP considerably faster (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 recommends rejection partly because of this overhead. (One reviewer notes the problem is of interest and accepts the contribution; this contrast is retained.)
7. **Insufficient architecture diversity**: Only ResNet-18 is evaluated. The method should be tested on other architectures (e.g., attention-based ViT) to ensure generalizability, as different mechanisms may interact differently with the proposed approach (Reviewer 2).
8. **Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art**: The experiments should include Cognitive Distillation (Huang et al., 2023) (as a direct baseline), Meta-SIFT (Zeng et al., 2022), and ASSET (Pan et al., 2023) (Reviewer 4). On CIFAR-10, the detection performance in Table 1 is worse than that reported in the CD paper (AUC > 90%) (Reviewer 3).
9. **Sensitivity to low poisoning rates**: As observed in SPECTRE (Hayase et al., 2021), detection methods are sensitive to the poisoning rate; experiments with lower poisoning rates are needed (Reviewer 4).
10. **Robustness of mask against adaptive attacks**: Strong adaptive attacks should be aware of the mask and could be designed to have low MSPC loss across multiple surrogate models (bilevel generation), not just a single model as tested (Reviewer 3).
11. **Input range assumption**: The motivation for the mask shift τ relies on the input being bounded to [0,1]. What happens when input is not constrained or is normalized differently? (Reviewer 4)
12. **Writing clarity**: The paper is poorly written in places, making it hard to grasp the core idea quickly; the relationship to SPC and CD should be summarized more accurately and the fundamental technical difference clearly explained (Reviewer 3). (Other reviewers found it well-organized.)

13. **Open questions**:
    - Could Eq. 5 (the threshold optimization) be incorporated with other detection methods like STRIP or ABL? (Reviewer 4)
    - What is the false positive rate (FPR) when there are no backdoor samples in the dataset? (Reviewer 4)
    - Need runtime analysis of the bi-level optimization and the optimization process for discrete w (Reviewer 1).
