- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dreamguider, a training-free guidance method for conditional generation with diffusion models. It avoids backpropagation through the full diffusion network by using zeroth-order guidance on both the Tweedie estimate and the noise/score prediction, introduces an empirical time-varying guidance scale, and includes a differentiable augmentation strategy. Reviewers are split: two (scores 5) find the principled hyperparameter selection and augmentation valuable and see clear improvements over prior work MGD; two (scores 3) consider the contribution limited, the core mechanism insufficiently justified, the experimental validation lacking (no speed comparison, marginal gains, no latent diffusion capability), and the differentiation from MGD unconvincing.

### Strengths
- The problem of reducing computational cost in training-free conditional generation is relevant and important.
- The proposed empirical guidance scale provides a principled way to determine step-size hyperparameters—a critical issue in prior methods that rely on handcrafted tuning. This is considered extremely valuable by reviewer 2.
- Guiding the noise/score prediction directly (instead of only the Tweedie estimate) is seen as a creative and interesting extension.
- The differentiable augmentation strategy is effective and has potential for wider applicability beyond this method.
- Experimental results show clear quantitative and qualitative improvements over MGD on several tasks (reviewer 3: “clear advantage”; reviewer 4: “performance gap looks good”). However, reviewer 1 disputes this as marginal (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient differentiation from MGD.** Multiple reviewers (1,2,3) note Dreamguider is built upon MGD. The claim of handling non-linear inverse problems is not supported by intuition or analysis; reviewer 1 requests a detailed explanation of why guiding through \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t)\) enables non-linear tasks while MGD’s guidance through \(\hat{x}_t\) does not. Reviewer 3 questions whether the extra gradient on \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t)\) provides any new information (it is linked to \(\hat{x}_t\) by Eq. 10) and suggests the method may be reformulable as MGD with different weighting. Reviewer 2 adds that of the four claimed contributions, only the tuning and augmentation are clearly presented.
- **Lack of justification for the core guidance mechanism.** The schedule of applying gradients to both \(\hat{x}_t\) and \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t)\) is not rigorously motivated. Reviewer 2 finds the connection between the schedule and semantic feature formation unconvincing; the effectiveness may simply come from a specific weighting. Reviewer 1 asks for derivation and a detailed comparison of the guidance mechanisms between Dreamguider and MGD.
- **Loss of latent diffusion model capability.** MGD handles latent diffusion models; Dreamguider does not. Reviewer 1 considers this a serious shortcoming and criticizes the paper for merely deferring the issue to future work without analyzing the cause (e.g., gradient vanishing through the VAE). The explanation provided is insufficient.
- **Missing speed comparison with baselines.** Reviewers 3 and 4 both request a speed-versus-performance plot (axes: time vs. metric) for the main experiments (Tables 2–4). The paper only provides a self-ablation speed plot (Figure 6). Reviewer 1 also finds the performance differences marginal and calls for statistical significance tests and a user study to validate the claims.
- **Unconvincing or incomplete experimental validation.** Reviewer 1 describes the showcased results as “not appealing” and the visual comparisons uncompelling, especially for non-linear tasks. The paper lacks a user study and does not report statistical significance. Reviewer 1 specifically requests qualitative results that clearly demonstrate advantages over MGD for non-linear problems.
- **Citation error.** Reviewer 2 notes that in Tables 2 and 3, the reference for MGD incorrectly points to DPS.
- **Applicability to flow-matching models.** Reviewers 3 and 4 ask whether the method can be applied to unconditional flow-matching models—an unexplored direction.
- **Stabilization techniques.** Reviewer 2 asks if the authors have used techniques such as time traveling or repaint to stabilize the sampling process.
