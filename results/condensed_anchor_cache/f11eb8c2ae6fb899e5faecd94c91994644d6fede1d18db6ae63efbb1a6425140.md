## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes InfoBoost, a synthetic time-series generator based on trend, periodic, and noise components. It claims that models trained exclusively on this synthetic data can outperform models trained on real data, and that the synthetic data enables training without needing real data or data statistics. The method is evaluated on unsupervised learning, forecasting, and data imputation tasks across several datasets.

### Strengths
- **Experimental breadth and intriguing results**: The method is tested on a wide variety of datasets (Reviewer 1). The results are intriguing, showing that synthetic data unrelated to the original dataset can boost downstream performance (Reviewer 4).
- **Important goal**: The aim of generating simulated data to aid learning when real data are limited, and doing so in a task-agnostic way, is worthwhile (Reviewer 3).
- **Use of a wide array of signals**: The proposed synthetic data generation incorporates rhythm, noise, and trend components (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and presentation**: The paper is hard to read, poorly structured, and contains many typos and incomplete sentences (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). References and citations do not follow ICLR guidelines (Reviewer 1). Key design choices are not discussed (Reviewer 1). A pseudocode and a dedicated step-by-step description of the method are missing (Reviewer 1). The main text does not clearly state the learning setup and goal; some clarifications are only in the supplementary material (Reviewer 3). The decomposer network structures are absent from the main manuscript (Reviewer 4).
- **Overstated claims**: The claim that the method can guarantee generalization without any real data or task-relevant inductive biases is not justified; no free lunch applies (Reviewer 3). The method is called “non-DL” and “without need for real data”, but Reviewer 1 notes that “no need for real data to train” ≠ non-DL (non-deep-learning), and Reviewer 3 similarly questions why it is called non-DL and how generalization is possible without any data or statistics. Reviewer 4 argues that the method cannot be considered a true data generation approach because it cannot replicate real data distribution without information from real data.
- **Lack of mathematical and methodological rigor**: The separation of rhythm, noise, and trend in Figure 1 is not orthogonal; in practice rhythm contains elements of noise and trend, and noise implicitly contains rhythm and trend (e.g., a random walk drifts over time). A mathematical justification for why these components can be modeled separately is missing (Reviewer 2). The authors do not discuss any guarantees of the simulation algorithm, and the training algorithm is unclear (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 suggests that working in the frequency domain for rhythm (explicitly modifying low-frequency peaks) would be more principled and amenable to certain domains like structural engineering.
- **Insufficient comparison with existing methods**: The method is not compared to STS (Standardized Time Series) or other synthetic time-series generation approaches, including classical frequency-mixing and amplitude-mixing methods (Reviewers 1, 2). The paper does not demonstrate why its overarching approach is superior or provides a uniquely distinct window (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing code and reproducibility**: The source code is not provided, and empirical results lack sufficient detail to be reproducible (Reviewer 1). The empirical results are considered very surprising, making reproducibility even more critical (Reviewer 1).
- **Specific unclearities and missing explanations**:
  * In Equation 2, the notation is not introduced (Reviewer 3). Equation 8 similarly lacks notation (Reviewer 3).
  * Figure 5 has too small and thin fonts; the table is not understandable based on the given explanation (Reviewer 3).
  * Figure 6: It is unclear whether the prediction task is sensible in each domain (Reviewer 3).
  * Figure 7: It is not clear what the figure shows and what can be concluded (Reviewer 3).
  * Section 3.2 says “only needs input data” – what data, and does that imply you need data? (Reviewer 3)
  * Section 4.1 mentions “the ‘sync’ group” and “random real-world subsets” – their precise use is unclear (Reviewer 3).
  * The learning task that the synthetic data is generated for is not stated clearly (Reviewer 3).
  * Generating trends (Section 3.1.3): the generated waveforms appear to differ only in period length – how different is the length? (Reviewer 3)
  * Section 3.2: What are the labels, broadcasting, and interpolation referred to? (Reviewer 3)
  * Page 8: What autoencoding performance is referred to precisely? (Reviewer 3)
  * The link between proposing a frequency-based method and addressing significant variations in the Introduction is weak (Reviewer 4).
  * The possibility of learning simulator parameters with simulation-based inference (SBI) is not explored (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority reviewer’s stronger criticism**: Reviewer 3 (score 1) was substantially more negative than the other reviewers (scores 3,3,3). This reviewer found the claims to be overstated, the writing unclear to the point of not introducing concepts before use, and the fundamental premise (generalization without any real data or inductive biases) implausible.
