## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DeferBad, a two-stage backdoor attack (DABF) that keeps the backdoor dormant during initial deployment and activates it only after the model undergoes fine-tuning on clean data. The goal is to evade detection methods that expect immediate malicious behavior. Reviewers had divergent opinions: one (score 5) considered the paradigm novel and the stealth improvement significant; another (score 3) found limited novelty and incomplete evaluation; a third (score 1) criticized the threat model as unrealistic and the experiments as insufficient.

### Strengths
+ The topic of deferred backdoor activation is timely and relevant.
+ The paper is clearly written, well organized, and easy to follow.
+ The attacker does not need any involvement to activate the backdoor after deployment.
+ Extensive experiments are conducted across various datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, GTSRB, etc.) and neural network architectures.
+ The approach introduces a new paradigm in backdoor attacks, significantly improving stealth by keeping the backdoor dormant initially and activating it only after fine-tuning.

### Weaknesses
+ **Weak threat model:** The attack assumes that the user inspects only the pre-trained model for backdoors, not the final fine-tuned model. In practice, users deploy the final model after fine-tuning and would naturally inspect that model. The proposed threat model does not align with real-world application scenarios.
+ **Insufficient evaluation of distribution shift:** Fine-tuning datasets used (removing some data or using adversarial data) exhibit only a slight distribution shift relative to pre-training data. The method is not tested under more realistic, larger shifts (e.g., fine-tuning an ImageNet pre-trained model on CIFAR-10). This makes it difficult to gauge real-world effectiveness.
+ **Outdated and incomplete defenses:** The backdoor detection and mitigation methods evaluated are mostly from 2019 or earlier. State-of-the-art defenses (e.g., [arXiv:2305.18651], model behavior-based methods such as NeurIPS 2022) are not included. GradCAM is not a backdoor detection or mitigation approach. The paper also does not discuss or propose potential defenses against DABF.
+ **Limited backdoor types tested:** DeferBad was only validated with BadNets and ISSBA attacks. Its generalizability to newer, more sophisticated attacks (e.g., Bpp, WaNet) is not shown, making it hard to conclude that the method works across diverse backdoor triggers.
+ **Lack of theoretical explanation:** The paper claims that selective layer updates create an “unstable equilibrium” that masks the backdoor, but provides no theoretical justification for this phenomenon or for why fine-tuning reliably reactivates the backdoor. The reasoning remains intuitive rather than rigorous.
+ **Contradiction with full-layer fine-tuning claim:** The paper claims DeferBad remains effective under full-layer fine-tuning, but Figure 2 shows a low attack success rate when zero layers are frozen (i.e., full fine-tuning). The backdoor reactivates only when many layers remain frozen.
+ **Variability in attack success rate not explained:** The results show that ASR varies significantly with the number of frozen layers during fine-tuning, but the paper does not discuss why these differences occur or what governs the optimal layer selection.
+ **Limited domain scope:** The experiments are limited to the vision domain; generalizability to other domains (e.g., NLP) is not considered.
+ **Unclear parameter (k) and inconsistencies:** The descriptions of parameter updates and freezing in Lines 283/295 conflict with Lines 333/334. The value of k (number of updated parameters) used in the concealment phase is not specified, and whether the same k is used in both concealment and activation is unclear. If k is the same, this would imply the attacker can control the fine-tuning, which is unrealistic.
+ **Statistical significance:** All reported results must be evaluated across multiple runs to ensure that the observed performance is not due to chance.
+ **Limited novelty compared to existing camouflage backdoor attacks:** Recent literature [1,2,3] has already discussed approaches operating on a similar principle of deferred activation. The related work section does not provide a thorough comparison to these methods, making it hard to position the paper’s contributions relative to prior art.
