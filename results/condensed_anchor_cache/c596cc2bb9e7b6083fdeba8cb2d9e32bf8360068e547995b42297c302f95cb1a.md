## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Seg‑LaneDet, a two‑stage monocular 3D lane detector that first uses a pre‑trained 2D lane detector to obtain instance segmentation maps, then employs a U‑shaped Lifting Module (ULM) to lift the 2D maps to 3D lane coordinates. A hierarchical loss (point‑level, lane‑level, scene‑level) is introduced. Evaluated on OpenLane and Apollo, the method achieves competitive F1 scores, especially in extreme weather and night scenes, but has significantly higher X/Z errors than state‑of‑the‑art methods like LATR (X‑errors: 0.483/0.850 vs. 0.219/0.259; Z‑errors: 0.362/0.745 vs. 0.075/0.104). Reviewers disagree on novelty: one finds the 2D‑to‑3D lifting paradigm innovative and the ULM valuable, while another notes that the combination of 2D lane segmentation and depth estimation has already been proposed by ONCE‑3DLanes/SALAD, and a third criticises the limited novelty from a straightforward UNet‑like module and the missing comparison with SALAD.

### Strengths
- **Innovative 2D‑to‑3D lifting paradigm**: Models 3D lane detection as segmentation‑and‑lifting, using 2D segmentation maps as sole prior for 3D lifting, reducing complexity and computational cost. This is an intriguing direction (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **U‑shaped Lifting Module (ULM)**: Central component that lifts 2D data to 3D by incorporating relative and absolute scale information; considered the most valuable design (Reviewer 1).
- **Hierarchical loss**: Point‑level, lane‑level, and scene‑level losses improve accuracy and consistency; the scene‑level loss shows promising results in ablation (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Extensive empirical testing**: Evaluated on large real‑world datasets (OpenLane, Apollo) under varied conditions (night, extreme weather, curves, intersections). Competitive F1 scores demonstrate robustness (Reviewer 1).
- **Modular and scalable design**: Relies on existing pre‑trained 2D lane detectors, allowing adaptability and integration with advances in 2D detection (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and presentation**: Paper is fluent, figures are illustrative, related work covers necessary topics; pipeline visualization is helpful (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and missing comparison with prior work**: The combination of 2D lane segmentation and depth estimation has already been proposed by ONCE‑3DLanes (SALAD). ONCE‑3DLanes jointly learns segmentation during training, making the proposed two‑stage approach simpler and less comprehensive. Neither ONCE‑3DLanes nor SALAD is adequately discussed or compared (Reviewers 2, 3). A comparison is strongly recommended to position the contribution (Reviewers 2, 3). The reliance on a straightforward UNet‑like module for 3D prediction does not clearly stand out against recent more innovative methods (Reviewer 2).
- **Subpar performance on error metrics**: X and Z errors are much higher than LATR: on OpenLane, X‑errors 0.483/0.850 vs. 0.219/0.259; Z‑errors 0.362/0.745 vs. 0.075/0.104. This raises concerns about real‑world reliability (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 1 and 3 also note performance limitations in precise X/Z accuracy as a known constraint of segmentation‑based approaches.
- **Dependency on pre‑trained 2D detection**: Performance is tied to the 2D segmentation quality; inaccuracies can propagate errors through the 3D lifting, especially in low‑visibility conditions (heavy rain, sharp curves). No analysis of how 2D detection errors affect 3D output, nor methods to make lifting more robust (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 questions why two‑stage instead of end‑to‑end training: first‑stage errors cannot be corrected, and segmentation inaccuracies could harm depth estimation.
- **Lack of analysis on better F1 in extreme conditions**: Table 2 shows better F1 scores in extreme weather and nighttime scenarios, but no explanation for this performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear notation and descriptions**:
  - Notation \(M_p\) in Figure 2 lacks explanation (Reviewer 2).
  - Definition of ASM is unclear: its inputs and outputs are not specified. Line 210 says ASM is shown in Figure 3 but no reference appears; it appears to take a single row of the feature map with same input/output dimensions. Meaning of "absolute scale" is unclear—does it equal depth? (Reviewer 3)
  - Scene‑level loss description (L265‑268) is confusing: "establish a system of linear equations using the 3D points and their corresponding pixel coordinates from the ground truth." Are these pixel coordinates derived from projection using camera parameters? If yes, how is this different from direct projection? If not, how are "accurate" pixel coordinates obtained? (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing analysis of gap to LATR**: No ablation or analysis on key factors contributing to the performance gap between Seg‑LaneDet and LATR (Reviewer 1). Request: if segmentation‑based, explain why it differs from LATR.
- **Missing explanation of UV supervision benefit**: The scene‑level loss uses UV supervision; it remains unclear how this benefits the ULM, whose primary objective is depth estimation (Reviewer 3).
- **Runtime concerns**: As a two‑stage approach, it may be slower than one‑stage models; no runtime or efficiency analysis is provided. Question: any optimizations to mitigate speed disadvantage? (Reviewer 1)
- **Typographical issues**: Table 5 has duplicate table names (Reviewer 1).
