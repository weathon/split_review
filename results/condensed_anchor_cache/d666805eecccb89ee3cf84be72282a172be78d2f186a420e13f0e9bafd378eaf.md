## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Cohesion, a coherence-based diffusion model for long-range dynamics forecasting in chaotic systems like fluid dynamics. It integrates turbulence concepts (Reynolds decomposition) with diffusion models, estimating the coherent part via a reduced-order model (ROM, Koopman operator) and using it as a conditioning prior for denoising. Forecasting is reframed as trajectory planning (inspired by RL/Diffuser), generating the entire sequence in one conditional pass rather than autoregressively. The framework supports temporal composition over subsequences, temporal convolution for consistency, and classifier-free guidance for zero-shot forecasts. Experiments on Kolmogorov Flow and Shallow Water Equation compare against SFNO using RMSE, MAE, MS-SSIM, and spectral divergence.

### Strengths

- **Novel integration of turbulence/diffusion principles.** The combination of ROM-based conditioning with diffusion is a novel approach for multi-scale/chaotic systems (R1, R2, R4, R5). Interpreting the Koopman operator as predicting coherent flow and diffusion as refining the fluctuating component provides a physics-inspired motivation (R5).
- **Non-autoregressive sampling.** Generating entire trajectories in one denoising pass (trajectory planning) offers orders-of-magnitude speedups with little performance loss, providing an intuitive accuracy-computation trade-off (R2, R3, R5).
- **Flexible guidance with ROMs.** Using reconstruction guidance conditioned on autoregressively-produced priors from a compute-efficient ROM is a useful technique, especially when the observation process changes (R3). Conditioning on partially-observed ROM outputs highlights real-world flexibility (R3). Zero-shot conditional sampling is interesting (R2).
- **Wide range of metrics.** Evaluation uses quantitative (RMSE, MAE, MS-SSIM) and physics-based (spectral divergence) metrics, providing strong empirical support (R1, R3, R5). Spectral divergence is important for chaotic systems where point-wise metrics are meaningless at long horizons (R5).
- **Well-motivated problem.** The targeted issues of instability in long-term predictions and computational inefficiency of autoregressive diffusion are both critical (R4). Design choices are promising and experiments exhaustive (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear novelty and insufficient prior work citations.**
  - The approach shares striking similarities with Rozet et al. [1]: Algorithms 1–4 mirror Algorithms 3,4,1,2 from that work; reconstruction guidance and temporal convolution are identical. No clear paragraph distinguishes contributions (R3).
  - Diffusion-based refinement for PDEs has been proposed (citations in Section 2); novelty appears primarily architectural (encoder-operator-decoder) and conceptually small (R5).
  - The Reynolds decomposition (RANS) is extended to a time-dependent setting without discussing theoretical foundation or validity (R4).
  - Appendix B.2 (MS-SSIM) and B.3 are directly copied from Nathaniel et al. [6] without citation; rephrase or cite (R3).
  - Citation formatting incorrect—citations should be in parentheses when not acting as subject/object (R4).

- **Weak baseline and evaluation.**
  - Only SFNO is used. For Kolmogorov Flow (square domain), SFNO (designed for spherical geometry) is unsuitable and potentially misleading (R1, R3, R4, R5).
  - Missing comparisons to: Lippe et al. 2024, Shysheya et al. 2023, Srivastava et al. 2023, Yu et al. 2023, Mardani et al. 2024, PreDiff, DYffusion, Markov Neural Operator (MNO), CNO, UNO, LSM, and an MSE-trained UNet (R1, R2, R3, R5). Bergamin et al. [1] is also relevant for its two-mode sampling (R5).
  - High correlation time should be computed and compared (R3). Provide computational speed comparisons (R3).
  - Performance plots lack error bars, making significance unclear, especially between Cohesion (R=1) vs. (R=T) (R3).

- **Overclaiming.**
  - **Reinforcement Learning:** Claiming RL connection is invalid; only borrowing non-autoregressive generation from Diffuser. No value function or reward function appears, misleading readers (R2, R4).
  - **Zero-shot forecasting:** Conceptually addressed but not validated experimentally (R2).

- **Lack of interpretability and theoretical explanation.**
  - How does coherent flow decomposition promote long-term stability? No theoretical explanation provided (R1). Unclear if predicted conditioning prior actually aligns with coherent flow (e.g., average behavior) (R5).
  - Koopman operator introduction (Lines 253-256) is vague: finite-dimensional approximation not discussed; encoder/decoder domains undefined; $\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{G}_E(\mathcal{X})$ undefined (R4).
  - Explanation of "zero-shot forecasts without classifier" and "multi-scale physical structure" is confusing (R1).

- **Missing ablations and hyperparameter analysis.**
  - Ablation study lacking, e.g., window size W (R2, R3). Appendix C.1 dropout/perturbation values are the lowest tested; unclear if lower would be better—should show convex behavior (R3).

- **Presentation and writing issues.**
  - Abstract has unclear sentences (e.g., "Nonetheless, Cohesion supports..." without advantage; "NFEs" not defined) (R2). Acronyms like NFE should be defined at first use (R4).
  - Section 3 starts with extensive background on score-based models; better to merge with background to distinguish novel aspects (R5).
  - Terminology confusion: "Cohesion" vs. "coherent" are too similar, creating ambiguity (R4). In figures, "cohesion" seems to refer to temporal aggregation, not the whole framework (R4).
  - Figures lack axis labels (Figs 5, 7, 8, 9); x-axis missing in Fig 5; legend small in Fig 8 (R3, R4, R5). Figure 1 confusing (color coding, two trajectories in (b)) (R3). Figure 6 interpretation unclear (spherical projection? signal only top half?) (R5).
  - Numerical results discussed too briefly (only three lines per experiment) (R4).
  - Typos: L091 "spatiotempral", L125 "deterministic priors", L235 should be $\nabla_{\mathbf{u}_k}$ (R3).

- **Time complexity.** No comparison of inference efficiency with other baselines to support speedup claims (R1, R4).

- **Questions overlapping with criticisms (included as weaknesses).**
  - Why use SFNO for non-spherical domains? (R1, R3, R4, R5)
  - Would Cohesion adapt to domains where coherent flow cannot be efficiently approximated by ROM? (R2)
  - How long are the rollouts and how hard to predict? (R2)
  - Could the framework be extended to non-Gaussian likelihood? (R3)
  - How are metrics computed over multiple samples: average or best-of-K? (R5)
  - Provide spectral divergence over time (R5).
