## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Random Orthogonal Subspace Adapter (ROSA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT). The method decomposes weight matrices via SVD into low-rank trainable subspaces and orthogonal fixed subspaces, then iteratively merges learned information while resampling new subspaces. It claims greater expressiveness than LoRA and zero inference overhead. Experiments are conducted on NLU (GLUE with RoBERTa) and NLG (GPT-2) tasks. Reviewers note the method has been published in ICML 23 [1] (Reviewer 1) and that theoretical analysis demonstrates increased expressiveness (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).

### Strengths
- The problem of PEFT is important and the idea is interesting (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Detailed theoretical analysis and formal characterization of the expressivity limitation of LoRA, showing ROSA is more expressive (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Experiments on NLU and NLG tasks with comparisons to LoRA and other PEFT methods (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Ablation studies are reported, justifying the components (SVD initialization, orthogonality, resampling) (Reviewer 2).
- The paper clearly discusses limitations, including the storage requirement after adaptation and restriction to single downstream tasks (Reviewer 2).
- ROSA does not incur large overhead during fine‑tuning (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
**Majority concerns (Reviewers 1 and 2) – Reviewer 3 (score 8) was more positive overall but still agrees on several points:**

- **Lost advantages of LoRA for inference and deployment.** LoRA stores and loads only small task‑specific adapters, enabling flexible plug‑in use. ROSA requires storing the entire adapted model, reducing practicality for multi‑task or multi‑domain settings (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Insufficient experimental validation.** (a) The GLUE benchmark omits the QQP task. (b) Ablation studies use CoLA and MRPC, both small datasets with high variance. (c) Experiments on larger models (e.g., 7B+ scale) are missing, yet PEFT is most relevant for such scales. (d) No real‑world datasets are used to verify the claimed expressive ability; the synthetic data experiment in Section 4.1 is unnecessary given the availability of real regression datasets (Reviewers 1, 3). (e) Testing on computer vision tasks (e.g., Stable Diffusion) is suggested, as LoRA is widely used there (Reviewer 1).
- **Training‑time and convergence concerns.** Table 1 shows that ROSA’s epoch + factorization time (~153 + 4 s) is essentially the same as full fine‑tuning (~157 s). If convergence speed is similar, the training‑time advantage of PEFT is lost. A detailed convergence analysis comparing ROSA with full fine‑tuning and other PEFT methods is needed (Reviewer 2). Figure 2 should include full fine‑tuning for a transparent convergence‑rate comparison (Reviewer 2).
- **Question about synthetic data experiment.** The assumption that LoRA is optimized over “full” weights without pre‑training is not typical for fine‑tuning; the experimental design needs better justification (Reviewer 1).

**Disagreements and minority views:**

- **Novelty and significance.** Reviewer 1 states that the core ROSA method was published in ICML 23 [1] and the improvements in this paper are not significant. Reviewers 2 and 3 did not raise this issue (Reviewer 1).
- **Claim about LoRA’s zero‑initialization being a limitation is disputed.** Reviewer 1 argues that starting from the pre‑trained checkpoint (as in LoRA) preserves model integrity, and both ROSA and LoRA use pre‑trained checkpoints, so this is not a genuine limitation. Reviewer 2 further notes that ROSA updates the entire model, effectively losing the pre‑trained knowledge for other tasks, making it similar to full fine‑tuning and unsuitable for multi‑task adaptation (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Request for additional analysis.** Reviewer 3 asks whether extra steps can be performed before merging to enforce orthogonality (a potential ablation or improvement). Reviewer 2 suggests discussing ROSA as an alternating‑optimization (Alt‑Opt) scheme to aid understanding.
