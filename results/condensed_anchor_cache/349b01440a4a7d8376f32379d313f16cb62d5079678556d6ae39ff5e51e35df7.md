## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes LEGO-Prover, a theorem-proving system for Isabelle that uses LLMs to build a growing library of verified lemmas (skills). The system combines multiple LLM components (Decomposer, Formalizer, Request Solver, Directional Evolution) to decompose problems, propose new lemmas, retrieve relevant lemmas from a growing library, and evolve existing lemmas. Evaluated on miniF2F, LEGO-Prover achieves pass rates of 57.0% (valid) and 50.0% (test) when combining human and model proofs, and 52.4% (valid) using only model-generated proofs. The library learning approach is novel in a mature proof assistant, and the paper provides ablations showing a 3.3% gain from the growing library. However, reviewers raise concerns about the strength of the improvements relative to baselines and ablations, fairness of comparisons (GPT-3 vs ChatGPT), clarity of the complex pipeline, and some presentation issues.

### Strengths

- Addresses a new problem: using a growing library of proven theorems (skills) to enhance theorem proving, analogous to how humans build on prior results. Reviewer 4 notes this is a novel direction not explored in prior LLM-based theorem proving. Reviewer 2 states this is the first time effectiveness of library learning has been shown in a mature proof assistant environment, beyond synthetic settings like DreamCoder.
- Modularity of the approach allows breaking proofs into intermediate steps and building bottom-up from simpler lemmas. (Reviewer 3)
- The use of LLMs to retrieve skills from a growing library effectively solves the issue that the grown library cannot be accessed during training. Reviewer 4 highlights that LLMs act as an oracle telling useful skills, a challenge without general models.
- The ablations are clarifying and helpful (Reviewer 1), and the ablation study indicates the skill library makes a difference (Reviewer 3).
- Analysis of skill usage frequency (verbatim vs. modified) and source (evolver vs. prover) is appreciated. (Reviewer 1)
- The related work is analyzed in depth. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper is relatively well-written with clear explanation of key components and illustrative examples. (Reviewer 2)
- The legos and other icons are nice. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

**Comparison to Baseline and Strength of Improvement**
- The main improvement over the best prior work (Subgoal-Learning) on miniF2F-valid is from 48% to 52.4% (4.4% gain), which is modest given the complexity and additional LLM queries. Reviewer 1 notes this could be improved by simply running more iterations of a simpler approach. Reviewer 3 questions the fairness of comparisons with Thor+expert iteration and Draft, Sketch, and Prove, as those used GPT-3 instead of ChatGPT, making it hard to isolate the method’s contribution from LLM capability differences.
- The abstract’s 48% to 57% improvement is between the baseline (100 attempts) and LEGO-Prover* (200 attempts: 100 human + 100 model). Reviewer 1 finds this misleading, as the combined approach uses twice the attempts. Reviewer 1 also notes missing miniF2F-test results for LEGO-Prover (model proof) in Table 1.
- Reviewer 4 points out that the gap on miniF2F-test is smaller (45.5% to 50.0%) than on valid, and the paper does not discuss this discrepancy.

**Ablation and Library Impact**
- The ablation shows only a 3.3% gain (47.1% to 50.4%) from the skill library on the validation set with 50 attempts. Reviewer 1 argues this is small considering the extra LLM queries (for every 3 problem attempts, 8 library-related attempts). Using those tokens for more problem-solving attempts might close the gap. Reviewer 3 adds that at test time the library gain is only about 1%, and the paper may be overselling the effect.

**Clarity and Readability of the Pipeline**
- The method is complex and hard to understand from the text and figures. Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 all request a high-level algorithmic description or pseudo-code to clarify the interactions between components. Reviewer 2 also suggests pseudo-code. Reviewer 1 notes the top-level loop (100 passes through miniF2F, concurrent library processing) is only explained around page 6 and should be presented earlier. Figure 2 is difficult to understand; Figure 1b does not depict the Request Solver and has unclear flow (Reviewer 1). Figures contain code fragments with small font and insufficient explanation (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 states figures are used as a replacement for text detail, e.g., the exact mechanisms for lemma retrieval and application are not clearly described in the text.
- Several missing or incorrect cross-references (e.g., “Table. ??”, “Figure ??” on pages 5, 8; “Figure 3(b)” should be “Figure 4”). (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- Reviewer 4 notes inconsistent citation formatting (e.g., “Subgoal-Learning Zhao et al. (2023)” should be “Subgoal-Learning (Zhao et al. 2023)”).

**Missing Details and Unaddressed Questions**
- Table 1 has cells marked “-” without explanation or justification. (Reviewer 4)
- The paper splits miniF2F into valid and test, but the method does not need training; Reviewer 4 questions the purpose, though it may be for comparison with prior works.
- Reviewer 4 cannot find in Zhao et al. (2023) the setting of 100 attempts per problem, contrary to the paper’s claim.
- Reviewer 3 asks about the computational cost of building and maintaining the library, which is not discussed.
- Reviewer 1 asks about the ablation: is it with or without the informal proof step? Does it include the autocorrect/sledgehammer? Does it get human or model proofs?
- Reviewer 1 questions how often lemmas are retrieved but not used, and suggests analysis of lemma usage frequency in correct solutions (e.g., histogram). They also note a mistake in Figure 3c: labels for “parameterize” and “identify key concepts” seem swapped.
- Reviewer 2 asks for elaboration on why the evolver can generate beneficial lemmas from problem statements, how “least amount of time” is defined for request selection, and why references are needed in the formalizer.
- Reviewer 2 also notes prior work on template-based lemma conjecturing (arXiv:2212.11151) that is missing from the related work.

**Minority Viewpoints**
- Reviewer 1 is more negative about the incremental gains, calling the comparison and ablation not strong, and the 48% to 57% claim misleading. Reviewer 3 is more critical of the overselling of the library effect and the fairness of baselines, but raised their score after the rebuttal (still lower than the others). Reviewer 4 initially rated 6 but raised to 8 after rebuttal, while maintaining concerns about presentation and missing details.
