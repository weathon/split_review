## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies a hallucination vulnerability in LVLMs: fictitious presupposition questions (FPQs), where questions assume non-existent objects. It introduces VFP-Bench (1,000 samples from CC3M) to evaluate FPQ handling, and proposes Antidote, a synthetic data-driven self-correction method using DPO with original (negative) and self-corrected (positive) responses. Applying Antidote to LLaVA-1.5/1.6 (7B/13B) yields over 50% improvement on VFP-Bench, 1.8–3.3% on POPE, and 30–50% on CHAIR & SHR, while maintaining general performance. Reviewers’ assessments range from score 3 (Reviewer 2, most critical) to score 6 (Reviewer 3, most positive), with intermediate scores of 5 (Reviewers 1 and 4). All reviewers note significant concerns about benchmark construction, missing comparisons, and experimental scope.

### Strengths

- Focuses on a specific, important hallucination type (FPQ) that is well-defined and novel; the paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4).
- VFP-Bench contains a balanced portion of FPQs and true presupposition questions (TPQs), avoiding bias toward over-corrected models (Reviewer 1).
- Antidote’s data synthesis pipeline is comprehensive, using a “factual assessor” to ensure no new hallucinations are introduced (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments cover multiple hallucination benchmarks (POPE, CHAIR, SHR) and general benchmarks (MMBench, MM-Vet), and consider catastrophic forgetting; Antidote shows significant improvement without noticeable forgetting (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Ablations include LoRa vs. full fine-tuning, which is important for understanding the method (Reviewer 3).
- Figures are clear and helpful (Reviewer 2).
- The method improves performance on general ImageQA benchmarks simultaneously (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Benchmark construction details are insufficient.**  
  – Curation criteria for the 1,000 VFP-Bench images from CC3M are unclear (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  – How questions are crafted (automatically vs. manually) is not explained (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
  – The four question types (item, knowledge, scene, activity) have uneven proportions (8.9% to 39.0%), with no justification (Reviewer 1).  
  – The process for selecting object candidates for TPQs and commonly associated objects for FPQs is unspecified (Reviewer 3).  

- **Missing comparisons with closely related prior work.**  
  – The paper does not cite or compare with MADBench (Li et al., 2024), which also studies misleading/deceptive prompts (e.g., asking “What color is the cat?” in a dog image). Reviewer 2 notes this is highly similar and requests discussion and comparison between VFP-Bench and MADBench.  
  – LRV-Instruction (Liu et al., 2023, cited in paper L143) uses paired data via SFT for a similar issue; results on VFP-Bench with LRV should be presented (Reviewer 3).  

- **Limited model and task generalization.**  
  – Antidote is only evaluated on LLaVA 1.5 and 1.6 (7B/13B), which share similar training data. Experiments on other architectures (InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL, MiniCPM-v2.5-8B) are needed, especially given the 1–3 hour training time (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
  – The synthetic data pipeline relies on LLMs and image generators, introducing potential biases (Reviewer 1).  
  – Generalization to out-of-domain tasks (e.g., chart understanding) is questionable, as Antidote’s image generator may not cover such domains (Reviewer 2).  

- **Simple prompting baselines are missing.**  
  – LVLMs can already detect object absence in POPE-style questions (Figure 1). Reformulating FPQs into POPE-type prompts (e.g., “Is there a car? If so, what color?”) might achieve similar improvements without complex training; this baseline should be included (Reviewers 1, 2).  

- **Necessity and effectiveness of the DPO preference optimization.**  
  – It is unclear whether improvements come from the synthetic data itself rather than the self-correction mechanism (Reviewer 1).  
  – Using original responses as negative examples and self-corrected responses as positive examples may be ineffective when the model’s initial accuracy is already high (≥50%). The authors should test on a stronger model like MiniCPM-v2.5-8B (Reviewer 4).  
  – Would supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on corrected responses work as well as DPO? This comparison is missing (Reviewer 2).  
  – Reviewer 3 notes a potential labeling error: positive and negative response assignments in lines 287–288 may be reversed.  

- **Evaluation methodology concerns.**  
  – GPT-4o is used as an evaluator on VFP-Bench, but its judgment accuracy is not validated (Reviewer 1).  

- **Minor issues.**  
  – The paper uses “car” and “wheels” as an example of co-occurring objects, but this example does not appear elsewhere (Reviewer 4).  
  – Co-occurring hallucination candidates are generated by prompting an LLM; no statistical validation shows they truly co-occur with existing objects (Reviewer 4).  
  – A faint “0” in “Large VLMs” in Figure 4 (Reviewer 2).
