## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HG-Adapter, a unified “pre-train, adapter-tuning” framework for heterogeneous graph neural networks. It addresses two limitations of existing prompt-tuning: insufficient adaptation of graph structures and limited labeled data. Dual structure-aware adapters capture homogeneous and heterogeneous structural information; a label-propagated contrastive loss plus two self-supervised losses extend supervision to unlabeled nodes. The authors derive a generalization error bound for prompt-tuning methods and show that HG-Adapter achieves a lower bound. Experiments on four datasets (ACM, DBLP, Aminer, Yelp) for node classification and clustering demonstrate improvements over fine-tuning and prompt-tuning baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novel dual structure-aware adapters** that capture both homogeneous and heterogeneous structural information, enabling task-specific adaptation for pre-trained HGNNs (all reviewers).
- **Effective label augmentation** via label-propagated contrastive loss and two self-supervised losses, with clear improvement shown in the ablation study (Table 2) (R1, R2, R4).
- **Solid theoretical foundation**: the paper derives a unified generalization error bound for existing prompt-tuning methods and proves that the proposed approach achieves a lower bound (R3, R4; however, R1 criticises the practical implications – see Weaknesses). One reviewer (score 8, high confidence) considers this a strong theoretical contribution, while most others (score 6) find the theorems to have limited practical guidance.
- **Two orthogonal solutions** for improving generalization: increasing prompt flexibility/complexity via dual adapters and improving data efficiency via self-supervised label augmentation (R1).
- **Comprehensive experiments** across multiple heterogeneous graph datasets and tasks, demonstrating generalization to different pre-trained HGNN models (R3, R4). The approach adapts to different backbone models (R1, R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The dual adapter design is seen as a straightforward application of existing adapter techniques to node features and graph structure, lacking a novel mechanism for heterogeneous graph specific interaction; the self-supervised losses are standard contrastive and reconstruction losses without significant modification or theoretical justification (R1). Note: R3 considers the dual adapters noteworthy, but most reviewers see limited innovation.
- **Unclear insights for HGNNs and weak theoretical guidance**: The paper does not provide new insights or hypotheses specific to heterogeneous graphs. Theorem 2.3 only proves the existence of an optimal parameter size \(| \bar{P}_M |\) without guidance on how to find or approximate it; Theorem 2.4 relies on a strong assumption that \(|P_A|\) is closer to \(|\bar{P}_M|\) without empirical evidence (R1). The motivation lacks toy examples to demonstrate how the highlighted limitations manifest in practice (R2).
- **Marginal empirical improvements**: As shown in Table 1, improvement over existing prompt-tuning methods (HGPrompt, HetGPT) is less than 1% on three out of four datasets; improvements over HERO are satisfactory only on Aminer (R1, R2). This raises questions about practical significance given the added complexity.
- **Missing ablation and parameter analysis**: An ablation study on each individual adapter and a parameter analysis on adapter size are not provided, but are necessary to understand the contribution of each component and whether performance can be further improved by varying adapter size (R1).
- **Questionable “first dedicated attempt” claim**: HGPrompt (Yu et al., 2024a) also uses a “pre-train, prompt-tuning” paradigm, so the claim of being the first is not appropriate (R2).
- **Noise in propagated labels**: The self-supervised technique cannot guarantee the accuracy of propagated labels. If the propagated labels are too noisy, it is unclear whether the method can still obtain satisfactory performance (R2).
- **Computational expense**: The dual adapter system and multiple losses (label-propagated contrastive loss + two self-supervised losses) may lead to significant increases in training time and memory consumption, especially on large-scale graphs (R4).
- **Limited diversity of graph types**: Experiments focus on academic datasets; validation on other domains (e.g., biological networks, industrial applications) is needed to demonstrate generalization (R4).
- **Scalability not addressed**: No analysis of how the method scales with increasing graph size and complexity (number of nodes/edges) is provided, which is critical for practical applicability to very large graphs (R4).
- **Need clearer explanations**: The implementation details of the dual structure-aware adapters and the label-propagated contrastive loss could be more clearly laid out (R3).
- **Need more detailed comparative analysis**: A more thorough comparison with specific baselines would help the reader understand the relative performance and why improvements are sometimes marginal (R3).
- **Explicit discussion of limitations missing**: The paper lacks a discussion of limitations, particularly for scenarios with highly variable graph structures or low-quality unlabeled data; future work considerations are also absent (R3).
