## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a framework to jointly learn representations from vision and proprioception in reinforcement learning (RL), building on the Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM). It systematically studies combining reconstruction-based and contrastive losses for different sensor modalities. Experiments are conducted on modified DeepMind Control Suite tasks (with distractions/occlusions), a new locomotion suite with egocentric obstacle avoidance, and a mobile manipulation task (OpenCabinetDrawer from ManiSkill2). The work compares model-free and model-based RL baselines across these settings.

### Strengths
- Writing is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments are solid and comprehensive, covering many joint representation designs (CV, CPC, reconstruction), ablations (concat, image-only, state-only), and baselines (model-free, model-based) under varied environment settings (Reviewer 1, 4). Code is provided with good reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- Introduces challenging new benchmarks: VideoBackgrounds, Occlusions, and the egocentric locomotion suite with 6 tasks (Reviewer 3).
- The observation that joint representation learning leads to more efficient RL than simply concatenating sensor data is useful for practitioners (Reviewer 2).
- Extends the RSSM model to multiple observation models to handle multi-modalities (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed framework is straightforward in both comprehension and implementation (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Notation and figure clarity:** Several specific notations are confusing (e.g., “Joint(CV+R)”, “Joint(CPC+R)” – it is unclear that “+R” stands for reward reconstruction) (Reviewer 1). Figures in the main paper (Fig. 2, 3, 4) interleave model-free/model-based and occlusion/locomotion results, making them hard to interpret (Reviewer 1). Bar charts of final performance averaged over settings would be more straightforward than curves (Reviewer 1). Figure 9’s caption incorrectly labels model-based results (Reviewer 1, typo).
- **Results not convincing enough to draw strong conclusions:** The concluding story is hard to digest due to many acronyms and missing baselines (Reviewer 4). For the open cabinet drawer task, the gain from joint representation is unclear (Reviewer 1). Claims such as “CPC approaches have a significant edge over reconstruction” in locomotion are not significant, and model-based locomotion results are missing (Reviewer 1). Many baselines are missing in some tasks (e.g., model-based results for locomotion, model-based baselines for OpenCabinetDrawer) (Reviewer 1, 4). The paper would benefit from separate discussions for model-free vs. model-based, locomotion vs. manipulation, and standard images vs. background changes (Reviewer 1). DMC results are saturated; more diverse domains (e.g., ManiSkill/RLBench/FrankaKitchen) would strengthen the conclusions (Reviewer 1).
- **Contribution viewed as weak by some reviewers:** The extension of RSSMs to model both image and proprioception is straightforward (Reviewer 2). Applying reconstruction loss to proprioception and contrastive loss to images is the current norm – not an exciting surprise (Reviewer 4). Adding more modalities (depth, surface normals, segmentation) and exploring appropriate losses would make the contribution stronger (Reviewer 2, 4). One reviewer (score 8) considered the contribution good, while others rated it fair or weak. This disagreement is noted.
- **Missing comparison and ablation:** Why is there no Joint(CV + CPC) or Joint(CPC + CV) combination? The paper shows distinct advantages for each formulation, so combining both contrastive paradigms is a natural ablation that is entirely overlooked (Reviewer 3). Also, missing comparisons: e.g., DenoisedMDP and DreamerPro – are these baselines using both modalities? (Reviewer 2).
- **Performance on-par in many cases:** The methods Joint(R+R) and Joint(CV+R) perform on-par in many figures (Fig. 8, Fig. 2, Fig. 3) (Reviewer 4). The gap between Joint and Concat in the cabinet task is surprising – Concat performs same as ProprioSAC, and it is unclear if Concat would eventually converge to Joint’s performance given more training (Reviewer 2).
- **Technical limitations and missing analysis:** The formulation in Equation (4) is not mathematically rigorous; if CPC is used to estimate mutual information, the KL part should be factorized differently (Reviewer 1). A thorough analysis of computational overhead introduced by the joint representation (dimensionality, processing) is missing (Reviewer 2). Using a fixed variance for the proprioception decoder in the OpenCabinetDrawer task may limit the model’s ability to adapt to varying uncertainty levels (Reviewer 2). It is unclear how losses for different observation modalities are weighted against each other (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear task and experimental details:** In the figures, task results are seemingly averaged – but tasks within a suite have very different sample complexities. Details on number of seeds, normalization, and how curves are averaged are missing (Reviewer 1). Are model-free results also using reward reconstruction from latent representation? (Reviewer 1). For Fig. 6 (right), are images reconstructed from a separately trained decoder to probe representations? (Reviewer 2). Are the OpenCabinetDrawer results model-free or model-based? Why only a SAC baseline is included? (Reviewer 1).
