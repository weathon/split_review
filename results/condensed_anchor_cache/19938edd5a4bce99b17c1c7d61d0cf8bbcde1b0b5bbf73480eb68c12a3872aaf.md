## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the projection head in contrastive learning from an information-theoretic perspective. It derives lower and upper bounds on I(Y;Z_1) (mutual information between downstream label Y and pre-projector features Z_1) that involve I(Z_1;Z_2) (information gap between pre- and post-projector features). The paper argues the projector should act as an information bottleneck minimizing I(Z_1;Z_2). Based on this, it proposes (1) a mutual-information regularizer estimated via matrix-based Renyi entropy (using normalized feature matrices \hat{Z}_1\hat{Z}_1^\top, \hat{Z}_2\hat{Z}_2^\top) and (2) two structural regularizations (discretized projector, sparse projector with top-k activation, k=0.001d or 0.2d). Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100 with SimCLR and Barlow Twins show 0.26–3.99 accuracy improvements. The paper also shows correlation between the bounds and validation accuracy over training epochs.

### Strengths
- Novel information-theoretic analysis of pre-projector features, viewing the projection head as an information bottleneck that filters task-irrelevant information.
- Proposed modifications (mutual-information regularizer and structural bottlenecking) are well motivated by terms in the derived bounds.
- Clear writing and easy to follow.
- Direct minimization of I(Z_1;Z_2) as a regularizer improves downstream accuracy, supporting the theoretical claim.
- Cross-comparison of multiple regularization types (mutual-information vs. structural) validates consistent improvement over standard contrastive loss.
- Provides an explanation for why aggressive bottlenecking between representation and projection layer is effective.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical foundation:** The lower and upper bounds are not tight (Fig. 2) and mix multiple terms (I(Z_1;R), I(Y;Z_2) in addition to I(Z_1;Z_2)). The argument that the projection head’s role is solely via I(Z_1;Z_2) remains unconvincing without experiments that fix other terms. Reviewer 4 requests explicit toy examples linking downstream accuracy to mutual information. Reviewer 4 notes the bound could be problematic for continuous Z_1 (Thm 3.2 assumes H(Z_1|Y,Z_2)≥0, which is not guaranteed). Reviewer 2 also questions whether the data processing inequality argument sufficiently justifies compression, since many SSL objectives maximize I(X;Z_2) and compression may discard useful information.
- **Empirical support gaps:**
  - Correlation between bounds and accuracy is weak: only shown for SimCLR on CIFAR-100 (Fig. 2), with a small number of epochs, and no correlational results for Barlow Twins or other datasets. The lower bound estimate in Fig. 2a is non-monotonic (peak at 80 epochs) while accuracy increases monotonically through 100 epochs, contradicting the claim that the lower bound “continuously increase[s]” (line 264). Reviewer 2 notes many quantities increase during training without explanatory power.
  - Experiments in Fig. 3 only show one training setting; it is unclear whether trends hold across hyperparameters (learning rate, weight decay, augmentation strength, contrastive temperature) and across Barlow Twins.
  - The two “structural” regularizers (discretized and sparse projector) were already known to improve downstream performance, so the paper does not demonstrate that they improve for the stated reason (I(Z_1;Z_2) minimization) rather than alternative hypotheses. The direct mutual-information regularizer is less effective than structural bottlenecks, suggesting the estimation may be poor; Reviewer 2 suggests a variational bound might work better.
  - Nonlinear evaluation (more powerful classifier) is not used, so it is unclear if performance is bottlenecked by information in the representation or by the classifier.
  - No results combining multiple proposed modifications to see if further improvements occur.
  - Figure 4 should compare methods across more datasets to check rank consistency; instead of plotting bounds vs. accuracy, plotting accuracy directly vs. I(Z_1;Z_2) would be more insightful.
- **Minor issues:** Typo “pipline” (line 152). Fig. 1 could show Z_1 used in downstream tasks. The choice α=2 for the matrix-based mutual information (line 250) is not justified; α=1 would correspond to Shannon mutual information. The impact/motivation of α is unclear. The corner case Z_1=Y (infinite mutual information) is not handled.
- **Missing comparisons:** Several related papers on the projection head are listed (Saunshi et al. 2022, Gui et al. 2023, Wen & Li 2022, Xue et al. 2024) and not discussed.
- **Application scope:** Whether the analysis translates to supervised learning (features before last layer) is not addressed.
- **Contrast among reviewers:** Reviewer 2 (score 8) finds the theoretical explanation plausible despite weaknesses, while Reviewer 4 (score 5) considers the theory insufficient and the experiments unclear. Reviewer 1 (score 6) says the theory is not strong enough to be a major contribution, so empirical results must be strong, but they are not convincingly tied to the bounds.
