## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the low true positive rate (TPR) of Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) under strict false positive rate (FPR) constraints by modifying upstream stages: (1) reducing the dropout rate during dataset partitioning to mitigate sampling bias, and (2) restricting attack aggregation to only the most vulnerable (outlier) data points instead of the entire dataset. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-Imagenet show that these modifications boost TPR at very low FPRs and achieve near-perfect AUC, across various attack strategies. However, a minority reviewer (Reviewer 3) strongly contests the validity of the threat model and the interpretation of MIA objectives.

### Strengths
- The paper provides interesting experimental investigations into how increasing the member/non-member ratio (via lower dropout) and subselecting vulnerable records improves evaluated MIA performance. (Reviewer 2)
- The discovery that the shadow model should approach the leave-one-out ideal state (lower dropout → higher TPR) is meaningful and intriguing. (Reviewer 1)
- Novel experimental observation that outlier detection methods like ApB can effectively identify data records with high information leakage. (Reviewer 2)
- Impressive experimental results: substantial boost in AUC; notable increases in TPR at incredibly low FPRs (even zero). (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The paper is well-structured and accessible. (Reviewer 1)
- Thoroughly evaluates challenges (sampling bias, attack aggregation) across multiple datasets. (Reviewer 4)
- Benchmarks proposed modifications against state-of-the-art methods (LiRA, RMIA) on various datasets. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Threat model & comparability**: One reviewer (Reviewer 3) makes a strong criticism: lowering the dropout rate creates extensive overlap between shadow model training sets and the target model’s training set. (In Carlini et al., 2022 and Zarifzadeh et al., 2024, each point appears in exactly half the reference models; reducing dropout artificially inflates success because shadow models inadvertently use data closer to the target model’s dataset.) This makes the paper’s claims less meaningful for practical scenarios where such overlap is improbable. The minority reviewer argues that the evaluation results are unrealistic and unsurprisingly outperform baselines. (Reviewer 3) Other reviewers also question the significance of performance under the modified sampling/selection schemes and whether comparisons with prior works are fair. (Reviewer 2, 1)
- **Objective of MIA**: The same reviewer (Reviewer 3) notes that the paper misunderstands MIA’s goal: MIA seeks to quantify privacy leakage across all available training data, not just the most vulnerable points. The claim that recent work advocates evaluating only vulnerable points is incorrect; recent work emphasizes improving low-FPR metrics, not selective evaluation. (Reviewer 3) Other reviewers agree that justification for focusing exclusively on outliers is insufficient, and the trade-off between targeting outliers and assessing overall privacy leakage is unclear. (Reviewer 4, 1) The paper does not provide MIA performance on non-outlier (inlier) samples to clarify this. (Reviewer 4)
- **Feasibility & robustness**: The paper lacks an in-depth exploration of why low dropout rate benefits selection of the most vulnerable samples, and how the method behaves under distribution shifts or data augmentation. (Reviewer 4) No comparison with defense mechanisms (e.g., differential privacy) under similar FPR conditions is provided, reducing actionability. (Reviewer 4) The relationship between dropout rate and AUC may depend on the dataset size vs. shadow pool size (e.g., 10k samples vs. 50k pool) – this is not addressed. (Reviewer 1) Additionally, the method relies entirely on the accuracy of the outlier detection method ApB; handling of missed or incorrectly identified points is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Novelty**: The novelty is unclear; privacy research on outliers has been extensively explored (e.g., “Membership Inference Attacks from First Principles”). If the approach is primarily filtering these samples, novelty is incremental. (Reviewer 1) The claim that evaluating average accuracy over the entire dataset is a flaw is debatable – it may simply be an alternative display of results, not a true improvement. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing experimental details & reproducibility**: Experimental setup is insufficiently described: exact architecture of ResNet-18, loss function, optimization algorithm (learning rate schedule, batch size) are not detailed for CIFAR-100 and Tiny-Imagenet. (Reviewer 4) Implementation details of baselines (RMIA, LiRA) are missing, making reproduction difficult. (Reviewer 4) Settings for shadow model pool size and victim model training data size are unclear; ablation studies on these parameters are needed. (Reviewer 1) The reduction in partitions claimed as an optimization is not clearly quantified; experiments do not explicitly demonstrate this benefit. (Reviewer 1) Code is not released. (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation issues**: The appendix is rough, with figures lacking captions and Table 4 missing all borders. (Reviewer 1) Citation style should be corrected (use \citet{} vs. \citep{}). (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational cost**: The additional cost of outlier detection (ApB) versus the savings from reduced partitions is not quantitatively assessed; scaling analysis with model complexity is missing. (Reviewer 4)
- **Questions that overlap with weaknesses/criticisms**:
  - How does reducing dropout rate affect the number of positive vs. negative samples for a given point, and its impact on TPR/FPR? (Reviewer 1)
  - What are the exact equations for computing MIA performance (accuracy, TPR, FPR, AUC) under the modified sampling/selection? How many member/non-member instances exist? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is the TPR in Table 3 computed over the entire training dataset or only the selected outlier subset? (If the latter, a naive strategy that always guesses “member” for non-outliers and “non-member” for outliers could achieve 0.95 TPR at zero FPR because outliers are 5% of the data.) (Reviewer 2)
  - Does the proposed partitioning strategy impact MIA results for inliers? (Reviewer 4)
  - Could the authors comment on the significance of evaluated MIA performance under modified schemes, e.g., by relating to a DP lower bound? (Reviewer 2)
