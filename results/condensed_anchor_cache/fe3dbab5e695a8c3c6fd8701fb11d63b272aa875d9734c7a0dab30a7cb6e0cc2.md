## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes "structured initialization" for Vision Transformers (ViTs) to improve performance on small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN) by reinterpreting the architectural inductive bias of CNNs as an initialization bias. The method initializes attention maps to mimic random impulse convolutional filters, using an optimization process (up to 10,000 Adam iterations) based on pseudo-inputs from positional encodings. The approach includes special designs (more heads, various conv kernel sizes). Reviewers agree the method is theoretically grounded and shows consistent improvements on small datasets but note that the improvement on ImageNet-1K is marginal. Three reviewers scored the paper 5, 5, and 3, with the third being substantially more critical of the core analogy, computational cost, and lack of thorough analysis.

### Strengths

- **Theoretical foundation:** Solid theoretical analysis connecting structured initialization to CNN inductive bias, rather than relying solely on empirical results (R1, R2).
- **Performance improvements on small datasets:** Significant gains on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN compared to conventional ViT initialization (R1, R3).
- **Interesting viewpoint:** By initializing attention maps to mimic CNNs, the method provides a novel way to introduce inductive bias into ViTs while maintaining flexibility for large-scale use (R2).
- **Scalability and flexibility:** Achieves good performance on both small and large (ImageNet-1K) datasets, demonstrating that the approach does not sacrifice large-scale capability (R3).
- **Special designs:** Incorporation of multiple heads and various initialization conv kernel sizes (3x3, 5x5) (R2).
- **Paper is easy to follow (R2).**

### Weaknesses

- **Core analogy may be overly simplistic and contradictory to attention mechanisms:** The method forces attention maps to mimic convolutional filters, which are spatially local and fixed, whereas attention is designed for flexible, dynamic global relationships. This appears to go against the original intent of Transformers (minimizing inductive bias). The paper insufficiently justifies why structured initialization is preferred over simply incorporating convolutional blocks (hybrid architectures) or using transfer learning from large-scale pre-trained ViTs (R1, R2, R3).
- **Impact on long-range dependencies and representation learning:** Imposing a rigid, convolution-like structure at initialization may limit the model's ability to learn long-range dependencies. Figure 6 shows attention maps remain localized even after training. The paper lacks quantitative validation (e.g., Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) similarity) to show whether this localized attention limits representation quality or learning ability (R2, R3).
- **Inconsistency between convolution filters and softmax outputs:** Convolution filters can have both positive and negative values, but the softmax in attention produces only non-negative outputs. The authors acknowledge the need for positive filters but do not explore how this affects the quality, flexibility, or expressivity of the learned attention maps (R3).
- **Pseudo-input bias and lack of analysis:** The optimization uses pseudo-inputs generated from positional encodings rather than actual data. This may introduce an unwanted bias; the paper does not explore different choices of pseudo-inputs or whether using real training data would be better. The fixed structure of positional encodings could interfere with tasks requiring permutation invariance (R3).
- **Substantial computational overhead without cost–benefit discussion:** The initialization optimization (up to 10,000 Adam iterations) is far more expensive than standard initialization methods. Yet the paper provides no discussion of computational cost versus benefit, especially since improvements on large datasets (ImageNet) are marginal. The method also requires selecting the optimizer, conv kernel size, and other hyperparameters, making it impractical (R2, R3).
- **Limited experimental scope:** Experiments are only on classification with one ViT backbone. Missing comparisons on DeiT or Swin-Transformer, and on detection or segmentation tasks, which would test versatility and generalization (R1, R2).
- **Marginal improvement on large datasets:** The gains on ImageNet are small, suggesting limited practical benefit in scenarios where large datasets are available (R2, R3).
- **Unjustified hyperparameter choices and lack of sensitivity analysis:** The method relies on hyperparameters (filter size, number of optimization iterations) that are not adequately justified. No thorough analysis of how variations affect results, undermining claims of robustness (R3).
- **Why impulse filters?** The reasoning behind choosing impulse filters over other structured filters is not sufficiently explained. Could other filter types provide better generalization? (R3) (Reviewer question that overlaps with a missing comparison/analysis.)
- **Long-term effects on deeper layers and convergence:** The paper does not discuss how the constraints from impulse filters affect deeper layers after fine-tuning or the overall convergence. Figure 3 suggests some effect, but analysis is missing (R3) (Reviewer question that overlaps with missing discussion.)
- **Missing details on initialization of other components:** The paper does not specify how feed-forward network (FFN) layers, normalization layers, and projection layers are initialized, nor why structured initialization is not applied to the value (V) component of self-attention (R1) (Reviewer questions that overlap with missing details/ablation.)
