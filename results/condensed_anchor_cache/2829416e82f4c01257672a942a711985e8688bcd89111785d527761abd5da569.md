## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper treats human annotator simulation (HAS) as a zero-shot density estimation problem, proposing a meta-learning framework with latent variable models. It introduces conditional integer flows and conditional softmax flows for ordinal and categorical annotations. Evaluations are performed on three real-world tasks: emotion recognition (MSP-Podcast), toxic speech detection (Jigsaw), and speech quality assessment (NISQA). Reviewers acknowledge the novel perspective of modeling label distributions and the ability to capture annotator variability, but raise concerns about quantitative performance compared to baselines, limited task diversity, missing comparisons with recent state-of-the-art methods, incomplete evaluation protocol, lack of robustness analysis, and missing discussions on practical applicability (e.g., single-annotation scenarios, zero-shot title). Scores range from 5 to 6.

### Strengths
- **Novel formulation:** Treating HAS as a density estimation problem captures annotator variability and bias, unlike majority-vote approaches. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Qualitative evidence:** Figures 1-2 show the proposed CNF predictions lie near sample means and exhibit realistic variability, better reflecting inter-annotator disagreements. (R1)
- **Model flexibility:** The use of a conditional normalizing flow (CNF) with an additional latent variable \(v\) provides flexibility beyond fixed distribution families. (R2)
- **New model classes:** Conditional integer flows and conditional softmax flows are introduced to handle ordinal and categorical annotations respectively. (R2, R3)
- **Evaluation across modalities:** The method is tested on three real-world human evaluation tasks from different domains (speech emotion, toxic text, speech quality). (R2, R3, R4)
- **Efficiency over ensemble:** The proposed method is much more efficient than the deep ensemble, which achieves the best performance in Tables 1 and 2. (R3)
- **Open-source code:** Training code is available to reproduce results. (R3)
- **Clear writing and sensible idea:** The paper is well-organized and the meta-learning approach for zero-shot unlabeled data is sensible. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient quantitative performance:** Ensemble (Tables 1,2) and Gaussian Process (Table 3) often outperform the proposed CNF on standard accuracy metrics, despite CNF’s better distribution matching. The paper does not explicitly identify and show improved performance on biased or majority-wrong samples. (R1)
- **Missing comparison to recent state-of-the-art:** Baselines (Ensemble, MCDP, BBB, CVAE, A-CNF, DPN, GP, EDL) do not include methods from the last two years; e.g., A-CNF (2021) is the most recent. The field may have advanced since. (R3, R4)
- **No zero-shot experiments as per title:** The title “zero-shot density estimation” implies generalization to unseen datasets or classes, but all experiments train and test on the same dataset. (R1)
- **Evaluation protocol not aligned with HAS goal:** The evaluation is supervised (test set predictions) rather than simulating annotations for classifier training: (i) train on labeled subset, (ii) generate simulated labels for held-out subset, (iii) train a classifier on simulated labels, (iv) test on true test set. (R2)
- **Limited task diversity:** Only three tasks; evaluations on more diverse annotation complexities (e.g., fine-grained, context-dependent) are needed to assess generalizability. (R3)
- **Lack of robustness analysis:** Sensitivity to hyperparameters, training data size, and noise levels (e.g., imbalanced data) is not studied. For imbalanced emotion categories (e.g., MSP-Podcast: angry vs neutral), the method may yield higher uncertainty in sparse regions. (R3; also R2)
- **Missing metric justification:** For continuous labels (e.g., valence-arousal), why use RMSE and absolute error of average standard deviations instead of the standard Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC)? (R2)
- **Motivation of additional latent variable \(v\):** It is unclear what conceptual role \(v\) plays beyond representational capacity, given \(p(y|z)\) already encodes annotator variability through \(z\). (R1)
- **Separate treatment of ordinal vs categorical not clearly motivated:** Standard classification often does not enforce ordering constraints; the paper could explore continuous annotation cases (e.g., speech annotations) as a toy experiment. (R1)
- **No extension to single-annotation scenarios:** Real-world datasets often have one label per sample, but the method requires multiple annotations. The paper does not discuss how to adapt or compare with noisy-label filtering methods. (R1)
- **Chicken-and-egg problem:** Most real-world labeling does not provide multiple annotations per sample, yet the method needs them for training—limiting practical deployment. (R1)
- **Time complexity not reported:** Training and inference times compared to baselines are missing; the claimed efficiency of CNF over ensemble is not quantified. (R4)
- **Missing discussion of closely related prior work:** The iterative latent label modeling of (Harrison et al., 2020; Sanborn & Griffiths, 2007) is highly relevant and should be discussed. (R2)
- **Differing use of “zero-shot”:** R1 questions the title’s relevance as no cross-dataset inference is shown; R2’s description (zero-shot for unlabeled samples) is narrower. (R1)
