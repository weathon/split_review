## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces BigCodeBench, a code generation benchmark consisting of 1,140 Python tasks that require diverse function calls from 139 libraries across 7 domains and following complex instructions. It has two variants: BigCodeBench-Complete (code completion from structured docstrings) and BigCodeBench-Instruct (programming tasks from natural language instructions). The benchmark was constructed using a semi-automated LLM-human collaboration pipeline. Evaluations of 60 state-of-the-art LLMs show top scores around 60% Pass@1, far below the human performance of 97%. Reviewers agree the benchmark is valuable and addresses an important gap, but two reviewers (8/10) express concerns about incremental technical contribution and data contamination risks, while the other two (10/10) praise its originality and thoroughness despite noting several missing analyses and minor presentation issues.

### Strengths

- Introduces a challenging benchmark that incorporates diverse function calls as tools and complex instruction following, going beyond existing benchmarks like HumanEval.  
- Demonstrates that current LLMs achieve at most ~60% Pass@1, with human performance at 97%, providing a clear gap for future improvement (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- Covers 139 libraries from 7 domains, offering broader library usage than prior function-calling benchmarks. The task cyclomatic complexity and number of function calls exceed those of comparable benchmarks (Reviewer 3).  
- The benchmark construction pipeline follows best practices: seed selection, cleaning, filtering, LLM-based generation with human feedback, and sandboxed code execution for quality assurance (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- Extensive evaluation across 60 LLMs (BigCodeBench-Complete) and 35 instruction-tuned LLMs (BigCodeBench-Instruct) adds credibility (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- The paper is well-structured and clearly explains the benchmark construction and evaluation processes. Limitations and future work are discussed in the appendix (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- The benchmark will be open-sourced, making it a significant resource for the community and highlighting directions for training data development (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- One reviewer (4) commends the "thorough analysis and structured approach" and detailed literature review, demonstrating the benchmark's effectiveness.  
- Another reviewer (3) calls the construction a "remarkable example of a perfect balance between LLM automation and human curation."

### Weaknesses

- Two reviewers (1 & 2) consider the technical contribution of the benchmark curation framework incremental, as the LLM-human collaborative approach is already widely adopted in LLM research (Reviewer 1).  
- **Data contamination risk**: Reviewers (2, 4) point out that the benchmark could become contaminated if included in training data. Reviewer 2 suggests releasing only the validation set and keeping the test set hidden (as done in NL2SQL benchmarks). Reviewer 4 asks for analysis on how the benchmark mitigates contamination (e.g., via rephrasing or variation in query structuring).  
- **Missing analysis of LLM failure modes**: Reviewer 4 notes that the paper lacks an analysis of *why* LLMs fail on the new benchmark (e.g., categorized failure examples). The paper should also compare against how an expert human performs, to establish the scope for baseline improvement (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing comparisons**: Reviewer 4 suggests comparing Table 3 against additional datasets such as MBPP Plus, NaturalCodeBench, and EvalPlus.  
- **Robustness to natural language variation**: Reviewer 4 observes that BigCodeBench-Instruct queries are well-structured; the paper does not analyze performance under NL ambiguity or variation in instruction format.  
- **Version incompatibility of libraries**: Reviewer 1 notes that rapid evolution/versioning of Python libraries can cause compatibility issues, and the authors should propose mitigation methods.  
- **Missing test case examples for visualization tasks**: Reviewer 2 requests examples of how test cases are constructed for tasks returning graph outputs (e.g., visualization).  
- **Missing detail on observation distillation**: Reviewer 3 asks how the paper distills observations such as "instruction-tuned LLMs omit essential import statements"—whether via static analysis of error logs or human involvement.  
- **Missing definition of "calibrated scores"**: Reviewer 4 calls for a definition in the description.  
- **Minor presentation issues**:  
  - Typo in line 182: "refractor" should be "refactor" (Reviewer 3).  
  - Line 300: "PL: Programming Language" is unnecessary in the table context (Reviewer 4).  
  - Table 1, Tool Statistics: # Call column should have all '/' aligned (Reviewer 4).  
  - Figure 6 could be more visually summarized (e.g., with a colored scatter plot by LLM parameter size) (Reviewer 4).  

*Note: Two reviewers (3 & 4) rated the paper highly (10/10) and considered it thorough, yet still identified the above missing analyses and minor flaws. The more critical reviewers (1 & 2, 8/10) focused on incremental contribution and contamination risk.*
