## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces GRAFT, a method to train vision-language models (VLMs) for remote-sensing images without textual annotations. The key idea is to align satellite image representations with ground-level internet image representations (from Flickr) in the CLIP embedding space, using co-located paired images. Two versions are trained: Sentinel-2 (10m resolution) and NAIP (1m resolution). The resulting models enable zero-shot, open-vocabulary classification, retrieval, segmentation, and visual question answering. Two reviewers rated the paper highly (8) and two moderately (6), reflecting disagreement on novelty and scope.

### Strengths
- **Novel alignment idea**: Using ground images as an intermediate representation to connect satellite images and language is original and promising for training a genuine remote-sensing CLIP model (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Large-scale data**: Authors collected nearly ten million images for pretraining, and the dataset is curated for practical use (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Strong experimental results**: The method achieves superior performance on multiple downstream tasks (zero-shot classification, retrieval, segmentation, VQA), outperforming supervised VLMs with gains up to 20% for classification and 80% for segmentation (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Sound design for 1-to-N mapping**: The paper develops both image-level and pixel-level models using two contrastive losses to address the one-to-many relationship between a satellite image and its co-located ground images (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical applications**: Demonstrated on annotation and question-answering capabilities (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Code/dataset not available**: Neither code nor dataset are released, hindering reproducibility and soundness verification (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited to RGB bands**: Training only on RGB satellite imagery significantly reduces applicability in agriculture, earth, and biological sciences (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing temporal alignment**: Unclear whether ground and satellite imagery are matched by timestamp, which is needed to monitor near-real-time changes or account for seasonality (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Geolocation accuracy**: The paper claims “accurate geo-location” but does not measure or verify accuracy or account for typical geotagging errors (~8m) (Reviewer 1, question).
- **OpenStreetMap feature age**: Section 3.4 uses OSM features without checking how old the features are (Reviewer 1, question).
- **No discussion of ethics**: No measures are proposed to avoid revealing personal details about households/properties visible in 1m satellite imagery (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Unclear why satellite-text alignment is worse**: The paper states that directly aligning satellite and alt-text representations is much worse than using the ground intermediate, but the explanation is insufficient (one stroke). It is unclear whether the alt-text contains more errors or is biased, making the textual supervision inferior (Reviewer 2).
- **Overstated claim about existing datasets**: Section 1 claims current RS image-text datasets contain only 10k samples, but RS5M has 5 million samples (Reviewer 3).
- **No ablation of CLIP initialization**: The satellite encoder is initialized from the CLIP image encoder; high performance may stem from CLIP pretraining rather than the alignment method. Training from scratch is needed to demonstrate real ability (Reviewer 3).
- **Model is not a complete VLM**: GRAFT only serves as a visual encoder for existing VLMs; it cannot independently perform text generation (VQA, captioning). Its cross-modal ability is limited by the pre-trained CLIP text encoder. The paper should discuss limitations when applied to other VLMs (Reviewer 3).
- **Residual domain gap**: Objects in natural images (size, orientation, placement) differ from remote-sensing views (top-down), and CLIP’s text descriptions are biased toward natural images. This may limit the method’s usage (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing related works**: Several relevant recent works are not reviewed, e.g., SatViT (IEEE GRSL 2022), Empirical Study of RS Pretraining (TGRS 2023), RS5M (2023), Advancing Plain ViT Toward RS FM (TGRS 2023), RSGPT (2023) (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty questioned**: One reviewer, while not expert, was not fully convinced of the innovation (Reviewer 4).
- **Low image quality**: Images in the manuscript are too low resolution to read (Reviewer 4).
