## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose COPER, an end-to-end deep multi-view clustering framework that fuses representation learning (via a novel permutation-based canonical correlation objective) and clustering (via consistent pseudo-labels across views). Experimental results on ten benchmark datasets and a theoretical connection to supervised LDA are provided.

### Strengths
- **Novel permutation-based CCA objective** (Reviewers 1, 4) – Reviewer 4 notes the permutation idea is interesting and is shown by ablation to enhance CCA embeddings.
- **Theoretical analysis** – Shows the learned embeddings approximate supervised LDA representations and provides an error bound due to false pseudo-labels (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **End-to-end design** – Integrates representation and clustering, avoiding the suboptimal two-stage pipeline (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Clear motivation and well-organized paper** (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation** across datasets of varying sizes and many baseline types (Reviewer 2).
- **Visualization and proofs** support the method (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient and outdated baselines** – The references in Line 41 are not up to date (Reviewer 1). Some selected comparison methods are designed for incomplete multi-view data or noisy correspondence, which are not appropriate (Reviewer 1). Important end-to-end multi-view clustering methods, such as the classical work in [1], should be compared or discussed (Reviewer 3). Recent self-supervised deep MVC methods (e.g., *“On the effects of self-supervision and contrastive alignment in deep multi-view clustering,”* CVPR 2023; *“Investigating and Mitigating the Side Effects of Noisy Views for Self-Supervised Clustering Algorithms in Practical Multi-View Scenarios,”* CVPR 2024) should also be included (Reviewer 4).
- **Pseudo-label alignment and sensitivity** – The method relies on initial pseudo-labels; if these are inaccurate, errors may propagate during self-supervised learning. The paper does not adequately analyze sensitivity to initial assignment quality or the risk of convergence to suboptimal solutions (Reviewer 2). *Reviewer 2 also considers the combination of correlation loss and pseudo-label estimation conceptually incremental; Reviewer 1, by contrast, finds the permutation objective novel.*
- **Motivation clarity** – The authors claim existing MVC methods are two-stage, but acknowledge a few end-to-end methods already exist; the motivation for COPER should be further clarified (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing parameter study** – No analysis of how hyperparameters affect performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Model architecture sensitivity and practical applicability** – The method requires dataset-specific model architecture and batch size settings (Table 7 & 9) and human-determined training epochs per step (D.4). Since no labeled data is available to tune these in practice, the method may have limited practical value (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 encourages using a uniform architecture across datasets for fair comparison.
- **Editorial errors** – Missing figure serial number in Line 244; “using using” in Line 292 (Reviewer 1).
- **Suggestion on presentation of comparisons** – Reviewer 1 recommends classifying compared methods into CCA-derived and non-CCA groups to directly demonstrate the effectiveness of new elements.
