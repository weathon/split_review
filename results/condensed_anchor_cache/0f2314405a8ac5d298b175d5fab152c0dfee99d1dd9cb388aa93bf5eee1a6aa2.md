## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents OmniPhysGS, a framework for text-driven physical parameter learning for 3DGS-reconstructed objects. The key novelty is making discrete constitutive model types learnable: each object is modeled as constitutive 3D Gaussians, where a physics-aware decoder assigns each Gaussian one of 12 predefined material classes (rubber, metal, honey, water, etc.). A learnable MPM simulation is used, and simulated renders are fed into a pretrained text-to-video diffusion model via SDS loss to guide both material class and parameter estimation. Two training strategies (grouping, multi-mini-batch) address long MPM sequences and limited video diffusion frames. Experiments show improvements of 3–16% in visual quality and text alignment over PhysDreamer, Physics3D, DreamPhysics. One reviewer (rating 8) particularly emphasizes that this is the first method to use learnable constitutive models in Gaussian Splatting, supports many materials, and is user-friendly via text prompts. Other reviewers (rating 6) acknowledge relevance but raise multiple concerns.

### Strengths
- Clear overall presentation of the framework; extensive comparisons and ablations.
- Novel technical design: modeling each 3D asset as constitutive 3D Gaussians with an ensemble of 12 domain-expert constitutive sub-models (rubber, metal, honey, water, etc.), functioning similarly to a Mixture of Experts. This is the first to use learnable constitutive models in Gaussian Splatting, enabling more general and realistic physical dynamics across elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, and fluid substances.
- Problem is relevant and important: prior works (PhysDreamer, Physics3D) are limited to elastic objects due to fixed constitutive models. The proposed approach adds flexibility to choose from different materials.
- User-friendly: scenes defined by user-specified prompts, with pretrained video diffusion model for supervision.
- Memory-efficient MPM solver implementation is useful.
- Two training strategies (grouping, multi-mini-batch) to overcome the challenge of numerous MPM steps and limited frames from video diffusion models.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited learner control:** scene controls such as initial velocity and external forces are not learnable. The framework essentially learns physical parameters given fixed, predefined interactions. Text descriptions must be crafted based on these known interactions.
- **Physical plausibility and realism concerns:**
  - Objects cannot be both elastic and sandy; heuristic blending of materials within a single object may produce dynamics that conform to unrealistic motions encoded in the video model (impossible in reality). This concern is echoed by multiple reviewers: dividing an object into particle groups with different predefined constitutive models may yield arbitrary material predictions inside an object (e.g., can example producing unreasonable results).
  - Figure 1 is unclear: mountain is depicted as non-elastic but collapses when a toy duck falls, inconsistent with assigned properties.
  - Collision experiments consistently show a gap between colliding objects (lack of physical contact), and the overall visual quality still falls short of PhysGaussian.
  - Some results look weird: e.g., at 0:49 in the video, a can hit by a toy duck keeps shrinking, not resembling metal.
- **Insufficiently convincing comparisons:** experiments only compare the same object with vastly different materials (rubber vs. sand, jelly vs. fluid). Since changing the material type in PhysGaussian requires only changing three fields (material, Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio), this comparison is weak. More persuasive would be optimizing parameters within the same material type—e.g., "hard rubber bear" vs. "stretchy rubber bear" or "hard metal can" vs. "soft metal can"—as PhysGaussian already demonstrated variable Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio (Figure 6 of that work). This would show that the model can learn material property variations, which is claimed (Eq.4: hyperelastic energy, plasticity return function, physical parameters are all learnable).
- **Lack of real-world experiments:** no experiments on real captured data like those in PhysDreamer and Physics3D.
- **Metrics not adequate for motion quality:** CLIP similarity scores applied to videos are not convincing for measuring motion realism; motion quality should be judged by human preference.
- **Unclear necessity of the neural network:** since it is per-scene test-time optimization (no generalization), why not directly optimize a 12-way one-hot softmax vector for material classification? What advantage does training a scene-specific neural network provide?
- **Confusing notation:** at L207 physical parameters are denoted as a single real scalar, but the supplementary material says it includes Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio.
- **How is the physics-aware decoder trained?** Material classes are discrete and sampling of \( j_i, k_i \) in Eq.6 is non-differentiable. This key novelty needs detailed discussion.
- **Memory issue:** Warp creates arrays from Torch tensor without copying, yet consumes much more memory than implementing MPM in PyTorch.
- **Reliance on pre-trained diffusion models:** quality depends on the diffusion model’s training data; this inherently limits generalization to novel or highly specialized materials. The method may lack robustness for complex or unusual scenarios where the guidance model does not capture specific material properties (e.g., multiple interacting objects, extreme deformations).
- **Handling of permanent deformation:** for viscoelastic or plastic materials, how does optimization handle failure (e.g., bad parameters causing the 3D model to break into pieces)? Could training get stuck?
- **Unclear material assumptions per Gaussian particle:** Is the proposed method able to handle material mixtures as claimed?
- **Parameter selection for multi-object scenes** is not described, especially where material differences are important.
- **Figure 3 could be improved:** add notes to show a scene with multiple model categories and how the classification decoder assigns constitutive models to different areas.
- **Water container shape specification:** in the wolf-on-water scene, how is the water container shape specified in the pipeline?
- **Missing comparisons/discussion** with related works that design learnable constitutive models: Nagasawa et al. "Mixing sauces: a viscosity blending model for shear thinning fluids" (ACM TOG 2019), Su et al. "A generalized constitutive model for versatile MPM simulation and inverse learning with differentiable physics" (Proc. ACM Comput. Graph. Interact. Tech. 2023). The latter is particularly relevant.
- **Interactions beyond two objects:** how does the method handle a single object interacting with an entire scene, or scenarios with more than two objects?

**Note:** One reviewer (rating 8) is more positive, highlighting that this is the first method to use learnable constitutive models in Gaussian Splatting, supports many materials, and is user-friendly via text prompts; however, they still raise concerns about reliance on diffusion models and unclear per-particle material assumptions.
