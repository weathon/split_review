## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers note that the paper proposes a plug-and-play ranking loss based on Kendall’s τ Coefficient to complement KL divergence in logit-based knowledge distillation, aiming to improve focus on low-probability channels and inter-class relationships. Experiments on CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and COCO with CNN and ViT combinations are commended by all. One reviewer (score 8) found the novelty and analysis of suboptimal points convincing, while three others (scores 5–6) considered the contributions modest, citing insufficient justification of core claims, limited performance gains, and missing ablation studies.

### Strengths
1. **Novelty and contribution**: The use of Kendall’s τ ranking loss in knowledge distillation is novel and provides a complementary way to capture inter-class relationships and avoid suboptimal solutions of KL divergence. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
2. **Plug-and-play design**: The loss is designed for straightforward integration into any logit-based distillation framework without modifying the underlying architecture, increasing practical utility. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)  
3. **Extensive experiments**: The paper evaluates on CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and COCO with various CNN and ViT teacher-student combinations, demonstrating robustness and generalizability. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)  
4. **Analysis of KL limitations**: The paper provides convincing arguments and gradient analysis showing that KL divergence is influenced by channel scale and tends to overlook low-probability channels. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
5. **Ablation insights**: The ablation studies (e.g., on hyperparameter \(k\) and different ranking loss forms) offer valuable insights into the method’s behavior. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear mechanism for low-probability channels**: The claim that the ranking loss specifically increases attention to low-probability channels is not clearly supported. The loss equally penalizes all ranking deviations regardless of magnitude; a gradient analysis showing different behavior for high vs. low probability channels is needed. (Reviewer 3) Visualizations of logits or attention maps before/after applying the ranking loss are missing. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
2. **Limited hyperparameter ablation**: Only the effect of \(k\) is studied; sensitivity to \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) is not explored. (Reviewer 1) This is especially concerning because the method introduces multiple hyperparameters while performance gains are limited. (Reviewer 4)  
3. **Justification of suboptimal point claim**: The claim that the ranking loss resolves the suboptimal issue depicted in Figure 1 lacks direct evidence; visual comparisons of logits with and without the ranking loss are not provided. (Reviewer 4)  
4. **Relation to other distillation losses**: The necessity of applying the ranking loss before softmax (while KD constrains after softmax) is not convincingly argued. (Reviewer 1) No experiments combine the ranking loss with Relation Knowledge Distillation (RKD). (Reviewer 1) Potential adverse effects when combining with other methods are not explored. (Reviewer 2)  
5. **Comparison with LSKD**: In Tables 1 and 2, LSKD often outperforms the proposed method. The paper does not adequately explain this, especially since LSKD does not explicitly target the KL limitation that the proposed loss addresses. (Reviewer 3)  
6. **Computational overhead**: No discussion on strategies to minimize the computational cost of the ranking loss. (Reviewer 2)  
7. **Class imbalance**: The potential advantage of the ranking loss in class-imbalanced scenarios is not examined. (Reviewer 2)  
8. **Dense derivations**: The differentiable form of Kendall’s τ is presented with dense notation and limited intermediate steps, reducing readability. (Reviewer 1)  
9. **Student initialization**: The effect of different student initializations on the stability of results is not studied. (Reviewer 1)  
10. **Minority positive view**: One reviewer found the novelty, analysis, and experiments highly convincing (score 8), whereas three reviewers rated 5–6, citing insufficient justification and marginal improvements.
