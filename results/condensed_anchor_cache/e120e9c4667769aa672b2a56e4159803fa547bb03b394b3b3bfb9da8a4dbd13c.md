- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 6, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies safe linear bandits with instantaneous linear constraints in non-convex (including discrete) arm sets. It relaxes the common star-convexity assumption by introducing a Local Point Assumption (Assumption 3). The algorithm NCS-LUCB uses double confidence intervals and a newly designed exploration bonus \(b_t(a)\). Both an upper bound \(\tilde{O}(d (1+\frac{\tau}{\epsilon \iota}) \sqrt{T})\) and a lower bound \(\Omega(\max\{d\sqrt{T}, \frac{1}{\epsilon\iota^2}\})\) are provided. Numerical results validate the theory.

### Strengths
- The problem of non-star-convexity in safe linear bandits is important; the star-convexity requirement is a significant limitation in prior work. (Reviewer 1)
- The use of double confidence intervals combined with the exploration bonus \(b_t(a)\) is interesting. (Reviewer 2)
- Assumption 3 captures local information around the origin and optimal point. (Reviewer 2)
- Providing both upper and lower bounds simultaneously strengthens theoretical contributions and makes the new assumptions more convincing. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow; visuals are effective. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- The problem with the bonus term in prior works (LC-LUCB) for non-convex arm sets is well explained via examples, and the intuition for the new bonus term is clear. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution.** The core result – \(\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})\) regret under Assumption 3 – adds little over existing work. Under condition (1) of Assumption 3, the results of Amani et al. (2019) immediately give \(\tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T})\) regret. Condition (2) appears contrived and merely a quirk of the analysis of Pacchiano et al. (2021). The only real innovation is the new bonus term (Equation (4)); the rest of the analysis is standard. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Assumption 2 appears unnecessary or erroneous.** This action yields initial zero estimates, but in the toy example (line 403) and experiment (line 491) the action set does not include \(a_0=[0,0]^\top\). Lemma 7 (line 877) requires \(\|\phi(a_t)\|\ge \epsilon\), which \(a_0\) does not satisfy. (Reviewer 2)
- **Assumption 3 is questionable.** 
  - Condition (1) seems too strong: if the action set contains a circle \(\|a_1\|_2=1\), it forces a smaller safe circle. But two basis safe actions should suffice to estimate \(\theta\) and \(\gamma\). Are all actions in that smaller safe circle necessary? Is there a counterexample with only two safe actions? (Reviewer 2)
  - Constraints on \(L\) and \(\tau\) are unclear: when \(L=100\) and \(\tau=0.01\), how does \(L-\epsilon\le 1\) hold? Does this imply restrictions on the relationship between \(L\) and \(\tau\)? (Reviewer 2)
- **The lower bound (Theorem 2) is essentially identical to that in Pacchiano et al. (2021);** the paper should explicitly discuss differences. Moreover, the non-convex case should be harder yet Theorem 2 gives \(\Omega(\sqrt{dT})\) while the convex-case lower bound in Pacchiano et al. (2024) is \(\Omega(d\sqrt{T})\) – this seems contradictory. How does \(\tau\) influence the lower bound? (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Discrepancy between upper and lower bound dependence on \(\iota\).** The lower bound is \(\Omega(1/\iota^2)\) while the upper bound is \(O(1/\iota)\). This needs clarification. (Reviewer 3)
- **The bonus term uses \(\iota\), which is not known in practice.** The paper mentions using the BOB technique (Zhao et al., 2020; originally from [1]) to address this, but does not further investigate it. As the bonus term is the main technical contribution, a complete solution is expected. (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational issues.** The algorithm may not be implementable beyond discrete action spaces. For non-convex continuous regions, how is \(a_t\) computed in Algorithm line 6? Even in convex regions, the bonus \(b_t(a)\) in (3) is complicated – is the optimization still convex and efficiently approximable? (Reviewer 2)
- **Overclaiming novelty regarding non-convex sets.** Prior works considered non-convex sets, including star-convex (Moradipari et al., 2021) and discrete sets (Pacchiano et al., 2021). The authors should be more precise (e.g., "first for non-star-convex sets with round-wise constraint satisfaction"). (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing comparisons and discussion.** The paper should compare to Amani et al. (2019), Pacchiano et al. (2024), and Moradipari et al. (2021) in detail, and discuss the price (additional regret) of non-convexity relative to convex arm sets. (Reviewer 4)
- **Incomplete explanation of an example.** In "What is the correct strategy?" how is \(b_t(a_1)=r^*-\frac13=\frac23\) derived? It appears different from equation (3). (Reviewer 2)
- **Request for clarification on a proof step.** Lines 324-328: why does \(\alpha\phi(a_*)\in \phi(A_t)\) imply the distance to \(\phi(A_t)\) is less than \(g_t^1(a)\)? (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor issues.** 
  - Line 040: reward should be *expected* reward. (Reviewer 1)
  - BOB method is adopted by Zhao et al. (2020) but first proposed by [1]; proper attribution needed. (Reviewer 4)
  - Examples in experiments are too simple: actions along the axis make the problem resemble a \(K\)-armed bandit. (Reviewer 4)
  - Assumption 1 bounds \(\max(\|\theta^*\|,\|\gamma^*\|)\le \sqrt{d}\); could use a generic constant \(B\) to better reflect dependence in regret. (Reviewer 4)
  - Consider providing problem-dependent upper/lower bounds involving reward and cost gaps. (Reviewer 4)
  - Related work section should be in the body; add citation [1]. (Reviewer 1)
