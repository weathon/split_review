## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data distillation method called “effect alignment” that creates compact synthetic datasets by matching endpoint training effects (differences between models’ probability distributions on real data after training on synthetic vs. real data) rather than aligning intermediate states (trajectory/gradient matching). An efficient approximation based on gradient distance is introduced, and experiments on several classification datasets show competitive accuracy, especially in bias-sensitive settings. The method is described as a variant of MTT (Dataset Distillation by Matching Training Trajectories) combined with Dataset Distillation (DD).

### Strengths
- **Originality**: The concept of effect alignment (matching endpoint effects rather than intermediate states) is novel in dataset distillation. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Theoretical foundation**: The method includes error approximation guarantees (Eq.6) that lend robustness. (R1)
- **Strong performance in some settings**: Outperforms baselines on digital datasets with IPC=1/10 and shows robustness in bias-sensitive scenarios. (R1, R2)
- **Good writing**: The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only classification tasks (e.g., MNIST, CIFAR, etc.) are evaluated; no regression or other tasks. Only one incomplete table is presented. (R1, R3)
- **Dataset diversity**: Experiments are conducted on a small number of datasets, raising questions about generalizability. (R1)
- **Performance not consistently superior**: The method performs worse on several datasets (e.g., other than digital datasets with IPC=1/10). The results in Tab.1 are not evidently strong enough to demonstrate superiority. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Unclear motivation vs. BPTT/gradient matching**: 
  - R4 argues that the raw effect-matching objective \(|\mathcal{L}(P,\theta^*_D)-\mathcal{L}(P,\theta^*_A)|\) is essentially equivalent to the original BPTT formulation \(\mathcal{L}(P,\theta^*_A)\) when the synthetic dataset is small, because \(\mathcal{L}(P,\theta^*_D)\) is constant. A more comparative analysis is needed.
  - The proposed approximation can be viewed as a variant of previous gradient matching; the benefits of replacing parameter matching with “effects” are unclear. (R2, R4)
- **Approximation error bound concerns**: Equation (6) shows the error is dominated by the farthest distance the network parameters move during training. Whether the bound is tight and practically useful is questionable; toy experiments or further analysis are encouraged. (R3, R4)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: 
  - The choice of time steps \(T\) affects the approximation gap; reducing \(T\) closes the gap but may yield suboptimal network performance. (R3)
  - R2 suspects the method’s advantage at large IPC may come from the “difficulty alignment trick” (control matching range) used in DATM; hyperparameters should be reported to clarify.
  - R1 asks for insight into hyperparameter selection (especially time steps) and memory/computation optimizations.
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**: 
  - Comparisons with more recent methods (e.g., TESLA, DATM) are missing; the method is compared only to older baselines. (R1, R2)
  - Ablation studies are required: comparing the proposed method with the original BPTT formulation and with gradient matching while keeping other factors the same. (R3, R4)
  - R2 notes that TESLA (using soft labels) always performs better than the proposed method, except in large IPC cases where the difficulty alignment trick may be responsible.
- **Incomplete paper**: R3 states the paper appears unfinished (only one incomplete table) and that the summarised contributions do not match the method.
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: R3 (score 1, confidence 5) is strongly negative, calling the paper unfinished and its experiments insufficient. R4 (score 3, confidence 4) is skeptical of the motivation and approximation, while R1 and R2 (scores 5, confidence 4) acknowledge novelty but note significant weaknesses in experimental validation and comparisons.
