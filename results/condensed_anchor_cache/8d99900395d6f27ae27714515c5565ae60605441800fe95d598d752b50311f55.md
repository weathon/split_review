## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Dynamic Tanh (DyT), defined as `DyT(x) = tanh(αx)` (with learnable scalar α), as a drop-in replacement for normalization layers like LayerNorm. The idea is inspired by the observation that LayerNorm’s input-output mapping is tanh-like and S-shaped. DyT is evaluated across image classification (ViT, ConvNeXt, MLP-Mixer), self-supervised learning (MAE, DINO), diffusion models (DiT), language modeling (LLaMA up to 7B), audio waveform pretraining (wav2vec 2.0), and DNA sequence pretraining (HyenaDNA). The authors report performance comparable to LN/RMSNorm without hyperparameter changes, and analyze the learned α (correlating with inverse input standard deviation) and the importance of initialization.

### Strengths

- The paper discovers that LayerNorm produces an S-shaped input-output mapping, directly motivating the DyT design (R1).
- DyT is a simple, novel element-wise operation that is computationally cheap and can replace LN without additional requirements (R1, R3, R4).
- Extensive experiments across diverse modalities, architectures, and learning paradigms (recognition, generation, ConvNets, LLMs, supervised and self-supervised) demonstrate broad applicability (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well written with clear introduction and sufficient related work; Figures 1 and 2 provide clear evidence for the motivation (R2).
- DyT provides new insights into the workings of LayerNorm/RMSNorm (R2).
- The method is computationally efficient compared to computing normalization statistics (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **No significant performance improvement over LN.** Additional evidence is needed to show why adopting DyT is advantageous given comparable performance (R3). Reviewers questioned the practical benefit (R3).
- **Missing analysis of training dynamics and stability.** The effect of DyT on gradients, large learning rates, and training stability is unexplored (R1, R2, R3). One reviewer noted that DyT might be less stable than LN based on LLM experiments (R2). No training/validation error curves comparing DyT and LN are provided (R3).
- **Missing comparisons to other normalization-free approaches.** The paper discusses FixUp, ReZero, SkipInit, NormFree-ResNets, and others in related work but does not implement and test against any of them (R3, R4). One reviewer notes that ReZero does show results on Transformers, and FixUp/SkipInit might also work; a NormFree-ResNets adaptation for Transformers should be attempted before claiming those methods do not work (R4).
- **Lack of rigorous justification and citation.** The abstract claims normalization layers generally produce S-shaped curves, but the observation is from LayerNorm specifically (BatchNorm would not). The nonlinearity of LayerNorm is already known and should be cited (e.g., ICML 2024 paper on nonlinearity of LN) (R2).
- **Limited LLM evaluation.** Evaluation on only 15 lm-harness tasks is weak; the paper should include MMLU/MMLU-Pro and show loss curves during training (R2).
- **No scaling or failure analysis.** The work tests only standard model sizes at standard training lengths, with little analysis for longer training, larger models, or pushing the method to failure (R4). A LLM.int8()-style analysis on numerical stability and low-precision training (fp8) / quantization effects is lacking (R4).
- **Missing computational efficiency experiments.** The paper claims DyT does not need activation statistics, but does not demonstrate how this impacts computational efficiency versus LN (R3).
- **Several unanswered questions that overlap with missing experiments or ablations:**
    - Do both LN layers per transformer block (before MHSA and FFN) exhibit the S-shaped behavior? (R1)
    - Does the same S-shaped observation hold for ConvNeXt models? (R1)
    - How does DyT performance change when LN is placed after MHSA/FFN (Swin-V2) rather than before? (R1)
    - Is there evidence for the claim that adjusting α initial value leads to faster early convergence? How does optimization converge well for larger α? (R1)
    - Does DyT demonstrate invariance to weight/dataset rescaling (as LN does)? If not, how sensitive is it? (R1)
    - What normalization was used during pretraining for the reported experiments? (R1)
    - In DiT, is only LN replaced with DyT, or also the scale-shift norm conditioned on time/text? (R1)
    - Were alternative S-shaped functions (e.g., x/(1+|x|)) considered and do they offer similar performance? (R1)
    - If learnable α already achieves the same effect, why is the tanh squash function needed? (R2)
- **Related work on tanh normalization is not new.** Traditional tanh normalization for feature scaling [2,3] and adaptive tanh normalization for deep learning [4] have been explored, and these lines of research should be included (R1).
- **Citation error.** The citation for ReZero is incorrect (R4).

*Note: One reviewer (rating 6) was more positive, finding the method novel and well executed, but still raised weaknesses about rigor, evaluation, and stability. Other reviewers (ratings 3, 3, 5) were more critical, particularly about the lack of performance gain and missing comparisons.*
