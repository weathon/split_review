## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Dynamic Low-Rank Sparse Adaptation (LoSA), a method that integrates low-rank adaptation (LoRA) into the sparsification of Large Language Models (LLMs) to recover performance lost due to pruning. LoSA dynamically sparsifies LoRA outcomes to match sparse weights, uses Representation Mutual Information (RMI) to determine layer-wise sparsity rates, and adjusts LoRA ranks based on layer reconstruction errors. Experiments on LLaMA, Vicuna, and OPT models show perplexity reductions (e.g., 68.73↓ for LLaMA-2-7B), zero-shot accuracy improvements (16.32%↑), and inference speedups (2.60× CPU, 2.23× GPU) with only 45 minutes of fine-tuning. Reviewers are divided: some find the approach novel with strong empirical results (ratings 8, 6, 6), while others argue limited novelty over prior work and insufficient baselines (ratings 3, 3).

### Strengths
- Addresses a practical problem: performance degradation in sparse LLMs, offering a solution that preserves inference speedups.
- Novel integration of sparsification into the LoRA adaptation process, with dynamic sparsity rate adjustment via RMI and rank allocation based on reconstruction errors. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Strong and consistent empirical results across multiple LLM architectures (LLaMA-2, Vicuna, OPT) and sparsity ratios (50%, 60%, 70%). Significant perplexity improvements (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B PPL from 12.07 with SparseGPT to 3.78 with LoSA) and zero-shot accuracy gains (e.g., 16.32%↑). Inference speedups reported on CPU and GPU (e.g., 2.60×, 2.23×). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
- Clear problem formulation, related work, and a detailed pseudocode (Algorithm 1) that connects each step. (Reviewer 2)
- No additional inference latency because adapted weights are merged post-training, maintaining the speed advantages of sparsity. (Reviewers 1, 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines:** The paper omits comparisons with adaptive LoRA methods (e.g., AdaLoRA, SoRA) and prior works on sparse PEFT using low-rank adaptation (e.g., [1-4] in reviewer 4). Comparisons are limited to SparseGPT and Wanda, which are post-training quantization methods without fine-tuning; a fairer comparison would include finetuning-based pruning methods for LLMs (e.g., LLM-pruner, SliceGPT, or a SparseGPT+sparse LoRA baseline where both base weights and LoRA weights are sparse and mergeable). (Reviewers 3, 4, 5)
- **Limited novelty:** Key components—dynamic layer-wise sparsity via MI, rank allocation, progressive pruning—are known from prior work (e.g., [A1-A7] in reviewer 5). The adoption of RMI largely follows [Zheng et al., 2021] with minor adaptation to LLMs. The idea of merging sparse LoRA with sparse weights is straightforward and explored in [A7]. (Reviewer 5)
- **Questionable assumptions and heuristics:**
  - Eq. 7 assumes higher rank reduces reconstruction loss, but given the sparsity mask M, higher rank may not affect the output (Eq. 1). Derivation for Eq. 7 and Eq. 9 is lacking. (Reviewer 2)
  - Eq. 5 assigns higher sparsity rates to layers with higher RMI importance, contradicting standard practice of preserving important layers; justification is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear implementation details:**
  - How rank increase/decrease is performed (e.g., random initialization for new dimensions, SVD-based truncation for reduction) is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
  - It is unclear which layers (e.g., Q/K/V/out projections) and weight matrices are sparsified and fine-tuned. (Reviewer 2)
  - Consistency of sparsity patterns between LoRA weights (BA) and model weights (W) is not clearly described beyond stating the same mask is applied; the shapes and dimensions of these matrices differ. (Reviewer 3)
  - The outcome of SparseGPT with standard LoRA is ambiguous: does it produce a dense or sparse model? The comparison with LoSA is not on equal footing. (Reviewers 2, 5)
- **Missing analysis and ablations:**
  - Computational overhead of the algorithm, especially sparsity mask computation at each fine-tuning step (and whether it is performed at every step or less frequently), is not reported or analyzed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
  - Ablation studies are limited; sensitivity of RMI and reconstruction-based decisions to different architectures and hyperparameters is not explored. (Reviewer 3)
  - No guidelines for setting fine-tuning steps (e.g., why T=5 is sufficient in Table 1). (Reviewer 2)
  - Relative contribution of RMI and rank-update computations to overall step time is not quantified. (Reviewer 1)
- **N:M sparsity concerns:** The 2:8 sparsity pattern may not be accelerated by existing GPU compilers (which typically support 2:4). Actual speedups for such patterns are uncertain. (Reviewer 5)
- **Marginal improvements:** Some gains over baselines are small, especially at lower sparsity rates. (Reviewer 4)
- **Generalizability:** Speedup results rely on specific sparse inference engines (DeepSparse, nm-vllm); generalizability to other deployment environments is unclear. Applicability of RMI to other compression techniques (e.g., quantization) is not discussed. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Unused equations:** Section 2.3 introduces Eq. 2 and Eq. 3 (RMI-based measures) but these are not directly used in the algorithm, causing confusion. (Reviewer 2)

Note: A minority of reviewers (ratings 3) found the contributions marginal and novelty limited, while the majority (ratings 6-8) found the approach effective with strong results. The contrast mainly centers on the breadth of baselines and the perceived novelty of the dynamic components.
