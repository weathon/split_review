## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses Automated Model Evaluation (AutoEval), which estimates the performance of a trained classifier on an unlabeled test set under distribution shift. The authors propose Meta-Distribution Energy (MDE), which converts per-sample energy scores (derived from logits) into a dataset-level statistic and then trains a regression model on synthesized datasets to predict accuracy. The method is validated on several image datasets (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet) and a natural language inference task (MNLI), and it shows a strong linear relationship between MDE and actual accuracy. A theoretical connection to classification loss is provided.

### Strengths

- **Novelty and theoretical grounding**: MDE introduces a statistical distribution of energy scores into AutoEval for the first time (Reviewer 5). The paper provides a theoretical proof linking MDE to classification loss (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Wide experimental coverage**: Experiments span multiple modalities (vision and NLP), datasets (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, MNLI), architectures, and also cover challenging scenarios like label noise and class imbalance (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). Algorithm 1 clearly outlines the pipeline (Reviewer 3).
- **Real-world relevance**: AutoEval is crucial for deploying models in practice, helping to assess performance when ground-truth labels are unavailable (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Efficiency and overconfidence mitigation**: The energy-based approach is designed to be more efficient and less overconfident than prior AutoEval methods (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing quality**: The paper is well-written, with smooth and clear presentation of key points (Reviewers 1, 5). Note: Reviewer 4 and Reviewer 1 flagged some grammar issues, but overall quality is high.

### Weaknesses

- **Overstated and unsupported claims** (Reviewer 3): The paper asserts “overconfidence issue,” “substantial storage,” and “computational cost” advantages without providing empirical evidence or comparisons against baselines like DoC and ATC. For example, MDE requires training a linear regression model, so its computational advantage over training-free methods is unclear (Reviewer 3, also raised by Reviewer 5).
- **Limited novelty and similarity to existing work** (Reviewers 2, 3): The core idea is essentially replacing the softmax score with an energy score; prior works (e.g., “Detecting Errors and Estimating Accuracy on Unlabeled Data with Self-training Ensembles”) have already connected OOD detection to AutoEval. The connection to energy-based models beyond using logits is not clearly established (Reviewer 1—suggesting better symbolism and missing EBM references [3–7]).
- **Unconvincing theoretical analysis** (Reviewer 3): The theory in Section 3.3 does not account for scenarios where an image is correctly classified with a low Softmax score. A more robust framework is needed.
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation**:
  - **Missing key datasets and benchmarks**: No results on ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-V2, or ImageNet-R (Reviewer 3). Natural shifts (e.g., BREEDs, i-WILDS) are mentioned in the supplementary but results are not shown (Reviewer 3). A real-world dataset with complex domain shifts (e.g., from references [1][2]) is needed (Reviewer 1).
  - **Missing comparisons**: The paper does not compare with recent methods like Nuclear Norm, Optimal Transport (“Characterizing Out-of-Distribution Error via Optimal Transport”), or ATC/DoC on ImageNet setups (Reviewer 3). Also, no comparison on domain-shift data with real labels (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of ablation and analysis**:
  - **Temperature T**: The range shown (1 to 10) is too narrow; experiments from 0.01 to 100 are needed, including behavior as T→0 and T→∞ (Reviewers 1, 5). The meaning of T and how to select it on a new dataset are not explained (Reviewers 1, 5).
  - **Synthetic data generation**: How is the synthesized test data generated for training the regression model? (Reviewer 1).
  - **Regression model influence**: Does the choice of regression model affect accuracy prediction? (Reviewer 1).
  - **Correlation metric**: How is “correlation” in Table 1 computed? (Reviewer 1).
- **Efficiency and time/memory comparison** (Reviewers 4, 5): The paper does not provide wall-clock time or memory usage comparisons between training-free and training-must methods, leaving claims of computational advantage unsubstantiated.
- **No source code** (Reviewer 4): Code is not released, which hinders reproducibility. (Reviewer 4 requested it be provided.)
- **Grammar and notation issues** (Reviewers 1, 4): Examples: “the information … then offer” should be “and then offers”; “the correct classified” should be “the correctly classified”; Eq(2) uses E(x) for both the energy and the normalization term (suggest using Z(x)); mathcal font in Eq(5,6) is usually for single letters.
- **Interpretability** (Reviewer 4): The method’s interpretability can be improved to help users understand and trust the predictions.

**Reviewer disagreement**: While Reviewers 4 and 5 rate the paper highly (8,8) and emphasize novelty, strong experiments, and theory, Reviewer 3 (rating 3) finds limited contribution, overstated claims, and insufficient experimental rigor. Reviewer 1 (rating 6) and Reviewer 2 (rating 6) have intermediate concerns, notably about the clarity of the problem motivation and relation to uncertainty estimation.
