## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies provable learning in Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (DEC-POMDPs). It first gives a hardness result showing worst-case sample complexity exponential in the number of agents. It then proposes algorithms and sample-efficiency guarantees for two subclasses: (1) factored models, where each agent’s transition and observation depend only on a limited set of other agents’ actions; and (2) memoryless policies, where agents act based only on current observations. The algorithms build on Optimistic Maximum Likelihood Estimation (OMLE). Regret bounds are provided for both global and local (Nash equilibrium) optimality.

### Strengths
- Clear theoretical claims, nicely interleaving core results with intuition (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Addresses an important and relatively untouched question in MARL – provable sample efficiency without exponential dependency on the number of agents (Reviewers 2, 4, 6).
- Algorithms appear novel in the DEC-POMDP setting (Reviewer 3).
- Thorough theoretical analysis with rigorous arguments on sample efficiency and optimality (Reviewer 3).
- Well-written: problem settings clearly explained, pseudocode and intuitive explanations given (Reviewer 4).
- Includes hardness bounds, treatment of both global and local optimality, and a relevant application (POMDP with knapsack constraints) (Reviewer 5).
- The theoretical approach is more novel compared to application-focused MARL work (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Proofs not in the main document.** All proofs are delegated to a 25+ page appendix. One reviewer could not read the supplementary material; another noted the paper is only a list of results with no indication of how they are proven and only two paragraphs of example. Per ICLR guidelines, reviewers are not required to read the appendix, which makes evaluation impossible for some. The paper was considered too long for ICLR (48 pages total) and potentially gaming the page limit (Reviewers 1, 5). *(Minority counterpoint: one reviewer acknowledged the proofs are dense but possibly unavoidable.)*
- **Restrictiveness and lack of justification for assumptions.** The factored model requires that each agent’s transition and observation depend only on the *actions* of a limited set of other agents, *not* on their states or observations. This is explicitly not stated in the text; the description is unclear on how local states are updated. The memoryless policy assumption is more restrictive than the finite-memory (length L) setting studied in the cited papers. The reward function also seems designed to facilitate theory. Some reviewers find these assumptions strong but potentially justified for a theoretical paper; others criticize that the paper does not discuss applicability to scenarios with temporal dependencies, complex inter-agent dynamics, or global state information. The connection to reward structures beyond the assumed form is not addressed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 6)
- **Lack of empirical evidence.** No experiments are provided to validate the algorithm’s practical performance, noise tolerance, or complex reward functions. (Reviewer 1)
- **Connection to local optimality / Nash equilibrium is unclear.** The paper’s definition of local optimality uses a joint value function; the typical Nash equilibrium definition involves individual agent rewards. The discrepancy is not clarified. (Reviewer 3)
- **Sample complexity / regret connection not clearly explained.** It is not transparent how regret bounds in Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 translate to sample complexity, nor how the local optimality algorithm improves efficiency over the global one. The paper lacks a clear comparison with existing DEC-POMDP literature on sample complexity. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing proof or citation for Theorem 3.1.** Two reviewers note the proof is not in the main paper or appendix (Reviewers 3, 4). Additionally, the notation “an” in Definition 4.2 may be inconsistent with “pa” in Definition 4.1 (Reviewer 3).
- **Factored model may not apply to the paper’s own application.** In the knapsack application (Sec. 4.4), episodes terminate when any agent exceeds its budget, meaning all agents can affect each other via termination. It is unclear how the factored assumption holds. (Reviewer 4)
- **Weakly revealing condition not discussed for real-world feasibility.** This assumption is critical to sample efficiency claims, but the paper does not discuss when it would hold in practice or what happens when it fails. (Reviewer 6)
- **Limited scalability discussion.** The paper does not address scalability with increasing number of agents and interaction complexity in the factored model, nor how to adapt to memory-based policies. (Reviewer 6)
- **Omission of related frameworks.** The paper does not compare against mean-field control approaches for exponential action space growth, nor discuss centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) methods. (Reviewers 3, 6)
- **Incremental contribution in factored MDP/MG part.** The factored model results are an extension of existing studies on factored MDPs/MGs with the weakly-revealing assumption. (Reviewer 2)
