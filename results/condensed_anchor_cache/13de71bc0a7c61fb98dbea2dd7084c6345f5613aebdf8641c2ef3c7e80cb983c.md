## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Adaptive Proposal Sampling (APS) and its variant a-GPS, a sampler that learns Gaussian proposals to approximate deep neural network posteriors, aiming to handle multi-modal landscapes. Evaluations include synthetic distributions, classification, and out-of-distribution detection on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet. The topic is important and the algorithm is simple to implement. One reviewer (score 1) found the paper far less acceptable, citing severe writing and theoretical deficiencies and stating it is not ready for ICLR; the other three (score 3 each) expressed moderate support but raised substantial concerns about methodology, experimental evaluation, and presentation.

### Strengths

- The proposed adaptive proposal sampler appears to be new in the literature (Reviewer 3).
- The studied topic of sampling on multi-modal distributions is important (Reviewer 2).
- The algorithm is simple to implement in practice (Reviewer 2).
- The authors report that their method tends to produce samples quicker than competitor methods (Reviewer 4).
- The method is evaluated on a series of classification and out-of-distribution detection tasks across several architectures (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

**Writing and Presentation (Reviewers 1, 4):**
- Presentation is suboptimal: parameters such as *c* and *λ* appear in the text long before they are introduced (λ only detailed in Algorithm 1); multiple typos (e.g., “full-gradient MCMC similar to SG-MCMC”, “SGLD has fast computations but suffers form inefficient explorations”, “Previous works on state dependent”); reference list contains many arXiv preprints instead of actual publications; Table 4 and several appendix tables have wrong highlights in the ECE and NLL columns (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical exposition is lacking: central notions are not defined – “transforming a pretrained into a Bayesian model”, “perfect sampler”, “mode-specific MH”, time step *t* and *θ_t*, *D_x*, *D_y*, *Conf* in Eq. (20). Theory is mixed with related work (Eqs. (1)–(3)) (Reviewer 4).
- Dropout’s absence in most results is not explained in the main text, only in one table where it is present (Reviewer 1).
- Minor but noted: cross-entropy is not simply “interpreted as” negative log-likelihood but is the negative of a categorical distribution (Reviewer 1); the posterior derivation in (15) could be simplified directly to (17) (Reviewer 1); the assumption of modes “separated by high loss area” is contradicted by literature showing many paths of similar loss between maxima (Draxler 2018, Garipos 2018) (Reviewer 1).

**Theory and Methodology (Reviewers 2, 3, 4):**
- Claims of exactness and “perfect sampler” are inaccurate; the method is only exact when the target is a diagonal Gaussian (trivial case). Without a Metropolis-Hastings correction step, the method is not exact. Many claims need modification to be rigorous (Reviewer 2). Additionally, the acceptance rate of the exact GPS can be low in high dimensions due to the curse of dimensionality (Reviewer 3).
- The methodology is confusing: the algorithm does not have a component to encourage exploring multiple modes; it is unclear how it finds diverse modes (Reviewer 2). For well-separated modes, the sampler lacks a mode-escaping mechanism and may get trapped in a single mode, underestimating uncertainty (Reviewer 3).
- Algorithm 1 essentially finds a Gaussian approximation to the target; how is this different from variational inference and what are the advantages (Reviewer 2)?
- The independence assumption between parameters in high-dimensional problems neglects complex correlations, making the sampler less accurate and potentially exploring highly improbable regions (Reviewer 3).
- The reason for introducing the variance limit *λ* is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- The method requires a pretrained solution (*θ_MAP*); it is unclear if it works from scratch (Reviewer 2). Unsurprisingly, a-GPS converges so fast that a burn-in period is unnecessary – but this is because of the warm start (Reviewer 2).

**Experimental Evaluation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4):**
- The proposed method does not demonstrate better performance than baselines. Competitor methods (especially SGD-MC) almost always outperform it; results in Table 13 clearly show SGD-MC better (except strange behavior in Table 6) (Reviewer 1). Overall, the approach is outperformed by competitors most of the time (Reviewer 4).
- On the toy unimodal Gaussian posterior, effective sample size (ESS) is reported, but ESS is not reported on the mixture model (Figure 2 RHS) (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental setups are confusing: it is unclear if the baseline NUTS in Section 3.1 also uses a pretrained solution; if not, claiming faster convergence than NUTS is unfair. Time comparisons may not include pre-training time (Reviewer 2).
- Several missing baselines and comparisons: Deep Ensembles and Multi-SWAG should be compared for multi-modal posteriors (Reviewer 4); variational Gaussian approximations like BayesByBackprop should be compared (Reviewer 4); SGHMC (Chen 2014) and adaptively weighted SGLD (Deng 2022) are suggested as necessary baselines for multi-modal and high-dimensional problems (Reviewer 3); SGLD is mentioned in related work but never used in experiments (Reviewer 1); MCMC baselines (e.g., Zhang et al.) should be included, and visualization of weight/function space to demonstrate diverse modes (Reviewer 2). LA is missing from Table 3 without explanation (Reviewer 1).
- The method is only tested on classification tasks; no regression tasks are evaluated (Reviewer 1). How the split in CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 into 5/50 classes is decided is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- Several networks diverged or failed without further details (e.g., VGG on CIFAR-100); this seems strange (Reviewer 1).
- Inference time comparisons raise questions: LA’s inference time is even less than MAP; the proposed method’s inference time is less than SWAG; it is not clear if the proposed method uses Bayesian model averaging (BMA) during inference (Reviewer 2).
- It is unclear whether applying the exact GPS to the numerical examples would improve results, and by how much (Reviewer 3).
- The paper lacks a deeper discussion of why the proposed approach should be picked over SGD-MC or other established baselines (Reviewer 1).
