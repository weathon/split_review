## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) for time-series data. The method reparameterizes a source-pretrained model’s weights using Tucker-style tensor decomposition, factorizing them into a core tensor and factor matrices. During target adaptation, only the core tensor is fine-tuned, reducing overfitting and improving efficiency. A PAC-Bayesian generalization bound is provided. Experiments on real-world datasets (e.g., AdaTime benchmark) demonstrate that the method reduces fine-tuned parameters and inference MACs by over 90% while maintaining performance, and can be integrated with several existing SFDA baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of tensor decomposition for time-series SFDA** – builds on concepts from Monarch architecture and block tensor train methods (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear relevance and significance** – problem of efficient adaptation with limited target data and on-device settings is well motivated. The method is simple and efficient, and connections to Lottery Ticket Hypothesis are drawn (Reviewers 2, 5, 6).
- **Thorough experiments** with multiple datasets and models; clear improvements over state-of-the-art (Reviewer 2). Also sufficient analysis of limitations (Reviewer 1).
- **Solid theoretical backing** – PAC-Bayesian bound provided for the selective fine-tuning strategy (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- **Good presentation and structure** – well-written, definitions and equations clearly laid out (Reviewers 2, 6). Supplementary materials are detailed (Reviewer 1).
- **Compatibility** – method can be integrated with various SFDA baselines to improve adaptation efficiency (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Weak connection to time-series specificity** – Multiple reviewers (2, 3, 6) note that the method appears to be a general SFDA approach rather than being tailored for time-series. No clear leverage of time-series properties (e.g., temporal structure) is provided, and the justification for channel-wise decomposition lacks theoretical grounding in time-series characteristics. The authors should clarify why their method is particularly suited for time-series or demonstrate its universality on vision datasets (Reviewers 2, 3, 6).
2. **Insufficient description of target domain adaptation** – The loss functions, optimization algorithm, and hyperparameters used for fine-tuning on the target domain are not presented. The paper needs to be self-contained and specific about the adaptation process to enable reproducibility (Reviewers 3, 6). Additionally, the number of fine-tuning epochs (2–3) is stated without justification; evidence linking this choice to dataset/model scale is missing (Reviewer 1).
3. **Missing comparisons and citations** – Several important baselines are not compared:
   - LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with similar parameter freezing/tuning – this would improve fairness (Reviewer 3).
   - Other foundation-model-style approaches (e.g., Tang et al., CVPR 2024; Litrico et al., CVPR 2023; Tang et al., ICLR 2023; Ding et al., CVPR 2022) are not cited or compared (Reviewer 3).
   - No ERM baseline is included in Table 1, hindering clarity on improvements (Reviewer 6).
   - The authors should discuss connections to Monarch (Dao et al., 2022) and Tensor Train architectures (Qiu et al., 2024) in related work (Reviewer 4).
4. **Theoretical assumptions limitations** – (Reviewer 4) The PAC-Bayesian analysis assumes the posterior \(Q(S)\) is an isotropic Gaussian matching the prior \(P\), which is unrealistic. A more realistic upper bound should incorporate weight differences and the distance between isotropic Gaussian and true posterior, but the distance metric remains undefined. (Reviewer 6) Questions the rationality of replacing the prior distribution \(P\) and sampled data \(S\) with the source network \(\mathcal{W}_{src}\) and fine-tuned network \(\mathcal{W}_{trg}\) in the PAC-Bayesian framework, originally designed for same-distribution generalization error.
5. **Justification for Tucker decomposition** – Reviewers 1 and 4 note that the paper does not provide sufficient reasoning for choosing Tucker-style decomposition over other tensor decompositions (e.g., CP, Tensor Train, other low-rank methods). While related work includes ablations, they are more empirical and do not constitute a direct justification. The insights in lines 201–204 (the linear operation as a sequence of sub-operations) need a more complete explanation for the backward pass; a proof in the appendix is requested (Reviewer 1). Also, line 238 does not reiterate the reasoning sufficiently (Reviewer 1).
6. **Limited novelty** – (Reviewer 6) Parameter decomposition for domain adaptation is a prevalent technique; the core idea of reducing parameter space is not new. The paper should better articulate the specific novel contribution beyond combining Tucker decomposition and selective fine-tuning. (Reviewer 4) The SFDA performance compared to LoRA aligns with expected results from existing work, making it somewhat incremental.
7. **Minor issues** – 
   - Typo after Eq. 3: “where” explains symbol \(V\) instead of \(U\) (Reviewer 1).
   - Tables and plots in results are too small (need 200% zoom). Please select the most important results to present clearly (Reviewer 3).
   - (Reviewer 2) A question about inference time comparison before and after factorization: does the reduced parameter count reduce inference time, or does the factorization overhead increase it?
   - (Reviewer 5) Can the proposed method be transferred to general DA tasks? If not, why? Also, how does it relate to selective fine-tuning of only BN layers or feature modules? Can it unify these methods?
   - (Reviewer 5) The theorem seems applicable to any selective fine-tuning method; what is the advantage of the proposed decomposition over others derived from the theorem?
