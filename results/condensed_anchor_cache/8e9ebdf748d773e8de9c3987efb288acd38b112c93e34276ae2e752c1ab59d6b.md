## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Cubic Decay Reservoir Network (CuDeRes) for anomaly detection in 3D Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) data. Normal GPR data are segmented into blocks, each fitted by CuDeRes using three reservoirs with spatial decay to capture multi-directional dynamics. The fitted models form a “Model Depot”; anomalies are detected via model dissimilarity (p-norm distance) and then clustered to identify anomaly types. Experiments on a real-world (non-public) GPR dataset show an F1-score of 91.9%, outperforming Patchcore-3D, STEAL, 3D VAE, and MemAE. Reviewers find the data modality interesting and the progressive blurring operation novel, but raise major concerns about reproducibility, missing baselines, insufficient ablations, lack of computational complexity analysis, and limited evaluation of anomaly typing.

### Strengths
- **Efficient use of limited normal data** – The framework requires only a small amount of normal data, making it practical for real-world applications where labeled anomalies are scarce.  
- **Novel application of multi-directional reservoirs** – The CuDeRes architecture incorporates three reservoirs with spatial decay to capture multi-directional dynamics in 3D GPR data (reviewer 1). The progressive blurring operation (blurring each pixel based on already blurred neighbors) is also considered interesting and specific (reviewer 2).  
- **Comprehensive experimental validation** – The model is tested across various anomaly types and compared to multiple baselines (Patchcore-3D, STEAL, 3D VAE, MemAE), demonstrating both classification and clustering performance (reviewer 1).  
- **Capability to distinguish anomaly types** – The method can differentiate between buried objects (e.g., manholes, pipes) using clustering algorithms like K-Means, Agglomerative, and Fuzzy C-Means (reviewer 4).  
- **Practical relevance** – Clear potential for structural health monitoring, especially for roads and urban infrastructure (reviewer 1).  
- **Well written** (reviewers 2 and 3), though reviewer 4 finds some parts difficult to follow (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Reproducibility and dataset issues** – Experiments are performed on a single, non-public dataset; no code is released. This makes it impossible for other researchers to compare or assess the method’s quality (reviewer 2). Furthermore, the paper lacks detailed description of the GPR data (device, frequency, soil type, depth, units), hampering assessment of generalizability (reviewer 4).  
- **Missing major baselines** – The related work mentions Liu et al. 2024 and Zhou et al. 2024 as close GPR-specific methods, but these are not included in the experiments (reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient ablation studies** – The paper does not ablate key components of CuDeRes. Missing ablations include:
  - Removing the spatial decay mechanism or using single-direction fitting instead of multi-directional (reviewer 1).  
  - Blurring based on observed neighbor intensities rather than already blurred values (reviewer 2).  
  - Testing alternative distance metrics (e.g., cosine similarity, Mahalanobis) or varying reservoir sizes (reviewer 1).  
- **Lack of computational complexity analysis** – The method is motivated by reducing computational demands compared to 3D-CNNs, yet no analysis of FLOPs, run-time, memory footprint is provided for either training or inference (reviewers 1 and 3). A theoretical or actual comparison with 3D-CNNs is needed to support efficiency claims (reviewer 3).  
- **Parameter sensitivity and tuning** – Performance depends on manually set parameters (reservoir size, spatial decay rate, especially θ), but no systematic sensitivity study or guidelines for optimal selection are given (reviewer 1). For example, reviewer 2 asks for a full sensitivity analysis on θ (currently only tested against θ=0). Reviewer 4 questions why the same decay coefficient θ is used for all three directions.  
- **Evaluation of anomaly typing (clustering)** – The clustering step for identifying anomaly types is not thoroughly evaluated. The paper lacks a confusion matrix, case studies, and analysis of how often the model confuses similar anomaly types (e.g., small void vs. looseness). The impact of different clustering algorithms is not explored (reviewer 1).  
- **Distance metric choice** – The p-norm distance for comparing models is not justified; alternative metrics (cosine similarity, Mahalanobis, learned adaptive metrics) are not considered. This may limit sensitivity to subtle anomaly patterns (reviewer 1).  
- **Unclear design choices and presentation issues**:
  - Why signal decay is assumed along the x and y directions (in addition to depth z) and why the decay coefficient is the same in all directions (reviewer 4).  
  - The segmentation step (equation 10) is not explained; it is unclear how data blocks are segmented and whether overlapping blocks are used (reviewer 4).  
  - The derivation leading to equation 15 equaling zero is unclear – it seems to imply W1_out and W2_out are everywhere equal, which needs justification (reviewer 4).  
  - The model is characterized as “next point prediction” but actually uses a blurred input version; a characterization as “reconstruction” may be more appropriate (reviewer 2).  
  - The description of random parameters W^x, W^y, W^z only vaguely states they satisfy the “Echo State Property” (reviewer 2).  
  - There are typos (e.g., “U is a specific data blocks”) and the writing is sometimes difficult to follow (reviewer 4).  
- **Disagreement on novelty and difficulty** – Reviewer 4 finds the novelty not high, noting that the example GPR signals appear quite distinct from each other (making the problem not very challenging for deep learning). Other reviewers (1,2,3) consider the method interesting and novel.
