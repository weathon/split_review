## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses heterogeneous multi-teacher knowledge distillation by proposing Multi-Expert Collaboration (MEC). It uses a student model with multiple expert classifiers to improve independence of heterogeneous knowledge, and an anchor-based Helmholtz Free Energy (HFE) self-normalization strategy to align knowledge across experts, ensuring consistent energy levels. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 show significant accuracy improvements over existing methods. Reviewers note the problem is important and the approach is sound, but raise concerns about novelty, experimental scope, and practical relevance.

### Strengths
- The problem of heterogeneous knowledge independence and alignment in multi-teacher distillation is important, especially in the era of foundation models.
- The proposed method (multi-expert classifiers within the student + HFE self-normalization) is intuitive and theoretically grounded in energy-based principles.
- Achieves substantial accuracy gains (over 10% average improvement) on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 compared to existing heterogeneous multi-teacher KD methods.
- Ablation studies clearly show the contribution of each component (Multi-Expert Representation Learning, Classifier Adaptation Learning, HFE constraint).
- Extensive experiments with multiple baseline methods and two dataset scales (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100) provide strong empirical evidence.
- The approach addresses both independence and alignment issues, which is a comprehensive solution.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The idea of shared representation learning and decoupled distillation already appears in prior work (e.g., Co-KA). Multi-expert representation learning (KL scattering + projection to a common feature space) is standard in domain adaptation and other literature. The overall technical contribution is limited, with some reviewers questioning whether the gains come from the modified inference (multi-headed HFE selection) rather than the KD training itself.
- **Experimental limitations:** Experiments are restricted to classification tasks on small-scale datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100) using ResNet-18. Gains may diminish at larger scales (e.g., with more data per expert or overlapping classes). No validation on multi-task setups, domain shifts, or real-world scenarios where experts come from different sources/random models. Missing comparisons with non-distillation multi-task learning methods to contextualize benefits.
- **Practicality:** The multi-classifier architecture and HFE normalization are computationally intensive, limiting applicability in real-time or resource-constrained environments. No quantitative comparison of training/inference time or computational overhead with baseline methods is provided.
- **Motivation and meaning:** One reviewer argues that if all labels are available (as in the experiments, Eq 11), training a single model from scratch is simpler and more efficient than training 5 teachers and fusing them. The need for knowledge distillation in this setting is questioned.
- **Clarity and presentation:** The HFE self-normalization strategy is not explained intuitively; the paper includes advanced mathematics without sufficient step-by-step illustration. Typos and a caption error regarding "Knowledge distillation" in the abstract are noted. Some reviewers found the overall presentation (e.g., figures, clarity of pipeline) lacking.
- **Limited comparative analysis:** No comparison with state-of-the-art KD methods beyond those listed, and no comparisons with more recent advanced methods. The analysis does not consider overlapping classes, different amounts of data per expert, or other realistic variations.
- **Minority negative reviewer (Reviewer 4):** This reviewer explicitly finds the work “meaningless” in the fully-supervised setting, arguing that training from scratch is simpler and achieves similar performance. They also question whether HFE is just an argmax operation. This contrasts with the more positive assessments of other reviewers who value the approach.
- **Scalability questions:** It is unclear how the framework performs as the number of experts increases – does performance degrade? Are there hardware/software requirements? No analysis provided.
- **Missing task generality:** Effectiveness should be tested on object detection, segmentation, or other tasks beyond classification to demonstrate versatility.
