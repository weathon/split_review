## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes the Simulation-Heuristics Model (SHM), a dual-process framework for intuitive physics: an Intuitive Physics Engine (IPE) dominates for short simulations, while a heuristic-based approach takes over beyond a time boundary. This is motivated by a pouring-marble task with varied conditions, where human predictions show two error patterns and IPE correlation diminishes for longer simulations. A linear heuristic model fit to simulation data replicates human behavior better beyond a threshold.

### Strengths

- Introduces an original dual-process framework (SHM) that integrates two previously separate computational methods into a unified model.
- Addresses original questions about how humans reason (simulation vs. heuristics) and how often they employ each strategy.
- Includes a human user study with a pouring-marble task that has more diverse physical properties than prior work (one reviewer noted the scenario is more complicated).
- The paper is well-written and organized, with clear argumentation and appealing figures (according to one reviewer).
- One reviewer (score 8, confidence 2) was notably more positive, finding the research topic interesting and the modeling effective.

### Weaknesses

- **Not suitable for ICLR**: Lacks AI/ML elements and learning representation; primarily human experiments. Better suited for CogSci or another conference (R1).
- **Only one task (pouring)**: Task scenarios are too simple and results may not generalize. The claim of a systematic methodology for learning heuristics is premature without at least one additional task (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer specifically recommends adding another task with similar settings (R2).
- **Missing VLM comparison**: How well can existing VLMs perform on the proposed tasks? (R1)
- **Poor writing/presentation**: Contributions and results are buried in details (R3). [conf=4]
- **Heuristic model issues**: The linear (quartic) heuristic model (Equation 2) may not adequately represent physical simulations; it is unclear whether other heuristics (e.g., neural networks) were considered or would perform better. What are the inputs to the heuristic model? (R3) [conf=4]
- **Time threshold selection not validated**: The switching threshold (68.2° simulation angle) was identified via grid search, but it is unclear whether a holdout validation set was used. With only 43 participants, this raises concerns about overfitting and statistical significance. (R3) [conf=4]
- **How can SHM outperform IPE?**: If the heuristic is trained to mimic or predict the simulation (Equation 1), it is not obvious how SHM can surpass IPE in matching human judgments. What data was the heuristic trained on? (R3)
- **No statistical tests**: The paper reports correlations and RMSE for models (Heuristic, IPE, SHM) but provides no statistical comparison (e.g., significance tests). (R3, R4)
- **Model fitting is unclear**: The objective of the grid search (optimizing threshold, IPE noise, and heuristic parameters) is not stated. Were separate cohorts used for model fitting and model evaluation? (R4)
- **Heuristic fitted to simulation, not human data**: The linear heuristic is trained on simulation data, not human judgments. This may not accurately represent human heuristics since humans have not seen those examples. (R4)
- **Alternative explanations not ruled out**: The findings could be consistent with a biased or miscalibrated physics simulation (e.g., wrong friction, mass parameters) that produces angle-dependent error, rather than a dual-process switch. (R4)
- **Time is a poor proxy for simulation difficulty**: Time alone may not capture other factors affecting simulation cost. (R4)
- **Figure 1c confusing**: Red is used for both the mean angle estimate and the A-shape points, causing visual confusion. (R4)
- **No code provided**, limiting reproducibility. (R4)
- **Generality of the dual-process claim**: Can the model handle scenarios where the boundary cannot be described with a single parameter? (R2)
