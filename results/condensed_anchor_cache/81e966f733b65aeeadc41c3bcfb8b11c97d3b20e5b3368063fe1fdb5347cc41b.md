## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a cross-modality contrastive distillation (CMCD) framework that uses CMD and CMC losses to exploit both positive and negative correspondences from paired but unlabeled data. It provides theoretical analysis linking downstream test error to the distance between source and target distributions and reports consistent gains of 2–3% across modalities (image, sketch, depth, audio) on recognition and segmentation benchmarks. Reviewers generally appreciate the practical problem setting and the breadth of experiments, but raise significant concerns about limited novelty, insufficient theoretical depth, missing experimental details, inadequate ablation, and lack of justification for why source-modality SSL benefits target fine-tuning. One reviewer (R1, rating 6) was more positive, viewing the approach as simple and effective with a solid theoretical component; the other three (R2, R3, R4, rating 5) were more critical, particularly of novelty and empirical validation.

### Strengths
- The approach is simple, reasonable, and effective across diverse cross-modal transfer settings.
- The paper provides theoretical analysis on error bounds and discusses how modality distance impacts performance.
- The writing is clear and easy to follow.
- The considered scenario (unpaired, unlabeled data) is practical for memory- and privacy-restricted settings and may stimulate follow-up research.
- Experiments cover multiple modalities (image, sketch, depth, audio) and two downstream tasks (recognition, segmentation), demonstrating superiority over baselines.
- One reviewer (R2) specifically considered the CMD and CMC losses novel; another (R3) noted the convergence analysis and detailed experimental settings as strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (R1, R3, R4; R2 disagrees):** The method is largely a straightforward adaptation of existing techniques: Eq. (2) adapts self-supervised distillation [Fang et al., 2021] to the cross-modal setting, and Eq. (3) is directly the CLIP loss [Radford et al., 2021]. The core idea of contrastive learning for cross-modal distillation is not new, and the losses are identical to those used in multi-modal learning (e.g., [2-3] cited by R4). The paper does not clearly articulate how its implementation offers a significant departure from these earlier works. One reviewer (R2) considered the losses novel, but this is a minority view.
- **Theoretical analysis limitations (R1, R2):** The bound only states that test error is controlled by the distance between source and target distributions; it does not prove that the proposed losses minimize this bound more effectively than previous methods. The claim that “more common information leads to higher probability of distilling more information” lacks quantitative evidence (e.g., a correlation with estimated total variation distance).
- **Unclear motivation (R2, R3):** The paper does not convincingly explain why self-supervised learning on the source modality—followed by alignment and fine-tuning on the target—outperforms simply performing SSL on the target modality alone, given that paired (labeled) data is available in the target domain. This empirical result is counterintuitive, and no analysis (e.g., feature visualization) is provided to clarify the benefit. Additionally, the paper does not consider using unpaired data in the target modality or the scenario where no labeled target data exists (R3 Q1/Q2).
- **Missing experimental details (R1, R2, R3):** Key parameters are not reported in the main paper: the values of M and m (number of distillation pairs), learning rates and number of epochs for each stage (SSL, alignment, fine-tuning), architectures used per modality, optimizer, loss functions for each stage, and hardware/run-time details. The downstream task for each dataset in Table 1 is not clearly specified (R3 Q3).
- **Insufficient ablation and architecture sensitivity (R3):** Only ResNet backbones are tested (Table 2 shows similar performance across variants); experiments with transformer-based architectures are absent, raising concerns about generalizability and robustness. The possibility of using both CMD and CMC simultaneously is not explored (R1 question).
- **Negative correspondence usage unclear (R3):** The abstract claims to leverage both positive and negative correspondence, but the paper does not clearly demonstrate how negative samples are actually used in the cross-modal distillation process, as opposed to during source pre-training.
- **Missing comparisons with related works (R4):** The paper omits comparisons with recent multi-modal distillation methods that use online distillation strategies ([1-2] cited by R4) and with LiT [4], which employs a locked image model for multi-modal tuning and is conceptually similar to cross-modality distillation. These comparisons are needed to assess relative advantages and novelty.
- **Minor issues (R1):** Figure 1 lacks clear labels for which subfigure corresponds to which method. There is a grammar error after Eq. (13). “Efficiency” should be “effectiveness” in the sentence opening Section 4. The claimed 3% margin on Sketchy is overestimated: the best baseline SOCKET+LE reaches 71.33%, making the actual margin less than 2%.
