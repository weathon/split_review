## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q-Sparse, a method to train sparsely-activated LLMs by applying top-K sparsification to activations and straight-through estimator (STE) for gradient flow. It claims full activation sparsity for efficiency gains, presents an inference-optimal scaling law, and demonstrates effectiveness on full-precision and 1-bit (BitNet b1.58) models across training-from-scratch, continued-training, and fine-tuning. Experiments cover commonsense reasoning and MMLU, with about 40% sparsity matching dense baselines at the same model size and training tokens.

### Strengths
1. **Scaling law analysis** (Section 3) is well-motivated, innovative, and potentially valuable to the community, showing sparsely-activated LLMs outperform vanilla counterparts under the same inference compute budget (FLOPs).
2. **Simple yet effective method**: Q-Sparse uses a straightforward top-K sparsification that can be implemented on modern hardware (CPU, GPU), and its performance is comparable to dense baselines with the same model size and training tokens.
3. **Comprehensive experimental scope**: Evaluations cover both dense and quantized (1-bit) LLMs, reflecting realistic deployment settings, and include pre-training, continued-training, and fine-tuning scenarios.
4. **Performance gap narrows with scale**: The gap between sparsely-activated models and dense baselines decreases as model size increases.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of real-world efficiency measurements**: The paper claims significant efficiency gains in inference (L013, L023) but only supports this with FLOPs and activated parameters. Multiple reviewers (2, 4) stress that metrics like wall-clock speed, throughput, peak memory, and energy consumption are needed, and testing on diverse platforms (e.g., H100, A100, L40) is required. *Reviewer 2 (score 3, confident 4) is particularly concerned that without such measurements the efficiency claims are unsubstantiated; Reviewer 4 (score 6, confident 4) agrees that FLOPs correlation with real speed can be weak.*
2. **Batched inference bottlenecks unresolved**: In batched settings, activated columns vary per sample, and if they are uniformly distributed, the full weight matrix may still need to be loaded, negating I/O benefits (Reviewers 1, 4). Decoding steps can be memory-bound at small batch sizes, further limiting speedup. The paper does not analyze weight sharing for batched inputs or measure efficiency in batched decoding.
3. **Incomplete FLOP accounting**: The computational overhead of dynamically computing top-K activations (e.g., O(NlogK), not easily parallelizable) is not included in the reported FLOPs (Reviewer 4). This could significantly affect the fairness of efficiency comparisons.
4. **Limited evaluation domains**: Only commonsense reasoning and MMLU are evaluated. To ensure generality, reviewers request math (GSM8K, MATH) and code (HumanEval, MBPP) benchmarks. *Reviewer 2 (score 3) considers this a major gap given that Orca’s capability is designed for open-ended generation.*
5. **Missing comparisons to MoE and other architectures**: MoE is a widely used sparsely-activated model family, yet no direct accuracy or efficiency comparison is provided (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 also asks about the connection between the proposed scaling law for Q-Sparse and scaling laws for MoE.
6. **Insufficient model diversity in experiments**: Table 1 alone is not enough to demonstrate method effectiveness across different architectures and datasets (Reviewer 3). A stronger evaluation would include additional models (e.g., Llama 3 8B, as suggested by Reviewer 2) and more varied architectures.
7. **Theoretical justification for STE is weak**: It remains unclear why gradients should be maintained for non-activated neurons (Reviewer 1). The core sparsity principle lacks theoretical explanation and is only described via formulas (Reviewer 3). A more rigorous derivation or proof would strengthen the approach.
8. **Novelty concerns**: Reviewer 3 (score 5) considers the method "trick novelty" – a simple application of known ideas (top-K, STE) to LLM components – and finds the paper more analytical than truly novel. *This contrasts with Reviewer 4 (score 6), who praises the method’s simplicity and effectiveness.*
