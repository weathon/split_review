## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to use an approximation to the Vietoris–Rips complex—the witness complex—to integrate topological graph features into GNN optimization for adversarial robustness. It introduces the Witness Graph Topological Layer (WGTL), which encodes both local and global topological features controlled by a regularized topological loss. Experiments on citation graphs using GCN show some robustness improvements.

### Strengths
- Use of persistence diagrams to propose a topological defense against adversarial attacks in GNN learning.
- The stability of the proposed pipeline is deduced from the known stability properties of persistence diagrams; theoretical analysis on stability for topology encodings and regularized topological loss is provided.
- The proposed strategy is intuitive for enhancing robustness in graph neural networks.
- Overall the draft is well-written (reviewers 2, 3) and improvements in robustness over vanilla GCN appear significant.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons.** Only one baseline was used per task; the paper primarily compares with vanilla GCN. More SOTA defense methods are needed (e.g., graph attention models, GCN/GNN with topological regularizers based on standard Vietoris–Rips complexes, methods that explicitly leverage higher-order neighborhood or connectivity patterns). One reviewer (score 6) stresses that simply demonstrating improvements over vanilla GCN is not convincing. A minority reviewer (score 5) also notes the lack of non-topological baselines.
- **Limited architecture evaluation.** Despite claiming integration with five GNNs, the entire set of experiments is conducted using GCN. The significant improvement may be attributed to GCN’s naive mechanism; experiments on more recent architectures (e.g., Graph Attention Networks, modern GCN variants) are needed to show generalizability.
- **Limited graph types.** The method has only been validated on homophilous citation graphs. It is unclear whether it would perform on heterophilous or molecular graphs, which have different characteristics.
- **Drawbacks of the witness complex not convincingly addressed.** The witness complex introduces instability due to landmark choice, hyperparameter sensitivity (number of landmarks, epsilon in epsilon‑net), higher computational cost for construction, and lack of functoriality. The paper does not explain how to overcome these concerns, particularly how to choose the number of landmarks.
- **Unclear why standard Vietoris–Rips complexes (with GPU acceleration) could not be used** for the same defense tasks.
- **Theoretical and formulation issues.** Formulations of theoretical results are not clearly stated; the dimensions of computed persistence diagrams are not specified; the reported computational complexity is inaccurate (e.g., it does not include the cost of geodesic distance on the graph). A number of specific errors appear in the text (e.g., “asymmetric matrix A” should be symmetric; definition of \(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\) leads to reversed inclusions; missing dimensions; missing reference to Barannikov 1994 for the first introduction of persistence barcodes and calculation algorithm).
- **Missing details.** The threat model should be moved to the main body (not just in the appendix). The auxiliary graph reconstructed from transformer output is not clearly explained.
- **Reviewer disagreement on contribution and evaluation strength:** two reviewers (5, 5) find the evaluation weak; one reviewer (6) acknowledges significant improvement but still has major concerns about experimental breadth.
