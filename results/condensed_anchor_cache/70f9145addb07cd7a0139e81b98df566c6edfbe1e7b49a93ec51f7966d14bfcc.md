## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper characterizes necessary and sufficient conditions for linear convergence of gradient descent on smooth (possibly non-convex) functions. It proves that the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition is necessary and sufficient for linear convergence in function values, and introduces the weak-quasi-strong-convexity (WQSC) condition as necessary and sufficient for linear convergence in distance to the solution set. Applications to neural networks are discussed but no experiments are provided.

### Strengths

- **Novelty of the PL necessity result**: Reviewers 1 and 4 consider the proof that the PL condition is necessary for linear convergence in function values to be novel and significant. Reviewer 4 notes that previous results considered narrower function classes and that the arguments appear correct and clearly stated.
- **WQSC condition broadens the problem class**: Reviewer 1 highlights that the WQSC condition is less restrictive than strong convexity and expands the set of problems for which gradient descent converges linearly in distance. Reviewer 1 also remarks that the result is simple and connects to theoretical insights (e.g., Theorem 10 in the appendix).
- **Clarification of conditions**: Reviewer 2 acknowledges that the paper clarifies the necessary and sufficient conditions for gradient descent linear convergence, covering both function value and iterate convergence.
- **Correctness and clarity**: Reviewers 1 and 4 find the results presented precisely and the reasoning sound (with Reviewer 4 assigning soundness 4, Reviewer 1 assigning soundness 4).
- **Disagreement over significance**: The above strengths are asserted by Reviewers 1 and 4; however, Reviewers 2 and 3 strongly dispute them, arguing the contributions are incremental and not novel (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty versus existing work (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)**:
  - Reviewers 2 and 3 state that Theorem 5 is essentially identical to Theorem 5 of Abbaszadehpeivasti et al. (2023). Reviewer 3 argues that the lower-bounded curvature condition claimed by the authors is a direct consequence of Lipschitz gradient continuity, making the additional assumption redundant, and asks for a concrete example where Lipschitz gradient does not imply that condition.
  - Reviewer 3 states that Theorem 7 differs from Theorem 5 in Alimisis (2024a) only by assuming a convex solution set instead of a singleton, which does not constitute a significant advancement; the core arguments and proof techniques are the same, and the added complexity is minimal.
  - Reviewer 2 argues that the WQSC condition reduces to the weak-strongly convex condition of Alimisis (2024a) when the solution set is a singleton, and that linear convergence under weak-strong convexity is already established.
  - Reviewer 4 describes the overall contribution as incremental, extending known results to convex solution sets without introducing new analytical techniques or insights.
  - Reviewer 2 questions whether the authors are the first to prove necessity of PL and notes insufficient comparison with the literature.

- **Proof error in Corollary 6 (Reviewer 2)**: Reviewer 2 identifies a contradiction: the step size η is first fixed in (0,2/L) but later treated as a variable approaching zero, invalidating the proof. The reviewer also considers the corollary unnecessary, as the PL condition can be obtained directly by setting μ ≡ 2c/(2η+3η^2L) for fixed c and η.

- **Technical issues with step size definition (Reviewer 2)**: Reviewer 2 asks whether the step size range in line 42 should be (0,2/L) or [0,2/L], and whether the step length η in lines 269 and 346 is a constant or allowed to converge to zero. The reviewer suspects conflation of these two cases.

- **Lack of experimental validation (Reviewers 1, 4)**: Reviewer 1 requests experiments on a WQSC problem to demonstrate linear convergence. Reviewer 4 stresses that the paper is purely theoretical, that the discussion of neural networks is limited and relies on Lipschitz-gradient assumptions not typically satisfied in practice, and that empirical validation would strengthen the claims.

- **Underdeveloped related work (Reviewer 4)**: Reviewer 4 notes the absence of discussion of the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality and its connection to PL and error bounds, and states that the paper would benefit from better contextualization within the broader convergence literature.

- **Realism of assumptions (Reviewer 4)**: Reviewer 4 questions whether there exist modern deep learning problems with a convex set of minimizers (beyond toy examples), and whether the relaxed assumptions in this paper cover functions not already captured by the weak convexity assumptions in Abbaszadehpeivasti et al. (citing Drusvyatskiy & Paquette 2019).

- **Reference and reliability concerns (Reviewer 2)**: Reviewer 2 notes that many references are from arXiv (line 46) and recommends citing original or classic sources, raising concerns about reliability of the cited results.

- **Strong divergence in overall assessment**: While Reviewer 1 (score 8) finds the contributions highly significant and novel, Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 3) and Reviewer 4 (score 6) consider the work incremental and insufficient for a top venue like ICLR; Reviewer 3 explicitly states the contributions do not meet the bar.
