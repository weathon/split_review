## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces FairlyUncertain, an axiomatic benchmark for evaluating uncertainty estimates in fairness contexts. The authors posit two axioms: consistency (uncertainty estimates should be stable under small pipeline changes) and calibration (uncertainty estimates should match observed randomness). Through experiments on ten datasets (binary classification and regression) with XGBoost and other ensemble methods, they find: (1) a simple, theoretically justified uncertainty estimate (natural uncertainty) outperforms complex ensemble approaches in consistency and calibration; (2) abstaining from uncertain binary predictions reduces error but does not alleviate demographic imbalances; (3) incorporating calibrated uncertainty in regression improves distributional fairness without explicit interventions. The benchmark is open-source.

### Strengths

- Addresses an underexplored and timely intersection of fairness and uncertainty in machine learning.
- Provides a standardized, theoretically grounded benchmark with clear axioms (Consistency Axiom 2.3, Calibration Axiom 2.4) that can guide evaluation.
- Extensive experiments across ten datasets and multiple tasks (binary classification, regression), using various uncertainty estimation methods (e.g., XGBoost ensembles, natural uncertainty).
- Open-source and extensible tool, encouraging community contributions.
- Interesting and surprising findings: abstention does not reduce demographic disparities in binary classification; regression fairness can improve without explicit interventions.
- Introduces Uncertainty-Aware Statistical Parity (UA-SP) for regression, a novel metric that incorporates uncertainty into CDF-based fairness evaluation.
- Good organization, clear motivation, and well-explained background/related work.
- Indirectly addresses model multiplicity concerns by evaluating consistency across hyperparameter variations.
- Abstention experiments and uncertainty-aware fairness metrics are practically useful.

### Weaknesses

- **Connection between fairness and the consistency/calibration axioms is insufficiently justified.** Reviewers note that sensitive attributes are not directly used in consistency or calibration metrics (e.g., consistency computes std dev across hyperparameter ensembles, calibration groups are defined by uncertainty levels, not demographic groups). The axioms appear more as general reliability criteria than fairness-specific properties. The paper would benefit from explicit scenarios where inconsistent or miscalibrated uncertainty systematically disadvantages a protected group.
- **Fairness component feels disconnected until Section 5.** The introduction of fairness alongside uncertainty should be made earlier, e.g., with a motivating example where uncertainty estimates are well-calibrated overall but biased against a specific group.
- **Paper is primarily an implementation/tool contribution.** One reviewer (score 5) argues it presents a framework and package rather than novel theoretical insights into fairness or uncertainty; others (score 6) find sufficient originality in the benchmark itself. This contrast should be noted.
- **Abstention analysis lacks depth.** While abstention reduces error but not demographic disparities, reviewers ask for deeper insight into why, and for discussion of conditions under which abstention could be counterproductive or introduce biases.
- **Limited model scope – primarily ensemble methods (XGBoost).** Extending to deep learning (MLPs, transformers) would test generalizability and align with current fairness research. Multiple reviewers raise this as a limitation.
- **Calibration metric implementation unclear.** How groups are formed for calibration (e.g., clustering of individuals with similar uncertainty estimates) is not explained. Also, the paper does not connect to the rich calibration literature (e.g., reliability diagrams, expected calibration error).
- **Formalisms issues.** Definition 2.2 includes conflicting phrasing: “differ only in hyperparameter settings” versus “there is some j …” (implying a single hyperparameter differs). Also, notation f(P) on L127 seems to misuse f (output of pipeline) as a function of P.
- **Disagreement with uncertainty type classification (Table 1).** Characterizing A/B types as unmeasurable and C/D/E as estimable from a fixed sample is conditional on distributional assumptions. Clarifying the underlying data-generating process would strengthen the contribution.
- **Consistency definition may not be a fairness axiom.** One reviewer (score 5) argues it is more like a Lipschitz condition; a fairness-specific axiom might require invariance of uncertainty to protected attributes, not just hyperparameters. Another reviewer notes that “a model that always returns 0 is consistent” by the given definition, which is counterintuitive.
- **Consistency metric (max std dev over individuals) is non-robust.** Using a 90th percentile or similar would be more stable.
- **UA-SP assumption of normal distribution is overly specific.** In binary settings, the CDF comparison for all outcomes in Y may not be meaningful; a more general formulation is needed.
- **Mixed objective in Section 5 (combining statistical parity and equalized odds) is unusual.** Standard practice selects one or the other.
- **Tables 2 and 4 lack thorough analysis.** Why certain methods perform better or worse across datasets is not discussed. Definition of Disparate Impact in Table 4 is not given.
- **Appendix demographic breakdowns should be integrated into main analysis.**
- **Questions about comparison with existing fairness evaluation literature** (beyond Section 6.1), and how this uncertainty-focused framework aids practical selection of fairness measures and mitigation strategies.
- **Reproducibility concerns** with complex architectures due to implicit assumption of consistency under slight hyperparameter variations.
- **Generalizability beyond ten datasets** is questioned; results may vary with more diverse data.
- **Why regression improves fairness without explicit interventions** remains unexplained.
- **Inclusion of additional fairness interventions and calibration metrics** is requested.
- **Table 1 framework does not tightly connect** to the rest of the paper.
- **How do results change if each fairness metric is optimized individually** (rather than combined)?
