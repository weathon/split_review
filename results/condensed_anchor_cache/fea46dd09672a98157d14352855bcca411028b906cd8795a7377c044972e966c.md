## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a Bayesian Non-negative Decision Layer (BNDL) that reformulates the final layer of a deep neural network classifier as a non-negative factor analysis using Gamma-distributed latent variables and Weibull variational inference. The aim is to improve both uncertainty estimation and interpretability (through sparsity and disentanglement) while maintaining or improving accuracy. Experiments on CIFAR and ImageNet benchmarks are reported. Reviewers found the methodology sound and the theoretical identifiability analysis interesting, but raised concerns about the experimental comparisons, justification of evaluation metrics, statistical significance, clarity of the Bayesian framing, and missing connections to related work (last-layer BNNs, evidential deep learning, NMF interpretability). The paper also contains numerous typos.

### Strengths

- The idea is rigorous and the implementation introduces minimal overhead over an existing network (R1).
- The method is generic enough to be added to arbitrary deep architectures (R1, R2).
- It performs well under varying experimental settings and architectures, and can keep or improve upon its non-interpretable counterpart while greatly improving interpretability (R2). One reviewer noted limited familiarity with SOTA in interpretability but found results significant (R2).
- The theoretical analysis (Section 4) demonstrating partial identifiability under certain assumptions is an interesting addition (R3, R4).
- The sanity testing for uncertainty/accuracy correlation in Section 5.1.1 is appreciated (R3).
- The proposed BNDL appears advantageous for uncertainty evaluation and disentangled representation learning (R4).
- The manuscript appears to be largely human-written (low ZeroGPT scores) (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Uncertainty estimation conceptual issues:** The distinction between epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty is not clearly defined; it is unclear whether the proposed model ensures epistemic uncertainty goes to zero in the large data limit (R1).
- **Uncertainty metric unclear:** The computation of \(n_{ac}, n_{au}, n_{ic}, n_{iu}\) in the uncertainty evaluation metric is not clearly explained (R1).
- **Model equations unclear:**
  - The difference between \(f_{NN}\) and \(f_{\lambda}\) in Eq. (9) is not well-defined (R1).
  - The transition from Gaussian to Gamma distributions in Section 3.2 is unclear; the sentence "Both \(\theta\) and \(\Phi\) are sampled from a Gaussian distribution" seems to contradict the Gamma assumption (R1).
  - Eq. (4): the first term inside the integral should be \(p(y|\Phi, \Theta)\) (R3).
  - Eq. (12): the left-hand side should be \(p(Y|X)\), as the right-hand side marginalizes over \(\theta\) and \(\Phi\) (R2).
- **Sparsity threshold arbitrary:** The fixed threshold of \(10^{-5}\) for sparsity measurement is arbitrary; it should be derived from an analysis of the weight distribution (R1).
- **Architecture differences unexplained:** The large performance difference when applying BNDL to ResNet versus ViT is not sufficiently explained (R1).
- **Limited baselines:** Experiments are limited to a small set of interpretability baselines (R2); comparisons to recent related methods (e.g., Wang et al. 2024, Duan et al. 2024) are mentioned only in passing (R2; see also R3).
- **Bayesian framing is crude:** Claiming that interpreting softmax input as a delta distribution over a latent variable is “Bayesian” is insufficient; a Bayesian approach requires a well-specified prior and posterior inference. The paper lacks justification for why the model qualifies as Bayesian beyond being probabilistic (R2).
- **Missing related work:**
  - The paper does not discuss last-layer Bayesian neural networks (LL-BNNs) (e.g., Harrison et al. 2024) or evidential deep learning / prior networks (e.g., Sensoy et al. 2018, Amini et al. 2020, Malinin et al. 2020) despite mechanical similarities (R2).
  - No reference to Guo et al. (2017) on overconfidence (R2).
  - Missing citation for DNNs and non-negative factor analysis in interpretability (e.g., NeurIPS 2022 paper) (R4).
- **Theorem 1 presentation:** Theorem 1 is not a theorem (as per statistical/ML literature standards) and its “proof” is merely discussion; the presentation should be changed (R4). Also, \(e_{(k)}\) is not introduced (R2).
- **Experimental comparison issues:**
  - Comparing to non-Bayesian/point-estimate models is not meaningful given the goal of uncertainty estimation; the paper should focus on comparison to Bayesian methods (R3).
  - PAvPU gains in Table 1 for ResNet are marginal compared to recent approaches (BM and CARD); statistical significance (p-values, number of repetitions) is not provided (R3, R4).
  - Sparsity values for competing approaches are not shown in Section 5.1.2 (R3).
  - No comparison to competing approaches in Section 5.2 (interpretability visualizations and Table 2) (R3).
- **Disentanglement metric not justified:** Using unsupervised disentanglement as a proxy for interpretability is not justified; disentanglement does not imply interpretability, and disentangled features may correspond to spurious correlations (R3).
- **Only one metric each for uncertainty and disentanglement:** Additional metrics would be beneficial (R4).
- **Lack of experiment details:** More verbal details on exactly how all experiments were performed are needed (R4). Also, the practical runtime increase compared to a simple fully-connected layer is not reported (R2).
- **Typos:** The paper contains numerous typos and missing articles, e.g.: row 178 "uncertainty-refers" (hyphen), 282 "Killback–Leibler", 345 "descirbed", 355 "we uses", 371 "Perforamce"; many equations lack proper punctuation; broken citations (e.g., Dosovitskiy et al. 2020, Kingma & Ba); missing articles throughout (R1, R2, R3, R4).
