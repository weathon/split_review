## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers evaluated the paper, with scores 5, 8, 6, 5. The paper studies the geometry of token-level internal representations in LLMs using intrinsic dimension (ID), neighborhood overlap (NO), and cosine similarity across layers, with experiments on three open-source LLMs (Pythia, Mistral, LLAMA) under shuffled and unshuffled prompts. Reviewers generally find the experiments sound and the topic interesting, but they diverge on the clarity of motivation, novelty relative to prior work (especially Cheng et al. 2023), and practical utility for interpretability. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive and sees potential significance if weaknesses are addressed; others are more critical about contribution and framing.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and plots are clear (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Experiments are methodologically sound, extensive, and thorough across multiple metrics, models, and noise levels (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The thoroughness is praised as “careful observational data” (Reviewer 3).
- The work advances prior research by analyzing geometry at the **token level** rather than treating a whole prompt as a single point in representation space (Reviewer 4). This finer-scale perspective is a valuable advance over prior work that summarized prompts as a single point (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The use of token shuffling as a comparison baseline for out-of-distribution input is useful and provides a contrast to structured in-distribution input, revealing how geometry changes when sequences are disrupted (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Metrics studied (ID, NO, cosine similarity) and the four main results fit together, telling a coherent story about information reduction across layers (Reviewer 3).
- The correlation between ID and loss, and the observation that ID peaks in early-to-middle layers, are interesting and potentially significant findings (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The neighborhood overlap metric and the comparison with shuffling provide a useful baseline, suggesting that observed consistency relates to real processing rather than architectural artifacts (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer (score 8) finds most experiments original and offering novel results, and believes the work could be significant if motivation and contextualization issues are resolved (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing motivation and unclear contribution (strongly emphasized by Reviewers 1, 2, 4).** The high-level motivation for studying embedding geometry to understand LLMs is not clear: how does this advance our understanding of what LLMs have learned or how they behave? (Reviewer 2). The takeaways from the observed geometric changes are not connected to model behavior or control (Reviewer 1). The paper does not clarify what one should do with the information that these metrics change in specific ways (Reviewer 1). The connection to practical interpretability (e.g., downstream task performance, attention patterns) is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work (Reviewers 3, 4).** The methodology and goals are extremely similar to **Cheng et al. 2023 (arXiv:2405.15471)** , which also studies geometric aspects of token representations, uses at least three target models in common, compares regular vs. shuffled prompts, examines loss–ID correlation, and measures ID and representation similarity between layers. The current paper feels like a relatively small advance (Reviewer 3). The metrics (cosine similarity, intrinsic dimension) are largely the same as in prior work, and while token-level analysis is new, many findings echo previous studies without substantially new insights (Reviewer 4). A systematic comparison to Cheng et al. is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient contextualization with prior work (Reviewers 1, 2).** Only a narrow sliver of related work on embedding geometry is mentioned; broader bodies of work studying LLM internals (probing, circuit discovery, feature attribution, mechanistic interpretability) are not discussed. The paper should position its geometric analysis within this literature and explain what complementary insights are offered (Reviewer 2). Missing references to David Bau’s work and mechanistic interpretability (Reviewer 1). The relationship to the “particles in a dynamical system” approach (Geshkovski et al. 2023; Cowsik et al. 2024) is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear or insufficient baseline conditions.** The shuffled condition is not the only relevant baseline; a comparison with a randomly initialized network would be informative (Reviewer 3). The claim that the shuffled case consistently yields lower NO than the structured case is not well supported by Figure 4, especially the average panel, which does not show a clear difference (Reviewer 4). This raises questions about the metric’s sensitivity and robustness.
- **Potential confound in the ID–loss correlation.** The result that prompts with higher ID have higher loss may simply reflect that high-ID prompts are more intrinsically complicated (e.g., longer) rather than revealing anything about model processing (Reviewer 3). The paper does not control for such confounds.
- **Uncertainty about training data for LLAMA and Mistral.** The experiments in Section 4.3 use the Pile dataset under the assumption that Pythia is trained on it but Mistral and LLAMA are not. It is not publicly known whether the Pile was used to train LLAMA 3 8B or Mistral; this uncertainty undermines conclusions drawn from that comparison, and must be made explicit (Reviewer 2).
- **Small sample size for most experiments (50 prompts maximum in Section 4.2).** This is exceptionally small in the context of the Pile and LLMs; the sampling procedure from the 2252 prompts after filtering is not explained (Reviewer 2). It is unclear whether results would generalize to a larger sample (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing explanation and intuition for metrics and diagrams.** The definition of cosine similarity could be removed for readability (Reviewer 1). The ID section and each metric section would benefit from a diagram to provide intuition (Reviewer 1). The shuffling algorithm (Algorithm 1) would be clearer with a diagram or example rather than pseudocode (Reviewer 1). The figure in Section 4.2 should explicitly note that LLAMA is the only model considered (Reviewer 2). Figure 3 left panel is mislabeled as “Angle” (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear interpretation of several experiments and results:**
  - What does “if the model is trained on a dataset that contains the input prompts, then it shows lower ID peaks and higher NO, suggesting better adaptability” mean? Adaptability to what? (Reviewer 1).
  - Why is it meaningful to progressively shuffle tokens? What do we expect to happen, and why do we care, given that text is not shuffled in practice? (Reviewer 2). The discussion in lines 490–494 does not answer this (Reviewer 2).
  - In Section 4.2.3, it is not stated which figure is being discussed (presumably Figure 4) (Reviewer 2).
  - The “distribution of tokens at the peak” paragraph (Figure 3) is not explained in Sections 3 or 4.2 as other experiments are; its interpretation is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - In Figure 5, why does Pythia show lower ID than other models in layers 1–20 but higher ID in later layers? (Reviewer 2).
  - What are “geometrical signatures of generalization” (line 107), and how are they relevant? (Reviewer 2).
  - The ID estimation details (n1, n2, TWO-NN estimator, assumptions, GRIDE usage) are not clearly described (Reviewer 2).
  - The shuffling algorithm: are blocks permuted or are tokens inside blocks permuted? The description is ambiguous; a concrete example is needed (Reviewer 2).
  - Why is “more comprehensive analysis required to confirm this statement” (line 404–405), and what kind of analysis? (Reviewer 2).
  - The statement that ID peaking at similar layers for OOD inputs “raises questions about the connection to the transformers’ architecture and the potential for formulating this phenomenon through analytic approaches” (lines 489–490) is vague; what specific questions? (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor errata:** line 183 typo/missing words; lines 487–489 incomplete sentence (Reviewer 2). First paragraph of introduction could be deleted (Reviewer 3). Some citations are from arXiv but were later published in peer-reviewed venues (e.g., Intrinsic dimension of data representations in deep neural networks was NeurIPS); update references (Reviewer 1). “Model transformers” should be “transformer models” in related work section titles (Reviewer 1).
