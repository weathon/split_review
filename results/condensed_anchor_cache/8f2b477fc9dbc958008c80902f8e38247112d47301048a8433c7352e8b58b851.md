## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReGRAF (Refinement via Gradient Flow), a training‑free module that can be added to auto‑prompting methods (e.g., PerSAM, Matcher) used with SAM. ReGRAF iteratively updates the query image’s visual embeddings by leveraging gradient flow from SAM’s mask decoder (logits and query embeddings) to better align with support‑image embeddings. The approach does not require learnable parameters. The authors provide theoretical analysis of convergence and convergence rate. Experiments cover semantic segmentation and part segmentation across five datasets, comparing against PerSAM‑F, Matcher, and their HQ‑SAM variants. ReGRAF is reported to consistently improve segmentation quality, but the gains are very small in most cases.

### Strengths
- Training‑free refinement that can be integrated into several auto‑prompting frameworks (PerSAM, Matcher) without additional training or architectural changes. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- Novel use of the decoder’s gradient flow to guide prompt sampling, reducing the need for manual prompt correction. (Reviewer 2)
- Theoretical guarantee of convergence and convergence rate, providing interpretability. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- Validated on both part and semantic segmentation datasets, showing broad applicability. (Reviewer 2)
- Minimal additional inference cost; Table 3 shows the running time of the iterative refinement is marginal compared to baselines. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper is well‑structured, with clear formulations and visualizations. (Reviewers 1,2,3; one reviewer (score 6) found it particularly easy to follow)
- The method alleviates the need for external human supervision, using the interactive correction ability of SAM itself. (Reviewer 2)
- Wealth of visualized results (e.g., Figures 4,5) illustrate cases of improvement (object‑to‑part hierarchy correction, recovery of missing components). (Reviewers 2,3)

### Weaknesses
- **Improvement magnitude is marginal.** In Tables 1 and 2, the vast majority of gains are below 0.5% (or below 1% except for two entries on one dataset). Given the inherent variance in segmentation metrics, these small increments provide weak evidence of effectiveness. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4 – consensus; one reviewer (score 6) also acknowledged this)
- **Lack of failure‑case analysis.** The paper only shows examples where ReGRAF improves the baseline; it does not show or quantify cases where performance degrades. A sample‑wise scatter plot of IoU before/after refinement would clarify the method’s advantages and limitations. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Insufficient hyperparameter study.** The method depends on several hyperparameters: the number of iterations T, an entropy regularization factor, and a step size. No sensitivity analysis or ablation is provided. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Missing ablation on number of refinement steps.** There is no experiment showing how performance changes with different numbers of iterations. (Reviewer 2)
- **Gradient inaccuracy with many iterations is poorly explained.** The paper states (Sec. “Hyperparameter setting”) that Equation (6) causes the gradient estimation in Equation (12) to become inaccurate with many iterations, but does not explain why (e.g., why approximation leads to inaccuracy, or why it should not converge near optimal). (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** Only PerSAM‑F, Matcher, and their HQ‑SAM variants are compared; other relevant one‑/few‑shot segmentation methods are omitted. The related‑work analysis is similarly narrow. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear what the method fundamentally addresses.** The qualitative examples show both semantic hierarchy correction (object→part) and detail correction (missing components), but the motivation and method explanation do not distinguish which problem ReGRAF is tackling. (Reviewer 2)
- **Performance on complex edges/thin objects is not evaluated.** The paper does not consider cases with fine structures, thin objects, or complex boundaries, where SAM often fails. (Reviewer 4; suggests related works on matting/dichotomous segmentation)
- **Specific counter‑example:** ReGRAF when integrated with HQ‑Matcher on COCO‑20i underperforms compared to the standard Matcher baseline, without explanation. (Reviewer 3)
- **Practical trade‑off:** Given the marginal improvements, the effort needed to tune hyperparameters for new tasks/datasets may not be justified. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Evaluation criteria are insufficient.** The paper relies solely on average IoU; more comprehensive criteria (e.g., distribution of improvements, per‑sample statistics) would better demonstrate the method’s effect. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority reviewer contrast:** One reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) was more positive, calling the paper easy to follow and noting marginal runtime cost, but still acknowledged the marginal improvements and lack of hyperparameter analysis. The other three (score 5) were less convinced, especially about the significance of the results and the depth of experimental analysis.
