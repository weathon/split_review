## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a new task, Interleaved Image-Text Comprehension (IITC), which requires models to answer questions using interleaved image and text that includes irrelevant information, and to ground the answer in one of several in-context images. The authors create the VEGA dataset (up to 8 000 tokens, 8 images, 295k multi-turn entries, 700 curated test questions) for IITC and a subtask, Image-Text Association (ITA). They benchmark multiple closed-source (e.g., Gemini-1.5-pro, GPT4V) and open-source MLLMs, showing only modest success. Fine-tuning Qwen-VL-Chat with a multi-task, multi-scale training strategy achieves 85.8% accuracy in image association and a 0.508 Rouge score. The dataset is drawn from scientific content (SciGraphQA). One reviewer (score 8) was more positive than the other three (scores 6, 6, 6).

### Strengths
- **First to study long-context interleaved image-text comprehension (IITC).** The task uniquely requires models to: (1) process interleaved images and text, (2) handle multiple images, (3) determine which image is relevant, and (4) ignore distracting information in both modalities. These features reflect real-world reading of image-text materials and fill a gap in existing datasets.
- **Large, well‑designed dataset.** VEGA contains 295k multi-turn entries with a curated test set of 700 questions, partitions for 4k and 8k token contexts, and thoughtful design elements such as explicit image index references (e.g., “[Picture 2](Figure. 3)”) that aid interpretability.
- **Broad evaluation.** The paper benchmarks both proprietary (Gemini-1.5-pro, GPT4V) and various open-source MLLMs, demonstrating that even state-of-the-art models achieve only moderate success, confirming the task’s difficulty.
- **Meaningful baseline.** Fine‑tuning Qwen-VL-Chat with a multi‑task, multi‑scale training strategy yields notable improvements on IITC and ITA, establishing a strong baseline for future work.
- **Honest reporting of limitations.** The authors explicitly show that training on VEGA does not improve performance on other VQA tasks, indicating that the gains are task‑specific.
- **Multi‑scale training is reasonable and effective (at least on ITA).** The training strategy is clearly described and its effectiveness is supported by ablation results.
- **Well‑structured and clear writing.** The main body of the paper is easy to read and understand.

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset construction appears artificial and lacks critical analyses.** There are no experiments showing how accuracy varies with number of images or context length (e.g., verifying that accuracy drops as context grows). The test set is created using similar procedures to the training data, raising concerns about in‑distribution overfitting and insufficient generalization evidence. (R1, R2)
- **Only one open‑source model (Qwen-VL-Chat) was fine‑tuned.** The training strategy—especially multi‑scale training—may be architecture‑dependent; its effectiveness has not been validated on other interleaved‑input VL models such as LLaVA‑interleaved or InternVL2. (R3, R4)
- **Missing key baselines and related dataset evaluations.** The evaluation omits strong interleaved‑understanding models (LLaVA‑interleaved, InternVL2) and does not test the fine‑tuned model on similar datasets (e.g., SlideVQA, MMLongBench-Doc, DUDE, DocVQA), limiting comparative insight and evidence of generalization. (R4)
- **ITA task’s real‑world applicability is questionable.** In realistic contexts like research papers, not every paragraph corresponds to an image; relevance is often implied rather than explicit, making ITA less directly useful. (R4)
- **Training method is not well motivated or connected to the literature.** The multi‑scale strategy is described without discussion of how it relates to similarly named approaches, and its design choices are insufficiently justified. (R1)
- **Potential quality issues in dataset construction.** The answer modification step (line 305) is not explained (rule‑based or LLM‑assisted?), and no quality checks (e.g., manual sampling, pass rate) are reported. (R3)
- **Poor figure readability.** Figures 1 and 2 are too small; text is nearly unreadable without heavy zoom. The appendix layout also needs optimization. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Unclear role of multiple evaluation metrics.** It is not explained what each metric (e.g., Rouge, accuracy) signifies or how they complement each other. (R1)
- **Ablation gaps.** Several experiments needed to solidify the approach are missing: (a) multi‑scale training without multi‑task training, (b) performance when training on ITA alone, with and without multi‑scale training. (R4)
