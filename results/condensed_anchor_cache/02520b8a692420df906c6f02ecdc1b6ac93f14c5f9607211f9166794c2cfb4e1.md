## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Partially Conditioned Patch Parallelism (PCPP) to accelerate diffusion model inference by dividing an image into non-overlapping patches and processing them on multiple GPUs. Unlike DistriFusion, which conditions each patch on the entire previous-step image, PCPP conditions only on parts of neighboring patches, reducing inter-device communication by about 70%. The method is evaluated with SDXL on 8 A100s and achieves speedups of 2.36–8.02× (vs. DistriFusion’s 2.32–6.71×) depending on GPU count and resolution, at the cost of some image quality degradation (higher FID, lower PSNR). The core ideas rely on the observation that adjacent diffusion steps are highly similar, enabling asynchronous communication and partial conditioning.

### Strengths
- The problem of slow diffusion inference is relevant to the community, and the explored idea is interesting (Reviewer 1).
- The method reduces computation in inference by conditioning only on parts of neighboring patches in each diffusion step (Reviewer 2).
- Addresses a practical problem; multi-GPU knowledge could extend to video generation (Reviewer 3).
- Significantly reduces communication cost, which can be highly beneficial when communication is the bottleneck (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
**Writing and Presentation**
- The paper does not read easily; clarity can be improved (Reviewer 1). Specific issues: Abstract line 12 (“restricting its use cases”) and line 38 (“significantly lesser computation…”) are awkward (Reviewer 1). Section 2.1 writing needs improvement (Reviewer 1). Figure 1 caption is confusing (Reviewer 2). Expression in eqn. (1) on page 4 is vague (Reviewer 2). Explanations in Sections 3.2 and 3.3 should be improved (Reviewer 2). L094 cites DPM-Solver++ but should cite classifier-free guidance (Reviewer 3).
**Missing Literature**
- Lacks comparison with related work such as PipeFusion (Wang et al., arXiv:2405.14430), which also employs patch parallelism for diffusion transformers (Reviewer 1).
**Novelty and Contribution (minority: Reviewer 3 most critical)**
- Contribution is not significant enough for the conference; insufficient novelty (Reviewer 2). The work is built on DistriFusion, simply identifying areas for further parallelization (Reviewer 2). The claimed observation (“difference between adjacent diffusion steps is nearly zero”) is similar to DistriFusion’s statement (Reviewer 2). Core ideas are quite trivial; arXiv:2401.05735 uses somewhat similar ideas to Figures 2 and 3 (Reviewer 3). Contribution rating: 1 (Reviewer 3) vs. 2 (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
**Experimental Incompleteness and Justification**
- Rationale for choosing the partial value of 0.3 is unclear; an analysis of varying partials (trade-offs in quality, latency, inference speed) is needed (Reviewer 1). No discussion on the selection of s in eqn. (2) (Reviewer 2). Missing ablation: why collective communication is replaced by point-to-point communication is not explained (Reviewer 2). Table 2 lacks details on the number of devices and the partial value used (Reviewer 1). No PSNR metric reported for the images in Figure 5; the reported value of 28.8 seems high given the visual quality (Reviewer 1). Only SDXL is tested; many novelties come from SDXL details. The approach assumes stability of attention across timesteps, which is not true for few-step samplers like DPM-Solver++ or LCM (Reviewer 3). Why use cfg 5 when SDXL default is 8? (Reviewer 3).
**Results Feasibility and Trade-offs**
- The trade-off between inference time and quality is not compelling enough to motivate adoption: FID is relatively high and PSNR lower compared to DistriFusion, and speed improvements are not consistently advantageous across resolutions (Reviewer 1). Although communication cost is significantly reduced (Table 1), latency is not much different from DistriFusion (possibly due to Amdahl’s law) and image quality is degraded (Table 2) (Reviewer 3).
