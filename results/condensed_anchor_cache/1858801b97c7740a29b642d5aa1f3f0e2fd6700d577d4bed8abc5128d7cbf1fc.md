## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Partial Channel Dependence (PCD) via a channel mask that combines a correlation matrix (relative dependencies) with learned domain parameters (absolute dependencies) to adjust channel dependencies dataset-specifically. The method is validated across forecasting, classification, imputation, and anomaly detection in full-shot, few-shot, and zero-shot settings. Reviewers agree on the novel direction and broad experiments but raise concerns about theoretical grounding, generality of the plugin, unclear motivation for foundation model design, missing baselines, and experimental inconsistencies that undermine the claimed effectiveness (scores: 3, 6, 6, 3, 5).

### Strengths
- **Novel concept and clear motivation**: The paper identifies and addresses implicit heterogeneity in channel dependencies across datasets, proposing PCD as an intermediate between channel independence and full channel dependence. The method is straightforward and the motivation is clearly articulated (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
- **Lightweight design**: The channel mask introduces very few parameters (α and β from domain parameters) yet effectively learns implicit inter-channel relationships, leading to performance improvements (Reviewer 1).
- **Broad empirical validation**: Extensive experiments across four tasks (forecasting, classification, imputation, anomaly detection) and diverse settings (supervised, few-shot, zero-shot) with thorough ablation studies and analyses, including a novel “masked channel prediction” experiment (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
- **Performance in challenging scenarios**: Demonstrates superior performance in few-shot and zero-shot learning, supporting the framework’s utility for time series foundation models (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Systematic framework for pretraining**: Proposes a systematic way to incorporate dataset-specific channel dependencies into pretraining, a valuable research direction (Reviewers 2, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical and motivational gaps**:
    - Lack of rigorous definition for “implicit heterogeneity”; no in-depth analysis explaining why the proposed method leads to performance improvements, nor how it relates to existing studies on channel dependence (Reviewer 1). The rationale for why existing models (e.g., Crossformer, iTransformer, TimeSiam, which learn attention patterns per dataset) truly overlook dataset-specific CD differences is not substantiated (Reviewers 1, 5).
    - The necessity of accounting for varying channel dependencies for a foundation model is not fully justified. Why a simpler fixed dependency structure would not work equally well is not discussed; the added complexity of variable dependencies may be unnecessary (Reviewer 2).
    - The global mask (correlation matrix) is computed once and reused at test time without adjustment. It may become outdated as temporal dynamics shift; stability across different time periods and domains is insufficiently addressed (Reviewer 5).
    - The choice of correlation as the metric for channel dependencies is not justified against alternatives like Granger causality, mutual information, or other measures. Potential issues from spurious correlations are not discussed (Reviewers 1, 5).
    - Handling of non-stationary data and changes in channel dependencies over time is not explained. The use of a sigmoid on the static correlation matrix may be insufficient to capture nonlinear and temporal variation in channel relationships (Reviewer 1).
- **Generality and technical limitations**:
    - The channel mask is only applicable to Transformer models that apply attention along the channel axis (e.g., iTransformer, UniTS). It cannot be used with CI models like PatchTST (which apply attention along time axis after channel flattening) or models like ST-MEM and UniTST, nor with MLP-based, CNN-based, or non-attention foundation models (TTM, TimesFM, Timer, MOIRAI, MOMENT). This severely limits the generality of the method as a plugin (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
    - The correlation matrix construction method is not specified in detail. References [7] and [8] focus on lag properties, but this work uses complete sequences without exploring lag. Different construction methods may invalidate the effectiveness of domain parameters, which are highly correlated with the construction approach; their separate effectiveness is not validated (Reviewer 4).
    - The CD ratio is computed after training and is model-dependent. No evidence shows that CD ratios are consistent across different models for the same dataset, raising doubts about its validity as a metric for channel dependence (Reviewer 1).
    - A critical component of the design—domain-specific global attention vs. shared local attention—is not motivated. The pair-wise dot product between global and local attention may yield low scores under scenarios with low global but high local correlation, failing to capture abrupt test-time changes (Reviewer 5).
    - The dataset identification requirement (knowing which samples belong to which dataset during pretraining) is a strong assumption that may not hold in real-world mixed-source scenarios (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient and inconsistent experimental results**:
    - Results for iTransformer and PatchTST in Table 3 differ significantly from those reported in the original papers (e.g., iTransformer MSE/MAE scores are much higher than in Appendix F, Table 8 of [4]). No explanation for discrepancies is provided, and a comprehensive comparison on ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2 across all prediction lengths is needed (Reviewers 3, 5).
    - Missing critical baseline comparisons: no comparison against the same architecture with full channel dependence and full channel independence, and no comparison against other plugins (e.g., LIFT[8]). The paper does not verify whether the approach can also improve CI models (e.g., PatchTST, PITS); if not, the claim of extending PCD to CI settings is unsupported (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
    - Lack of validation on synthetic datasets with uncorrelated channels to verify that CM yields a low CD ratio and avoids introducing spurious dependencies (Reviewer 1).
    - The impact of varying input sequence length on results with and without CM is not examined (Reviewer 3).
    - Many recent MLP- and CNN-based baselines (RLinear, ModernTCN) and GNN-based models (CrossGNN) are omitted (Reviewer 3).
    - Only a few foundation models are tested; many existing ones (TimesFM, Timer, MOIRAI, MOMENT) are not considered, and for TimeSiam the encoder used is not specified (classification tasks not evaluated) (Reviewers 1, 4).
    - Zero-shot experiments on new datasets do not explain how the domain parameter is handled for unseen domains (e.g., substituted from a similar domain). The correlation matrix definition (training period only?) is unclear (Reviewer 5).
    - Computational overhead and memory requirements compared to full dependence and independence cases are not quantified (Reviewer 2).
