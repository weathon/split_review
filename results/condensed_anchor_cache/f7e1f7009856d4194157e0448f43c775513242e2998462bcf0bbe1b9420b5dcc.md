## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Depth Pro, a foundation model for zero-shot metric monocular depth estimation. It produces high-resolution (up to 1536×1536) depth maps with sharp boundaries and high-frequency details in sub-second time (0.3 seconds for 2.25 MP on a standard GPU). The model estimates metric depth with absolute scale without requiring camera intrinsics or metadata. Technical contributions include a multi-scale vision transformer, a hybrid training protocol combining real and synthetic datasets, dedicated boundary evaluation metrics, and single-image focal length estimation. Extensive experiments show state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets and strong generalization, with the model outperforming prior methods such as DepthAnything, DepthAnything v2, and Marigold. Reviewer opinions are mixed: one finds the work highly significant (score 10), while another rates it low (score 3) due to limited novelty and synthetic data concerns; the other three are moderately positive (ratings 6, 8, 6). All acknowledge impressive results, but many point to insufficient method description and unclear novelty attribution.

### Strengths

- **Important task and impressive results**: Monocular depth estimation is a crucial problem with significant application prospects. The model delivers impressive visualizations (e.g., Figure 1) and achieves averaged best performance on multiple datasets with great generalization to open-world images (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5).
- **High-resolution and fast inference**: Depth maps at resolutions as high as 1536×1536 are produced, thanks to the multi-scale vision transformer architecture. The method also maintains competitive inference times—sub-second processing—a claimed efficiency that should be isolated via ablation (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 1 notes need for running time numbers).
- **Camera intrinsic estimation**: The model can estimate camera focal length from a single image with superior accuracy, eliminating the need for image metadata. This is a practical advancement critical for real-world scenarios where camera calibration is challenging and enhances depth map scaling and generalization (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
- **Boundary accuracy**: The method excels in distinguishing boundaries and preserving fine details compared to prior work (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). Enhanced boundary evaluation metrics are introduced, addressing a gap in existing benchmarks (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Qualitative results show clearly better object boundary preservation (Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive training and evaluation**: The model is trained on a large collection of diverse real and synthetic datasets, representing substantial effort in data processing. Detailed training procedures are described, enabling reproducibility. Ablation studies are thorough, and experiments cover a wide range of datasets, avoiding cross-dataset contamination where possible (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5). The pre-trained model is valuable to the community (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong zero-shot performance**: The model demonstrates strong zero-shot metric depth estimation and outperforms state-of-the-art methods such as DepthAnything, DepthAnything v2, and Marigold across multiple dimensions. Evaluation on downstream tasks like novel view synthesis showcases adaptability (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- **Structured training curriculum**: The two-stage training protocol is specifically tailored to handle the strengths and weaknesses of synthetic and real-world datasets, enhancing generalization and boundary details (Reviewer 3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 10) is very positive, emphasizing the model’s high resolution, comprehensive training, and significant potential for downstream tasks, even while noting limited novelty. In contrast, another (Reviewer 3, score 3) finds limited architectural novelty and heavy reliance on synthetic data, questioning generalization to real-world environments. The remaining three reviewers (scores 6, 8, 6) generally appreciate the performance but note weaknesses in clarity and novelty attribution, with Reviewer 1 finding poor writing and Reviewer 4/R5 asking for clearer identification of key components.

### Weaknesses

- **Method section is unclear and incomplete**: Model details are missing, making it hard to follow the approach and experimental evaluation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5). Specific issues:
  - How are features 3–6 obtained? The image is split into patches at resolutions {1x,3x,5x} of 384², but none matches the feature resolutions at 48² and 96² (Reviewer 1).
  - The architecture of the patch encoder is not specified (Reviewer 1).
  - The architecture of the focal length head and how it fuses multi-scale features with features from the extra “image encoder for focal length” is not described, despite this being critical for understanding absolute depth estimation without camera parameters (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 3 is unclear: along which dimension does concatenation operate? What is the specific implementation of the Merge operation? (Reviewer 5). At Line 234, the statement “produces a feature tensor at resolution 24×24 per input patch (features 3–6 in Fig. 3)” does not seem to coincide with the figure label (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited novelty; heavily built on existing approaches**: The loss functions and training strategy draw heavily from DPT (Ranftl et al., 2022), and the metric depth estimation follows ideas from Metric3D (Yin et al., 2023). The core architecture and training methodology do not present a significant departure from prior work. The paper would benefit from a more detailed analysis of architectural differences and a more thorough ablation study isolating the impact of each architectural component (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The reliance on a pretrained ViT backbone and lack of exploration of alternative architectures or modifications to the ViT limit architectural novelty and raise questions about transferability to domains significantly different from pretraining (Reviewer 3). The claimed efficiency gains need supporting ablation isolating each component’s contribution (Reviewer 2).
- **Heavy reliance on synthetic data for boundary-sensitive training**: The second-stage training emphasizes synthetic datasets for boundary sharpness, which could impact generalization to real-world environments, particularly in complex or unstructured settings where boundaries are less distinct. The paper does not adequately address the potential for overfitting to the specific characteristics of synthetic data (e.g., idealized lighting, object shapes). The evaluation of boundary sharpness on real-world data is not sufficiently detailed to fully validate the effectiveness of the synthetic-based training (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of temporal consistency analysis**: Depth consistency across video frames is not explored. Given the model’s metric-scale depth predictions, assessing its utility in video-based tasks such as visual SLAM, camera pose estimation, and 3D reconstruction would be valuable. The lack of temporal analysis is a notable gap; the paper should at least acknowledge limitations and suggest future directions (Reviewer 2).
- **Most impactful components are not clearly identified**: Among the technical contributions (curriculum learning, focal length estimation, patch encoder with multiple scales, multi-scale derivative objective), it is ambiguous which most significantly affect overall performance metrics (e.g., abs_rel, δ₁) and boundary accuracy. A tradeoff between overall performance and boundary accuracy may exist concerning certain hyperparameters or loss components—this is not investigated (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 1 questions why boundaries are better; Reviewer 5 asks whether high-resolution training alone is the reason). 
- **Performance gaps on some datasets**: According to Table 1, Depth Pro has a significant gap with the best performing methods on datasets such as ETH3D. No analysis of why this discrepancy occurs is provided (Reviewer 5).
- **Other unanswered questions and missing details**:
  - How does the model handle images of arbitrary scale? (Reviewer 1)
  - Running time numbers should be reported explicitly, not just in Figure 1 (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 4 is only mentioned in lines 457–458 with no details beyond the caption; it is hard to understand how DepthPro helps novel view synthesis (Reviewer 1).
  - Would the results differ if the loss function used depth instead of canonical inverse depth? (Reviewer 4)
  - For final depth calculation, does the method use the equation \( D = f_{px} / (w C) \) with predicted inverse depth \( C \), predicted focal length \( f_{px} \), and ground-truth width \( w \)? (Reviewer 4)
  - Are all experiments evaluated with metric scale depth, without median-scaling? (Reviewer 4)
  - Have experiments on fine-tuning DepthPro been conducted? If so, is there significant performance improvement over zero-shot? (Reviewer 4)
  - What is the training time and GPU resource requirement for the proposed method? (Reviewer 4)
  - Marigold and Depth Anything also use synthetic data for detail improvement; Depth Pro’s boundaries are significantly better—what is the main reason? Is it the high-resolution training (Depth Pro uses high-resolution while comparative methods use low-resolution)? (Reviewer 5)
  - According to Table 13, Depth Pro appears to use more training data than competitors. Can the scale of training data be compared explicitly? (Reviewer 5)
