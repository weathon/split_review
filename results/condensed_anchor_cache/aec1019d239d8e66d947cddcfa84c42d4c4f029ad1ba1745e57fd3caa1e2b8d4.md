## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a neural operator architecture (MgNO) that defines neurons as bounded linear operators connecting function spaces, eliminating the need for lifting and projecting operators. It uses multigrid V-cycle structures to efficiently parameterize these linear operators and naturally accommodates various boundary conditions. A universal approximation theorem (Theorem 3.1) is proven. Numerical experiments on Darcy, Helmholtz, and Navier-Stokes equations show competitive accuracy and efficiency against existing neural operators (FNO, DeepONet, etc.).

### Strengths
- Novel formulation of neural operators where neurons are bounded linear operators acting on function spaces, removing the need for lifting and projecting operators.
- MgNO leverages multigrid (V-cycle) and multi-channel convolutional forms to parameterize linear operators efficiently, naturally handling different boundary conditions.
- The multigrid/multiscale representation is conceptually appealing and reminiscent of efficient wavelet approximations of operators (Beylkin, Coifman, Rokhlin).
- Empirical evaluations show superior performance across multiple PDEs (Darcy, Helmholtz, Navier-Stokes) with various boundary conditions. Experiments are well-executed and provide convincing evidence that MgNO works well compared to other models, without significantly increasing complexity.
- The paper is clearly written with solid ideas; a universal approximation property (Theorem 3.1) supports the approach.

### Weaknesses
- Many ideas may already be present in MgNet; the paper lacks a discussion of this relation.
- Equation (9) is unclear: typical values of c are not stated.
- The motivation via exact encoding of boundary conditions, while sound, is also easy to implement with other neural operators (e.g., FNO via zero padding).
- No comparison with FNO is provided in Figure 7 (Helmholtz). A comparison or combination with the approach in arXiv:2301.11509 is suggested.
- Limitations and drawbacks of MgNO are not explicitly mentioned.
- Scalability to larger datasets or more complex problems is not addressed.
- The universal approximation theorem may not extend to general Banach spaces for X and Y.
- The discretization still uses mesh size h=1/d; performance and efficiency with respect to d are not analyzed, and the impact on MgNO’s behavior is missing.
- Parameterization with multi-channel inputs: it is unclear whether the V-cycle can be easily extended to multi-channel inputs; if not, the significance is compromised.
- The dependence of n on ε in Theorem 3.1 is not discussed.
- The importance of approximation capabilities is questioned: they are necessary but not sufficient, and FNO’s ability to handle different resolutions is not due to approximation capability alone.
- Whether Theorem 3.1 can be extended to deep networks (Section 3.2) and how network depth plays a role is not addressed.
- One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, praising clarity and execution, while the other three reviewers (scores 6) were more critical, especially regarding missing analyses and comparisons.
