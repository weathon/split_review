## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PERTURBQA, a benchmark for evaluating LLMs on biological perturbation reasoning tasks (differential expression prediction, direction of change, gene set enrichment), and SUMMER, a framework combining knowledge graphs, retrieval-augmented generation, and chain-of-thought prompting. The method is presented as interpretable and competitive with existing models. Reviewers are split: two very positive (score 6), one moderately positive (5), and two negative (3), indicating strong disagreement about novelty, baseline selection, and experimental depth.

### Strengths
- Novel application of language-based reasoning to perturbation prediction and gene set enrichment, making the task accessible to LLMs and interpretable for biologists (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- PerturbQA provides a set of real-world tasks (differential expression, direction, enrichment) across multiple datasets, enabling holistic benchmarking (R1, R3, R5).
- SUMMER’s framework combines RAG, COT, and knowledge graphs to produce interpretable outputs, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art models on the proposed tasks (R1, R3).
- Clear presentation with detailed examples and case studies; well-organized and easy to follow (R2, R4, R5).
- Thorough evaluation against multiple graph and language baselines, including GAT, GEARS, and MLP (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of combinatorial and multi-gene perturbation evaluation**: The method only handles single gene-gene pairs and does not scale to predicting all gene expression changes at once (e.g., 102 genes in GEARS, 210 combinations in scGPT). The paper provides no evidence it can handle multiple perturbations or concurrent outputs (R1, R4).
- **Insufficient baselines and missing comparisons to state-of-the-art models**: Key baselines are absent, including pretrained single-cell foundation models (scGPT, Geneformer, CellPLM) which outperform GEARS by ~50%; graph-based reasoning methods (GRN models, KG embeddings); and recent graph RAG or graph-to-text approaches. This limits the conclusiveness of the evaluation (R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Questionable evaluation metrics for gene set enrichment**: ROUGE-1 recall and BERTScore may not capture biological relevance. Human evaluation, LLM-as-judge, or domain-specific metrics (functional enrichment scores, semantic similarity) are needed (R1, R2).
- **Risk of data leakage and overfitting to known knowledge**: The LLM (LLaMA3) has likely been exposed to extensive biological literature, potentially giving it prior knowledge. The retrieval mechanism may directly provide the answer (e.g., “A turns on C”), turning prediction into reading comprehension. The method relies on a fixed knowledge graph built from known interactions, making it a retrieval system rather than a discovery engine (R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Marginal technical novelty**: Combining LLMs with knowledge graphs and retrieval for biological reasoning has been widely studied (graph-to-text, graph RAG, GenePT, GEARS). The paper does not sufficiently distinguish its contribution from prior work, and the related work discussion is incomplete (R3, R5).
- **Insufficient error analysis and experimental insight**: The experiments show SUMMER outperforms baselines but provide little analysis of failure modes, conditions under which the model struggles, or successful/unsuccessful prediction examples. There are no biological analyses such as predicted expression profiles or cell state visualizations (R1, R2, R4).
- **Sensitivity to prompt engineering**: The interpretability advantage comes with added complexity in prompt design, and the paper does not analyze how performance varies with prompt formulation (R1).
- **Generalization concerns**: It is unclear how well the method performs on new or sparsely annotated datasets (e.g., less-studied cell lines or organisms) or in low-data settings (R1).
- **Multi-hop reasoning not handled**: SUMMER only retrieves one-hop neighbors of the perturbation and target genes, so it cannot perform deeper multi-hop causal reasoning (R2).
- **Unclear advantage of textual representation over structured numeric data**: The justification for converting perturbation tasks into text (PerturbQA) rather than using formalized graph structures and numeric expression data is weak; the text descriptions are not certified for correctness (R5).
