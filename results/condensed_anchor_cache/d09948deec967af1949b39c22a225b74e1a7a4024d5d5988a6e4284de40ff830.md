## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the neural wave equation, a novel approach for irregularly sampled sequence data that models hidden state evolution continuously across both time and depth using a non-homogeneous wave equation parameterized by a neural network. D’Alembert’s analytical solution enables denser hidden-state connections. Experiments on person activity recognition, Walker2d kinematic simulation, sepsis prediction, and stance classification show competitive or superior performance compared to existing baselines, especially heat-equation-based methods.

### Strengths
- **Strong theoretical foundation** with clear mathematical derivations and the analytical solution of the wave equation (R1, R3).
- **Very innovative idea** (R2 – minority strongly positive: “huge potential impact”); using the wave equation to model latent space can capture physical/natural phenomena, and the non-homogeneous formulation \(F(z,t)\) makes the model generalizable (R2).
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation** across diverse datasets (person activity recognition, Walker2d, sepsis, stance classification) (R1, R2, R3).
- **Thoughtful comparison** with existing approaches, especially heat-equation-based methods, and detailed ablation studies showing the importance of different components (R1).
- **Problem of fixed architectural depth is important** (R3).
- **Experimentally dominated baseline methods** in most metrics (R2); experiments on different scenarios show effectiveness (R3).
- **Method is creative** – continuous modeling across time and depth using the wave equation allows denser dependencies in hidden states (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for wave equation over other PDEs could be stronger** – unclear why wave equation is the best choice and what benefits it provides over other PDEs; the connection between theoretical advantages and empirical improvements needs clarification (R1, R3).
- **Presentation is not optimal**:
    - Figure 1 is hard to interpret; fonts too small; curved/straight lines difficult to distinguish; recommend redesigning using PDF/SVG (R2).
    - Figure 2 should be an ablation study; needs better explanation of how depth affects the three compared models; using \(t'\) for depth is confusing given \(t\) is time (R3).
    - Figure 3 needs explanation of the blue arrows (R3).
    - Algorithm description requires significant rewrite; current version is hard to understand (R2).
    - Section 3 and 4 need clearer exposition (R3).
- **Introduction and related work need better structure** – e.g., what is hidden-state depth in real-world problems? Why are PDE-based models better for continuous depth? (R3)
- **Memory consumption issues are noted but not thoroughly addressed** – no exploration of reduction techniques (R1).
- **Limited discussion of computational complexity/trade-offs** – how does running speed compare to existing methods? Is the algorithm fast in practice? (R1, R2)
- **Implementation details about boundary conditions** could be more explicit; how are boundary conditions handled in practice? (R1)
- **Sensitivity to wave speed parameter \(c\)** – how sensitive is the model to this choice? (R1)
- **Performance with very long sequences** is not evaluated; how does the model scale? (R1)
- **Neural wave equation may not perform as well as baselines on some datasets** – possibly because the wave equation acts as a strong prior that does not fit the data; the limitation when latent dynamics follow another PDE needs discussion (R2, R3).
- **Scope limitation** – can it be applied to general sequence modeling or only irregularly sampled data? Why only the latter? (R2)
- **Experiments need more detailed analysis and visualization**:
    - For each experiment, state the purpose (e.g., how depth or missing data affects performance), depth per dataset, group baselines by categories as in Figure 1, separate Table 1 by experiment, and add visualizations of predicted vs. ground truth sequences (R3).
    - Ablation study could be extended (R2).
- **Appendix on the solution of the wave equation lacks proper citations** (R2).
- **Minor**: Line 302 formatting error (R2).
