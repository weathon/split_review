## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes the RKIQT framework for no-reference image quality assessment (NR-IQA) by distilling comparison knowledge from non-aligned reference images. It introduces a Masked Quality-Contrastive Distillation (MCD) method and an inductive bias regularization strategy to inject complementary biases from CNN and INV networks into a ViT backbone. The method eliminates the need for reference images during inference and achieves high performance on multiple IQA benchmarks. However, novelty, experimental completeness, and writing clarity are disputed.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated: leveraging comparison knowledge (used in FR-IQA) for NR-IQA without reference images at inference time is a promising and practically relevant direction.
- The introduction of MCD to distill knowledge from non-aligned references is a new idea that could potentially enhance NR-IQA.
- The complementary strategy between ViTs and CNN/INN architectures (distilling inductive biases) is conceptually reasonable and creative.
- Distillation from multi-networks and reference-based methods is a good approach for blind IQA.
- Extensive experiments across eight standard IQA datasets demonstrate a commitment to empirical validation.
- The resulting model attains high performance on multiple benchmarks, outperforming many NR-IQA methods and being competitive with some FR methods (two reviewers highlight this, another notes underperformance versus DISTS and IQT).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work:** The idea of using pseudo/reference knowledge in NR-IQA is longstanding. Key related works (Chen et al. 2022 [FPR], Tian et al. 2023, Ma et al. 2021) are not discussed or compared, making the claimed novelty questionable. Two reviewers (R1, R4) consider the work more an engineering solution than a novel academic finding; one notes that no new technique is proposed. The novelty of using knowledge distillation for IQA is also limited compared to prior KD-based work (Yin et al. 2022).
- **Missing comparisons:** The paper lacks performance comparisons with recent SOTA methods: TOPIQ, FPR (Chen et al. 2022), and Re-IQA (results on LIVEFB are missing from Table 1, even though the original paper and code provide them). On KonIQ and SPAQ, performance is merely similar to SAMA (2024-AAAI), a simple sampling strategy.
- **Performance claims vs. evidence:** The claim that the method “utilizes less input… while achieving better performance than some FR-IQA methods” is contradicted by the results: the model underperforms DISTS and IQT (FR methods). Additionally, on large-scale datasets (KADID, LIVEFB) the method does not outperform simpler models. Performance on authentic image datasets is sub-optimal compared to synthetic ones, raising questions about learned distortions from the NAR teacher.
- **No validation set:** No validation set is used; model selection and hyperparameter tuning appear to rely on test set results, which violates standard ML practices. Multiple reviewers question how the training epoch is chosen.
- **Ablation studies:** Ablation studies are conducted only on LIVEC or LIVEC+KonIQ, which may not be representative. No ablation verifies the effectiveness of the reference image itself in the non-aligned reference teacher. The claim that adjusting inductive biases ensures rapid convergence and prevents overfitting is not clearly supported.
- **Non-aligned reference teacher design:** It is unclear how LQ-HQ pairs are formulated, how many HQ images are needed per LQ, and what influence different pairings have. Directly computing feature differences between HQ and LQ images is physically meaningless without alignment. Also, the teacher is trained only on KADID and then used for all experiments, making it unclear if the method would work if reference images were taken from the highest-quality images in authentic datasets.
- **Inductive bias tokens:** The inclusion of three different inductive bias tokens is not justified; their relative importance is not tested.
- **Inconsistencies and errors:** SRCC on TID2013 differs between Table 1 and Table 8 (no explanation). In Line 478, a reported result of 0.86 falls outside the stated range of 0.32 to 0.7—needs clarification. Figure 4 activation maps highlight only birds in the last two columns, failing to focus on image distortions (e.g., background artifacts). Figure 2 is described as disorderly and needs restructuring.
- **Writing and presentation:** The paper is not easy to understand; contributions are decentered, and the core motivation is difficult to follow.
- **Cross-dataset generalization:** No experiments test generalization by training on authentic distortions and testing on synthetic distortions (or vice versa), which is important for IQA.
- **Fairness of comparisons:** Results of compared methods seem directly copied from original papers. Different train/val/test splits may affect the fairness of performance comparisons.
