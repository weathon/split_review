## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses causal effect estimation in the presence of both latent confounders and latent post-treatment variables, a challenging scenario where standard proxy-variable methods risk bias. It proposes the Confounder-identifiable Variational Autoencoder (CiVAE) to disentangle these latent components, provides theoretical identifiability results, and shows that unbiased estimation is possible under specific assumptions. Experiments on simulated and real-world data demonstrate that CiVAE outperforms existing methods in terms of robustness. All reviewers agree that the problem is important and the empirical results are strong; one reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the clarity and theoretical depth, while others (scores 5 and 6) were more critical about assumptions and presentation.

### Strengths
- The problem of latent post-treatment bias is well-motivated and understudied (all reviewers).
- The proposed CiVAE method shows strong empirical performance, outperforming existing baselines on both simulated and real-world datasets (all reviewers).
- Figures 1 and 2 are very helpful for understanding the problem setup (reviewer 1).
- The paper provides a practical algorithm with sound theoretical results, including identifiability proofs (reviewers 2, 3).
- The core idea is clearly explained and the paper is well-written; proofs are provided in the appendix (reviewer 3).
- A code link is included, enhancing reproducibility (reviewer 3).
- The paper offers valuable empirical validation on both simulated and real-world benchmarks (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Notation is ambiguous: \(K_Z\) (footnote 3), \(p(X\mid C)\), and the matrix representation of \(p(C, X)\) are undefined; this hinders understanding of the conditional independence assumptions (reviewer 1).
- Section 4 (theoretical analysis) is heavy, lacks high-level intuition, and the assumptions are not clearly stated or justified; proofs are dense without sufficient explanation of key steps (reviewer 1).
- The real-world dataset used in experiments is not publicly available, limiting reproducibility (reviewer 1).
- The assumption of strict separation between latent confounders and post-treatment variables (i.e., that treatment cannot change confounders) may be violated; the paper does not address scenarios where treatment affects latent confounders directly (reviewer 1).
- Assumption 1 (injective mapping \(f\) from the combined latent space to observed covariates) is a strong, untestable condition. It is more restrictive than typical assumptions in the literature, and no sensitivity analysis is provided for violations. Two reviewers (2 and 4) consider this a major limitation and request such experiments; the remaining reviewer did not flag this concern.
- The analysis of interactions among latent variables (Section 4.5) considers only three specific cases, but interactions may also arise from unobserved confounders not covered by those cases (reviewer 2).
- No guidance is given on how to determine the dimension of \(Z\); different choices may affect ATE estimation and latent confounder identification, and no ablation study is provided (reviewer 2).
- There is a typo in equation (8) (reviewer 2).
- Inconsistency in Figure 2: a bi-directional path is shown between \(M\) and \(Y\), but in the text \(M\) is treated as a strict mediator. The authors should clarify the directionality and consider different structural cases (e.g., \(T \to M \to Y\) vs. \(T \to M \leftarrow Y\)). Moreover, line 269 claims \(M\) forms a fork structure with \(Y\), which is inconsistent with the figure (reviewer 3).
- The paper considers only \(p(Z\mid Y,T)\) without justification; depending on the relationship between \(M\) and \(Y\), different conditional forms for \(p(Z)\) may be required (reviewer 3).
- If \(Z\) includes confounders \(C\), it is unclear whether \(C\) is identifiable under CiVAE; even the standard CEVAE lacks identifiability without additional auxiliary information such as that used in iVAE (reviewer 3).
- A relevant related work on iVAE for front-door adjustment ([1]) is missing from the related work section; it should be discussed (reviewer 3).
- The practical significance of *latent* post-treatment variables is questionable; the provided example does not convincingly demonstrate why these variables must be latent rather than directly observed, and more compelling real-world examples are needed (reviewer 4).
- The independence assumption between latent confounders \(C\) and latent post-treatment variables \(M\) (Fig. 1c) is strong and unlikely to hold in realistic settings; the paper does not discuss the consequences if this assumption is violated (reviewer 4).
