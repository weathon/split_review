## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Diffusion Actor-Critic (DAC), an offline reinforcement learning algorithm that formulates KL-constrained policy iteration as a diffusion noise regression problem. DAC directly represents the target policy as a diffusion model, using soft Q-guidance from the Q-gradient to prevent out-of-distribution actions. For critic training, it employs a Q-ensemble and a lower confidence bound (LCB) to stabilize value estimation. Experiments on D4RL benchmarks show DAC outperforming state-of-the-art methods in almost all environments, with stable convergence.

### Strengths
- **Innovation and theoretical contribution**: The idea of directly modeling the target policy as a diffusion model and rigorously transforming the KL-constrained policy iteration into a diffusion noise regression problem is novel and provides clear theoretical justification (R1, R2, R3, R4). One reviewer (score 8) particularly highlights the originality of combining diffusion models with policy optimization and the effectiveness of soft Q-guidance in overcoming overestimation.
- **Effective OOD action prevention**: The soft Q-guidance mechanism adjusts the intensity of Q-gradients according to noise scale, effectively preventing out-of-distribution actions and enhancing stability (R1, R2). This addresses a core challenge in offline RL.
- **Strong experimental performance**: DAC achieves state-of-the-art results on D4RL benchmarks across locomotion, antmaze, and other tasks, with stable convergence and robustness (R1, R2, R3). Extensive experiments and thorough ablation studies are conducted, demonstrating the ability to capture policy multimodality (R4).
- **Clarity of writing**: The paper is well-written and includes illustrative examples (R1, R2). (One reviewer, however, found the structure chaotic—see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with relevant methods**: Several recent offline RL works are missing, including 2024 papers such as AlignIQL and Diffusion Policies creating a Trust Region (R4), as well as RORL, In-sample Learning via Implicit Value Regularization, BPPO, Implicit Trust Region, and Uni-o4 (R1). A brief summary of works combining online RL and diffusion modeling (e.g., Policy Representation via Diffusion, Q-Score Matching, DAC with Entropy Regulator) is also missing (R4). Evaluation is limited to D4RL; experiments on Adroit, Kitchen, Atari, or real-world robotic tasks are absent or only in the appendix (R2, R4).
- **Confounding contribution from Q-ensemble and LCB**: It is unclear whether the performance improvement comes from the proposed diffusion policy-constrained iteration or from the Q-ensemble design, since Q-ensemble methods can solve offline RL without extra regularization (R1). Ablations (Fig. 4) suggest the Q-ensemble is important; baselines like DQL should add a Q-ensemble for a fairer comparison (R1). Similarly, ablation experiments for LCB and diffusion step size are missing, making it difficult to attribute performance gains (R4). The lower confidence bound may introduce systematic bias in Q-values; its significance across different task settings is not analyzed (R2).
- **Lack of sensitivity and ablation analyses**: No sensitivity analysis is provided for the pessimism factor ρ (R1, R4), the η parameter in the objective (R2), or the choice of soft Q-guidance weight parameters across different environments (R2, R3). These choices could lead to oscillations, divergence, or gradient vanishing, especially with high target policy update frequency (R2). The paper also does not explore the sensitivity of soft Q-guidance to the quality (suboptimality) of the offline data (R2).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Notation is complicated (R1). One reviewer found the article structure chaotic and lacking focus, making reading difficult (R3). The translation from Equation 13 to Equation 14 appears incorrect: the target policy appears to be replaced with the offline dataset, likely meaning the behavior policy (R1). Additionally, no specific hyperparameter configuration details are provided (R2).
- **Concerns about Q-function and soft Q-guidance reliability**: Soft Q-guidance relies on accurate Q-value estimation; overly optimistic or pessimistic Q-values would affect the final policy, and uncertainty propagation is not considered (R2). In Equation 15, the Q-function is well-trained only within the data distribution, but the noisy inputs x_t at intermediate diffusion steps can diverge significantly from the dataset, raising questions about extrapolation errors in Q-gradients (R4). The advantages of soft Q-guidance over other methods (e.g., hard guidance) are not sufficiently pronounced in the results (R3).
- **Specific missing experiments and analyses**: No ablation for LCB or diffusion step size (R4). The paper does not analyze the impact of estimating entropy in the EDP (R4). For the antmaze task with sparse rewards, more effective reward mechanisms or exploration strategies are needed; it is unclear how DAC would be adjusted for such settings (R2). Whether combining different guidance strategies could enhance robustness is not explored (R2).
