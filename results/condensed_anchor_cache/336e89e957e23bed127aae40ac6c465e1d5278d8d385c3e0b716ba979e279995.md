## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SlotSAM, a method that reconstructs features from a pretrained encoder (SAM) in a self-supervised manner using Slot Attention to create object-centric representations, which are then injected as object tokens into the segmentation model. The goal is to improve robustness under real-world shifts (distribution and prompt shifts). Experiments on natural, medical, and robotic image datasets show improvements over baselines. The idea is interesting, but the presentation, motivation, and experimental validation have significant issues, especially in clarity and scope justification.

### Strengths
- The topic is relevant for the conference and the approach of integrating object-centric learning into segmentation foundation models is novel and sensible (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The method appears to improve segmentation performance over considered baselines (R1, R3).
- One reviewer finds the paper well-written with high-quality figures and good logic (R2); however, other reviewers strongly disagree on clarity (see Weaknesses).
- The idea of obtaining high-quality representations from the foundation model and projecting them as object tokens is well-motivated (R4).
- The architecture of SlotSAM seems reasonable, and the experiments are extensive (R4).
- The results are promising on benchmark datasets (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation** (major issue across R1, R3, R4; contrast: R2 considers the paper well-written):
  - The paper is challenging to read; the method explanation is overly convoluted and unclear (R1, R3, R4).
  - Many notations are not explained (e.g., Lines 126–134); the difference between strong and weak augmentation, and the roles of anchor, student, and teacher models are unclear (R4).
  - Specific implementation details are ambiguous: linear transformation \(\mathcal{K}_\beta\) is defined for slots but used to process \(\mathbf{z}\) (Line 150–153), the query as a function of slots (Line 154), the architecture of the slot-decoder (Line 156), and how “nonlinear combination” is performed (Line 188) (R4).
  - Terms like “prompt shift,” “uniform representation,” and “Object Token” are not clearly introduced or motivated (R1, R3).
  - Figures, especially Figure 7, are unintuitive, poorly presented, and lack proper captions explaining what is shown (R3).
  - Several sentences need rephrasing (e.g., “To avoid disrupting the optimization preference…”) (R1).
  - Minor: typos and a repeated equation (Line 151) (R1).

- **Motivation and positioning**:
  - The context is not clearly established; comments on Slot Attention are misleading and omit recent relevant work (e.g., DINOSAUR) (R1).
  - The claim of “redefining the reconstruction target of Slot-Attention as high-level features” is not unique; prior work has explored this (R1).
  - The related work section on object-centric learning is limited (R1).
  - The prompt shift formulation is unclear and not well motivated (R3).
  - The method is presented as broadly applicable to vision foundation models, but experiments only use SAM; no investigation of applicability to other models (e.g., DINO, MAE, JEPA) (R1, R3). This mismatch undermines the claimed scope.

- **Experimental issues and missing analysis**:
  - Table 2 results are unclear; the \(\Delta\) row only shows improvement over SAM, but improvements over WESAM are minimal in some cases (R1).
  - There is no discussion of different SAM prompt types and their expected impact, nor explanation of degrees of weak supervision (R1).
  - In Table 1, SlotSAM outperforms fine-grained mask supervised fine-tuning on some datasets but not others; no insights into this discrepancy (R4).
  - Ablation studies focus on implementation details rather than providing interesting insights; missing ablation replacing slot attention with simpler alternatives (R1).
  - The strong claim that object-centric learning reduces reliance on domain-specific variables lacks supporting ablation with non-slot-based representation mechanisms (R1).
  - No detailed experiments on how object tokens interact with the base model’s existing tokens (R2).
  - The model shows significant sensitivity to slot count, especially in complex scenarios; optimal configuration requires manual tuning, limiting generalization consistency (R2).
  - No ablation on the number of GRU iterations; the choice of GRU over MLP is not justified (R2).
  - The feasibility of integrating the slot mechanism into intermediate encoder outputs is not explored (R2).
  - The trade-off regarding higher slot count (training speed, competition effects) is not examined (R2).
  - Why reconstruction via Slot Attention leads to object-centric representations is not explained with theoretical or experimental evidence; the information bottleneck justification is vague (R2). This applies equally to the object token injection part (R2).
  - Could minor architecture changes in SAM (already trained on massive data) achieve the same improvement? Not addressed (R2).
  - It is not stated whether code will be made public (R3).
