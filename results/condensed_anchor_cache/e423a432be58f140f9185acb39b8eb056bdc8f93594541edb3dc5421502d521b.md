## Merged Review

### Summary
SEABO proposes an offline imitation learning method that assigns rewards based on Euclidean distance to the nearest neighbor in a single expert trajectory (via KD-tree), then applies offline RL. It is evaluated on D4RL tasks.

### Strengths
- Novel and simple: efficient (KD-tree, no neural network), easy to implement and reproduce, minimal overhead over offline RL.
- Focus on single-expert-demonstration IL, an area of growing interest.
- Thorough empirical validation across many D4RL locomotion tasks and some manipulation (Kitchen); strong performance over prior reward learning and offline IL methods, beating more complex alternatives using optimal transport (R2). Post-rebuttal Kitchen results considered strong by R2.
- Sensitivity analysis on parameters α and β (R4).
- Evaluation with only observations (R4).
- Interesting discussion on different search algorithms in Section 5.4 and Appendix C (R1).
- Limitations section in appendix appreciated (R1).
- Clear presentation (all reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Euclidean distance concerns**:
  - Treats each state dimension independently, may not capture state-space manifold; e.g., small joint-angle changes can cause large end-effector changes, making reward misleading (R1).
  - In cliff-walking or discontinuous reward environments, proximity to expert does not guarantee near-optimality (R3).
  - Lacks justification for Euclidean distance; suggestion to use metric that considers MDP dynamics (R1).
  - R2 asks for explanation of how distance is computed on transition tuple (state/action/next_state separately? weighting?).
  - R1 asks whether alternative distance metrics can be used and how much modification is required.

- **Evaluations restricted to deterministic environments (MuJoCo)**:
  - Single expert trajectory may be insufficient in stochastic environments; valid transitions can be penalized if not in expert trajectory (R4).
  - R4 questions effect of stochasticity on performance and inherent assumptions on transition kernel.

- **Multiple optimal paths**:
  - Method fails when alternative equally valid solutions exist (e.g., navigation left vs right); SEABO assigns low rewards to the non-demonstrated path while BC would work (R4).
  - R3 questions if the strategy is always good, especially with discrete events.

- **Curse of dimensionality**:
  - Nearest-neighbor search degrades with high-dimensional states; closeness becomes less meaningful (R3).
  - R3 asks for explicit analysis of curse-of-dimensionality effects.

- **Missing baselines**:
  - Only IQL and TD3_BC; should include recent offline RL methods like Trajectory Transformer, Diffuser (R1). Also missing percent-BC baseline (simple offline IL) (R3).
  - R1 notes chosen baselines may not be strongest competitors, potentially overestimating SEABO’s relative performance.

- **Table labeling issue**: “(oracle)” should be omitted from Tables 1,2,3,6; using ground-truth reward does not make task more challenging (R1).

- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: Only empirical; makes it hard to predict failure conditions or compare with other offline IL algorithms (R4, R3).
  - R4 asks about assumptions on state-action distribution and why experiments restricted to medium-level tasks (not expert/random).
  - R1 asks if reward comparisons (Figures 2,12) were done in AntMaze and Adroit.

- **Limited task scope**: Mostly locomotion where precision is not critical; R2 suggests evaluation on challenging long-horizon manipulation (IKEA Furniture Assembly) to test limits.

- **Minor**: “Search” in title may imply planning methods; suggest “nearest neighbors” (R3).

*Note: While most reviewers acknowledged the method’s simplicity and strong empirical performance, one reviewer (R3) was more critical about the lack of theoretical analysis and potential failure cases in high-dimensional or discontinuous reward settings. Reviewer R2 was notably positive, praising the thorough evaluation and performance over complex alternatives.*
