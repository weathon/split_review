- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 8, 3, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces the Diffusion-PINN Sampler (DPS), which uses a physics-informed neural network to approximate the log-density of the forward SDE marginals (solving the log-density Fokker-Planck equation), thereby providing the score for the reverse diffusion process for sampling from unnormalized densities. The authors provide convergence guarantees bounding sampling error by the PINN residual. Experiments on synthetic targets (Gaussian mixtures, Funnel, Double-well, Rings) show improvements over baselines. Reviewers acknowledge the interesting connection to PDEs and the rigorous analysis but raise concerns about novelty, high computational cost, limited experimental scope, insufficient baseline comparisons, and the practical relevance of the theoretical bounds.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The theoretical analysis in Section 5 provides convergence guarantees and error bounds relating the PINN loss to the sampling error (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The identification of the failure of the score FPE and the motivated solution (solving the log-density FPE) is a clear contribution (Reviewer 2).
- Using PINNs for this problem is relevant and timely (Reviewer 2).
- The experimental evaluation uses appropriate probabilistic metrics (KL, Fisher divergence, L² of log-density) and includes parsimonious illustrations (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed method shows consistent and often large improvements over the considered baselines on the tested problems (Reviewer 2).
- The connection to PDEs is interesting, and the mathematical derivation is rigorous (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns.** The idea of employing PINNs for diffusion-based sampling has been previously proposed in [1,2,3,4]; in particular, [3] presents the same algorithm (log-density FPE) with more extensive experiments (Reviewer 3). Also, DPS is already a well-known acronym for Diffusion Posterior Sampling, creating a naming conflict (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited experimental scope.** All experiments are on synthetic, low-to-moderate-dimensional targets (Gaussian mixtures, Funnel, Double-well, Rings). Scalability to higher dimensions (e.g., d>100) and real-world problems (e.g., Boltzmann distributions, images) is not demonstrated (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 4 specifically requests experiments on Gaussian processes, Bayesian neural networks, and real data.
- **Computational cost.** The PINN loss involves Laplacian and gradient operators, making training expensive in high dimensions (Reviewer 1). Evaluating the gradient of the learned log-density at every reverse step adds cost compared to direct score matching (Reviewers 1, 2). The use of Hutchinson’s estimator may increase variance (Reviewer 3). No time/resource analysis is provided (Reviewers 1, 2). It is unclear whether the method is computationally cheaper than standard MCMC given that initial collocation points are generated with LMC (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient baselines.** The paper does not compare to modern diffusion-based sampling methods beyond those in [1,2] (Reviewer 1), nor to standard MCMC (HMC) or particle-based VI (SVGD) when the score is accessible (Reviewer 4). The related work section is too short and misses many recent methods (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Practical relevance of theoretical results.** The convergence bounds rely on Grönwall-like arguments and unknown constants, limiting quantitative guidance (Reviewer 3). Assumption 1 (compact domain for error bounds) is not plausible; the theorems may still hold for light-tailed distributions on ℝᵈ, but this is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Parametrization and loss design.** The parametrization in (12) (√γ(t) u_θ) imposes a strong inductive bias and may be infeasible when querying the energy function is expensive; an ablation without this parametrization is requested (Reviewer 1). The regularization term ℓ_reg is ad-hoc – regressing onto the boundary condition of the score instead of using (11b) seems unmotivated (Reviewer 1). The claimed improvement from ℓ_reg is not clearly visible in Figure 5 (center, right); corner plots on a subset of dimensions would be more informative (Reviewer 2).
- **Example 1 / Fisher divergence issue.** The claim that the Fisher divergence can be arbitrarily small while KL stays large is misleading; actually KL also goes to zero, but at a different scale (Reviewer 3). The example uses a Gaussian mixture with unbounded variance as τ→0; it is unclear whether the same phenomenon occurs for bounded-variance real-world distributions (Reviewer 4). The “score error” in Figure 4 is not defined (Reviewer 3).
- **Sampling with collocation points.** Generating collocation points via LMC may fail for multimodal targets with widely separated modes (Reviewer 3). Algorithm 1 requires access to the unnormalized density via LMC sampling for initial collocation points, but how this is used in practice is not fully explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Conditional sampling.** The method could be extended to conditional generation (posterior problems), but no such experiment is shown; it would be useful to see whether accurate prior scores benefit conditional tasks, e.g., generating from a single mode of the 9-Gaussians problem (Reviewer 2).
- **Metrics.** Only KL, Fisher, and L² log-density are reported. Additional metrics (Wasserstein, ESS, ELBO, comparison to expectation reference values) would strengthen the evaluation (Reviewer 3). In Table 1, KL is reported only on the first dimensions for Funnel and Double-well, which may be misleading; using more dimensions or other metrics (e.g., Sinkhorn divergence) is suggested (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor issues.** Several typos and inconsistent notation occur (e.g., “Fokker-Planck” vs “Fokker Planck”, missing commas, “denotes” vs “denote”) (Reviewer 3). The paper incorrectly claims that trajectory-based alternatives require differentiating through SDE solvers for off-policy training (Reviewer 3). The interpolation path is described as linear but is linear only in log-space (Reviewer 3). Figure 3 title “Sampling performance” should be changed to “Samples from different methods” (Reviewer 2). In (20), the sum over N time steps could increase with N, which is not intuitive (Reviewer 3).
