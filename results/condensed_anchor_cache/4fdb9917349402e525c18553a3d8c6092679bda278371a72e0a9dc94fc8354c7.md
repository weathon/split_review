## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes MINDE, a family of mutual information (MI) estimators based on score-based diffusion models. By interpreting the Girsanov theorem, the authors express KL divergence and entropy via score functions, leading to four variants (conditional/joint diffusion, with/without normal reference). The method is evaluated on the Czyz benchmark (40 tasks, though one reviewer notes this conflates parameterizations) and consistency tests on MNIST. Multiple reviewers appreciate the creativity and the strong empirical results, but raise concerns regarding clarity of exposition, missing comparisons to generative estimators (DoE, GM) and prior diffusion-based work, lack of error bars and finite-sample analyses, and limited discussion of computational cost. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive about the novelty and derivation, whereas others (score 6) question the clarity of contributions and note that connections between score functions, KL, MI, and entropy are known.

### Strengths

- The derivation of the building blocks for KL divergence and entropy estimation is well-organized and clear (R2, R4).
- Applying the SDE framework of diffusion models to MI estimation is creative and opens new directions (R1, R2, R4).
- The experimental evaluation is strong, covering challenging aspects such as sparsity, dimensionality, long tails, transformations, data processing, and consistency (R1, R3, R4). The use of the Czyz benchmark and self-consistency tests (independence, data processing, additivity) is appreciated (R1).
- Results show that MINDE outperforms existing alternatives (MINE, NWJ, D-V, InfoNCE, etc.) on most benchmark settings (R1, R3, R4). Reviewer 3 notes this is particularly impressive given the importance of MI estimation in high dimensions.
- References to relevant literature are adequate (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and accessibility:** The paper is difficult to follow due to heavy measure-theoretical notation and a lack of high-level intuition connecting SDEs/diffusion to MI estimation. Several reviewers request better exposition, including pseudocode (e.g., Algorithm 1 and 2), visual aids, and a clearer explanation of why SDEs are used (R1, R3, R4). Reviewer 1 states they were “completely lost” despite familiarity with information theory.
- **Role of generative reverse process:** The paper frames the method as using generative models, but only forward diffusion (score functions) is employed. Suggested reframing as score function estimation rather than generative modeling (R2).
- **Missing comparisons to relevant methods:**
  - No comparison to generative MI estimators based on difference of entropies (e.g., DoE, GM using normalizing flows) (R4). This is a significant oversight given MINDE-σ uses the same principle.
  - No comparison to prior diffusion-based MI estimator (R4); advantages over that work are not elaborated.
  - No comparison to classic plug-in estimators using kernel density estimation or KSG (from sklearn) (R1).
  - No comparison of wall-clock training time or memory requirements against any of the baseline methods (R3, R4). Learning score functions from diffusion may be overkill for MI; a time/resources comparison is needed.
- **Lack of error bars and statistical significance:** Claims of outperforming alternatives on 35/40 tasks are not supported by error bars or statistical tests. Without such evidence, the ranking may not be reproducible (R1).
- **Finite-sample properties:** No analysis of accuracy as a function of sample size, especially for small N (e.g., hundreds), which is common in biomedical applications. Reviewer 3 requests plots of test error vs. training set size (100 to 100k) across varying dimensions.
- **Clarity of contributions:** The connections between score functions, KL divergence, MI, and entropy are known in the literature. Reviewers question whether the extension to diffusion processes is non-trivial and what the core novel contribution is (R3). Reviewer 4 disagrees and considers the derivation solid and novel, but this point is contested.
- **Assumptions and limitations:** The absolute continuity assumption required by the Girsanov theorem may not hold given the neural network architectures used for score function approximation (citing 2011.09421). The paper should articulate the underlying assumptions and which datasets would benefit from MINDE (R3).
- **Misrepresentation of tasks:** The paper claims 40 tasks, but one reviewer notes this is actually about 10 tasks with multiple parameterizations, which is misleading (R1).
- **Source code not provided** (R2).
- **Minor issues:** Typo: “Radon-Nikodyim” should be “Radon-Nikodym” (R2). Figures 1 and 2 are too small to read (R4). The paper would benefit from an explicit discussion of the connection to MINE (title) (R2). The term “confounding factors” (Section 5.1) needs clarification (R2).

Note: While Reviewer 4 is more positive overall (score 8), the weaknesses listed above represent concerns shared by multiple reviewers, particularly regarding clarity, missing benchmarks, and computational costs. Reviewer 3 and others with score 6 express more skepticism about the novelty and presentation.
