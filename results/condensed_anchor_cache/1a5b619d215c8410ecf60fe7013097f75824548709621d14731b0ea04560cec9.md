## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviewers evaluate Diffusion-TS, a diffusion-based model for multivariate time series generation that uses an encoder-decoder transformer with disentangled trend and seasonality representations and a Fourier-based loss. The model is trained to directly reconstruct the sample rather than the noise and extends to conditional tasks (forecasting, imputation) without architectural changes. All three reviewers agree that Diffusion-TS outperforms existing baselines (TimeGAN, TimeVAE, Diffwave, Cot-GAN) on several datasets, especially for high-dimensional and long-term generation. However, there is disagreement on novelty and clarity: Reviewer 2 (rating 5) strongly contests the claim of being the first DDPM-based framework for unconditional/conditional time series synthesis, citing prior work (Lim et al., 2023), while Reviewers 1 (rating 8) and 3 (rating 6) find the combination of trend-seasonality decomposition with diffusion novel. Concerns are raised about the lack of ablations for key components, limited conditional evaluation, inconsistent metrics, and unconvincing qualitative results.

### Strengths

- The idea of combining a trend-seasonality decomposition with diffusion for time series generation is novel, and the method is generally well presented (R1, R3).
- The model explores both unconditional and conditional generation using a single model (R2).
- The experiments are generally well-constructed, using a variety of datasets and evaluation metrics, and include ablation studies in the appendix (R3).
- Diffusion-TS outperforms existing generative models (TimeGAN, TimeVAE, Diffwave, Cot-GAN) in unconditional generation across multiple metrics (R1, R2, R3). The performance gap is especially clear for higher-dimensional datasets and long-term generation (Table 2) (R3).
- Qualitative results show that the predicted trend and seasonality components behave as intended (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and Related Work Claims:** One reviewer (R2) strongly contests the claim that "little attention has been given to leveraging the powerful generative ability for general time series production" and the assertion of being the "first DDPM-based framework for both unconditional and conditional time series synthesis." They point to prior work (Lim et al., 2023) that also proposes a DDPM-based model for time series generation with conditional guidance, limiting the novelty, particularly of Sec. 3.4. Another reviewer (R1) considered the idea generally well presented, and R3 found the combination novel, but R2’s criticism is significant.
- **Writing and Presentation:** R2 rates the paper as poorly written, not telling a coherent story; Sec. 3.2 reads like an arbitrary combination of ideas; there are typographical errors (e.g., "synthetic" instead of "synthesis", "DSDI" instead of "CSDI"). R1 and R3 rate presentation as 3 (good), but R2 gives 2 (fair).
- **Lack of Ablations for Key Components:** It is unclear which design choices are crucial to performance, especially the trend-seasonality decomposition, the polynomial regressor, and the Fourier synthetic layers (R2, R3). Ablation studies are mentioned in the appendix but do not isolate these components. R2 asks for ablations and references results from Lim et al. (2023) which does not use these components. R3 asks what the "w/o interpretability" model is and whether it sets the trend/seasonality terms to zero (Appendix C.2). The interaction between these components and the diffusion process is not well-defined (R3).
- **Conditional Generation Evaluation is Limited:** The conditional tasks (forecasting and imputation) are only compared against Diffwave and CSDI, which are closely related (R2). Missing comparisons with established time series forecasting/imputation baselines (R2). The choice of Diffwave as a baseline is questionable given its design for audio, not multivariate time series (R2). It is also unclear how the CSDI and Diffwave baselines were trained (R2).
- **Inconsistent Experimental Metrics:** The metrics reported vary across experiments (e.g., correlational and context-FID in Table 2, discriminative and predictive scores in Figure 7 and Appendix C.1/C.2), making cross-task comparisons difficult (R3). It is unclear why certain metrics were chosen for specific experiments.
- **Qualitative Results Are Not Entirely Compelling:** In Figure 3, the trend components appear very smooth and may not capture complex trend dynamics (R3). The t-SNE visualizations in Figure 4 do not clearly show better alignment with the data distribution than TimeGAN, and the assessment is subjective (R3). R1 also questions whether column b in Figure 3 is the ground truth and column a the noisy input.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Generalization to Non-Seasonal Data:** R1 questions the impact of hyperparameters on results and convergence speed, and notes that the method may work well only on typical seasonal data. They specifically ask about performance on data with big jumps (e.g., stock prices) and note that the Google stock data used (2004‑2019) excludes the large changes in 2020 and 2022, while other datasets (electricity transformer, blood oxygen) are stable. This raises concern about generalization to datasets with unique features.
- **Questions about Experimental Setup:** R2 questions why Diffwave performs so much worse on long time series given that the sequence lengths are smaller than those used in audio, suggesting a possible bug. R3 asks why Diffusion-TS performs better with longer time series (which should be harder) and how MAE is computed in Figure 6 (only over missing data or full series?). R3 also asks about parameter count comparisons between Diffusion-TS and competitors. R1 asks for more specific dataset names/links.
- **Missing Comparison for Unconditional Generation:** R2 suggests that a comparison against a CSDI-like model for unconditional generation would be a better baseline and ablation for the proposed components.
