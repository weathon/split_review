## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a framework for achieving long-term group fairness in dynamical systems where current decisions affect future individual features. The system is modeled as a time-homogeneous Markov chain, and a time-independent policy is identified that converges to a targeted fair stationary distribution, regardless of initial conditions. The problem is formulated as a constrained linear optimization, and the Markov chain convergence theorem guarantees uniqueness. Several fairness metrics are discussed, and simulations are conducted on a loan repayment dataset.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clearly presented, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).
- The problem is important, interesting, and relevant to the ICLR community (R1, R2, R3).
- The framework is flexible and general, allowing various long-term fairness objectives to be incorporated (R2, R4).
- Convergence to the targeted stationary distribution is guaranteed under the standard Markov convergence assumptions (R4).
- Simulations are extensive (R1).
- One reviewer rated the paper highly (score 5), emphasizing its clear presentation and relevance, though this reviewer noted low confidence and still raised concerns (R3). The other three reviewers rated the paper 3, indicating disagreement on overall quality.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) found the contribution thin. Reviewer 1 notes that Theorem 4.1 is a known result and the fairness discussions are standard, with too much space devoted to basic MDP/Markov chain concepts. Reviewer 2 states no new computational or analytic tools are developed. Reviewer 3 points out strong similarity to (Zhang et al., 2020), with inadequate discussion of differences; the models and techniques appear very similar. Reviewer 4 argues the framework is a reformulation of existing models and the advantage over prior work is unclear. (Confidence: R1 fairly confident, R2 confident, R3 low confidence, R4 confident.)
- **Unrealistic modeling assumptions:** Reviewer 2, with high confidence, questions the Markovian assumption (memoryless, linear dynamics) and the assumption that the controller can induce a single uniquely attracting stationary distribution. They cite recent work (e.g., [1]) showing such dynamics can be chaotic with infinitely many stationary distributions, in stark contrast to this paper's perspective. The assumptions seem chosen for tractability without sufficient justification. A connection to game-theoretic models is requested.
- **Insufficient empirical evaluation:** Reviewer 3 notes only one dataset (FICO) is used, whereas (Zhang et al., 2020) used two; this raises generalizability concerns. They ask why only one dataset was considered. Reviewer 4 finds the simulation description unclear, specifically what the target fairness state is and why \(P(Y=1|S=0)\) would differ from \(P(Y=1|S=1)\).
- **Unfair comparison to RL:** Reviewer 4 observes that the paper claims improvement over RL-based methods which require large amounts of training data, but here the model is assumed known, reducing the problem to an optimization with known parameters. This is not a fair comparison, and the claimed advantage is questionable. (Confidence: high.)
- **Unclear advantage of unified framework:** Reviewer 4 questions what the framework adds over existing long-term fairness constraints—does it offer faster solutions for complex, large-state dynamics, or is it merely a reformulation?
- **Excessive background exposition:** Reviewer 1 notes that significant space is used to explain basic MDP and Markov chain properties, which detracts from novelty. (Confidence: fairly confident.)
