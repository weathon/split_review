## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper systematically evaluates the self-verification capabilities of LLMs (GPT-4) on reasoning and planning tasks (Game of 24, Graph Coloring, STRIPS planning). It finds that self-critique/iterative prompting does not improve performance and can even degrade it, while the use of a “sound” external verifier significantly boosts accuracy. The study challenges the assumption that verification is easier than generation for LLMs. Reviewers generally agree the paper is well-written and the experiments are clear, but they raise concerns about limited domain scope, the fairness of comparing to oracle verifiers, insufficient analysis of why self-critique fails, and overclaimed conclusions. One reviewer notes a notable exception: in the Blocksworld domain self-verification improved accuracy from 40% to 55%, which contradicts the paper’s broad negative claim.

### Strengths
- Novel systematic evaluation of LLM self-verification, challenging the common assumption that LLMs can effectively self-critique and iteratively improve their own outputs (all reviewers).
- Clear writing and well-structured experimental setup; methodology is described in sufficient detail to allow reproducibility (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Careful dataset construction to avoid memorization – authors generated new instances and modified task solution formats to ensure validity (R3, R4).
- Extensive related work section that clearly identifies problems in prior studies of self-verification (R2).
- All claims are substantiated by appropriate experiments (R2).
- Significance for understanding limitations of LLMs in safety-critical or reliability-demanding applications (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited domain scope**: Only three tasks (Game of 24, Graph Coloring, STRIPS planning) are evaluated; findings may not generalize to other reasoning types (e.g., common sense, nuanced context, long-term dependencies). Reviewers suggest more varied tasks are needed (R1, R2, R4).
- **Task difficulty and counterexample**: The paper’s broad conclusion that self-verification never helps is contradicted by the Blocksworld results, where self-verification improved accuracy from 40% to 55%. The verification performance varies across tasks (especially False Negative Rate), indicating that self-verification can be beneficial when verification is easier than generation (R3).
- **Comparison to oracle verifier**: Using a “sound” (ground-truth) verifier makes the improvement unsurprising. The paper does not explore whether LLMs can serve as effective approximate verifiers in domains where perfect verification is not feasible (e.g., creative writing) (R4).
- **Lack of analysis on failure causes**: The discussion does not include specific examples of LLM self-critique failures or a deep analysis of why the model fails to verify its own solutions (R1).
- **No computational cost/efficiency comparison**: Practical deployment considerations (e.g., latency, cost of external verifiers vs. self-verification) are omitted (R1).
- **Missing theoretical implications**: The paper does not connect its findings to computational complexity arguments about verification vs. generation, which could provide deeper insight (R1).
- **Overclaiming and model status**: The claim that GPT-4 is “state-of-the-art” is outdated; the model no longer holds SotA status on many benchmarks. This undermines credibility (R4). The exact GPT-4 version should be specified, and more recent models should be tested (R3, R4).
- **Generalizability of experimental protocol**: The choice of tasks and assumptions about task difficulty are not explicitly discussed, leaving doubts about whether the findings will hold for other transformer-based auto-regressive models or different reasoning scenarios (R2).
- **Minor writing issues**: Multiple typos, typesetting problems, and incorrect use of `\citep`/`\citet` citations (R2 minor, R4).
