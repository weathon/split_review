## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EfficientQAT, a quantization-aware training (QAT) framework for LLMs with two phases: block-wise training of all parameters (Block-AP) and end-to-end training of quantization step sizes (E2E-QP). Block-AP reduces memory by training one block at a time; E2E-QP globally optimizes scales. Experiments claim state-of-the-art across 7B–70B models at low bits. Example: 2-bit Llama-2-70B trained on a single A100-80GB in 41 hours with <3 point accuracy degradation (69.48 vs. 72.41).

### Strengths
- Two-phase approach is novel: Block-AP is the first method to directly train all parameters block-wise, expanding the solution space and reducing accuracy loss at low bits. E2E-QP then trains only quantization step sizes end-to-end, capturing cross-block interactions. (All reviewers)
- Block-wise QAT reduces GPU memory and total training time compared to traditional full-model QAT. (R2, R3)
- Good performance on small models (LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B) with less than 4 bits. (R2)
- Concrete example: 2-bit Llama-2-70B on a single A100-80GB GPU in 41 hours with less than 3 points accuracy loss (69.48 vs. 72.41). (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The method is a straightforward adaptation of existing block-wise/layer-wise quantization and pruning techniques for LLMs (e.g., GPTQ, Layer-Wise Quantization, Streamlining Redundant Layers). Training part of the model while freezing others to save memory is already explored in (Li et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2023). The overall QAT follows traditional weight and scale learning. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Unfair comparisons.** The proposed method performs weight-only quantization, while several baselines (OmniQuant, LLM-QAT) quantize both weights and activations. This makes direct accuracy comparisons misleading. (R1, R2)
- **Inadequate performance versus PTQ methods.** In Table 1, EfficientQAT does not outperform AWQ (a post-training method) on several settings: 4-bit LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, LLaMA-3-70B, and 3-bit LLaMA-3-70B. In Table 3, it is worse than QuIP# and AQLM in almost all settings. In Table 15 (2-7B model), it has the lowest accuracy among all methods. AWQ typically costs less resource than QAT methods, but still achieves better performance—this needs discussion. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Training data and setting mismatch.** EfficientQAT uses 4096 samples from RedPajama with 2048 sequence length for Block-AP and 4096 for E2E-QP. Post-training quantization baselines (GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant) originally use only 128 calibration samples from WikiText or C4. The results in Table 13 show that using Wiki or C4 as training datasets yields worse performance. This makes the comparison unfair, especially since Figure 3 shows high sensitivity to number of samples. The paper does not specify whether baselines were recalibrated with the same data or what settings were used. (R2, R3)
- **Missing key baselines.** LoRA-based methods (QLoRA with GPTQ weights, QA-LoRA, IR-QLoRA, PEQA) are only in Table 4, not in the main results tables. They should also be included in training time comparisons (Table 9) with the same training data and settings. (R2)
- **Missing ablation and baselines for block-wise training.** It is unclear whether the improvement comes from Block-AP or from E2E-QP. An ablation is needed: apply E2E-QP to weights from post-training methods (GPTQ, AWQ, QA-LoRA) and compare to using Block-AP weights. (R2)  
  A more direct baseline is to train all blocks in one round instead of block-wise. The paper should compare with that to demonstrate that block-wise training actually saves time or improves accuracy (e.g., PPL/time trade-off). (R3)
- **Training time comparison not solid.** Table 9 compares training time across methods, but different methods use different amounts of training data. Using less data naturally leads to lower time; a fair comparison requires the same training data for all methods. (R3)
- **Missing 16-bit activation results.** The paper does not report results with 16-bit activations, even though activation quantization is a major source of overhead in LLM quantization. (R2)
- **Block-AP still uses full-parameter fine-tuning per block**, which may be more resource-heavy than LoRA-based methods. Since LoRA methods optimize globally, it is questionable whether Block-AP’s local optimization is beneficial. (R2)
- **Questions incorporated into weaknesses:** See points above regarding training data settings, missing comparisons with LoRA-based methods, missing ablation for E2E-QP on PTQ weights, missing baseline of training all blocks at once, and missing 16-bit activation results. (All reviewers)
