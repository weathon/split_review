## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Chain-of-Experts (CoE), a multi-agent framework using LLMs to solve complex operations research problems. CoE assigns specific roles to 11 expert agents (powered by in-context learning and reasoning) and a conductor agent that orchestrates them via forward thought construction and backward reflection using external feedback. The framework requires no parameter updates. The authors also release ComplexOR, a new benchmark of 20 expert-annotated OR problems. Experiments on LPWP and ComplexOR using GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Claude-2 show CoE outperforms state-of-the-art LLM-based prompting methods. Ablation analyses quantify contributions of each design choice.

### Strengths

- CoE is an interesting and novel framework for multi-agent collaboration (Reviewer 3). The methodology is straightforward, easy to implement, and achieves strong empirical results across two benchmarks (Reviewer 1).
- The use of external feedback (from program execution) for backward reflection is innovative and boosts performance (Reviewer 1). CoE’s design is grounded in real-world OR applications and proves effective (Reviewer 3).
- No training is required, making CoE applicable to both open-source and proprietary LLMs (Reviewer 3).
- The paper releases a new OR dataset (ComplexOR) that, based on experimental outcomes, is more challenging than existing ones (Reviewer 1). A combination of existing techniques is effectively applied to OR problems (Reviewer 4).
- Presentation is reasonably clear (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and comparison with prior work**: Novelty is somewhat limited—the “planning with feedback” approach has been explored in many existing works (e.g., A Survey on Large Language Model based Autonomous Agents). The authors should provide a more in-depth comparison (Reviewer 1). The multi-expert framework essentially uses the same LLM with different prompts; the distinction between experts is primarily in prompt design rather than a fundamentally different architecture (Reviewer 1). How does CoE differ from Solo Performance Prompting? (Reviewer 1 question). It would be interesting to compare to a baseline that applies CoE but uses the same model to play all roles (Reviewer 3 question).

- **Dataset size and significance**: ComplexOR is very small (20 problems). The results on it may not be significant, and the paper should provide more details on the consistency of results across multiple runs (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 finds the results “not sense” given the small dataset.

- **Lack of ablation on components**: There is no detailed evaluation of individual expert agents, nor of the conductor’s contribution (Reviewer 2). The impact of using less competent LLMs (smaller or open-source models) is not explored (Reviewer 2 question).

- **Fairness of comparison**: Comparisons with other models may not be fair because CoE calls the LLM differently (e.g., more times, different roles). The paper should add measurements of how different methods use the LLMs (e.g., number of calls, token cost) (Reviewer 2).

- **Clarity and motivation of the framework**: The description of CoE is not completely clear; it should be better motivated and begin with background on OR problems (Reviewer 4). More details are needed on how the experts are specialized (Reviewer 3).

- **Generality and benchmark choice**: CoE was not tested on well-established reasoning benchmarks, which would increase its impact on the LM community (Reviewer 3). Is CoE suitable for other reasoning tasks? What would differ if applied elsewhere? (Reviewer 4 question).

- **Notation issues**: Equation 3 reads as if applying the LLM to a prompt template outputs new parameters, which does not align with prompting. The use of θ notation in Eqs. 2 and 3 may be unnecessary (Reviewer 3 question).
