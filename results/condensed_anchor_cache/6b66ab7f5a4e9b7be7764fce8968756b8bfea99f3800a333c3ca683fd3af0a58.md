## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an offline tracking framework leveraging object permanence for labeling large-scale autonomous driving datasets. It consists of three components: (1) an off-the-shelf detector and online tracker to generate initial tracklets, (2) a Re-ID module for tracklet association incorporating motion and lane map information in a matching matrix, and (3) a track completion module that fuses time query embeddings for trajectory regression to handle occlusions. Experiments are conducted on nuScenes under different evaluation setups. Reviewer assessments diverge on novelty and significance (scores: 5, 5, 3, 3).

### Strengths
- Addresses an essential problem: reducing expensive human labor in labeling autonomous driving datasets. (R1)
- Quantitative and qualitative results show some superiority over compared approaches. (R1)
- The framework is considered novel by some reviewers: embedding both motion and lane map to obtain the final matching matrix, and fusing time query embeddings for trajectory regression. (R2)
- The method is evaluated under different setups, demonstrating effectiveness in addressing occlusion situations. (R2)
- The neural models seem innovative and novel; the attempt to combine tracklet association with a priori knowledge of lane maps in an end-to-end framework is promising. (R3)
- The paper proposes a method to handle occluded object tracking. (R4)
(Note: R1 and R4 found limited technical contribution and novelty; R2 and R3 found some novelty.)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and technical contribution**: The approach heavily relies on off-the-shelf detectors/trackers, and components (tracker, Re-ID, track completion) employ established techniques, resulting in a relatively straightforward solution. (R1, R3, R4) The core concept of object permanence for tracking was already introduced in previous work (Tokmakov et al. 2021). (R1, R2) The three steps of MOT (tracklet generation, association, completion) are not new (Zhang, Li, Nevatia 2012). (R2) The paper does not clearly articulate how it innovates beyond combining existing components; the track completion module appears to be a direct adaptation of existing techniques. (R1) Reviewer disagreement: R1 and R4 rated contribution poor (1), R2 and R3 fair (2).

2. **Insufficient improvement over baselines**: The proposed method does not show significant improvements over the compared Immortal tracker in Tables 1–3. (R1) One reviewer questions whether the Immortal tracker has a specific design that makes the comparison unfair. (R1) The abstract claims a 45% improvement in IDS, but experiments do not confirm this: Table 2 shows the causal MOT method (Wang et al., 2021) achieves 109 IDS, while the proposed method achieves 147+ IDS depending on the version. (R2) The performance measures in Tables 1 and 3 also do not show clear evidence of improvement over the causal MOT approach. (R2) A reviewer asks why the proposed method is superior to the compared causal trackers. (R3) Another questions whether the method with approx. AMOTP 0.603 and IDS 145 allows viable labeling of datasets. (R3)

3. **Missing implementation and training details**: The paper lacks crucial hyper-parameters (learning rates, batch sizes, optimization algorithms, data augmentation) and specifications of off-the-shelf detector/tracker configurations, making reproduction difficult. (R1)

4. **Clarity and understandability**: The paper is difficult to follow; many details are presented in a coarse rather than detailed manner, and it is unclear what exactly the contribution is. (R2)

5. **Questions about design choices and missing analyses**:
   - In tracklet association, the separation of map and motion data may lose complementary information; map information could enhance motion association. (R4)
   - Appearance features could serve as a pivotal factor for tracklet association when objects re-emerge after occlusion. (R4)
   - How does map information affect association when an object undergoes occlusion and a simultaneous lane change? This adds complexity that is not investigated. (R4)
   - Why is the proposed method better suited for offline labelling than online trackers? (R3)
