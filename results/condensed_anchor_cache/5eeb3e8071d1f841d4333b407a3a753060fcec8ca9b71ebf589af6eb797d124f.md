## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose a framework integrating domain knowledge (expressed as Knowledge Graphs) into neural network training, inspired by control barrier functions (CBF) from control theory. They design a differentiable constraint that enforces the model’s internal representations to respect the graph structure of the domain, aiming for “domain-grounding” (adherence to underlying physical/causal processes). The method is applied to spatiotemporal reasoning tasks on CLEVRER and CLEVRER-Humans (video question answering). The paper includes theoretical analysis (forward invariance property, reformulation to a soft-constrained problem) and reports improved QA accuracy and interpretability scores compared to standard GNN baselines. Reviewer opinions diverge strongly: three reviewers rated the paper 3 (marginally positive) and one gave 1 (clearly negative), citing serious presentation and clarity issues.

### Strengths
- The analogy to dynamical system constraints and control barrier functions provides a principled way to impose inductive biases in neural networks, with the forward invariance property receiving particular attention (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Merging neural processing with symbolic information (knowledge graphs) for spatiotemporal reasoning is timely and promising (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of training networks whose parameters abide by ground-truth graph structures is clever, and the analysis of converting constrained optimization into a soft-constrained formulation is appreciated (Reviewer 4).
- The approach improves domain-grounding and question-answering accuracy while offering an interpretability score that quantifies constraint compliance (Reviewer 1).
- The method helps recover graphical structures underlying physical trajectories (e.g., collision event graphs) (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited related work and novelty concerns:** The paper’s view of “domain grounding” is too narrow, omitting substantial prior work on grounding (e.g., visual scenes, external knowledge, retrieval) and missing citations to relevant Neuro-Symbolic AI works (e.g., De Raedt et al., Goyal & Bengio), GNNs in vision (Senior et al.), and semantic regularization losses (Diligenti et al., Xu et al.) (Reviewers 1, 2). It is not clearly explained how the CBF-inspired formulation differs from a standard auxiliary training loss or from existing methods that constrain hidden representations (e.g., “Embedding Symbolic Knowledge into Deep Networks”) (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Scaling and domain specificity:** Defining the KG constraint requires strong human prior knowledge, making the method highly domain-specific and potentially difficult to transfer to other domains (Reviewer 1).
- **Irrelevant and confusing examples:** The paper extensively describes drone dynamics and knowledge graphs for drone navigation, which are completely irrelevant to the CLEVRER experiments. This detracts from clarity and relevance (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Poor presentation and missing details:** The paper is poorly organized; many critical modelling details are in figure captions, and the figures (e.g., Figures 3, 6) are confusing. The authors should reorganize with a supplementary section (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Specific missing details include:
  - The exact form and prediction task of the knowledge graphs used in CLEVRER experiments (Reviewer 3).
  - How ground-truth graphs are derived from CLEVRER and CLEVRER-Humans (collision events, domain models) (Reviewer 4).
  - Understanding of the proposed graph construction: what are α and β in the “asymmetric inner product”? How are nodes derived from input x and the last hidden layer? Is the model sensitive to the initial choice of the ground-truth graph? (Reviewer 2).
  - The motivation for not using standard GNNs and the absence of ablation studies showing the importance of the complex method in Figure 3 (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical guarantees vs. practical compliance:** The forward invariance property requires initial states satisfying the constraints, but this is hard to enforce at the start of learning. The paper does not discuss what happens when the model is not fully compliant or how it behaves in those scenarios (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear training/test paradigm:** It is unclear whether the knowledge graph is provided at both training and test time, and how it is queried for collision events in CLEVRER (Reviewer 4).
- **Inappropriate baselines:** Experimental comparisons are made against transductive KG methods that do not straightforwardly relate to the CLEVRER QA task; the chosen baselines and their relevance should be justified more clearly (Reviewer 3).
- **Assumption of uniform edge relations:** The method appears to assume knowledge graphs where all edges encode the same relation, limiting applicability to more heterogeneous graphs (Reviewer 4).
