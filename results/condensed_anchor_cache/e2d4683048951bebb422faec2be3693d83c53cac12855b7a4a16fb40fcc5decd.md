## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces three enhancements for diffusion model controllability: training with separate content and style encoders, a generalized composable diffusion model (GCDM) for multi-condition sampling with dependent conditions, and a timestep-dependent weight schedule. Three of four reviewers are negative (score 3) and one is positive (score 6). The minority positive reviewer praises the disentanglement and controllability, while the negative reviewers raise significant concerns about novelty, scalability, hyperparameter tuning, lack of ablations, and questionable style-content separation.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: controllability and feature disentanglement in diffusion models (all reviewers agree this is a worthwhile direction).
- Simple, easy-to-understand approach (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical improvement over Composable Diffusion Models (CDM) on specific composite conditions (e.g., “octopus next to pyramid”, “bear with car”) (Reviewer 1).
- Achieves good quantitative performance on FFHQ, AFHQ, and LSUN datasets (Reviewer 4; also mentioned by Reviewer 1 as improved over baselines).
- Novel disentanglement of content and style latent spaces, offering enhanced controllability (Reviewer 2’s main strength; others note this but question its effectiveness).
- The GCDM formulation is a straightforward extension of CDM (Reviewer 1 notes limited novelty, but Reviewer 2 views it as a strength for composition).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** (Reviewer 1): The core addition (joint conditioning in Eq. 3 vs independent conditions in CDM) is simple; Proposition 3.2 only states known relations (GCDM generalizes CDM, CDM generalizes CFG). No fundamentally new insight.
- **Scalability is unclear** (Reviewer 1): Joint terms grow combinatorially with number of conditions. Example: with 4 conditions, the model must evaluate 7+ conditional scores, making the method intractable for many conditions.
- **Excessive hyperparameters** (Reviewer 1): At least 9 new hyperparameters (a, b, α, λ, β_i, t1, t2, t3) are introduced; no guidance on selection or sensitivity analysis. Improvements may be due to hand-tuning.
- **Missing ablation studies** (Reviewer 1, 3): The paper proposes several orthogonal components (timestep scheduling, AdaGN layer, separate encoders) but does not isolate their contributions. For example, the difference between sigmoid and linear weighting schedules in Figure 15 is very small (Reviewer 3).
- **Style encoding is not truly stylistic** (Reviewer 4): The “style” condition often looks like another object feature condition (e.g., both faces, cat → tiger). All tested pairs are semantically close (faces, cats/dogs, cats/tigers), undermining the claim of general style transfer. No experiment with a clearly different style (e.g., a painting) is provided.
- **Weak comparison to Swapping Autoencoders (SAE)** (Reviewer 3): In Figure 4, SAE results appear more plausible than the proposed method; the authors do not show significant advantage over this strong baseline.
- **Poor comparison to DiffAE+MagicMix** (Reviewer 3): The presented results have very low image quality (using x_600 as input); the authors did not explore other parameters or provide additional results.
- **Unclear exposition and formatting** (Reviewer 1): Equations are not numbered, variables are undefined (e.g., ε_t in the first unnumbered equation). Sections 3.1 and 3.3 are hard to reconcile (what is ε(x_t,t,c1,c2)? Are z_s and z_c just c1 and c2?).
- **Complexity and training from scratch** (Reviewer 2, 3): The method requires retraining large diffusion models with two extra encoders, making it impractical for large-scale text-to-image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion).
- **Overlapping latent spaces** (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewer 4): It is unclear whether the content and style latent spaces are truly independent; no verification (e.g., quantitative disentanglement metric) is provided.
- **Heuristic style encoder** (Reviewer 2): The style code formulation is based on human heuristics rather than a data-driven approach; it is unclear which styles can actually be controlled.
- **Missing quantitative comparison of GCDM to CDM** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not provide quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, CLIP score) for the GCDM component against existing composable models.
- **Lack of demonstration on diverse conditions** (Reviewer 4): The method is only shown on similar pairs; its effectiveness on truly diverse content–style combinations is untested.
- **Minority positive reviewer disagreement** (Reviewer 2 vs all others): Reviewer 2 gives a score of 6 and views the disentanglement as a key strength, while the other three reviewers (scores 3 each) consider the method’s limitations severe.
