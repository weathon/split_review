## Merged Review

### Summary
VEnhancer is a generative space-time enhancement framework that uses a single video diffusion model to simultaneously increase spatial resolution and frame rate of text-to-video generated videos. It is built by taking a pretrained video diffusion model (I2VGen-XL backbone) and adding a space-time control branch (ST‑Controller) conditioned on the low-frame-rate, low-resolution input video. Training relies on space-time data augmentation and video-aware conditioning, including noise augmentation and downscale factor embeddings. The model is evaluated on AI-generated videos, achieving improved metrics (MUSIQ, DOVER) and human preference over existing video super-resolution and space-time super-resolution methods.

### Strengths
- Unified model that performs both spatial and temporal upscaling in one framework, supporting arbitrary scaling factors in space and time.
- Effective integration of a pretrained video prior with a ControlNet-like branch, enabling end-to-end training.
- Outperforms current state-of-the-art video super-resolution and space-time super-resolution methods on AI-generated videos in both quantitative metrics and human evaluation.
- Improves spatial details, temporal smoothness, and reduces artifacts/flickering; demonstrated as a valuable complement to open-source text-to-video models (e.g., VideoCrafter-2, top on VBench).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited architectural novelty.** The ST‑Controller closely follows ControlNet (copying the UNet encoder, zero-initializing the output layer). The paper lacks justification for why this design is optimal for video super-resolution and does not explore alternative conditioning methods (attention, adaptive fusion). This concern is raised by multiple reviewers (R3, R4) with high confidence; R4 in particular finds the core idea not substantially different from ControlNet. (R1 and R2 did not flag this issue, creating a notable disagreement.)
- **Video-aware conditioning reuses existing techniques.** Noise augmentation is taken from Cascaded Diffusion Models (Ho et al., 2021), temporal embedding is standard, and conditioning on downscale factor resembles FPS conditioning in Make‑A‑Video. The role of the σ parameter is not well motivated; its effects are not analyzed in the main paper (R3, R4).
- **Space-time data augmentation lacks quantitative analysis.** The impact of different augmentation strategies is only ablated in the appendix, not the main paper. The method for handling different interval `m` is unclear, and the conditioning on `m` is not fully explained (R3, R1).
- **Missing comparisons with related diffusion-based video super‑resolution methods.** Upscale‑a‑Video is not compared. More broadly, the paper relies heavily on visual results and lacks rigorous quantitative comparisons of computational cost, memory usage, and inference time against state‑of‑the‑art approaches (R2, R3). This leads some reviewers (R3) to characterize the work as “engineering rather than research.”
- **Limited generalization.** The method is only tested on AI-generated videos; no experiments on real‑world videos are provided, limiting practical applicability (R4). Additionally, a separate VEnhancer must be trained for each base text-to-video model, preventing it from being a standalone toolkit (R2).
- **Missing inference details and efficiency analysis.** The inference procedure (initialization of latents, pseudocode) is not specified, and no inference times or performance across different spatial/temporal scales are reported (R1). The complexity of diffusion models likely leads to long inference times (R2).
- **Dataset and training details insufficient.** The paper does not discuss how the 350K subset was filtered from Panda 70M (R2). Details of the AIGC2023 test set (number of videos, resolutions, lengths, release plans) are missing (R4).
- **Unexplained metric discrepancies.** Improvements on Aesthetic Quality, Degree of Motion, and Smoothness are minor compared to MUSIQ and DOVER; the gain on CogVideoX‑5B is much smaller than on VideoCrafter‑2. No explanation is provided (R4).
- **Notation and clarity errors.** Wrong notation for sequence indexing (R1), incorrect illustration of noise addition in Fig. 2 (latent vs. pixel space), typos (“ramdom”), misplaced period in the “Conclusion and Limitation” title (R1, R3). Presentation scores of 2 from two reviewers indicate expository weaknesses.
