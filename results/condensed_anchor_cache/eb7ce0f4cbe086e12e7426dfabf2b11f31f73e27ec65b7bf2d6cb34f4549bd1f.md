- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 6, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SLR-AVD, which uses an LLM to generate multiple visual descriptions per class, a VLM to embed them, and sparse logistic regression to select discriminative descriptors for few-shot classification. Reviewers generally agree the idea is novel and the theoretical analysis on invariance is interesting, but raise serious concerns about the marginal empirical gains, insufficient ablations, unfair comparisons (especially with CoOp), lack of clarity in presentation and experimental details, and misalignment between theory and method.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of the approach**: Extracting multiple visual features per class via an LLM and using L1-regularized logistic regression to select the most discriminative prompts is a new idea (R1, R2). The combination with finetuning schemes like LP-FT is instrumental (R4).
- **Motivation and soundness**: The method is well-motivated; both visual descriptions and sparsity are principled ways to improve robustness (R3). The generated descriptive features are proven to retain substantial information about true labels and be invariant to domain shift (R1). The theoretical analysis on feature compression/invariance in CLIP’s joint VL space is novel and inspiring (R4).
- **Potential impact**: The work fits within visual representation learning under text supervision and provides insights that can benefit the community (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal zero-shot gains and limited experimental evidence**:
  - In Table 1, ZS-VD performs worse than ZS, and ZS-AVD only yields marginal improvements over ZS: 0.74 on IN, 0.74 on IN-V2, 0.13 on IN-R, 0.23 on IN-A, 0.27 on ObjectNet (R1). This undermines the motivation for generating descriptions (R2). Reviewer 2 notes that Section 4.1’s statement that “class names are probably one of the strongest prompts” contradicts the paper’s own motivation (R2).
  - The few-shot results in Tables 2 & 3 show SLR outperforming FT and LP, but this is expected because SLR derives classifiers from CLIP’s text encoder rather than training from scratch with few samples (R4). Comparisons with simpler baselines (e.g., Menon & Vondrick’s average ensemble, WaffleCLIP random descriptors, k-NN classifiers on both vision and VL features) are missing (R4).

- **Lack of ablations and systematic comparisons**:
  - No ablation of the sparse logistic regression: how does a plain logistic regression (L2) or an MLP with similar parameter count compare? (R2, R3). Also, dropping descriptions entirely or using random projections instead of the U matrices is not ablated (R3).
  - No overall comparison table with a consistent backbone (e.g., ViT-B/16) across all methods (ZS, LP, MLP probing, Full Finetuning, CoOp, Wise) and a standard few-shot setting (e.g., k=16) (R3).
  - Few-shot evaluations are shown only up to 4 or 16 shots; performance across 1,2,4,8,16,32 shots and larger training sets (64,256,1024) is missing, which would clarify at what scale the method is most beneficial (R3).
  - The impact of the number and diversity of generated prompts on performance is not explored (R2).

- **Unfair comparison with CoOp**:
  - The paper states that CoOp injects a strong prior by using class names directly, so they interpolate the learned head to “make it fair” – this justification is unclear and the adjustment biases the comparison (R3). CoOp should be compared directly without modification, and with ViT models (not only ResNet-50) (R3).
  - Combining the proposed method with Wise and comparing to plain CoOp is not a proper baseline (R3).

- **Theoretical analysis misaligned with claimed contribution**:
  - The analysis in Sec 3.3 shows that CLIP’s joint VL space features (H_avd) are more compressed and invariant than raw vision features, but this does not explain why using descriptions is better than using default templates (e.g., ensemble of templates) (R4). Figure 2 shows I(H_avd; A) is almost identical to I(H_cp; A), implying descriptions introduce no additional invariance; I(H_avd; Y) is higher, yet the zero-shot gain in Table 1 is marginal (R4). The connection between theory and the proposed method is weak (R4).

- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Training and inference are unclear: loss function not specified; the three W matrices (W_vd, W_cp, W_avd) and their roles as zero-shot classifiers are not explained clearly; regularization and feature selection (top-3 per class) are not mathematically defined (R1). Notation in Sec 3.1 (U and W) is confusing and would benefit from a diagram (R2). The paper has many acronyms with unclear distinctions (e.g., WISE-FT+SLR-AVD vs WISE-FT+LP); an expanded acronym table is needed (R3). Figure 4 is unclear, sub-caption in bottom-right corner is blocked (R1), symbols are inconsistent, and the optimal checkpoint is not shown (R3).
  - Figure 3 caption incorrectly states “the x-axis represents…” (should be y-axis) and the y-axis label is missing (R2). A missing closing parenthesis in Figure 1 (R2). The phrase “WLOG” is used undefined in Section 2 (R2). CLIP is referred to as “self-supervised” in the Introduction without justification (R2).

- **Hyperparameter and evaluation issues**:
  - Hyperparameter selection is not clearly described; for domain generalization, validation OOD data is used to choose alpha for Wise models, which may not reflect realistic settings (R3). The OOD test set in Section 4.2 is not specified (R1).
  - In Figure 4 (top), WISE-FT+LP (linear probing) is compared with WISE-FT+SLR-AVD (which appears to finetune the entire model); both should either update only the linear layer or do full finetuning for a fair comparison (R3). The difference between WISE-FT+LP and WISE-SLR is unclear (R3).
  - The number of learnable parameters for SLR-AVD vs linear probing is not reported (R3).

- **Evidence for intuitive feature selection**:
  - Section 5 claims sparse logistic regression “selects intuitive features”, but no evidence is provided (e.g., which descriptions are selected for which classes) (R2). The paper does not show whether L1 regularization selects different descriptors than L2 or k-NN (R2).

- **Minority reviewer note**: Reviewer 2 is substantially more negative (score 3, contribution 1) than the others (scores 5,6,5), arguing the contribution is poor and gains are not significant; this contrast should be noted.
