## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper extends the linear representation hypothesis to non-binary concepts (categorical and hierarchical) by proposing vector representations for features (e.g., is_animal) and polytope representations for categorical concepts. The authors formalize hierarchical relationships as orthogonality between representation vectors and validate these theoretical claims empirically on Gemma-2B and LLaMA-3-8B using over 900 concepts from WordNet. The work focuses on the unembedding layer and uses linear discriminant analysis (LDA) to estimate concept vectors, testing whether the geometry predicted by theory (orthogonality of hierarchical levels, simplex structure of co-hyponyms) is observed.

### Strengths

- **Innovative extension of the linear representation hypothesis** to cover non-binary, hierarchical, and categorical concepts using vectors and polytopes – a novel and intuitive perspective. [R1, R3]
- **Mathematical rigor and clear formalization**: The causal inner product (CIP) is used to define orthogonality for causally separable concepts (Equation 2.2), and theorems (e.g., Theorem 4 on magnitudes, Theorem 8 on hierarchical orthogonality) are derived with precise definitions and assumptions. This adds theoretical depth to prior work on binary concept directions. [R1, R3]
- **Exemplary quality and clarity** of the paper: well-written, easy to read, with clear visualizations. [R1]
- **Solid empirical validation**: The experimental setup (WordNet hierarchy, LDA estimation, 900+ concepts) convincingly tests hierarchical orthogonality, including a shuffling control that confirms the structure is non-random. [R1, R3]
- **Significance for LLM interpretability**: The results reveal intuitive linear structure in the unembedding layer and offer a promising foundation for analyzing more complex concepts in deeper layers (e.g., via detokenization or “inner vocabulary”). [R1] The approach may enhance downstream tasks like knowledge extraction and structured generation. [R3]
- **Emergent structure in LLMs** (especially at the token level) is a noteworthy scientific observation, contributing to discourse on whether LLMs encode geometric conceptual models. [R4]
- **Novel way to represent ontological hierarchies** in LLM representation spaces, with solid underlying theory. [R2]

### Weaknesses

- **Causal Inner Product (CIP) clarity and justification**: The paper relies heavily on CIP but provides insufficient explanation within the text. The definition of causal separability is vague; concrete examples of what is and is not causally separable are needed (e.g., is "red" causally separable from "apple"? is "running" from "jogging"?). The definition may be too stringent – e.g., "is_bird" and "is_nocturnal" are only defined on a shared domain (animal), but the current statement requires well-definedness everywhere. It is unclear how CIP differs from a standard Euclidean inner product and why that difference is crucial for semantic relationships. [R2, R4]
- **CIP’s role and necessity are unclear**: The mathematical setup assumes a single Euclidean embedding space and unembedding space for just the final token position, yet no further use is made of LLM architecture – raising suspicion that the same analysis could apply to any vector model. Where exactly is CIP used beyond proving Theorem 8? The polytope convexity is never exploited, and the Figure 1 example is a special case (at most one node per level has children), so a simpler characterization (“different levels are orthogonal, co-hyponyms form clusters”) might suffice without CIP. [R4]
- **Choice of transformation in CIP**: The paper does not explain how the invertible matrix **A** and constant vector **γ** in Equation 2.2 are determined, nor whether this choice generalizes across models. A discussion of parameter sensitivity and its impact on orthogonality results is missing. [R3]
- **Word-Token-Concept gap**: The methodology for extending WordNet-derived conceptual hierarchies (word-level) to LLM token-level representations is unclear. Only a brief mention appears in the appendix (line 955). It is not explained how tokenization issues are handled or how findings on WordNet are cross-validated with Gemma and LLaMA token-level vectors. This omission undermines the empirical claim that the geometric structure holds at the token level. [R4]
- **Limited evaluation scale and scope**: The dataset contains fewer than 600 nouns and 400 verbs – a relatively small sample for a large-scale validation. Only nouns and verbs are tested; it is unclear how the theory applies to other parts of speech (adjectives, adverbs) or abstract concepts (e.g., justice, beauty). [R2]
- **Need to situate work within vectorial semantics and ontology literature**: The paper does not compare thoroughly with prior work on hyponymy representations (e.g., using density matrices, hyperdimensional computing, or Gardenfors’ conceptual spaces). The claim of conceptual advance over “directional cones” may misrepresent the broader research context. Discussing connections to knowledge graphs and ontologies could strengthen applicability. [R2, R4]
- **Approximation and robustness issues**: The paper acknowledges that exact linear representations may not emerge due to optimization stochasticity, but does not quantitatively analyze deviations from perfect orthogonality or their impact on hierarchical reliability. Methods to measure or improve alignment (e.g., enforcing orthogonality, adjusting magnitudes) are not discussed. [R3]
- **Downstream impact not explored**: Hierarchical orthogonality is presented as a theoretical finding, but its practical consequences for interpretability or utility in hierarchical tasks (taxonomy creation, clustering, classification) are not demonstrated. Examples or case studies of improved model behavior are missing. [R3]
- **Missing citation clarity**: The CIP concept is closely derived from prior work (Park et al.) and should be more explicitly credited. Reproducing key results (e.g., a duality theorem) would help readers understand why embedding and unembedding spaces are treated as Euclidean and isomorphic. [R4]
