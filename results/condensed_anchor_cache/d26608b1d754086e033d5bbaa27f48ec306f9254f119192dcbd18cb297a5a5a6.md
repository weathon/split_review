## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Early Fusion VLA (EF-VLA), which uses frozen CLIP with early fusion of vision-language tokens before feeding them to a transformer policy, aiming to leverage CLIP’s alignment without fine-tuning. The method is evaluated on LIBERO simulation and real-world pick-and-place tasks, showing improvements over baselines.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and novel insight** (Reviewer 1, Score 6, Confidence 4): Argues against fine-tuning the vision encoder and proposes decoupling vision-language alignment from manipulation control.  
- **Simple method, effective on the tested tasks** (Reviewer 1).  
- **Well-structured and easy to follow** (Reviewer 2, Score 3, Conf. 4).  
- **Thorough experimental setup** (Reviewer 2): Includes real-world robotic experiments alongside LIBERO evaluations, strengthening validity.  
- **Meaningful research direction** (Reviewer 3, Score 1, Conf. 5): Studying early vs. late fusion is relevant for robot learning; real-world experiments are appreciated.

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading “early fusion” claim** (Reviewer 2): Fusion occurs at the final layer of the vision encoder, not at intermediate layers. The paper does not explore fusion at shallower layers, limiting the claim’s generality.  
- **Incorrect characterization of prior VLA models as “late-fusion”** (Reviewer 3): LLaVA and OpenVLA use large LLM backbones (8B–110B parameters) to fuse modalities; the visual tokens go through many more parameters than the encoder, so calling them “late-fusion” is inaccurate.  
- **Presentation is unclear** (Reviewer 3): Multiple undefined variables (e.g., \(X_{res}, X_{attn}\) in Eq. 2, L176); equations are poorly explained and appear directly taken from ClearCLIP without proper exposition; Figure 3’s cosine similarity is not described in the main text.  
- **Confusing Figure 3** (Reviewer 1): The analogy to attention using CLIP score is unclear, especially whether query/key/value match the attention equations on the same page.  
- **Lack of justification for key design choices** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):  
  - No exploration of simpler fusion methods (e.g., concatenation, linear layer) or rationale for hyperparameters in cross-attention pooling (Reviewer 1).  
  - Asymmetric use of learnable text embedding \(f'_l\) but no learnable visual embedding \(f'_v\) (Reviewer 2) – is this principled or an oversight?  
  - In LF-VLA, why use attention pooling instead of directly using CLS tokens from each encoder? (Reviewer 2) – a common practice that needs justification.  
  - Use of final LayerNorm for text features but post-attention LN for visual features – why? (Reviewer 3).  
  - Additional L2 normalization described at L207 – is it the same operation as the earlier LN? (Reviewer 3).  
- **Contradiction between “dense visual features” motivation and compression to a single token** (Reviewer 3): The aligned vision-language tokens \(f_{vl}\) are compressed into one token via cross-attention pooling. Why perform per-patch alignment only to pool it away? Can the pooling be done directly? How does a single token provide dense features, especially given only light-weight learnable parameters?  
- **Cross-attention pooling is not described** (Reviewer 3).  
- **Real-world evaluation limited to pick-and-place tasks** (Reviewer 3): This narrow scope may not fairly compare with other VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, OCTO) that are designed for more varied tasks.  
- **Significant architectural differences between EF-VLA and baselines** (Reviewer 3): EF-VLA’s policy head is much smaller (4 attention blocks, 512-dim) than OpenVLA/OCTO; the comparison involves fine-tuned versions of those larger models. Additionally, EF-VLA takes multi-step inputs (and outputs) while baselines do not – the performance gain could stem from this input format, not from early fusion. A fair study would modify the baselines to incorporate early fusion and retrain them.  
- **Highly dependent on CLIP** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3): The method is tailored to ClearCLIP and may not generalize to other VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, BEiT, CoCa). The paper provides no discussion of adaptation or performance with other vision encoders.  
- **Technical contribution is minor** (Reviewer 3): The method heavily relies on ClearCLIP, contributing mainly a pooling scheme.  
- **Baselines not evaluated in simulation** (Reviewer 3): Only one simulation environment (LIBERO) is used; no simulated comparisons to OCTO or OpenVLA.  
- **Missing ablations on input-output timesteps** (Reviewer 3): Because EF-VLA uses multiple historical observations and predicts multiple actions, this design choice is not ablated, making it impossible to attribute improvements solely to early fusion.
