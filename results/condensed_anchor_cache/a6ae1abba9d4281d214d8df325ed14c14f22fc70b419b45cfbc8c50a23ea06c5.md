## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a storage-efficient 3D Gaussian splatting representation based on a hierarchical tree structure. Only a subset of splats (parents) are stored; child splats and their attributes (position, color, scale) are predicted on-the-fly by tiny MLPs. Adaptive Tree Manipulation (ATM) optimizes the tree via growth and pruning. The method achieves a 20× reduction in disk footprint versus original 3DGS while improving PSNR and maintaining comparable SSIM/LPIPS, and a 2–5× reduction versus recent compact methods. The approach is demonstrated on mobile devices and AR glasses. Two reviewers (scores 8) find the idea novel, effective, and clearly presented; one reviewer (score 5) finds limited technical novelty and insufficient comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Predicting child splats and attributes from parent splats using a small neural network, storing only parent splats, is new and merits publication (Reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer (Reviewer 1) contests this novelty (see Weaknesses).
- **Storage reduction with quality retention**: 20× disk reduction vs. 3DGS, 2–5× vs. other compact methods, while improving PSNR (e.g., on Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks & Temples) and keeping comparable SSIM/LPIPS (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Effective optimization**: ATM and warm-up training produce stable optimization and high quality (Reviewer 1).
- **Extensive ablation studies** demonstrate the necessity of each component (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Mobile and AR deployment**: Shown to run effectively on mobile devices and AR glasses (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Clear exposition**: Good introduction, survey of existing work, and detailed method description (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty (disagreement)**: The hierarchical tree structure closely follows anchor-based representations (Scaffold-GS); adaptive child manipulation was introduced in HAC (with more efficient learnable masks); hash-grid usage appears in HAC and CompactGS. While two reviewers deem the idea novel, one reviewer sees it as an incremental combination (Reviewer 1).
- **Slower rendering speed**: Unlike 3DGS, the method requires both parent and child splats during rendering (L458), so the “lightweight” claim applies only to storage, not compute. This could mislead about overall efficiency (Reviewer 1).
- **Incomplete comparison to compact baselines**: Missing several recent methods that address 3DGS compression: HAC (ECCV 2024), Compressed3D (CVPR 2024), Reduced3DGS (I3D 2024), SOG (ECCV 2024), Compact3D (ECCV 2024). Additionally, CompactGS results in Table 1 may not include post‑processing (quantization of the hash grid) that this paper applies, making the comparison unfair (Reviewer 1).
- **No code release** for independent verification of the procedure (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient method illustration**: Only one figure (Fig. 2) explains the pipeline, which makes it hard to follow the process (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing training time** – expected to be higher than 3DGS (Reviewer 1 question).
- **Missing number of Gaussians** for 3DGS in quantitative results, needed to support the claim that fewer Gaussians are used (Reviewer 1 question).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Typo “patent” instead of “parent” in Fig. 2 (Reviewer 2); inconsistent sub‑section title format (e.g., ending with “:”, “.”, or space) (Reviewer 3).
