## Merged Review

### Summary
MeshLRM is a two-stage feed-forward large reconstruction model for high-quality mesh generation from sparse (four) posed images. The first stage trains a NeRF-based LRM with progressive low-to-high resolution scaling. The second stage fine-tunes the model using differentiable marching cubes (DiffMC) and a novel ray opacity loss, replacing volume rendering with mesh rendering. The encoder is simplified by removing DINO features, improving speed. All reviewers acknowledge the resulting meshes are of high quality and the approach is practically effective, but they raise concerns about novelty, breadth of comparisons, evaluation on real-world data, and insufficient justification of key design choices. Reviewer 1 (score 8) found the contributions impactful and well-supported, while Reviewer 4 (score 5) was more critical of the framing and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- Integrating DiffMC into the LRM framework is a straightforward yet impactful contribution that enables end-to-end mesh reconstruction and improves surface quality (R1, R4).
- The ray opacity loss effectively eliminates floaters, stabilizes DiffMC training, and is technically sound (R1, R3, R4). R3 considers it novel.
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with extensive ablations that support design choices (R1, R2, R3).
- The insight that MLP over-parameterization slows rendering is valuable and applicable to other LRM approaches (R1).
- Reconstructed meshes exhibit high quality, smooth geometry, and are noticeably better than the NeRF-based baseline (R2, R4).
- The overall engineering design (progressive resolution scaling, simplified encoder) is sound and effective (R2). Convergence speed is improved (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and overclaiming**: The approach largely combines existing components (LRM + diffMC) with limited new insight (R2, R3). The title is misleading because the network outputs a density field, not a mesh directly (R2). The claim of “end-to-end optimization” is imprecise given the two-stage training (R4).
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: Only Instant3D-LRM and LGM are compared. Missing comparisons with more recent open-source methods: InstantMesh (which also uses “LRM+differentiable mesh extraction”), GeoLRM, MeshFormer, and TripoSR (R1, R3). This limits demonstrating state-of-the-art performance (R3).
- **Evaluation on real-world data lacking**: Experiments are limited to synthetic datasets; generalization to real-world images with complex lighting, textures, and materials is unexplored (R3). No evaluation on datasets like OmniObject3D (R3).
- **Qualitative degradation and over-smoothing**: While quantitative metrics improve, qualitative results show reduced high-frequency details and over-smoothing (e.g., frog legs merging with the stand in Fig. 5) compared to the baseline (R1). NeRF-based LRM achieves better PSNR than the mesh-based model (R4).
- **Ray opacity loss not compared to simpler alternatives**: No comparison to direct density regularization (penalizing density in empty space) or background mask losses (R1). The novelty of the specific formulation is unclear (R1).
- **Choice of DiffMC not justified**: No rationale for DiffMC over DMTet, FlexiCubes, or other differentiable isosurface methods (R1, R4). Questions: Why are density fields considered more compatible with Marching Cubes than SDFs? (R4). InstantMesh uses FlexiCubes with no floater issues – what are the advantages of DiffMC? (R4).
- **Impact of density threshold not explored**: The choice of isovalue for DiffMC can significantly affect mesh quality, but no ablation is provided (R1, R2).
- **Necessity of the NeRF pretraining stage (stage 1) not fully justified**: If the ray opacity loss stabilizes mesh training, why is stage 1 essential? Can the model be trained directly on mesh representation from scratch? (R3, R4). No illustration or ablation comparing with/without NeRF pretraining (R4).
- **Missing analyses and ablations**: 
  - Which parts of the network require fine-tuning in the second stage? Only the tiny MLPs or deeper layers? (R2)
  - No quantitative comparison between NeRF-based LRM and mesh-based LRM rendering quality and geometry accuracy (R4).
  - No quantitative comparison demonstrating the improvement from progressive resolution training (R4).
  - The removal of DINO features is claimed without a proper ablation comparing with/without DINO in MeshLRM (R4).
  - Computational cost of differentiable marching cubes vs. alternatives not analyzed (R2).
- **Clarity and framing issues**: Definition of “post-optimization” (Line 56) is unclear because SDF-based methods can extract meshes directly without post-optimization (R4). The paper should explain why converting NeRF to mesh leads to a drop in quality (Line 66) and clearly distinguish between standard Marching Cubes (post-hoc) and DiffMC (R4). Grammatical issues noted (e.g., missing comma) (R4).
- **Reproducibility**: No mention of future code or model release, which hampers community progress (R3).
- **Outdated context**: The paper appears not to have been updated since its arXiv publication six months prior, failing to cite or compare with relevant recent work (R1).
