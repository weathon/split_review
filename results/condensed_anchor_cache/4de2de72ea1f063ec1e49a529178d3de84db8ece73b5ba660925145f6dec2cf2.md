## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes multi-task fine-tuning for domain-specific LLMs in finance. Through extensive experiments (over 200 training runs) on a small model (Phi-3-Mini), the authors find that multi-task fine-tuning on a cocktail of related financial and auxiliary tasks can significantly boost performance on a target task, even enabling Phi-3-Mini to outperform GPT-4o on financial benchmarks. The study also explores using general instruction data (Orca) as regularization and mathematical data to improve numerical reasoning. The paper claims that fine-tuning improves task-specific performance but does not necessarily enhance broader domain knowledge or complex reasoning.

### Strengths
- If code and all models are released, the work could be a valuable resource for research on data selection and model merging.
- The paper is well-written with a clear structure and logical flow.
- The evaluation metrics for different task types are reasonable and appropriate (though could be more comprehensive).
- The visualizations are informative and insightful.
- The paper presents a thorough analysis of multi-task fine-tuning, including an extensive experimental setup involving over 200 models.
- Strong empirical evidence is provided for the benefits of multi-task fine-tuning, with significant performance gains on financial benchmarks.
- Exploration of general instruction-following (Orca) and mathematical data adds valuable insights into regularization and numerical reasoning abilities.
- The incremental fine-tuning approach allows isolating the impact of individual datasets and exploring interactions between datasets.

### Weaknesses
*A minority reviewer (score 3) expressed stronger reservations, particularly about the limited task/dataset scope and lack of statistical rigor. Their concerns are integrated below.*

- **Unfair best-vs-single baseline comparison**: The paper selects the best-of-200 multi-task models evaluated on the test set and compares it to a single single-task fine-tuned model without a comparable hyperparameter search. A comparable budget (varying epochs, learning rate, data shuffling, early stopping) should be given to single-task fine-tuning to obtain a fair best-vs-best comparison.
- **Lack of task selection guidance**: There is little insight into how to choose tasks for fine-tuning beyond noting that general instruction tuning with Orca datasets helps. Prior works on principled data selection (Xie et al. 2023; Xia et al. 2024) are not discussed.
- **Missing details on the 200+ models**: The paper repeatedly claims “over 200 models” but does not include the full implemented model list in the main body, nor does it explain the model selection process. The analysis focuses almost exclusively on Phi-3-Mini without justifying this choice.
- **Limited dataset variety and task scope**: The training datasets and evaluated financial tasks are few and less diverse than in prior work (e.g., Open-finllms, Xie et al. 2024). This raises concerns about the novelty and comprehensiveness of the approach.
- **Lack of repeated experiments and error bars**: Model comparisons rely solely on point estimates of accuracy. No repeated experiments, error bars, or significance levels are reported. Some results (e.g., Table 2 comparing fine-tuned Phi-3-Mini with other models) are closely matched, making statistical rigor essential for robustness.
- **Potential format-adherence bias in GPT-4o comparison**: When comparing to GPT-4o, it is unclear whether gains are due to improved performance or better format following, since rule-based evaluation may penalize GPT-4o for correct answers in the wrong format. Using an LLM judge (as done for FinanceBench) could help but was not applied consistently across all comparisons.
- **Lack of theoretical insights**: The paper does not provide theoretical justification for why multi-task fine-tuning is effective.
- **Narrow scope of compared fine-tuning strategies**: The method is only compared against single-task fine-tuning. Other strategies such as adapter-based fine-tuning or prompt-tuning are not considered. The approach is similar to multi-task deep neural networks (MT-DNN, Liu et al. 2019 ACL) but this connection is not discussed or compared.
