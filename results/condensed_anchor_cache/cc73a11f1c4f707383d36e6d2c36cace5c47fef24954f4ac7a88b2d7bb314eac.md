## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DPS-CM, an enhancement of Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) for inverse problems. The authors argue that injecting clean measurements (y) early introduces high-frequency errors. DPS-CM instead uses a “crafted measurement” y_t, generated by a separate reverse diffusion process starting from y, to form the posterior gradient. The update rule becomes a convex combination of gradients toward y and toward a smoothed estimate ŷ₀. The method is evaluated on two datasets for Gaussian deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting, nonlinear deblurring, and tasks with Poisson noise, showing improvements over DPS and several baselines.

### Strengths
- The motivation (avoiding premature high-frequency injection) is intuitively appealing and clearly explained.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The algorithm is easy to implement, requiring only minor changes to the original DPS method.
- Numerical validation is extensive, covering multiple inverse problems, and shows consistent improvements over DPS.
- A comprehensive study of posterior sampling errors is provided.
- The core idea of combining a DDPM reverse trajectory with corrupted measurements as a condition is novel.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification for posterior sampling.** The paper states it performs “posterior sampling from p(x_t | y_t)”, but does not explain why this yields a valid sample from p(x|y). Under a joint diffusion targeting p(x,y), the procedure samples the joint distribution, not the posterior conditional on a fixed observation y. This is a critical, unaddressed issue.
- **Invalid use of the pre‑trained score for crafted measurements.** The crafted y_t are diffused using the same score network s_θ trained on clean images x_0. For many inverse problems (e.g., images with a missing half, CT/MR imaging, phase retrieval), the measurement distribution differs substantially from the training distribution, so the pre‑trained score is inappropriate. Claiming x and y lie on “close manifolds” requires extensive justification that is absent.
- **Inconsistency in the derivation.** The paper approximates ∇log p(y_t|x_t) using a DPS‑style estimate of E[y₀|y_t]. If one could sample exactly from p(y₀|y_t), the correct Monte‑Carlo estimate would average over many y₀ samples, which would retain high‑frequency information – contrary to the paper’s central motivation. The DPS approximation used instead suppresses high frequencies, but this discrepancy is not discussed.
- **Unfair hyperparameter tuning.** In Appendix A, Table 5, DPS‑CM hyperparameters are carefully fine‑tuned, while baseline methods use their original settings. This may overstate the performance gains of DPS‑CM.
- **Implementation concerns for crafted measurements.** Sampling crafted measurements from the forward diffusion (p(y_{1:T}|y_0)) is theoretically simple, but the paper uses a reverse DDPM process. The authors could have used forward sampling or the exact backward expression p(y_t|y_0,y_{t+1}) directly. The connection between Algorithm 1 (lines 3‑6) and the estimation of E[y₀|y_t] is not clearly explained or theoretically derived.
- **Instability due to random crafted measurements.** The method relies on a single sample from the reverse DDPM process; different draws can lead to different reconstructions (reviewer request: show results for multiple crafted measurement samples).
- **Incremental and engineering‑oriented contribution.** The overall approach is a simple convex combination of two likelihood terms, with added hyperparameters that are difficult to tune. The paper lacks deeper theoretical connections, making the contribution appear incremental.
- **Poor literature review.** Relevant work that uses noised observations y_{1:T} inside the backward process is omitted or insufficiently discussed, e.g., RePaint (Lugmayr et al.), conditional diffusion for protein motifs (Trippe et al.), and the filtering perspective of Dou & Song (2024). Similarities and differences are not examined.
- **Potential mathematical inconsistency.** For the trivial case A=I, η=0, x₀=0 constant, y=0. The right‑hand side of the equation in lines 306‑307 is identically zero, while the left‑hand side ∇log p(y_t|x_t) can be arbitrarily large (example with n_x, n_y Gaussian). This undermines the claimed equivalence.
- **Unclear notation and unexplained expectations.** It is not clear whether y_t (noisy measurement) and y_t (crafted measurement) are defined in the same way. Why is E[y₀|y_t]=y in one line but E[y₀|y_t]=ŷ₀ elsewhere?
- **No ablation on the trade‑off parameter μ_t.** The paper does not investigate whether simply tuning μ_t in the original DPS formulation could achieve similar performance to DPS‑CM.
- **Missing details on metric for hyperparameter selection.** The evaluation metric used to choose optimal hyperparameters is not stated.
