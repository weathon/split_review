## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a non-parametric kernel relative test for detecting machine-generated text (MGT). It tests whether the distribution of a test text is statistically significantly closer to human-written text (HWT) than to MGT, thereby relaxing the two-sample test assumption that HWTs must follow the seen HWT distribution. A kernel optimization algorithm is developed to select the best kernel for MGT detection. Experiments are conducted to show reduced false positive error compared to two-sample tests and improved detection over SOTA non-parametric and parametric detectors.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and the motivation is reasonable. (R2, R3)
- The method is simple, effective, logically coherent, and clearly described. (R3)
- The paper has a solid theoretical foundation and the method is highly interpretable. (R3)
- The proposed relative test can reduce the false positive rate observed in existing two-sample test detectors. (R1)
- The kernel optimization algorithm in the relative test is novel and improves both effectiveness and efficiency of detection. (R1)
- Writing is clear and easy to follow. (R2)
- Experimental results indicate good performance. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparisons and limited baselines.** The experiments only compare with Bino, which is a zero-shot method; this is insufficient and may be unfair because the proposed method is not zero-shot. (R1, R2, R3) The paper needs comparisons with:
  - Other classification-based detectors (e.g., FEW-SHOT DETECTION OF MACHINE-GENERATED TEXT USING STYLE REPRESENTATIONS [ICLR’24], Threads of Subtlety: Detecting Machine-Generated Texts Through Discourse Motifs [ACL’24]) (R2).
  - Logits-based detectors (e.g., DetectGPT [ICML’23], Fast-DetectGPT [ICLR’24], DNA-GPT [ICLR’24], DALD [NeurIPS’24]) (R2).
  - Other training/classifier-based approaches, as the kernel optimization implicitly involves training (R2).
  - General text anomaly detection methods, since the approach resembles a universal anomaly detection solution (R3).
- **Assumptions are too strong and unrealistic.** (R3) The method assumes non-overlapping distributions between MGT and HWT, and a subspace assumption that is overly idealized and lacks empirical or theoretical justification. These assumptions are not aligned with real-world text generation where overlap is significant. The paper does not provide a clear justification for these assumptions. (R3)
- **Lack of specificity to machine-generated text detection.** The proposed method treats MGT detection as a generic text anomaly detection problem, without addressing unique challenges such as subtle stylistic differences, adversarial generation, or domain-specific patterns. (R3)
- **Heavy dependence on the feature extractor.** The method fine-tunes a RoBERTa model on GPT-2-generated text as a feature generator (line 200). The paper does not explain why this choice is made, nor does it compare alternative feature extractors (e.g., other LLMs or embeddings). (R1, R2) The advantage over training-based approaches needs clarification. (R2)
- **Insufficient evaluation scope.** 
  - Datasets used are too simple; the paper should include more challenging and diverse datasets such as RAID [ACL’24] which covers 22 LLMs with different settings. (R2)
  - Robustness under adversarial attacks is not validated. (R2)
  - Non-English datasets are missing; results on non-English settings are needed. (R2)
- **Limited novelty relative to existing work.** Similar studies exist, notably "Detecting machine-generated texts by multi-population aware optimization for maximum mean discrepancy" (ICLR’24), both in methodology style and experimental design. (R1, R2)
- **Inadequate literature review.** The paper fails to reference recent comprehensive surveys such as "A Survey of AI-generated Text Forensic Systems: Detection, Attribution, and Characterization", "A Survey on Detection of LLMs-Generated Content" (EMNLP’24), and "On the Possibilities of AI-Generated Text Detection". (R2)
- **Unclear assumptions from empirical results (line 88, line 280-).** More intuitive and theoretical clarification is needed for those assumptions. (R1)
