## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FLAN (FLow Attention for NAS), a hybrid neural predictor combining DGF and GAT, with a unified encoding scheme that merges structural, learned, and score-based (e.g., ZCP) encodings to enable transfer learning across cell-based NAS search spaces. The method is evaluated on multiple benchmarks encompassing 1.5 million architectures. The authors also release the dataset of encodings and the code.

### Strengths
- The motivation and design of a unified encoding across NAS spaces is important and relevant for transfer learning (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- FLAN achieves superior sample efficiency and Kendall tau correlation in performance prediction, and improves sample-based NAS (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Large‑scale study over 13 NAS search spaces (NB101, NB201, NB301, NDS, TransNASBench‑101) using 1.5M architectures across different tasks/datasets (Reviewer 1).
- Detailed ablation on FLAN’s design components (e.g., DGF vs. GCN, GAT, skip connections) (Reviewer 3).
- The method permits easy integration of new encodings, such as future zero‑cost proxies (Reviewer 2).
- Transfer experiments and search experiments are performed, demonstrating some sample‑efficiency gains on cell‑based spaces (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The introduction and related work are well‑written and easy to follow; Figures 1–3 are nicely done (Reviewers 1, 2, 3 early).
- Code and the collected encoding dataset are released, facilitating future research (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited transfer scope**: Transferability is only shown on CIFAR‑10; moving to a new dataset would require retraining from scratch. No ImageNet or other tasks are evaluated (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3 notes NAS results are only on cell‑based CIFAR‑10).
- **NAS search experiments are confined to NB101**; extending to NB201 (Reviewer 2) or other spaces (Reviewer 1’s question on Table 5) is missing.
- **Pre‑training cost is not included**, and Table 4 shows each target has its own distinct source space, so the claimed “transfer” benefit may not reflect a single pre‑trained model (Reviewer 2).
- **Unified encoding limitations**: The “unique numerical indices” restrict FLAN to a small number of predetermined search spaces; the encoding is not guaranteed to be unique because score‑based attributes (ZCP, latency, FLOPs) can admit different architectures with similar values (Reviewer 3). Moreover, the claim that global graph search is intractable is considered weak (Reviewer 3).
- **Unconvincing GNN claim**: The argument that DGF solves oversmoothing is not well supported; GAT outperforming GCN is expected from prior work, and DGF’s gains may simply come from additional skip connections (Reviewer 3). This makes the paper’s focus unclear (GNN problems vs. NAS predictors).
- **Experimental inferences unsupported**:
  - t‑SNE plots (Fig. 4) are over‑interpreted; the claim that CATE scatters by computational complexity is not clearly visible, and the plot is cluttered with small text and unlabeled red dots (Reviewer 3).
  - The statement that “score‑based encodings have diminishing returns with more samples” is contradicted by Table 2, where the ZCP row monotonically improves and FLAN_ZCP loses to TAGATES at low samples (Reviewer 3).
  - In Tables 3/4, FLAN does not dominate across all transfer tasks, and no reasoning is provided for which predictor configuration (Arch2vec, CATE, ZCP, CAZ) to choose when labeled target data are scarce (Reviewer 3). This reduces practical usefulness.
- **Missing experimental details**:
  - Number of trials for FLAN vs. TA‑GATES in Table 2 is unclear (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Table 4 does not state whether the entire source space is used for pre‑training (Reviewer 2).
  - Source spaces for Figs. 5/6 and Table 15 are not specified (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 15 numbers may not match Tables 3/4 (e.g., FLAN_ZCP on NB101 at 128 samples) – inconsistency (Reviewer 2).
  - Zero‑Cost NAS (W) in Table 5 is not described or cited (Reviewer 2).
  - DARTS_{LRWD} in Table 15 is not described (Reviewer 2).
  - The line above Table 4 claiming “64% improvement” from ZCP is not clearly derived from the table (Reviewer 2).
  - Typo: “FLAN^T_{CAZ}” should be “FLAN_{CAZ}” (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues**: Writing becomes subpar after Section 4.1; typos exist (e.g., “DF+GAT x NB201”) (Reviewer 3). Figures 5/6 are dense and hard to read; colors and marker shapes are insufficient (Reviewer 3).
- **Questions that point to missing rationale**: Why separate DGF‑GAT modules for normal and reduce cells instead of using a single graph? (Reviewer 3). Why does Table 2 list “7290 samples” for NB101 if all architectures are used? (Reviewer 3). Would the choice of unique search‑space index affect transfer predictions? (Reviewer 1, also related to Reviewer 3’s point about predetermined spaces).
- **Missing comparisons**: The work would benefit from comparison with prior predictor survey papers [1][2] and with CDP, an older cell‑based transfer method (Reviewer 1 minor; Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 rated the paper highly (8), finding no major weaknesses; Reviewer 2 had moderate concerns (6); Reviewer 3 was strongly critical (3), citing unsupported claims, writing issues, and limited contributions. The negative reviewer’s assessment is that the paper does not meet the bar for acceptance.
