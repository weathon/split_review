## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a theoretical framework to analyze the feature learning dynamics of diffusion models compared to classification models. Under assumptions of a two-class label setup and two-layer neural networks, the authors show that diffusion models, due to the denoising objective, learn balanced and comprehensive representations of both signal and noise, whereas classification models focus on easy-to-learn components (dominantly signal or noise). Theoretical results (Theorem 1.1, Theorem 3.1) demonstrate that learning in diffusion models grows proportionally to sample size × SNR², while classification exhibits a “jump” when that product exceeds a threshold. Experiments on synthetic data and an edited MNIST dataset validate these findings. The work provides novel insights into why diffusion models can learn weak features, addressing a gap in existing theoretical studies that focus mainly on distribution learning and convergence.

### Strengths
* The first theoretical framework specifically designed to analyze feature learning dynamics in diffusion models, addressing a gap in existing theoretical literature that focuses primarily on distribution learning and convergence.
* Clear mathematical results (Theorem 1.1, Theorem 3.1) with rigorous proofs and visualization (Figure 1) showing key differences in feature learning speed and outcomes.
* Validation through experiments on synthetic data and MNIST strengthens the credibility and practical relevance of the framework.
* Comparison between diffusion models and classification models reveals that diffusion learns balanced signal+noise representations, whereas classification focuses on one or the other depending on SNR – a meaningful contribution to the discriminative vs. generative perspective.
* The problem is well-motivated; the essential results are clear even to non-experts.
* The theoretical findings are innovative and valuable for understanding internal mechanisms of diffusion models and neural networks.
* The results are well-organized and explained; the paper is generally pleasant to read.

### Weaknesses
* **Presentation and notation**: Notations are introduced without prior definition (e.g., `m` in line 188; `d`, `n`, `r`, `γ`, `k` in Section 1.1; confusing subscripts on `W` and `x` in Section 2). Redundant content between Sections 1.1 and 2. Figures 2 and 4 lack Y-axis labels. Figure A.1 should be moved to the main text (Section 5.1).
* **Limited generality of the theoretical model**: The analysis uses a shallow two-layer network, which may not capture dynamics of deeper architectures (ResNets, Transformers) used in practice (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). The model ties two layers for diffusion but fixes the second layer to ±1 for classification; the impact of this architectural difference on theoretical results is not explained (Reviewer 5).
* **Strong assumptions**: The theoretical results rely on strict assumptions: orthogonal signals, a single feature, high-dimensional spaces, constant-order α_t and β_t (Condition 3.1), and particular network initialization. These are unlikely to hold in real-world data, limiting practical applicability (Reviewers 1, 3, 5, 6). The analysis also assumes noise is unstructured and uniform with a global SNR, neglecting spatially varying SNR and correlated noise patterns (Reviewer 6).
* **Missing comparison with other generative models**: It is unclear whether the balanced learning regime is unique to diffusion models or a general property of generative models; comparisons with GANs, VAEs, and flow-based models (including flow-matching) are absent, which limits the scope of conclusions (Reviewers 2, 5).
* **Limited experimental scope and depth**: Experiments use only single-label, low-dimensional feature spaces and only a few discrete SNR values (0.75 and 6.75) without finer granularity. Higher-dimensional features, multiple labels, more complex datasets, and additional SNR settings are needed to demonstrate generalizability (Reviewers 2, 6). The synthetic data may not represent real-world non-linear relationships and higher dimensionality (Reviewer 6). One reviewer (4) found the result unsurprising, as training on Gaussian-noised samples naturally yields broader features than training on clean samples – a minority negative opinion on novelty.
* **Insufficient discussion of practical implications and downstream applications**: The paper focuses on theoretical analysis without exploring how balanced features translate to improvements in real-world tasks (e.g., adversarial robustness, generalization in low-data regimes, or applications in image generation, classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, reinforcement learning) (Reviewers 3, 5).
* **Unresolved questions about architecture and loss variations**: No analysis of whether conclusions extend to deeper networks, different noise schedules (e.g., if constant-order α_t/β_t limits generality), alternative loss functions (score matching, reconstruction loss), or accelerated training methods that might affect feature learning dynamics (Reviewers 3, 5).
* **Figure 6 caption inconsistency**: The caption describes a low-SNR case but the setting is described as high SNR, causing confusion (Reviewer 6).
* **Missing rationale for experimental choices**: The motivation behind selecting specific SNR values (0.75 and 6.75) and why the observed ratio of learned signal to noise does not change dramatically across these values is not explained (Reviewer 6).
