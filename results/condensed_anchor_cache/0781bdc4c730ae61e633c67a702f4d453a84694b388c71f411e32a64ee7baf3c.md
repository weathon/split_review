## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents an \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-differentially private algorithm for recovering clusters in well-clustered graphs with \(k\) nearly-balanced clusters. The approach extends a prior result by Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2023) that handled only \(k=2\). The algorithm uses an SDP relaxation to obtain a low-dimensional embedding, applies Gaussian noise to the Gram matrix of the embedding, then runs \(k\)-means. The authors prove a misclassification ratio close to the best-known non-private bound and show that pure \(\epsilon\)-DP would incur substantially larger error. Experiments on synthetic datasets compare their algorithm against a randomized-response baseline. Reviewers’ scores are 6,6,6,8, with one reviewer (R4) notably more positive.

### Strengths
- **Extension from \(k=2\) to general \(k\)**: The algorithm works for any fixed number of nearly-balanced clusters, while the previous reference only handled two clusters.
- **Near-optimal utility**: The misclassification bound almost matches the state-of-the-art non-private algorithm; the privacy cost appears only as a poly(\(\phi_\mathrm{in}/\phi_\mathrm{out}\)) multiplier, which is argued to be natural and likely necessary.
- **Simple, general approach**: The core recipe (SDP + Gaussian noise + \(k\)-means) is conceptually clean, and the theoretical analysis is nontrivial.
- **Rigorous privacy proof** (R4): Checked and found correct; the overall presentation is well-organized, clear, and easy to follow.
- **Promising experiments**: Empirical results on synthetic datasets show the proposed algorithm outperforms a randomized-response baseline (RR+SDP) in terms of utility.
- **Practical relevance** (R4): The setting (SBM-like graphs) is popular and appears in many real networks; running time on small-scale data is acceptable.

**Disagreement on novelty**: Most reviewers view the technical contribution as solid but not groundbreaking (combining existing tools). R4 is more enthusiastic, calling the paper a “natural follow-up of a well-established result” and praising its thoroughness.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear relation to stochastic block model**: The paper does not clarify whether the “well-clustered” definition is a generalization, a special case, or orthogonal to SBM; this comparison is important for context.
- **Assumption stability under DP adjacency** (R1): Two graphs differing by one edge may not both satisfy the well-clustered property (one may be well-clustered, the other not). The paper does not discuss this potential violation or its impact on privacy/utility.
- **Lack of intuitive explanation of the SDP**: The role of the regularizer, the trace constraint (tr\((X)=n\)), and why this particular formulation is needed are not explained. Motivational text is missing.
- **Design choice for DP noise**: Noise is added to the Gram matrix \(X\) rather than directly to vertex embeddings; the trade-offs (geometric distortion, clustering accuracy) are not discussed.
- **Potential error in Lemma 4.4** (R2): The upper bound on \(\sum_{i=1}^k|E(C_i,V-C_i)|\) is claimed to be \(\phi_\text{out}\cdot m/2\) but should be \(\phi_\text{out}\cdot 2m\); also the derivation of the second smallest eigenvalue via the Rayleigh quotient lacks step-by-step justification.
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - Baseline (RR+SDP) is run *without* the regularizer; the rationale for this removal is not given, making the comparison unfair or hard to interpret.
  - The paper does not report the values of \(\epsilon\) and \(\delta\) used in the experiments.
  - Metrics (AMI, NMI) are introduced but not defined; it is not stated whether larger values are better.
  - The experimental scope is narrow: only a few synthetic datasets, limited parameter exploration, and no real-world graphs; generalization is unclear.
- **Missing discussion of balance parameter** (R3): Whether the balance factor \(c\) appears in the bound of Peng et al. (2015) is not clarified, which is crucial for understanding the baseline.
- **Gap between upper and lower bounds**: The upper bound uses \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-DP but the lower bound is only for pure \(\epsilon\)-DP; the paper does not justify why the relaxed notion is necessary or whether a pure-DP upper bound can be obtained (e.g., via Laplace mechanism on the \(\ell_1\)-sensitive part).
- **Computational cost of SDP**: The algorithm requires solving an SDP, which is heavy for large graphs; no discussion of approximations or combinatorial alternatives is provided.
- **Volume-based balance assumption** (R4): Defining balance by graph volume is strong because it excludes clusters with comparable sizes but different densities; the earlier CCDEIST setting used only vertex counts and was less restrictive.
- **Presentation weaknesses in Theorem 1** (R4):
  - Differential privacy is not defined before the theorem (no definition or pointer).
  - The notation \(G_1 \Delta G_2\) is never defined.
  - The utility–privacy trade-off is unclear until Lemma 4.3; a more unified statement would help.
- **Missing preliminary definitions**: The notion of \((\kappa,D_1,D_2)\)-strong convexity is introduced without definition in the preliminaries.
- **Lack of motivation and broader appeal** (R4): The introduction does not effectively motivate why well-clustered graphs are important for the ICLR audience; referencing the SBM connection or downstream applications would help.
- **Incomplete references**: Several papers are cited only by arXiv versions; authors should check and update conference publication status.
