## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DiverseFlow, a training-free inference-time method that uses determinantal point process (DPP) coupling to improve sample diversity in flow generative models under a fixed sampling budget. It is evaluated on text-to-image generation (polysemous words), large-hole inpainting, class-conditional synthesis, and a toy mixture-of-Gaussians dataset. Reviewers agree the problem is well-chosen and the methodology is sound, but raise significant concerns about visual quality, quantitative evaluation, the role of CFG, method complexity, and the strength of the empirical evidence. One reviewer (score 8) is more positive, praising the focused problem and experiments, while the other three (score 5) are more critical.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and niche problem: generating diverse samples from flows without excessive repeated sampling, using a fixed budget.
- The proposed coupling of DPPs with ODE-based flow sampling is theoretically sound and derived from quantum physics, offering a clear mathematical basis.
- The method is training-free and operates at inference time.
- Experiments span multiple domains: text-guided generation with polysemous words, large-hole inpainting, class-conditional synthesis, and a controlled toy setting.
- The paper is clearly written, with proper background and mathematics.
- The method shows measurable diversity improvements over IID sampling and Particle Guidance in quantitative plots (aesthetic score vs. diversity). The controlled synthetic experiment (Figure 9) demonstrates mode coverage.
- A thorough limitation section is provided in the appendix (noted by one reviewer).

### Weaknesses
- **Visual quality and mode mixing issues**: Generated images often show degraded quality (e.g., Fig. 3b: boxer merges with rope; Fig. 3d: white crane has unrealistic neck). For polysemous prompts, the method mixes concepts rather than selecting one mode (e.g., “a famous boxer” yields a sports boxer and a dog in the same image; mechanical crane takes shape of the animal; deer buck coin). Improvements in diversity are subtle in several cases (Fig. 4e, Figs. 3,4,5,8).
- **Insufficient quantitative evaluation**: The paper relies heavily on qualitative image grids; only a few quantitative measures (aesthetic score, diversity, FID) are tucked in the appendix. Missing standard metrics like precision & recall (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019). The diversity metric used is not fully justified.
- **CFG guidance scale and trade-off analysis**: Experiments in Figure 5 use a CFG scale of 8, which is unusually high and known to hurt diversity. The diversity without CFG is arguably largest. No ablation shows the method’s effect across varying CFG strengths; a plot of FID/aesthetic vs. diversity with different CFG scales is needed to demonstrate that DiverseFlow lies outside the existing trade-off.
- **Method complexity and scalability**: The gradient computation of eigenvalues is O(k³). For small k (2–10), independent repeated sampling might achieve similar diversity at lower cost. The coupling also requires all K samples in memory simultaneously (not discussed in the main text). The added gradient term may destabilize ODE paths, possibly explaining the need for up to 100 sampling steps.
- **Lack of comparison to simpler baselines**: Variance-based diversity (e.g., maximizing variance of features) could serve as a natural, simpler baseline and should be compared.
- **Need for broader experimental scope**: No experiments with non-polysemous prompts or sentence-level prompts (e.g., “A boxer is running on a trail” vs. “A boxer”). The method’s applicability to other generative model families (diffusion models) is not addressed. Whether the gradient constraint can be applied during training is not discussed.
- **Paper exposition issues**: Figure 2 captions have rendering issues and missing notation (e.g., green dots, notation of source/target distributions, how γ_t is constructed). Abbreviations CFM, SB-CFM, OT-CFM are used without definition (Section 6.4). CFG abbreviation is missing at line 344. The statement that Particle Guidance has “poor quality” is unclear—needs clarification that PG has higher diversity at lower aesthetic score. A direct matched-comparison is missing.
- **Selection bias and reproducibility**: The paper does not describe the selection procedure for shown examples. In Figure 8, both Particle Guidance and DiverseFlow change the original duplicate image into the same modified image, which seems unlikely for independent models.
- **Minor issues**: Figure 9b shows DiverseFlow exactly at the maximum diversity axis (10); more trials should be added to allow future comparisons. The sampling budget measurement unit is ambiguous. The paper’s claim that the model “maintains high quality” while improving diversity is not supported when compared to no-CFG baseline.
- **Missing limitation discussion in main text**: Memory cost and resolution implications of the ODE coupling are not mentioned in the main paper (only briefly in appendix).
