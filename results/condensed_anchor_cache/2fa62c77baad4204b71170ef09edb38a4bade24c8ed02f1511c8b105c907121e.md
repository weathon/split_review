## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes self-supervised fine-tuning of sentence embeddings using consecutive text crops with InfoNCE loss, evaluated on MTEB clustering tasks recast as kNN accuracy. All reviewers note that text crops outperform dropout-based SimCSE and that SSL embeddings approach supervised quality, but they raise serious concerns about the central claim, experimental design, and novelty. Reviewer 1 (rating 3) is more negative, emphasizing lack of originality and missing SOTA comparisons; Reviewers 2 and 3 (both rating 5) are more positive in general evaluation but strongly criticise overclaiming and test‑set leakage.

### Strengths
- **Systematic comparison of augmentation strategies** for contrastive learning, showing that text crops outperform popular dropout‑based augmentation (SimCSE) (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Demonstrates that SSL alone can produce sentence embeddings approaching supervised quality**, reducing dependence on labeled data (R1, R3).  
- **Well written and easy to follow**, with thorough analysis and sufficient substance including a detailed appendix (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaiming and misleading central claim**: The method fine‑tunes on the same dataset used for evaluation, which is a transductive / test‑set leakage setting. The statement “As SSL does not have access to class labels, this does not present overfitting issues” is misguided and should have been addressed with K‑fold cross‑validation or held‑out evaluation. The title “Is self‑supervision enough for training sentence embeddings?” is ambiguous because pre‑training (language modeling) is also self‑supervised; the paper actually studies fine‑tuning on the target dataset (R2, R3).  
- **Lack of originality and insufficient novelty**: Self‑supervised learning and data‑augmentation techniques like cropping and dropout are already well‑established; the paper does not introduce new methods or provide novel insights (R1).  
- **Missing comparisons with current state‑of‑the‑art models**, such as BAAI’s BGE models, OpenAI, or Voyage AI (R1).  
- **Evaluation is limited**, omitting many popular MTEB benchmarks (R1).  
- **Practical value unclear**: despite tuning on the test set, the approach does not outperform SBERT. The paper does not show whether applying the method to SBERT would yield SOTA, nor does it evaluate downstream improvements (e.g., in RAG pipelines) (R2).  
- **Overclaiming in adjacent claims**: “large improvements” should be “improvements on that dataset”; the claim that “the majority of the improvement was due to generic sentence‑level adaptation, while domain adaptation had a smaller effect” is too strong given that experiments stay within the same domain (scientific literature clustering) (R2).  
- **No deep analysis of why text crops outperform SimCSE**, contrary to the conclusion in Gao et al. (2021); this discrepancy is not discussed or investigated (R3).  
- **The statement “STS performance does not correlate with nearest‑neighbor quality” is not supported** – the result is not shown in the paper (R3).  
- **Experimental design choices are not justified and could bias results**:  
  - Why was MPNet chosen? No justification given; results for other models would strengthen the paper (R2).  
  - Why train with cosine similarity but evaluate with Euclidean distance? (R2).  
  - How was the learning rate selected? It could have been chosen to favor cropping over dropout (R2).  
  - Would results hold on out‑of‑domain data (e.g., CCNews instead of PubMed)? (R2).  
- **No discussion of limitations**: are there tasks where self‑supervised contrastive learning underperforms supervised learning? (R3).  
- **Incorrect citations** for sentence‑representation work (should be Kiros et al. 2015 and Hill et al. 2016, not Reimers et al.) (R2, minor).
