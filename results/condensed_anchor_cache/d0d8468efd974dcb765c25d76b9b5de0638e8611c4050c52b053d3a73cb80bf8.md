## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a unified framework for importance and adaptive sampling in SGD, using an efficient importance function based on the loss gradient of the output layer. The method keeps a memory of sample importances across epochs to guide non-uniform mini-batch selection and weighting. Experiments on classification and regression tasks (image and point-cloud datasets) show faster convergence in wall-clock time with low computational overhead. Reviewers find the approach interesting and practically useful, but raise concerns about theoretical justification, algorithmic clarity, limited experimental scope, and presentation quality.

### Strengths
1. **Well-motivated and intuitive approach**: The paper is placed well within the existing literature and the method is easy to understand (Reviewers 3, 2). The visualization in Figure 1 effectively illustrates how the algorithm weights data points (Reviewer 1).
2. **Low computational overhead**: The weight calculation is relatively cheap, making the approach attractive given the demonstrated gains in training time (Reviewer 2).
3. **Practical speedups**: The experiments report wall-clock improvements, not just epoch-based metrics, which is a strong point (Reviewer 3).
4. **Broad benchmark coverage**: Experiments span multiple datasets and tasks, including classification and regression on images and point clouds (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
5. **Important research direction**: Variance reduction for SGD is highly relevant to the ICLR community (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of theoretical guarantees**: No convergence analysis for Algorithm 1 is provided, making it unclear under what conditions the importance sampling probabilities can be quickly approximated or whether the method can get stuck in suboptimal regions (Reviewer 4). A reviewer (R4) specifically emphasizes this as a major omission.
2. **Unclear motivation for the proposed importance function**: 
   - The derivation around Eq. 5–6 is hard to parse; the Lipschitz constant is written as input-dependent, and the bound seems to assume Lipschitz networks, limiting generality (Reviewer 3).
   - The paper does not explain why this importance function is better than the one in DLIS, beyond being more implementation-friendly (Reviewer 3).
   - Reviewer 4 notes that the connection between the analytical derivation for cross-entropy and the generalization to regression tasks is not clearly explained.
3. **Insufficient algorithmic details**: 
   - The subroutines `ComputeSampleImportance` and `ComputeImportanceSampling` (Algorithm 1) are not explained, making it difficult to assess their complexity or impact (Reviewers 1, 4).
   - It is not clear how the algorithm efficiently uses the approximate probabilities to sample data points (e.g., data structure mapping) (Reviewer 4).
   - The "general framework" in Section 4 is described at a too high level; additional pseudocode is needed (Reviewer 4).
4. **Limited experimental scope**:
   - Experiments only cover MLPs and CNNs; performance on modern transformers is not shown (Reviewer 3).
   - No comparisons to popular methods like Adam, momentum variants, or other variance-reduction techniques (Reviewer 4). A minority reviewer (R4, score 3) finds the evaluation not comprehensive, while other reviewers note the coverage is typical.
   - Lack of analysis on potential drawbacks: robustness to label noise, generalization, and overfitting behavior (Reviewers 2, 3). The use of an epsilon to avoid focus collapse suggests potential instability in the sampling distribution (Reviewer 2).
   - Memory requirements (storing importances per sample) and non-contiguous memory access from non-uniform sampling could affect scalability and data loading times (Reviewer 2).
5. **Presentation and formatting issues**:
   - Math typos: subscripts in Eqs. (8)–(11) need correction; Eq. (4) explanation confusing; Eq. (6) uses both \(x\) and \(x_i\) (Reviewer 1).
   - Citation style makes it hard to distinguish main text from references (Reviewer 1).
   - The title is grammatically awkward ("Adaptive" is an adjective, unlike "Importance") (Reviewer 2).
   - Missing figure reference on page 9; last figure on page 9 is uncaptioned (Reviewers 1, 3).
   - Several minor writing issues: incomplete sentence ("Recently, We..."), \(N\) used before introduction (Reviewer 3).
6. **Missing related work discussion**: The paper should discuss more recent importance/adaptive sampling methods, e.g., those sampling proportionally to loss (arXiv:2104.13114) or dynamic mini-batch selection (arXiv:2306.10728) (Reviewer 1).
7. **Double-blind violation**: The paper references "Recently, We propose..." from an earlier work, which conflicts with anonymization (Reviewer 2).
8. **Specific unclear aspects (also posed as questions)**:
   - Confusion about Ours IS vs. Ours AS: Is IS \(w(x)=1/p(x)\) and AS \(w(x)=1/N\)? If so, the claim that AS subsumes Santiago et al. is inconsistent because AS uses non-uniform sampling without weighting (Reviewer 1).
   - Cost of the first epoch: does it run standard SGD to estimate initial importance \(q\)? (Reviewer 3)
   - What is the value and sensitivity of the momentum parameter \(\alpha\) in Eq. (7)? (Reviewer 3)
   - Why does DLIS performance degrade on the Oxford Flowers dataset? What is the sparsity? (Reviewer 3)
   - What is meant by "full gradient norm" in the last figure? (Reviewer 3)
   - What does it mean that in Eq. (3) each weight \(w(x_i)\) remains constant at \(N\)? (Reviewer 2)
   - Beyond weight calculation overhead, what are other limitations or drawbacks? (Reviewer 2)
