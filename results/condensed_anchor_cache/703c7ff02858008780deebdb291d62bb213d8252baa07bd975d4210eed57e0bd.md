## Merged Review

### Summary

This theoretical paper studies the phenomenon where overparameterized two-layer ReLU networks trained by gradient flow on noisy data from a linear teacher fail to interpolate the training data for large sample sizes and instead converge to a solution that minimizes the test loss. The main result confirms this “optimization threshold” and identifies early-phase alignment of neurons towards a few critical directions as the cause. The paper also provides concentration bounds for the extremal vectors driving alignment, with weaker assumptions than the main result, and reports numerical experiments extending the theoretical setting. Reviewers agree the problem is interesting and the theoretical insight into optimization thresholds is new, but they express significant concerns about the gap to realistic settings, the restrictiveness of the assumptions, the clarity of the notation and paper structure, the looseness of sample complexity bounds, and insufficient comparisons to prior works.

### Strengths

- **Novel theoretical result for optimization threshold:** The paper provides a non-trivial theoretical analysis showing that two-layer ReLU networks do not always interpolate but instead exhibit a simplicity bias that can improve generalization. Reviewers note that theoretical work on the optimization threshold is scarce and this result is interesting and somewhat surprising (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Well-written and detailed discussion:** The paper is well written and the main result is accompanied by a detailed discussion of its assumptions, implications, and limitations (Reviewer 1). The presentation of the main theorem with explicit assumptions is appreciated (Reviewer 4).
- **Useful concentration bounds:** The concentration bounds for extremal vectors may be useful for future work (Reviewer 1).
- **Proofs and experimental support:** Proofs are provided in the appendix and appear to follow established methods (Reviewers 1, 2). Numerical experiments, including those in Appendix A.2 showing that large gradient steps alone do not explain simplicity bias, are interesting and informative (Reviewer 1) and provide supporting evidence (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalization beyond previous works:** The results generalize over prior works (Boursier & Flammarion, Tsoy & Konstantinov) to more datasets (beyond XOR) and with unbounded sample complexity (Theorem 2) (Reviewer 2).
- **Motivation and potential impact:** The paper aims to explain a well-documented empirical phenomenon, and the simulation data agrees with the theory (Reviewer 3). The work strengthens the case for simplicity bias in neural network optimization and has potential relevance to understanding generalization in in-context learning and diffusion models, though much work remains (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Large gap to practical settings:** The theoretical setting (two-layer ReLU networks, gradient flow, small initialization, linear teacher with strong simplifying assumptions) is far removed from the motivating applications (transformers, diffusion models). Reviewers question whether the identified mechanism (early neuron alignment) actually drives the phenomenon in those models, noting that other factors (e.g., stability issues studied by Qiao et al. NeurIPS 2024, or attention layers) could be more relevant. One reviewer is not convinced that the ReLU network problem is well motivated by phenomena in transformers and diffusion models (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Restrictive assumptions not probed empirically:** The paper relies on gradient flow (not SGD with minibatches and finite learning rates), ReLU activations, and specific initialization. Reviewers note that modern architectures use different activations (GeLU), optimizers (AdamW), and initialization schemes, and the paper does not empirically test whether the results hold under these variations. The reliance on gradient flow is a significant simplification that may not reflect practical training dynamics (Reviewer 3).
- **Sample complexity bounds are loose and poorly scaled:** The sample complexity bounds have a more-than-cubed dependence on input dimensionality \(d\), which makes it hard to relate to practical tasks where dimensionality often exceeds sample size. The analysis does not provide sufficient insight into the scaling of the optimization threshold with dimensionality. The experiment in Figure 1 should be repeated for at least two more values of \(d\) (beyond 5) to see if the threshold \(n^\star\) evolves with \(d\) at a different rate in practice (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient comparison with prior works:** The paper does not adequately discuss how its results fit with previous theoretical work on two-layer ReLU networks that shows simplicity bias can also lead to overfitting [1,2]. The dataset used is very similar to that in prior work [4] (symmetric input distribution, linear target), and it is unclear what new technical challenges are addressed or how the contribution goes beyond [4] (Reviewer 4). One reviewer also notes the setting and results are similar to Boursier & Flammarion and Tsoy & Konstantinov, though with some generalization (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity, notation, and structural issues (reviewer disagreement):** While some reviewers found the paper well written and clear, others criticized its readability.
    - Notation problems: \(B(0,1)\) is undefined (presumably the zero should be bold); the norm on the LHS in L299 is undefined; the definition of \(\mathfrak{D}_n(w,\theta)\) in L153 is malformed (two left braces, one right brace); \(\theta\) contains \(w\) so \(\theta = 0\) implies \(w=0\) but they appear as separate arguments; the distinction between \(w\) as a general vector and \(w_i(t)\) as a time-dependent parameter is confusing; the set in L153 lacks explicit types; the multiplication in L143 may be element-wise but is not specified. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
    - Structure: Sections are disjointed due to interleaving of results, discussions, and proof sketches. There is no separate related work section, only scattered mentions. The flow would benefit from presenting each result in a consistent order (idea, context, statement, implications). Section 2 (extremal vectors) is hard to follow: concepts like the set \(\mathfrak{D}\) and the domination property of initialization are introduced with little explanation and only contextualized after Proposition 1. (Reviewers 2, 4)
    - Figure 1: The value of \(\sigma^2\) is hard to spot; making it a tick on the y-axis would help. The figure does not include information about the distance between neuron weights and the true OLS data model (e.g., mean cosine similarity after training), even though Theorem 2 states neurons cluster into one of two states. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Missing experiments and ablations:**
    - Optimization threshold dependency on network width \(m\) is not explored; the paper should provide an additional experiment with a second value for \(m\) (similar to the larger \(d\) in the appendix) and clarify what \(m\) was used in Figure 1. (Reviewer 3)
    - The paper should investigate the effect of different activation functions (e.g., GeLU), training algorithms (AdamW), and initialization schemes (default schemes) on the observed phenomena. (Reviewer 3)
    - Could the authors empirically or theoretically discuss if sample complexity influences when the early training phase (directional alignment) occurs? (Reviewer 2)
- **Unanswered questions requiring clarification:**
    - Does the paper consider stochastic gradient descent or only gradient descent/flow? If SGD, data samples in each minibatch may not always satisfy Assumption 1. (Reviewer 4)
    - In line 135, what distribution are \((\tilde a_j, \tilde w_j)\) drawn i.i.d. from? (Reviewer 4)
    - Definition 1 is not self-contained: it misses the object that possesses an extremal vector (should be \(G_n\)). The connection between \(G_n\) and \(\mathfrak{D}\) should be made more explicit; can the concept be visualized in a low-dimensional example? (Reviewer 3)
    - How essential is the ReLU activation for the main result? (Reviewer 1)
    - How much compute was needed for the experiments? (Reviewer 1)
    - The paper conjectures an order \(n\) effective width at the end of training in the orthogonal training data setting; how does this relate to Boursier et al. NeurIPS 2022 which found an effective width of only 2? (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor errors:**
    - In the parentheses in L325, it should be \(S_-\). (Reviewer 3)
    - References should be checked for publication status and provided with clickable links. (Reviewer 1)
