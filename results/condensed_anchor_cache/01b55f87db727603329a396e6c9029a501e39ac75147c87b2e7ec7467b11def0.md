## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes multi-step extragradient methods for unconstrained, structured non-monotone variational inequalities satisfying the weak Minty variational inequality (weak MVI) condition. The key idea is that using multiple exploration steps (forward operator evaluations) per iteration allows the condition on the problem constant \(\rho\) to be significantly relaxed: from the previously known \(\rho > -1/(2L)\) to roughly \(\rho > -(1-1/e)/L\). Both finite-step schemes (two-step, \(n\)-step) with known stepsize choices and an adaptive algorithm (Max Distance Extragradient, Algorithm 1) that dynamically determines the number of inner steps are analyzed. Convergence guarantees at an \(O(1/k)\) rate are established, generalizing the hyperplane projection approach of Pethick et al. (2022, AdaptiveEG+).

### Strengths

- The paper addresses a challenging problem and provides convergence guarantees in a unifying way, with good intuition for the generalization of AdaptiveEG+ from the perspective of weak MVI halfspaces.
- The idea is approachable and nicely introduced: it is interesting that an explicit scheme can extend the range of \(\rho\) through such a simple and elegant approach (repeated forward operator evaluations).
- The authors do a great job in the introduction motivating the problem, explaining their approach, and clearly stating their main contributions; the paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- A comprehensive analysis of stepsize requirements for intermediate steps is given, with explicit connection to the relaxation of the weak MVI parameter constraint.
- The adaptive exploration scheme (Algorithm 1) provides a principled way to find an intermediate point satisfying the required inequality without pre-specifying the number of inner steps.
- The work opens up exploration of other subroutines for the extrapolation step.

### Weaknesses

- **Knowledge of \(\rho\) required.** The convergence guarantee for all proposed methods (including Algorithm 1) depends on knowing the problem parameter \(\rho\). The paper only suggests heuristics for estimating \(\rho\), and it is uncertain whether convergence holds with a noisy estimate. This is a practical limitation.
- **Convergence rate unchanged.** The \(O(1/k)\) rate is the same as in prior work (AdaptiveEG+). While the wider range of \(\rho\) is a theoretical improvement, the numerical improvement appears slight (constant factor) and the experiments show that CurvatureEG+ (from prior work) performs similarly to the proposed MDEG.
- **Presentation of theoretical results is convoluted (Sections 3.1/3.2).** The theorems are presented with many added parameters without explanation of what they mean or how they position the results relative to the literature. For example:
  - The purpose and importance of Lemma 2.4, especially the role of \(\lambda_k\), are not adequately explained; it is unclear how Algorithm 2.9 satisfies its assumptions.
  - Theorem 3.2 is presented without a clear statement of its usefulness or what it implies.
  - The derivation of inequality (3.1) is not explained.
  - In Theorem 2.5, the assumption on \(\rho_0\) is not stated.
- **Main insight not explained in the main paper.** A clear explanation of why taking multiple extrapolation steps allows a weaker weak MVI condition is missing from the main text; the discussion is relegated to the appendix. This is a critical omission for the paper’s central contribution.
- **Experiments section lacks depth.** The figures (Figures 3-5) are given a full page without proper in-depth comparison of the results, connection to the theoretical findings, or discussion of how the proposed methods compare to baselines. A comparison with the Optimistic method (Choudhury et al., 2023) for \(\rho > -1/(2L)\) is missing.
- **Algorithm 1 has worse complexity on structured problems.** When the operator \(F\) has more structure (e.g., is cocoercive or strongly monotone, so the subroutine itself converges), the max-distance algorithm can be less efficient. It is unclear what observed oracle complexity would be on such problems compared to, e.g., EG+.
- **Several missing details and ambiguities in the presentation:**
  - Figure 1 is included without any reference in the text; its purpose and connection to the operator framework are unclear (it mentions “range of gradient” while the paper deals with general operators).
  - The notation \(S^c\) in equation (2.6) is undefined.
  - Proof of Lemma 2.3 is omitted (even if trivial, it should be included for completeness).
  - The intuitive meaning of the Max Distance Extragradient algorithm (when the projection distance stops increasing) is not explained in the main paper; the discussion from Appendix B.1 and Figure 6 should have been included.
  - The statement “the key is to calculate stepsize from projection and assure its positiveness” is not properly connected to equation (3.1); the nature of the projection is unclear.
  - The minimal \(n\) required to achieve the \(\rho_0\) in Theorem 3.4 is not stated, which would clarify oracle complexity.
  - The phrase “lower bound” is used in a potentially confusing way (e.g., after Theorem 3.4); “range of \(\rho\)” would be clearer.
  - There are unspecified variables (e.g., \(\alpha_k\), \(\gamma_k\) in Section 2.1 are not defined; “recommended parameter” requires specification).
  - Before Section 2, the inequality \(\rho > -1/L\) should be \(\rho > -1/(2L)\).
  - Assumption 2 is missing \(z \in \mathbb{R}^d\).
  - On page 4, the phrase “single forward operator evaluation” is missing the word “single”.
  - Whitespace usage is inefficient (e.g., page 6 could compress the inner loop notation).
- **Unaddressed questions about Algorithm 1:**
  - Can any formal convergence guarantee be proved for Algorithm 1?
  - The algorithm suggests using the stepsize choice of \(n\)-step EG, but since the horizon is adaptively chosen, it is unclear how the condition \(\sum_i \gamma_i < 1/L\) is guaranteed.
- **The analysis for \(n\)-step EG yields non-tight bounds.** For \(n \geq 3\), the derived bound \(\rho > -(1-1/e)/L\) is not tight, and the current analysis gives a better range with \(n=3\) than in the limit \(n \to \infty\). It is unclear whether larger \(n\) further improves the range, and what the tight characterization would be.
- **Missing references.** Lemma 2.4 is closely related to Solodov and Svaiter (1999, Lemma 2.1); this should be cited. The connection to approximation of the prox (Nemirovski, 2004) and the characterization of cohypomonotone problems (Bauschke et al., 2019) could also be acknowledged.
