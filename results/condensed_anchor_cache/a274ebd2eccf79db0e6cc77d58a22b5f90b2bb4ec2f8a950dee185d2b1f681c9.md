## Merged Review

### Summary

Celcomen is a generative graph neural network (GNN) grounded in causal inference that disentangles intra- and inter-cellular gene regulation in spatial transcriptomics (ST) and single-cell data. It can be prompted by perturbations to generate spatial counterfactuals, offering insight into experimentally inaccessible states. The method is validated on both synthetic and real data, including human glioblastoma and fetal spleen. The paper provides theoretical identifiability and uses a Mean Field Theory approximation for scalability.

### Strengths

- The paper presents the first causally identifiable model for spatial transcriptomics data, a strong theoretical contribution supported by a proof of identifiability. Reviewer 2 was particularly positive about this foundation (rating 8 vs. 6 from the other two reviewers).
- The motivation is well explained, and the results on the impact of a perturbed spot on surrounding cells are convincing, showing why cell communication must be considered in spatial omics.
- The idea of linking gene regulatory network (GRN) inference among neighboring cells with entropy maximization under Lagrange multipliers, then modeled via a GNN, is novel. Theorem 1 is inspiring.
- The implementation addresses scalability (e.g., Mean Field Theory approximation) to handle large-scale problems.
- The problem is timely and important; the method appears well-grounded and robust.
- Demonstrations on real data are promising, especially the disentanglement of intra- vs. inter-cellular programs in human glioblastoma and the observation that in silico knockouts affect signal propagation in a biologically expected manner.

### Weaknesses

- Notation is inconsistent; for example, \(s^i_{\alpha}\), \(s^\alpha_{i}\), and \(s_{\alpha i}\) are used to refer to the same concept.
- Baseline comparisons are missing. Quantitative measurements of perturbation accuracy (e.g., comparisons with ground truth or alternative methods) are lacking. Without baselines, it is unclear how accurate the predictions are.
- There is a recognized gap between synthetic and real data for causal models. The paper does not discuss the similarity/difference between the generated in-silico perturbation data and real data, which should be addressed.
- The three assumptions on which the entire model relies are not discussed in terms of biological or technological implications. They are not compared to classical causal learning assumptions (faithfulness, Markov condition, causal sufficiency). It is unclear under which conditions (e.g., specific tissues or ST technology limitations) these assumptions may not hold. Reviewer 2 emphasized this as a main weakness.
- The method currently disregards information on cell types or other functional clustering, which can be instrumental for understanding cell-to-cell communication (e.g., causal relationships may differ within vs. between cell types). This limitation should be acknowledged, its impact discussed, and possible extensions proposed.
- It is unclear why the spatial structure of cells in tissues should be captured well by a DAG. This fundamental assumption needs justification.
- The claim “we observed a strong positive correlation between these two gene-gene interaction matrices” is not supported by the results: in the supplement the maximum correlation is 0.4–0.5. This phrasing needs to be revised and the connection to identifiability clarified.
- The gene-gene interaction matrices (Fig. 5) are not evaluated against known biological interactions. Many evaluation approaches exist and are crucially missing.
- Intuition for Theorem 2 is not provided in the text.
- The related work section lacks a subsection on generative models and disentanglement for single-cell and spatial transcriptomics data.
- The discussion section is missing a thorough discussion of the approach’s limitations.
- Runtime information (clock time for the experiments) is not provided.
- The explanation of how “least number of forces” translates to maximizing entropy needs clarification (Section 3.1).
- Minor issues: some references lack parentheses; additional examples beyond VisiumHD should be given for spatial transcriptomics; connections between Fig. 1, its caption, and the text are unclear; several sentences are strangely phrased or inexact (e.g., “Suppose for instance that nature imposes the colocalization of genes..”, “Since half of the time, the nearest neighbor of a nearest neighbor is also a nearest neighbor”); the phrase ““Least” here is meant in the sense of entropy” needs explanation; “for an ant forced to walk on the surface of a table, this force happens to be electromagnetism?” is unclear; after Theorem 1, text repeats numbered equations; typos include “demonstrates that Celcomen’s usefulness as a toy model for mechanistic interpretability since” and “resolutionFarah”.
