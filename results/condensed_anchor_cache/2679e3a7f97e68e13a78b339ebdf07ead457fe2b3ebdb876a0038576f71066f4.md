## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Amortized Posterior Sampling (APS), a variational inference approach that trains a conditional flow model to minimize divergence between the variational distribution and a posterior defined by a pre-trained diffusion model. Once trained, the flow model produces posterior samples with a single NFE, amortized over measurements. Experiments cover image restoration (denoising, inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution) on 32×32 CelebA, manifold signal reconstruction, and climate data imputation, showing faster runtime than several diffusion inversion baselines. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (one at 5) and it was rejected. The higher-rated reviewer acknowledged the soundness and speed but also cited limited experiments and training-on-test-set issues.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-structured, and mathematically sound (Eq. 11).
- The conditional normalizing flow approach enables one-step posterior inference, offering substantial speed improvements over iterative diffusion inversion methods (DIS).
- APS is tested on both Euclidean and non-Euclidean data (images, manifold signals, climate data) across multiple tasks (denoising, inpainting, Gaussian deblurring, super-resolution, imputation), demonstrating the method’s versatility.
- The method is unsupervised (no paired training data required).
- Related work is addressed in proper context; the speed-accuracy trade-off is clearly shown.

### Weaknesses
- **Training on test set contradicts speed claim:** APS requires training on the test set itself (or a test-batch) to learn the conditional flow. This stands in opposition to the “fast sampler” narrative, and the cost of that training is not amortized over large numbers of unseen test inputs. *Reviewer 1* explicitly asks how test set size affects performance; *Reviewer 3* notes training is time-consuming and requires test images, while zero-shot methods (e.g. DPS) avoid this.
- **Limited novelty:** A very similar approach – “Diffusion Prior-Based Amortized Variational Inference for Noisy Inverse Problems” (ECCV 2024) – also uses unsupervised diffusion prior distillation with amortized variational inference for inverse problems. That work is neither discussed nor compared. Additionally, RED-Diff (ICLR 2023) already applies a variational objective to diffusion-based inverse problems; the primary distinction here is the amortized neural network, leaving the diffusion prior loss nearly the same. *Reviewer 3* adds that APS appears to simply combine a reconstruction step and the normalizing-flow step from Score Prior into one step.
- **Missing experimental comparisons with key baselines:** The paper compares only against MCG, DPS, and Noise2Score. Several important baselines are absent that would clarify where APS stands: DDNM (ICLR 2023), Pi-GDM, FPS, FPS-SMC, RED-Diff, Score Prior, and the ECCV 2024 amortized method. There is also no direct diversity comparison (e.g., against Score-Prior by Feng et al. or DPS/DDNM). *Reviewer 1* asks for diversity benchmarking.
- **Sample quality and diversity are low:** Samples are noisy and contain artifacts – e.g., CelebA denoising in Fig. 1 (row 1) and results in Table 2 / Fig. 6. Diversity among posterior samples appears minimal. *Reviewer 4* asks if the observed background noise is specific to the vectorized input representation or a broader limitation of APS.
- **Experiments on small / unorthodox datasets:** 32×32 CelebA is very low resolution, making it hard to judge meaningful differences. The non-Euclidean datasets are simple and constrained. *Reviewer 3* asks for higher resolution tasks; *Reviewer 1* recommends testing on 128×128 images and notes that the resolution may be an inherent limitation.
- **Loss of zero-shot flexibility:** APS requires training a new conditional flow for each measurement operator or set of tasks, losing the advantage of zero-shot DIS methods that can adapt to any operator at test time without additional training.
- **Unified metrics are missing:** Metrics vary across experiments (MSE, FID, SSIM), making it difficult to compare performance consistently. *Reviewer 3* asks for a unified framework.
- **Failure cases not analyzed:** No discussion of when or why APS fails – e.g., on highly ill-posed problems where the prior diverges from the true posterior, or on out-of-distribution problem classes. *Reviewer 4* specifically asks about generalizability to entirely unseen problem classes and ill-posed settings.

**Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 5) is more positive about the paper’s mathematical soundness and runtime gains, but nonetheless emphasizes the critical issues of test-set training and limited experiments. The other three reviewers (score 3) converge on the weaknesses listed above, particularly the lack of novelty and missing comparisons.
