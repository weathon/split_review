## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper identifies and characterizes artifacts in feature maps of supervised and self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs). These artifacts manifest as high-norm tokens during inference, predominantly in low-informative background regions, which the model repurposes for internal computation (storing global, non-local information). The problem is observed in most ViTs (DINOv2, DeiT-III, OpenCLIP) except DINOv1. The authors propose appending dedicated “register” tokens (inspired by Memory transformers) to the input sequence, which removes the artifacts, yields smoother feature maps and attention maps, and improves performance on dense prediction tasks and unsupervised object discovery for most models. The solution is simple, effective, and sets new state-of-the-art for self-supervised models on dense visual tasks.

### Strengths

- The identification and characterization of the artifact (high-norm outlier tokens) is an important and novel observation (all reviewers). The step-by-step analysis using token norms, linear classifiers, and nearest-neighbor experiments (Table 1, Figure 5) is solid, compelling, and coherent (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The discovery that DINOv1 does not exhibit this behavior is surprising and provides useful contrast (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed solution—adding register tokens—is simple, elegant, and highly effective at removing the artifacts and restoring spatial interpretability (all reviewers). The method is not entirely new (Memory transformer) but the motivation through redundancy and attention artifacts is original and interesting (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The experiments are convincing and comprehensive (Reviewer 1). The paper shows that registers bring back spatial interpretability in feature/attention maps, improve object discovery (LOST, TokenCut) for DINOv2 and DeiT-III, while image-level classification performance stays consistent (Reviewers 1, 2). The ablations on register count, placement, and initialization shed light on behavior (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is very well-written with clear narrative, insightful graphs (especially Figure 4 and Figure 5), and helpful visualizations (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The limitations statement at the end of Sec 2.2 is appreciated (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **OpenCLIP shows no improvement on object localization despite artifact removal (Table 3 vs Figure 7):** In Table 3, OpenCLIP+reg does not outperform OpenCLIP on LOST/TokenCut, contradicting the statement “for all models on all datasets, adding registers improves unsupervised object discovery.” This discrepancy needs explanation. Minor labeling error: Figure 7 should say OpenCLIP, not CLIP (Reviewer 1, also noted by Reviewer 4).
- **No experiments showing that the behaviors of outlier tokens are eliminated or transferred to registers:** The paper does not demonstrate that the properties ascribed to outlier tokens (e.g., global information prediction accuracy in Table 1, norm patterns in Figure 5) are now exhibited by register tokens (or that image tokens revert to being more local). Do registers inherit the global information role? How does CLS token norm compare? (Reviewer 4)
- **Alternative methods to reduce patch redundancy remain unexplored:** The paper does not investigate other self-supervised models like MAE (where patch-level reconstruction might already discourage this redundancy) or simpler solutions such as directly penalizing high norms. The current approach, though effective, feels like a workaround rather than a fundamental fix (Reviewers 2, 3; also raised as a question by Reviewer 2).
- **Weak experimental design for classifier proving global info (Table 1):** The authors “choose a single token at random” (either high-norm or normal) to train a logistic regression classifier for image class prediction. Random token selection introduces high variance; using all tokens or a pooled/summarised representation would be more robust. The reported accuracy differences may not be reliable (Reviewer 3).
- **Conflict: registers improve classification but not dense tasks:** Performance on ImageNet classification improves almost monotonically with more registers, but dense tasks like segmentation (ADE20K) and depth (NYU) do not benefit (Table 4). If repurposing redundant patches is harmful for local information, dense tasks (which rely on local features) should benefit more. This suggests registers capture something beyond just local patch redundancy and warrants further investigation into what registers actually learn (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also questions why LOST with DINOv2+reg still underperforms DINO; need qualitative examples to explain. DINOv2’s mask-image-modeling objective should theoretically discourage such artifacts—why doesn’t it? (Reviewer 4)
- **Additional compute cost:** Adding 4–16 register tokens increases compute by 2–6% (Reviewer 2).
- **Open question about register attention patterns:** Different register tokens end up focusing on different spatial areas of the object. Does this undermine the argument that registers are merely storing global information (as the outlier tokens did)? (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing analysis on norm/attention variance across heads for outlier tokens:** Would like to see norm-related metrics and visualizations across heads—do all heads repurpose the same high-norm tokens? (Reviewer 2)
- **Why do more registers hurt NYU depth performance?** Table 4 shows a drop for DINOv2+reg with 4 vs 16 tokens on NYU depth. Clarification needed (Reviewer 2).
- **What happens when registers are added to DINOv1 (which does not need them)?** Does the nature of what registers learn differ from DINOv2? (Reviewer 2)
- **Torralba and Efros reference (page 9):** The paper states that label bias is less of a concern with self-supervised methods, but data-source bias remains (e.g., Instagram vs iNaturalist). The statement is confusing and should be elaborated (Reviewer 4).
