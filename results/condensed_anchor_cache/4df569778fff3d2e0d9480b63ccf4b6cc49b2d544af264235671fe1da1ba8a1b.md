## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Gap Preserving Distillation (GPD) to address the challenge of large performance gaps between teacher and student models in knowledge distillation. GPD trains an additional dynamic teacher model alongside the student, using Inverse Reparameterization (IR) and Channel-Branch Reparameterization (CBR) to enable parameter sharing and maintain a reasonable gap throughout training. Experiments on ImageNet-1K classification show that GPD outperforms baselines with static teachers and also works in scenarios without a pre-trained teacher. The problem is well-motivated, and the approach is novel.

### Strengths
- The problem of effectively performing knowledge distillation with a large teacher-student performance gap is important and well-motivated (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 2 specifically notes lines 54–61).
- The proposed approach is novel: co-training a dynamic teacher to adaptively manage the gap, with IR and CBR for efficient parameter sharing (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, with clear descriptions, intuitive figures, and rigorously detailed algorithms (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The method identifies the performance gap as a key challenge and introduces a dynamic adjustment mechanism that effectively narrows it, demonstrated by significant improvements across multiple models and datasets. Extensive ablation studies validate the method’s effectiveness (Reviewer 1).
- The method shows adaptability across various training settings, including training from scratch and fine-tuning (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical explanation (Reviewer 1, confidence 3):** The paper lacks a deeper explanation of why a large performance gap harms knowledge transfer. Controlled experiments testing different gap sizes and analysis of underlying mechanisms (e.g., over-confident predictions, irrelevant features) are needed.
- **Overengineering and unclear necessity of design (Reviewer 1, confidence 3 – minority more critical):** The necessity of complex structures (IR, parameter sharing) for gap reduction is not clearly justified. Simpler alternatives (e.g., using a teacher with closer capacity) are not explored. The connection between design choices and the core problem needs more elaboration.
- **Missing comparisons with teacher-assistant methods (Reviewer 2, confidence 4):** The paper does not compare with closely related KD methods that use teacher assistants, such as Mirzadeh et al. (AAAI 2020), Son et al. (CVPR 2021), Ganta et al. (ICBK 2021), and Zhou & Ai (arXiv 2024). Without these, effectiveness is less convincing.
- **Inappropriate baselines in Section 4.2 (Reviewer 2, confidence 4):** When comparing GPD without a static teacher, baselines are networks trained without any KD techniques. Since GPD uses an additional dynamic teacher, self-distillation methods would be more appropriate baselines.
- **Efficiency and training cost analysis missing (Reviewers 2 and 3, both confidence 4):** The size of the dynamic teacher must still be tuned via branch and channel expansion ratios, reducing practicality. The computational overhead of reparameterization during training is not analyzed; no detailed training cost comparison with baselines is provided.
- **Struggles with extremely large teachers / Table 6 (Reviewers 2 and 3, both confidence 4):** In Table 6, a larger ViT-L teacher leads to worse student performance than a smaller ResNet-101 teacher even with GPD, suggesting the method may still struggle with extremely large teachers. Reviewer 2 further notes that the comparison is confounded by architectural differences (CNN vs. ViT) and recommends using same-architecture pairs for consistent evaluation.
- **Contradiction in Figure 1 (Reviewer 2, confidence 4):** The paper claims reparameterization yields exactly the same accuracy as the student, but Figure 1 shows weights modified with added noise, which appears contradictory.
- **Counterintuitive claim about faster convergence (Reviewer 2, confidence 4):** The claim (lines 68–69) that the dynamic teacher “converges faster than the student” is not supported by evidence.
- **Transformer reparameterization unclear (Reviewer 3, confidence 3):** The method description focuses on convolutional models; it should explain how reparameterization is achieved for transformer architectures.
- **Abstract too lengthy (Reviewer 3, confidence 3):** The abstract covers every aspect in detail and would benefit from conciseness.
