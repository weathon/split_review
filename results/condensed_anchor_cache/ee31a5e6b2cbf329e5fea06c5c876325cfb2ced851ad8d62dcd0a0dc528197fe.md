## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Neural Fourier Modelling (NFM), a compact method for time-series analysis that models data directly in the Fourier domain. NFM leverages two Fourier transform properties: (i) treating finite-length series as continuous-time functions and (ii) performing data manipulation (resampling, timespan extension) as frequency extrapolation/interpolation. Two key modules are proposed: Learnable Frequency Tokens (LFT) and Implicit Neural Fourier Filters (INFF). Experiments on forecasting, anomaly detection, and classification show NFM achieves strong performance with very few parameters (<40K) and handles varying sampling rates at test time. Reviewers generally find the work novel and well-motivated, but raise significant concerns about clarity, scope of baselines/comparisons, limited classification evaluation, missing discussion of related works, and insufficient explanation of architectural choices.

### Strengths
- The work is novel and the idea of shifting focus to the Fourier domain is innovative; reviewers acknowledge they are unaware of these ideas being proposed before and the paper is transparent about differences from FITS. (R1, R3, R4)
- The method effectively decouples data size and representation size, enabling resolution-invariant models with very few parameters (<40K). This is an exciting research direction. (R1, R2)
- Experimental results are comprehensive and show strong performance across forecasting, anomaly detection, and classification tasks, often outperforming SOTA with far fewer parameters. (R2, R4, R5)
- The ablation study is comprehensive, allowing assessment of component contributions. (R4)
- Appendices are well-documented and provide deeper understanding. (R2)
- The limitation section acknowledges the model is not suitable for irregular time series. (R2)
- One reviewer (R5, score 8) is notably more positive, praising the thorough experiments and clear figures. The majority of reviewers (scores 5,6) are positive but identify clear shortcomings.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and organization (R2, R1, R3):** Two reviewers (R2, R1) find the paper (excluding appendices) difficult to follow. R2 states contributions and positioning are unclear, the architecture is not well-explained, and crucial parts are relegated to appendices. R1 notes the MLP Channel Mixer, MLP Mixer, and Predictor components are insufficiently discussed, e.g., why the specific order of interleaving time and frequency domain operations? In contrast, R3 and R4 find the paper well-written and easy to follow, indicating a split in reading experience. The abstract and introduction also have several typos (R1, R3). 
- **Insufficient related work discussion (R1, R2, R3):**
    - The paper does not discuss compact models or other resolution‑invariant approaches, despite compactness being a central claimed benefit. (R1)
    - The method heavily builds on Implicit Neural Representations (INR) – input projection (SIREN), LFT, INFF – but fails to cite or discuss related INR time‑series works (e.g., iHyperTime, Deep Time‑Index Models, Time Series Continuous Modeling). (R2)
    - LFT resembles frequency‑enhanced mechanisms from prior works like FEDformer; differences and strengths should be clarified. (R3)
    - Paper heavily builds on MLP‑Mixer/TimeMixer but does not cite them (R1). N‑BEATS also learned Fourier basis coefficients and should be cited. (R1)
- **Limited classification evaluation (R1, R2, R4, R5):**
    - Only a single classification dataset (SpeechCommands) is used, which is audio data that favors frequency‑domain processing. (R1, R2, R4) 
    - Baselines for classification are not SOTA; reviewers request comparisons on the UCR/UEA benchmark suite (R4, R5) and non‑deep learning methods like MiniRocket and HIVE‑COTE (R5).
    - Handling three tasks makes the paper feel unfocused, and the SOTA claim in classification is undermined by the weak benchmark choice. (R2)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons (R1, R5):**
    - In long‑term forecasting, better models such as TimeMixer (Wang et al. 2024) are not shown. While parameter parsimony is a strength, it would be acceptable not to be best‑in‑class everywhere. (R1)
    - No comparison with Fourier Neural Operator (FNO), which has been successful on time‑series tasks; a discussion between neural operators and NFM is requested. (R5)
- **Architecture limitations and missing experiments (R2, R3, R4):**
    - The architecture cannot handle new samples (new channels) during inference nor accept covariates – these limitations are not discussed. (R2)
    - How to choose K_N and K_L? Are they fixed for all time series? (R3)
    - Can the model handle both multivariate and univariate time series? (R3)
    - The ablation study needs expansion to isolate contributions of the newly proposed blocks (LFT, INFF) versus existing Mixer components, e.g., by ablating the order of operations and the residual connections. (R1, R3 [INFF vs. Frequency Channel Attention], R4)
    - What if everything is kept in the frequency domain, i.e., removing the iDFT and performing channel mixing also with Fourier features? (R4) 
    - In Table 4, sampling rates are always SR<1 (training has higher rate than test). What about SR>1? (R4)
- **Minor concerns and corrections (R1, R3, R4):**
    - Figure 2 is difficult to understand – overlapping series in time domain; using standard frequency‑domain magnitude plots would be clearer. (R4)
    - Inconsistency: abbreviation inverse DFT is “IDFT” in text but “iDFT” in Figure 3. (R4)
    - Parameter counts in tables are misleading: NFM projects c‑channel series to d dimensions (channel‑invariant), so baseline parameter counts should be given as ranges, not fixed values that may vary across datasets. (R4)
    - The scaling case in Figure 6(a) uses ETTm1 (only 7 channels, all d>c); a more representative case like Traffic (800+ channels) would be better. (R4)
    - Several typos: missing “and” in l.047, “a” → “the” in l.152, full stop typo l.166, “switching” → “switch” l.169, “Mixer blcok” Fig.3, missing closing parenthesis l.314, etc. (R1, R3)
- **Technical questions that overlap with weaknesses (R1, R4):**
    - Why is there no residual around the MLP Channel Mixer and MLP Mixer blocks individually, only around the stack of all mixer blocks? (R1)
    - What is r in Appendix D1, l.935? (R1)
    - What does “can be learned without a‑priori” mean (l.262)? (R1)
    - Can LFT be replaced by a single learned vector V[k] when only a single sampling rate is observed? (R1)
    - How is the complex‑valued ReLU in INFF defined? C is not ordered, so max operation is unclear. (R1)
    - NFM outperforms FITS except on ETTh2; any insights about what is special about that dataset? (R4)
