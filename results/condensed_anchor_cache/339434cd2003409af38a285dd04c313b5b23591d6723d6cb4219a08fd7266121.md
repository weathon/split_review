## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SonicSim, a customizable simulation platform built on Habitat-sim for generating synthetic data with moving sound sources. It supports scene-level, microphone-level, and source-level adjustments. Using SonicSim, the authors created SonicSet, a benchmark dataset combining LibriSpeech, FSD50K, FMA, and 90 scenes from Matterport3D. They evaluate multiple speech separation and enhancement models on SonicSet and compare generalization to real-world data (5-hour real-world recording without reverb, RealMAN dataset). Models trained on SonicSet show better generalization to real-world scenarios than those trained on other synthetic datasets. The platform and dataset are publicly available.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a challenging and important problem**: Simulating moving sound sources realistically is difficult, and SonicSim provides a customizable toolkit that fills a gap in training/evaluation data for speech separation and enhancement with moving sources (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Multi-level flexibility**: The platform allows adjustments at scene, microphone, and source levels, enabling diverse scenario generation (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Comprehensive benchmarking**: A wide range of state-of-the-art speech separation and enhancement models are evaluated using multiple metrics (SI-SNR, SDR, WER, PESQ, DNSMOS, etc.) in well-organized experiments (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Bridging the synthetic-real gap**: Comparisons with real-world datasets (RealMAN and a custom real-world recording) provide evidence that SonicSet synthetic data generalizes better to real conditions than other synthetic datasets (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Reproducibility**: The authors provide open-source code and dataset, supporting reproducible research (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient detail on moving source simulation**: The paper lacks a clear mathematical or algorithmic description of the trajectory function (Section 3.2.2). It is unclear whether the speed of the moving source is a factor in the simulation. More precise specification is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of ground truth validation**: No direct comparison of simulated and real-world acoustic conditions (e.g., comparing simulated RIRs with measured RIRs from the same 3D scene). Recording data with a moving source in a real room and simulating the same scene would quantify the simulation accuracy (Reviewers 1, 4). Such validation is especially needed to support claims of improved reverberation realism (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited real-world testing**: The real-world evaluation relies heavily on the RealMAN dataset (speech enhancement) and a single 5-hour real-world recording (speech separation). Additional testing on diverse in-the-wild examples (actual conversations, not playback) and a perceptual listening study would strengthen claims of real-world effectiveness (Reviewers 2, 4). One reviewer noted that subjective MOS ratings (like DNS Challenge 2023) would be more convincing than objective metrics alone (Reviewer 2).
- **MacBook Pro playback for speech creation**: The real-world dataset was recorded by playing speech from a MacBook Pro speaker, which has different frequency response and directionality than a human mouth. An artificial mouth would be more appropriate (Reviewer 2). (This is a specific criticism of the real-world validation data, not of SonicSim itself.)
- **Missing advanced evaluation metrics**: The paper uses PESQ and DNSMOS but does not include more advanced full-reference or no-reference metrics such as NISQA or SQAPP, which could provide a more nuanced quality assessment of synthetic vs. real data (Reviewer 3).
- **No evaluation of other audio simulation challenges**: Several features mentioned (obstacles, room geometry, room surface materials) are not studied or ablated. Their impact on model performance remains unexamined (Reviewer 1).
- **Microphone type and spatial configuration not showcased**: The possibility to simulate monaural vs. binaural or different microphone types is mentioned but not demonstrated or analyzed (Reviewer 1). How to add new microphone types is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Fixed noise levels limit robustness testing**: SonicSet uses fixed LUFS levels for environmental and musical noise. Experiments with varying SNRs are needed to test model robustness to diverse noise conditions (Reviewer 4).
- **Model selection rationale unclear**: The paper does not explain why specific separation/enhancement models were chosen, nor does it analyze how platform capabilities support different model types (Reviewer 4).
- **Flexibility of scene layouts and materials unclear**: It is not described exactly which scene properties can be adjusted vs. fixed (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues**: Key details (training setups, dataset splits, evaluation metrics) are moved to supplementary material; moving them to the main text would improve readability. The Introduction is overly detailed (Reviewer 4).
- **Anomalous results in Table 6**: SI-SNR and WB-PESQ values for Inter-SubNet are much lower than reported in the original Inter-SubNet paper. This needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
- **Dataset naming unclear**: The 5-hour real-world dataset used for validation (Section C.5) is not given a name—it should be distinguished from SonicSet (Reviewer 2).
- **Table 5 metric description ambiguous**: It says DNSMOS is used but also lists MOS Sig, MOS BAK, MOS Overall. Clarify which model (DNSMOS or SIGMOS) provides each metric (Reviewer 2).
- **One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive** about SonicSim’s value as a tool filling an important gap, while the other three reviewers (score 6) raised more substantial concerns about validation, presentation, and missing experiments. This contrast is preserved in the strengths and weaknesses above. (Reviewer 2 vs. 1,3,4)
