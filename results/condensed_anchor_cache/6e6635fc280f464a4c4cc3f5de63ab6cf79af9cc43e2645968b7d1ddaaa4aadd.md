## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a "First-order Local Influence" (FI) metric, grounded in information geometry, to quantify LLM and VLM sensitivity to parameter and input perturbations. The authors apply FI to saliency analysis for VLMs, and to model quantization and model merging for LLMs. Two reviewers rated 3 (confidence 3 and 4) and one rated 5 (confidence 3). The higher-scoring reviewer found the approach theoretically grounded and practically valuable, while the lower-scoring reviewers raised major concerns about novelty, missing baselines, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The paper presents interesting and novel applications of saliency-inspired influence measures to model quantization and model merging (R1, R3).
- The motivation for using saliency maps to improve robustness is strongly argued (R1).
- FI offers a theoretically grounded approach for assessing both parameter and input sensitivity, which can support robustness improvements (R2).
- Experiments are conducted on models from 1.5B to 13B parameters across diverse applications (quantization, merging), demonstrating practical value (R2, R1, R3).
- The method appears effective at identifying input regions and parameters that have a large effect on model output (R3).
- The specific application of identifying sensitive parameters to preserve during quantization/sparsification is appreciated (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparisons to existing baselines** (R1, R3): No baselines are provided for any of the three applications. Specifically:
  - For saliency / input pixel sensitivity: missing comparison with Grad-CAM (R1: [Simonyan et al. 2013]; R3: [Selvaraju et al. 2016]) and subsequent work, including quantitative analysis.
  - For parameter sensitivity / pruning: missing comparison with random parameter selection (R1), pruning methods surveyed in [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.06767] (R1), and approaches like Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) methods (e.g., Frantar & Alistarh 2023; Yu et al. 2024) (R2) and Shah et al. 2024 (R3).
  - For model merging: missing comparison with merging methods surveyed in [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07666] (R1).
- **Novelty is unclear** (R1): The method is not clearly distinguished from existing saliency maps and adversarial perturbation analysis. A thorough related-work analysis and direct comparisons are needed.
- **Missing relevant prior work** (R2): Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM; Foret et al. 2021) is also omitted, yet relevant for contextualizing FI’s robustness contributions.
- **Clarity issues** (R1): The paper is hard to follow, using unnecessarily abstract notation (e.g., l(ω|y,x,θ) could be written as log-likelihood; f(ω) as negative predicted probability). Some terms like h_j are not defined. Simplification would improve readability.
- **Too broad scope** (R3): Trying to cover both input sensitivity (images) and parameter sensitivity (quantization/merging) in one paper spreads the analysis thin. A focused study on one application would be more effective.
- **Questionable assumption about protecting high-FI parameters** (R2): During distillation/model merging, protecting high-FI parameters might preserve influence from irrelevant or “nonsensical” inputs, potentially hurting performance.
- **Missing broader framing** (R2): The paper is positioned around LLMs/VLMs, but stability concerns apply to general ML. A broader context would strengthen the paper.
- **Missing computational cost details** (R3): The compute overhead of computing FI is not reported.
- **Incomplete analysis of VLM prompt sensitivity** (R3): How different prompts affect the influence map, and the semantic closeness of images and prompts, requires further investigation.
