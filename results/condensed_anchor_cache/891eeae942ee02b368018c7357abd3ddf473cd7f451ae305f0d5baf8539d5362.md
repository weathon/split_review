## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Noise-Free Score Distillation (NFSD), a simple modification to Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) that decomposes the SDS update into three components—condition, domain, and noise residual terms—and removes the undesired noise term by estimating the domain term via a negative prompt. This allows using a normal Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scale, preventing over-smoothing and improving fine-grained details in text-to-3D and text-to-image generation. The decomposition also provides insights into existing methods like Delta Denoising Score (DDS) and Variational Score Distillation (VSD). Qualitative results demonstrate improved visual quality compared to SDS and other baselines.

### Strengths
- The method is simple, intuitive, and easy to implement, building on SDS with minimal changes.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written; formulations and visualizations effectively convey the motivation and decomposition.
- The core decomposition of SDS into condition, domain, and noise components is novel and insightful. It provides a compelling explanation for the large CFG weights used in DreamFusion and offers a unified perspective to understand prior improvements such as DDS and VSD.
- The qualitative results show clear improvements in 3D generation (e.g., fine-grained details, reduced blurriness) compared to SDS and other baselines.
- The decomposition effectively identifies the source of SDS ambiguity (distribution mismatch between generator and real images) and explains why previous methods improved SDS (one reviewer was particularly positive about this contribution).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous mathematical justification:** The score decomposition and the estimation of the domain term using a negative prompt rely on empirical assumptions without a principled derivation. The assumption that a negative prompt captures the "out-of-distribution" domain and can cancel the noise term \(\delta_N\) is not theoretically grounded.
- **Sensitivity to negative prompt choice:** Estimating \(\delta_D\) via \(\delta_{C=p_{\text{neg}}}\) (Eq. 6) lacks clear rationale, and the results may depend heavily on the specific negative prompt used (e.g., "ugly, blurry, low resolution, etc."). It is unclear whether this is generalizable across different scenes, renderers, or artifact types. One reviewer questioned whether a better generator \(g(\theta)\) or training a model to estimate the bias \(\delta_N\) could achieve the same effect.
- **Insufficient quantitative evaluation and ablation studies:** The paper relies almost entirely on qualitative results. There are no quantitative metrics (e.g., CLIP R-Precision, FID, IS, CLIP score) to objectively assess improvement. Ablation studies are missing to isolate the contributions of each removed term (noise prediction \(\delta_N\) vs. injected noise \(\epsilon\)) and to validate design choices such as the timestep threshold (\(t=200\) selected without justification; why not \(t=100, 300, 400\)?), the use of both terms in Eq. 6 versus just the second part, and the necessity of including \(\delta_D\).
- **Unclear distinction from standard negative prompt trick:** While presented as novel, the method's core idea—using a negative prompt to refine unconditional score estimates—bears strong resemblance to existing negative prompting techniques. A more formal argument clarifying the fundamental difference would deepen the contribution.
- **Lack of efficiency or convergence comparison:** The claim that NFSD is more efficient than VSD is not supported by quantitative evidence (e.g., number of function evaluations or optimization steps needed). One reviewer also suggested comparing NFSD to SDS in terms of diffusion model function evaluations (NFEs).
- **Questions about specific design choices and assumptions:**
  - The assumption that the magnitude of \(\delta_D\) is preserved while \(\delta_N\) decreases monotonically in the backward process (Section 4) is questioned.
  - The technical correctness of the decomposition in Eq. (3) was questioned: \(\delta_C\) is defined using both conditional and unconditional scores (not solely on the conditional score term), which may conflate the intended separation.
  - The prediction error maps in Figure 4 were described as unintuitive, especially the less-noisy map at \(t=1000\) compared to \(t=1\).
  - The definition and universality of the negative prompt for the domain term are not addressed.
  - It is unclear whether simply using \(s \delta_C\) without \(\delta_D\) is sufficient.
- **Missing experiments and comparisons suggested by reviewers:**
  - Provide samples with different seeds for the same prompt to demonstrate diversity.
  - Show results of SDS using the same negative prompts as NFSD to isolate the effect of the proposed modification.
  - Test compatibility of NFSD with ProlificDreamer's variational particle optimization (replacing LoRA).
  - For 2D image generation (Figure 7), use a standard benchmark like MS-COCO with metrics (FID, IS, CLIP score).
  - Evaluate on the DreamFusion CLIP R-Precision benchmark.
  - Include visualizations corresponding to Fig. 3 for the proposed \(\delta_D\) estimation (Eq. 6) and show results of \(x_{\text{OOD}} + \delta_D + \delta_N^{\text{OOD}}\) to clarify notation.
