## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies conservative contextual bandits with non-linear cost functions, proposing two algorithms (C-SquareCB and C-FastCB) that combine Inverse Gap Weighting (IGW) and online regression oracles. They prove that the safety constraint (performance within a \((1+\alpha)\) factor of a baseline) is satisfied with high probability and that regret is sublinear in horizon \(T\) (C-SquareCB) or sublinear in \(L^*\), the optimal cumulative loss (C-FastCB). Experiments on real-world data demonstrate improved regret compared to linear baselines. Reviewers found the paper well-written and timely, but raised concerns about novelty, rigor, and practical details.

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly written and mostly easy to follow, with proof roadmap and intuition provided (R1, R2).
- It fills a gap in the conservative bandit literature by extending beyond linear representations to general function classes, using an online regression oracle and IGW exploration (R1, R2, R3).
- The safety constraint satisfaction and sublinear regret (in \(T\) or \(L^*\)) are provably guaranteed (R2, R3).
- Experiments on real-world data demonstrate superior performance compared to linear baselines (R1, R2).
- Reviewer 1 found the analysis sound and rigorous and the experiments convincing; Reviewers 2 and 3 also noted the empirical support.

### Weaknesses

- Novelty and technical contribution are unclear: the paper combines two established sub-fields (safe bandits and general function contextual bandits) but does not sufficiently delineate the technical challenges and new ideas compared to Kazerouni et al. (2017), Foster & Rakhlin (2020), and Foster & Krishnamurthy (2021). The safety criterion and time-varying exploration schedule need more emphasis (R1, R3).
- Algorithmic similarity: Algorithm 1 and 2 are almost identical except for the regression oracle; more focus on Algorithm 2 (tighter bound) would highlight technical novelties (R3).
- Rigor and completeness concerns:
  - The proof invokes Lemma 2 of Foster & Rakhlin (2020) which requires Assumption 3 to hold for all sequences, but the authors only prove it with high probability; this discrepancy may affect validity of theoretical guarantees (R2).
  - Theorem statements lack completeness: no indication of how to set input parameters (e.g., \(\gamma\) for Algorithm 1) to achieve stated regret bounds; phrases like "appropriate choice of parameter" are vague (R3).
- Practicality issues:
  - The neural network width required for Theorem 5.2 is \(\Omega(\text{poly}(T))\), which may be impractically large for long horizons; computational cost and polynomial dependence on \(T\) are not detailed (R2).
  - The exploration parameter \(\gamma_t\) in Algorithm 2 depends on unknown \(L_i^*\), making it unclear how to set it in practice (R1, R3).
  - For C-FastCB, the optimal cost is unknown; how to run it without that knowledge is not explained (R1).
- Experiment reproducibility is lacking: details on hyperparameter tuning, setting of \(\delta\), and how C-FastCB is run are missing (R1, R3).
- Significance of regret bounds: any algorithm satisfying the inequality in Assumption 2 automatically satisfies the safety constraint, and the trivial policy that always selects the baseline also satisfies the constraint but without sublinear regret; this observation should be discussed to clarify practical relevance (R2).
- Data-dependent bound: when \(L^* = O(1)\), regret bound becomes \(\tilde O(\log T)\); is there any instance-dependent metric (mean gap) hiding in the constant? (R1)
- Assumption \(\alpha<1\) in Definition 2.2; can this be extended to \(\alpha\ge1\)? (R2)
- Line 160: what is \(H\) in “inf”? (R2)
- Baseline policy data is not used in the oracle; why not use this noiseless data to improve estimation? (R3)
- Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) definition and regret bound: NTK matrix defined using chosen features may be problematic; perhaps should be defined based on all action contexts as in Zhou et al. (2020) (R3). Regret bound shows dependence on \(K\) instead of effective dimension; what enables this? (R3)
- Activation functions: difference between \(\phi\) (smooth Lipschitz) and \(\sigma\) (sigmoid)? (R3)
- Perturbation constant and ensemble size \(S\) do not appear in regret bound; how are they chosen and do they affect regret? (R3)
- Typos:
  - Algorithm 2 Line 8: possible missing \(\sqrt{\log\delta^{-1}}\) term? (R1)
  - Line 836: missing sqrt term involving \(m_{\tau-1}\) and Reg (R1).
  - Line 295: “Line 7” should be “Line 8” (R3).
