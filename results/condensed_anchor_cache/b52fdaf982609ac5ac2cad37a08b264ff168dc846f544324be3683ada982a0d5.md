## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Neural Exploratory Landscape Analysis (NeurELA), a framework that replaces hand-crafted Exploratory Landscape Analysis features in Meta-Black-Box Optimization with a learned, attention-based neural network. It uses a two-stage attention architecture, trained via neuroevolution across multiple MetaBBO tasks, to dynamically profile optimization landscapes in an end-to-end manner. The approach is tested on several MetaBBO algorithms, including zero-shot generalization and fine-tuning, and on synthetic and real-world (protein docking) problems.

### Strengths
- The motivation is strong: automating feature extraction removes the reliance on human-crafted ELA features and makes MetaBBO fully end-to-end (R1, R2, R4).
- The two-stage attention architecture is novel and interesting (R1, R2). However, one reviewer (R3, rating 3) noted strong similarity to Deep-ELA (Seiler et al., 2024), differing only in using a linear transformation instead of kNN embedding, and considered the originality low.
- NeurELA consistently outperforms all baselines across multiple MetaBBO tasks (R1, R2, R4). In particular, it shows clear improvements over traditional ELA features (R4).
- Extensive experiments and ablations are performed, covering multiple MetaBBO algorithms, zero-shot generalization, fine-tuning, and real-world protein docking (R2, R4). The validation addresses research questions on performance, adaptability, efficiency, and generalization (R3).
- The code is shared (R2).
- The paper is well-structured and clear (R3). (Note: R2 found the presentation needs work; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost and training scalability**: The framework introduces a tri-level optimization (landscape extractor + meta-policy + target function), increasing overall cost relative to existing MetaBBO (R1). Training the extractor with an evolution strategy (CMA-ES) on 3,296 parameters for only 500 evaluations raises concerns about convergence and scalability to larger networks (R1). The computational overhead is noted as “quite big,” and gains over the original variant (Λ₀) are not large even with many samples (R2). The authors acknowledge training efficiency issues with larger models (R4).
- **Originality and comparison gaps**: One reviewer (R3, rating 3) argued the approach is very similar to Deep-ELA (Seiler et al., 2024). The only architectural difference is using a linear transformation instead of kNN embedding for population encoding (R3). The paper does not compare NeurELA to Deep-ELA or other recent methods (R3).
- **Limited task coverage and analysis**: Experiments lack detailed analysis in higher-dimensional optimization scenarios, where many MetaBBO algorithms struggle (R3). The wall-clock times reported are very short (milliseconds), making algorithmic differences indistinguishable from system noise; more complex benchmarks or higher dimensions are needed (R3). The authors could better analyze when/why zero-shot generalization fails (R4). It remains unclear what the minimum number of training tasks is for good zero-shot performance (R4).
- **Interpretability**: There is limited discussion of what features the network learns compared to traditional ELA metrics, and no theoretical justification for why the two-stage attention architecture works (R3, R4).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Many typos and hard-to-read paragraphs (R2). Specific unclear points include: the definition of “one epoch” in Fig. 4 and how “ZeroShot” and “Finetune” can be compared on the same epoch scale (R2); the definition of the performance metric in Fig. 3 is missing (R1); the paper states training uses a Slurm CPU cluster (line 356) but claims GPU parallelization for the architecture (line 479) – this discrepancy needs explanation (R3); several citation and grammar errors (R4: e.g., “an universal,” wrong citation styles, typos).
- **Methodological questions**: If baseline methods were given the same extra computational budget (e.g., for hyperparameter tuning or feature selection), would the performance gain remain? (R1). Is there empirical evidence that the ES optimizer for the outer loop has converged? (R1). What BBO/MetaBBO algorithms are supported? (R1) – the authors assume population-based evolutionary algorithms; integration with other BBO methods is unclear. What is the exact dimensionality of NeurELA features and its impact on performance? (R1).
