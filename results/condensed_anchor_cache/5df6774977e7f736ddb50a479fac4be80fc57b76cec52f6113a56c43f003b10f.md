## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MaxInfoRL, a framework that combines intrinsic rewards based on information gain (estimated via ensemble dynamics) with Boltzmann and ε-greedy exploration to improve exploration in reinforcement learning. An auto-tuning mechanism balances intrinsic and extrinsic objectives. Sublinear regret is proven in the multi-armed bandit setting. The method is applied to off-policy algorithms like SAC, REDQ, and DrQ, and tested on state-based and visual continuous control benchmarks, claiming superior performance over baselines on hard exploration tasks including sparse-reward and vision-based humanoid locomotion.

### Strengths

- **Problem significance**: Addresses a core challenge in RL—efficient directed exploration with sparse rewards (R1, R4); broadly important (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Clear presentation**: Well-organized, logically structured, easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Simple and sensible idea**: Combining existing ideas (info-gain maximisation, Boltzmann exploration) in a tractable way (R1); intuitive methodology using ensemble dynamics and auto-tuning (R4).
- **Strong empirical evidence**: Extensive experiments across diverse domains, including hard exploration and visual control tasks; consistently outperforms baselines in sample efficiency (R1, R3, R4). Code provided (R1).
- **Theoretical result**: Theorem 3.1 for the bandit setting demonstrates sublinear regret; inclusion of any theory is appreciated (R2, R3). The convergence result is considered important (R3).
- **Effective methodology**: The framework integrates intrinsic and extrinsic exploration seamlessly, using ensemble dynamics to estimate information gain and auto-tuning to balance objectives (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty / theoretical contribution**: The method essentially combines two existing ideas (info-gain as intrinsic reward and Boltzmann exploration from SAC) without introducing fundamentally new concepts (R1). The trade-off mechanism (auto-tuning, temperature parameter) builds on existing techniques, lacking substantial novelty (R4). One reviewer (score 5) is particularly critical of the originality and theoretical soundness.
- **Weak theoretical justification**:
  - The bandit regret bound is sublinear but not strong (order \(\mathcal{O}(T^{1-\alpha}\log(T)^k + T^{2\alpha})\) for \(\alpha\in(0.25,0.5)\)); many approaches (UCB, Thompson sampling) achieve the Lai & Robbins lower bound of \(\log(T)\) (R3).
  - The theoretical result is only in the bandit setting; the link to full RL (MDP) is not established, and it is known that optimal bandit behaviour does not imply optimal MDP behaviour (e.g., posterior sampling fails in full MDPs – cited reference [1]) (R1, R3).
  - A stronger regret bound in the full RL or tabular setting would be desirable and would allow more intuitive theoretical comparison (R2). No proof of optimality is given even for the bandit case.
  - The derivation on p.4 uses an upper bound on information gain without clarifying that this is a crude approximation (e.g., assuming independent multivariate normal with same variances) (R1). Equation (7) introduces an arbitrary one-to-one trade-off between information (in nats) and reward, with arbitrary scale (R1).
- **Missing related work / insufficient literature context**: The paper does not discuss the rich literature on Bayesian RL and deep exploration (e.g., methods achieving deep exploration since at least 2018) (R3). This omission weakens the contextualisation of the contribution.
- **Incomplete empirical evaluation**:
  - Comparisons are made against algorithms that do not have strong exploration properties (SAC, REDQ, DrQ), which some reviewers consider a straw man; the evaluation should include recent exploration-focused methods such as those in [2][3][4][5][6] (R3). Another reviewer (score 8) also requests more comparisons with existing algorithms (R2).
  - No ablation isolating the role of Boltzmann exploration vs. pure info-gain is provided, despite the claim that random-action exploration remains important (R1). This makes the reason for combining both unclear.
  - The \(\epsilon\)-greedy method described seems ill-founded: the exploratory action is chosen according to the optimal Q-function for intrinsic rewards, but the policy then immediately reverts to exploit, potentially never gathering the promised information (R1).
- **Practical issues and missing analyses**:
  - Computational overhead of maintaining and training ensemble models is not discussed or quantified, which is critical since some baselines prioritise speed and scalability (R1).
  - The paper does not address the weaknesses or limitations of the proposed method (R1).
  - The auto-tuning description (bottom of p.5) is incomplete: as written, the dual optimisation would drive \(\alpha\) to 0 or infinity; it becomes auto-tuning only when gradually updated (e.g., via SGD). This should be stated explicitly (R1).
  - The claim on p.6 that arbitrary policy entropy can be attained is generally not true (R1).
  - The meaning of the constraint “(iii) it is tight” on p.6 is not clarified (R1).
  - The use of “c.f.” is repeatedly misused (should mean “compare to”, not “see”) (R1).
  - Equation (8) would benefit from more explanation or intermediate steps (R1).
  - Equation (12) should make the dependency of the value function on the policy explicit (R1).
