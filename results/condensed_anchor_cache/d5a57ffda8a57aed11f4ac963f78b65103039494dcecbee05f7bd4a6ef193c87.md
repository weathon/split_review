## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Think-on-Graph (ToG), an LLM-KG integration paradigm where the LLM acts as an agent performing iterative beam search (search and prune) on a knowledge graph to discover reasoning paths and generate answers. Reviewers find the approach novel, well-motivated, and experimentally strong, achieving SOTA on 6 out of 9 datasets without training. However, concerns are raised about the method’s reliance on strong LLMs (especially GPT-4 for top performance), high computational cost, unclear handling of relation semantics across KGs, incomplete baseline comparisons, insufficient study of hallucination and knowledge conflict, and lack of in-depth theoretical analysis. Reviewers split on contribution (scores: 6,6,8,8), reflecting disagreement on the strength of empirical validation and practical feasibility.

### Strengths
- Novel and intuitive method: the LLM⊗KG paradigm with iterative beam search (search+prune) is new and reasonable. (all reviewers)
- Strong experimental performance: achieves SOTA on 6 out of 9 datasets without training; significant improvement over non-KG prompting. (R1, R3, R4)
- Plug-and-play: no additional training cost, flexible integration with different LLMs and KGs. (R1, R3)
- Extensive ablation and analyses: depth/width of search investigated, component analysis, limitations in Appendix A, case studies and technical details in appendix. (R1, R2)
- Clear presentation: well-written, clear motivation, easy to follow, good figures. (R1, R3, R4; though R2 notes writing improvements needed)
- Knowledge traceability and correctability: ToG allows verification and correction via expert feedback. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on strong LLMs**: Best performance (SOTA on 6/9) is achieved only with GPT-4; the advantage diminishes significantly with weaker models (Llama-2-70B, ChatGPT) – see Tables 1,2. Minority reviewers (R1, R2) view this as a critical limitation; R4 encourages results on small 7B/13B models.
- **High computational cost**: Iterative beam search requires multiple LLM calls per query. Reviewers note lack of detailed analysis (average number of calls, latency, API cost), making it hard to assess practical feasibility for real-time or large-scale applications. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Inconsistent method description**: The pruning process is described as using current reasoning paths, but the prompt in Appendix G.3 does not include those paths – raises reproducibility concerns. (R3)
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: Missing baselines: stronger prompting methods (e.g., self-ask), web search/retrieval augmentation (R1). The “Prior Prompting SOTA” column in Table 1 has blanks that should be filled for fair comparison. (R2)
- **Hallucination problem insufficiently studied**: Paper motivates with hallucination but only provides a preliminary error analysis in Appendix D.2. (R2)
- **Lack of in-depth analysis**: The paper is empirically driven and lacks theoretical or methodological depth. (R2)
- **No study of knowledge conflict**: Not evaluated how ToG behaves when KG knowledge contradicts LLM parametric knowledge. (R4)
- **Sensitivity to KG quality and relation semantics**: Not evaluated for low-quality KGs (sparsity, noise, outdated) – a potential vulnerability (R4). Also, relation formats vary across KGs (e.g., Freebase hierarchical, Yago clear); paper does not discuss how LLM understands them without additional training. (R3)
- **Limited to path-based reasoning**: Complex queries requiring counting, intersection, union, negation (e.g., GrailQA) may not be solvable; no discussion. (R3)
- **Comparison to prior progressive reasoning methods**: Lacks discussion with learnable search/prune mechanisms (AdaProp, A*Net). Possibility of learnable pruning enhancement not considered. (R2)
- **Outdated knowledge**: How do the used KGs address the limitation of out-of-date knowledge? (R2)
- **Efficiency comparison not performed**: No decoding time or cost comparison across methods. (R1)
