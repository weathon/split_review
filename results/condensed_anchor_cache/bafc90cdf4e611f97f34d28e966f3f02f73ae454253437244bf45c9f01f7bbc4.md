## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper tackles video face re-aging with a focus on temporal consistency. Contributions include (1) a synthetic video dataset generated via a pipeline (StyleGAN, SAM, OSFV, FILM), (2) a baseline architecture using a recurrent U-Net with image and video discriminators, and (3) two new metrics: Time Region Wrinkle Consistency (TRWC) and Time-Age Preservation (T-Age). Experiments compare 10 existing re-aging methods on VFHQ and CelebV-HQ, including user studies. One reviewer (score 5) finds this establishes a strong baseline and highlights the effectiveness of synthetic data, while the other three reviewers (scores 3) consider novelty limited and raise significant concerns about detail and scope.

### Strengths

- **Comprehensive evaluation:** 10 existing state-of-the-art re-aging methods are compared on VFHQ and CelebV-HQ, with necessary ablation experiments. The paper is overall well written.
- **Addresses temporal consistency:** The work focuses on the challenging and important factor of temporal coherence in video face aging, and provides an end-to-end approach including data generation, architecture, and evaluation metrics.
- **Innovative data generation pipeline:** The proposed pipeline for creating a synthetic video dataset addresses the lack of paired video data with consistent identities and varying ages, enhancing training data quality and applicability.
- **New evaluation metrics:** TRWC and T-Age provide a more effective means of assessing age transformation quality and temporal coherence in videos, offering nuanced evaluation beyond traditional methods.
- **Strong baseline contribution (minority view, Reviewer 3):** The architecture, dataset creation, and metrics collectively establish a valuable baseline for future video re-aging research. The approach demonstrates that even a simple GAN-based architecture can leverage synthetic video datasets to yield compelling results in realism and temporal coherence, as shown through extensive qualitative and quantitative analysis.

### Weaknesses

#### Dataset and Resource Concerns

1. **Lack of dataset detail:** Insufficient information about the synthetic dataset’s size (number of videos, total duration), diversity (age ranges, ethnicity, face shape, facial hair), and visual samples. Without details on age distribution and facial feature variability, it is impossible to assess robustness across different age transitions or generalizability. Reviewer 4 also asks for more meta information (number of identities/subjects, total duration).
2. **Unclear resource availability:** The authors do not state whether the dataset, pipeline code, or trained models will be publicly released, hindering reproducibility and follow-up research.
3. **Limited age progression scope:** Generated videos show age changes only in the facial area, neglecting hair (color, texture, hairline) and neck skin (texture, wrinkles). This inconsistency reduces realism, as subjects appear mismatched between facial and other features. Reviewer 3 asks why the approach does not generate changes in other regions and how this might be addressed in future work.

#### Motion Generation and Technical Clarity

4. **Missing motion generation details:** Section 3.1.3 lacks clarity on the stopping condition for intermediate frames (fixed or adaptive), and whether motion interpolation is linear or more complex. Reviewers request specification of the number of intermediate frames and the interpolation method’s impact on temporal consistency.
5. **Temporal consistency enforcement unsubstantiated:** No explicit constraint for consistency between frames during learning. How does the trained network achieve temporal coherence? Could TRWC be adopted as a loss function? The paper should discuss architectural details or loss functions that maintain consistency during both learning and inference.
6. **Equation (8) choice ambiguous:** Why validate on the generated image \(\hat{I}\) rather than the delta image? Can delta images be used directly to measure similarity? An analysis and ablation study comparing choices and their effect on metric values is recommended.
7. **Typos:** Several typos (e.g., line 311, symbols in Figure 2) are present; the paper needs thorough proofreading.

#### Novelty and Comparison Gaps

8. **Limited novelty (majority view, Reviewers 2 and 4):** Most components are off-the-shelf or inspired by prior work:
   - Data generation pipeline relies entirely on existing methods: StyleGAN + SAM for single-frame aging, OSFV for key frame generation, FILM for motion interpolation.
   - Architecture uses a recurrent U-Net with common losses, input fashion (5 channels with age masks) borrowed from Zoss et al., and a PatchGAN discriminator from Isola et al.
   - T-Age metric is based on an off-the-shelf age classifier (Rothe et al.).
   - The paper is characterized as an engineering combination rather than presenting novel contributions, falling short of the novelty expected for acceptance.
9. **Missing baseline comparisons:** 
   - Table 1 compares three image-based re-aging methods but does not include SAM; reviewers ask for comparisons with SAM and diffusion autoencoders (Preechakul et al.).
   - Figure 4(b) does not show CUSP results.
   - Table 2 (user study) includes no video-based face re-aging method. Why?
10. **SAM limitations and error accumulation:** The data generation uses SAM, which fails to preserve attributes (identity, pose, expression) as acknowledged in line 475. The paper does not provide technical modifications to mitigate SAM’s errors, nor does it show quantitative comparison on attribute preservation. Such errors likely accumulate with OSFV.
11. **Statistical significance unclear:** In line 468, the claim that a 0.18 difference in TRWC (Table 1) is “evident by the user’s choices” needs more detailed explanation.

#### Additional Questions and Missing Ablations

12. **Image and video discriminator loss (line 292):** How is the discriminator loss updated without ground truth in the total objective function?
13. **Input masks (spatial masks M^inp and M^tar):** What is their purpose? Are they the same size as the input image? Do they have uniform values? Visual examples would help.
