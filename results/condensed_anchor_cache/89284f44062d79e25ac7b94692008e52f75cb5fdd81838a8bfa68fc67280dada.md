## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes π2vec, a method to represent black-box policies as feature vectors by combining pretrained foundation model state representations with successor features. Successor features are trained via Fitted Q-learning on offline datasets, and a policy embedding is obtained by averaging its successor features over a set of canonical states. Linear regression on these embeddings predicts the performance of held-out policies. The method is evaluated across 5 domains (including real robots) using correlation, NMAE, and regret.

### Strengths
- **Novel combination**: The idea of merging successor features with pretrained foundation models to represent policies by their induced state changes (rather than parameters or actions) is new and interesting. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Thorough empirical investigation**: Extensive experiments across 5 domains, including real robots, demonstrate the method generally outperforms the baseline action-only representation. (R1, R3; one reviewer [R3] found these results strong, while others [R1, R2] raised concerns about baseline comparisons—see Weaknesses)
- **Choice of multiple metrics**: Using correlation, NMAE, and regret captures relative performance, absolute error, and best-policy ordering, providing complementary insights. (R1)
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (R4; note that R2 rated presentation 2/4, indicating some clarity concerns—see Weaknesses)
- **Relevance for OPE**: The policy embedding space could directly support offline policy evaluation and imitation learning. (R1, R2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited theoretical analysis / missing intuition**: There is insufficient insight into *why* combining successor features with foundation models produces effective policy embeddings. Unclear whether the foundation model features capture task-relevant aspects or merely provide a high-dimensional space for easier linear separation. The paper does not analyze how specific feature properties (e.g., visual vs. state, pretraining objective) affect success, forcing users to enumerate all possible feature choices. (R1, R3)
2. **Baseline comparisons are insufficient**:
   - Only one baseline is compared (action-only representation). Because the proposed method uses state-action data while the baseline uses only actions, it is unclear if performance gains come from the proposed idea or from having access to richer information. (R1)
   - A potentially relevant baseline (unspecified, but could provide ranking results) is neither included nor explained as unsuitable. (R2)
   - No comparisons are made to standard offline policy evaluation benchmarks (e.g., D4RL, ExoRL for image-based tasks) or to state-based observations, despite the method’s focus on visual features. (R4)
3. **Missing ablations and assumption analysis**:
   - The method implicitly assumes that (i) historical policies’ performance covers the unseen policy and (ii) the offline dataset provides sufficient state coverage. No experiments test degradation when these assumptions are violated (e.g., unseen policy is much better, dataset covers only 50% of states the unseen policy visits). (R2)
   - The averaging of successor features over canonical states appears ad hoc; alternative aggregation methods (e.g., attention, clustering) are not explored. (R3; also R1 asks about how the “best policy” for regret is determined—feature-dependent or -agnostic)
4. **Metric clarity and appropriateness**:
   - The paper does not give explicit equations for correlation, NMAE, and regret. (R4)
   - The relationship between correlation and the number of evaluations needed to find the best policy is not justified; correlation could be arbitrarily bad yet still be used to claim efficiency. (R1)
   - The discussion of results (points (i), (ii), (iii)) is confusing, e.g., (ii) says NMAE is better but (iii) says regret is better—it is unclear which metric should be prioritized for the intended use case. (R1)
   - For the task of offline policy selection, ranking metrics (e.g., Mean Average Precision, DCG) would be more relevant than absolute value error. (R4)
5. **Restricted to offline setting**: The method requires pre-collected offline datasets and cannot be applied in an online interaction setting. Its performance depends critically on the quality and diversity of the offline data, which is often not guaranteed. (R3; also R1 questions the effect of having online trajectories from behavior policies)
6. **Missing evaluation on standard benchmarks and state-based observations**: The paper focuses on visual features without clear justification. It should be evaluated on state-based observations (e.g., how to select base features in that case) and on established offline benchmarks like D4RL or ExoRL. (R4)
7. **Missing citations**: The original successor representation paper (Dayan, 1993), and related works (Reinke et al. 2023, Brantley et al. 2021, Lehnert et al. 2020) are not cited. (R1)
