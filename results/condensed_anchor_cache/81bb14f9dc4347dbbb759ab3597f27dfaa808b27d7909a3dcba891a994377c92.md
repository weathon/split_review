## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses fast, sparse-view articulated human avatar reconstruction and rendering using a learned iterative feedback update framework on a multi-resolution Gaussians-on-Mesh representation. The method combines a low-resolution mesh for efficient computation with high-resolution Gaussian anchors for fine details, and refines the canonical representation by extracting pixel-aligned features from input images. It achieves reconstruction under 1s, rendering at 95.1 FPS at 1024×1024, and reports PSNR/LPIPS*/FID of 24.59/111.26/51.42 on THuman2.0, outperforming prior art. Two reviewers rated the paper 8 (accept, good), and two rated 6 (weaker acceptance). Reviewer 2 raised their score to 8 after the rebuttal.

### Strengths
- The iterative feedback update mechanism is novel and clearly motivated, refining predictions by addressing visible errors from input views to improve reconstruction accuracy and detail within under one second.
- Achieving animatable human avatar reconstruction from sparse images in different (not necessarily multi-view consistent) poses is highly practical and offers greater flexibility than multi-view approaches.
- The multi-resolution Gaussians-on-Mesh representation balances efficient computation with high-quality rendering, and avoids the Gaussian split/growing heuristics that can be annoying to tune.
- The method shows fast, efficient reconstruction and promising results on out-of-distribution avatars.
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and clear (reviewers 1,4). The introduction and related work are easy to follow.
- Extensive experimentation on THuman2.0 and AIST++ shows clear quantitative and qualitative improvements over previous methods (Tables 1,2,3).
- The ablation study helps justify the effectiveness of iterative step choice and subdivision (reviewer 4; note: reviewer 3 criticizes the ablation for focusing on hyperparameters rather than core components – this contrast is kept below).
- Reviewer 2 notes that the method “gets rid of the Gaussian split/growing heuristics” as a strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to key baselines:**  
  – GoMAvatar, from which the method is derived (line 121-123), is not compared. The iterative-feedback also bears close similarity to work using pixel/texel aligned features.  
  – Recent works [1,2,3] that report better metrics are not included; a comparative analysis or discussion of technical differences is needed (reviewer 3).  
  – Large reconstruction model-based human rendering approaches (e.g., [1,2] in reviewer 4’s list) are not discussed; comparison of inference time, training time, resolution, and PSNR would be valuable.  
  – Traditional mesh-based human representations ([3,4,5]) are not directly compared; advantages/trade-offs in rendering quality, animation flexibility, and FPS should be discussed.
- **Cross-domain generalization and artifacts:**  
  – The method shows significant performance degradation when applied across domains, particularly affecting the facial region (as shown in the video). The novel pose representation also displays black hands (reviewer 1).  
  – Cross-domain facial artifacts persist despite the iterative feedback; the cause may be data bias, yet the case is showcased without discussion of limitations or potential solutions (e.g., face-aware regularization) – reviewer 3.  
  – In the cross-domain generalization video, not all input images are shown, making evaluation difficult (reviewer 3 question).  
  – Evaluation on out-of-distribution avatars (e.g., drastically different body types, dynamic poses, motion sequences) is missing; animation quality for those cases is not assessed (reviewer 4 question).
- **Dependence on SMPL priors and limitations of fixed mesh topology:**  
  – Heavy reliance on SMPL/SMPLX models poses a risk: inaccurate SMPL estimates (especially for loose clothing) can lead to significant errors (reviewer 1).  
  – Mesh topology limits the geometry that can be captured, e.g., hair (Figure 4, Appendix C), loose clothing like skirts/dresses. This is a fundamental trade-off (reviewer 2).  
  – The method may be susceptible to body pose misalignment: when the pose is imperfect, the feedback network extracts features from incorrect locations, affecting reconstruction quality (reviewer 2).  
  – The approach may struggle with rendering loose clothing; examples from NeuralAM are suggested for evaluation (reviewer 4).
- **Methodological and training clarity issues:**  
  – The model does not appear to be trained on two datasets simultaneously to support both tasks, limiting its ability to handle mixed input types effectively (reviewer 1).  
  – In Figure 1, the mesh shown appears to be a GT-mesh rather than an SMPL model; it is unclear if GT mesh is utilized (reviewer 1 question – overlapping with a missing clarification).  
  – Since Gaussian splatting has a strong tendency to overfit, how is pose generalization achieved? Are novel poses from the dataset or truly unseen? (reviewer 1 question – indicates a potential weakness in evaluation).
- **Ablation study limitations:**  
  – The ablation focuses mainly on hyperparameters (number of iterations, subdivision) rather than analyzing the effectiveness of core components. An evaluation of the iterative feedback mechanism, coupled-multi-resolution representation, and module-wise contribution is needed (reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation:**  
  – Minor errors: line 156 “estimate” should be “estimation”; line 234 “translation → scale?” (reviewer 1).  
  – Some sentences could be clearer, e.g., line 143-145 could be rephrased to “Prior work [cite] regularizes the Gaussians and enables animation using parametric models…” (reviewer 2). Line 180-181 repeats information already given in lines 175-179 (reviewer 2).  
  – In the novel pose synthesis video, the number of input images should be made clear (reviewer 3 question).
- **Missing memory/VRAM profile:**  
  – No discussion of GPU memory consumption for training or rendering; it is unclear if the method works on commodity-level GPUs (reviewer 2).
