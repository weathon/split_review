## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a unified lightweight network for infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) in complex scenes (noise, rain, overexposure, low-light). The method operates in the frequency domain, exchanging amplitude and phase between modalities, and adds a spatial domain branching strategy. It claims real-time performance (0.033 s for 640×480 images) and integrates image restoration with fusion to avoid error accumulation. Two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 5) find the approach lacking novelty and question the fairness of the experimental comparison; three reviewers (scores 8, confidence 5) praise the frequency-domain perspective, the multi-modal guidance, and the impressive experimental results across four challenging scenarios.

### Strengths
- The idea of using frequency domain information (amplitude/phase separation) to address IVIF in complex scenes is interesting and, according to one reviewer, the first work to do so in this domain.  
- The multi-modal interactive guidance mechanism combines frequency-domain and spatial-domain learning, using the amplitude of infrared images to guide visible restoration and the phase of visible images to enhance infrared images, enabling richer feature extraction and suppression of interference.  
- The lightweight design achieves real-time processing (0.033 s for 640×480 images), which is critical for practical applications such as autonomous vehicles.  
- Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments on four complex scenarios (noise, rain, overexposure, low-light) demonstrate significant performance improvements and outperform existing state-of-the-art methods on multiple metrics.  
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and provides detailed methodological description and motivation.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical contribution**: The method based on exchanging amplitude and phase in the frequency domain lacks novelty (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Similar frequency-domain approaches have been widely applied in other fields (e.g., FourLLIE for low-light enhancement); the paper does not clearly explain how its multi-modal interactive guidance leverages the unique characteristics of infrared and visible modalities beyond what is already achieved in single-modality tasks (Reviewer 5). Additionally, it is unclear why the proposed method achieves better performance than pure image restoration (Reviewer 3).  
- **Mutual guidance mechanism limitation**: The guidance relies on complementary characteristics. If one modality is poor (e.g., infrared sensor occluded or malfunctioning), the amplitude/phase may not provide sufficient valuable features, leading to suboptimal fusion (Reviewer 1).  
- **Spatial domain module and artifact handling**: The Spatial Domain Module uses convolution which extracts all local features including degradation artifacts; max pooling may not filter out high-intensity artifacts. The paper lacks an explanation of how the network distinguishes useful features from degradation artifacts and how the loss function guides this process (Reviewer 5). The final fusion result directly combines source image information with feature maps, risking the introduction of artifacts or noise from the source images; no mitigation strategy is discussed (Reviewer 5). There is no clear optimization strategy for reducing redundant information, so non-critical pixels may be mistaken as valuable (Reviewer 3). The balance between frequency-domain and spatial-domain information is not fully considered; relying solely on frequency may lead to inaccurate spatial details (Reviewer 3).  
- **Training and evaluation fairness**: Data selection: only 1,083 image pairs are available in the MSRS training set, yet the authors randomly selected 1,000 pairs without clear justification. If augmentation was intended (e.g., adding Gaussian noise, scaling), it should have been applied uniformly to the entire set (Reviewer 1). Comparison methods likely were not re-trained on the authors’ specific scenario-wise training setup, while the proposed method was trained separately for each scenario, making the comparison less credible (Reviewer 1). SSIM is used both in the loss function and as an evaluation metric, biasing results in favor of the proposed method (Reviewer 1). The non-reference metrics (SF, AG) are both gradient-based and highly correlated; information-theory metrics like entropy are missing (Reviewer 1). The choice of MSEC’s reconstruction structure for the overexposure scenario is insufficiently explained (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing comparisons with recent SOTA**: The paper should compare with MURF (Xu et al., 2023), SegMiF (Liu et al., 2023), DDFM (Zhao et al., 2023), and EMMA (Zhao et al., 2024) (Reviewer 2).  
- **Practical applicability concern**: The proposed method requires separate training for each scenario. In real-world conditions, multiple degradations can co-occur (e.g., rain and overexposure simultaneously), but the method has no mechanism to choose which trained model to use, potentially leading to suboptimal results. In contrast, existing comparison methods handle all scenarios with a single model (Reviewer 1).  
- **Minor issues**:  
  - Typo in Line 018: “, We.” (Reviewer 5).  
  - In Table 2, the rain column value for LRRNet (0.8050) should be highlighted in red (Reviewer 5).  
  - Figure 5 caption should mention “complex scenes” (Reviewer 4).  
  - Figure 5 labels “DWT: Discrete Wavelet Transform”, but DWT is not explained and does not appear in the figure (Reviewer 5).  
  - Sections 4.4 and 4.5 could be combined (Reviewer 4).  
  - Source code should be made public (Reviewer 4).
