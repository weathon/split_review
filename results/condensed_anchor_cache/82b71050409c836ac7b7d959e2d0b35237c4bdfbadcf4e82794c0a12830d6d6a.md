## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces GeoRCG, a two-stage framework for 3D molecule generation that first generates a geometric representation (using pretrained encoders UniMol/Frad) and then generates a molecule conditioned on that representation. Using EDM as the base generator, it reports improvements in unconditional generation on QM9 and GEOM-DRUG, and claims an average 31% improvement in conditional generation over state-of-the-art methods. The approach reduces diffusion steps from 1000 to 100. However, multiple reviewers note that performance on the larger, more realistic GEOM-DRUG dataset does not consistently beat existing SOTA, many crucial evaluation metrics for DRUG are missing, and the method’s generalizability to real-world drug discovery tasks is unsubstantiated. One reviewer (score 8) found no major weaknesses and praised clarity, while four other reviewers (scores 6,5,5,3) raise significant concerns about evaluation completeness, baselines, and practical applicability.

### Strengths

- Innovative two-stage conditioning on geometric representations simplifies the generation task and addresses O(3)/SO(3) symmetries (R1, R2).
- Latent diffusion reduces inference steps from 1000 to 100 without performance drop, offering speed improvements (R2, R4).
- Generates more realistic bond length and angle distributions on QM9 compared to prior methods (R2).
- Guidance mixed with low-temperature sampling yields strong QM9 results, clearly visualized with heat maps in Figure 4 (R4, R5).
- Clear, well-structured presentation with thorough background and appendix review (R3, R5).
- Conditioning on a cheap representation generator pushes the complexity of conditional generation to a more manageable sub-problem (R4).
- All choices are communicated and motivated; important limitations are outlined in the Conclusions (R5).

### Weaknesses

- **Incomplete evaluation on GEOM-DRUG.** Only atom stability (0.86) is reported for DRUG; crucial 3D metrics (bond length/angle distributions, connectivity, overall molecule stability) are missing (R2, R4). From MiDi, EDM on DRUG achieves only 5.5% molecule stability and 40.3% after OpenBabel, and connectivity is not reported for any method (R4). Performance on DRUG does not beat SOTA and lags behind existing models, raising concerns about generalization beyond the toy QM9 dataset (R3, R4).
- **Reliance on static bond-length lookup table is questionable.** Bond lengths vary within a ~10% interval depending on molecular configuration and the energy calculation method (GFN2‑xTB for GEOM-DRUG); a static table may produce inaccurate structures (R2).
- **Insufficient analysis of pretrained encoders.** The paper does not compare encoders trained on different datasets, with different architectures (GNN vs Transformer), or with different pretraining objectives (contrastive vs masked language modeling) (R1).
- **Missing ablation of symmetry removal.** The impact of removing O(3) and S(N) symmetries on downstream properties (e.g., chirality) is not analyzed; selective preservation of symmetries is not explored (R1).
- **Limited conditional generation scope.** Conditioning is only demonstrated on simple scalar properties (HOMO-LUMO gap, polarizability); no clear extension to complex properties like binding affinity or ADMET is provided (R1). This limits relevance to real drug discovery (R1, R4).
- **Misleading claim about GEOM-DRUG.** The statement that many DRUG structures “lack the equilibrium conditions necessary for pre-training methods” is inaccurate: all GEOM-DRUG molecules have optimized geometries with respect to GFN2‑xTB (R2). The original GEOM paper is not referenced (R2).
- **Missing comparisons with directly bond‑predicting models.** Methods like JODO, EQGATDiff, and SemlaFlow—which generate the full graph (including bonds) without external software—are not compared (R2). Fast-generation baselines (EquiFM: 200 steps, SemlaFlow: 20 steps) are also omitted, undermining the claimed speed advantage (R2).
- **Unclear metrics and missing clarifications.** “EDM” should be defined in the abstract; “improved quality” is vague and should specify the exact metrics used; the “31% performance improvement” must name the metric and benchmark (R5). The step reduction from 1000 to 100 is a 90% reduction, but this percentage is not stated (R5).
- **Impact of low-temperature sampling on DRUG baselines unknown.** Low temperature is used for GeoRCG in Table 1; its effect on prior DRUG methods has not been tested (R4).
- **Framework only demonstrated with EDM.** It is unclear how GeoRCG generalizes to other base models beyond EDM (R4).
- **Sampling-step analysis missing for DRUG.** Performance vs. number of diffusion steps on DRUG is not reported (R4).
- **Minor:** Tables use red/green colors that are not accessible to color‑blind readers (R5).
