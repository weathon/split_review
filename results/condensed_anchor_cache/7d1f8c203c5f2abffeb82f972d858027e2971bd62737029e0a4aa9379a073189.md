## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) for object detection by generating synthetic calibration data without accessing original training data. The proposed framework has two main components: (1) an adaptive sampling method (Relabel) that uses predictions from a pre-trained full-precision detection model to refine bounding box and category labels during synthetic data generation, approximating real-world object distributions; (2) a quantization-aware training (QAT) process that integrates feature-level distillation for effective knowledge transfer. The method is evaluated on MS-COCO and Pascal VOC, achieving state-of-the-art results at low bit-widths (e.g., 4.2% mAP improvement for YOLOv5-m at 5-bit while using only about 1/60 of the calibration data required by LSQ trained on the full training set).

### Strengths

- The problem is clearly expressed and the motivation (zero-shot quantization for object detection, addressing localization and class imbalance) is well-stated. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The idea of Relabel (using the pre-trained model’s predictions to refine bounding boxes and categories during synthetic data generation) is good and effectively improves synthetic data quality. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper claims a reduction in the number of synthetic samples needed, thus reducing training time, which is essential for quantization. (Reviewer 1)
- The bounding box and category sampling strategy provides a data-efficient, privacy-sensitive approach suitable for real-world deployment. (Reviewer 2)
- Integrating feature-level distillation into QAT improves knowledge transfer and performance in low-bit settings, significantly narrowing the gap with full-precision models. (Reviewer 2)
- The framework demonstrates superior performance on both MS-COCO and Pascal VOC, even in ultra-low-bit scenarios, highlighting robustness and practical utility. (Reviewer 2)
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the proposed method. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- By requiring only a fraction of the typical calibration data, the method enhances scalability for deployment on resource-constrained devices. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity of the proposed method:** The explanation of the Relabel process in Section 3.2 is difficult to follow. Formula 5 mentions supervision done by GT, but in the zero-shot setting GT is replaced by the “teacher” model. The initial stage initializes image and position with Gaussian noise; it is unclear how the teacher model obtains information to complete the optimization. It is also not elaborated whether the “teacher” model is the model being quantized or a separate model. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of detailed explanation for feature distillation:** The paper should provide a detailed explanation of f_l after formula 7 – which specific layers are distilled? (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient qualitative evaluation:** The main paper should include qualitative results of synthetic data generated by the proposed method compared to baselines and [1], as well as qualitative results comparing quantized and full-precision networks on real images (bounding box accuracy, class label correctness). (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing baseline in Table 5:** The false positive sampling method from [1] is not included in Table 5, which is a significant oversight for fair comparison. The authors should explain why this baseline was omitted and provide the results. (Reviewer 1)
- **Purpose of weak baseline unclear:** The weak baseline using Gaussian noise in Table 5 is unclear. The authors should explain why it was included, how Gaussian noise input evaluates the adaptive sampling method, and what conclusions can be drawn. (Reviewer 1)
- **Category imbalance analysis missing:** The paper claims category imbalance (L78-79) but does not provide examples or quantitative evidence (e.g., class distribution comparison via KL divergence, chi-squared statistic) showing that previous methods struggle and how the proposed method addresses it. (Reviewer 1)
- **Clarification needed on fine-tuning claim:** The paper claims in L80-81 that “fine-tuning strategy for zero-shot quantized detection network with synthetic calibration data has not been studied,” but [1] also uses synthetic data for fine-tuning. The authors should clarify the specific differences in their fine-tuning approach (e.g., use of STE, intermediate feature maps for distillation) and provide a more detailed comparison of distillation loss functions and training procedures. (Reviewer 1)
- **Complex pipeline:** The framework combines synthetic data generation, adaptive sampling, and feature-level distillation within QAT, creating a complex pipeline that may hinder practical adoption. The interaction between components is not fully transparent, and the adaptive sampling method’s dependence on the pre-trained model’s internal representations may introduce hard-to-diagnose biases. (Reviewer 2)
- **Synthetic data quality sensitivity:** The method relies heavily on synthetic data quality for calibration, which may introduce variability in performance. The paper lacks a sensitivity analysis of how variations in synthetic data generation parameters (e.g., bounding box size distributions, category sampling probabilities) affect the final quantized model’s accuracy. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited architecture evaluation:** The framework is only tested on YOLOv5 and Mask R-CNN. Broader experimentation across a wider range of architectures (e.g., transformer-based detectors, other single-stage models) is needed to confirm generalizability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential poor handling of rare classes:** The synthetic category distribution may not capture the long-tail distribution of real-world object categories, potentially leading to suboptimal performance on less frequent classes. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of calibration set size analysis:** The paper lacks a systematic study of how calibration set size affects performance at different bit-widths. It is unclear whether a fixed calibration set size is optimal across all bit-widths. (Reviewer 2)
- **Over-reliance on distillation:** The method’s effectiveness depends heavily on feature-level and prediction-matching distillation, which may limit flexibility for scenarios where distillation is not feasible (e.g., when the full-precision model is unavailable) or for future quantization techniques that do not use distillation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing time cost for generation stage:** In Appendix C, only data amount and training process cost are compared; the time consumption of the sample generation stage (first stage) should also be reported. (Reviewer 3)
- **Question about label sampling:** On page 1, classification tasks randomly sample category labels for data synthesis; why can detection tasks not directly take ground-truth labels (location and size) for generation? (Reviewer 3)
