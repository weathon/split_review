## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AudioMorphix, a training-free audio editing method that treats editing as part of a morphing cycle (morphing/demorphing). It uses an off-the-shelf diffusion model, optimizes latents with reference audio, designs energy functions for guidance, and manipulates self-attention features. The method aims at addition, removal, and style transfer. The authors also introduce a new evaluation dataset, AudioSet-E.

### Strengths
- Training-free approach to audio editing is interesting and could open new directions for generative audio editing.
- The concept of reference audio-based audio editing (using another recording to edit a target area) is an interesting and under-explored topic.
- The proposed energy functions for addition and removal are novel contributions.
- The method shows performance improvements over DDIM inversion in the reported objective metrics (e.g., on the new dataset).
- A new dataset/benchmark for audio editing (AudioSet-E) is introduced, which, if publicly released, would be a useful resource for the community.
- The paper provides technical depth in the design of spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) and energy functions, and demonstrates broad applicability across addition, removal, and style transfer.
- The paper includes a good survey of related work and appears to involve rigorous ad hoc trials to achieve the reported objective results.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is very hard to follow. The overall framework is not clearly explained; an overall system diagram connecting all components is missing. Core concepts (e.g., “energy functions,” “addressing audio transparency,” “following empirical findings in the image domain” – with no references) are left undefined. Figures (especially Figure 1) are confusing and difficult to read; the relationship between audio examples and the processing method is unclear. The text contains approximations and expressions that make little sense (e.g., “one pixel correlated with more than one sound track,” “orthogonal directions of reference audio not unique in a manifold,” “z_c and z_r distributed on a sphere,” “sound track being transparent”). “Style transferring” appears in Figure 1 but is never defined in the paper.
- **Missing comparisons with existing methods**: The experimental comparison is insufficient. Only DDIM inversion and DDPM inversion are compared, while several recent zero-shot audio editing methods are not included: [1] Manor & Michaeli (DDPM inversion), [2] Zhang et al. (MusicMagus, cross-attention control for music), [3] MEDIC (Liu et al., disentangled inversion), [4] Ditto (Novack et al.). Reviewer 2 notes that both methodologies and experiments should be compared, especially given the similarity of MusicMagus. Additionally, for removal, the method is not compared with established approaches like target sound extraction or text-queried sound separation.
- **No subjective evaluation**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4, R5) strongly argue that objective metrics (CLAP score, KLD, FAD) alone are insufficient for audio generation/evaluation. Human hearing is the gold standard; listening tests or subjective scoring are necessary to assess editing fidelity, consistency with reference audio, and overall quality. The absence of subjective evaluation makes it hard to gauge the true performance.
- **Dataset and mask details missing**: The construction of the binary mask used for local editing is not explained. Details of AudioSet-E are lacking (e.g., examples of edit instructions, how ground truth is obtained). Reviewer 2 notes that the ground truth audios shown on the demo (e.g., “remove bird song”) do not correspond well to the textual queries. Reviewer 4 requests showcasing a few samples from the benchmark.
- **Demo and code issues**: The demo page loads very few samples even after multiple attempts. No code link is visible (though the authors claim one). The demo shows only input audio and ground truth with a text query, but no reference audio – this is confusing because the proposed editing is reference-audio-based. Reviewer 5 also notes that no reference audio is provided on the demo website, making it difficult to verify the method’s effectiveness.
- **Novelty attribution unclear**: It is not clearly stated which components are novel contributions and which are inspired from prior work. For example, tangent projection and memory bank are not novel; energy functions and SLERP appear to be the original contributions, but they are not clearly delineated. The authors should list their original contributions explicitly in the introduction.
- **SLERP not verified**: The use of spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) is not compared with linear interpolation (LERP) to confirm the assumption that SLERP is more effective.
- **Contradiction between “training-free” and “gradient-based optimization”**: Reviewer 3 questions whether a “training-free framework” that relies on “gradient-based optimization” is a contradiction.
- **Weak experimental analysis**: The analysis of results is superficial. No insights are given on why AudioLDM and Tango perform very differently (Tango better on KL, AudioLDM better on FAD), or why DDIM inversion achieves the highest IS across all tasks. The paper claims “AudioMorphix provides more accurate and realistic image edits” (presumably audio edits), but in the Addition and Removal tasks, Tango performs worse than DDIM and AUDIT in FAD; in the Replace task, AudioLDM performs worse than DDIM in KL. These contradictions are not addressed.
- **Effectiveness of the method**: Several reviewers question the actual editing quality. In the provided examples (Figure 1), addition and removal sometimes also add or remove other content (e.g., in the third addition picture, original content disappears; in the first removal picture, new content appears). This calls into question the basic principles of preserving unedited content and ensuring edited content is valid. Without subjective evaluation and with missing reference audio in the demo, it is difficult to prove the method’s effectiveness.
- **Missing ablation studies**: There is no analysis of the contribution of individual components, such as the usefulness of energy guidance or the memory bank. Reviewer 1 specifically requests such analyses.
- **Unexplained variables and rationales**: Variables like \(\tilde{x}_r^t\) and \(F_t^c\) are not explained. The justification for using CLAP without text input is missing. The rationale for using different metrics on AudioSet-E vs. MusicDelta is not provided.
- **Methodological questions**: Several reviewers raise questions that highlight weaknesses: (a) Does region-specific objective (keeping rest unchanged) ignore global consistency? (b) Is the added/deleted content required to be exactly consistent with reference audio or only semantically consistent? (c) Can simple signal-level mixing (e.g., controlling SNR) achieve addition without generation? (d) Using reference audio is cumbersome; text-based editing would be simpler in practice – what are the application scenarios? (e) Do audio samples from the removal task look less impressive than those from AUDIT on the demo page, casting doubt on the reported objective scores? Reviewers request small-scale listening tests to verify correlation.
- **Grammatical and formatting errors**: The paper contains errors such as “re-sample” → “re-sampling,” “proposed” → “propose,” “capabable” → “capable,” “re-wroten,” “apllied,” “AudioMorhix,” and “In the community of images” instead of “in the computer vision community.” The submitted paper uses an incorrect template (Reviewer 5).

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: Reviewer 2 (rating 5) was more positive than the others (ratings 3,3,3,3). While R2 found the topic interesting and the energy functions effective, the majority of reviewers raised substantial concerns regarding clarity, completeness of comparisons, lack of subjective evaluation, and questionable effectiveness, leading to overall weak assessments (mostly score 3).
