## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Concept-Enhanced Feedback Disentanglement (CFD) for out-of-distribution (OOD) molecular representation learning. CFD uses two variational encoders to decompose features into distribution-agnostic (invariant) and spurious components, then fuses molecule-aware concepts (capturing critical substructures) into the invariant features. An iterative feedback mechanism progressively refines the disentanglement over multiple steps, and a self-supervised objective improves the encoders’ disentangling capability. Experiments on GOOD and DrugOOD datasets show superior OOD performance over state-of-the-art baselines, including on unseen tasks. Reviewers find the method novel and effective but raise concerns about missing ablations, insufficient theoretical grounding, limited chemical interpretability, unclear design choices, and high computational complexity.

### Strengths
- **Novel iterative feedback mechanism**: Progressively refines disentangled representations across multiple iterations, which is a novel application in molecular representation learning and overcomes limitations of one-step methods (Reviewer 1).
- **Integration of concept mining**: Focuses on invariant substructures, enhancing both generalization and interpretability; a thoughtful combination of disentanglement and concept-aware enhancement (Reviewer 1).
- **Extensive evaluation**: Experiments on multiple real-world datasets (GOOD, DrugOOD) demonstrate robustness and generalizability; strong results on both classification and regression tasks (Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive ablation and hyperparameter analysis**: Includes ample ablation studies showing effectiveness of each module, and explores impact of hyperparameters (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing clarity**: Paper is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).
- **Superior OOD performance**: Method performs well on OOD prediction tasks; significant gains over multiple baselines (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient analysis of feedback mechanism**: No ablation comparing different iteration counts or a one-step/non-feedback approach to quantify the contribution of the iterative process (Reviewer 1). Also unclear whether concatenating features across steps adds redundancy; the multi-step may still retain spurious correlations. A comparison using only features from the final step is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing cross-domain/task generalization**: Not evaluated on other molecular tasks (e.g., drug toxicity, protein-ligand binding affinity) that require different structural knowledge (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear design choices and missing details**:
  - How are μ_c and σ_c of the concept learned? What is the motivation for the residual term that assigns substructure features to concepts? (Reviewer 2)
  - The invariance in Eq.1 lacks association with the methodology; how is the environment e in Eq.1 obtained? Does it require spurious labels? (Reviewer 2)
  - Does “concept” represent the environment from Eq.1? Are f(·) in Eq.1 and φ(·) in Line 305 the same? (Reviewer 2)
  - The meaning of “concepts that do not specialize to one particular type or class of molecule” (Line 251-252) is ambiguous (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear role of contrastive loss**: The contrastive loss only enforces similarity between the GNN latent vector and the general latent vectors, but there is no direct link to downstream tasks—how does it ensure the general embedding is useful? (Reviewer 3)
- **Choice of KAN not fully justified**: Why KAN over conventional MLPs? Claim of “learnable activation functions” needs specific rationale (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of computational cost analysis**: The iterative model is complex; no analysis of training/inference computational power required (Reviewer 3). Dimensional growth from concatenating features each step introduces high redundancy (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited chemical interpretability**: Learned concepts are not tied to known molecular features (polarity, hydrogen bonding, functional groups); method could be applied to any graph OOD problem, limiting its insight for chemists (Reviewer 4).
- **Concept mining resembles iMoLD**: The concept mining appears similar to Residual Vector Quantization in iMoLD; distinction is not explained (Reviewer 4).
- **Lacks theoretical support**: No proof (analogous to MoleOOD’s Theorem 1 and 2) that minimizing the loss function helps acquire invariant features with sufficient predictive power across environments (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing visualizations/decoder analysis**: No experiments (e.g., visualizations) showing concept importance across iterations, or decoder analysis to check if concepts align with known scaffolds (Reviewer 3).
- **Poor baseline adaptation**: No explanation why baselines CIGA, DisC, and CAL fail on GOOD-ZINC and GOOD-PCBA (Reviewer 4).
- **Under-explored hyperparameter sensitivity**: No ablation on sensitivity of the method to variations in K (number of concepts?) (Reviewer 2).
