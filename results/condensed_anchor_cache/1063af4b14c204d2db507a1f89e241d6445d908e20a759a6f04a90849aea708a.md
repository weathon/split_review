## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach for verifying the robustness of Binary Neural Networks (BNNs) using Semidefinite Programming (SDP) relaxations derived from sparse Polynomial Optimization. The method encodes the verification problem as a polynomial optimization problem (POP) and applies SDP relaxations to obtain lower bounds for certifying robustness against adversarial attacks (both ℓ∞ and ℓ2). Compared to traditional MILP–based verification, the SDP-based method achieves tighter bounds (up to 55% tighter) and significant speedups (average 4.5–11.4×, up to 50× under severe attacks) while handling continuous input spaces without quantization. All four reviewers agree the problem is important and the approach is novel, but they raise concerns about experimental scope, fairness of comparisons, theoretical clarity, and missing evaluations on more complex datasets and architectures.

### Strengths

- **Novel use of SDP for BNN verification**: Employing sparse polynomial optimization and SDP relaxations is an innovative approach that goes beyond simple linear relaxations and exploits structural properties of BNNs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Significantly improved bounds**: The SDP relaxations provide strictly tighter bounds (up to 55% over traditional LP relaxations), proven theoretically in Thm. 3.1 and Thm. 4.1 (Reviewers 2, 4, and Reviewer 3 agrees on more precise lower bounds).
- **Efficient computation**: Experimental results demonstrate considerable speedups over MILP-based methods (average 4.5–11.4×, up to 50× in severe attack scenarios), particularly for high-dimensional BNNs and large input perturbations (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Broad norm compatibility**: The method accommodates both ℓ∞ and ℓ2 adversarial attacks, expanding applicability; ℓ2 robustness was not previously considered in BNN verification (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Handles continuous input spaces**: The approach avoids input quantization and mitigates numerical issues associated with floating-point calculations (Reviewer 1; but see weaknesses on the floating-point claim).
- **Deep theoretical analysis**: The theoretical contributions (Thm. 3.1 and 4.1) prove the advantage of the more complex verification algorithm and the use of tighter first-order SDP relaxations (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited experimental scope – datasets and architectures**: Experiments are performed on only two models (Reviewer 1) and primarily on MNIST-based networks, which are relatively simple and shallow. It is unclear how the method scales to more complex datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, ImageNet) or deeper BNN architectures, where the accumulation of approximation errors in the SDP relaxation could become more pronounced (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 3 (score 5) further questions the persuasiveness of such limited evaluations.
- **Unfair or incomplete comparison with MILP**: The MILP solver is allowed to terminate as soon as a feasible solution is found, while the SDP solver is run to optimality, making it difficult to assess true comparative performance (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the paper does not state whether Gurobi runtimes use multi-threading or single-threading, further complicating efficiency comparisons (Reviewer 3). MILP methods aim to prove whether a property holds or fails, whereas the proposed method estimates bounds; comparison should ideally focus on verification success rates and scalability, not solely on bound tightness (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparison with state-of-the-art BNN verification techniques**: The paper compares only with LP and MILP baselines. There is no comparison with SMT-based approaches (e.g., Amir et al.) or other recent relaxation-based methods, which would help contextualize advantages and limitations (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 4 also questions whether the compared approaches are taken from the literature or arbitrarily constructed.
- **Theoretical exposition is hard to follow**: Several crucial details are missing or presented out of order: (i) The derivation from the subgradient of ReLU (Chen et al., 2020) to encoding the sign function is not justified; the exact equations from the reference should be stated (Reviewer 4). (ii) The output of `sign(0)` is not defined, and the weight range is inconsistently given as `{-1,0,1}` in (4) but `{-1,1}` later in Sec. 3, affecting normalization bounds (Reviewer 4). (iii) Key concepts like “cliques” (how determined), the variable `d` (relaxation order), and the operator `nv(A)` are introduced after their first use, making the text hard to parse (Reviewer 4). (iv) Steps to derive constraints (e.g., from (7) to (17)) are too quick and need more explanation (Reviewer 4).
- **Motivation for the approach is not fully convincing**: The paper claims existing methods are incompatible with continuous input data or limited to ℓ∞ perturbations, but ℓ∞ is the primary standard and most BNN verifiers already support continuous input spaces. Moreover, for methods designed for discrete input regions, one can treat the second layer as the new input layer to verify continuous inputs (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of verification performance metrics**: Table 3 reports bound tightness but does not show how often these bounds actually lead to verified robustness (i.e., proving the property holds). It is unclear if tighter bounds translate into more instances being verified (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing statistical rigor in experiments**: Results are reported as single values without indicating the number of instances averaged or standard deviation; this makes it impossible to assess variability (Reviewer 4).
- **Unsupported claims about floating-point inaccuracies**: The abstract and contribution section claim the method mitigates floating-point numerical issues, but this is not supported by any analysis or experiments; the term “floating-point” appears only twice and without justification (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also asks why the approach does not suffer from floating-point inaccuracies.
- **No thorough runtime analysis**: The paper claims the approach does not suffer from exponential running time (Sec. 6) but provides only average speedup factors, not a formal complexity analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing repeatability package**: No code or data is provided to reproduce the results (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Parenthetical data in Tables 1 and 2 (column `τ_{tighter,cs}^1`) is not explained (Reviewer 3); Fig. 2 lacks axis labels and ticks (Reviewer 4); the running example (Fig. 1) is introduced late and not used consistently (Reviewer 4); it should be stated more clearly that only the classification setting is considered (Reviewer 4).
