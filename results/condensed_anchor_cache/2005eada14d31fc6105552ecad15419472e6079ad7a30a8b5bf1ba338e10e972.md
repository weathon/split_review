## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies gradient methods for functions satisfying the \((L_0,L_1)\)-smoothness property, a generalized smoothness class motivated by deep learning. It establishes a parallelism with classical \(L\)-smooth optimization, deriving tighter first-order upper and lower bounds (Lemmas 2.2, 2.3) and using them to analyze fixed-stepsize GD, normalized stepsize methods, Polyak stepsizes, and Nesterov-type acceleration for convex and nonconvex cases. For nonconvex problems the rate matches the best known; for convex problems the authors obtain improved rates: \(O(L_0R^2/\varepsilon + L_1^2R^2)\) for GD and accelerated methods, and rates for normalized/Polyak stepsizes that do not require knowledge of \(L_0,L_1\). The paper is well-written, thorough, and provides a useful reference for the \((L_0,L_1)\)-smooth class.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly demonstrates a parallelism between classical \(L\)-smooth theory and the \((L_0,L_1)\)-smooth setting, including a cocoercivity inequality and algorithm design strategies; this perspective has not been presented before with this level of clarity.
- The derivation of tighter first-order upper and lower bounds (e.g., Lemmas 2.2, 2.3) is a key contribution that should aid future work on this function class.
- Improved convergence rates for convex problems: \(O(L_0R^2/\varepsilon + L_1^2R^2)\) for fixed-stepsize GD, and an accelerated rate \(O(\sqrt{L_0R^2/\varepsilon} + L_1^2R^2)\).
- The normalized gradient method and the gradient method with Polyak stepsizes achieve the same rate without requiring knowledge of \(L_0\) or \(L_1\).
- For convex cases, Theorems 3.2 and 5.1 dispense with the \(L\)-gradient Lipschitz assumption that was needed in prior work (Koloskova et al. 2023, Hubler et al. 2024), while improving the rates.
- The comparison with Gorbunov et al. (2024) is comprehensive and valuable for readers.
- The explicit optimal step size (eq. (11)) is practically useful.
- The paper is generally well-structured and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation issues** (multiple reviewers): Many specific problems need correction:
  - Lemma 2.2: uses \(\phi(t)=e^t-t-1\) while the appendix uses \(\phi\) for something else; notation should be consistent.
  - Lemma 2.3: the bound \(\phi_*(\gamma)\ge \frac{\gamma^2}{2+\gamma}\) appears abruptly; the relevant discussion should be placed nearby.
  - Pg. 4, lines 164–165: the sentence “we present some examples to support the choice of generalized smooth assumption” is unclear.
  - Proposition 2.6 and the subsequent discussion seem tangential; could be moved to appendix. Also, “\(M\)-Lipschitz twice continuously differentiable” is used without definition.
  - Line 183–184: a dangling “for some” after a period.
  - Theorem 3.1: it should be assumed that \(f^*:=\inf f>-\infty\); also missing assumptions about existence of a minimizer \(x^*\) in convex-case theorems.
  - Theorem 3.2: it is unclear whether \(\|x_0-x^*\|\) is denoted as \(R_0\); notation inconsistent across theorems (also used as \(R\) in Theorem 4.2 without definition).
  - Eq. (20) and eq. (43): exponent 2 is missing in \(R_{k+1}^2\) and \(g_k^2\) factors seem missing; the explanation of the derivation (lines 297–298) is inadequate.
  - Discussion on complexity (lines 304–310): does not clearly connect the \(O(L_1^2R_0^2)\) term to the phase where the function behaves like a smooth one; contrast with Section 6.
  - Lemma 4.1 is introduced without explaining how (22) is used in the analysis.
  - Lines 349–350: the claim “time-varying stepsizes can be eliminated” is a conjecture, not proved; should be reworded.
  - Unclear why the no-exponential-dependency statement (lines 378–380, 483) is worth emphasis; no prior work giving \(\exp(L_0),\exp(L_1)\) rates for the same setup is cited.
  - The rate comparison in lines 400–401 (“Our rate is better than \(O(L_0R^2/\varepsilon + \sqrt{L/\varepsilon}L_1R^2)\)”) does not specify the source of the compared rate.
  - Sections 4–6 should clearly state at the start that they assume convex functions.
  - Algorithm 1 is typeset as an algorithm environment, while the authors’ own procedure (lines 427–431) is not.
  - Step 1 of the accelerated method (Section 6) requires a point \(\bar{x}\) with \(f(\bar{x})-f(x^*)\le \frac{L_0}{8L_1^2}\); this needs knowledge of \(f(x^*)\) or \(R\) to stop—a practical limitation that should be explicitly explained.
  - Theorem 6.1: missing convexity assumption; also inconsistent notation: uses \(x_0\) while the text uses \(\bar{x}\).
  - Lines 477–478: two occurrences of \(R\) may refer to different quantities (\(\|x_0-x^*\|\) vs. \(\|\bar{x}-x^*\|\)).
  - Figure labels (NGD, GD1, GD2) are not explained in the caption; Figure 2 should specify that \(L_0 = \left(\frac{p-2}{L_1}\right)^{p-2}\) per Example 2.4.
  - Additional errors noted by Reviewer 3: line 257 “absolte” → “absolute”; eq. (14) should be \(\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2L_0+3L_1\|\nabla f(x)\|}\) (not 2 in numerator per Lemma B.1); line 641: \(s\) should be defined as \(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\), otherwise sign in inequality at 643 is wrong; line 648: \(\phi^*(\gamma)\) should be \(\phi_*(\gamma)\) for consistency; line 651: missing gradients of \(y\).

- **Limited numerical experiments** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4): Experiments are only on the simple function \(\|x\|^p/p\). The paper would benefit from more diverse and complex examples, including:
  - Nonconvex examples from deep learning (e.g., loss functions from neural networks).
  - Real-world datasets.
  - The softmax function, where \(L_0,L_1\) can be analytically evaluated (suggested by Reviewer 3).
  These would better demonstrate practical implications and validate the theoretical findings.

- **Step-size dependency on \(L_0,L_1\) in Theorem 3.1** (Reviewer 3): The step-size depends on these constants, whereas Hubler et al. 2024 gives a constant-free method. This limits applicability when the constants are unknown. Could backtracking line search replace this dependency? (Reviewer 3 question). The authors should discuss this.

- **Tighter bounds’ practical impact not demonstrated** (Reviewers 2, 4): The authors claim that the bounds in Lemma 2.2 are tighter than previous ones, but no numerical example or theoretical argument shows how this tightness improves the final convergence rates (e.g., constant factors). Reviewer 4 asks whether the tighter bounds lead to a strictly better rate or only simplify derivation of optimal step sizes. This ambiguity reduces the perceived contribution.

- **Accelerated method (AGMsDR) limitations** (Reviewer 4):
  - The two-stage approach requires knowledge of \(L_0\) and \(L_1\) (or at least their ratio and upper bounds) to determine the number of steps in the first stage, whereas standard GD only needs one paramater \(L\) satisfying \(L\ge L_0, L\ge L_1\). This makes the accelerated method less practical than the non-accelerated ones.
  - The method also requires a line search (minor).
  - **Critical concern** (Reviewer 4): The acceleration is applied only after the first stage reaches a region where \(\|\nabla f(x)\|\le L_0/L_1\), which makes the right-hand side of the smoothness condition at most \(2L_0\)—i.e., the function becomes standard \(2L_0\)-smooth. Thus the acceleration essentially solves a standard smooth problem, and the term \(L_1^2R^2\) merely counts iterations to enter that region. Acceleration does not help to enter the region faster. Reviewer 4 therefore views this part as a less novel contribution—its main value is the bound on oracle calls to enter the bounded-gradient region. **Minority contrast**: Reviewers 1,2,3 did not raise this issue; Reviewer 4 is more critical of the accelerated method’s novelty.

- **Missing analysis for the stochastic case** (Reviewer 4). This limits practical value.

- **Proposition 2.6** (Reviewers 1,2): The results are predictable and the proofs straightforward. Consider moving to appendix.

- **Missing discussion of challenges** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not explain the key difficulties in analyzing \((L_0,L_1)\)-smooth functions compared to \(L\)-smooth functions, nor does it highlight any novel theoretical tools or techniques developed to overcome them.

- **Closure under sum** (Reviewer 2): The authors note that the class is not guaranteed closed under standard operations like summation. A concrete counterexample showing that the sum of two \((L_0,L_1)\)-smooth functions is not necessarily \((L_0,L_1)\)-smooth should be provided; also additional conditions that would guarantee closure.

- **Reproducibility of notation**: Inconsistent symbols (e.g., \(R\) vs \(R_0\), Theorem 6.1 notation) need harmonization.

- **Incorrect wording** (Reviewer 3): Line 269 “the smallest number K of iterations required to achieve …” is incorrect because one could start with \(\|\nabla f(x_0)\|<\varepsilon\) (K=0). Should read “obtaining a stationary point requires at most …”.

- **Theorem presentation inconsistency** (Reviewer 3): Theorem 5.1 should be presented in the form “\(K\ge\)” to match other theorems.
