## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Physiome-ODE, a benchmark for irregularly sampled multivariate time series (IMTS) forecasting, generated from 50 biological ODE models from the Physiome Model Repository. A new metric, Joint Gradient Deviation (JGD), is proposed to measure dataset complexity and select challenging instances via rejection sampling. The authors evaluate several IMTS forecasting models (including ODE-based and non-ODE methods) and show that, unlike on existing small benchmarks, ODE-based models can display their strengths. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) finds the benchmark a significant contribution; the other three reviewers (scores 3, confidence 3–4) raise substantial concerns about methodology, missing baselines, and clarity of the data generation.

### Strengths
- **Novel benchmark for IMTS forecasting**: Physiome-ODE provides a larger and more diverse set of 50 datasets derived from real biological ODEs, filling a gap where only four datasets were commonly used. (All reviewers)
- **Use of JGD for dataset complexity**: The introduction of Joint Gradient Deviation (JGD) as a metric to measure gradient variance and dataset difficulty is creative and well-justified, helping to select challenging instances. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3) Reviewer 4 notes that JGD correlates with the lowest prediction error among tested models, which is a useful property.
- **Broad and biologically relevant data generation**: The benchmark is derived from ODEs that are inherently multivariate and irregularly sampled, making it relevant for healthcare and biology. (Reviewer 1)
- **Comprehensive evaluation revealing model diversity**: The evaluation shows that no single model is best across all datasets, which is useful for understanding strengths/weaknesses of existing methods. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- **Wider empirical foundation**: Experiments on 50 datasets allow assessment of model performance variability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Code and metric provided**: The authors provide code to test existing methods and introduce a new complexity metric. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear data generation process and ad-hoc choices**: The integration of observation noise is not clearly explained: Eq. (2) does not connect to Eq. (3), and noise is added later without explicit formulation. The paper would benefit from a stochastic differential equation (SDE) framework. (Reviewer 2) The parameter noise and initial condition selection are ad-hoc; a common σ_const is used and non-sensical trajectories (e.g., explosions) are simply dropped, potentially invalidating the biological meaning of the ODEs. Expert knowledge about parameter constraints, positivity, order, and logarithmic scales is not systematically applied. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing baseline methods**: The selection of baselines lacks rigor – simple statistical models (linear regression, ARIMA) and more complex models (neural SDEs, TimeMixer, TimesNet) are absent. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3) The study focuses only on ODE-based models and overlooks recent advances like TimeMixer and TimesNet, limiting the breadth of comparison. (Reviewer 3)
- **Theoretical and empirical gaps for JGD**: 
  - The claim that JGD scales super-exponentially with the Lipschitz constant is not supported by empirical data or an example. (Reviewer 2)
  - The theoretical explanation of JGD is excessively mathematical without sufficient intuitive explanation, making it hard to assess reliability. (Reviewer 2)
  - No comparison to other known complexity metrics (variance, entropy) is provided. (Reviewer 2)
  - The metric assumes faster oscillatory processes are less predictable, but this may miss systems like the Lorenz system that change regimes suddenly but evolve smoothly between. (Reviewer 4)
  - The correlation between JGD and prediction error could be spurious. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing sensitivity analysis**: There is no sensitivity analysis on the numerical solver or noise parameterization, calling into question robustness under different experimental conditions. (Reviewer 2) The impact of data stationarity is not considered. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear training/evaluation protocol**: The paper says models predict the last 50% after observing the first 50%, but it is unclear how ODE models were trained – e.g., whether loss was computed on long sequences or on single-step ahead prediction. If the latter, methods may not have had a fair chance to learn constant fallback, yet the constant model performs excellently, so this needs clarification. (Reviewer 4) The 5-fold cross-validation may not be sufficiently representative and could introduce bias. (Reviewer 3)
- **Semi-synthetic nature limits interpretability**: The benchmark is generated from ODE models rather than real patient data, which limits direct clinical relevance. Overfitting to specific ODE dynamics is a concern, and closeness to real biological processes is not measured. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of clear differentiation from existing benchmarks**: The added value over existing IMTS benchmarks is not demonstrated with a quantitative comparison (e.g., using common evaluation metrics, distribution of time series characteristics). (Reviewer 1) Contribution is not clearly articulated compared to other time series forecasting benchmarks. (Reviewer 3)
- **JGD optimization lacks theoretical guarantees**: Existence and uniqueness of the maximum in Eq. (12) are not discussed, and bounds on the spread parameter are not given. (Reviewer 2) Lemmas 1 and 2 (Appendix B) use finite-sample approximations without stating assumptions on function continuity and differentiability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Selection process may miss subtle variations**: The majority strategy for maximizing JGD could filter out ODE instances with clinically important but low-gradient variations, limiting applicability to personalized medicine. (Reviewer 1)
- **Computational intensity**: Generating Physiome-ODE is computationally intensive, but the paper lacks an analysis of runtime and memory requirements on typical hardware, which would help assess usability. (Reviewer 1) (Reviewer 4 also questions feasibility.)
- **Minor**: Calling ODE constants “parameters” is more standard, or at least mention both terms. (Reviewer 4) Also, the scaling of time (e.g., seconds vs. hours) is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)

**Reviewer Disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) is substantially more positive about the contribution and the soundness of the benchmark, while the other three (scores 3) are critical of data generation choices, missing baselines, and lack of clarity. The positive reviewer sees Physiome-ODE as an important step forward; the critical reviewers view the methodology as ad-hoc and the evaluation incomplete.
