## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ESCAPE, a rotation-equivariant shape completion framework. It selects high-curvature anchor points, encodes input points as distances to those anchors, and uses a transformer (modified AdaPointTr) operating in distance space to output distances of completed points. An optimization solves for Cartesian coordinates. The approach aims to be robust to arbitrary rotations without pose estimation or rotation augmentation.

### Strengths
- **Significance**: Point cloud completion under unknown orientation is an impactful and challenging problem [R1, R3].
- **Novel representation**: The distance-to-anchor encoding is a straightforward way to make non-equivariant architectures equivariant, and is easy to implement [R1, R2, R3, R4]. One reviewer finds this the main strength [R4].
- **Robustness results**: The method shows robustness to arbitrary rotations without requiring rotation augmentation during training [R1, R2, R3]. Experiments highlight the limitations of non-equivariant methods under random rotations and the benefits of ESCAPE [R2].
- **Anchor selection**: The high-curvature anchor point selection is a novel contribution [R1, R3].
- **Adaptation of AdaPointTr**: The paper introduces a rotation-invariant adaptation of AdaPointTr [R3].
- **Improved cases**: In some cases, ESCAPE achieves better shape completions than compared methods [R1].

### Weaknesses
- **Exaggerated claim and missing prior work**: The claim of being “first end-to-end rotation-equivariant shape completion” is inaccurate. Several prior works exist:
  - *SO(3) Rotation Equivariant Point Cloud Completion using Attention-based Vector Neurons* (Wu & Miao, 3DV 2022)
  - *SE(3)-Equivariant Attention Networks for Shape Reconstruction in Function Space* (ICLR 2023)
  - *3D Equivariant Graph Implicit Functions* (ECCV 2022)
  - *SCARP: 3D Shape Completion in Arbitrary Poses for Improved Grasping* (ICRA 2023)
  - *Vector Neurons: A General Framework for SO(3)-Equivariant Networks* (Deng et al., ICCV 2021)
  - *Frame Averaging for Equivariant Shape Space Learning* (Atzmon et al., CVPR 2022)

  These should be discussed in related work and compared. One reviewer (score 5) is more positive but still notes the omission [R1, R2, R3]. The Vector Neurons paper is mentioned in the ablation but not in related work or results [R3].

- **Unfair baseline comparisons**: Non-equivariant methods were trained *without* rotation augmentations, which is a standard alternative to equivariant networks. Results comparing with baselines trained *with* augmentations are missing [R1, R2, R3]. Also missing: comparison with PCA canonicalization of rotated inputs [R1] and with noisy versions of point clouds [R1].

- **Worse on oriented inputs**: The method performs worse than reference methods (e.g., AdaPointTr) on canonical (non-rotated) inputs [R4].

- **Anchor selection issues**:
  - Motivation for curvature over random sampling (e.g., FPS) is not justified. No ablation comparing high-curvature anchors vs. random FPS anchors [R1, R3].
  - In partial point clouds with large missing regions (e.g., half an airplane), no anchor points may be chosen near the missing part, likely causing blurry completion [R3].
  - The paper describes anchor selection as “Clustering” (Section 3.1), but results show “Ball Query” performs best. The actual method used is unclear [R3].
  - Due to randomness in FPS, the anchor set can differ across runs, breaking strict equivariance. The method is not provably equivariant [R3].

- **Insufficient architectural and reproducibility details**:
  - The transformer architecture is not self-contained; readers must refer to AdaPoinTr and FoldNet papers [R1, R2, R4]. A clear description of the exact changes (self-attention, DGCNN alterations, final point count *m*) is missing [R1, R4].
  - No code provided [R1].

- **Notation and presentation problems**:
  - Chamfer Distance (CD) is defined between point clouds (line 309) but later used with distance inputs (line 314) [R1].
  - Variable *m* is used for both points in a cluster and total completed points [R1].
  - Content exceeds the 10-page limit (11 pages) [R2].
  - Table captions should be on top [R4].
  - Equation numbers are missing [R4].
  - Missing citations: *Shape Distributions* (Osada), *Flex Convolution* [R4].

- **Correctness and consistency concerns**:
  - The optimization problem (Eq. 1) may have non-unique solutions; its relation to anchor selection and partial completeness is unaddressed [R1].
  - The paper does not specify whether normal vectors are required for the Laplacian curvature computation [R3].
  - Inconsistent results: Table 1 shows identical CD values for normal and rotated inputs (unexpected given partial equivariance), but Table 3 shows differences [R3].
  - Table 4 reports identical results for DGCNN across conditions; dataset and metric are unspecified [R3].

- **Limited evaluation scope**: The representation is not tested on other tasks (e.g., classification) to demonstrate its generality [R3]. The time for anchor selection and optimization relative to the forward pass is not reported [R1, R4].

- **Missing experimental details**: Exact point cloud resolution (*N*), number of anchor points (*M*), training time, hardware, memory, and inference time are not provided [R4].

**Minority positive contrast**: One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) found the invariant encoding and experimental demonstration of benefits to be strong contributions, while three other reviewers (scores 3) raised substantial concerns about novelty, comparisons, and presentation.
