## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LVLM-Count, a training-free, divide-and-conquer pipeline to improve object counting in large vision-language models (LVLMs). The pipeline consists of four stages: area detection, target segmentation, object-aware division, and counting aggregation. It is designed to handle high object counts and overlapping objects without additional fine-tuning, and claims good generalization to unseen datasets. A new Emoji-Count benchmark is introduced. Reviewer opinions diverge: two reviewers (R2, R4) are positive (scores 6) but with concerns, one (R1) is moderate (score 5), and one (R3) is notably negative (score 3), questioning novelty, experimental depth, and broader baselines.

### Strengths
- **Training-free and flexible**: The method enhances LVLMs without additional training or fine-tuning, making it adaptable to new datasets and scenarios where labeled data is scarce (R1).  
- **Creative divide-and-conquer approach**: Effectively manages complexity in densely populated scenes with high object overlap (R1, R4).  
- **Simple and intuitive pipeline**: Clear presentation with well-drawn figures (R3).  
- **Strong reported performance**: Obtained results are strong across several benchmarks (R2, R3).  
- **Novel object-aware division**: The method for dividing images without cutting through objects of interest is a meaningful contribution for LVLM visual perception (R4).  
- **Emoji-Count benchmark**: While generation is not complex, it provides a useful testbed for evaluating counting ability; the benchmark is also considered interesting (R3, R4).  
- R3 acknowledged good performance margins, but noted the benchmark’s simplicity; R4 found the benchmark very interesting. Overall, the strengths are recognized despite disagreement on novelty.

### Weaknesses
- **Dependency on initial stages**: Performance heavily relies on the accuracy of object detection and instance segmentation (e.g., GroundingDINO, SAM); failures in these stages (e.g., due to occlusion or camouflage) can cascade and degrade overall counting (R1).  
- **Limited comparisons and baselines**:  
  - Comparisons with other state-of-the-art counting methods are insufficient (R1).  
  - Only one LVLM (GPT-4o) is used; experiments should include more LVLMs (e.g., LLaVA series, Gemini) to demonstrate generalizability (R4).  
  - Missing baseline comparisons suggested include:  
    (i) LLM + GroundingDINO: counting masks directly after segmentation (to validate the importance of region division) (R3).  
    (ii) Passing SAM masks to GPT-4o for counting (direct comparison with the proposed object-aware division) (R3).  
    (iii) Comparison with the divide-and-conquer strategy from Xiong et al. 2019 (Ref 1) to avoid repeated counting (R3).  
- **Ablation study gaps**:  
  - No ablation of each pipeline stage (e.g., including/excluding the target segmentation stage) (R1).  
  - Effect of using super-resolution on sub-regions is not examined (R3).  
  - Performance gain from object-aware division versus a naive division strategy is unclear (R4).  
  - Impact of the area detection module on final accuracy is not isolated (R4).  
- **Novelty concerns** (noted by R3, score 3): The divide-and-conquer idea is standard for counting problems (summing counts over regions) and has appeared in prior work (e.g., Xiong et al. 2019, “From open set to closed set: Counting objects by spatial divide-and-conquer”). As a result, the full pipeline is not sufficiently novel and could be seen as a baseline design for using LVLMs for counting. (R2 and R4 did not share this strong novelty concern.)  
- **Inconsistent handling across datasets**: Paper states (lines 241-242) that different approaches are used for different datasets, which contradicts the claim of a single generalizable method (R2).  
- **Unclear definitions**:  
  - What constitutes “simple” vs. “complex” counting questions is not explained (R2, line 349).  
  - The scope of “large number of objects” (tens, hundreds, thousands?) is not defined (R3).  
  - The specific LLM used in the pipeline is not clarified (R3).  
- **Lack of real-world application examples**: The introduction mentions industry, healthcare, environmental monitoring, but no real-world examples are tested or demonstrated (R2).  
- **Efficiency missing**: No analysis of training or inference time, despite using multiple pretrained models (LLMs, GroundingDINO, GPT-4o, Real-ESRGAN) (R3).  
- **Insufficient qualitative results**: Only one dataset is shown; more qualitative examples (e.g., specific images from Emoji-Count: image_00001.png, image_00005.png, etc.) are needed (R4). The illustrative examples in Figs. 3–5 lack challenging counting scenarios, and simply counting masks from GroundingDINO might suffice (R3).  
- **Metrics need expansion**: Current metrics (e.g., EA) evaluate approximate counts; additional metrics such as Accuracy with tolerances (Acc_{+-0}, Acc_{+-3}, Acc_{+-5}, Acc_{+-10}) are recommended to capture exact- vs. approximate-count performance (R4).  
- **Prompting and response guarantees**: The method must ensure the LVLM focuses only on counting and does not generate irrelevant responses; the prompting strategy to enforce this is not detailed (R4).  
- **Additional dataset suggestion**: Evaluation on PASCAL VOC would provide further diversity (R3).  
- **Scalability limits**: The upper bound on the number of objects (e.g., 500, 1000) is not investigated; it is unclear how the method scales (R4).
