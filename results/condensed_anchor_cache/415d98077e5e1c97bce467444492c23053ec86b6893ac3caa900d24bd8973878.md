## Merged Review

### Summary

DensBO dynamically ensembles four surrogate models (GP, RF, ET, GB) during Bayesian optimisation by maintaining a weighted ensemble; weights are set to 1 for the model with lowest MSE on the most recently sampled point and updated via an exponential moving average with smoothing factor α.  The method is evaluated on YAHPO Gym and JAHS-Bench-201 (859 instances) using a batch update every 8 iterations (as in HEBO/SMAC).  A key finding is that α=1 (model selection) often outperforms weighted ensembling.  One reviewer (score 6) found the paper positive, while two reviewers (scores 3) and one (score 5) raised substantial concerns about evaluation, missing baselines, and experimental design.

### Strengths

*   The paper is well‑written, clearly communicating the method and the intuition behind dynamic surrogate selection.
*   The empirical evaluation is extensive, covering over 800 optimisation problems, and the codebase is documented, making results appear replicable.
*   The dynamic behaviour is demonstrated: RF is preferred early (few observations) and GB later, matching expected scaling properties.
*   The ablation of the smoothing factor α is insightful; α=1 (model selection) is often superior to weighted ensembling, a finding the community should note.
*   The method is robust to the hyperparameter α when set to a high value.
*   DensBO consistently outperforms both individual surrogate models and a static equal‑weighted ensemble across many benchmarks.
*   The paper shows results at different budgets, honestly noting that GP surpasses DensBO in very low budgets because of ensembling overhead.

### Weaknesses

*   **Flawed evaluation metric and missing absolute differences.**  The primary ranking metric is biased because the surrogate set contains clusters (five ENS variants, three tree‑based, one GP), inflating ranks against the single GP.  Absolute performance deltas are not shown; initial strong GP performance may make later gains from DensBO appear small.
*   **Missing essential baselines.**  The paper omits: (i) a simple baseline using GP for continuous‑only spaces and RF otherwise; (ii) an oracle “best single surrogate per problem” baseline and a baseline selecting one surrogate based on MSE on initial points; (iii) comparisons to static ensembling with carefully selected weights and to prior dynamic ensembling methods for BO [1,2,3]; (iv) the model‑selection approach of [6] (inner‑loop BO for surrogate choice), especially relevant because α=1 is best.  The authors claimed the GP‑or‑RF baseline was infeasible yet possess results for GP and RF on all problems – a red flag.
*   **Non‑standard experimental setup.**  Models are updated every 8 iterations (batch strategy) without justification; the paper does not acknowledge that results may apply only to parallel BO, nor does it explore sensitivity to this batch size.
*   **Poorly motivated weight‑update mechanism.**  Weights are set according to MSE on the most recently sampled point rather than the full dataset; the effect of using the full dataset is not ablated.  The choice of three tree‑based models (RF, ET, GB) instead of only RF and GP, the weight initialisation, and the oracle weighting are not ablated.
*   **Missing epistemic uncertainty in ensemble variance.**  Equation 6 averages individual standard deviations but omits the epistemic uncertainty (model disagreement) term derived in [10]; the impact of this omission is not discussed.
*   **Related work and contextual gaps.**  Meta‑learning approaches for BO and the ensembling methods from the NeurIPS BBO challenge (2nd place, warm‑start winner) are not discussed.  The claim that GPs fail above 20 dimensions relies on old references and is disputed by recent works [1,2].  The statement that this is the first dynamic ensemble for HPO should be moderated in light of [6].
*   **Insufficient technical details.**  Handling of categorical features with scikit‑learn tree models is not explained, nor is the computation of uncertainty for GB.  The impact of the number of initial points is not ablated.
*   **HEBO baseline conflates multiple components.**  The single‑GP baseline uses HEBO’s fully Bayesian GP hyperparameters and MACE acquisition ensemble; the contribution of the inherent model ensembling within HEBO should be decoupled.
*   **Lack of log‑scale regret plots.**  Log regret would better illustrate performance differences, especially at later stages.
*   **Multiple minor presentation issues.**  Missing capitalisations, incorrect or missing citations (e.g., for Adam, origination papers for BO, MACE reference), extraneous colons, missing full stops in equations, unclear notation in Algorithm 1 (μ and σ as vectors, λ_t not defined as vector), missing specification of initial weights and number of random trials in Figure 2 caption.
