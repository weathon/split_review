## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper integrates Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) into the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering pipeline, replacing MLPs for anisotropic color modeling, and introduces a phase regularization technique that progressively optimizes training from low to high frequencies. The approach builds on Scaffold-GS, Spec-Gaussian, and Fre-GS. Evaluations on real-world unbounded scenes (Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples, DeepBlending) show improved rendering quality, but results are not consistently superior to Spec-Gaussian in terms of PSNR, and several methodological concerns remain.

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of KAN into 3DGS (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).** Using KAN for anisotropic color modeling is innovative and may theoretically benefit from the locality property of B-Splines for capturing details. Reviewers 2 and 3 view this as a novel and promising contribution; Reviewer 1 also acknowledges the theoretical advantage.
- **Phase regularization provides a mechanism to control frequency details during training (Reviewers 1,2,4).** The approach is clearly derived and the front figures are well presented (Reviewer 4).
- **Problem is well-stated and the paper is overall well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewers 2,3,4). Ablation studies and visual results are appreciated; the method shows good visual results on real-world scenes (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Significant visual improvements are demonstrated** (Reviewer 2), especially in removing floaters and enhancing clarity (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 rates the contribution more positively (score 6) than Reviewers 1 and 4 (score 3 each).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty relative to FreGS and Spec-Gaussian (Reviewers 1,3,4).** 
   - The phase regularization is very similar to FreGS (Equation 7 vs. FreGS Eq. 6; Equation 17 vs. FreGS Eq. 13). The claim that only the phase term is regularized is not justified; amplitude also carries spatial information. A direct experimental comparison with FreGS is missing (Reviewer 1).
   - Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.2 largely replicate Spec-Gaussian (with KAN replacing MLP); Section 3.2.3 replicates Fre-GS. The paper appears to be a KAN version combining Spec-Gaussian and Fre-GS (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 notes the preliminaries section contains too much background and feels cobbled together.
   - Reviewer 2 considers the direct combination of KAN and GS in the well-explored area of neural GS appearance to be somewhat trivial, though the results are appreciated.

2. **Missing evaluations and comparisons (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).**
   - No evaluation on synthetic shiny/scenes with specularities (e.g., datasets used in Spec-Gaussian or mitsuba scenes). This is critical for assessing high-frequency detail and specular modeling (Reviewer 1,3).
   - No comparison of training time or rendering speed (FPS) – a key advantage of 3DGS (Reviewers 2,3). The computational cost of KAN vs. MLP should be quantified (Reviewer 4).
   - No comparison of the number of Gaussians used by different methods, which directly affects metrics (Reviewer 3).
   - Missing direct comparison between KAN and the MLP used in Spec-Gaussian, including computational cost and memory usage (Reviewer 1).
   - Missing comparison with Mip-Splatting, which also focuses on high-frequency details (Reviewer 4).
   - Baseline Spec-Gaussian is not peer-reviewed; alternative peer-reviewed baselines should be included (Reviewer 4).

3. **Experimental results are not fully convincing (Reviewers 1,3,4).**
   - In Table 1, the proposed method ranks second in PSNR on real datasets, underperforming Spec-Gaussian. This raises questions about the effectiveness of the claimed improvements (Reviewer 1).
   - Ablation studies: “No KAN” variant (presumably with MLP) outperforms the full method on Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks&Temples in SSIM and LPIPS. This suggests KAN may not be effectively capturing high-frequency details (Reviewer 1). The ablation configuration is unclear: does “Phase Regularization (Ours)” include KAN? What is “No Kan” compared to? The ablation table needs clear labeling (Reviewer 4).
   - The paper claims improved high-frequency detail modeling, yet experiments focus on floater removal rather than specular highlights (Figure 7). Demonstrating gains in specular scenes (like Figure 5) would be more relevant (Reviewer 3).
   - There is a potential risk of overfitting to high-frequency details at the expense of overall fidelity, as indicated by lower PSNR compared to Spec-Gaussian. Analysis of this trade-off is needed (Reviewer 4).

4. **Experimental setup and reproducibility concerns (Reviewers 1,3,4).**
   - Results for Scaffold-GS on Mip-NeRF 360 are notably lower than originally reported, while results on other datasets match. The authors do not state whether models were retrained. A detailed description of the experimental setup is necessary (Reviewer 1).
   - Hyperparameters are not provided (e.g., scalar terms in Equation 19: λ_prod, λ_phase). This hinders reproducibility and sensitivity analysis (Reviewer 1).
   - The resolution used for Mip-NeRF 360 is unclear: did the authors follow Mip-NeRF 360 downsampling (×4 outdoor, ×2 indoor) or the 3DGS setting (uniformly cropped to 1600 px)? (Reviewer 3)

5. **Motivation and justification of KAN is insufficient (Reviewer 4).**
   - The paper does not clearly explain why the locality property of KANs benefits the modeling of specular highlights, global scene properties, or view-dependent effects. More detailed architectural justification is needed (Reviewer 4).

6. **Writing and presentation issues (Reviewers 1,4).**
   - Writing lacks clarity in places: confusion between “spherical Gaussians” and “spherical harmonics” (Lines 253-254); grammar issues in Line 259-260; mislabeling “\lambda_{prod}” as “\lambda_{}” in Line 402 (Reviewer 1).
   - Error in Section 3.2.4: “and λ□ are scalar values” (Reviewer 4).
   - Figure on page 9 is too small to read (Reviewer 4).
   - Citation format is unsuitable (names mixed into sentences, e.g., “Lu et al. (2023)” should be parenthetical) (Reviewer 4).

7. **Additional questions / missing analyses (Reviewers 1,3,4).**
   - How does the model perform under varied lighting conditions and on metal surfaces? (Reviewer 4)
   - What are the causes of the observed PSNR decrease? (Reviewer 4)
   - Visual results for ablation conditions (removing components) are requested (Reviewer 4).
