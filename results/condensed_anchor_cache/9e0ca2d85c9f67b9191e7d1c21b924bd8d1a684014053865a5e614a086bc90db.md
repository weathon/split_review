## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers assessed the paper on conditional sampling for DDPMs using momentum-driven noise-free guidance. Two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 3–4) rated the contribution as fair (2) and the soundness as good (3) / fair (2); one reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) rated both contribution and soundness as good (3). All agreed that identifying inaccurate clean estimations at early denoising stages as the root cause of performance degradation is a valuable motivation, and that the proposed momentum gradient filtering and guidance suppression are reasonable. However, they raised concerns about limited novelty, insufficient performance improvements, missing comparisons, potential harmful effects of gradient suppression, and the relevance of the style transfer experiment.

### Strengths
- The studied topic of conditional sampling in denoising diffusion probabilistic models is important and widely applicable in AIGC (R1).
- All reviewers agreed that the observation that inaccurate clean estimations, especially during early denoising, cause the performance gap between clean-estimation–based and noised-sample–based methods is a clear and well-motivated finding (R1, R2, R3).
- The proposed momentum-driven gradient filtering to stabilize the conditional clean estimation gradient is sound; using historical gradients to constrain current gradients is reasonable (R1, R3).
- The guidance suppression scheme, motivated by larger prediction errors early in denoising, is also reasonable (R3).
- The method achieves state-of-the-art results on conditional generation (Table 1) and arbitrary style transfer (Section 5.2, 5.3) compared to previous methods (R1).
- One reviewer (score 6) rated the contribution as good (3), while two (score 5) considered it fair (2) – this contrast in overall assessment is noted.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: Momentum is known to improve gradient stability. Figure 2b shows that filtered and unprocessed gradients have almost identical trends rather than a gradual stabilization, indicating the method only alleviates early-stage noise to some extent (R1). Additionally, momentum gradient updating may not be the first work in diffusion reverse sampling; the related work [1] (adaptive momentum sampler for unconditional sampling) should be discussed and differentiated (R3).

2. **Limited performance improvement**: In Table 1 (Section 5.1), the proposed method shows only limited gains over *raw clean guidance* on metrics such as sFID, Precision, and Recall for DiT, and Precision/Recall for ADM (R1).

3. **Missing comparisons**:
   - Only clean-estimation–based methods are compared; no results for noise-guidance–based methods (e.g., classifier guidance) (R1).
   - For FreeDoM and Universal Guidance, only ADM+FreeDoM results are provided; DiT+FreeDoM and DiT/ADM+Universal Guidance are missing (R2).
   - Qualitative results (Figures 5, 6, 7 in appendix) lack comparisons with other baselines (R1).
   - In the style transfer task, results of other works on improving clean-estimation guidance are absent (R2).
   - The paper should compare against other state-of-the-art diffusion models and guidance techniques beyond its own method variations (R2).

4. **Style transfer task appropriateness**: One reviewer (score 5) argues that the style transfer experiment is not directly related to the paper’s main goal of bridging the gap between clean and noised sample-based methods, and recommends either removing Section 2.3 or providing a clear explanation for its inclusion (R2).

5. **Insufficient proof of gradient instability**: The claim in Section 4.2 that the clean classifier/guidance function is less reliable than a noise-robust classifier should be supported with more direct evidence, e.g., visualizing gradients of the clean classifier over the denoising process (similar to Figure 2) (R2).

6. **Potential harmful effect of gradient suppression**: Although the motivation is reasonable, Algorithm 1 suppresses conditional gradients early in denoising, when semantic and layout are being constructed. This may destroy the semantic matching between the condition and final generation, particularly for tasks with stricter requirements such as inverse problems (R3). This concern is raised by the more positive reviewer (score 6).

7. **Missing experimental details**:
   - In Section 5.1, it is unclear which table is being discussed (R2).
   - Section 5.2 does not define _I_cs_ (R2).
   - The specific sampling cost (e.g., training overhead, inference steps) of clean-estimation guidance techniques is not reported; please provide comparisons (R1).

8. **Trade-off in style transfer ablation**: In Section 5.3, *Raw+Filtering & Suppression* achieves the best _L_content_ but only third-best _L_style_. This suggests the method may favor content preservation over style transfer. The reviewer asks for discussion on the trade-off between content preservation and style transfer (R2).
