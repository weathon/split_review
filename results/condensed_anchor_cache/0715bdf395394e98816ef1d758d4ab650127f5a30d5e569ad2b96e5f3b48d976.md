## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes InnerSightNet, an algorithm for interpreting deep neural networks by grouping learnable units (e.g., kernels in CNNs) into a network of neurons, then clustering them into communities based on representational attributes. The three-phase approach (neuronization–aggregation–evaluation) aims to reveal information flow, identify key communities, and detect noisy neurons. Experiments on MNIST and AFHQ show that closing or pruning certain communities degrades accuracy, while others can be removed to reduce model size with minor loss.

### Strengths
- The idea of grouping neurons into communities for interpretability is clever, original, and provides a fresh perspective beyond single-layer or single-neuron analysis. [R1, R3]
- The three-phase structure (neuronization, aggregation, evaluation) is well-organized; Algorithm 1 clearly outlines the steps. [R1, R3]
- The interdependence of communities (e.g., c₁ and c₂ in the first CNN layer) is visible both in Table 1 and the 2‑D topological representation in Figure 3. [R1]
- InnerSightNet adaptively determines the best number of communities and can be applied to network pruning while maintaining competitive performance. [R2, R3]
- A detailed evaluation is provided through accuracy drop tests and sensitivity analyses, showing the impact of each community. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and justification of core equation (Eq. 1):** The use of KL divergence between kernel distributions and division by normalized cosine similarity (either +1 or −1) is insufficiently motivated. The separation into positive/negative regions (κ⁺, κ⁻) and the definition of data \(X_i\) used for computing kernel correlations are not clearly explained. [R1, R1‑question]
- **Notation and reproducibility:** The aggregation step is notation-heavy and lacks definitions for variables such as \(l_d\), \(\tau_{g_k, j}\), and \(s\). This hinders understanding and reproduction of the algorithm. [R1]
- **Shallow community function analysis:** The discussion linking observations from panels (a) and (b) to community roles (e.g., c₁₀ in the first layer) is superficial. A more rigorous, direct link between community structure and specific input features/patterns is needed to substantiate claims about importance. [R1]
- **Insufficient baseline explanation:** The paper compares InnerSightNet with Filan et al. (2021), Hod et al. (2021), and Liu et al. (2023) but does not adequately describe their algorithms (e.g., clustering techniques, geometric embeddings, training modifications). Without this, it is hard to assess novelty and advantage. [R2]
- **Scalability and computational complexity:** No analysis of runtime, convergence of the EM algorithm, or multi-step startup is provided. It remains unclear how InnerSightNet scales to deeper networks (e.g., transformers) or models with millions of neurons. This limits practical applicability. [R2, R3]
- **Transferability:** Experiments are limited to image recognition (CNNs/MLPs). The paper does not discuss applicability to NLP architectures (RNNs, transformers) or other modalities. [R3]
- **Limited practical improvement:** The reported accuracy gains from noise reduction and pruning are marginal. More concrete evidence of significant performance or efficiency benefits is needed. [R3]
- **Missing experiments and ablations implied by reviewer questions:**  
  - Are the accuracy drops in Figure 1a and Table 1 reported with or without retraining the network? [R1‑question]  
  - Why are not all combinations of communities within a layer (e.g., c₀ and c₁ together) or across layers closed? Is there a reason such experiments were omitted? [R1‑question]  
  - If method ‘fits to noisy data’ (lines 458–464), what are the practical implications for model pruning? [R1‑question]  
  - The claim that binary tasks (cat/dog) have fewer effective neurons than 10‑class tasks (MNIST) could be tested by restricting MNIST to two classes (e.g., 0 vs 1). Such a control experiment is missing. [R1‑question]  
  - What is the runtime of InnerSightNet compared to baselines? How fast does the algorithm converge for different network sizes? [R2‑question]
- **Presentation issues:** Axis/legend labels in figures are barely readable; there are multiple typographical errors in citations (e.g., “David et al. Bau et al. (2017)”, “Newman et al. Newman (2006”, “Lange et al. Lange et al. (2022)”, “Wanatabe et al. Wanatabe et al. (2018)”); “sgn” should be “sign”; in Eq. (11) δ is reused confusingly; LaTeX formatting of c_{10} is broken from line 370 onward. [R1]
- **Minor difference in reviewer assessment:** One reviewer (R3, contribution 2) was less positive about the contribution than the other two (contribution 3), and noted that the usability improvements are limited. [R3]
