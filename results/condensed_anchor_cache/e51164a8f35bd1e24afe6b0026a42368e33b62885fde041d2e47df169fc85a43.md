## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies black-box optimization with a gradient-free oracle that returns function values with stochastic noise. It proposes Zero-Order Accelerated Batched SGD (ZO-ABSGD) for µ-strongly convex functions with higher-order smoothness (β≥2). The theoretical analysis claims optimal iteration complexity (Table 1) and introduces the maximum noise level Δ as a third optimality criterion. Experiments compare with ZO-VARAG and ARDFDS on the a9a dataset.

### Strengths
- The paper generalizes Accelerated SGD (Vaswani et al., 2019) to the setting of a biased gradient oracle and mini-batches (R1, R2), and combines this with gradient estimates from a zeroth-order oracle using kernel approximation (R1, R4).
- It provides a thorough analysis of the maximum noise level Δ and improves iteration complexity compared to previous studies (R3).
- The proof details are solid and support the proposed theory (R3, R4).
- The paper is clearly organized and presents an interesting kernel-approximation idea for zero-order optimization (R4).
- Numerical experiments illustrate the advantages of the proposed algorithm (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing clarity:** Symbols are introduced before their definitions (e.g., \(e\), \(r\) in Definition 1.3) and some terms appear without definition (e.g., first-order Accelerated SGD algorithm, \(\eta\), \(\tilde{R}\) in Lemma 2.4/2.5) (R1, R2, R3). Figure 1 seems unnecessary for a theoretical paper (R1). The definition of oracle complexity (vs. iteration complexity) should be introduced in the introduction (R4).
- **Limited technical contribution:** Convergence of Accelerated SGD is already known; extending it to biased and mini-batch settings appears straightforward. Bias and second-moment estimates of the zeroth-order oracle are also well-established in the literature (R1). Biased noise has already been studied in zeroth-order methods (Akhavan et al., 2023, Assumption B) (R2). More details about new technical tricks for handling biased noise are needed (R2, Question 4).
- **Maximum noise level Δ interpretation:** It is unclear why considering Δ as a function of target accuracy \(\epsilon\) (diminishing with \(\epsilon\)) is advantageous; previous work treated Δ as a problem-dependent constant and converged to the minimizer. This work does not converge to the minimizer with constant Δ (R1, R2). Table 1’s comparison is unfair because Δ depends on \(\epsilon\) in this work and because the proposed method uses mini-batches while iteration complexity is reported; oracle complexity would be the more relevant metric (R1).
- **Experiments are insufficient:** Only one dataset (a9a) is tested; experiments on more datasets are needed to demonstrate efficiency (R3). The noise type used in experiments is not specified (R1, Question 4). The final accuracy of ZO-VARAG is large despite the noise level being small; this requires explanation (R1, Question 4).
- **Assumptions and scope:** The strong convexity assumption is not discussed; it is unclear whether the algorithm would perform well on convex or non-convex problems satisfying weaker conditions (e.g., Polyak-Łojasiewicz) (R4). The bounded bias assumption (Assumption 2.3) is standard but its justification is thin; it could be too restrictive if the bias varies with iteration or gradient norm (R4). The advantage of kernel approximation is not briefly discussed in the introduction (R2, Question 1). Clarify what “third optimality criterion” and “the standard two” refer to and how they are represented in experiments (R3, Question 3).
- **Other questions/comments:** In Definition 1.3, does \(e\) and \(r\) mean two queries at the same point \(x_k\)? (R2, Question 2). In Figure 2(b), why does the red curve converge to minimizers even in the case of bias? (R1, Question 3). Explain the unique practical advantages of ZO-ABSGD compared to ARDFDS (R3, Question 1).

**Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R1, score 3) considered the contribution limited and the writing problematic, while another (R4, score 6) found the work solid and well-organized. R4 noted low familiarity with the topic and indicated willingness to adjust score based on other reviewers.
