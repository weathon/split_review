## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces WIND, a two-stage watermarking framework for images generated by diffusion models. It uses the initial noise of the diffusion process as a distortion-free watermark. During generation, Fourier patterns are embedded into the initial noise to encode a group identifier. During detection, the group is first retrieved via the Fourier identifier, and then an exact match for the reconstructed noise is searched within that group. This two-stage approach aims to improve detection efficiency and provide robustness against both forgery and removal attacks, achieving state-of-the-art results in empirical evaluations.

### Strengths
- The two-stage framework (group identification via Fourier patterns then exact noise matching) is a novel adaptation of multi-level indexing to watermarking, improving detection efficiency and scalability. Multiple reviewers highlight the innovation of hierarchical retrieval.
- The method is distortion-free because it leverages the inherent initial noise used by diffusion models, avoiding external watermarking processes that degrade image quality.
- The approach explicitly tackles both forgery and removal attacks, unlike several prior works that focus on only one.
- Robustness is demonstrated against a battery of attacks, including distortive transformations, removal, and forgery. The two-tier verification and Fourier embedding contribute to persistence under adversarial conditions.
- The methodology is clearly described, with theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) and empirical evaluations on Stable Diffusion v2. The paper maintains good flow, explains concepts, and has few typos.
- The framework generalizes to the inpainting task (R1). It also offers a faster detection variant to partially mitigate search complexity dependence on the number of watermarks N and noise groups M.
- The work shows that initial noise from diffusion models can serve as a robust watermark, providing a fresh perspective that combines deep learning with traditional watermarking principles.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model evaluation**: Only tested on Stable Diffusion v2. Missing evaluations on SDXL, consistency models, transformer-based diffusion architectures, GAN-based generators (via alternative inversion), and at higher resolutions. The prompt template used for generation is not specified.
- **Robustness evaluation not comprehensive**: The paper focuses on image transformations and regeneration attacks, but does not evaluate adaptive attacks where the adversary has greater capability, such as transfer-based, query-based, or white-box attacks (e.g., WEvade). Also, the evaluation excludes comparison to other watermarking families (e.g., HiDDeN) without justification. The sensitivity to inference steps (only 50 steps tested) is not explored; further testing across 20, 50, 100, and 200 steps is needed.
- **Novelty concerns**: One reviewer (score 5) argues that WIND does not fundamentally diverge from the concepts established in Tree-Ring and RingID; the addition of two-stage detection is a novel enhancement but builds on the same theoretical foundation of Fourier-based noise watermarking, with the primary innovation being grouping and search-space reduction. Another reviewer notes that some claims (lines 78–84) are already addressed in Tree-Ring [1], yet the paper presents them as a contribution, and the introduction does not adequately discuss [1] and [2].
- **Motivation and logic unclear**: The abstract lacks smooth logical connection between the problem statement and the proposed solution. The relationship between the third and fourth paragraphs of the introduction is confusing, and the vulnerability of existing methods to attacks exploiting the distributional inconsistency of reconstructed noise is not clearly stressed.
- **Theoretical proof for Theorem 4.1 is not rigorous**: The proof is more empirical than mathematical; it does not provide a probability bound for low false positives, making it less convincing.
- **Missing quantitative image quality metrics**: For non-synthetic image watermarking (Section 5.2), only FID is reported. Metrics like CLIP score, SSIM, PSNR, and side-by-side qualitative comparisons are lacking. Overall, quantitative analysis of watermarked image quality is absent. For example, in Fig 5 and Fig 9, text in watermarked images is not retained (e.g., traffic signs, aircraft text).
- **Insufficient analysis of experimental results**: Descriptions of tables and figures (e.g., Table 2) are clear, but deeper analysis of why similarity changes and how they affect robustness is missing. No ablation on perturbation strengths vs. detection accuracy is provided.
- **Detection threshold not determined**: The paper does not explain how threshold τ (used for detection) is chosen in experiments.
- **Efficiency and scalability challenges remain**: The method requires searching through a large number of initial noise records, which may hinder real-time applications and resource-constrained devices. The multi-stage process adds complexity (managing group identifiers, Fourier transformations), and scalability with growing watermark counts is unclear. A runtime comparison with competing methods is not provided (supplementary suggestion).
- **Missing experimental details**: The trade-off between inversion steps, watermarking overhead, and detection accuracy is not discussed. The group index embedding should be included in Algorithm 1. The placement of Section 5.2 (watermarking non-synthetic images) and the Algorithms section could be moved before the experiments section for better coherence.
- **Potential vulnerability to advanced attacks**: Attackers may successfully invert the model; the implications for watermarking effectiveness are not discussed.
- **Minor typos**: Missing parentheses for citations in lines 88 and 137.
