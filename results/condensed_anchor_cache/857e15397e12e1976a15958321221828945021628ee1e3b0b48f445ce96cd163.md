## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ClearSR, a diffusion-based real-world super-resolution method that leverages latent LR embeddings (from a VAE encoder) to guide ControlNet via two modules: a Detail Preservation Module (DPM) and a Structure Preservation Module (SPM). It also introduces a Latent Space Adjustment (LSA) strategy during inference to balance fidelity and generative detail. Overall, reviewers find the motivation clear, the methodology reasonably presented, and the experimental setup adequate. However, they raise several concerns about terminology, missing baselines and comparisons, insufficient complexity analysis, unclear design choices, and limited novelty.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear and supported by observations (e.g., Figure 2 showing ControlNet does not preserve LR information well).
- The analysis of the relationship between image fidelity and generative capacity, and the attempt to address it via LSA, is reasonable.
- The two designed modules (DPM for detail, SPM for structure) and the LSA strategy are interesting and sensible.
- The experimental arrangement is reasonable, and ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed strategies.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The use of a pre-trained VAE encoder as an initial feature extractor for LR images is a good approach to preserve fidelity.
- The observation that realistic details largely come from final inference steps and the subsequent LSA mechanism to move away from the LR latent in later steps enhances flexibility.
- While most reviewers rated the paper similarly (scores 5,5,5), one reviewer found the evaluation to show reasonable improvement (score 6).

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing comparisons and baselines**: Several strong baselines are missing, particularly SUPIR. The comparison to similar LSA-like strategies in DiffBIR and PASD is not thorough enough.
2. **Computational complexity not reported**: The additional modules (DPM, SPM, window cross-attention) increase model parameters and inference time, but the paper lacks any quantitative evaluation of model complexity (parameter count, FLOPs, inference time) relative to ControlNet and other state-of-the-art methods.
3. **Terminology confusion**: The paper classifies detail as high-frequency and structure as low-frequency. However, edges, which represent structure, are high-frequency information. More appropriate terminology is needed.
4. **LSA strategy concerns**:
   - The experimental results do not clearly demonstrate an improvement in fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) over other methods.
   - The choice of hyperparameters α and β is not explained; it appears case-by-case and may be tough to tune, especially under severe degradation where adding LR guidance could cause blurry outputs.
   - The CFG settings (CFG value, negative prompt) used during inference are not reported; the LSA control method needs to be compared in detail with standard CFG control.
5. **Control signal distribution puzzle (related to Figure 2)**: The proposed method achieves a low KL divergence between the control signal and the LR latent embedding. This suggests that the control branch output is being pulled closer to the LR latent distribution. Reviewers question why not use the LR latent embedding directly, and note that past work (DiffBIR, PASD, SeeSR) indicates the control branch should remove degradation and approach the HR distribution, making the proposed behavior puzzling. The paper should clarify whether it follows the same noise-addition process as ControlNet and explicitly state the difference.
6. **Missing implementation details**:
   - The number of inference steps is not reported.
   - Figure 10 does not provide the names of the comparison methods.
   - The description of the image-level feature **p** in Figure 3 is unclear: how it is integrated into the Stable Diffusion UNet (concatenated, added, cross-attention?) and its role in the diffusion process.
   - The choice of LoRA rank (set to 16) and its impact on performance is not discussed or optimized.
7. **Misstatement about PASD (Line 190)**: The paper says PASD uses the CLIP image encoder to extract LR features, but PASD actually uses the CLIP text encoder for conditioning, not the image encoder. This distinction is important because the CLIP image encoder is designed for high-resolution semantic extraction, not low-level SR features.
8. **DPM vs. SPM fidelity trade-off**: Table 2 shows that SPM improves fidelity while window-based cross-attention in DPM weakens fidelity (as measured by PSNR). The paper does not adequately explain why DPM degrades fidelity; an ablation study removing DPM entirely is needed, along with discussion of the fidelity vs. perceptual quality trade-off.
9. **Window cross-attention justification**: The paper should provide empirical or theoretical justification for choosing window cross-attention over full-attention, and explain how the window size was determined.
10. **Novelty concerns**: One reviewer finds the overall novelty relatively weak despite the good motivation.
