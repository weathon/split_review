## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MWP-MISTAKE, a dataset of math word problems with correct and incorrect reasoning steps generated via rule-based transformations and smaller language models (SLMs). It benchmarks multiple LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4, GPT-4 Turbo, Claude-3-Opus, Llama-3-8b-finetuned, O1, etc.) on mistake detection and correction, and explores contamination and memorization issues. Reviewer opinions diverge: one is moderately positive (score 6), one is moderately negative (score 3), and two are neutral (scores 5, 5). The dataset is considered helpful and the evaluation broad, but concerns about realism of rule-based errors, dataset reliability, limited novelty, missing analyses, and insufficient mitigation strategies weaken the contribution.

### Strengths
- **Novel dataset**: MWP-MISTAKE fills a gap by targeting reasoning-error detection and correction rather than final-answer accuracy. It includes both rule-based mistakes (six crafted rules) and SLM-generated mistakes, providing a structured assessment. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Comprehensive benchmarking**: A wide range of models is evaluated (GPT-4o, GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4, GPT-4 Turbo, Claude-3-Opus, Llama-3-8b-finetuned, O1, etc.) across multiple source datasets (SVAMP, GSM-8K, MATH, MATHBENCH, JEEBENCH). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Thorough analysis**: The paper explores question variations, memorization, and contamination, offering insights into models’ capabilities and limitations. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Relevance**: The focus on reasoning errors is important for educational tools and real-world applications, and the findings highlight directions for improving LLM robustness. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Clarity of construction**: Dataset construction and experiment setups are generally clear, and the distinction between rule-based and model-generated mistakes is well-defined. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of the main finding**: The result that LLMs remain weak in mistake detection/correction is not novel and has been reported in prior work (e.g., [1][2]). (Reviewer 3)
- **Dataset realism and reliability concerns**:
  - Rule-based mistakes (e.g., shuffling steps or numeric values, deleting steps) may lack natural coherence and may not mimic authentic human reasoning errors, potentially distracting models and inflating difficulty. The authors should refine these to better reflect typical student errors (Reviewer 1). Overlap with criticism: Reviewer 2 asks whether GPT-3.5 Turbo can correctly solve the problem in Figure 1, which relates to error realism.
  - The correct reasoning steps in MWP-MISTAKE are generated by GPT-4 and manually verified, but there is no detailed statistics on the proportion of data manually checked, the rate at which GPT-4’s steps were completely correct, or how incorrect GPT-4 outputs were handled. This weakens data quality assurance. (Reviewer 3)
  - Deleting reasoning steps that still yield a correct answer raises ambiguity about whether models should detect such partial solutions as errors. The paper should investigate how this affects detection rates. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing analyses and metrics**:
  - The paper does not evaluate the accuracy rate of *generated reasoning steps* by models when correcting mistakes. (Reviewer 3)
  - The F1 metric (line 186) is not clearly defined: is it step-level or path-level detection performance? (Reviewer 2)
  - Table 3 uses averages across unbalanced data sizes without explanation. Also, detection performance decreases on dataset D but increases on SM (e.g., JEEBENCH); why? (Reviewer 2)
  - The claim (lines 253-255) that GPT-3.5 Turbo performs similarly to GPT-4 and even surpasses it on GSM-8K is not well supported by Table 4. (Reviewer 2)
  - The claim (lines 258-262) that Llama-3-8b-finetuned rivals GPT-4 due to domain-specific fine-tuning lacks a check for memorization/contamination. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited scope and practical solutions**:
  - The primary contribution is evaluation; the paper offers limited practical solutions or recommendations for improving mistake detection and rectification. (Reviewer 4)
  - While rectification abilities are discussed, results show advanced models still struggle with consistent correction, underscoring a need for stronger error-handling frameworks—but the paper does not propose such frameworks. (Reviewer 4)
- **Contamination and generalization issues**:
  - Data contamination is identified but mitigation strategies are not detailed, weakening result reliability. (Reviewer 4)
  - Models show reduced performance on newer, complex datasets like JEEBENCH, indicating limited generalization. (Reviewer 4)
  - In Section 4.4, GPT-4o’s high ROUGE-L could be due to contamination; qualitative cases comparing LLMs and contamination scores for Llama-3-8b-finetuned and O1 are missing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing self-error detection study**: The paper only examines mistakes generated by smaller models, not those generated by the same or competitive models. Exploring whether GPT-4/GPT-4o can detect their own mistakes would add substantial value. (Reviewer 1)
- **Omitted related work**: The paper overlooks relevant evaluation benchmarks like “MR-GSM8K: A Meta-Reasoning Benchmark for Large Language Model Evaluation”. (Reviewer 2)
- **Evaluation setup hard to follow**: The evaluation methodology lacks clarity. (Reviewer 3)
- **Typos and referencing issues**:
  - “corect” (line 105), “their” (line 154). (Reviewer 3)
  - Citation issue at line 44. (Reviewer 2)
  - Figure 2 is not referenced in the paper. (Reviewer 1)
  - Lines 208-209 possibly refer to Table 2 vs. Table 7 without clear distinction. (Reviewer 1)
- **Other specific questions**: Why does GPT-4o underperform on both simpler and more complex datasets? (Reviewer 1) Which mistake types do models consistently fail to detect? (Reviewer 1) The results for model performance on solving questions alongside error detection are not presented; it would help to see if correctly solved questions are easier to detect errors on. (Reviewer 2)
