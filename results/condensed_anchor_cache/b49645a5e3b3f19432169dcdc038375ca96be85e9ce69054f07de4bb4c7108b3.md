## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Multi-View Permutation of Variational Auto-Encoders (MVP) for incomplete multi-view learning, using cyclic permutations in the VAE latent space to model inter-view relationships, partition latent variables, and derive an ELBO. Experiments on seven datasets show strong clustering and generation performance. Reviews are mixed: one reviewer (score 8) praises the novelty and thorough experiments, while others (scores 5, 6, 6) raise significant concerns about reproducibility, missing experimental details, computational cost, and limited novelty, though they acknowledge the method is well-motivated.

### Strengths
- The application of cyclic permutations in the VAE latent space is a novel and innovative approach to modeling inter-view relationships, robustly capturing consistency across incomplete multi-view data.
- The method is rigorously tested across seven diverse datasets (including PolyMNIST, MVShapeNet, CUB, Scene‑15) under varying missing ratios, with strong performance particularly in high‑missing‑rate scenarios. Results are reported with multiple random seeds and deviations, adding credibility.
- Extensive experiments include ablation studies, additional tests on relatedness, and visualizations that confirm MVP’s superiority over existing methods (e.g., MVAE, MMVAE, MVTCAE) in both clustering and generation tasks.
- The paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis and proofs for the ELBO derivation with cyclic permutations.
- The motivation (handling irregularly missing multi‑view data) is clearly described and well‑structured, making the paper easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- Incomplete data processing (e.g., padding, masking) is relegated to the appendix; the main text lacks a clear description of these steps, hindering readability.
- Computational complexity is insufficiently analyzed. MVP introduces the overhead of generating and applying \((V-1)!\) permutations (where \(V\) is the number of views), making its complexity closer to MMVAE and higher than MVAE/MVTCAE. The paper does not provide a breakdown of time/space complexity per stage (encoding, transformation, decoding), FLOPs, or how the cost scales with the number of views and latent dimensions. Such analysis is crucial for scalability.
- The assumption that the first \(k\) dimensions of the latent variable capture shared information across all views is not justified. The paper does not explain how \(k\) is set on different datasets, nor does it investigate the sensitivity of performance to the choice of \(k\). A principled selection method is lacking.
- Generated results are only shown on RGB‑type datasets (PolyMNIST, MVShapeNet). The method’s generalizability to non‑image modalities (e.g., CUB with image+text) is not demonstrated, raising concerns about applicability beyond visual data.
- Experimental results for some baselines appear inconsistent with published numbers (e.g., DVIMC on Scene‑15 at missing rate \(\eta=0.1\) is lower than the original paper). Additionally, the adaptation of Completer (originally designed for two‑view data) to multi‑view datasets is not explained, compromising reproducibility and fairness.
- Convergence analysis is missing. The paper would benefit from loss curves and visualizations of latent space evolution during training to verify optimization stability.
- The novelty is limited; VAE and ELBO for invariant feature learning have been extensively studied in incomplete multi‑view clustering (see e.g., [1‑4]). The paper needs to clearly differentiate its approach from existing methods and discuss how it goes beyond standard VAE frameworks.
- Code is not provided, making the reported significant performance gains difficult to verify. Releasing code is essential for assessing authenticity and fairness.
- Construction details of the PolyMNIST and MVShapeNet datasets (line 406) are unclear. Using newly constructed datasets to evaluate previous methods may be unfair; the paper should clarify how baselines were adapted to these datasets.
- The randomness introduced by the random padding strategy (line 20) is not addressed. The paper should discuss mitigation strategies or its impact on results.
- The explanation that “underlying semantic information remains invariant” under random variable reordering is vague. The paper should clarify what specific semantic information is invariant, whether this holds for arbitrary permutations or only constrained ones, and provide quantitative verification beyond qualitative claims.
- Sensitivity to cyclic permutation settings (e.g., cycle length, random vs. cyclic permutations) and regularization parameters is not analyzed. A deeper sensitivity analysis is needed to assess stability and guide parameter selection.
- The cyclic permutation technique appears conceptually similar to disturbance and re‑alignment strategies in *Partially View‑Aligned Clustering* (NeurIPS 2020). The paper should discuss differences in optimization, transformations, and problem setting.
- Network architectures (number of layers, activation functions, hidden dimensions, encoder/decoder configurations) are not fully detailed, limiting reproducibility.
- Minor clarity issues: Figure 1 would benefit from highlighting Single‑view and Complete‑view partitions; the transition from \(Z_0\) to \(Z_1\) is hard to follow (linking to Appendix A.2 would help); Figure 1b should use distinct colors/shapes for views and samples; the reference to “The second term” in line 303 should be accompanied by Equation 1.
