## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper critically evaluates ReAct-style prompting by introducing systematic variations to prompts in the AlfWorld and WebShop domains. It finds that LLM performance is minimally influenced by interleaved reasoning traces or their content; instead, performance is primarily driven by high similarity between demonstration examples and the query. The authors conclude that perceived reasoning abilities stem from exemplar-query similarity and approximate retrieval, not inherent reasoning.

### Strengths
- The paper conducts a detailed and wide-ranging sensitivity analysis of ReAct-style prompting, exploring multiple components (guidance location, guidance type, example-query resemblance) across several LLMs and domains. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The motivation to critically examine what makes ReAct effective is highly valuable to the research community. (Reviewer 2)
- The work is well-structured, with clear experiments, and the tables and figures effectively communicate results. (Reviewer 3; but Reviewer 2 and 4 criticized presentation clarity)
- The findings raise important questions about the role of reasoning traces in ReAct, challenging common assumptions and offering a perspective that may guide future research. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Many of the core observations – that LLM performance depends on example-query similarity and that explicit reasoning traces are often unnecessary – are already well covered in prior work (e.g., “Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations”, research on demonstration selection, and the fact that “think step by step” alone can elicit chain-of-thought). The paper does not cite or discuss such related literature sufficiently. (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 2 also notes the conclusion feels obvious)
- **Incomplete analysis of model behavior**: The paper lacks detailed analysis of what the LLMs actually generate under the altered prompts. For example, it does not check whether models ignore the demonstration structure and still produce interleaved reasoning during inference. When performance drops (e.g., in Domain, Instance variations), it is unclear whether errors stem from reduced reasoning abilities or from simple surface-form mistakes. Claims about “task-specific reasoning trace” (lines 416–418) ambiguously refer either to traces in demonstrations or to traces generated by the model; results only show that demonstrations without reasoning traces are not essential, but models could still generate good traces themselves. (Reviewer 4)
- **Narrow scope and lack of generalizability**: The experiments are limited to AlfWorld and WebShop. It is unclear whether the same reliance on example-query similarity would hold in more complex environments, tasks with less structured action spaces, or different LLM architectures. The study does not address scalability to other domains. (Reviewer 3)
- **Weak technical contribution**: The paper offers only a critique of ReAct-style prompting and provides no novel method, improvement, or concrete recommendations to reduce the reliance on example-query similarity. A proposed framework or remedy would significantly strengthen the impact. (Reviewer 3)
- **Confounding of prompting vs. model capabilities**: Because black-box LLMs are used, it is difficult to separate the effect of the prompting method from the underlying model’s inherent capabilities. The paper does not provide transparency (e.g., via ablation of model size or architecture) to clarify this. (Reviewer 3)
- **Experimental design concerns**:  
  - The RQ1 variants are essentially human‑rewritten versions of the original ReAct prompt, not fundamentally different. A fairer comparison would let the model generate its own plan prompt from the user query. Designing prompts based on potential behaviors biases the results. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The suggestion that giving LLMs a complete action plan upfront (as in Figure 2) is feasible is questionable; real environments can change unpredictably, making it impossible to pre‑specify all contingencies. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Unsurprising results**: That similarity between demonstrations and queries matters is an obvious conclusion, especially because LLMs rarely encounter agentic structure data in training; in‑context learning is naturally critical. Reporting this specific finding lacks deep insight. (Reviewer 2; echoed by Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: The paper contains extensive prompt text that distracts from the narrative, struck‑through words without explanation, and figures with font sizes too small. Model names are inconsistently referred to (“GPT-3.5-instruct” vs. “GPT-3.5-Turbo” vs. API names like “gpt-3.5-turbo-0125”). The baseline “Act” in Table 1 is never introduced. Overall the paper is not yet ready for publication. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4 also noted presentation issues)
- **Unresolved experimental observations**:  
  - Table 1 shows GPT‑3.5 outperforming GPT‑4o, which the authors attribute to “brittleness of claims of ReAct.” This interpretation is counterintuitive – stronger models should be more robust to suboptimal prompts. Examples explaining this result are needed. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Webshop accuracy is very low compared to other published work; the reason for this discrepancy is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
