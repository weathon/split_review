## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ActorAttack, a multi-turn LLM jailbreak attack method inspired by actor-network theory. It models semantically linked actors as attack clues to generate diverse attack paths towards harmful targets, concealing harmful intents across multiple queries. ActorAttack is evaluated against existing single-turn and multi-turn attacks across LLMs including GPT-o1. The authors also release SafeMTData, a dataset of multi-turn adversarial prompts and safety alignment data, and show that models safety-tuned on this dataset are more robust to multi-turn attacks.

### Strengths
- Novel adaptation of actor-network theory for multi-turn jailbreak prompt sequence generation (Reviewer 2).
- Attack method is suitable for both black-box and white-box LLMs (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed method demonstrates a better attack success rate (Reviewer 3). Fine-tuning experiments indicate it is effective in enhancing model security (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-arranged and readable (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / technical contribution.** The overall idea is largely consistent with Crescendo (Reviewer 1). The actor-network theory only assigns a role for LLM to generate the initial clue; no strict theory or inspiration is used in the process (Reviewer 1). The primary contribution is the identification of attack clues from LLMs, but there is no quantitative analysis of why these clues are more effective than those in existing works (e.g., CFA, CoA). It is unclear whether they introduce more possibilities or if they differ from prior approaches (Reviewer 3). The dynamic multi-turn prompt modification only involves repeated generation to reduce toxicity, with no heuristic strategy guidance (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of root cause analysis.** Why does multi-turn surpass single-turn? No in-depth mechanism analysis or supporting experiments are provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons.** Only one multi-turn baseline, Crescendo, is included. The authors should compare with other multi-turn attacks such as CoA (Yang et al., 2024) and CFA (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). The selected single-turn baselines are not state-of-the-art; more effective attacks like AutoDAN, ReNeLLM, and MultiLingual (see easyjailbreak.org/leader-board) are missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete evaluation / selective results.** The ablation study uses only 50 instances and two victim models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5), which cannot convincingly support that ActorAttack surpasses Crescendo (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). For the multi-turn comparison, why not evaluate on all five victim models listed in Table 1? This suggests selective presentation of results (Reviewer 3). Only GPT-4o is used to judge ASR; consider human evaluators, Llama-guard model, and moderation API (Reviewer 1).
- **Method clarity.** Section 3 could be written better; the adaptation of actor-network theory to attack clue generation is unclear (Reviewer 2). The algorithm pseudocode uses 4 different LLMs, but a clear description of all four models is missing (Reviewer 2). Notations in the algorithm are not explained in the text (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing defense comparisons.** The experiments do not compare with state-of-the-art defense mechanisms (Reviewer 2). The experiments section needs to be refined (Reviewer 2).
- **Efficiency concerns.** The method relies on multiple LLMs to generate attack clues; what is the time required to generate a jailbreak attack sequence? Using 4 models raises questions about practicality in real use cases (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The authors should discuss time costs (Reviewer 3).
- **Dataset concerns.** The number of samples in the SafeMTData dataset is unclear. The ablation uses only 50 samples, raising concerns about generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- **Diversity evaluation justification.** It is unclear if using the embedding function of MiniLMv2 for diversity evaluation is common practice; provide more evidence (Reviewer 1).
- **Figures lack details.** The interpretation of results in Figures 4(a) and 4(b) is unclear; add additional details in the caption (Reviewer 2).

*Reviewer disagreement:* Reviewer 2 (score 6) finds the adaptation of actor-network theory novel and the method suitable for both black-box/white-box settings. Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 5 each) view the contribution and evaluation as weak. The minority positive opinion should be weighed alongside the majority’s criticisms regarding limited novelty and incomplete evaluation.
