## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MatchMask, a mask-centric generative data augmentation pipeline for label-scarce semantic segmentation. It adapts StableDiffusion via a Layer-Timestep Adaptive Adapter (LT-Adapter) to generate synthetic image-mask pairs from a few ground truth masks, then trains a segmentor on real and synthetic data, with an optional semi-supervised extension (MatchMask++) that pseudo-labels unlabeled data. Experiments on VOC, COCO, and ADE20K show improvements over baselines in limited-data and semi-supervised settings. Reviewers note that the writing is clear and the motivation is understandable, but raise concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, insufficient technical justification, and limited domain scope.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written, clearly organized, and easy to understand (R1, R2, R4).
- The motivation is clear: mask-centric generation can produce more accurate semantic masks than text-centric methods (R2).
- Two variants (MatchMask and MatchMask++) are provided to serve different needs, increasing general usefulness (R1).
- Extensive experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over several state-of-the-art methods in few-shot and semi-supervised semantic segmentation (R2, R3).
- MatchMask++ shows that the approach benefits from additional unlabeled data (R3).
- Fine-tuning is parameter-efficient due to a variant of LoRA for StableDiffusion (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / over-claiming**: The idea of adapting diffusion models to few-shot semantic segmentation is not new. The paper should cite DDPM‑Seg (ICLR 2022) and compare with DatasetDDPM and DDPM‑Seg (R3). Other existing works in this line (DiffuMask, FreeMask, SegGen, DatasetDM, Dataset Diffusion, Freestyle layout-to-image, FreeMask) are not adequately compared or even cited (R1, R4). The claimed contributions around “which layers to fine-tune” via Gradient Probe Method are presented without rigorous justification; many works have already shown that early layers capture layout and later layers capture details (R3).
- **Adapter design and comparison**: The LT‑Adapter module lacks clear technical superiority over existing adapters such as ControlNet and FreeStyleNet. No experimental comparison with those methods is provided (R1, R3, R4). The improvement may simply come from additional sampling on pseudo‑labeled images rather than from an innovative adapter, making the comparison in Table 2 unfair because baselines [1] and [2] do not use pseudo‑labeled sampling (R4). The text claims that LT‑Adapter provides “layer-adaptive cross-attention fusion” and “time-adaptive LoRA scaling,” but how this differs methodologically from prior work (e.g., FreeStyleNet) is not clarified (R4).
- **Reliance on masks**: The method depends on ground truth or pseudo masks, which are themselves expensive or noisy to obtain in real applications. This limits scalability to large‑scale generation (R2). Pseudo‑labels contain semantic noise; the paper does not explain how semantic inconsistency is handled when using them for sampling (R4).
- **Limited problem scope**: Experiments are confined to semantic segmentation; the method’s applicability to instance or panoptic segmentation is not shown (R1). The paper only evaluates in very low‑data regimes; it would be stronger to demonstrate improvements when adding synthetic data to the full ADE/COCO real training set (R1). The definition of “label‑scarce” is vague (R3).
- **Domain generalization unclear**: The datasets (VOC, COCO, ADE20K) have a small domain gap to StableDiffusion’s training distribution. It is unclear whether MatchMask would work for medical images, remote sensing, or rare categories with minimal fine‑tuning (R2, R3).
- **Technical presentation gaps**: The Gradient Probe Method is insufficiently explained; the word cloud in Fig. 6 does not aid understanding (R3). No clear principles show why LT‑Adapter’s fine‑tuning is beneficial over [3]’s method (R4). The balance between real and synthetic training samples is not discussed (R1 – question). Performance in a fully‑supervised setting compared to FreeMask is missing (R3 – question).
- **Typos and writing flaws**: Sec 3.3 header says “Adaptater” (R1). Other typos: “MathcMask” (line 152), “filtering startegy” (line 107), “Intergration” (line 513), “to to” (line 476). “Adaption” is used where “adaptation” is standard in academic writing (R3).
