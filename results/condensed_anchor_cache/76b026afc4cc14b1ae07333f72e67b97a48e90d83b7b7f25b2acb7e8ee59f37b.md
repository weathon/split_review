## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Backtracking Correction (BC), a method to enhance LLMs for RAG by reformulating stepwise reasoning into a multi-step decision-making process. BC employs self-play to correct the model’s own CoT errors in a backward manner, starting from the final step and backtracking to previous states, thereby simplifying learning and improving robustness against noisy documents. The method aims to reduce reliance on extensive annotations. Experimental evaluations on open-domain QA datasets (SQuAD, etc.) show improvements over some baselines, though one reviewer notes the gains are modest and inconsistent across benchmarks.

### Strengths
- Novel perspective: transforming stepwise RAG into a backtracking decision process, simplifying complex reasoning into manageable steps (R1, R3, R5). The use of self-play to generate corrected CoT mitigates the lack of annotations (R3).
- Well-written, well-structured, with informative figures that make the method easy to follow (R2, R4).
- Well-defined problem with a reasonable proposed method (R2).
- Eliminates the need for extensive external annotations, potentially reducing cost and complexity (R1, R5).
- Results generally favor the proposed method over strong baselines, including fine-tuned LLMs and proprietary models (R3, R4, R5). One reviewer (R4) notes modest gains; another (R1) finds the improvements not consistently significant.
- The paper includes an example (Table 2) and some analysis to illustrate how the method works (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparison and generalization**: Only one model (Llama-2-7B) is shown comparing BC against baseline training methods (R3, R4). Reviewers note that BC was also applied to Llama-3-8B (R2, R4), but a fair comparison would require using the same base models (e.g., LL2-7B-base, LL3-8B-base) as baselines, or incorporating chat models into the BC training (R2). Experiments with other model families and more complex RAG-QA tasks (e.g., multi-hop, long-form QA) are missing (R3, R4). One reviewer (R4) specifically requests comparisons of other models in the "Fine-tuned w/retrieval" section using BC.
- **Inconsistent or modest performance**: Three out of four benchmarks (excluding SQuAD) show either comparable performance or underperformance relative to baselines (R1, minority). One reviewer (R1) with rating 5 expresses lower confidence in the results, while the other four (rating 6) see only modest gains.
- **Absence of scaling and efficiency analysis**: The paper does not explore how BC scales with an increasing number of documents or reasoning depth (R1, R5). The binary decision tree grows rapidly with more documents, and no strategy for sampling paths is discussed (R2, R5). There is no analysis of wallclock training time or inference computational cost compared to baselines (R1, R4, R5). This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess practical feasibility, especially for large datasets or multi-hop questions (R5).
- **Missing ablation studies and implementation details**: The paper needs more ablation studies, especially for the efficiency claim and scaling with chain length (R1). Key parts of Section 3.3 are unclear: notation (e.g., Π_ref, o_t⁻), inputs/outputs after Eq. (6), and derivations for Eq. (10) and (12) are not immediately clear (R3). The presentation of Sec. 3.3 is overly complex and would benefit from subsections or a simple figure (R4). Equation numbering contains errors, causing confusion in citations (R5).
- **Data and annotation concerns**: The claim of "without additional data or annotations" is questionable because the model used for the original annotation (SCS, line 326) is a chat model, and it is unclear why the backbone model for BC was set differently (R2). One reviewer (R1) asks whether BC still requires labels for the reasoning chain as training data.
- **Dataset limitations**: Evaluation datasets (SQuAD, etc.) are fairly old and most likely appear in the training data of many investigated models (R4). They are mostly short-form factoid QA; generalization to more complex RAG-QA tasks is not shown (R3).
- **Lack of deeper analysis**: Only one example is shown in Table 2; more examples are needed to demonstrate how CoT correction works (R3). No analysis of relevance/irrelevance classification accuracy is provided, only end-to-end answering performance (R4). The paper does not analyze error types per reasoning step or handle extreme cases (all relevant, all irrelevant, ambiguous relevance) (R5).
- **Minor issues**: There is a typo ("err") in the abstract (R2). The name "backtracking" is somewhat unintuitive compared to typical backtracking algorithms (R3). Reviewers also ask about conditional dependency between document relevancy (R1), shuffling document order (R3), and the claim that BC training data/reference models are dynamic whereas DPO's are static (R1).
