## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of \(k\)-Nearest-Neighbor (kNN) attention, a sparse attention mechanism where each token attends only to its \(k\) closest tokens. It reformulates self-attention as an expectation over softmax distributions, then uses lazy Gumbel sampling combined with \(k\)-MIPS to obtain an unbiased estimate of self-attention with sub-quadratic time/space complexity. It also proposes novel algorithms for approximating self-attention gradients via 1-step random walks, achieving sub-quadratic complexity with high-probability bounds. Empirical experiments demonstrate scalability and effectiveness. Connections to prior work on Gumbel sampling and the probabilistic interpretation of attention are noted, though the novelty and clarity of the contributions are debated.

### Strengths
- **Relevance and impact:** Addresses the important problem of quadratic complexity in attention, particularly for long sequences (Reviewers 1,2,5).
- **Rigorous theoretical analysis:** Provides approximation bounds and complexity guarantees for kNN attention and gradient computation, connecting kNN attention to lazy Gumbel sampling and median-of-means boosting (Reviewers 1,5).
- **Elegant framing:** Reformulating self-attention as an expectation over softmax distributions is a well-known interpretation (Reviewers 2,4) but is applied here in a novel and elegant way to enable the theoretical analysis (Reviewer 5).
- **Novel gradient approximation:** Proposes a sub-quadratic algorithm for approximating self-attention gradients via Markov chain estimation (1-step random walks) with theoretical runtime bounds, which could have practical impact (Reviewers 1,2,3,5).
- **Clear pseudocode:** Algorithms are presented with clear pseudocode (Reviewer 4).
- **Open-source code:** Code is publicly available, supporting reproducibility (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Well-structured:** The paper is generally well-organized and presented (Reviewers 1,3); reviewer 1 finds it easy to read, though others raise clarity issues (see Weaknesses).
- **Empirical validation:** Experiments show scalability and reduction in computational/memory overhead (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Originality and novelty disputed:**
  - The interpretation of self-attention as an expectation is not new; similar formulations appear in prior work (e.g., arXiv:2304.04556) and should be explicitly cited as the source of the idea, not presented as a first contribution (Reviewers 2,4).
  - Much of Section 2 applies results from Mussmann et al. (2017) to attention; Theorems 5, 7, 8 and Lemma 6 should cite that work, and the novelty of the contribution relative to that work is unclear (Reviewers 3,4).
  - Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.3 restate known results (with a new proof); the paper should clarify that the results themselves are not novel (Reviewer 4).
  - Reviewer 5, however, views the framing and application as a “beautiful insight”, indicating disagreement on what constitutes sufficient novelty.

- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Multiple reviewers find the paper difficult to follow; it lacks a clear statement of contributions and what is new (Reviewers 2,3). Reviewer 1 disagrees, finding it well-written.
  - Notation problems: Gumbel\((a,b)\) and Bin\((a,b)\) are not defined (Reviewers 3,4); “BIN” in Algorithm 1 undefined (Reviewer 4); “\(k_k\)” in eq. (3) confusing (Reviewer 2); “\(T\)” in line 129 not explained (Reviewer 2); inconsistent dot notation in eq. (3) (Reviewer 4).
  - “kNN index” is insufficiently explained (Reviewer 4).
  - Equation (5) derivation is unclear: the multiplication of variance and expectation seems to drop an \(O_{ij}^{-1}\) term; a lower bound on \(O_{ij}\) should be stated (Reviewer 4).
  - Theorems lack precise assumptions (e.g., norms of \(Q,K,V\), source of randomness for \(1-\delta\)) (Reviewer 2). Theorem 8 omits the boundedness assumption on \(V_{sj}\) or the condition \(\|V\|_\infty=O(\log n)\) (Reviewer 4). Theorem 2’s time complexity should be clarified as expected time (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure mislabeling: the figure at the top of page 9 has no number (should be Fig 3); later references to Fig 3(a) are ambiguous and the figure at the bottom of page 9 should be renumbered (Reviewers 1,4).
  - Reference [2] is listed as arXiv but was published at UAI 2017 (Reviewer 4). Citation format inconsistent (Reviewer 4).
  - Line 222 (page 5) is unclear (Reviewer 4). Line 60 is confusing regarding connection to backward pass (Reviewer 2).
  - Lemma 1: “multiplicative estimate” is not defined (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing or incomplete proofs:**
  - No full proof provided for Theorem 10 (Reviewer 2), Theorem 2 (Reviewer 3), Theorem 29 (Reviewer 3). Theorem 4 and the proof sketch in line 181 are incomplete (Reviewer 2). Theorem 5 statement and proof are vague (Reviewer 3). Theorem 7 is stated as “easily derived from Algorithm 1” without justification (Reviewer 3).
  - The proof of Lemma 6 should use the moment-generating function of the Gumbel distribution explicitly (Reviewer 3).
  - Algorithm 2 takes \(\epsilon\) and \(\delta\) as parameters but they do not appear in the algorithm steps; their role is unclear, and no guidance on plausible ranges is given (Reviewer 3).

- **Experimental evaluation is insufficient and lacks validation:**
  - Experiments are qualitative and do not directly validate the theoretical bounds (Reviewer 2). Need benchmarks against other efficient attention methods (e.g., Nyströmformer, Skyformer) (Reviewer 3).
  - **Gradient approximation runtime:** On one reviewer’s laptop, the approximated gradient took approximately 100× longer than standard gradients (0.5831 s vs. 0.0061 s for kNN vs. traditional attention with \(B,H,N,D\) = 256,8,500,32, \(k=5\)); the paper must explain under what settings speed advantages are expected and provide code demonstrating it (Reviewer 2).
  - The backward pass experiment (Sec. 4.2) shows no speed comparison; true benefit would require larger \(n\) (e.g., \(n\sim1000\)) (Reviewer 1). The NanoGPT experiments do not clearly report time improvements (Reviewers 2,3).
  - Experimental parameters are missing: what is \(n\) for the perplexity/approximation-error experiments? Range of \(n\) for the \(k\) vs. \(n\) experiment? (Reviewer 1). Error definition in “Efficiency of kNN Attention” is not given (Reviewer 3).
  - Legend labels are ambiguous: “kNN” and “our algorithm” are not clearly defined (Reviewer 3). In Fig 3(a), “Brute Force” is not defined (Reviewer 4). Why does \(k=n^{1/4}\) outperform \(k=\sqrt{n}\) in that figure? (Reviewer 4).
  - Convex vs. non-convex distinction in the experimental setting is unclear; attention approximation is inherently non-convex due to softmax (Reviewer 3).

- **Gap between theory and practice:**
  - Theory suggests optimal \(k=\sqrt{n}\), but experiments show good performance for \(k=n^{1/8}\), much smaller. The paper does not explain this gap or whether capturing the tail of interactions (via the sampling outside top-\(k\) in Sec. 2.3) is critical for small \(k\) (Reviewers 1,5). Theorem 8’s condition \(k\propto\sqrt{n}\) may be unrealistic for large \(n\) (Reviewer 3).
  - No practical guideline for selecting \(k\) is derived; sensitivity to the distance metric used for nearest neighbors is not explored (Reviewer 5).
  - The paper would benefit from a discussion on how the method compares to other sparse attention and low-rank linear attention methods under various sequence lengths and computational constraints (Reviewer 5).

- **Additional weaknesses and questions:**
  - How do the two approximation methods in Sections 2.2 and 2.3 compare directly? (Reviewer 4).
  - Would performance change if \(Q,K,V\) were sampled from more biased, real-world distributions? (Reviewer 4).
  - Could the gradient-approximation algorithm be applied to other modalities (e.g., Vision Transformers)? (Reviewer 5).
  - Code is provided but lacks a setup.py for easy installation (Reviewer 2).
  - Algorithm 3 uses “lg” instead of “log”; the selection of \(N\) in that algorithm is not explained; what is \(1^n\)? (Reviewer 3).
