## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses local fairness, ensuring model fairness within subregions unknown at training time. It proposes ROAD (and BROAD) combining Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) with adversarial learning: an adversary predicts the sensitive attribute from predictions, and instance-level re-weighting prioritizes locally unfair regions. Two implementations: BROAD uses adversary loss directly; ROAD uses a neural network to predict weights. Experiments on three datasets show Pareto dominance in accuracy vs. local fairness and improved fairness generalization under distribution shift.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important practical issue of local fairness, which is a step towards ensuring the reliability of group fair learning methods. The problem formulation is clear and practically relevant.
- The combination of DRO with adversarial learning is novel and effective. The setting does not require specifying subgroups during training, allowing fairness w.r.t. any subregion via worst-case distribution.
- The experimental results are strong: ROAD achieves consistent Pareto dominance over baselines across three standard datasets for different fairness levels (shown using trade-off curves). Results also demonstrate improved fairness generalization under distribution shift.
- The paper is well-written with clear exposition and concise descriptions of the two implementations (BROAD, ROAD). One reviewer (score 8) particularly praised the novelty and clarity of the main contribution, and found the method simple yet effective.

### Weaknesses
- **Baseline reliability and comparison fairness:** Some baselines in Fig 3 and 4 do not span all ranges of fairness, raising concerns about whether hyperparameter search was sufficiently exhaustive. This could unfairly penalize methods with limited achievable fairness range. It was also questioned whether the best results for ROAD were selected with a non-learnable λ_g (Sec A.5.4) to achieve the claimed Pareto dominance.
- **Complexity and lack of guidance:** The problem formulation is overly complex with multiple nested minimization problems. The proposed approach has many hyperparameters and involves unstable min-max adversarial optimization; the paper does not provide sufficient guidance on hyperparameter choice or discuss computational cost. No strong convergence guarantees are provided even for linear models.
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation:**
  - Global fairness results (e.g., average demographic parity) are not reported; only local fairness (worst DI) is shown.
  - Results with Equalized Odds (EO) and local fairness are missing.
  - Demographic parity under distribution shift is not reported.
  - The uncertainty set's ability to capture real-world distribution shifts in Sec 4.2 is unclear; particularly the relevance of the assumption q(s)=p(s) for that shift.
- **Conceptual and theoretical concerns:**
  - The definition of local fairness may be problematic when subregions are very small (e.g., age bins with few samples); disparity could arise from small sample size rather than bias. It may be necessary to incorporate priors like a lower bound on subgroup ratio (as in DRO) or consider insights from Blind Pareto Fairness (ICML 2021) on small groups leading to uniform classifiers.
  - The choice of uncertainty set Q and the assumption of equal group priors (q(s)=p(s)) need better justification. It is unclear if this implies constant proportions across subregions and whether that assumption is reasonable for local fairness applications.
  - Why distribution q helps characterize local fairness is not clearly explained; DRO normally serves as an upper bound over subregions.
  - It was questioned why the authors work with the adversary formulation in Eq. (4) instead of the simpler robust constraint in Eq. (3); instance-level weighting could potentially be applied to (3) directly without the additional argmin.
  - Implementation details need clarification: how to implement w_g when f outputs a single probability (binary case); whether the s in Eq. (4) is properly positioned; whether the Softmax weights in ROAD correspond to Shannon entropy regularization.
  - The novelty of the method relative to Michel et al 2022 needs justification.
- **Comparison of BROAD vs ROAD:** It is unintuitive that BROAD (exact) performs worse than ROAD (NN). The paper's explanation (smoothing) could be verified; perhaps a simple EMA of BROAD weights would suffice. Additionally, the weight normalization in Fig 4 left (are values unnormalized? Should r be positive and less than 1?) needs clarification.
- **Missing limitations and related work:** The paper does not address limitations such as computational scalability compared to group fairness methods. It omits relevant prior work on fairness and adversarial learning. The introduction's first paragraph lacks citations for claims about models perpetuating biases.
