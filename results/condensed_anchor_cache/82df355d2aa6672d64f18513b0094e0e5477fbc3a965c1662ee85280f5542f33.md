## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses CATE estimation under hidden confounding by combining a large observational dataset with a small RCT dataset that provides only outcomes (no covariates). It proposes two regularizations – Marginals Balancing (MB) and Projections Balancing (PB) – plus a pseudo‑confounder generator, and aligns learned potential outcomes with RCT outcomes. Experiments show the combined MB+PB method outperforms baselines on synthetic and real‑world data. Reviewers are split: one is moderately positive (score 6) about the problem setting and flexibility, while the other three are negative (scores 3, 3, 1) due to core assumptions, lack of theory, and insufficient evaluation.

### Strengths
- The problem of CATE estimation under hidden confounders is important, and the setting where only outcomes (no covariates) are available from the RCT is realistic for privacy‑sensitive applications (e.g., medical data). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The proposed regularizations are model‑agnostic, allowing use with any CATE model (e.g., neural networks). (Reviewer 2)
- The idea of enforcing a balancing constraint to learn CATE is novel and interesting. (Reviewer 4)
- Overall presentation is clear (Reviewer 2) – though other reviewers disagree on clarity; this contrast is noted.

### Weaknesses
- **Critical unrealistic assumption**: The identification strategy hinges on the assumption that potential outcome distributions are *identical* in the RCT and observational populations. RCT populations systematically differ from observational populations (e.g., sicker patients and minorities are typically underrepresented), so this assumption is almost never plausible in practice. The paper does not discuss this limitation. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Lack of theoretical justification**: No formal analysis is provided for why the pseudo‑confounder generator and the outcome alignment reduce bias, under what conditions the method converges to the true CATE, or how regularization parameters affect bias reduction. The paper also lacks guarantees for the generated confounder. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: 
  - Methods for CATE estimation under hidden confounding (e.g., sensitivity analysis, partial identification) are not discussed or compared. (Reviewer 3)
  - The factual learner baseline is known to be biased; using it as a comparator gives misleading insights. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient evaluation**: Only one low‑dimensional synthetic dataset with a very simple data generation mechanism is used. Proper evaluation of CATE estimators requires multiple semi‑synthetic datasets with higher‑dimensional unobserved confounding. (Reviewer 3)
- **Method limited to binary treatments**; this is a disadvantage compared to existing CATE estimators that handle continuous or multi‑category treatments. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation issues**: 
  - Errors and limited flow hinder readability (Reviewer 3). 
  - Extensive use of bold font is distracting (Reviewer 4). 
  - Section 3.1 on hidden confounding is unnecessary and unfocused (Reviewer 3). 
  - Definitions and notation are unclear (e.g., “pseudo‑confounder”, “g” in Proposition 3.2, Equation 8 needs more explanation). (Reviewer 3)
- **Counter‑intuitive result**: MB+PB sometimes outperforms methods that use RCT covariates (e.g., CorNet on ACTG dataset). This requires explanation. (Reviewer 3)
- **Questionable real‑world setting**: Scenarios where RCT covariates are unavailable are rare; examples and a justification of plausibility are needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational complexity** is not discussed, which could be an issue for large datasets. (Reviewer 2)
- **Figure 8 concerns**: 
  - The PEHE (even in sqrt scale) remains high, making the method’s usefulness unclear. 
  - Obs‑Oracle PEHE increases with log(Gamma) even when no unobserved confounding is present – this is unexplained. 
  - MB+PB is not robust to Gamma as claimed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Influence of RCT sample size**: The effect of varying the small RCT dataset size on estimation quality is not thoroughly examined; general conclusions cannot be drawn from the current experiments. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing discussion of transportability**: The paper does not acknowledge that even the weaker assumption of equal conditional means (E[Y|X]) is often considered too strong; the paper’s stronger assumption of identical full distributions is even more problematic. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor omissions**: 
  - Limited referencing (e.g., line 70, 356). (Reviewer 3)
  - Consistency assumption is implicitly assumed but not stated (line 35). (Reviewer 3)
  - “Performance metric” heading on line 58 does not contain a metric. (Reviewer 3)
