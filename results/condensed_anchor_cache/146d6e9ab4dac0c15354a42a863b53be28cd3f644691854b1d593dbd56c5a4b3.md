## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Guided Newton-Raphson Inversion (GNRI) for diffusion inversion, applying Newton-Raphson with L1 norm dimensionality reduction and a guiding regularization to avoid out-of-distribution solutions. It achieves inversion within 0.4 seconds on an A100 GPU for few-step models (SDXL-Turbo, Flux), enabling interactive editing. Experiments on Stable Diffusion, SDXL-Turbo, and Flux show improved speed and quality over existing inversion methods across tasks including image editing, interpolation, and rare concept generation. Reviewers were divided: two rated 8, one 6, one 5. Positive reviewers praised speed and clarity; the more critical reviewer questioned the novelty of the norm reduction and noted similarities to prior work, as well as memory usage and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- Novel application of Newton-Raphson with two key modifications: scalar L1 norm to reduce Jacobian dimensionality and a guiding regularization to keep latents in-distribution. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- Exceptionally fast inversion: ~0.4 seconds on A100 for SDXL-Turbo and Flux, enabling real-time interactive editing. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Extensive empirical validation across three models (SD, SDXL-Turbo, Flux) and multiple tasks (reconstruction, editing, interpolation, rare concept generation). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Clear motivation and well-structured method description. (Reviewer 2)
- High reconstruction and editing quality, outperforming prior FPI methods (ReNoise, AIDI) in both speed and fidelity. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)

### Weaknesses
- The fixed-point iteration (FPI) concept is not entirely new (AIDI, ReNoise). (Reviewer 1)
- The norm reduction in Sec. 4.1 is standard; ReNoise uses l2-norm and ExactDPM uses gradient descent on the l2-norm. The guiding regularizer in Sec. 4.2 resembles Pan et al.'s Anderson acceleration, which also prevents large changes. The significant performance drop when λ=0 (Fig. C2) suggests instability of the unguided NR (NRI). (Reviewer 4)
- Memory usage: GNRI requires gradients (torch.grad) leading to high memory consumption, whereas gradient-free methods like AIDI and ExactDPM can use smaller GPUs (ExactDPM reported using a smaller GPU than A100). (Reviewer 4)
- Objective in Eq. 9 combines residual r(z_t) and regularization G(z_t) in a way that seems contradictory: a solution to r(z_t)=0 yields G(z_t)>0, making F(z_t)>0, so the method solves an approximate rather than exact inversion. It is unclear why GNRI achieves a lower residual than NRI despite the additional regularization (Fig. 3a). What is the residual when setting z_t = mu_t (G=0)? (Reviewer 1)
- Missing comparisons: No results for DDPM Inversion (from “An Edit Friendly DDPM Noise Space: Inversion and Manipulations”) on reconstruction speed and quality, nor on PIE-Bench editing. (Reviewer 3)
- Missing analysis: PSNR vs. NFE would clarify inversion efficiency. (Reviewer 2)
- Unclear whether the method supports multiple-object editing. (Reviewer 2)
- The baseline in Figure 3 is unspecified—should clarify whether it is AIDI or ReNoise. (Reviewer 1)
- Rare concept generation results (Table 3) do not show significant improvements over existing methods. (Reviewer 4)
- Writing inconsistencies and errors: referencing Eq. 5 instead of Eq. 4 (L238); function F defined both as ℝ^D→ℝ^D (L203) and ℝ^D→ℝ^+ (Eq. 9); duplicate citation (L577); incorrect year for EDICT (CVPR 2023, not 2022). (Reviewers 1, 4)
- Figure 2 has a linebreak issue. (Reviewer 2)
- The back-propagation per iteration in Eq. 10 adds cost compared to ReNoise; the paper’s efficiency numbers (Fig.4, Table 1) show similar speed to DDIM and better than ReNoise, but the reason for this discrepancy is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
- More visual comparisons for editing tasks on PIE-Bench are requested. (Reviewer 3)
