## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes I-ADV, an intensity-based voxel-wise adversarial attack on LiDAR point cloud detection. It uses voxel partitioning, a gradient enhancement technique combining 3D angle and distance features, and an extremum-based gradient fusion strategy. Evaluated on KITTI (car category), it claims state-of-the-art attack success rates (83.9–99.1%) against several detectors. Two reviewers rated the paper 3 (weak reject) and one rated it 6 (weak accept), reflecting substantial disagreement: Reviewer 3 found the contributions stronger and more promising than the other two.

### Strengths
- **Innovative attack direction**: First work to attack point cloud intensity, which is novel and thought-provoking (all reviewers).
- **Strong attack performance**: Achieves significantly higher attack success rates than selected geometry-based baselines (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Includes ablation studies, transferability evaluation, and scenario-level interference discussion (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Real-world design efforts**: Voxel-level perturbations (rather than per-point) enhance physical applicability; algorithm design and theoretical feasibility (Figure 1) are clearly presented (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Clear exposition**: Writing is accessible, with good motivation, explanation of method components, and easy-to-understand conceptual diagrams (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset evaluation**: Only tested on KITTI (single dataset, car category), limiting generalizability. No introduction of the dataset or its difficulty levels (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Lack of physical-world validation**: Experiments are entirely digital; the title’s “Invisible Stickers” concept is not realized. No concrete sticker materials, mechanisms, or real-world deployment pictures are provided (Reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer notes that controlling reflection intensity across an entire vehicle is physically infeasible (Reviewer 2).
- **Unreasonable experimental setup for practicality**:
  - Global perturbations on all points are allowed; controlling intensity across a whole object surface is unrealistic (Reviewer 2).
  - Maximum perturbation bound is 1 ([0,1] range), which adds to practicality concerns (Reviewer 2).
  - At small voxel sizes (~0.015m estimated from Figure 3a), most voxels contain one point; Equation (8) then forces positive gradient, pushing all intensities toward 1. The authors did not report the specific voxel sizes used in that ablation or visualize results at the minimum voxel size (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions about the source of attack success**: Visualizations (Figure 4) show that attacks tend to extreme intensities (mostly blue/low or red/high), and attack performance correlates with number of high-intensity points. A recent study [1] found that simply increasing intensity reduces detector confidence. The reviewer questions whether I-ADV’s gain comes from pushing intensities to 1 rather than from the gradient enhancement/fusion techniques, and asks why not directly set all intensities to 1 (Reviewer 2).
- **Component analysis omission**: The extremum-based gradient fusion contributes most (Table 3) but the paper neither explains its motivation nor analyzes why it outperforms other strategies. The contributions of 3D angle and distance features appear minimal (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Model applicability and comparison gaps**:
  - Only effective on intensity-aware detectors; geometry-based (xyz) detectors are more common in practice (Reviewer 1).
  - White-box attack requiring gradient access, limiting real-world applicability (Reviewer 1).
  - No evaluation on transformer-based detectors (Reviewer 1); more recent attack methods should be compared (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experimental details**:
  - How were victim models trained? Were official pre-trained models used? Was intensity used during training? A table of experimental settings would help (Reviewer 1).
  - Line 248: “access the gradients” is confusing; Algorithm 1 line 6: function L (loss) is not defined (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 3 x-axis labels repeat subplot titles (Reviewer 2).
