## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MAGNeT, a masked generative sequence modeling method operating on multi-stream audio tokens from the EnCodec neural audio codec. It uses a single-stage, non-autoregressive transformer trained to predict spans of masked tokens, with inference gradually constructing the output through multiple decoding steps. Additional techniques include: span masking instead of individual token masking; a rescoring method leveraging an external pre-trained model (MusicGen, AudioGen) to rank predictions; annealing of the classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale during inference; and a hybrid version that fuses autoregressive and non-autoregressive generation. The model achieves competitive results on text-to-music and text-to-audio generation compared to baselines (MusicGen, Mousai, AudioLDM2, MusicLM) with a 7–10× speedup in latency over autoregressive baselines (depending on batch size). Ablation studies and analysis of design choices are provided.

### Strengths
- **Span masking strategy.** Masking spans of tokens rather than individual tokens is an important methodological contribution, well-justified through an analysis of EnCodec’s impulse response (shifted impulse function). It greatly impacts performance and mitigates information leakage between neighboring tokens. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Restricted context for RVQ levels.** The use of full self-attention for the first RVQ token stream and smaller attention spans (derived from the LSTM’s receptive field) for higher code streams is intuitively motivated and supported by ablation studies. This design choice is insightful for understanding dependencies between multi-level RVQ tokens. (R2)
- **Speedup.** The 7–10× reduction in latency is remarkable and particularly valuable for music generation, where many tokens must be sampled due to long contexts. (R1, R3)
- **Accuracy-latency tradeoff analysis.** The analysis of decoding strategies for first-level vs. higher-level RVQ tokens is helpful for designing efficient decoding. The analysis methods are generalizable to future codec models and will likely become useful tools. (R2)
- **Comprehensive evaluation.** Both objective metrics (FAD, KL divergence, CLAP score) and subjective metrics (MOS, audio-text relevance via crowd-sourced evaluations) are used. Ablation studies clarify the contribution of individual components. (R3)
- **Model rescoring and CFG annealing.** The rescoring technique (using MusicGen/AudioGen) and annealing of the CFG parameter are noted as enhancements to robustness and quality, though their novelty is debated (see Weaknesses). (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty overclaim and concurrent work.** The title “Masked Audio Generation” suggests a first proposal, but SoundStorm and VampNet (arXiv:2307.04686) already applied masked generative modeling to audio. The authors should change the title to reflect a set of improvements and include at least one of these as a baseline. (R1)  
  R2 and R3 also mention concurrent work: R2 recommends discussing differences with VampNet; R3 notes omission of “Masked Autoencoders that Listen” which uses a similar span masking idea.
- **Limited novelty of some components.** Aside from span masking, the other techniques (rescoring, CFG annealing, hybrid model) are not critical or novel. The rescoring method improves FAD only marginally. (R1)  
  R2 and R3 consider these contributions valuable but do not dispute the novelty concern directly; R2 requests quantitative analysis of the CFG annealing.
- **Missing quantitative analysis of CFG annealing.** The paper argues for annealing the CFG scale during inference but does not provide a table showing performance for different starting/ending values and their impact on generation quality. This makes the claim less rigorous. (R2; also noted as a question by R1: “why does CFG anneal perform better?”)
- **Dataset details insufficient.** The description of training datasets is lacking: which baselines were trained on which data? (MusicGen, Mousai use the same dataset; MusicLM, AudioLDM2 use different data.) A table or paragraph with size, composition, and preprocessing steps is needed for fair comparison. (R2, R3)
- **Counterintuitive model size result.** The smaller 300M model performs better than the 1.5B model in some metrics; this is not explained. (R1)
- **Restricted context improvement unclear.** The intuition that restricted context only improves training/inference speed does not explain why it also improves objective metrics. Clarification is needed. (R1)
- **Scheduler formula issue.** The masking scheduler γ(i; s) = cos(π(i−1)/2s) is poorly defined because at i=1 it returns 1, contradicting the intended behavior. (R1)
- **Reproducibility concerns.** The heavy engineering involved makes reproducing the results extremely difficult for the community. (R3)
- **No quantitative analysis of rescoring.** While R1 notes the improvement is marginal, no systematic ablation is shown to isolate its effect beyond the stated FAD numbers. (Implied by R1’s criticism.)
- **Minority reviewer disagreement.** Reviewer 1 (score 6) is less convinced overall, questioning the novelty, marginal gains, and unexplained behaviors. Reviewer 2 (score 8) and Reviewer 3 (score 8) are more positive, praising the thorough justification and analysis. This contrast should be considered when assessing the paper’s contributions.
