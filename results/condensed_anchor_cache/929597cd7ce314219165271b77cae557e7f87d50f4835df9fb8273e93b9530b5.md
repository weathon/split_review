## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a structured benchmark of 12,000 plate geometries with finite element method (FEM) solutions for vibroacoustic frequency response prediction. It proposes a frequency query operator model (FQ-Operator) that maps plate geometries to frequency response functions, combining operator learning with implicit shape encoding. The method is evaluated on the benchmark and compared against DeepONets, Fourier Neural Operators, kNN, and other architectures.

### Strengths
- The problem (frequency response prediction for vibrating plates) currently lacks a structured benchmark, making the dataset a valuable contribution.
- The data is generated by solving a plate theory differential equation with FEM, which provides theoretical convergence guarantees.
- A thorough literature review on related operator learning and vibroacoustic methods is given.
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow; limitations are explicitly discussed.
- The proposed model is reasonable and shows some novelty in its formulation, though the major contribution is the benchmark and problem framing.
- The choice of baselines includes simpler methods like kNN (based on a low-dimensional autoencoder embedding), which is positive for comparison.
- Ablation studies are included in the appendix.
- The code is well-organized overall, with a clear folder/module structure.
- Reviewers acknowledge that the paper addresses an important application of machine learning in science and holds a unique position in this domain.

### Weaknesses
- **Methodological novelty and conference fit**: Several reviewers question whether the work has sufficient methodological contribution for a top machine learning conference (ICLR). Reviewer 1 rates contribution as "fair" and worries about limited audience; Reviewer 3 initially scores 5 but later indicates the paper better fits a physics venue, suggesting changes to title, abstract, and framing to better target ML readers. Reviewer 5 rates contribution higher (4/4) but also expresses initial uncertainty about fit (confidence 1). There is clear disagreement on this point.
- **Missing details on data generation**:
  - The FEM implementation is underspecified: element type (shell/solid), number of nodes, interpolation order, and linear solver algorithm are not reported. This affects assessment of accuracy and computational cost.
  - The aggregation method (mean squared velocity) is not justified; references or standard practice are not provided.
  - The setting assumes a single excitation point with zero initial displacement; it is unclear if this is standard and whether the plate is static.
  - The accuracy with which the FEM solution satisfies the original differential equation is not quantified.
- **Evaluation methodology**:
  - Results are reported on a single test split without cross-validation or repeated subsampling, making it hard to assess generalization and variability. (Reviewer 5 later noted that cross-validation results were provided in the rebuttal, but the paper as submitted lacked this.)
  - Model sizes (e.g., number of parameters) are not reported, so it is unclear if the proposed method outperforms baselines due to larger capacity. Hyperparameter tuning details are absent.
  - Table 3 (transfer learning setup) does not include baseline performances; these comparisons are critical.
  - No experiments test generalization to frequencies not seen during training, despite operator models being touted for continuous frequency queries.
- **Real-world validation**: The dataset is entirely synthetic; no experiments on real-world measurements are conducted. The domain shift between simulation and reality is not discussed. (Reviewers note that real data acquisition is expensive but suggest even a small real-world experiment would strengthen practical relevance.)
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Mesh geometry figures are blurred and not clearly readable.
  - Figure 6 visualizations are criticized: subfigures (b) and (d) do not appear similar enough, and baseline comparisons are missing.
  - The mechanical model (plate theory equations) should be in the main text rather than relegated to the appendix, as it is essential for understanding the dataset and methodology.
  - The term "grid-based models" is confusing; FNO can work on regular grids, so the distinction from other grid-based methods needs clarification.
- **Repository and reproducibility**:
  - The anonymous repository uses Weights & Biases for logging without explanation.
  - The training command in the README does not match the folder structure.
  - The setup script lacks specific package versions (except PyTorch CUDA), risking numerical inconsistencies across environments—critical for scientific computing.
- **Additional concerns**:
  - No noise is added to the data; predicting clean data from large models is less challenging from a data-fitting perspective.
  - How easily plate geometries with the described beading patterns can be acquired in practice is not discussed.
  - The claim that "geometry is represented with a neural network" in the introduction is considered imprecise from an ML perspective.
