## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses image interpolation with diffusion models for natural images (not generated by diffusion models). Existing spherical linear interpolation (slerp) fails because the encoding noise no longer obeys the expected distribution. The authors propose NoiseDiffusion, which corrects invalid noise by adding subtle Gaussian noise and imposing a clipping constraint to suppress extreme values, then performs interpolation in the noisy image space and injects raw images to avoid information loss. Experiments on bedroom and cat images qualitatively compare NoiseDiffusion against slerp and slerp combined with SDEdit.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly identifies and addresses the limitations of spherical linear interpolation for natural images, explaining the failure via the concentration of probability in high dimensions and mismatched noise levels, supported by a numerical experiment (Fig. 3) and Theorem 1.
- Theorem 2 guides the solution, leading to a novel, simple interpolation method that combines an SDEdit-like stage, latent interpolation, and denoising. The method generalizes standard slerp and slerp+SDEdit as special cases for specific parameter choices.
- Qualitative results demonstrate superiority over existing methods (slerp and slerp+SDEdit) on bedroom and cat images.
- The approach introduces free parameters to address contrast and trade-off consistency with each source image, and appears to have considerable practical utility; expectations are that it will be of practical use.

### Weaknesses
- **Parameter selection**: The method introduces many empirical parameters (α, β, γ, μ, ν, and boundary in Section 3.5, plus interpolation coefficient λ in slerp). Practical guidance for selecting these parameters remains unclear; ranges and their impact on the final result are not well-defined, making reproduction and application in different contexts difficult. While the effect of each parameter is experimentally demonstrated, systematic tuning guidance is lacking.
- **Limited evaluation**: Experimental evaluation is limited to qualitative comparisons on a small set of images (bedroom and cats), without quantitative metrics over a large test set. The method does not perform equally well across all image types (see Figure 6), indicating a potential limitation in applicability. Further validation on diverse image domains and a summary metric to quantify improvement over a test set are needed. One reviewer specifically requested thoughts on how performance could be assessed over a large dataset.
- **Computational and implementation concerns**: Computational overhead and potential limitations in practical scenarios are not discussed. The experiments assume a simplified scenario with zero drift coefficient (μ(x_t,t)=0), which may not reflect real-world complexities. The cost of the clipping operation is not addressed. Boundary control methods are introduced in Section 4.4 but not explained in the main text; Appendix B (which is referenced) feels incomplete and only contains an additional figure.
- **Presentation and literature quality**: Presentation quality rated fair by one reviewer (2/4) and excellent by another (4/4), indicating mixed opinions on clarity and completeness. The Introduction is considered too short, and the number of references is too small. Literature review on diffusion probabilistic models for image generation is insufficient. Key prior work (SDEdit) is not briefly described, and the methods introduced in Section 2.1 lack references. Minor typographical issues persist: missing spaces after periods and commas, and a typo on page 2 after Eq. (1) where g should be replaced with σ. One reviewer noted that the proof of Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 should explicitly point to Appendices A and B in the main body.
- **Clarity issues**: The variable ε_t in Eq. (4) is not explicitly defined; it should be clarified whether “Gaussian noise” is i.i.d. or AWGN. These issues were identified in an initial review and reportedly addressed in the revised paper.
