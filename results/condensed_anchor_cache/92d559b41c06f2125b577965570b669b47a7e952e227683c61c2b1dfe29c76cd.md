## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the correlation between input noise and generated images in diffusion models using deterministic DDIM sampling. It shows that a one-step approximation of the mapping learned by these models closely relates to Zero-phase Component Analysis (ZCA) inverse whitening transform. Based on this observation, the paper proposes a model-agnostic simulated annealing method to sample correlated noises and demonstrates applications in image variation generation and editing, including improvements to SDEdit.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The analysis of noise-image correlation with image whitening operators is novel and interesting (Reviewer 1). The phenomenon is observed across many diffusion models (Reviewers 4,6).
- The idea is well-motivated and supported by detailed analysis, with comparisons to other de-whitening alternatives (e.g., PCA, Cholesky) (Reviewers 2,3).
- The paper demonstrates that the first-step inference approximates a linear transformation that is model-agnostic (Reviewer 4).
- The simulated annealing algorithm for correlated noise is simple and effective for generating image variations and editing, yielding decent generation quality (Reviewers 2,6). The editing experiment is nice (Reviewer 3).
- Qualitative results applied to SDEdit are promising (Reviewer 5); the method better preserves image structure at high noise levels compared to traditional approaches (Reviewer 6).
- The optimized noise can be applied across different diffusion models, enhancing generalizability (Reviewer 6).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of quantitative results and comparisons:** The paper provides only qualitative results (mostly on a few images), without quantitative metrics or comparisons to existing baselines. This makes it difficult to objectively assess performance advantages (Reviewers 1,2,5). Specifically, no performance/efficiency comparison with other DDIM inversion techniques (Reviewer 2) and no quantitative evaluation for SDEdit (Reviewer 1). Results appear to be cherry-picked from few images (Reviewers 3,5).
- **Novelty concerns:** The observed correlation is not surprising given that DDIM is deterministic (Reviewer 1). Fixed-point iteration to find noise corresponding to an image has been claimed in prior work: "*The Emergence of Reproducibility and Generalizability in Diffusion Models*" (ICML24) and applied to editing in "*On Exact Inversion of DPM-Solvers*" (CVPR24), "*ReNoise*" (ECCV24), and "*Lightning-Fast Image Inversion*" (Arxiv23) (Reviewer 5). The claim that the first step is similar to ZCA de-whitening is overclaimed; better theoretical understanding exists via Optimal Transport (ICLR23) (Reviewer 5).
- **Insufficient theoretical justification:** The paper lacks a rigorous mathematical explanation for why Hypothesis 1 holds (Reviewer 1). The analysis is based on observed assumptions without rigorous proof (Reviewer 6). There is no real theory for why ZCA emerges specifically (Reviewers 3,5). The discovery appears purely observational; Equation (8) is presented without theoretical motivation (Reviewer 6).
- **Second part (editing and simulated annealing) is trivial:** Checking after one denoising step whether the output is similar to the original is expected to yield the shown results (Reviewer 3). The potential applications are limited because the finding only holds for the first step; image variation generation is not widely discussed (Reviewer 4). The phenomenon is not amazing because any non-linear function can be approximated by a linear function within a small interval (Reviewer 4).
- **Too narrow on ZCA:** Focusing solely on whitening is too narrow; other linear transformations (e.g., identity) may also yield good performance, as suggested by experiments in Section 4 (Reviewer 4). Stronger evidence is needed that ZCA is the best approximation, with comparison to more commonly used linear transformations (Reviewer 4).
- **Efficiency concerns:** Simulated annealing requires multiple iterations, affecting efficiency (Reviewer 6). No comparison of computation cost or search time versus other baselines (Reviewer 1). The advantages of simulated annealing over gradient-based optimization are not discussed (Reviewer 5).
- **Dependence on data distribution:** The effectiveness of ZCA de-whitening depends on the data distribution, which may limit performance on unseen datasets (Reviewer 6).
- **Specific technical questions:**
  - Why was \( t = 0.98T \) chosen? Could \( t = T \) be used instead? (Reviewer 6)
  - For different models, the assumption implies that the optimal solution for the same \( z_0 \) should be the same, which seems counterintuitive given that \( \epsilon_{\theta} \) differ (Reviewer 6).
  - SDEdit performs best at 40-60% timesteps, which contradicts the paper's hypothesis that \( t \) needs to be very large (Reviewer 6).
  - How does the method compare to inversion-based methods that achieve precise reconstruction (e.g., Noise Map Guidance, Inversion-free editing, PnP Inversion)? The proposed method cannot achieve complete reconstruction; what are its advantages for complex scenes (with rich backgrounds or multiple objects)? (Reviewer 6).
  - Could simply adding random noise to \( z_0 \) to obtain \( z_t \) and then denoising already yield good results, since \( z_t \) still contains information from \( z_0 \)? (Reviewer 6).
  - Why not use DDIM inversion to get results similar to those in Figure 1? (Reviewer 1).
  - The hypothesis about the gap between Diff and ZCA being due to the subset of ImageNet could be tested by varying the data size used to compute ZCA (Reviewer 3).
  - Please provide additional applications for the discovery (Reviewer 4).
  - The claim that noise learning is correlated is not surprising, as diffusion models learn different levels of detail throughout the process, not just Gaussian noise; the justification for Equation (6) is not well-founded (Reviewer 3).
