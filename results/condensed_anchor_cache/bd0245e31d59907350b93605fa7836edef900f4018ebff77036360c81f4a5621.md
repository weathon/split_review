## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Meshtron, an autoregressive mesh generation model that generates meshes from point clouds with up to 64K faces at 1024-level coordinate resolution. The approach uses an hourglass neural architecture, truncated sequence training, sliding window inference, and a robust sampling strategy to reduce training memory by >50% and achieve 2.5× faster throughput compared to existing methods. The method is validated on artist-created meshes and text-to-3D generated meshes.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge of generating high-quality meshes with significantly higher face counts (64K) and coordinate resolution (1024-level) than prior work.  
- Clear articulation of motivation and intuition behind the design, supported by real-world data observations.  
- Well-executed ablation studies; architectural choices are reasonable, well explained, and easy to understand.  
- The training procedure (hourglass architecture, truncated sequences, sliding window) allows scaling to longer and more complex meshes while reducing memory and improving throughput.  
- The intermediate latent representation exploit the geometric construction of meshes, and the appendix is useful for reproducibility.  
- The method demonstrates versatility by conditioning on various mesh types (artist-created, text-to-3D).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear justification for Hourglass Transformer in relation to last-vertex perplexity** (R1, R3). The paper claims that last-vertex tokens have high perplexity, motivating the hourglass design, but does not explain how hierarchical processing/multi-scale features specifically mitigate that perplexity compared to other architectures. There is no obvious special treatment of last-vertex tokens in the hourglass structure.  
- **Overstated claim about prior work** (R2). The claim that previous works are limited to 1.6K faces is not accurate; papers such as FlexiCubes [Shen et al. 2023] and Deep Marching Tetrahedra [Shen et al. 2021] achieve higher face counts and are not compared. This weakens the claimed novelty.  
- **Insufficient experimental comparisons** (R1, R2, R3, R4). Only one baseline is compared; missing comparisons with methods like MeshGPT, CLAY [1], FlexiCubes, DMTet, and other high-face-count mesh generation or iso-surfacing techniques. A dedicated Related Work section is also missing.  
- **Lack of robustness evaluation on noisy or imperfect inputs** (R1, R3). The paper uses clean, curated point clouds but does not test on noisy point clouds, varied noise levels, or partial point clouds. This limits assessment of practical utility.  
- **Impact of point cloud density not explored** (R2). No analysis of how varying point densities affect mesh quality.  
- **Missing details on sliding window inference** (R4). It is unclear how the sliding window size is chosen during inference and how a larger mesh is generated from truncated training sequences.  
- **Unclear meaning of intermediate representation** (R4). The latent representation is vaguely described; Section 3.1 should be expanded to clarify its explicit meaning (vertex/face representation).  
- **Claim of 64K-face generation not supported by examples** (R4). No actual 64K-face meshes are shown in the paper or supplementary material. Is this a theoretical limit or demonstrated result?  
- **Inappropriate phrasing** (R4): The sentence “MESHTRON does not work from time to time” is not suitable for a scientific paper.  
- **Global conditioning mechanism unclear** (R4). How is the input point cloud used? Is it fed and then the sequence used for cross-attention?  
- **Scalability beyond 64K faces not addressed** (R1). Real scenes often require millions of faces; the paper does not discuss whether the method can handle such scales.  
- **Minor reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 3 (score 3) is notably more critical, emphasizing the unclear causality, missing noise experiments, and lack of related work. Reviewer 2 (confidence 2) expressed willingness to change rating if the comparison issue is clarified, indicating lower confidence. Reviewer 4 (score 5) still found key details insufficient.
