## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data augmentation method for text-to-image generation on small datasets: linearly extrapolate text features, use the extrapolated features to retrieve images from the web, then apply outlier detection. It also introduces a Recurrent Affine Transformation (RAT) block and NULL guidance for the diffusion model. The approach is evaluated on CUB, Oxford, and COCO.

### Strengths
- The proposed approach appears effective in Table 1 (R1). Extensive analysis and ablation studies are provided (R1, R3, R5).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear figures illustrating data linear extrapolation and network architecture (R2, R5). The method is simple and well-explained (R3).
- Experiments cover three datasets (CUB, Oxford, COCO) with comprehensive ablations (R3, R5).
- The RAT block and NULL guidance are experimentally effective (R2). Synthesizing fake sentence features via projection is intriguing (R2).
- One reviewer (score 5) particularly praised: augmentation using an external source, extrapolating text embedding based on k-NN of the image, architecture design, and ablation validating different components (R4).
- The setting of small-scale text-to-image datasets is interesting (R5).

### Weaknesses
**Presentation and Motivation**
- Poor presentation: unclear motivation, lack of smooth narrative explaining why data extrapolation is necessary or beneficial (R1). The reason for retrieving images from the web is not well-explained; the overall pipeline rationale is unclear (R1).
- Unreasonable layout: only Sec 3.1 and 3.2 relate to Data Linear Extrapolation; the rest of Sec 3 does not clearly connect (R5). Weak connection between data linear extrapolation and the other two contributions; the three contributions feel isolated, failing to form a complete story (R5).

**Task and Method Justification**
- Does not clearly justify why a text-to-image generation model needs to be adapted for datasets like CUB, Oxford, and COCO; unclear whether the focus is image distribution shift or text description distribution shift (R2).
- Why train from scratch on small datasets when many well-trained general models exist (e.g., Stable Diffusion, SD‑XL, SD3/3.5, FLUX.1, DALL‑E)? Needs comparison with full fine-tuning and LoRA fine-tuning with varying ranks (R2).
- Should report performance of recent models (SD, SDXL, SD3) under zero-shot, LoRA-fine-tuned, and full-fine-tuned settings (R2). Also compare with synthetic captions from VLMs like GPT‑4 and BLIP (e.g., DALL‑E 3) (R2).
- Why choose image retrieval over generating synthetic datasets with generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion)? (R1). Could this approach benefit other downstream tasks like classification? (R1).
- Limited application scenario: text-to-image datasets are abundant; text descriptions may be abundant and similar to COCO, potentially avoiding outlier detection (R5). What if a fine-grained classifier cannot be trained on the original dataset? (R5).
- Use of “extrapolation” may be closer to interpolation (least squares projection) (R2). Recurrent Affine Transformation (RAT) may be similar to the one in RAT-GAN; need citation and discussion of differences (R5). Synthesizing fake sentence features should cite DeCap [ Li et al., ICLR 2023 ] and analyze differences (R2).

**Novelty and Impact**
- Limited novelty: retrieval-augmented diffusion is not new; retrieval-based methods are common in industry (R3). Using third-party images and outlier detection is an engineering approach rather than a research-driven idea; does not meet the ICLR bar (R4). Even the more positive reviewer (score 5) noted limited novelty (R4).
- NULL guidance appears the same as Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) (R3). RAT and NULL guidance do not provide clear novel insights over AdaIN and CFG (R2). RAT should be compared with AdaNorm, AdaRMSNorm, cross-attention, and other text guidance architectures in recent diffusion models (R3).

**Evaluation and Comparisons**
- Qualitative results (Figure 4) are not promising: e.g., the man’s face is not properly generated, and the prompt “red and green flower” has no green flower present (R1). More samples needed to illustrate diversity (R5).
- Quantitative evaluation insufficient: lacks time complexity analysis; benefit over standard fine-tuning on pretrained models unclear (R3). Missing alignment metrics such as CLIP‑Score or human evaluation (R3).
- Table 1 comparison may be unfair: other baselines are trained on each dataset alone, while this method adds a web-crawled dataset (R2, R3). Table 2: after data linear extrapolation, performance is still similar to VQ‑Diffusion and GALIP (R5). Using all three tricks brings only slight improvement over UViT; need to test data linear extrapolation on existing methods like RAT‑GAN and UViT separately and collectively (R5).
- Table 6 includes models only up to 2022; should include 2023‑2024 models based on Stable Diffusion (R2). Claim that the model uses fewer pre-training images than Parti may be misguided; efficient training on large datasets is generally more important (R2).
- Tested only on CUB and MS‑COCO; need stress tests for more limited scenarios (R4). Unclear when the approach fails if outlier detection relies on having enough paired text-to-image data (R4). What does “ID” represent in Table 2? Not explained (R1). Why MS‑COCO? Estimate of necessary number of images would improve practicality (R1). If the method is for small datasets, clarify (R1).

**Practical Concerns**
- Feasibility: how long does it take to retrieve millions of images from the web and filter outliers? Can this serve as a realistic augmentation method? (R1). Double training time is required for superior performance (R5). Need time complexity comparison between data collection and traditional training on a similarly sized additional dataset (R3).
- Privacy and legal issues: source of collected images must be clarified and ensure legal compliance (R5).
- Minor issues: Line 032‑033 typo; excessive use of “we” in Lines 076‑083; supplementary material has ample space so text descriptions should not be omitted (R5).
