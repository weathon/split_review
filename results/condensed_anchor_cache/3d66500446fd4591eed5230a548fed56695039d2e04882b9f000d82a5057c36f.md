## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends invariance learning from discrete to continuously indexed domains. It theoretically shows that discretizing continuous domains and applying existing methods (IRMv1, REx) fails when the number of domains is large and per-domain samples limited, then proposes Continuous Invariance Learning (CIL) that enforces conditional independence between labels and continuous domain index given learned features via an adversarial procedure. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets (Alipay, Wilds-time) show CIL outperforms baselines.

### Strengths
- Investigating invariant learning for continuous domains is an interesting and important extension of prior discrete-domain work.
- The theoretical analysis is a strong contribution: it proves existing methods fail when the number of domains |𝒯| ≥ O(√n) (with finite n per domain) and demonstrates CIL can handle even |𝒯| ∈ O(n). The theoretical claims are complemented by empirical verification.
- The paper proposes an insightful “duality” between domain and label via conditional independence; this allows CIL to potentially handle both continuous-domain discrete-label and discrete-domain continuous-label settings.
- CIL achieves state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive set of tasks, including synthetic data, HousePrice, Insurance Fraud, Alipay autoscaling, and Wilds-time, demonstrating practical utility.
- The paper could serve as a benchmark for future work on generalization over continuous domains, and the experimental setup is extensive.
- The paper’s flow of arguments is good and the motivation is well-conveyed.
- Applications on production systems (Alipay) and Wilds-time showcase real-world relevance.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern (minority view):** One reviewer argues the main strategy for measuring conditional independence (comparing two predictors for domain index) is not novel—InvRat (Chang et al., 2020) and IIBNet (Li et al., 2022) used the same idea for discrete domains, swapping y and e is trivial due to symmetry of conditional mutual information. Other reviewers found the approach insightful and appreciated the extension to continuous domains. The paper does not adequately discuss this relationship or claim novelty.
- **Problem definition ambiguity:** Several reviewers question whether the “continuous” nomenclature is warranted. Real-world datasets (HousePrice: built year in 1‑year increments; Insurance Fraud) use effectively discrete-time steps, not truly continuous indices. The theoretical analysis assumes countably many domains (e.g., cannot handle irrational indices like π), so the method is only proven for a large but countable set, not a truly continuous domain.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** The paper does not compare with environment inference approaches that also avoid manual splitting (e.g., Creager et al., 2021 “EIIL”; Liu et al., 2021 “HRM”). Such methods should perform better than manually splitting continuous domains.
- **Correlated domains not tested:** All datasets have ground truth labels independent of the domain index. Experiments missing a toy setting where labels and domains are correlated with varying degrees across domains; it is unclear how CIL handles such scenarios.
- **In-domain performance is poor:** Tables 3 and 4 show CIL often has suboptimal accuracy and high variance on in-domain (ID) predictions (e.g., Alipay autoscaling ID accuracy erroneously listed as 885.7%, which appears to be a formatting error; other ID columns also have suspiciously high values or large std).
- **Discrete-label assumption limits generality:** The method assumes discrete labels (classes) for the conditional independence formulation, but prior methods assume discrete domains. The paper does not test the reversed setting (discrete domain, continuous label) despite claiming the method can handle it.
- **Presentation and formatting issues:** Several reviewers note small fonts in tables and figures, numerous grammar errors (e.g., “in among”, “variance of the losses in among domains”, “to extracted”, “Since is hard to validate”, “All The remaining data”, repeated sentence in Appendix D.1). Tables need cleanup: extra parentheses, missing spaces, missing periods in captions, uncapitalized headers. Notation inconsistency: both 𝒯 and ℰ used for domain indices. Table 5 includes SWA baseline but it is not mentioned in the main text.
- **Questions and clarification requests that overlap with criticisms:**
    - How were environments manually split for baselines (IRMv1, IIBNet, etc.)? Clustering? This is not described in the main text or supplementary.
    - Is the assumption that there are continuous and infinite classifiers for each domain (w_t) realistic? How does the theory handle unobserved domains?
    - In the formulation, if the losses of g(Φ(x),y) and h(Φ(x)) are similar, does that guarantee y ⊥ t|Φ(x)? The paper does not prove the converse.
    - Fig. 5 shows a step-up in p_s(t); explanation needed.
    - In the “Remark on the settings and assumption” paragraph, how does the penalty scale linearly with the number of environments under the spurious mask? The domain-wise loss variance should be δ_R, not scaling with |ℰ|.
    - Why mention L1 loss in the introduction but only use L2 loss in Section 3?
    - Is O(√n) the slowest rate |𝒯| can grow for REx/IRM to fail with non‑zero probability? A more precise statement would help.
    - Appendix F’s proof is unclear: notations undefined; the assumption “each domain contains one sample” contradicts “infinite samples per domain” in the main text; the final inequality is confusing (G(1/4) > 1/4?).
    - How sensitive is CIL to the size of the invariant features φ(x)? What NN architectures and hyperparameters were used?
