## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TimeCapsule, a model for long-term time series forecasting that unifies existing techniques (e.g., redundancy reduction, multi-scale modeling) into a 3D tensor (temporal, variate, level) with mode production for compression and dependency capture. It employs an internal forecast in the compressed representation space with a JEPA loss. The model is evaluated on LTSF benchmarks, achieving performance comparable to state-of-the-art. Reviewers found the paper’s direction innovative and presentation clear, but raised concerns about insufficient justification of design choices, lack of ablation depth, limited task generality, and the JEPA integration.

### Strengths
- **Clear presentation and organization**: Well-written, logically organized, easy to follow. Figures (especially Fig. 2) effectively convey the complex architecture (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- **Innovative perspective**: Treating time series as a 3D tensor (temporal, variate, level) is a new insight (R3, R4).  
- **Novel application of JEPA**: Applying Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to time series forecasting is interesting and demonstrates effectiveness (R1, R2).  
- **Comprehensive analysis of SOTA methods**: Paper categorizes and summarizes core techniques in LTSF (R1, R3).  
- **Efficiency**: Mode Specific Multi-head Self-attention uses low-rank transforms to replace patching, achieving promising computational efficiency. TimeCapsule shows superior accuracy and speed (R1).  
- **Extensive ablation studies**: Ablations and compute-efficiency analyses validate core components (R1, R2).  
- **Performance**: Achieves performance comparable to SOTA models on challenging benchmarks (R1, R2, R3).  

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient explanation of multi-scale representation**: How the additional “level” dimension captures multi-scale temporal dependencies is not clearly defined. The paper should explicitly define multi-scale levels and variate dependencies (R1, R3, R4).  
- **Ambiguity in design choices and ablations**:  
  - Motivation for combining components lacks evidence that the unified model outperforms individual parts; even the ablation study does not address this (R3).  
  - Rationale for applying EMA over input time-series (Eq. 15) and introducing Gaussian noise (L. 251) is unclear; parameter choices (smoothing factor, variance) and their impact are not explored (R2).  
  - The coefficient for the JEPA loss is not reported (R2).  
  - The order of T‑, L‑, V‑TransBlocks (series) is not justified; the parallel alternative is not compared experimentally (R1, R4).  
  - The decoder uses MLPs; a comparison with Transformer decoders is missing (R4).  
- **JEPA integration lacks depth**:  
  - The JEPA context/target overlap differs from I‑JEPA (here context and target do not overlap); this design choice needs more experimental justification and a more thorough study of JEPA for time series (R4).  
  - Gains from JEPA loss appear marginal; it is unclear whether a simpler self-supervised approach would yield similar results (R2).  
  - The paper does not demonstrate that JEPA pre-training helps diverse downstream tasks (e.g., forecasting, classification, irregular TS imputation) (R2).  
  - The specific inductive bias introduced by JEPA (L. 281‑283) is not explicitly stated (R4).  
- **Limited task and dataset evaluation**:  
  - Only long-term forecasting is considered; generality to other tasks (classification, self-supervised learning, irregular TS, imputation) is not established (R2).  
  - Only one subset (PEMS04) of the PEMS dataset is used; conclusions drawn from a single example dataset are insufficient to demonstrate general advantages/limitations (R4).  
  - The lookback window is set to an unconventional 512; the paper notes larger lookbacks are more effective but does not discuss limitations of this choice or other potential constraints (R4).  
- **Unclear methodological details**:  
  - The explanation of decomposition classification and level processing is vague; adding visualizations or outputs after each TransBlock would clarify the correspondence to functions (R4).  
  - The equation for sequence–mode product (e.g., \( \times_2 \) in Eq. 2) is not explained in the main text (R3).  
  - Notation inconsistency: both “MvTS” and “MTS” are used to refer to multivariate time series (R2).  
- **Figure readability issues**: Some figure text sizes are small; Fig. 2 components below RevIN may be misleading (use more intuitive tensor shapes) (R1).  
- **Code not provided for reproducibility** (R4).  
- **Unclear classification criteria in Fig. 1**: The four categories (e.g., “Generalized Linear Dependency” vs. specific modeling techniques) are not on the same level; justification for grouping diverse approaches together is missing. The “…” in the overlapping area is not explained (R3).  
- **Lack of analysis for specific ablation results**: In Table 2, for ETTh2 and ETTm2, accuracy with original vs. residual information connections is similar, and the gap grows with horizon; no analysis is provided (R1).  
- **Model complexity concern**: The encoding–decoding structure (series of Transformer blocks followed by series of MLPs) may increase complexity, but this is not discussed (R3).
