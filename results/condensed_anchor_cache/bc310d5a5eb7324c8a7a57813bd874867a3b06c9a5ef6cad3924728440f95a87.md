## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MEND, a meta-demonstration distillation method that compresses lengthy ICL demonstrations into compact vectors via a two-stage process (meta-distillation pretraining and fine-tuning). Reviewers generally find the approach well-motivated and the method effective, with thorough analysis and ablation studies showing gains over Vanilla ICL and existing distillation models on GPT-2 and T5. However, concerns are raised about the limited scale and modernity of the evaluated models, insufficient baselines (e.g., CoT), missing experimental details (e.g., standard deviations, model size/FLOPs), and the inference efficiency measurement. One reviewer (R3, score 8) noted no major weaknesses, while another (R2, score 5) considered the experimental settings insufficient. R1 (score 6) and R4 (score 6) also had reservations.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated approach**: MEND combines hypernetwork training with knowledge distillation (two-stage: pre-training and fine-tuning) to predict demonstration context vectors, enabling efficient ICL without task-specific retraining. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Thorough empirical evidence**: MEND matches or outperforms Vanilla ICL and other state-of-the-art distillation models across seven diverse ICL task partitions using decoder-only (GPT-2) and encoder-decoder (T5) models, while significantly reducing computational demands. (R1, R3)
- **Detailed analysis and ablation study**: The paper justifies the necessity of each component (e.g., two-stage training, distillation losses) and provides clear insights into the contributions of different parts. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Clear presentation**: The method is easy to understand and well-organized. (R2, R4)
- **Investigation across model sizes**: Experiments with GPT-2 (multiple sizes) and T5 demonstrate the method’s effectiveness. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model scale and modernity**: The evaluated models (GPT-2 XL, T5-large) are relatively small and outdated. Larger models actually benefiting from ICL (e.g., GPT-J, GPT-Neo, OPT, LLaMa, Flan-T5) should be tested to increase impact. (R1, R2) – R3 did not raise this concern.
- **Insufficient baselines**: The paper lacks comparisons with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which often outperforms vanilla ICL. Additionally, distilling CoT demonstrations should be explored. (R2) Missing baselines also appear in figures: prompt tuning is absent from Figure 4a, and vanilla ICL with truncated demonstrations is missing from Figure 4b. (R3)
- **Inference efficiency measurement needs improvement**: It is unclear whether key/value caching is used during generation (which would reduce the cost of long demonstrations). The reported time/memory breakdown only covers inference with obtained meta-demonstrations, ignoring the cost of the distillation model processing input demonstrations into vectors. (R1, R3) – R4 also requested model size and FLOPs of MEND. (R4)
- **Missing experimental details**: Standard deviations are not reported for Table 2. (R1) The robustness to different formats for joining demonstrations (e.g., space vs line break) is not studied. (R1) Whether the demonstration distillation model starts from pre-trained weights or random initialization during pre-training is unclear. (R3) The size of the distillation vectors relative to PromptTuning is not stated. (R4)
- **Unclear training dynamics and loss terms**: The influence of the lambda controller balancing distillation loss is not explained, nor how the two-stage process connects to the claim of avoiding task-specific retraining. (R4) In Table 4, what does L_hidn represent? If it is L_distill, the second-to-last row (pretraining loss for both stages) shows performance far from CLM, requiring explanation. (R4) Also, in the “No pretraining” row, what lambda is used? (R4)
- **Restrictive assumption**: Equation (1) assumes labels in demonstrations are from a unique set. This may not hold for tasks like factual QA where test labels may not appear in the demonstration set. (R3)
- **Unexplained behavior**: The finding that No Input perturbation (only labels) often improves performance needs clarification – why does this happen and what does it imply about label significance? (R3)
- **Need for more insightful explanations**: The paper could better explain why MEND does not compromise the in-context learning ability of the LLM. (R4)
- **Presentation issues**: Grammar errors and typos appear (e.g., “evident an misalignment”, “between teacher student’s”, “cannot leveraging”, “a in-depth” should be “an in-depth”). (R1, R3) Figure 5 uses colors with low contrast. (R4) Notations could be more self-contained. (R4)
