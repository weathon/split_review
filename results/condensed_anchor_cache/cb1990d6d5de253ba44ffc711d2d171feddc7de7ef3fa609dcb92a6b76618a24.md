## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a feed-forward deep learning framework for long-term 3D point tracking without test-time optimization. It uses a coarse-to-fine approach with a transformer-based cost volume fusion module that integrates past appearance and motion information to handle occlusions. Experiments on synthetic datasets (TAP-Vid Kubric, PointOdyssey, FlyingThings) show that the method outperforms 2D tracking methods projected to 3D (even with ground truth depth and pose) and scene flow chaining. All reviewers acknowledge the problem is important and the framework is a first of its kind in deep learning for 3D point tracking, but major concerns include missing comparisons with closely related works (SpatialTracker, SceneTracker, FlowNet3D, optimization-based methods), lack of real-world evaluation, writing clarity issues, and insufficient qualitative comparisons. One reviewer (R3) is slightly more positive (score 6 vs. 5), noting the idea and improvements are nice, but still shares concerns about missing real data and limited runtime analysis. Overall, the paper shows quantitative improvements on synthetic benchmarks but its practical impact and novelty are not fully convincing.

### Strengths

- First deep learning framework for long-term 3D point tracking that generalizes to new points and videos without test-time optimization (R1, R4).
- Transformer-based cost volume fusion effectively integrates appearance and motion over time and handles occlusions (R2, R3).
- Extensive quantitative evaluation on synthetic datasets (Kubric, PointOdyssey, FlyingThings) showing clear improvements over 2D tracking (e.g., PIPs, TAP-Net) and scene flow chaining baselines, particularly under occlusion (R1, R2, R4).
- Ablation studies validate the contribution of the fusion module and coarse-to-fine design (R4).
- Paper is well-written and well-structured, especially the related work section (R3, minority positive view; note: other reviewers identify writing weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons with closely related works.** Multiple reviewers note that SpatialTracker (CVPR24) and SceneTracker are not cited or compared, despite having similar inputs/outputs and goals (R1, R4). Reviewer 2 points out that FlowNet3D also predicts point cloud tracking without test-time optimization and the difference is not clarified. Reviewer 3 notes no comparison with test-time optimization methods (Wang et al. 2023, Luiten et al. 2023) despite the paper’s claim of being “online.” Reviewer 4 requests comparison with optimization-based 3D trackers.
- **Lack of evaluation on real-world data.** All evaluations are on synthetic datasets (Kubric, PointOdyssey, FlyingThings). No quantitative or qualitative results on real benchmarks (e.g., KITTI Scene Flow, TAP-Vid DAVIS) are provided, making it difficult to assess generalizability to varied, real-world environments (R1, R2, R3). R1 specifically recommends testing on TAP-Vid DAVIS. R3 notes the complete absence of real-data results even in the supplement.
- **Insufficient qualitative comparisons.** Only CoTracker is used for qualitative comparison; more methods should be included (R2).
- **Unclear “online” claim and missing runtime analysis.** The paper calls the method “online” but provides no computational cost comparison against test-time optimization methods or runtime analysis on a per-video basis (R2, R3). Some runtime info appears in the supplementary, but no cross-method comparison (R3).
- **Writing clarity issues.** The method section is unclear (R4). Table 2 has numbers like (-26.2) (-15.3) that are not explained (R2). Figure 3’s “novel view” is black and inconsistent with the original view color (R2). The metric EPE3D used in Table 1 is not defined (R4).
- **Novelty concerns.** Individual components (coarse-to-fine, transformer, cost volume) are not novel and similar to prior 2D tracking works like PIPs and Harley et al. (R3). One reviewer (R3) initially had doubts about novelty and only raised the score to 6 after rebuttal, while other reviewers (ratings 5) still see limited contribution.
- **Training data question.** It is unclear whether the model is trained on the combination of Kubric and PointOdyssey (R1).
