## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper revisits object hallucination in LVLMs, attributing it primarily to insufficient decoupling of visual features rather than lack of visual understanding. The authors propose PATCH, a parameter-efficient tuning method that uses adaptive virtual tokens to extract object features from bounding boxes via a Cascade Mask R-CNN detector, aiming to reduce hallucinations. Experiments on POPE and PhD datasets across three LVLMs show hallucination reductions. One reviewer (R3, score 8) found the paper clear, the experiments supportive, and ablations mostly sufficient. The other three reviewers (R1: 6, R2/R4: 5) raised concerns about novelty, experimental rigor, and the practical scope of the method.

### Strengths
- **Parameter efficiency**: PATCH freezes LVLM parameters and optimizes only virtual tokens, making it lightweight and practical (R1, R2).
- **Hallucination reduction**: The method achieves strong results on multiple hallucination benchmarks (POPE, PhD) across three baselines (R1, R4).
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and motivated by a preliminary study (R1, R3, R4).
- **Experimental support and ablations**: Most design choices are ablated; experiments support main claims (R3). Reviewer 3 (score 8) found the approach simple and useful.
- **Plug-and-play spirit**: PATCH can be added without retraining large backbone (R4), though concerns about the claim remain (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / similarity to existing work**: The approach heavily relies on a pre-trained object detector (Cascade Mask R-CNN) and fine-tuning the VLM, which is common in prior works (GLaMM, LISA, Groundhog). The paper does not clearly articulate how PATCH’s feature extraction/fine-tuning differs architecturally from these methods, nor does it compare against them. Soft prompt tuning alone is not seen as a novel contribution beyond existing tuning techniques (R1, R2).
- **Insufficient ablation on virtual tokens**: No ablation isolates the contribution of virtual tokens (e.g., tuning only the projector on the same data). A granular analysis of token count, initialization, and interaction with other components is missing (R1, R2, R4).
- **Plug-and-play claim is misleading**: The method requires fine-tuning for each new model or scenario, contradicting a true plug-and-play claim. Additionally, the detector is query‑irrelevant (detects all objects regardless of question), so manual selection of when to activate PATCH is needed, limiting zero-shot use (R1, R4).
- **Incomplete baseline comparisons**: Missing comparisons with relevant methods: zero-shot visual prompt methods (Set-of-Mark), fine-tuning approaches (GLaMM, LISA, Groundhog), and training-based hallucination mitigations (HA-DPO, HACL). Not all baselines are compared across all LVLMs in the main table, and HA-DPO/HACL may not have been trained on the same data as PATCH, making comparisons unfair (R1, R2, R4).
- **Preliminary analysis is unclear and insufficiently supported**:
  - Definitions of “correct detection” and “wrong detection” in Table 1 are not explained (R2).
  - The claim that hallucinations stem from the visual projection module is not convincingly supported: Table 1 shows many hallucinations (308 vs. 191) were not even detected by the detection model, suggesting the encoder is also a major source. The same table using prompt_2 is needed (R2, R3).
- **Motivation contradiction and detection redundancy**: The paper argues detection information may be redundant, yet the method injects detection results plus soft visual tokens. The detector detects all objects irrespective of the query, raising concerns about practical applicability and information overload (R2, R4).
- **Unfair experimental setup**: Using part of the test data for training gives PATCH an in-domain advantage over baselines (R2).
- **Missing experimental details and overclaimed results**:
  - Decoding strategies and other reproducibility-critical details are omitted (R2).
  - Claims about enhanced semantic alignment (lines 316–317) are overstated without empirical support (R2).
  - The paper claims negligible extra computational cost, but ignores the fact that a separate object detector must be trained/used, adding parameters and cost (R3).
- **Limitations of the detector**:
  - PATCH cannot provide attributes (colors, shapes) that are common sources of current LVLM hallucinations, nor recognize objects beyond the detector’s training classes. No analysis when the queried object is absent or beyond the detector’s class set (R4).
- **Absence of utility benchmarks**: No evaluations on general capability benchmarks (MME, MMBench, SEED) to confirm that hallucination mitigation does not degrade other LVLM abilities (R4).
- **Incomplete related work**: Recent advances in LVLMs (Qwen2VL, LLaVA-OneVision) and relevant hallucination methods (GAVIE) are not discussed (R2).
- **Open methodological questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Are the detections actually necessary, or could additional soft-prompting with more learned tokens alone achieve similar results? Related: why is bounding‑box location (four coordinates) necessary for object-existence VQA? A baseline of soft-prompting a text‑only LLM on detection results without images is missing (R3).
  - How is the object information obtained (ground truth vs. predicted bboxes)? How are objects not present in the image handled? (R4)
  - What does “From Pixels to Tokens” in the title refer to? (R4)
