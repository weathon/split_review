## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a novel setting, Active Test-Time Adaptation (ATTA), which integrates a limited number of queried labels into test-time adaptation (TTA) to mitigate distribution shifts and catastrophic forgetting. A theoretical analysis (binary classification) provides a learning bound and shows that selective sample selection via entropy minimization can alleviate forgetting. The authors present SimATTA, a practical algorithm combining incremental clustering and selective entropy minimization. Experiments on PACS, VLCS, Office-Home, and Tiny-ImageNet-C show that SimATTA outperforms prior TTA methods in accuracy and is competitive with more demanding Active Domain Adaptation methods. However, several reviewers raised concerns about efficiency, theoretical claims about entropy, practical oracle limitations, overfitting risk, and clarity.

### Strengths

- **Novelty and significance**: The ATTA setting is innovative, breaking the conventional train-test paradigm by combining active learning with TTA. It provides a formal problem definition and theoretical guarantees, filling an important gap in the TTA literature (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).  
- **Theoretical analysis**: A learning bound is established, incorporating composition of training data, estimated distribution shift, and ideal joint hypothesis. The theory demonstrates that selective sample selection through entropy minimization can mitigate catastrophic forgetting (Reviewers 1,2).  
- **Empirical evaluation**: Extensive experiments on four benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, Office-Home, Tiny-ImageNet-C) show state-of-the-art accuracy under distribution shifts and resilience to catastrophic forgetting. Comparisons with TTA, ADA, and related settings are thorough (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).  
- **Simplicity and generality**: SimATTA is simple, effective, and model-agnostic; it can be applied to any pre-trained model including linear classifiers and deep architectures. The supplementary material includes a FAQ section and discussions on practical utilities (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
- **Completeness**: The main paper and supplement provide comprehensive background, related work, ablation studies, and detailed proofs (Reviewers 1,2,4).

### Weaknesses

- **Efficiency concerns**: SimATTA is significantly slower than prior TTA methods (e.g., Table 3: SimATTA 736.28s vs Tent 68.83s and EATA 93.14s). The paper lacks a detailed analysis of time/space complexity for the incremental clustering and model update steps, and does not discuss potential optimizations like parallelization. This raises questions about scalability and real-time applicability (Reviewers 1,3,4).  
- **Theoretical flaw about entropy**: The claim in Section 3.2 that entropy directly measures distribution distance between the model distribution and a test sample is incorrect. This casts doubt on the theoretical foundation of the proposed method (Reviewer 4).  
- **Oracle assumptions**: The framework requires a reliable oracle (e.g., human expert) for queried labels. The paper does not address practical limitations such as oracle availability, cost, fallibility, or the impact of noisy labels on adaptation (Reviewer 3).  
- **Overfitting risk**: Continual adaptation during test-time may lead to overfitting, especially when the test set is small or biased. The proposed balanced entropy sample selection may not be sufficient; the paper lacks an analysis of generalization to unseen data beyond the specific test set used (Reviewer 3).  
- **Insufficient ablation and visualization**: No diagram illustrates the interaction between incremental clustering and entropy-based sample selection, making the algorithm hard to follow. The ablation study does not clearly demonstrate the individual contribution of each component (Reviewers 1,4).  
- **Inconsistent results**: The performance of SimATTA with B≤500 on PACS differs between Table 2 (TTA comparison) and Table 8 (ablation), which needs clarification (Reviewer 1).  
- **Limited advantage at low budget**: In Appendix H.2, when B≤500, SimATTA shows performance close to other methods with no distinct advantage (Reviewer 4).  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Many hyperparameters (entropy thresholds e_l, e_h, centroid budget NC(t), centroid increase k) are introduced without clear guidelines for selection across datasets. While partly inherent to active learning, this requires more discussion (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unvalidated claim about LLMs**: The paper states ATTA can be applied to large language models (LLMs) but provides no experimental results. Given LLMs’ computational demands and different behavior, this claim needs empirical validation (Reviewer 1).  
- **Clarity of experimental settings**: Terms like domain-wise data stream, random stream, and post-adaptation are not explained well; a dedicated section would help (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing details**:  
  - The meaning of “steps=10” and whether SimATTA uses steps=1 or steps=10 is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
  - The handling of a batch size of 1 (extreme case) is not addressed (Reviewer 1).  
  - The relationship between the query budget and the cluster centroid number is not described (Reviewer 2).  
  - Some baseline results are puzzling: (a) Enhanced TTA on Tiny-ImageNet-C severity 5 is poor; (b) CLUE on VLCS (Table 4) is even worse than random selection (Reviewer 1).  
  - A possible typo exists in Section 5.2: “their selected pseudo-label samples” may be a mistake (Reviewer 1).  
- **Contrasting view**: Reviewer 3 (score 5) is notably more negative than the other reviewers (scores 8,8,6), emphasizing oracle limitations and overfitting as fundamental weaknesses that the paper does not adequately address. Reviewer 4 initially raised the entropy flaw but later raised the score after the rebuttal; nevertheless, the criticism is retained here.
