## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents TokenFlow, a method for text-driven video editing using a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model without finetuning. It enforces temporal consistency by editing keyframes with extended attention, computing nearest-neighbor correspondences from DDIM inversion features, and propagating keyframe tokens to all frames during the diffusion process. The approach is simple, lightweight, and compatible with existing image editors like PnP-Diffusion, Stable Diffusion, and ControlNet. Several reviewers note that results are visually impressive and temporally consistent, especially compared to prior video editing methods.

### Strengths
- **Impressive temporal consistency**: The edited videos show significantly improved temporal consistency in both texture and structural details compared to prior methods (Text2LIVE, Gen1, etc.) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Ablation studies show TokenFlow is critical to achieving good consistency (Reviewer 1).
- **Novel and sensible model design**: The idea of enforcing diffusion feature consistency via nearest-neighbor feature propagation from keyframes is novel (Reviewers 1, 4) and combines previously introduced components (keyframe sampling, feature aggregation, inter-frame correspondence) in a sensible way (Reviewer 2). The use of NN (rather than pixelwise flow) for correspondence is considered new by one reviewer (Reviewer 4).
- **Simple, lightweight, and compatible**: No training or finetuning is needed; the method is built on off-the-shelf components (DDIM inversion, PnP-Diffusion). It can work with any diffusion-based image editor (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The algorithm is easy to implement and code is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Helpful findings about diffusion features**: The analysis and visualization of UNet features on video tasks are valuable for future research (Reviewers 1, 3). The observation that internal features offer a shared and consistent representation across frames is promising and aligns with concurrent work on DIFT (Reviewer 3).
- **Good writing and presentation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with helpful illustrations and algorithm pseudocode (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The key components (keyframe sampling, feature aggregation, feature propagation across frames) have been introduced in prior works. It is unclear which parts of Section 4.1 are newly proposed versus borrowed (Reviewer 2). The contribution rating is considered fair by two reviewers (Reviewers 2, 4 giving 6/10; conversely, Reviewers 1 and 3 give 8/10, finding the idea novel).
- **Temporal flickering on high-frequency patterns**: Compared to Text2LIVE and Gen1, TokenFlow still shows flickering in textures (e.g., running-dog example: ground flickers; cutting-bread example: bread and background flicker) (Reviewer 4). The authors should analyze the cause and include it in Limitations.
- **Evaluation metric concerns**:
  - CLIP score for edit fidelity is not discriminative and does not capture visual quality; a user study on visual quality and fidelity is needed (Reviewer 3).
  - Temporal consistency measured by optical flow and warping may over-penalize edits that change object shape and favor only color/texture edits (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing critical comparisons and ablations**:
  - Comparison with Pix2Video is missing (Reviewer 1).
  - Comparison with PnP-Diffusion + feature propagation (mentioned in the paper) is missing from the main paper or supplementary material (Reviewer 4).
  - Ablation on keyframe sampling interval (tradeoff between computation and smoothness) is missing; a dynamic keyframe scheme for occlusion cases would be beneficial (Reviewer 2).
  - Ablation on which features are replaced: Are only self-attention features replaced? What about ResBlock features or attention masks? (Reviewer 1’s question; overlaps with missing ablation).
  - Runtime comparison with baselines is missing (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Limited structural deviation and motion editing**: The method strictly follows the source video’s structural layout and motion, limiting generative capability for edits that change shape, add new objects, or modify motion (e.g., making a walking person run) (Reviewers 2, 3). The correspondence-based approach seems constrained to color/texture/style transfers (Reviewer 3). The authors should discuss ways to relax consistency constraints around object boundaries (Reviewer 2).
- **Occlusion and video length handling unclear**: It is not addressed how the method handles targets partially occluded for a few frames (Reviewers 3, 4). The maximal video length TokenFlow can handle is unknown (Reviewer 4).
- **Feature-level vs RGB consistency**: It is unclear whether ensuring feature-level consistency guarantees RGB output consistency (Reviewer 4).
- **Choice of NN correspondences vs. optical flow**: Why NN instead of dense optical flow? Could downsampled flow be used? (Reviewer 4; overlaps with missing analysis).
- **Post-processing deflickering**: The post-processing deflickering used in the supplementary material is not described (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor typos**: "to operate on more then a single frame", "keyframess'", uppercase after Eqs. (4) & (5) (Reviewer 3).
