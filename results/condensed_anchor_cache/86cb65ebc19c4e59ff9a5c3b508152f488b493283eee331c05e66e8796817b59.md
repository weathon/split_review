## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores weak-to-strong generalization for CLIP-based image classification. It proposes Class Prototype Learning (CPL), which initializes class prototypes from CLIP text features, then distills logits from a pre-trained weak model (e.g., ResNet, CvT) into these prototypes using unlabeled data. During inference, only image features and learned prototypes are used (no text encoder). Experiments on the DomainNet dataset (six domains: Clipart, Infograph, Painting, Quickdraw, Real, Sketch) show a 3.67% improvement over baseline methods. The paper is scored 3 by four reviewers, 5 by one reviewer, and 3 by another (overall negative consensus).

### Strengths
- The paper is the first to explore weak-to-strong generalization for CLIP-based classification (Reviewers 2, 3, 6; high confidence for the first claim, moderate for the remaining).
- The proposed CPL method is simple, straightforward, and easy to implement, requiring only learning class prototypes (Reviewers 2, 5, 6; reviewer 5 gave score 5, indicating positive opinion on simplicity).
- Efficiency is improved by removing the text encoder during inference (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments show consistent improvements over competing CLIP tuning baselines across weak models (Reviewer 3, 4).
- The extension of weak-to-strong generalization to vision-language models is considered interesting and meaningful (Reviewer 3; also noted by Reviewer 4 as “an ambitious story”).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (multiple reviewers, high severity):** The method is described as “naive,” “straightforward,” and “essentially using the original KD loss.” Using the text encoder for initialization and dropping it during inference has been proposed in previous works [1–3]. Class prototype learning is closely related to training cosine classifiers initialized with CLIP text prompts, and knowledge distillation with unlabeled data is already explored in semi-supervised learning. The paper does not clearly delineate novel contributions beyond combining existing techniques.
- **Limited experimental scope (Reviewers 2, 3, 6; high severity, high confidence):** Experiments are only conducted on the DomainNet dataset. The method should be tested on ImageNet (including variants: -V2, -A, -R, -Sketch) and smaller standard datasets (Flower102, DTD, Pets, StanfordCars, UCF101, Caltech101, Food101, SUN397, Aircraft, EuroSAT) to demonstrate generalizability. The current evaluations do not support claims of robustness across real-world distributions.
- **Problem setting and motivation are unclear (Reviewers 1, 4, 5, 6; high severity):**
  - The weak model is trained with ground-truth labels, yet the strong model is prevented from directly using those labels. This makes the setup artificially constrained and less compelling. Why not simply fine-tune the strong model with the labeled holdout set (Strong Ceiling)? The paper does not justify the added complexity.
  - The setup assumes a weak model already aligned with the target categories, which is often infeasible in practice (e.g., a vision-only model may not have the same classes). Practical limitations are not addressed.
  - The weak-to-strong goal of mitigating harmful outputs is not evaluated; experiments only measure accuracy on standard classification without any analysis of harmful biases or adversarial robustness.
- **Insufficient multi-modal demonstration (Reviewer 6, confirmed by Reviewer 4):** The paper only performs image classification (unimodal task) with CLIP. Claims of extending weak-to-strong to multi-modal tasks are unsubstantiated; true multi-modal datasets (e.g., retrieval, VQA) should be tested. The method also does not generalize to unseen classes or generative VLMs (LLaVA, BLIP2).
- **Writing and presentation quality (Reviewers 3, 4, 5, 6; moderate to high severity):**
  - Imprecise statements: In line 242–243, “students” should be “teachers”; line 489, “RLFH” should be “RLHF.”
  - Missing references for well-known terms (RLHF, AdaptConf) and lack of introduction to prototype learning.
  - Data split descriptions (L315–L323) are confusing and hard to understand.
  - The paper lacks visual demonstration of results; relevant content should be added to the appendix.
  - Implementation details are missing: the strong model’s image/text encoder configuration is not specified; the appendix containing details was not accessible during review.
- **Missing analyses and comparisons (Reviewers 1, 5; moderate severity):**
  - No complexity analysis (training/inference FLOPs or runtime) is provided to substantiate efficiency claims.
  - The baseline “Strong Ceiling” and “KD+LP” are not adequately described (hyperparameters, architecture, how data splits are used). From one reviewer’s understanding, CPL is exactly KD between two models using logits, but the paper does not clarify how CPL differs from KD+LP.
  - Related works on unsupervised learning with CLIP [4–5] are not discussed.
- **Specific reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms (included as weaknesses):**
  - Can the method help CLIP recognize unseen classes? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does the method address unique challenges of aligning visual and textual representations beyond standard logit-based KD? (Reviewer 5)
  - What is the relationship to semi-supervised learning? (Reviewer 5)
  - The reviewer notes that following KD principles, the two-step approach should outperform direct fine-tuning, but the paper does not explore this. (Reviewer 5)
  - A reviewer questioned the GPU memory specification (V100 has 32 GB, not 40 GB as stated). (Reviewer 6)

**Minority positive opinion:** One reviewer (score 5) appreciated the simplicity and ease of following, but also raised fundamental concerns about methodology, motivation, and experimental detail. All three other reviewers (score 3) also acknowledged the interesting direction but found the execution insufficient. The overall consensus is strong rejection.
