## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GLA-Pool, a two-channel graph pooling layer that integrates global topological structures (cliques) and local node-level information via attention, together with a data augmentation strategy for small-scale datasets. Experiments on eight graph classification benchmarks compare against thirteen baselines.

### Strengths
- Well-organized and clearly written (R1).
- The idea of combining global and local topology in pooling is straightforward and well-motivated (R2).
- The topic is important and the study provides extensive comparisons on multiple datasets, showing strong performance on most (R2, R4).
- Ablation studies and visualizations are included to analyze components (R1, R4). However, one reviewer notes the visualizations do not effectively motivate the use of cliques (R2), and the ablation is considered limited by multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) – these issues are detailed in Weaknesses.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and incremental contribution** (R1, R2, R3, R4). The method is described as simply combining CliquePool and SAGPool with GCN and GAT for attention, lacking key modifications (R4). The local topology pooling is nearly identical to SAGPool (R1). The paper offers marginal contributions over existing work and needs better positioning (R2). Missing comparison with substructure-counting methods (e.g., [1] "Uplifting any GNN…", [2] "Improving GNN expressivity…", [3] "Boosting cycle counting power…") and spectral clustering-based pooling (MinCutPool) (R2, R3). The classification into LTS and GTS is not clearly distinguished from selection/grouping-based pooling (R3, [2], [3]).

2. **Insufficient motivation and justification for design choices**:
   - Why combining GCN and GAT in the dual-channel yields more robust node rankings than SAGPool’s single strategy is not explained (R1). The motivation for using GCN and GAT as two separate views is unclear; what if only one is used? (R4).
   - The data augmentation drops low-degree nodes, claiming core patterns are represented by high-degree nodes, but no evidence or references support this; in molecular datasets functional groups (e.g., benzene rings) often involve low-degree nodes, making the assumption questionable (R1, R2).
   - How the graph-level representation \( M_e \) is generated is not specified (R2).

3. **Inconsistent experimental settings and unfair comparisons**:
   - Results in Table 1 are cited from previous works that use different evaluation splits (e.g., MuchPool: 10-fold, Wit-TopoPool: 90/10, this paper: 10-fold with 80/10/10), making direct comparison unfair (R4).
   - The proposed data augmentation is applied to GLA-Pool but the baselines do not use it, creating an unfair advantage (R3, R4).
   - The poor performance of GAT (47.6 on Reddit-B) and GIN (57.49 on NCI-1) is unexplained; more discussion is needed (R4).
   - It is not reported which GNN backbones are used for the reported results; the claim that any backbone is applicable lacks experimental support (R4).

4. **Limitations in dataset scale and scope**:
   - All experiments are on small, binary classification datasets; larger-scale benchmarks (e.g., OGBG-MOLHIV, ZINC, ogbg-molpcba, ogbg-ppa) should be included to demonstrate scalability and generalizability (R2, R4).

5. **Notation and presentation issues**:
   - Notation is inconsistent (e.g., bold characters used for matrices, vectors, and scalars interchangeably; in Eq. (2), \( C \) is a set but should be \( |C| \)) (R2).
   - In Eq. (6), \( S(idx,:) \in \mathbb{R}^{N^{l+1} \times 1} \) cannot element-wise multiply \( X'(idx,:) \in \mathbb{R}^{N^{l+1} \times d} \) without a transformation (R4).
   - The combination of global and local information is only depicted in Figure 1 and not described in the main text (R1).
   - Visualizations do not effectively support the use of cliques; given the limited presence of large cliques, capturing cycles might be more informative (R2). (One reviewer found the presentation good (R3), but the majority (R1, R2, R4) rate presentation as fair.)

6. **Ablation study is limited** (R1, R2, R4). It covers only some components; deeper analysis of node augmentation, the dual-channel design (GCN vs GAT individually), and the effect of the global vs local channels is missing.

7. **Time complexity comparison** (Figure 3) reports lower time for GLA-Pool than CliquePool, but it is unclear whether the time for maximal clique extraction is included (R4).
