## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper rethinks model ensemble in transfer-based adversarial attacks. It defines common weakness of model ensembles via two properties: flatness of loss landscape and closeness to local optimum of each model. The authors propose Common Weakness Attack (CWA) that uses sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) and a cosine similarity encourager (CSE) to promote these properties. Theoretical analysis connects transferability to Hessian norm and distance to local optima. Experiments on image classification (NIPS2017, diverse architectures), object detection, and the large vision-language model Google Bard show CWA improves transferability, especially against adversarially trained models, and combines well with prior attacks (MI, VMI, SSA).

### Strengths

- The paper tackles an important problem: understanding and improving transferability of adversarial examples for black-box attacks. (R2)
- The proposed method (CWA) appears novel and is well-motivated by a clear theoretical framework. (R1, R2, R3, R4) The derivation from second-order approximation and decomposition into flatness and closeness terms provides strong intuition. (R1, R3)
- The algorithm is reasonably implementable. (R2)
- Extensive empirical validation across numerous architectures, datasets (classification, detection, vision-language), and attack combinations (MI-CWA, VMI-CWA, SSA-CWA) demonstrates consistent improvements over prior methods. (R1, R3, R4) The black-box attack against Google Bard is a notable practical demonstration. (R2)
- The theory is supported by mathematical proofs and convincing analysis. (R4)
- The method is particularly effective against adversarially trained models. (R1, R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing CWA-only baseline**: Table 1 only reports results of CWA combined with other attacks (MI, VMI, SSA). The performance of CWA alone (without momentum or other augmentations) is not shown, making it difficult to isolate the contribution of CWA. (R1)
- **Ablation study incomplete**: An ablation over different norm decompositions for the Hessian (e.g., operator norm) is desired to justify the use of the Frobenius norm. (R1)
- **Novelty clarification needed**: The paper should more clearly differentiate CWA from prior uses of sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) and existing ensemble attack methods (MI, VMI, SSA). SAM has been previously used, and the novelty relative to these techniques is not sufficiently discussed. (R1)
- **Explanation for effectiveness on adversarially trained models**: The significant gains against AT models require explanation, and the fairness of evaluation (how the AT ensemble is trained) should be clarified. (R1)
- **Presentation and readability issues**: Several reviewers found the paper hard to follow. Section 3 mixes prior results with proposed contributions, making it difficult to separate original from reused material. (R2) Figures 1 and 2 lack axis labels, legends, and clear definitions of plotted quantities (e.g., \(x_t\), \(x_t^f\) in Fig 2 are undefined); they are not informative. (R2) Equations (4) and (5) are confusing for readers not familiar with SAM/CSE. (R3) Typographical errors exist (e.g., "researches" instead of "research", missing subscript in Theorem 3.1). (R2)
- **Missing mathematical description of prior ensemble attacks**: To contrast with CWA, the paper should formally describe how previous ensemble attacks (e.g., simple PGD on averaged logits) operate. (R2)
- **Complexity analysis lacking**: No comparison of wall-clock time or computational cost relative to prior PGD-style attacks is provided. (R2)
- **Unrealistic claim**: The statement that the goal is to fool all models in the ensemble is unrealistic and not supported by the experiments. (R3)
- **Imperceptibility concern**: The perturbation shown in Figure 3 is not imperceptible to humans, which could be a limitation for practical threat models. (R3)
- **Experimental description gaps**: In "Results on normal models" (Sec 4.2), only MI is discussed while VMI and SSA results are omitted; the analysis should also cover recent SOTA like SSA. (R4) The paper should explain why VMI-CWA sometimes degrades performance compared to VMI alone on specific target models (Swin-S, MaxViT-T in Table 1). (R4)
- **Additional missing analyses**: The order of classifiers in Algorithm 1 may affect results, but this is not explored. (R2) The applicability of CWA to the white-box setting (single model) is not investigated. (R2) The term "test error" in Sec 3.1 is ambiguous (error on black-box model?); the meaning of the test error in the theoretical derivation needs clarification. (R2)
- **Reviewer disagreement**: While most reviewers find the contributions solid, Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4 (score 6) note significant presentation and analysis gaps, contrasting with the more positive assessments (score 8) of content and clarity.
