## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper empirically investigates grokking phenomena in data regimes below the critical data threshold, using knowledge distillation (KD) from a grokked teacher model to accelerate generalization in a student model. The authors present findings on the role of weight decay, the possibility of grokking under low-data conditions via KD, and transfer between tasks varying only in modulus P. One reviewer (R2, score 5) found the propositions insightful and helpful to the field, while three reviewers (R1, R3, R4, scores 3) raised substantial concerns about experimental scope, clarity, novelty, and practical applicability.

### Strengths
- Timely and important topic (R3).
- Novel use of knowledge distillation in the context of grokking (R3).
- Provides counterintuitive insights by questioning the necessity of weight decay and low weight norms for grokking (R1, R2). *Note: R3 disputes the novelty of this finding, see Weaknesses.*
- Some experiments (e.g., Figure 3) are intuitive and well-presented (R4).
- The paper is generally well-written (R1), though other reviewers note room for improvement (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**:  
  - Evaluated only on modulo arithmetic (different modulus P); not tested on other binary operators, tasks, or real-world datasets (R2, R3, R4). Claims of general conclusions are unsupported without broader evaluation (R2).  
  - No verification on more complex models or real-world data, raising doubts about scalability and practical applicability (R1, R3, R4). R3 notes difficulty connecting toy setting to real-world applications.

- **Novelty concerns**:  
  - The finding that reducing weight norm is not necessary for grokking is already shown in prior works (e.g., Kumar et al. 2024, Golechha 2024) (R3). R1 and R2 considered this a strength, but R3 argues it is not new.

- **Experimental setup lacks clarity and justification**:  
  - Reasons for using 30000 epochs and data fractions (30%, 20%, 10%) are not justified; unclear if they follow prior works (R3).  
  - The ground-truth critical data size threshold is not discussed or estimated; experiments may still be above it (R2).  
  - Only data percentages are given, not absolute sample sizes – impossible to gauge how low the data really is (R4).  
  - Section 5: motivation for training two separate models and distilling into a larger model is unclear; implication not explained (R3).  
  - Continual learning experiment uses only two tasks – too narrow to be useful; terminology “pretraining” is confusing in a CL context (R4).

- **Mathematical and notational issues**:  
  - “Distribution” is loosely used without proper definition; confusion between modulo notation P and distribution notation p; input space description (line 196) unclear; random variable not specified (R4).  
  - Inconsistency: P is treated as constant modulus in line 184 but as the last token of output in line 190 (R3).  
  - Figure 1 not referenced in the text (R3).  
  - Figure 2a: test accuracy stuck near zero even with 30% training data – why no improvement? (R2).  
  - Figure 3 color scheme makes it hard to distinguish shades/linestyles (R4).  
  - Statement in line 375 contradicts Figure 1(b) top-right panel; what is \(f_M\) tested on in all plots? (R4).

- **Missing analysis and theoretical support**:  
  - No theoretical insights or guarantees provided; theoretical understanding would help explain the counterintuitive propositions (R2, R4).  
  - Effect of task similarity between p1 and p2 on grokking not explored; transfer learning literature not discussed (R4).  
  - No verification that a grokked model reduces generalization delay on the student model for a broad range of tasks (R3).  
  - The role of weight decay: R3 notes prior counterexamples, reducing the claim’s novelty (see above).

- **Writing and presentation issues**:  
  - Motivation for research questions Q1–Q3 and for studying grokking with KD is unclear (R1).  
  - Some parts lack citations for smooth reading (e.g., rows 48, 50–51, 201–207) (R3).  
  - Row 97: “pre-grokked” likely should be “post-grokked” or “grokked” (R3).  
  - Section 4 bullet points on advantages of KD are not elaborated; referencing other works is unhelpful (R4).  
  - More intuition on why KD makes a difference would be helpful (R3).
