## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors evaluate eight commonly used representational similarity measures alignment-based, CCA-based, inner product kernel-based, and nearest-neighbor methods across 19 models (pre-trained ImageNet, penultimate layers), 17 datasets, and 9 behavioral metrics (classification-based). They compute correlations between representational and behavioral similarity matrices. They report that linear CKA and Procrustes distance best distinguish trained from untrained models and align with behavioral measures, while linear predictivity and CCA show only moderate alignment. The paper is framed as guidance for NeuroAI research on selecting behaviorally meaningful metrics.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a useful and important question: deeper understanding of representational similarity measures and their functional relationship.  
- Comparisons to actual classification behavior of models is an interesting and potentially valuable new viewpoint.  
- The basic results fit with earlier analyses and appear solid.  
- The evaluation is systematic and covers many metrics (8 or 12 depending on k‑NN counting) and behavioral metrics (9), offering benchmarks and guidance for the community.  
- Figures 4 and 5 provide a clear and simple message.  
- The paper is mostly straightforward and well-written.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope and motivation issues:**  
  - The emphasis on methods that align with network outputs (i.e., behaviors) is parochial and poorly motivated; one reviewer argues that distinguishing models with similar outputs but different mechanisms is more interesting from a neuroscience perspective. A much more balanced presentation is needed.  
  - The use of “behavior” is awkward; the study only considers classification tasks, which makes the results sound more general than they are. The success of CKA and Procrustes may be partly due to the discrete nature of the classification task; other task types might yield different correspondences.  
- **No technical novelty:** The paper simply compares existing methods; no new methods are introduced.  
- **Implementation doubts for RSA and linear encoding models:** The formula for RSA comparisons is wrong; classic RSA requires standardization of X values and an important vectorization step implied in τ. The linear encoding model description omits dimensionality reduction, regularization, and cross-validation. This raises doubts whether state-of-the-art implementations were used.  
- **Missing methods and limited scope of representations:** Several commonly used methods in practice are missing. The focus is exclusively on penultimate layers of ImageNet-trained networks, which is a small subset of representations. How the relation to behavioral measures breaks down for earlier layers would be informative.  
- **Lack of theoretical analysis:** No attempt is made to provide theoretical justification or understanding of why Procrustes and linear CKA show good agreement (or why CCA does not, despite being in the same family with only a whitening difference per Williams et al. 2021). Some relevant literature discussing connections between measures exists.  
- **Coarse analysis:** The paper covers only coarse overall patterns; a deeper dive into specific network pairs where measures disagree, or tasks where they behave differently, would be valuable. The high correlations in Figure A.3 (all tasks producing very similar similarity structures among models) is surprising and unexplained.  
- **Incomplete feel:** The paper reads like a methods paper even though no new methods are introduced; sections 1.1 and 1.2 could be relegated to appendices. Apart from measuring correlation, there is little interpretation or follow-up scientific hypothesis and analysis. It has the feel of exploratory analysis without deeper investigation.  
- **Negatively correlated metrics:** Some metrics are negatively correlated with behavioral metrics; it is unclear whether this is noise or meaningful.  
- **Variability of model instances:** Only one pre-trained instance per model is used; the variability of the metrics over different instances is not considered.  
- **Figure captions need improvement:** Captions should include more information about what properties are being averaged and what is being correlated (e.g., what the dots in Figure 3 represent).
