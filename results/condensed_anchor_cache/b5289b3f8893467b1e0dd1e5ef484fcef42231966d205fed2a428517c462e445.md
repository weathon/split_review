## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a learning-based diffeomorphic deformation network (DDOT) that uses the sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD) as the objective function to deform an initial mesh into a target shape given volumetric input. Meshes are represented as probability measures (via oriented varifolds), generalizing set-based point-cloud approaches. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows SWD yields a fast statistical rate for approximating surfaces, independent of dimensionality. The method is applied to cortical surface reconstruction, employing a neural ODE for deformation. Experiments on multiple brain datasets demonstrate improved geometric accuracy, lower self-intersection ratio, and higher consistency compared to state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths
- **Novel representation**: triangle meshes as probability measures (continuous, empirical, discrete) via oriented varifolds, generalizing set-based approaches in a learning-based deformation network (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Novel metric for mesh deformation**: sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD), with solid theoretical justification including Theorem 1 (faster convergence rate than alternatives, linear complexity) (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **DDOT framework** effectively integrates neural ODE for diffeomorphic deformation, producing smooth, high-quality meshes without self-intersections (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Thorough experiments** on cortical surface reconstruction show clear improvements over competing methods on EMD, SWD, ASSD, CN, SI across multiple datasets; reconstruction quality is suitable for geometric analysis (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Low self-intersection and good consistency are also demonstrated (Reviewer 2).
- **Generally well-written** with clear logic and detailed theoretical derivation (Reviewers 1, 2) – though some reviewers note presentation issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: optimizing meshes with Chamfer distance (CD) or Earth mover distance (EMD) is not new; SWD has already been shown beneficial for point-cloud optimization [2]. Replacing one distributional loss with another appears incremental (Reviewer 4). The probabilistic interpretation may be unnecessary from a practical standpoint (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear representation in position-orientation space**: the cost function is not explained; why not use position space alone? The Wasserstein distance is not invariant under rigid motion of the mesh, which may be problematic (Reviewer 2). Also, the regularity of the optimal transport map (whether diffeomorphic) is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of clarity on oriented varifolds**: a brief introduction is needed for readability; the paper is math-heavy and hard for non-experts (Reviewers 1, 3). Several notations are left unexplained (e.g., the # operator in Eqn. 5) (Reviewer 4).
- **Genus-zero limitation**: initial and target surfaces are likely genus-0; it is unclear whether DDOT maintains topology during deformation and whether it can be extended to shapes of other topology (Reviewers 1, 3). The paper acknowledges a limitation requiring good initial mesh quality, but more details on applicability are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Scope limited to cortical surfaces**: results are restricted to brain structures; the method should be tested on common 3D shape benchmarks to demonstrate generality (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Missing experimental comparisons**: improved variants of CD (e.g., density-aware CD [4]) are not compared (Reviewer 4). The speed advantage of SWD over CD (Fig. 3) requires further explanation given that efficient CD computation methods exist (Reviewer 4).
- **Initial surface details**: it is unclear whether the initial mesh in DDOT is the same as that used in competing methods (e.g., extracted from white matter segmentation) (Reviewer 1).
- **Disagreement in assessment**: while Reviewers 1 and 2 evaluate the paper very positively (strong theory, clear writing, solid experiments), Reviewers 3 and 4 find limited novelty and presentation issues, and Reviewer 3 expresses low confidence due to difficulty understanding central parts.
