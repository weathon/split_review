## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies DIET, a self-supervised method that labels each example by its datum index and trains with a supervised loss. The authors provide theoretical analysis showing that for a linear encoder with MSE loss, DIET is equivalent to spectral contrastive loss. They prove DIET tends to learn less-noisy features but may not capture all features, and show that feature normalization and a projection head can address this. To reduce DIET’s large memory footprint (classifier head of size N × m), they propose S-DIET, which uses batch cross-entropy and a multistep AdamW update, and report improved linear probe accuracy on CIFAR and ImageNet-100.

### Strengths
- Provides theoretical proofs connecting DIET to spectral contrastive loss (linear encoder, MSE loss) – considered interesting by all reviewers.
- Addresses the well-motivated practical problem of DIET’s high memory demand.
- Empirical evidence that S-DIET matches or improves performance on CIFAR and ImageNet-100 (Table 5 shows promising improvements).
- Conducts a detailed and insightful ablation study.
- Introduces concrete modifications for S-DIET: batch cross-entropy loss and multistep AdamW update.

### Weaknesses
- **Fragmented narrative**: The theoretical analysis (Section 4, equivalence to spectral CL) is not clearly connected to the practical modifications in Section 5 (feature normalization, projection head). The paper lacks a cohesive argument linking these parts – noted by multiple reviewers.
- **Missing code/pseudocode**: No code or pseudocode is provided, making it difficult to understand the algorithm’s exact sequence of operations and to verify the proposed memory-efficient modifications. This also hampers assessment of the computational overhead of the new modules.
- **Unrealistic assumptions**: The theoretical analysis relies on strong, unrealistic assumptions that limit its practical relevance:
  - All theoretical results are derived under a linear encoder; extension to non-linear models is not discussed (cf. recent work Wang 2023).
  - The use of MSE loss in Section 4 is not justified. The original DIET uses cross-entropy; it is unclear whether Theorem 4.5 holds with cross-entropy.
  - Assumption that \(W_H\) is an isometry is very strong; no constraint in the loss enforces this.
  - The toy training example in Section 5 (two features, one low-noise, one high-noise) is unrealistic; its applicability to real data is unclear.
  - Theorem 5.1 relies on this toy example; generalization to more realistic settings is not shown.
- **Memory scaling limitation of DIET**: The classifier head has dimensions N × m, scaling linearly with dataset size. Even if not always fully loaded in memory, storing such a large head (e.g., millions of rows for ImageNet) is a fundamental drawback compared to fixed-size projection heads in other contrastive methods.
- **Disconnect in Theorem 4.3**: The introduction of \(W_1\) in Theorem 4.3 is unclear and appears disconnected from the practical S-DIET implementation.
- **Motivation and focus unclear**: The paper claims memory-efficiency as its core contribution, but the analysis and conclusions (about features, encoder dimensions, projection heads) are not adequately tied to memory usage. Why DIET is chosen over other CL algorithms that also reduce sample size (e.g., few-shot SSL) is not justified; DIET additionally requires labeled information.
- **Incomplete literature review**: Important related work (e.g., Wang 2023) is missing; a more comprehensive survey is needed.
- **Simplicity of the memory-efficient idea**: The core modification (batch cross-entropy + multistep update) is described as straightforward, and its overall contribution may be limited.
- **Computational overhead**: The introduction of new modules (e.g., batch CE, multistep updates) may increase computational cost; experiments comparing memory and runtime are necessary but not provided.
