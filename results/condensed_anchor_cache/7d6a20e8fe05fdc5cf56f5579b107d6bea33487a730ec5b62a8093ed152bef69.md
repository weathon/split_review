## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the importance of a time series foundation model handling cross-domain heterogeneity, and appreciate the clarity, open-source release, and large pre-training corpus. However, they raise substantial concerns about limited experimental rigor, overclaimed generalization, insufficient novelty, missing comparisons and ablations, and unclear mechanisms for variate relationship modeling. The paper is seen as having engineering value but falling short on empirical validation and conceptual advances.

### Strengths
- **Targets an important problem**: building a generalisable time series foundation model that addresses high variance across domains and tasks (all reviewers).
- **High-quality presentation**: well-written, clear, with good organization and figures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Key idea of conditioning on variables/domains**: the tokenizer with domain-specific variable signatures is a sound approach to distinguish heterogeneity (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Large pre-training corpus**: uses 640,187 samples and 11 billion time points across 8 domains, and provides public data and weights (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- **Competitive results in Tables 1, 2, 3** compared to baseline TS models (Reviewer 1, 5).
- **Multiple experimental axes**: includes ablations of method, dataset and model sizes, and visualizations of embeddings (Reviewer 2).
- **Variate embedding visualizations are interesting and insightful**, particularly for EEG where spatial sensor arrangements provide a strong prior (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Focus on EEG signals is a novel and promising approach** to model a specific system observed under different configurations (e.g., TDBrain and SEED with 19 channels vs. LEMON with 62 channels) (Reviewer 3).
- **Engineering value** from open-sourcing the model and code (Reviewers 2, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental rigor and interpretation**: results are presented as advertisements without explanation of how runs were conducted or why performance gains occur (Reviewer 2). Overstated claims: e.g., L423–L424 says "embeddings of domains with shared high-level semantics cluster together [e.g., mono/stereo audio, banking/economics]" but Temperature is closer to audio than Banking to Economics (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experimental details**: baseline selection, pre-training/fine-tuning procedures, and details on variable definitions and overlap between training/testing sets are lacking or relegated to appendix without explanations (Reviewers 2, 4). PCA plots (Figs. 3, 7, 8) are not explained and lack variance explained (Reviewer 2).
- **No comparison with state-of-the-art TS foundation models**: e.g., arXiv:2405.02358 (Reviewer 1), and missing comparison with supervised deep models for classification (TimesNet, ModernTCN) and variate-centric regression models (iTransformer) (Reviewer 5).
- **No comparison to single-dataset task-specific models**: unclear if pre-training provides benefit over training from random initialization on each dataset (Reviewers 2, 4). No zero-shot generalization experiments (Reviewer 5).
- **No evaluation of prompting capability** (e.g., few-shot prompting) on the foundation model (Reviewer 1).
- **Overclaimed cross-domain generalization**: experiments primarily on EEG where sensor spatial relationships provide a strong prior; claims of generalization to truly distinct physical systems are not substantiated (Reviewer 3). Unclear how the model handles non-linear dynamic relationships (e.g., Ohm’s Law for voltage and current), which are common in real-world data (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient handling of temporal heterogeneity**: same patch size used for all domains, failing to adapt to different sampling rates; shared patch projector does not address differences in temporal dynamics across domains (Reviewers 4, 5). Lack of ablation on patch sizes or frequency-adaptive tokenization (e.g., Moirai uses different patch sizes per frequency) (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited novelty of variate embeddings**: similar approaches (learnable embeddings for heterogeneity) already exist in spatiotemporal prediction [2][3] (Reviewer 4). The encoder/decoder architecture is very similar to MAE without clear adaptation for time series modality (e.g., mask ratio exploration) (Reviewer 5).
- **Learnable signatures only distinguish variates/domains, but do not explicitly capture inter-variate relationships**: the mechanism for encoding dependencies and correlations between variates is not explained (Reviewer 5). Signatures are limited to seen variates/domains during inference, which restricts real-world applicability (Reviewer 5).
- **Unclear or unjustified design choices**: dual masking strategy (25%–75% ratio) lacks ablation and naming is overstated; masking ratio impact on temporal dependencies not studied (Reviewer 5). Channel-mixing via variable embeddings not compared to channel-independent strategy with textual descriptions [1] (Reviewer 4).
- **Unexplained performance discrepancies across similar datasets**: e.g., ETTh1 vs. ETTh2 in Table 3 show opposite behaviour between OTiS and baselines (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Present pipeline remains tedious**: supervised fine-tuning is still needed after large-scale pre-training; overall improvement over random initialization not demonstrated (Reviewer 5). Fixed context padding for univariate time series may waste compute (Reviewer 5 question).
- **No definition of “variable”** provided, and impact of variable correlation not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **How domain tokenizer adapts to unseen domains** with distinct variate structures is unclear (Reviewer 3). Sub-domain adaptation within same domain (e.g., electrical transformers vs. power generators) is not clarified (Reviewer 3). Broader question: at what granularity should “domain” be defined? (Reviewer 3).
