## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper compares classifiers trained on synthetic vs. real data across several visual settings (ImageNet, fine-grained categories, rare scenarios like object scale, brightness, occlusion). It identifies limitations of synthetic classifiers, attributes them to generative model shortcomings, and proposes RealTune—fine-tuning a synthetic classifier on a small set of real data (e.g., 40k images, ~3% of ImageNet) to close the gap.

### Strengths
- The paper covers a wide range of quantitative comparisons (standard ImageNet accuracy, fine-grained accuracy, rare scenarios) and digs into specific failure modes. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The exposition is clear and easy to follow, with a logical progression from analysis to mitigation. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The analysis in Section 3 (e.g., class consistency rate in Figure 4a) and the careful breakdown of how generative model limitations (brightness, object scale, occlusion) affect downstream classifiers are thoughtful and detailed. (Reviewer 2)
- The authors use systematic metrics: YOLO for object scale assessment, CIELAB for brightness evaluation, and ImageNet-X for rare scenarios, lending rigor to the analysis. (Reviewer 4)
- The exploration of mixed data pretraining (Section 4.3, Table 3) shows that combining real and synthetic data can sometimes outperform using either alone, providing practical insights for limited-data settings. (Reviewer 4)
- RealTune is practical and cost-effective, requiring only a small amount of real data and training time (e.g., 1 hour on a single RTX 3090). (Reviewer 4)
- The comparison in Table 2 (random vs. curated real subsets) is a useful addition that acknowledges the difficulty of targeting rare failure modes directly. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Claims of synthetic-classifier underperformance are not clearly supported.**
   - In Figure 2, the fine-grained accuracy gaps are very small (e.g., CLIP-Real-64M 83.5 vs. CLIP-Sync-371M 83.1; ViT-Real-0.25M 86.21 vs. ViT-Sync-2M 85.4) and may not be statistically significant or beyond model sensitivity. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - In Figure 3, the reported relative accuracy difference is only about 0.5%, and positive values exist, indicating poor consistency in the claimed direction. (Reviewer 3)
   - The failure modes examined (dark/light images, small objects, person occlusion) are highly correlated between real and synthetic classifiers (Figures 3, 8), so they do not appear to be specific to synthetic training—real models show the same weaknesses. (Reviewer 2)

2. **Lack of comprehensive baselines and comparisons.**
   - No comparison with simple data augmentation techniques (e.g., cropping/resizing for scale, brightness augmentation for illumination, occlusion augmentation) that could trivially address the reported failure modes. (Reviewers 1, 2) *What tests of data augmentation were done, if any?* (Reviewer 2)
   - No comparison with class-rebalancing methods to mitigate the class imbalance issue noted in Figure 4b. (Reviewer 1)
   - No comparison with prior works that train classifiers on a mixture of synthetic and real data (the paper surveys such works but does not compare against them). (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - No comparison with other fine-tuning or data-distillation techniques; SynTune is the only baseline, and it is not clear whether vanilla and SynTune in Figure 6 also use the additional 40k real samples. If not, RealTune's advantage is simply from extra data. (Reviewer 1) *How does RealTune compare to transfer learning or data distillation methods?* (Reviewer 4)
   - No comparison with training on a large set of LAION images that match ImageNet labels, or with models pretrained directly on LAION (e.g., CLIP) and then fine-tuned on small real subsets. (Reviewer 2) *How would other self-supervised pretraining strategies fare when fine-tuned on a small random subset of ImageNet?* (Reviewer 2)
   - In some settings (e.g., ViT-Real-1M), RealTune performs *worse* than vanilla, which is not explained. (Reviewer 1)

3. **The generative model side is under-analyzed.**
   - The paper does not discuss the scale of data used to train the image generator (Stable Diffusion was trained on billions of LAION image-text pairs). This makes the comparison “synthetic vs. real” misleading: a model trained on that much data can reproduce near-copies of real images, so it is not truly synthetic. (Reviewer 2)
   - The claim that generative models fail to produce fine-grained images is not generally true. Several works (e.g., SphericGAN, Semi-Supervised Single-Stage Controllable GANs) are designed for fine-grained image generation. The paper should study synthetic classifiers trained on data from such models. (Reviewer 3)

4. **Evaluation scope is narrow and lacks diversity.**
   - The study is limited to vision tasks; findings may not generalize to other modalities (text, audio). (Reviewer 4)
   - The rare-scenario evaluation uses only ImageNet-X; other challenging benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-C for corruptions, ObjectNet for viewpoints, dynamic backgrounds, motion blur, domain shifts) are absent. (Reviewer 4)
   - The analysis focuses on a few specific scenarios (scale, brightness, occlusion by humans) but does not consider broader concepts or other rare conditions. (Reviewer 1)

5. **The mixed training strategy (Section 4.3) is insufficiently justified.**
   - The choice of using 7.7% real data (for ImageNet-100) is arbitrary, and no ablation studies explore different ratios of real to synthetic data. (Reviewer 4)
   - The paper does not explain why certain data-mixing combinations outperform others. (Reviewer 4)

6. **Novelty and significance are limited.**
   - Pretraining with synthetic data and then fine-tuning with a small amount of real data has been explored in prior works. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - The paper’s central question (whether synthetic classifiers are as good as real ones) is already heavily addressed in the literature, and the findings here do not offer a new technical contribution. (Reviewers 1, 3)

7. **Presentation issues in figures.**
   - Figure 4 uses bar plots that make differences in class consistency and frequency hard to distinguish; heatmaps or swarm plots would be clearer. (Reviewer 4)
   - The synthetic data distribution visualization does not clearly break down which classes are imbalanced or how this affects model performance. (Reviewer 4)

8. **Minor but important context missing.**
   - The citation about running out of data (Villalobos et al. 2024) refers to trillions of images per year, while the paper uses tens of thousands of images—this undermines the motivation of data scarcity. (Reviewer 2)
   - *Does RealTune generalize across datasets like COCO or medical imagery?* (Reviewer 4)
   - *How does the quality/diversity of the fine-tuning data affect RealTune?* (Reviewer 4)
   - *How does RealTune’s computational efficiency compare (energy, scalability) to alternatives?* (Reviewer 4)
