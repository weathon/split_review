## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ELEGANT, a certified fairness defense for GNNs via randomized smoothing that is applied as a plug-and-play, re-training-free component to any optimized backbone GNN. Reviewers acknowledge the importance and novelty of targeting fairness certification, and find the theoretical analysis detailed and the experiments thorough across multiple datasets and architectures. However, concerns are raised about the limited theoretical novelty (direct application of existing certified robustness), practical applicability of the perturbation-budget assumptions, complexities introduced by Monte Carlo estimation, insufficient justification of the observed bias mitigation effect, and missing discussion of limitations regarding fairness metrics that require ground-truth labels. Reviewer disagreement centers on novelty: reviewers 1 and 4 consider the theoretical contribution solid, while reviewer 3 finds the analysis strategy incremental. Reviewer 2 expresses moderate concern about practicality, and reviewer 3 is the most critical overall (score 5), particularly on contribution to bias mitigation.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and novel problem: certifying the fairness of GNNs against adversarial perturbations (R1, R3, R4).
- Theoretical certification analysis is detailed, step-by-step, with rigorous proofs covering node attributes and graph structure (R1, R4). Reviewer 3 also acknowledges the theoretical guarantees.
- Comprehensive experimental validation across multiple real-world datasets (e.g., income, recidivism) and different GNN backbones (R2, R4). The experiments show the method is effective in practice.
- The framework is re-training-free, plug-and-play, and does not require assumptions about the GNN structure, making it flexible and easy to deploy (R4).
- The paper is well-written and fluent, with clear explanations of methodology and setup (R2, R3).
- Interestingly, the certified fairness method empirically improves fairness (debiasing) even beyond certification (R1). (Note: some reviewers question the source of this improvement; see weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical novelty**: The certification analysis is a direct application of existing certified robustness (randomized smoothing) to the fairness setting. Reframing the problem from accuracy to fairness is straightforward and does not introduce new analysis strategies (R1, R3). Reviewer 1 notes the contribution in theoretical analysis is limited; reviewer 3 explicitly says the analysis is built on prior work and lacks novelty. (Contrast: reviewers 1 and 4 still find the analysis solid, but reviewer 3 considers it incremental.)
- **Practical applicability concerns**: The assumptions about perturbation budgets and fairness certification requirements may not align with real-world scenarios, limiting deployment feasibility. The Monte Carlo estimation and probabilistic guarantee calculation add complexity, especially for large-scale networks. Further simplification or performance analysis is needed (R2).
- **Unclear source of fairness improvement**: The observed debiasing effect may simply be a by-product of the added noise, not a genuine contribution to bias mitigation. No theoretical analysis is provided to explain why bias decreases without an explicit debiasing mechanism (R1, R3). Reviewer 1 requests further theoretical analysis of this phenomenon; reviewer 3 states the fairness improvements are “probably just a by-product of the applied noise.”
- **Limitation for fairness metrics requiring ground-truth labels**: The framework cannot be directly used with metrics like Δ_EO (equal opportunity) that require ground-truth labels. The authors claim “similar observations on Δ_EO” without explaining how ELEGANT is applied to certify Δ_EO, and they never mention this limitation in the paper (R3).
- **Need for rigorous definition of “unnoticeable” perturbations**: The paper mentions constraints on node attributes and graph structure that are “unnoticeable” but does not provide a rigorous definition in the context of graph perturbations (e.g., based on a distance metric or perceptual threshold). Without this, it is hard to assess practical implications (R4).
- **Unaddressed graph type assumptions**: It is unclear whether ELEGANT applies to directed/undirected, weighted/unweighted, heterogeneous, or dynamic graphs. The current discussion assumes a relatively simple graph structure (R4).
- **Missing experiments**: Experiments are suggested on large graphs with non-synthetic edges (R1). The paper does not evaluate on large-scale real-world graphs.
- **Poisoning attack not addressed**: It is not discussed whether the method can defend against poisoning attacks, where perturbations are applied during training, not just at test time (R1 question).
- **Presentation detail**: The general theoretical results in Appendix A.9 could be introduced earlier to help readers follow the theorems (R4).
