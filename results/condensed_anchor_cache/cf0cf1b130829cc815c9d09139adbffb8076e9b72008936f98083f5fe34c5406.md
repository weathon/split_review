## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes 3DGraphLLM, which constructs a learnable representation of a 3D scene graph (node features for objects, edge features for semantic relationships) and uses it as input to a Large Language Model (LLM) for 3D referred object grounding. Off-the-shelf models (Uni3D for objects, VL-SAT for edges) extract features, which are projected into the LLM token space; the LLM and projectors are finetuned on grounding datasets (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, RIORefer, and also ScanQA, Sqa3D, Scan2Cap for training). Experiments report improvements over baselines that do not use relationships. Three reviewers score the paper 6 each, one scores it 3. The high-score reviewers find the idea well-motivated and technically solid, but the low-score reviewer is more critical, and all point to significant weaknesses regarding unfair comparisons, marginal gains, domain gaps, and reliance on external segmentation/relationship models.

### Strengths

1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4).
2. The idea of explicitly incorporating semantic object relationships into the latent scene representation for LLMs is well-motivated and addresses a clear gap in prior object-centric approaches (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
3. The proposed approach is simple and effective, converting object relationships to LLM token space so the model receives more complete 3D scene graph information (Reviewers 3, 4).
4. The ablation study is well-designed and covers several relevant design choices (Reviewers 1, 4). (Note: Reviewer 4 also notes that the two-stage training (GT first, then predicted) is not ablated — see Weaknesses.)
5. The authors identify and address technical issues such as the domain gap between 3RScan and ScanNet and the presence of duplicate objects in nearest neighbors, showing technical thoroughness (Reviewer 2).
6. Experimental results are relatively sufficient and convincing (Reviewer 2). (Contrasted by Reviewer 1 and others.)

### Weaknesses

1. **Unfair comparison due to different base LLMs.** The paper uses LLAMA3-8B-Instruct while most baselines (e.g., Chat3Dv2, Grounded 3D LLM) use Vicuana-7B (based on LLAMA 2), making direct performance comparisons problematic (Reviewer 1). Further, 3DGraphLLM-0 (zero neighbors, claimed equivalent to Chat3Dv2) outperforms Chat3Dv2 by more than 10% in Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (Table 4 vs Table 2), raising questions about the claimed equivalence (Reviewer 3). Performance of 3DGraphLLM-0 under GT segmentation is also inconsistent between Tables 3 and 4 (Reviewer 3).  

2. **Marginal improvements in realistic settings at high cost.** When using predicted instance segmentation (Mask3D), the gain from adding the scene graph is only 1–2% (Table 4), while the method adds significant complexity (~800 extra tokens per query) and overhead from generating scene graphs (Reviewer 1). With predicted masks, the boost drops from 5.6% (GT) to under 2% (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 states they would increase rating with more evidence of benefits in grounding tasks while not hurting QA/captioning.  

3. **Overclaimed SOTA status.** The paper claims SOTA on ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer in the introduction. However, baselines like 3D Vista and M3DRef-CLIP outperform the proposed model on ScanRefer (Table 2), and stronger expert models (BUTD-DETR, PQ3D) outperform by large margins (Reviewer 1). The claim should be revised to "SOTA among LLM-based visual grounding models." On Multi3DRefer, 3DGraphLLM outperforms M3DRef-CLIP, but the baseline is trained on much less data (Reviewer 1).  

4. **Domain gap between 3RScan (VL-SAT training) and ScanNet (testing) is not convincingly addressed.** The paper claims VL-SAT generalizes well, but there is no established evidence in the literature for strong generalization from 3RScan to ScanNet. The generalization ability of VL-SAT across these datasets requires more empirical backing (Reviewer 2).  

5. **Anomalously high cross-dataset results raise concerns.** With GT segmentation and k=2 neighbors, the paper reports Acc@0.5 of 49.0 on RIORefer→ScanNet evaluation (Figure 5). This is dramatically higher than the Cross3DVG result for RIORefer→ScanNet (13.34) and also much higher than the RIORefer→RIORefer test result in Cross3DVG (20.21) (Reviewer 2). Possible explanations (GT segmentation, strong RIORefer performance) do not easily account for this gap given the model's performance on ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer is not that far above SOTA expert models. This discrepancy raises concerns about experimental setup or potential overfitting on RIORefer. Reviewer 2 asks for more experimental results on RIORefer and explanations; a good response would increase rating.  

6. **Missing results.** Table 4 lacks the result for 3DGraphLLM-2 with OneFormer3D segmentation when the minimal distance filter is set to 1cm, which is critical to validate the minimal distance strategy (Reviewer 2). The paper also does not report results on ScanQA, SQA3D, or Scan2Cap despite training on them; reporting these is important for completeness and to show the graph representation does not hurt other tasks (Reviewer 1). The two-stage training procedure (first on GT segmentation, then on predictions) is not ablated (Reviewer 4).  

7. **Alignment between VL-SAT relationships and grounding task descriptions is unclear.** VL-SAT is trained on 160 objects and 27 relationships. It is unclear how these 27 (primarily spatial/geometric) relationships correlate with the more diverse language in ScanRefer. This mismatch may limit the effectiveness of edge features (Reviewer 2).  

8. **Minimum distance filter is not adaptive.** The 1cm threshold for filtering nearest neighbors requires manual tuning and is highly dependent on the scale and density of point clouds. Applying the approach to new domains (e.g., tabletop scenes, multi-floor multi-room scenes) would likely require different thresholds, and no guidance is given (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 additionally suggests that duplicate detections should be handled with 3D NMS rather than this naive filter (Reviewer 4).  

9. **The object-centric flat graph representation leads to wasteful tokenization.** In the proposed format (obj1, obj1-NN1, obj1-NN2, …), the feature vector F₁ʸ is repeated k times per object, and even more if an object is a neighbor of multiple objects. An alternative flattening (list objects, then list relations) might be more efficient (Reviewer 4).  

10. **LLM common sense knowledge is not exploited.** It is unclear what the pretrained LLM contributes beyond being a trainable decoder: the method does not demonstrate out-of-distribution generalization or use of common sense for tasks other than grounding (Reviewer 4). The paper should at least compare with training the same architecture from scratch (no LLM pretraining) to isolate the benefit (Reviewer 4). The fact that expert models still outperform 3DGraphLLM on ScanRefer further suggests LLM pretraining is not effectively leveraged (Reviewer 4).  

11. **The method is heavily reliant on the upstream segmentation and scene graph models.** Even if the LLM generalizes, it cannot recover from failures of these encoders. This is verified by the large drop from GT segmentation (5.6% improvement) to predicted segmentation (<2%) (Reviewer 4).  

12. **Why use Uni3D for object encoding instead of VL-SAT's own object encoder?** Since VL-SAT provides both object and edge encoders trained jointly, using its object encoder might yield better compatibility with the edge features (Reviewer 3).  

13. **Common failure cases are not discussed** (Reviewer 3).  

14. **Disagreement among reviewers:** One reviewer (Reviewer 1, score 3) is substantially more critical, highlighting weak quantitative results, unfair comparisons, and modest gains, while the other three reviewers (scores 6) find the approach well-motivated and technically sound despite the weaknesses listed above.
