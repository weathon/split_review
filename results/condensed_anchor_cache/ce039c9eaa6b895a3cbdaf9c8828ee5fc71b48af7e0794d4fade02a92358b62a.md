## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LAMP, a pruning method for large language and vision models that uses inter-block error compensation via a sliding window, progressive block pruning approach to reduce performance degradation at high sparsity levels. It reports improvements over SparseGPT and Wanda on OPT, Llama 2, and SAM models across perplexity, accuracy, and IoU metrics. Reviewer opinions are sharply divided: two reviewers (Ratings 5,5) find the method promising with solid experiments, one (Rating 3) sees incremental novelties and incomplete evaluation, and one (Rating 1) strongly criticizes clarity, practical relevance, and experimental rigor. All reviewers raise concerns about novelty, experimental scope, and the clarity of the algorithm and comparisons.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge of pruning large models for memory/resource efficiency, with high practical applicability.
- Core innovation of inter-block error compensation (fine-tuning the next block to compensate for pruning errors of the current block) is described in detail with theoretical analysis, and shows better performance than SparseGPT and Wanda at high sparsity levels.
- Applicable to both language (OPT, Llama 2) and vision (SAM) domains, demonstrating robustness across tasks and metrics (perplexity, accuracy, IoU).
- Experiments cover multiple models and include comparisons with SparseGPT and Wanda; the method works better than these baselines at high sparsity.
- Good explanation of related works.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation issues:** Algorithm 1 is hard to follow; variable $z_j^{2'}$ in line 8 is undefined and contradicts Figure 2 and Appendix A. The description of block error compensation (mixed-precision fine-tuning in Sec 4.6) lacks discussion of gradient changes, hyperparameter selection (α and β in Algorithm 1, line 8), and the combined impact of the two hyperparameters (Figs 3/4, 5/6). Writing needs improvement and more data analysis. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Insufficient novelty:** The progressive block pruning strategy and layer error propagation have been considered in prior work (e.g., sparsity rearrangements in [1], sliding block compensation in CBQ [1] for quantization, not cited). The method is incremental. [R2, R3, R4]
- **Limited practical relevance:** Primary improvements are shown at very high sparsity levels where model performance is already unacceptable; at sparsity <50% improvements over baselines are marginal. Practitioners are more interested in moderate-sparsity settings. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation:**
    - Missing comparison with recent SOTA pruning methods such as Pruner-Zero [2]. [R3]
    - Comparison with Wanda is unclear: it is not stated whether the baseline is untuned Wanda or Wanda with tuning compensation (LoRA/full fine-tuning), making the comparison ambiguous. [R4]
    - Comparison with SparseGPT is unfair because SparseGPT uses no fine-tuning while LAMP uses progressive tuning; time and compression efficiency of LAMP are not discussed. [R4]
    - Vision model evaluation is limited; more vision models and detailed analysis of vision results are needed. [R1, R3]
    - Ablation studies (Sec 4.4, 4.5) are performed only on opt-125m; need larger and more general models. [R3]
    - Missing ablation for the incremental contributions of block pruning and sparsity rearrangements. [R3]
    - Missing ablation on the specific effect of inter-block error compensation on error propagation. [R1]
    - Scalability to larger models (e.g., 70B) is not demonstrated or discussed. [R4]
- **Computational overhead and efficiency:** The trade-off between performance gains and increased complexity from the error compensation mechanism is not convincingly analyzed; the computational cost, training time, and GPU memory overhead of progressive tuning are not addressed. [R2, R4]
- **Reproducibility:** Missing experimental details (e.g., exact hyperparameter settings across tasks, model-specific configurations) and no code provided. [R2]
- **Unaddressed design choices and assumptions:**
    - Equations (1) and (2) impose equal sparsity across blocks, but earlier and later blocks are more sensitive; is this strategy optimal? [R4]
    - The key difference between pruning vision models and language models is not explained. [R3]
    - The method uses two layers per block; the effect of setting more layers per pruning block is not explored. [R3]
    - Applicability to multi-modal models is not considered. [R3]
