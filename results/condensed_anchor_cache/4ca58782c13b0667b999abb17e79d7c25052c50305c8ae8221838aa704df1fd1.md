## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PreferDiff, a diffusion model (DM)-based recommender with a tailored ranking objective. Reviewers largely found the idea of integrating ranking loss into DMs intuitive and the experiments solid, but expressed concerns about novelty, reliance on a single dataset (Amazon review), overlap with DPO, evaluation choices, and practical efficiency. One reviewer was notably more critical (score 5) of the evaluation methodology and baseline performance, while others (scores 6) were more positive but still identified important limitations.

### Strengths
- **Intuitive and reasonable objective design:** The transformation of BPR into a log-likelihood ranking objective for DMs is well-motivated and the development appears correct.
- **Effective use of multiple negative samples:** Incorporating multiple negatives with a gradient-weighted focus on hard negatives enhances user preference modeling and item separation.
- **Improved performance and faster convergence:** PreferDiff achieves superior recommendation accuracy and converges faster than baselines like DreamRec.
- **Balance between generative and preference learning:** The joint optimization (adding MSE loss for stability) shows practical value in maintaining training stability.
- **Clear presentation and experimental setup:** The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and provides enough detail for reimplementation.
- **Varied experimental validation:** Ablation studies and experiments across multiple categories of Amazon review data support the approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited originality and overlap with DPO:** Several mathematical formulations and the objective function closely resemble Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). One reviewer found the claimed theoretical contribution weak and the novelty minimal; another noted that the approach does not introduce substantial modifications beyond DPO’s framework.
- **Single dataset concern:** Only Amazon review data (multiple categories) is used. Reviewers question generalizability and recommend additional datasets (e.g., Yahoo!, Criteo).
- **Evaluation methodology unclear:** The paper does not clearly justify the use of full-ranking evaluation over the mainstream leave-one-out method. It is unclear whether these evaluation choices affect ranking of baselines or how the full-ranking method handles the large item space computationally. Baseline performance (TIGER, LLM2BERT4Rec) is inconsistent with original papers; the authors must specify if these are reproduced results and explain discrepancies (e.g., using VQREC’s ID quantifier instead of TIGER’s RQVAE).
- **Unconvincing explanation of the ranking loss:** The \(L_{BPR-Diff}\) loss is described, but it is not clearly explained how it specifically leverages diffusion model properties beyond standard ranking losses. The connection to the score function needs to be more explicit.
- **Missing ablation and analysis on timestamps:** For generative models, the role of timestamps in generating positive/negative samples is crucial; an ablation study is needed.
- **Unaddressed observations about convergence and sensitivity:** Why does PreferDiff converge faster than DreamRec? Why are diffusion models more sensitive to \(d_{model}\)? These observations lack explanation.
- **Stability concerns with hybrid loss:** Equation (12) adds back MSE loss to mitigate stability issues, but no instability observations are reported. Could the ranking loss alone be more stable? Should the root cause of instability be explored?
- **Dependency on large embedding dimensions:** Performance heavily relies on high embedding sizes, limiting scalability and increasing cost. Questions about regularization or pruning techniques are unanswered, and the optimal range for hyperparameter \(\lambda\) is not discussed.
- **Slowness in training and inference:** PreferDiff is reported to be slow; an efficiency-effectiveness trade-off figure (hit rate/NDCG vs. speed) is requested.
- **Limited sequence length:** Appendix D shows max interaction history of only 10 or 20. Is this a fundamental limitation of DM-based recommenders? Performance with longer sequences is not evaluated.
