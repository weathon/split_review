## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Divergence-Regularized Discounted Aggregation (DRDA), a multi-round learning method for solving partially observable stochastic games (POSGs). DRDA extends a discounted variant of Follow the Regularized Leader to POSGs with imperfect information and infinite horizon, introducing a generalized Nash distribution (GND) as a regularized solution concept. The authors prove linear last-iterate convergence of single-round DRDA under hypomonotonicity and show that multi-round DRDA converges to an exact Nash equilibrium under a unique rest-point assumption. Experiments on normal-form games, extensive-form games, and a Markov game demonstrate near-exponential convergence rates and outperform baselines including moving-magnet MMD.

### Strengths
- Addressing general-sum, multiplayer, partially observable stochastic games is a difficult problem, and work attempting to prove convergence in this setting is important to the community.
- Last-iterate convergence is valuable compared to average-iterate algorithms common in the field.
- Experimental results look impressive, with DRDA outperforming existing baselines across benchmarks and converging at near-exponential rates.
- The paper is well written given the high complexity of the topic.
- Linear convergence is shown when the regularization parameter exceeds the hypomonotonicity constant; the hypomonotonicity analysis is intriguing and likely to inspire future follow-ups.
- Introduction of the generalized Nash distribution extends the quantal response equilibrium concept to POSGs via divergence regularization.
- Convergence proofs are provided for both single-round and multi-round DRDA, strengthening the theoretical foundation.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumptions undiscussed or insufficiently justified:** No discussion is provided on the assumptions used. Specifically:
  - The realism of the local hypomonotonicity assumption is not addressed. One reviewer questions whether the claim that it is not strong is supported by only Kuhn poker experiments.
  - The unique rest-point assumption: Is it a feature of the game, the learning dynamics, or both? Do the games in the experiments satisfy this? What happens if they do not? Another reviewer asks if this assumption is needed because finding NE outside constant-sum games is PPAD-hard.
- **Continuous-time results, not discrete-time:** The theoretical results are in continuous time; the actual algorithm used in experiments is a discretization (Euler method for single-round DRDA, SDRDA) with a learning rate schedule that is not mentioned. The relation to Theorem 2 is unclear. The algorithm should be correctly labeled as multi-round DRDA (MDRDA) to avoid confusion.
- **Limited experiments:** The games studied are small-scale and tabular. Only single seed runs are reported; no parameter sweep is described. The experiments lack thoroughness and discussion.
- **Incorrect or unclear presentation aspects:**
  - The claim that POSGs unify NFGs, EFGs, and MGs is incorrect: POSGs cannot express untimeable games nor imperfect-recall games, unlike EFGs.
  - Grammar and flow issues (e.g., inconsistent tense, missing hyphen, incomplete sentence “On the other hand…”).
  - Minor notation concerns: “general value function” is unclear at first read; “Nash distribution” is distinct from “Nash equilibrium” but not explained; the sum over player convex combinations in Theorem 1 uses `max` rather than `sum` – both zero at equilibrium, but the choice may matter.
- **Connection to prior work needs more highlighting:** Theorems 1, 2, and 4 appear similar to results in [Sokota] and [Perolat] extended to POSGs. The connection to moving-magnet MMD is discussed in the appendix but is hard to follow because of different notations. The innovation may be limited to proving linear convergence under hypomonotonicity.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms, missing comparisons, or ablations:**
  - Can anything be said about which equilibrium is selected by DRDA compared to other methods?
  - How does multi-round DRDA depend on the gap of single-round DRDA from its rest point? Is this unknown?
  - Please explain the spikes at the beginning of each new round in Figures 1/2. In Figure 2 (3-player Kuhn), the DRDA levels (ignoring spikes) are not monotonic; how can it converge overall? Is there a measurable quantity that is monotonic (e.g., regret or gap from regularized equilibrium)?
  - Run the algorithm on typical POSG examples like the tiger problem and compare to methods like HSVI.
  - Compare also with Smoothed Predictive Regret Matching+ (with restarts).
  - What are the core challenges unique to the POSG setting compared to traditional EFGs, and how do they affect algorithm design?
  - What advantages does DRDA offer over existing perturb-based methods? Can prior methods like R-NaD or APMD be directly applied to POSGs, or are there specific limitations that DRDA addresses?
  - How much tuning did the experiments require? Are the parameters of baselines (e.g., R-NaD, MMD) tuned? Were Q-values or counterfactual values used for those baselines?
  - The existence of the rest point and Theorem 1 (relation between GND and rest point) seem trivial – why are these non-trivial results?
  - The reviewer also raises tension between the complexity results for computing equilibria in general-sum games and the claims made.
  - Minor: Line 225 notation `v` is more like a Q-value; line 299: does a GND always exist? Perturbation away from the base policy yields a bound involving a negative value that may not hold.
