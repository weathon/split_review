## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes G2PTL, a domain-specific pre-trained model for delivery addresses. It constructs a large-scale heterogeneous graph from real-world logistics data (delivery routes, AOI co-location) and pre-trains the model using three tasks: Whole Word Mask, Geocoding, and Hierarchical Text Classification (HTC). The model aims to combine textual semantics with geographical relationships. Downstream tasks evaluated are address entity extraction, normalization, geolocation coding, and ETA. G2PTL has been deployed in production.

### Strengths
- Novel graph construction from logistics data (delivery routes, AOI co-location) that captures geographical neighborhood beyond direct distance; the strategy of using subgraphs sampled from a larger heterogeneous graph for training is innovative. [R1, R2, R3]
- Domain-specific pre-trained LM for delivery addresses, effectively combining textual semantics and geographical relationships in a multi-modal framework. Reviewer 3 highlights this as a novel contribution (jointly using graph and text information), whereas Reviewer 2 notes that the components (whole word mask, geocoding, Graphormer) individually are existing techniques but the graph construction itself is novel. [R1, R2, R3 – minority contrast on novelty]
- Strong performance across several logistics tasks (Geocoding, ETA for Pick-up, address entity prediction, address entity tokenization) with thorough ablation experiments demonstrating effectiveness. [R1, R2, R3]
- Paper is clearly written and easy to read. [R2]
- Work includes a thorough analytical review. [R1]

### Weaknesses
- Figure 1 is unclear: the left side shows a delivery route between node 1 and node 2, but the right side labels the same edge as “001” indicating “no delivery route”, causing a contradiction. [R1]
- The method combines existing techniques (whole word mask, geocoding, Graphormer). While Reviewer 3 considers the multi-modal combination a novel contribution, Reviewer 2 states it lacks novelty as a combination of existing technologies. [R2, R3 – minority disagreement]
- Missing important experimental details: inference efficiency, data distribution of pre-training tasks, convergence analysis, parameter selection, optimizing strategies, and statistical significance tests. [R2]
- Scalability and inference limitations: unclear how to handle new addresses not in the fixed heterogeneous graph; the model appears to require a complete graph at inference, which limits real-world deployment where the graph may be incomplete or incorrect. Robustness to noisy/incorrect map input is unknown. [R2, R3]
- The pre-training uses less than 100K samples, and it is unclear how performance scales with larger data or if the model could be trained with text information alone. [R3]
- The paper only evaluates text-based downstream tasks; it is unclear if G2PTL can be fine-tuned for graph-related tasks like link prediction or node classification. [R3]
- Lack of rationale for balancing pre-training tasks (e.g., HTC seems most important from ablation) and for subgraph sampling from the entire graph versus creating subgraph-level models; both decisions require further justification. [R2]
- At inference, it is unclear whether graph input is mandatory; if the task is address normalization without graph input, can the model still be used? Also unclear what graph size (global vs local) is needed. [R3]
