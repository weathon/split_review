## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a disentangled representation learning framework that combines two existing assumptions—sufficient changes in latent variable distributions (via auxiliary variables) and structural sparsity of the mixing process. By showing these assumptions complement each other, the authors derive identifiability guarantees with less restrictive constraints. They implement two generative models (CG-VAE and CG-GAN) using a domain encoding network and sparse mixing constraints. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets support the theoretical results. All three reviewers rated the paper 6 (accept), but one reviewer (R3) rated contribution 3, while others rated contribution 2.

### Strengths
- Provides theoretical understanding of identifiability in disentangled representation learning, with clear and well-structured mathematical derivations and proofs (R1, R2, R3).
- Proposes two generative implementations (CG-VAE and CG-GAN) using a domain encoding network and sparse mixing constraints (R1).
- Originality: combines two existing methods (sufficient changes and sparse mixing) in a compatible way, guaranteeing identifiability (R2).
- Includes both synthetic and real-world experimental validation (R1, R3).
- Schematic graphical model is helpful; theorems are presented and illustrated clearly (R3).
- Contribution visually understandable; solid proofs (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Notation and clarity issues (R1):** Notations are heavy and hard to follow. Need a comprehensive notation table at the beginning. Specific examples: In equation (2), meaning of subscripts of \(\mathbf{x}_{k,(1)}\) is unclear; notation \(\backslash k\) (all indices but \(k\)) is undefined; in equation (6), a second integral appears to be missing. 
- **Typos and grammatical errors (R1, R3):** Line 157: "contributions" should be "contribute" (R1). Line 349: "\(q(z|x)\) is the an encoder" (R3).
- **Theorem A3 sentence confusing:** Hard to read due to length (R3).
- **Missing quantitative evaluation (R2, R3):** Only qualitative t-SNE plots provided; need quantitative disentanglement metrics (e.g., metrics from [1] and [2] suggested by R2; MSE, \(R^2\), or cross-entropy suggested by R3). t-SNE itself introduces dimensionality reduction, making disentanglement assessment unreliable (R2).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons in synthetic experiments (R1, R3):** No comparison with nonlinear ICA methods (R1) or other baseline methods (R3). Specifically, R1 suggests comparing CG-VAE and CG-GAN with relevant nonlinear ICA methods. R3 notes CG-GAN was not applied in synthetic experiments, and no baseline methods are included.
- **Missing real-world comparison with CG-VAE (R3):** Authors apply CG-GAN to real-world data but not CG-VAE; no explanation why VAE is unsuitable for these datasets.
- **Unclear why VAEs and GANs were chosen as implementations (R2):** Many VAE variants exist for disentanglement; the authors do not discuss whether the framework applies to those variants or why comparisons to TC-VAE, FactorVAE, HFVAE were not made (R2 mentions these specific models).
- **Missing ablation/design choices (R3):** In Eq. (9), why is the sparsity loss \(L_s\) chosen as absolute sum rather than squared sum? No analysis of trade-offs or stability. Also, "CG-VAE-S" appears before being introduced.
- **Limited experimental results in main content (R3):** The experiment section is too simple; more comprehensive comparisons and quantitative results are needed.
- **Conceptual explanation missing (R1):** Need intuition why sufficient changes for component-wise identification require the second derivative assumption, while subspace identification does not.
- **Line 158 relationship unclear (R3):** The paper states \(x_k\) contributes \(z_j\) via derivative \(\partial z_j/\partial x_k\); the causal direction (why not \(z_j\) contributes \(x_k\)) needs explanation.
