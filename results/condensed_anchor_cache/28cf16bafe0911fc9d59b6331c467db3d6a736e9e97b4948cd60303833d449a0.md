## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Implicit In-context Learning (I2CL), which generates a condensed "context vector" from demonstration examples and injects it into the model’s residual streams during inference. The goal is to achieve few-shot performance at zero-shot inference cost. Experiments on 8–9 text-classification tasks across three model architectures (primarily Llama-2) show I2CL is comparable to vanilla ICL, slightly worse on average (≈1%), while being less sensitive to example order and selection. The method also yields a novel "task-id" representation. Reviewers rated the paper 6, 8, 6, 6.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and efficiency improvement:** I2CL presents a unique approach to reducing ICL’s computational and memory overhead by absorbing demonstrations into activation space, achieving few-shot performance at zero-shot cost (R1, R2, R3). This is an important advancement for resource-constrained applications (R1).
- **Interesting activation-merging direction:** The idea of performing activation merging to learn new skills at negligible cost is an interesting and effective direction (R2, R4). The method is simple, easy to understand, and easy to implement (R3, R4).
- **Scales well with more demonstrations:** In contrast to vanilla ICL, I2CL scales well with increasing demonstration count (R2, R3). Vanilla ICL can even degrade after 5-shots, while I2CL does not suffer from this (R3 observation, left as unexplained weakness below).
- **Robustness to ordering and choice of demonstrations:** I2CL is less sensitive to the order and selection of demonstration examples than vanilla ICL, addressing a major weakness of traditional ICL (R2, R3).
- **Useful "task-id" concept:** The observation that calibrated linear coefficients can function as effective task-ids is a novel contribution that could enhance task similarity detection and enable transfer learning (R1, R3).
- **Comprehensive experiments and clear presentation:** The paper provides comparisons with diverse baselines (task vector, label anchors, prompt compression), careful ablation studies on learned coefficients, and is well-structured and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair cost comparison (methodology):** The major claim of zero-shot cost is undermined by the fact that Table 1 does not include the optimization cost of noisy self-calibration (Section 2.4). This calibration requires multiple inference passes on demonstration examples and Adam optimization, taking 1–2 minutes on a single A100 (R3). It is unclear whether this cost is counted in inference time (R3). Only #cached parameters are considered as memory cost (R3).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations:**
  - No comparison with few-shot parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, which also require compute for weight training (R2). The paper has two computational costs (context vector extraction and coefficient training) that should be compared to PEFT (R2).
  - Only one simple prompt is used; other prompts (e.g., SUBJ, which is very simple for Llama-2 few-shot) should be tested (R3).
  - Evaluation is limited to text classification; should include open-ended generation and multi-step reasoning for broader applicability (R1).
  - Scaling to larger LLMs beyond Llama-2 is not explored; it is unclear whether efficiency gains preserve with model scale (R1). Caching large context vectors for larger models may be infeasible (R2).
  - Potential transfer to other modalities (e.g., vision) is not discussed (R1, referencing Zhang et al. 2023).
  - More ablation studies in general are needed for deeper insights (R4).
- **Performance gaps and unexplained behaviors:**
  - I2CL is about 1% worse than vanilla ICL on average, with notable performance drops on simple datasets (e.g., SST-2, MR) that are not explained (R3).
  - Performance varies across tasks compared to prompt-compression baselines; no analysis of which task characteristics drive these differences (R4).
  - In Figure 3, vanilla ICL performance decreases after 5-shots, which contradicts the expected increasing trend; this is not explained, and the dataset used in that figure is not specified (R3).
  - It is unclear what information the context vector captures—e.g., does it encode classification labels or only task-level features? How does it handle nuanced variations within a single task type? (R1)
- **Lack of theoretical/mechanistic analysis:** The paper relies solely on experimental evidence and lacks a clear explanation of why I2CL outperforms prior approaches; it feels more like a technical report than a scientific study (R4).
- **Limited model accessibility:** I2CL depends on accessing intermediate activations during inference, restricting its use in closed/black-box models; adaptations for such environments are needed (R1).
