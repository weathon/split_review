## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a lifelong instruction tuning (LiIT) scenario for multimodal LLMs where old and new datasets remain in a growing pool. To handle redundancy and computational growth, the authors propose LAMP (Lifelong and Adaptive Multi-way Pruning): pseudo-skill clusters are formed via gradient-based grouping, an adaptive data selector (from a pool of scoring functions, including a new Image Grounding score) picks the most important samples per cluster, and cluster-wise permanent pruning removes semantically redundant samples. Experiments across varied tasks (VQA, multilingual, grounding, reasoning, language-only, multi-image) show that LAMP mitigates catastrophic forgetting, improves forward transfer, and uses only a fraction of the original data. Reviewers note the setting’s practical relevance but raise concerns about novelty, computational cost, evaluation scope, and some methodological weaknesses.

### Strengths

- The lifelong instruction tuning setting (previous datasets remain in the pool) is more practical than traditional continual learning for current multimodal LLMs, since using all available data is critical. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
- The framework thoughtfully addresses key difficulties of data selection in growing datasets with carefully designed pipelines (Sections 3 and 4) and demonstrates clear research objectives and logical structure. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- The proposed method mitigates catastrophic forgetting (especially for rare tasks) and supports forward transfer, enabling skill retention and adaptation to new tasks. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- Cluster-wise data pruning manages dataset growth, keeping training scalable and resource-efficient. (Reviewer 3)
- Experiments are thorough, with comprehensive ablation studies validating individual components and showing convincing results over baselines. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; the narrative reflects deep understanding of the field. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Method novelty**: Gradient-based clustering and ensemble scoring functions for data selection have been explored in prior work; the method (except the Image Grounding score) is not specifically designed for multimodal LLMs and could apply to broader domains (e.g., pure LLMs), but the paper only evaluates on multimodal tasks, lowering impact and novelty. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
- **Practical applicability**: The sequential dataset processing may not align with real-world MLLM training, which typically integrates diverse tasks simultaneously in a unified dataset. This raises questions about the method’s relevance to common training scenarios. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational cost**: The paper does not adequately address the computational complexity of k-means clustering and pairwise cosine similarity for large-scale instruction datasets. k-means is expensive, and pairwise similarity scales quadratically with sample count. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4) No efficiency analysis is provided comparing the time and cost of the proposed method versus the random pruning baseline; it is unclear how the cost of data selection compares to the training cost at step *t*. (Reviewer 4) 
- **Random pruning baseline**: In Table 2, random pruning already serves as a strong baseline; the performance improvements from the more complex LAMP pruning are marginal (e.g., 47.0% vs. 47.2% on some metric), and the added complexity and overhead may not be justified. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4) Similarly, multi-task learning (46.1%) even underperforms random pruning (47.2%), suggesting that pruning more data can sometimes yield better results—this is counterintuitive and needs clarification. (Reviewer 4)
- **LITE-LAMP sensitivity**: In Table 2, LITE-LAMP uses 4× less data (25k vs. 100k) but its relative improvement drops significantly (99.7 vs. 109.7). This indicates performance gains are highly sensitive to training data volume and may diminish in low-resource scenarios, raising scalability concerns. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited evaluation**: The evaluation focuses on short-answer tasks and omits open-ended visual chat (long responses), which is essential for assessing comprehensive MLLM capabilities. (Reviewer 4) Additionally, “accuracy” on LLaVA-Bench is not clearly defined. (Reviewer 4)
- **Scoring function limitations**: Only four scoring functions are used in practice; the claim that the framework can be seamlessly extended with new functions is not supported by ablation experiments on additional functions or combinations. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3) Selecting only one function based on average entropy may be suboptimal; combining multiple functions could yield more robust and representative selection. (Reviewer 1)
- **Figure clarity**: In Figure 2, subfigure C’s axes are unlabeled, making the depicted relationship unclear. Subfigure A’s colored regions and their connection to forgetting are not explained. (Reviewer 1) Text in all images is not very clear and appears distorted, hindering readability of labels and annotations. (Reviewer 1)
- **Clarification needed**: In Table 2, “data size at *t*” is not clearly explained. (Reviewer 4) Also, t-SNE visualizations are known to be stochastic across seeds; have the authors manually tuned these for the method and baselines? (Reviewer 2)
