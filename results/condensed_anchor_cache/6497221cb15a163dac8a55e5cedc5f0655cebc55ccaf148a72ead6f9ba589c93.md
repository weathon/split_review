## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes sCellTransformer (sCT), a convolutional-transformer foundation model for single-cell and spatial transcriptomics. It processes multiple cells jointly, uses up to 20,000 protein-coding genes, discretizes gene expression tokens, and incorporates spatial positional encodings. Evaluations include zero-shot imputation, cell typing, and clustering. Reviewers note interesting architectural ideas (convolution, learned gene normalization, spatial tokens) but disagree on novelty and rigor: two reviewers (scores 6,6) find the approach promising and biologically motivated, while two others (3,3) consider the improvements straightforward, benchmarking insufficient, and real-world utility unclear.

### Strengths
- Attention to biological properties of scRNA-seq data (discreteness, over-dispersion) often overlooked by other models (R1).
- Architecture enables processing multiple cells at once, providing contextual information beyond single-cell-at-a-time models (R1, R3).
- Incorporation of spatial information via spatial positional tokens (R1, R4: “first single-cell transformer to specifically attempt this”).
- Use of convolutional layers for efficiency (R2) and enabling scaling to all ~20k genes (R4).
- Learned gene normalization is a novel architectural addition (R3).
- Extension of imputation to whole-cell level (R3).
- Ablation experiments are well-designed and provide insights into model design (R3, also noted by R1 as “promising performance”).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient benchmarking against non-foundation baselines**: No comparisons with simpler methods (logistic regression, scVI, scANVI, k-NN on raw data, PCA, linear models). Recent benchmarking studies show transformer foundation models often do no better than these (R4). Clustering lacks even basic baselines like scVI or PCA embedding of raw data (R1). For imputation, missing domain-specific methods gimVI and scFoundation (R3).
- **Limited downstream tasks to support “foundation model” claim**: Only gene imputation and clustering tested; missing tasks like gene perturbation prediction, GRN inference, cell-cell communication (R2, R3). The model is more accurately described as an imputation model (R3).
- **Real-world utility unclear**: Masking randomly chosen genes (<6% expressed) often targets zeros, making imputation meaningless (R4). Motivation for whole-cell imputation (entire spot dropout) not clearly justified (R4). The paper does not articulate what *new* biological analyses sCT enables beyond existing tools (R4).
- **Architectural novelty questioned**: Learned Gene Normalization is essentially layer normalization, Positional Embeddings are standard sinusoidal; stacking 50 cells creates pseudo-bulks and may lower granularity rather than model intercellular dependencies (R2). The convolutional architecture is not compared with a simple MLP, and the rationale for “local” vs “global” patterns in non-sequential gene expression data is not explained (R3).
- **Discretization method not properly motivated or compared**: Binning is based on raw data but does not address raw count heterogeneity across studies; scGPT already used binning, so the claimed novelty is overstated (R1, R2). No comparison with alternative discretization methods (e.g., from openreview.net/pdf?id=gdwcoBCMVi); choice of bin edges justifications and how continuous outputs of other models are mapped to bins are unclear (R1, R3).
- **Preprocessing and evaluation issues**:
  - Library size differences not handled; uniform binning may be confounded by variable sequencing depths across cells (R4).
  - Baseline results suspicious: scGPT MAE of 260 on log-transformed/binned data; no sanity check with simple baselines (predict zero or mean) (R4).
  - Table 3 lacks metric description and sparsity level definitions (percentage of zero counts per gene?) (R1).
  - For spatial imputation, it is unclear whether compared models were fine-tuned on the same training data (R3).
  - Held-out data in HEST may have data leakage: some FOVs are replicates of training data (R3).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - “First principles formulation” not clearly explained (R3: should remove claim if not justified).
  - Definition of “sequence” (stack of cells) not introduced early enough; confusing for non-experts (R1).
  - Table 1 referenced before MCC defined; dashes and layer normalization effects unclear (R1).
  - Table captions discuss results rather than leaving interpretation to text (R1).
  - Figure 3 lacks titles, lacks UMAP from real gene expression for comparison; claiming “corrects” batch effect is unsupported without visual comparison with uncorrected data (R1).
  - Terminology: “gene expressions” should be “expression values” (R1).
  - Claim that spatial data is scarce is too strong given current availability (R1).
  - Incomplete sentences (e.g., “with a decay rate of …”) (R3).
  - Missing mention that 50 cells (not just 1 and 10) were tested (R1).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms** (summarized as weaknesses):
  - How are lower vs higher bin edges chosen when mapping continuous model outputs? (R1)
  - How many neighbors for KNN in whole-cell imputation? (R1)
  - What is the rationale for the convolutional design (local vs global patterns)? (R3)
  - How does learned gene normalization differ from original layer norm? What is a “subsequence”? (R3)
  - Could the contribution of single-cell data be assessed by pretraining on spatial data only? (R3)
  - How are cell types assigned in a training-free manner? (R3)
- **Reviewer disagreement on severity**: Two reviewers (R1, R3) rate the model promising (soundness 2, contribution 3 and 2), while R2 and R4 are highly critical (soundness 2 and 1, contribution 2 and 1). R4 particularly highlights that the results may be artifacts of the evaluation setup and lack rigorous baselines.
