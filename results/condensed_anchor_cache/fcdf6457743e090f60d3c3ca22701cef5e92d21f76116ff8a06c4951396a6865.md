## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper demystifies GNN-to-MLP distillation by arguing that GNNs primarily act as regularizers rather than increasing expressivity. The authors propose replacing the GNN with three explicit techniques: (1) a smoothing/consistency loss, (2) a label-histogram structural embedding, and (3) iterative pseudo-label smoothing. Through experiments on several homophilic datasets, they show that an MLP trained with these components can match or exceed GNN distillation without ever training a GNN. The paper also notes limitations on heterophilic graphs.

### Strengths
1. **Interesting and timely insight**: The paper is the first to unify GNN distillation methods under a hypothesis that GNNs serve as consistency regularizers and leverage unlabeled data via message passing; this perspective is experimentally validated. (R1, R3, R4)
2. **Clarity and readability**: The writing is clear and straightforward; one can easily follow the motivations and the experimental design. (R2, R4)
3. **Simple yet effective replacement**: The proposed techniques – particularly the label histogram as a structural embedding – are simple, reasonable, and achieve performance comparable to GNN distillation without training a GNN. (R1, R2, R4)
4. **Notable empirical improvements**: In several datasets the proposed method yields strong improvements over existing methods. (R1, R2)
5. **Ablation studies and code provided**: The paper includes ablation studies to understand each component, and the code is publicly available. (R1)

### Weaknesses
1. **Soundness of key claims** (R2, R4, R3):
   - The claim (lines 132-133) that "MLPs overfit" in few-label settings is not supported; overfitting describes a train-test gap, not just poor performance. Authors should provide train-test performance gaps of GNNs vs MLPs to support this. (R2)
   - The claim (lines 139-140) that distillation "increases the size of the training set with GNN pseudo-labels" is not well validated. An ablative experiment replacing GNN pseudo-labels with MLP's own pseudo-labels (self-training) is needed. (R2)
   - Proposition 1's equivalence of GNN distillation loss and consistency loss is proved only for a linear setting; actual MLPs have nonlinear activations. A proper theoretical analysis for the non-linear case is missing. (R4)
   - Significance of the label histogram is not deeply investigated; little theoretical or empirical insight into why it works or on which nodes it helps is provided. (R2, R3)
2. **Novelty concerns** (R2, R4):
   - Regularizing MLP with graph structure is not new. Missing citations (e.g., [1], [2]) and the smoothing-regularization loss appears equivalent to the contrastive loss in [4] under homophily, but without negative pairs. The paper should clearly distinguish. (R2)
   - Iterative pseudo-labeling is a common practice in few-label GNN learning (e.g., [5]); its motivation in a distillation context (especially when labels are sufficient) is unclear. The connection between GNN distillation and few-label learning is not well justified. (R2)
   - The three proposed techniques are largely combinations of existing ideas; the overall technical contribution is incremental. (R4)
3. **Experimental limitations** (R2, R4, R1):
   - Main experiments are in the transductive setting; the inductive setting (the core benchmark for GNN-to-MLP distillation) is relegated to the appendix. In the inductive setting, the proposed method is even surpassed by GLNN in some datasets and by NOSMOG in many, questioning its effectiveness. (R2)
   - Only GraphSAGE is used as the GNN teacher (Table 3); results with other teachers (GCN, GAT, APPNP) are needed. (R1, R2)
   - All datasets are small; results on larger-scale benchmarks (ogb-arxiv, ogb-products) are missing. (R4)
   - Several strong baselines from related work (e.g., VQGraph, FF-G2M) are not compared. (R4)
   - Homophily ratio for each dataset should be reported to understand result variation across homophily levels. (R1)
   - Impact of degree: the proposed techniques rely on neighborhood data (label histogram, consistency loss). How does performance vary with node degree? Are there studies on noisy neighbors? (R1)
   - Inference time and training cost comparisons are missing. (R2)
4. **Heavy reliance on homophily assumption** (R2, R4):
   - The analysis and methods explicitly depend on strong homophily; performance on heterophilic graphs is not explored and the paper's claim that "neither distillation nor direct regularization yields strong performance under heterophily" is not supported by evidence. This limits applicability to real-world mixed-pattern graphs. (R2, R4)
   - “Neighborhood smoothing” is an overly strong assumption; the method may not extend to general graphs. (R4)
5. **Minor issues** (R2, R3, R1):
   - The analysis should use "GCN" explicitly instead of "GNN" since only GCN architecture is analyzed. (R2)
   - In the inductive setting, how is the label histogram computed for unobserved test nodes? Does it access the graph during inference? (R2)
   - The iterative pseudo-labeling process: where do pseudo-labeled nodes come from? Are they used for testing, potentially causing evaluation unfairness? (R3)
   - Impact of γ in Eq. 3 (weight to self vs. neighbor) is not analyzed. (R1)
   - Section 4.3.1: the causal reasoning between optimizing MLP without neighbor usage and applying prediction smoothing to pseudo-labels is unclear. (R3)
