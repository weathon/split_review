## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies activation sparsity in decoder-only Transformer LLMs, proposing a new metric PPL-p% sparsity that is performance-aware. It investigates scaling properties with training data, activation functions (ReLU vs. SiLU), width-depth ratio, and parameter scale. Reviewers find the metric novel and the analysis interesting, but raise significant concerns: limited model scale (up to 1.2B parameters), insufficient validation on downstream tasks, lack of practical demonstration of efficiency gains, questionable generality of the proposed scaling laws (fitted with four-parameter curves, limited training tokens), and unsurprising results (ReLU sparser than SiLU). One reviewer is more positive (score 6) highlighting the metric and design guidelines, while others (scores 5) note missing experiments and limited cohesive contribution.

### Strengths
- Novel metric PPL-p% sparsity, which is performance-aware and adaptable across architectures, building upon CETT (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The paper presents interesting empirical analyses of activation sparsity scaling with training data, activation functions, width-depth ratio, and parameter scale, with clear visualizations (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Provides actionable design guidelines for promoting sparsity, such as optimal width-depth ratios (Reviewer 3).
- Raises the interesting question of when a model can be considered sparse and identifies potential factors influencing sparsity (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited model scale and generalizability**: Experiments are limited to models up to 1.2B parameters, while state-of-the-art LLMs exceed 10B or even 100B parameters. The scaling behavior of activation sparsity may not be consistent at these scales, and the proposed laws lack validation on larger models (Reviewers 1, 3). Additionally, results may not generalize to other LLM families (Reviewer 2). One reviewer (R3) specifically calls for empirical validation on models with hundreds of billions of parameters.
2. **Insufficient downstream task evaluation**: The proposed PPL-p% metric is compared only on perplexity, which may overfit to this criterion (Reviewer 2). The paper does not explore how increased sparsity impacts performance on downstream tasks such as commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension, question answering, or text summarization (Reviewers 2, 3). Without such evaluation, the practical utility of the sparsity metric and the design guidelines remains unclear (Reviewer 3). The paper also inadequately addresses where sparse networks perform worse or better (Reviewer 4).
3. **Lack of practical demonstration**: The paper does not tie its insights together to produce a single model embodying all recommendations (optimal activation, training data, width-depth ratio) and compare its sparsity and downstream performance to a baseline (Reviewer 2). More broadly, the paper claims that creating sparse LLMs is of broad interest, but the cited works focus on removing sparse parts for speed, not on the performance impact of modifications (Reviewer 4). Furthermore, different tokens activate different channels, making it difficult to apply uniform activation patterns; the proposed method may not generalize across tokens, potentially negating efficiency gains (Reviewer 3). Detailed quantitative results on memory and computational efficiency improvements are missing (Reviewer 3).
4. **Validation and rigor of scaling laws**: The claimed "empirical laws" are not sufficiently motivated and validated. A function with four parameters (Eq. 4) is fitted to curves, raising questions about generality (Reviewer 4). The paper uses an insufficient number of training tokens; for larger models (e.g., 0.8B and above) up to 200B tokens are typically required for convergence, and experiments rely heavily on predicted scaling curves (e.g., Figure 4), casting doubt on the reliability of the proposed scaling laws (Reviewer 3). The monotonicity assumption underlying the binary search for PPL-p% (relationship between PPL and CETT) is not rigorously proven (Reviewer 1).
5. **Narrow activation function comparison and missing architecture variants**: The paper only compares ReLU and SiLU. Practical LLMs often use SwiGLU, GELU, Swish, and different gating mechanisms in FFN layers, which may significantly affect activation sparsity (Reviewers 1, 3). The key result that ReLU produces sparser networks than SiLU is unsurprising given the known "dying ReLU" problem (Reviewer 4).
6. **Unverified claims about sparsity-performance trade-offs and inconsistency with MoE literature**: The paper states that overly high or low sparsity ratios may lead to severe performance degradation, but this claim lacks experimental validation. Notably, in many MoE models, higher sparsity levels correlate with improved performance, which contradicts this statement. A more nuanced discussion accounting for different model types and training regimes is needed (Reviewer 3).
7. **Incomplete analysis of width-depth trade-offs**: The paper highlights optimal width-depth ratios for sparsity but does not examine the associated performance trade-offs, such as model convergence, training stability, and final task performance when deviating from these ratios. This limits understanding of the flexibility of the proposed design guidelines (Reviewer 3).
8. **Lack of interpretability demonstration**: The paper suggests that activation sparsity can improve model interpretability, but provides no specific examples or methods to demonstrate this link (Reviewer 1).
