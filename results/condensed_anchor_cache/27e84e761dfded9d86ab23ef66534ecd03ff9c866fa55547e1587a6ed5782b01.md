## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces LARP, a novel video tokenizer for autoregressive (AR) generative models. It replaces traditional patchwise tokenization with a holistic approach using learnable queries to capture global, semantic representations. A lightweight AR transformer is integrated as a training-time prior to align the token space with downstream AR generation, improving the learnability of tokens. Experiments show state-of-the-art FVD on UCF-101 class-conditional generation and competitive results on Kinetics-600, with additional evaluations on reconstruction and frame prediction. The method is motivated by enabling unified multimodal LLMs.

### Strengths

- **Novel approach to video tokenization**: The holistic tokenization (learnable queries gathering global information) is a clear departure from patchwise encoding and is considered interesting and innovative (R1, R2, R4). The method allows an arbitrary number of tokens, offering flexibility.

- **AR prior model is simple yet effective**: Integrating a lightweight AR transformer during tokenizer training effectively addresses the gap between reconstruction quality and generation quality, making tokens more friendly for autoregressive modeling (R2, R3, R4). The design is well motivated by an insightful analysis of two limitations in current AR tokenization (R4).

- **Comprehensive experiments demonstrate effectiveness**: The paper provides evaluations on video reconstruction, class-conditional generation, and video frame prediction (R1, R2, R4). LARP achieves state-of-the-art FVD on UCF-101 and outperforms AR baselines on Kinetics-600 (R1, R2). Ablation studies confirm key design choices (R2, R3). The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (R4).

- **Inspiring message**: That properly considering downstream generation tasks when training tokenizers can significantly improve performance (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Similarity to prior holistic tokenization work (TiTok [1])**: The holistic tokenization idea is similar to TiTok on images. The paper does not adequately clarify the differences in query initialization, attention mechanism, loss functions, or input/output format beyond the AR prior (R2, R4). One reviewer (R4) specifically asks for a detailed comparison to strengthen the novelty claim.

- **Limited analysis and validation of the AR prior model**:
    - No quantitative analysis of the AR prior’s prediction accuracy on held-out data or its generalizability (R4). The prior model is only 21.7M parameters, whereas prior works (VideoPoet, Video-LaVIT) require 7B+ LLMs to predict next tokens in video latent spaces; this disparity needs justification and analysis of AR model capacity’s impact on generation (R4).
    - The mechanism by which a lower AR prior loss leads to better generation is not fully explained; an extreme example (all tokens identical) would yield low loss but cause codebook collapse and generation failure (R3).

- **Missing evaluation metrics and tasks**:
    - The paper uses only FVD on UCF-101; common metrics like FID and IS are absent, which would strengthen soundness (R4).
    - Benefit or penalty on other tasks (e.g., video editing, stylization, inpainting, outpainting) is not investigated (R2). One reviewer (R2) specifically asks whether holistic tokens would hurt appearance preservation in editing tasks.
    - Scenarios where LARP underperforms or fails are not discussed (R1). The training stability over longer periods or under different regimes is unclear (R1). Computational cost and complexity of training with the AR prior are a concern (R1).

- **Lack of intuitive analysis and validation of claimed “global and semantic representations”**:
    - The claim that holistic tokenization captures more global and semantic representations is not validated experimentally (R3). For instance, adding the AR prior to standard patch tokens (Fig. 2a) could be compared to show the specific benefit of the holistic design.
    - No visualizations of the learned latent space (e.g., heatmaps of token ordering, t-SNE plots) are provided to illustrate how the AR prior and token ordering work (R4).

- **Specific missing ablations and clarifications**:
    - Table 1: LARP-L-Long with 1024 tokens achieves rFVD 20, while MAGVIT-v2 with 1280 tokens achieves rFVD 8.6. Since LARP supports arbitrary token counts, what would the rFVD be with 1280 tokens? (R3)
    - It is unclear whether a transformer encoder-decoder structure could serve as the autoregressive prior instead of the additional AR model (R3).
    - The paper uses an encoder-only transformer for the video tokenizer; the role of query-based attention is not fully disentangled from the AR prior (R3).
    - For Kinetics-600 frame prediction, the input format to the LARP encoder at inference is not clarified given the patch size described in Sec. 4.1 (R2).

- **Missing discussion of temporal ordering**: Since video data is already naturally ordered by timestamps, it is unclear why the tokenizer does not use this ordering directly, and whether the learned ordering reflects temporal structure (R4). This question indicates a potential suboptimal design.

- **Minor issues**: Several typos (line 270: “back to the continues” → “back to the continuous”; line 459: “hilighting” → “highlighting”) (R3).
