## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Generative Subspace Adversarial Active Learning (GSAAL), a method using a GAN with multiple discriminators to learn marginal class probabilities in different subspaces while a single generator models the inlier distribution. It aims to simultaneously address multiple views (MV), inlier assumption (IA), and curse of dimensionality (CD). The paper provides a mathematical formulation of MV, convergence guarantees, complexity analysis, and experiments on synthetic and 22 real datasets.

### Strengths

- The problem is important: the paper tackles three critical issues (IA, CD, MV) in tabular outlier detection, with a clear and well-motivated objective. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- The subspace (multiple views) issue is clearly presented and formalized. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- The technique appears sound, with robust theoretical support including a mathematical formulation of multi-view approaches, a convergence guarantee for the discriminators, and a complexity analysis. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- Presentation is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 1). *Note: Reviewer 2 and 3 rated presentation lower (2, 2); see weaknesses.*
- Experimental results are sufficient, covering both synthetic and real datasets. (Reviewer 1)
- **Reviewer 2 (rating 5, confidence 5) was notably more positive**, highlighting the effective addressing of multiple views, the strong theoretical backing, and the extensive experimental validation.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty is limited.**  The core idea of one generator with multiple discriminators has been explored in prior works such as MCL-GAN and DREAM; these should be discussed. The main difference from previous GAN-based outlier detection (e.g., GAAL) is the addition of multiple discriminators, which is not a significant advance. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Insufficient justification for handling multiple views via multiple discriminators.**  It is unclear why learning multiple discriminators addresses the MV/subspace issue. Theorem 1 is not sufficient to support the claim. (Reviewer 3)  Details are missing on how the d attributes are partitioned into k groups for the k discriminators (e.g., random?). A necessary baseline would be to apply another OD method to each attribute group and combine results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited superiority on real datasets.** In Table 5, GSAAL achieves the best results on only 5 out of 22 real datasets, and is outperformed by many baselines on the majority of datasets. This indicates the method does not consistently outperform existing methods. (Reviewer 2, 3)  This severity is highlighted by the fact that the method is a deep learning approach yet is often beaten by simpler baselines (e.g., in Table 3, none of the compared methods are deep learning except Deep SVDD). (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons with recent deep learning methods.** The baselines are outdated; many deep learning-based tabular anomaly detection methods from after 2020 should be compared, including MCM (Masked Cell Modeling), ICL (Internal Contrastive Learning, ICLR 2022), SLAD (Scale Learning, ICML 2023), and NeuTraL AD (Neural Transformation Learning, ICML 2020). Why was Deep SVDD compared on real datasets but not on synthetic datasets? Why were deep learning methods omitted from the time cost comparison in Figure 3? (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Clarity issues in figures and presentation.**
    - Figure 1 is unclear: it lacks a decision boundary in the 3D space visualization, and the cluster-based and locality-based methods it references are not defined. (Reviewer 2)
    - The overall framework of the proposed method needs a clearer illustration. (Reviewer 2)
    - The paper describes the training process (Algorithm 1 in Appendix) but omits the inference process, which is needed for a complete understanding. (Reviewer 2)
- **Incomplete description of synthetic data experiments.** In Section 4.1.1, the method for generating outliers is not described. The high-dimensional setting is unrealistic because most real dataset features are mutually correlated. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unaddressed GAN convergence challenge.** Given the well-known difficulty of training GANs, what can be said about the convergence of the proposed method? (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of motivation for active learning.** The use of active learning in the context of outlier detection is not clearly motivated; a more detailed explanation is needed. (Reviewer 1)
