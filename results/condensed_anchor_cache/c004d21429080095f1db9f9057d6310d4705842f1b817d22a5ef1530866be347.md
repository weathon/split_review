## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MeFBO, a first-order stochastic gradient method for federated bilevel optimization (FBO) that uses a Moreau envelope–based min-max reformulation to avoid requiring the lower-level objective to be strongly convex. The method is Hessian-free. Theoretical convergence guarantees and communication complexity are provided, and experiments on federated hyper-parameter tuning and hyper-representation with MNIST are reported. Reviewers’ scores range from 3 to 6. Opinions are mixed: Reviewer 3 (score 6) finds the paper well-written and comprehensive; Reviewer 4 (score 3) is critical of the problem formulation, experimental scope, and novelty; Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 5) acknowledge the relaxed assumptions and empirical work but raise concerns about limited novelty, parameter sensitivity, and presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- The method relaxes the strong convexity assumption on the lower-level objective and is Hessian-free, offering potential communication and computation efficiency in federated learning (R1).
- The theoretical analysis provides convergence and complexity results under weaker assumptions and clarifies the relationship between step size, number of participants, and local iterations (R1).
- Experimental results on challenging settings (e.g., imbalanced data) demonstrate efficiency and superiority over existing methods; Reviewer 2 notes interesting observations on the sensitivity of prior FBO methods (R1, R2).
- The paper studies the problem both theoretically and empirically (R3).
- The paper is well-written with good coverage of related work (R3).
- A Moreau envelope–based method and some theoretical analysis are provided (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty – combination of existing ideas.** Multiple reviewers find the contribution incremental. R1 states the method appears to be a combination of existing works and it is unclear whether the data‑heterogeneity analysis adds novelty. R2 notes that the method directly benefits from the Moreau envelope of Liu et al. (2024) and the communication complexity does not significantly improve over SimFBO. R3 feels the relaxation of lower-level convexity uses existing analytical tools, and the motivation for avoiding Hessian-vector products is only partially convincing because such products are not expensive in auto-diff frameworks (e.g., PyTorch). R4 points out that first-order methods for nonconvex distributed centralized (federated) problems were already studied two years ago [1] and no comparison with that work is provided.
- **Concerns about the penalty approximation and stationarity.** R4 questions whether the exterior penalty approach (approximating the constrained problem (2) with unconstrained (4)) guarantees that an optimal solution of (4) is optimal for (2). The convergence analysis targets a stationary point of the approximation, which may deviate from the stationary point of the original problem.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation.** Several reviewers note limitations: (i) only the MNIST dataset and 2–3 layer MLPs are used (R3, R4); (ii) only hyper-parameter optimization tasks are tested, not other bilevel tasks such as meta-learning or neural architecture search (R4); (iii) no systematic study of varying degrees of data heterogeneity – only i.i.d. vs. non-i.i.d. without isolating the effect (R1); (iv) claims of superiority in i.i.d./non-i.i.d. settings lack detailed explanations (R4); (v) concerns about the efficiency of the additional projection step (Eq. (12)) when applied to high‑dimensional deep learning models (R4).
- **Presentation and readability issues.** R2 finds the presentation confusing: the first‑page question mixes privacy and communication, yet privacy is not addressed; there is an inconsistency in describing Xiao & Ji (2023) as AID‑based in line 40 and ITD‑based in line 132. R4 states readability is poor, with numerous notations insufficiently explained (e.g., ∇₁ and ∇₂ in Eq. (9) are not clearly defined). (Contrast: R3 found the paper well‑written.)
- **Many tuning parameters.** R1 notes the algorithm has many parameters (local update rounds, server/client learning rates, penalty parameter, proximal parameter), making selection difficult in practice, and asks for guidance.
- **Convergence analysis issues.** R2 identifies an error in Eq. (109): the left‑hand side should be the expectation of the hypergradient norm, not the hypergradient itself. R2 also questions whether communication‑cost comparisons with prior works are properly framed (prior works use a small hypergradient‑norm‑squared criterion; a more detailed comparison is needed). R4 raises concern about the complexity of the projection problem (Eq. (12)) in high dimensions.
- **Missing comparisons.** R2 calls for a more detailed communication‑cost comparison with prior works. R4 asks for additional convergence‑rate comparisons with existing distributed centralized (federated) bilevel methods. R3 questions whether bilevel optimization is actually used in practical federated learning applications.
- **Motivation concerns.** R3 finds the motivation not very convincing: relaxing lower‑level convexity is not clearly a practically required improvement; the Hessian‑vector‑product avoidance is only partially motivated. R4 finds the main problem uninteresting because first‑order methods for nonconvex distributed problems were already studied.
