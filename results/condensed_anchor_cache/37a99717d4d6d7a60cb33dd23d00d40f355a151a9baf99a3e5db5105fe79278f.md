## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Hierarchical Diffuser (HD), a two-level diffusion-based planning method that combines a high-level Sparse Diffuser (SD) to generate jumpy sub‑goals and a low‑level planner to fill in detailed state‑action sequences. HD is designed to improve computational efficiency and generalization for long‑horizon offline RL tasks. Experiments on Maze2D, AntMaze, MuJoCo Gym, and FrankaKitchen show that HD outperforms the non‑hierarchical Diffuser and other hierarchical baselines in both performance and training/planning speed. The paper also analyzes the relationship between diffusion kernel size, data coverage, and generalization.

### Strengths
- The hierarchical structure is a natural fit for long‑horizon planning, and the method is simple to implement (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The jumpy high‑level planning (Sparse Diffuser) provides a larger receptive field at lower computational cost, and the kernel‑size experiments validate this trade‑off (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- HD is faster than Diffuser because the high‑level sequence is shorter and low‑level plans can be solved in parallel; replanning only requires the low‑level planner (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Discretizing planning into sub‑goals leads to more diverse solvable scenarios; the low‑level planner becomes task‑agnostic, and the SD with dense actions better fits the sparse objective, a typical bottleneck in hierarchical RL (Reviewer 2).
- Strong empirical results, especially on AntMaze and other long‑horizon tasks (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The analysis of receptive field vs. data coverage ratio is novel and offers valuable insight for future diffusion‑based planning research (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The paper is well‑organized and clearly written, with thorough theoretical analysis and well‑designed experiments (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to existing methods.** The architecture consists of two diffusers (one sparse for sub‑goals, one standard for segments) which is an incremental extension of Diffuser. The HDMI algorithm already uses a hierarchical diffusion approach; this paper’s main additional contribution is the generalization study (Reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer considers the contributions not significant (Reviewer 2); another finds the improvement on MuJoCo insufficient and notes that current SOTA diffusers (e.g., [1] with 167 on large Maze2D) perform better (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing important baselines and ablations.**
  * Decision Diffuser (state‑only diffusion) is not compared, and no ablation is provided to justify why state‑only diffusion is insufficient for estimating the return J (Reviewers 2, 4).
  * No comparison between CNN and transformer architectures (e.g., as used in Decision Diffuser) is given (Reviewer 4).
- **OOD generalization evaluation is too simple.** The tested out‑of‑distribution tasks lack complexity (e.g., other papers add coins or obstacles); harder OOD scenarios should be considered (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 suggests emphasizing generalization advantages more.
- **Insufficient discussion of key design issues.**
  * The smoothness of trajectory segments at connection points is not addressed (Reviewer 1).
  * The paper uses a PD controller for low‑level action generation but does not discuss its limitations (e.g., inaccuracies, oscillations) in scenarios requiring precise control (Reviewer 4).
  * No detailed analysis of the computational cost of the hierarchical approach vs. standard Diffuser (e.g., time, memory) is provided (Reviewer 4). While HD is stated to be faster, the cost comparison is not broken down (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing experiments and justifications.**
  * Only one goal selection method (fixed time interval) is evaluated; other simple heuristics (spatial intervals, reward scale, as used in HDMI) are not explored (Reviewer 3).
  * The paper does not demonstrate the quality of the generated sub‑goals, which is critical in hierarchical frameworks (Reviewer 3).
  * The training process is ad hoc (sub‑goals generated first, then low‑level trained with them as supervision); there is no feedback from the low‑level to the high‑level planner, and it is unclear whether end‑to‑end training is possible (Reviewers 2, 3). This can lead to non‑optimal behavior when low‑level paths overlap or do not reach exact sub‑goals (Reviewer 2).
  * The choice of return‑guided sampling over the inpainting technique used by Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, and HDMI is not justified (Reviewer 3).
- **Inconsistent performance and missing details.**
  * HD improves only one of three MuJoCo environments, so it is unclear whether the method consistently helps across tasks (Reviewer 1).
  * For Maze2D tasks, it is not specified whether diffusion‑sampled actions or a separate controller is used (Reviewer 2).
  * The number of trajectories sampled for sub‑goal sequences and for individual segments is not reported (Reviewer 2).
  * No explanation is given for why, in Fig. 3, the standard Diffuser fails to cover the full data distribution during execution despite possibly being able to do so, and why HD fails to cover two short branches (Reviewer 4).
- **Specific concept not clearly explained.** The meaning and contribution of SD‑DA (Sparse Diffuser with dense actions) is unclear; it appears to improve only walker2d, and predicting detailed actions may undermine the benefit of high‑level planning (Reviewer 1).
