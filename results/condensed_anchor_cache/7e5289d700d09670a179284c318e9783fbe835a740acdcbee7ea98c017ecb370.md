## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DMEMM, a diffusion-based offline RL planning method that incorporates transition dynamics and reward functions through additional modulation losses to improve trajectory coherence. The method includes transition-based diffusion modulation loss, reward-aware modulation, and dual-guided sampling. Experiments on D4RL locomotion and Maze2D show strong performance. Three reviewers score 3, one scores 6.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, straightforward, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).
- The idea is simple and clear (R1), and the motivation for improving trajectory coherence via transition consistency is convincing (R3).
- The method is a practical solution to an important problem (R2).
- The approach of utilizing transition dynamics and cumulative reward modulation in diffusion models is intuitive and novel (R3). The transition-based modulation loss is a particularly effective technical contribution for enforcing transition consistency (R3).
- Ablation studies are comprehensive, breaking down each component and showing its role (R3).
- Experimental results look good overall (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient benchmarks**: Evaluation is limited to D4RL locomotion and Maze2D. Missing standard environments such as AntMaze, Franka Kitchen, manipulation tasks, and image-based tasks (R2, R3). Given diffusion models’ origins in image domains, testing on image-based environments would strengthen credibility (R3). Reviewer 3 asks to add such environments.
- **Missing important baselines**: Decision Diffuser is not included despite being a strong diffusion-based planner that uses inverse dynamics to avoid transition inaccuracy (R1). The paper should also compare to diffusion policy, which achieved avg. normed return 88.0–89.0 on D4RL locomotion, higher than DMEMM’s 87.9 (R2). Reviewer 1 requests inclusion of Decision Diffuser.
- **Ambiguous motivation**: The claim that fixed isotropic variance and disregard for rewards lead to mismatch between generated and desirable trajectories is not convincingly justified. It is unclear if adding a transition loss truly improves consistency when diffusion already models trajectory distributions (R1). The claimed problem from (Wu et al., 2019) is not found in that paper; more analysis and examples are needed (R4).
- **Limited novelty**: Adding a loss on transitions is incremental (R1). It is unclear how formula (9) attaches to the diffusion loss; the reward model appears to fit the Q-function (without discount), and formula (11) is Q-weighted fitting — overall unimpressive (R4).
- **Results not fully convincing**: The method does not achieve SOTA when compared with other recent diffusion-based offline RL methods (R2, R4). Ablation differences in Table 3 (e.g., DMEMM-w/o-λtr vs DMEMM-w/o-tr-guide) appear insignificant; significance testing is needed (R4). On Maze2D, performance degrades as size increases compared to HD-DA, raising scalability concerns (R2). Ablation study in Table 3 only covers part of the environments (R1).
- **Clarity issues**: Notation for subscripts/superscripts on τ (e.g., time step, trajectory segment) is not clearly defined, hindering understanding of mathematical formulations (R3). The Preliminaries model RL as a quadruple (S,A,T,R) without discount factor γ, deviating from standard RL; if by design, this should be explained (R4).
- **Reproducibility**: No code is provided, and the paper does not commit to releasing code, limiting reproducibility and verification (R3). Reviewer 2 asks if code will be published.
- **Missing discussion**: The paper lacks discussion of diffusion models in the context of imitation learning, a subset of RL, which is needed to clarify advantages of diffusion for planning (R3). Reviewer 3 requests to address this and discusses references [1,2].
- **Open questions and further analyses needed**: 
  - How does DMEMM handle highly stochastic transitions or non-stationary rewards? (R3)
  - What are the primary computational trade-offs of dual-guided sampling compared to model-based/model-free RL? (R3)
  - How sensitive is DMEMM to weighting of transition- and reward-based modulation losses, and what practical guidelines exist for setting these parameters across environments? (R3)
  - Can the method be adapted to hierarchical or multi-step decision-making? (R3)
- **Autoregressive generation reduces importance**: Since the paradigm executes only the first action per generation, the need for accurate full transitions is diminished (R1).

*Note: One reviewer (R3, score 6) is significantly more positive, particularly on motivation, novelty, and technical contribution, while the other three (scores 3) find the contributions incremental and the evidence insufficient.*
