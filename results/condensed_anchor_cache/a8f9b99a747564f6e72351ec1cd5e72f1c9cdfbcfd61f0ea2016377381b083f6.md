## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents KGARevion, a knowledge graph (KG) based agent for medical question answering. It fine-tunes an LLM on a KG completion task (using PrimeKG) then performs three actions: **Generate** relevant medical fact triples from the question, **Review** the triples by checking them against KG embeddings (TransE) and an LLM to filter errors, and **Revise** to add more facts and correct incorrect triples. The final answer is produced by an LLM prompted with the revised triples. The agent is evaluated on multiple gold-standard medical QA datasets (open-ended and multi-choice) and on a new benchmark, MedDDx, which uses semantically similar incorrect answers and varying difficulty levels. KGARevion outperforms 15 baselines by over 5.2% on gold-standard datasets and achieves a 10.4% improvement on the new complex datasets. The approach is designed to be versatile, working with different base LLMs and KGs.

### Strengths
- **Medical Application**: Addresses a challenging, knowledge-intensive domain (medicine) with a structured reasoning pipeline.
- **Original Architecture**: The generate‑review‑revise‑answer pipeline is novel; the integration of a revision step is specifically innovative for handling complex medical queries requiring precise domain knowledge.
- **Thorough Evaluation**: Compared against many competitors on multiple open-ended and multi-choice datasets, plus ablation and sensitivity analyses. Results show state-of-the-art performance.
- **Creative Benchmark**: MedDDx, built with incorrect answers semantically similar to correct answers and separating questions into difficulty levels, is a creative and useful contribution.
- **Reproducibility**: Code is provided and appears well-documented.
- **Versatility**: Works with various base LLMs and KGs; the approach is generalizable beyond medicine (though not demonstrated).
- **High-Quality Figures**: Especially Figure 2, which clearly illustrates the pipeline and helps understanding.
- **Significance**: Offers an effective tool that integrates LLM non-codified knowledge with KG structured knowledge for accurate medical QA.

### Weaknesses
- **Complexity and Reusability**: The approach requires many components: a separate entity recognizer (UMLS mapping), two LLMs (generation/revision and review), a KG for fine-tuning, and a KG embedding model. This reduces reusability; condensing into one LLM and dataset would increase accessibility.
- **Data Leakage Concern (Reviewer 2, later partially addressed)**: MedDDx is derived from STaRK‑Prime, which was built from PrimeKG—the exact KG used for fine‑tuning. This creates a risk of data leakage. Further, other benchmarks (e.g., BioASQ) also likely overlap with PrimeKG sources (DO, GO, UMLS, DrugBank). The second KG used (ogb‑bioKG) has unresolved provenance and may share similar sources. Reviewer 2 originally recommended omitting overlapping results, but after the rebuttal (where additional benchmarks from a different lineage were added and clarifications given) the reviewer was satisfied. Nevertheless, the overlap remains a potential weakness.
- **Presentation / Clarity Issues**:
    - The introduction is loosely connected; paragraphs 1 and 2 are not clearly tied to the main motivation.
    - Figure 1 is placed in the introduction without experimental context and does not clearly support the narrative; it should be moved or removed.
    - The motivation against RAG is unclear because the authors acknowledge that KGs can also be incomplete or incorrect.
    - Section 3.2 (Review, fine‑tuning stage) is very hard to understand; more structure (sub‑headings, numbered steps) and alignment with Figure 2 are needed.
    - PrimeKG is not mentioned in Section 3 (Approach), making it unclear that the KG used for fine‑tuning is external, not the triples from the “Generate” step.
    - The revise and answer sections do not specify the exact processing methods/models used.
- **KG Relevance Questioned (Reviewer 3)**: Results in Section 4.4 barely depend on the choice of KG (PrimeKG vs. ogb‑bioKG). This raises the question of whether the fine‑tuned KG is necessary at all. A control experiment with an unrelated KG (e.g., Freebase15k‑237) is requested to test if performance remains similar.
- **Missing Related Work**: Fact‑checking approaches in knowledge graphs (e.g., Ciampaglia et al. 2015, Shi & Weninger 2016) are not discussed, and the review step is not compared to them.
- **Unspecified Details / Questions that Overlap with Criticisms**:
    - What model is used for the Revise step? If an unfine‑tuned LLM, does it have the capability to correct erroneous triples? (Reviewer 1 also asked about using UMLS facts for KB completion.)
    - How are triples involving entities not present in the KG (incomplete knowledge) handled? Are they accepted as credible or further processed?
    - How does each step handle multi‑hop reasoning paths? The generate and revise steps appear not to support paths; the review step may indirectly via KG embeddings.
    - Example question (Section 3) seems to imply a yes/no answer, but the system returns a set of answers—clarification needed.
    - TransE training details (prediction task) and choice of TransE for KG embeddings are not explained.
    - The description dictionary D(r) (Section 3.3) is referenced but not provided in the appendix.
    - Figure 3 y‑axis label (“accuracy”) but no legend for blue vs. orange plots.
- **Minority Reviewer Contrast**: One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 8) was more positive, noting high originality, quality, clarity, and significance, but still identified incomplete descriptions in the methodology (e.g., revise and answer processes). This contrasts with the other reviewers (scores 6) who raised more substantial concerns about complexity, data leakage, and presentation.
