## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses high variance in on-policy (and later off-policy) evaluation in reinforcement learning by proposing a behavior policy that minimizes variance under safety constraints. The authors formulate an optimization problem, provide theoretical proofs of unbiasedness and variance reduction, and empirically demonstrate on GridWorld and MuJoCo that the method reduces variance while satisfying cost budgets, outperforming baselines like on-policy Monte Carlo.

### Strengths
- Addresses a novel and important problem: incorporating safety constraints into policy evaluation to reduce variance without risking damage during execution (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The motivation is well-presented, e.g., using a Google data center example, and the background section is clear (Reviewer 4).
- The proposed method is simple, built on a principled optimization formulation, and comes with theoretical guarantees (feasibility, unbiasedness, variance reduction) (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- The literature review is thoughtful and comprehensive, though one reviewer suggests a more organized safe RL classification using survey papers (Reviewer 3 positive, Reviewer 4 suggests improvement).
- Experimental demonstrations are solid: results are averaged over many runs, compared against strong baselines, and show improved variance reduction under any given cost budget (Reviewers 1,4). The method performs well in GridWorld and MuJoCo (Reviewer 4).
- It is a good idea to first develop theoretical results in the contextual bandit setting and elegantly present key ideas there (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Safety constraint form**: More discussion is needed on when the constraint “expected cost of behavior policy ≤ (1+ε)×expected cost of target policy” is sufficient or insufficient in practice (Reviewer 1).
- **Innovation depth**: While the problem is novel, the approach to variance minimization under safety constraints lacks significant technical innovation (Reviewer 2). (Contrast: Reviewer 1 finds the method “simple” as a strength.)
- **Comparison with safe RL literature**: The experimental section should include comparisons with a broader range of safe RL methods, not just on-policy MC, to better demonstrate advantages; also, how different policy evaluation methods affect the performance of a constrained RL algorithm should be compared (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Transformation from (12) to (13) is potentially too conservative**: The constraint (12) requires total cost below a threshold, but (13) uses a per-step condition that may shrink the feasible set. If (13) has an empty feasible set, how is Theorem 3 derived? The relation between (12) and (15,16) (e.g., equivalence, conservative approximation) should be clarified (Reviewers 3,4).
- **Offline dataset and coverage**: The connection to offline datasets is unclear – if the dataset lacks sufficient coverage, can a reliable behavior policy still be obtained? (Reviewer 2)
- **Applicability to continuous state-action spaces and one-step methods**: The paper does not explain how the method applies to continuous spaces or to mainstream one-step policy evaluation methods like TD (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical details missing**: 
  - Actual variance numbers (magnitude) should be provided in a table (like Tables 3,4) beyond just Figures 1,2; the practical importance of the achieved variance reduction is not fully convincing (Reviewer 4).
  - Reproducibility is low – no source code, little experimental setup information (e.g., how safety costs are introduced in MuJoCo, how the offline dataset is generated, effect of dataset size) (Reviewer 4).
- **Undiscounted vs discounted setting**: The implications of using the undiscounted setting should be discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity on off-policy evaluation**: The paper does not explicitly state it deals with off-policy evaluation until line 160; consider adding “off-policy” to the title or abstract (Reviewer 4).
- **Safety threshold (12)**: The reason for the RHS (1+ε) not depending on target policy π is unclear – possibly a typo (Reviewer 4).
