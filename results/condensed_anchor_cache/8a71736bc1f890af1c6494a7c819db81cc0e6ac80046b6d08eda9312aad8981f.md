## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q-conditioned state entropy (QCSE) as an intrinsic reward to improve offline-to-online reinforcement learning by encouraging exploration of low-frequency states and penalizing high-frequency ones, implicitly achieving State Marginal Matching. The method is tested on CQL and Cal-QL (≈13% and 8% improvement), plus other algorithms (SAC, IQL, TD3+BC). Reviewers acknowledge the idea has some novelty and the experiments are comprehensive, but raise major concerns about theoretical correctness, clarity of derivations, missing baselines, and hyperparameter sensitivity.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and perspective**: The connection between offline-to-online learning and exploration is a relatively fresh perspective (R2). The Q-conditioned state entropy mechanism provides a new way to tackle distribution mismatch (R1, R4). One reviewer (R1) notes the idea is "novel" though another reviewer (R1) also finds the contribution "not very significant" (see Weaknesses).
- **Strong empirical performance**: Experiments show significant improvements over base algorithms (≈13% for CQL, ≈8% for Cal-QL) and good results across multiple tasks (R3). The evaluation covers modularity, advancement, and a wide range of tasks (R2).
- **Clarity of presentation** (R1): The paper is "highly readable and understandable" overall, though other reviewers (R2, R3) strongly disagree regarding the method description (see Weaknesses).
- **Versatility**: QCSE can be integrated into various offline RL algorithms beyond CQL and Cal-QL (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical issues and insufficient derivation**: Multiple reviewers find the theoretical arguments flawed or unclear.
  - Equation (1): The constraint “π := argmax_π …[R(τ)]” while the objective maximizes state entropy is misaligned; it is unclear how these two are optimized simultaneously (R3). Reviewers disagree on whether this resembles standard max-entropy RL.
  - The interpretation in lines 203–209 is contradictory: the authors state “narrowing down the domain S2”, but maximizing J₂ should expand S₂; this inconsistency is not resolved (R2).
  - The equivalence between state entropy maximization and KL minimization (Eq. (1) vs. SMM) is not adequately explained; maximizing entropy gives uniform-like distributions, not matching a target p* (R3).
  - The proof in Equation (4) uses an inequality direction that appears wrong: it claims ρ_π ≤ p* in S₂, which should be reversed if KL divergence is being minimized (R3).
  - The notation p(s) is ambiguous; it is not clear if it equals ρ_π(s) (R3).
  - The derivation from Eq. (1) to Eq. (2) (Critic Conditioned State Entropy) is missing. The roles of S₁ and S₂ in Eq. (1) are not mapped to the implementation; maximizing H(s|Q) is not linked to Eq. (1) (R2).
  - The origin of Eq. (3) is unclear; the paper simply cites literature without showing the derivation from Eq. (2) (R2).
  - The concept of “critic conditioned state entropy” (H(s|Q)) is poorly defined: it is not clear what p(s|Q(s,π(·|s))) means, especially how a probability can be conditioned on a scalar value (R3). The paper lacks intuitive explanation.
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis and limitations discussion**: The paper should discuss limitations and potential challenges more thoroughly (R1).
- **Missing baselines and incomplete comparisons**: Several key recent offline-to-online methods are omitted, notably ENOTO (Q-Ensemble based) and BR (R4). From the reported curves, these baselines appear to outperform QCSE in the same setting (≤250k online steps), yet no comparison is provided (R4). A broader range of SOTA methods should be included (R1).
- **Unclear performance on challenging datasets**: It is not shown whether QCSE handles medium-expert/expert datasets, which are known to be difficult for offline-to-online algorithms (R4).
- **Empirical performance still correlated with baselines**: Even with QCSE, results are still highly correlated with the base algorithm’s performance (e.g., walker2d-medium-replay); this suggests the method does not fully achieve optimal policies as the theoretical claim implies (R3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: QCSE relies on the number of k-nearest neighbors and the weight λ, but the analysis of these hyperparameters is limited. The tested k-range is narrow, and no guidance on selection or interaction with the base algorithm’s hyperparameters is provided (R1, R4). The method may be sensitive to these choices (R4).
- **Minority weakness on novelty**: One reviewer (R1) states that the contribution is “not very significant,” despite the overall positive view of the idea; the paper does not adequately differentiate from prior exploration strategies (R3 asks why QCSE is better than simple entropy maximization).
