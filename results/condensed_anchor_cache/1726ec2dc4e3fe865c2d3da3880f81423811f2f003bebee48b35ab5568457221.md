## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DisFormer, an architecture for unsupervised visual dynamics prediction that extends Slot Attention and SlotFormer by learning disentangled object representations. The model first extracts object masks and features (via Mask R-CNN), then uses iterative slot-attention over individual object representations to decompose them into “blocks” that are expressed as linear combinations of a small set of learnable concepts. A transformer predicts the next set of latent object representations, and a permutation module aligns predicted and ground-truth object order during training. Experiments on three synthetic datasets (2D Bouncing Shapes, 2D Bouncing Circles, OBJ3D with CLEVR) show improved pixel-level and position prediction compared to baselines, especially in zero-shot transfer settings. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)

### Strengths
- The motivation to learn disentangled object representations that separate dynamics-relevant attributes is intriguing and thought-provoking. (R3)
- The architecture is clearly motivated and intuitively designed, effectively applying slot-attention on top of object-centric representations to achieve disentanglement. (R3, R4)
- The paper provides a comprehensive comparative analysis, including an ablation study (DisFormer vs. DenseFormer), and shows consistent improvement over previous methods on several benchmark datasets. (R1, R2, R3; note R2 and R4 find the ablation insufficient – see weaknesses)
- The architecture addresses several challenges in training disentangled models in interesting ways. (R1)
- The abstract, introduction, and proposed model section are well-written (except the permutation module section). (R3)
- The figure of the model architecture is well-drawn and easy to understand. (R3)
- Related works and their differences with this work are well-discussed. (R4)

### Weaknesses
**Clarity and Presentation:**
- The explanation of the task and the actual problems comes too late; tasks are described only in the experiment section, and only one picture depicts the task. (R1)
- Many explanations are confusing or lack detail:
  - The concept of a “Block” is unclear. (R1) The paper says “each slot now represents a block, which is iteratively refined by taking attention over a latent object representation” – it is not clear why attention is needed after obtaining object masks. (R1)
  - The permutation module’s motivation is insufficiently explained; it is only used during training and is supervised with ground-truth information, so it is unclear why a learned permutation is needed instead of directly permuting features. (R1, R3)
  - The statement that the model is both “unsupervised” and “self-supervised” but also trained with supervised data (Mask R-CNN labels) is inconsistent. (R1)
  - Several symbols and steps are unclear: what is $k$ in Algorithm 1? (R3) In Section 3.2, how exactly are block vectors projected onto the concept space? Why does this projection lead to disentanglement? (R2, R3) In Section 3.4, is the process essentially projecting block vectors through matrix $U$, computing cosine similarity, and aligning similar representations as the same object? (R3)
- The manuscript’s clarity and organization could be improved overall. (R3)
- Minor typos: page 4 “and is a hyperparameter”; page 9 “we swap the positions swapped”. (R1)

**Experiments and Evaluation:**
- The datasets are simple toy datasets: 3 of the 4 are 2D bouncing balls; CLEVR is visually simple. (R1, R4) The original SlotFormer used CLEVRER, which is harder. (R4) The reviewer questions whether results would generalize to more complex scenes. (R1)
- No thorough ablation study is provided. The only ablation replaces the object extractor with an MLP (DenseFormer), which seems like an unreasonable substitution (replacing Mask R-CNN with an MLP). (R1, R2) There are no ablations on key components: the effect of slot-attention for decomposition, the number of slots/concepts, the loss weights, or the training phases. (R2, R4) Reviewer 3 acknowledges “comprehensive comparative analysis, including ablation studies” but does not detail any additional ablation beyond DenseFormer.
- The core claim of disentanglement is not convincingly demonstrated:
  - No quantitative measure of disentanglement (e.g., mutual information between concept vectors and ground-truth attributes). (R2)
  - Visualization of disentanglement (Section 4.4) is lacking; only a few examples swapping both color and shape are shown, and it is not clear whether the dynamics module truly decouples dynamics from appearance or simply masks image regions. (R1, R2, R3) Separate demonstrations for shape and color would be more effective. (R3)
  - It is unclear whether learned concept vectors correspond to interpretable attributes (shape, color, material) or which attributes contribute to dynamics prediction. (R2, R3)
- No discussion of particular success or failure cases (e.g., occlusions, fast motion, complex interactions). (R2)
- Missing comparison with SysBinder, despite being mentioned in the text. (R3)
- The “transfer learning” setup (Section 4.3) is unclear: two variants of the training set are created, but how is this different from the evaluation setting? It may be more accurately called generalization. (R2, R3)
- The permutation module’s ability to produce correct permutations even for 10 time-step separated objects is claimed but lacks ground-truth verification (no ground truth permutations available). (R1)
- Quantitative results could be bolstered with more illustrative examples showing where the proposed model excels. (R3)

**Methodology Details:**
- Mask R-CNN is trained with labeled data, whereas baselines (e.g., SlotFormer) do not use labeled data; this discrepancy is not addressed. (R3)
- The permutation module is only used during training – why not use it at test time for alignment? (R3)
- Training involves two phases (first block extractor, permutation module, decoder; then dynamic model); reasons and benefits are not explained. (R3)
- Multiple loss functions are used; no ablation studies on their individual impact. (R3)
- Hyperparameters (number of slots, number of concepts, loss weights) and training details are missing; it is unclear how they were tuned for DisFormer and baselines. (R2, R4)
- How object positions are estimated for all methods when evaluating position error is not described. (R2, R3)
- DenseFormer (the only ablation) is not well explained: how does it differ from SlotFormer? Are there scenarios where it fails more often? (R3)
