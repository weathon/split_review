## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FastSBDD, a two-stage generative model for structure-based drug design. The first stage uses a binding-affinity predictor to select unlabeled molecular graphs (topology only) from existing ligand databases; the second stage optimizes atom types and positions on that graph to improve molecular properties (QED, SA). The method reports strong property scores, up to 1000x faster inference, and 100x fewer parameters compared to state-of-the-art methods (DiffSBDD, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff) on Crossdocked2020. Theoretical analysis on GNN expressivity and generalization bounds is also provided. Reviewers are split: two rate 5 (positive but with significant reservations), two rate 3 (critical of core assumptions and experimental fairness).

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and problem importance**: The paper addresses a gap in SBDD – that prevailing models ignore binding affinity during training – and proposes a metric-aware optimization approach. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Strong empirical performance and efficiency**: FastSBDD achieves competitive or better Vina score, QED, SA, and diversity while being orders of magnitude faster and smaller. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Simple and effective method**: The two-stage design is straightforward and yields practical speed gains. (R2)
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides theoretical justification (LU-3DGNN expressivity, generalization bounds) that is lacking in most prior SBDD work. (R3, R4)
- **Code provided**: Implementation is publicly available. (R2)
- **Writing clarity**: Most reviewers (R2, R4) found the paper well-written and easy to follow, though R1 notes exceptions.

### Weaknesses
- **Fundamental concern: method does not achieve de-novo design**  
  The unlabeled molecular graph is sampled from existing ligand databases (e.g., ZINC, ChemBL), so no new 2D scaffolds are generated. R2 questions whether novelty is a limitation unless all possible ligands are covered; R4 states the method is more akin to drug repurposing than de-novo generation and should be compared accordingly. R3 notes that using additional datasets gives FastSBDD an unfair advantage over baselines that only train on Crossdocked2020.

- **Rationality of the two-stage approach is suspectable**  
  R1 finds the “disentanglement” label inappropriate – it is simply conditional independence. R2 questions the independence assumption: why can the unlabeled graph sampler be independent of the protein? R3 argues that ignoring protein–ligand interactions in the second stage (atom types, positions) contradicts structural biology, as specific interactions (hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, hydrophobic contacts) are critical for binding. R4 asks whether bond types are preserved in the unlabeled graph and notes that fixing the graph structure may severely limit atom-type flexibility.

- **Fairness of experimental comparisons**  
  R2 and R3 highlight that FastSBDD includes multiple filtering/optimization steps (binding-affinity selection, QED+SA filtering in atom assignment), while baselines are pure generative models without such post-processing. The paper should compare against optimization-based methods like MARS and RGA (R3), or at least apply similar screening to baselines (R2). R3 also notes missing important SBDD baselines, e.g., DecompDiff.

- **Binding affinity scoring model: accuracy and preliminary experiments**  
  The affinity predictor uses only the unlabeled 2D molecular graph, ignoring 3D protein–ligand interactions. R2 questions its accuracy and suspects that the high \( R^2 \) of Vina scores reported in Sec 3.1 and Appendix A may stem from redocking molecules rather than true prediction – further clarification is needed.

- **Theoretical analysis: connection to main claims**  
  R1 finds the theoretical sections (e.g., Sec 5.2 generalization bound in Eq 14) not clearly linked to the paper’s practical contributions. R2 asks whether the LU-3DGNN result (that distinguishing G1, G2 requires protein–ligand inter-message passing) implies a better model should use ligand 3D information, contradicting the paper’s design.

- **Diversity evaluation insufficient**  
  R4 argues that reporting overall diversity is less meaningful than showing diversity of generated ligands for each template unlabeled graph – the extent of chemical variation from the original ligand should be analyzed. R2 similarly notes the method cannot generate novel 2D graphs.

- **Writing clarity (dissenting view)**  
  R1 states the paper is not clear: Eq (1) is not self-inclusive, assumptions and conclusions are ambiguous, and the term “disentangled” is misused. This contrasts with R2 and R4 who found the paper clear.
