## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DM3D, a post-training weight pruning method for 3D object detection that uses a second-order Taylor approximation to minimize “detection distortion” (accuracy and confidence loss). The method computes layer-wise Hessian information and uses dynamic programming to allocate sparsity globally. It is designed as a plug-and-play, orthogonal complement to spatial sparsity methods (e.g., SPS-Conv). Experiments on KITTI, nuScenes, and ONCE with SECOND, CenterPoint, PointRCNN, Voxel-RCNN, and PV-RCNN show significant FLOPs reductions (up to 3.89× on ONCE for CenterPoint) with minimal or no mAP loss.

### Strengths
- A generic weight pruning framework that is orthogonal to spatial pruning methods and can be combined with them (e.g., SPS-Conv) to further reduce computation.  
- The method is accompanied by detailed theoretical derivations and proofs for the second-order Taylor approximation of detection distortion.  
- Acquisition of Hessian information and layer-wise sparsity search have low computational complexity (polynomial time via dynamic programming).  
- The approach focuses on lightening 3D detection (reducing memory and FLOPs), which is timely for real-world deployment.  
- The paper is well organized and easy to follow; experiments are adequate and conclusions are reliable.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and intuition**: The introduction does not clearly explain why weight pruning is especially needed for voxel-based 3D detectors, lacking empirical observations or examples that justify the design choices (R3, R4). The connection between rate-distortion theory and the proposed approach is not illustrated concretely (R4).  
- **FLOPs vs. actual speed**: Only FLOPs reductions are reported; the method’s effect on actual inference latency, runtime, and weight file size is not provided. Multiple reviewers request runtime measurements and deployment time for the pruning process itself (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Incomplete comparisons**:  
  - Ada3D is compared only on nuScenes, SPS-Conv only on KITTI (R1).  
  - The combination with spatial sparsity is tested only with SPS-Conv; experiments with Ada3D or other spatial pruning methods are missing (R2).  
  - No results on large-scale benchmarks like Waymo or Argoverse (R1).  
- **Missing / inconsistent baseline results**:  
  - In Table 1, many metrics for baseline methods (e.g., PV-RCNN) are absent; the authors should reproduce them for fair comparison (R1).  
  - In Table 3, the reported Mod. AP for Voxel-RCNN is inconsistent with the original Voxel-RCNN and SPS-Conv papers (R2).  
  - The last three rows of Table 1 lack specific per-distance accuracy results (R2).  
- **Unexplained performance anomalies**: In Table 1, PV-RCNN shows a large increase in pedestrian AP after pruning, while SECOND and CenterPoint do not. The reason is unclear – possibly due to a different 2D head architecture or other factors (R1, R3).  
- **Applicability scope**: One reviewer notes the method is restricted to voxel-based point cloud detectors (R3), while another says it adapts to any Conv-based 3D detector (R1). This discrepancy should be clarified; in any case, the paper does not discuss non-voxel methods (e.g., point-based or range-view) (R3).  
- **Novelty concerns**: Using a second-order Taylor approximation for pruning/quantization error is common in model compression; Section 4.2’s contribution appears incremental (R3).  
- **Outdated detector baselines**: Experiments rely on older detectors (PointRCNN, SECOND, CenterPoint). Validation on newer multimodal detectors (e.g., PillarNeXt, Leveraging vision-centric multi-modal expertise) would be more convincing (R4).  
- **Methods section clarity**: The theoretical derivations (Eq. 2–5 and surrounding text) are overly formulaic and repetitive, making it hard to connect the input point cloud *P* and detector *D* to the actual pruning process (R4).  
- **Missing related work on quantization**: The paper omits relevant compression works such as LiDAR-PTQ (ICLR 2024) and BiPointNet (ICLR 2021) (R3).  
- **Calibration set selection**: The choice of calibration set *D_c* for different datasets and models is not explained (R3).  
- **Search time per layer**: It is unclear whether earlier or later layers have different search times; this timing information is missing (R3).  
- **Limitations not discussed**: The paper does not mention limitations, such as the computational overhead of the pruning process itself (R3).  
- **Writing inconsistencies**: “Hard” is labeled as “high” in Tables 4, 5, and Figure 2; “easy”, “mod”, “hard” appear in lowercase in Table 6, inconsistent with other tables (R2).
