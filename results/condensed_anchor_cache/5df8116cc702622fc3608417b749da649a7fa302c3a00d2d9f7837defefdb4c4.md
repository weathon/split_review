## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces an unsupervised method to discover interpretable and influential directions in the latent space of trained image classifiers. It models directions as linear “signal” classifiers and uses an Uncertainty Region Alignment loss to align regions where the model is uncertain with those where the learned signal classifiers are uncertain. The method is tested on a synthetic dataset and on Places365 with ResNet18, comparing against IBD and showing improved concept detection. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem and the theoretical motivation, but raise substantial concerns about clarity, comparison breadth, evaluation scope, and interpretability guarantees.

### Strengths

- **Novel unsupervised framework** – The paper proposes an unsupervised method for discovering latent directions that are both interpretable and influential, advancing over prior work that requires annotated concept datasets or predefined concepts (R1, R4, R5). The problem of finding influential/causal latent directions is an active and high-utility research area (R2).
- **Theoretical grounding** – The method is well-motivated theoretically, extending prior models to a multi-label setting and introducing new loss functions such as the Uncertainty Region Alignment loss (R1, R5). It is a valuable extension to supervised discovery methods like CAV (R5).
- **Empirical validation on synthetic and real data** – Experiments on synthetic data recover ground truth signals, and on Places365/ResNet18 the method achieves high scores on numerical interpretability metrics (R1, R3, R4, R5). The experiments are carried out accurately and with care (R5).
- **Ablation studies** – The ablation studies on the loss components are extensive and interesting (R5).

### Weaknesses

#### Clarity and Presentation
- **Hard to read** – The paper is difficult to follow. The methods section makes many references to the appendix and to other papers without defining key terms; the experiments (especially Sections 4.1 and 4.2) are poorly explained and their connection to Section 2 is unclear (R2, R3, R5). Terms from prior work (e.g., S¹, S² interpretability metrics, RCAV, Pattern CAV, Network Dissection, Broden dataset) are used without introduction or context (R3, R5). Figures 1 and 2 are never referenced in the main text (R5).
- **Missing motivations and overview** – The writing could be improved by including more motivation for each loss component (R4) and by providing a general overview figure that visually communicates the steps and concepts (R5). The intent of the method and how the experiments validate practical applications for model correction/trust are not clarified (R2).

#### Comparison with Prior and Alternative Methods
- **Insufficient comparison with existing methods** – The paper lacks thorough comparison on both interpretability and influence metrics. For interpretability, comparisons with methods that learn disentangled representations or concept-based models (e.g., CRAFT, Concept Bottleneck Models) are needed. For influence, a broader set of techniques that identify influential latent directions should be included (R1). The paper is a close follow-up to Doumanoglou et al. (2023; 2024) with only minor adjustments, yet it does not clearly differentiate its contributions (R2).
- **Missing comparison with PCA / ICA / Dictionary learning** – The underlying additive signal+distractor model is exactly the setting where classical methods like PCA, ICA, or dictionary learning apply. These are not discussed or compared, and the paper does not explain what conceptual gap necessitates the proposed approach (R3). More generally, the relation to sparse autoencoders and dictionary learning in recent interpretability literature is not addressed (R2).

#### Evaluation Scope and Generalizability
- **Limited evaluation** – The method is tested only on a synthetic dataset and a single real-world benchmark (Places365 with ResNet18). Broader evaluation on datasets with varying complexity (e.g., fine-grained categories, different modalities) and on deeper or more complex architectures (not just ResNet18) is needed. The impact of different optimization strategies or data augmentations is also unexplored (R1).
- **Scalability and computational concerns** – How the method scales with larger models and datasets, and whether there are computational bottlenecks, is not addressed (R1 question).
- **Applicability to other data types** – It is unclear whether the method applies to text or time series data, and what modifications would be required (R1 question).

#### Interpretability and Grounding of Learned Directions
- **Unclear whether recovered signals are interpretable** – Unsupervised methods cannot guarantee that directions align with semantic concepts. The paper lacks experiments measuring cosine similarity with ground truth concept vectors on real datasets (e.g., Places365). A significant performance gap between unsupervised and supervised methods is evident in Table 4, raising doubts about the authenticity of the learned vectors and weakening the claim of interpretability (R4). No visualizations of recovered signal vectors are provided to demonstrate what makes them interpretable; the paper should discuss whether each direction admits an unambiguous assignment to a semantic concept (R3).
- **Dependence on NetDissect for labeling** – Although the paper claims to overcome the need for annotated concept datasets, Broden is still used to annotate concept labels (e.g., via NetDissect). It is unclear how NetDissect is applied and why the proposed method is preferable to using NetDissect alone (R2 question, R3 minor point).

#### Methodological Clarity and Robustness
- **Loss functions and optimization** – The paper defines six heuristic loss functions, but it is unclear how they are combined, how regularization parameters are set, and what exact optimization problem is solved. The margin loss “M” is not consistently defined (R3). The comment that raw inclusion of the three loss terms does not converge (line 297) is not elaborated; no quantitative measures of robustness (e.g., standard deviation or confidence intervals over seeds for the tables in Section 4) are provided (R5).
- **Conceptual definitions missing** – Terms like “Significant Direction Count (SDC)” and “Significant Class-Direction Pairs (SCDP)” are introduced without defining what “significantly influences” means (R3). The data generation model in Eq. (1) is not shown to be identifiable; for example, if s₁ and s₂ are signals, then s₁+s₂ might also be considered a signal direction (R3 conceptual question). There is no simple test to determine whether a candidate direction is signal or distractor (R3 question).

#### Reproducibility
- **No published code** – The paper does not provide a link to an anonymous code repository, hindering reproducibility and adoption by other researchers (R5).

**Minority contrasting view:** One reviewer (R2, score 3) is notably more negative, finding the contribution unclear as a close follow-up to Doumanoglou et al. and the writing inadequate for understanding the explanations or their practical utility. In contrast, another reviewer (R5, score 6) finds the theoretical contribution strong and the experiment design acceptable as proof-of-concept, though still requiring improvement in clarity and robustness.
