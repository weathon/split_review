## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces incremental predictive coding (iPC), a biologically-plausible variant of predictive coding networks that eliminates the update locking problem by interleaving or parallelizing neural dynamics and synaptic weight updates, following Neal & Hinton's incremental expectation maximization. iPC simultaneously updates hidden activations and weights (without waiting for either to converge). Experiments on image classification (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet) and language modeling (conditional and masked) show iPC achieves accuracy comparable to backpropagation and lower calibration error, while being faster and more stable than standard predictive coding. The analysis is limited to hierarchical Gaussian generative models with fixed identity covariance.

### Strengths

- Advances the state of the art for predictive coding, which remains a highly promising idea without a commensurately performant implementation. Extends PC results beyond previous small-scale experiments.
- iPC achieves accuracy comparable to backpropagation (BP) on several benchmarks and lower calibration error than BP, which is impressive.
- The efficiency gain (faster convergence, stable training) is a notable contribution.
- Brings PC algorithms closer to biological plausibility by allowing computations to run simultaneously in real time, removing the need for a global control signal to switch between inference and learning.
- Strong, comprehensive set of experiments against comparison models. Experimental descriptions in the appendix and code availability enhance reproducibility.
- The paper does an excellent job introducing predictive coding and meticulously working out the details of the EM objective for the hierarchical Gaussian generative model.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of conceptual novelty (minority view – Reviewer 4; other reviewers less negative on novelty but note lack of insight):** The core algorithmic innovation (incremental EM interleaving E and M steps) is directly taken from Neal and Hinton's well-known incremental expectation maximization. The hierarchical Gaussian generative model is also well-known. There is nothing conceptually new beyond applying this existing idea; the paper sorely lacks novelty of contribution.

- **No clear explanation of *why* iPC works better than standard PC:** The intuition for why the algorithm works is not clearly explained. For example, why does using a smaller number of inference steps (smaller T) converge faster in terms of energy (Figure 2)? How does the "out-of-equilibrium" method avoid the convergence issues of standard PC? Little insight is provided as to the mechanism behind the improved performance.

- **Analysis limited to Gaussian feedforward networks:** The theoretical analysis and learning rules (Eqs. 6, 7) are derived only for hierarchical Gaussian generative models with fixed identity covariance. This limits the range of applicability. Questions: Can these ideas be extended to networks with substantial recurrence? How much biological plausibility is lost if the Gaussian and feedforward assumptions are relaxed to mirror more cortical-like networks?

- **Biological plausibility limitation – weight transport problem:** The proposed method still suffers from the weight transport problem (Eq. 6 requires the transpose of the forward mapping). This limitation should be discussed in Appendix A (A Discussion on Biological Plausibility).

- **Clarity issues in presentation (Reviewer 3):**
  - The derivation referenced in the supplementary (page 3: "to the supplementary") is not actually present in the supplementary material.
  - Table 1 caption does not specify whether accuracies are train or test.
  - The sentence preceding the "Comparison with BP" section (page 6) seems redundant (repeats previous sentence).
  - The phrase "reported the highest accuracy obtained" in Figure 3 caption is ambiguous: train or test set?
  - The statement that iPC performs better on standard CNN than on AlexNet contradicts Table 1 (CNN ~72% vs. AlexNet ~80%).
  - Figure 7 plots lack y-axis labels (accuracy? energy? loss?) and captions for Figures 7 and 8 need more explanation.
  - Artificial empty space in top right of page 19 due to Figure 6 caption.
  - Meaning of notation O(max n^l, n^l) on page 17 is unclear.

- **Double-blind oversight (Reviewer 1):** The authors have archived the paper publicly, revealing their identities, breaking the double-blind review process. It is not clear if this is an oversight.

- **Is alternating without convergence truly novel?** (Reviewer 2): It is hard to believe that simply alternating weight and hidden activation updates every step (without running either to convergence) hasn't been tried before. The paper does not convincingly argue why this simple modification works now.
