## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes TraDiffusion++, a training-free method that extends TraDiffusion with a Hierarchical Guidance Mechanism (HGM) for fine-grained trajectory-based image generation with Stable Diffusion. HGM includes Control Loss, Suppress Loss, and Fix Loss operating at different cross-attention resolutions. Most reviewers find the paper well-written and the ablations convincing, but they raise major concerns about insufficient comparisons with existing baselines, limited technical novelty relative to prior training-free guidance methods, and the practical usability of trajectories compared to masks. Reviewer 4 is substantially more critical (score 3) than the other three (scores 6,5,5), citing the contribution as minor and heavily inspired by [1]. Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 5) also request broader comparisons and theoretical positioning. All reviewers agree that more diverse examples and comparisons are needed.

### Strengths

- **Good presentation.** The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and uses clear qualitative figures to motivate each component (R1, R2, R4). Reviewer 4 notes figures illustrating pipeline details.
- **Reasonable contributions over TraDiffusion.** The three losses (CL, SL, FL) are clearly motivated and appear to improve over the baseline TraDiffusion (R1, R2, R3). R1 states each component "seems reasonable contributions and innovations over the main baseline."
- **Extensive ablation experiments.** Quantitative and qualitative evidence supports each module individually (R1, R3). R1 says "Extensive quantitative and qualitative evidence are used to justify the contributions of each individual module."
- **Potential practical value.** Training-free scribble-based control is more user-friendly than costly labels like depth or canny edges (R1). R2 calls it "simple and effective training-free method."
- **Careful study of attention layers.** R3 praises the "careful study of different attention layers" for layout and shape control.

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient comparison with existing baselines.** The main table (Table 1) compares only to TraDiffusion and plain Stable Diffusion. Several baselines are missing:
    - Training-based methods: ControlNet, ControlNext, InstanceDiffusion (R1, R2). These can easily be adapted to scribble input (R1, R2).
    - Training-free layout/guidance methods: [1] (Chen et al., 2024) for bounding boxes, Universal Guidance [2], Plug-and-Play Diffusion Features [3], and feature injection methods [4] (R1, R2, R3). R1 suggests a bbox-guided baseline as an intuitive baseline, even if not directly comparable.
    - Segmentation-based approaches [5,6] because the "Coordinate Transformation" dilates trajectories to resemble masks (R4). A comparison with segmentation-based attention modulation (e.g., [5,6]) is needed to determine if trajectories offer any advantage over masks (R4).
- **Limited technical novelty.** R4 strongly criticizes that the main contribution (three losses) is "relatively minor and heavily inspired by [1]" (Chen et al., 2024). The key difference is using a trajectory mask instead of bounding boxes; at low-resolution layers the method becomes identical to [1] for small trajectories (R4, Fig. 5). R3 also asks for more elaboration on how control loss differs from the backward guidance in [1]. R2 suggests the authors should "elaborate more about what the differences between energy-based methods and the other gradient-based method" like [1] and advantage over feature injection.
- **Limited diversity of qualitative examples.** Examples focus primarily on animals; inclusion of other objects is needed (R1). More visualization of complex/overlapping trajectories would be valuable (R3). R4 notes that some generated images look unnatural/distorted (e.g., surfer, teddy bear in Figure 8, kid and bus in Figure 15) and questions the cause.
- **Usability of trajectories vs. masks.** R4 argues that "trajectories" are less intuitive and user-friendly than masks, especially for articulated objects. Drawing a natural pose trajectory requires artistic skill, whereas masks give the model freedom. R4 suggests a user-study to compare usability. Evidence: unnatural/inuitive trajectories can lead to distorted objects (e.g., lambda shape for cat in Fig. 6, teddy bear in Fig. 8, kid and bus in Fig. 15).
- **Insights from Section 3.2 are not new.** R4 notes these insights (SD focuses on layout at low resolutions, shape at high resolutions) were discussed in earlier works [2-4]. How do the insights differ? (R4)
- **FID degradation.** R4 asks for the reason behind degraded FID when losses are added.
- **IOT metric unclear.** R4: "IOT is not clearly explained. You mention that you predict a mask with Yolo. Do you compute the IOU between the trajectory and the segmentation mask? If yes, why do you think this is a suitable metric for evaluating abidance to the provided trajectories?"
- **Missing ablation consistency.** R3 asks when conducting qualitative ablation, can the authors use the same text and trajectory prompt to ensure soundness?
- **Theoretical positioning.** R2 recommends discussing how the energy-function formulation differs from gradient-based methods [1] and analogizing diffusion generation to SGD (citing [4]) for a more theoretically sound paper.
- **Experiments on better diffusion models.** R2 suggests it is necessary to conduct experiments on stronger diffusion models; the extra cost is low.
- **Minor technical questions.** R4 asks: In equation (4), is the denominator needed? Why is the guidance function computed 5 times during inference?
