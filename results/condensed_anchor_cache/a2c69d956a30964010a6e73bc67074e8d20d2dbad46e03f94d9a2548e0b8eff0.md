## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends PSA to multi-view scenes (MVPSA) using identifiable representations. It aggregates view-specific slots via Hungarian matching and models viewpoint information separately. Theoretical identifiability guarantees are provided, and experiments are conducted on synthetic datasets (CLEVR, MOVi, plus newly proposed MV-MOVI-C and MV-MOVI-D). Reviewer 1 (score 5) finds the work strong, citing the theoretical guarantees, viewpoint-agnostic design, and scalability tests. Reviewers 2–4 (scores 3,3,3) are more critical, questioning the novelty, empirical scope, and significance; Reviewer 4 explicitly states the empirical validation is “extremely poor” and the work may be a step backward compared to existing 3D/real-world methods.

### Strengths
- Provides identifiability guarantees specifically for multi-view scenarios, extending a limitation of single-view setups (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The model is viewpoint-agnostic, requiring no additional view-conditioning information, improving robustness without extra supervision (reviewer 1).
- Empirically validates scalability on large-scale datasets (e.g., MV-MOVI-C/D) and using complex decoders (transformer-based) (reviewer 1; however, reviewers 2–4 dispute the scale as only 3,000 scenes and note poor segmentation/reconstruction).
- Addresses occlusion and spatial ambiguity in multi-view settings (reviewer 3).
- Theoretical justification is solid and easy to follow (reviewer 4; reviewer 2 finds proofs difficult to understand – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of innovation**: The method is essentially PSA extended to multi-view via Hungarian matching and convex combination of slot representations; multiple reviewers (2,3,4) find it a straightforward extension with insufficient novelty. Reviewer 4 states this “sidesteps the internal difficulty of single-view object-centric learning” and “might be considered a step backward” given prior multi-view works tackle more complex, real-world scenes.
- **Missing comparisons**: The paper does not compare against existing multi-view object-centric methods such as SIMONe (Kabra et al., 2021) and OCLOC (Yuan et al., 2024) (reviewers 2,3). Reviewer 4 additionally notes lack of comparison with unposed 3D object-centric methods (e.g., [1], [3], [4], [5]), few-view calibration methods (DUSt3R [2]), and segmentation models applied to NeRF/Gaussian Splatting scene representations. These omissions prevent assessment of the method’s relative performance.
- **Poor empirical validation**: Experiments are restricted to simple synthetic scenes (CLEVR, MOVi). Reviewer 4 argues multi-view object-centric learning on these datasets is “nearly a solved task” and recommends testing on more complex, in-the-wild datasets (Kitchen-Shiny, OCTScenes, ScanNetv2). The segmentation and reconstruction visualizations (Appendix F) show objects only partially captured or multiple objects merged into a single segment, indicating poor performance in complex multi-view scenes (reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Dataset scale concerns**: The proposed MV-MOVI-C/D datasets have only 3,000 scenes. Reviewers 3 and 4 question calling these “large-scale” and note limited object diversity compared to existing MOVi variants, which restricts generalizability.
- **Theory readability**: Some theorems and proofs are difficult to understand; more detailed explanations or examples would improve readability (reviewer 2).
- **Unclear method details**:
  - Expectation-Maximization (EM) for GMM in Figure 2: unclear whether GMM parameters are learned jointly across views or independently, whether EM is repeated per view or a one-time initialization, and how learned GMMs are used in subsequent stages (reviewer 1).
  - Viewpoint-specific slots representation as GMMs: lack of initialization details, assumption of slot independence (needs justification), batch-wise vs. per-view parameter updates, and computational complexity of aggregating GMMs across views (reviewers 1,2).
  - Hungarian matching: no analysis of how the algorithm ensures correct matching across views without falling into local optima, especially when objects are heavily occluded or hidden (reviewers 2,3). The model may also fail if an object is completely invisible from one viewpoint.
  - Viewpoint representation learning: how viewpoint information is learned and used for object-centric representation learning is unclear; the paper models it separately but provides no visualization or analysis (e.g., viewpoint interpolation/sampling) to validate its contribution (reviewers 2,3). This raises the question of the purpose of modeling viewpoint information separately if it does not aid object representation (reviewers 2,3).
- **Presentation issues**: Figure text is too small (reviewer 3); line 214 reference to “parameters described in 6” should be “Equation 6” (reviewer 1); line 213 formula appears to have a typo (incomplete brackets) (reviewer 2). The related work section omits recent methods like SIMONe and OCLOC (reviewer 3).
- **Additional clarification requested**: The difference between “viewpoints” and “frames” in the dataset (Appendix C.2) is not explained (reviewer 2).
