## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Latent Macro Action Planner (L-MAP), an offline RL algorithm for high-dimensional continuous action spaces in stochastic environments. L-MAP learns a discrete latent space of temporally extended macro-actions via a VQ-VAE to reduce action dimensionality. A causal transformer is learned as a latent transition model, and planning is performed using MCTS with progressive widening. Experiments on stochastic and deterministic MuJoCo tasks, plus Adroit hand manipulation, show L-MAP outperforms model-based baselines in stochastic domains and performs on par with model-free methods in deterministic ones. The reviewers agree on the novelty of using pre-constructed latent search spaces and macro-actions but have mixed views on experimental breadth and clarity of component contributions.

### Strengths

- **Novel temporal abstraction approach**: Pre-constructing the latent search space using a learned prior before MCTS expansion shows clear benefits over searching without this prior (R1).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The paper covers multiple tasks with strong model-based and model-free baselines, including the high-dimensional Adroit hand task that demonstrates scalability to 24-DoF control (R2, R3).
- **Integration of MCTS with learned priors**: The conditional widening MCTS combined with continuous latent macro-actions is a thoughtful design for stochastic continuous control (R3).
- **Latency and planning trade-off**: L-MAP provides a way to trade off decision latency and planning horizon depending on user requirements; macro-actions and the pre-constructed search space allow faster decision-making (R2).
- **Informative ablation study**: Ablations over macro-action length, planning horizon, and other hyperparameters provide insights into design choices (R2). The paper shows benefits of the learned prior over random initialization (R1).
- **Effective in stochastic environments**: L-MAP consistently outperforms existing model-based methods in stochastic continuous control tasks while matching strong model-free baselines (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear contribution of individual components**: The algorithm has many layers of complexity (VQ-VAE, transformer prior, UCT, progressive widening, parallel expansion). No ablation isolates the effect of UCT, progressive widening, or parallel expansion, and hyperparameter values \(M, N, B, \alpha\) for these components are not reported (R1).
- **Limited stochastic environment evaluation**: L-MAP only separates from baselines in the two stochastic environments (Hopper, Walker). Its performance is worse in deterministic tasks, and no experiment studies how performance varies with increasing levels of stochasticity (R1, R2).
- **Missing comparison to macro-action extensions of baselines**: The two transformer-based baselines (TT, TAP) do not use temporal action compression. It is unclear whether they would benefit similarly from macro-actions. A direct comparison to versions of TT or TAP that learn macro actions is needed (R1).
- **Unexplained performance drop in deterministic settings**: L-MAP is less performant in deterministic environments despite being the best in stochastic ones. No theoretical or empirical explanation is provided (R2).
- **Missing definition and clarity in equations**: The symbol "sg" in equation (2) is not defined (R3). The loss function for reconstruction in the VQ-VAE is not specified; choice of loss (e.g., L1 vs. L2) can significantly affect reconstruction fidelity (R3). The instantaneous rewards \(r_i\) used in the return computation (Equation 1) are not explained (R3).
- **Misleading baseline inclusion**: Table 4 includes a column labeled "DT" without explaining whether this refers to Decision Transformer. DT is not mentioned elsewhere in the paper, making the comparison unclear (R1).
- **Missing justification for key claims**: The claim on lines 213–217 lacks a proof, empirical result, or citation (R3). The motivation for using temporal abstractions over robust control or robust RL methods is not discussed; the paper could engage with the existing work on viscosity solutions to Hamilton-Jacobi equations in continuous RL (R3).
- **No evaluation in off-policy settings**: The paper focuses on offline RL, but it is unclear how L-MAP would behave in off-policy settings (R3).
- **Minor presentation issues**: The introduction could benefit from concrete examples of stochastic offline RL environments where planning efficiency gaps are known (R3). There is a missing connecting word between lines 76–77 (R3). The qualification "particularly for online approaches" (L127–128) is unnecessary since the paper deals only with offline RL (R3).
