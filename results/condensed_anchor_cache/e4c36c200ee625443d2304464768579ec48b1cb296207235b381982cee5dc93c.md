## Merged Review

### Summary

This work presents CulturalBench, a benchmark of 1,227 human-written and human-verified multiple-choice questions to assess LLMs’ cultural knowledge across 45 global regions, covering 17 topics. Questions are verified by five independent annotators. Two setups are introduced: CulturalBench-Easy and CulturalBench-Hard (same questions, different phrasing). Evaluations on 30 LLMs show that GPT-4o achieves 61.5% accuracy on the Hard setup vs. human 92.6%, with large sensitivity to question phrasing (GPT-4o: 27.3% difference). Models struggle with questions having multiple correct answers and consistently underperform on South America and the Middle East. The benchmark is curated using an AI-assisted red-teaming pipeline called CulturalTeaming.

### Strengths

- Coverage of 45 global regions, including underrepresented ones (Bangladesh, Zimbabwe, Peru), evaluated with 30 LLMs.
- Human verification by five independent annotators, with an AI-assisted red-teaming pipeline (CulturalTeaming) for question generation and quality assurance.
- Two evaluation setups (Easy/Hard) reveal LLMs’ sensitivity to question phrasing and provide a more challenging evaluation than prior culture benchmarks.
- Human performance (92.6%) far exceeds the best LLM (GPT-4o at 61.5% on Hard), highlighting significant gaps.
- Interesting findings: models perform better on North America, South Asia, West/South Europe than on South America, East Europe, Middle East; provider models (e.g., Qwen-2-72B, Mistral Large) do not excel on their own region’s culture.
- Broad topic coverage (17 topics) discovered through an open-ended annotation process.
- Well-written, complete, and well-packaged paper.

### Weaknesses

**Dataset Size and Statistical Reliability**
- The total of 1,227 questions is low compared to other recent datasets. Per-region counts are very small (e.g., Canada has only 7 questions). This may lead to either underestimation or overestimation of performance; a sample size of at least 30 per region is typically needed for statistical analysis (Central Limit Theorem). (Reviewer 1)
- Limited instances per sub-region (20–100) raise concerns about statistical power to draw meaningful conclusions about LLMs’ cultural understanding in specific areas. Performance differences could be due to random chance. The trend in culture evaluation favors depth on specific regions over a catch-all benchmark. (Reviewer 4)

**Question Format and Evaluation Methodology**
- Multiple-choice format is unstable/unpredictable for LLMs; the authors do not consider generation tasks as an alternative. (Reviewer 2)
- How to define the correct answer for questions with multiple correct answers, considering cultural diversity? (Reviewer 5)
- The binary question format (2 choices) is not clearly explained as “hard.” Typically, a challenging benchmark has many more options (e.g., MMLU-Pro with 10 choices). (Reviewer 4)
- The experiment in Sec. 5.1 (last part) computing semantic similarity of cultural name with answers may only prove cultural relativity. A better control experiment would randomize answer choices and re-evaluate to see if models rely on heuristics. (Reviewer 5)
- The study only uses English questions, not native languages, despite the cultural focus. (Reviewer 1)
- Some questions appear generic, applicable to multiple countries by simply replacing the country name; answers may vary within a culture. (Reviewer 1, example provided: “remove your shoes” in Bangladesh)
- Lack of detail on how answers of varying length from different annotators were handled, and what generation parameters (temperature, etc.) were used for LLM-based question generation. (Reviewer 1)
- Human performance (92.4%) computation is not explained. (Reviewer 1)
- It is not explained how extra tokens in model outputs were handled during evaluation (i.e., whether models always produced the correct answer format). (Reviewer 1)

**Comparison with Existing Work and Novelty**
- The paper criticizes prior work (e.g., web-scraped benchmarks) for using LLMs in the process, but this work also uses LLMs (brainstorming, classification), making the criticism contradictory. (Reviewer 1)
- The claim that low performance necessarily means “challenging” is not justified. (Reviewer 1)
- Culture question-answering is not novel given prior works (CultureLLM, CultureBank, SeaLLM); the paper does not propose more diverse data types (only multiple choice) and does not provide a detailed comparison with those works. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper labels topic-specific benchmarks (food, etiquette, etc.) as “narrow” and of “poor diversity.” This is a negative characterization; such benchmarks have their own value and are considered semantic proxies of culture. Depth on a specific aspect can provide solid empirical insights, unlike a catch-all benchmark with small test instances. (Reviewer 4)
- The main focus is on describing the human annotator setup with AI assistance; a detailed comparison with other approaches (e.g., CultureBank using social media) is missing, including a discussion of advantages and limitations. (Reviewer 3)

**Experimental Depth and Generalizability**
- Experimental results are not counterintuitive enough (GPT-4o leads most regions); more in-depth analyses are expected. (Reviewer 2)
- Section 5.2’s in-depth analysis (question type and versions) is only done for commercial models; a similar analysis on open-weight models (Llama, Qwen, Aya) is missing, limiting generalizability. (Reviewer 4)
- No experiments on whether CulturalBench can help enhance models’ cultural knowledge (e.g., via fine-tuning, prompting, in-context learning). (Reviewer 5)
- Self-knowledge evaluation (e.g., model indicating “I don’t know”) is not compared between LLMs and humans, though the option exists for annotators. (Reviewer 2)

**Data Curation and Coverage**
- The classification of questions into topics was done using GPT-4o-mini instead of manual annotation; this is subjective and lacks a guideline. One could argue, e.g., “how to greet people” is a daily life activity, so topic boundaries are unclear. (Reviewer 1)
- The reasoning for limiting to “most 3–7 questions per country” is not justified; no reference is given that culture can be defined by such a small number. (Reviewer 1)
- The “discovery approach” is highlighted but not sufficiently justified in terms of its benefits. (Reviewer 1)
- The number of topics initially discovered by annotators is not reported; it could have been more than 17. (Reviewer 1)
- No prompts are provided in Appendix C. (Reviewer 1)
- For multi-cultural countries, no approach is described to handle within-country cultural diversity or multiplicity of identities (ethnicity, religion, current residence). Annotator selection criteria are not detailed, especially for multi-cultural regions. (Reviewer 3)
- The benchmarking platform has no fine-grained cultural classification and no consideration for multiplicity of identities, which may have a non-negligible impact on the benchmark. (Reviewer 3)
- There is no discussion on how to grow and develop the benchmark beyond the current 1,227 questions; a static benchmark will quickly become outdated and unrepresentative. (Reviewer 4)
