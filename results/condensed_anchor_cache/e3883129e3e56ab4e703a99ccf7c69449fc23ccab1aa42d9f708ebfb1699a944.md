## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers broadly note that ClassroomKD proposes a dynamic multi‑mentor knowledge distillation framework with sample‑level mentor selection and adaptive weighting. The paper is clearly written and includes experiments on CIFAR‑100, ImageNet, COCO Keypoints, and MPII Human Pose. However, opinions diverge: two reviewers (R1, R3) find novelty limited and improvements marginal, while another (R2) considers the framework highly innovative and well‑validated. A fourth reviewer (R4) is moderately positive but notes specific weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Clear writing and presentation**: The paper is well‑structured, easy to follow, with helpful figures that clearly differentiate the approach from prior work (R1, R2, R3, R4). Good coverage of the literature (R3) and in‑depth analysis of existing methods and their limitations (R4).
- **Innovation in dynamic selection**: While multi‑teacher settings are not new, dynamically ranking and selecting mentors per sample is considered interesting and novel (R1). R2 goes further, calling the dynamic mentor selection and adaptive teaching strategies highly innovative and a novel simulation of classroom dynamics.
- **Sound theoretical foundation**: The theories behind the Knowledge Filtering (KF) and Mentoring modules are reasonable, with a sound loss function (R2). The experimental design and setup are reasonably good (R3).
- **Comprehensive experiments on CIFAR‑100**: The experiments on CIFAR‑100 are thorough, including complete ablation studies (R1). Detailed settings and in‑depth result analysis provide strong evidence (R2). Some experimental results look good (R4).
- **Coverage of diverse tasks**: The framework is evaluated on image classification (CIFAR‑100, ImageNet) and 2D human pose estimation (COCO, MPII), showing applicability across domains (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and similarity to prior work** (R1, R3): The sample‑conditioned teacher weighting idea was previously proposed in MetaTeacher (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2022, [Ref‑A]), which included a more advanced bilevel meta‑learning optimization. R1 also notes similarity to another dynamic multi‑teacher framework [A] not explicitly named. R3 states this work appears to be a subset of Ref‑A’s techniques. Disagreement: R2 finds the approach highly innovative (contrast noted).
- **Questionable design rationale for teacher filtering** (R1, R3): R3 strongly questions the choice to activate only teachers with higher probability on the true class label, arguing that less confident teachers may provide a helpful softening signal when the student is overconfident, as the key knowledge in distillation is the class distribution, not just the maximum probability. R1 similarly questions the filtering rationale. R3 also asks for better justification of how Eq. (8) represents the performance gap, expressing similar concern.
- **Insufficient experiments on larger datasets** (R1, R3, R4): Improvements on CIFAR‑100 are marginal (often <0.5%), and the method may be overfitted to that dataset (R1, R3). On ImageNet and COCO pose estimation, only the baseline NOKD (no distillation) is compared; no state‑of‑the‑art multi‑teacher method is included (R1). R1 also asks to clarify the meaning of “AVER” performance in the tables and explain the underlying method used. R4 observes that improvement over single‑teacher methods is modest, while theoretically multiple mentors should yield larger gains, suggesting further analysis.
- **Missing comparisons and references** (R1, R3, R4): R1 requests comparisons with at least one state‑of‑the‑art multi‑teacher method on ImageNet or COCO. R3 notes missing references for methods like DGKD (line 50) and for methods mentioned in Fig. 1 caption; also “performance gap” (line 52) and “capacity gap” (line 40) are mixed and not properly defined/discussed in the Introduction. R4 suggests adding recent knowledge distillation references (e.g., Logit Standardization, Class Attention Transfer from the past two years) and notes that the indicator label for Active mentors in Figure 2 should be “M’”.
- **Need for broader applicability and limitations analysis** (R2): R2 (more positive) identifies missing discussions on: (a) potential for negative transfer in tasks with significantly different feature spaces; (b) computational overhead of dynamic mentor selection and adaptive teaching (training time, resource consumption); (c) sensitivity of the framework to the number of mentors and performance degradation with too many or too few; (d) adaptation to other domains (NLP, recommendation systems) – e.g., how mentors/students would translate to sequential data or user‑item interactions, modifications needed to loss functions, and challenges with limited labeled data or online learning.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity** (R2): The paper lacks a sensitivity analysis of hyperparameters, particularly the temperature τ (including optimal range for different tasks/datasets) and the weighting factors for the knowledge filtering and mentoring losses. The potential for overfitting to specific hyperparameter settings and guidelines for selecting appropriate values for new tasks should be discussed.
