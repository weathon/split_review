## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses preference heterogeneity in RLHF by extending DPO. It proposes EM-DPO to cluster annotators into latent preference types via an importance-weighted EM-style optimization, then MinMax-DPO to combine the resulting policies into a single equitable policy that minimizes worst-case regret across subgroups. Experiments are conducted on a synthetic bandit task and on the IMDB dataset using GPT-2. The approach is motivated by the need to handle diverse annotator populations, a clear and important problem.

### Strengths
- **Important and well-motivated problem**: Addressing preference heterogeneity in LLM alignment is a significant and timely research question. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Novel approach**: Clustering annotators via latent variable models and then learning robust policies directly through DPO (rather than reward modeling) is innovative and interesting, differentiating the work from existing robust alignment methods such as Maxmin-RLHF (Chakraborty et al., 2024). (R1, R3, R4)
- **Sound min-max regret objective**: The minimax formulation to produce a policy that minimizes worst-case regret across subgroups is principled and well-motivated, providing a clear way to enforce fairness. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Clear motivation and design explanations**: The paper clearly explains why preference heterogeneity matters and why the proposed two-stage algorithm (EM then min-max) is a plausible solution. (R2, R4)
- **Potential applicability to real-world biases**: The method could be suitable for mitigating gender, occupational, and other demographic biases in LLM alignment. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient and unconvincing experiments**: The empirical evaluation is too narrow. Only one synthetic bandit environment (with exactly two subgroups, which makes identifiability trivial) and one LLM experiment (GPT-2 on IMDB) are presented. Real-world datasets with known annotator labels or diverse preferences (e.g., Durmus et al., 2023; Kirk et al., 2024) are missing. (R1, R2, R3, R4) Two reviewers (R1, R4) rate the paper lower (score 3) partly because of this weakness, while two others (R2, R3) rate it higher (score 5) despite it.
- **Lack of statistical rigor**: Many results (e.g., Figures 2 and 4) lack error bars from multiple seeds; results may not be statistically significant. (R4)
- **Poor presentation and clarity from Section 4 onward**: Variables are introduced without clear definition (e.g., \(j\) is defined a page after its first use; the range of latent factors is not stated; \(|H|\) in Algorithm 1 is undefined). Notation is inconsistent. Figures 2 and 3 lack sufficient detail, and Figure 4 is hard to parse (a Pareto curve might be better). Algorithms have no line numbers and appear as screenshots. (R1, R4) Reviewers note that the writing quality overall needs polishing (including LaTeX usage like \citet vs. \citep). (R2)
- **EM-DPO is brittle without regularization**: The EM-style algorithm can quickly degenerate; the paper does not discuss constraints or regularization on posterior counts to ensure identifiability, raising doubts about soundness. (R1)
- **Unrealistic problem setting assumptions**: In practice, per-prompt response pairs are labeled by very few raters, and raters have population bias—the paper does not verify that the raters represent a good population distribution. (R3) The assumption that annotator and prompt are uncorrelated may often be violated (e.g., users label their own prompts); the effect of such correlation on learned clusters is not discussed. (R4)
- **Oversimplifying within-cluster homogeneity**: The model assumes subgroups are internally homogeneous, which may not hold in real-world preferences; no discussion of intra-group variance or strategies to handle it. (R3)
- **Lack of rigorous hyperparameter selection for \(K\)**: The number of latent groups is chosen without a rigorous method; groups may be prompt- or topic-dependent, and the paper does not verify that they are discrete clusters rather than a continuous spectrum. (R3)
- **Approach is specific to DPO**: The latent heterogeneity framework should generalize to reward modeling in RLHF, not just DPO. (R3)
- **Missing related work**: Other robust alignment approaches (e.g., Chakraborty et al., 2024; Ramesh et al., 2024) are not cited or discussed. (R4)
- **No empirical analysis of EM-DPO clusters**: In the LLM experiment, the quality of the learned clusters is not examined—e.g., do they align with known annotator attributes? How does varying \(K\) affect the final MinMax policy? (R4) Similarly, regarding the minimax objective, why does equation (18) use only the positive part of regret? The choice is not justified. (R1)
- **Reproducibility not ensured**: No anonymized code repository is provided. (R4)
- **Unexplored alternative objectives**: The paper does not compare the min-max regret objective to a natural alternative: minimizing the expected risk under the soft cluster assignments from EM. (R1)
