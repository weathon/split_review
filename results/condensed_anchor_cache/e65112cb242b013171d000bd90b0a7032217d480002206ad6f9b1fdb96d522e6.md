## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces X-DRIVE, a dual-branch latent diffusion model for joint generation of LiDAR point clouds (represented as range images) and multi-view camera images with cross-modal consistency. The key innovation is a cross-modality epipolar condition module that enforces alignment by matching queries from one modality along epipolar lines in the other modality. The method supports multi-level controllability via text, 3D bounding boxes, images, and point clouds. Experiments are conducted on the nuScenes dataset. Reviewer 3 is notably more critical (score 5) than others (score 6), citing marginal quantitative improvements and insufficient ablations. Reviewer 2 raises serious concerns about LiDAR generation fidelity and downstream utility. Reviewer 4 questions theoretical depth and experimental completeness.

### Strengths
+ First framework for joint generation of LiDAR and multi-view camera data with cross-modal consistency (R1, R2, R4).
+ Cross-modal epipolar condition module is a neat, intuitive approach for alignment, building on established multi-view epipolar attention literature (R1, R2).
+ Multi-level controllable generation via text, bounding boxes, images, and point clouds (R4).
+ Paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2).
+ Multi-view consistency results are strong (R2).
+ Addresses a practical problem: eliminating the need for motion compensation post-processing to align LiDAR and camera data (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns** (R1, R4): The core idea of using epipolar geometry to enforce consistency across views has been extensively explored in multi-view diffusion models [1,2,3]. The paper needs to clearly articulate how its approach differs beyond applying it to a different modality pairing (LiDAR-camera) and what specific challenges are addressed. Theoretical foundation for cross-modal consistency is weak; the reliance solely on epipolar geometry may not fully resolve 3D spatial ambiguity (R4).

- **Limited experimental validation** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
  - Experiments are conducted on only one dataset (nuScenes), raising generalizability concerns (R1).
  - No evaluation of generated data utility in downstream tasks (e.g., 3D object detection, trajectory prediction) – a critical missing experiment for claimed applications (R2, R4).
  - Marginal improvements in Table 1: For JSD in C→L, X-DRIVE surpasses MagicDrive by only 0.002, while RangeLDM surpasses LiDARGen by 0.106. For FID in L→C, X-DRIVE surpasses MagicDrive by only 0.19, whereas MagicDrive surpasses BEVGen by 8.65 (R3).
  - Lack of quantitative comparison with more baselines; Table 1 is sparse and should be divided by modality (R3).
  - Ablation studies (Table 3) are presented at a very coarse level; detailed component-wise analysis of the cross-modal epipolar module, impact of different condition types, hyperparameter sensitivity, and training strategies are missing (R3, R4).
  - No results showcasing long-tail or out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., rain, cut-ins) in both modalities simultaneously, only visuals in one modality (R2).

- **LiDAR point cloud fidelity issues** (R2):
  - Mapping point clouds to range images introduces information loss due to discretization and occlusion, which is not recovered when reconstructing back to point clouds. This is a critical issue because the sensor data is point clouds, not range images (W1 in R2).
  - Qualitative results show unrealistic LiDAR patterns: ground points lack concentric ring precision, breaks in rings appear as random noise even in clear conditions, object shapes (vehicles, pedestrians) are not clearly discernible and lack characteristic point patterns seen in real data or prior works like RangeLDM [3] (W2 in R2).
  - The generated point clouds do not fulfill the stated objective (L64) of producing dense points on prominent objects (e.g., cars in Fig. 4) (R2).

- **Resolution and architecture concerns** (R4): The current resolution is too low; distant objects have no geometric shapes, while point clouds may have relatively complete shapes at distance. A larger resolution or super-resolution model is needed, but resolution increase alone cannot improve distant geometry. Suggest adopting more advanced architectures (DiT instead of U-Net, T5 instead of CLIP) and investigating video generation capabilities (R4). Reviewer 2 also notes the absence of exploration of denoising directly in pointcloud space (Q2).

- **Computational details missing** (R1, R2): No information on number/type of GPUs used, training/inference time, or computation time split between modalities. Reviewer 2 asks about cost relative to unimodal generation (Q4). Reviewer 3 asks about additional time/memory costs of the cross-modal module.

- **Typos** (R3): Examples include "several works emerges" (L103), "We extend the vanilla diffusion models to modeling" (L186-187), "Our FID metric is outoerformed by MagicDrive" (L423-424). Need thorough proofreading.

- **Minor issues** (R1): Possible mistake in Eq. (5). Reviewer 2 questions the baseline combination of MagicDrive+RangeLDM in Table 1 – was there any alignment step? (Q1). Reviewer 2 also questions whether zero initialization from ControlNet was empirically necessary (Q5) and notes that LiDAR-to-camera results appear much better than camera-to-LiDAR or multimodal results, asking whether a sequential approach (first generate camera, then condition LiDAR) would be feasible (Q6). Reviewer 3 asks about meaning of distribution in Figure 1(b) and how LiDAR generation is achieved in Figure 2. Reviewer 4 asks for code release and documentation.
