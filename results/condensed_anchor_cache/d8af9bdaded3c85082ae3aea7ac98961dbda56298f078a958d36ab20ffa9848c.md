## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SpikerIR, an SNN-based image restoration model that uses asymmetric ANN-SNN knowledge distillation (H-KD) to accelerate convergence and improve performance. The teacher is an ANN (Restormer) and the student is an SNN. The model achieves much fewer parameters (1/300 of the teacher) and lower energy consumption, but performance is limited compared to SOTA ANN models.

### Strengths
- The introduction of SNNs to image restoration is interesting and novel for the task (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The method is simple yet effective (Reviewer 2).
- SpikerIR achieves comparable performance to ANNs with significantly reduced parameters and energy consumption, demonstrating higher energy efficiency (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The proposed H-KD training strategy enables rapid learning from ANNs and speeds up the training process of SNNs, a significant advancement since training SNNs is typically more time-consuming (Reviewer 3).
- The paper provides both quantitative and qualitative results across different image restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring, dehazing), strengthening validity (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is very limited** – knowledge distillation has been widely explored in other CV tasks, and the main ideas have already been validated elsewhere (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Performance is significantly worse than SOTA ANN models** (Restormer, PromptIR, AdaIR) – e.g., on Test100 31.55 dB vs. 31.79 dB (Table 2), and similar gaps in Tables 3 and 4 (Reviewers 1, 4). One reviewer suspects the SNN upper limit may not exceed Restormer (Reviewer 1).
- **Questionable experimental results** – in Table 1, the student SpikerIR surprisingly has higher PSNR than the teacher Restormer on σ=15, which is not convincing (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons** – lacks comparisons with other SOTA lightweight restoration methods (distillation, pruning, sparsity) (Reviewer 1), and with the latest method from reference [37] (Reviewer 3). Also, no comparison for the rain removal task, though deblurring comparisons are provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation studies and fair comparison details** – patch size choice can affect results; no ablation study reported (Reviewer 3). The phrase “were trained for different numbers of epochs to optimize performance” is unclear – was this adaptive or manual? (Reviewer 3). Also, no comparisons with other knowledge distillation methods (Reviewer 2).
- **Heavy reliance on ANN teacher quality** – SNN performance depends on ANN and cannot surpass ANN; the paper lacks discussion or experiments on how the method performs with ANNs of varying quality or strategies to mitigate this dependency (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Lack of real-world evaluation and deployment evidence** – missing NIQE results and visual comparisons; no demonstration that the approach can be deployed on real-world devices to showcase SNN efficiency against traditional IR models (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient motivation for using SNN in IR** – the paper seems like a simple attempt; SNN cannot achieve comparative performance, raising concerns about SNN suitability for IR. More motivation from published works or experimental findings is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **FLOPs are not meaningful for comparing different computation methods** – actual running times with device and settings should be reported instead (Reviewer 1).
- **Uncertainty about baselines** – no officially open-source dehaze model for Restormer; did the authors train it themselves? (Reviewer 1).
- **Potential for improvement with attention mechanisms** – could upgrading SNN with CNN-based channel or spatial attention boost performance? (Reviewer 4).
- **Formatting and grammatical errors** – e.g., in Table 3, the unit for FLOPs should be (G) instead of (G); conference reference formatting is inconsistent (Reviewer 3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers rated the paper 5 and two rated it 3, reflecting a split between those who see value in energy efficiency and SNN application to IR (minority positive) and those who find limited novelty and insufficient performance to justify the approach (minority negative).
