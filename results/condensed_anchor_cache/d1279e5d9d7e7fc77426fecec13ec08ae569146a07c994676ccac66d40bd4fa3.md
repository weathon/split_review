## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes using LLM-generated synthetic data to improve transformer-based stance detection models for online political discussions, and introduces a synthetic-data-based active learning method (SQBC) to select informative unlabelled samples for annotation. Experiments on the German X-stance dataset (translated via NLLB-330M) show that fine-tuning with synthetic data raises F1 from ~0.69 to ~0.72, and the full pipeline reaches ~0.75. The method is sound and the experiments are extensive, but improvements are modest (~2–3%), the theoretical justification for why synthetic data helps is weak, and the active learning component does not clearly outperform a random baseline. Novelty is limited: data augmentation with LLMs is standard, and while applying it to stance detection is relatively new, the technical contribution is incremental.

### Strengths

- **Method adaptable to other text classification tasks** – leveraging LLM-generated synthetic data to improve smaller transformer models that are more reliable for online deployment (Reviewer 1).
- **Clean experimental methodology with proper ablations** – including varying synthetic data size, comparing with meaningful baselines (e.g., LLM zero-shot, fully labeled data), and honest reporting of limitations (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Good visualization of synthetic data alignment** with real data distributions, showing that synthetic samples fill gaps in the feature space (Reviewer 3).
- **Careful translation pipeline** – used NLLB-330M for German-English-German round-trip translation with explicit quality assessment (Table 8 shows reasonable samples), rather than defaulting to a simpler tool (Reviewer 3).
- **SQBC approach is somewhat novel** – using synthetic data as a reference distribution for active learning, even if its practical advantage is limited (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Reasonable baseline comparisons** including a Mistral-7B zero-shot baseline that performs poorly, suggesting synthetic data is better at generating plausible variations than at making decisive judgments (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and impact** – using synthetic data to augment models is not new; applying it to stance detection is one of many NLP tasks, and the generation/training method is standard. The synthetic-data-based active learning idea is novel but empirically not clearly better than random selection (Reviewer 2). One reviewer notes the method is a significant contribution due to adaptability, but others see it as incremental (Reviewer 1 vs. 2, 3).
- **Weak theoretical justification** – no analysis of why synthetic data helps beyond “more data”. No examination of decision boundaries, feature space geometry, or entropy/diversity of generated samples. The paper does not quantify whether synthetic data introduces genuinely new information or merely interpolates between existing data points (Reviewer 3).
- **Modest performance gains (~2–3% F1) for considerable computational overhead** – using Mistral-7B for hours on 1 A100 GPU. No detailed cost-benefit analysis or comparison with alternative, cheaper synthetic data methods. Environmental impact is mentioned only in passing (Reviewer 3).
- **Active learning component feels separate** – the connection between synthetic data and informative sample selection is not well-established; no explanation of why synthetic data serves as a good reference distribution vs. other active learning strategies (uncertainty sampling, query-by-committee) (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Experiments limited to one dataset** – German X-stance only (with translation), no English dataset evaluation. The authors chose translation over direct prompting in German without clear explanation of why Mistral-7B could not generate German content or whether multilingual LLMs were considered. Generalizability to other stance detection datasets or languages is not shown (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Ablation study (Section 5.2) unclear** – shuffling instances to test “content vs. size” is poorly explained: if synthetic data are single text instances with labels, shuffling questions should not affect model outcomes. The term “misaligned” is confusing (Reviewer 1).
- **Scalability concern** – fine-tuning a separate model for each debate question, as done here, does not scale to the many questions in online political discussions, even though the main motivation is robust online deployment (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing dataset overview in main text** – X-stance statistics (e.g., number of comments per question) are only in the appendix; the main text would benefit from a high-level summary (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues** – Section 4.2 (general setup) is hard to follow; the active learning method should first give a high-level intuition before details; the abbreviation SQBC is used before being defined; Figure 4 is information-dense to the point of being hard to parse; some ablations feel perfunctory (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Unanswered questions about synthetic data failures** – no report on whether the LLM ever generated wrong-stance content (e.g., “not in favor” when “in favor” was requested) or refused to generate; such failure cases could affect conclusions (Reviewer 1).
