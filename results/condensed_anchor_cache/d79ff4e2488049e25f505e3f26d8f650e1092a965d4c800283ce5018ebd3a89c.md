## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the interplay between Temperature Scaling (TS) calibration and Conformal Prediction (CP) for deep classifiers. It empirically shows that TS improves class-conditional coverage of adaptive CP methods (e.g., APS, RAPS) but surprisingly and sometimes drastically increases prediction set sizes. The authors provide a theoretical analysis explaining this phenomenon, derive mathematical properties of TS's effect on set sizes, and offer practical guidelines to trade off set size and conditional coverage. Reviewers acknowledge the detailed experiments and theoretical insights, but raise concerns about the strength of the theory, mathematical consistency, practical applicability, and the limited scope.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an interesting and important question: the influence of temperature scaling on conformal prediction outcomes regarding group coverage and set length (R1, R3).
- The empirical experiments are painstakingly detailed, very scientific, and provide clear evidence that TS harms set sizes of APS and RAPS while improving group coverage (R1, R3). Datasets, models, and CP methods are concretely examined.
- The paper demonstrates that TS facilitates a trade-off between set size and group coverage (R3).
- The theoretical analysis offers insights into the empirical findings and explains why temperature scaling affects conformal prediction set sizes; the theory is considered correct and general-purpose by some reviewers (R1, R2, R3). Specific mathematical properties (e.g., Theorem 4.4) are derived and validated empirically (R2).
- The paper is well-written and clearly conveys the core idea (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Mathematical inconsistency in derivations (R2):** The analysis in Eq. (3) relates the difference in accumulated probability and threshold before/after scaling. However, Eq. (5) translates the problem to comparing sums of exponentials using \(z^q\). Because \(\sum_{i=1}^M \exp(z_i^q/T) \neq \hat{q}_T\) when \(M\) is not the true label of \(z^q\), it is unclear how Eq. (3) and Eq. (5) are equivalent. The difference between using the true label's rank versus the predicted label's rank for cumulative probabilities is not negligible and may invalidate the subsequent analysis (R2).
- **Unreasonable assumptions in Theorem 4.4 (R2):** The assumption that \(\Delta z\) is preserved as \(z_1\) increases (line 458) is unnatural; counterexamples exist where \(\Delta z\) increases with \(z_1\). The claim that quantile sample \(z^q\) has larger dominant entry (\(\Delta z\)) than typical \(z\) lacks proper justification, because the quantile is based on scores, not directly on logit differences (R2).
- **Inconsistency between theoretical bounds and empirical results (R2):** The empirical critical temperature is reported as \(T^* = 1.524\), yet this falls between the theoretical ranges \((0,0.813)\) and \((1.25,4.81)\). This challenges the paper’s claim that “the bounds in Theorem 4.4 do not require unreasonable values of \(\Delta z\) and \(T\)”. Using the median of \(\Delta z\) to estimate \(T_{\text{critical}}\) may not be reliable; the paper should clarify how the critical temperature is derived from the bounds and why the empirical value does not align with the predicted ranges (R2).
- **Proposed guideline using \(T_{\text{critical}}\) has limited practical value (R2):** The theorem may fail to provide an accurate estimation of \(T_{\text{critical}}\). Even if accurate, simply applying \(T_{\text{critical}}\) falls short of achieving the group-wise coverage provided by Mondrian conformal prediction. The paper does not present empirical evidence demonstrating how the proposed guideline enhances CP performance in practice. A detailed comparison with Mondrian CP, especially in scenarios prioritizing group-wise coverage, is needed (R2).
- **Theoretical results are somewhat weak and of limited direct applicability (R1):** The theorems serve mainly for post-hoc explanation of the phenomenon rather than providing actionable guidance for practitioners (R1).
- **Limited scope to classification tasks (R3):** The findings do not extend to regression tasks, which do not typically face this issue (R3). Additionally, the analysis is limited to Temperature Scaling; other important calibration methods (e.g., histogram binning) are not considered (R2, minor).
- **Expected nature of the finding (R3):** Some reviewers found it somewhat intuitive that TS might negatively affect set size. Because TS is a post-hoc calibration method rather than an end-to-end training approach, it is not surprising it may not be optimal for CP set size. For example, if a network consistently predicts the correct label with high confidence, the ideal band for a 5% error rate would be set size 1, which an infinite or very small temperature could achieve, whereas TS likely would not (R3).
- **Clarity issues in proofs and writing (R1, R2):** Mathematical proofs lack clarity; e.g., in the proof of Theorem 4.1, the first equation (lines 750-755) is stated without proper justification (R2). A typo/English language check is needed; in particular, "guidelinse" in Section 5 should be "guidelines" (R1, R2).
- **Missing explicit expression for computing \(\hat{T}_{\text{critical}}\) (R2, question):** The paper does not provide an explicit formula for practitioners to compute the critical temperature, making the theoretical result harder to apply.
