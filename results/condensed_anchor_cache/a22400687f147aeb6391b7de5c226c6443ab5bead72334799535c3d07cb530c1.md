## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses confidence estimation for LLM reasoning under limited samples by proposing two methods: Perplexity Consistency (PC) and Reasoning-pruning Perplexity Consistency (RPC). PC integrates the model’s token-level prediction probabilities with self-consistency (MC sampling over reasoning chains) to reduce variance. RPC further prunes low-probability reasoning paths. The authors provide theoretical analysis of variance reduction and evaluate on seven math and code benchmarks, reporting 2–5% accuracy improvements over baselines. One reviewer (R1, rating 6) is positive, two (R2 and R4, ratings 3 and 5) are moderately critical, and one (R3, rating 3) is strongly critical of clarity and empirical significance.

### Strengths
- The problem of efficient confidence estimation for LLM reasoning is important for reliability (R2, R4, R3).
- The paper is clearly written with a natural flow and includes necessary preliminaries (R1); one reviewer (R3) strongly disagrees on clarity (see Weaknesses).
- Experiments are well designed across multiple datasets (MATH, GSM8K, AIME, OlympiadBench, MathOdyssey, HumanEval, MBPP) and models (InternLM-2-MATH-Plus 7B, DeepSeek-Coder-33B, Llama-3.1-70B) (R1, R4).
- The theoretical motivation and analysis of variance reduction are appreciated (R4, R2), though theorems have shortcomings (see Weaknesses).
- The method improves reasoning accuracy on several benchmarks (R2, R1, R4) and generalizes naturally from math to code (R1).
- Being data-free and sampling-efficient is a contribution (R1, implied by R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Confidence vs. performance distinction unclear.** Many results (Figure 2(a,c), Figure 4, Table 2) report accuracy improvements rather than calibration; the paper should clarify whether the method is primarily for confidence estimation or for improving performance by modifying generation (R2). The intervention of pruning tokens is only mentioned at the end of the methodology, but should be stated earlier (R3).
- **Marginal and conditional improvements.** The reported gains are 2–5% (R3). In Figure 4, improvement only emerges when n > 30 (R3). Figure 2(c) shows temperature-based improvement beyond n=1 is <2% (R3). ECE results often show little or limited advantage over self-consistency (R4). No statistical significance tests are provided, so it is unclear whether improvements are consistent or due to chance (R3, R4).
- **Theoretical statements are incomplete or questionable.** Theorem 1 states “With a proper assumption, …” but the assumption is not mentioned in the main text (R2). Theorem 2 may not hold if \(p_{\theta}^{(TL)}\) is taken as log-likelihood and the answer distribution is uniform; missing assumptions (R2). The definition of \(\mathbb{I}_T\) is loose (R3). The claim that \(\mathbb{I}_C\) has a Bernoulli distribution is unjustified, especially because Jaccard similarity (non-binary) can be used for \(\mathbb{I}_C\) (R3). Equation 4 and 5 in the appendix have a typo (\(t < m\) vs. \(t \le m-1\)) (R3).
- **Presentation clarity is poor for some.** The paper needs proofreading to fix grammatical and structural issues (R3). “Reasoning path” is loosely defined as ordered output tokens, which conflicts with mechanistic interpretability usage; a clearer definition should be given early (R3). Claims about improved complexity are not backed by experimental measurements (R3). The phrase “accurate LLM probabilities” is used repeatedly without formal definition (R3).
- **Evaluation gaps.** Not tested on lower temperatures (0.3, 0.7) that are standard in practice (R4). No evaluation on base models without RLHF/instruction tuning, which are often better calibrated (R4). Not applied to other reasoning types like commonsense reasoning (R1). Data contamination is a concern for the MATH dataset and other benchmarks; how this is assessed or discussed is missing (R1). Performance on hallucination cases is not examined (R3). Motivational analysis (Figure 2a) shows AIME curve not converging for shown n; more samples should be displayed to make the point (R3). It is not clear for which difficulty levels the method helps; e.g., Figure 6 shows SC worse than RPC possibly due to one bin, but no explanation for that large difference (R4).
- **Additional parameters in RPC.** The RPC method introduces parameters that need to be learned on a training dataset, which adds overhead; details are not provided (R4).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms (missing experiments, ablations, clarifications):**
    - Is the method more well-calibrated for certain distributions? (R2)
    - How does accuracy compare to greedy temperature scaling? (R2)
    - What is formally defined as “accurate probability”? (R3)
    - What constitutes a sufficient sample size? (R3) – the paper mentions 100 samples, but the figures suggest half that is enough.
    - How does the technique perform on hallucination cases? (R3)
    - Provide qualitative examples showing token probabilities and generated answers to illustrate where the method peaks (R4).
    - Provide intuition for the significantly better peak performance on MathOdyssey compared to AIME and OlympiadBench (R4).
