## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Lagrangian Proximal Gradient Descent (LPGD), a framework for learning convex optimization models. LPGD smooths the possibly non-differentiable loss via a Lagrangian divergence, enabling gradient-based training when gradients are uninformative (e.g., discrete optimization). The update can be computed by rerunning a forward solver on a perturbed input, and the method generalizes several prior approaches. Reviewers expressed mixed assessments: two found the paper well-written and the ideas promising (R2, R3, partially R4), while one strongly criticized its precision and accessibility (R1). There is broad agreement that the experimental evaluation is insufficient and that convergence guarantees are lacking.

### Strengths
- The problem considered and the motivation are interesting (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper makes connections with classical nonsmooth optimization concepts (Lagrangian, Moreau envelopes, mirror descent, Bregman divergences) (R1).
- The subtle issue of assuming unique solutions is pointed out, and some effort is made to address it in the appendix (R1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2, R3). *[Contrast: R1 found the presentation poor and imprecise.]*
- The technical content is solid; the idea of constructing lower and upper Lagrange-Moreau envelopes and averaging them (analogous to central differencing) is promising and validated by experiments (R3).
- The method provides a new update that generalizes classical Bregman divergences and can be a versatile tool for optimization tasks (R4).

### Weaknesses
- The paper is not written with sufficient precision: problem statements, goals, and assumptions are unclear; the notation and flow are difficult to follow (R1).
- Many claims are unsubstantiated or lack proofs (e.g., Proposition 4.2, 4.3; smoothing justification; applicability of Danskin’s theorem) (R1).
- The main result is only an asymptotic gradient approximation (as smoothing parameter → 0); no convergence rate or practical convergence guarantee is provided (R1, R4). R1 finds this result unsatisfactory even if correct.
- The literature survey is incomplete: missing key references (Rockafellar, FISTA, Nesterov acceleration, Figueiredo et al.) (R1); details of the most relevant existing methods for computing (approximate) gradients of ℓ(x^⋆(w)) are not surveyed (R3).
- The assumption of a unique solution is very strong; the discussion in Appendix F/G does not clearly show how to relax it or how the results change (R1).
- No explicit algorithm is given; it is unclear how to implement the updates (e.g., how to solve for z^* and \tilde z^τ, what algorithm to use, how to handle inexact solves) (R1).
- The experimental section is too small and lacks real-world relevance; comparisons with state-of-the-art methods are missing (R2). The toy experiment alone does not demonstrate practical advantages (R2).
- The background section contains unnecessary notation (e.g., vector μ not used later) and could be shortened to make room for stronger experiments (R2).
- The Moreau envelope definition is missing the requirement that f be proper and lower semicontinuous (R2).
- The claim that LPGD generalizes many existing methods is not fully justified beyond the three examples (R3).
- In the experiment (Figure 2), it is unclear why LPGD (even in terms of epochs) is faster than baseline GD; if both methods use GD, the poor performance of GD may be due to inaccurate gradients from the chosen baseline (Agarwal 19b) (R3).
- There is no experiment on a neural-network-type constrained optimization problem to demonstrate the difference from classical methods (R4).
- Some sentences are unclear or potentially inaccurate, e.g., “Note that for a general loss, the optimization (16) and (17) may diverge” – it is incorrect to say a loss “diverges”; the update may diverge (R3). Also, the meaning of “diverges” for the value of an optimization problem is ambiguous (R1).
- The paper claims to handle nonlinear objectives or learnable constraints, but later focuses on linear loss due to divergence issues; the handling of nonlinear loss is not clarified (R1).
- The equation (12) uses the same variable x on both sides inconsistently (R1).
- Several technical details are missing: why the Moreau envelope is smooth and with which Lipschitz constant (R1); the proof of eq. (13) is missing or needs a reference (R1).
