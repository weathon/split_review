## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a DP-SGD variant (Logit-DP) for non-decomposable loss functions (e.g., contrastive loss) that aims to achieve $O(1)$ gradient sensitivity in batch size $n$. It provides theoretical bounds on $L_2$ sensitivity and privacy guarantees, and reports experiments on CIFAR-10 pre-training and CIFAR-100 fine-tuning. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the setting is novel, but raise serious concerns about the theoretical claims, experimental rigor, and clarity of exposition.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated problem** (R1, R5). Differentially private pre-training with non-decomposable losses (contrastive, CLIP-style) is important and timely; most existing DP-SGD work focuses on decomposable losses (R1). The method provides an interesting solution for DP-training models with non-decomposable losses (R5).
- **Novel setting** (R4). The paper develops DP algorithms specifically for coupled objective functions (contrastive loss), and the algorithms are designed and analyzed explicitly for this class.
- **Clear exposition and rigor** (R3). The paper is easy to follow, includes substantial background, and the empirical results are clear. The algorithmic limitation (computation bottleneck) is carefully discussed.
- **Consistency with prior DP-SGD** (R2). The implementation of Logit-DP is the same as DP-SGD, and several lemmas regarding $L_2$-sensitivity conditions are proved.
- **Theoretical analysis** (R2, R3). The paper proves lemmas on $L_2$-sensitivity conditions; the proofs are rigorous.

### Weaknesses
- **Core sensitivity claim is questionable** (R1). Theorem 4.2 gives bound $(G_1+G_2+(n-1)L)B$. For contrastive loss Lemma 4.5 bounds this by $B_{\text{contrastive}}=2(1+(n-2)e^2/(e^2+(n-1)))$, which appears $O(n)$ for large $n$, contradicting the claimed $O(1)$ sensitivity. R1 is uncertain but asks authors to clarify; if incorrect, score would drop.
- **Theoretical depth is shallow** (R4, R5). Derivations are “almost trivial” (R4) – mostly expansions and triangle inequalities (R5). Parameters $L, G_1, G_2$ are technical artifacts rather than meaningful generalization (R5). The DP guarantee in Corollary 4.4 is loose: noise of order $n\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}/\epsilon$, while prior work (Huang et al.) achieves $\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}/\epsilon$ because the loss is not divided by $1/n$ (R5).
- **Lack of discussion on privacy-utility tradeoff** (R5). Noise can always be added; the impact on model performance is crucial but not analyzed.
- **Experimental validation is insufficient and unclear** (R2, R3, R5).
  - Only “vanilla” contrastive loss and spread-out loss are analyzed; no extension or lemmas for modern losses like SimCLR or InfoNCE (R2).
  - BatchNorm excluded without baseline comparison; no discussion of LayerNorm’s privacy compatibility (R2).
  - Figure 1 omits bounds/percentiles for Non-Private and Naive-DP; tables lack standard deviations (R2).
  - Training budget unrealistic: ResNet18 on CIFAR-10 for 2 epochs and generic conv model for 20 epochs (vs. typical hundreds); fine-tuning 10 epochs excessive (R2).
  - CIFAR-100 fine-tuning accuracy below 20% (Table 4) unexplained, suggests experimental issues (R2).
  - Hyperparameter sweep missing $\epsilon$ values (Sections 5.1, 5.2) (R5). Claim that Naive-DP does not reduce $L_X(w)$ is not justified beyond Figure 1; unclear if learning rate was constant across batch sizes (larger batches typically require larger LR) (R5).
  - Figure 2 shows Logit-DP outperforming the non-private model, which is counterintuitive (R5).
  - No experiments on language models or ImageNet (even subset) or Transformers, despite abstract claiming applicability to large language models (R3).
- **Algorithmic and presentation issues** (R3, R4, R5).
  - Algorithm 1 is unclear: $\bar g$ from (5) does not relate to $\bar g$ or $\bar g_{ij}$; hidden for loop between lines 4 and 5 should be explicit to show computational bottleneck (R3).
  - Per-sample clipping is required for the proof but per-batch clipping alone should suffice for privacy (Huang et al.); authors need to clarify theoretical reasoning beyond empirical justification (R5).
  - Minor errors: line 156 mentions Eq. (2.4) but it is not defined; gradient of $K_z(w)$ undefined (R4). Line 3 of Algorithm 1 for loop counting incorrect (R3). Title uses “DP-SGD” though method applies to other optimizers; should be expanded (R3). Section title line 824 should be “Fine-tuning on CIFAR100” (R2). Notation for $\ell_2$-sensitivity is inconsistent (R2). Redundant “non-private” in line 405 (R2).
- **Missing discussion on private fine-tuning** (R2). The paper inadequately addresses privately fine-tuning a model that was not pre-trained with privacy; “privately pre-trained” embedding model is not clearly defined (R2).
- **No public code** (R2). Hinders reproducibility (R2).
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: R1 is optimistic but conditional on resolving the sensitivity question (current score 6); R2 is strongly negative (score 1) due to experimental and theoretical weaknesses; R3 and R5 are moderately positive (scores 5) but with significant concerns; R4 is critical (score 3) calling contributions trivial. The sensitivity issue is the most critical point of contention.
