## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a latent covariate shift (LCS) paradigm for multi-source domain adaptation, proposing a causal generative model with latent content (z_c) and style (z_s) variables where p_u(y|z_c) is invariant across domains. The authors present identifiability results for z_c under regularity conditions (Proposition 4.2) and implement a VAE-based method (iLCC-LCS). Experiments on synthetic data, TerraIncognita, and PACS are reported, showing performance improvements over baselines. The paper is generally well written and situated in the literature.

### Strengths
- The problem of relaxing assumptions in domain adaptation (e.g., covariate shift, conditional shift) is important. Most reviewers find the LCS paradigm (allowing label distributions to vary while p_u(y|z_c) remains invariant) to be novel and interesting.
- The causal framing and identifiability analysis are presented. The method shows promising empirical results on TerraIncognita and synthetic datasets, outperforming several baselines (noted by Reviewers 1, 2, and 4).
- The paper is clearly written, considers multiple baselines and datasets (TerraIncognita, PACS), and is well situated in the literature (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer (score 1, high confidence) acknowledges the considered problem is interesting, though they raise severe concerns below.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient justification of causal graph assumptions** (raised by all four reviewers):
   - There is no clear motivating example mapping the variables (u, z_c, z_s, y, x) to a real-world scenario; the difference between z_c and z_s is unclear (R1).
   - The assumption of an edge z_c → z_s is central but not justified for the datasets used (e.g., TerraIncognita, PACS); without it, the graph collapses to that of prior works [1,2] (R3).
   - The invertibility of g_c and g_{s2} makes the introduction of separate z_c and z_s variables seem redundant; one could merge them with n_c and n_s. The technical reasons for this hierarchical structure are not explained (R1).
   - The absence of a direct edge between X and Y is a strong assumption, particularly for tasks like segmentation; the authors' attempt to justify it (interpreting nodes as distinct regions) lacks technical depth (R4).
   - The assumption that latent noise variables n_i follow a two-parameter exponential family is strong; its impact on identifiability and practical implications when true noise distributions deviate are not discussed. The assumption that such noise can be automatically separated also needs more justification (R4).

2. **Identifiability analysis has gaps and potential flaws**:
   - Proposition 4.2 shows identifiability of z_c but it is not clear whether p_u(y|z_c) (the invariant conditional) is identifiable or recoverable from data (R1).
   - The identifiability proof relies on conditions from non-linear ICA (e.g., exponential family noise); these conditions are not adequately explained or validated within the framework (R2).
   - One reviewer (R3, high confidence) argues the proof is flawed: the d-separation argument in Eq. (19) does not guarantee that n_c only depends on hat{n}_c, as the inclusion of hat{n}_s does not block the dependency between n_c and y given u. Furthermore, the parameterization of the variational posterior does not follow the dependency constraints of Fig. 2(a), because given x (a collider), n_c and n_s become dependent (R3).
   - The identifiability analysis is described as a simple application of [3] (R3).

3. **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**:
   - Several reviewers (R2, R3) find the contributions limited. The causal graph, learning method, and identifiability analysis are very similar to existing works [1,2]; the only novel addition (z_c → z_s edge) is not well motivated (R3). One reviewer (R2) notes the approach appears to be a latent representation disentanglement mechanism from a causal perspective.

4. **Experimental and evaluation concerns**:
   - The method shows limited performance when the label distribution shift is small (D_kl < 0.3, Table 3 in Appendix). Common evaluation datasets (Digits-five, Office-Home, DomainNet) are omitted, making it difficult to compare with existing methods and assess generalizability (R2).
   - The baselines compared (e.g., IRM) do not use unlabeled target data, while the proposed method uses the entropy term L_ENT (Eq. 9). Without an ablation study, it is unclear whether performance gains come from this additional term or from the claimed identifiability. Moreover, if identifiability is theoretically proven, the entropy term may be redundant (R3).
   - t-SNE is used for visualization, but it can distort high-dimensional geometry and is not suitable for quantitative evaluation; numerical measures should be considered (R2).
   - Questions about practical training: how easy is it to train the VAE with the complex loss function? How much target data is needed to achieve a good model? (R4)

5. **Missing discussion and limitations**:
   - The paper lacks a discussion of limitations; there is no mention of the assumptions' restrictiveness or scenarios where the method might fail (R4).
   - The work is not compared to Alabdulmohsin et al. (2023), which also investigates latent shift; it would be useful to discuss how the n and z variables relate to their proxies and when each method is more suitable (R4).

6. **Minor issues**:
   - The indices i and j in Equation (1) are not defined (R1).
   - In Figure 2(b), n_c and n_b appear shaded as observed variables, which seems like a typo (R1).
   - The paper uses superlatives that seem overly optimistic given the lack of a proper discussion (R4).
