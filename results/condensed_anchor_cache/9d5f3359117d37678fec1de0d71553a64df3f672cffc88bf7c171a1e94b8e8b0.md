## Merged Review

### Summary

All five reviewers find the problem of agnostic conditional classification with halfspace selectors interesting and well-motivated, and they consider the positive algorithm and negative hardness results to be novel contributions. Scores range from 5 to 8 (mean ≈ 6.2). Reviewer 4 (score 8) is most positive, praising the clarity and the proof sketch; Reviewer 5 (score 5) is most critical, questioning the practical motivation and coverage metric. The central consensus is that the paper makes significant technical progress, but suffers from strong assumptions (Gaussian marginals, finite concept class) and a suboptimal error rate, leaving gaps with the lower bound and limiting applicability to selective classification.

### Strengths

1. **Interesting and well-motivated problem.** The study of conditional classification under an agnostic setting, with halfspace selectors, is novel and connects to list-decodable learning and learning with abstention (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
2. **Positive result gives the first polynomial-time PAC-learning algorithm for homogeneous halfspace selectors**, achieving error $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\mathrm{opt}})$ under Gaussian marginals (R2, R3, R5). The algorithm runs in time polynomial in $|\mathcal{C}|$, $d$, and $1/\epsilon$ (R3, R4, R5).
3. **The reduction from conditional classification to one-sided agnostic classification is novel and neat**, and the proof techniques are non-trivial (R2, R3, R5).
4. **Both the positive algorithm and the cryptographic hardness lower bound are important contributions** to the area (R2, R3).
5. **The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow.** The main body gives a clear technical overview (R1: “reads smoothly”; R4: “easy to follow”; R5: “clear presentation”). A minority notes some unclear parts, which is preserved in weaknesses (R3, R2).
6. **The algorithm handles a finite class of classifiers, and the paper discusses extensions via robust list learning** (R1, though the extension is not fully explained – see weaknesses).
7. **The paper is first to design an algorithm for agnostic learning homogeneous halfspaces with respect to conditional error** (previous work focused on error over the full domain) (R5).

### Weaknesses

1. **Strong dependence on a finite classifier class $\mathcal{C}$ and scalability concerns.** The algorithm explicitly requires $\mathcal{C}$ to be finite (R2, R5). Runtime $O(d\,|\mathcal{C}|/\epsilon^6)$ becomes exponential when $\mathcal{C}$ is an $\epsilon$-discretization of halfspaces ($|\mathcal{C}| \approx \epsilon^{-d}$) (R4). The paper discusses using robust list learning for larger classes, but does not give a clear, formal explanation of how to apply it (e.g., to a VC class or to dense linear separators) (R5; R3 also questions the list-learning connection). Moreover, Theorem 1 only guarantees a good selector per concept, not a single selector that works across all concepts; no theorem quantifies the error of the selector output in Algorithm 1 step 11 (R2, confidence 2).
2. **Strong reliance on Gaussian marginals.** The analysis hinges on symmetry and tail properties of the Gaussian (e.g., every homogeneous halfspace has probability mass 1/2). It is unclear whether results extend to sub-Gaussian or other distributions (R1: “whole analysis only works for Gaussians”; R4). This severely limits practical applicability (R1).
3. **Suboptimal error guarantee $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\mathrm{opt}})+\epsilon$ and mismatch with hardness.** The rate is not optimal; it is not known whether better rates (e.g., $O(\mathrm{opt})$) are possible (R3, R5). The abstract on OpenReview incorrectly claimed $O(\mathrm{opt})$ (R5). The lower bound for general halfspaces is additive $\mathrm{opt}+\epsilon$, which does not directly address the $\sqrt{\mathrm{opt}}$ rate, leaving a gap in the hardness characterization (R3).
4. **Lack of coverage (accept rate) consideration in selective classification.** The paper only measures conditional error, ignoring the fraction of accepted examples – a standard metric in selective classification. Under the Gaussian, every homogeneous halfspace has a fixed reject rate of 1/2, making the risk-coverage tradeoff trivial and the setting less practically meaningful (R5: “doesn’t make sense to use a selector that has to throw away half of the examples”).
5. **Misleading “sparse” classifiers claim.** Sparsity is described as limiting the cardinality of the classifier set, not the number of non-zero parameters within each classifier, which conflicts with common usage (R1).
6. **Presentation issues.**
   - The paper is dense; more informal theorem/lemma statements would help (R2).
   - Some theorem statements contain grammatical errors (e.g., Proposition 3.2) (R3).
   - The motivation for Theorem 2.1 (list learning) in the preliminaries is unclear; its use later is not obvious (R3).
   - The choice of ReLU as the convex surrogate for conditional classification error is not sufficiently justified, and the intuition for initializing PSGD at both $w^{(0)}$ and $-w^{(0)}$ is unclear (R1, R4).
   - Reviewer confidence varies; R2 (confidence 2) raises the not-guaranteed single-selector point, while most others have confidence 3–4.
