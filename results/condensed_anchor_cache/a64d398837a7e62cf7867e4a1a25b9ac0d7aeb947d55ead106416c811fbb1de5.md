## Merged Review

### Summary
MatExpert is a three-stage LLM-based framework for materials discovery: retrieval (contrastive learning to find a similar material), transition (CoT reasoning to outline property modifications), and generation (produce ALX then CIF). It uses a large curated dataset from NOMAD (over 2 million materials) and achieves state-of-the-art results on validity, stability, diversity, and novelty compared to prior methods.

### Strengths
- Design mirrors the expert-driven process by decomposing generation into retrieval, transition, and generation stages, enabling iterative refinement and step-by-step reasoning (CoT). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Integration of Robocrystallographer enriches crystal data with textual descriptions, improving retrieval and interpretability. (R3)
- Contrastive learning effectively aligns structure and property embeddings; this multimodal alignment is novel. (R3)
- Use of fine-tuning methods (LoRA, distillation) enhances efficiency and scalability. (R4)
- Evaluation includes stability computed with DFT (not only proxy metrics). (R2)
- Large-scale dataset from NOMAD (2 million materials) provides a comprehensive testbed. (R1, R4)
- Achieves strong results on benchmarks: validity, diversity, stability, outperforming SOTA. (R1, R3)
- Writing style and related work are well presented. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- Multi-stage design adds computational complexity and likely increases training time vs. single-step models. (R1)
- Cumulative errors: if the retrieved material is far from the target, later stages cannot correct it; error propagation is not sufficiently discussed or prevented. (R1, R3)
- Technological innovation is considered limited by two reviewers (R1, R3): multi-step generation and CoT have been explored in other works (Miret et al., Zhang et al., Chiang et al.); the paper should elaborate how its approach diverges and why the CoT reasoning is not merely a standard implementation. One reviewer (R4) finds the chain-of-experts framework novel, but the majority view notes incremental novelty. (R1, R3, R4)
- Unclear data usage: for unconditional results on MP-20, it is not stated whether NOMAD data was also used to train MatExpert; for conditional results, whether CrystalLLM and MatExpert were trained on identical data is unclear. (R2)
- Figure 5 results are not well quantified: 11 bars but only 9 labels; similar colors make parsing difficult; unclear if MatExpert is clearly better. (R2)
- Figure 6 (ablation) visualization is unclear; a numerical table of ablation values would better show the contribution of each component. (R3)
- Retrieval stage limitations: using a retrieved material as a template may bias generation toward structures similar to the template (a known limitation of substitution methods); paper does not discuss this or test how often the number of elements changes vs. the template. (R2, R3)
- Design choice of structure retrieval over NLP corpora (e.g., textual descriptions) is not justified; it is unclear why chemical structures provide a more compact representation without losing information. (R3)
- Pathway generation clarity: how are pathways generated by GPT-4 reliably reproduced in real lab settings? The paper should reference [4] and discuss pathway quality/safety. (R3)
- Unconditional generation evaluation appears contradictory: the paper claims “no property constraints” yet randomly selects a material from the database in the first stage, providing a structure input. This makes the evaluation potentially unfair. (R4)
- Missing stability measurements for out-of-distribution generated samples: predicted energy above convex hull and DFT relaxation success rate should be reported; Table 2 lacks stability measures. (R4)
- Stronger baselines from CDVAE research are missing from Table 1; the dataset used to train CDVAE must be specified (if only MP-20, the comparison is unfair). (R4)
- Heavy reliance on NOMAD may cause overfitting; testing on unseen data sources is needed to assess generalization. (R1)
- Inference speed comparison to CrystalLLM is not provided. (R2)
- S.U.N metric from Zeni et al. (2023) should be computed for better comparison. (R2)
- How does the framework handle multiple potential synthesis pathways? What is the stepwise success rate, especially for generating valid ALX representations? (R3)
- Can the method handle user queries without specifying formation energy and band gap (e.g., “material composed of Mn, Ge with high electrical conductivity”)? What happens if the target material does not exist? (R3)
- Equation (1): what similarity function is used? (R4)
- What prompts were given to the model? How much overlap exists between conditioned properties and the NOMAD data? (R4)
- Figure 5: are all structures relaxed? Does the figure include all generated samples or only those that passed validity? (R4)
- Check energy value differences between model inputs and generated samples; do OOD samples pass validity or relaxation? (R4)
