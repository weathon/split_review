## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies "dormant neurons" in Dynamic Sparse Training (DST)—neurons that produce many zeros and contribute little to succeeding layers. The authors propose NeurRev, a pruning criterion based on weight change (delta) to search for and prune dormant neurons, thereby revitalizing them. The method adds no extra computation and is evaluated on ResNet-family CNNs on CIFAR and ImageNet, plus runtime overhead simulations on edge devices. Results show improved accuracy and lower system overhead compared to existing DST methods.

### Strengths

1.  **Novel observation and solution**: The dormant neuron problem in DST is a novel finding that has been generally overlooked. The delta-based pruning criterion is an innovative approach to addressing it (R1, R2, R3).
2.  **Practical motivation**: The paper draws a clear connection between DST's update frequency and system overhead, motivating a low-cost dynamism (R2). Edge-device simulations demonstrate real-world applicability (R1, R3).
3.  **Technical soundness and clarity**: The method is well-motivated, supported by visualizations of neuron outputs, and clearly written (R1, R3). Experiments on multiple models/datasets and inclusion of runtime overhead on real hardware strengthen the contribution (R1).

**Minority contrast**: Reviewer 1 (score 8, confidence 4) rates the paper's soundness and presentation as “excellent” (4/4) and contribution as “good” (3/3), while Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 6, confidence 4 and 3) rate soundness and contribution lower (3/3 and 2/3 respectively). The stronger enthusiasm from R1 is tempered by the other reviewers' concerns about evaluation scope and fairness.

### Weaknesses

1.  **Limited architectural scope**: All experiments are on ReLU-based CNNs (ResNet family). Transformer models with GeLU activation, MobileNet, and other compressed networks are not tested, leaving generalization unclear (R1, R2). The method's reliance on post-ReLU zeroing may fail with activations like leaky-ReLU that do not produce exact zeros (R2).
2.  **Unfair comparison concern (R3)**: Table 2 results are reportedly taken from original papers. The paper uses a rarely-seen learning rate of 1.024 and an optimized cosine scheduler for ImageNet, raising concern that performance gains stem from the training recipe rather than NeurRev. It is not confirmed that Table 1 uses a consistent training recipe across methods.
3.  **Missing comparison with related pruning criteria (R1)**: The paper should explicitly compare and contrast its delta-based criteria with the "magnitude increase" criteria from *Deconstructing Lottery Tickets: Zeros, Signs, and the Supermask* (NeurIPS 2019), highlighting why NeurRev better addresses dormant neurons.
4.  **Insufficient ablation and justification of pruning decisions (R2)**: 
    - How is the proportion of dormant neurons to prune determined? The “search and awake” process only prunes negative weights; what if there are not enough non-zero negative weights to prune?
    - Why are some weight changes zero (given weight decay is used, per Appendix A)? (R3)
5.  **Missing details on computational overhead and system evaluation (R2)**: The evaluation setup and simulation details are not clearly explained. Time/space complexity and trade-offs are not quantified. How are update frequencies chosen for baselines in Figure 5? (R3)
6.  **Robustness unexplored**: The method's robustness to different data distributions and optimization algorithms (e.g., optimizers other than SGD) is not examined (R2). Is NeurRev designed to work best with a particular optimizer?
7.  **Minor**: Page 8, Section 3.2: “Figure D” should be “Figure 5” (R3).
