## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Attentive Regularized Communication (ARCOMM), an attention-based communication method with a message regularizer for Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning. It also investigates lossy compression via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to reduce message size. Experiments are conducted on SMAC environments.

### Strengths
- The paper is relatively easy to follow (R2) and the method section is clear (R4).
- Investigating lossy message compression through DCT to reduce message sizes and analyzing its effectiveness in limited-communication channels is an important direction (R3, R4; R1 also notes communication overhead is an important direction).
- The results show that agents can still learn under lossy communication, and the messages learned support the motivations of the method (R3).
- The proposed method shows some improvement over the baselines used (R4).  
*Note: Two reviewers (3,4) are more positive on these aspects, while two (1,2) found the contribution and improvement limited (see Weaknesses).*

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited.** The attention module is not novel (TarMAC first used attention; many follow-up works) (R1, R2, R4). The message regularizer resembles AEComm (reconstructing observation), which is not cited (R4). The contribution is incremental, adding only a regularizer to TarMAC (R3).
- **Experiments are weak.**
  - Only SMAC is used; parameter sharing may be more important than communication there (R3).
  - Too few SMAC maps; at least 6–8 should be included for a comprehensive comparison (R1).
  - Baselines are not up-to-date: missing I2C, VBC, TMC, MAPPO, IA2C, and other communication-based methods (R1, R2, R4).
  - Performance improvement is not significant; results have high variance, making conclusions weak. The improvement does not seem to apply across methods/scenarios; the claim that ARCOMM is better across all scenarios is disputed (R1, R2, R4). It is also surprising that communication baselines perform worse than no-communication baselines (R2).
  - Figure 3 compares QMIX+ARCOMM with QMIX without communication, which is not meaningful; comparisons against other communication methods are needed (R1, R2).
  - Compression results are weak: DCT is only tested on easier environments (e.g., 3s_vs_5z) where communication is not necessary (R4). Only DCT is explored; no other compression techniques or different levels of compression are studied (R3). It is also unclear whether learned parameters are used in the compression (R1).
- **Missing ablations and analyses.**
  - Need ablation on the hyperparameter k (compression level) (R4).
  - Need more ablation to identify which component (attention vs. regularizer) drives improvement; especially to explain how ARCOMM outperforms TarMAC given the similarity (R2).
  - Dropping parameter sharing in SMAC would help isolate communication effects (R3).
  - Computational complexity and scalability for larger numbers of agents are not analyzed (R3).
  - It would be informative to add ARCOMM to other existing communication methods, not just QMIX and VDN (R4).
- **Related work is incomplete.** Many relevant works using attention modules and communication compression are not discussed or compared: I2C, VBC, TMC, AEComm, and others (R1, R2, R4).
- **Writing imprecision.** The second paragraph of the introduction is repetitive; the meaning of “quality” of messages is vague; “overload for the learning networks” is unclear (R4). The relationship between entropy and “ambiguity” is not elaborated (R4).
- **Questions with critical implications.**
  - For compression: Why is DCT applied only on the last dimension? (R4)
  - Can the authors provide an example of using DCT to compress message vectors? (R3)
