## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ParCon, a collaborative perception architecture for V2X systems that connects modules in parallel rather than sequential order. The goal is to improve 3D object detection robustness to noise (e.g., communication latency, pose errors) and reduce computational cost. Experiments on simulated (OPV2V, V2XSet) and real-world (DAIR-V2X) datasets show gains in detection accuracy and lower FLOPs.

### Strengths
- **Parallel architecture for noise robustness.** ParCon proposes a novel parallel module connection that allows each module to process information independently, mitigating noise propagation and improving resilience under various noise conditions (latency, heading, localization noise) (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Attention-based fusion.** The framework incorporates agent-wise (A-Att) and spatial-wise (S-Att) attention modules to effectively fuse diverse information types (Reviewer 2).
- **Thorough experimental evaluation.** The method is benchmarked on three datasets (OPV2V, V2XSet, DAIR-V2X), including both simulated and real-world data. Experiments cover practical V2X issues (communication latency, pose errors) and include detailed ablation studies on individual components (Reviewer 1,2,3,4).
- **Computational efficiency.** ParCon reduces FLOPs compared to sequential baselines (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 notes the results are encouraging for effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness.
- **Clear presentation (minority view).** Reviewer 3 finds the writing clear and the motivation/method well stated, though other reviewers disagree (see Weaknesses). Reviewer 3 also commends the thoroughness of the ablation study.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient theoretical insight.** The parallel connection is described as an incremental engineering optimization rather than a fundamental algorithmic contribution (Reviewer 1). The design resembles an ensemble of off-the-shelf modules (PointPillars, Sparse ResNet, attention) with no new learning paradigms (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 notes the method is closely related to V2X-ViT, replacing window attention with CNN-based S-Conv and Dilated Neighborhood Attention Transformer, but lacks justification and exploration of these choices. Reviewer 3 questions whether the parallel design truly surpasses sequential or ensemble approaches, and asks for ablation studies comparing parallel vs. sequential vs. ensemble, as well as analysis of what specific features each module extracts and how they complement each other.
- **Writing quality issues.** The term “module” is used vaguely and inconsistently—does it refer to a computational block, a neural network component, or a specific processing unit? (Reviewer 1). Phrases like “manages noise independently” and “compensates limitations” lack specificity, and the description of the Channel Compression Layer (CCL) is overly long yet unclear (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes typos (e.g., equations on lines 171 and 211) and unclear distinction between sub-manifold sparse convolution and sparse convolution. Reviewer 3, however, finds the writing clear (minority opinion).
- **Insufficient analysis and justification.** The paper does not explain why the parallel architecture improves noise robustness (Reviewer 1,4). For example, how do the modules independently manage noise? What are the underlying mechanisms? (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 requests attention map or feature activation visualizations under different noise conditions to show information flow. The motivation for parallel over sequential is not backed by a clear rationale (Reviewer 4). The tested heading angle range is narrow, making experiments unrealistic for real-world deployment (Reviewer 4).
- **Confusion over S-Att effectiveness.** Results for the spatial attention module (S-Att) are inconsistent: Table 8 shows only a minor gain from S-Att, while Table 9 indicates a large improvement (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 also flags that on DAIR-V2X under moderate noise, S-Att’s contribution appears trivial, and performance is inconsistent with other metrics.
- **Missing related works.** Several relevant collaborative perception papers are not cited: Li et al. 2021 (NeurIPS), Li et al. 2022 (RAL), Huang et al. 2024 (ICRA), Yang et al. 2024 (NeurIPS), Su et al. 2023 (ICRA), Su et al. 2024 (RAL) (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear experimental details and questions.** (a) What is the precise definition of “module” in ParCon, and how does it differ from components in sequential architectures? (Reviewer 1). (b) Does “Original” in Table 8 refer to a sequential setup? If so, the performance gain might simply come from increased model size, not the parallel design (Reviewer 4). (c) Reviewer 2 asks for inference runtime metrics (not just FLOPs) for real-world applicability. (d) How does ParCon handle relative pose errors across agents? (e) Why does the “No Fusion” value in Table 1 (0.754/0.602) differ from the “Without A-Att” value in Table 8 (0.774/0.645)? (Reviewer 3). (f) What do the lines in “Depth 3” of Figure 6 represent? (Reviewer 3).
