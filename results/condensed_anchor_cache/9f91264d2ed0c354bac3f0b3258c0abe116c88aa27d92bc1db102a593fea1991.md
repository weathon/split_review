## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a compositional framework for conditional image generation in discrete latent spaces of VQ models, using masked generative transformers. By combining conditional log-probabilities under a product-of-experts assumption, it enables control with multiple conditions without specialized training. Evaluated on Positional CLEVR, Relational CLEVR, and FFHQ, it achieves a 63.4% average error-rate reduction over prior state-of-the-art and an average FID of 24.23, with speedups of 2.3×–12× over continuous methods. It also demonstrates applicability to pretrained text-to-image models. Reviewers note strong empirical results and efficiency, but raise concerns about novelty (similarity to classifier-free guidance and continuous compositional methods), limited evaluation scope, missing quantitative text-to-image results, FID degradation, computational overhead, and insufficient theoretical differentiation.

### Strengths
1. The approach is the first to apply compositional generation to discrete latent spaces, complementing prior work focused on EBMs or continuous diffusion models.
2. The method is straightforward, effective, and intuitive, producing strong empirical results across multiple datasets.
3. Achieves substantial error-rate reduction (average 63.4% over nine settings) and outperforms the next-best approach in FID in 7/9 settings.
4. Integrates with pretrained text-to-image models (aMUSEd) without fine-tuning, showing practical applicability.
5. Offers significant computational efficiency, with 2.3×–12× speedups compared to continuous methods, and leverages the MaskGIT framework for high throughput.
6. Demonstrates interesting properties for out-of-distribution (OOD) synthesis and negative prompting (NOT operation).
7. The paper is well-written and its results generally support the claims.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and theoretical contribution are weak.** The method is essentially classifier-free guidance or a product-of-experts composition applied to discrete masked generative models, similar to "concept conjunction" in continuous compositional diffusion models (Liu et al., 2022). The paper does not clearly articulate why continuous composition techniques cannot be directly applied to discrete spaces, nor does it explain what this work adds beyond aMUSEd and existing compositional methods. The derivation (Equation (3) from (2)) is not fully justified and may face the same issues raised in Du et al. (2023). The claimed theoretical novelty requires stronger justification.
2. **Ambiguous independence assumption.** Lines 191-192 state conditions are "statistically independent of each other," which could imply a graphical model with \(c_i\) pointing to \(x\). The conditional independence assumption given \(x\) should be explicitly stated and justified, especially since conditions may have underlying dependencies.
3. **Missing quantitative evaluation for text-to-image generation.** The qualitative results on T2I (Figures 12, 13) lack metrics such as CLIP score, making it difficult to assess robustness and generalizability. The complete Figure 13 should be in the main paper.
4. **FID trade-off and diversity concerns.** On FFHQ, the method achieves error-rate reduction but degrades FID significantly, especially as the number of conditioning components increases (Table 3). This loss of diversity is not adequately analyzed, and the trade-off between conditional accuracy and image quality/scalability needs thorough examination.
5. **Computational overhead from multiple forward passes.** Each condition requires an additional forward pass in an already slow generation process. The paper does not adequately address the trade-off between increased control and computational cost, nor does it provide a detailed efficiency comparison with alternative approaches.
6. **Limited dataset scope and complexity.** The evaluation uses only CLEVR and FFHQ. CLEVR is visually simplistic; FFHQ uses only three binary attributes. More complex datasets with more attributes and real-world compositional relationships are needed to test generalization.
7. **Missing evaluation of the OR operation.** Only AND and NOT are demonstrated. OR is a fundamental Boolean operation and its absence limits the conclusions about compositional capability.
8. **Entanglement and control quality.** Conditions are entangled with other attributes, as seen in Figure 6 (e.g., "No Glasses" affecting gender or hair). Low image resolution is also a concern.
9. **Unclear effect of weighting parameters.** The weight range (-3 to +3) shown in Figure 6 may require tuning per attribute; interactions between weights for multiple components are not analyzed.
10. **Missing experiments and ablations requested by reviewers:**
    * How does the method perform on autoregressive-based models?
    * Why does FID vary so much with the number of components (Table 3)? What are the exact trade-offs controlled by sampling temperature and number of tokens per timestep?
    * For OOD synthesis, under what conditions does the model fail? Can it generate more than eight objects on Positional CLEVR?
    * What happens when synthesizing both \(c\) and \(-c\) (e.g., "Glasses" and "No Glasses")?
    * Is it necessary to maintain all conditioning signals until the end of generation, or can they be switched mid-process?
11. **Reference format** is noted as potentially incorrect (e.g., missing "International Conference on Machine Learning" appearances in bibliography).
