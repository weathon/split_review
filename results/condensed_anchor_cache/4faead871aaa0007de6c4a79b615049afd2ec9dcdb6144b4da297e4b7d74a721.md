## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies stochastic sparse linear bandits and proposes relaxing the compatibility condition to require it only on the optimal arm, which is shown to be strictly weaker than existing assumptions. An algorithm (FS-WLasso) that combines greedy selection with forced exploration is presented, and polylogarithmic regret is proved under the weaker condition. Numerical experiments confirm performance. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical contribution but raise concerns about verifiability, reliance on unknown parameters, presentation clarity, and novelty.

### Strengths

- **Weakening of assumptions** (all reviewers): The paper meaningfully relaxes the compatibility condition from all arms to only the optimal arm, and removes other diversification requirements. This is a solid contribution that broadens applicability of high-dimensional bandit algorithms.
- **Clear comparison with prior work** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2): Figure 1 and Table 1 clearly sketch the relation to existing literature, and extensive comparisons in multi-parameter setups (Remark 1) highlight the versatility of the setting.
- **Novel proof technique** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2): The inductive “domino” proof scheme (cyclic induction, Lemma 6) is interesting and carefully designed to maintain the compatibility condition under the weaker assumption.
- **Thorough preparation** (Reviewer 2): Various remarks and supplementary material address anticipated questions (e.g., fixed arm set case) and emphasize significance/novelty.
- **Intuitions for algorithm design** (Reviewer 3): The paper provides good intuition for the algorithm’s design.
- **Contribution to the literature** (Reviewer 3): The weaker condition and the algorithm are a good addition to the field.

### Weaknesses

- **Verifiability of Assumption 3** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3): The paper does not clearly explain how the compatibility condition on the optimal arm (Assumption 3) is more verifiable in practice than existing conditions. No examples or guidance are given for assessing whether the optimal arm satisfies it in a real scenario. Moreover, Reviewer 3 notes that the claim “strictly weaker” requires counterexamples showing Assumption 3 holds while existing conditions fail – such examples are not provided.
- **Dependence on unknown parameters** (Reviewer 2): The algorithm requires prior knowledge of the sparsity level \(s_0\), the gap \(\Delta_0\), and other parameters. The result is not sparsity-agnostic, and these parameters are less general than, e.g., noise level \(\sigma\) or context norm bound \(x_{\max}\). The reliance on multiple parameters (including \(\Delta_*\) and \(\phi_*\)) is seen as a drawback compared to methods with stronger environmental assumptions but less required prior information.
- **Tunability of \(M_0\)** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2): The paper suggests treating the forced-sampling iteration number \(M_0\) as a tuning parameter, but this could equally be applied to prior methods with stronger assumptions, undermining the significance of weakening assumptions. Reviewer 2 questions why \(M_0\) depending on multiple variables implies tunability – it might instead be a core limitation. The trade-off is described as “an algorithm easy to run but with strong assumptions” vs. “works on weaker assumptions but requires more environment information”; some may view this as only incremental improvement over Oh et al. (2021).
- **Uniform exploration choice** (Reviewer 2): In Algorithm 1 line 5, uniform exploration is used; the reviewer suggests \(a = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{X}_t} \|x\|_{V_t^{-1}}\) as a more intuitive way to guarantee the minimum eigenvalue. The paper does not justify why uniform is preferred.
- **Proof sketch lacks emphasis on key techniques** (Reviewer 1): The proof sketch in Section 3.3 does not clearly articulate how the analysis circumvents the need for a compatibility condition on all arms, making it difficult to appreciate the methodological novelty and understand the contribution.
- **Paper structure** (Reviewer 3): The main ideas (especially the condition and the induction technique) should be in the main text, not relegated to the appendix. The current structure impairs readability and understanding of novelty.

*Note:* Reviewers 1 and 2 are more positive overall (scores 8 and 6), while Reviewer 3 is more critical (score 5), especially regarding presentation and the lack of counterexamples. The disagreement primarily concerns the significance of the relaxation relative to the added parameter knowledge required and the clarity of the exposition.
