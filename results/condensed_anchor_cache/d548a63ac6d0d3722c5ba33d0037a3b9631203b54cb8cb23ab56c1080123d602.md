## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Epigraph Robust Constrained Policy Gradient Search (EpiRC-PGS) algorithm for Robust Constrained Markov Decision Processes (RCMDPs). It reformulates the problem via an epigraph form to circumvent gradient conflicts that arise in the Lagrangian max-min formulation, then uses binary search with a policy gradient subroutine. The algorithm identifies an ε-optimal policy with Õ(ε⁻⁴) policy evaluations in tabular settings under known uncertainty sets. Empirical evaluations on low-dimensional RCMDP benchmarks show EpiRC-PGS reliably finding feasible, low-cost policies and outperforming Lagrangian-based methods.

### Strengths
- Novel application of the epigraph form to RCMDPs, effectively resolving gradient conflicts inherent in the Lagrangian formulation (R1, R3, R5). This is a significant advancement over prior approaches that can become trapped in suboptimal solutions due to conflicting gradients from objective and constraint functions (R1, R5).
- First algorithm with theoretical guarantees for near-optimal policy identification in RCMDPs, providing convergence to an ε-optimal policy (R3, R4, R5). Proofs are mathematically sound (R4).
- Empirical evaluations across several RCMDP settings demonstrate that EpiRC-PGS reliably converges to low-return, constraint-satisfying policies and significantly outperforms Lagrangian baselines in robust settings (R1, R3, R5). A toy example clearly illustrates the challenges (R5).
- Well-structured paper with good intuition and presentation (R4, R5). The binary search (bisection) idea is interesting (R2).
- Addresses an important problem in the MDP field (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty disagreement**: One reviewer (R2) finds the underlying idea simple and the epigraphical reformulation standard and not technically surprising, contrasting with the positive novelty assessments from other reviewers (R1, R3, R5).
- **Double-loop computational intensity**: The EpiRC-PGS algorithm has a double-loop structure that is computationally intensive, limiting real-time applicability (R1, R3, R5). The binary search (inner loop) and projection step in policy updates, repeated in the outer loop, cause significant overhead, especially as state/action spaces grow (R5). A single-loop alternative could improve efficiency (R3). The paper lacks detailed analysis of how inner-loop cost scales with state space size and whether function approximation (needed for high-dimensional spaces) would be practical (R1).
- **Reliance on strong assumptions**: Algorithm effectiveness requires Assumption 2 (initial distribution covers all states). This is impractical in robust settings because it necessitates exploring potentially unsafe states, contradicting robust control goals (R3, R5). No analysis is provided of how performance degrades with only partial coverage (R3). Additionally, Assumptions 3, 4, and 5 are required for accurate estimation of robust policy evaluation and gradient; these may be hard to satisfy in complex or noisy environments (R5).
- **Limited to known uncertainty sets**: The algorithm works only when the uncertainty set is exactly characterized and known a priori (e.g., its center and shape are known). A key robust MDP application is ensuring policies trained on an unknown model P₀ do not deviate when the true model is close but unknown—the algorithm cannot provide guarantees in that case. This drawback should be highlighted in abstract/introduction (R4).
- **Convergence rate and complexity discussion**: The O(ε⁻⁴) complexity is not fast; the paper contains no discussion of complexity (R2). It is unclear how results depend on the gap between transition kernels in the uncertainty set (R5). The computational complexity of generating a subgradient estimate with O(ε²) error should be elaborated (R4).
- **Scaling and practicality**: Experiments are limited to low-dimensional cases; it is unclear if EpiRC-PGS maintains efficiency in higher-dimensional spaces (e.g., 2D driving with velocity and distance) (R1, R2, R4). Scaling with the size of the state space and of the uncertainty set needs clarification (R4). Empirical evaluation could benefit from more complex or real-world applications to assess practical robustness (R5).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Writing is extremely confusing; e.g., line 205: "Does π^★ ∈ … hold?" is unclear given the exact phrase in line 204 (R2). Many diamond-shape symbols appear to be typos (R2). The underlying idea of Algorithm 1 is poorly presented (R2). Assumption 5 is used in Theorem 3 but appears later in the paper (R4).
- **Missing analyses**: The paper does not analyze alternative subgradient or non-gradient-based approaches for the epigraph-form RCMDP problem (R3). No categorization of related works distinguishes methods that assume fully characterized uncertainty sets from those that do not (R4).
