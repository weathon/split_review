## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper obtains high probability excess risk bounds of order up to \(O(1/n^2)\) (up to logarithmic factors) for empirical risk minimization (ERM), projected gradient descent (PGD), and stochastic gradient descent (SGD) under strong convexity (or PL condition), smoothness, and Lipschitz continuity. The core technique is uniform stability of gradients rather than the loss function itself. The results are compared favorably with existing bounds and are claimed to be the sharpest in the literature (Table 1). Dimensionality-dependent lower bounds on sample size are avoided.

### Strengths
- Well-written and clearly organized; main contributions are clearly articulated (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- Bounds for ERM and SGD do not require lower bounding \(n\) by the dimension \(d\), improving over Xu & Zeevi 2024 (Reviewer 1).  
- Table 1 provides an informative comparison; bounds for ERM, PGD, and SGD are the sharpest in the literature, especially in sample complexity (Reviewer 2).  
- Clear comparison with existing results: Remarks 4 and 7 vs Fan & Lei 2024 and Xu & Zeevi 2024; improved excess risk bounds: Remark 9 removes dimensionality dependency vs Zhang & Zhou, and Remark 11 allows \(O(1/n^2)\) rates vs Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy 2021 (Reviewer 3).  
- This is the first-known fast rate (\(O(1/n^2)\)) in high probability in the algorithmic stability framework (Reviewer 4).  
- One reviewer (score 8) is highly positive, calling the results impressive and the paper clearly written, while other reviewers (scores 5–6) are more critical of novelty and assumptions.

### Weaknesses
- Technical novelty is limited: the proof essentially combines Zhang & Zhou 2019 and Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy 2021, applying stability analysis to gradients instead of the loss and then leveraging existing tools (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 notes the novelty is not compelling, and Reviewer 4 adds that Theorem 1 is a refinement of Theorem 3 in Fan & Lei 2024 (replacing a constant \(M\) with gradient variance).  
- The \(O(1/n^2)\) excess risk bound is only achievable when the noise-at-optimum \(F(w^*) = O(1/n)\), a significant restriction (Reviewer 1).  
- The tail bound in Theorems 4–6 scales as \(O(\log^2(1/\delta))\), which is suboptimal compared to the \(O(\log(1/\delta))\) tail in Klochkov & Zhivotovskiy 2021; the authors do not explain why a tighter tail is not achievable (Reviewer 1).  
- The bounds have a linear dependency on \(1/\mu^2\) (\(\mu\) is the PL parameter), which can dominate the convergence rate when \(\mu\) is small (common for non-strongly-convex or ill-conditioned problems), diminishing the practical utility of the \(O(1/n^2)\) rate. A missing factor of \(1/\mu^2\) is noted in the proof of Theorem 4 (Reviewer 3).  
- The analysis in Section 4 assumes strong convexity with a sample-size-dependent lower bound on \(\mu\) (\(n \ge 16\gamma^2\log(6/\delta)/\mu^2\), i.e., \(\mu \ge 4\gamma\log^{1/2}(6/\delta)/\sqrt{n}\)). This restricts applicability to the original loss without the regularizer; fast rates for the unregularized loss are not guaranteed (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 asks whether Section 4 results can guarantee fast rates for the original loss without the regularizer.  
- For SGD, gradient complexity is high: Reviewer 3 notes that Remark 13 requires \(T = n^4\) to achieve the bounds, raising computational cost concerns. Reviewer 4 states that existing fast rates for SGD use one-pass (\(T=n\)), whereas here \(T = n^2\) (or \(n^4\) per Reviewer 3), representing a compromise. The large number of iterations can be prohibitive for large-scale datasets (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- The analysis relies on strong assumptions (Lipschitz continuity, strong convexity, smoothness) that may not hold in practice (e.g., deep learning). The paper should discuss limitations and explore extensions (Reviewer 2).  
- Reviewer 3 flags a potential inconsistency in Lemma 3: the left side involves \(w_t^i\) and \(w_t\), but the right side only involves \(\epsilon(w_t)\); the authors should check this.  
- Minor writing issues: “under strongly convexity” in the Abstract, \(w^*\) undefined, “inequality which provide” in Section 3 (Reviewer 3).  
- The minority reviewer (score 8) is more positive overall, acknowledging the results are impressive despite limited novelty, while the other reviewers (scores 5–6) emphasize the limitations more strongly. This contrast reflects differing assessments of the paper’s contribution.
