## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a framework for converting a context-free grammar (CFG) derived from the algebraic matrix language MATLANG into a GNN architecture. The authors demonstrate that 3-WL can be captured by a CFG, propose a reduced CFG that preserves the same expressiveness, and develop a GNN model (G²N²) that provably matches 3-WL. Experiments show the model is competitive and efficient across downstream tasks.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- The topic is timely and practically relevant (R3).
- The contribution is strong: it presents a novel framework for designing GNN architectures from CFGs, exploiting formal language equivalence (R1, R2). This direction is promising for future research (R1).
- Experiments are strong: G²N² is both provably expressive and performs competitively compared to existing architectures, and the experiments confirm the suitability of the approach (R2, R3).
- G²N² is the strongest GNN among those with provable expressivity (it dominates PPGN) (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern (R1):** The CFG \(G_{\mathcal{L}_3}\) appears to be a straightforward derivation of MATLANG, making the framework's novelty unconvincing.
- **Insufficient validation – theoretical and empirical (R1):**  
  *R1 finds the validation of the reduced CFG and the corresponding GNN inadequate both theoretically and empirically.*  
  - **Finite-depth expressiveness (R1, R2):** Both reviewers question how G²N² matches 3-WL with a fixed number of layers, given the reduced CFG may require more rule-application steps than the original \(G_{\mathcal{L}_3}\) to generate the same string. R2 similarly asks about expressiveness after \(L\) rule applications (in practice, GNNs are finite depth) and which CFGs are preferable when they have the same expressive power.  
  - **Missing benchmark datasets (R1):** The paper should evaluate on ZINC-12k and ZINC-full, which are widely used benchmarks for molecular property prediction.  
- **Varying performance and utility of provable expressivity (R2, table 6):** G²N² outperforms other architectures on some datasets but not others. R2 asks: given this pattern, what is the practical utility of provable expressivity beyond the model's performance on these datasets?  
- **Lack of principled efficiency explanation (R3):** The paper shows experimentally that MATLANG-based GNNs are more efficient than standard 3-WL-based GNNs, but provides no principled (e.g., theoretical) explanation for why this is the case. R3 finds this a significant gap and is dissatisfied with relying solely on experimental evidence.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R3) is notably more positive about the paper (score 8) than the other two (both score 6), who raise the above concerns about novelty, validation, and explanation. The contrast is preserved in the weaknesses above.
