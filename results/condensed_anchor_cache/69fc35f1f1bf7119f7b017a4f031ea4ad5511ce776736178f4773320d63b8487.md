## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method to estimate the test accuracy of a DNN classifier without any test data. It generates prototype vectors per class, perturbs them toward class boundaries, and uses aggregated pairwise cosine similarities to bound test accuracy. Evaluation is on ResNet-14/18 with CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Reviewers find the problem interesting and the methodology intuitive, but raise major concerns about insufficient evaluation, unclear motivation, and writing quality. Two reviewers rate the paper 3/5; one rates it 5/5, noting the method “seems to work fine” while still calling for broader validation.

### Strengths
- Novel and innovative idea: estimating test accuracy without any test data (R1, R2, R3). R2 calls it “innovative and attractive”; R3 says it is an “interesting problem to study”.
- Smart, intuitive methodology (R3).
- The proposed method appears to work for bounding test accuracy on the tested configurations (R3).
- Empirical evaluation includes two models (ResNet-14 and ResNet-18) and two datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) (R1).
- Overall paper flow is straightforward and easy to follow (R2).  
*(R1 and R2 note that presentation needs improvement, but R2 specifically praises the flow.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evaluation for a general claim.**  
  Experiments are limited to two similar ResNet models and two small datasets (CIFAR-10/100). At least one additional model on a larger dataset (e.g., ImageNet) should be checked (R3). This also makes it hard to assess how generalizable the method is (R2).  
  - R2 further notes that the trained ResNet18 on CIFAR10 achieves only ~85% accuracy, whereas known baselines exceed 90% (e.g., kuangliu/pytorch-cifar), undermining confidence in the reported results.
- **Unclear significance and motivation.**  
  The importance of estimating accuracy without any test data is not well justified. Test accuracy depends on the test distribution; in real-world scenarios data shifts, so the practical value of a no-data estimate is unclear (R1). The introduction does not provide a broader assessment (R1).  
  *Minority view:* R3 finds the problem interesting, but still considers evaluation insufficient.
- **Missing related work.**  
  The references (only 19 papers, 2 being datasets) do not cover important sample-efficient test accuracy estimation methods that use a few test examples (e.g., Kossen et al. 2021, 2022; Chen et al. 2020; Li et al. 2019). Including them would clarify why a zero-test-data approach is important compared to sample-efficient alternatives (R1).
- **Presentation and writing quality are poor.**  
  - Many typos: “ResNet14”, “CIFAR10 and 500” (R2); grammar errors: “The metric for assessing the classifier, we use its weight vectors” (R3).  
  - Equation issues: In eq. (9) two types of G appear; eq. (10) has two different types of H, making reading difficult (R2).  
  - Overuse of “We believe” (five times) and sub-optimal language (R3).  
  - Abstract and introduction contain basic training details that distract from the main contribution (R1).  
  - The paper should consistently refer to “DNN classifiers” instead of general “DNN” (R1).
- **Table 1 shows suspicious homogeneity.**  
  All numbers in Table 1 are identical (R2). The authors should explain this (R2). Also, what happens if less than 25% of data is used? (R3).
- **Lack of comparison to sample-efficient estimation methods.**  
  The paper does not compare against methods that estimate test accuracy with a few labeled examples (R1). Such comparisons are needed to demonstrate the advantage of requiring zero test data (R1).
- **Method applicability and generalization concerns.**  
  Learning rates and training settings follow a fixed pattern, so it is unclear how robust the method is across different hyperparameters or architectures (R2). The authors should discuss generalizability to larger datasets (R3).
