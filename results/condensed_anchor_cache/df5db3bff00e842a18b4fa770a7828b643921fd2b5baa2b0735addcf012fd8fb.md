## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BDQL, an offline RL algorithm that uses a diffusion model for behavior cloning and SARSA for critic learning, then improves the policy via deterministic policy gradient without explicit policy constraints. To stabilize training fluctuations from OOD actions, Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) is introduced. Experiments on D4RL MuJoCo tasks demonstrate the two-stage training phenomenon. Reviewer 3 (score 5) finds the discovery of this property a strength, while Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 3) are more critical, citing unconvincing performance, restrictive assumptions, and unclear contributions.

### Strengths
1. The paper lists important preliminaries so even readers unfamiliar with diffusion models can understand the context.
2. Ablation studies are thorough and illustrate the contribution of each component.
3. The experiment compares with a variety of baselines.
4. The introduction of diffusion models is a good trial for the offline RL community; although some papers have combined diffusion models with offline RL, this paper contributes ideas in this area.
5. The presentation is clear and easy to follow.
6. A good property of one-step RL with estimated diffusion behavior policies is discovered, leading to a simple and clear algorithmic design (particularly highlighted by Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **SARSA assumptions are restrictive.** The SARSA update requires the offline data to come from trajectories and assumes a single stationary behavior policy. This is not guaranteed in general offline datasets, limiting applicability to many real-world scenarios (Reviewer 1).
2. **Critic choice is questionable.** In the ideal asymptotic case with perfect optimization and good coverage, the critic converges to the Q-function of the behavior policy, which may not be strong. The resulting diffusion policy is essentially the argmax of that Q-function, so performance is at best as good as the behavior policy. It is unclear how the algorithm can surpass the behavior policy, which is the goal of offline RL (Reviewer 1).
3. **OOD argument is unconvincing.** The claimed mechanism for handling OOD actions seems like an alternative form of regularization with behavior cloning using a diminishing coefficient. This could explain training instability. The connection between the method and OOD generalization is not clearly established; the method appears to be a behavior-cloning variant with time-varying regularization (Reviewer 1, echoed by Reviewer 2).
4. **Performance is not competitive.** The results of BDQL on D4RL tasks are close to other competitors, and BDQL-SWA is even inferior to several modern offline RL methods. Even the "practical performance" of BDQL-SWA in offline scenarios is not good enough (Reviewers 1, 2, and 3). This contrasts with the positive assessment by Reviewer 3 who still notes trailing performance.
5. **Only validated on simple tasks.** The two-stage training property is only shown on MuJoCo tasks, which are relatively simple. Experiments on more complex datasets (e.g., AntMaze, Adroit, heteroskedastic datasets) are missing (Reviewer 3).
6. **Missing baselines and comparisons.** Important offline RL baselines such as BEAR and the recent SPOT method are not included. The paper should also explicitly compare with Chen et al. [1] who previously used diffusion models for behavior modeling and clarify the differences (Reviewers 2 and 3).
7. **Theoretical analysis is absent.** The claim that BDQL solves the OOD problem and that OOD only causes fluctuations is weak without theoretical support (Reviewer 2). The property is only validated empirically (Reviewer 3).
8. **No concrete algorithm box provided**, making reproducibility harder (Reviewer 1).
9. **The term “theoretical performance” is confusing.** It appears to refer to on-policy validation performance, not a theoretical guarantee. Better names such as “online validation performance” or “best performance” are suggested (Reviewers 1, 2, and 3).
10. **Questions about algorithm details.** 
    - Why is the output of the diffusion policy a deterministic action rather than a distribution? If it is stochastic, Equation (8) should involve an expectation over actions, and it is unclear if it still follows the deterministic policy gradient theorem (Reviewer 3).
    - The SWA idea of averaging model parameters at different training times seems similar to the target actor/critic trick used in many offline RL methods. The novelty is unclear (Reviewer 2).
11. **Computational cost of diffusion models is not discussed.** Diffusion models are time-costly; an ablation study on computation time or efficiency would be helpful (Reviewer 2).
12. **Section 2.2 is unnecessary** since neither method described there is used in the paper (Reviewer 1).
