## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies sparse optimization problems with an ℓ₀ sparsity constraint and an additional convex constraint Γ. It proposes an iterative hard-thresholding (IHT) algorithm with a novel two-step projection (TSP) procedure. The authors derive an extension of the three-point lemma for the TSP operator and provide convergence guarantees under restricted strong convexity/smoothness (RSC/RSS) assumptions. The analysis covers deterministic, stochastic, and zeroth‑order settings.

### Strengths
- Understanding optimization with sparsity constraints is a long‑standing open question; this is one of few works that formulates and provides analysis for this setting (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is written clearly, with a clear state‑of‑the‑art, problem description, notation, and exposition (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed TSP operator is novel and natural; the extension of the three‑point lemma is interesting and serves as the main technical tool for the convergence guarantees (Reviewer 3).
- The convergence results are interesting and likely of interest to the sparse signal recovery community (Reviewer 3).  
  *Contrast*: Reviewer 2 gave only a minimal strength statement (“studies rate of convergence of IHT on sparse regression with extra constraints”) while Reviewer 3 finds the technical contributions novel.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited guarantees due to sparsity condition**: Theorem 1 requires the sparsity constraint \(k\) to satisfy \(k \ge \frac{4(1-\rho)^2 L_s^2}{\rho^2 \nu_s^2} \overline{k}\), which is a weak sparsity condition (k lower‑bounded by the square of the condition number). Hence no guarantee is provided for fixed sparsity k, even if k is an arbitrarily large constant (Reviewer 1). The reviewer questions whether the analysis can be extended to any large constant k; this missing analysis is a weakness.
- **Similarity to prior work**: The work is very similar to existing literature (Jain et al., 2014; Nguyen et al., 2017; Li et al., 2016; Shen & Li, 2017; de Vazelhes et al., 2022). Adding an extra constraint Γ may not make a fundamental difference (Reviewer 2). *Contrast*: Reviewer 3 finds the TSP operator and proof technique novel, but Reviewer 2 disagrees on the significance.
- **Lack of experimental validation**: No comparison with existing methods is provided in the experiments; an empirical validation on synthetic data would help illustrate the results (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 also notes that the assumptions are never verified with a practical example.
- **No proof outline**: There is no outline of the proof, making it difficult to understand the novelty in the proof technique compared to the literature (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation and notation issues**: Notations (e.g., \(H_k\) in Theorem 1) are defined informally inline, making the main results hard to read (Reviewer 1). The interpretation of \(\rho\) in the main result is unclear; Remarks 3 and 4 could be presented as the main theorem with Theorem 1 as a technical lemma (Reviewer 1). Assumption 3 is written in the form of a definition (Reviewer 3). In Remark 1, the fourth bullet should be the second bullet (Reviewer 3). In Algorithm 2, the notation \(\mathcal{S}\) is confusing—the index set \(\mathcal{S}_t\) should be a subset of \([n]\) and the summation index should be \(i_t\) instead of \(i\) (Reviewer 3).
- **Convergence only in objective value**: The analysis focuses on objective value convergence, but it is unclear whether convergence of the iterates to the global minimum (assuming uniqueness) can also be established (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 further states that “the solution only converges to stationary points” (though the paper claims global guarantees under RSC/RSS); this point of limited convergence is a weakness.
- **Motivation for extra constraint**: It is not clear why enforcing an extra constraint Γ is meaningful; the problem formulation may be questionable (Reviewer 2).
