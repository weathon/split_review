## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces DynaPrompt, a dynamic test-time prompt tuning approach for vision-language models that leverages an online prompt buffer to extract beneficial information from previous test samples while mitigating error accumulation. It selects and optimizes only the most relevant prompts for each test sample using two metrics: prediction entropy (for relevance) and probability difference (for diversity). When no relevant prompt exists, new prompts are appended and inactive ones are deleted. Experiments across 14 datasets show consistent improvements over TPT and variants (CoOp+TPT, MaPLe+TPT). The method introduces only one additional hyper-parameter (buffer size) thanks to adaptive thresholding.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and arguments are clearly articulated (all reviewers).
- The motivation is strong, clearly illustrating the potential benefit of moving to the online scenario while showing the non-triviality of extending TPT to this setup (Reviewer 4).
- The technical contribution is simple, well-reasoned, and effectively solves a clearly identified issue – error accumulation during online test-time tuning (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Dynamic prompt selection and updating (based on entropy and probability difference) is distinct from prior work primarily focused on data manipulation (Reviewer 2).
- The dynamic prompt appending and deletion strategy effectively incorporates new data distribution information (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments are thorough and convincing, demonstrating consistent improvements over baselines (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Despite increased time costs with larger prompt buffer sizes, experimental outcomes confirm the method’s effectiveness (Reviewer 1).
- Only one additional hyper-parameter (buffer size) is needed due to adaptive thresholding (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ImageNet evaluation**: There are no results on the ImageNet dataset; Reviewer 4 stated they would raise their score if ImageNet results were added. This is a significant omission (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing backbone ablations**: Evaluation on other CLIP vision backbones (e.g., ViT-B/32, ResNet-50) is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparison**: The paper overlooks a relevant comparison method [1] (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing CoOp+DynaPrompt in Table 2**: Results for CoOp + DynaPrompt are not included; why? (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient detail on data augmentation set X_n**: The paper does not adequately discuss the source and nature of X_n (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear design rationale for prompt selection metrics**: Why is entropy used specifically for relevance and probability difference for diversity? Why does a direct combination (selecting prompts that satisfy either metric) yield effective results? Would a two-step selection satisfying both conditions simultaneously be more advantageous? (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing ablation on initial prompt sensitivity**: The initial prompts might affect final performance; related experiments are needed (Reviewer 1). Also, did the authors consider whether comparable performance could be achieved by running online test-time tuning from scratch using a prompt length equal to the final model’s total length? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing experiment on initializing a set of prompts from scratch**: Would a buffer initialized with multiple identical prompts (with slight randomness) lead to collapse? An experimental result or thoughtful discussion would help (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear extension to visual prompts**: Could the method be extended to incorporate visual prompts, making it multimodal? When integrated with MaPLe, is it applied only to the textual branch? (Reviewer 1)
- **Increased computational time**: The dynamic updating and selection at each test step introduces additional computational overhead (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear prompt deletion mechanism**: Does “deleting a prompt” mean removing the entire prompt or setting its parameters to zero? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing detail on test sample order in sensitivity experiment**: The paper tests sensitivity to sample order but does not explain how the order for each round was set (Reviewer 1).

*Note: Reviewer 4 (score 8, confidence 5) is notably more positive than the others (scores 5 and 6), yet is also the strongest critic of missing ImageNet evaluation. Their high confidence and specific demand for ImageNet results should be given particular weight.*
