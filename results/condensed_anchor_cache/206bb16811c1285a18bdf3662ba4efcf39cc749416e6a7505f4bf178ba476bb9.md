## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper observes that training UNet-based consistency models with low resources and small batch sizes is unstable, and proposes a Transformer-based generator that produces implicit neural representation (INR) parameters, enabling any-resolution, one-step image generation. The method includes a reconstruction pre-training phase before consistency training. Experiments show improved FID and training stability compared to the original UNet baseline under low-resource settings. Reviewers rate the paper 6/5/5/5, with Reviewer 1 being more positive about the direction, while others (2,3,4) note limitations in novelty, comparisons, and efficiency.

### Strengths
- The idea of using consistency training for image function generation (INR) is an interesting direction, naturally allowing multi-resolution sampling and decoupling resolution from model parameters (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The proposed reconstruction pre-training is simple and effective, leading to faster convergence and better denoising (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The method reduces computational overhead (GPU memory) compared to fixed-resolution UNet generators and supports any-resolution sampling (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Improved FID and training stability are demonstrated on common datasets under low-batch-size setups (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear why INR improves low-resource stability**: The paper does not explain the intuition why modeling as INR helps training stability; it is unclear whether the reconstruction pre-training could also be applied to the UNet baseline. (Reviewer 1)
- **High FID values**: Even with the proposed method, FID values are very high (though attributed to training budget). The cause is not thoroughly investigated—architecture choice or training procedure may contribute. Results are less competitive compared to replacing UNet with DiT (Table 2). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Insufficient comparison with any-resolution generation works**: The claim of any-resolution generation should be compared with recent methods such as [1,2]; the current discussion lacks detailed comparison of advantages/disadvantages. (Reviewer 1)
- **Questionable training efficiency claim**:
    - Comparison is only against a low-batch-size CM-UNet (batch 32). To demonstrate efficiency, comparison should also include a CM-UNet trained with its optimal batch size, not just a low-resource setup. (Reviewer 2)
    - Pre-training requires 30 epochs; total training time for CM-UNet vs. CM-Func and a breakdown of pre-training computational cost (parameters, time per epoch) are missing; this challenges the claimed efficiency. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Incremental novelty**: INR is already widely used in other 2D generation frameworks; the paper does not adequately distinguish its approach (training methodology, architectural choices) from existing INR-based methods. It also lacks a clear explanation of how high-frequency noise fitting is handled in one-step generation. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient related work on INR and high-resolution strategies**: The review misses alternative high-resolution generation methods (e.g., patch-based); also missing discussion of limitations of existing INR-based diffusion models (e.g., two-stage pipelines) and how the proposed method overcomes them. (Reviewer 3) Additionally, a relevant reference (Zhuang et al., “Diffusion probabilistic fields,” ICLR 2023) should be cited. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparisons with other one-step diffusion methods**: The paper does not compare with ADD, DMD, or other one-step methods. Trade-offs in sample quality, computational cost, training stability, and advantages for arbitrary-resolution generation are not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Need higher-resolution experiments**: Experiments are only at 64 and 128 resolutions; to justify arbitrary-resolution generation, results at 512x512 or 1024x1024 are needed. Without these, scalability is unvalidated. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited evaluation metrics**: Only FID and a few other metrics are reported; additional no-reference/perceptual metrics (NIQE, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, LPIPS, MANIQA, DISTS) are missing and would provide a more comprehensive assessment. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear evaluation in Table 2**: How multi-resolution FPS was measured for CelebA 64 is not explained; methodology for this metric should be clarified. (Reviewer 2)
- **Effectiveness with larger batch sizes**: It is unknown whether the proposed method continues to outperform when trained with batch sizes larger than 32. (Reviewer 2)
- **Artifacts at extrapolated resolutions**: The supplementary shows quality improvement over bilinear interpolation at high resolution; it is unclear whether extrapolation (resolution higher than training resolution) produces artifacts. (Reviewer 1)
- **Transformer usage straightforward**: The paper uses Transformers in a relatively standard way; a clearer explanation of how the method differs from other Transformer-based diffusion models (e.g., for consistent generation across resolutions) is needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor typo**: The paper uses “Eq. equation” instead of “Eq.” when referencing equations. (Reviewer 3)
- While Reviewer 1 (rating 6) finds the idea interesting and the pre-training effective, Reviewers 2–4 (rating 5) raise concerns about computational cost comparisons, incremental novelty, missing comparisons to other one-step methods, and insufficient evaluation at high resolutions.
