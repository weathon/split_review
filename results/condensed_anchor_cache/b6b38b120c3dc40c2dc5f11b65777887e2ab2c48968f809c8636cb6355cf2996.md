## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RA-DIT, a two-stage fine-tuning method to retrofit any LLM with retrieval capabilities: (1) fine-tune the LM to better use retrieved information, and (2) fine-tune the retriever to return results preferred by the LM. Experiments show that each stage improves performance, and combining both yields additional gains. The best model (RA-DIT 65B) achieves state-of-the-art on knowledge-intensive zero- and few-shot benchmarks, outperforming prior in-context RALM approaches by up to +8.9% (0-shot) and +1.4% (5-shot) on average.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated practical approach: provides a lightweight, accessible fine-tuning procedure between costly retrieval-aware pretraining and suboptimal post-hoc integration (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Thorough experimental setup: detailed documentation of instruction-tuning datasets and stages; solid baseline comparisons and ablation studies (Reviewer 1). Experiments on instruction tuning are solid, and the ablation is convincing (Reviewer 3).
- Strong results on knowledge-intensive tasks compared to previous methods (Reviewer 2). Outperforms other baseline models (Reviewer 4).
- Method well-motivated and clearly described, enhancing reproducibility and practical applicability (Reviewer 1, 4). Paper is well presented (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation clarity**: Figure 1 and its caption could be expanded for a quick overview. The interdependence of the two fine-tuning steps (LM fine-tuning uses the retriever; retriever fine-tuning requires the LM to score outputs) should be emphasized more (Reviewer 1).  
- **Retriever fine-tuning marginality**: Fine-tuning the retriever yields only very marginal improvements (Table 5). Despite this, the paper is written to encourage this step, potentially overstating its importance (Reviewer 2).  
- **Hallucination risk**: Training the LM to ignore wrong retrieved chunks may encourage hallucination when the answer is not in the retrieved context and the required knowledge is not stored in LLM parameters (Reviewer 2).  
- **Baseline instruction tuning**: For the Llama 65B baseline, it is unclear whether instruction tuning was applied using the same blended dataset. In Table 4, improvement from IT 65B vs. RA-IT 65B is marginal with top-1 chunk (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing experimental details (retrieval setup)**:
  - What is the length of retrieved chunks? How many chunks (top-?) are used? Was packing top-5 together into the prompt tried? (Reviewer 2)
  - In Table 2, why is Llama 65B so poor on NQ? Was it evaluated in a closed-book (non-retrieval) setting? (Reviewer 2)
  - Could top-5 results be provided for Table 4? (Reviewer 2)
- **Training/data scaling**: Early stopping occurs after ~500 steps. How does dataset size scale with this? Can the cap of 7000 data points per domain be varied, and does performance change? The small number of steps raises overfitting concerns not adequately analyzed (Reviewer 3).
- **Domain selection**: The choice of domains for instruction tuning lacks rigorous justification. Is there a criterion for selection? Does joint training on multiple domains actually help? The rationale seems ad hoc (Reviewer 3).
- **Retriever sensitivity**: It is unclear how the model performs with different retrievers (e.g., Contriever, DPR). Do the gains generalize or are they specific to the chosen retriever? (Reviewer 3)
- **Novelty concerns**: The architecture primarily relies on existing models with minor modifications, limiting originality (Reviewer 4).
- **Accuracy of reported ATLAS scores**: Discrepancies exist between the reported ATLAS 64-shot fine-tuning results in Table 2 and the scores from the ATLAS paper (Table 10). This undermines credibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing strong baseline comparison**: The paper does not compare with FID+RS (Hofstätter et al., 2022). Based on that paper’s reported scores, FID+RS would outperform the proposed model while being significantly smaller (11B vs 65B), raising efficiency questions (Reviewer 4).
- **Concurrent work not discussed**: InstructRetro (instruction tuning after retrieval-augmented pretraining) should be mentioned and discussed in related work (Reviewer 2).
- **Interdependence of fine-tuning steps**: It appears the final algorithm fine-tunes the LM using the pretrained retriever, then fine-tunes the retriever using the already fine-tuned LM. What would be the performance drop if the two steps were done independently? (Reviewer 1)
