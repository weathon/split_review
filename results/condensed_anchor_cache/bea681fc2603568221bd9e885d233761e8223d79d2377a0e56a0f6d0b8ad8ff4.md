## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RLang-Dyna-Q, an algorithm that grounds natural language advice into a formal language (RLang) covering all MDP elements – policies, plans, reward, transition functions – and then uses the grounded advice to generate synthetic transitions within a Dyna-Q framework. Experiments in Minigrid and VirtualHome domains show that RLang-Dyna-Q outperforms both a random policy and Dyna-Q alone. A user study with 10 undergraduate students (each providing one piece of advice, 5/10 yielding improvements) and a section on automated semantic labeling (Sec 4.3) are included. Ratings are split: two reviewers gave 5 (lower) and two gave 6 (higher). More critical reviewers question the baselines, reliance on human annotations, and generality; more positive reviewers praise the range of experiments and the automation of language-to-MDP translation.

### Strengths
- Writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow. The flow from motivation to solution to experiments is effective. [R1, R2, R3]
- The idea of grounding natural language advice to every element of an MDP (not just reward or policy) and then using that grounding to generate synthetic transitions is sound and addresses a meaningful gap. [R1, R2, R3]
- A broad range of experiments is presented, including comparisons to Dyna-Q, an ablation study (policy-only, transition-only, plan-only advice), a user study (Table 2, 10 undergraduates, each giving one piece of advice, 5/10 leading to improvements), an automated labeling variant (Sec 4.3), and a comparison to SayCan in the appendix. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The paper blends conventional planning literature and formal specification with LLMs, leading to demonstrable improvements over tabular RL solutions. [R2]
- The user study and efforts to reduce reliance on expert annotations (Sec 4.3) are appreciated. [R1, R2, R3]
- The algorithm description is clear, and the automation of language-to-MDP component translation streamlines the process of incorporating human advice. [R4]
- The research topic is contextualized nicely; the contribution is important. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Baselines and generality:** Only Q-learning (tabular) is tested. More modern RL algorithms (e.g., PPO with a small neural network) are needed to demonstrate generality. The current results do not show applicability to function approximation. [R1, R2]
- **Insufficient baselines:** Comparisons are limited to the nearly three-decade-old Dyna-Q and a random policy. Key missing baselines include:
  - LLMs that directly generate actions or transitions (without RLang’s intermediate representation). [R1, R2]
  - LLMs that generate Python code or programmatic solutions (even without RL refinement, to measure out-of-the-box performance). [R2]
  - RLang itself (without Dyna-Q). [R2]
  - SayCan (appendix only, not main paper). [R3]
- **Heavy reliance on human-annotated advice:** Each environment requires human-annotated natural language advice, which is a strong prior compared to all RL baselines. The paper should discuss methods to reduce or bypass this need (e.g., using LLMs to generate advice directly for any given environment). The reliance limits scalability. [R1]
- **Method clarity and independence:** The method is heavily dependent on prior work (RLang). It is not clear how the Q-table relates to RLang vocabulary files or declarations; the algorithm does not stand alone and feels like the “second half” of RLang. [R2]
- **Lack of analysis on advice types and success factors:** No discussion of which types of advice (policy, transition, plan, reward) work best or how they contribute to the combined policy. Is the contribution task-dependent? The paper does not justify why encoding *every* MDP element is necessary. [R2, R3]
- **User study inconsistency:** Nearly identical advice from participants 5 and 10 yields significantly different performance (33% increase vs. 2% increase). The paper does not explain what differentiates good vs. bad advice for RLang-Dyna-Q. [R2]
- **In-context learning limitations:** The language model is used via in-context learning; making the LM trainable and training end-to-end with the RL system might improve capability. [R4]
- **Expressiveness of human language for RLang:** Some natural language advice cannot be converted to RLang (as noted in the experiments). A more natural intermediate representation might be needed. [R4]
- **Expert advice is dense and low-level:** The advice used in main experiments (shown in textboxes on page 8) is verbose and almost program-like. It is not surprising that an LLM can ground such structured advice. [R3]
- **Missing ablation and analysis for specific results:**
  - No ablation study for HardMaze (Figure 4). [R3]
  - Why does RLang-Dyna-Q-combined perform worse than RLang-Dyna-Q-plan in Figure 6? [R3]
  - Why does the DynaQ baseline fail in Figure 6? [R1]
  - Training is cut off at 30-75 episodes – why such a small budget? A comparison showing episodes-to-max-return would be useful. [R2]
  - Section 4.3 (automated semantic labeling) lacks quantitative evaluation (e.g., grounding accuracy, impact of grounded symbols on agent performance). [R1]
- **Stylistic issues:** Font size in Figure 1 and reward curves in Figures 2-6 is too small (Figure 7 looks better). Capitalize “Perception” in the left figure for consistency. Consistent legend colors and orders across methods on page 8 would improve comparability. There is a broken reference on line 164. [R1, R3]
