## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes VeRA (referred to as ELoRA in the reviews), a parameter-efficient finetuning method that reduces trainable parameters by roughly an order of magnitude compared to LoRA while maintaining performance. It uses a single pair of randomly initialized low-rank matrices shared across all layers and learns small layer-wise scaling vectors instead of training the full low-rank matrices. The method is evaluated on GLUE, E2E, image classification, and instruction tuning of 7B/13B LLaMA-2 models. Three reviewers score the paper 8, one scores it 5; the minority reviewer finds the evaluation limited and the writing not well structured.

### Strengths
- Simple and elegant idea that works well in practice, with a 10x reduction in trainable parameters versus LoRA while achieving comparable or better performance (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Comprehensive evaluation across model sizes (RoBERTa-base/large, LLaMA-2 7B/13B) and multiple datasets, with results reported over 5 random seeds (R1, R2, R4).
- Detailed ablation study investigating the influence of each component (R1, R4).
- Paper is well-written, easy to follow, with clear illustrations and results (R1, R2, R4).
- Novel approach that tackles an important practical problem (R1, R2, R4).
- All claims are backed by experimental evidence (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Experiments are restricted to NLP/LLM tasks. The method is not tested on other modalities (e.g., diffusion models for computer vision, convolutional or transformer-based vision models, multimodal models). Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) suggest adding such experiments to assess generalizability; R2 and R4 explicitly request CV or multimodal results.
- **Instruction tuning evaluation concerns**: The instruction tuning assessment (Tables 4, 5) relies on GPT-4 queries over a very small dataset, which raises reproducibility issues (the GPT-4 API may change) and high variance. R1 recommends replacing these with established benchmarks (MMLU, BIG-Bench) and including more models beyond LLaMA-2; R1 also suggests moving those tables to the appendix and adding experiments on larger models.
- **Missing generative task evaluation**: R3 points out that the method is only evaluated on classification and summarization tasks, which are more resilient to parameter reduction, and should be tested on generative tasks such as translation and question answering. This aligns with R1’s broader call for more diverse tasks.
- **Incomplete explanation of counterintuitive results**: R3 notes that Table 3 shows VeRA achieving better instruction finetuning scores than LoRA despite having fewer trainable parameters, and the paper provides no explanation for this behavior.
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No theoretical justification for why shared random matrices with scaling vectors work, under what conditions they perform well, or what the limitations are. R4 and R3 both request such analysis (e.g., expressivity of the low-rank structure, optimization landscape, conditions for superiority over LoRA). R3 explicitly asks for a theoretical proof.
- **Missing analysis of learned structure**: R1 asks whether the learned weights of VeRA approximate those of LoRA, and how the random projections achieve good performance. This overlap with a missing analysis should be addressed.
- **Missing measurement of training time and compute benefits**: R2 asks about training time, GPU memory, and latency advantages of VeRA over LoRA. The paper does not report these practical benefits.
- **Unclear experimental presentation**: R3 criticizes the reporting format: Figure 3 and Figure 4 each show results for a single task (why not report mean GLUE scores?). This presentation makes the results harder to interpret.
- **Missing related work comparison**: R1 notes that similar ideas (random matrices for PEFT) have been proposed for text-to-image generation (LiDB / Lightweight DreamBooth) and should be cited to provide context.
- **Possible anomalies in results**: R4 questions whether the very high RTE score for LoRA on RoBERTa-base (Table 2) is a mistake, and why PEFT algorithms outperform full finetuning on RTE—R4 suggests this could be overfitting. These points warrant clarification.
- **Suggested missing ablations / extensions**: R1 asks whether using different random matrices per layer (at negligible storage cost for seeds) could increase capacity—this ablation is not explored. R2 asks about extending the method to large image models and multimodal models.
- **Minority reviewer (R3, score 5) explicitly states concerns about performance degradation and limited evaluation**: R3 finds the evaluation only on a limited set of GLUE benchmarks, and that “performance can be hurt using this method.” This contrast with the other reviewers’ more positive assessments should be noted.
