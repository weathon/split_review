## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Flat Hilbert Bayesian Inference (FHBI), an algorithm that combines sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) with Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD) in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS). It extends generalization bounds from finite-dimensional parameter spaces to infinite-dimensional functional spaces and evaluates on the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 datasets) against seven baselines. Reviewer opinions are split: two reviewers (R1, R3) are strongly positive (ratings 8), praising the theoretical grounding, clarity, and consistent empirical gains; one reviewer (R4) is moderately positive (6) but questions the definition of the “general posterior” and the practical necessity of the detour; one reviewer (R2) is negative (3), unconvinced by the theoretical derivation and raising concerns about notation, sample complexity, and experimental controls.

### Strengths
* Theoretical contribution: extends generalization bounds from finite-dimensional parameter spaces to infinite-dimensional functional spaces, providing a theoretically grounded Bayesian inference algorithm (R1, R3).
* Algorithmic novelty: incorporates SAM into SVGD, improving stability; FHBI generalizes both SVGD and SAM (R2, R3).
* Clear presentation: paper is well‑written, with an instructive step‑by‑step derivation of approximations and bounds; motivation is easy to capture (R2, R3, R4).
* Strong empirical evaluation: comprehensive experiments on VTAB‑1K (19 datasets across diverse domains) show consistent improvement over seven baselines; the sharpness advantage of FHBI is empirically validated (R1, R2, R3).
* Potential for applications such as LoRA‑style fine‑tuning is noted (R4).

### Weaknesses
While one reviewer (R1, rating 8) stated “I don’t see any particular weaknesses,” other reviewers raised substantial concerns:

**Theoretical issues**
* The definition of the “general posterior” \(p(\theta|\mathcal D)\) appears incorrect: the population posterior should be \(\exp(-n L(\theta))p(\theta)\), but the authors omit the sample size \(n\). This undermines the claim of targeting the true posterior (R2, R4).
* Notation problems: the empirical posterior should be denoted \(p(\theta|S)\) not \(\mathbb{P}_S\); the prior should be \(\mathbb{P}_\theta\). The form of the “exponential average loss” is not clearly derived; the term “general loss” is unfamiliar (R2).
* Theorem 2: the function \(h(1/\rho^2)\) is undefined in the main paper; ignoring the \(\mathcal{O}(\rho^2)\) term forces \(\rho\) to be very small, which in turn makes \(h(1/\rho^2)\) large, weakening the bound (R2).
* Sample complexity is not reflected in the bounds; claiming to approximate the true posterior \(p(\theta|\mathcal D)\) is impossible with only \(n\) limited samples (R2).
* The connection to SAM appears a posteriori; more analytical depth beyond the cases \(\rho=0\) and \(m=1\) is needed to justify SAM as a motivation (R3).
* The practical benefit of targeting the “general posterior” is questionable—the derivations introduce multiple loose bounds and approximations, and the detour through the general posterior is not adequately justified (R4).
* The paper emphasizes “improving generalization” as a design goal, but the generalization performance appears to be an empirical observation rather than a theoretically driven feature. Clarification between intended design and observed outcome is needed (R4).

**Missing experiments and comparisons**
* The method is only tested in a fine‑tuning regime. Training from scratch on larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet‑A, ImageNet‑C) and scientific ML benchmarks would strengthen the empirical significance (R1).
* Missing comparison: FHBI should be compared against SGLD+SAM (Nguyen et al. 2023, Yang et al. 2023) in addition to SVGD (R2).
* No comparison against SAM is provided in the setting of Section 6.2 (R3).
* The effect of data augmentation is not controlled: was it used in all baselines or only for FHBI? (R2).

**Experimental details and hyperparameters**
* Runtime of FHBI grows rapidly with the number of particles compared to SVGD; the computation bottleneck is not identified and no mitigation is suggested (R2).
* The roles and choices of \(\rho\) and \(\epsilon\) are unclear. In experiments \(\rho\) and \(\epsilon\) are chosen quite differently; explanation or heuristics for their selection are needed, especially as one might expect them to be similar (R3).
* No ablation study on the kernel choice within the RKHS is provided; heuristics for selecting an appropriate kernel for the parameter space would be useful for practitioners (R3).

**Presentation issues**
* The loss \(\ell\) should be explicitly tied to the negative log‑likelihood; it currently gives the impression it can be freely chosen (R4).
* The sentence above Eqn (5) referencing “the maximization problem above” is ambiguous; the authors should use \label and \ref for clarity, and note that Eqn (5) is an approximation, not an exact solution (R4).
* The iterative procedure below Lemma 1 should be labeled and its mapping to Algorithm 1 made explicit (R4).
