## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a constrained Bayesian optimization algorithm for the decoupled setting, where the objective function and constraints can be evaluated independently at different inputs. The method uses GP-based confidence bounds to decide adaptively whether to query the objective or a constraint, and it provides a theoretical cumulative regret bound. Experiments on synthetic and real-world problems demonstrate the algorithm’s query efficiency. Multiple reviewers note that the decoupled setting is seldom studied and that the algorithm’s theoretical guarantee is a significant contribution.

### Strengths
1. **Novel and practical setting** – The decoupled constrained BO problem has received little prior attention, and allowing separate evaluations of objective and constraints mirrors realistic scenarios, potentially reducing cost or time (Reviewers 2, 3).  
2. **Theoretical guarantee** – The proposed algorithm extends GP‑UCB convergence results to constrained BO, offering a provable performance guarantee (Reviewers 1, 3).  
3. **Clear, interpretable design** – The key ideas rely on confidence intervals, and the connection to active learning aids understanding (Reviewers 1, 4).  
4. **Well‑organized presentation** – The paper is clearly written, and the illustrative figures (e.g., Figure 1) are helpful (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
5. **Empirical effectiveness** – Experiments on both synthetic and real‑world benchmarks show the algorithm’s query efficiency (Reviewers 2, 4).  
6. **Easy to follow** – The formulation and algorithm are straightforward and well explained (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Motivation for decoupled setting is insufficient** – Reviewers 2 and 4 find it unclear why one would evaluate objective and constraints at different inputs. No real‑world application with that property is described, and the chosen real‑world benchmark does not appear to require decoupling. Reviewers ask whether independent standard BO runs or multi‑objective BO could be used instead. *Contrast: Reviewer 3 views the decoupled setting as a strength (practical, flexible), highlighting disagreement on the strength of the motivating argument.*  
2. **Unconventional and problematic regret definition** – The regret in eq. (3) sums objective function values and constraint violations. Reviewers 1 and 2 note that these may have very different ranges, making the combined regret uninformative about convergence. Moreover, the optimal point *x\** is defined w.r.t. the objective, while the constraint violation term *r_c* is independent of *x\**, so it is unclear why it should be part of the regret. Reviewer 1 acknowledges that this definition avoids infinite instantaneous regret when querying infeasible points, but argues it lacks sufficient discussion.  
3. **Missing links to active learning literature** – The paper highlights a connection to active learning but does not discuss how its concepts (e.g., “uncharted area”, “ν_t‑relaxed feasible confidence region”) relate to existing active learning methods. Reviewer 1 notes that these ideas resonate with prior work, but no comparison or citation is provided.  
4. **Tractability of optimization sub‑problems is unclear** – Reviewer 2 asks how the optimization problems in Algorithm 1, Lines 3 and 4, are solved. The definition of O_t (involving the uncharted area and the feasible confidence region) seems to make solving Line 3 intractable, but no solver or approximation is specified.  
5. **Experimental plotting and analysis gaps** –  
   * Plotting of UCB‑C’s regret in Figure 2g is unclear: when evaluations occur at both the objective and constraints in each iteration, does the x‑axis advance by one per query or by (#constraints + 1) per iteration? (Reviewer 3)  
   * Only the summed regret is shown; it does not reveal whether the final solution is feasible. Reviewers request separate regret plots for objective and constraints (Reviewer 4).  
   * In Figure 2(h), the standard error of UCB‑D is notably larger than that of other baselines; the authors should explain why (Reviewer 4).  
6. **Why not evaluate both functions when feasible?** – Reviewer 3 asks, when a query point is feasible, what advantage does the strictly decoupled algorithm (UCB‑D) have over evaluating the objective and constraints together?  
7. **Technical derivation questions** – Reviewer 2 asks: (a) How does the last equality in eq. (8) hold after adding the vertical exploration bonus? (b) How does Lemma 3.1 guarantee that O_t is non‑empty with probability > 1 − δ?  

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R3, rating 8, confidence 3) listed no weaknesses and praised the decoupled setting, while the other three reviewers (R1, R2, R4; ratings 8, 5, 6; confidence 4) raised multiple concerns, especially about motivation, regret definition, and experimental details.
