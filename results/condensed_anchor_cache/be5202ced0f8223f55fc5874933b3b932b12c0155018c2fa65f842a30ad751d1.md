## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses class-incremental learning under strict constraints (constant model size, no pre-training, no memory buffer) by extending generative experience replay with a time-aware regularization method. The method dynamically adjusts the weights of the reconstruction and KL-divergence losses in the generative objective, using the inferred task order (from predicted class IDs) to decay importance for earlier tasks. Built on brain-inspired replay (BI-R) [Van de Ven et al. 2020], the proposed approach is evaluated on pairwise MNIST, permuted MNIST, and CIFAR-100 (10 tasks). Reviewers generally find the problem important and the method clearly presented, but raise concerns about novelty, limited experiments, diminishing plasticity, missing ablations, and insufficient comparisons with recent work. One reviewer (R4, score 6) is notably more positive, praising the strict setting and clarity, while others (scores 3) see limited contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a practical and challenging problem: class-incremental continual learning under a strict setting (constant model size, no pre-training dataset, no memory buffer). [R1, R4]
- The proposed time-aware regularization provides a reasonable approach to adjusting the weights of generative loss terms. [R1]
- The methodology is straightforward and clearly presented. [R1, R3, R4]
- Exploring brain-inspired approaches is appreciated as an alternative to common methods. [R2]
- The problem definition and background in Section 2 are helpful for understanding the scenario. [R2]
- The summary in Section 8 is handy. [R2]
- Empirical results are promising and show superior performance compared to previous approaches. [R1, R3]
- Despite numerous hyperparameters, case (8) in Figure 4 suggests low sensitivity to \(a\) and \(b\). [R3]
- The authors tackle a challenging problem in a strict setting, constraining model growth and not allowing storage of previous samples. [R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The time-aware regularization resembles a time-decay factor used in prior work (e.g., BioSLAM [1] Section 5.B.3). The paper does not clearly distinguish its unique contributions from that mechanism. [R1]
- **Insufficient ablation on scheduling:** The choice of exponential decay for \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) lacks theoretical or intuitive justification. An ablation comparing different time-aware functions (e.g., linear, polynomial, exponential) is needed. [R1, R3]  
  - The rationale for the scheduling in Section 4.4 is unclear; displaying \(\alpha(\widehat{y})\), \(\beta(\widehat{y})\), and their ratio with discussion would help. [R3]
- **Limited experimental scope:**  
  - Only 10-task configurations are used on CIFAR-100. Ablations with different numbers of tasks (e.g., 5 or 20 tasks) are missing. [R1]  
  - Experiments on large, high-resolution datasets like ImageNet are absent; the quality of VAE generation on such datasets may be a limitation. [R1]  
  - No comparisons with more recent state-of-the-art methods beyond BI-R (2020) and EWC (older). [R3]
- **Missing experimental details:**  
  - It is unclear how output units are added (incrementally for new classes or shared). [R4]  
  - Permuted-MNIST is typically task-incremental; the paper mentions 10 tasks but does not specify if this means 10 random permutations (each with 10 digits) or a single permutation per digit. The reported EWC accuracy of 27% is puzzling given that the original paper achieves >97% in a task-incremental setting. This discrepancy needs explanation. [R4]  
  - For CIFAR-100, what are the 10 tasks? Is incremental classification performed 10 classes at a time? [R4]
- **Diminishing plasticity and plasticity-stability dilemma:** Figure 3 shows that the proposed method's performance drops sharply on later tasks (e.g., tasks 9 & 10), indicating reduced plasticity over time. The paper does not adequately address this limitation or discuss the plasticity-stability dilemma. [R3]  
  - R2 asks: does the proposed method limit plasticity as more classes are seen?
- **Questionable metric choice:** The FID score is redundant; better stability metrics like BWT (backward transfer) should be used. [R3]
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - Figure 1 is unclear; arrow colors and the 'brain-inspired replay' arrow need better explanation. [R2, R4]  
  - The example in Section 4.3 is unclear: was class 0 seen in the first task? What does "t" represent (task ID?)? What is meant by "predicted numeral class label"? [R2]  
  - Section 6 can benefit from presentation polishing. [R3]  
  - Section 7 is not an analytical analysis. [R3]  
  - Minor typos: "stor and the", "laten space" in Fig. 6(b), missing capital letter in Section 8.2. [R4]
- **Extended neuroscience connection is verbose** and could be condensed to make room for implementation details, enhancing reproducibility. [R3]
- **Concerns about the generative replay scenario:**  
  - Privacy motivation is weak: generating data similar to past distribution risks data replication; using latent representations might be better. [R2]  
  - The paper does not provide a concrete application or scenario where generating inputs (rather than using a memory buffer) is advantageous. [R2]  
  - The method's contribution to the fundamental issue of generative model modal collapse during recursive replay is not discussed (see Shumailov et al. 2023). [R3]
- **Missing exploration of alternatives:**  
  - Have the authors considered generating latent representations instead of inputs? [R2]  
  - Have they considered using a conditional VAE to allow generating a proper representation of all previous classes with one distribution? [R2]  
  - Why is LwF considered a replay-based method? [R2]  
- **Clarity of ablation figures:** Figure 4 and Figure 5 are hard to interpret.  
  - What does the red curve in Figure 4 represent, what is its upper bound, and why is its performance low? [R3]  
  - In Figure 4, the y-axis indicates a relative value—is it relative to BI-R? [R3]  
  - What do \(\overline{d(\alpha)}\) and \(\overline{d(\beta)}\) in Figure 5 represent? [R3]  
  - How were \(K_{\alpha}\) and \(k_{\beta}\) chosen? [R3]  
  - The colors and figures in the ablation study are difficult to understand. [R2]
