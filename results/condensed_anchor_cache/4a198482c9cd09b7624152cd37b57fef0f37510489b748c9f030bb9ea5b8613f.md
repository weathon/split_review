## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates backdoor attack vulnerabilities in multi-task model merging, identifying two key challenges: backdoor succession (backdoors persist after merging) and backdoor transfer (backdoors propagate to clean models). It proposes Defense-Aware Merging (DAM), a meta-learning-based bi-level optimization method with dual masks (Task-Shared mask and Backdoor-Detection mask) to find a safety-aware shared subspace that mitigates both task interference and backdoor effects. Experiments on CLIP Vision Transformer across six image classification tasks compare DAM against ten merging baselines and three post-defense methods. DAM reduces attack success rate (ASR) by 2-10 percentage points with about 1% accuracy loss. Some reviewers consider the method's novelty incremental and gains marginal over existing defenses like SAU and AWM; others find the theoretical grounding and extensive evaluation convincing. Concerns about threat model clarity, computational cost, insufficient background, and writing clarity are raised. Two reviewers assign a score of 8 (high), and two assign a score of 6 (moderate).

### Strengths

1. **First work to reveal backdoor attack vulnerabilities in existing model merging methods**, with observations of backdoor succession and backdoor transfer (Reviewer 2, also implicit in Reviewer 1 and 4).
2. **Sufficient theoretical grounding** including proofs and mathematical explanations in Sections 2 and 3 (Reviewer 1).
3. **Extensive empirical evaluation** against many baselines: individual fine-tuning (3 baselines), multi-task merging (10 baselines), and post-defense methods (3 baselines), across multiple configurations (number of attacks, poisoned/clean models). Additional robustness experiments are in the appendix (Reviewer 1).
4. **Comprehensive comparison** with existing backdoor defenses (e.g., ANP, AWM, SAU) (Reviewer 2).
5. **Meta-learning approach is a reasonable way to balance effectiveness and efficiency**, with interesting different objectives for outer and inner levels (Reviewer 3).
6. **Well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewer 1, 4).
7. **Code and data provided for reproducibility** (Reviewer 1).
8. **Multi-task merging is a hot and meaningful research direction** (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Threat model clarity (Reviewer 2).** The paper needs to clarify: (a) attacker knowledge (does the attacker know the other models or the merging algorithm?), (b) defender capabilities (access to clean data, ability to inspect individual models?), and (c) how backdoor triggers propagate or amplify from poisoned to clean models during merging.
2. **Incremental novelty and marginal defense gains (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 3 and 4 echo concerns).** DAM is seen as a straightforward combination of two existing tasks (model merging and backdoor defense) via dual masks. The defense gain is marginal compared to existing post-hoc defenses like SAU and AWM, and the complexity of bi-level optimization may not be justified. A thorough analysis of computational overhead and resource requirements relative to those methods is missing. (Minority view: Reviewer 2 is more critical; Reviewer 3 also questions computational expense.)
3. **Lack of comparison with backdoor attacks in fine-tuning and continual learning (Reviewer 2).** The paper should discuss how unique challenges of model merging (no training data access, combining models for different tasks) differ from those scenarios and how this impacts defense design.
4. **Insufficient background on specific backdoor attacks (Reviewer 2).** The paper assumes familiarity with BadVit, TrojVit, LoRA-as-an-Attack, WAG, and the concept of "BadMerging." A brief introduction to how these attacks function, especially in vision transformers, would help. Also clarify what existing safeguards are broken by BadMerging.
5. **Writing and clarity issues (Reviewer 2 mainly; also Reviewer 1 and 4).**
   - Define "accuracy" and "attack success rate" more clearly (Reviewer 1).
   - Explain how the Backdoor Detection Mask differs from existing trigger inversion or synthesis methods (Reviewer 2, Q1).
   - Clarify what "subspace" refers to in Figure 4 (Reviewer 2, Q3).
   - Explain how DAM is combined with BadMerging in Table 6 (Reviewer 2, Q2).
   - Define what LoRA-as-an-Attack and WAG are in Table 5 (Reviewer 2, Q4).
   - Clarify the distinction between backdoor succession and backdoor transfer; they appear to express the same meaning (Reviewer 4).
   - Move or justify having the related work section in the appendix (Reviewer 1, Q1).
   - Update the reference: Wu et al., 2024 is withdrawn; use an available source (e.g., BackdoorBench) to support points in Sec 3 (Reviewer 4).
6. **Computational cost of bi-level optimization (Reviewer 3).** Justify sample efficiency and empirically showcase the algorithm's running time.
7. **Task-specific performance under backdoor attacks (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 4).**
   - If each task exhibits different performance levels under the same backdoor attack, test each task individually (Reviewer 3).
   - In Figure 2(a), RESISC 45 and EuroSAT are from the same domain, yielding high ACC and high ASR. In Figure 7(a), ASR drops sharply when merging different task-specific models. Provide an experiment comparing ASR drop when merging a backdoor model with several models (backdoored or clean) from different domains vs. similar domains, to assess whether simply merging with clean models already mitigates backdoors and thus reduces DAM's advantage (Reviewer 4, Q1).
8. **Design choices in the objective and masks (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 4).**
   - Why can performance and safety terms be linearly combined without a tradeoff coefficient? (Reviewer 3, Q1)
   - Why are backdoor trigger and target labels assumed known during merging in Eq. (2)? What if the synthesized perturbations are incorrect or inaccurate? Can the method still be effective with only rough knowledge of the backdoor attack? (Reviewer 3, Q2-3)
   - Why learn two separate masks M1 and M2 instead of a single mask that combines both utilities? Table 7 shows ASR increases when only M2 is used, which suggests dependency; this needs explanation (Reviewer 4, Q2).
9. **Accuracy maintenance (Reviewer 1).** DAM is good at minimizing ASR but less effective at maintaining or increasing accuracy (though explanation is provided and the task is acknowledged as challenging). This is a minor observation rather than a strong weakness.
