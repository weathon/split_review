- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 5, 5, 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The proposed SuperMark is a training-free watermarking framework that embeds watermarks into Gaussian noise and uses pretrained diffusion-based super-resolution (SR) models to denoise and produce watermarked images. Extraction uses DDIM inversion. Reviewers acknowledge the training-free nature, flexibility, and good performance under adaptive attacks, but criticize limited novelty (similar to Gaussian Shading, combining off-the-shelf components), mediocre performance (low PSNR/SSIM, not SOTA robustness), lack of efficiency analysis, insufficient ablation, missing comparisons, and questionable necessity of the SR module. There is disagreement on computational cost: some say it is lower, others say it is high and impractical. Security concerns about key-based extraction are raised. Presentation issues are noted. Overall, the paper is seen as exploratory with limited contribution.

### Strengths
- **Training-free**: The method requires no training or fine-tuning of the diffusion/SR models, simplifying implementation and broadening accessibility (Reviewers 2,3,4,6,7,8).
- **Flexibility and transferability**: SuperMark adapts across different datasets, SR models, watermark injection methods, and image resolutions, demonstrating versatility (Reviewer4). It works relatively well even across different distributions without retraining (Reviewer3).
- **Adaptive attack evaluation**: The manuscript includes tests with specific “Adaptive Attacks”, which is a strength (Reviewer1). Supplementary material provides numerous examples of watermarked images (Reviewer1).
- **Clear pipeline illustration**: Figure 2 is clearly illustrated and facilitates easy comprehension (Reviewer8). The writing and presentation are generally good and easy to follow (Reviewer3).
- **Comprehensive ablation**: An extensive ablation study on important hyperparameters is presented (Reviewer3).
- **Candor in attribution**: The paper clearly points out the parts of the framework that come from other works (Reviewer5).
- **Lower computing cost claim (minority)**: One reviewer notes the method has lower time and computing cost compared to other watermarking methods (Reviewer6; but this is disputed by others – see Weaknesses).
- **Novel insight into diffusion robustness (minority)**: The paper attempts to highlight the inherent robustness of the diffusion process against distortions (Reviewer8; others find this claim unsupported).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The method is largely a combination of existing off-the-shelf techniques (watermark injection from Gaussian Shading, diffusion-based SR from pretrained models). No novel algorithm or module is introduced (Reviewers 1,3,4,5,6,7,8). The framework is described as a “super-resolution-variant” of Gaussian Shading, with the only difference being the replacement of the DPM-solver with an SR sampler (Reviewer5). The contributions at the end of the Introduction are very general and do not represent major advancements (Reviewer1). There is no theoretical contribution throughout the paper (Reviewer7). The lack of distinction from prior work, especially Gaussian Shading, weakens the contribution (Reviewer5,7).
- **Mediocre performance**: The method does not achieve state-of-the-art in either fidelity or robustness. PSNR is only 32.5 in Table 1, which is low and severely affects image fidelity (Reviewer8). PSNR/SSIM are lower than methods like CIN, MBRS, and StegaStamp (Reviewer5). Extraction accuracy is lower than StegaStamp in most cases (Reviewer5). Under normal distortions, average accuracy does not show a significant lead over SepMark, and under adaptive attacks it is not the best (Reviewer4). The trade-off between fidelity and robustness is not compelling (Reviewer8). The comparison is unfair because visual quality is not controlled when assessing robustness; a fair evaluation should keep fidelity at similar levels (Reviewer4).
- **Lack of efficiency analysis**: The paper does not compare model size, MACs, FLOPs, or inference time with existing methods for both embedding and extraction separately (Reviewer2,7,8). Stable diffusion is well-known for its intensive computation, slow inference, and large model size; if it uses 10× more resources while performing similarly, the contribution is diminished (Reviewer2). Most watermarking injection occurs on mobile devices (phones/laptops), making the stable diffusion model impractical (Reviewer2). In contrast, one reviewer claims lower time/cost (Reviewer6) – this disagreement is noted.
- **Insufficient ablation and missing experiments**: Ablation studies are incomplete; e.g., the impact of removing the SR model is not analyzed (Reviewer8). Experiments against SOTA attacks (e.g., Evading Watermark Based Detection, CCS 2023) are missing (Reviewer8). Missing comparisons under varying distortion levels (different JPEG quality factors, noise levels, blur, crop) – only fixed attacks are reported (Reviewer1). The specific parameters of attacks (JPEG quality, G Noise level, G Blur kernel, Crop percentage) in Tables 1/3 are not reported (Reviewer1). No analysis of the effect of different SR models on performance (Reviewer7). There is no ablation on the necessity of replication and encryption in the injection strategy (Reviewer5).
- **Unnecessary SR module**: The use of super-resolution is questionable because both input and output images are normal resolution; the SR model can be replaced by any other diffusion-based sampler (Reviewer5,6,7). The necessity of SR is not justified; the method just introduces a diffusion process, and the performance advantages come from the diffusion itself, not from any novel design (Reviewer5,6). SR may impair watermark extraction (Reviewer7). Since diffusion can generate large-resolution images directly, the need for an SR module is unclear (Reviewer7).
- **Security concerns**: The adopted Gaussian Shade injection requires encryption to preserve a uniform distribution, and extraction requires a key. If an attacker obtains the same image, security is entirely dependent on the key, which must be transmitted over a secure channel, making the scheme more like steganography than watermarking. Traditional watermarking methods do not require a secure channel for a key. The paper should include a section discussing this trade-off (Reviewer5).
- **Unsupported claims**: Several key claims lack theoretical or empirical backing. “Diffusion process holds inherent robustness against different distortions” is stated without proof and may only apply to stable diffusion, not all diffusion methods (Reviewer1,6). The claimed insight about the encoder–noise layer–decoder architecture trade-off between robustness and fidelity is already well-established and not new; the authors do not offer a new perspective or solution (Reviewer1,4). The expression that the method is “more flexible and general” for both AIGC and real images is arbitrary; recent AIGC watermarking focuses on in-process methods because post-processing can be bypassed (Reviewer6).
- **Missing methodological details**: Which pretrained VAE is used is not mentioned in the main paper (Reviewer3). The adoption of Tree-Ring’s watermark injection method is confusing: Tree-Ring (Wen et al. 2024) operates in the Fourier frequency domain, while the manuscript discusses spatial embedding; the supplementary description of “low-frequency modes” is superficial and it is not clear how frequencies are calculated (Reviewer1). The difference from Gaussian Shading is not discussed (Reviewer7).
- **Presentation issues**: The abstract is too lengthy and not concise (Reviewer7). Figure 1’s double-headed arrow between the two “m” is unclear; Figure 2 lacks a legend, affecting readability (Reviewer6). The text font size in equations is smaller than the main text (Reviewer7). It is not clear how the framework seamlessly integrates with various watermark injection methods and pre-trained SR models (Reviewer7).
- **Limited AIGC relevance**: The paper attempts to connect to AIGC watermarking, but post-processing methods can be bypassed by malicious users; it is better positioned as a real-image watermarking attempt without forcing AIGC connections (Reviewer6).
- **Specific missing comparisons (from reviewer questions)**: The paper does not report what “normal distortions” means (level of JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, etc.) (Reviewer1). Tables 1/3 do not specify the attack parameters (e.g., JPEG quality factor, G Noise standard deviation, G Blur kernel size, Crop percentage) (Reviewer1). The claim “Tree-Ring is 0-bit watermark method” is inaccurate because it is a Fourier domain method (Reviewer1). The paper does not compare the computation cost of embedding vs. extraction separately for each method (Reviewer2). Given the extra cost of stable diffusion, why not fine-tune a small diffusion model for embedding (mobile) and another for extraction (server) to show superiority? (Reviewer2). How does the inference time of the diffusion process compare to conventional encoder-noise-decoder frameworks? (Reviewer8).
