## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces AVG-LLaVA, an LMM that adaptively selects visual granularity based on the input image and instruction, extending LLaVA-NeXT with a visual granularity scaler (multiple pooling layers) and a visual granularity router (Transformer + MLP + voter). A training paradigm RGLF aligns router predictions with LMM preferences using a ranking loss without extra manual annotations. Experiments on 11 benchmarks claim superior performance with fewer visual tokens and faster inference (e.g., 85.3% token reduction, 2.53× speedup on AI2D). Reviewer opinions converge that motivation is sound, but raise concerns about novelty, complexity, insufficient ablation, and ambiguous attribution of gains.

### Strengths

1. The adaptive visual granularity selection based on both image and instruction is a novel and intriguing research direction, and the attempt to train the router directly in the VLM with a ranking loss (RGLF) is impressive. (Reviewer 4)
2. Experimental results on multiple benchmarks demonstrate that AVG-LLaVA can achieve better performance compared to LLaVA-NeXT while consuming substantially less computation (e.g., large visual token reduction and inference speedup). (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of novelty in core components.** The multiple pooling operation in the visual granularity scaler is common (similar to SPPNet), and the router (Transformer + MLP + voter) is a standard approach; the combination is incremental. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4) The overall motivation is highly similar to the Matryoshka model. (Reviewer 2)
2. **Training pipeline is complex and costly.** The original two-stage pipeline becomes four stages, with stages 2 and 3 training the vision encoder and LLM, significantly increasing computational burden, resource requirements, and hyperparameter tuning complexity. No detailed breakdown of the computational cost for each stage is provided, making it hard to assess practical feasibility. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4)
3. **Repeated training data confounds improvement attribution.** The training data for stages 2, 3, and 4 are identical. It is unclear whether performance improvements come from the proposed method or simply from training for three epochs on the same SFT data. The authors should provide a comparative experiment training LLaVA-NeXT with repeated SFT data two or three times and present the results. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2)
4. **Performance is not uniformly superior.** On some benchmarks (e.g., GQA, ScienceQA) the method underperforms relative to baselines, raising the question of whether token reduction is always beneficial. (Reviewer 2)
5. **Limited effectiveness on OCR/text-rich tasks.** For TextVQA and ChartQA, visual tokens are almost entirely retained (e.g., ChartQA token-per-grid 99.1%, speed 0.97× without improvement), suggesting the router is nearly ineffective there and any gain may come from repeated training. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3)
6. **Insufficient analysis and ablation.** The paper lacks direct rigorous evidence for the selection of granularity (e.g., visualizations of attention maps or actual token clipping for different prompts, like Figure 1). There is no exploration of alternative granularity selection approaches (e.g., task-specific fine-tuning, manual selection, or patch-wise granularity selection that might save more tokens in text-rich scenarios). (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4)
7. **Missing details for reproducibility and comparison.** The paper should provide total token numbers for each method in the main performance comparison, absolute inference speed values (not just relative), and a detailed breakdown of accuracy per granularity level (e.g., in Table 3). (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4)
8. **Concerns about robustness.** The router’s heavy reliance on instruction tokens raises questions about robustness when instructions are ambiguous or noisy. The model has not been evaluated in real-world scenarios with less curated, noisier data. (Reviewer 2)
9. **No demonstration of generality to other visual encoders.** It would be beneficial to show that AVG-LLaVA also works with native dynamic resolution visual encoders (e.g., Qwen2-VL). (Reviewer 1)

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more positive about the research focus and experimental effectiveness, while Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 (scores 5) are more critical of novelty, complexity, and confounds, with Reviewer 2 raising the strongest concerns about repeated training data and OCR performance.*
