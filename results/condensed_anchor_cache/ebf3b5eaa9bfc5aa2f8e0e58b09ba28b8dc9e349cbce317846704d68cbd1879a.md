## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Disentangled Representation Learning (DRL) methodology to improve interpretability in microscopy image classification. The approach first trains an Ada-GVAE model with weak supervision on a synthetic Textures-dSprites dataset (introduced in the paper) designed to reflect simple textures for interpreting microscopy images. This model is then used to encode images from three real-world microscopy domains (plankton, yeast vacuoles, human cells), with optional unsupervised fine-tuning via a beta-VAE. The resulting low-dimensional, interpretable features are fed into downstream classifiers (gradient-boosted trees and MLPs). The study aims to show that the DRL framework provides a trade-off between accuracy and interpretability.

### Strengths
- Addresses the significant challenge of disentanglement for improving classifier interpretability, particularly for biological/medical images [R1, R4].
- Evaluates recent weakly-supervised DRL ideas in a more realistic application domain [R1].
- Presents an alternative to learning disentangled representations directly from RGB images by using large-scale pretrained features (DINO) [R1].
- Introduces a new synthetic sprites dataset (Textures-dSprites) to facilitate interpretation of microscopy images [R1].
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear organization and logical flow [R2].
- The application of weakly-supervised DRL to real-world image analysis is a promising research direction [R2].

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and technical contribution:** The work applies existing DRL methods (Ada-GVAE, beta-VAE) to microscopy without introducing fundamentally new concepts, techniques, or substantial modifications [R1, R2, R3, R4]. The only apparent novelty is the application to microscopy, which is insufficient for ICLR [R2, R3].
- **Missing baselines and comparative experiments:** No comparison with other DRL methods or alternative approaches is provided [R3, R4]. Crucially, there is no comparison of classification performance without the proposed disentanglement procedure—e.g., training a classifier directly on RGB images or on DINO features without any modifications [R1, R2]. Such comparisons are needed to understand the tasks’ difficulty and the trade-off of using disentanglement [R1].
- **Hypothesis that DINO features are already disentangled:** It is possible that DINO features already capture disentangled factors, and the proposed strategy merely assigns names to those factors rather than achieving new disentanglement via the VAEs [R1]. If this hypothesis holds, the value of the method is limited to annotating factors rather than discovering them [R1]. No experiment is designed to test this [R1].
- **Unclear method details and insufficient justification:**
  - Why Ada-GVAE is chosen and how the model is fine-tuned (what exactly is fine-tuned, and how?) [R4].
  - The difference between the proposed method and that of Dapueto et al. is unclear [R4].
  - The claim that disentanglement learned from synthetic images can transfer to microscopy images lacks theoretical and empirical evidence [R4].
  - How an RGB image is directly fed to the classifier (GBT/MLP) is unclear [R4].
  - In line 322, the statement that after fine-tuning, scale and texture become more relevant than scale and shape is not explained [R4].
- **Evaluation issues:**
  - Reliance on classification accuracy alone is insufficient to assess representation quality; disentanglement and classification performance are not synonymous [R4].
  - Figure 6 shows that the OMES metric does not indicate better disentanglement with the proposed method [R4].
  - Evaluated classifiers (GBT, MLP) are simple; more sophisticated models (e.g., CNNs) should be used for fair assessment [R4].
- **Scope and domain justification:** The work is too narrowly focused on microscopy images; demonstrating on natural images would strengthen the case [R2]. The authors do not adequately justify why DRL is specifically suited for microscopy image analysis, nor do they articulate what novel challenges or requirements this domain brings to DRL [R2, R4].
- **Presentation and clarity problems:**
  - Informal language (e.g., "thanks"), multiple word/sentence/structural errors; Section 2.2 resembles ChatGPT output [R3].
  - Figures are low-resolution, small; captions lack essential details (e.g., explanations of OMES, MIG, DCI, balanced accuracy) [R3, R4].
  - Excessive use of multiple highlighting styles (underscoring, bold, italics) [R3].
  - Missing Related Work section; duplicate section titles (both 2.2 and 3.5 are "DISENTANGLEMENT EVALUATION") [R4].
  - Important metrics are not explained in text or captions, hurting reproducibility [R3, R4].
- **Minority reviewer concern (R3, rating 1):** The reviewer found the novelty extremely limited and the presentation severely lacking, lowering the overall assessment far below the other reviewers (who rated 3). R3 explicitly states that the only contribution is the application of DRL to microscopy, which does not suffice, and that the absence of comparisons and poor presentation substantially weaken the paper.
