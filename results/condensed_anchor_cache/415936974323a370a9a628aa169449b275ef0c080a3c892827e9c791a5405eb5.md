## Merged Review

### Summary
PivotMesh is a scalable framework for generating compact, sharp 3D meshes by modeling native mesh distributions. It uses a transformer-based auto-encoder to encode meshes into discrete tokens, decoding hierarchically from face to vertex level. A novel pivot-vertex guidance strategy first generates coarse mesh representations, then generates full mesh tokens autoregressively. The method is evaluated on ShapeNet, Objaverse, and Objaverse-XL, and it outperforms PolyGen and MeshGPT across various tasks including unconditional generation, point cloud-guided generation, mesh variation, and coarse mesh refinement.

### Strengths
- **Innovative pivot-vertex guidance**: Generating pivot vertices before faces is a novel approach. Ablation studies (without pivot guidance, degrees selection) validate its effectiveness (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Scalability and generality**: The framework extends native mesh generation to large-scale datasets, demonstrating versatility on both small (ShapeNet) and large (Objaverse, Objaverse-XL) datasets (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Thorough quantitative and qualitative comparisons across multiple datasets and tasks (unconditional generation, variation, refinement, point-cloud guided) show high-quality results with sharp details and complex geometries (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The framework outperforms existing methods in all settings (Reviewer 2).
- **Diversity and originality analysis**: A systematic analysis ensures the diversity and originality of generated objects (Reviewer 2).
- **Transformer-based auto-encoder**: Enables encoding of triangle meshes into discrete tokens (Reviewer 3) and outperforms PolyGen and MeshGPT when conditioned on pivot vertices (Reviewer 3).
- **Downstream applications**: Demonstrates value beyond unconditional generation, including mesh variation and coarse mesh refinement (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient diversity evaluation**: The analysis of diversity across generated shapes is not systematic or quantified. More diverse shapes with complex topologies (e.g., holes, thin structures like the ficus in NeRF-Synthetic data) should be tested (Reviewer 1, confidence 5).  
- **Missing direct comparisons with state-of-the-art**: Comparisons with concurrent works such as MeshAnything(2), EdgeRunner, and MeshXL are needed to demonstrate superiority (Reviewer 1, confidence 5). Reviewer 2 suggests including MeshXL quantitative results (even if trained on different data) as a benchmark for reference.
- **Overclaiming and insufficient discussion of limitations**:  
  - The introduction and abstract should be revised to avoid claiming that existing works cannot handle large datasets, as several concurrent studies (e.g., MeshXL) demonstrate some success (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).  
  - Lacks a comprehensive discussion of failure cases and limitations. The paper acknowledges insufficient control, sometimes producing undesired geometries, but offers no systematic analysis (Reviewer 1).  
- **Computational efficiency concerns**: The auto-encoder and autoregressive transformer each use 24 layers, making the method memory-intensive and less efficient (Reviewer 3, confidence 3).  
- **Clarity of conditional generation**: It is confusing how the discrete mesh tokens from the auto-encoder and the pivot vertices are combined for conditional mesh generation; the paper does not explain this interaction clearly (Reviewer 3, confidence 3).  
- **Incomplete dataset characterization**:  
  - The paper mentions face filtering (lines 303-306). A distribution map summarizing the number of vertices and faces in the training and test meshes would help clarify the data (Reviewer 3).  
  - The estimated sequence lengths reported in Table 1 would be more informative if actual lengths were measured on real dataset samples (Reviewer 2).  

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) was notably more positive, finding fewer weaknesses (only the overclaiming issue) and rating soundness 4, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 6) raised more detailed concerns about diversity, comparisons, efficiency, and clarity.
