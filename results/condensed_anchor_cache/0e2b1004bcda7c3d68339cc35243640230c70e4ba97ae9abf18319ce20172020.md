## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes D2G (Debiased Learning with Distribution Guidance) for Generalized Category Discovery (GCD). The framework addresses label bias between known and unknown classes, variations in certainty among unlabeled samples, and semantic distribution shifts. D2G co-trains an auxiliary debiased classifier alongside the GCD classifier, introduces a semantic distribution detector (based on multiple one-vs-all binary classifiers) in a separate feature space, and applies a curriculum learning strategy guided by distribution certainty. All reviewers note that the writing is clear, the motivation is sound, and the framework achieves state-of-the-art results on several GCD benchmarks. Reviewers agree the framework is novel, efficient at inference (modules can be discarded), and incorporates multiple components (debiased learning, distribution detection, curriculum learning) into a single-stage training pipeline. However, reviewers raise concerns about insufficient ablations, lack of analysis for some design choices, performance drops on certain datasets, limited comparisons, and potential overparameterization.

### Strengths
- The motivation for addressing label bias is well-grounded: prior GCD methods apply soft supervision uniformly to unlabeled data, resulting in weaker supervision for unknown classes. D2G aligns with this motivation.
- The approach yields clear performance improvements on multiple GCD benchmarks (e.g., CUB, Stanford, Herbarium19 generally improved, though with noted exceptions).
- The framework is efficient at inference since all introduced modules (auxiliary classifier, distribution detector) can be discarded; no extra computational burden.
- The paper is well-written, organized, and easy to follow, with clear explanations of technical details.
- The topic has significant theoretical and practical importance, addressing a gap in existing GCD literature.
- The introduction of a debiased learning framework specific to GCD, with a multi-feature distribution approach (semantic distribution detector + auxiliary debiased classifier + curriculum learning), is considered innovative and novel.
- The technical contributions are well-structured and effectively evaluated; the integration of components reinforces overall performance.
- The method is the first to consider both label bias and semantic shift in a unified GCD framework, trained in a single stage.
- Extensive experimentation on public GCD benchmarks demonstrates effectiveness, with consistent gains over strong baselines (e.g., SimGCD) across most datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Performance inconsistency**: There is a performance drop on Herbarium19 compared to the baseline (SimGCD), raising concerns about generalizability, especially on long-tailed/imbalanced datasets. Additionally, gains on CUB are marginal, and on ImageNet-1K the number of comparison methods is limited, making results less reliable.
- **Insufficient ablation and component analysis**: Several design choices lack proper ablation studies. Examples: (1) the number of layers in the MLP prior to the OVA module, (2) loss weights (e.g., λ in Eq. 14), (3) learning rates, (4) the impact of removing the debiased learning strategy entirely (e.g., "w/o debiased learning, w/ auxiliary classifier, w/o semantic dist. learning, w/o dist. guidance" as a control), and (5) the effect of different scaling functions/parameters in Eq. 10. Most ablations are reported only on Stanford; broader validation across datasets (as requested for debiased learning and auxiliary classifier components) is missing.
- **Comparison under identical training conditions**: D2G finetunes more parameters than SimGCD (which only trains the last block). The paper does not compare under the same training setup (e.g., also increasing capacity in SimGCD), making it unclear whether gains are due to the proposed modules or simply increased model capacity.
- **Lack of analysis on the feature space**: All modules can be discarded at inference, implying improvements stem from better DINO CLS token discriminability. However, no visualization or quantitative analysis (e.g., feature space before/after D2G) is provided to support this claim.
- **Need for justification of OOD techniques for GCD**: The semantic distribution detector is essentially a set of one-vs-all binary classifiers (OOD-style). The connection between detecting out-of-distribution samples and improving clustering for GCD is not well established, as the objectives differ. The choice of an MLP for this purpose and its architecture (e.g., number of layers) are not adequately justified.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The fixed threshold of 0.5 for the OVA classifier appears arbitrary; the paper does not compare with adaptive thresholds or analyze its impact across datasets. Similarly, hyperparameters in Eq. 14 (loss weights) are not shown to be derived from experiments or validated via ablation, and their sensitivity is not discussed.
- **Risk of negative transfer and overfitting**: The paper lacks discussion on potential negative transfer if debiasing is not carefully controlled, especially given the auxiliary classifier could introduce its own biases. For fine-grained datasets with limited unlabeled data, the risk of overfitting during assisted debiasing is not addressed.
- **Need for error bars**: Variation across GCD benchmarks can be large; the paper should report all results with error bars from three independent runs (as SimGCD does in its supplementary), to allow proper statistical comparison.
- **Training cost analysis missing**: While inference is efficient, the computational overhead of the auxiliary components during training (e.g., training time, memory) is not analyzed, which is important for practical deployment.
- **Other issues**: In Fig. 1(d), the brown dish line is invisible. D2G is suboptimal compared to InfoSieve on ImageNet-1K (Tab. 2), but no specific analysis is provided. The need for a second classifier (instead of applying debiased losses directly to the original GCD classifier) is not clearly explained. The impact of removing the MLP prior to the OVA module is not evaluated.
