## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CoMNet, a ConvNet architecture inspired by the structural organization of cortical modules in the biological visual cortex. It aims for efficiency across multiple dimensions (network depth, parameters, FLOPs, latency, branching, memory budget) and proposes a Multi-Dimensional Efficiency (MDE) evaluation protocol. CoMNet is shown to outperform several representative ConvNet designs (ResNet, ResNeXt, RegNet, RepVGG, ParNet) in the MDE setting. Reviewers disagree substantially: one finds the biological motivation deeply flawed and misleading (score 3), two find the architecture reasonable but with limited novelty and insufficient experiments (scores 5, 5), and one (less familiar with the field) praises clarity and performance (score 6).

### Strengths
- The method simultaneously analyzes several diverse and reasonable performance metrics (latency, depth, branching, FLOPs, parameters) and compares against strong baselines (ResNet, ResNeXt, RegNet, RepVGG, ParNet), showing competitive outcomes. (R1, R2, R3)
- The paper is well-written and presents its ideas clearly. (R2)
- The proposed model achieves higher performance with fewer computation cost and latency. (R2)
- The biological inspiration is explored systematically: the authors identify columnar structure, shared input/input replication, limited synaptic connections, massive parallelization, and lateral connection inhibition from the visual cortex, then translate these into neural equivalents step-by-step, building the CoMNet-unit in a well-founded manner. (R3) – *Note: R1 heavily disputes the accuracy of these biological claims (see Weaknesses).*
- The design focuses on 5 crucial efficiency dimensions, producing a model that balances representation ability and hardware efficiency. (R3)
- The analogy with cortical columns provides an interesting perspective on group convolutions, and the paper makes concrete links between biological organization and network components that are understandable to non-neuroscientists. (R4)
- The architecture is simple yet performs well. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Biological motivation is mischaracterized and misleading (R1 – confident, detailed):** Section 3 contains many errors. Specific examples: ocular dominance columns are mislabeled as “modules in shallower layers” while they coexist with orientation columns; Mountcastle (1997) is cited for “a column contains only a few neurons (70-100)” but Mountcastle refers to a minicolumn (a column has many minicolumns); the claim that a 70-100 neuron unit limits connectivity is false (cortical cells have orders of magnitude more connections); the claim that receptive fields are small regardless of depth is false (deeper neurons have receptive fields tens of degrees wide); the statement that “cortical modules cannot communicate except at output via pyramidal neurons” misses that the majority of cortical cells are pyramidal, yet the paper associates pyramidal cells with a 1×1 conv at output while most model neurons are non-pyramidal. Because of these errors, the biological grounding does not justify the architecture; the paper should purge biology and explain innovations relative to prior networks with a proper ablation study. (R1)
- **Novelty is weak (R3 – absolutely certain):** Most ideas inspired from the visual cortex (weight sharing, shortcut, group convolution) have already been used in ConvNets. For each design point, the contribution is not novel. The modules are based on unproven hypotheses, and different solutions exist from other biologically inspired frameworks. (R3)
- **Experiments are insufficient (R3, R2, R4):** Comparisons are not comprehensive. Different models are evaluated at different resolutions and scales, making it hard to draw fair conclusions. Only partial works are compared (e.g., EfficientNet only appears in one regime, ParNet in another). The paper only compares earlier works (most recent 2021); more recent CNNs (e.g., RevCol, MogaNet) and Vision Transformers should be included. (R2, R3, R4) The ViT comparison is only in the appendix and should be in the main body with modern backbones. (R2) Experiments on segmentation and detection would strengthen the paper. (R2)
- **Ablation study is limited (R1, R3):** The analysis of skip connections and width is present, but the reasons for the architecture’s performance remain unclear. Key aspects like the number of lateral connections and number of stages are not ablated. (R1, R3) The novelty relative to Inception blocks (multiple parallel streams with uniform structure and greater depth) needs clarification and ablation to explain why CoMNet outperforms RepVGG. (R1)
- **Relationship to group convolutions and ResNeXt is not thoroughly analyzed (R4 – high confidence):** The comparison in Sec 4.7 is vague. Input Replication (IR) followed by group convolution is equivalent to a regular convolution, so if ResNeXt performs a sequence that includes IR+group conv, the difference reduces to stacking more group convs without between-group recombination. The paper should study this incrementally: what happens when varying *l* (making it smaller, adding 1×1 recombinations after every group conv, or for *l*=1)? Is the model then the same as ResNeXt? The “no blocks, only stages” claim is unclear because residual connections between conv applications are analogous to blocks. The advantage of this design choice over blocks with cross-column combination should be clarified. (R4)
- **Presentation issues and unclear details (R1, R4):** The paper contains unsubstantiated claims (e.g., “exponential param growth” – depth gives linear, width quadratic) and missing definitions (Fig 2d: parameter “b” not described; Eq1: T_ccm = CCM(.) not used; no definition of CCM in terms of convolutions). Variant names (A0, B1, C1) are listed only in an appendix table, not described in the text. Table 2 shows no difference in params/flops for plain vs Structural Reparameterization yet a speed increase – is that correct? Fig 1: ParNet is mentioned in the abstract but not shown in the figure. These need clarification. (R4) Additionally, the ablation on skip connections and width in the appendix is noted but does not justify the architecture. (R1)
- **Table 1 anomaly (R3 – absolutely certain):** R0, R1, R2, and R3 have the same latency, which seems impossible from experience. (R3)
- **Missing explanation for Structural Reparameterization combination (R3):** Table 2 mentions combining CoMNet with Structural Reparameterization; what method is that? (R3)
- **Related work omission (R4):** The older “network-in-network” paper (13) is relevant for interpreting 1×1 convs as applying a small MLP, similar to interpreting stacked group convs as columnar operations. (R4)

**Reviewer disagreement on biological motivation:** R1 strongly criticizes the biological motivation as factually wrong and misleading, recommending its removal. R3 lists it as a strength but also notes that each design point has been used before. R4 finds the biological parallels interesting and understandable, supporting the motivation. This contrast should be noted.
