## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a universal self-supervised learning framework (U-SSL) that trains on multiple graphs with disparate node/edge features by first homogenizing them via graph-specific encoders into a common space, then learning generalisable features with a shared universal encoder. The method is evaluated on node classification in citation graphs using the NAGphormer backbone and PairSim pretext objective. One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) views the problem as important and the results promising, while three reviewers (scores 3, confidence 4 each) raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental validity, reproducibility, and generality, collectively recommending rejection.

### Strengths
1. **Problem importance and novelty**: The research question of learning universal representations across different graphs with heterogeneous features is interesting and practically significant (R1, R3, R4). All reviewers agree on the relevance of the topic.
2. **Simple, intuitive framework**: The two-module design (graph-specific encoders → shared universal encoder) is straightforward, easy to understand, and implementable (R1, R3, R4). R2 notes the concept is simple but useful in engineering practice.
3. **Writing and organization**: The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4). R3 finds the definitions of concepts like “universal self-supervision” comprehensive.
4. **Promising transferability evidence**: When trained on [CoraFull, Cora-ML, DBLP, Citeseer, PubMed] and adapted to Arxiv, U-SSL outperforms SSL performed only on Arxiv itself (R1). R2 and R3 also acknowledge the approach shows some transferability.
5. **Comprehensive experiments on certain dimensions**: R2 notes the paper includes “comparatively comprehensive experiments on efficacy, efficiency and scalability.”
6. **Finding on domain gap**: Section 8.5 shows that including graphs with a larger domain gap negatively affects universal embedding quality, which is consistent with expectations (R1, appears as a strength but also a suggestion for more experiments).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and shallow insights** (minority reviewer positive, but others negative):
   - R2: The method is “too straightforward” with insufficient insights; contribution is “poor” (score 1 on contribution).
   - R3: While the problem is novel, the approach itself is not particularly deep.
   - R1 acknowledges the approach is “simple” but considers the overall research question and results promising.
   - *Agreement across reviewers*: The technical novelty is weak; the paper reads more like an engineering contribution without fundamental algorithmic insight.

2. **Narrow experimental scope – only one backbone, one pretext task, one downstream task**:
   - R1, R3: Only NAGphormer is used as the graph encoder. Lacks experiments with established GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE), making results potentially specific to transformer architectures.
   - R1: Only PairSim as SSL objective (missing contrastive learning, masked node prediction) and only node classification as downstream task (missing graph classification, link prediction). This narrow scope limits conclusions about universality.
   - R2: Choice of SSL task guided by downstream task is “heuristic / too specific”; pre-training should be generalizable to many tasks.
   - *Severe limitation*: The paper cannot claim “universal” features or a “foundation graph model” without testing multiple backbones, pretext objectives, and downstream tasks.

3. **Unconventional and irreproducible experimental setup**:
   - R1, R3: Data splits are not specified anywhere; the performance values reported (e.g., supervised baseline on Arxiv at 61%) are far below what is commonly achieved (e.g., simple 2-layer GCN >70%). This discrepancy raises serious doubts about the experimental setup.
   - R1: Table 1 shows a “significant gap” between supervised baselines and SSL methods, which is not typical in graph SSL literature (e.g., DGI). This suggests either weak SSL implementation or non-standard splits.
   - R1: No code or detailed experimental settings provided; PairSim objective lacks precise mathematical formulation and implementation details. Reproducibility is severely limited.
   - R3: The paper does not clarify splits; the results “do not align with the common splits used for the corresponding baselines.” R3 recommends conducting experiments under standard SSL settings (contrastive, masked graph autoencoder).

4. **Insufficient generality and applicability**:
   - R4: Experiments only on citation graphs; the framework may not generalize to social networks, code graphs, or other complex networks with different structures and feature sets.
   - R4: The proposed method only works for graphs that already have node features; real-world graphs often lack features, which severely restricts applicability.
   - R3: The graph-specific encoders are trained only on seen graphs; there is no mechanism to adapt to an unseen graph where no graph-specific encoder has been learned (i.e., how to obtain universal embeddings for a completely new dataset without retraining). R3 suggests exploring zero-shot or few-shot adaptation.
   - R4 notes that for citation graphs specifically, one could use a pre-trained language model to generate unified node features from paper abstracts, potentially making graph-specific encoders unnecessary.
   - *Contradiction*: R1 sees the need for more transfer experiments; R3 and R4 see the current treatment of unseen graphs as incomplete.

5. **Practical utility questioned**:
   - R4: In Tables 1 and 2, U-SSL sometimes underperforms simple full fine-tuning baselines, and those baselines are more efficient. What is the practical advantage of U-SSL? The paper does not clearly articulate when U-SSL should be preferred over cheaper alternatives.
   - R1: The claim that U-SSL bridges the gap between SSL and supervised performance is not supported by the provided results (see weakness 3).

6. **Questions that overlap with criticisms / missing experiments**:
   - R1: Why add an additional data augmentation step before the linear layers? Ablation is needed.
   - R1: Include more transferability results beyond Arxiv to convincingly claim adaptability.
   - R4: Are the same backbone models used for baseline, SSL, and U-SSL? (Implies possible unfair comparison.)
   - R4: Why not include OGBN-Arxiv during pre-training? If more similar citation networks are used, downstream performance may increase.
   - R1, R3: Clarify the definition of “similar” vs “dissimilar” nodes in PairSim without label information; provide the exact objective function.
   - R1: The results for node classification are inconsistent with prior work (e.g., Arxiv 61% vs typical 70%+). Explain the gap.

7. **Minority disagreements**:
   - R1 is notably more positive about the framework’s transferability and the relevance of the problem, scoring 5. R1 also suggests that section 8.5’s finding about domain gap is a strength (though they request more experiments). However, R1’s own weaknesses (missing details, inconsistent numbers, narrow scope) align with the other reviewers, even though R1 considers the paper salvageable. The other three reviewers (score 3) are consistently negative about novelty, experimental rigor, and scope. This split indicates that even the more positive reviewer acknowledges major flaws.
