## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CLIP-LAD, a training-free few-shot logical anomaly detection method that leverages CLIP. It uses a coarse-to-fine segmentation pipeline: clustering CLIP features into regions, coarse prompts for foreground/background separation, fine-grained prompts for part-level segmentation, and class histograms stored as memory for anomaly scoring. Experiments on MVTec LOCO show some good results. Reviewers generally found the pipeline simple and effective on this dataset, but raised significant concerns about novelty, contribution, and fairness of evaluation.

### Strengths
- Simple pipeline with clear, easy-to-understand components.
- Addresses the under-explored logical anomaly detection (LAD) task with an easy-to-implement solution.
- Experimental results demonstrate effectiveness on MVTec LOCO under few-shot settings.
- Paper is well-written with clear organization and effective visual aids.

### Weaknesses
- **Reviewer disagreement on overall contribution:** Reviewer 2 (rating 5) was moderately positive, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (ratings 1 and 3) found novelty and technical contribution very limited.
- **Limited novelty:** The method is a simple combination of existing techniques (K‑Means clustering, hierarchical segmentation, open‑vocabulary segmentation with CLIP, memory‑bank comparison) without new insights or technological innovation.
- **Insufficient differentiation from prior CLIP‑based AD works:** No discussion or comparison with few‑shot/zero‑shot CLIP methods like WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, or InCTRL. A separate related‑work section on CLIP‑based AD is missing.
- **Unclear component contribution:** No ablation study showing which part of the pipeline contributes most to performance.
- **Unfair comparison due to prompt annotation:** The method requires detailed, manually crafted fine‑grained prompts for each object part, incurring extra annotation effort. Competing methods (e.g., EfficientAD, PatchCore) do not need such labels, making the comparison inequitable. The task essentially becomes weakly‑supervised segmentation.
- **Limited evaluation dataset:** All experiments are on MVTec LOCO, which has a clear background. Robustness on more challenging datasets (e.g., DigitAnatomy) or with background noise injection is not demonstrated.
- **Confusing task definition:** The paper claims “logical anomaly detection” but no high‑level reasoning process is used; the difference from standard anomaly detection remains unclear.
- **Technical issues needing clarification (from Reviewer 3):**
  - The number of clusters per category: fixed or variable?
  - How are the detailed prompts generated, and how does prompt quality affect performance?
  - Equation (2): The histogram difference ratio is not minimized when histograms are identical (e.g., identical histograms yield score 3, while completely different histograms (0,0,1) yield score 2.5). This should be corrected.
  - Line 268 states “select the maximum value from the anomaly map as the resulting anomaly score”, but Equation (3) takes the max over the memory bank. Please clarify.
