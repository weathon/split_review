## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a rotation-equivariant local descriptor by replacing standard convolution with group-equivariant steerable CNNs, fusing multi-scale rotation-equivariant features via multi-head attention with positional encoding, and introducing a directional uncertainty weighted descriptor loss. The method is evaluated on rotated-HPatches, rotated-MegaDepth, and rotated-YFCC100M datasets. Reviewers note the importance of the problem and the novelty of combining rotation equivariance with position embeddings, but raise significant concerns about missing ablation studies, incomplete comparisons to SOTA methods and matchers, and insufficient justification of performance gains.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses a crucial problem in computer vision: extracting rotation-equivariant local features (keypoints, descriptors), with broad applications beyond image matching. (R1, R2, R4)
- The idea of combining rotation-equivariant local descriptors with global positional embeddings is a reasonable and novel fusion of two explored ideas into a single solution. (R1, R2)
- Using multi-scale rotation-equivariant feature fusion and a directional uncertainty weighted descriptor loss (PCA-based shift) is sound methodology. (R3, R4)
- The paper is generally well-written; figures clearly illustrate the framework. (R3)
- Experimental results on rotated datasets (e.g., Figure 5) show improvement over some descriptors, and most real-world dataset results are positive. (R2)
- The method is compared with mainstream descriptors and achieves state-of-the-art on the tested benchmarks. (R3)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing ablation analysis:** All four reviewers noted the absence of ablation studies. The paper introduces two major components (rotation-equivariant convolution + positional encoding) but does not isolate their individual contributions. It is unclear whether performance gains come from the specific design or simply from increased model complexity. Ablation of fusion strategies, position encoding methods, number of pyramid levels, and the loss function is needed. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient experimental comparison and justification:**
  - Reviewer 2 strongly criticized the evaluation: The paper claims to address SOTA descriptors (AWDesc, SuperGlue, LoFTR) but only compares to AWDesc; SuperGlue, LoFTR, and widely used classical or learnable matchers (RANSAC variants like MAGSAC, AdaLAM; deep matchers like SuperGlue, ACNe, NG-RANSAC) are not compared. Dense matching (LoFTR) is not discussed. Many influential descriptors (Superpoint, D2-Net, R2D2) are missing.
  - Reviewer 2 further notes that the proposed method loses to the 2011 method BRISK in Table 1, which undermines the claim of solving rotation equivariance; the authors’ explanation (planar dataset) was deemed unconvincing.
  - Reviewer 1 noted that without ablation one cannot tell if the extra improvement comes from positional embedding rather than equivariance, and that such embedding could be injected during matching rather than in the descriptor.
- **Efficiency and practical applicability not addressed:** Reviewer 3 points out that runtime is not reported; a breakdown of descriptor extraction (feature extraction, position encoding, fusion) is needed to assess suitability for real-time applications.
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Too much introductory material (e.g., explaining FPN, multi-head attention) that dilutes the contribution. (R1)
  - Typographical errors: “are feed” instead of “are fed” in Figure 2 caption. (R3)
  - Unclear or insufficient figure/table captions (Figures 3,4,5; Tables 1,2,3). (R4)
  - Section organization could be improved: subheadings in Section 3 should follow a more natural flow (extraction before fusion). (R4)
- **Missing citations and related work discussion:**
  - Reviewer 4 identifies missing citations: a learning-based rotation-equivariant keypoint detector (Lee et al., CVPR 2022); the PCA-shift loss should cite Lee et al. (BMVC 2021) and Yan et al. (CVPR 2022).
  - Related work section does not adequately discuss similarities/differences with prior papers. (R4)
  - Incorrect citation formatting (citation in the middle of a sentence makes it incomplete). (R4)
  - Illumination invariance is mentioned in the introduction but not addressed by the paper. (R4)
  - Citation examples given for image matching/3D reconstruction are from an image retrieval paper; additional citations are needed. (R4)
- **Dataset description:** No separate section explaining the MegaDepth-series and YFCC100M datasets. (R4)
