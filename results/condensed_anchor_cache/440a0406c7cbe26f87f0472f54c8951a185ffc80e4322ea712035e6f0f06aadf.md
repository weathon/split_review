## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a causal-invariant Bayesian (CIB) neural network for out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. The architecture combines a variational encoder, an inference module, and a partially stochastic Bayesian neural network. An intervention mechanism is introduced by leveraging contextual information from the dataset via Mixup strategies. The method is shown theoretically to approximate reasoning under causal interventions. Experiments on CIFAR10 and OfficeHome report improved OOD performance over point-estimate counterparts. The paper provides an anonymized code repository.

### Strengths

* The idea of exploiting causal mechanism invariance to improve out-of-distribution generalization is interesting. [Reviewer 3]
* Applying causal interventions in a Bayesian inference pipeline and showing improvements over point-estimate networks is novel. [Reviewer 2; note: two other reviewers contest novelty.]
* Another novel aspect is considering the dataset in the causal graph, deriving a theoretical result, and executing it with partially stochastic networks. [Reviewer 2]
* The paper provides a methodology to learn causal representations in an unsupervised manner. [Reviewer 2]
* The underlying concepts for Bayesian networks and causality are adequately explained. [Reviewer 2]
* The formulations are clearly presented and illustrated with theoretical and empirical evidence. [Reviewer 3]
* The method outperforms the VAE on two image classification datasets (CIFAR10, OfficeHome). [Reviewer 1]
* The authors provide an anonymized code repository for review. [Reviewer 2]

### Weaknesses

**Novelty and Positioning:**

* Using causality or Bayesian methods for domain generalization is not new; prior works have investigated these directions (e.g., Lv et al. 2022, Ouyang et al. 2023 – causality-inspired; Xiao et al. 2021 – Bayesian for domain-invariant features). [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
* The paper does not sufficiently discuss related works or compare against them. Missing references include [1]-[5] (Lv et al., Ouyang et al., Xiao et al., Derakhshani et al., Yu et al.). [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
* One reviewer (2) found the intervention-in-Bayesian-pipeline novel, but two reviewers (1,3) consider the approach an integration of existing techniques without clear novelty. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
* The proposed architecture appears to be a combination of existing methods; the novel aspects beyond integration are not clarified. [Reviewer 3]

**Motivation and Clarifications:**

* The motivation for using Bayesian neural networks for disentanglement is unclear. Rationale for combining domain-invariant and specific parts during inference should be elaborated. [Reviewer 1]

**Experiments and Comparisons:**

* Comparison methods are insufficient. Only a single baseline (VAE) is compared; results in Table 1 do not even convincingly outperform the vanilla baseline. The method should be compared against all causally-inspired and Bayesian domain generalization methods (e.g., Lv et al., Ouyang et al., Xiao et al., Derakhshani et al., Yu et al.). [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
* Only two simple datasets are evaluated (CIFAR10 and OfficeHome). The widely used DomainBed benchmark (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, Terra, DomainNet) should be included. [Reviewer 1]
* The evaluation is limited to relatively simple transformations (e.g., translation on CIFAR10). More complex domain shifts (object pose, illumination, partial occlusions, different backgrounds) are not explored, which limits evidence for robustness. [Reviewer 2]
* The method is only tested on image classification with CNN backbones. Experiments on other architectures (e.g., ViT-B-16) and tasks (e.g., time series) are missing. [Reviewer 1]

**Ablations and Analysis:**

* Missing ablation studies for the KL divergence term on weights to demonstrate the necessity of the Bayesian component. [Reviewer 1]
* It is unclear how much of the performance improvement is due to the proposed intervention design versus simply using Bayesian inference over point-estimate methods. [Reviewer 2]
* Visualizations of learned representations (e.g., t-SNE plots) showing how the method clusters data from different domains and how interventions affect them are missing; this would help understand the domain gap and analyze improvements. [Reviewer 2]

**Method and Implementation Details:**

* The training and evaluation algorithm is described without sufficient clarity: how the theoretical result is translated into a practical training algorithm, how the intervention is implemented during training, and how the partially stochastic network is optimized are not clearly explained. [Reviewer 2]
* The trainer.py file in the anonymized code repository is empty, hindering reproducibility. [Reviewer 2]
* Without a reconstruction loss, it is unclear how the method ensures that no domain-invariant information is lost during disentanglement. [Reviewer 1]
* The objective function contains two KL terms; computational complexity (e.g., runtime or FLOPs) should be reported in a table. [Reviewer 1]
* The choice of hyperparameters (e.g., weighting coefficients for each term) is not explained. [Reviewer 1]
