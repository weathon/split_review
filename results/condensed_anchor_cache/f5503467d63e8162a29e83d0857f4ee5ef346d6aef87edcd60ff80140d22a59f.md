## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies piecewise-stationary multi-armed bandits where the number of change points \(M\) is unknown. It proposes a “diminishing exploration” (DE) mechanism that decays forced exploration over time. DE can be combined with any change-detection-based algorithm (e.g., M-UCB, GLR-UCB, CUSUM-UCB) without requiring prior knowledge of \(M\). The authors derive a regret bound of \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{MKT})\) and claim \(\mathcal{O}(KT)\) computational complexity. A heuristic skipping mechanism (Algorithm 2) is added to ignore potentially unnecessary arms. Simulations on synthetic data and a Yahoo dataset show that DE-equipped algorithms often outperform their uniform-exploration counterparts.

### Strengths
- Nonstationarity is an important real-world challenge; the paper targets the “active methods” line of work and removes the restrictive assumption that \(M\) is known a priori.
- The diminishing exploration idea is novel in this setting, does not require knowledge of total variation, and can be paired with a range of existing change-detection algorithms.
- The theoretical regret bound \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{MKT})\) is provided with clear explanations of the theorems; the bound is near-optimal.
- Comparison table (Table 1) helps situate the contribution relative to prior work.
- The algorithm is easy to implement and computationally lightweight.
- Extensive numerical experiments on synthetic and real data show that DE can significantly reduce regret compared to baselines.
- The paper is well-written and clearly presents the main ideas (majority view; one reviewer found the writing not smooth – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty concerns and relation to prior work**:
   - Wei & Luo (2021) already studied the setting without knowledge of \(M\) and achieved optimal regret with \(\mathcal{O}(KT)\) complexity, undermining the claimed novelty. Aside from changing the exploration rate, it is unclear what other novelties are introduced. (Reviewers 2, 3)
   - *Minority positive*: One reviewer calls the work “authentic and original” but simultaneously gives the lowest contribution score (1), suggesting internal inconsistency. (Reviewer 4)

2. **Restrictive or poorly justified assumptions**:
   - The piecewise-stationary assumption is not justified; real-world nonstationarity may involve gradual drifts or more complex patterns, and the paper does not discuss this limitation. (Reviewer 1)
   - Assumption 4.2 appears unnatural and unnecessary; can the regret bounds be derived without it? (Reviewer 2)
   - The typical minimum-distance-between-change-points assumption conflicts with decreasing exploration: as time from the last change increases, the probability of a new change rises, yet the algorithm explores less. This logical contradiction is not addressed. (Reviewer 4)

3. **Missing ablations and parameter guidance**:
   - The skipping mechanism (Algorithm 2) is a heuristic; no ablation studies show its impact on regret or computational cost, and the tuning parameter \(\eta\) lacks guidance. (Reviewer 1)
   - The key parameter \(\alpha\) is never explicitly stated; the regret bound depends on it, making it hard to deploy the algorithm without additional tuning. (Reviewer 3)

4. **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
   - Empirical comparisons should include algorithms designed for *general* nonstationarity (sliding window, adaptive learning rates) to assess performance beyond the piecewise-stationary setting. (Reviewer 1)
   - The setup description is insufficient: parameters for baselines and the proposed method are not clearly stated, and the environment is not detailed enough for reproducibility. (Reviewer 4)
   - Synthetic experiments use a very small number of arms; performance in large-arm scenarios (critical for real-world applications) is not explored. (Reviewer 4)
   - GLR-UCB and CUSUM-UCB with and without DE perform very similarly on the Yahoo dataset as \(T\) scales, yet no explanation is given for why DE does not yield larger improvements in these cases. (Reviewer 1)

5. **Missing justifications and unaddressed questions**:
   - The claim of \(\mathcal{O}(KT)\) computational complexity in Table 1 lacks a formal statement or proof; further demonstration is needed. (Reviewer 3)
   - The base algorithms M-UCB and GLR-UCB are not described; what does “M” stand for? (Reviewer 1)
   - Why not consider the total-variation (TV) line of work in nonstationary bandits? (Reviewer 3)
   - Experiments state that evaluations beyond 20,000 timesteps are infeasible due to runtime, yet Figure 4d appears to show \(T=200,000\). (Reviewer 1)
   - What real-world application motivates reducing forced exploration? If the answer is “large number of arms,” then why are experiments limited to few arms? (Reviewer 4)

6. **Presentation quality dissent**: One reviewer finds the writing not smooth and in need of improvement, contrasting with the majority view that the paper is clearly written. (Reviewer 4)
