## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SOPHY, a new benchmark for soft-body physical reasoning from videos, comprising four simulated task types (rope, cloth, fluid, ball) and associated question-answering pairs. The authors evaluate several state-of-the-art visual and language models and find they still fall short of human performance. Reviewer opinions vary: some find the benchmark technically novel and valuable (reviewer 1, rating 6; reviewer 2, rating 6; reviewer 4, rating 5), while reviewer 3 (rating 3) questions the depth of analysis and conceptual consistency, and reviewer 4 raises serious concerns about the definition of “soft body” and the physical validity of the chosen properties. All reviewers agree the problem is interesting, but disagree on the quality of implementation and evaluation.

### Strengths
- The paper targets an underexplored problem – physical reasoning for soft objects – and the motivation for bridging the gap between human and AI in this domain is sound (reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The benchmark covers four distinct soft-body scenarios (rope, cloth, fluid, ball) that are lacking in existing datasets, adding value to the community (reviewers 1, 3).
- The task and question-answer pair generation is technically novel, with careful design of environment and templates (reviewer 1).
- The render quality is decent, aiding visual clarity and interpretation (reviewer 3).
- The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation of several baselines, including human performance, random/frequent answers, non-visual models, and visual models such as CNN and MAC, with useful analysis (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (reviewer 4; reviewer 1 rated presentation 4/4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope and extensibility**: The benchmark is restricted to four task types and pre-defined question templates, making it unclear how to extend to other tasks, environments, or free-form questions (reviewer 1).
- **Conceptual confusion about “soft body”**: The paper treats liquids (fluid) as a soft body, which contradicts standard definitions (e.g., Wikipedia: soft-body dynamics applies to solid objects). This is a fundamental physics concept mistake (reviewer 4).  
  - A related question from reviewer 3: “Should tasks related to reasoning on liquids and soft objects be evaluated in different ways? The comprehension of physics for soft objects and liquids may represent divergent capabilities.”
- **Incoherent physical properties**: The listed properties – mass, friction, elasticity, density, deformability, stretchiness – are not at the same level. Elasticity and stretchiness are both forms of deformability; stretchiness and deformability lack real-world measurability. Other important properties such as plasticity and viscosity are omitted (reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient dataset size**: With only 500 videos, the dataset does not cover the infinite possible deformations of soft bodies and may lack variance in scene setup (reviewer 4).
- **Unsupported or incomplete analysis**:
  - In Section 4.2 “Physical Property”, the claim that ALPRO performs best in rope and stays competitive elsewhere is not discussed – why do other models slightly outperform it in non-rope scenarios? Is it because of scenario-specific biases or ALPRO’s limitations? (reviewer 3)
  - In Section 4.2 “Dynamics”, only ALPRO and HCRN are discussed; the failure of other models (whether due to temporal modeling or visual feature extraction) is not explained (reviewer 3).
  - In Section 4.2 “Scenario Analysis”, only cloth and rope are discussed; fluid and ball scenarios are omitted, preventing a full understanding of model generalization (reviewer 3).
- **Lack of separation between physical reasoning and language understanding**: The conclusion that “AI models struggle with answering physical questions” conflates physical reasoning with question comprehension, because the evaluation does not isolate language understanding from physics ability (reviewer 3).
- **Redundant conclusion**: Previous work (e.g., [1]) already established that AI models lack physical reasoning; the paper’s claim that “they struggle on our benchmark” adds no new insight (reviewer 3).
- **Questionable claim about prior benchmarks**: The paper states that previous benchmarks cannot change mass and friction, but many are based on physical simulators and could do so; this claim needs justification (reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues**: Figure pie charts (Fig 3, 5-7) lack precise ratio numbers and consistent labeling (e.g., “Mass”, “Mass Change”, “Mass Goal” appear but no “Shape”, “Shape Change” or “Tension” equivalents) (reviewer 4). Also a writing error: Section 4.2 references “Table 1” when it should be “Table 2” (reviewer 3).
- **No proposed solution**: The paper does not suggest how to improve performance based on the findings (reviewer 1).
- **Generalization to real-world unknown**: It remains unclear whether models trained on SOPHY would transfer to real-world videos, and the paper does not discuss a path toward real-world physical reasoning (reviewer 4).
