## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DisentangledSSL, a self-supervised approach that disentangles shared and modality-specific representations in multimodal data using a conditional entropy bottleneck (CEB). Theoretical analysis covers optimality when the Minimum Necessary Information (MNI) point is unattainable. Experiments on synthetic data, vision-language prediction (MultiBench), and molecule-phenotype retrieval show competitive performance.

### Strengths
- Novel perspective: disentangling modality-specific and shared information into separate representations is original and effective; the application of CEB to multimodal self-supervised learning is novel.
- Rigorous information-theoretic framework with extensive theoretical proofs for optimality of both representations, specifically handling cases where MNI is unattainable.
- Comprehensive evaluation on synthetic and real-world datasets (vision-language, molecular-phenotype) across multiple tasks, with top performance in almost all tasks; experiment details are provided in the appendix for reproducibility.
- The two-step training algorithm is supported by an optimality guarantee.

### Weaknesses
- **Foundational assumptions questioned (Reviewer 3, high confidence):**  
  – “Shared information between modalities is precisely what is relevant for downstream tasks” is not universally accurate; task relevance depends on scenario. Shared information is vague across modalities—e.g., in a dog breed identification task, visual features (ear shape, fur pattern) and textual features (breed name, temperament) are modality‑specific yet critical. The paper does not address such scenarios.  
  – The claim that the modality gap causes misalignment oversimplifies; other factors (alignment methods, distributional shifts, domain shifts, sampling biases) also contribute. Their interaction with the proposed method should be discussed.  
  – **Graphical model flaws:** Figure 2 conflates generative and inference processes (the X → \hat{Z} path is inference, not generative). This is easily fixed by removing it or using separate diagrams. More severely: the assumption of a single shared latent variable \(Z_c\) between two modalities may not hold; separate but connected variables \(Z_c^1, Z_c^2\) (causal or common cause) is more realistic. This undermines the justification. Implications of using separate latent variables should be analyzed.

- **Missing experiments and ablations:**  
  – Synthetic experiments cover only unattainable MNI; attainable MNI experiments (e.g., adding Gaussian noise with varying variances) are needed to demonstrate the bound’s reliability (Reviewer 1).  
  – No comparison with related methods CoCoNet and SimMMDG, which also separate shared/modality‑specific information. SimMMDG could be adapted to self‑supervised learning; an experimental comparison is missing (Reviewer 4).  
  – Ablation studies on hyperparameters \(\beta\) and \(\lambda\) in MultiBench datasets are absent; sensitivity studies are needed to validate the importance of separating the two types of information (Reviewer 4).

- **Performance concerns and instability:**  
  – In Figure 4(b), the superiority over JointOPT is not significant; the JointOPT hyperparameter front is very close (Reviewer 1).  
  – Table 1: the three versions (shared, specific, both) are unstable—e.g., DisentangledSSL‑shared performs well on MOSI but fails on MUSTARD. The paper should explain this behavior (Reviewer 1).  
  – In Table 2, performance gains are quite limited. Clarify whether small improvements are meaningful in this domain and provide reasoning for the small margin (Reviewer 1).  
  – Although DisentangledSSL‑both is best in most tasks, there are cases where shared or specific alone top (e.g., MUSTARD, by a large margin). Provide guidelines for when to use each configuration (Reviewer 2).

- **Theoretical and exposition gaps:**  
  – Transition from Eq. (1) to Eq. (3) is unclear: why \(I(X^1;X^2)\) is omitted; a detailed explanation of how it is treated as a constant with respect to \(Z^1\) is needed (Reviewer 4).  
  – The heavy use of information‑theoretic notation makes the method hard to read for those not interested in proofs; include a pseudocode or algorithm block in the main text or appendix to clarify training steps (Reviewer 2).

- **Technical errors:**  
  – Typos: In Eq. (6) and the line below, \(Z^2\) should be \(X^2\); in Figure 3, \(I(X^1;Z^1)\) should be \(I(X^1;X^2)\) (Reviewer 4).  
  – The paper does not satisfy the page limit (Reviewer 1).

- **Questions:**  
  – Are there alternative implementations for computing \(I(Z^1;\hat{Z}_c^{1*})\) in \(\mathcal{L}_s^1\)? How does their performance compare to the orthogonal loss? (Reviewer 4)  
  – (Reviewer 2’s question above on configuration guidelines is already included in performance concerns.)
