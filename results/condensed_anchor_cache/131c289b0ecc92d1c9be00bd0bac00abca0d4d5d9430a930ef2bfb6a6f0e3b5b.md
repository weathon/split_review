## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers describe PRISM as a federated learning framework for generative models that uses the strong lottery ticket hypothesis to learn stochastic binary masks over a frozen random initialization, communicated instead of full weights, combined with maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) loss and a mask-aware dynamic moving average aggregation (MADA) to handle data heterogeneity. Experiments on MNIST, FMNIST, CelebA, and CIFAR10 show strong gains in generative performance, communication efficiency, and memory savings, with differential privacy optionally applied. The paper is generally well-written and clearly presented, but there is substantial disagreement about novelty (some see it as a straightforward combination of existing techniques), and multiple reviewers question the experimental setup, scalability, and completeness of cost analysis.

### Strengths
- **Novelty (mixed views):** R1 considers the algorithm “novel for learning the binary mask in the SLT hypothesis under FL” and R2 sees a “degree of innovation” in incorporating SLT and MMD into FL. R3 and R4, however, view it as a simple application of FedMask combined with MMD loss and aggregation (citing Refs [1-2]), so novelty is debated.
- **Strong empirical results:** PRISM outperforms all baselines by a good margin while reducing communication and memory costs (R3: “strongest aspect are the empirical results”; R1: “considerable gain compared to the baseline approaches”).
- **Well-justified components:** MMD loss and MADA are well motivated for addressing data heterogeneity and client drift (R1, R3). MADA is described as an interesting idea that improves performance empirically.
- **Privacy preservation:** PRISM introduces an (ε, δ)-differential privacy mechanism to alleviate privacy leakage (R2).
- **Clear presentation:** Writing quality, diagrams (especially Figure 1), and organization of results (tables and figures) are praised as effective and helpful (R1, R3, R4). The related works are well composed (R4).
- **Experimental scope:** Experiments cover IID, non-IID, and privacy vs. no-privacy settings (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and tailoring to generative models:** Several reviewers note that PRISM essentially combines FedMask (mask communication) with MMD loss (used in GANs) and MADA, each existing individually, and that the process could be applied to any ML model by substituting the loss (R3: “I do not see how PRISM is tailored towards generative models”; R4: “central methodology appears to be just a combination of [1-2]” – Sparse Random Networks for FL and Strong Lottery Tickets in Generative Models).
- **Incomplete communication and computational cost analysis:** 
   - Downlink communication cost is not reduced: the server must send weight-importance scores (potentially as large as full model) to all clients (R3).
   - No analysis of the computational cost (time and memory) of generating and applying the random binary masks, especially for large networks (R2).
- **Scalability concerns:** The paper lacks experiments on large-scale or high-resolution datasets (e.g., ImageNet subsets) or larger models, making it difficult to assess practical applicability (R2, R4). R2 explicitly requests such experiments.
- **Questionable non-IID experimental setup:**
   - Partitioning is done by splitting datasets into 40 segments based on class labels and randomly assigning four per client, rather than a Dirichlet/LDA split that is more standard (R3, R4).
   - Performance sometimes *improves* in non-IID settings (e.g., CelebA), which is counterintuitive and suggests the non-IID split is not severe enough (R3).
   - Only 10 clients with 100% participation are used, which is more representative of cross-silo than the cross-device scenario implied by the paper’s resource-efficiency motivation. For a realistic cross-device setting, many more clients with low participation would be needed (R4).
- **Datasets and results not sufficiently challenging:**
   - MNIST digits generated by PRISM (Figs. 2/3) look problematic even for simple models; the IID scenario should be easily mastered (R4).
   - CIFAR10 and CelebA are not “complex” enough to support claims of “state-of-the-art image generation” (R4). With no theoretical results, the empirical contribution on these datasets is considered insufficient (R4). R1 also questions whether GANs on simple datasets still represent a significant result for the community.
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:**
   - No comparison with a purely server-side (non-federated) SLT baseline (R1 question).
   - No comparison with centralized training, which is easily achievable for these datasets (R4).
   - The paper would benefit from comparison with diffusion-based generative models in FL (R1 question).
- **Missing details:**
   - Architecture specification is not described in the paper text; readers must consult the code (R4).
   - Hyperparameter tuning procedures are not discussed (R4).
   - The source of MADA is unclear – is it proposed here or derived from other literature? (R3 question).
   - Clarification needed on model memory savings (Line 242): are memory savings from storing only the sign of frozen weights? (R3).
   - Explanation of λ decay (Line 276) – is it tied to learning rate decay? (R3).
- **Practical applicability and robustness:**
   - No discussion of an industrial or academic setting where PRISM would be deployed (R4 question).
   - Robustness to random seed not evaluated (R4 question).
   - The paper lacks theoretical results; given the limited experimental difficulty, this is a weakness (R4).
