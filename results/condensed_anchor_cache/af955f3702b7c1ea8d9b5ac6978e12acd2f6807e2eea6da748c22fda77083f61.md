## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper connects Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD), a numerical method for Koopman theory. The authors propose DMD-GNN models that use low-rank eigenfunctions from DMD to approximate the dynamics of feature propagation on graphs. They provide theoretical analysis linking the DMD-estimated operator to the original dynamic operator and present extensive experiments on node classification, link prediction, long-range interactions, spatial-temporal graphs, and large-scale graphs (OGB-ArXiv). The DMD-GNN models achieve competitive or state-of-the-art results across multiple tasks. Reviewers recognize the novelty of bridging GNNs and DMD, but express concerns about clarity of motivation, implementation details, baseline choices, and the strength of the performance gains.

### Strengths
- **Novel perspective**: Connecting DMD (Koopman theory) with GNNs is interesting and original (R2, R3, R5). The idea of using DMD to capture dynamic patterns in graph data is potentially important for understanding complex network behaviors (R1). The work lies at the intersection of two typically distinct fields (R3).
- **Well-written and structured**: The paper is generally well written and easy to follow, with clear background on GNNs and DMD (R2, R3, R5). Section titles (e.g., “How GNNs Resonate with Dynamic Systems”) help guide the reader (R3). High-level layout is appreciated (R3).
- **Extensive experiments**: The authors validate DMD-GNN on directed graphs, large-scale graphs, long-range interactions, spatial-temporal graphs, and link prediction (R2, R5). The experiments are thorough and support the claims (R2, R5). The integration of DMD with a learning architecture is considered mostly original (R3).
- **Theoretical and practical grounding**: Section 3 (Koopman theory) and Section 4 (DMD basics) are well explained (R5). The paper provides enough derivation to explain the proposed model (R4). The results show that refined features improve GNN performance, opening the door to physics-informed or biologically-plausible systems (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation and mechanism**: Multiple reviewers find the motivation for using DMD in GNNs insufficiently articulated. It is unclear to what extent GNN dynamics actually influence performance, and what specific insights DMD modes provide beyond a generic low-rank approximation (R2). The paper does not explain how DMD modes refine feature propagation or which properties of these modes lead to performance gains (R4). The connection between the identified DMD modes and the actual behavior of GNNs is not demonstrated (R2).
- **Lack of algorithm/pseudocode and model clarity**: The model implementation is poorly described. Standard DMD requires explicit eigendecomposition, but how $\Psi$ and $\theta$ are determined in Eq. (14) is unclear (R2). The summary of the model procedure (Lines 291–293) is unclear (R1). No pseudocode or detailed algorithmic description is provided (R4, R5). Figure 1 is confusing as an illustration: it does not show where the DMD module is applied (R4), and a more detailed description would help (R5). For non-experts in both fields, the derivations in Sections 5 and 6 are hard to follow (R3). An additional intuitive figure outlining the arguments of Section 4 is needed (R3).
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons**: Some baselines are relatively weak. For long-range graph datasets, baseline models that explicitly address over-squashing or bottlenecks (e.g., spectral rewiring, curvature-based methods) are missing (R2). The model is not compared to GIN, which is maximally expressive (R5). APPNP is not cited or explained (R5). In Table 1, some best results are close to simple MLP performance, making the improvements less convincing (R1). The claim that DMD-GNNs “outperform the baseline models across the majority of datasets” (Line 425–426) is too strong, as most improvements are minor and/or not significant (R5). ACMP is listed as a baseline in the text (Line 411) but does not appear in the tables (R5). For link prediction, VGAE is used instead of the state-of-the-art SEAL framework (R5). ChebNet is only used for spatial-temporal dynamics without justification (R5).
- **Missing analysis on frequency, graph subclasses, and scalability**: The relationship between DMD modes and frequency-based GNNs is only briefly mentioned in Section 6; a detailed comparison on specific datasets is needed (R2). The analysis on graph subclasses (sparse, dense, small-world, etc.) is insufficient (R1). Scalability claims are weak: using $A^2$ (power s=2) requires storing a potentially dense matrix, which is prohibitive for large graphs (R3). The repeated matrix multiplications in DMD updates, even with low-rank approximations, pose computational challenges if rank $r$ is not sufficiently small relative to the number of nodes. No runtime or memory figures are reported (R3). The authors should include actual runtime plots/numbers to give a sense of the cost (R3).
- **Theoretical and equation issues**: The main proof is very similar to the proof in “Data-Driven Linearization of Dynamical Systems” (R1). The equation in Line 133 is likely wrong (R5). Line 237 states $K = M \cdot F$, implying $M=1$, which is probably a mistake (R5). There are ambiguities: Eq. (12) vs Line 237 regarding K, what $W^{(l)}$ is in Eq. (14), the usage of “rate” in Line 280, what $Df(0)$ is in Eq. (15), and whether $X$ in Lemma 1 should be $X^{(l)}$ (R1). The pseudo-inverse of $H^{(l)}$ was already defined in Line 227 but not used in Eq. (11) (R5). The sentence on attention-based GNNs (Lines 90–93) is confusing (R5).
- **Missing details in experiments**: For node classification, the number of hidden nodes used in baselines is not stated; a comparison of model parameters is missing (R5). For link prediction, average accuracy is used instead of ROC-AUC; why? (R5). The sensitivity analysis for homophilic graphs uses $\xi=0.85$ but only goes up to $\xi=0.8$ (R5). Why was Euler discretization used? Would other discretizations be interesting? (R5). The results of Appendix C.2.4 should be briefly mentioned in Section 7.2 (R5). Table 3 shows that DMD-GCN does not improve performance on all datasets; further explanation of which features help or harm the model is needed (R4). The ranks used in different datasets are not specified; how is the number of filters decided? (R4).
- **Other issues**: The related work section is very general; differentiation from previous work is unclear (R5). The Koopman theorem states that a linear process can be found in infinite-dimensional space, but DMD approximates in finite dimensions, potentially ignoring information. Will this affect performance on more complex datasets? (R4). The paper does not discuss the model’s potential and challenges in specific fields (e.g., bioinformatics, social network analysis) (R1). Minor issues: Figure 2 is too small and should be divided into (a) and (b) (R5). Spelling/grammar errors: Line 146 (underlying), heading “Resonate” not “Resonate with,” Table 1 caption missing period (R5). Missing abbreviation definition: KNN in Line 466 (R5). Line 523–524 has a grammatical error (R5).
