## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies federated offline policy learning from heterogeneous observational bandit data across multiple clients. The proposed algorithm combines federated averaging (FedAvg) with cost-sensitive multi-class classification (CSMC) oracles for local policy updates, using doubly robust policy value estimation. The authors provide finite-sample upper bounds on global regret (against a mixture distribution of clients) and local regret (characterized by distribution shift to other clients). Experimental results on simulated data demonstrate regret dependence on client heterogeneity.

### Strengths
- The problem of offline policy learning under heterogeneous data sources with federation is well-motivated and practically significant (R1, R4). This is considered the first work on off-policy learning in a federated setting (R1).
- The paper is clearly written, with well-defined assumptions, detailed descriptions of the algorithm, and thorough regret analysis (R1, R2, R4). Definitions are introduced without ambiguity; presentation is excellent (R4).
- Theoretical analysis provides both global and local regret upper bounds, capturing the effect of client skewness and heterogeneity (R2, R3). The local regret analysis complements the global bound and highlights discrepancies due to client heterogeneity (R2).
- Experimental results demonstrate the impact of client heterogeneity on federated policy learning and suggest a skewed mixture for global policy training to mitigate distribution shift (R3).
- Quality is good: theoretical results appear solid, experimental details are provided (R4).
- The originality lies in considering federation under this specific problem setting (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The algorithm is a straightforward application of FedAvg to the CSMC setting (R1, R2, R3). The paper does not clearly articulate unique technical challenges encountered when extending offline policy learning to the federated setting, nor specific novel techniques in the regret analysis beyond standard distributed optimization (R1, R2). The regret bound seems to follow standard analysis without novel insights from heterogeneity (R1). Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) request a clearer explanation of the technical contributions and how heterogeneity impacts regret analysis.
- **Optimization assumption of exact maximizer**: The analysis assumes access to the exact maximizer of the doubly robust objective, which is unrealistic under federated settings with non-concave, non-convex objectives. The paper does not discuss how the regret bounds would change if the optimization procedure only converges to an approximate solution (R1, R3). The claim that optimal policies are achievable "via some additive term and appropriate policy class selection" is vague and lacks rigorous justification (R3).
- **Strong assumptions**: Several assumptions are detailed but not adequately discussed. The local ignorability assumption (treatment assignment independent of potential outcomes given covariates) is strong and may not hold in real-world scenarios; implications of violations are not discussed (R2). The overlap assumption (non-zero treatment probability for each covariate) is strong, especially in high-dimensional spaces (R2). Assumption 1-(c) seems to necessitate full exploration of all actions, which contradicts some offline RL literature showing full coverage may not be necessary; the paper does not justify why this is needed in the federated setting or discuss impact on applicability (R3). The paper mentions some assumptions can be relaxed but lacks details (R2).
- **Limited empirical evaluation**: Experiments are conducted only on simulated data with a simple heterogeneous setting, limiting generalizability (R2, R3). No comparisons with other baseline algorithms are provided—only a comparison with a non-federated approach (R2). Missing comparisons include: centralized approach where data is pooled, other federated learning algorithms adapted to policy learning, or methods for handling heterogeneous data sources (R2, R3). The paper should include experiments on real-world datasets and explore more complex heterogeneous settings (R2, R3).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Can nuisance parameters be learnt jointly rather than required to be separately known or estimated? (R2)
  - How does heterogeneity impact convergence compared to standard supervised learning tasks? Are there specific challenges related to the two-stage process of estimating nuisance functions in a federated setting? (R3)
  - What are the properties of the policy class that guarantee convergence to the optimal policy? How does the choice of policy class interact with the federated nature? (R3)
  - How to assess the plausibility of assumptions like local ignorability and overlap in practice? (R2)
- **Minor issues**: Notation like \(X^{C}, Y^{C}\) not formally defined (R1). The paper would benefit from adding a proof sketch for main results (R4).
