## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies in-context learning (ICL) for linear regression by training transformers from scratch and testing out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. It replicates and extends Garg et al.’s experiments across different model scales (e.g., 9 layers, 6 attention heads) and distributions of weights/inputs. The paper argues that transformers do not learn a general algorithm because they systematically fail to generalize to OOD settings (e.g., different ranges/variances). Reviewers note the work highlights important limitations but lacks a convincing explanation for what is learned and why it fails.

### Strengths
- Focuses on an important and challenging problem: understanding the capabilities and limitations of in-context learning (R1, R4).
- Conducts thorough experiments across multiple transformer sizes and a variety of OOD settings (R2, R3).
- Clear motivation for the study (R3).
- Writing is clear and easy to understand (one reviewer’s positive view; R4).
- Code and detailed reproduction instructions are provided (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is limited. The central observation (transformers fail to generalize OOD for linear regression) has been noted in prior work (e.g., Figures 5,6 of [1] cited by R1; similar conclusions in R4’s references [1] and [2]). The paper does not propose methods to improve the problem (R3, R4). The related work section misses several relevant empirical and theoretical studies on ICL for regression (R2, R4).
- The paper does not convincingly explain what the models actually learn or why they fail OOD. R1 suggests the models might be learning a preconditioned gradient descent that is optimal for the in-distribution but suboptimal OOD, yet this is not explored. R2 notes the hypothesis about induction heads is interesting but lacks theoretical or experimental support (e.g., mechanistic interpretation). R3 also asks for an explanation of the failures. (R1, R2, R3)
- The paper does not rule out that the training procedure is suboptimal. Transformers have the expressivity to represent algorithms, and better training (e.g., using multiple distributions, hyperparameter search) might enable OOD generalization. R1 explicitly asks whether the authors tried training with multiple distributions and performed hyperparameter search. (R1)
- Writing and presentation issues. Several typos exist (e.g., line 047 missing period; other errors). Notation is unclear in places (e.g., what $\sigma$ represents in $f_{i,\sigma}$; “9L6AH” is non-standard; $D^T_F$ vs $D^t_F$). The claim in line 190 (coefficients in $[-1,1]$ vs $N(0,1)$) is unclear and lacks a supporting figure for zero MSE. The organization is informal: experiments are presented sequentially without clear motivations or connections; mixing different model scales and distributions makes it hard to extract the main conclusions (R2, R3). One reviewer found the writing clear; others note these issues.
- The empirical study uses small transformers trained from scratch, which may not reflect the ICL behavior of large pretrained LLMs, limiting the generalizability of the claims (R4).
- Missing direct comparison to specific prior works. R4 asks how findings differ from [1] (“Trained Transformers Learn Linear Models In-Context”) and [2] (“Transformers as Statisticians: Provable In-Context Learning with In-Context Algorithm Selection”).
