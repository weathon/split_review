## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the trajectories of token embeddings across transformer layers (the “lines of thought”), finding that independent trajectories cluster along a low-dimensional, non-Euclidean manifold and can be approximated by a stochastic equation with few parameters. The authors propose a Langevin dynamics model with linear drift. Reviewers find the direction interesting and the perspective novel, but they raise significant concerns about motivation, organization, experimental validation, and practical utility. A minority reviewer (rating 8) finds strong empirical evidence and a well‐motivated theoretical justification, while the other three (ratings 6) see the paper as vague, lacking concrete contributions, and insufficiently validated.

### Strengths
- The question posed and the resulting findings are very interesting (R1, R3).
- The development of a linear approximation to the distribution of trajectories is appreciated (R1).
- The flow‐based perspective on embedding dynamics is novel and may lead to new interpretability methods (R2).
- Strong evidence is provided to back up the claims: the result is computationally cheap to obtain, model‑agnostic, and empirically backed by measuring the KL divergence of the output distribution after dimensionality reduction via SVD (R4).
- The theoretical justification for the proposed model (Langevin dynamics and the Fokker–Planck flow) is well motivated and explained in the appendix (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and practical relevance**: It is unclear why the reader should care about the findings. The paper lacks concrete takeaways or applications – for example, using the model to predict next tokens, distilling a network into a smaller one for a specific task, or improving LLMs in a measurable way. Multiple reviewers call for a discussion of practical implications (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **No new theory**: The theoretical contributions are limited to standard Langevin dynamics formulations. The projection to lower dimensions and linear approximations are noted to be quite lossy, raising doubts about how well the observations hold in practice (R2). The Gaussian assumption does not hold in early layers (R3).
- **Limited experiments and model generality**: Experiments are only performed on a few models. For Mistral 7B and Llama models, the last layer behaves as an outlier, and it is unclear why this happens or whether the central hypothesis still holds for those layers (R2). There is also no evidence that the same path structure holds for larger or more complex architectures (e.g., LLaMA-3) (R3).
- **Insufficient validation of the predictive model**: The visual comparison of extrapolated vs. actual activations in Figure 3 is insufficient; a more concrete metric (e.g., a baseline for KL divergence, a measure of cluster overlap with transparency, or a task‐level benchmark after projecting away dimensions) is needed. The claim that a KL divergence of ~0.7 is “not much” lacks context – what are the downstream effects of discarding 75% of the activations? (R4) Figure 3 also shows only u2 and u3 without u1, and it is unclear why (R3).
- **Writing, organization, and notation issues**: 
    - The paper is often hard to follow: in Section 3.1 the findings are presented before the methodology; Section 3.2 starts abruptly with “The fast decay of the singular values…”; Section 3.4 is titled “Langevin Dynamics” but the term is not introduced earlier (R1).
    - Algorithm 1 is overkill – caching activations can be described in one sentence (R4).
    - Notation is inconsistent: parentheses vs. square brackets for indexing (e.g., line 145 vs. 155), and the formula in lines 255–256 substitutes one rotation matrix for another but leaves another symbol unsubstituted (R1, R4).
    - The term “pilot” is unclear (R1), and the independence of pseudo‑sentences (line 171) is questionable because they are chunks from a single text (R1).
    - References to many different figures within a single paragraph make reading difficult (R1).
- **Minor issues and specific questions** (treated as weaknesses when they overlap with missing experiments or confusions):
    - The meaning of “the uncertainty (or stochasticity) introduced here accounts only for the loss of information of considering the token without its prompt” (footnote 8) is unclear (R3).
    - The paragraph between lines 195–201 and the one between lines 248–251 need clarification (R1).
    - Is there a connection to neural ODEs? (R2)
    - For Figure 2(c), what precisely is the baseline? The paper says “random text,” but is that the KL divergence between output distributions of two unrelated inputs, or random noise injected into the unembedding matrix, or something else? (R4)
    - A citation is needed if the KL divergence metric after SVD is defined elsewhere (R4).
    - Would a non‑linear manifold (e.g., an autoencoder) allow even lower dimensionality while preserving KL divergence? (R4)
    - Does it make sense to use a different PCA basis at each layer, rather than one basis for the entire trajectory? (R4)
