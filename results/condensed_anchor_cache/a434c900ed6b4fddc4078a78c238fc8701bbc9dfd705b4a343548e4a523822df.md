## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in prompt-based continual learning by exploiting hierarchical taxonomies of labels/concepts and an optimal transport-based method to capture class relationships. The authors introduce a regularization loss to focus on challenging knowledge areas, aiming to improve class discrimination and reduce confusion. Evaluation on Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet benchmarks shows improved accuracy and lower forgetting compared to state-of-the-art methods. Reviewers find the combination of hierarchical taxonomy and optimal transport interesting and the evaluation effective, but raise concerns about clarity, motivation, missing details, and insufficient evidence for key claims.

### Strengths
- The use of hierarchical label/concept taxonomy is novel for continual learning, helping connect old and new classes and improve stability and plasticity (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The proposed regularization loss is novel and encourages focusing on challenging areas (R3).
- Experimental results show significant improvement in accuracy and lower forgetting across multiple benchmarks (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-organized with logical flow (R4).
- The approach efficiently focuses on distinguishing visually and semantically similar classes (R4).
- It demonstrates the importance of organizing data meaningfully (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Writing and presentation: The paper is poorly written with unclear messages and equations; the problem statement is difficult to follow (R1, R2). For example, the denominator in Eq. 1 lacks clear description of X set (R1). The algorithm description is confusing and the logical structure is hard to understand (R2).
- Motivation and connection to catastrophic forgetting: The paper does not clearly articulate how improving class separation within hierarchical groups directly mitigates catastrophic forgetting (R1, R4). Why is separation only enforced within groups and not between groups or for all samples? (R4). There is no direct connection established between the contrastive loss and addressing forgetting, and no citation to support this link (R1).
- Missing details on method components:
    - Memory modality and task description are not specified (R1).
    - Losses and predictions for training/testing are not clearly explained; the energy-based function and why it is not applicable during training are unclear (R1).
    - The construction of the hierarchical tree taxonomy: number of nodes, leaf groups, and how the tree is built per dataset are not described (R2). The method relies on a custom-designed tree per dataset, limiting generalization (R1).
    - Generation of expert knowledge: which GPT version was used and how the prompt was designed (R3).
    - The weight matrix Γ computed via optimal transport: it is unclear whether it is fixed after initial computation or recalculated for new tasks, and how it adapts to shifting distributions in continual learning (R4).
    - Prompt ensemble strategy: the motivation for using prompts from previous tasks to initialize the current prompt is not explained (R2).
- Contradictions and inconsistencies:
    - L227 vs L282: The paper states goals of learning a classifier for hierarchical taxonomy and training backbone for maximum distinguishability, which appear contradictory (R2).
    - The claim that overlap between new and old tasks is a source of forgetting is questioned; it may be severe domain/class shifts instead (R2).
    - There is a large difference in Wasserstein distance magnitude between Table 1 and Figure 6 (R2).
- Missing evidence and visualizations:
    - No visualization (e.g., t-SNE) or quantitative data is provided to support the claim that new classes cause the latent space to become fuller and old class representations to shift (R4).
    - No evidence of "active knowledge transfer" between tasks; no experimental results or metrics demonstrating transfer (R4).
    - Visualizations of the training strategy's impact on model performance and the testing strategy's impact on prediction are missing (R3).
- Missing related work discussion: The introduction should discuss mutual learning/continual learning methods, specifically citing two given papers (R3).
- Minor: There is a typographical issue: “”->“” (R2).
- The method requires strong assumptions about a predefined hierarchical structure per dataset, which limits adaptability to datasets without such a taxonomy (R1).
