## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Policy Disentangled Variational Autoencoder (PDVAE), which extends TD-VAE by introducing a discrete latent code (called “policy”) that separates policy from state. PDVAE learns unsupervised policy representations and environment dynamics conditioned on the policy, enabling generation of videos aligned with a specified policy that can be altered during generation. The method is evaluated on three datasets: Moving MNIST, KTH action dataset, and VizDoom. It is compared against MoCoGAN (with and without labels) and outperforms MoCoGAN without labels in terms of video quality and policy accuracy.

### Strengths
- Novel architecture building on TD-VAE that disentangles policy and state; the derivation introducing the latent variable by decomposing the ELBO is neat. (R1, R3)
- Compelling argument that disentanglement is necessary to generate visual representations of agents with intentionality in dynamic environments. (R1)
- The method is consistently better than MoCoGAN (without labels) in policy accuracy and video quality; importantly, MoCoGAN uses labels while PDVAE does not. (R1, R2)
- The architecture is sophisticated with carefully designed loss, and the smooth transition on change of the policy latent is intriguing. (R3)
- Interesting problem with many potential applications (gaming, movie production, robotics). (R2)
- Writing is clear and easy to follow. (R2, R4)
- Methodology appears sound overall. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation is insufficient for a 2024 submission.** Only three simple benchmarks (Moving MNIST, KTH — limited to walk/run, VizDoom) and a single baseline (MoCoGAN, 2018). Papers from three years ago (e.g., Dreamer, ICLR 2020) use more difficult settings. (R1, R2, R3, R4; R4 is most confident)
- **Missing comparisons with relevant baselines:** Why not compare with TD-VAE (from which the method is built) even if it cannot generate policy-conditioned rollouts? Could TD-VAE be prompted with a demonstration to achieve similar results? (R2) Also missing comparisons with modern text2video diffusion models (e.g., Control-A-Video) that might solve the task zero-shot. (R2)
- **Limited novelty.** The method conditions on discrete actions, not on “policy” in the reinforcement-learning sense (function over actions). This has been achieved by earlier works such as World Models (NIPS 2018) and Trajectron++ (ECCV 2020), which also introduce discrete modes for future modeling. (R4) One reviewer notes the term “policy” could be confusing and suggests clarifying that it is a set of learnable embeddings disentangled from the latent space. (R3)
- **Lack of ablation study.** Despite the sophisticated architecture, no experiments analyze the importance of its components. (R3)
- **Experiments only on toy discrete-action environments.** No evaluation on real videos or continuous action spaces (e.g., driving videos); unclear if the policy extraction module and policy-conditioned dynamics model generalize. (R2, R4)
- **Results are not very strong overall** (though reasonable and well-motivated). (R1)
- **Writing issues:** Repetitive, not always well organized, and contains grammar mistakes. (R1, R3) Specific errors: missing explanation of “codebook” and its relation to action space (R1); unclear sentence on page 4 (“…given as the observations do…”) (R1); grammar mistake on page 8 (R1); missing space between Figure 8 caption and main text (R3).
- **Missing supplement:** Request to include generated videos for better demonstration. (R3)
- **Reviewer questions that align with criticisms:**
  - Why not compare with TD-VAE? (R2)
  - How does PDVAE work on real videos and continuous action spaces? (R2, R4)
  - How does it compare with modern text2video diffusion models; could they solve the task zero-shot? (R2)
  - Is the performance comparable to Dreamer v3 or Dreamer (2020) on the tasks they evaluate? (R4)
  - How is the policy space visualized as a 2D scatter in Figure 6? (R3) (This is a technical question, but overlapping with missing clarity; included here.)
- **Minority positive reviewer contrast:** One reviewer (score 5, confidence 2) finds the results reasonable and well-motivated despite weaknesses, and emphasizes that PDVAE outperforms MoCoGAN without labels. The other three reviewers (score 3) are more critical, particularly regarding evaluation and novelty.
