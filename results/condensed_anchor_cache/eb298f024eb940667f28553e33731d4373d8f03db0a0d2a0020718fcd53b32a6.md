- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 8, 5, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a hierarchical uncertainty estimation framework for deep learning-based image registration, applied to brain MRI. It models three levels: (1) a voxel-wise Gaussian distribution (mean and standard deviation) for the deformation field, (2) uncertainty in the fitted transformation parameters, and (3) uncertainty in downstream tasks (e.g., segmentation via atlas propagation). Uncertainty from level 1 is used to weight contributions during transformation fitting. Experiments on public brain MRI data show the proposed uncertainty estimates correlate better with registration error than Monte Carlo dropout. The method uses NiftyReg to generate ground-truth transformations, a Demons transformation model, and is evaluated on registration-based segmentation propagating MNI atlas labels.

### Strengths
- **Clear framework and presentation**: The three-level uncertainty hierarchy is clearly laid out. The paper is well written, related works are well introduced, and figures (e.g., showing variations from transform sampling) are useful and well-organized (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Validation of aleatoric uncertainty**: The analysis demonstrates that the aleatoric uncertainty at the first level correlates better with coordinate prediction error than the common practice of Monte Carlo dropout (Reviewer 1).
- **Flexibility**: The framework is flexible and can be applied to a broad range of registration techniques (Reviewer 3).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Diverse experiments are performed to evaluate each level of uncertainty, and the results are mostly convincing (Reviewers 1 & 3; Reviewer 2 disputes sufficiency – see below).
- **Insightful comparisons**: The empirical comparison with other methods provides useful findings (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear impact on Demons model**: Incorporating uncertainty in the fit did not improve the Demons transformation model. The authors’ explanation (that the Demons loss is used in the segmentation loss, causing average coordinates to land near the optimum) is unclear; they should expand on this and discuss whether improvement is expected with an alternative to Demons (Reviewer 1).
- **Figure formatting**: Panel labels in Figure 2 are badly formatted and need to be made clearer (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient motivation and background**: The paper does not clearly explain the background, motivation, or knowledge gap; it does not make a strong case for why uncertainty estimation is important in brain registration, leaving the value and impact unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient experimental baselines**: The method is only compared to a single baseline, RbR (arXiv 2024, unpublished). More established and published baselines are needed for a robust evaluation (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of statistically significant improvement**: From Table 1, the introduction of uncertainty estimation does not provide a statistically significant improvement in registration accuracy, raising questions about its practical value (Reviewer 2).
- **Reliance on supervised learning**: Most recent work in brain registration uses unsupervised learning due to the cost of collecting accurate transformation ground-truth for 3D MRI. The paper remains supervised, which is less aligned with current trends and less practical for real-world applications with limited labeled data (Reviewer 2).
- **Unreliable ground-truth generation (NiftyReg)**: Ground-truth transformations are generated using NiftyReg, a tool introduced ~15 years ago, now outperformed by newer methods. This may yield inaccurate ground-truth, undermining result reliability and implying the method can only perform as well as NiftyReg at best. No quality control is mentioned for the automatically generated ground truths (Reviewers 2 & 3).
- **Limited novelty**: The main contribution is adding an uncertainty loss term; the other loss terms and network architecture are based on existing work, making this an incremental improvement (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation study**: The total training loss includes multiple terms marked as optional, but no ablation study is performed to assess whether each term is necessary (Reviewer 2, question).
- **Downstream task evaluation only qualitative**: Experiments on the downstream task are limited to qualitative results; segmentations are not overlaid on the T1w image, so only differences between segmentation maps can be assessed, not which one better matches the anatomy (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear error definition and missing practical details**: Figure 2 does not specify what the error corresponds to exactly. Also, run time cost and other practical considerations for applying the approach are not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing relevant related works**: The authors could add recent works, especially Zhang et al. (2024) “Heteroscedastic Uncertainty Estimation Framework for Unsupervised Registration,” as well as the broader survey by Chen et al. (2023) (Reviewer 3).
- **Epistemic uncertainty implementation details**: The choice and implementation of Monte Carlo dropout for epistemic uncertainty are not clearly explained or justified (Reviewer 2, question).
