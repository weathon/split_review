## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes extrapolation failures in Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) using Fourier spectrum shifts, introduces the Weighted Wasserstein-Fourier (WWF) distance to quantify these shifts, and proposes a transfer learning strategy to mitigate extrapolation errors. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of studying PINN extrapolation and the proposed metric, but raise substantial concerns about theoretical grounding, applicability, and practical significance.

### Strengths
- Novel analysis of PINN extrapolation failures through spectral shifts (R1, R2, R3). One reviewer (R2, score 8) considers this a significant analysis of the basic PINN structure, with excellent presentation (presentation score 4).
- Introduction of the Weighted Wasserstein-Fourier (WWF) distance as a measure to visualize and quantify spectral shifts (R1, R2, R3). R1 notes it can be a good evaluation tool.
- Clear and well-written presentation overall (R1, R2). R1 and R3 note the paper is easy to follow; R2 grades presentation as excellent.
- The paper proposes a transfer learning strategy that reduces extrapolation errors (R1, R3).
- The topic of PINN extrapolation is novel and promising (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical grounding**: The paper lacks a theoretical explanation for why spectral shift causes extrapolation error; no mechanistic explanation, perturbation theory, or stability analysis is provided (R1). Conclusions are drawn from observations on specific PDEs and may not generalize (R3). The blockwise nature of the WWF requires true solutions, which are unavailable in practice, limiting applicability (R3). One reviewer (R4) notes that a rigorous mathematical bound for out-of-domain PINN error could be derived, contrasting with the paper’s empirical approach. R1 and R3 rate soundness as 2 (fair).
- **Misapplication of spectral bias**: Using spectral bias (a training phenomenon) to explain extrapolation (testing) is problematic; the cited reference does not make such a claim (R3). This undermines the hypothesis.
- **Unclear motivation and limited effectiveness of transfer learning**: No explanation is given for why pre-training on a related PDE mitigates spectral shift (R1). The benefit over directly training a new PINN on the full domain is not justified (R3). Empirical results are unconvincing: the L2 extrapolation error on the Schrödinger equation (imag) remains 290%, and there is no comparison with other state-of-the-art extrapolation techniques (R1). Transfer learning on the full domain is considered an unfair comparison because similar PDEs were already trained on the entire domain (R1, question).
- **Questionable significance and novelty**: The extrapolation problem of PINNs is not significant because one can always finetune or train a new model on the new domain (R4). The concept of spectral shift as distribution shift is not novel; it parallels domain adaptation/generalization in computer vision using KL divergence and other metrics (R4). Thus the claimed novelty is limited (R1, R4). R1 also notes that improvement is marginal and further work is needed for practical usefulness.
- **Missing analysis on temporal dynamics**: The analysis does not consider cases where a PDE has short-time spectrally stable behavior but long-term spectral shift emerges; the difference may come from trajectory length, not equation type (R2). A user might feel safe extrapolating a "safe" PDE but then enter a dangerous domain without warning (R2). Additionally, questions remain about why diffusion/reaction-diffusion equations show constant WWF distance and similar WWF differences to Burger’s/Allen-Cahn but do not exhibit extrapolation failures (R3, questions 2, 3).
- **Presentation issues**: Figures placement could be improved (R1). Figures can be denser to save space, and table borders could be nicer (R3). Citation format deviates from ICLR standard and may cause text overflow (R1, minor).
