## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes VIRSCI, an LLM-based multi-agent system for scientific idea generation that simulates team collaboration through five phases: Collaborator Selection, Topic Selection, Idea Generation, Idea Novelty Assessment, and Abstract Generation. Reviewers disagree on overall quality: two rate it highly (score 5, confidence 4 and 3) citing originality, comprehensive experiments, and clear advantages over single-agent methods; two rate it lower (score 3, confidence 3 and 5) raising concerns about evaluation validity, limited innovation, and missing human validation. All reviewers acknowledge the novel multi-agent framework and rich ablation studies (team size, freshness, diversity, discussion patterns) as strengths, but weaknesses center on insufficient evaluation of idea feasibility, lack of human evaluation, potential data leakage from the 2000–2014 dataset, and a narrow domain scope (CS only).

### Strengths

1. **Novel multi-agent framework for scientific idea generation**: First application of LLM-based multi-agent systems to scientific discovery, mimicking real collaborative processes (R1, R2). The 5-step framework (Collaborator Selection, Topic Selection, Idea Generation, Idea Novelty Assessment, Abstract Generation) is promising and robust (R2).
2. **Rich experiments and ablation studies**: Extensive investigation of multiple factors—team size (optimal at eight members), team freshness, research diversity, discussion patterns—strengthening the validity of conclusions (R1, R3, R4). The exploration of team size effect on novelty is particularly insightful (R4).
3. **Clear advantages over single-agent methods**: Multi-agent approach outperforms single-agent baselines in novelty and impact (R1, R2).
4. **New dataset and evaluation metrics**: CD, CI, and HD metrics are logically sound for measuring novelty (R3). The simulation consistently aligns with Science of Science findings (e.g., new teams produce more innovative research) (R1).

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of human evaluation and feasibility validation** (R3, R4, and echoed by R1, R2): The paper relies entirely on LLM-based novelty metrics without human annotators or expert reviewers. Reviewers across scores agree that feasibility, practicality, and reasonableness of generated ideas are unassessed; ideas may be novel but nonsensical due to hallucination (R3). High-scoring R1 and R2 also explicitly ask for analysis of usability and practical feasibility, with R2 suggesting adding "usefulness" and "practically-feasible" metrics. No preliminary experiments beyond abstract generation are conducted (R2).
2. **Potential data leakage compromising novelty evaluation** (R4, confidence 5; partially R3): The dataset uses papers from 2000–2014; LLMs may have been trained on these papers, so generated "novel" ideas may simply reproduce pre-existing concepts. This undermines the validity of the novelty assessment. R3 also notes limited temporal scope (2000–2014) may miss recent trends.
3. **Limited domain and temporal scope** (R1, R3): Experiments only cover computer science; the fixed time window (2000–2014) cannot reflect rapid recent developments. Expanding domains and years is needed for generalization.
4. **Limited methodological innovation** (R4): The framework applies existing LLM multi-agent techniques without introducing novel mechanisms specific to research idea generation. This is a minority view (not mentioned by R1 or R2).
5. **Poor description of agent roles and interaction structure** (R1): The organizational structure and interaction mode of agents are unclear; differences between scientist agents aside from the team leader are not well described.
6. **Ablation only under 5-turn discussion** (R1): The ablation study examines only one turn setting; whether conclusions hold for other turn counts (e.g., 1, 10) is unknown. This weakens evidence that individual components are necessary.
7. **Unsatisfactory justification for design choices** (R1, R2): The sequential execution in team discussion may not guarantee optimal effect (R1); the simple increment of 1 in the adjacency matrix lacks justification—a distribution function or explore-exploit model might be better (R2).
8. **Dependence on underlying LLM capabilities** (R2): System effectiveness may vary with the LLM; team size and turn count may need adjustment per model. No analysis of how to adapt parameters for different models is provided.
