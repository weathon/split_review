## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DoT (Diversity of Thoughts) to overcome two limitations in existing iterative LLM agent methods: (i) limited decision-space exploration due to repetitive reflections, and (ii) inability to reuse knowledge from previously solved tasks. DoT includes a diverse reflection module that reduces redundancy and a task-agnostic memory bank for cross-task knowledge retrieval. Experiments on programming benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, LeetCodeHardGym) with various LLMs show up to 10% Pass@1 improvement; integrating DoT’s diverse reflection with Tree of Thoughts yields a 13% improvement on Game of 24. The method is modular and simple to implement.

### Strengths

- Well-motivated: analysis reveals repetitive reflections in existing methods (e.g., LATS) and quantifies redundancy (Table 1). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- Well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- Method is general, modular, and supports integration with orthogonal approaches (e.g., ToT). (Reviewer 4; also noted by Reviewer 3 as simple to implement)
- Experiments are varied and well chosen with appropriate base LLMs and tasks. (Reviewer 4)
- Results show consistent and clear improvement over recent SoTA methods (Reflexion, LATS, ToT), with up to 10% improvement on programming benchmarks and 13% on Game of 24. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)

### Weaknesses

*Note: One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 8) considers these weaknesses minor, while other reviewers (scores 5,5,6) raise more substantial concerns.*

- The two main components (Diverse-Reflection and Memory Bank) appear independent without a strong theoretical connection; the work seems more an engineering combination than a cohesive advancement. Comprehensive ablation studies are lacking to determine which component drives performance gains. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 1 notes contribution is limited because using memory to improve diversity is widely explored)
- The second limitation (inability to leverage cross-task knowledge) lacks supporting analysis; adding a task-agnostic memory seems disconnected from the paper’s motivation. More evidence is needed to justify its usefulness. (Reviewer 2)
- The evaluation for efficiency claim is problematic: LATS primarily aims for accuracy, not efficiency; improvements are incremental (2-10%), test sets are relatively small, and no statistical significance testing is reported. (Reviewer 3)
- Baseline comparison: The Reflexion baseline likely uses only 1 reflection, while DoT uses many more, making the comparison unfair. A better baseline would be Reflexion with k sampled reflections to isolate the benefit of diverse generation. (Reviewer 2)
- DoT-bank cost calculation is misleading: it does not include the cost of building the memory bank (which scales with number of failed tasks). It is also unclear whether the memory bank is used for every task or only for failed tasks. (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 3 questions cost calculation)
- The paper lacks detailed analysis of why diversity in reflections leads to better results. The reliability of the one-shot prompt for generating diverse reflections is not discussed, and no metrics quantifying diversity of generated reflections compared to baselines are provided. The prompt “Ensure your reflections are accurate… Aim for diversity” appears ad-hoc. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Key technical details about the memory bank implementation and retrieval mechanism are not fully explained: how are “relevant trajectories” defined and retrieved (e.g., cosine similarity, K most similar)? What ensures diversity in retrieved trajectories? (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 4 specifically notes risk of low diversity if K most similar are chosen)
- The choice of models across tasks seems arbitrary (e.g., HumanEval uses Sonnet 3.5 and Llama-3.1, LeetCodeHardGym only Sonnet 3.5; GPT-3.5 has a different setting in Table 5 vs Table 4). (Reviewer 2)
- For the integration with ToT, it is unclear if the only difference is that ToT samples k thoughts in parallel while ToT+Diversity samples k thoughts in one shot; further clarification needed. (Reviewer 2)
- The t-SNE evaluation in Figure 4 may not be robust: it is unclear whether embeddings were computed jointly or separately; showing DoT reflections more dispersed within the same space would be stronger. (Reviewer 4)
- The description of retrieval of previous trajectories (experiment in Table 9) could be clearer. (Reviewer 4)
- Evaluation focuses mainly on programming tasks with visible or synthetic unit tests, limiting demonstrated applicability to broader reasoning tasks. (Reviewer 3)
- Minor issues: missing citation of Self-Refine (Madaan et al.), improper citation formatting (e.g., L41, L105, L350), and an unreferenced figure (L700). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- How is cost calculated for different model types (especially Llama models where costs are in GPU hours rather than API calls)? Why is the cost difference between iterative and one-shot sampling surprisingly small in Table 10? (Reviewer 3)
- How does the evaluator LLM produce evaluations (numeric score or textual)? (Reviewer 4)
- What is the effect of varying k in k diverse-reflections? How reliable is the one-shot prompt approach? (Reviewer 3)
- Does the method require more tokens per call due to longer context? A quantitative analysis of average input/output token lengths compared to baselines is missing. (Reviewer 3)
