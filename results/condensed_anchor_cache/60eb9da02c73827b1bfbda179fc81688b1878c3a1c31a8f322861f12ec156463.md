## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an empirical risk minimization approach using a pseudo-Huber loss with concomitant scaling, simultaneously optimizing the mean and a robustification parameter to achieve a self-tuning estimator for the mean of a scalar random variable under finite variance (or sub-Gaussian noise). The authors claim the method avoids cross-validation or Lepski’s method, attains the Cramér–Rao lower bound asymptotically, and is algorithmically adaptive to both finite-sample and large-sample regimes. Numerical experiments are provided.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a fundamental problem in statistical learning: robust mean estimation. (Reviewer 2, 3, 4)
- The joint optimization of the mean and robustification parameter is computationally efficient compared to cross-validation or Lepski’s method, and the estimator is shown to be efficient in both finite-sample and asymptotic senses. (Reviewer 2)
- The presentation of results, motivation, and intuition is extremely clear. (Reviewer 2)  
  *Explicit note: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 6) is very positive about the paper’s clarity, efficiency, and contribution; other reviewers (scores 1, 3, 3) are critical.*
- The paper is a nice exercise in basic statistics and probability, and the proofs appear correct. (Reviewer 1)
- Understanding the theoretical properties of mean estimation with pseudo-Huber loss and adaptive robustification (previously studied in Ronchetti & Huber, 2009) would be a significant contribution. (Reviewer 4)
- The authors aim to tackle the optimal finite-sample and asymptotic performance guarantees raised by Catoni’s seminal work. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison to Lee & Valiant (2021)**: The authors repeatedly avoid comparing with [Lee and Valiant 2021], which addresses optimal sub-Gaussian mean estimation without knowledge of the variance. That estimator achieves optimal finite-sample guarantees and asymptotic variance (“as accurate as the sample mean for the Gaussian of matching variance”) without requiring prior knowledge of the exact variance. The omission is significant, as [Lee and Valiant 2021] presents a concentration result with an optimal dependence factor of σ(1+o(1)) before √(2log(1/δ)), while the current submission’s concentration result (Theorem 3.2) shows a gap and depends on a numerical constant C. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Suboptimal theoretical bound**: The generalization bound in Theorem 3.2 has an extra √(log n) factor compared to optimal performance. Worse, the constant C is scaled by σ (line 1480 and derivation), so it may grow arbitrarily large; the real dependence on σ is unclear. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 3 also notes a gap in the concentration result.)
- **Self-tuning claim is overclaimed**: The algorithm is not completely self-tuning—it requires two tuning parameters v₀ and V₀ that must bracket the unknown variance (Section 3). No practical guidance is given on how to select these bounds in the numerical experiments. In contrast, [Lee and Valiant 2021] does not rely on such assumptions. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Unjustified choice a=0.5**: Fixing a=0.5 by inspecting the population loss is fundamentally unjustified. The authors should provide numerical evidence that this choice is good. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient numerical experiments**: The paper does not numerically consider heavy-tailed noise distributions such as Pareto or Cauchy. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear novelty and overlap with existing methods**: The idea of jointly optimizing the estimator and robustness parameter already appears in Owen’s (2006) work (sklearn Huber regression). The authors should clarify differences and comment on the connection. (Reviewer 1) Similarly, the connection to the Ronchetti & Huber loss should be clarified. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Title is too vague; it should be more specific (e.g., include “mean estimator”). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
  - Inconsistent notation: \(\hat\mu(\hat v)\) and \(\hat\mu(\tau)\) used interchangeably; \(\sigma_u\) defined before \(\sigma_{x^2}\), and \(\sigma_{x^2}\) is too specific. (Reviewer 3)
  - Subsection 3.2 has sloppy language and less clear presentation. (Reviewer 2)
  - The introduction is confusing: it includes irrelevant details (definition of sub-Gaussian variables) and is generic; the paper only studies sub-Gaussian noise with finite variance. (Reviewer 1)
  - The specific loss is redefined multiple times until Equation 2.5; everything before is superfluous. (Reviewer 1)
  - Typo in captions of Figures 3 and 5 (“distributution”). (Reviewer 2)
  - Several inaccuracies: Line 34 (“assumed Gaussian shape”), Line 36 (unclear statement about mean not finite), Line 59 (role of τ unclear), Line 74 (d not defined), Line 140 (reference to sub-Gaussian performance), Line 141 (v = σ, adding v serves no purpose). (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing practical guidance**: The authors should clarify conditions in Theorem 3.1 regarding v₀, V₀, and σ, and provide concrete examples with classical distributions and when these conditions are met. (Reviewer 3)
- **Uncertain generalizability**: It is unclear whether the self-tuning approach can be extended to more general loss functions beyond the quadratic/linear family. (Reviewer 2)
