## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FoMoH, a forward-mode-only optimization method that uses second-order directional derivatives on random hyperplanes to precondition stochastic gradient estimates. It employs hyper-dual numbers to compute Hessian projections with \(O(K^2+K)\) forward-mode calls per step, generalizing line search to a hyperplane search. Theoretical results on convex quadratics relate the update to Newton steps. Experiments on the Rosenbrock function, logistic regression, and MNIST with a CNN show improved per-iteration performance over forward gradient descent (FGD). One reviewer (score 6) finds the work significant and well-written, while two others (score 3) raise major concerns about rigor, novelty, and practical evaluation.

### Strengths
- Original idea of exploiting second-order directional derivatives to precondition forward-mode gradients, avoiding backpropagation entirely (R1, R2).
- Paper is mostly well-written and easy to follow, with clear notation and a good introduction to dual/hyper-dual numbers (R2, R3).
- Theoretical results (Theorems 1–2) show the method’s advantages over FGD on quadratics and its connection to Newton’s method (R1, R2, R3).
- Empirical results demonstrate superior performance over FGD in terms of optimization iterations on the tested problems (R1, R3).
- The significance of developing a performing forward-mode-only optimizer is high, and the work makes a concrete step in that direction (R2, minority positive).
- The community may benefit from the clear exposition of hyper-dual numbers for second-order information (R2).

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical Rigor**
- Proofs are not rigorous: Theorem 1 is a corollary of Theorem 2 and the limit statement \(\lim_{t\to\infty} \theta_t = \theta^*\) has no rate—only convergence rates like those in Theorem 2 are meaningful (R1).
- The setting of each claim is insufficiently detailed (what algorithm, what \(\theta_t\), expectation over what randomness); appendix must include full assumptions (R1).
- Proof of Theorem 2 is not well detailed: the step \(\tilde\theta_{t+1} = \tilde\theta_t + P(\tilde\theta^* - \tilde\theta_t)\) for quadratics needs a complete linear algebra derivation, not intuitive arguments (R1).
- Expectation notation is used loosely; e.g., writing \(\tilde\theta_{t+1} = \mathbb{E}[\tilde\theta_t + P(\tilde\theta^* - \tilde\theta_t)]\) is incorrect—should be \(\mathbb{E}[\tilde\theta_{t+1}] =\) (R1).
- Section B.4 contains errors: reaching a critical point does not imply a minimum without convexity; conditional expectations must be used; the claimed linear rate is not actually linear—need to recheck the reference (R1).
- A full detail of the proof is required before acceptance (R1).

**Empirical Evaluation**
- No runtime comparison is provided; the method requires Hessian inversions and overhead may be high—need runtime until convergence to assess practical usefulness (R3). Also missing memory footprint comparisons and a complexity table (R2).
- Scalability to modern architectures (e.g., ResNet18, transformers) is not demonstrated; prior work (Fournier et al., 2023) shows forward gradient works on ResNet18, but FoMoH’s applicability is unclear (R3). A larger-scale experiment, e.g., fine-tuning LLMs, would strengthen the work (R2).
- Only MNIST is used for image classification, which is known to be easy; additional datasets should be considered (R1).
- Batch-size sensitivity analysis is missing; second-order methods can be sensitive to mini-batch size (R1, also R2 asks whether mini-batch updates are used and how sensitive the method is).
- Missing comparison with alternative forward-mode methods mentioned in the abstract (e.g., orthogonal methods) (R1).
- The learning rate scheduler (mentioned as important) is not specified in the main paper (R2).
- What “BP” stands for in experiments is not defined—is it plain SGD or an adaptive method? (R2).
- Why FoMoH-1d consistently underperforms FGD and why FoMoH-BP performs on par with BP is not discussed (R2).

**Novelty and ML Relevance**
- The work does not clearly address machine-learning-specific difficulties: large datasets (SGD) and non-convexity (R3). The mathematical tools are fundamental, and the problem setting seems general—novelty in the ML context is questionable (R3). One reviewer (R2, minority) considers the contribution significant.

**Implementation and Clarity**
- The method does not need hyper-dual numbers; nested forward-mode AD (e.g., JAX’s `jax.jvp`) provides a simpler implementation that any user could code in ~50 lines, which would aid adoption. Algorithm 1 is unnecessarily complicated (R1).
- Many missing details in the main paper: full mathematical definition of FoMoH-BP (R1); the logistic regression model (is it classification? 7850 parameters suggests an MLP) (R1); the CNN architecture used for MNIST (R1); the origin of Eq. (1) and how the \(\kappa_i\) expressions are derived (are they set by design to mimic Newton?); and an implementation example showing how (hyper-)dual numbers actually compute JVPs and bilinear Hessian products (R2).
- The paper mentions “linesearch” (line 243) but the algorithm does not actually perform a linesearch (R1).
- The claim about analog optical systems computing derivatives (reference) is unclear; the cited reference does not mention AD in its abstract (R1).

**Clarification Questions That Overlap with Criticisms**  
- Is it correct that the method performs Newton steps in the sampled subspaces when the learning rate is 1? (R2) – needs explicit explanation.  
- The \(\kappa_i\) need not be positive, correct? – this should be discussed (R2).  
- Could the method be relevant for forward-mode gradient-based hyperparameter optimization? (R2) – not explored.
