## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces OfflineLight, an offline reinforcement learning model for traffic signal control (TSC), built on a proposed general offline actor-critic framework (Offline-AC). It also releases TSC-OID, the first offline interaction dataset for TSC, collected by running state-of-the-art RL policies on real-world traffic datasets. Experiments claim that offline RL can achieve high performance without online interaction, that OfflineLight matches or exceeds recent online RL methods, and that it generalizes after training on only 20% of the dataset.

### Strengths

- **Motivation and significance**: The work addresses a practically important challenge—trial‑and‑error training is costly and unsuitable for high‑stakes TSC environments. Shifting to offline RL is a necessary and important direction (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Public dataset release**: The authors collect and release the first offline dataset for TSC (TSC-OID), which is commendable and should benefit the community by facilitating further research (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is mostly well‑written with a clear description of motivation and algorithm; the reasoning behind offline‐AC design is clearly explained (Reviewer 2, but note Reviewer 1 and 3 found presentation issues – see Weaknesses).
- **Experimental consistency**: Experiment results are consistent with the claims, and the transferability discussion is essential to justify the motivation for offline training (Reviewer 2).
- **Reasonable performance**: The proposed method shows reasonable performance across several real‑world datasets (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing problem formulation**: The paper does not clearly define state, action, reward (global or per agent) nor formulate the problem as a POMDP or multi‑agent framework. Most RL‑based TSC methods operate decentralized under partial observability; this omission makes it hard to understand the setting (Reviewer 1). The reward function for the TSC RL formulation is also not stated (Reviewer 3 question).
- **Insufficient dataset description and quality analysis**: Critical details about the dataset are lacking:
  - Which RL algorithm(s) and exploration strategies were used to generate the data? (Reviewer 1, 2)
  - How many policies were used? Were they trained on all datasets or only some? If they were trained on all datasets, transferability evaluation becomes questionable (Reviewer 2).
  - The dataset collection procedure (Appendix B.2) mentions only three epochs of training, which may be insufficient to obtain high‑reward solutions (Reviewer 1).
  - No statistics are provided on reward distribution, state/action diversity, or maximum reward contained, which are essential for judging dataset quality and understanding Offline RL behavior (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Novelty concerns**: Offline‑AC appears to be a combination of existing techniques: Eq. 3 is the same as Eq. 7 in BRAC, Eq. 5 is the same as Eq. 2 in CQL. The novelty of the framework is not clear beyond application to TSC (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Mathematical errors and unclear notation**:
  - Eq. 1 is incorrectly defined (missing initial state distribution) (Reviewer 3).
  - The definition of $\pi^*$ after Eq. 2 is problematic (Reviewer 3).
  - $Q(s,a)$ in Section 4.2.2 lacks the expectation and contains unclear notation ($r^n$) (Reviewer 3).
  - In Section 4.2.1, $J(\theta)$ sums over $n,t$ but $Q$ is written independently of $n$ and $t$ (Reviewer 2).
  - The sentence “the Critic is applied to address overestimated values of the Critic” is confusing (Reviewer 3 question).
  - “where $\pi_{\theta'}$ is the target policy network by constraining the policy” – unclear if this is an exponential moving average as in BEAR (Reviewer 3 question).
  - “Applying PSR to **overfitting** to narrow peaks” – wording is contradictory (Reviewer 3 question).
- **Overclaiming and missing comparisons**:
  - Table 1 shows CQL has similar overall performance but shorter error bars, so the claim “more robust performance among offline‑RL methods” is overclaimed (Reviewer 3).
  - The paper claims “new insights into the OOD problem of offline RL” without being specific (Reviewer 3 question).
  - The conclusion that Offline‑AC is “the abstract method” needs more experimental justification (Reviewer 2).
  - The comparison in Appendix Figure 4 is unconvincing: if MPLight performs well on the transferred dataset, the transfer ratio is low; an alternative metric is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing multi‑agent interactions**: OfflineLight treats each traffic signal as an independent agent but does not consider interactions between them, which many TSC works address via CTDE or graph neural networks. Including such interactions could substantially improve performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Self‑containment**: The main paper is not self‑contained; Section 6.6 and 6.7 should be moved to the main text, and Section 5 could be reduced (Reviewer 3).
- **Language quality**: There are many typos, inaccurate words, punctuation, and notation errors that make the paper hard to follow (Reviewer 3). Note: Reviewer 2 found the presentation good, while Reviewer 1 rated presentation as fair – this disagreement is preserved.
- **Experiment details missing**: It is unclear how offline RL models are trained and evaluated in the New York scenario where no offline dataset is supplied (Reviewer 1 question). The number of seeds used for Table 1 is not reported (Reviewer 3 question).
