## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Grounded-VideoLLM, a Video-LLM designed for fine-grained temporal grounding. It addresses limitations in existing Video-LLMs by adding a temporal stream to encode frame relationships and using discrete temporal tokens for timestamp representation. A multi-stage training scheme (captioning → temporal grounding) and an automatically curated grounded VideoQA dataset are employed. The method shows strong performance on temporal sentence grounding, dense video captioning, grounded VideoQA, and general video understanding tasks. Overall, the architectures and training scheme are natural and the paper is well-written. However, several reviewers noted limited novelty and insufficient fair comparisons with prior work.

### Strengths
- Addresses important research questions about temporal understanding in LLMs and generating interpretable responses for video queries (Reviewer 2).
- The two-stream encoding, unified temporal tokens, and three-stage training scheme are natural and well-motivated (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The paper is well-written, organized, and easy to follow; model figures are informative (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Grounded-VideoLLM performs well on both fine-grained temporal grounding benchmarks (e.g., temporal sentence grounding, dense video captioning, grounded VideoQA) and general open-ended VideoQA tasks, outperforming prior methods (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The step-by-step training strategy and curated grounded VideoQA dataset enhance temporal reasoning capability (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Potential data leakage in zero-shot evaluation**: Table 2 reports zero-shot results, but ActivityNet-Captions knowledge may have been seen during training; this needs clarification (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty of two-stream encoder**: The temporal stream largely follows well-known work (SlowFast, Two-stream Convolutional Networks) by sampling more frames and pooling temporally. Ablations in Table 6 only remove the temporal stream; missing ablations include: removing the spatial stream, replacing the image encoder with a video encoder, and using only the temporal stream with different token counts for key vs. non-key frames (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Unified temporal tokens are not novel**: The idea of dedicated timestamp tokens has been proposed in LITA (ECCV 2024) and Vid2Seq (CVPR 2023). A fair comparison with plain-text timestamp representations (e.g., Shikra) and other temporal modeling methods (e.g., Momentor’s Temporal Perception Module) is missing (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Unfair base model comparisons**: The observed performance gains over LITA, VTimeLLM, etc. may stem from using a more recent base language model (Phi3.5-Vision-Instruct) rather than the proposed method. Ablations using the same base model as prior works are needed (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Computation cost not analyzed**: Adding a temporal stream introduces many additional tokens; decoder-only LLMs have quadratic time complexity. A comparison of token counts and model throughput with SOTA Video-LLMs is required (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Missing evaluations on long video understanding**: Benchmarks such as EgoSchema and MovieChat are challenging for temporal reasoning and should be reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Two-stream impact on general VideoQA**: Table 6 only shows temporal grounding results; it is unclear if the two-stream encoder also improves performance on general open-ended VideoQA benchmarks (Reviewer 3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6, confidence 5) was more positive, highlighting the step-by-step training and dataset curation, while three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5, confidence 4–5) expressed substantial concerns about novelty, fair comparisons, and computational overhead.
