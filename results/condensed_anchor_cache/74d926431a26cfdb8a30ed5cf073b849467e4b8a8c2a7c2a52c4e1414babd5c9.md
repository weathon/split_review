## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NTK-score, a zero-shot NAS proxy derived from the Neural Tangent Kernel, using three metrics: trainability (via NTK eigenvalues), expressivity (via kernel regression), and generalization (via square loss). The metrics are aggregated via Borda Count in the SABoC-NAS framework. Experiments are conducted on NAS-Bench-201, DARTS, and ResNet search spaces, evaluating ranking correlation and search performance in NAS and network pruning.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- Introduces a novel kernel regression-based metric for expressivity, extending prior NTK work (Reviewer 1).
- First work to employ the Borda Count approach to combine multiple NTK-derived characteristics (trainability, expressivity, generalization) for architecture ranking, offering a more holistic lens than single-metric proxies (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- Validated on two applications: Neural Architecture Search and Network Pruning (Reviewer 1).
- NTK provides a solid theoretical foundation for understanding network characteristics (Reviewer 2).
- Presents comparisons to state-of-the-art zero-shot NAS proxies (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to prior NTK proxies:** A significant portion of the proposed metrics is borrowed from existing literature (TE-NAS, TEG-NAS). The main technical contribution is the Borda Count aggregation, which is considered simple (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 notes marginal improvement over AZ-NAS and Zen-NAS.
- **High computational cost:** The method incurs substantial computational overhead compared to other zero-cost proxies. For example, on NAS-Bench-201, SABoC-NAS is ~46× more expensive than Zen-NAS (Table 3). In Table 5, the search cost reaches 137h, yet the performance is worse than Zen-NAS. Reviewer 4 finds the computational demands unreasonably high for claimed scalability (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient benchmark evaluation and lack of robustness:** 
  - Results on other popular NAS benchmarks (e.g., NAS-Bench-101, TransNAS-Bench-101) are absent (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
  - The Kend-τ correlation coefficient is only reported for CIFAR-100 on NAS-Bench-201, despite the availability of meta-data for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet16-120 (Reviewer 4).
  - The experimental settings for DARTS are atypical for zero-cost NAS, and the results lack competitiveness; test accuracy on ImageNet is not reported (Reviewer 2).
  - The tau value presented in Figure 2 is incorrect (Reviewer 2).
  - Robustness to changes in search space constraints, weight initialization, and data domains (image/text/speech) is not demonstrated. The NAS-Bench-201 search space is relatively restrictive (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical justification is insufficient:** 
  - The use of NTK eigenvalues to represent trainability, expressivity, and generalization lacks rigorous mathematical grounding and risks oversimplification (Reviewer 4).
  - The transition from zero-shot NAS to NTK is asserted without evidence or citation for the claim that networks with better generalization have more similar NTK eigenvalues (Reviewer 4).
  - Reviewer 3 raises a general criticism: NTK-based proxies only study behavior at initialization; there is no Bayesian posterior interpretation of trained neural networks from the NTK (citing He et al. 2020). In NAS, we care about trained network performance, not initialization properties (Reviewer 3).
- **Borda Count aggregation is questionable:** The Borda Count method is used without sufficient justification. It may not effectively capture the balance across diverse metrics compared to weighted combinations or Pareto optimization. Equal weighting of the three metrics (Equation 12) may be suboptimal (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 2 questions which metric is most important).
- **Trainability metric may be less significant:** Emphasis on trainability is questioned, as NAS typically prioritizes final performance over training speed (Reviewer 2). The choice of 100-epoch training accuracy as an auxiliary measure of trainability is not adequately justified (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experimental validation:** 
  - Whether other zero-cost proxies can be combined via Borda Count for improved ranking is not explored (Reviewer 1).
  - Generalization ability of searched architectures is not verified in out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., CIFAR-10-C or -P) (Reviewer 1).
  - Applicability to transformer search spaces (e.g., HW-GPT-Bench for language models) is not considered (Reviewer 3).
  - Practical relevance is questionable given the high search costs, especially for large-scale benchmarks (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues:** The paper is frequently repetitive, poorly phrased, and has structural weaknesses that obscure arguments. The introductory section lacks cohesion. There is no graphical abstract (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 notes Presentation score 2.

Note: Reviewers 2, 3, 4 assigned scores of 3, while Reviewer 1 assigned 5; this positive minority view is reflected in the stronger strengths from Reviewer 1, but the weaknesses list remains comprehensive.
