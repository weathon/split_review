## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PhASER, a framework for domain-generalizable classification of nonstationary time series. The method uses the Hilbert Transform (HT) for phase augmentation, separate encoding of magnitude and phase from the STFT, and a residual broadcasting mechanism to integrate phase features. Evaluations on five datasets (HAR, sleep-stage, gesture) against ten baselines show average 5% improvement (up to 13%). Reviewers find the topic relevant and the combination of components novel, but raise major concerns about mathematical rigor, theoretical justification, baseline selection, and clarity of the core claims.

### Strengths
- Domain generalization for nonstationary time series is an important and relevant problem. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- The combination of Hilbert Transform augmentation, separate magnitude/phase encoding, and residual broadcasting is novel and adds to existing augmentation strategies. (R2, R3, R5)
- Empirical evaluation is extensive, covering multiple domains (HAR, sleep, gesture) with an ablation study; results show consistent improvements (avg 5%, up to 13%) over baselines. (R1, R3, R5)
- Two reviewers (R1, R4) were particularly positive about the experimental validation and organization, rating the paper 5. R4 highlighted enhanced generalization across domains and robust feature integration through separate encoding and residual broadcasting.

### Weaknesses
- **Mathematical formalism is imprecise and flawed.** Equation (1) defines a probability on the LHS but has a location-scale model on the RHS with unclear ranges; notation \(Pr_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_x}(x)(t)\) is unclear; Definition 2.2 lacks clarity on source domains, independence, and sampling; HT is miswritten as \(HT(x(t))\) instead of \(HT(x)(t)\); the variable set \(\mathbf{x}\) is defined as \(\mathbb{R}\) instead of \(\mathbb{R}^T\); function composition \(F\circ g\) is used non-standardly as \(g(F(x))\); the beta divergence is not standard and does not satisfy divergence properties; Equation (9) is incomplete with undefined terms. (R2, R5)
- **Definition of nonstationarity is nonstandard and restrictive.** The piecewise constant distribution assumption (Eq. 1) excludes stationary time series and is restrictive for many real-world datasets with complex dynamics. The connection to standard weak stationarity is not discussed, nor is performance on stationary/near-stationary data. (R1, R2, R5) The term “nonstationary statistics” is used frequently but never clearly defined. (R3)
- **Justification for phase augmentation preserving discriminative features is insufficient.** The paper does not explain why changing the phase (which carries timing and alignment information) does not alter discriminative content. A detailed justification linking phase to class labels is missing. (R1) The effect of phase shift on the distribution is not explicitly shown; it is unclear why adding phase-shifted data simplifies classification. (R5)
- **Theoretical analysis is weak and unconvincing.** Theorem 2.6 is unclear and not properly justified; “nonstationary statistics” remains undefined. (R3) The theoretical section (Sec 2.5) appears redundant and weakly connected to the method. (R1) No formal guarantees (generalization bounds, convergence, error analysis) are provided. (R5) The risk in Theorem 2.5 is not defined. (R5)
- **Baseline selection is problematic.** Baselines are mostly domain generalization algorithms not designed for time series; standard time series classification models (e.g., from the UCR archive, functional data analysis) are missing. Close to half of the baselines come from non-published articles. (R2, R5) The authors should compare with Diversify in the augmentation step (R1) and with other multimodal fusion approaches for the residual broadcasting mechanism. (R4)
- **Ablation study lacks statistical rigor.** Differences between components are minimal and no standard deviations or confidence intervals are reported, making conclusions about component contributions weak and possibly due to randomness. (R5)
- **Implementation details and intuition are missing.** The feature broadcasting mechanism is described only from an implementation standpoint without an intuitive explanation or connection to the literature. (R2) Benefits compared to other multimodal fusion methods are not explained. (R4) The paper claims domain generalization but does not specify which components contribute to it. (R2) Separate magnitude/phase encoding lacks justification: why encoding them separately works better than time-domain signals or concatenation? (R5)
- **Choice of wavelet transform not considered.** Given that wavelet transform offers multi-resolution advantages for nonstationary signals, the paper should justify why only STFT/HT is used. (R4)
- **Clarity and presentation issues.** Poorly formulated sentences appear (e.g., “the joint distributions […] are similar but distinct”, “diversify non-stationarity”, “DFT is applicable for signals that are stationary and periodic”). (R2) The Related Work section is placed at the end, which is non-standard. (R5) OOD classification is mentioned only in the conclusion but not addressed in the method. (R5) ADF test should report p-values instead of raw statistics. (R2)
- **Limited evaluation metrics.** Only accuracy is reported; it is unclear if datasets are balanced, and other metrics (precision, recall, F1) are missing. (R5)
- **Concerning assumptions and random components.** The paper assumes time series \(x\) is characterized by a deterministic function (L186), contradicting typical stochastic modeling of time series. (R5) The random window length \(W_i\) and uniform distribution on non-negative integers for \(p_i\) in the augmentation are not justified. (R5)
