## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses content leakage in visual style prompting for text-to-image generation by extending classifier-free guidance (CFG) with swapping self-attention and proposing negative visual query guidance (NVQG). Additional techniques include stochastic encoding and color calibration. The method is training-free and achieves state-of-the-art performance, as shown by extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments on T2I and I2I tasks. Reviewers acknowledge the practical importance of preventing content leakage and the thoroughness of the studies.

### Strengths
- Sets a new state-of-the-art for visual style prompting; quantitative and qualitative assessments demonstrate superior effectiveness.
- NVQG is an intuitive and effective approach to prevent content leakage.
- Comprehensive experiments aid understanding of the pipeline and key components; thorough studies provide useful insights for the community.
- Method is training-free and achieves outstanding preference for style transfer without content leakage.
- The paper is well written, logically structured, and easy to follow.
- Experiments are detailed with extensive comparative and visual analyses supporting the main contributions.

### Weaknesses
- Certain design decisions lack in-depth analysis or theoretical derivation:
  - Determining the optimal layers for balancing style and content, exchanging self-attention, and color calibration (Reviewer 1, high confidence).
  - The derivation of equations in Section 2.2 is not sufficiently clear (Reviewer 4).
- Innovation is limited because the proposed CFG with swapping self-attention and NVQG mainly combine previous work (Reviewer 4).
- Competitiveness against existing methods is unclear:
  - Comparisons are outdated; recent state-of-the-art methods (DEADiff, InstantStyle-Plus, CSGO, InstantStyle, StyleDrop) are missing (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 explicitly asks whether content leakage occurs in these newer methods, and suggests direct comparison to highlight the strengths of the proposed approach.
  - Missing quantitative metrics for style transfer quality across models; relying solely on selected visual examples may be cherry-picked (Reviewer 3, confidence 3).
  - Although the paper uses quantitative metrics (e.g., Figure 5) to evaluate content leakage, these metrics are not applied to compare with other models, even though content leakage is the main problem addressed (Reviewer 4).
- Insufficient analysis of computational efficiency: runtime and memory usage are not evaluated; the method involves multiple attention computations among latent variables and multiple steps per inference (Reviewers 2, 3). With each new {reference style, input image} pair, these steps must be repeated (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation studies are incomplete:
  - Quantitative ablation for stochastic encoding and color calibration is missing. It is unclear whether these components are the most crucial, and if so, the effectiveness of CFG swapping and NVQG is diminished. Exploring their utility in other diffusion methods would also be interesting (Reviewer 1).
- Writing and presentation issues:
  - Typo on Line 18: "employ" should be "employs" (Reviewer 1).
  - Inconsistent terminology: NVQG in the introduction vs. NVG in Section 3 (Reviewer 4).
  - Figures 3 and 4 are not aligned; a large number of instances where K and V are combined in formulas makes them hard to understand (Reviewer 4).

*Note: Reviewer 1 (rating 8, confident) is the most positive, considering the method state-of-the-art and the components effective. Reviewer 3 (rating 5, less confident) is the most negative, emphasizing missing comparisons, lack of quantitative style metrics, and insufficient efficiency analysis.*
