## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes TaLoS, a sparse fine-tuning approach for task arithmetic that selectively updates only parameters with low gradient sensitivity to enable weight disentanglement without explicit linearization. The method first identifies a per-task parameter mask and constrains updates to those parameters during fine-tuning. Experiments on vision (SVHN, Cars, DTD) and NLP tasks (using T5-small and T5-large) show improved task addition and negation performance, as well as computational and memory efficiency, compared to baselines like LoTA.

### Strengths

- The motivation is clear and the writing is easy to follow.
- The method is simple and intuitive, and the experiments on mask calibration (Figure 4) interestingly reveal a pattern in the model weights.
- TaLoS consistently outperforms baseline methods (including LoTA) in both task addition and negation, highlighting the effectiveness of sparsity for task localization.
- The approach offers significant efficiency gains in training/inference time and memory usage compared to linearization-based methods.
- The empirical evaluation is comprehensive, covering vision and language domains and including analyses of weight disentanglement and function localization.
- Novel combination of sparse fine-tuning with task localization contributes to the model editing literature.

### Weaknesses

1. **Applicability limited to retraining from scratch.** The method requires fine-tuning from the pre-trained checkpoint with the proposed mask; it cannot be applied to already existing fine-tuned models (R1).

2. **Mask construction and algorithm are unclear in the main text.** The process for building the mask (line 236 refers to $\mathbf{x}$ without specifying whether a single batch or multiple rounds are used). Algorithm 1 in the appendix introduces "rounds" but this term and the number of rounds/iterations, which significantly affect computational cost, are never mentioned in the main text (R1). Additionally, the key algorithm description in line 229 ("a procedure through which we constrain the parameters with the largest ... to remain constant and update the only the ones where ... ≈ 0") is grammatically flawed and hard to follow (R3). The pruning criteria (e.g., bottom‑k values) used in Figure 1 are not readily apparent (R2).

3. **Mask sparsity ratio is undiscussed and unablated.** The level of sparsity (e.g., whether 90% is used throughout) is only mentioned in the caption of Figure 4, not in the main body. This hyperparameter critically affects single‑task performance before merging, yet no ablation or analysis of the trade‑off between sparsity and single‑task accuracy is provided. Absolute fine‑tuned results (before merging) are not given; from Figure 3 it appears TaLoS underperforms on tasks like SVHN, Cars, and DTD (R1, R4). The impact of the sparsity hyperparameter $k$ in Equation 8 is also not sufficiently discussed (R3).

4. **Lack of theoretical justification for key claims.** The connection between gradient sensitivity and task interference is not rigorously justified. Several specific gaps are noted:
   - The experiment in Figure 1 focuses on zero‑shot pruning, yet the method applies the insight to fine‑tuned checkpoints without establishing why the relationship holds post‑finetuning (R1).
   - It is intuitive that changing low‑sensitivity parameters does not affect the function for task $t$, but it is less clear why it would not affect other tasks after fine‑tuning. The paper does not provide evidence (e.g., measuring the impact of these parameter changes on other tasks’ performance or analyzing overlap of low‑sensitivity parameters across tasks) to support this (R2).
   - If gradient norms are not uniform across layers, the approach could inadvertently prune important parameters; further insights are needed (R2). Similarly, the specific conditions or limitations (e.g., model size/architecture variations) under which removing parameters deemed unimportant for one task does not degrade other tasks are unclear (R2).
   - The claim that "This inherent property of sparse finetuning increases the likelihood that the linearization condition will hold… effectively rendering explicit network linearization unnecessary" is stated as fact rather than intuition and is not empirically demonstrated (R1).
   - No theoretical analysis or proof of convergence is provided (R3).

5. **Presentation issues and errors.** 
   - In Table results for T5‑large, the absolute value 77.31 > 76.20 makes the reported +2.87 gain incorrect (R1).
   - The caption of Figure 1 lacks clarity, particularly in explaining that "sensitivity" refers to gradient values (R3).
   - References are missing: lines 47 (missing citations from Ties and TALL masks), 137‑138 (missing reference [1]), and line 194 (subscript notation error) (R1).
   - Contributions listed (especially contribution 1) are too vague and could be claimed for any model merging method (R1).

6. **Unfair comparison for task negation.** All post‑hoc baselines are designed for task addition, not negation. The paper should explicitly state this limitation and frame the negation experiments as an extension of their original purpose (R1).

7. **Related work organization is suboptimal.** The model merging and the task arithmetic paragraphs should be merged; Fisher merging should be discussed with task arithmetic. A separate paragraph on fine‑tuning methods specifically designed for merging (the paper’s baselines) is missing (R1).

8. **Unclear efficiency claims and metrics.**
   - The statement that "existing task arithmetic solutions are strongly tied to model linearization which leads to computational bottlenecks" is questionable; Task Arithmetic/TIES do not have such bottlenecks (R1).
   - In Table 3, the nonlinear fine‑tuning appears to have lower runtime than the proposed method, but the paper describes TaLoS as more efficient. The reasons for the large differences in forward/backward pass time and peak memory usage are not explained; they might be due to excluding entire layers from the mask (R1).

9. **Lack of discussion on integration with other merging techniques.** The paper does not explore or discuss how TaLoS might be combined with methods like Ties‑Merging or AdaMerging, which could further improve performance (R4).

*Note:* Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms (e.g., testing “extreme random changes” to low-sensitivity parameters (R2), uniformity of parameter sensitivity (R2), conditions for pruning (R2), mask sparsity sensitivity (R4), evidence of parameter overlap across tasks (R4)) are incorporated into the corresponding weaknesses above.
