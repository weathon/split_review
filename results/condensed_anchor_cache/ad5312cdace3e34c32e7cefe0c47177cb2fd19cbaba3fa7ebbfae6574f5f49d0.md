## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Multi-scale Triplane Network (MTN) with a progressive learning strategy for SDS-based text-to-3D generation. The architecture uses four triplanes transitioning from low to high resolution, and the learning scheme adjusts camera radius and diffusion time steps over training to shift focus from coarse to fine details. The method aims to improve fine-grained detail and high-resolution optimization. Evaluations are against Stable-Dreamfusion, Latent-NeRF, DreamGaussian, and ProlificDreamer.

### Strengths
- The coarse-to-fine multi-scale triplane architecture is a reasonable design for SDS optimization, enabling progressive refinement of global shape and local details (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The progressive learning strategy (reducing camera radius and time step) incrementally improves detail, addressing the common difficulty of balancing global structure and fine-grained textures (Reviewer 2).
- Generated meshes are directly suitable for 3D printing with minimal post-processing, as demonstrated in Figure 9 (Reviewer 4).
- The progressive learning scheme is generalizable to other SDS-based text-to-3D generation models (Reviewer 4).
- Ablation studies (multi-scale network and progressive training) confirm the effectiveness of the proposed components (Reviewer 3; though one reviewer questioned the ablation settings—see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Inadequate comparisons with recent state-of-the-art methods.** The paper omits several important baselines: MVDream (noted by Reviewer 1), LucidDreamer (CVPR 2024, noted by Reviewer 2), Fantasia3D (noted by Reviewer 3), and GSGEN (noted by Reviewer 4). Additionally, DreamGaussian performs poorly as a text-to-3D baseline, making it unsuitable for a fair comparison (Reviewer 4). The comparison with ProlificDreamer is only qualitative, and the ProlificDreamer results in Figure 4 appear inferior to those in the original paper; quantitative metrics (e.g., R-Precision) should be reported for all methods (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Limited technical novelty.** One reviewer (Reviewer 3, confidence 5, score 3) strongly questions novelty, noting that multi-scale representation is already explored in XCube (CVPR 2024) and Locally Attentional SDF Diffusion (SIGGRAPH 2023), two-stage SDS optimization appears in Magic3D (CVPR 2023), and progressive time-step sampling is used in Dreamtime (ICLR 2024). The paper insufficiently discusses these works and does not clearly justify how MTN overcomes their limitations. In contrast, other reviewers found the design reasonable, but Reviewer 4 also notes missing references to related triplane-based 3D generation works.
- **Unclear ablation settings.** The resolution of the triplane used in the single-triplane ablation and the number of iterations for that ablation are not specified, making it difficult to assess whether the comparison is fair (Reviewer 3). The resolution of the rendered image at each scale is also not reported (Reviewer 1 question).
- **Generated quality is inconsistent and often blurry.** In Figure 2, many shapes (e.g., “corgi,” “motorcycle,” “train,” “bunny”) appear blurry with very uniform color, lacking fine-grained texture. This stands in contrast to the higher-frequency textures shown in Figure 7; the paper does not explain this large quality discrepancy (Reviewers 1 and 4). The claim about “automatic color rendering” for common categories requires clarification (Reviewer 4 question).
- **Misleading argument against 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).** The paper claims that 3DGS methods require post-processing to convert to mesh, adding overhead, while triplane representation does not—but triplane also requires marching cubes for mesh extraction. Moreover, 3DGS-based methods are approximately 10× faster than MTN, which far outweighs the conversion cost. A more nuanced trade-off comparison between speed and quality is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Object size inconsistency from rendering prompts.** Using prompts like “zoomed out DSLR photo” (Figure 4) results in physically smaller objects (e.g., smaller pancake pile and rabbit), implying that generated object size depends on rendering parameters. This raises practical concerns, as users would need to check and resize results, whereas other methods generate objects with consistent size (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing evidence for random time-step sampling at the end of training.** The claim that re-introducing random sampling maintains color vibrancy is unsupported; specific examples or experimental results are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **No discussion of limitations** (Reviewer 2 question).
- **Missing experiments:** The paper does not visualize the shape from each triplane scale to verify disentanglement of structural scales (Reviewer 1). Whether the approach can scale to 1024-resolution triplanes was also questioned (Reviewer 1).
