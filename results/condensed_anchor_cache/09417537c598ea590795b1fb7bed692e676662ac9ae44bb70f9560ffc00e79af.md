## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PLENCH, the first benchmark for systematic and fair evaluation of deep partial-label learning (PLL) algorithms. It identifies and addresses three overlooked issues: (1) model selection in PLL is nontrivial but never systematically studied; (2) experimental settings are highly inconsistent; (3) no real-world partial-label image dataset suitable for modern architectures exists. The authors propose three model selection criteria (Covering Rate CR, Approximated Accuracy AA, and Oracle Accuracy OA with/without early stopping) with theoretical guarantees, create a human-annotated dataset PLCIFAR10 (Aggregate and Vaguest versions) via Amazon Mechanical Turk, and benchmark 27 algorithms on 11 datasets. Key findings: simpler algorithms often match or outperform complex ones, no single algorithm dominates all settings, and model selection practices are crucial.

### Strengths
- Model selection in PLL is a relevant, underexplored topic, and this paper is the first to systematically investigate it (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The proposed dataset PLCIFAR10, with human-annotated partial labels, provides a realistic testbed and adds significant value to the PLL literature (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The paper establishes a solid theoretical link between the proposed metrics (CR, AA) and expected accuracy, with theoretical guarantees (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The experiments are extensive, covering 27 algorithms from top venues and 11 real-world datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The paper points out a critical issue: many recent PLL studies achieve state-of-the-art by using an unfair validation setting (clean ground-truth labels), potentially misleading the research direction; simpler prior methods can outperform them in a fair benchmark (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written, accessible, and comprehensive (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Model selection criteria as contribution**: Oracle Accuracy (OA) is standard in the literature; it should be presented as a baseline, not as a novel contribution (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of experimental validation for criteria**: No experimental evidence is provided that the proposed criteria (CR, AA, OA, OA w/ ES) actually lead to better test performance. For a given ML method, does the model selected with these criteria outperform the model without explicit selection? A numerical comparison (e.g., how often each criterion yields the best model across 27 algorithms) is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Confusing terminology**: The paper uses “model selection” to refer to what is actually hyperparameter tuning (selection within a family). This should be clarified to avoid confusion (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear toy example (Figures 1a, 1b)**: Bar colors are inconsistent with the legend; the x‑axis contains floating numbers instead of method names; the pilot experiment (line 75) is insufficiently explained (e.g., meaning of training/validation, subcaptions). This makes it hard to understand the demonstration of how different validation settings affect performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Notation issues**: Eq. (5) uses an expectation around accuracy, but accuracy is already an expected value; the notation is cumbersome (Reviewer 3).
- **Proof of Proposition 1**: The second equality in the proof is unclear—how is the subtraction of two expectations turned into a single expectation, given that \(S\) depends on \(x\) and \(y\)? Lemma 1 is introduced but its conclusion does not clearly connect to the proof; the proof only uses the definition of ambiguity degree from Lemma 1, not its conclusion (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear distinction between PLCIFAR10-Aggregate and PLCIFAR10-Vaguest**: The descriptions in the paper appear identical (“the aggregation of all partial labels from all annotators”). The difference needs to be clearly explained; the same issue applies to the two synthetic datasets mentioned at lines 286-287 (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unclear use of early stopping (ES)**: Lines 245-247 suggest ES is applied to everything except OA. It should be clarified whether ES is used with CR and AA (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing discussion of related benchmarks**: The paper should compare its benchmark with [1] Schmarje et al., NeurIPS 2022, and [2] Goswami et al., NeurIPS 2023, which also address benchmarking in noisy/ambiguous labels (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarification needed**: Please explain \(p(x,y)\) and \(p(S|x,y)\) in Eq. (4) more thoroughly (Reviewer 4). Also, access to annotator identities and how privacy was ensured for the human-annotation dataset should be described (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues**: Figure 2 subfloats are not top-aligned (Reviewer 1).
