## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes FreCoformer, a Transformer-based model for time series forecasting that operates in both the frequency and time domains. The model includes a frequency patching operation, cross-channel attention to extract consistent representations, and a divide-and-conquer linear module to capture both long-term and short-term variations. A lightweight variant using Nyströmformer approximation is also introduced. Experiments on multiple real-world datasets show that FreCoformer outperforms PatchTST in most settings (41 top-1 and 21 top-2 out of 64 cases). Reviewers view the work as solid with promising performance, but raise significant concerns about the novelty of the designs, the overclaim of “divide-and-conquer,” the marginal gains over baselines, insufficient efficiency analysis, and missing experimental details.

### Strengths
- **Innovative frequency-domain patching and attention**: The application of patching in the Fourier domain combined with Nyströmformer is a novel contribution to forecasting (R1, R3). The frequency-domain operation successfully captures high-frequency dynamics that time-domain models miss (R3).
- **Strong empirical results**: FreCoformer outperforms PatchTST in most experimental settings (R1) and achieves top performance on multiple datasets compared to recent baselines (R1, R2, R4). Ablation studies validate each design component (R4).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Experiments use multiple datasets and solid baselines, with fair comparisons (R2). The paper also attempts to reduce computation cost while preserving performance (R2, R3).
- **Clear writing**: The paper is well-structured and easy to understand (R4). Visualizations (e.g., high-frequency attention) provide useful insights (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaim of “divide-and-conquer” framework**: Several reviewers note that the divide-and-conquer module is essentially a dual-path structure (attention in frequency domain, MLP in time domain), not a true tree or multi-scale hierarchy (R1, R2). The naming suggests deeper algorithmic decomposition that is not realized. One reviewer (R2) questions whether this is just a two-layer linear aggregation similar to previous work. Another (R4) finds the concept not novel, as dual-branch time/frequency processing is well known.
- **Insufficient novelty and similarity to existing methods**: The cross-channel attention, divide-and-conquer idea, and Nyström approximation are argued to be direct adaptations of prior work (R3: [1], [2], [3], PatchTST [4], Nyströmformer [5]). Frequency patching merely shifts the domain of PatchTST’s patching. One reviewer (R3) states that the paper merely combines existing modules without introducing new insights; another (R4) notes the core contribution is diluted by multiple borrowed components.
- **Marginal performance gains over PatchTST**: Given that frequency-domain signal is a bijective projection of the time-domain signal, the theoretical difference from PatchTST is limited, and the empirical improvement in Table 3 is small (R2).
- **Missing efficiency analysis**: Only GPU memory is compared. Training time, number of parameters, FLOPs, and inference latency are needed to justify the dual-branch design (R4). The paper does not show efficiency comparisons against competing methods (R4).
- **Insufficient experimental validation and missing analyses**:
  - No random control experiments; model variance and hyperparameter sensitivity are not reported (R1).
  - No direct empirical evidence that the divide-and-conquer framework handles both long-term and short-term variations as claimed (R3).
  - The test data loader in the provided code uses `drop_last = True`, which discards test samples. Correcting this would affect reported numbers (R1).
  - The channel-wise attention design deviates from the standard [6] without justification (R3).
  - The procedure for selecting $m$ landmark columns for the Nyström approximation is not detailed (R3).
  - Figure 3(a) claims advantage in short-term variation identification, but the reviewer cannot see which parts support this; specific markings are needed (R3).
  - The paper’s explanation of DFT matrix visualization (Figure 3(b)) is confusing; the DFT matrix is fixed for a given input length (R3).
  - The argument about high-frequency shortcoming and spurious correlations (Figure 1) is too strong, as the figure does not directly prove spurious correlations (R3).
- **Design choices not justified**:
  - Why attention in frequency domain but MLP in time domain? Why not use attention or MLP in both domains? (R1)
  - The time-domain module in the code includes both trend differences and local differences, but the paper’s Section 3.2 only mentions first-order differences. Clarify (R1).
  - The different channel settings across datasets (Table 2) are not explained, possibly due to data length (R4).
- **Minor issues**: Typos (e.g., “Attentione”) (R2).
