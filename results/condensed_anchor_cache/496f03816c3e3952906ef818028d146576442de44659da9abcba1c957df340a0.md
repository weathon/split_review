## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides a theoretical and empirical analysis of why pre-projection (encoder) representations often outperform post-projection representations in contrastive learning, even though the former are not directly optimized. Using linear and simple non-linear models, the authors show that training dynamics produce layer-wise progressive feature weighting (deeper features become more unequal), making lower layers more normalized and less specialized. They characterize three scenarios where lower layers are beneficial: (1) data augmentation disrupts useful features, (2) downstream-relevant features are too weak or too strong in pre-training, and (3) in supervised contrastive and supervised learning for robustness. Experiments on synthetic data, CIFAR-10/100, UrbanCars, and shifted ImageNet support the theory. An alternative to the projection head is also proposed.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and under-explored problem: why the projection head improves representation learning.
- Provides rigorous theoretical analysis that reveals the implicit bias of training algorithms leading to unequal feature weighting across layers.
- Identifies concrete scenarios where pre-projection representations are superior, with clear theoretical characterizations (e.g., Theorems 3.6, 4.2, Corollary 3.7).
- Extends the analysis to supervised contrastive learning and supervised learning, showing how the same mechanism improves robustness.
- Extensive related work discussion.
- Empirical results on multiple datasets (CIFAR-10/100, UrbanCars, shifted ImageNet) verify theoretical findings.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model and dataset complexity.**  
  - The theoretical analysis is based on two-layer linear or diagonalized non-linear networks. How results extend to deeper networks, especially decisions about which layers to discard beyond the projection head, is not discussed (Reviewer 1, 4).  
  - Experiments are restricted to small-scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and simple architectures. It is unclear whether the conclusions hold for larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and deeper convolutional or transformer networks (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (R4) noted the simple data model may be acceptable if commonly used, but others (R1, R3) emphasized the need for more complex validation.  
  - The theoretical analysis of distribution shifts is limited to linear models; extension to non-linear models is not addressed (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing empirical validation on real-world datasets for the identified scenarios.**  
  The advantages of pre-projection representations under different data augmentation types are demonstrated only on synthetic data. Real-world verification (e.g., on CIFAR-10/100 or ImageNet) of the effect of augmentation strength and relevance on feature weighting is lacking (Reviewer 1).

- **No comparison with alternative regularization methods.**  
  Most benefits of pre-projection representations arise when pre-training signal does not align with downstream tasks. Other regularization techniques (weight decay, dropout, early stopping) might achieve similar effects without a projection head. The paper notes that weight decay diminishes the advantage (end of Sec. 3), but does not systematically compare such alternatives (Reviewer 4). The risk of overfitting due to increased parameter count of the projection head is also not discussed (Reviewer 2).

- **Lack of guidance on projector design and depth choice.**  
  The architecture of the projection head (number of layers, dimensions, activations) strongly influences downstream performance, but the paper offers no design guidelines based on theory. Similarly, for multi-layer networks, how to choose the appropriate depth of projection heads and the trade-off between representation power and diversity/robustness is not explored (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).

- **Assumptions and limitations not fully discussed.**  
  The analysis of non-linear models uses a diagonalized network simplification that may not capture dynamics of fully connected networks. Discussions of limitations and generality of assumptions are missing (Reviewer 3).

- **Specific technical clarifications and missing explanations.**  
  Several definitions, experiments, and figures require clarification:  
  - In Definition 3, details about \(\phi_i\) should be added (Reviewer 3).  
  - Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.7: Do the conditions hold for non-linear models? (Reviewer 3).  
  - The meaning of \(\alpha_i\) in Setting 2 (Sec. 5.1) and the observed weight spikes in Figure 1 (right, \(\phi_i=0.2\) vs. \(0.4\)) need explanation (Reviewer 3).  
  - Experiment in Figure 3b: with \(s=1\) (only MNIST digit) and \(p_{\text{drop}}=1\) the augmented image would be completely black—how does this affect results? (Reviewer 3).  
  - Table 2: WaterBird supervised learning results for no-projection and pre-projection are nearly identical—why? (Reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 4: what exactly is plotted? (Reviewer 3).  
  - The abstract on OpenReview differs from the PDF (Reviewer 1).  
  - Reference formatting is inconsistent (some full conference names, some abbreviations) (Reviewer 1).
