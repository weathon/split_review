## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper empirically evaluates generative data augmentation for image classification using internal (trained on target dataset) and external (pre-trained large-scale, e.g., Stable Diffusion) generative models. The study finds that synthetic data can improve performance, especially when real data is scarce, but requires significantly larger volumes to match real-data gains. It provides empirical formulas for the equivalent synthetic-to-real data ratio. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, ImageNet-10, and ImageNet-100 with Vanilla-DDPM and Stable Diffusion 2.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and clearly highlights main findings (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Extensive and rigorous empirical analysis on widely used datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-10, ImageNet-100) with detailed experimental setup and implementation (Reviewers 1,2,4,6).
- Novel quantification of the equivalence between real and synthetic data augmentation, offering empirical formulas that guide the required synthetic-to-real ratio for comparable gains (Reviewers 1,4,6).
- Demonstrates clear benefits of generative augmentation under data-scarce conditions and explores the impact of baseline training set size and generative model quality (Reviewers 1,2,6).
- Includes valuable ablation over the ratio of synthetic data used (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and technical contribution**: Most findings (synthetic data improves classification, real data is more sample-efficient, diminishing returns) replicate earlier works (e.g., arXiv:2304.08821, arXiv:2204.08610) without new insights. The study merely works out exact accuracy improvements on three datasets and offers no deeper theoretical or methodological advance (Reviewers 3,4,5,6). Some reviewers acknowledge clarity but rate contribution low (Reviewers 3,4,5).
- **Limited dataset diversity and generalizability**: Experiments are confined to CIFAR-10 and ImageNet subsets (ImageNet-10, ImageNet-100). This restricts generalizability to more complex, high-resolution, or domain-specific datasets. Using only ImageNet-100 may hide scaling issues present in full ImageNet-1k. Missing evaluation on challenging real-world conditions (e.g., autonomous driving with lighting, weather, object variations) (Reviewers 1,5,6).
- **Lack of theoretical grounding**: The equivalence formulation between real and synthetic data is presented without sufficient theoretical justification. No rationale is given for why the relationship holds or under what conditions it might break down, limiting broader applicability (Reviewers 5,6).
- **Inadequate analysis of synthetic data quality and characteristics**: The paper does not deeply investigate how quality attributes (diversity, fidelity, artifacts) affect classification performance and explain diminishing returns (Figures 1b, 1c, 5). FID and IS are used as quality metrics; newer measures such as Clip-MMD are not considered (Reviewers 5,6,4). The claim that synthetic data is less sample-efficient may stem from current generative models being optimized for quality over diversity and not matching natural image distributions (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons across generative model families**: Only diffusion models (Vanilla-DDPM, Stable Diffusion 2) are used; no comparison with GANs or normalizing flows. The choice of models is not justified, and the rationale for using different image encoders (CLIP, DINOv2) at different generation stages is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **No exploration of synergy between internal and external generative models**: The two setups are treated separately; combining them (hybrid approach) is not investigated, especially for limited labeled data (Reviewers 5,6).
- **Computational cost ignored**: No details on GPU hours, memory, or energy consumption for training generative models and generating synthetic datasets. Practical feasibility for resource-constrained environments is not assessed; Figure 5 could benefit from a discussion of associated computational costs (Reviewers 1,5,6).
- **Limited evaluation metrics**: Only accuracy is reported. Missing robustness to adversarial attacks, generalization to out-of-distribution samples, and variance across runs. Table 1 only shows basic comparisons without investigating the impact of model size or architecture (Reviewers 4,5,6).
- **Missing class-level analysis**: The effect of the synthetic-to-real ratio may vary across classes; some classes may benefit more than others. This fine-grained analysis is absent (Reviewer 2).
- **Inadequate ablation on synthetic dataset size and class balance**: Systematic analysis of how results change with varying synthetic sizes and class balance is lacking (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing details**: In Equation (1), the number 15571.2 is unexplained (Reviewer 5). Percentage correct results on CIFAR-10 are requested (Reviewer 5).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: Notably, Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 6,5) found the paper well-written, rigorously executed, and providing practical guidelines; while Reviewers 3–6 (scores 3) considered the novelty insufficient and the analysis too limited to make a strong contribution.
