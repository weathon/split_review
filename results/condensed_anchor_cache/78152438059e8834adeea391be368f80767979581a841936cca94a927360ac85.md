## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MoEDM (Mixture of Expert Diffusion Models), a personalization method that applies layer-wise pruning (trimming bottom layers of the U-Net), duplicates remaining layers with selective activation based on timestep, and employs knowledge distillation to reduce data dependency. Dynamic routing selectively activates neurons to improve sampling speed while maintaining performance. Experiments are conducted on ImageNet and FFHQ at 64×64 and 256×256 resolutions, with FID and KID metrics. Text-to-image results are shown but with limited examples. Three reviewers gave a rating of 3 (fair) and one reviewer gave a rating of 5 (good), indicating a minority positive view.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles the critically important and practically relevant problem of expediting diffusion models, with broad application potential.
- The concept of integrating Mixture of Experts (MoE) into diffusion models is an intriguing avenue for investigation.
- The proposed method can incorporate model acceleration techniques such as DPM-Solver.
- The paper stands as a pioneering study in attempting to build mixtures of diffusion expert models, backed by a fair amount of experiments.
- The sparse MoE structure enhances inference efficiency for text-to-image generation tasks; experimental results show good inference performance (according to Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The technical novelty is weak:
  - Layer removal has precedents (e.g., SDXL).
  - MoE in diffusion models is not new (e.g., EDiff‑I; RAPHAEL: “Text-to-Image Generation via Large Mixture of Diffusion Paths”).
  - Sparse pruning with MoE is widely used (e.g., “Task-Specific Expert Pruning for Sparse Mixture-of-Experts”).
  - Use of distillation appears tangential; the rationale for integrating it with the other efficiency-oriented methods is unclear.
- **Poor presentation and lack of technical detail.**
  - Terms such as “all-encompassing arsenal” are introduced but never explained.
  - The claim that deploying a general-purpose diffusion model is “inefficient and wasteful” is not adequately justified, and Figure 1 does not illustrate the point; similarly, the claim that existing methods “fall short in preserving performance” is made without supporting qualitative or quantitative experiments.
  - “Minimal computational cost” is not defined.
  - In Section 3.1, the statement that convolutional layers “constitute approximately 80% of the model’s parameters” is given without the calculation method.
  - It is unclear how the channel sensitivity metric $\mathcal{S}_c$ is calculated and what its computational overhead is.
  - Whether the gating vector $\mathcal{G}$ is trainable or how it is determined is not specified.
  - The pruning algorithm is not explicitly described.
  - Dynamic gating for constructing the mixture of experts lacks clarity: MoE typically involves training with a load‑balancing loss, but no such details are provided.
  - After removing intermediate layers, it is not clearly stated whether the model is fine‑tuned with the original dataset; loss functions are not expanded upon.
  - Expert balance operation needs clarification.
- **Insufficient experimental validation.**
  - Efficiency gains appear largely attributable to intermediate layer removal; the relative contribution of the other innovations is unclear.
  - Experiments are limited to a single class from ImageNet, not demonstrating generalizability.
  - Text-to-image results only show a few curated images; broader comparisons are needed.
  - Only 64×64 and 256×256 resolutions are provided; diffusion models can generate 512×512 (e.g., Stable Diffusion). Performance for higher resolutions appears only marginally improved.
  - Comparisons with model acceleration methods are missing: DPM‑Solver, DPM‑Solver++, DDIM, ToMe.
  - Comparisons with existing personalization methods are missing: CustomDiffusion, DreamBooth.
  - Comparisons with pruning baselines are missing (e.g., Fang et al., “Structural Pruning for Diffusion Models”).
  - Ablation on the number of experts is not provided; most experiments show a consistent 50% inference speed boost.
  - Whether the method applies to architectures beyond U‑Net (e.g., DiT) is uncertain.
  - The evaluation metrics FID and KID are actually insensitive to generated image quality and cannot evaluate image quality well (Reviewer 4).
- **Computational cost concerns.** The method aims to reduce computational cost, yet training requires 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, and even sampling requires a single A100 with 80GB of memory.
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms / missing experiments.** (These are incorporated above: e.g., missing comparisons with acceleration/personalization methods, missing ablations, unclear details about pruning/gating, why “setting diffusion models to zero works.”)
