## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ISG, a multi-level evaluation framework for interleaved text-and-image generation, along with ISG-Bench (1,150 samples, 21 tasks) and ISG-Agent, a compositional agent using a “plan-execute-refine” pipeline. The framework evaluates at holistic, structural, block, and image levels via atomic question-answering. Experiments show that unified models underperform, while compositional approaches (including ISG-Agent) achieve large improvements but remain suboptimal on block and image levels. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty, coverage, and timely nature of the benchmark, but raise concerns about evaluation methodology, reliance on MLLM judges, task selection criteria, missing experimental details, and the lack of error analysis and future direction discussion.

### Strengths
- First comprehensive benchmark and multi-granular evaluation framework for interleaved text-and-image generation, filling a notable gap.
- ISG-Bench provides a large standardized dataset (1,150 samples, 21 task categories) covering a wide range of generation scenarios, offering options for fine-grained analysis.
- The multi-level evaluation (holistic, structural, block, image) with atomic question-answering addresses the unreliability of pure MLLM scoring and provides interpretable feedback.
- ISG-Agent is a novel, reasonable baseline using planning, tool-use, and refinement, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the benchmark.
- The analysis provides insights such as MLLM-as-a-Judge biases (e.g., “image-quality bias”).
- The paper is well-written and organized, with sufficient discussion of related work and evaluation pipeline.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing experimental details**: Descriptions of model configurations, training procedures (epochs, learning rates, batch sizes, optimizers), and hardware are absent, hindering reproducibility. The process for generating VQA and reasoning questions is vague. *(Reviewer 1; high severity)*
- **Reliance on MLLM judges introduces biases and cascading errors**: Using GPT-4o for both question generation and answer verification can produce flawed questions that invalidate subsequent evaluation. Biases like “image-quality bias” are acknowledged but not mitigated; no human study is conducted to assess alignment with human judgment. The evaluation remains noisy similar to pure MLLM-scoring. *(Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 5; high severity, strong consensus)*
- **Task definition and selection criteria are unclear**: The benchmark lacks explicit rationale for which categories are included, how sample counts (50/100) are determined, and whether an “ideal” distribution exists. Some tasks (e.g., Image Decomposition, simple segmentation) are overly simplistic and resemble basic computer vision tasks, diluting the benchmark’s focus on the unique challenges of interleaved generation. *(Reviewers 3, 5; moderate severity, minority concern from R3)*
- **Benchmark may be a combination of existing approaches**: The framework appears to combine elements from prior works (e.g., OpenLeaf, InterleavedBench) and the most important metric (holistic evaluation) has already been studied. This raises questions about incremental contribution. *(Reviewer 4; moderate severity, minority view)*
- **Lack of error analysis for ISG-Agent and limited future direction insight**: The paper does not dissect failure cases (e.g., which visual reasoning or generation steps fail, bottlenecks in the pipeline, error propagation between modules). Experiments do not suggest whether unified or agent-based approaches are more promising, nor do they compare with closed-source unified models like GPT-4o demos to illuminate relative strengths and weaknesses. *(Reviewers 1, 5; high severity)*
- **Evaluation complexity and unified metric possibility**: The multi-step, multi-MLLM evaluation is complex; it remains unclear whether a single MLLM could evaluate all granularities in a simpler, unified way. *(Reviewers 3, 4; moderate severity)*
- **Benchmark suitability for unified models**: Since ISG-Agent (compositional) performs well, the tasks may not be ideally suited to test unified models. The benchmark might better serve evaluation of agent systems. *(Reviewer 3; moderate severity, minority)*
- **Additional concerns**: 
  - ISG-Agent should be compared with other agent systems, not only with end-to-end models. *(Reviewer 2)*
  - The cost and resource requirements for evaluation (multiple GPT-4 calls) are not reported, making it unclear how comparable ISG-Agent’s cost is to direct generation models like Show-o. *(Reviewer 2)*
  - No ablation study examines the importance of images in the queries (e.g., text-only input setting). *(Reviewer 2)*
  - Table 1 lists 30 samples for OpenLeaf (An et al., 2023) while the original paper states 660; this discrepancy needs explanation. *(Reviewer 5)*
