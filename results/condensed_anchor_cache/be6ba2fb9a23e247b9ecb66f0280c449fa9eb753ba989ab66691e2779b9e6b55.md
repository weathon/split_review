## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates hallucinations in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by analyzing “anchor tokens” and the eigenspectrum of the attention weight matrix. It finds that over-propagation of anchor tokens, driven by polarized variance in the query/key eigenspectrum, causes the model to neglect visual information and hallucinate. Based on this, the authors propose Dynamic Token Propagation Mechanism (TAME), a plug-and-play decoding strategy that dynamically intervenes in the eigenspectrum variance to reduce over-propagation without adding inference time. Experiments on InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, and Shikra show consistent improvements on CHAIR, POPE, and GPT-4 assisted hallucination evaluation, and TAME can be integrated with existing decoding methods (Beam, VCD, ICD, SID).

### Strengths
- Strong motivation with in-depth theoretical analysis linking anchor token propagation, eigenspectrum variance, and hallucinations.
- Simple, plug-and-play decoding strategy (TAME) that incurs no additional inference time.
- Consistently improves hallucination metrics across InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, and Shikra on CHAIR, POPE, and GPT-4 assisted evaluation, surpassing baselines like Greedy Decoding and VCD.
- Easily integrates with multiple decoding strategies (Beam, VCD, ICD, SID) without requiring additional training or data.
- Extensive experiments validate both the causal analysis and the effectiveness of the proposed method.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty** – The impact of anchor tokens on LVLM hallucination was first proposed by OPERA [1]; the paper does not adequately distinguish its additional contribution.
2. **Limited hallucination types** – TAME primarily addresses object hallucinations but shows limited improvement on the MME benchmark, suggesting weak performance on attribute/relation hallucinations. A granular analysis of different attribute hallucination types (color, size, material) is needed.
3. **Simplicity of evaluation benchmarks** – The paper uses only simple evaluation sets like MS COCO; more challenging benchmarks are recommended.
4. **Missing side-effect metrics** – Only hallucination metrics are reported. Less hallucination may lead to less detail or reduced user-friendliness; the method’s impact on other quality metrics should be measured and discussed.
5. **Output length vs. perception trade-off** – Qualitative results show TAME tends to shorten outputs; this could cause insufficient image perception and missing details. How to measure and balance this trade-off is not addressed.
6. **Missing layer ablation** – The paper does not study applying TAME to specific layers vs. all layers; optimal layer configuration and potential computational efficiency gains are unexplored.
7. **Figure clarity** – Color schemes in Figures 4 and 5 are confusing; higher contrast and more distinct differentiation are needed.
8. **Typos** – Line 301: “Conversly” → “Conversely”; Line 333: “7,3” → “7.3”.
9. **Unclear definitions** – The introduction mentions “summary tokens” without defining them, leaving the distinction between summary tokens and anchor tokens unclear.
10. **Limited method explanation** – The method is presented via a single formula; a smoother transition from the theoretical derivation and clearer description of application scenarios are needed.
11. **Empirical-only analysis** – The paper does not discuss the underlying causes or triggers of the observed phenomena, relying solely on empirical analysis.
12. **Title scope mismatch** – The method appears applicable to any LLM, yet the title claims it only for multimodal LLMs.
13. **Baseline realism** – In Table 1, baselines with max token 512 generate very high hallucination rates (>30%); it is unclear whether such numbers align with real user experiences (e.g., Gemini, ChatGPT).
14. **Writing quality** – Many citations are not in the correct format and the text would benefit from refinement.
