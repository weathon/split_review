## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a semantic invariant watermark for large language models (LLMs) that uses an auxiliary embedding LLM to generate semantic embeddings of all preceding tokens, which are then transformed into watermark logits by a trained watermark model. This approach aims to balance attack robustness (especially against synonym substitution and paraphrasing) and security robustness (against reverse-engineering attacks like Sadasivan et al.). The method is novel but reviewers raised significant concerns about detection practicality, statistical rigor, experimental comparisons, and adaptive attack vulnerability.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea with good motivation**: Using a semantic representation of previous tokens to determine watermark logits (rather than a hash of the n-gram) is novel and well-motivated for resistance to paraphrasing attacks. The method mimics the green-list/red-list partition (Kirchenbauer et al.) via embedding and mapping networks producing ±1 logit perturbations, and the training scheme for the watermark network is intuitive.  
- **Improved robustness trade-off**: Compared to KGW, the method achieves similar or better robustness against established attacks while maintaining similar or better perplexity and increasing robustness against the reverse-engineering attack of Sadasivan et al. The approach provides a better trade-off between attack robustness and security.  
- **Computational efficiency**: Watermark logits can be generated in parallel with LLM logits, causing only marginal latency increase during generation.  
- **High detection accuracy**: Achieves high accuracy in detecting text generated by LLMs under the tested settings.

### Weaknesses
- **Detection requires language model inference and prompt access (major limitation)**: The detection algorithm (Section 4.3) necessitates the logits of tokens, mandating language model inference during detection. This is impractical for users with restricted computational resources and extends detection time significantly compared to KGW. More critically, accessing LLM logits implies knowledge of the prompt, which is often unfeasible in real-world scenarios (e.g., analyzing text from unknown sources). This dependence on the prompt severely limits practical applicability. (Reviewer 4 emphasized this as a fundamental flaw; Reviewer 3 also noted higher detection overhead.)  
- **Lack of rigorous false-positive analysis**: The detection method does not provide a stringent statistical affirmation about the false positive rate, unlike KGW. This leaves it vulnerable to misidentification of non-watermarked text as watermarked, a critical concern in practice. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Weak experimental comparisons to the most robust baseline (KGW-1)**: The authors compare against KGW-k with larger k values, which obscures true performance. KGW-1 is more robust to text modifications (since each token affects only the next token's green/red list). In beam search scenarios (Table 1), KGW-1 outperforms SIR, raising questions about the necessity of SIR when KGW-1 already shows superior robustness, efficiency, and a more systematic detection methodology. The paper also does not systematically explore the impact of parameters δ and γ on the proposed method, nor report how these were set for KGW-k. (Reviewer 4, echoed by Reviewer 3 on beam search weakness)  
- **Limited attack scenarios considered**: The paper addresses only synonym substitution and paraphrasing (Section 6.2) and the Sadasivan et al. attack (Section 6.3). It does not evaluate attacks like Emoji attack, discrete perturbations, or syntactic changes from Kirchenbauer et al. (ICML 2023). A more comprehensive range of attack scenarios is advisable. (Reviewer 2)  
- **No evaluation of adaptive attacks leveraging knowledge of the method**: Could a motivated attacker use a semantic embedding model of their own (even without access to the watermark model) to better estimate or remove the watermark? The tight correspondence between semantic embeddings and watermark logits might make such an adaptive attack feasible, possibly requiring fewer observed contexts than KGM-n. This constitutes a serious security flaw. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Risk of repetitive text generation**: The semantic embeddings may become insufficiently sensitive as the context grows (new tokens add small changes), causing the same logits to be repeatedly boosted and leading to repetitive text. The example in Appendix A appears more repetitive than KGW examples. The evaluation is limited to perplexity; no metrics for repetitiveness or other quality aspects are reported. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Susceptibility to prefix injection attacks**: An adversary could introduce an adversarial prefix to significantly alter the semantic meaning of an entire paragraph, potentially breaking the watermark. Also, if users share generated text online without including the prompt (e.g., prompt injection with "ignore everything before"), the method's robustness is unclear. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Detection equation (7) is unclear**: The notation appears to index by time step rather than by observed token, making it ambiguous whether detection works as described (i.e., checking if the watermark logit entry for the observed token is 1). Better notation or explanation is needed. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Multiple keys/users require separate watermark models**: To support multiple keys, the model provider must train a separate watermark model per key, which is expensive. It is also unclear how to ensure sufficiently distinct watermark logits for each key and maintain distinguishability during detection. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Limited generation length (200 tokens) and absence of longer sequence experiments**: All experiments use only 200 tokens. Longer sequences may affect both robustness against paraphrasing and text quality (e.g., more repetitive). The impact of different fixed context lengths for the semantic embedder (short vs. long) on robustness, security, and text quality is not explored. Longer context also has implications for cut-and-paste attacks. (Reviewers 1 and 3)  
- **Practical definitions and applications of the watermark are not elaborated**: The concept of "watermark" in the LLM scope, the reconstruction process (Section 4.3), the theoretical or practical capacity of the watermark, and real-world application scenarios (e.g., copyright identification) are not sufficiently explained. (Reviewer 2)  
- **No exploration of watermark logit symmetry at individual time steps**: Figure 2c shows the distribution of watermark logits is very symmetric (≈50% ±1) in aggregate, but it is not shown whether individual time steps deviate significantly from this even split. (Reviewer 1, question)
