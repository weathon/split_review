## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes FDA, a framework to generate synthetic data that balances fairness (Demographic Parity) and faithfulness to the original labels. It uses two sub-models: M_fair (enforces fairness) and M_faithful (preserves fidelity). The trade-off is controlled by a user-specified parameter α∈[0,1]. The authors provide theoretical convergence guarantees and an upper bound on downstream unfairness. Experiments are conducted on UCI Adult and COMPAS tabular datasets, comparing against FairGAN and DECAF. One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) found the theoretical contributions strong and the framework promising; the other three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) raise significant concerns about incremental novelty, limited evaluation, and the method’s scope.

### Strengths
- The framework provides explicit, controllable trade-off between fairness and faithfulness, supported by theoretical proofs (convergence in probability as α→1, unfairness bound for downstream models).  
- Can be combined with GAN-based methods (e.g., DECAF) to improve synthetic data utility in downstream tasks while balancing fairness.  
- The problem of fair synthetic data generation with provable guarantees is timely and relevant.  
- The paper is well-written and organized (noted by two reviewers); the algorithm and goal are clearly stated.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental innovation relative to prior work** (Reviewer 2, 3): The modeling of M_faithful and M_fair closely resembles M_assoc and M_mask from Jiang et al. (2021). Specific similarities include Eq.(4) vs. Eq.(4) in [1], Eq.(2) vs. Eqs.(1,2) in [1], Fig.1 in both papers, and Algorithm 1 vs. Steps 0–3 of Algorithm 1 in [1]. The paper’s positioning against fair representation learning and other controllable fairness methods (e.g., Agarwal et al., 2018) is insufficiently clarified.
- **Limited and potentially unfair empirical evaluation**: Experiments only use two tabular datasets (UCI Adult, COMPAS). No evaluation on image datasets (e.g., CelebA, Waterbirds) is provided, limiting generalizability. Baselines (FairGAN, TabFairGAN, DECAF) generate full synthetic data (X, Y, S), while FDA only transforms the target label Y. This makes the comparison unequal (Reviewer 4). Additionally, hyperparameter tuning plots (Figs. 2,3) vary α only for FDA; baselines are shown as constants. More extensive comparisons, including with methods that allow controllable fairness-accuracy trade-offs (e.g., Agarwal et al.), would better highlight FDA’s advantages and limitations.
- **Method only generates labels, not the full joint distribution** (Reviewer 3, 4): Synthetic data differs from original only in the target variable Y; features X and sensitive attribute S remain unchanged. This limits applicability (e.g., scenarios where marginal label generation is needed are not motivated). The distribution of (Ŷ, X, S) will not match the original (Y, X, S), so the goal of generative modeling is not met. If Y is changed significantly and a downstream model fits Ŷ closely, prediction performance on that instance will be poor.
- **Scalability concerns** (Reviewer 1): The method relies on hyperparameters σ² and M. Reported runtime on Adult (63k samples, 14 features, Core i5-12600K, 16 threads) is 1.3 seconds (~23μs/instance). Scaled to image datasets (e.g., 784 features), this may not be efficient. No computational shortcuts or approximations are discussed.
- **Only Demographic Parity (DP) is considered** (Reviewers 1,2,3): The framework and experiments focus solely on DP. Alternative fairness metrics (Equalized Odds, Predictive Parity, Min-max fairness) are not explored, limiting practical usage.
- **Potential theoretical issue with Eq.(6)** (Reviewer 4): When M→∞, the distribution in Eq.(6) appears to degenerate to a point mass at 0, not at Y_i. The correctness of the proposed distribution should be clarified.
- **Lack of practical guidance for hyperparameters** (Reviewer 1): No heuristics are provided for setting σ² and M to achieve optimal trade-offs.
- **Use of “data augmentation” is misleading** (Reviewer 3): The method only transforms the target variable, does not increase dataset size, and does not generate new features. The term is overstated and better clarified. “Faithfulness” refers only to faithfulness to the original target variable, not the full data distribution (Reviewer 3).
- **Benefit unclear when the fair model itself has controllable trade-off** (Reviewer 3): The method uses a fair model (Line 203) that already can balance fairness and predictive performance (e.g., Agarwal et al.). The added value of FDA is not well motivated.
- **Presentation and reproducibility issues** (Reviewer 2): Notation is unclear (e.g., “m ∈ M” undefined), and details for replicating M_fair and M_faithful are insufficient. A reproducibility statement is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Guarantee of user-specified fairness level** (Reviewer 3): Line 206 says f can be any fair model. If that model cannot achieve the desired DP (e.g., DP=0), the guarantee that α achieves the expected fairness level is questionable.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms** (folded into weaknesses above):  
  - How does FDA perform with high-dimensional datasets? (Scalability issue)  
  - How would it perform with alternative fairness measures (Equalized Odds, Predictive Parity)? (Only DP considered)  
  - What is the difference from training a fair model and using its predictions as Ŷ? (Method only generates labels)  
  - What is the main advantage of generating Y only vs. full joint generation? (Applicability limitation)  
  - How can FDA be combined with DECAF given they generate different components? (Clarity of combination)  
  - Does the method achieve a better fairness-DP tradeoff than post-processing methods? (Insufficient comparison)
