## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes External Knowledge Attention (EKA), which integrates an extra attention head into each LLM layer to dynamically fuse retrieved knowledge via a memory module. The approach uses vectorized knowledge representations (encoded by BERT) and controls knowledge injection across layers. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) found the work novel and experiments comprehensive; the other three reviewers (scores 3, confidence 4,3,4) were highly critical, citing methodological gaps, weak experimental design, outdated benchmarks, marginal gains, and overclaims. The majority view is that the paper has significant flaws.

### Strengths
- The core idea of injecting external knowledge through a separate attention head *within* each LLM layer (not just concatenation) is novel and interesting, especially applied to decoder-only models (most prior latent knowledge work used encoder-decoder models like T5) [R2].
- Using vectorized representations for knowledge retrieval enables a more nuanced, semantic-level integration with the LLM’s internal representations [R4].
- The memory module that dynamically adjusts knowledge integration across different LLM layers provides granular control over when and how external information influences reasoning [R4].
- The experiments and ablation studies are set up systematically and demonstrate the design choices of the proposed components (knowledge attention, memory, layer-wise fusion) [R3]; one reviewer called them "comprehensive" [R1].

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair and weak experimental setup.** Baselines in Table 4 use different backbone models and some are optimized with training data while others are not; the table has missing data. Improvements over base LLaMA are only 1–4% (Tables 2,3,4), which is marginal given the extra training/inference costs [R3].
- **Datasets are outdated and not standard for LLM QA evaluation.** General-domain evaluations use older datasets; the community typically benchmarks on Natural Questions, TriviaQA, MuSiQue, and Mallen et al. (2023). Multi-hop datasets are small and simple (except GrailQA) [R1, R3].
- **Methodology is poorly described and has inconsistencies.** Notations are inconsistent (e.g., \(s_{l,u}\) vs \(s_{l,n}\), \(f_K\) vs \(f_{KA}\), \(H^S\) differs in Eq.3 vs L145, Eq.9 error). Equation 2 has an error (\(s_i\) should be \(s_u\)). The difference between \(f_{KA}\) (L101) and \(f_K\) (Eq.1) is unclear. Equation 1's subtraction is unexplained [R2, R3].
- **Intuition is not validated and claims lack support.** The claim that standard RAG suffers from "less converged integration" is not cited or experimentally verified. The paper does not directly test whether the proposed method resolves this claimed issue [R2].
- **Missing ablations and justifications.** The removal of positional embeddings (L131) is not justified and no ablation is provided. It is unclear how a single knowledge instance is encoded as a *single* vector (BERT outputs sequences). The choice of BERT over more advanced encoders is justified [R2, R1?] — all reviewers note this gap.
- **Overclaims and missing details.** The paper claims "works with various pre-trained LLMs" but only tests LLaMA 7B/13B. It states three runs with average/std but this is not shown in results [R3]. The approach is closer to fine-tuning than instruction tuning; performance on non-RAG tasks is not preserved or evaluated [R2].
- **No robustness or transferability analysis.** The paper does not test the method under noisy or irrelevant retrieved content, nor compare to noise-resistant baselines. The model's ability to handle conflicting information from multiple sources is not addressed [R4]. Transferability across datasets (e.g., train on WebQSP, test on GrailQA) is not shown [R1, R3].
- **Scalability concerns and retraining cost.** It is unclear whether new attention/parameter matrices must be trained for each LLM, limiting practical applicability. How the method scales to very large retrieval contexts (beyond BERT's limit) or complex questions is not discussed [R4]. The architecture's complexity (memory modules, extra attention) may hinder scaling [R1].
- **Missing tests.** The approach should be tested on more recent models (e.g., Llama3) and additional multi-hop datasets (HotpotQA, MuSiQue) [R1]. Relevant recent memory-based RAG methods are not cited [R2].
- **Presentation errors.** Figure reference is missing (L109: "Figure ??"); Table 4 and Table 5 have redundant caption explanations [R1].
