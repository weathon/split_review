## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Toto, a decoder-only transformer-based foundation model for time series forecasting, with a focus on observability metrics. Key contributions include a proportional factorized space-time attention mechanism that alternates space-wise and time-wise blocks, and a Student-T mixture model (SMM) head for probabilistic forecasting. The model is trained on one trillion data points (75% proprietary observability data from Datadog, plus open-source and synthetic data). It is evaluated on public long-sequence forecasting (LSF) benchmarks and a private observability benchmark. Scores: 3, 5, 3, 3; one reviewer (R2) is notably more positive, praising clarity and the dataset contribution, while the other three find the technical contributions incremental and the experiments insufficient.

### Strengths
- The application to time series forecasting in observability is valuable and addresses an important practical problem; using a foundation model that can be deployed without local fitting is a sound approach.
- The design of proportional factorized space-time attention (alternating channel-mixing and time-wise blocks) is described clearly and offers potentially efficient processing of multivariate series.
- The proprietary observability dataset captures characteristics absent from existing datasets; this is a step toward unified benchmarking.
- The paper is well-written, concise, and provides an insightful discussion of observability-specific data properties.
- The dual perspective (space-wise + time-wise) attention and pre-training validation are positive aspects.
- R2 (score 5) specifically notes the writing style and clear methodology as strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation**: The paper’s objective is ambiguous. If the goal is an observability model, why a foundation model rather than any deep forecasting backbone trained on similar data? If generalization, more comprehensive benchmarks are needed (e.g., Chronos benchmark; LSF is too simplistic). If efficiency of factorized attention, standard training suffices.
- **Incremental technical novelty**: The factorized attention is a minor modification of Arnab et al. (ICCV 2021); the Student-T mixture head is an extension of the mixture distribution already used in Woo et al. (ICML 2024) and Moirai, and GluonTS provides a generic mixture implementation. The contributions are too incremental for ICLR.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
    - Only 6 public datasets are used, of which 4 are ETT variants (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2) – too narrow for a foundation model. A broader benchmark (e.g., Chronos’s) is needed.
    - The evaluation setup is inconsistent: For the LSF benchmark (Table 1), Toto uses a rolling stride of 512, while baselines (e.g., Moirai, TimesFM) are reported with stride 1 from earlier work (Zhou et al. AAAI 2021, Woo et al. ICML 2024). This difference in number of evaluation windows may inflate Toto’s performance, making results incomparable.
    - For the observability benchmark, only zero-shot baselines are compared. Since Toto is pre-trained on 75% observability data, it is not surprising it outperforms. Fairer comparisons require fine-tuned versions of baselines, as well as task-specific deep models (TFT, TiDE, etc.) and classical models.
    - No empirical evidence of the computational efficiency of the factorized attention is provided.
    - Missing baselines in zero-shot setting: Chronos, Timer; in full-shot: TimeMixer; for observability: Timer.
    - Percentage metrics (e.g., MASE, sMAPE) are often discouraged in forecasting literature (Hyndman & Koehler, 2006).
- **Lack of reproducibility**: The proprietary observability dataset and the model weights are not released. The code was not made available to reviewers, limiting verification.
- **Missing experimental details**:
    - Historical context length not specified in Section 4.1; sampling strategy and quantity not given for Section 4.2.
    - For the observability benchmark, prediction length and historical context window configurations for Toto and baselines are unclear.
    - Sampling strategy for Chronos and Moirai in Section 4.2 is not justified; why follow original sampling? Is a sampling quantity of 20 fair for Chronos?
    - Sampling quantity for Toto in Section 4.2 is not stated (is it 200?). How would increasing sampling quantity affect baselines?
    - The claim (line 303) that “for more precise tails, we can choose to spend more computation on sampling” is not demonstrated in experiments.
    - No parameter sensitivity analysis is performed.
    - The paper does not conduct any serious investigation or ablation of the model’s components.
- **Minor**: The term “foundation model” loses meaning with a domain-specific qualifier like “observability”; “pretrained model” would be more appropriate.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: R2 (score 5) is more positive and sees value in the dataset and methodology, but the other three reviewers (scores 3,3,3) find the contributions marginal and the experimental validation weak. R3 and R4 explicitly state they would raise scores if the LSF evaluation is corrected and the benchmark expanded, respectively.
