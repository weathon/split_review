## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes second-order algorithms LEN (for convex-concave) and LEN-restart (for strongly-convex-strongly-concave) for minimax optimization. The algorithms combine lazy Hessian updates (reusing the Hessian for \(m\) iterations) from Doikov et al. (2023) with extragradient steps from Adil et al. (2022) and Lin et al. (2022). For convex-concave problems, the method achieves a computational complexity of \(\tilde{O}((N+d^2)(d + d^{2/3}\epsilon^{-2/3}))\), improving over previous methods by a factor of \(d^{1/3}\). For strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems with condition number \(\kappa\), the complexity is \(\tilde{O}((N+d^2)(d + d^{2/3}\kappa^{2/3}))\), again enjoying a \(d^{1/3}\) speedup. The results are well-organized and supported by numerical experiments on both real and synthetic datasets. Reviewers gave scores 8,8,8,6; the paper was accepted.

### Strengths
- **Better computational complexity.** The proposed LEN and LEN-restart algorithms improve the computational complexity over existing second-order methods by a factor of \(d^{1/3}\), while maintaining the same iteration complexity (Reviewers 1, 3). The improvement holds for both convex-concave and strongly-convex-strongly-concave cases.
- **Well-structured and clear presentation.** The paper is easy to follow, with a logical flow and clear exposition of technical details (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Strong mathematical content and original analysis.** The paper develops new technical tricks to handle errors from lazy Hessian updates within the extragradient framework for minimax problems. One reviewer (Reviewer 2) found the results mathematically strong, original, and well-presented, noting that the analysis requires “principally new ideas” despite building on prior work.
- **Practical appeal.** The method reduces computational cost without sacrificing oracle complexity, making it more attractive for high-dimensional problems. Experimental results support the theoretical improvements (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Interesting extension of lazy Hessians to minimax.** It is non-trivial to see that the lazy Hessian idea from convex optimization (Doikov et al. 2023) carries over to saddle-point problems, and the paper demonstrates this successfully (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / incremental nature.** The core idea combines two existing lines of work (lazy Hessians from Doikov et al. (2023) and extragradient methods from Adil et al. (2022), Lin et al. (2022)). Three reviewers (1, 3, 4) considered the work incremental, lacking fundamentally new ideas. The main novelty lies in the technical proof details for bounding errors from lazy updates in the extragradient setting, but the high-level approach is not new (Reviewer 4). (In contrast, Reviewer 2 found the paper original; this minority view is noted in Strengths.)
- **Weak motivation for second-order methods in minimax.** The motivation for using second-order (rather than first-order) methods in convex-concave minimax is weak. The iteration complexity difference between first- and second-order methods is minor for saddle-point problems, unlike in convex optimization. The analysis focuses on \(\epsilon\)-dependence but does not adequately account for the much higher per-iteration cost of second-order methods (Hessian computation and inversion). Constant factors hidden in big-O notation may be large, making first-order methods more practical for reasonable error tolerances (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of auxiliary subproblem accuracy.** The paper does not discuss the required accuracy for solving the auxiliary subproblem (e.g., the cubic-regularized subproblem) to guarantee the desired precision for the main problem (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation.**  
  - The claimed \(O(d^{1/3})\) improvement is only significant in high dimensions, but no experiments on low-dimensional problems are provided to verify the pattern (Reviewer 1).  
  - The choice of \(m\) (number of iterations between Hessian updates) affects performance, and its effect is not explored experimentally. Reviewer 1 suggested following Doikov et al. (2023) to add such experiments.  
  - The theory uses the gap function as the convergence measure, while experiments report gradient norm or point distance. The paper should clarify the connection (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited scope of problem classes.** The results are restricted to convex-concave (and strongly-convex-strongly-concave) minimax problems. There is no clear extension to nonconvex-nonconcave functions, variational inequalities, or settings with different strong convexity/concavity constants (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 4 specifically asked if bounding the MVI error directly could allow extension to general MVIs.
- **Missing discussion of lower bounds.** The paper does not discuss computational lower bounds, despite existing complicated lower bounds on iteration complexity for these problems (Adil et al. 2022). A lower bound in computational complexity would help contextualize the achieved improvement (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues.**  
  - The derivation of the second inequality in eq. (17) is unclear (Reviewer 3).  
  - The sentence on lines 155–160 was difficult to read and should be rewritten (Reviewer 3).  
  - Minor typo: in Definition 3.3, the middle of the inequality should be \(f(x^\star, y^\star)\) (Reviewer 4).
