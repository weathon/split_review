## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CREMA, a generalizable and efficient modular modality-fusion framework for video-language reasoning. It augments multiple modalities (e.g., depth, optical flow, audio, point cloud, thermal, touch) from videos without extra human annotation, uses a Q-Former with modality-specific parameter-efficient modules to extract features, and introduces a self-gated multimodal query fusion with sequential training and adaptive early exit. Evaluated on 7 tasks (including MUSIC-AVQA, SQA3D, NExT-QA, TouchQA, ThermalQA), CREMA outperforms or matches strong multimodal LLMs (OneLLM, BLIP-2, SeViLA) while reducing over 90% trainable parameters. Two reviewers (out of five) rate it 8, praising the innovation and broad applicability; the other three rate it 6, citing concerns about fusion flexibility, analysis depth, and novelty.

### Strengths
- **Generalizable, modular framework**: Handles multiple modalities while controlling token count and parameter growth. A single Q-Former processes all modalities with small, independent modality-specific parameters, avoiding interference. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Modality-sequential training**: Prevents overfitting/underfitting to any specific modality. (R1, R3)
- **Innovative architectural components**: Self-gated multimodal query fusion and modality-adaptive early exit—first use of gradients to decide early exit and first method for early stopping by modality. (R2)
- **Clear writing and easy to follow**. (R2, R3)
- **Broad evaluation across diverse domains**: Audio-video QA (MUSIC-AVQA), 3D situated QA (SQA3D), touch/thermal QA, etc. Competitive performance especially when many modalities are used. (R1, R3, R5)
- **Ablation studies**: On choice of early exit and modality fusion method (Tables 7, 8). (R5)
- **Parameter efficiency**: Over 90% reduction in trainable parameters vs. baselines. (R4)
- **Validated through extensive fine-tuning and zero-shot evaluations**. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Self-gated fusion inflexibility**: Concatenates tokens along channel dimension, so inference must use exactly the same modality combination as training (e.g., if point cloud was used during training, it must be present at inference). This limits flexibility for missing modalities. (R1)
- **Zero-shot setup unclear**: Which datasets were used for training/optimization before zero-shot evaluation on SQA3D and MUSIC-AVQA? Which specific model variant was used for each zero-shot experiment? (R1)
- **Missing related works**: Does not discuss MultiPLY and X-VILA, both multimodal LLMs handling various input modalities. (R1)
- **Lacks analysis of why multi-modality helps**: e.g., depth/surface normal aiding MUSIC-AVQA (Table 1) is unintuitive. No visualization or attention analysis (e.g., self-gate scores) to show model reliance on each modality. (R3, R4)
- **Confounding capacity vs. modality benefit**: Adding depth and normal increases trainable parameters from 9M to 38M. Gains may come from increased capacity, not the new modalities. A controlled experiment varying unimodal parameter count is needed. (R3)
- **Unfair comparison due to different encoders**: Uses pre-trained encoders (ZoeDepth, Unimatch, NLL-AngMF, BEATs, ViT-G) unknown to baselines; more explanation needed to verify fair comparison. (R5)
- **Limited novelty**: Model-specific queries and fusion are subtle technical changes. (R5)
- **Prioritizing video as primary modality lacks quantitative justification**: Sensitivity analysis across tasks and modality combinations is missing. (R4)
- **Q-Former fixed-length tokens may discard important details**: Claim that it removes irrelevant information is qualitative; needs empirical evidence (e.g., ablation on token lengths). (R4)
- **Back-propagation decomposition by modality**: May limit cross-modal interactions; it is a trade-off for efficiency, but the impact on reasoning quality is not quantified. (R4)
- **Adaptive early exit equation unclear (line 284)**: Gradient > threshold triggers exit, but gradient scale is large early and small near convergence; the condition seems reversed. (R5)
- **Fusion equation (3) uses sigmoid**: Appears only to scale the video query; justification or necessity not provided. (R5)
- **Applicability to advanced baselines**: Not demonstrated beyond BLIP (e.g., LLaVA). (R2)
- **Missing total parameter count and inference speed** (only trainable parameters reported). (R3, Q2)
- **Why called video-language model?**: Tasks like SQA3D use RGBD input, not necessarily video. (R3, Q1)
- **Question about handling remote sensing imagery**: Would specific adaptations be needed? (R4, Q6)
- **Why fully connected layer (dashed box in Fig. 1) for dimension alignment?**: A lighter-weight alternative like interpolation is not compared. (R4, Q2)
