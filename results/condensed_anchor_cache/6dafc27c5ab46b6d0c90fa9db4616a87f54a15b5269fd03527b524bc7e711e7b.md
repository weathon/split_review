## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the first DP algorithms for bilevel optimization in the central model. Unlike prior work (Chen, 2024, which uses local DP and second-order information), the proposed method is fully first-order, avoids Hessian computations, and provides (ε,δ)-DP guarantees for both empirical and population losses under smooth upper-level and strongly convex lower-level objectives. A mini-batch variant is also analyzed. Two reviewers (ratings 8) view the contribution as clear and the paper well-written, while a third (rating 5) finds the practical significance unclear and the experimental validation missing. A fourth (rating 6) also requests empirical evidence and a deeper discussion of technical challenges.

### Strengths
- First DP algorithm for bilevel optimization that is fully first-order (avoids Hessians) and operates under central DP, covering both ERM and population risk.
- The framework works with different inner algorithms, depending only on their convergence rate and DP parameters.
- The paper is well-organized, with clear proofs and a mini-batch variant that shares similar convergence properties.
- Contribution is timely and addresses an important problem (private bilevel optimization), as noted by multiple reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of empirical evaluation:** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2) note the absence of any experiments, which is uncommon and limits assessment of practical effectiveness. The authors defer experiments to future work.
- **Insufficient explanation of technical novelty:** R1 and R2 request a clearer description of the technical challenges when moving from non-private to private bilevel optimization, and whether DP introduces significant additional complexities beyond assembling known tools. R3 remarks that the analysis appears to be “a process of assembling the right tools and carefully applying them,” lacking new ideas for private optimization.
- **Missing comparative analysis:** R2 points out that no clear comparison is made with existing methods (e.g., Chen, 2024, even though it operates under local DP). A question remains: When the scale of Gaussian noise is identical, can the performance between central and local DP methods be effectively compared?
- **Questionable claim of “any desired privacy”:** R2 argues that “adjusting a parameter to achieve a specific ε,δ value is almost always possible in all DP algorithms” and that meeting any privacy specification does not necessarily imply efficiency. The claim as stated is vague.
- **Reliance on strong convexity of the lower-level objective:** R4 (and a question from R2) notes that the assumption that g(x,y) is strongly convex in y is restrictive for many bilevel applications. The paper should discuss practical implications and whether this can be weakened to only convexity.
- **Potential for improvement in convergence guarantees:** R4 points out that the error rate for bilevel ERM and the additive factor on inverse batch size in the minibatch variant could be improved.
- **Computational cost:** R4 asks for a more detailed examination of the practical implementation and computational cost of calculating gradients in nested optimization, even if the method is fully first-order.
- **Terminology question:** R3 questions whether calling the gradient-norm guarantees “ERM” is standard for non-convex upper-level problems—the paper should clarify this usage.
