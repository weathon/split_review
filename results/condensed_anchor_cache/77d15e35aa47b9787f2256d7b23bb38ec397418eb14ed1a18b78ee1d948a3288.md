## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Disentanglement of Invariant Functions (DIF), a method to discover invariant dynamics (the shared physical law) across multiple environments where ODE-driven dynamical systems undergo interventions that change not only coefficients but also functional forms. DIF uses a hypernetwork-based encoder-decoder, grounded in a causal graph, to disentangle an invariant function \(f_c\) from environment-specific dynamics \(f_e\). An information-theoretic principle enforces independence between \(f_c\) and the environment. Experiments on three ODE systems (pendulum, damped pendulum, powered pendulum) show that DIF outperforms meta-learning and invariant learning baselines in predicting the shared dynamics, and symbolic regression (PySR) is applied post hoc to interpret the learned invariant function. The paper also introduces a new hypernetwork implementation that improves efficiency. Reviewers agree the problem is novel and important, but raise significant concerns about theoretical rigor, clarity, experimental scope, and restrictive assumptions.

### Strengths
- **Novel Problem and Setting:** Addresses a challenging and under-explored problem—learning invariant dynamics when environments differ beyond function coefficients (e.g., entirely different functional forms like damping or powered terms). This goes beyond prior work. (Reviewers 1, 4, 5)
- **Causal Perspective and Hypernetwork Design:** Grounds the method in a causal graph and uses a hypernetwork to explicitly disentangle invariant from environment-specific components. This provides a principled framework. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Information-Theoretic Principle:** The proposed independence constraint between extracted invariant functions and environments offers theoretical grounding and a clear optimization target. (Reviewer 3)
- **Empirical Performance:** DIF outperforms all baselines (meta-learning and invariant learning) on the tested ODE systems in terms of prediction accuracy and ability to recover the invariant function. The new hypernetwork implementation yields significant speedups. (Reviewers 2, 5)
- **Clarity and Figures (mixed):** The paper is well-written and easy to follow for some reviewers; the figures are appealing and illustrate key ideas. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; but Reviewer 5 strongly disagrees on clarity.)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical Issues and Insufficient Rigor:**
  - Theorem 3.1 is poorly stated: “satisfying equation 2” is unclear because equation 2 is a real number (mutual information), not a condition. Moreover, the mutual information between a Dirac delta (true function) and any other function is zero, undermining the theorem. (Reviewer 4)
  - The adversarial training used to enforce independence between \(f_c\) and the environment lacks a thorough theoretical justification; a more detailed analysis is needed to assess its robustness. (Reviewer 2)
  - Lemma 3.2 is imprecise: the equivalence between cross-entropy and MSE under Gaussian relaxation is not fully explained; the connection between objective values and minimizers is ambiguous. (Reviewer 4)
  - The claim that the causal mapping \(f \to X\) is injective is unrealistic; multiple functions can generate the same output on a finite interval. (Reviewer 4) The encoder-decoder framework is not injective in practice (dimensionality reduction), conflicting with the theoretical requirement. (Reviewer 3)
- **Restrictive Assumptions:**
  - **Assumption 1** (perfect similarity/deterministic knowledge of the intervention distribution) is extremely strong and essentially impossible to guarantee; the optimization may not converge to a reliable solution if \(P(X_p)\) is misspecified. (Reviewer 3)
  - The method requires explicit environment labels for each trajectory; this limits applicability in real-world scenarios where such labels are unavailable or dynamic. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - The method depends on an accurately specified causal graph, which may not be feasible in complex systems. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited and Insufficient Experimental Evaluation:**
  - Experiments are primarily on simple 1D pendulum systems (with varying parameters); only three test cases are shown, raising doubts about scalability and generalizability to more complex or real-world dynamical systems. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - In one test case (Figure 9b in Appendix), DIF fails to accurately forecast the invariant dynamics; the claim that it captures the “most important frequencies” is not supported by evidence. (Reviewer 3)
  - No comparison of the discovered symbolic form to baselines, e.g., running PySR directly on each environment separately, or using existing symbolic regression methods for ODEs (like ODEformer, PySR). This makes it hard to assess the added value of invariant function learning. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - Missing ablations on the number/type of interventions and the amount of trajectory data needed to robustly identify invariant dynamics. (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper does not evaluate on real-world datasets with noise or missing data, and does not discuss how the method handles noisy observations or extensions to PDEs. (Reviewer 4, also Reviewer 2’s question on noise)
- **Clarity and Presentation Problems:**
  - The two-phase process (function prediction and forecasting) is not clearly highlighted; details are scattered. Forecasting is ambiguous: sometimes a numerical integrator is used, sometimes a neural network; need explicit statement of what is used in which context. (Reviewer 5)
  - The output of the hypernetwork is unclear—whether it directly outputs symbolic form or a neural network that is later interpreted via symbolic regression. The figures and line 163 suggest symbolic basis functions, but Section 5.6 uses PySR afterward. (Reviewer 5)
  - Figure 2 (lower part) is confusing: partial assignments, arrow flows, and what the symbolic outputs represent are not explained. (Reviewer 5)
  - The exact form of the normalized RMSE used in experiments is not given; the discrepancy between small deviations in Figure 4b and high NRMSE (~0.35) in Table 2 is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
  - Numerous grammatical and spelling errors throughout (e.g., line 170, 275, 890, 1177; eq. 22e). (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
  - Limitations and future work are relegated to Appendix G instead of appearing in the main text. (Reviewer 5)
- **Missing Comparisons and Baselines:**
  - No comparison to independently trained networks: one for invariant mechanism only and one for full dynamics, to test the advantage of explicit disentanglement. (Reviewer 3)
  - Related work on transformer-based symbolic regression for dynamical systems (Becker et al. 2023, d’Ascoli et al. 2024, Seifner et al. 2024) is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
  - No evaluation on PDEs, even though derivatives lend themselves to straightforward extension. (Reviewer 3)
- **Concerns about Independence Enforcement:**
  - The adversarial discriminator may not fully remove environmental information from the invariant embedding; shared environmental details across interventions might still leak into \(f_c\). (Reviewer 3)
- **Function Composition Not Clearly Defined:**
  - The generative process combining \(f_c\) and \(f_e\) into the overall dynamics function \(f\) is not well-specified; it is unclear how \(g_{comp}\) works when functions come from different systems (e.g., Lotka-Volterra vs pendulum). (Reviewer 4)
