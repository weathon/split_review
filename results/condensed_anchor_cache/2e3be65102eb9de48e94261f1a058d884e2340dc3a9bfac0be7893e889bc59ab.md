## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GIE, a method to erase inappropriate concepts (NSFW content, style copyright infringements) from text-to-image diffusion models without fine-tuning. It injects "growth inhibitors" – reweighted attention maps based on target-concept features – into the cross-attention layers during generation. A Suppression Scale Adapter dynamically controls the injection strength. The method is evaluated against eight baselines, showing strong erasure of implicit unsafe prompts (e.g., "nude") while preserving image quality and semantics.

### Strengths
- Addresses a significant and timely problem: reducing generation of NSFW and copyright-infringing content. [R1, R4]
- Does **not** require fine-tuning of cross-attention layers, which distinguishes it from most prior concept-erasing methods. This improves efficiency and makes it widely applicable to architectures similar to Stable Diffusion. [R2, R3, R4]
- The Suppression Scale Adapter avoids the need for per-concept training or additional fine-tuning for different concepts. [R2]
- Qualitative results show that spatial details and overall image quality are largely preserved after erasure, especially compared to baseline methods. [R2]
- Extensive visualizations illustrate the effectiveness of the approach. [R1]
- GIE erases harmful and other concepts exceptionally well compared to baselines. [R3]
- The proposed components are largely based on observations that support the validity of the method. [R3]
- The topic and motivation are innovative and significant. [R4]
- One reviewer [R4] found the writing clear and easy to follow, though another [R1] found sections difficult to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification and intuition.** It is unclear why inserting reweighted attention maps (the growth inhibitors) leads to concept erasure, what the new maps in M_replace represent, and why this reweighting is more effective than prior attention-modulation approaches. The method deviates from naive suppression of the most related attention map, but the mechanism is difficult to understand. [R3]  
  - Related: one reviewer [R2] identifies a contradiction: the proposed injection scheme (M*_{target_concept} × V) effectively weakens every token embedding because V is a function of the token embedding c, contradicting the paper’s claim that a naive weakening approach would not work. This needs clarification.
- **Missing ablation studies.** There is no ablation on:
  - The role of each component (e.g., the adapter). [R1]
  - The positioning of the growth inhibitors (insertion right before [EOT]) – no justification or ablation is provided for why that specific position is chosen. [R2]  
  - The determination of starting/ending positions for the feature extraction (Section 4, line 260). [R1]  
  - The purpose of SOT and EOT tokens in the process is not clarified. [R1]
- **Missing comparisons and related work.** The paper does not discuss or compare with:
  - At least one prior method that also targets implicit concept removal in diffusion models (reference [1] in review). [R1]  
  - Prior works on LLMs for enhancing diffusion models (references [1][2][3][4] in review). [R4]
- **Limited generalization analysis.**  
  - The adapter is trained on a limited dataset; its ability to generalize to a broader range of concepts (where intermediate layer values vary widely) is questionable. [R3]  
  - The method is only demonstrated on Stable Diffusion 1.4. Additional experiments on other SD models are needed to confirm effectiveness and show that the same configuration (weights w, suppression levels, injection positions) transfers. [R3]  
  - The approach requires a high level of engineering (adapter design, correct injection). Optimal hyperparameters may be concept- or model-dependent. [R3]  
  - There is no analysis of the potential damage to other (appropriate) concepts when removing inappropriate ones, despite the likelihood of concept entanglement. [R4]
- **Partial erasure.** Qualitative results show that erasure can be incomplete. For example, after erasing “Van Gogh” and “Nude”, the image still preserves Van Gogh’s color style (Figure 7 bottom row, also Figure 13). [R2]
- **Missing quantitative multi-concept erasure.** No quantitative results are provided for erasing more than two concepts, and qualitative results only cover up to two concepts. [R2]
- **Clarity of writing** – one reviewer [R1] found the paper difficult to follow in certain sections; another [R4] considered it clear. The issues include unclear motivation for SOT/EOT tokens and vague description of the feature extraction.
- **Missing practical details.**  
  - The definition of the NSFW removal rate metric (whether it is the average ratio over test prompts computed via NudeNet) should be explicitly stated. [R3]  
  - The cost (time and expense) of using GPT-4o for data processing is not reported; listing this would benefit the community. [R4]  
  - The handling of multi-token concepts (e.g., “Van Gogh” → tokens [Van], [Gogh]) is unclear: how are the multiple tokens reduced to a single representation? Is the simultaneous suppression strategy from Section 4.3 used? [R2]  
  - The method for using CLIP to determine whether specific objects exist in generated images (line 415) is not explained. [R3]
