## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents LyCORIS, an open-source library integrating multiple fine-tuning techniques for Stable Diffusion, including LoRA, LoHa, LoKr, DyLoRA, GLoRA, GLoKr, etc. It also proposes a comprehensive evaluation framework using metrics for fidelity, controllability, diversity, base model preservation, and image quality, and performs extensive experiments analyzing hyperparameters (training epochs, learning rate, trained layers) and prompt types across concept categories. One reviewer (score 8) found the theory and experiments solid with 57 pages of analysis, while three reviewers (score 6) noted concerns about contribution and novelty, with one suggesting it is more appropriate for a benchmarking/datasets track.

### Strengths
- The theory and experiments are both solid, with over 57 pages devoted to analyzing fine-tuning techniques (Reviewer 1).
- Experiment details are very clear (Reviewer 1).
- The library supports a comprehensive set of matrix factorization techniques: LoRA, LoHa, LoKr, DyLoRA, GLoRA, GLoKr, and more (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Developing an open-source library is significant for fostering advancement in the field; LyCORIS offers a relatively more comprehensive set of algorithms compared to existing open-source libraries (Reviewer 2).
- The comprehensive benchmark evaluates multiple algorithms from various perspectives, addressing a gap in the text-to-image field (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive experiments for different algorithms and parameters, with detailed analysis of mainstream fine-tuning algorithms (Reviewer 2).
- Considers comprehensive evaluation metrics: fidelity, controllability, diversity, base model preservation, and image quality (Reviewer 3).
- The theoretical part of the paper appears sound (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Results of the framework combined with ControlNet are not presented (Reviewer 1).
- Efficiency (time and GPU memory cost) of different approaches is not provided and analyzed (Reviewer 1).
- It would be better to provide results on other versions of Stable Diffusion, such as SD2.0 and SDXL (Reviewer 1).
- The paper does not clearly articulate the advantages of LyCORIS compared to HuggingFace's PEFT library, which supports a wider range of pre-trained models and includes the same methods. The design choices that make LyCORIS more accessible to less experienced users are not specified (e.g., simplified API, pre-configured training scripts, extensive documentation). The claim that LyCORIS covers more methods for text-to-image fine-tuning needs substantiation with a detailed algorithm comparison (Reviewer 2).
- Based on the extensive experiments, the paper does not explore whether there could be a more optimal algorithm or design compared to existing ones. It would be beneficial to suggest novel algorithm designs or modifications guided by observed correlations between hyperparameters and performance (Reviewer 2).
- The paper mainly focuses on LoRA-based fine-tuning strategies; it does not clearly explain whether it can be expanded to other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., [1] and [2] as cited in review) (Reviewer 3).
- The conclusions about the performance of different fine-tuning methods are not clearly presented in the experimental section; maybe tables can more straightforwardly represent final conclusions (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer (score 6) believes this work is more appropriate for a benchmarking/datasets track rather than the main track, and finds it challenging to evaluate innovation because the improvements and variants on LoRA are relatively straightforward. The reviewer advises providing clear insights through experiments and specific suggestions (Reviewer 4). In contrast, another reviewer (score 8) rated the contribution as excellent (4), indicating a disagreement on novelty.
