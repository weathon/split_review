- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces CALM (Competence-based Analysis of Language Models), a framework that uses causal probing with gradient-based interventions (GBIs) to assess whether LLMs’ internal representations align with a human-specified causal model of linguistic properties. The framework is applied to BERT and RoBERTa on a set of lexical inference tasks from ConceptNet/LAMA. Reviewers find the approach creative and the theoretical framing interesting, but consider the empirical contributions preliminary and insufficient. They note the models are outdated (6-year-old architectures), the tasks are simple and limited to lexicosemantic relations, the baseline comparisons are weak, and the results do not demonstrate novel scientific insights or predictive power. A minority reviewer (R4) is particularly skeptical that the framework, as instantiated, will yield meaningful insights, while another (R1) states the paper is “too lightweight” but expresses general liking. All agree that more substantial experiments, comparisons to state-of-the-art interpretability methods, and validation on more complex models and tasks are needed.

### Strengths

- **Creative causal probing with gradient-based interventions (GBIs):** The use of gradient-based adversarial attacks to perform targeted interventions on internal representations is novel and extends prior probing techniques. (R1, R3)
- **Formal notion of competence:** The mathematical framing of linguistic competence in terms of causal models is interesting, soundly presented, and a useful step toward decoupling underlying knowledge from spurious performance. (R2, R4)
- **Relevant goal:** The attempt to go beyond behavioral evaluations to understand internal causal structure is important for interpretability and robustness. (R2, R4)
- **Promising initial results and thorough appendix:** The case study on BERT/RoBERTa shows that the competence metric is at least correlated with accuracy, and the appendix provides detailed methodology. (R1)
- **Potential to broaden probing techniques:** GBIs allow targeting a wider range of properties than prior methods, addressing some limitations in causal probing. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of substantive empirical findings.** The experiments are preliminary and do not yield clear or surprising insights. BERT and RoBERTa are only “moderately competent” on the lexical tasks, but this is already known from prior work. The paper does not provide any case where the framework explains or predicts unexpected model behaviors (e.g., out-of-distribution failures). (R1, R2, R4)
- **Outdated models and limited scope.** The focus on BERT and RoBERTa (2018/2019) is unusual in 2024. No autoregressive or larger LLMs are tested, raising questions about scalability and relevance to the current research landscape. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Weak or missing comparisons.** The paper includes only one very weak baseline (Section 6.1) and does not compare to state-of-the-art interpretability methods. Specifically, related work such as CausalGym (Arora et al. 2024), SyntaxGym, BLiMP, and the comprehensive survey of Mueller et al. 2024 are not cited or compared. (R1, R2)
- **Limited novelty beyond GBIs.** The overall approach uses existing datasets (ConceptNet) and the established method of causal probing. Apart from the gradient-based intervention strategy, the contributions are incremental. Reviewers request a clearer separation between what is novel and what is application. (R3, R4)
- **Misleading framing as “linguistic competence.”** The framework only addresses a narrow set of lexicosemantic relations, not the broader notion of linguistic competence. Several reviewers question the adoption of the competence/performance distinction, noting it is fraught in linguistics and used here in an unusual way (conformity to prior theoretical causal models rather than uncovering the model’s own internal knowledge). (R2, R3)
- **Lack of evidence for utility.** The abstract claims CALM can “explain and predict behaviors,” but the only empirical demonstration is a correlation between the competence metric and accuracy over a handful of task/model pairs. No concrete examples are given of predictions that would be impossible without the framework. (R4)
- **Experimental setup issues.** (i) Figure 4 uses different y-axes for accuracy and competence, making direct comparison difficult. (ii) The linguistic phenomena studied are basic compared to those in SyntaxGym/CausalGym (agreement patterns, long-distance dependencies). (iii) The paper does not show that models are even successful at the tasks—Figure 4 indicates poor performance—making interpretability results hard to interpret. (R2, R3)
- **Lack of analysis/interpretability.** The title and abstract promise “analysis” of model representations, but the paper is essentially an evaluation framework. The interpretable aspects are not clarified, and there is no demonstration of how CALM yields new insights about model behavior. (R3, R4)
- **Several minor presentation issues.** Missing citations, missing article/prepositions (e.g., “study *a* model M’s”), undefined abbreviations (GBI, FGSM, PGD) at first use, inconsistency between Pr and P in Eq (1), unclear rationale for intersection in top-k, and some spelling errors (e.g., “lama_” in x-labels, “competence” vs. “competence”). (R3, R4)
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with criticisms:**
    - *Why should one use CALM over alternatives?* (R1) — No clear advantage shown empirically.
    - *What surprising finding has been derived?* (R1) — None.
    - *Why only BERT/RoBERTa?* (R2) — No justification for excluding autoregressive models.
    - *Why not study tasks where models succeed, to reveal their causal structure?* (R2) — The paper focuses on tasks where models fail, limiting insight.
    - *How is the framework useful for predicting behavior?* (R4) — Only a weak correlation is shown, no predictive demonstration.
    - *How to distinguish between removing information and perturbing the intended property?* (R4) — The GBI method’s specificity is unvalidated.
    - *Why use intersection in top-k rather than a probability measure?* (R4) — No justification.
    - *What are “environmental properties” and how do they differ from causal properties?* (R3) — Not explained.
    - *Why focus on lexicosemantic tasks?* (R3) — Motivation lacking, generalizability questionable.
