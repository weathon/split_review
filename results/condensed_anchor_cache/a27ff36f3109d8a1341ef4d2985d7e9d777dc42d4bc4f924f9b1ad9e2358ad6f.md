## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces Rectified Linear Networks (ReLNs), a subset of Gated Deep Linear Networks (GDLNs), designed to capture the training dynamics of finite-width ReLU networks. Under a strong alignment assumption (Assumption 2.1, L159) that the dataset correlation matrices are mutually diagonalizable and that network weights align with singular vectors, the authors prove an equivalence between ReLU networks and ReLNs for single-hidden-layer networks. Using this equivalence, they derive exact analytical learning dynamics on a synthetic hierarchical task (one-hot inputs, sparse binary outputs, multiple contexts). They demonstrate that ReLU networks exhibit an implicit bias towards structured mixed selectivity—where neural units are active across multiple contexts in structured, reusable ways—and that this bias is amplified with more contexts and additional hidden layers. The predicted loss of ReLNs matches empirical ReLU loss (Figures 4, 5). A clustering algorithm is proposed to identify the gating structure of ReLNs from ReLU representations. Reviewers strongly disagreed: two reviewers rated 8, one 5, one 3. Positive reviewers found the approach novel and clarifying; the negative reviewer questioned the necessity of ReLNs and generalizability.

### Strengths

- The introduction of ReLNs as a GDLN variant to study ReLU dynamics is novel and offers a new angle on understanding feature learning in finite ReLU networks (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Theoretical proof of equivalence between ReLU and ReLN for finite-width single-hidden-layer networks, with clear presentation of informal and formal versions, proof sketch, and detailed appendix (reviewer 3).
- Analytical tractability of learning dynamics under Assumption 2.1, providing mechanistic understanding of how bias toward learning speed explains why 2-layer ReLU networks learn common and context-specific but entangled (mixed-selective) representations (reviewer 4).
- Empirical verification on a synthetic hierarchical dataset: predicted loss of ReLN matches empirical loss of ReLU networks (reviewer 3).
- The paper shows that ReLU networks are biased towards structured mixed selectivity and node reuse, which is amplified with more contexts and hidden layers (reviewers 1,3).
- Clear exposition of background work (GDLN, eigenmode decomposition) and figures/captions (reviewer 3).
- The approach of identifying linear submodules learned by ReLU networks and using this for tractability is interesting; natural modules to learn the structure are exactly recovered with learning speed (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Strong assumptions limit generality.** Assumption 2.1 (mutual diagonalizability of dataset correlation matrices and weight alignment to singular vectors) is highly restrictive. Reviewers 3 and 4 note that mutual diagonalizability implies specific data structure unlikely in real-world datasets; weight alignment constrains training dynamics, excluding many realistic scenarios. The paper does not analyze when these assumptions hold or break, even for 1-layer networks beyond the synthetic task (reviewer 3).
- **Unclear necessity of ReLN framework.** Reviewer 2 questions why ReLU’s training dynamics and mixed selectivity cannot be derived directly from ReLU networks, since they are already nonlinear. The paper does not adequately explain why existing analytical tools for nonlinear networks are insufficient for the observed phenomena.
- **Scalability and real-data evaluation missing.** All experiments are on a small synthetic dataset with one-hot vectors and sparse binary outputs. Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 strongly criticize the lack of experiments on realistic datasets (e.g., MNIST, CIFAR) and varied architectures (MLPs, CNNs). Reviewer 2 notes that computational complexity for larger data and networks is not discussed. Reviewer 4 specifically asks whether an appropriate GDLN can be identified for more complex datasets and architectures.
- **Assumption 2.1 already violated for deeper networks.** Reviewer 4 and the authors acknowledge that the silent alignment assumption (Assumption 2) does not hold for 2-hidden-layer ReLU nets. In Figure 6, ReLU networks show larger, sharper loss steps, contrary to the authors’ claim of “sharing the same characteristics” (reviewer 4).
- **Gating structure identification is a bottleneck.** The clustering algorithm (Appendix B) is not detailed, and its robustness and sensitivity to hyperparameters are unclear (reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 worries that evaluating the model at different training stages is dangerous if the learned function evolves complexly; intermediate functions may be unrelated to the final function. Scalability of the clustering to realistic datasets is unaddressed.
- **Definition of “feature learning” is vague.** Reviewer 2 requests a rigorous definition of “feature learning” and what it means for a pathway to be “doing feature learning” (line 302). The current description is too vague to clearly understand the claims.
- **Empirical match is not exact.** Reviewer 4 notes that in Figure 4 the learning curves of ReLU and ReLN do not exactly match, contradicting the claim that the ReLN exactly describes the ReLU dynamics.
- **Novelty relative to prior work is insufficiently highlighted.** Reviewer 1 would have appreciated a clearer comparison with Saxe et al., 2022 and explicit statement of novelty.
- **Lack of hyperparameter details.** Reviewer 1 asks for a hyperparameter table and clarification on whether hyperparameters in Figure 2 are analytically derived.
- **Unclear definition of “strictly modular” and “structured mixed selectivity.”** Reviewer 1 requests upfront definitions of these terms to contrast with the paper’s findings.
- **Dependence on number of neurons not discussed.** Reviewer 4 notes that the paper never explicitly mentions how results depend on the number of neurons.
- **Other issues:** Reviewer 4 points to typos in lines 30, 392, 462–463, 473, 476, 477, 503, 527. Reviewer 3 and 4 raise additional questions about why the largest singular value is not learned first (Figure 4, 5), the source of node-reuse bias in the dynamics, whether SGD would yield different dynamics, and whether the theory could inform learning of disentangled representations. These overlap with missing experimentation or theoretical gaps.
