## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a dynamic network embedding method that jointly preserves structural proximity (dense communities) and structural equivalence (similar node roles) via dynamic graphlets and a temporal-structural random walk. The walk samples nodes based on both temporal proximity and evolving structural similarity, controlled by a hyperparameter α. The method is evaluated on five real-world networks for node classification and compared against five baseline methods. Reviewers agree the paper is well-written and addresses a relevant goal, but they raise major concerns about novelty, baseline selection, experimental rigor, and incremental performance gains. One reviewer (score 6) was notably more positive, describing the unified framework as novel and the experiments well-organized with consistent improvements, but still noted several limitations. The other three reviewers (score 3) found the contribution limited and the evaluation insufficient.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-structured, clearly written, and easy to follow (all reviewers).
- It is the first method to unify structural proximity and structural equivalence in a single embedding for dynamic networks (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The use of dynamic graphlets to define structural equivalence and to construct a temporal-structural random walk is novel and interesting (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The introduction of the α parameter provides flexibility to balance proximity and role learning (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The approach is technically sound (Reviewer 1). A time complexity analysis is provided and supports computational feasibility (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed method outperforms the selected baselines on node classification across five datasets, with consistent improvements (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Low novelty and incremental contribution.** The method heavily relies on existing techniques (temporal graphlets from Hulovatyy et al., DeepWalk, CTDNE) and offers only modest extensions (Reviewers 1, 4). Even the more positive reviewer (score 6) notes the performance gains are incremental.
- **Weak and outdated baselines.** None of the baselines combine both structural proximity and equivalence, making the comparison less informative (Reviewer 1). Missing recent state-of-the-art methods such as Rossi et al. (2020) and Çelikkanat et al. (2022) (Reviewers 3, 4). It is also unclear whether static baselines were run on aggregated networks or individual time slices, and whether baseline hyperparameters were optimized or left at defaults (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited evaluation scope.** The experiments focus solely on node classification, a static downstream task. Additional tasks (edge prediction, clustering, label prediction) are needed to demonstrate versatility (Reviewers 2, 3). Static tasks do not fully reflect the method’s dynamic capabilities (Reviewer 2).
- **Marginal improvements and lack of significance testing.** Many improvements are within standard deviation margins (Reviewer 2). No paired t-test or other significance test was performed (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter α tuned on test set, introducing bias.** The optimal α appears to be chosen based on test performance, and no validation set is specified (Reviewers 2, 4). The same concern applies to baseline hyperparameter selection (Reviewer 4).
- **No artificial network study.** The optimal α is found to be very small, limiting evidence for the method’s claimed flexibility. Controlled experiments on artificial networks with known structural regimes could demonstrate when a larger α is necessary (Reviewer 2).
- **No visualization or interpretability of embeddings.** The unified embedding prevents isolating contributions from proximity vs. equivalence; no qualitative analysis is provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing discussion of key related work.** The paper does not discuss the work of Hoff (2007), which unifies homophily and stochastic equivalence via the choice of proximity metric, a connection directly relevant to the paper’s goal (Reviewer 2).
- **High computational complexity and scalability concerns.** The time complexity scales quadratically with the number of nodes (N²) if applied to the entire network, making the method potentially infeasible for large-scale networks (Reviewers 2, 4). The authors should clarify whether the N² cost is for the whole network or just graphlet enumeration, and discuss strategies for scalability (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Does not address interactions between temporal order and structural equivalence.** The random walk alternates between the two components but does not model their dependencies, which could be important for complex dynamics (Reviewer 3). Varying α at a per-instance or per-graphlet level was not considered (Reviewer 3).
- **Notation and technical clarity issues.** In Definition 3.3.1, the meaning of σᵢ is ambiguous (Reviewer 4). The temporal order property stated after sampling from P_S may not always hold (Reviewer 4). The motivation for using a static approach to capture structural equivalence in a temporal network is insufficiently justified (Reviewer 4). The effect of latent dimensionality on performance is not examined (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing larger dataset evaluation.** The model should be tested on larger or more recent datasets to validate scalability and robustness (Reviewers 3, 4). It is unclear whether node labels are assumed fixed over time; if so, static methods using only recent time steps might be strong baselines (Reviewer 4).
