## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Plan-RAG, a framework shifting from *retrieve-then-reason* to *plan-then-retrieve*. It decomposes complex queries into a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of atomic sub-queries, enabling parallelized retrieval and generation without fine-tuning language models. Instead, it uses frozen experts (e.g., relevance critic) to guide retrieval and answer generation. Evaluated on multiple QA datasets, Plan-RAG is claimed to outperform baselines like Self-RAG and RQ-RAG in accuracy, relevance, and hallucination reduction. Reviewer scores are 3, 3, 5, 6, reflecting disagreement. One reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive, while two reviewers (score 3) are more critical.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and motivation** (several reviewers): The plan-then-retrieve paradigm and DAG-based query decomposition are seen as a novel contribution, enhancing interpretability and trustworthiness for multi-hop queries.
- **Technical soundness and evaluation** (reviewers 1 & 4): The approach is evaluated on multiple QA datasets with ablation studies showing the contribution of individual experts (relevance and critic). The framework achieves strong performance without fine-tuning.
- **Clarity** (reviewers 1, 3, 4): The motivation, key concepts (DAG, plug-and-play experts), and illustrative diagrams are clearly presented; the framework is easy to understand.
- **Modularity** (reviewers 1 & 4): The use of frozen LMs as plug-and-play experts makes the system adaptable to different models and reduces retraining needs.
- **Attribution and hallucination reduction** (reviewer 4): The DAG structure improves traceability and reduces hallucinations by design.

### Weaknesses
1. **Unfair baselines and experimental comparison** (reviewers 1, 2, 3):  
   - Plan-RAG uses Llama-3-8B-Instruct as the backbone, while Self-RAG and RQ-RAG use Llama-2-7B/13B, which are weaker models.  
   - The DAG planning step uses GPT-4o, a much stronger model than any baseline. This makes the comparison invalid.  
   - Experiments should be repeated with identical backbone models (e.g., all using Llama-3-8B or GPT-4o for all methods) to ensure fairness.  
   - The vanilla LLM baselines are weak; stronger models (GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude-3) should be included for more meaningful comparisons.  

2. **Limited novelty** (reviewers 2 & 3):  
   - The core idea of "plan-then-retrieve" is not new; similar works exist (e.g., [1]) and the approach resembles multi-agent RAG, tool-planning, and existing query decomposition methods (RQ-RAG, RA-ISF).  
   - The contribution is viewed as incremental engineering rather than a significant conceptual advance.  

3. **Missing ablations and analysis** (all reviewers):  
   - No ablation comparing Plan-RAG *with* vs. *without* the DAG structure (reviewer 4).  
   - Ablation only covers two experts (relevance, critic); other experts (e.g., generator, summarizer) are not analyzed (reviewer 1).  
   - No error analysis: which module (planner, retrieval, expert) introduces the most errors? (reviewer 4).  
   - No evaluation of *Attribution* (reviewer 2) – the paper claims improved attribution but does not compare to baselines.  
   - Efficiency and debuggability are claimed but not empirically demonstrated (reviewers 1, 3, 4). No runtime or latency measurements are provided; the multiple LLM calls likely increase cost and time.  
   - Only tested on an 8B model; generalization to larger models (e.g., GPT-4) is unknown (reviewer 4).  

4. **Single-hop performance** (reviewers 1 & 3): Plan-RAG performs poorly on single-hop queries, and the DAG decomposition seems unnecessary for simple tasks. The paper does not explain why or how the framework could be adapted (e.g., a light version).  

5. **Heavy reliance on DAG quality** (reviewer 1): The framework depends on accurate automatic DAG generation—a non-trivial problem for ambiguous or diverse queries. No analysis of DAG reliability, failure cases, or fallback strategies for incorrect plans.  

6. **Presentation and consistency issues** (reviewers 1, 2, 3):  
   - Figure 2 shows 7 agents but only 4 are explained; an illustrative example of each component's role is missing.  
   - Typos: Line 238: “q → q̃” (should be indexed); Line 279: “retrieval(s)” confusing; Line 314 missing space; symbol \(v\) in Eq (1) undefined (likely should be \(q\)).  
   - Table 6 is only in the appendix, not mentioned in the main text.  
   - The term “attribution” is not defined for the RAG context (reviewer 3).  

7. **Unresolved questions overlapping with weaknesses**:  
   - Why is Plan-RAG poor on single-hop tasks? (reviewer 3).  
   - What is the advantage of one-pass planning versus iterative planning (e.g., ReAct)? (reviewer 3).  
   - Why is the ablation limited to two experts? (reviewer 1).  
   - How does the Critic Expert affect retrieval quality when it reduces the number of retrievals? (reviewer 2).
