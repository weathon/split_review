## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Variance-Covariance Regularization (VCReg), an adaptation of VICReg's variance and covariance components for supervised learning. VCReg encourages high-variance, low-covariance representations and is applied to both final and intermediate layers. Experiments across image/video transfer learning, long-tailed learning, hierarchical classification, and self-supervised tasks show improvements. The method aims to address gradient starvation and neural collapse.

### Strengths

1. **Clear motivation and writing.** (R1, R3) The paper is well-structured and the motivation is generally clear.
2. **Extensive empirical evaluation.** (R1, R2, R3) VCReg shows convincing improvements on supervised pretraining (e.g., ImageNet) transferred to various datasets, as well as on class-imbalanced and hierarchical tasks. Experiments cover multiple tasks, architectures, and settings (transfer learning for images/videos, long-tailed learning, SSL, hierarchical classification).
3. **Sound conceptual basis.** (R2, R3) The benefits of VCReg are explored thoroughly in Section 5 (e.g., gradient starvation, neural collapse), and the idea of whitening-style regularization for supervised learning is sensible given prior work on feature diversity.
4. **Diverse experimental scope.** (R2) The thorough evaluation across tasks is both interesting and convincing.

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty and practicality.** (R1, R3)  
   - Reviewer 3 (score 3) argues VCReg is essentially VICReg applied to supervised learning; removing the invariance component further narrows novelty. They emphasize that in contemporary practice, supervised learning is used for fine-tuning, not general-purpose pretraining. They request a direct comparison of SL+VCReg against leading SSL methods (e.g., DINO, VICReg) on downstream transfer tasks to demonstrate true novelty.  
   - Reviewer 1 similarly sees VCReg as a special case of VICReg for supervised settings, not a novel approach.  
   - The computational overhead from intermediate-layer application and specialized backward-pass adjustments is noted by R1, and R3 adds that selecting hyperparameters α, β across architectures requires significant cost with only modest improvements. R3 suggests discussing automated hyperparameter optimization.

2. **Lack of theoretical grounding.** (R2) The paper does not provide a theoretical explanation for how VCReg improves generalization, e.g., its impact on decision boundary or expected risk. R2 recommends relevant references (Maurer et al., 2009; Namkoong et al., 2017; Ranran et al., 2020; Tolstikhin et al., 2013).  
   - Reviewer 1 also asks how VCReg specifically addresses generalization in relation to gradient starvation and transfer learning.

3. **Uncertainty about robustness to distribution shift.** (R1) Without VICReg’s invariance component, VCReg’s generalization under distribution shift is unclear, and empirical evidence to justify this is missing.

4. **Insufficient method details and reproducibility concerns.** (R2, R3)  
   - How spatial dimensions are handled in covariance calculations and whether covariance is computed per feature dimension is unclear. R2 and R3 request explicit formulas.  
   - How intermediate layers are selected for applying regularization is not explained (R3).  
   - Both reviewers recommend a step-by-step algorithm or pseudocode for clarity and reproducibility.

5. **Conflicting or questionable experimental results.** (R1, R3)  
   - **Table 4 (SSL integration):** R1 finds it puzzling that SimCLR+VCReg does not improve, while VICReg+VCReg does (VICReg already has an invariance component). An explanation is needed.  
   - **Table 6 (CDNV analysis):** R3 points out that ConvNeXt with VCReg shows *weaker* clustering ability than standard ConvNeXt, contradicting the claim that VCReg prevents neural collapse. Also, it is unclear how unlabeled data was used for CDNV in a supervised context.  
   - **Table 4 (reproduced baselines):** R3 notes the reproduced VICReg performance is even worse than SimCLR, which contradicts prior SSL knowledge, undermining confidence in the experimental setup.  
   - R1 also asks why removing the invariance component is beneficial and whether it reduces robustness.

6. **Missing comparison to leading SSL methods.** (R3) The paper does not directly compare SL+VCReg against state-of-the-art self-supervised methods (e.g., DINO, VICReg) on downstream transfer tasks. Such a comparison is seen as necessary to demonstrate practical relevance.

7. **Minor issues.** (R2) Typo in line 076: “suppresses” should be “surpasses”.

*Reviewer disagreement:* Two reviewers (scores 5) find the empirical evidence largely convincing and the method well-presented, while one reviewer (score 3) strongly questions novelty, practicality, and the reliability of key experiments (Tables 4 and 6).
