## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies learning optimal contracts in principal-agent problems where the principal commits to outcome-dependent payments over multiple rounds, the agent’s action is unobservable, and the principal has no information about the agent. The focus is on settings where the agent’s action space is small. The main result is an algorithm that, with high probability, learns an approximately optimal contract using a sample complexity of \(\tilde{O}(m^n)\) (polynomial in the outcome space size \(m\) when the number of actions \(n\) is constant). The algorithm is then converted into an explore-then-commit online learning algorithm that achieves \(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{4/5})\) regret.

### Strengths
- **Original idea and clean execution.** The result improves previous sample complexity bounds for learning optimal contracts when the number of actions is constant (Reviewer 1, 3). The idea of meta‑actions to group together actions with similar outcome distributions, combined with iterative polytope refinement, is simple and elegant (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Important problem framing.** Learning the principal’s optimal strategy under unknown agent type is an active topic; existing work has largely focused on Stackelberg/security games, and the paper extends this to the contract‑design setting where the action is unobservable (Reviewer 2).
- **The characterization is interesting and well‑executed** (Reviewer 2). The writing is generally clear (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Honest‑response assumption insufficiently justified.** The paper assumes the agent always best‑responds honestly to the principal’s contracts, even though a sophisticated agent aware of the learning process would have strong incentives to manipulate its responses to mislead the principal. This modeling concern is especially acute when the agent’s utility is private information. The paper does not discuss the validity of this assumption in practice or provide application domains where it holds (Reviewer 2). *(Reviewer 1 and 3 did not raise this issue.)*
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work.** The core algorithmic approach—iteratively building two polytopes \(U_d\) and \(L_d\)—closely follows Peng et al. [2019]; the principal modification is replacing actions with meta‑actions via an Action‑Oracle, which appears to be a straightforward adaptation given the unobservable action setting. The relaxed constant‑volume assumption relies heavily on Lemma 4 of Zhu et al. [2022] (continuity of the principal’s utility). The paper does not clearly delineate the specific technical challenges that were overcome or how the contribution differs substantially from these earlier works (Reviewer 2). *(Reviewer 1 acknowledges the connection to Letchford et al. [2009] and Peng et al. [2019], but does not judge the novelty as insufficient; Reviewer 1 instead expects a discussion of the challenges of extending that approach.)*
- **Overclaim regarding “solves an open problem by Zhu et al.”** Zhu et al. [2022] studied settings with a prior distribution over the agent’s type, and their open question is posed in that setting. This paper studies the no‑prior setting and, moreover, uses a different bounded‑contract definition (sum of payments bounded vs. per‑outcome payment bounded). Thus, claiming to solve their open problem is a mischaracterization (Reviewer 3). *(Reviewer 1 and 2 did not mention this issue.)*
- **Motivation for the no‑prior setting is weak.** The sample‑complexity problem seems poorly motivated: a principal interacting repeatedly with the same agent to learn an optimal contract is unnatural. The scenario would be more plausible when there is a large candidate pool and the principal has no prior distribution, but the paper does not argue this (Reviewer 3). *(Reviewer 1 and 2 did not comment on motivation.)*
- **Minor technical concerns.**  
  - Theorem 1 is more of an observation or proposition than a theorem, as it states a fairly obvious fact in the literature (Reviewer 1).  
  - Definition 1 should be an assumption, not a definition; treating it as a high‑probability event seems unnecessary, and it effectively restricts the learning problem to a subclass where the approximately optimal contract has bounded payments (Reviewer 1).  
  - The notation is not very readable; using dot products over the outcome space instead of element‑wise products would improve clarity (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing lower bounds and open directions.** There is no lower bound for the sample complexity when the action space is small, nor a discussion of whether the approach can be extended beyond constant action size under other assumptions (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unanswered technical question.** Reviewer 2 asks how Lemma 8 of this paper differs from Lemma 4 of Zhu et al. [2022], and whether the result can be directly implied from their continuity property. The paper does not address this distinction.
