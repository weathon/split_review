## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Asymmetric Representation-regularized Adversarial Training (AR-AT) to improve the robustness-accuracy trade-off. AR-AT addresses two issues in invariance regularization: (1) gradient conflict between invariance loss and classification objectives, and (2) the mixture distribution problem from diverged clean vs. adversarial input distributions. Solutions include asymmetric invariance loss with stop-gradient operation and a predictor, plus a split-BatchNorm (BN) structure. Experiments on CIFAR, Imagenette, and with multiple architectures are presented. Reviewers rated the paper 6, 8, 8, 6; two are more positive (8) and two more negative (6).

### Strengths
- Well written, easy to follow, and clearly organized; target issues are clear and each solution step is motivated.
- In-depth analysis of gradient conflict and mixture distribution problems provides new insights for adversarial defense.
- Experiments validate effectiveness on multiple datasets and architectures; each proposed technique is shown to be effective via ablation.
- The perspective on gradient conflict connects previously disparate threads in adversarial robustness.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns.** The proposed stop-gradient operation and predictor are commonly used in self-supervised learning; the overall novelty may be limited (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient theoretical justification for gradient conflict.** The paper does not clearly distinguish the observed gradient conflicts from typical gradient noise in mini-batch stochastic optimization, nor does it explain why resolving these conflicts specifically improves adversarial robustness (Reviewer 4). A deeper theoretical examination is needed.
- **Gap between local optimization dynamics and global distributional properties.** Gradient conflicts observed at the mini-batch level may not reflect conflicts in the full input distribution; the paper lacks a rigorous analysis of how the proposed method affects the overall loss landscape and generalization (Reviewer 4).
- **Mixed distribution problem lacks sufficient evidence.** More experiments and analysis are needed to substantiate claims about the mixture distribution issue (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 notes that Table 17 shows resolving the mixture distribution alone significantly improves performance, while addressing gradient conflict alone contributes little (e.g., (1)vs(2) and (5)vs(6)). Standard deviations should be reported to make the results more convincing.
- **Incomplete comparison with baselines.** Most baselines are from before 2023; more recent and advanced baselines are needed (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The comparison may not be comprehensive given the large body of adversarial training literature (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 notes that results may not be SOTA, referencing the DM-Improves-AT repository. ImageNet experiments should be incorporated (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing error bars / standard deviations.** In Table 3, some baseline methods provide error bars while others do not (Reviewer 2). Standard deviations are also missing for the component ablation in Table 17 (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure and text issues.** Figure 3’s increase in \(||z-z'||_2\) over time despite the regularization term, and why stop-grad exacerbates it, is not explained (Reviewer 3). Figure 4 is missing (Reviewer 3). Lines 200‑201 and Figure 2: plotting curves of only minimizing the second term would strengthen the claim (Reviewer 3). Minor error: “clean representation z^' ” in line 191 (Reviewer 1).
- **Computational overhead.** The paper does not discuss whether AR‑AT introduces additional computational cost compared to baselines (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
