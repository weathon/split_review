## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FM-TS, a rectified Flow Matching framework for unconditional and conditional time series generation. The authors claim that FM-TS reduces the computational cost of diffusion models by using continuous trajectory optimization and that models trained on unconditional generation can be directly applied to conditional tasks (forecasting, imputation) without retraining, aided by a “t-power sampling” method. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: one reviewer (score 1) finds the paper not ready for publication due to reproducibility, clarity, and unsubstantiated claims, while another (score 5) considers the efficient rectified-flow design and t-power sampling innovative and the unconditional experiments solid. Two reviewers (score 3) see the topic as valuable but point to insufficient novelty, limited evaluation, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Application of flow matching to time series**: The attempt to apply Flow Matching (a technique promising for images) to the less explored domain of time series generation is interesting (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Potential efficiency gains**: The ODE-based formulation of FM-TS could reduce training and inference passes compared to iterative diffusion models (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **t-power sampling innovation**: The t-power sampling method, which generalizes unconditional models to conditional tasks without retraining, is considered novel and promising (Reviewer 2).
- **Unconditional generation experiments**: The unconditional generation experiments are well-designed and deliver solid results across multiple metrics and datasets (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Commendable effort**: Addressing time series generation is valuable, and the paper tackles both conditional and unconditional settings (Reviewer 3).
- **Clear presentation and literature review**: One reviewer finds the paper clearly presented and the literature review adequate (Reviewer 4). (Note: Reviewer 1 disagrees on clarity – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Readability and contradictory statements**: The paper suffers from poor organization, unclear transitions, and conflation of “generalization” and “computational requirements.” Specifically, lines 056–058 make contradictory claims about diffusion models’ ability to capture long-range dependencies (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **No code / reproducibility**: Code is not provided, and the appendix is minimal. This makes it impossible to verify results and hinders reproducibility (Reviewer 1). The experimental improvements in the tables do not clearly align with the contributions claimed in the introduction (Reviewer 1).
- **Underdeveloped unconditional-to-conditional generalization**: The key claim that unconditional FM-TS can be used for conditional tasks without retraining is not adequately justified. The mechanism behind Algorithm 1 and t-power sampling is not explained, no intuition or example is given, and no comparisons with conditionally trained models are provided (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **t-power sampling missing details**: Implementation and design choices of t-power sampling are absent. Its role in adapting unconditional to conditional generation is unclear (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Logit-normal distribution not convincingly superior**: The ablation study (Table 4) shows logit-normal sampling performing comparably or inferiorly to uniform sampling; the paper does not explain its theoretical or practical advantage (Reviewer 2).
- **Oversimplification of imputation vs. forecasting**: The paper treats imputation and forecasting as nearly identical, differing only by the mask M, ignoring that imputation often requires bidirectional information while forecasting uses unidirectional data (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty / insufficient distinction from prior work**: The paper appears as a straightforward application of rectified flow to time series without addressing time-specific challenges (causality, seasonality, trends). It does not clearly differentiate itself from CFM-TS (ICML 2024) or other ODE-based time series works (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Inadequate evaluation for forecasting and imputation**: Squared error is used as the sole metric; for generative models, CRPS or Negative Log-Likelihood would be more appropriate. Baselines are limited – point-estimation models like PatchTST and TS-Mixer are missing. The imputation missing-data rates (only 70%, 80%) lack justification; rates from prior work (10%, 50%, 90%) should be included (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing runtime comparisons for training**: The main motivation is computational efficiency, but Figure 3 only shows inference speed. Training runtime (per epoch, total time, convergence) is not compared with baselines (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Limited efficiency comparison in Figure 1**: Figure 1 only compares FID against one baseline, which is not a comprehensive efficiency analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Predictive score not significantly better**: For forecasting/imputation, the squared-error results are not substantially better than baselines. Figure 4 does not clearly indicate good performance, and Figure 5’s bar-like PCA plot for diffusion baselines is unexplained. Limited interpretation of why results look the way they do (Reviewer 4).
- **High standard deviations in Table 2**: Table 2 reports high standard deviations, suggesting inconsistent performance, which undermines the SOTA claim (Reviewer 1).
- **Vague conclusion on conditional generation**: The conclusion that the unconditional model can be “directly used for conditional generation” is made without reference or explanation (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues**:
  - In line 242, the function \( v: \mathbb{R}^{l \times d} \times [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^{l \times d} \) should include \( t \) as an argument (Reviewer 3).
  - Font sizes of legends and labels in Figures 4 and 5 need improvement (Reviewer 3).
  - Captions of Tables 3 and 4 should clarify the evaluation metrics used (Reviewer 3).
