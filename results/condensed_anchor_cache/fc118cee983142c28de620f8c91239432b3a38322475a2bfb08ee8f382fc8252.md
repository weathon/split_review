## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the problem of computing similarity between a query point and a private dataset under differential privacy. Given a private dataset \(X \subset \mathbb{R}^d\) and a similarity function \(f\), the goal is to output a differentially private data structure that approximates \(\sum_{x \in X} f(x,y)\) for any query \(y\). The functions considered include distance functions (e.g., \(\ell_p\) norms for \(p=1,2,\ldots\)) and kernel density estimates (Gaussian, exponential, Cauchy kernels). The approach leverages low-dimensional structures via provable dimensionality reduction (adapting JL matrices), approximation theory, and one-dimensional decomposition of functions. The paper provides theoretical results that improve on prior work (e.g., Huang and Roth 2014) in terms of error bounds and query time, with the query time parameters \(d\) and \(\alpha\) decoupled for KDE queries. It also includes empirical results on CIFAR-10 for \(\ell_1\) queries, dimensionality reduction, and DP classification, showing advantages over state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths
- The problem is important for differential privacy and has many applications.
- Solid theoretical results that improve on prior work (specifically Huang and Roth 2014) in error terms and query time for commonly used distance functions (including \(\ell_p\)) and KDE queries (Gaussian, exponential, Cauchy).
- New results for previously unstudied queries, e.g., \(\ell_p\) distance queries.
- Thorough comparisons with state-of-the-art theoretical results.
- The algorithms are relatively simple and implementable; techniques (e.g., decomposing distance into one-dimensional approximations, dimensionality reduction, functional approximation) are clear.
- Sufficient empirical results on \(\ell_1\) queries, dimensionality reduction, and DP classification that support the theory and show runtime and accuracy improvements over prior work.
- Paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow.
- Original and significant contribution to DP.

### Weaknesses
- The improvement over prior work is only under certain settings. For \(\ell_p\) norms, the relative error seems higher (worse) than prior work, while the additive error is improved; this trade-off is not fully explored and may not be ideal for applications where relative error is primary.
- For \(\ell_1\) functions, there is a \(\sqrt{d}\) gap in the additive error. The decomposition into one-dimensional queries introduces composition overhead under pure DP. While a \(\sqrt{d}\) improvement over naive \(O(d)\) composition is shown, it is unclear whether this \(\sqrt{d}\) factor is inherent to the approach or if alternative strategies could give tighter bounds. A deeper discussion of the limitation and potential alternatives is missing.
- For \(\ell_1\) distance queries, the error at \(\varepsilon = 1\) is very large. Experiments show substantial error at low \(\varepsilon\), raising concerns about practical applicability in high-privacy regimes. The absolute error magnitude at low \(\varepsilon\) is a significant limitation.
- Technical insights regarding privacy are somewhat limited. The explanation of how the techniques interact with privacy—especially the reduction of smooth kernels to exponential sums—lacks sufficient detail in the main body. The privacy analysis could benefit from a more thorough discussion.
- Several parts of the paper lack clarity or missing justification:
  * The discussion before Theorem 1.4 is unclear.
  * The justification for why Corollary 3.2 allows expressing \(1/(1+h(x,y))\) in the stated way is not fully explained.
  * The last equality in a mathematical display on page 7 is not justified.
  * The comparison in Figure 3 is unclear and needs more explanation.
  * The caption of Figure 1 could be more detailed.
- The paper would benefit from including more important formal results and algorithms in the main body rather than the appendix.
- The paper only considers pure differential privacy; it does not discuss approximate DP or zCDP. It is an open question whether the results extend to those variants.
- Some relevant related work (e.g., arXiv:2106.00001 on DP dimensionality reduction) could be cited.
- The definition of differential privacy should be placed in the main text for completeness.
- The paper does not discuss what other notions of distance might be interesting to study.
