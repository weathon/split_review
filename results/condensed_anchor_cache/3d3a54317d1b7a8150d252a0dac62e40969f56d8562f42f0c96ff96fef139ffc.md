## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes NuwaTS, a framework that repurposes pre-trained language models (PLMs) for general time series imputation via specialized embeddings (patch, missingness, statistical) and contrastive learning, with a plug-and-play fine-tuning approach. The authors introduce a variable-wise dataset splitting protocol to evaluate cross-variable and cross-domain generalization. Experiments on multiple datasets (seventeen million samples) show NuwaTS outperforms domain-specific models. Reviewers are split: two rated 5 (positive), two rated 3 (less positive). Positives highlight the novel designs and thorough experiments; negatives cite marginal gains, univariate formulation limitations, lack of variance reporting, and insufficient justification for PLM use.

### Strengths
- Imputation is an important step in real-world time-series analysis; a general-purpose foundational model is interesting and useful (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed domain-specific (p-tuning style) embedding is novel and enables lightweight plug-and-play fine-tuning (Reviewer 1).
- Comprehensive experimental results across diverse settings, including imputation, zero-shot, few-shot, and forecasting (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Ablation studies are also comprehensive (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well written and organized (Reviewer 3).
- The effort to deal with cross-domain generalization is valuable, as domain patterns differ significantly (Reviewer 3).
- The code base is well organized for reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- Imputation-specific zero-shot modeling is new; missing information embeddings may benefit other time-series tasks with missing data (Reviewer 3).
- Novel designs: statistics patch embedding, missingness embedding, time series patch contrastive learning, and p-tuningv2 style domain-specific fine-tuning (Reviewer 4).
- Experiments are conducted thoroughly (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal improvements over PatchTST**: The proposed NuwaTS has only marginal improvements over the PatchTST (one-for-all) variant (Reviewer 1, Weakness 3). Table 4 zero-shot performance gain seems marginal compared to PatchTST (Reviewer 2, Weakness 2). In Table 6, improvements from the proposed designs are not significant enough (Reviewer 4, Weakness).
- **Lack of variance reporting**: No variances of the results are provided; time-series imputation performance often depends on seed settings (Reviewer 2, Weakness 1). Please provide variances for results (Reviewer 2, Questions Q2). 
- **Motivation for using PLMs**: The motivation for using PLMs for imputation is missing. Are there specific advantages over standard transformer time-series models (e.g., PatchTST, iTransformer, Autoformer, Ti-MAE)? How does pre-training on natural language help time-series imputation? Would a randomly initialized LM achieve the same accuracy? (Reviewer 1, Questions Q1).
- **Univariate formulation limitation**: The paper defines time-series imputation as a univariate problem, leveraging variable-wise division. However, multivariate imputation can leverage cross-variate correlations, and methods like SAITS, BRITS are optimized for multivariate settings, so the comparison may be unfair (Reviewer 1, Weakness 2 and Questions Q2). Modeling each individual dimension separately is a limitation; the method is not applicable to datasets with strong cross-dimensional interactions, contradicting the “one-for-all” claim (Reviewer 4, Weakness).
- **Cross-variable generalization concerns**: Is it possible to impute a new dimension never exposed to the model? The generalization performance relies on random variable splitting, and the marginal few-shot results on ETT may reflect high variable correlations rather than genuine generalization. The paper should include experiments or discussions with data trained on one pattern and tested on variables with completely different missing patterns (Reviewer 2, Questions Q1). Cross-variable generalization is essentially domain generalization; proposing a 1:1:1 variable-wise split as a novel benchmarking strategy is insufficient (Reviewer 3, Weakness).
- **Statistical embedding justification unclear**: The motivation behind statistical embeddings is not clear; an ablation study to demonstrate significant drop without them would help (Reviewer 1, Questions Q3).
- **Notation clarity**: Line 198: $Z_{i,(p)}$ notation needs clarification; $I$, $p$, $D$, $N$ are not defined before. Line 213: $z_{i,(p)}$ and $r_i$ are not defined; why is it multiplied with the mask ratio? (Reviewer 1, Questions Q4, Q5).
- **Limited real-world missing data experiments**: While synthetic masking experiments are illustrative, adding more experiments on real-world datasets with missing values would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 1, Questions Q6).
- **Forecasting results raise doubts**: Table 8 results: For forecasting (an extreme imputation case), the model seems worse or only comparable to baselines despite using many more parameters (Reviewer 2, Weakness 3).
- **Baselines limited to predictive models**: The baselines only include predictive models; missing probabilistic imputation models (VAE, GAN, Diffusion). Provide reasons if those are not applicable (Reviewer 2, Questions Q3).
- **Need more analysis beyond ablations**: The paper mainly lists experimental results; more insights and analysis would be beneficial (Reviewer 3, Weakness).
- **Inter-series correlation not shown for imputation**: Although inter-series correlation information is used during fine-tuning for forecasting, it is not shown that such information can be used for imputation (Reviewer 4, Weakness).
