## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LEMON, a lossless model expansion method that initializes a scaled model using the weights of its smaller pre-trained counterpart. It addresses symmetry breaking by setting unequal fan-out weights and handles indivisible width increments via average expansion, particularly for LayerNorm. The method is designed for Transformer modules and is accompanied by an optimized learning rate scheduler that decays more rapidly than training from scratch. Experiments on Vision Transformers and BERT show 56.7% and 33.2% computational cost reduction, respectively, compared to training from scratch.

### Strengths

1. **Clear motivation and practical importance.** The paper focuses on scaling neural networks efficiently by reusing knowledge from smaller pre-trained models, a well-motivated and timely problem. (R1, R2, R3, R4)

2. **Technically sound and well-supported.** The method is accompanied by detailed proofs in the appendix. The techniques for symmetry breaking (unequal fan-out weights) and handling indivisible width increments (average expansion) are rigorously analyzed and ensure a lossless initial mapping. (R1, R2, R3)

3. **Versatility across Transformer modules.** The expansion is designed locally for individual components (e.g., LayerNorm, Multi-head Attention), making it compatible with different Transformer variants. The paper explicitly addresses Pre-LN Transformers and shows lossless properties for post-norm variants in the appendix. (R2, R3, R4)

4. **Insightful training recipe analysis.** The authors go beyond initialization and experimentally study learning rate and scheduler design, showing that these choices greatly affect the performance of expanded models. An ablation study isolates the effect of the expansion algorithm from the optimized training configuration. (R2, R3, R4)

5. **Strong empirical results on ViT and BERT.** LEMON reduces computational cost by 56.7% for ViT and 33.2% for BERT compared to training from scratch, while achieving similar or better performance with fewer epochs. It also outperforms the recent LiGO method in computational savings. (R2, R4)

6. **Excellent presentation.** Diagrams are easy to understand, and the paper is well-organized. (R1, R4: Presentation score 4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited architecture evaluation.** Experiments are conducted only on Vision Transformers (ViT) and BERT (pre-norm variant). No results are provided for CNNs (e.g., ResNet18→ResNet50, EfficientNetB0→B4) or for post-norm Transformer variants. The claim of compatibility with different architectures lacks empirical verification on final performance metrics beyond the pre-norm case. (R2 W1, R3 W2)  
   - Specific suggestions: test expansion across different block types (Basic→Bottleneck in ResNet) and on NAS-designed networks like EfficientNet. (R2 W1)  
   - Performance evaluation on post-norm Transformers is needed, not just the lossless proof in the appendix. (R3 W2)

2. **Training convergence and fair comparison concerns.** In Figure 7(c,d) (BERT language modeling), the loss curves are still decreasing when training is stopped; training until convergence is needed to verify that the method reaches the same final performance as training from scratch. Moreover, Table 2 is confusing as to whether improvements come from model expansion or simply longer training. (R3 W1, R1 W3)

3. **Lack of theoretical analysis for learning rate and scheduler effects.** Sections 5.1 and 5.2 only provide experimental observations on learning rate and scheduler behavior. A deeper theoretical explanation (e.g., analyzing the Hessian spectrum, gradient magnitude dynamics, or the optimization landscape of expanded models) is missing. (R2 W2)

4. **Early training accuracy drop.** A significant drop in accuracy occurs during early training stages, which may be unacceptable for real-time systems. A suggestion is to use a mask that gradually transitions trainable parameters to smooth this process. (R4 W3)

5. **Novelty concern (minority reviewer).** One reviewer (score 6) finds the novelty limited, describing the key idea as a simple extension of net2net and insufficient for a top-tier venue. The other three reviewers (scores 6,8,8) consider the contributions sufficient, but this criticism should be noted. (R1 W4)

6. **Clarity on capacity and baseline comparisons.** The statement that “the expanded model will never gain more capacity than the source model” is confusing and seems contradictory. Also, it is unclear whether the baseline expansion methods in Section 6 are lossless; if not, the gap with the original model should be reported; if they are lossless, an explanation is needed for why LEMON outperforms AKI (which also breaks symmetry) in Figure 8. (R1 W1, R1 W2)

7. **Missing comparisons with other initialization methods.** The paper should be compared with Mimetic initialization (arXiv 2305.09828), a relevant work mentioned in the related work section. (R3 W3)

8. **Limited demonstration of real-world applicability.** More examples of practical application scenarios would help illustrate the method’s effectiveness and usefulness. (R4 W1)

9. **Unanswered question about larger learning rates.** In Figure 6b, what happens to performance when the learning rate is set larger than the default value? (R4 W2)

10. **Incremental expansion question.** Would incremental expansion (small→middle→big→huge) yield better performance than direct expansion? (R2 question)
