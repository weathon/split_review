## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DRUPI, a method that augments dataset reduction (coreset selection and dataset distillation) by synthesizing privileged information—specifically feature labels and attention labels derived from a pre-trained model—alongside the reduced dataset. The authors argue this provides additional supervision and find that effective feature labels must balance discriminative power and diversity. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet subsets show performance gains when integrated with methods like DC and MTT. Reviewers overall find the idea interesting but raise serious concerns about novelty (similarity to knowledge distillation and prior soft-label methods), insufficient experimental breadth (missing strong baselines and large-scale ImageNet-1k results), lack of storage/computation cost analysis, questionable generalizability (high-resolution, ViT models, large IPCs), and the theoretical analysis being too generic. Two reviewers (R1, R5) are more critical, rating the paper 3, while three (R2, R3, R4) rate it 5 but still note significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- [S1] The introduction of privileged information (feature labels, attention labels) extends the traditional data-label paradigm for dataset reduction and is straightforward to implement.
- [S2] The approach integrates well with existing dataset reduction methods (DC, MTT), demonstrating flexibility and compatibility.
- [S3] Extensive experiments across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny ImageNet, ImageNet subsets) and method categories (coreset selection, distillation) are presented.
- [S4] The paper provides a theoretical framework based on VC theory to support the method, though several reviewers find it insufficiently specific.
- [S5] Charts and figures are clear and easy to understand (R3).
- [S6] The distilled dataset shows some ability to generalize across various architectures (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work:**
  - The use of feature/attention labels and MSE loss closely resembles knowledge distillation and prior works like Re-labeling ImageNet and FKD (R1, R4). The paper does not sufficiently acknowledge or compare with knowledge distillation techniques.
  - The method is not new in concept; reviewers had naturally considered this idea but faced challenges (R5). The core idea of using pre-trained model outputs as supervision has been extensively explored outside dataset reduction.
- **Theoretical analysis:**
  - The VC theory analysis (Section 5) is general and not specific to the proposed method, providing little insight into why privileged information works or how to design it (R1, R3, R5). It feels included for formal completeness rather than substantive contribution (R5).
  - Analysis of feature label balancing (discriminative vs. diverse) is considered trivial and lacks deeper discussion or visualization of how it affects synthetic data (R3).
- **Experimental scope and baselines:**
  - Only applied to older distillation methods DC and MTT; missing comparisons with stronger and more recent methods such as SRe2L, RDED (R1, R5), DREAM, DataDAM, SeqMatch (R2), and distribution matching/gradient matching approaches (R5).
  - Missing results on full ImageNet-1k at 224x224 resolution; experiments are limited to smaller subsets (Tiny ImageNet, ImageNet-64), which reduces practical impact (R1, R5). No testing on 256x256 ImageNet-1k (R2).
  - Many experiments show only marginal improvements over baselines (Table 1, Table 2), and for larger IPCs (e.g., IPC 100, 200) the improvement shrinks, questioning scalability (R3).
  - Missing ablation studies on the effects of soft labels, ZCA, EMA (R2).
- **Storage and computational cost:**
  - The paper does not analyze the additional storage overhead introduced by privileged information, especially feature labels from intermediate layers, which is much larger than soft labels. E.g., on full ImageNet-1k, soft labels alone for IPC 50 are tens of gigabytes (R5).
  - No reporting of training time, memory usage, or storage comparison with baselines (R2).
- **Generalizability:**
  - Cross-architecture generalization only tested on convolutional models (e.g., ConvNet); not tested on ViT models as teacher/student (R2, R5). Potential issues with dimensional gaps in feature maps (R5).
  - Not evaluated on high-resolution datasets or practical domains like clinical or aerial images (R2).
  - Does the use of privileged information violate the core paradigm of dataset condensation, which aims to replace the original dataset? If the evaluation requires the privileged information (feature labels) during training, the reduced dataset is not self-contained (R5, also R3 question 4 about evaluation without feature label matching).
- **Implementation and clarity gaps:**
  - Method description focuses on distillation (bi-level optimization) but does not explain how privileged information is learned with coreset selection, despite significant improvements in Table 1 (R1, R3). Code in supplementary material lacks this part (R3).
  - Equation 6 and the explanation of feature label MSE loss are unclear and lack sufficient detail (R1).
  - Presentation errors: line 253 typo "Figure 3(a)(c)" (R5).
  - Misunderstanding of dataset condensation taxonomy (line 125): authors' classification excludes algorithms like RDED that synthesize data without training, and incorrectly assume all training-based condensation uses bi-level optimization (R5).
- **Specific missing experiments requested by reviewers:**
  - Direct comparison with knowledge distillation baselines (R4).
  - Evaluation of model trained on reduced dataset *without* using feature labels during inference (i.e., same evaluation as MTT/DC) to see if privileged information truly improves dataset quality or just adds supervision at train time (R3).
  - Application to downstream tasks (object detection, segmentation) and domains like NAS or continual learning (R2).
  - Visualizations of synthetic images for different IPCs (R2).
  - Ablation study addressing redundancy between attention labels and feature labels (R1: "are these two labels redundant?").
