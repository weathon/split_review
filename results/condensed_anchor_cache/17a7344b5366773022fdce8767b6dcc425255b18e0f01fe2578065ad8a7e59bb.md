## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CLIPDrag, a method that combines text-based and drag-based editing for precise and ambiguity-free image editing. It introduces Global-Local Motion Supervision (GLMS) to fuse text (global) and drag (local) gradients, and Fast Point Tracking (FPT) to accelerate convergence. Reviewers generally find the motivation clear and the integration of dual inputs novel, but concerns are raised about insufficient comparisons, limited novelty in components, and missing experimental details. The scores show strong disagreement: two reviewers rate 8, one rates 5, and one rates 3 (the most critical reviewer).

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of text and drag signals**: The paper presents the first algorithm to combine text-guided and drag-guided editing, leveraging both global and local information for more controlled, precise edits (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Global-Local Gradient Fusion (GLMS)**: The decomposition of the text gradient into components parallel and perpendicular to the drag gradient is an interesting and effective approach to unify the two modalities (R2, R3). Experiments show notable performance improvements (R3).
- **Efficient Fast Point Tracking (FPT)**: The method improves editing speed by guiding handle points toward target positions faster (R1).
- **Extensive ablation studies**: Ablations on point tracking, GLMS, text control, etc. demonstrate clear performance gains (R1). Results are well-presented (R4).
- **Qualitative results**: Representative examples provide easy intuition and clarity (R1). The paper shows a deep technical understanding of the challenges in combining editing signals (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient and narrow comparisons**: The paper only compares with DragDiffusion (Figure 6), lacking comprehensive quantitative and qualitative comparisons with recent methods such as SINE, Paint by Example, FlexiEdit, RegionDrag, InstantDrag, LightningDrag, StableDrag, and others (R2, R3, R4). This limits the demonstration of CLIPDrag’s advantages. Additional comparisons should include sequential application (text after drag, drag after text) to highlight the benefit of joint editing (R2). More quantitative metrics (e.g., identity preservation, edit accuracy) are needed (R4).
- **Unconvincing motivation example (Figure 1)**: The example only shows a failure of drag-based editing; the proposed result appears visually similar to drag-only editing (statue example), questioning the added benefit (R2). A better example would illustrate a scenario where text refines a subtle drag edit (e.g., adjusting a smile). (R2)
- **Limited novelty in gradient combination**: Equation (6) combines gradients linearly. A non-linear combination might yield better results, and alternative fusion strategies should be compared (R2).
- **FPT novelty and analysis**: Searching handle points along the path from handle to target has been explored in DragGAN and DragDiffusion. The paper lacks side-by-side comparisons of point searching strategies, clear explanation of the search-space constraint and its impact, and rigorous analysis of computational complexity and convergence properties (R1, R4). The assumption that nearest-neighbor points move monotonically toward the target is not justified given the non-linear optimization (R1).
- **Unclear handling of distinct effect regions**: The paper does not explain how text-based and drag-based editing affect different image regions and how these regions are defined, integrated, or adjusted during editing (R2).
- **Missing identity preservation explanation**: Intuition for how text signals preserve the object’s original identity beyond citing DragDiffusion is lacking (R1). The paper should elaborate on conditions where text prompts particularly enhance identity preservation (R4).
- **Incomplete theoretical justification for gradient manipulation**: The gradient accumulation assumes continuous latent code; why such gradient manipulation still leads to plausible images is unclear (R1).
- **Figure 2 workflow unclear**: The diagram uses SD rather than a VAE; if CLIP guidance is applied, the latent should ideally be converted to the pixel domain to align with CLIP (R3).
- **Ablation visual differences subtle**: In Figure 8, the visual differences between subfigures (b) and (c) are hard to discern; more detailed quantitative ablations are needed (R3).
- **Missing performance metrics**: No inference time comparisons are reported. A breakdown of time spent on different stages (point tracking, image generation) is needed to understand computational bottlenecks and practical applicability (R4).
- **User input optimization**: Adding a text prompt increases user effort compared to drag-only methods. The paper could explore automatic text generation (e.g., via GPT-4V) to reduce burden (R4). The sensitivity of results to prompt quality and specificity is not discussed (R4).
- **Missing analysis of failure cases and parameter choices**: Common failure cases when text and drag conflict are not shown (R1). The number of denoising iterations and its impact on editing quality are not explained (R1). How masks (shown in one example) are incorporated into the framework is not described (R1). The paper should provide examples with and without drag for specific text prompts (e.g., "The sculpture is smiling and not showing his teeth") to isolate contributions (R2).
