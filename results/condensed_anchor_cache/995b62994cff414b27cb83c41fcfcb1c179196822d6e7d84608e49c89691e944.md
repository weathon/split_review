## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents RouGE, a gated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) structure combined with low-rank adapters in a parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT) framework. It is designed to improve the robustness of SAM-based segmentation models on degraded images (low-light, rainy, blurred). RouGE uses multiple probability gates to decompose image conditions and activate a set of low-rank experts. Experiments on synthetic degraded datasets show improved performance over other adaptation/prompting methods while tuning only 1.5% of parameters.

### Strengths
- The motivation of addressing robustness in SAM-based models for real-world degraded conditions is timely and important.
- The idea is simple, the paper is easy to follow, and the use of gated MoE with low-rank experts provides a parameter-efficient approach (only 1.5% parameters).
- The method shows promising performance improvements and superior results compared to other adapt/prompting methods on both degraded and clean images.
- The evaluation is broad: multiple types of degradation (low-light, rainy, blur) are tested and comparisons with various PEFT methods are provided.
- Qualitative experiments in the main text and appendix are comprehensive and clearly demonstrate performance gains.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and MoE design limitations**:
  - Integrating MoE with low-rank adapters for SAM may not provide new insights; similar MoE designs are already used in image restoration (e.g., [1]).
  - The probability gate design is general (relies on image features for implicit decision-making) and not specifically tailored for degradation handling.
  - The proposed RouGE activates all experts, losing the inference-speed advantage of conventional MoE; it resembles a weighted combination of gating functions rather than sparse expert activation. In fact, the structure appears very similar to a concatenation of layer norms with dimension changes, limiting its novelty.
  - A minority of reviewers (scores 3) consider the method naive compared to advanced MoE work, while a majority (scores 5) find the approach sensible but still require more evidence of novelty.

- **Missing and insufficient comparisons**:
  - The paper only compares with one base SAM model; generalization across other SAM-based works is unclear.
  - No comparison of the proposed PEFT strategy against MoE with full fine-tuning, which is needed to evaluate the benefit of parameter efficiency.
  - The baseline adapter integration uses a sequential setup, while parallel configurations (e.g., AdaptFormer) can yield better performance – RouGE is not tested in a parallel MoE adapter setting, weakening the comparison.
  - Variability (variance/confidence intervals) of results is not reported, raising potential cherry-picking concerns (e.g., taking the best RouGE performance vs. lower performance from counterparts). One reviewer notes that in Table 1, layer norm (LN) already improves the baseline hugely with only 10% of RouGE’s trainable parameters, making RouGE’s contribution less convincing.

- **Unclear or missing implementation details**:
  - The image feature \(F_t\) is not clearly defined; its generation process and role are ambiguous. If CLIP is used for extraction, the computational load is significant and requires a full pipeline breakdown.
  - The object detection model used for bounding boxes is not specified, affecting reproducibility.
  - The adapter’s bottleneck dimension is omitted.
  - The paper claims \(2 \times 6 = 12\) adapters but does not explain why the total trainable parameters are fewer than in AdaptFormer (a quantitative breakdown is needed).

- **Incomplete ablation studies**:
  - Ablations on critical hyper-parameters are missing: number of experts (lazy vs. trainable), adapter dimensions, RouGE layer placement, and GPU memory/computational overhead.
  - There is no in-depth analysis of which experts are activated for which degradation type and how the weighting varies with input – i.e., the gate’s behavior is not characterized.

- **Real-world degradation validity**:
  - Most test datasets contain only synthetic degradation (e.g., low-light, rainy, blur from simulation), so the claim of “real-world” robustness is questionable. The paper does not evaluate on genuine real-world degraded images.
  - Figures 2 and 3 in the motivation only use synthesized degraded images; it is unclear if SAM’s performance drop holds for real-world degradations. Furthermore, if degradation is only synthetic, one could simply add such synthetic degradations to SAM’s pretraining data to improve performance directly – a baseline that is not discussed.

- **Manuscript quality issues**:
  - There are many typos (e.g., “Musk” instead of “Mask” in Figure 1); consistency can be improved.
  - Figure 1 is confusing: the “clean image” is shown as the same as the rainy image, while blurry and low-light images have different content.

- **Missing experiments** (requested by reviewers):
  - Test RouGE on real-world degraded images at both training and inference (and clarify whether training data is real or generated).
  - Add ablations on the number of lazy experts, trainable experts, adapter design, and layer placement.
  - Provide quantitative analysis of why the method has fewer parameters despite using an MoE structure.
