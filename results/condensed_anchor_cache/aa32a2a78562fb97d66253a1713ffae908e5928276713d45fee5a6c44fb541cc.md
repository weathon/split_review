## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DP-BiTFiT, which applies DP-SGD to fine-tune only bias terms (BiTFiT) of pre-trained models. The main claim is that this approach matches state-of-the-art DP accuracy while being significantly faster (2–30×) and more memory-efficient (2–8×) than full DP fine-tuning, and also works with long sequences and high-resolution images. Reviewers generally agree that the method is simple and the empirical results are extensive, but there is strong disagreement on novelty and practical significance. One reviewer (score 8) finds the work strong and well-executed, while another (score 3) judges it as incremental and limited in performance; others rate 5–6, noting efficiency benefits but lacking algorithmic innovation.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency and scalability**: DP-BiTFiT achieves large speedups and memory savings over full DP fine-tuning, both in theory (asymptotic complexity) and in experiments across multiple model families (ViT, ResNet, RoBERTa, GPT-2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Experiments cover image classification (CIFAR-10, ImageNet), text classification, and language generation, with ablation on model size, architecture, and privacy levels (ε=1–8).
- **Clear motivation and analysis**: The paper provides intuitive reasoning for why bias-term fine-tuning is suitable for DP (very few trainable parameters, no activation storage needed) and includes theoretical breakdowns of time/space complexity (Figures 1–2, Section 3).
- **Model-agnostic and parameter‑efficient**: DP-BiTFiT does not modify the network architecture and trains only ~0.1% of parameters, making it applicable to many existing models.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Several reviewers (R2, R3, R5) note that combining BiTFiT with DP-SGD is a straightforward adaptation of existing methods. The paper’s claim of “substantial algorithmic innovation” is not supported; no clear alternative “naive” combination is contrasted against.
- **Performance gaps**: DP-BiTFiT sometimes underperforms other parameter‑efficient DP methods (e.g., DP‑LoRA) on accuracy (R2). Non‑private baselines occasionally show worse perplexity than private results (Table 4), suggesting possible hyperparameter or statistical‑significance issues (R3).
- **Lack of intuition for bias-term effectiveness**: The paper does not explain why updating only biases can match full fine-tuning performance under DP, nor why bias terms across different modules (attention, fully connected, convolutional) should have similar importance (R4). This limits general understanding of when the method will work.
- **Generality concerns**: Some recent LLMs (e.g., PaLM) omit biases for training stability, and models with convolution layers lacking bias terms may not benefit (Remark 4.2) (R4). It remains unclear how well DP-BiTFiT transfers to complex tasks like reasoning (GSM8k, MMLU) (R4).
- **Memory breakdown missing**: Figure 1 does not separate memory into base model, trainable parameters, activations, and optimizer states; a more detailed breakdown would clarify the source of savings (R1).
- **Figure 4 is confusing**: The x‑axis labels (“maximum throughput of algorithm”) are ambiguous; DP‑BiTFiT appears both on the axis and in the legend, making interpretation difficult (R1).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Table numbers and titles should appear before the table (Tables 7–16) (R3); misuse of \citep vs. \citet (R3, R5). R4 rates presentation as “poor” overall.
- **Dubious operation counting**: Counting “operations” without big‑O notation is misleading (e.g., “3Bp” sums multiplications and additions as if equal), despite the authors’ claim that big‑O is “not precise” (R5).
- **Unclear gradient explanation**: The statement “bias gradients do not need the output gradient” is confusing; a step‑by‑step derivation is needed to show how per‑sample bias gradients are computed without materializing the full activation gradient (R5).
- **Missing comparison with other DP PEFT methods**: Theoretical and empirical comparisons (speed, memory, throughput) are only shown against full DP fine-tuning; DP‑LoRA and other parameter‑efficient DP approaches are not included (R3, R5).
- **Reproducibility concern**: Results attributed to Bu et al. for CIFAR‑10 (98.9% in Table 5) cannot be located in their paper; clarification is required (R1).

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: While one reviewer (score 8) praises the empirical contribution and organization, others (score 3) consider the contribution merely incremental and the performance benefits modest. This contrast is reflected in the novelty criticisms above.
