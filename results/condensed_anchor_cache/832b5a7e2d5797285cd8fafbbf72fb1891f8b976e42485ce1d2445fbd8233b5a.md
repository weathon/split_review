## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewer opinions diverge sharply. One reviewer (score 5, confidence 3) found the approach innovative and competitive on high-dimensional benchmarks, praising the use of the action functional to transform the loss landscape. The other four reviewers (scores 1, 1, 3, 3; confidences 3–4) raised severe and overlapping concerns about writing quality, algorithmic clarity, experimental validation, and the lack of comparisons with established methods. Two reviewers gave the lowest soundness/presentation scores and described the paper as very poorly written and unconvincing. A minority positive view exists but is strongly contradicted by the majority.

### Strengths

*   **Novel formulation.** The paper introduces an original loss function derived from the action functional, which transforms a rough input landscape into a benign one for neural network parameters (Reviewer 1).
*   **Successful high‑dimensional validation.** The method approximates global minima on thousand‑dimensional test functions and is competitive with existing neural‑network‑based optimizers (Reviewer 1).
*   **Important problem domain.** Finding ground‑truth structures for molecules and clusters when no training data exist is a valuable and under‑served problem (Reviewer 5).
*   **Innovative core idea.** Mimicking the energy landscape with a neural‑network class (PLNet (Wang et al. 2024)) that has no local minima is a novel approach (Reviewer 5).
*   **Advantage on selected test functions.** Experiments show clear improvement over Adam and StriderNet on the two test functions used (Reviewer 5).
*   *Potential significance.* Reviewer 4 noted that if the method indeed found global minima for arbitrary non‑convex functions it would be a “holy grail”, but emphasised that the current evidence does not support this claim.
*   Reviewer 2 acknowledged there *could* be merit but found it impossible to evaluate due to the poor presentation.

### Weaknesses

*   **Poor writing and presentation.** The paper is very poorly written, the algorithm is poorly motivated, and the pseudocode (Algorithm 1) is unintelligible (repeated index *i*). Sections 4.2 and 4.3 are ambiguous (Reviewers 2, 4, 5). Notation is inconsistent: it is unclear whether the inputs to the neural net are points or trajectories (lines 5–6 vs. 19–20) and what the variable *t* refers to (line 111) (Reviewer 5).
*   **Insufficient related work.** The literature review is thin. The paper claims (line 47) that only three deep‑learning papers address global‑minimum search, ignoring many context‑specific DL approaches (Reviewer 4). State‑of‑the‑art methods such as RoseTTAFold, GOFEE, and AlphaFold 3 are not discussed (Reviewer 3).
*   **Weak experimental validation.**  
    * Benchmarks compare only with GD and Adam, omitting classical optimization methods such as Nelder‑Mead, conjugate gradient, and BFGS/L‑BFGS (Reviewer 4).  
    * Only two test functions from a pool of 30 are evaluated, raising the danger of overfitting to those synthetic cases (Reviewer 5).  
    * The numerical results are inconclusive (Reviewer 3) and do not convincingly demonstrate generalizability. The risk that the method ignores downward spikes in a landscape (because it fits a smooth trunk) is not ruled out (Reviewer 5).  
    * No comparison with well‑established physics/chemistry global‑optimization methods (e.g., basin‑hopping, simulated annealing) is provided (Reviewer 1).
*   **Limited applicability.** The derivation relies on overdamped Langevin systems (low‑temperature, high‑friction). No discussion or extension to inertial Langevin systems or more general dynamics is given (Reviewer 1).
*   **No analytical justification for generality.** The method is presented as completely general, yet no proof or rigorous argument is offered. The empirical evidence is far from convincing (Reviewer 4). Key phrases are undefined: line 121 (“most‑likely ground state” – under which distribution? is this the global minimum?), line 109 (“squared error deviation from gradient descent” – no proper definition) (Reviewer 4).
*   **Model explanation is unclear.**  
    * What exactly is a “benign” loss landscape? (Reviewer 3)  
    * Why use a path‑integral approach instead of simply simulating the Langevin equations? (Reviewer 3)  
    * Why was PLNet (Wang et al. 2024) chosen? That paper describes a mathematical condition, not a concrete architecture, and implementation details are missing (Reviewer 5).  
    * How can a neural network that has no local minima (by construction) faithfully model a ground‑truth function that has many local minima? (Reviewer 4)
*   **Lack of realistic experiments.** The test functions are synthetic and not clearly linked to the target problem of biomolecular structure prediction (Reviewer 3).
*   **Low overall quality.** Two reviewers (R2, R3) gave the lowest possible scores for soundness, presentation, and contribution, stating the paper cannot be salvaged in a rebuttal (Reviewer 2) and does not present compelling novelty (Reviewer 3).
