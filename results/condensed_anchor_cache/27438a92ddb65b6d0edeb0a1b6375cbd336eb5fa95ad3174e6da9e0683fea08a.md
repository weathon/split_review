## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses class imbalance (the “Matthew effect”) in semi-supervised semantic segmentation by reframing the task as a conditional discrete data generation problem. The proposed DiffMatch integrates generative models into SSL and introduces a mathematically derived debiased adjustment at each sampling step to reduce overfitting to head classes. Experiments on natural, remote sensing, and medical image benchmarks show competitive or superior results, especially under limited labels and severe imbalance. Reviewers find the motivation clear and the theoretical analysis thoughtful, but raise concerns about the soundness of using diffusion for segmentation, the handling of noisy pseudo labels, overclaimed generality to non-long-tail datasets, computational costs, and missing implementation details. Reviewer 1 (score 8) is more positive, while Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 6 each) are more cautious, with Reviewer 3 questioning the practical utility and extensibility.

### Strengths
- Novel integration of generative models into SSL for semantic segmentation, offering a fresh perspective to mitigate class imbalance (Matthew effect).
- The mathematical derivation of the debiased adjustment is ingenious and provides theoretical insights beyond heuristic fixes (Reviewer 1).
- Clear presentation and easy-to-follow motivation with thorough analysis of the Matthew effect (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Extensive evaluation across multiple domains (natural images, remote sensing, medical) demonstrating performance gains, especially in low-label, high-imbalance scenarios (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Technically innovative in combining conditional discrete data generation with debiased adjustments; experimental results show strong performance on both head and tail classes (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Soundness of diffusion for segmentation**: Diffusion models are not yet mainstream in visual perception tasks and do not rank as top performers in segmentation; using them here may be conceptually strained (Reviewer 3).
- **Noisy pseudo labels**: The primary challenge in SSL segmentation is noisy pseudo labels. DiffMatch aligns predictions but does not guarantee correctness, and the noise/denoise process adds no new information beyond labeled data (Reviewer 3).
- **Overclaimed generality to long-tail datasets**: Remote sensing (WHU-CD) and medical (ACDC) datasets used to show domain transfer are binary or single-organ, lacking long-tail classes. Better choices (e.g., multi-organ/tumor datasets) would be needed to support the claim (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also suggests comparing with recent ECCV 2024 work MPMC for fairness.
- **Incomplete reporting of computational cost**: Training-stage cost compared to baseline is not reported, only inference (Table 5). The reason why few diffusion steps suffice and the impact of new loss functions on efficiency are unexplained (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Lack of implementation details**: The underlying framework (e.g., teacher-student scheme) is not specified; can DiffMatch be applied to transformer-based architectures? (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing ablation/analysis**: 
  - The factor *t* introduced in Appendix C needs a clear derivation, reasoning, and discussion of its impact if removed (Reviewer 1).
  - No distribution visualization or other metric to measure prediction alignment (beyond final segmentation) (Reviewer 3).
  - Authors should explore performance per category/scenario to identify sensitivity (Reviewer 2).
- **Extensibility and baseline claims**: DiffMatch is claimed as a solid future baseline, but diffusion models are not an extendable design for SSS research, and the paper does not release code/checkpoints/training logs equivalent to UniMatch’s strong codebase (Reviewer 3). Related work discussion could be expanded to better position the study (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor issues**: Figures 2 and 3 are too small and captions insufficiently explain mathematical symbols; the presentation is hard to follow before reading Section 3 (Reviewer 3). A typo in L19 (“its their”) (Reviewer 1). Limitations of the method should be discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Open questions** (overlap with criticisms):
  - Would dynamic adjustment of sampling steps reduce computational cost? (Reviewer 1)
  - Can DiffMatch be applied to other domains like semi-supervised classification? (Reviewer 1)
  - How to measure alignment effectiveness via distribution visualization? (Reviewer 3)
  - What are the efficiency contributions of the new loss functions? (Reviewer 3)
