## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reverse-engineers one-hidden-layer MLPs trained on group composition (specifically \(S_5\)). It proposes a new algorithm based on \(\rho\)-sets—sets of vectors permuted by irreps—which yields a unified explanation reconciling previously disparate observations (irrep sparsity from Chughtai et al., coset concentration from Stander et al.). The explanation is translated into a “compact proof” (a verifier program) that lower-bounds model accuracy faster than brute force (≈3× faster), attaining non-vacuous bounds (≥95% accuracy) for roughly 45–50% of trained models. For the other half the proof gives vacuous bounds, indicating the explanation does not cover those solutions. The paper is rigorous and presents a more complete description than prior work, but is highly technical and limited to \(S_5\); reviewers disagree on the significance of the 50% success rate and on the general utility of compact proofs as an interpretability metric.

### Strengths
- The paper discovers an elegant, non-obvious algorithm for group composition in a single ReLU layer that multiple prior works missed, and it clarifies how earlier observations (irrep sparsity, coset concentration) follow from this construction.
- The compact proofs are highly detailed and rigorous, going through every key mathematical step without hand-waving.
- This work demonstrates compact proofs as an interpretability metric in a substantially more complex setting than previous work (Gross et al.).
- The authors honestly report and discuss weaknesses: the proof fails on many networks, the attempted V_coset proof gives vacuous bounds, and actual runtimes are reported transparently.
- The paper exposes limitations of causal interventions (like those used in Stander et al.) as evidence for explanations, because the proposed verifier provides a more quantitative test.
- The evaluation is thorough: it shows that the explanation covers only a subset of possible solutions, and it uses the compact proof to quantify explanation quality.
- One reviewer (R3) notes that overall the contribution is solid, with no significant weaknesses.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope and generality:** The study is confined to \(S_5\); it is unclear whether compact proofs can be applied to datasets without closed-form solutions (e.g., language tasks, CIFAR-10). Reviewer 1 questions the broader applicability; Reviewer 2 is skeptical that the method will scale beyond toy networks, though the paper does not claim it does.
- **Modest speedup:** The compact proof is only ≈3× faster than brute force, not the 120× that asymptotic reasoning might suggest. Reviewer 2 stresses that the practical coefficient matters more than asymptotic complexity.
- **Interpretation incompleteness (50% success rate):** The compact proof yields non-vacuous bounds for only about half the models. Reviewer 1 finds this “concerning” and argues it implies the \(\rho\)-set interpretation is not general enough; Reviewer 2 notes that the failures correspond to imperfect explanations but still acknowledges this as a limitation.
- **Clarity and exposition difficulties:** The paper is highly technical and builds on three prior papers, making it hard to follow even for readers familiar with representation theory. Reviewer 1 states that the \(\rho\)-set construction and how it was discovered are unclear, hampering reproducibility. Reviewer 2 found Sections 4 and 5 confusing, and the term “compact proof” cryptic until page 7. Specific complaints include: Figure 1 being hard to parse, the definition of compact proof needing intuitive explanation earlier, and the need for a clearer flow through the technical derivations. Reviewer 2 suggests reversing the order of presentation (start with Eqn 4, then show equivalence to Eqn 3, then derive neuron forms). Both R1 and R2 request more explicit step-by-step explanation of how the \(\rho\)-set interpretation was arrived at.
- **Weak link between compact proof and verification of mechanistic explanation:** Reviewer 2 is skeptical that a compact proof of an accuracy bound directly validates a mechanistic explanation, especially because accuracy can be over-determined (multiple parallel components each sufficient). Reviewer 2 also questions whether a good explanation should always enable faster proofs or be robust to worst-case guarantees.
- **Missing direct evidence for the explanation:** Reviewer 2 would like to see quantitative tests of the explanation’s predictions beyond the compact proof, e.g., whether parameters match the predicted form (MSE, correlation), whether substituting predicted parameters preserves performance, and whether neuron activation patterns match the derived form. Reviewer 1 similarly asks for a clearer demonstration of how the \(\rho\)-set interpretation was identified mechanistically.
- **Title claim contested:** Reviewer 1 argues that “Unifying” is too strong when the explanation works for only ≈50% of models, undermining the claim of broad unification.
- **Questions from reviewers that overlap with criticisms:**
    - Can compact proofs be used on datasets without closed-form solutions? (R1, Q1)
    - Did the authors try other ways of modeling V_coset? If so, how many? (R1, Q2)
    - In Section 3.2, why is the number of neurons taken equal to the embedding dimension? Is this necessary or coincidental? (R1, Q3)
    - How well does the square of the minimal \(\rho\)-set size correlate with the probability that a representation is learned? (R2, Major Comment 5)
    - Why does the minimal \(\rho\)-set size sometimes exceed the group order? (R2, Minor Comment 14)
- **Citation issue:** The paper cites Geiger et al. for Causal Scrubbing, but the original work is Chan et al. (2022). (R2, Minor Comment 8; R3, Question)
- Additional minor clarity points (R2): Line 243 should note that \(w^i_l(x)\) is a lookup table (construction, not conclusion); Eqns 2 and 3 could be clarified by replacing \(b^T \rho(x)a\) with \(a^T \rho(x^{-1})b\); the explanation that embeddings are constant on a coset for a neuron (Line 194) could be misread as a property of all networks rather than a constructed one; the definition of “compact proof” should be distinguished from a standard mathematical proof; and the term “family of cosets” on Line 196 needs clarification.
