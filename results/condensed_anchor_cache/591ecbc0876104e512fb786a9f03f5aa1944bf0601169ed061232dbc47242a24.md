## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces semantic-level peripheral information—contextual, experiential, and perturbative—to improve Visual Speech Recognition (VSR). The proposed framework integrates these cues with a large language model (LLM) and achieves Word Error Rates of 24.5% on LRS3 and 33.4% on AVSpeech, setting a new state-of-the-art for similar hours of training data.

### Strengths
- **Novel concept**: The paper is the first to introduce peripheral information (especially experiential cues) into machine lip-reading, broadening the traditional focus on cropped lip regions. This idea is considered innovative by all reviewers, though one notes that previous work used raw frames, cropped faces, or lips with surrounding areas. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Clear performance improvement**: The method demonstrates a 2.2 WER (14.3% relative) improvement over the baseline without peripheral information on LRS3, and achieves SOTA results on that dataset for comparable training data hours. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Strong motivation**: It is well-established that context aids human lip-reading, and this work explores that principle at scale for the first time. (Reviewer 1)
- **Several ablation studies**: The paper includes ablation experiments examining different types of peripheral information. (Reviewer 4)
- **Effective LLM integration**: The framework successfully leverages LLMs for contextual modeling and reasoning, enhancing VSR performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing quality**: One reviewer found the paper “well written and easy to follow”; other reviewers rated presentation low (1–2), citing unintuitive terminology and typos. (Reviewer 4 positive; Reviewers 1, 2, 3 negative)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient exploration of which peripheral information is useful**: The paper does not systematically identify which cues are beneficial, which are not, or how to extract/filter useful information. Concrete examples of how peripheral information affects lip-reading and the differences before/after its use are missing. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**:
  - Not compared with methods that combine LLM logits during decoding (e.g., Prakash 2021) or re-rank beam search predictions with a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Prajwal 2022). (Reviewer 1)
  - Not compared with the highly similar model in “Large Language Models Are Strong Audio-Visual Speech Recognition Learners”, which achieves 24.0 WER on LRS3 with only 48M trainable parameters—this paper’s performance (24.5 WER) is inadequate given additional data and LLM. (Reviewer 2)
  - On LRS3, the 2023 paper “Jointly Learning Visual and Auditory Speech Representations from Raw Data” obtained 24.4 WER without an LM and 23.1 with an LM using the same training data (1,759 unlabeled + 433 labeled hours); this paper uses extra data and an LLM yet gets 24.5 WER—a weaker result. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing analyses and experiments**:
  - No experiment to isolate the importance of visual information: could a pre-trained VSR model’s output be provided to an LLM with peripheral context and LoRA-fine-tuned to predict corrected transcripts? (Reviewer 1)
  - No analysis of cases where peripheral information helped versus hurt. (Reviewer 1)
  - Ablation study design is flawed: components are added sequentially instead of being removed from the full model (e.g., F – EPI, F – PPI, F – CPI) to clearly show each type’s benefit. (Reviewer 4)
  - The visual encoder AV-Hubert is no longer SOTA; the paper does not test with more recent encoders (e.g., AV-Data2vec, Raven, AutoAVSR) or multiple encoders to demonstrate generalizability. (Reviewer 4)
  - It is unclear why perturbations are considered “peripheral information” rather than data augmentation; a justification is needed. (Reviewer 4)
  - The AVSpeech test set is trimmed to match LRS3 size—why not use the full test set? (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - The paper introduces many unintuitive terminologies (e.g., “peripheral vision” analogy stretched) and abbreviations, making the straightforward idea unnecessarily hard to understand. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2 calls Figure 1 unrepresentative)
  - Several typos: “projecotr” (Fig. 2 caption), “funcition” (line 310), incomplete sentence in line 356. (Reviewer 4)
- **Scalability and computational cost**: Reliance on LLMs for processing peripheral information may limit applicability in resource-constrained environments; training/inference cost is not discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Data diversity**: Experiments use only LRS3 (English) and AVSpeech (mostly English); real-world diversity (accents, languages) is not addressed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Code not available**: No public code release hinders reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing implementation details**: No description of how the mouth ROI is cropped (e.g., facial landmarks). (Reviewer 4)
- **Requested parameter count**: How many trainable parameters does the entire model have (contrast with 48M in the cited work)? (Reviewer 2)
