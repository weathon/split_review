## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes AdvFooler, a lightweight defense against query-based black-box adversarial attacks on NLP models. It adds Gaussian noise to the latent representations at each layer during inference, aiming to confuse the attacker’s word-importance estimation. The method requires no additional training overhead, is attack-agnostic, and is evaluated on two datasets (AGNEWS, IMDB) with BERT-base and RoBERTa-base models against three attacks.

### Strengths

- **Simplicity and low overhead**: The method is "dead simple to implement and use" (Reviewer 4), requires no additional computational overhead during training or testing, and is pluggable (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The added compute is negligible (Reviewer 4); one reviewer notes that ease of implementation would make it highly adoptable and could have high practical impact (Reviewer 4).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The authors conduct extensive evaluations with two BERT models, two datasets, three attack methods, and include qualitative analyses (Reviewer 2).
- **Effectiveness against several attacks**: The method demonstrates reasonable robustness improvements (Reviewers 3, 4) and is "reliably top 3 among the methods" on many tasks (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical and empirical analysis**: The paper provides both theoretical and empirical justification for the randomization approach and its impact on clean accuracy (Reviewer 4).
- **Well-written**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow (Reviewer 3).

One reviewer (score 8) finds the method’s simplicity a "significant strength" and believes it is "strongly worth disseminating" despite not being SOTA, drawing an analogy to dropout approximating ensemble training (Reviewer 4). Other reviewers (scores 5,6,3) are less enthusiastic, noting the method does not outperform prior work.

### Weaknesses

- **Does not achieve state-of-the-art robustness**: On Table 2, for the AGNEWS dataset, AdvFooler under BERT-base shows lower accuracy under attack (AuA) than RanMASK, and under RoBERTa-base both TMD and RanMASK outperform it (Reviewer 2). Similarly, on IMDB with RoBERTa, TMD reliably outperforms (Reviewer 4). Reviewers 1 and 3 also note performance is not superior to prior works.
- **Hard to draw clear robustness-accuracy tradeoff conclusions**: The comparison lacks standard deviations across multiple runs; a 1 pp difference can be significant but is not interpretable without error bars (Reviewer 4). Tuning of baselines (e.g., RanMASK, SAFER) to match the accuracy or robustness of AdvFooler is missing, so it is unclear if any method is Pareto-dominant (Reviewer 4).
- **Noise scale selection unclear**: The authors claim noise scale ν is chosen so clean accuracy drops at most 1% on the test set, but Table 2 and 3 do not consistently reflect this (Reviewer 2). In Figure 4, AuA curves are not monotone, raising the question of whether AuA is also considered when selecting ν (Reviewer 2). Also, from Figure 4 it appears hard to choose a consistent noise scale across different datasets and models (Reviewer 3). The clean accuracy drops very little at really high noise rates, which is not explained (Reviewer 4).
- **Motivation and mechanism not fully explained**: Why does adding noise fail to degrade benign performance, yet confuse attackers? (Reviewer 3). The empirical observation that small noise does not change prediction is noted but not fundamentally justified (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited attack scope**: The method is designed only for query-based black-box word substitution attacks. It is not validated against white-box attacks (e.g., gradient-based like HotFlip), decision-based attacks, or other threat models, which is a significant limitation for claiming generality (Reviewer 3). The white-box evaluation using HotFlip (Appendix C7) may be misleading because the attacker likely has access to unnoised parameters, which can be reverse-engineered from multiple queries; the paper should either give full white-box access or tone down claims (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation and comparison studies**:
  - No comparison with adding DP-style noise to embeddings to contrast the approaches (Reviewer 1).
  - No study of adding different noise scales to different layers instead of the same scale (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing baseline: comparison with diverse ensemble methods designed for adversarial robustness, such as the ADP regularizer (pang19a PMLR v97) (Reviewer 4).
  - Missing baseline: the paper uses ASCC, but DNE (Dirichlet Neighborhood Ensemble) outperforms ASCC significantly; DNE should be compared (Reviewer 4).
- **Compute time results absent**: Compute time comparison across methods is claimed to be in supplemental materials but was not found; this would be a key advantage of AdvFooler and should be shown (Reviewer 4). It is also unclear if simple adversarial training on word substitutions is significantly more compute-intensive (Reviewer 4).
- **Adversary can circumvent the defense**: The attacker could use a proxy model (e.g., same architecture) to infer important words via attention values, or use brute-force substitution over small spans (as in the considered datasets) – these scenarios are not addressed and may weaken the defense’s practical utility (Reviewer 1).
- **Prediction consistency not analyzed**: How often does the randomized model (F_advfooler) produce the same prediction as the clean model (F) under different noise rates? The claim in Section 3.1 lacks a clean comparison; Figure 4 partly addresses this but a more direct analysis would help explain why clean accuracy suffers so little (Reviewer 4).
