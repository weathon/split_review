## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Volterra-like higher-order convolution (HoConv) operator for the first layer of a simple CNN, designed to capture multiplicative pixel interactions inspired by biological vision. Evaluated on synthetic datasets and small benchmarks (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Imagenette), it reports modest accuracy gains over a plain CNN. Reviews are split: two reviewers (ratings 6) find the idea rigorous and the synthetic analysis instructive; two reviewers (ratings 3) consider the experimental evidence too weak to support the claims and question the biological plausibility.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and theoretical grounding (R1, R4):** The notion of higher-order convolutions is novel, and the Volterra-series formulation is clearly presented (R4: “the equations were clear”). The approach is inspired by nonlinear processing in biological visual systems.
- **Some empirical improvement (R2, R3):** “Extensions of neural networks to more non-linear behaviour are certainly of interest and the authors do find some improvements” (R2). Results on synthetic data and on MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10/100 show HoConv beating the simple CNN baseline (R3: “convincing results on synthetic, then standard visual datasets”).
- **Focus on explainability and small-scale analysis (R3, contrasting with the weakness from R1/R2):** Reviewer 3 specifically praises the decision to use synthetic data and “relatively small datasets to allow for comprehensive testing and analysis. This allows to better explain *why* the model brings something to the community, something often missing in papers just exhibiting accuracy results.”
- **Use of Representational Similarity Analysis (R1):** RSA provides “additional insight into the method’s internal workings” and reveals representational differences between HoConv and standard layers.
- **Rigorous introduction (R4):** The concept is introduced in a self-contained way, making the mathematics accessible.

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental setup too small and simple (R1, R4, R2):**  
  - “The experimental setup is overly simplistic, relying heavily on toy datasets and limited architectures, making it difficult to assess the method’s effectiveness in real-world scenarios” (R1).  
  - “The models and datasets are too small to claim improvement over regular CNNs … models here are so small that residual connections … are not even used” (R4).  
  - R2: “They only evaluate on small datasets and with the exception of MNIST the performance of both their model and the CNN they compare to is far from the state of the art.” For FashionMNIST, HOG features + SVM or a vanilla 2‑layer convnet without preprocessing both achieve ~92.5%, higher than the ~90–92% reported here.
- **No scaling to deeper/larger architectures (R1, R2, R4):**  
  - No experiments with ResNet, ImageNet, or even moderately deep networks. “There is no exploration of how this method could scale to deeper networks (e.g., ResNet) or more complex tasks (e.g., ImageNet)” (R1).  
  - R2: “To establish a new architecture the authors should test different scales of their model … show that their advantage is not just a side effect of a slightly better match in training parameters.”
- **Missing hyperparameter and training details (R4):** “There is nothing in the paper about image preprocessing, image augmentations, loss functions, learning rates, dropout probabilities and everything else that should be properly controlled for (and more importantly, stated in the paper).”
- **Biological inspiration claim is weak (R2):**  
  - “Volterra kernels are an early idea to capture non-linear functions at all, but they are not a good way to capture biologically plausible non-linearities. The receptive fields and non-linear interaction over space in early visual processing are well understood and look nothing like the classical polynomial proposed by the authors.”  
  - “Conversely the authors method allows the approximation of any non-linear function including ones very far from biological plausibility.”  
  - R2 recommends comparing to VOneNet (dicarlolab/vonenet) if the biological argument is to be made.
- **RSA analysis not clearly insightful (R2, R3):**  
  - R2: “I am still quite uncertain what we can learn from the RSA analyses. What insight is provided by similarities between stimuli are generally lower in HoCNN than in the CNN?”  
  - R3 questions the use of “Absolute difference between the two RDM” (Fig. 6c) and suggests a dissimilarity metric based on probability distances might be more justified. R3 also notes that the claim “This alignment between model performance and natural image statistics suggests that our approach effectively captures the structure of visual information in the natural world” would be better supported by directly showing how well higher-order statistics are captured rather than using RDM on intermediate representations.
- **Incomplete comparisons and ablation (R1, R2, R3):**  
  - R1 asks: “How does the explicit modeling of higher-order interactions compare with the traditional method of adding more layers in terms of computational cost and performance improvement?”  
  - R1 also asks to clarify whether architectural differences were controlled in Table 1.  
  - R2: “If the addition helpful because the convnet is too simple in itself? If so, does the advantage go away if you use a deeper network afterwards?”  
  - R3 asks for quantitative evidence resolving the tied-weight issue (Sec. 2.1) and whether results hold for larger kernel sizes.
- **Limited use of higher-order only at first layer (R3):** “The higher order representation is introduced in the input, but nothing limits from using it in other layers: what do you expect to happen if it introduced at other levels of the hierarchy?”
- **Paper structure and wasted space (R3, R4):**  
  - R4: “The introductory text goes to page 3, there are a bunch of equations with summations taking up essentially a page, gigantic figures 1 and 2 that basically re‑explains the equations, and just like that we are already at page 6 … This leaves only 3 pages of real results that I am reviewing.”  
  - Minor typos: Figure 2 indices should start at i=1 not i=0; line 430 “analized” (R3).
