## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a Cost-Sensitive Loss (CSL) function that dynamically adjusts class weights per epoch using a reinforcement learning mechanism to address class imbalance in long-tailed classification. The CSL is designed as a regularizer that can be integrated with existing loss functions. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-LT, reporting top-1 accuracy. Code is publicly available.

### Strengths

- The problem of long-tailed learning is practically important and relevant (all reviewers).
- The idea of dynamically adjusting class weights using reinforcement learning is innovative and valuable (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 3 notes the adaptive approach).
- The proposed method can be integrated with existing loss functions (Reviewer 2; also implied by Reviewer 1).
- The paper succinctly summarizes existing approaches to long-tailed learning (Reviewer 3).
- The method focuses on a regularization approach, which is computationally efficient (Reviewer 3).
- The authors provide code implementation for reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 2 states the paper is well‑written (minority positive view; other reviewers find presentation poor).
- Reviewer 4 states the method outperforms existing methods on benchmark datasets with significant improvement (note: other reviewers dispute this as an overstatement due to weak baselines).

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and organization** (majority view): The paper is hard to follow; definitions (e.g., \(e_i\), “semantic values”, “entropy storage”) appear after first use or remain unclear. Concepts are used without clear definition. The structure is poorly organized, with numerous formatting issues. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; Reviewer 2 finds it well‑written, creating a contrast.)
- **Outdated baselines**: Compared methods are all before 2020. Missing recent SOTA approaches such as PaCo (ICCV 2021), BCL (CVPR 2022), Logit Adjustment (ICLR 2021), and others listed by reviewers (e.g., [1-5] from Reviewers 2 and 4). Claims of outperforming SOTA are overstated given the weak baselines. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Missing datasets**: Experiments omit widely adopted large‑scale long‑tail datasets: iNaturalist‑LT and Places‑LT. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Incomplete evaluation**: Only top‑1 accuracy is reported. No per‑group accuracy for Many/Medium/Few‑shot categories, which is standard to demonstrate tail‑class improvement. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of ablation and hyperparameter analysis**: No ablation study of the proposed method or sensitivity analysis of hyperparameters. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **No theoretical foundation**: No theoretical analysis (e.g., convergence); the method is purely empirical. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Missing statistical rigor**: Results are reported without error bars. The number of independent experiments is not stated; statistical significance is not assessed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear implementation details**: 
  - The reinforcement term calculation is not explicit and not shown in the algorithm. (Reviewers 1, 2)
  - How the validation set is produced during training (and whether it is also long‑tailed) is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
  - How entropy \(H_i\) is calculated is not specified. (Reviewer 2)
  - It is unclear whether \(N_{pred,i}\) depends on the sample size of class \(i\) and how the loop over \(k\) in Equation (1) works per data point. (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - The method maintains a record of class‑specific features, but this detail is omitted from the abstract and introduction. (Reviewer 3)
- **Potential side effects**: Forced reduction of \(N_{pred,i}\) for majority classes may cause under‑fitting; no analysis of side effects is provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Efficiency comparison not discussed**: Training time, a claimed advantage of CSL, is not sufficiently compared with other cost‑sensitive (IA, MI) or long‑tail methods. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Baseline results questionable**: Line 374 suggests baseline numbers are taken from original papers without re‑running under the same conditions, making fair comparison uncertain. (Reviewer 4)
- **Misleading or overclaiming statements** (Reviewers 1, 3): 
  - “Seemingly satisfactory yet biased overall results” may not hold under proper evaluation metrics.
  - The claim that existing CSLs “depend on static weight schemes” might be inaccurate (some methods do adjust weights dynamically).
  - Overuse of the word “novel” (e.g., in the GitHub README).
- **Typo**: “Class‑balances loss” should be “Class‑balanced loss” (line 495). (Reviewer 1)
