## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends Neural Context Flow (NCF) and its Contextual Self-Modulation (CSM) mechanism for meta-learning. Three main contributions are presented: $i$CSM, which generalizes CSM to infinite-dimensional function spaces by using a context-conditioned MLP; StochasticNCF, which approximates meta-gradients via subsampling environments for scalability; and FlashCAVIA, a more efficient implementation of the CAVIA meta-learning framework integrated with CSM. The work also explores higher-order Taylor expansions for context modulation. Experiments are conducted on dynamical systems (optimal control, forced pendulum), image completion, and sine regression, comparing against baselines like NCF, CAVIA, CoDA, and MAML.

### Strengths
- Context-based meta-learning is a well-motivated and underexplored area; the paper addresses an interesting problem.
- The proposed extensions ($i$CSM, StochasticNCF, FlashCAVIA) are orthogonal, natural, and potentially valuable contributions to the NCF framework.
- The approach is demonstrated across diverse tasks (dynamical systems, image completion, curve fitting), showing broad applicability.
- In curve-fitting experiments, the method shows robustness to hyperparameter choices (order of Taylor expansion, number of training steps) and consistently outperforms previous algorithms.
- The paper demonstrates that gradient-based meta-learning can be effective for image completion (high-number prediction settings), broadening its application scope.
- Qualitative analyses (e.g., smoothing behavior from CSM to FlashCAVIA) provide intuitive understanding.
- The writing is relatively clear, with background introduced early, and the results appear reproducible.
- The study advances the applicability of CSM to a wide spectrum of tasks and modalities.

### Weaknesses
- **Contribution novelty and clarity**: The paper lacks a clearly defined contribution beyond a combination of existing techniques (NCF, CAVIA, Taylor expansion). Each individual extension is incremental: StochasticNCF is essentially random subsampling of environments; FlashCAVIA is a JAX port of CAVIA with minor changes (no new CUDA kernels); $i$CSM is conceptually similar to context-conditioned hypernetworks (e.g., CoDA). The core message is hard to distinguish from prior work.
- **Limited significance for ICLR**: Given the incremental nature over a recent, not-yet-established prior method (NCF, also submitted to ICLR), the relevance to the broader meta-learning community is not compelling. The majority of experiments are on toy domains (sine regression, parametric ODEs); the paper lacks large-scale benchmarks (e.g., mini-ImageNet, Mujoco) that are standard for meta-learning and were used in the original CAVIA work.
- **Insufficient motivation and explanation of $i$CSM**:
  - The mapping from context to MLP weights is unclear; the dimensionality of the flattened weight tensor relative to the MLP architecture is not explicitly defined. A concrete example with a specific MLP architecture is needed.
  - The claim that $i$CSM can leverage Lie group symmetries and translation equivariance (Lines 192–194) is not justified.
  - It is unclear how $i$CSM differs from existing hypernetwork-based approaches like CoDA, and why a context size of 256 was used for CoDA (original paper uses 1 or 2).
- **StochasticNCF lacks theoretical and empirical justification**:
  - It reduces high-order Taylor expansion to a first-order SGD estimate; this may be simply a special case of a first-order approximation, not a new contribution.
  - The specific conditions under which it provides advantage over standard first-order methods are not specified, and no empirical evidence is given.
  - The connection to higher-order Taylor expansion is not clearly justified.
- **FlashCAVIA's novelty and role are unclear**:
  - It is described as a "powerful upgrade" but presents no new theoretical advance over CAVIA (Algorithm 1 is nearly identical). The custom optimizer benefit is not validated with equal gradient steps (e.g., same number of inner-loop updates).
  - The choice of CAVIA over other meta-learning algorithms is not motivated.
  - Its relationship to the other contributions ($i$CSM, StochasticNCF) is not explained; it appears separate from the main approach.
  - Performance metrics (accuracy, training time) for FlashCAVIA-100, MAML-100, CAVIA-100 are missing, making it impossible to assess practical value or scalability.
- **High-order Taylor expansion necessity ambiguous**:
  - Figure 1 shows no substantial performance impact from higher-order terms, questioning whether a simpler linear context modulation would suffice. The paper does not provide compelling evidence for the necessity of this component.
- **Experimental issues**:
  - Results vary widely across tasks, with no consistent trends (e.g., FlashCAVIA best on sine regression but image completion performance does not co-vary with K).
  - Limited baselines: not evaluated consistently across experiments; important comparisons missing (e.g., CAMEL (Blanke & LeLarge, 2024) for higher-order expansions).
  - Significant underfitting is observed in Figure 3 (forced pendulum, optimal control), raising doubts about how effectively CSM contributes; it is unclear whether CSM is causing the underfitting.
  - Claims of efficiency and scalability are not validated with runtime measurements or comparisons to state-of-the-art meta-learning algorithms.
  - Several essential meta-learning benchmarks (e.g., mini-ImageNet, RL tasks) are absent, as noted in Section 4.2.
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - The introduction is misleading: it suggests a systematic comparison of contextual meta-learning but only studies NCF and CAVIA.
  - Introduction of task dimensionality (line 064) is confusing; the connection to $i$CSM is unclear.
  - Numerous notational errors and inconsistencies (e.g., $k$ and $K$ used interchangeably; $N$ vs $K$ not explained).
  - Overuse of acronyms/abbreviations (InD, OoD, CSM, CNP, GBML, NCFs, etc.) makes the text hard to follow.
  - The paper lacks schematic figures to provide an overview of the methodology.
  - The paper does not address the additional parameters introduced by CSM compared to Neural Processes or MAML, nor how the number of parameters impacts performance (e.g., in curve fitting experiments).
- **Minority disagreement**: Reviewers 1 and 2 were more positive (score 5) but still raised significant concerns; Reviewers 3 and 4 (score 3) found the contributions minor and the evaluation insufficient. The more negative reviewers emphasize the incremental nature and lack of broad validation, while the more positive ones acknowledge the diverse tasks and hyperparameter robustness but agree on missing baselines, unclear motivation, and clarity issues.
