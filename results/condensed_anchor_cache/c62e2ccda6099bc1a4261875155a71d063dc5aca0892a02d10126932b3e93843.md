## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes three contributions: AutoCap – a high-quality, efficient audio captioning model using a compact audio representation and metadata; GenAu – a scalable transformer-based audio generation model up to 1.25B parameters; and AutoReCap-XL – a dataset of 57M ambient audio clips with automatically generated captions, claimed as the largest audio-text dataset. The authors demonstrate improvements in captioning (CIDEr 83.2, 3.2% improvement, 4× faster inference) and generation (4.7% FAD, 22.7% IS, 13.5% CLAP score improvements) over prior work.

### Strengths

- Paper is well-written with clear illustrations and a crisp presentation.
- No evident technical flaws (per majority reviewers); reasoning about how good captions improve generation is well conveyed.
- AutoCap uses a Q-Former to compress HTSAT audio features into fewer tokens, reducing inference time, and adopts a two-stage training strategy (first train Q-Former + CLAP-projector on weakly labeled data, then fine-tune on AudioCaps).
- The pipeline for constructing AutoReCap-XL by filtering videos without automatic transcripts to exclude speech/music is straightforward yet effective.
- Both AutoCap and GenAu achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets.
- The idea of leveraging external metadata (video captions/titles) to enhance captioning is interesting.
- Open-sourcing the dataset, code, and checkpoints (if done) would be a significant asset to the research community.
- The dataset is substantially larger than any prior audio-language dataset, and the paper shows it improves model quality.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons and ablations (critical)**
  - Stable Audio 1.0 [7] comparison is missing from the generation results table (Table 4), despite the authors sharing audio samples from it. A fair comparison with Stable Audio trained on the same data is essential.
  - No generation results of GenAu without AutoReCap-XL or of baselines trained with the re-captioned data to isolate whether gains come from architecture or data scaling. Only one ablation (GenAu w/ U-Net) is provided.
  - No baselines (e.g., Stable Audio, AudioLDM) are trained on AutoReCap-XL to demonstrate that the proposed architecture is superior, not just the larger dataset.
  - For AutoCap, comparisons with large audio language models [1,2,3,4] are missing; these have become standard for audio captioning.
  - Missing comparison/discussion with prior work using visual modality for audio captioning (SOUND-VECAPS [4], [5]).
  - Missing ablation on the necessity of using both FLAN-T5 and CLAP text encoders in GenAu; single-encoder results are not provided.
  - No analysis of GenAu’s performance when trained solely on AutoReCap-XL.

- **Unclear or inaccurate claims**
  - Fig. 1 says "CLAP Encoder has one token" – CLAP uses HTSAT audio encoder with intermediate representations; the paper likely uses a pooled/CLS token, which should be specified as "CLAP audio encoder".
  - The claim "4× fewer tokens" from Q-Former ignores that embeddings from BART (and possibly other components) also contribute; baseline clarification is needed. Increase in parameter count due to Q-Former is not discussed.
  - Q-Former has been used for audio before [3]; training it end-to-end on small datasets (AudioCaps) raises soundness concerns.
  - "Metadata" is ambiguous: it is derived from video captions (Panda-70M) and titles, not from raw audio, limiting applicability to settings where such metadata is unavailable or unreliable. No robustness ablation shows sensitivity to metadata quality.

- **Dataset quality and vocabulary limitations**
  - AutoReCap-XL has a very constrained vocabulary of only 4,461 unique words, which may limit diversity.
  - AudioCaps (used for fine-tuning AutoCap) also has limited vocabulary (4,892 unique words), hindering ability to generate long/detailed prompts.
  - The dataset has not been extensively analyzed for caption accuracy or downstream task performance; human evaluation or verification strategy is missing.
  - When metadata lacks detail, captioning struggles to disambiguate sounds, capture temporal relationships, and differentiate foreground/background noises.
  - The filtering method to exclude speech/music lacks details: what NLP techniques, lexicon, thresholds/confidence scores are used? How are ambiguous cases handled? No distribution analysis of non-ambient sound classes or potential bias from heavy filtering.

- **Technical issues and missing discussions**
  - Typo in Table 5: last row "Quality" column shows "-".
  - Inconsistent use of "Q-Former" vs "Qformer".
  - Typo in Appendix A.2: "AutoReCap" should be "AutoCap".
  - The freezing/unfreezing paradigm is widely used in DCASE challenges; missing citations.
  - GenAu architecture is very similar to Stable Audio (DiT, time embeddings, attention modules); a detailed discussion is missing.
  - Novelty of GenAu is questioned: other models with fewer parameters generate comparable audio.
  - The generation model largely follows Huang et al. (2023) with 1D-VAE and LDM, feeling more like an engineering scaling effort.
  - FiT architecture modifications (if any) are not clarified; computational cost of dual text encoders is not discussed.
  - The limitations section should be moved to the main body.

- **Other reviewer concerns**
  - (Minority reviewer – R3, rating 3) is more critical, questioning the soundness of Q-Former training, the focus on "audio" (since human speech is excluded, paper should reframe as "environmental sounds"), and the lack of verification for synthetic data. This contrasts with other reviewers who found no major technical flaws.
