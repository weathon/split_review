## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Preference-Attended Multi-Objective Decision Transformer (PA-MODT), which adds a preference-attention block to the MODT architecture for offline MORL. It is tested on D4MORL datasets. While some reviewers note that PA-MODT achieves good performance and sheds light on transformer modifications in MORL, the majority find the novelty very limited, the motivations unclear, the writing and definitions problematic, and the analysis of why the method works insufficient. One reviewer found the paper clearly written, but another strongly disagreed, calling it disorganized. Reviewers also raise concerns about the correctness of the Pareto efficiency explanation, missing error bars, and insufficient experimental evidence (taking variance into account, Table 1 may not show significant improvement). Overall, scores are 3,3,3,3; soundness ranges from 1 to 3 (one reviewer gave soundness 1 and contribution 1, more negative than others).

### Strengths
- PA-MODT achieves good performance and surpasses baseline algorithms on the D4MORL benchmark.
- The related work section is fairly comprehensive.
- The paper provides insight into how modifying the transformer architecture (e.g., adding a preference-attention block) can affect MODT performance in MORL.
- A number of empirical experiments are conducted.
- One reviewer found the paper clearly written and easy to follow (contrast: another reviewer found the writing disorganized and definitions unclear).

### Weaknesses
- Many typos and formatting issues: inconsistent reference format and Figure 1 caption (not ICLR compliant), symbols for vectors better in bold, typo “s_{t+t}” (line 168), “matrices” should be “metrics” (line 198, Section 5.3 title), expressions in Eq.(1)–(5) are not concise/not formalized.
- The Pareto efficiency explanation in Section 3 is incorrect and incomplete. One reviewer notes a point β is Pareto efficient iff no other point α behaves no worse for any objective while satisfying R_i^β < R_i^α for some i. Another reviewer provides a concrete counterexample: the solution set {[0.25,0.75], [0.75,0.25]} contains two Pareto-efficient points inconsistent with the given explanation.
- The motivation for modifying the transformer architecture is not clearly clarified. The key features for PA-MODT’s construction are proposed without sufficient evidence or reasoning.
- The novelty and contribution are very limited: the main addition is a new attention module to the existing MODT framework, which is insufficient for ICLR acceptance. The method only fits transformer-based architectures in MORL, and its properties are not clearly clarified.
- The paper lacks serious explanations and analysis of why the proposed architecture works better. The presentation is confusing (one reviewer states “cannot understand why the proposed method is effective”). The actual method description and explanation take up less than a page; figures are too large and lack information.
- No discussion of limitations.
- Taking variance into account, the results shown in Table 1 may be insufficient to demonstrate that PA-MODT significantly outperforms the baseline methods (suggesting the need for statistical tests or larger confidence intervals).
- Error bars in Figure 5 (Appendix A.1) appear to be lost or missing, which may weaken the reliability of the arguments in Section 4.
- The sparsity metric should be better evaluated to provide sufficient evidence and understanding.
- The experiments in Section 5.1 and 5.2 do not clearly show how the results reflect the property and superiority of the proposed method. Similarly, the relationship between the experiments in Section 5.3 and the main contribution is unclear.
- In Table 5, the training steps for PA-MODT are not pre-fixed (evaluation every 5k steps). It is unclear whether the final result in Table 1 is the best score from all evaluations, and if all baselines were evaluated in the same way.
