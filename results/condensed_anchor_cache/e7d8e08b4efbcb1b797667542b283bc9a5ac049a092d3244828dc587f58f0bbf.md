## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ViT-UWA, an adapted Vision Transformer backbone for underwater dense prediction tasks. It introduces three components: High-Frequency Components Prior (HFCP) to inject high-frequency information from underwater images, a Detail Aware Module (DAM) to produce a detail-focused multi-scale convolutional feature pyramid, and a ViT-CNN Interaction Module (VCIM) for bidirectional feature fusion between ViT and CNN branches. Evaluations on semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and object detection (e.g., USIS10K) show strong results (e.g., 46.4 box AP, 44.2 mask AP). The work is generally well-written and experiments are extensive.

### Strengths

- Useful enhancement of the standard ViT architecture for underwater imagery; encouraging SOTA comparisons on multiple datasets (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Paper is smooth, easy to understand, and methods are introduced clearly (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Effectiveness and efficiency appear good compared to related works (Figure 2); advantages for underwater images with specific detail degradation (Figure 3) (Reviewer 4).
- Visualization results look better than compared methods (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation experiments illustrate the effectiveness of HFCP and DAM (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 also notes good performance.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of originality:** The combination of existing modules (adaptive DC in HFDConv, Deform Attention in VCIM) and the HFCP (simply concatenating a high-pass filtered original image) makes the contribution insufficient; the work lacks novelty (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also questions whether the contributions are designed by simply considering related works.
- **Missing physics-based underwater correction:** The method does not model wavelength-dependent attenuation/scattering (e.g., Sea-Thru), which are inherent to underwater environments. High-frequency signal attenuation depends heavily on conditions like turbidity, depth, and lighting; ignoring color shifts and reduced contrast could limit generalizability (Reviewer 1). How does the model learn these different degradations beyond detail loss? (Reviewer 4). Multiple reviewers ask for clarification on handling underwater physics (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Ablation experiments lack rigor:** Only aggregate indicators show HFCP usefulness; no visuals demonstrate that the module actually captures high-frequency information (Reviewer 2). For DAM ablation, only removal is tested; simple alternative CNN or Transformer structures are not compared to show uniqueness (Reviewer 2). Also, what would happen if HFCP is directly removed from Table 7 (instead of replacing with USUIR)? Have better supervised underwater image enhancement methods been tried? (Reviewer 3).
- **Efficiency evaluation incomplete:** Missing reporting of Parameters, FLOPs, training time, and inference time; these are needed for real-world applications (Reviewer 4). Figure 2 shows some efficiency comparison but details are insufficient.
- **Motivation not fully convincing:** High-frequency and detail information are important in any scenario, yet ViT-UWA performs only mediocrely on COCO (Table 10) while excelling underwater; the reason for this discrepancy is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Specific questions as weaknesses/criticisms:** Why does VCIM use Deform Attention instead of ordinary cross attention? (Reviewer 3). Need discussion about SAM 2 vs ViT-UWA (Reviewer 4). Typo in line 504 (“increase” should likely be “decrease” by 2.3 and 0.6 AP) (Reviewer 3).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 3 (score 5) is the most negative, citing insufficient contribution and unconvincing motivation. Reviewer 1 (score 8) is the most positive, praising the enhancement and results. The other reviewers (scores 6,6) are moderately positive but raise clear concerns about novelty, ablation rigor, and physics modeling.
