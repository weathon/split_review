## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Rényi Neural Processes (RNP), which replace the KL divergence in standard Neural Process (NP) objectives with Rényi divergence to mitigate the impact of prior misspecification caused by parameter sharing between the prior and posterior. The Rényi divergence introduces a hyperparameter α that scales the density ratio, dampening effects of a misspecified prior. The authors unify variational inference (VI) and maximum likelihood (ML) objectives under a single framework and evaluate on GP regression, image inpainting, real-world regression, and differential equation modeling.

### Strengths
- **Important problem and intuitive remedy.** The paper identifies a key failure mode in latent variable NPs – prior misspecification due to parameter coupling between prior and posterior – and offers a simple, easy-to-implement correction by replacing the KL with Rényi divergence (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Theoretical contributions.** The paper provides derivations for the Rényi NP objective, unifies VI and ML objectives via α, and offers a theoretical analysis of how RNP addresses prior misspecification (Reviewers 1, 4). The approach of rewriting the ML objective as minimizing KL between empirical and model distribution and then applying Rényi divergence is praised as an interesting perspective (Reviewer 4).
- **Comprehensive experimental validation.** Experiments cover GP regression, image inpainting (MNIST, EMNIST), real-world regression (Song, Skillcraft), and DE tasks (Hare-Lynx, Lotka-Volterra). Baselines include CNP, NP, AttnNP, TNP, with ablation on α and K (Monte Carlo samples) (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The experimental section is well-laid out, with each subsection connected to an explicit empirical question (Reviewer 3).
- **Consistent improvements and practical expressiveness.** RNP consistently improves log-likelihood, mitigates oversmoothing, and yields more reliable uncertainty quantification across multiple benchmarks, including strong baselines like TNP (Reviewers 2, 3). The method is simple and can be deployed to any NP variant by replacing the loss function (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear presentation.** The paper is well-written with thorough literature review and clear, intuitive figures (Reviewer 4). The guidance for choosing α and ablation studies are appreciated (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient differentiation.** Using Rényi divergence for robust variational inference is not new; prior work on α-divergences and f-divergences addresses similar issues. The paper does not adequately discuss how RNP differs from these earlier efforts (Reviewer 1). A simple baseline – separately parameterizing posterior and prior – is not compared, which would better demonstrate the method’s advantages (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 considers the combination of existing ideas insufficient for ICLR.
- **Unclear definition of prior misspecification and disconnect with experiments.** The paper’s motivation (context-target mismatch, e.g., noisy contexts) seems distinct from the experimental validation which uses train/test distribution shift (domain shift). The relationship between these two types and why Rényi divergence helps domain shift is not explained (Reviewer 4). Additionally, RNP shows gains on well-specified datasets (standard GP/inpainting) without a clear reason, suggesting benefits beyond just addressing prior misspecification (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Need for more rigorous theoretical analysis of misspecified prior effects.** The claim that KL leads to biased posterior variance and underfitting is supported only by an anecdotal single-sample figure (Fig. 2b). An analytical form of the bias, especially for posterior variance, would greatly strengthen the motivation. A theoretical analysis in a simplified setting is requested (Reviewer 3, W2).
- **Impact on ML objective unclear.** It is not explained how the prior (used only in the variational objective) affects the ML objective. Since the prior is not directly involved in the likelihood term, the connection between prior misspecification and the maximum-likelihood variant of NP needs clarification (Reviewer 3, W6).
- **Equation imprecision: missing reparameterization trick.** The RNP objective (Eq. 10) appears to take a Monte Carlo estimate of an expectation over parameters being optimized without explicitly invoking the reparameterization trick. The authors should state how gradients are obtained (Reviewer 3, W5).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and computational overhead.** The choice of α is crucial but no systematic tuning methodology is provided; improper selection leads to suboptimal performance and extensive experimentation is required (Reviewers 1, 2). The additional Monte Carlo sampling to estimate the Rényi divergence incurs repeated evaluations of encoder/decoder networks, creating a computational bottleneck. The overhead is not quantified – a secondary axis showing compute time growing with K is missing (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing experiments.** The tabular regression experiments (Pace & Barry, 1997) mentioned in the paper are not present in the main text or appendix (Reviewer 3, W8).
- **Interpretability issues.** Neural Processes already suffer from poor interpretability; the introduction of Rényi divergence further obscures decision-making, hindering adoption in fields like healthcare or finance (Reviewer 2).
- **Confusion about TNP variant and references.** The paper discusses TNP-A (autoregressive) but likely uses TNP-D in experiments. The reference for TNP (Maraval et al., 2024) is inappropriate as that work only applies TNP to Bayesian optimization (Reviewer 4). Also, the claim that ANP models dependencies between target points is incorrect; that is GNP (Reviewer 4).
- **Inconsistent results on EMNIST.** For TNP-D, RNP (using L_RNP) outperforms the baseline in Table 1 but performs worse on EMNIST in Table 2. This discrepancy is not explained (Reviewer 4).
- **Log-likelihood calculation on context set is unclear.** How is log-likelihood computed when predicting the context set? (Reviewer 4).
- **Proposition 3.2 wording.** The proposition reads as if misspecification is inevitable, but an “if” is needed to indicate it is conditional (Reviewer 3, W4).
- **Gradient of Rényi divergence could be misleading.** The argument implicitly assumes the gradient ignores the dependence of the normalizing constant in the Rényi divergence’s exponentiated ratio; the authors should check the derivation (Reviewer 3, Q1).
- **Assumption needed in Appendix A.3.** The step from Eq. 20 to 21 may require the prior to be well-specified (Reviewer 3, Q2).
- **Extension to large language models.** The authors suggest possible extension to prompt design in LLMs but provide no details or insights (Reviewer 1).
- **Figures are too small.** Graphs are difficult to read without extensive zooming; legends are hard to make out. Figure 3b has inconsistent line colors, and Figures 4a/4b lack a legend for the red dot indicator (Reviewer 3, W12).
- **Typos and grammatical errors.** Multiple errors throughout: line 035 “advance” should be “advantage”; line 061 “poster variance” → “posterior variance”; line 074 “achieve the better” → “achieve a better”; line 152 “overestimate” → “overestimated”; line 216 missing space “inA.3”; lines 233 and 239 p_φ should be q_φ; line 269 dz should be removed from Eq. 12; line 404-405 confusing phrasing about α; Table 1/2/3 captions duplicate arrows (Reviewer 3, W13).
