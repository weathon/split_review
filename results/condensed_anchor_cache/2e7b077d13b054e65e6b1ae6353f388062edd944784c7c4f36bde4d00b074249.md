## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies transformers whose depth grows logarithmically with input length (log-depth). It proves that such transformers can simulate finite automata (regular language recognition) and solve graph connectivity (reachability) when input length is bounded—tasks known to be beyond fixed-depth transformers under standard complexity conjectures. The model is a “universal transformer” with repeated layers, saturated attention, and layer normalization. The authors argue that logarithmic depth scaling is more computationally efficient than widening the model or adding chain-of-thought (CoT) steps. Reviewers generally find the question important but raise major concerns about clarity, assumptions, missing experimental validation, and insufficient justification of the claims.

### Strengths
- **Novel depth-scaling perspective.** Shifts focus from fixed-depth to dynamic log-depth transformers, addressing how bounded-length inputs can be handled (R1, R3). R2 notes this is interesting for theoretical foundations, though impact on current constant-depth architectures is limited.
- **Rigorous theoretical construction.** Proofs (Theorems 1–2) for regular language recognition and graph connectivity via log-depth transformers are provided, with attention to masked pre-norm and averaging-hard attention (R1, R3). R2 finds lemmas well explained.
- **Comparison with scaling alternatives.** The paper argues log-depth is more efficient than expanding width or adding O(log n) CoT steps, and places both methods in TC⁰ (Theorems 3–4) (R1, R2). R2 says this could inform efficient scaling strategies, possibly combined with CoT.
- **Practical architectural choices.** Uses saturated attention (not hard attention) and incorporates layer normalization, aligning with realistic implementations (R4).
- **Well-chosen related work.** The paper connects to expressive capacity literature and contributes significantly to that domain (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experimental validation.** No empirical results are provided for the claimed tasks. Reviewers uniformly call for experiments varying depth, width, and attention heads to verify theoretical predictions and the trade-offs in Figure 1 (R1, R2, R3, R4). The dismissal of CoT with “a couple of lines” is seen as unfair given CoT’s demonstrated potential on arithmetic and graph problems (R2).
- **Motivation and bounded‑context assumption.** With bounded input length, all sizes become constant, making asymptotic results less persuasive. The coefficient in the exponent (e.g., 2^{O(d)}) matters but is not discussed (R4). The analogy to regular expressions capturing context‑free fragments is unclear (R3).
- **Unclear model definition.** The (s,r,t)-universal transformer lacks clarity: terminology (sequence-to-value vs. decoder), masking (learned mask vector vs. causal masking in Lemma 1), ambiguous notation (L^l, r-layer (l−s) mod r), and the use of limits/exponential for averaging-hard attention (vs. standard hardmax) are not sufficiently explained. Section 2.2 (“memory management”) appears disjointed (R3). R2 questions whether results hold for standard transformer architecture given specific assumptions (masked pre-norm).
- **Missing configurations in main theorems.** Theorems 1 and 2 only specify depth O(log n) but omit required width, number of attention heads, and other hyperparameters. These are essential for understanding the model’s feasibility (R4, R1).
- **Insufficient justification of depth‑over‑width claim.** The statement that depth scaling is more efficient than width or CoT is presented as a conclusion from Theorems 1–2 vs. Theorem 3, but no detailed argument supports this strong claim (R3). Practical trade-offs (e.g., increased training time for deeper networks) are not discussed (R1).
- **Prior work overlap not fully clarified.** Lemma 3 of Merrill & Sabharwal (2022) shows a constant-depth transformer can simulate a depth‑d threshold circuit, and since regular language recognition and connectivity are in TC¹, comparable results may already exist. The paper should explicitly differentiate its contribution (R4). R3 also questions whether Theorem 4 is original (cited as “Anonymous, p.c.”) despite a proof in the appendix.
- **Implications for fixed‑depth transformers are unclear.** The paper aims to illuminate bounded-length behavior of fixed-depth models, but only provides results for dynamic depth. The brief reference to an experiment (lines 76–83) is insufficient (R3).
- **Not self‑contained.** The paper is intricate and assumes substantial background; an informal introduction would help non‑specialists (R2, R3).
- **Theorems 3 and 4 poorly justified.** These core results are not well motivated or explained (R2).
- **Terminology issue in Theorem 2.** The problem is “reachability” (path between two nodes), not “connectivity” (all nodes connected), which is misleading (R3).
