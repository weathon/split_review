## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper analyzes the PEPITA forward-only learning algorithm, connecting it theoretically to adaptive feedback alignment (AFA) and the Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm. It shows that PEPITA’s learning dynamics can be approximated by AFA and tracks these dynamics analytically for a shallow two-layer network. The authors also propose variations (PEPITA-Hebbian, PEPITA-time-local) and empirically evaluate performance improvements from weight mirroring, weight decay, and activation normalization on small fully-connected networks for MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100.

### Strengths

- Novel connection between feedback alignment and forward-only algorithms. This is interesting because both aim to improve biological plausibility; exposing this relationship may benefit research on both types of algorithms. The authors already demonstrate transfer by showing weight mirroring (developed for feedback alignment) can improve PEPITA.
- Theoretical understanding of forward-only learning dynamics is mostly lacking; work shedding light on how PEPITA learns is very welcome.
- Extensive experiments with convincing results.
- The paper is well-written and clearly articulates its aims and contributions.
- The Hebbian/anti-Hebbian approximation works as well as the original PEPITA, which is a good sign for biological plausibility (for both PEPITA and FF, since they work similarly).
- By connecting three major biologically-inspired approaches (PEPITA, Forward-Forward, and an approximation of backpropagation via Feedback Alignment), the paper takes an important step toward unifying these methods.
- Section 4.2 gives a well-structured summary of experimental results.

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical analysis limited to shallow 2-layer networks.** The adaptive feedback rule is derived only for a single hidden layer; it is unclear how alignment would evolve or be maintained in deeper architectures. Many benefits of deep learning come from learning hierarchical representations across multiple layers. The analysis is disjoint from the experimental results on multi-layer PEPITA networks, and the lack of theoretical support for multi-layer networks makes it hard to understand fundamental mechanisms in more complex settings.
- **Similarity to Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) only holds for the first layer** (Eq. 4); it is unclear for other layers.
- **Performance gap to backpropagation is large** (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100). The reported accuracies are significantly lower than typical backprop results. This gap would likely increase for larger networks, and it is unclear if observed scaling trends hold. The paper should include an analysis of scaling behavior with respect to network depth and width.
- **No experiments with convolutional networks**, despite CIFAR-10/100 performance typically improving dramatically with convolutions. No justification is given. Results on convnets are needed to assess generalizability.
- **Experimental evaluation uses only small, fully-connected networks** not well suited to complex vision datasets. Only image classification (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100), with minor accuracy improvements.
- **No experimental comparison to Forward-Forward**, even though the paper claims a connection between PEPITA and FF.
- **The definition of “forward-only” is unclear.** PEPITA requires a direct global error feedback to inputs, unlike local Forward-Forward. If PEPITA is considered forward-only, is DFA also forward-only? The paper does not adequately distinguish PEPITA from DFA, and the claim that PEPITA outperforms DFA in locality or activity freezing is not justified with definitions.
- **PEPITA-TL (time-local) performs much worse** than PEPITA-Hebbian in some settings (Fig. 3a vs. Fig. S1: 1-layer PEPITA-TL drops to ~40% on CIFAR-10). This needs explanation; if due to hyperparameter differences, how does non-TL PEPITA compare under the same conditions? Similarly, Table 1 shows the considered rule has much worse performance – why should it be considered if it adds no properties while being worse?
- **Some derivations appear contradictory.** Section 3 and Section 5 assume that the error-induced modulation \(Fe\) is much smaller than the input \(x\), so it can be ignored. But in Section 5 this modulated sample is used as a negative sample for Forward-Forward. If the modulation is small, why would it constitute a valid negative sample? This reduces the reliability of the connection between PEPITA and FF.
- **Overall novelty of experimental improvements is limited** (one reviewer: “not enough contribution in empirical or theoretical perspectives”). While the combination with theoretical analysis makes a contribution, the empirical results alone offer minor accuracy gains.
- **Weight mirroring pre-alignment inconsistency**: In Figure 3c, pre-mirroring should increase alignment (decrease the angle) before the first epoch, but all three lines start at the same value.
- **Missing experimental combinations**: “weight-decay + normalization + WM” and “weight-decay + normalization + no WM” are missing from Table 2. Can weight decay and activation normalization not be combined?
- **Minor issues**: Figures can be hard to read (Fig. 2b overlap of PEPITA and AFA hard to see without zooming; Fig. 3 needs more contrasting colors). Reference to Figure 2c in the last paragraph of Section 3.2 seems mismatched with the actual subfigure order. Table 1, line 1 has wrong citation for FA. Spelling: “analitically” (contribution vi) and “gpus” (should be “GPUs”). Table 1 should include PEPITA-Hebbian performance. Appendix could be included in the main PDF.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms/missing experiments:**
  - Do the trends in Figure 3 (weight decay, normalization, weight mirroring) hold for MNIST and CIFAR-100?
  - If the learning rate of PEPITA-TL is reduced, would performance match PEPITA-Hebbian, since the anti-Hebbian error terms would then coincide?
  - Can PEPITA-Hebbian be considered temporally local because it is a long-term plasticity rule? The PEPITA-TL approximation adds the Hebbian term immediately after the first forward pass (~100 ms) – too short for long-term changes. Is PEPITA-Hebbian then already temporally local if both parts of the update happen for the same network state?
  - What is the difference between the analysis in Section 3.2 and previous analyses for DFA?
  - How does the derivation in Section 5.1 connect to contrastive Hebbian learning (Xie & Seung, 2003) or equilibrium propagation (Scellier & Bengio, 2017)?
  - Which approach do the authors consider their primary contribution? Table 1 labels PEPITA-TL as “ours” but it is discussed only briefly and left to future work.
