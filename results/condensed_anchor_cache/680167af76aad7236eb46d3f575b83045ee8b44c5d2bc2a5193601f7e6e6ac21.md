## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Self-supervised randomIzed Masked Augmentation (SIMA) for learning robust locomotion skills without explicitly modeling environmental observation noise. SIMA uses a masked reconstruction approach to recover latent state representations from noisy observations by exploiting correlations in multivariate time series, then trains a student policy via imitation learning on denoised trajectories. Experiments on pybullet locomotion tasks claim a 15.7% improvement over baselines. Reviewers diverged strongly: R1 (rating 8) praised the writing, novelty, and thoroughness; R2 (rating 3) criticized marginal improvements, suspicious figures, and missing comparisons; R3 (rating 6) noted theoretical and empirical strengths but raised concerns about unseen noise robustness and unfair comparisons.

### Strengths
- High-quality, well-structured writing; excellent figures; precise introduction and methodology (R1, R3).
- Novel topic with thorough coverage of related work (DRL, observation de-noising, masked multivariate time series modeling) (R1).
- Addresses an important problem (sim2real transfer under non-stationary noise) and is well-motivated by multi-sensory integration (R2).
- Approach is overall sound, with theoretical guarantees (DRPOP formulation) and empirical case studies showing state reconstruction and policy distribution effects (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Performance declines and suspicious Figure 4:** SIMA shows sharp drops across all tasks; Figure 4 has sudden performance drops that later recover, suggesting a possible experimental mistake (R1, R2). Some baselines (e.g., RL-DR, RL-vanilla in Walker2D) are still increasing, so more training steps should be visualized (R1). R2 finds improvements over baselines marginal.
- **Too many abbreviations (DRPOP, SIMA, VICOR, RMA, MASOR)** make the paper hard to follow (R1, R2). Additionally, “RMA” conflicts with a different sim2real transfer locomotion paper (R2).
- **Missing comparisons:** The idea of masked modeling for RL is not new; comparisons with prior masked modeling methods (e.g., [1], [2] per R2) are needed. Comparisons with state representation learning methods for locomotion (e.g., [1], [2] per R3) are also missing. Since SIMA uses a more complex training objective, comparing only with simple encoders (filters, LSTM) is unfair (R3). Also, a comparison with generative models like BetaVAE and Beta-TC-VAE for masked encoding is suggested (R1).
- **No robustness evaluation on unseen noise distributions:** The paper does not test whether the policy remains robust under shifted or unseen noise distributions after training, which is essential for real-world deployment (R3). It also does not analyze performance when noise distribution changes (R3 question).
- **Need to justify teacher policy:** Why must an RL algorithm generate the teacher policy? Could classic control be used instead? (R3 question).
- **Missing experimental details:** How are scores in Appendix E calculated (why can scores exceed 99 under clean settings)? (R3 question). How is the policy distribution drawn (t-SNE on actions? How are actions sampled?) (R3 question).
- **Lack of analysis for performance drops:** Causes of sharp performance declines should be explained (R1).
