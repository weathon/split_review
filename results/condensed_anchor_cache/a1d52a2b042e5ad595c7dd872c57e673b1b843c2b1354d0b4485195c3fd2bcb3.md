## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper establishes a novel connection between rate encoding in SNNs and randomized smoothing, giving the first provable robustness guarantee for SNNs against l1-bounded adversarial perturbations. It also introduces adversarial training algorithms for rate-encoded models and reports improved state-of-the-art empirical robust accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-100. Reviewers disagree on novelty and thoroughness: two score 8, one score 6, one score 5.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical connection:** The link between rate encoding and randomized smoothing is a unique insight (R1, R2). The mathematical derivation, especially connecting perturbation δ and inference time T in Theorem 5, is convincing (R4). The extension to SNNs with Lemmas 3 and 4 is non-trivial (R1).
- **Theoretical foundation:** Provides a certified robustness guarantee for SNNs under l1-norm, which is novel for this domain (R1, R2).
- **Comprehensive empirical work:** Combines theoretical results with various adversarial training settings (R1). The proposed adversarial training strategy notably enhances state-of-the-art empirical robust accuracy (R2). The topic of certification-based robust training for SNNs is interesting (R3).
- **Clear exposition:** The paper is well-structured and explains relevant concepts clearly (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity on novelty and state-of-the-art claims:** It is unclear whether the novelty lies in adapting randomized smoothing to SNNs, modifying the attack, or something else (R1). The abstract claims “novel algorithm … improves state-of-the-art,” but the experiments only compare “with state-of-the-art adversarial training algorithms,” without precisely defining what constitutes the SOTA in this field (R1). The certified radii reported are much smaller than those for ANNs, which should be discussed explicitly (R1). One reviewer (score 5) argues the contribution is incremental given existing works exploring encoding and robustness (e.g., CVPR 2023, ICCV 2021, ICASSP 2022, Neural Networks 2022) and that some studies show rate-coded SNNs can be vulnerable (R2).
- **Missing empirical details and reproducibility:** The paper lacks a comprehensive description of experimental settings (R1). Missing details include: number of PGD iterations, all training hyperparameters for each adversarial training method, computational cost (GPUs, time) (R1). Insufficient details hinder thorough understanding (R1).
- **Lack of comparison between certified and empirical robustness:** The two measures are treated independently. A direct plot of certified robustness alongside empirical robustness (e.g., in Fig 1(d) for varying epsilon) is needed to visualize the gap (R1). While tightness is not claimed, such a comparison would help assess practical implications (R1).
- **Significant clean accuracy loss:** Rate-encoded SNN suffers a large drop in clean accuracy compared to constant-encoded SNN (CIFAR-10: 91.29% vs 83.22%; CIFAR-100: 70.85% vs 55.27%), which hinders practical application (R4).
- **Limited robustness generalization:** Under Gaussian noise attacks, constant-encoded SNN outperforms rate-encoded SNN (CIFAR-10: 90.62% vs 78.62%; CIFAR-100: 66.46% vs 50.43%), while also defending well against FGSM(R) and PGD(R) (R4). Thus, the claimed superior robustness of rate-encoded SNN does not generalize across different attack types (R4).
- **Statistical nature of the bound:** The bound is obtained through repeated sampling (m times), making it a statistical rather than rigorous deterministic bound. This should be clarified (R3).
- **Robust training framework is unclear:** The described method appears to be simply adding noise to inputs repeatedly; this seems trivial (R3).
- **Misleading table labels:** The label “RAT” is not found in the tables, causing confusion (R3).
- **Counterintuitive empirical results:** PGD-adversarially trained networks perform worse than FGSM-adversarially trained ones when tested against PGD, which contradicts expectations. The authors should explain this, including the PGD adversary used during training (R1). Additionally, why do FGSM(R) and PGD(R) attacks not work on constant-encoded models? Does rate encoding make gradients noisy and disrupt attacks? (R1).
- **Missing important baselines and comparisons:** The paper should include experiments on converted SNNs and provide theoretical analysis for that setting (R2). The literature review should be expanded to cover more recent SNN attack/defense works (R2). The paper should discuss [Liang et al., NeurIPS 2022] regarding robust SNNs (R3).
- **Minor issues:** Incorrect claim that certified methods based on convex relaxations are restricted to ReLU; [1] generalizes to other activations (R1). The differentiability of g with respect to x should be explicitly noted even if f is not differentiable (R1). Language/formatting errors exist (e.g., whitespace before citations, p.2 row 25) (R1).
