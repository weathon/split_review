## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FairDen, a fair density-based clustering algorithm that, unlike prior fair clustering work, handles density-connected clusters, categorical attributes, multiple sensitive attributes, and noise. The core idea is to model density-connectivity via a continuous similarity matrix and then incorporate a fairness-balancing constraint into a normalized cut objective (Equation 11–12). The resulting constrained spectral problem is solved by reshaping the Laplacian.

### Strengths
- **Gap in the literature:** First fair clustering algorithm specifically for density-based clustering. DBSCAN is widely used, so a fair version addresses a natural question. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Handles difficult data types:** Supports categorical attributes, mixed-type data, and multiple sensitive attributes simultaneously, unlike many fair baselines. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Noise handling:** Inherently detects and excludes noise points, a distinguishing property inherited from density-based approaches. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Principled formulation:** Leverages spectral clustering theory with a clear derivation of the optimization objective (Equation 12). Implementation is submitted. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Solid experimental results:** Achieves good balance and density-based clustering quality on real-world datasets; outperforms existing density-based and fair-oriented methods on both aspects. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Ability to form arbitrary-shaped clusters** while achieving fairness. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost:** Complexity is O(d·n³) due to spectral decomposition. The paper does not provide runtime experiments, especially on larger datasets; only small real-world datasets are used. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Scalability concerns unaddressed:** No optimization techniques or approximations (e.g., sketching, GPU acceleration) are experimentally validated. A claim about GPU reduction is made but unsupported. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Rigid fairness constraint:** The constraint in (11)–(12) enforces strict equality of group representation per cluster. Several reviewers note this is unrealistic and overly restrictive. A relaxed version (approximation in matrix-norm sense) would be more practical. (Reviewers 2; also asked by Reviewer 1 and 2 if the method can be extended to softer or multiple fairness constraints.)
- **Incremental novelty:** The continuous distance formulation (ddc) is from Beer et al. (KDD 2023) and the spectral solution is standard in fair clustering literature. The contribution largely reduces to modifying the Laplacian—considered insufficiently innovative for a top venue by some reviewers. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Insufficient related work coverage:** Missing citations to recent fair clustering methods that consider proportionality (Chen et al., ICML 2019), probabilistic fairness (Esmaeili et al., NeurIPS 2020), bounded cost (Esmaeili et al., NeurIPS 2021), and doubly constrained fairness (Dickerson et al., NeurIPS 2024). (Reviewer 4)
- **Weak baselines and outdated comparisons:** The only density-based baseline is DBSCAN; other conventional clustering methods are not compared. The latest fair comparison method is from five years ago. Table 3 omits results from other fair clustering methods. (Reviewer 4)
- **Low ARI/NMI scores:** In Tables 2 and 3, ARI and NMI values are often close to zero. The paper says higher is better, but these low values raise questions about clustering quality. (Reviewer 1)
- **Poor presentation and self-containedness:** The paper assumes the reader knows the KDD 2023 paper, DBSCAN details, and concepts like ultrametrics without adequate explanation. Notations are unclear (e.g., Sᵢ, r_ij, k, quantifier in Eq. (10), inverse ultrametric relevance). Algorithm 1’s output is ambiguous. Many non-standard abbreviations (iff, s.t., w.r.t.) should be spelled out. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- **Figure 1 is unclear:** Visual comparisons are hard to interpret; clusters look similar across methods and mapping balance/DCSI values is not intuitive. The caption mentions DCSI before its definition. Suggested to break into multiple figures. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Missing noise evaluation:** Noise points are excluded from some evaluations, but an experiment designed to explicitly show the advantage of handling noise is absent. (Reviewer 1)
- **Scattered limitations:** Points about reduced performance on small sensitive groups are spread throughout; a dedicated limitations section is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Omitted details:** vol(c) should be vol(c_l) in the text; the output of Algorithm 1 is not clearly stated. (Reviewer 4)
- **Parameter dependence:** No ablation experiments on how results vary with parameters (e.g., number of clusters, balancing weight). (Reviewer 4)
- **Sensitive attribute combinations:** The handling of multiple sensitive attributes (e.g., Black women) via Equation 7 is not explained in physical terms. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minimal data type requirements:** Not addressed—e.g., how many numerical features are needed relative to the number of sensitive attributes. (Reviewer 3)
- **Extendibility of the fairness constraint:** It is unclear how the constraints in (11) could be adapted to different or multiple fairness criteria without case-by-case reformulation. (Reviewer 1)
