## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies continual reinforcement learning under sequential multi-task MDPs where only one Q-function is maintained. It proposes measuring catastrophic forgetting via a weighted squared difference between optimal value functions across tasks (MDP difference). It analyzes finetune (lower bound) and multi-task learning (upper bound) baselines, then proposes a practical algorithm that reweights historical and current Bellman targets to trade off stability and plasticity. Experiments are limited to tabular grid-world and small MDP domains.

### Strengths
- The idea of capturing drift in value functions as a metric for continual forgetting is sensible and interesting. (Reviewer 1)
- Good assumptions (e.g., known task boundaries) narrow down the problem to a tractable formulation. (Reviewer 1)
- The attempt to formalize catastrophic forgetting is commendable and the specific technical choices appear original, even if significance remains unclear. (Reviewer 1)
- The approach in Definitions 1–3 is simple and beautiful and should be pursued further. (Reviewer 2)
- The analysis of the optimal form (weighted sum of previous Q-functions) seems novel. (Reviewer 3)
- Providing an empirical study, even if preliminary, is commendable. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- The main algorithm is derived from the proposed objective (4) without sufficient justification. The objective depends on both the MDPs and the value estimation algorithm, making conclusions unclear. (Reviewer 1)
- The claimed results on the existing upper‑bound (sequential multi‑task RL) and lower‑bound (Finetune) algorithms do not add much technical insight beyond prior work. (Reviewer 1)
- Experiments are limited to tabular settings (grid‑world and small MDPs). The scale is too small to draw general conclusions; the results serve only as a sanity check. (Reviewers 1, 2)
  - Unaddressed: what happens when MDPs differ more widely? Can a changed state/action structure be handled, or is a fixed structure integral to the approach? (Reviewer 2)
- Theoretical analysis is trivial and not fruitful: Theorem 1 is a direct application of prior work, essentially showing that fine‑tuning quickly adapts to the current task and forgets previous ones. (Reviewer 3)
- Proposition 1 appears interesting but lacks further discussion, e.g., studying cases where fine‑tune is significantly worse than multi‑task learning. (Reviewer 3)
- The connection between the practical algorithm (Section 5.1) and the optimal solution (Section 4.2) is not formally discussed. The special weight form in Equation (5) is not carried into the algorithmic design; the theory and algorithm appear largely irrelevant. (Reviewer 3)
- The setting is too trivial: only one Q-function is maintained to memorize all tasks. Many papers study more comprehensive setups, such as adaptive memory or separate Q-functions for discrepant tasks. (Reviewer 3)
- Missing baseline: for the tabular case, the optimal (approximate) algorithm could be implemented and compared but is not. (Reviewer 3)
- Another naive but meaningful baseline – simply averaging the previous Q estimates – is not compared. (Reviewer 3, question)
- The MDP difference metric is presented without alternatives; a comparison of various metrics would be needed to justify the specific choice. (Reviewer 2)
- The convergence analysis of baseline algorithms becomes uninteresting when they are outperformed in the later experiments. (Reviewer 2)
- Notation and presentation issues (detailed by all reviewers):
  - Definition 2: distance notation appears as `d^2_2` instead of `d^w_2`. (Reviewer 1)
  - "s sequence" (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - Inconsistent task indices (t vs. i). (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - Super‑/subscript errors in the main update rule for the t‑th MDP. (Reviewer 1)
  - "conduct a function" → "construct a function". (Reviewer 1)
  - "it suggest" without referent. (Reviewer 2)
  - "it is less studied about" is ungrammatical. (Reviewer 2)
  - Definition 3 ends in the middle of a sentence. (Reviewer 2)
  - "will leads" → "will lead". (Reviewer 2)
  - "Due to … is …" makes no sense; perhaps "Due to … being …". (Reviewer 2)
  - "Finetuen" typo. (Reviewer 2)
  - Need to introduce `\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_i)` notation. (Reviewer 3)
- Unresolved technical questions that overlap with criticisms:
  - Optimal Q‑function does not uniquely identify the transition/reward pair; the paper uses it to capture both variations. (Reviewer 3)
  - Definition 1 and 2 overlap; Definition 1 is subsumed by Definition 2 under uniform weights. (Reviewer 3)
  - How are queries made on previous `\hat Q_t` when training sequentially with only one Q‑function? Notation `T_\pi` is unclear. (Reviewer 3)
  - How are Monte Carlo samples drawn from `\rho_t^{\pi}` when `\pi` itself is still being learned? (Reviewer 3)
  - Is `\alpha` in (7) normalized? Without normalization the update could lead to a trivial solution. (Reviewer 3)
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (Reviewer 2) rated the paper 5 (positive, contribution 3, found the approach simple and beautiful), while the other two gave 3 (contribution 2 and 1, found limited significance and insufficient justification). The minority positive view is noted, but the majority criticism remains that the contribution is limited and the theoretical/experimental support is insufficient.
