## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a joint optimization framework for zero-shot mixed-precision quantization (ZMPQ) that alternately optimizes synthetic calibration data (via a Gram-Gradient matrix approach) and layer-wise bit-width allocation (via quadratic optimization). It aims to exploit the correlation between data quality and bit assignment. Experiments on ImageNet with ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and MobileNetV2 show competitive results against prior zero-shot quantization methods. Reviewer scores: 6, 5, 8 – Reviewer 3 is notably more positive than the other two.

### Strengths
- Proposes a unique joint optimization approach for data generation and bit allocation, filling a gap in zero-shot mixed-precision quantization research (R1).
- Demonstrates superior or comparable performance to state-of-the-art methods under low-bit settings and varied model budgets, verified on multiple architectures (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, MobileNetV2) (R1).
- Provides a well-rounded suite of ablation studies that demonstrate the robustness of the method (R1).
- Inherently considers the correlation between the quality of the generated calibration dataset and the bit allocation to the model’s layers (R2).
- The paper is well-organized and clearly stated (R2).
- The topic of proposing a mixed-precision quantization method for ZSQ from the perspective of data quality is well motivated (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient explanation of Gram-Gradient matrix and Equation (6):** The connection between the Gram matrix and data optimization is not clearly established. The derivation from Equation (5) to Equation (6) is unclear, and the mathematical justification for why the Gram-Gradient matrix is effective for quantization is lacking. More elaboration is needed for readers not deeply versed in the topic (R1, R2).
- **Need for visual/qualitative results:** The paper would benefit from figures or visualizations to explain concepts like gradient matching (e.g., gradient distribution or direction comparisons) between synthetic and original data. Current illustration is insufficient (R1).
- **Table notation ambiguity:** The W/A notation (e.g., 2/2 and */2) in Tables 1 and 2 is unclear. The asterisk’s meaning in the mixed-precision context needs explicit explanation. Additionally, the average bit width of the models obtained by the proposed method should be calculated and reported in the table for easier comparison (R1, R2).
- **Outdated references:** All references are from 2023 or earlier. Recent (2024) works on mixed-precision or zero-shot quantization should be included (R2).
- **Validation set assumption vs. ImageNet evaluation:** The paper assumes a validation set for explanation (Section 3.1), but evaluation is on ImageNet. The relation between these two sets needs careful explanation. If the validation set does not exist in ZSQ, would formulas (6) and (8) change or simplify? (R2, also R3 doubts the assumption that matching Gram-Gradient matrices from synthetic datasets indicates correctness of features).
- **Lack of synthesized data comparison:** The paper should compare the synthetic data generated by the proposed method with that from existing methods and provide deeper analysis or explanations (R2).
- **Gradient perspective may be passive:** Optimizing from a gradient perspective given poor-quality synthetic data might be ineffective; gradient-free search could be a better option. The authors should discuss or justify this (R3).
- **Unfair quantizer configuration comparisons:** The quantizer setups of the compared methods are not well ablated. For example, AdaSG only quantizes weights using minmax and naive STE, while the proposed Genie+MPQ uses weight & activation quantization and AdaRound. This mismatch makes it impossible to observe the superiority of the generated images from Table 1. The paper should carefully list quantizer baselines for all methods for a fair comparison (R3).
- **Missing CIFAR benchmark:** Traditional benchmarks on CIFAR datasets are not adopted; the authors should explain why (R3).
- **Generator configuration not aligned with comparison:** The generator configuration differs from the compared method (Genie’s generator is a newer version); further ablation is needed to isolate the effect (R3).
- **Lack of overhead analysis:** No analysis of computational/memory overhead of the proposed method is provided (R3).
- **Need for 3-bit experiments:** The 4-bit case is relatively simple; existing 3-bit quantization benchmarks (e.g., AdaDFQ/GDFQ series) still show poor accuracy. The authors should present experimental results for 3-bit quantization (R3).
- **Unclear quantizer details for proposed method:** The paper does not elaborate whether per-channel quantization and naive STE are used for the proposed method, as done in compared methods like GDFQ/Qimera/AdaDFQ (R3).
- **Potential applicability to PTQ:** The method may also work for conventional PTQ when the calibration set does not fit model statistics. The authors should provide targeted explanations or experiments on PTQ to corroborate (R3).
- **Minority view:** Reviewer 2 notes that the joint optimization is only alternate/iterative and lacks a deeply coupled mechanism, suggesting the two parts are not truly jointly optimized (R2).
