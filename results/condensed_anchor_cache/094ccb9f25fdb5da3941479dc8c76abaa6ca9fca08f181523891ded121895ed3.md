## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper benchmarks internal representations of mental states in LMs using linear probing on the BigToM dataset across Llama-2 and Pythia models. It addresses five research questions: effect of model size, fine-tuning, prompt variations, probe memorization, and activation steering via Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA). While Reviewers 2 and 4 found the study comprehensive and insightful (scores 5), Reviewers 1 and 3 considered it disjointed, lacking clear takeaways, and offering only incremental extensions (scores 3). There is disagreement on the significance of the contributions and the strength of the connection to Theory of Mind.

### Strengths
* The paper is well structured, easy to follow, and includes a comprehensive discussion of related work (Reviewer 1).
* Provides extensive experiments across diverse settings (model size, fine-tuning, prompts, memorization, steering), offering useful observations on LM behavior in ToM (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
* Investigates how internal representations of beliefs evolve with model size and training stages, and how prompt variations affect probing performance — findings Reviewer 4 considers important and insightful.
* Introduces Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) as a probe-free steering method. Reviewer 2 found this interesting for the probing and interpreting community; however, other reviewers questioned its novelty and interpretation.

### Weaknesses
* The paper is disjointed; results appear scattered across five research questions without a coherent narrative or clear, enlightening takeaways (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 suggests focusing on one or two questions in greater depth.
* Probing accuracy is a weak measure of representation quality. Steering results or control tasks (e.g., Hewitt and Liang, 2019) are needed to confirm that probes capture meaningful representations rather than high-dimensional artifacts (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 also noted the empirical results do not offer significant new insights.
* The probing task's connection to Theory of Mind is questionable. The probe may pick up surface correlates (e.g., narrative structure) rather than genuine mental states; the setting does not address multiple protagonists or no-protagonist cases (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 similarly questioned whether the conclusions truly reveal mental representations, as the methods are universally applicable to general probing.
* The claim that CAA avoids training a probe is misleading: computing the steering vector still requires a labeled dataset and amounts to a simple linear regressor (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 asked whether a simple mean difference is sufficient for general probing and whether any trends with model size or training exist.
* The analysis of fine-tuning (RQ2) is insufficient: only comparing base vs. chat versions of open-source models does not control for different fine-tuning datasets or strategies; a more detailed analysis is needed (Reviewer 4).
* The description of results is confusing. “Sensitivity to prompt variations” is trivial; the phrase “even when such variations should be beneficial” lacks a clear contrast (Reviewer 1). Similarly, the statement about improving reasoning performance (Line 091) should specify Theory of Mind reasoning, not general reasoning (Reviewer 4).
* The $k$-principal component ablation is unconvincing; control tasks would be a better check that probes are not simply memorizing (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 also questioned RQ4: if probes memorize training data, held-out test accuracy may suffice, but the deeper concern is that probes learn the task despite the LM not truly representing beliefs.
* Experiments appear as incremental extensions of prior probing work on BigToM (Reviewer 3). The use of Llama 2 instead of Llama 3 (available for a while) is not justified (Reviewer 1).
* Missing experimental details: how were pre-trained base models used (few-shot demonstrations or instructions)? (Reviewer 3). Why is protagonist belief detection insensitive to prompt variations in Figure 7, while the paper later claims robust representations from an omniscient perspective? More references and analysis are needed (Reviewer 4).
