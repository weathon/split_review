## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies whether modern GANs (StyleGAN, BigGAN) serve as good density models for outlier detection and generative classification. Using annealed importance sampling (AIS) on a relaxed likelihood (adding noise to the generator output), the authors estimate log-likelihoods and find that GANs assign higher likelihood to out‑of‑distribution (OOD) images (e.g., flat images, SVHN) than to training data (CIFAR‑10). They attribute this to the dominance of the z‑reconstruction error in the likelihood, and show that reconstruction is often better for OOD data than for training examples. The work is purely empirical, with experiments limited to subsets of CIFAR‑10, SVHN, and ImageNet.

### Strengths
- **Interesting and timely question**: evaluating GANs’ density‑estimation ability is important, especially as GANs continue to produce realistic samples. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Clear writing and presentation**: the paper is generally well‑structured and easy to follow; plots are readable. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Novel link between likelihood and z‑reconstruction**: the observation that the z‑reconstruction term dominates likelihood assignment is considered novel and potentially insightful by some reviewers (3,5). (Reviewer 3,5)
- **Empirical support for a known issue**: the work provides additional evidence that GANs are poor density models, complementing earlier findings. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Fits into recent OOD detection literature**: the study relates to ongoing work on failure modes of neural density models. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and originality**: the overall finding—that GANs do not learn the true data distribution—has been known from earlier work (e.g., Arora et al. [2]). The paper largely confirms this without providing a deep new explanation; it heavily builds on the AIS framework of Wu et al. [1]. (Reviewers 1,2,3). A minority of reviewers (3,5) found the z‑reconstruction analysis novel, but the majority view is that the contribution is incremental.
- **Weak motivation**: it is unclear why GANs—which are trained adversarially and not with a maximum‑likelihood objective—should be expected to perform well as density estimators. The paper does not adequately justify the premise. (Reviewers 1,3,4). Similarly, the claim that GANs “cannot reconstruct training images when only z is optimized” is not justified; the optimization problem (Eq. 7) is NP‑hard [Inverting Deep Generative Models], making failure plausible but not definitive. (Reviewer 4)
- **Methodological concerns with likelihood estimation**:
    - Adding Gaussian noise to define a density over $\mathbb{R}^n$ (Eq. 1) is necessary but arbitrary; the choice of noise variance critically affects results and is not sufficiently discussed. (Reviewers 1,4,5)
    - The AIS likelihood calculation is “unprincipled” because the density is not inherent to the GAN; it is an artifact of the noise augmentation. (Reviewer 4)
    - Equation 6 is an inequality (ELBO), not an equality as written. (Reviewer 1)
- **Weak analysis of the z‑reconstruction error**:
    - The error exists because a k‑dimensional latent cannot represent all of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (a known issue in compressed sensing with generative priors [GANprior]). The noise added to the manifold confounds the interpretation—is the poor likelihood due to the noise or the missing dimensionality? A noise‑to‑signal ratio could help. (Reviewer 4)
    - The L2 reconstruction term comes only from the added Gaussian noise, not from the GAN itself, weakening the link between reconstruction quality and density capture. (Reviewer 3)
- **Contradiction with prior work**: earlier studies have used GANs successfully for anomaly detection [3] and have shown memorization of training samples [4,5]. The paper’s opposite findings raise doubts about whether the estimated log‑likelihoods are faithful to the true model. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited and potentially biased experimental setup**:
    - Only subsets of datasets are used (200 samples from 10 classes of ImageNet, small samples from CIFAR‑10 and SVHN). This risks missing modes that the GAN might have learned; full‑dataset results would strengthen the conclusions. (Reviewers 2,5)
    - No justification is given for the selection of classes (e.g., ImageNet10, CIFAR‑10 classes), so results might be artifacts of class choice. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing analysis of the optimized latent z**:
    - It is not verified that the optimized z values stay within the latent distribution the GAN was trained on (e.g., Gaussian range). If they fall outside, KL divergence and likelihood estimates become unreliable. (Reviewer 5)
    - Figure 1 shows that reconstructions of out‑of‑distribution images (SVHN, flat) are visually as poor as reconstructions of training images; only generated images reconstruct well—a predictable outcome because the GAN is optimized to generate those modes. The claim that GANs “copy images completely outside the original training distribution” is overstated. (Reviewer 5)
- **Lack of theoretical grounding and rigorous analysis**:
    - The work is purely empirical; no theoretical argument or deep understanding of the training dynamics is provided. (Reviewer 3)
    - The paper does not discuss existing theoretical results on GANs as minimax optimal density estimators under certain assumptions. (Reviewer 3)
    - The root cause of poor density modeling is not clearly stated; the title’s “Why” is never answered beyond “the likelihood is dominated by reconstruction error.” (Reviewers 2,3)
- **Writing and clarity issues**:
    - The paper’s contributions are not clearly summarized at the start; the reader is often unsure what is original vs. prior work. (Reviewer 2)
    - The style switches between asking “how well GANs estimate density” and “why they are poor,” making the narrative less focused. (Reviewer 4)
    - Specific details are lacking: how the conditional likelihood $p_\theta(x|c)$ is defined (Section 2.2), how flat images are generated, and why the density in Figure 4 exceeds 1. (Reviewers 1,5)
- **Insufficient code quality**: the provided code lacks library versions, reproduction instructions, and is not ready for independent verification. (Reviewer 1)
