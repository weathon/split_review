## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers evaluated the paper on IDInit initialization. Reviewer 1 (score 8, confidence 3) described it as well-structured with extensive numerical evaluations, but noted a need for theoretical justification of the momentum part and better justification of design choices. Reviewer 2 (score 6, confidence 3) found it a simple addition to identity-control methods with competitive results but had concerns about clarity of Figure 2, lack of comparisons in Figures 8/Table 3, vague claims, and an inadequate definition of dynamical isometry. Reviewer 3 (score 6, confidence 4) praised the novel initialization, clear presentation, and theoretical support for rank constraints, but pointed out suboptimal performance on a 110-layer network in Table 1, lack of validation on self-supervised or diffusion models, and absence of convergence rate analysis. Reviewer 4 (score 5, confidence 3) found the core idea interesting and practical but considered the paper very hard to follow, with unclear notation, unconvincing experiments (Sections 4.1–4.3), and insufficiently controlled comparisons. Overall, the reviews are mixed, with Reviewer 1 more positive and Reviewer 4 most critical, while Reviewers 2 and 3 are moderately positive with notable weaknesses.

### Strengths
- Problem tackled is practical and of interest to the deep learning community (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The core idea of IDInit is simple, easy to implement, and a novel addition to the family of identity-control initialization (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The numerical evaluations are extensive, covering multiple baselines, neural architectures (including ResNet, GPT-2, etc.), and datasets (ImageNet, text classification, LLM pre-training) (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well-structured and well-written, with most concepts carefully explained and forming a good story (Reviewer 1; contrast Reviewer 4 finds presentation poor).
- The investigation into mitigating the dead neuron problem with small perturbations in IDInit is interesting and potentially valuable (Reviewer 2).
- The hyperparameter experiments (Section 4.1) show that IDInit can lead to a broader range of effective hyperparameter configurations, which is insightful (Reviewer 2).
- Several claims are theoretically supported, notably how the method addresses the rank constraint problem in non-square weight matrices (Reviewer 3).
- The method achieves competitive or better performance compared to other identity-control schemes in most studied cases (Reviewer 2). Decent performance improvements are claimed (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- The paper is very hard to follow; it frequently relies on citing prior works without summarizing their results, has inconsistent notation, and contains spelling/grammar errors (Reviewer 4). Clarify notation between Figure 1 and Equation 1 (Reviewer 4). The term “residual stem” should be introduced and defined earlier (Reviewer 2). Remove the phrase “In this paradigm” from lines 134–135 for clarity (Reviewer 2).
- Figure 2 (a motivation plot) is unclear: what model, dataset, task are used? What does “Rectangle Loss” mean? Are lines averaged over runs with standard deviation? Where is the “Random” curve? Is the learning rate tuned per method? Does this network have activation functions? Why is the rank issue significant when the dimension difference is only 40 out of 280? Why is Adagrad used instead of SGD or Adam? (Reviewers 2 and 4)
- Figures 8 and Table 3 (largest networks tested) lack comparison with other identity-control methods and do not report mean/standard deviation over multiple runs to assess statistical significance (Reviewer 2).
- Vague claims in lines 110–114 and 115–116: “significantly improved accuracy” and “almost all the best performance” are not tied to specific tasks, datasets, or baselines; be specific or move to experimental results (Reviewer 2).
- Definition and understanding of dynamical isometry is slightly inadequate—achieving criticality is not equivalent to attaining dynamical isometry (see Pennington 2017). While not central, it raises doubts (Reviewer 2).
- Section 3.1.1’s convergence analysis contains an unclear statement: “as η is usually 1e-1 and both training pairs…” – labeling a learning rate as “standard” is vague; discuss constraints or boundaries for η instead (Reviewer 2).
- Some design/hyperparameter choices in Section 4 (lines 434–439, 449–454, 464–474, 504–509, 517–523) are not justified with references (Reviewer 1).
- The momentum part (Section 3.1.2 or related) needs more theoretical justification (Reviewer 1). Figure 4 compares momentum vs. no momentum but does not account for the effective learning rate increase due to momentum—the difference is not surprising (Reviewer 4).
- IDInit is suboptimal in some cases; e.g., Table 1 shows that for a 110-layer neural network, the final accuracy does not surpass the baselines (Reviewer 3).
- IDInit has not been validated across diverse learning paradigms such as self-supervised learning methods or diffusion models (Reviewers 2? Actually Reviewer 3 says this; Reviewer 2 also wishes for extended hyperparameter experiments). Provide additional experiments (Reviewer 3’s question).
- The paper lacks theoretical analysis on the convergence rate of IDInit (Reviewer 3’s question).
- In Appendix A3, clarify the upper index notation in x_i^{(0)} and the change to 3 in Π_1. Theorem 3.1 should include constraints on batch size since it influences gradient rank (Reviewer 2’s question).
- Equation (3): how is τ determined for different activation functions? It is not specified (Reviewer 2’s question).
- The “loose” condition introduced in lines 314–318 (adding random noise to leading matrices) is not mentioned again—are later experiments conducted with this change? Does it make a difference in practice? (Reviewer 4’s question)
- Section 4.1: The hyperparameter sweep granularity is too high, and the best performance for Kaiming initialization occurs at the edge of the sweep, making the comparison unconvincing (Reviewer 4).
- Section 4.2: The hyperparameter comparison is not fair; hyperparameters need to be tuned per method to avoid bias favoring IDInit (Reviewer 4).
- Section 4.3: The exact setup without IDIC is not explained well (Reviewer 4’s question).
- Figure 3: Use separate symbols for the number of features on the skip path and the intermediate dimension; here D_i is used twice for different dimensions (Reviewer 4).
- Remove the word “Some” from the caption of Figure 8 (Reviewer 2 minor). Clarify what “Identity-1” means in Figure 2 (Reviewer 2’s question). How do results in Figure 2 change with varying matrix sizes? (Reviewer 2’s minor question)
