## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ResDex, combining residual policy learning with a mixture-of-experts (MoE) for universal dexterous grasping. The method trains geometry-unaware base policies on clustered objects using only proprioception, then learns residual actions and expert weights via a hyper policy. It achieves 88.8% success on DexGraspNet (3,200 objects) with no generalization drop on unseen objects and trains within 12 hours on a single GPU. Reviewers were split: two (scores 8,8) were highly positive about the novel combination, strong performance, and training efficiency; two (scores 6,6) acknowledged contributions but were more critical, citing concerns about complexity, missing real-world validation, and insufficient support for efficiency claims.

### Strengths
- Novel integration of residual policy learning and MoE for dexterous grasping; base policies trained on object clusters using only proprioception, hyper policy mixes them with state info.
- Curriculum of two reward functions: first encourages similarity to demonstrated grasps, second encourages grasping success; good trade-off between natural and optimal grasps.
- State-of-the-art success rate (88.8%) on DexGraspNet, significantly outperforming prior RL methods (UniDexGrasp, UniDexGrasp++).
- Avoids complex curriculum design of prior multi-task RL; almost zero performance drop on unseen objects and categories.
- Training efficiency: completes all tasks within 12 hours on a single 4090 GPU.
- Geometry-unaware base policies enhance generalization.
- Appropriate ablations validate components (e.g., effect of residual actions, number of experts, MoE vs. single policy).
- Well-organized and clearly written presentation with helpful figures and tables.
- Comprehensive comparisons with baselines and ablation studies.

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing real-world experiments**: Unclear if simulator gains transfer to real robots. Shadow Hand is expensive; suggest evaluation with more accessible hands like LEAP Hand. Also unclear if sophisticated hand is necessary for high performance.
2. **Training efficiency claim unsubstantiated**: No controlled comparison to baselines (e.g., UniDexGrasp) with matched parameter counts and hardware; only training times in appendix without direct contrast.
3. **Generalization evaluation limited to simulation**: Only tested on DexGraspNet; should include datasets with varying textures, shapes, sizes to demonstrate robustness (contrast with reviewer who stated zero drop on unseen).
4. **Complexity of approach**: Combining multiple base policies and MoE adds coordination overhead and hyperparameter tuning difficulty, contradicting residual RL’s goal of reducing exploration burden; computational overhead during training and inference.
5. **Number of experts k not intuitive**: Performance peaks at k=4 and drops thereafter, counter-intuitive for >3,000 objects. Paper incorrectly claims “increasing k leads to slight performance gain” when data show decline. Needs discussion of why four experts suffice.
6. **Rotation representation for action summation**: Base policy actions summed with predicted weights; unclear how rotations (non-Euclidean) are handled in this summation.
7. **Contradiction in “geometry-unaware”**: First performs geometry-aware clustering to train base policies, then claims geometry-unaware base policies; final MoE is geometry-aware. Clarify definitions or merge steps.
8. **Grasp synthesis component not explained**: How is the grasp synthesis part done? Missing detail.
9. **Ambiguity in base policy training**: Sections 4.1/4.2 unclear whether base policy is trained on single or multiple objects; specify early. Also: are object positions/orientations randomized during training?
10. **Missing discussion of limitations and failure cases**: Which scenarios still challenge the method?
11. **Missing algorithm block in Section 4.4**; minor citation issues (misuse of \citep vs. \citet).
12. **Terminology**: “geometry-unaware” could be “geometry-agnostic.”
13. **How is g sampled in Eq. 2?** Does random sampling cause gradient interference?
14. **Notation ambiguity**: Are q and q_t in lines 160/237 hand joint positions or joint angle configurations?
15. **MoE motivation**: Base policy gives single grasping pose due to argmax; could other multimodal policy training methods address this?
16. **Hyper policy dynamics**: How does π^H learn to weight base actions without having a^B_{t,i} as input? Could λ_t collapse to mean or one-hot?
17. **Vision-based policy setup unclear**: Cameras, placement, point cloud processing, evaluation in simulation or real?
18. **Ablation training set reference (line 421) ambiguous**: Refers to six objects or DexGraspNet training set?
19. **Reward definition scattered**: Should be grouped together for clarity.
