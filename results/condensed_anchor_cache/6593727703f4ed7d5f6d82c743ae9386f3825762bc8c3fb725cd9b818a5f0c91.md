## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes SC-4DGS, a pose-free optimization pipeline for dynamic Gaussian Splatting from monocular videos. It replaces traditional SfM (e.g., COLMAP) with DUSt3R for initial point cloud and camera pose estimation, introduces a batchwise optimization strategy to handle dense frames, and uses DynMF for dynamic motion representation. Multiple regularization terms are added. The authors also introduce the Kubric-MRig benchmark dataset with controlled camera and object motions. Experiments compare against RoDyNeRF and static pose-free methods.

### Strengths

1. **First pose-free 4D Gaussian Splatting pipeline.** Reviewers note this is among the first works to achieve pose-free dynamic view synthesis with Gaussian Splatting, providing valuable insights for future research (R3).

2. **Batchwise optimization for DUSt3R.** A key novelty is adapting DUSt3R via batchwise optimization to enable self-calibration in dynamic scenes without SfM, addressing memory/computation limits and making the method scalable (R1, R2). This removes dependency on pre-calibrated camera data (R1).

3. **Kubric-MRig dataset.** The new benchmark fills a gap by including ground-truth camera poses and multiple views for dynamic scenes, enabling joint evaluation of calibration and rendering quality (R1, R2, R3). It incorporates extensive camera and object motion (R3).

4. **Detailed mathematical formulations.** The paper provides thorough derivations that build a strong theoretical foundation and aid understanding of the model and optimization (R3).

5. **Improvements over prior dynamic neural fields.** SC-4DGS shows large improvements compared to the previous pose-free dynamic method RoDyNeRF (R3). Experiments cover both dynamic and static scenes (R4).

6. **Integrated pipeline.** The method combines DUSt3R, DynMF, and novel optimization into a single pipeline for pose-free dynamic rendering (R2).

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited originality and reliance on existing models.** Multiple reviewers find the contribution incremental. The method largely combines DUSt3R and DynMF, replacing COLMAP with DUSt3R without significant structural innovation (R1, R3, R4). Reviewer 3 notes "the primary change appears to be the replacement of COLMAP with DUSt3R." Reviewer 1 describes it as "an engineering improvement rather than a theoretical innovation." In contrast, Reviewer 2 sees the batchwise optimization as providing novelty for scalability.

2. **Heavy dependence on DUSt3R initialization.** Any failure in DUSt3R directly causes failure of the method (R2, R3). The robustness to suboptimal initial poses from DUSt3R is not evaluated (R2, Q1). It is unclear whether the joint optimization can correct such errors (R2). The method's contribution is questioned if it merely combines a two-step process without addressing inherent pose estimation errors (R2). Experiments on large-motion datasets like DAVIS or iPhone are missing to test DUSt3R's limits (R4, Q2).

3. **Missing critical comparisons.** The paper lacks comparisons with:
   - SfM-based methods (COLMAP) combined with 4DGS to show where SfM fails and SC-4DGS succeeds (R2, R3, R4). COLMAP does not completely fail on dynamic scenes due to exhaustive matching (R4).
   - Other pose-free dynamic reconstruction methods such as casualSAM, LEAP-VO, ParticlesfM (R4).
   - Time comparisons: no quantitative training time reduction compared to COLMAP or other baselines despite claiming DUSt3R’s high complexity (R3, R4).

4. **Insufficient comparison and evaluation on dynamic scenes.** For dynamic scenes without ground-truth poses, the only baseline is RoDyNeRF (R3). Experiments are conducted on just one public dynamic dataset, which is insufficient given the multiple regularization terms (R4). Additionally, the paper mischaracterizes RoDyNeRF as implicit when it uses an explicit neural voxel radiance field (R3, Q1).

5. **Dataset limitations and concerns.**
   - Simplicity: background is a simple ground plane lacking depth variation; objects are geometric shapes or basic scanned objects, not realistic complex shapes like humans, animals, or vehicles (R1). Objects are mostly rigid, no non-rigid deformations (R3, Q5).
   - Ratio of dynamic to static objects: only 1–3 objects are dynamic among 10–20 static objects and background, so static objects dominate and could overwhelm dynamic errors, especially as the camera scans (R1, Q1).
   - Monocular vs. multi-view inconsistency: the 100 cameras in Kubric-MRig have wide variation and rapid switching, so monocular sequences may resemble stereo video, conflicting with the stated monocular setting (R3, Q5). The iPhone dataset is claimed to lack “large motion,” but one reviewer disagrees, citing examples like ‘spin’, ‘apple’, ‘space-out’ (R4).
   - Novel view evaluation only at time 0: this introduces potential bias; evaluation across multiple time steps is needed (R1, Q2).

6. **Writing, clarity, and presentation issues.**
   - Figure 1 lacks a legend/annotations and is hard to follow; Figure 4 would benefit from a horizontal arrangement of camera rotations (R1).
   - Sections 3.1 and 3.3 contain excessive detail (eight equations for 3DGS basics, many variables used once), hindering readability; a concise overview is recommended (R1, R3).
   - In the ablation study, an uninformative sentence merely repeats the abbreviation (e.g., “SB uses the strided batch technique”) and references a non-existent “Appendix 4.4” (R2).
   - Table 1 terms “wide viewpoints” and “large motion” lack quantitative measures (e.g., angular range, displacement magnitude) (R3, Q2).
   - The paper does not specify whether ground-truth camera intrinsics are used or optimized (R4, Q1).

7. **Missing analysis and ablations.**
   - Need for experiments showing specific scenarios where SfM fails but DUSt3R succeeds, to justify replacing COLMAP (R2, R3, R4).
   - Ablation on the ratio of dynamic to static objects in the dataset (R1, Q1).
   - Motion mask samples from RoDynRF are not provided; their effectiveness is questioned (R4, Q3).
   - Broader implications or extensions (e.g., to other 3D reconstruction models, robotics, AR) are not discussed (R1).
