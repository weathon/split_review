## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies graph neural networks (GNNs) to predict tropical cyclone formation and evolution, modeling maximum sustained wind speed (VMAX) as nodes with temporal edges over 6-hour steps. The authors provide their dataset and code as attachments. Two reviewers (R2, R3) find the work severely lacking in presentation and scientific rigor, while one reviewer (R1) acknowledges the interesting application but also notes major weaknesses. All three agree that the paper is not ready for publication at ICLR.

### Strengths
- **Interesting application of GNNs to weather data** – the approach is potentially useful for the research community (R1).
- **Considers two recent comparable models** for weather forecasting (R1).
- **Graph structure and methods are well described** (R1).
- **Code and data are provided as attachments** (R1).  
  **Note:** The other two reviewers (R2, R3) did not list any strengths; R3’s comment “Courage shall be encouraged?” is sarcastic, not a genuine strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Exceeds the page limit** – flagged by all three reviewers (R1, R2, R3).
- **No baseline comparison** – the paper does not compare results against any existing methods (neither physical models nor machine learning approaches). This is a critical omission (R1, R2, R3). Specifically, R1 asks for a detailed comparison with other approaches/benchmarks; R2 notes that ML techniques for tropical cyclone evolution are well-studied and must be cited and compared; R3 emphasizes that even if the method is pre-existing, the authors should present multiple alternatives and justify why theirs works better.
- **Inadequate literature review** – missing key works: Keisler (2022) on global weather GNNs, and Rasp et al. (2023) on WeatherBench 2 (R1). R2 adds that the state-of-the-art in ML for TC evolution is not discussed.
- **Novelty is unclear** – simply applying a GNN to a new dataset may be insufficient for publication (R1).
- **Experiments lack rigor** – the paper presents a single run without repetition or ablation studies; no baseline or alternative is tested (R3).
- **Poor presentation quality** (multiple issues):
  - Table 2 lists raw data points (actual vs. predicted) and takes a full page; a figure or summary metrics would be appropriate (R3).
  - Figure 1 is not informative (R1).
  - Figure 3 is uninformative, just shows training metrics without insight; text is too small and unreadable; it depicts a “typical graph architecture” rather than the proposed one, which would be more useful (R1, R3).
  - Figure 4 is redundant because its information is already in Figure 5 (R3).
  - Figure 5 needs transparency adjustment for clarity (R3).
  - Equations are not explained (e.g., equation (2) is unlabeled as a loss, equation (3) is the normalized Laplacian) (R1).
- **References do not follow the conference format** (R1).
- **Code issues** – the provided code contains many similar/empty files and unrelated tutorials; comments are missing (R1).
- **R2 states the study is not ready for publication at ICLR**; R3 recommends the authors read at least 10 recent ICLR papers to understand standards for presentation and content.
- **Minority contrast:** Reviewer 1 (score 5, soundness/presentation/contribution all 2) is more positive than the other two reviewers (both score 1, all scores 1), but still identifies the same fundamental weaknesses regarding missing comparisons, limited novelty, and presentation flaws.
