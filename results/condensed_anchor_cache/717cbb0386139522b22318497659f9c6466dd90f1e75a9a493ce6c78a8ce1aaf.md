## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MolBlend, a multimodal molecular pretraining method that aligns 2D and 3D modalities at the atomic-relation level. It constructs a unified relation matrix blending 2D bond/edge and 3D distance information, then uses a Transformer to recover modality-specific representations. The approach is framed as a “blend-then-predict” self-supervised learning framework with theoretical connections to mutual-information maximization.

### Strengths
- Novel idea of using a unified relation matrix to blend 2D and 3D molecular information for representation learning (R1, R2, R4).
- Theoretical analysis provides insights into the method, framed within mutual-information maximization (R1, R2). One reviewer (score 8) found the theoretical proof particularly interesting, contrasting with the more critical assessments from other reviewers (scores 5, 5, 6).
- The paper is well-written, structured, and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- The work is the first to align 2D and 3D modalities at the atomic-relation level rather than at the coarse molecule level (R4).
- Extensive experiments on both 2D and 3D molecule property prediction tasks demonstrate the method’s performance (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and rationale**:
  * The claim that 2D and 3D representations are “intrinsically equivalent” is not well justified; prior work treats them as complementary, and the advantage of reframing them is unclear (R2).
  * The motivation for feeding both modalities as one unified data structure into a single model rather than using separate branches or models is not explained (R2).
  * The rationale for emphasizing atomic-relation level alignment over atom-level alignment (e.g., predicting 3D coordinates from a 2D graph) is not sufficiently reasoned; empirical comparison would help (R4).
- **Representation and relation matrix limitations**:
  * The relation matrix construction loses substantial information, especially from 3D structures; the method is similar to “One transformer can understand both 2d & 3d molecular data” (ICLR 2023) (R1).
  * Notations in Sec 3.1 are misleading: the relationship between relation matrices \(R_{spd}\), \(R_{edge}\), and \(R_{distance}\) is unclear; it is not specified whether they are redundant or provide unique information (R2). The masking process for \(R_{2D3D}\) is not clearly described (R2). Equation 1 has an incorrect subscript notation (R2).
  * The distance modeling is invariant; a more advanced equivariant model would be preferable to account for the geometric properties of 3D space (R2).
  * The choice of encodings for 2D/3D modalities and their influence on pretraining is not investigated (R3).
- **Missing ablations and comparisons**:
  * Ablation studies on blending two masks (e.g., combining only two of the three relation matrices) are missing; Table 4 only compares blending three masks vs using one mask (R1, R2).
  * Missing comparisons to relevant baselines in related work: Xu et al. (ICML 21), Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 21), Zaidi et al. (ICLR 23) (R3).
  * The conclusion in Section D.1 (on different 3D networks) is not well supported because only one 3D model was tested (R1).
  * No discussion of training time cost of different pretraining methods (R3).
  * Results tables should explicitly include a column indicating which backbone models are pretrained/comparing (R2).
  * The citation of 3D InfoGraph is wrong and needs correction (R2).
- **Experimental setup and results**:
  * It is unclear whether all baselines are run on the same splits or results are cited (R1). Table 4 does not directly show the effect of blending two masks (R1).
  * The experimental findings are underwhelming; in Tables 1 and 2, MolBlend’s performance does not fare well against prior studies (R4).
  * Different datasets and tasks are used for ablation without justification (R1).
- **Theoretical analysis**: While a strength, additional theoretical insights on why MolBlend outperforms existing methods would be beneficial (R3).
- **Clarity issues**:
  * Figure 1b is confusing: does it imply input can be either 2D or 3D and output can be both 2D and 3D? Clarification needed (R2).
  * The title could be improved to explicitly state the modalities considered (R2).
