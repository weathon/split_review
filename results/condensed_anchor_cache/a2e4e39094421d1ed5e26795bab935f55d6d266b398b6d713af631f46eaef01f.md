## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a web-based pipeline that uses GPT-4o Mini to convert counseling session transcripts (from the MEMO dataset, 212 transcripts total) into visual mind-map summaries using PlantUML syntax. Human evaluation on 20 randomly selected samples by three non-clinical evaluators (Information Technology researchers) assessed accuracy, completeness, conciseness, and coherence. The tool is seen as a practical adaptation of LLMs for visual summarization in mental health, though the work is described by two reviewers as an engineering demonstration lacking research depth.

### Strengths
- **Creative integration of LLMs with visual summarization**: The use of GPT-4o Mini combined with PlantUML to generate mind maps from qualitative counseling data is a novel and original contribution. This moves beyond traditional text-based summaries and addresses an underexplored area in the mental health domain. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Relevant problem and potential tool usefulness**: Reducing cognitive load for therapists via quick, visual overviews of session notes is a legitimate and practical goal. Mind maps are an effective, quicker way to communicate information. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Implementation of a web-based tool**: The authors developed an actual web-based summarization tool that people can use, which is commendable for practical impact. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Clear explanation**: The paper is straightforward, well-structured, and easy to follow, with useful visual examples of the mind maps. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Positive preliminary human evaluation**: The tool produced reasonably good mind maps according to the human evaluation performed. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Very small and non-representative sample for evaluation**: Only 20 transcripts are used from the MEMO dataset, which contains 212 transcripts. This small, unrepresentative sample limits generalizability to diverse real-world counseling scenarios. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)  A larger and more varied dataset is needed to capture a wider array of counseling situations. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient evaluation setup and lack of objectivity**: Human evaluations were conducted solely by three participants who are Information Technology researchers, not mental health professionals. The small evaluator pool (three) combined with lack of domain expertise compromises the quality and reliability of the assessment. Reviewers question why mental health professionals were not involved and why only three participants were used. (Reviewers 1, 4)  Additionally, the evaluation is subjective and lacks reproducibility; incorporating quantitative metrics such as adapted ROUGE or BLEU scores would strengthen objectivity. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **No comparison with alternative summarization formats or methods**: The paper does not compare mind maps against text-based summaries, concept maps, or other visual formats, yet claims that mind maps reduce cognitive load. Without this comparison, the claimed benefit remains untested. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  Specifically, it is unclear how visual models compare to textual models on information capture. (Reviewer 3)
- **No comparison of different LLMs**: Only GPT-4o Mini is used as the summarizer. There is no comparison with other LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, LLaMA) to show performance differences or justify the choice. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Limited novelty and contribution**: The paper does not propose a new problem, a new dataset, a novel method, or contain interesting analysis/observations. The prompting is straightforward (structured output parsed into a mind map), and the main contributions are the prompt and the web tool. Two reviewers consider it an engineering/application paper fit for a demo track, not a research main track. (Reviewers 2, 3)  Several ablations are missing (e.g., prompting variations, different output structures). (Reviewer 3)
- **No demonstration of cognitive load reduction**: While the paper aims to reduce cognitive load, no evidence or study is presented to show that mind maps actually achieve this compared to text summaries. (Reviewer 1)  Follow-up questions on this point remain unanswered. (Reviewer 3: “Did you ask therapists to review the mind map models and get feedback? What was the response like?”)
