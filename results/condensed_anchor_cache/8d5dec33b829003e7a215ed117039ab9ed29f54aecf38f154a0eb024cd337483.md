## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses training instability due to attention entropy collapse in softmax-based transformers and proposes Lipschitz-kernel-based attention as a remedy. The authors argue that the non-Lipschitz softmax leads to entropy collapse and gradient explosion, while Lipschitz attention maintains stable entropy.

### Strengths
- **Important and relevant problem**: All reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) recognize the research question of attention entropy collapse and training instability as significant for understanding and improving transformers.
- **Clear explanation**: The paper, especially Section 3, is generally well-organized and explains the problem of attention entropy collapse and its importance in an accessible manner (R1, R3, R4). One reviewer (R2) strongly disputes clarity (see Weaknesses).
- **Interesting and original approach**: The idea of attributing entropy collapse to the lack of Lipschitz property and proposing Lipschitz kernel attention is seen as interesting and original by R3 and R4.
- **Controlled experiments**: The paper provides controlled experiments that empirically demonstrate kernel-based attention does not suffer from attention entropy collapse (R3, R4).
- **Mathematical explanations**: The paper includes mathematical derivations to support its arguments, e.g., in Section 3.5 (R3). (However, multiple reviewers question the sufficiency and novelty of these derivations – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The core ideas (softmax entropy collapse, replacement with kernel/Lipschitz attention, mathematical analysis) have been extensively explored in prior works: Zhai et al. 2023 (entropy collapse), Hron et al. 2020, Bai et al. 2023, Fu et al. 2023, Wortsman et al. 2023a, Ramapuram et al. 2024, Jha et al. 2024 among others. The specific method in Eq. 5 is not new ([1,2]), and the mathematical derivations in Section 3.5 have been introduced elsewhere. The paper does not sufficiently differentiate its contributions from these existing works (R2, R3, R4). One reviewer (R2) finds the paper reads more like a report than a research paper due to the lack of novelty.
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis**: 
  - The main mathematical argument (line 419) uses an upper bound; an increase of that bound does not prove an increase of the original quantity, so the claim that “the norm of the attention probabilities increases” is unsupported (R2).
  - The paper provides no theoretical explanation for how lack of Lipschitzness leads to entropy collapse (R4 Q2). The overall theoretical grounding is insufficient to generalize beyond the controlled experiments (R4).
  - Definition 3.2 is mathematically nonsensical: the left-hand side is not a function of *j* but is indexed by *j* (R2).
- **Inadequate experimental setup and generalizability concerns**:
  - Details of data and model settings are missing. Section 3.1 does not provide enough information to reproduce results (R1, R4). The experiments in Figure 2 lack details on training data and sampling (R1).
  - The experiments use a simple transformer with SGD, an extremely high learning rate (5e-1), hidden dimension 3, and only 1 attention head (R3). These choices are far from typical transformer settings (e.g., Vision Transformers) and raise serious concerns about generalizability (R3, R4). The paper does not include experiments on standard or larger architectures to validate the claims (R3, R4).
  - The claim (line 062–063) that entropy collapse leads to gradient explosion and prevents convergence contradicts the fact that many softmax-based transformers (e.g., ViT) converge successfully (R4 Q3). The paper does not address this apparent contradiction.
- **Unclear connection between attention concentration and instability**: The paper does not convincingly explain why attention concentration on a few tokens should cause training instability, given that attention’s purpose is to focus on relevant tokens (R1).
- **Speculative and unsupported claims**:
  - The central claim that Lipschitz-kernel attention stabilizes training remains speculative. The authors only “expect” it to mitigate entropy collapse (line 227) without definitive proof (R2).
  - It is unclear whether the benefit stems solely from stable entropy or there are other factors (R1 Q4).
  - The paper does not investigate whether any Lipschitz activation on the real line could replace softmax (R4 Q4). The variance definition effectively reduces to a temperature parameter in softmax, already analyzed in Zhai et al. (R2).
- **Presentation and writing quality issues** (according to R2; R1 and R3 and R4 found the presentation clear in parts):
  - Numerous writing errors: missing verb (line 40), extra word “bridge” (line 51), repeated discussion of quadratic bottleneck (lines 128 and 133), missing articles (lines 55, 138, 142), incomplete sentence (line 112), unreadable lines (114–117, 151–154), inconsistent tenses, grammar error (line 205).
  - Figure 1.a should use a different scale for better visibility.
  - Definition 3.1 is redundant given the preceding paragraph.
  - Plots are not averaged over multiple runs.
  - Unclear reference: “Previous approaches” (line 50) not clarified (R3).
  - The explanation for quadratic-to-linear complexity reduction (line 148: O(ND²)=O(N)) needs justification (R3).
