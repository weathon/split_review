## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) framework to constrained/ bounded domains by combining reflected Brownian motion (via reflected FBSDEs) with Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF). The authors derive reflected forward-backward SDEs with Neumann and Robin boundary conditions, extend divergence-based likelihood training to bounded domains, and study approximate linear convergence of IPF. The method is evaluated on image datasets and compared to reflected diffusion models (Lou & Ermon, 2023).

### Strengths
* The extension of the Schrödinger Bridge framework to the reflected (constrained) setting is new and technically sound. The construction is cleanly presented, with full explication of strengths and limitations. [R1, R2, R3]
* The theoretical analysis of convergence (including speedups) is interesting and valuable for complicated geometric base spaces. [R1, R2]
* The approach provides a neural entropic optimal transport method for constrained domains, which is novel – other diffusion-based or simple mapping approaches (e.g., diffeomorphic mapping in [1]) are not capable of this. [R3]
* Generation performance on standard image benchmarks is good, and extensive experiments (including scaling to high dimensions) are provided as a comparison to [1]. [R2, R3]
* The connection to reflected FBSDEs and IPF is technically sound and well-motivated by the need for boundary enforcement in real-world generative modeling. [R1, R3]

### Weaknesses
* **Unclear motivation and benefit.** The paper does not adequately justify why one would extend SB to the reflected setting beyond being a straightforward combination of reflected SDEs [1] and SB/IPF [2,3]. The claimed advantages (e.g., reduced integration time via SB) are not convincingly demonstrated; many works on improving diffusion model speed (distillation, better samplers, etc.) are more efficient. It is not clear how the method improves over existing reflected diffusion models or what specific benefits (e.g., fewer diffusion steps, advantages of entropic OT on constrained spaces) it offers. [R2, R3]
* **Correctness of likelihood training / ELBO.** A critical concern: the claim that Proposition 1 gives a valid ELBO and that Algorithm 1 performs likelihood training is only true at equilibrium. The log density terms \(\log y_T\) and \(\log y_0\) in Algorithm 1 depend on the backward diffusion simulation and therefore on the network parameters; they do not coincide with the log densities of the target measures unless IPF has converged. Ignoring gradient-through-diffusion for these terms is unjustified unless convergence is assumed (in which case further IPF iterations are unnecessary). This undermines the theoretical justification of the training procedure. The same authors of [3] have shown that IPF coincides with regular IPF and does not require this (incorrect) likelihood derivation. [R2, R3] 
* **Strong assumption A4 and insufficient theoretical novelty.** Assumption A4 is very strong; the paper claims similarity to [4] but that assumption only holds for one IPF iteration. The stability of the approximation across multiple IPF iterations is not discussed and is unclear. Moreover, it is not clear what the differences are between the theoretical section and the prior work of [3] (Provably Convergent Schrödinger Bridge) – comparison or discussion is missing. [R2]
* **Empirical evidence is not convincing enough.** The generated image quality appears worse than the baseline [1] (Reflected Diffusion Models). If the claim is faster generation, a comparison of Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) is needed. More importantly, a challenging example such as a high-dimensional simplex would be required to demonstrate that the method retains its advantages over [1] in general geometries. [R1, R2]
* **Missing comparisons and citations.** There are other constrained-domain diffusion methods (e.g., Fishman et al. 2023) and SB/OT on manifolds (Thornton et al. 2022) that are not adequately compared or discussed. In the introduction, [5] should be cited alongside [6] as concurrent work. Section 1 incorrectly states that Shi et al. 2023 uses IPF (it uses IMF), and the claim about Vargas et al. 2021 performing SB on high-dimensional examples is incorrect (max dimension 4). [R2, R3]
* **Algorithmic and presentation clarity.** The algorithmic differences between the introduced method and the reflected FBSDE approach of [7] should be specified. In equation (3), the index \(t\) in \(x_t\) is missing. The reflected FBSDE framework would benefit from more emphasis on the introduced quantities. It would also be interesting to understand whether the dynamic thresholding procedure used in [8] can be accommodated. [R2]

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* While Reviewer 1 found the technical construction clean and the theoretical speedup promising, Reviewers 2 and 3 were less convinced about the real added value and raised serious concerns about theoretical correctness and experimental substantiation. The weaker empirical results and missing comparisons are noted by all reviewers to varying degrees.
