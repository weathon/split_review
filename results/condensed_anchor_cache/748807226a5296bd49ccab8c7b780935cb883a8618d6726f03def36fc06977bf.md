## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a framework (SDU) for private inference from latent diffusion models. It introduces polynomial approximations for nonlinear operations and a score distillation method to reduce the number of denoising steps performed in the encrypted domain, thereby lowering latency. The evaluations are conducted only on Stable Diffusion v1.5 without comparisons to prior privacy-preserving diffusion work. Reviewers diverge strongly: one (score 1, confidence 4) finds the approximations poorly analyzed, the experiments insufficient, and the scenario unclear; one (score 5, confidence 2) sees the problem as important and the approximations as potentially useful but cannot follow the presentation; a third (score 3, confidence 3) questions novelty and experimental validity. The positive assessment from the second reviewer comes with low confidence.

### Strengths

- The problem of reducing computational overhead in private diffusion model inference is well-motivated and practically relevant.
- The idea of approximating the conditional distribution \(p(z|y)\) using randomly sampled \(p(z)\) to avoid expensive encrypted computation on the condition is a clever conceptual contribution.
- The paper introduces new approximations for non-linear functions (e.g., scaled tanh for GeLU) and a score distillation method to reduce the number of denoising steps; these may be useful if properly validated.
- Some empirical results on Stable Diffusion v1.5 are provided, showing that the method can produce images competitive with the original while using fewer steps.

### Weaknesses

- **Extremely limited experimental evaluation:** Only one model (SDv1.5) and one benchmark dataset are used, with no comparison to any prior privacy-preserving diffusion method (e.g., HE-Diffusion (Chen & Yan 2024)) or even to a standard non-private diffusion baseline. Without baselines, the reported FID, CLIP-Score, and latency numbers are uninterpretable.
- **Lack of approximation-function analysis:** The paper claims improved approximations for non-linear functions (reciprocal, LayerNorm, GroupNorm, Softmax, GeLU) but provides no accuracy analysis, no comparison to recent approximation methods (e.g., Qu & Xu 2023; Luo et al. 2024; Lu et al. 2023), and no discussion of error propagation or impact on diffusion process stability/convergence.
- **Unsubstantiated claims and missing details on approximations:**  
  - The cubic approximation using tanh for GeLU is standard (Hendrycks & Gimpel 2016, “Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELUs)”) and should be cited; the paper incorrectly states that GeLU includes a sigmoid.  
  - Scaling the approximation range by \(\sqrt{d}\) increases error proportionally by factor \(d\) – a risk that is not analyzed.  
  - The Taylor degree for the erf approximation is not specified; the number of iterations for exp (claimed 20 in Appendix B) is inconsistent with achieving stable approximation in \([-10,10]\) per Figure 2b.  
  - For CrypTen, the specific approximation methods and computation counts used for each non-linear function are not described.
- **Presentation is confusing and definitions are incomplete:**  
  - In the preliminaries, \(\epsilon_\theta\) is defined circularly, and \(\epsilon\) (the random noise) is never defined. The score function appears to be vector-valued but this is unclear.  
  - In Algorithm 1, lines 9 and 10 depend on \(\hat{\epsilon}_\theta\), which is not defined except via equations (7) and (8) that reference themselves.  
  - The overall scenario is difficult to follow; the contributions of the approximation improvements vs. score distillation are not clearly separated.
- **Unanswered methodological questions:**  
  - Why pre-train the encrypted model on public data to match the original model’s error, instead of tuning it first and then using Score Distillation only if needed?  
  - What is hyperparameter \(m\) in Algorithm 1, and how are all hyperparameters configured to trade off latency and performance?  
  - How robust is the framework across different types of diffusion models (e.g., different backbones, unconditional vs. conditional)?  
  - An analysis of the trade-off between privacy and performance is missing, yet crucial for practical adoption.
- **Performance should be measured in an actual MPC environment, not in plaintext**, to demonstrate real latency and communication benefits.
