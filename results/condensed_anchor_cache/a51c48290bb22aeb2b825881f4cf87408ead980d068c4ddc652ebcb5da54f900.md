## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies multi-label classification with a budget constraint (exactly $k$ labels per instance) under macro-at-$k$ metrics, which linearly decompose into label-wise confusion matrices. The authors establish the form of the Bayes optimal classifier (Theorem 4.1 with constants $a_j, b_j$) and derive a Frank-Wolfe algorithm to approximate it, building on prior work in non-decomposable losses. Experiments on four benchmark datasets (with MLP and sparse linear base classifiers) compare macro-at-k against top-k heuristics, showing gains in precision, recall, and F1 at different $k$ levels, though the largest dataset favors simpler baselines. The theoretical contributions are considered solid and the problem setting is novel, but the empirical evaluation is limited in scope and several important baselines and analyses are missing.

### Strengths

- **Theoretical soundness**: The paper provides rigorous derivation of the Bayes optimal classifier and consistency guarantees for macro-at-$k$ metrics, with key lemmas included in the main text (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Clear problem formulation**: The formulation in terms of confusion matrices/tensors is simple and well-presented, handling notation-heavy material carefully (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical algorithm**: A Frank-Wolfe based algorithm is derived as a practical solution for optimizing the proposed metrics (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments on four datasets (using MLP and sparse linear classifiers) show that the proposed approach often improves precision, recall, and F1 compared to straightforward top-k heuristics (Reviewers 1,3). Consistency between Macro-R_prior and Macro-R_FW is confirmed for the recall metric (Reviewer 4).
- **Novelty of setting**: The budgeted macro-at-k setting in multi-label classification is new and offers new knowledge for extreme multi-label problems (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons and baselines**:
  - Several methods known to perform well on tail labels are not discussed or compared, e.g., Menon et al. (ICLR 2020), Zhang et al. (EACL 2023) (Reviewer 2, Q2).
  - The paper is highly related to Schultheis et al. (NeurIPS 2023) – the difference should be discussed and empirical comparison provided (Reviewer 2, Q4).
  - A comparison to binary relevance learning (treating each label independently) is missing; such a baseline would demonstrate empirically that macro-at-k metrics cannot be sufficiently tackled by ignoring the budget constraint (Reviewer 3). – Similarly, comparison to a simple top-k baseline already present, but the independence failure case should be highlighted.
  - The proposed method appears to greatly sacrifice instance-wise metrics for macro-average gains (Table 2). The authors should investigate whether an interpolated objective could flexibly control this trade-off (Reviewer 2, Q1).
  - Balanced accuracy experiments (where the closed-form solution is easy to compute) are missing (Reviewer 4, question).

- **Insufficient connection to known results**:
  - The constants $a_j, b_j$ in Theorem 4.1 mirror those in weighted binary classification (Lemma 2 of JMLR 18-226); this connection and the relation to existing multi-label results are not surfaced enough (Reviewer 4). Theorem 4.4 could be linked to known special cases (Reviewer 4).
  - The general closed-form solution depends on optimal values, which by itself is not novel (Reviewer 4).

- **Lack of intuitive explanation**: The connection between the Frank-Wolfe algorithm and the optimization of macro-at-k metrics lacks intuitive explanation, e.g., how the linear approximation relates to the metric structure (Reviewer 3). The theoretical results would benefit from more intuition to ease understanding (Reviewers 3,4).

- **Limited empirical scope**: Only four datasets are considered; though the main focus is theoretical, a broader evaluation or at least a synthetic toy dataset with known distribution to verify consistency would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 2, Q3; Reviewer 3). – One reviewer (Reviewer 3) notes that the empirical study is limited but acceptable given the theoretical focus.

- **Missing analysis of computational complexity**: The paper does not discuss computational time or how it scales with budget $k$ and other parameters, nor compare costs across heuristics (Reviewer 1). – Practical rule-of-thumb guidance for selecting heuristics is also absent (Reviewer 1, Q2).

- **Writing clarity diverges**: One reviewer finds the paper not easy to follow (Reviewer 2), while another praises its presentation and language as very good (Reviewer 3). The minority reviewer identifies clarity issues that should be addressed.

- **Minor typos**: Page 4 “define in Table 1”; page 7 “tensor measure measure” (Reviewer 3).

- **Unclear novelty in proofs**: The challenges or novelty in the proofs for Theorem 4.4 over and above known work are not highlighted (Reviewer 4).
