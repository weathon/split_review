## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Neuronal Embeddings via MultimOdal Contrastive Learning (NEMO), a CLIP-based contrastive learning framework that jointly embeds extracellular action potential (EAP) waveforms and autocorrelograms (ACGs) of spiking activity. NEMO is evaluated on two downstream tasks: cell-type classification (PV, SST, VIP inhibitory interneurons) using opto-tagged Neuropixels Ultra recordings from mouse visual cortex, and brain-region classification (10 broad areas) using the public International Brain Laboratory (IBL) brain-wide map dataset. The authors demonstrate that NEMO, with minimal fine-tuning, outperforms state-of-the-art multimodal methods including PhysMAP and VAE-based approaches, as well as a fully supervised method, in terms of balanced accuracy and macro-averaged F1. The work is among the earliest efforts to apply contrastive learning for joint modeling of EAP and spiking-activity data, though theoretical innovation is limited. NEMO is particularly valued for its ability to differentiate VIP from SST cells and for its accessible electrophysiological modalities.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, with clear organization, well-made figures, and a thorough review of relevant literature; almost no typos.
- Supplementary material is extensive and provides many additional details.
- The training and experimental setup are carefully chosen and sound; limitations are discussed.
- Novel application of CLIP-based contrastive learning to simultaneously leverage two electrophysiological modalities (waveforms and ACGs) for cell-type and brain-region classification.
- NEMO achieves state-of-the-art results: on opto-tagged visual cortex data it outperforms PhysMAP, VAE, and a fully supervised method; on IBL brain-wide data it also shows improvement (e.g., 0.05 higher balanced accuracy than VAE in Figure 3e).
- The method relies on accessible electrophysiological recordings and requires only minimal fine-tuning for downstream tasks.
- NEMO successfully distinguishes between VIP and SST cells, a challenging and valuable capability in systems neuroscience.
- This is the second work to use contrastive learning for cell-type classification and the first to combine two modalities; the main components (CLIP, ACG encoder) come from prior work, enhancing reproducibility.
- The multi-modal approach outperforms single-modal models, consistent with previous studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical contribution**: The study largely adapts existing frameworks (CLIP contrastive learning and the ACG encoder) without introducing components specifically designed for the unique characteristics of electrophysiological data. Readers may expect more data- and problem-specific innovations. *(R2)*
- **Lack of statistical significance testing**: In brain-region classification, NEMO’s improvement over VAE is minimal (~0.05 balanced accuracy in Figure 3e). Similarly, joint training shows little benefit over independent training (Figure 5b difference ~0.01; Supplementary Table 9 shows independent NEMO sometimes better: 0.84 vs 0.83, 0.83 vs 0.84, 0.87 vs 0.88). The paper does not provide statistical tests to determine if these differences are meaningful, making it difficult to assess the practical significance of reported gains. *(R4)*
- **Missing core-assumption justification**: The authors assume a robust pairwise correlation between spike waveform and firing patterns (analogous to CLIP’s image-text relationship), but this assumption lacks strong neuroscience evidence. For example, layer 5 pyramidal neurons and cortical PV neurons both fire many spikes but have broad and narrow waveforms respectively; burst firing can be evoked in both excitatory and SST neurons. The paper does not address this fundamental limitation. *(R4)*
- **Incomplete acknowledgment of prior contrastive-learning work for cell types**: CEED (Vishnubhotla et al., 2023) used SimCLR for unsupervised cell-type classification and benchmarked against WaveMap. The current citation (“Contrastive learning has been applied to raw electrophysiological recordings (Vishnubhotla et al., 2024)”) is insufficient; the differences, similarities, and specific contributions of NEMO beyond CEED should be explicitly discussed. *(R4)*
- **Missing data augmentation ablations and validation**: The choice of additive Gaussian noise as the sole augmentation is presented without rationale or ablation. Reviewers request: an ablation experiment measuring the importance of augmentations; justification for why Gaussian noise was chosen over other types; validation of augmentations on ACG images with examples; and exploration of the impact of other augmentation types (e.g., amplitude jitter, electrode dropout, or augmentations directly on ACG images). *(R1, R2, R3)*
- **Insufficient architectural and training details for baselines/clustering analyses**: The descriptions of control models are too short. Specifics needed: VAE encoder/decoder architectures, loss functions, optimization parameters; explanation of 10D and 500D latent spaces in VAE baselines; clustering parameters for UMAP and subsequent algorithm; method for determining number of clusters; stability of clustering across random seeds (e.g., adjusted Rand index). *(R1, R3)*
- **Missing comparison of representational power**: A critical comparison is missing: NEMO without fine-tuning versus VAE without fine-tuning. This would highlight the representational quality of the learned embeddings independent of downstream supervision. *(R2)*
- **Lack of rationale for input neuron count and effect of varying it**: The authors restrict input to five neurons per forward pass without explanation. An analysis of how performance changes with different numbers of input neurons would clarify the design choice. *(R1)*
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis missing**: Hyperparameters are fixed across all methods and both datasets (due to limited labels for cell-type classification). A sensitivity analysis showing whether separate hyperparameter optimization would change comparative results is needed. *(R1)*
- **Computational efficiency and in vivo applicability unaddressed**: The paper emphasizes “in vivo,” but does not report computational efficiency or whether NEMO supports real-time processing. *(R2)*
- **Minor technical issues**: Citation errors (e.g., IBL et al., Radford et al. 2021 missing comma, Chen et al. (2020) missing preposition); Table 6 referenced but nonexistent (likely Table 1); Figure 3 legend has typo “Supervise” instead of “Supervised”; Supplementary Figure 7 blurred; wording “multi-unit” should be “multi-neuron” (Figure 3b,c titles); Figure 5b caption “then” should be “than”; Section 7 missing preposition “in”. *(R1, R3)*
- **Figure labels and references**: Figure 1a text says “Neuropixels 1.0” but should say “Neuropixels Ultra” for the VIP/SST/PV opto-tagged dataset (IBL uses Neuropixels 1.0). Figure 3b and 3c are not referenced in the text. Consider changing “unit” to “neuron” for consistency. *(R4)*
- **Metrics definition**: Clarify how macro-averaged F1 and balanced accuracy are computed (e.g., unweighted mean of per-cell-type F1; unweighted mean of sensitivity and specificity). *(R4)*
- **Additional experiments requested** (without requiring new experiments): (1) Definition of “channel” in the waveform template (max-amplitude channel vs. multi-channel results in Supplement E). (2) Application to PhysMAP’s public datasets (S1, A1, V1/Hippocampus) if already available. (3) Clustering on latent embeddings directly (without UMAP) and modularity index definition. *(R1, R4, R3)*
