## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents DiffDock-Pocket, a diffusion-based docking algorithm that, given a known binding pocket, predicts the conformation of a ligand and the sidechains within that pocket. It extends DiffDock by conditioning on the pocket and modeling sidechain flexibility. The model claims state-of-the-art performance on the PDBBind benchmark, with especially strong results on apo (in-silico generated) structures, where it reportedly achieves more than twice the performance of current methods while being more than 20 times faster than other flexible approaches. Reviewers note the method builds on DiffDock, provides an anonymous repository, and includes extensive ablation studies, but express mixed views on novelty and evaluation thoroughness (scores: 3, 6, 5, 6).

### Strengths

- **Novel handling of sidechain flexibility in diffusion docking**: One reviewer (R2) specifically highlights that the way of considering the all-atom structure of sidechains pertaining to a target pocket in the diffusion process is novel. (R2: “novel way of considering the all atom structure of the side chains”)
- **Well-written and clearly presented**: Multiple reviewers (R2, R3, R4) praise the paper’s clarity, logical structure, and comprehensive appendix.
- **Extensive experimental evaluation and ablation**: R4 notes the detailed ablation study in the appendix and transparency about a potential steric-clash bottleneck (Table 5). R3 appreciates the anonymous repository with replication resources.
- **Strong baseline choice and significant improvements**: R4 commends the choice of robust baselines and notes the significant improvement, especially on apo structures. R2 finds the results convincing, and R3 states the method results in superior performance in site-specific docking compared to existing methods.
- **Transparency on limitations**: R4 appreciates the authors’ honesty regarding steric clashes compared to energy-based methods (GNINA) in Table 5.

### Weaknesses

- **Incremental contribution and unclear novelty**: R3 and R4 consider the technical contribution incremental, primarily extending DiffDock with sidechain prediction. R3 notes the claim that previous models only consider rigid receptors is misleading – prior residue-level methods do not need sidechain conformations because they do not use atomic details, and neither the prior models nor DiffDock-Pocket account for backbone flexibility. R4 remarks that sidechain flexibility has already been incorporated in other (non-public) diffusion tools like NeuralPlex. In contrast, R2 views the all-atom sidechain modeling as novel, but the discrepancy in perception is noted.
- **Differences from DiffDock insufficiently explained**: R2 states that details of how sidechain conformations are incorporated into the diffusion process and how this differs from DiffDock are unclear; the introduction of the δ term is not sufficiently explained. R4 asks whether architectural changes beyond pocket restriction account for the improvements, and whether the confidence model judgment differs from DiffDock’s.
- **Reliance on known pocket limits applicability**: R2 points out that the model assumes prior knowledge of the pocket, which restricts use for allosteric sites or poorly characterized pockets. R3 notes that evaluation is only on site-specific docking with ground-truth pockets; evaluation with predicted pockets would enhance practical applicability.
- **Incomplete comparison baselines**: R1 criticizes that only computational methods (SMINA/GNINA) are compared, while other deep learning methods are missed. R4 adds that GNINA and SMINA perform poorly on holo structures in Table 2, yet sidechains in holo are already correct, raising questions about baseline implementation. R1 also asks about VINA’s performance (generally considered best but worse in Table 3).
- **Lack of per-protein analysis and visualizations**: R1 requests 2-3 examples comparing the 3D atom-level structure of the pocket with the PDB structure, rather than only overall performance. R2 asks for an analysis of performance across protein classes (e.g., membrane proteins) and on proteins with large conformational changes between apo and holo; R2 also asks what proportion of conformational changes come from sidechain vs. backbone and how the model would perform if backbone changes dominate.
- **Risk of data leakage in pocket curation**: R3 warns that the method for selecting the pocket center and sidechains (within 3.5Å of any ligand) relies on the ground-truth ligand position, which could cause data leakage. The paper mentions users will manually set parameters during inference, but further clarification is needed. R2 raises a related concern about information lost by discarding amino acids far from the binding site (e.g., for membrane receptors where distant conformational changes matter).
- **Potential training bias on in-silico structures**: R3 questions whether the large improvement with ESMFold2 structures may be due to additional training on that dataset; an ablation without in-silico structures is needed. R1 also notes that VINA’s poor performance in Table 3 (compared to the common belief that VINA is strong) might be related to the pocket‑ligand setup.
- **Sidechain conformer matching details insufficient and impact unclear**: R2 points out that the conformer matching step is not explained well; it is unclear how inaccuracies in computationally generated structures affect final docking results (R2 asks about bond-length heterogeneity). R4 asks whether conformer matching is applied only during training or also in inference (Tables 1–2).
- **Evaluation only with RMSD and single thresholds**: R1 criticizes the exclusive use of RMSD; other metrics such as TM-score should be included. R1 also asks why Table 1 uses a 2Å success criterion and Table 2 uses 1Å, and requests success rates at multiple thresholds (1/1.5/2Å).
- **Minor inaccuracies in scientific claims**: R1 notes that the introduction’s statement “When a small molecule binds, the 3D structure and function change” is not universally true (e.g., antibodies, intrinsically disordered proteins, structural proteins like 1POK), and that abstract states function changes without clear justification. R1 also queries how to quantitate “new” and “different” 3D structures.
- **Missing ablation on trained DiffDock without sidechain**: R3 questions whether experiments training DiffDock with a given pocket but without sidechain flexibility were conducted; such an ablation would better highlight the benefit of sidechain modeling.
- **Need for more comprehensive speed comparison**: R3 notes the paper claims traditional methods become slower with sidechain flexibility, but a direct performance and speed comparison of traditional models with and without sidechain flexibility is missing.
