## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the robustness of multimodal language model agent systems to adversarial attacks that force agents to take arbitrary actions. The authors introduce the Agent Robustness Evaluation (ARE) framework, which models an agent as a directed graph where nodes are system components and edges represent data flow; edge weights measure how likely an attack is to succeed after that component. They create VWA-Adv, a dataset of 200 manually crafted adversarial tasks and evaluation functions in a realistic threat model built on top of VisualWebArena. With imperceptible perturbations to a single product image (less than 5% of total web page pixels), they break agents using black-box frontier LLMs (GPT-4) that incorporate reflection and tree-search, achieving attack success rates up to 67%. They find that adding components that improve benign performance (e.g., an evaluator in reflexion, a value function in tree search) can open new vulnerabilities, increasing attack success relatively by 15% and 20%. The paper examines text-only and image-based attacks against several models and evaluates several simple defenses, finding limited gains.

### Strengths
- The evaluation looks at the entire deployed system, not just the LLM component, which is critical because individual components can amplify or reduce robustness.
- The ARE graph formalization (nodes = components, edges = information flow, edge weights = adversarial influence) allows systematic decomposition of attack success and helps understand where vulnerabilities arise. One reviewer (R1) found this formalization useful; another (R2) questioned whether the framework is clearly motivated as representative of real scenarios.
- The curation of 200 adversarial tasks and evaluation functions in VWA-Adv is a significant contribution that can accelerate security research in this area. The design choices and threat modeling are considered sound by R1 and R4.
- The paper provides valuable insights into how new components affect robustness (e.g., improving benign performance but increasing vulnerability).
- It is well-written, easy to follow, and includes comprehensive evaluations (R3).
- The baseline defenses (prompting and consistency checks) show limited gains, highlighting the need for more research on robust defenses (R2).

### Weaknesses
- Missing discussion of (indirect) prompt injection attacks and how “text access” attacks differ from them (R1).
- Figure captions are too short; e.g., Figure 3’s caption does not explain subfigures or what an arrow with “1.0” in the bottom right means (R1).
- Evaluation tasks are not self-contained; details should be included in the appendix (e.g., how evaluation primitives from Koh et al. 2024a are used) (R1).
- The narrative that “component x improves security if uncompromised but decreases it if compromised” gives a false sense of security; when evaluating security one should consider the worst case, and the insights should be rephrased accordingly (R1).
- The framework’s representativeness of real scenarios and real usage of integrated LLMs is not clearly motivated or justified (R2, R4). This includes a lack of discussion on how ARE might be adapted to other environments beyond web-based scenarios (R4).
- The threat model is limited to an attacker who is a legitimate user with limited capabilities. One reviewer (R1) finds this realistic, but another (R2) argues it may not encompass the full range of threats (e.g., attackers with more sophisticated capabilities or internal access).
- Defenses explored are basic (the authors themselves call them “simple”) and show limited effectiveness. More sophisticated and robust defenses are needed, and it would be useful to investigate performance losses caused by different defenses and to evaluate a state-of-the-art defense setup on ARE (R2, R4).
- The attacks are well-engineered versions of existing attacks adapted to the agent setting. This means the true risk could be higher as more advanced attack strategies emerge (R2).
- The contribution is primarily the construction of the evaluation framework; more discussion on the technical challenges would strengthen the paper (R3).
- Spelling errors: e.g., “We” instead of “Web” in the abstract (R4).
- Limited to text and image modalities; extension to video and sound would be valuable future work (R4).
- Only tested on VisualWebArena; further studies in other settings, including multi-agent interactions and agents using function calling, would improve generalizability (R4).
