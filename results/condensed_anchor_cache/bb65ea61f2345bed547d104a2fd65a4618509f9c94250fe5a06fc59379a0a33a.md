## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides theoretical analysis of transferable representation learning in CLIP and proposes a novel regularization technique that improves zero-shot performance. The theory seems sound, and the empirical results on CC3M demonstrate effectiveness. The paper is well-written and contributes to understanding CLIP’s principles.

### Strengths
- Well-written, easy to follow.
- Provides theoretical bounds/analysis on CLIP training and zero-shot transferability, with mathematical proofs that appear sound.
- Proposes a simple yet effective regularization technique that improves zero-shot performance.
- Demonstrates originality and innovation in uncovering the principles behind CLIP’s effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset scale concern (multiple reviewers):** Experiments are only on CC3M (smaller than CLIP’s full 400M dataset). It is unclear whether the regularization holds or scales to larger, more diverse datasets; the regularization might over-constrain capacity when trained on larger data. The authors should explore dataset size scaling.
- **Ablation missing (Reviewer 2):** The paper proposes only regularizing positive pairs, yet there is no ablation comparing this to variants that regularize both or negative pairs only. Also, can the regularization work in CyCLIP?
- **Comparison to existing methods missing (Reviewer 2):** Contrastive learning has widely studied NCE variants and temperature behavior (e.g., [a],[b],[c]); it is not surprising that adding regularization on positive pairs improves margins. How does the proposed solution compare to those methods?
- **Harmful effect on DTD (Reviewer 1):** In Table 1, regularization hurts performance on DTD. The paper lacks an analysis of why it negatively impacts DTD and what specific characteristics of that dataset cause the sensitivity.
- **CyCLIP inconsistency (Reviewer 1):** The reported CyCLIP results in Tables 1 and 2 are inconsistent with the original CyCLIP paper. The authors should clarify the experimental setup and justify differences.
- **Theoretical assumptions and clarity (Reviewers 2, 3):** Derivations rely on several assumptions. Some notations (e.g., “one-to-one mapping”, “one-to-one matching”) are unclear and would benefit from formal definitions. The example of “blue sky”/“white cloud” as unique features may be a special case; statistics on the frequency of such missing elements and a precise definition of “unique features” are needed.
- **Limited downstream tasks (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):** Evaluations are only on zero-shot and linear probing. The paper should include other tasks (e.g., retrieval, image captioning, VQA, object detection) to assess broader applicability. Is the method equally effective for retrieval?
- **Limited scope of method (Reviewer 4):** The paper only analyzes CLIP; it does not explore applicability to other contrastive learning approaches. Can one simply replace the CLIP component with CLIP+Reg in all CLIP-related work to get better performance?
- **Minority reviewer positivity (Reviewer 1, score 8):** One reviewer was notably more positive, finding the theory sound, the regularization easy, and the paper well-written, while others (score 6) raised more concerns about experiments, comparisons, and assumptions.
