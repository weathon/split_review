## Merged Review

### Summary
LQ-LoRA proposes an iterative algorithm to decompose each pretrained weight matrix into a quantized fixed component (NF4 or sub-3-bit) and a high-precision low-rank component, which together approximate the original matrix. The low-rank factors are used as initialization for fine-tuning while the quantized component stays frozen. Three contributions relative to QLoRA are: (1) an iterative initialization minimizing Frobenius reconstruction error; (2) an integer linear programming formulation for dynamic assignment of bit-width/block size per matrix under a total memory budget; (3) a data-aware version weighting parameters by Fisher information. Experiments on RoBERTa and LLaMA-2 (7B, 70B) show improvements over QLoRA, GPTQ-LoRA, and LREC baselines, especially in aggressive sub-4-bit regimes. LQ-LoRA also enables compression (e.g., 2.75-bit LLaMA-2-70B with 27GB memory).

### Strengths
- The iterative initialization of the low-rank component (rather than zero) in the decomposition Q+L₁L₂ directly addresses the suboptimal starting point of QLoRA, and this is shown to be beneficial under aggressive quantization (<4 bits). (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- The approach is simple and can be combined with any quantization scheme (e.g., NF, RTN, GPTQ). (Reviewer 1)
- The ILP-based dynamic bit allocation across layers and the data-aware Fisher-weighted decomposition are interesting contributions that provide flexible control over memory budgets. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- Extensive evaluation across multiple tasks (C4 language modeling, MMLU, OpenAssistant instruction tuning, GLUE) and model families (RoBERTa, LLaMA-2 7B/70B) convincingly demonstrates where LQ-LoRA gains. (All reviewers)
- The paper is the first to explicitly treat the case that Q + L₁L₂ may not equal the original W and to optimize the decomposition accordingly. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Hardware deployability**: The ILP outputs bit-widths as low as 2–3 bits, and the method inherently mixes precisions (Q is low-precision, L₁/L₂ are 16-bit). Such low precisions are not natively supported on current GPUs (e.g., 2-bit arithmetic). The simulations rely on PyTorch flexibility; this deployment bottleneck should be acknowledged. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing comparisons with block-wise PTQ methods**: LQ-LoRA should be compared to combinations of LoRA (with zero low-rank initialization) using stronger quantization techniques such as BRECQ, OPTQ, FlexRound, or GPTQ. These methods minimize X(q(W)) ≈ XW, potentially making the data-aware decomposition less necessary. The current comparison with GPTQ-LoRA is insufficient; LREC at 4-bit already matches LQ-LoRA on MMLU, weakening the motivation. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Ablation on ILP importance**: It is unclear how much of the gain comes from the ILP bit allocation vs. the iterative initialization. Show performance of LQ-LoRA *without* the ILP (e.g., uniform NF4). (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing baselines in Table 2**: Include regular LoRA (no quantization) and pure quantization without LoRA to contextualize improvements. (Reviewer 2)
- **Fixed rank selection**: The low-rank component is fixed at rank 64 for most experiments. Could the ILP also choose the rank per matrix? (Reviewer 2)
- **Inference overhead**: Because L₁L₂ cannot be merged into the quantized Q (which is in low precision), LQ-LoRA inherits QLoRA’s inference overhead—it cannot be fused into a single linear layer. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational overhead of iterative algorithm**: The alternating minimization incurs additional latency during initialization; this cost is not reported or discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Theoretical grounding**: The algorithm is empirically motivated without error bounds. The related work by Saha et al. (2023) on joint low-rank and low-precision factorization derives Frobenius-norm bounds and could be used to provide principled guarantees. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Clarity on figures**: Figure 1’s y-axis (absolute error) should be normalized by ||W||_F; the “less quantization error” claim in the caption is unfair because the right panel explicitly uses a low-rank residual while the center does not. A table version of Figure 3 showing the exact memory breakdown (quantized vs. low-rank) would aid interpretation. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Minor clarifying questions** (should be addressed in revised text):
  - The claim “LoRA obviates the need to allocate memory for storing gradients and optimizer states” should be rephrased as “LoRA does not require storing gradients for all parameters, only for the low-rank adapters.” (Reviewer 1)
  - Why choose NormalFloat over simpler RTN? RTN avoids Gaussian assumptions on weights. (Reviewer 1)
  - Where does δ = ½(1/30 + 1/32) come from? Does it change for flexible quantization resolutions? (Reviewer 1)
  - What is meant by “lossless” NF quantization on page 3? (Reviewer 1)
  - In Algorithm 1, does B denote quantization budget or number of blocks? (Reviewer 1)
  - Page 6: clarify “weight matrix F has homogeneous rows or columns.” (Reviewer 1)
  - Is Table 4 from the data-aware or data-agnostic variant? And the C4 perplexity 7.93 (rank 64) does not appear in Table 2. (Reviewer 1)
  - Does the ILP budget (and the “bits per param” column) include the low-rank components? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is the only difference between QLoRA+ILP and LQ-LoRA the initialization? (Reviewer 2)

- **Minority critical view on novelty and motivation** (to be explicitly kept): While Reviewers 1–2 rate the paper highly (scores 8), Reviewers 3–4 are more reserved (scores 6, 5). They note that LQ-LoRA is essentially a refined version of QLoRA with the same core limitation (inference overhead) and that stronger quantization methods already exist. Reviewer 4 finds the contribution and soundness only “fair” and argues that the experimental motivation is weakened by the close performance of LREC 4-bit.
