## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper empirically investigates camera bias in person re-identification (ReID) models. The authors measure camera bias across unseen domains and show it amplifies under data distribution shifts. They revisit feature normalization (test-time batch-wise mean/variance per camera) as a debiasing method, analyze why it is effective (addressing low-level image properties and body angle), and validate it across backbones and training methods. Additionally, they explore camera bias in unsupervised ReID, observe negative effects of camera-biased pseudo labels, and propose simple training strategies to mitigate bias, achieving significant improvements with minor modifications.

### Strengths

- Thorough and comprehensive investigation of camera bias in ReID, with quantitative measurement and analysis across unseen domains, diverse learning methods, and model architectures.
- Well-written paper with clear motivation, rich insights, and visual aids (Reviewer 3: “lots of interesting insights”).
- The proposed feature normalization is simple yet effective at test time, with sufficient experimental and theoretical analysis explaining why it works; applicable to detailed bias factors such as low-level image attributes and human viewpoint bias (Sun and Zheng, 2019).
- Important new observation: existing camera-aware methods reduce bias, but unsupervised/self-supervised models remain highly biased even on seen domains, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- The analysis of camera-biased pseudo labels in unsupervised learning and the suggested simple training strategies (discarding bias clustering, parameter adjustments) are valuable contributions that show performance gains with minor modifications to existing algorithms.
- One reviewer (majority opinion) praises the paper for being sound, insightful, and for championing a research style that does not require brand‑new methods if the analysis is solid.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and comparison concerns (minority reviewer, rating 6):**  
  The feature normalization technique is similar to normalization methods already explored in domain generalization (DG) and domain adaptation (DA) (e.g., Domain-specific batch normalization, CVPR 2019; Batch normalization embeddings for deep domain generalization, Pattern Recognition 2023). The authors should highlight the advantages of their approach over existing DG methods and include additional DG/DA baselines in experiments, especially in the cross‑dataset setting (training on MSMT, testing on other datasets) where direct comparison with supervised/unsupervised methods is considered inappropriate by this reviewer.

- **Limited improvement in supervised ReID:**  
  In Table 3, the improvement from the proposed method in the supervised setting (highlighted in red) is marginal; more analysis is needed to explain why.

- **Unsupervised setting and camera‑aware baselines:**  
  Since ICE‑CAM and PPLR‑CAM already incorporate camera information, the gains in the unsupervised setting (gray) may stem from that information. It is unclear whether the proposed method can further enhance ICE‑CAM, and such an experiment is missing.

- **Missing analysis on scalability to many cameras:**  
  Reviewer 1 raises the question of whether bias disappears or is suppressed by multi‑view learning when the number of cameras becomes very large (e.g., 10k). This scalability aspect is not discussed.

- **Spatial feature distribution visualization lacking:**  
  Reviewer 2 notes the absence of a t‑SNE scatterplot of feature distributions to visually reveal camera bias effects.

- **Another type of camera bias not addressed:**  
  The paper focuses on domain differences (image style, viewpoint) but does not discuss bias due to imbalanced sample counts across cameras or differing identity label distributions per camera.

- **Training strategy description too vague:**  
  The description in Section 5.3 of discarding bias clustering is too simple; it does not clarify whether this drastically reduces the number of training samples or how clustering parameter settings correlate with camera bias (Reviewer 2). No ablation or discussion on these choices is provided.

- **Missing comparisons with newly proposed ReID methods:**  
  Reviewer 1 points out that some recently proposed ReID methods are not included in the experiments, which weakens the evaluation scope.
