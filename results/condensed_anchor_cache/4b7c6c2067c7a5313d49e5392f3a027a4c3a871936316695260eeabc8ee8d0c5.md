## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies implicit sparse regularization in continuous sparsification methods for deep neural networks. Using the mirror flow framework with a time‑dependent Bregman potential, the authors analyze the implicit bias of reparameterized mask‑weight optimization (as in Spred). They propose PILoT, which dynamically adjusts regularization strength and includes a specific initialization scheme. Theoretical convergence and optimality guarantees are provided for underdetermined linear regression, and experiments on CIFAR‑10/100 and ImageNet demonstrate performance gains, especially at high sparsity.

### Strengths
- The theoretical analysis of reparameterization with time‑varying weight decay is solid and novel (R1, R2, R4).
- The derivation of the Bregman potential offers insight into the L1 regularization effect and explains why Spred converges faster than Lasso (R2).
- Developing a practical algorithm (PILoT) that is directly inspired by the theoretical insights is appreciated (R2).
- The problem of neural network sparsification is interesting, and applying implicit/explicit regularization to pruning appears novel (R3).
- Experimental results show improvement over previous works (R3).
- One reviewer found the paper well‑written and easy to follow, with sensible illustrations and solid technical contribution (R4). (This contrasts with the majority’s view on presentation; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation:** The writing is unclear in several sections (1, 2, 3, 5), with imprecise language and a lack of smooth logical flow between the introduction, theory, and experiments (R1, R2, R3). The transition from theoretical analysis to the PILoT algorithm is not clearly articulated (R1). Explanation of notation (e.g., m²) is non‑standard (R2). Variables u₀ and v₀ in Algorithm 1 are not explained, creating a disconnect (R1). Figure 4’s legend is confusing (“mw LRR” and “x LRR” should be renamed) (R1). Minor typos: missing parenthesis below Eq (6) (R3); missing dot in bold text in Section 1.1; incomplete sentence at line 213; formatting issue at line 404 (R4).
- **Theoretical claims:** The abstract states that implicit L1 regularization is a key factor in continuous sparsification, but lines 54‑65 imply it is exclusive to reparameterization – an inconsistency (R1). The claim that the potential R attains its global minimum at initialization x₀ when R is the L1 norm is not sufficiently justified, and the connection to promoting sparsity is unclear given that prior work uses small initialization to obtain L1 regularization (R2, R3). Theorem 2.2: the definition of α_∞ is ambiguous; if α_t → 0 then α_∞=0 makes the statement odd, and if α_∞≠0 then convergence to a minimizer of f under ongoing regularization is questionable (R3). Theorem 2.3: the explanation of how implicit bias recovers unbiased prediction while minimizing Bregman potential is unclear (R2). The discussion of the bias‑variance trade‑off in explicit vs. implicit regularization is insufficient (R2). The explanation that Eq (6) shows why Spred outperforms Lasso is unconvincing because classical LASSO solvers may not suffer from the gradient‑flow limitations, and the comparison is not adequately motivated (R3).
- **Algorithm and experiments:** Reparameterization doubles the parameter count; computational and memory costs are not discussed (R1). The rationale for duplicate entries in Table 1 (slightly different sparsity levels) and the related hyperparameter configurations are not explained (R2, R3). The schedules of α_k used in experiments are missing (R3). In Algorithm 1, increasing α_k when the sparsity condition ‖m_k⊙w_k‖₁ ≥ K is met is counterintuitive (decreasing α_k would seem to promote sparsity), and the motivation for setting β=1 as a discretization of the gradient flow is unclear (R3). The connection between the mirror flow framework and the initialization scheme is not well established (R2).
- **Scope and assumptions:** The implicit regularization result (Theorem 2.3) holds only after the model fits the data perfectly, which may not be realistic in practice (R4).
- **Missing references:** Related work on implicit sparse regularization should be cited: Vaskevicius et al. (NeurIPS 2019), Li et al. (NeurIPS 2021), Zhao et al. (Biometrika 2022), Li et al. (NeurIPS 2022), Li et al. (ICLR 2023) (R4).
