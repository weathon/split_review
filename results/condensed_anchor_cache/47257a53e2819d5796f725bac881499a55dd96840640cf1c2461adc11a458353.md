## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a comprehensive evaluation framework for concept erasure in language models based on three criteria: innocence (complete knowledge removal), seamlessness (maintaining fluent generation when prompted for the erased concept), and specificity (preserving unrelated task performance). It also introduces the Erasure of Language Memory (ELM) method, which uses targeted low-rank updates with separate losses (erasing, conditional fluency, retention) to address all three dimensions. ELM is evaluated on WMDP (biosecurity, cybersecurity) and Harry Potter erasure tasks, using multiple-choice questions, perplexity, MTBench, and MMLU. Baselines include RMU, RepNoise, and WHP. Ablation and robustness (GCG adversarial attacks) are also studied. Results show ELM achieves advantages in some settings (seamlessness in WMDP, innocence in Harry Potter) but not consistently across all experiments and models.

### Strengths
- The proposed three criteria (innocence, seamlessness, specificity) are insightful, comprehensive, and practical for evaluating concept erasure.
- The motivation and derivation of the method in Equations (3)–(5) is clear and easy to follow.
- The overview of the individual losses helps in understanding the ELM design.
- The idea of maintaining seamless generation after concept removal is interesting and addresses an important gap.
- The model unlearning/knowledge erasure problem is important and practical.
- The ablation studies in Section 5.3 are thorough and informative.
- The presentation is well-written and easy to follow overall.

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete and inconsistent experiments:** Comparisons with baseline methods (RMU, RepNoise) are only reported for Zephyr-7B; results on Mistral-7B and Llama3-8B are missing, limiting generalizability. The Harry Potter experiment uses a different setup (Llama2-7B, WHP baseline instead of RepNoise), making cross-experiment comparisons difficult. The results also tell different stories across experiments (ELM shows clear advantage in seamlessness on WMDP but in innocence on Harry Potter) without discussion of this discrepancy.
- **Limited practical significance and missing statistical analysis:** The performance advantage of ELM over RMU appears small in several settings. For example, on the virology benchmark (Figure 2), RMU outperforms ELM in accuracy, and the authors do not explain why. No statistical significance tests are provided to assess the robustness of the reported improvements.
- **Insufficient seamlessness evaluation:** Measuring seamlessness only with perplexity (PPL) is insufficient. Example responses in the main text and appendix show that ELM outputs are less gibberish than baselines but still not genuinely meaningful or acceptable as seamless generation.
- **Question about necessity and novelty of the conditional fluency objective:** Reviewers questioned why the erasing objective alone is insufficient for maintaining fluency, given that the model could learn to respond to alternative concepts instead. A more detailed explanation of what gap the conditional fluency objective fills is needed. Moreover, the overall approach of combining forget and retain losses is not new (e.g., RMU already uses both). The fluency objective is incremental, and the method bears similarity to emulated fine-tuning/decoding-time alignment techniques (e.g., [1], [2]), which should be discussed and compared more thoroughly.
- **Lack of justification for low-rank adapters:** The claim that full fine-tuning may lead to overfitting is not supported by comparative experiments. Empirical evidence demonstrating overfitting compared to alternative approaches would strengthen the argument.
- **Limited benchmark scope:** Evaluation focuses on multiple-choice tasks. Open-ended generation tasks would provide a more comprehensive assessment of model capabilities after erasure.
- **Robustness evaluation too narrow:** Adversarial attacks tested are limited to GCG. A wider variety of attack methods should be included to demonstrate universal robustness.
- **Ablation could be more rigorous:** Instead of simply removing one loss component (Equation 12), varying the hyperparameters λ1, λ2, λ3 would better reveal their individual and combined effects and help develop a control framework for trade-offs.
- **Presentation issues:** Figure 3 (probing and activation analysis) uses an elusive legend and captions, making it impossible to relate the analysis to the data. Several typos: line 51 “fine tune” → “fine-tune”; lines 243–244 quotation marks incorrectly formatted; line 288 “\citet” should be “\citep”; line 294 “Llama3-7B” should be “Llama3-8B”; Table 3: the R-PPL of WHP should also be highlighted. An ethics statement section is missing before the references.
- **Minority reviewer contrast:** One reviewer (score 3) was notably more critical than the others (scores 5), raising additional concerns about limited experimentation, insufficient justification for design choices, and lack of open-ended generation evaluation, which are all reflected above.
