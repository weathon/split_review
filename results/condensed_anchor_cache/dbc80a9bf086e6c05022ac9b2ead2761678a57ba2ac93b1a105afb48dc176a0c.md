## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework to ground large pretrained video models to continuous actions via self-exploration, without external supervision (rewards, action labels). It uses a conditional video generative model to produce future frames as visual goals, then trains a goal-conditioned policy via iterative self-supervised learning with chunked action prediction and random action bootstrapping. Evaluated on 8 tasks in Libero, 6 in MetaWorld, 4 in Calvin, and 12 in iThor Visual Navigation, the method matches or exceeds behavior cloning baselines trained on expert demonstrations.

### Strengths
- Unsupervised grounding of video models to actions eliminates the need for expensive action annotations, directly addressing the video-to-action mapping problem. (R1)
- Strong performance across multiple simulation environments (Libero, MetaWorld, Calvin, iThor) and domains (robotic manipulation, visual navigation), often outperforming supervised methods. Demonstrates adaptability and robustness. (R1, R2, R3)
- Comprehensive ablation studies show effectiveness of each proposed component (chunked action prediction, random bootstrapping, video guidance). (R1, R2, R3)
- Video-guided exploration enables targeted, efficient data collection by focusing on task-relevant state spaces, especially beneficial for complex long-horizon tasks. (R1)
- The method is unique in leveraging video models to enable strong goal conditioning for the policy without action labels, using a self-learning paradigm rather than standard RL. (R3)
- The paper is well-motivated, clearly written, and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3)
- The authors sufficiently address most limitations in the paper. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on random action bootstrapping and lack of clarity**: It is unclear whether the method can learn without any successful actions from random bootstrapping. The paper does not clarify how many successful random trajectories are needed, nor does it report the success rate of the policy after random action bootstrapping alone (before video-guided fine-tuning). The algorithm should explicitly indicate that random bootstrapping is also performed periodically during the self-supervised loop (currently only shown before the loop in Algorithm 1). Details on implementation (e.g., values of a_low and a_high in Eq. 3 for each experiment, frequency of random bootstrapping, number of extra random trials) are missing. This is a significant concern because if random actions never produce any goal-achieving states, the policy may have no way to start learning. (R3)
- **Sensitivity to video generation model quality**: The method heavily depends on the quality and generalization ability of the pretrained video generation model. The paper lacks discussion on failure modes when generated videos are unrealistic or physically implausible, or contain artifacts and inconsistencies. There is no analysis of how performance degrades when the video model generalizes poorly to new or dynamic environments. (R1, R2)
- **Computational cost and efficiency**: The computational requirements are high due to chunk-level action prediction and the need to train and use the video generation model. The cost of the self-supervised training (number of rollouts and iterations) is not compared to the cost of training a standard Behavior Cloning (BC) policy using expert demonstrations. This comparison should be included in the limitations section to assess practical applicability. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Potential for compounding errors in iterative refinement**: Errors in the generated videos could propagate and degrade the goal-conditioned policy during the iterative self-supervised process, but this potential issue is not explored or discussed. (R2)
- **Limited real-world validation**: The method is only evaluated in simulation (Libero, MetaWorld, Calvin, iThor). No real-world experiment is provided. The paper should discuss the possibility of sim-to-real transfer or the challenges of deploying in real environments. (R3)
- **Novelty of mean sampling in action chunking**: The proposed 'mean sampling' in Section 3.3 is not clearly differentiated from existing diffusion policy action generation schemes (e.g., by Chi et al.). The explanation in lines 239-242 is insufficient to understand what is novel about this sampling method over standard diffusion policy approaches. (R3)
- **Random exploration may fail for high-precision tasks**: The reliance on random exploration may not achieve high performance in tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation or exact positioning, limiting applicability. (R1)
- **Questions on computational efficiency using distilled video models**: Could computational efficiency be improved by using distilled video diffusion models that generate videos faster? Would this affect performance? (R1)
- **Dependency on video model at test time**: If the video model used at test time changes (e.g., to a different pretrained model), does the policy still produce viable actions? A simple experiment replacing the video model at test time would help understand this dependency. (R3)
- **Minor issues**: Indentation error in Algorithm 1 lines 10-12 making it hard to read. Typos: line #147 should say "benefits both sides"; line #423 should say "natural" not "nature". In Figure 2, caption needs more detail (what do the pairs of images show?). The metric for evaluating success/failure of rollouts should be mentioned. Line #314 overclaims by stating video-model is zero-shot but policy requires self-supervised training afterwards. (R1, R3)
