## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AutoLoRA, a guidance technique for diffusion models fine-tuned with LoRA. It combines LoRA fine-tuning with an AutoGuidance-like strategy to trade off between the diversity of the base pre-trained model and the consistency of the LoRA-tuned model. Classifier-free guidance is applied to both models. The authors claim improved diversity and quality, evaluated on metrics such as Diversity, Character Presence Score (CPS), Prompt Correspondence (PC), and Style Adherence (SA). Experiments focus on Stable Diffusion and specific LoRA modules (e.g., Disney Princess, Pixel Art). Reviewers differ substantially: one rated the paper very low (score 1) citing marginal novelty and flawed metrics, while another rated it moderately (score 5) finding it novel and well-written; two others gave score 3, noting both potential and significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The core idea of using a guidance trade-off between the base model and the LoRA-fine-tuned model (similar to classifier-free guidance) is simple, easy to implement, and practical for the community.
- The paper is generally well written and easy to follow; the methodology is presented with clear mathematical formulations and a well-organized structure.
- The combination of AutoGuidance and LoRA expands on traditional guidance approaches and offers a new inference scheme that can increase generated image diversity while maintaining prompt correspondence. Several reviewers see originality in this combination.
- Reasonable evaluation metrics are proposed (Diversity, CPS, PC, SA) to assess diversity and consistency, and the experiments explore how AutoLoRA parameters influence diversity.
- AutoLoRA addresses an important problem: mitigating the lack of diversity in LoRA-fine-tuned diffusion models, particularly those that overfit to specific domains.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is marginal / trivial combination.** Several reviewers note that AutoLoRA is a straightforward extension of AutoGuidance, simply replacing the unconditional/conditional pair with the base/LoRA-tuned model pair. One reviewer states the paper lacks justification for why combining these two techniques is particularly suitable for fine-tuning. Another stresses that the method appears trivial and that the introduction should be revised to be consistent with the actual results (see diversity concern below).
- **Diversity improvement is disputed and weakly supported.** 
  - Table 1 reportedly shows that LoRA alone achieves higher diversity across different LoRA scales than AutoLoRA, contradicting the paper’s motivation that LoRA reduces diversity.
  - The only metric showing improvement, Div-CPS, is questioned: the calculation of CPS (Character Presence Score) is unclear, how specific characters are defined is not explained, and the metric seems unworkable for stylizing LoRAs.
  - The new metrics Div-CPS and Div-SA lack validation or benchmarks; their construction (multiplying diversity by CPS or SA) mixes quantities with very different ranges (e.g., diversity 1–2, SA 4.0–4.2), and normalization is not considered.
  - Quantitative improvements over CFG are not substantial across all experimental setups, and the experiments center on a narrow set of LoRAs (Disney Princess, Pixel Art) and models (Stable Diffusion, SDXL). More diverse LoRAs and prompts are needed.
- **Theoretical analysis is missing.** The paper does not provide a theoretical justification for why AutoLoRA balances diversity and consistency, nor does it differentiate from standard AutoGuidance beyond the model choices. One reviewer explicitly questions whether using the LoRA-tuned model as the “better” version is reasonable, since its lack of diversity may limit overall diversity; the main difference from Eq. 5 (AutoGuidance) is precisely this choice, which could be problematic.
- **Missing comparisons and ablations.** 
  - No comparisons with other fine-tuning methods such as DreamBooth or Textual Inversion, and no general image-alignment/text-alignment metrics like ImageReward or HPS (as used in DreamBooth).
  - Ablation studies for the separate CFG scales \(w_1\) (base model) and \(w_2\) (LoRA-tuned model) are missing; only the overall effect of \(\gamma\) is explored.
  - Performance should be compared to more advanced methods, not just the baseline; a simple fair baseline would be sampling 2× images to increase diversity (since AutoLoRA requires 2× inference steps). The 2× inference time for SDXL is noted, making its improvements less surprising.
- **Qualitative results are not convincing.** 
  - Figures 1, 3, and 4 fail to clearly show a visual superiority of AutoLoRA over CFG; readers can hardly tell the difference. Additional explanations in captions are needed.
- **Writing and structure issues.** 
  - Several reviewers note poor language, typos (e.g., incorrect quotation marks), and fluency problems.
  - The related work and preliminaries sections are too detailed (approximately 1.5 pages), decreasing focus on the proposed method.
  - The introduction discusses AutoGuidance too early, before the problem is properly motivated.
- **Inference complexity is increased significantly.** The dual-model requirement (both base and LoRA models at each diffusion step) raises computational cost, yet this trade-off is not directly evaluated. One reviewer suggests comparing with 2× baseline sampling as a fair control.
