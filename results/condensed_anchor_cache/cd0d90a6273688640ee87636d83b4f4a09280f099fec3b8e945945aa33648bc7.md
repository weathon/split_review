## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends the strong-weak augmentation scheme in semi-supervised semantic segmentation to the output space via Sinkhorn Output Perturbations (SOP). The method adds structured pseudo-label noise by interpolating between one-hot and softmax distributions, guided by a Sinkhorn optimizer and patch partitioning. The approach is designed as a plugin orthogonal to existing methods and achieves state-of-the-art results on Cityscapes (with notable gains under low-data regimes: 76.0% vs. 75.0%) and competitive but not state-of-the-art performance on Pascal VOC 2012. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of output-level perturbations but raise significant concerns about insufficient justification, limited improvements on Pascal VOC, and the need for clearer ablation studies and theoretical grounding.

### Strengths
- **Novel perspective**: Perturbing the output instead of input or features is an interesting and underexplored direction (R1, R2, R3, R4). The method introduces structural noise that depends on the global class distribution of an image (R2).
- **Orthogonal and practical**: SOP can be plugged into any segmentation model and combined with other semi-supervised strategies (e.g., UniMatch) without architectural changes (R1, R3).
- **Strong results on Cityscapes**: The method surpasses prior methods on Cityscapes, especially with very few labeled samples (76.0% vs. 75.0%) (R1). Performance is convincing in this setting (R3).
- **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, with a clear motivation (R2, R3, R4). The research topic of perturbed predictions (softmax-mining, beta interpolation, input patching) is interesting (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of justification and rationale**: The paper does not provide sufficient insight into why perturbing the teacher’s output improves performance or why random noise would be effective (R1, R4). The claim that it helps escape from “unfavorable local minima” (added in rebuttal) still lacks solid evidence (R1). The rationale appears contradictory: the augmentation depends on the model’s current status, yet aims to guide the parameter update away from amplifying that same signal (R4).
- **Insufficient analysis of SOP vs. random noise**: It is unclear why the proposed Sinkhorn-optimized perturbation (SOP) outperforms simply adding random noise to the teacher output. The paper lacks an analysis of the frequency, magnitude, and class-specific effects of SOP perturbations compared to random noise (R1).
- **Missing clear ablation study**: No direct comparison of no output perturbation, random noise, and SOP is provided. An ablation with quantitative metrics and visualizations of perturbed outputs is needed to isolate the effect of each component (R1).
- **Limited performance improvement on Pascal VOC 2012**: The method fails to achieve state-of-the-art results in at least one experimental setting on Pascal VOC 2012 (Table 2) (R2, R4). While R3 finds the performance convincing on Cityscapes, other reviewers note that the method does not demonstrate similar effectiveness on Pascal VOC and question its generalizability and robustness (R2, R4). R4 specifically highlights the dependency on UniMatch to achieve competitive results, raising concerns about standalone utility.
- **Concerns about pseudo-label accuracy**: The method introduces more inaccurate pseudo-labels (by perturbing them), yet the model improves—a counterintuitive result that needs investigation. Reviewers ask: Are the augmented pseudo-labels more accurate than the original ones? Quantitative results on pseudo-label quality should be presented (R4).
- **Questions about the use of global class distribution**: The perturbation uses per-image class distribution (Eq. 5). It is unclear whether such local distributions meaningfully influence per-pixel predictions; the reviewer suggests considering dataset-level (whole training set) class relationships instead (R2).
- **Limited novelty and related work discussion**: R3 finds the novelty limited despite practical value and requests more explanation of what is truly new. The related work section is concise and could benefit from more extensive discussion (R3).
- **Typos and presentation issues**: There are a few typos (e.g., citation formatting in the introduction) (R4).
