## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that the paper tackles a novel and important problem (spatial audio generation via latent diffusion), contributes a large-scale dataset (BEWO-1M), and proposes a well-designed model (SpatialSonic) with multimodal conditioning. Strengths include the dataset’s scale and pipeline, the one-stage azimuth-aware framework, and comprehensive evaluations. Weaknesses center on the reliance on simulated data, unconvincing spatial quality in demos, image conditioning feeling like a distraction, and unclear metric definitions. Reviewer 2 is notably more critical of the spatial audio quality and the role of image conditioning; Reviewer 3 finds the pipeline and framework descriptions hard to follow; Reviewer 4 praises the stereo effect but questions data preprocessing and image data volume.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem and approach**: The paper is the first to tackle spatial (stereo) audio generation using latent diffusion models with controllable azimuth-aware guidance. The one-stage framework avoids error propagation and computational inefficiencies of multi-stage models.
- **Significant dataset contribution**: BEWO-1M is the first large-scale spatial audio dataset (>1M stereo audio-caption pairs), constructed via a sensible pipeline including sound event detection, LLM (GPT-4) generation of longform descriptions with explicit distance/room-size inferences, and spatial audio simulation. The dataset is intended to be made available to the community.
- **Comprehensive methodology and evaluations**: The azimuth-based guidance, multimodal fusion (text, image, positional conditions), and classifier-free guidance are clearly described and replicable. Both subjective (N=15) and objective evaluations (including mono-channel benchmarks and a proposed stereo-out metric, FSAD) show consistent outperformance over baselines (AudioLDM2, Make-An-Audio2, etc.). One reviewer called the stereo effect in demos “amazing.”
- **Well-structured paper**: The paper is logically organized from dataset creation to model design and evaluation.

### Weaknesses
- **Dependence on simulated data**: BEWO-1M is built largely on simulated and GPT-transformed data, which may limit generalization to real-world audio with greater variability. The authors are asked whether they evaluated on real-world datasets (e.g., the dataset in [1]) and how they plan to adapt or extend the dataset to real-world audio (e.g., augmentation, collaborative collection).
- **Limited spatial realism despite superiority claims**: Several demos (e.g., “A duck is quacking on the left,” “A dog is barking on the right”) do not convincingly convey the intended direction. One reviewer (R2) notes that only 2–3 samples felt spatially correct, and the relative improvement over baselines may not be perceptibly large. The issue may stem from room acoustics simulation.
- **Image conditioning feels like a distraction**: One reviewer (R2) suggests that the image conditioning task should be moved to the appendix, with greater focus on text- and azimuth-conditioned spatial audio quality and additional ablations.
- **Insufficient clarity in descriptions** (Reviewer 3): The dataset construction pipeline and model framework are not easy to follow.
- **Metric definition incomplete**: The FSAD metric (Frechet Stereo Audio Distance) is briefly described; reviewers ask for a clear explanation of its computation, why it is better suited for stereo-out evaluation, and how meaningful percent improvements are. Also unclear is the role of classifier-free guidance – how critical is it for spatial quality?
- **Random cropping may affect quality**: The use of sound activity detection followed by random 10-second crops is seen as somewhat arbitrary; the authors are asked whether this degrades audio quality.
- **Low image data volume**: Despite the ease of collecting image-audio pairs (per the pipeline in Figure 2), the image data in Table 1 is sparse. Why?
- **Missing analysis by sound category**: Performance differences by sound type (e.g., engine/dog bark vs. piano/duck) would be informative, as simulation and LLM priors likely differ (indoor vs. outdoor, moving vs. static).
- **Generalization limitations of the image encoder**: The authors note limited ability to handle diverse/open-world scenes; reviewers ask how this could be addressed.
- **High computational cost**: Training requires substantial hardware; the authors are asked about potential strategies to reduce load (e.g., model distillation, optimization).
- **Code/algorithm not available**: The training process is not reproducible without code.
- **Lack of comparisons with stereo-specific generators**: Baseline comparisons use mono-focused models (AudioLDM2, Make-An-Audio2); are there any existing stereo-specific methods?
- **Potential for video as data source**: Incorporating video could make the task more promising, but is not explored.
- **Extension to 5.1 channels and higher sampling rates**: Anticipated challenges and planned solutions are not discussed.
- **Audio-language pretraining utility**: One reviewer asks whether BEWO-1M could benefit other tasks like text-to-audio retrieval or audio captioning; inferior performance is acceptable but worth investigating.
