## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Critic-CoT, a framework to train LLMs for step-wise, chain-of-thought (CoT) self-critique and refinement. The method constructs natural language critique and refinement data without human annotation by sampling solutions, assuming correct final answers imply correct intermediate steps, and using a stronger model (GPT-4-Turbo) to generate step-level feedback. Two inference strategies are tested: iterative refinement (critique then produce new output) and critic-as-filter (re-ranking with the critic). Experiments on GSM8K and MATH are reported. Three reviewers gave a score of 5 (weak reject); one reviewer gave an 8 (strong accept), praising the motivation and practical data construction but also noting unsatisfactory results and a need for deeper analysis.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and the method is well motivated. [Reviewer 1]
- The data construction for step-level natural language critique is interesting and practical. Generating a large amount of step-wise feedback data via a strong LLM (GPT-4-Turbo) is valuable; such a dataset may be even more valuable than the MATH SFT data itself. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4]
- The Critic-as-Filter approach shows consistent gains over majority voting. [Reviewer 2]
- The performance of the critic model is evaluated directly using F1 and accuracy, which is relatively novel (most prior work only evaluates indirectly via downstream tasks). [Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4]
- The step-wise self-evaluate design and the generated feedback dataset are very practical. [Reviewer 4]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The framework is similar to existing self-reflection-based approaches and does not provide fundamentally new insights. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
- **Methodological concerns:**
  - The self-critique ability is distilled from a stronger model (GPT-4-Turbo), so improvements likely come from the supervision signal rather than from genuine self-critique. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3]
  - The data construction (Section 3.1) samples LLM outputs and assumes that if the final answer is correct, all intermediate steps are correct. This assumption is not validated, and the noise from sampling is not evaluated. Human evaluation of critique data quality and the percentage of perfect CoT reasoning should be reported. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3]
  - The use of “distant supervision” is unclear in the math reasoning context (it has a specific meaning in information extraction). The paper should rephrase. [Reviewer 4]
  - Formulation is confusing: the representation (e.g., l_i = +1 or -1) does not clearly incorporate the CoT critique text. [Reviewer 3]
- **Weak experimental results:**
  - Improvements on GSM8K and MATH are marginal, especially for the iterative refinement setting on MATH/MATH500. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
  - Self-consistency (majority voting) alone often outperforms or matches the proposed Critic-CoT improvements. For example, Critic-CoT + Llama-3-70B-Instruct achieves 91.7%, but with additional Maj1@96 it reaches 94.8%, surpassing iterative refine. The native model + Maj1@96 already yields 94.1%. The paper does not adequately explain this. [Reviewer 4]
  - The iterative refine approach without re-ranking on MATH is not significant; this suggests that self-critique accuracy is low and needs more detailed analysis. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4]
- **Missing experiments/analyses:**
  - No experiments on broader reasoning tasks beyond math (e.g., commonsense reasoning). [Reviewer 1]
  - Lacks strong baselines using other NL critic models or deterministic reward models (PRM, ORM). [Reviewer 3]
  - No in-depth analysis demonstrating that the model genuinely achieves self-reflection; the experiments do not effectively prove this. [Reviewer 4]
  - The advantage of using CoT for critique (vs. simpler feedback) is not well justified. [Reviewer 3]
  - The generated feedback dataset is not provided in the supplementary materials. [Reviewer 4]
- **Missing details and methodological gaps:**
  - How to define one step? How to identify `pred` from an attempt? For tasks without easily extractable answers (beyond math with number patterns), the method cannot be directly applied; majority voting also requires answer extraction. [Reviewer 3]
  - The data generation uses 10 samples per instance labeled by GPT-3.5 and 16 retries with GPT-4-Turbo for refinement—this can be seen as best-of-10 data augmentation; gains may come from in-domain data augmentation rather than from the critique-Co. [Reviewer 3]
- **Missing references:** Prior work on LLMs’ inability to self-correct (Huang et al., 2024, ICLR; Zhang et al., 2024, ACL) is not cited. [Reviewer 4]
