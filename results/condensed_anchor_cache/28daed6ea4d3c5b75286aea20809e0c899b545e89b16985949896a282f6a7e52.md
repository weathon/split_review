## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces I-Con, a framework that unifies over 11 machine learning methods (clustering, spectral methods, dimensionality reduction, contrastive learning, supervised learning) by minimizing an integrated KL divergence between a supervisory and a learned representation. The authors provide theoretical proofs connecting these methods, and demonstrate a practical application by transferring ideas across domains to achieve a +8% improvement in unsupervised classification on ImageNet-1K. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical unification and cross-domain transfer, but raise concerns about novelty, scope, clarity, experimental validation, and practical guidance. One reviewer (score 8) is more positive, praising the writing and solid improvements, while others (scores 5–6) find the framework less innovative and the experimental support limited.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical unification**: Provides a single equation (KL divergence minimization) that unifies clustering, spectral methods, dimensionality reduction, contrastive learning, and supervised learning. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Comprehensive proofs**: Detailed mathematical proofs linking 11 different approaches are presented in the appendix. (Reviewer 1)
- **Cross-domain transfer**: Enables leveraging successful components from one domain (e.g., debiased contrastive learning, t-SNE, SCAN) to improve methods in another (e.g., unsupervised classification). This is demonstrated concretely with spectral clustering, t-SNE, debiased contrastive learning, and SCAN. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **State-of-the-art empirical results**: Achieves an 8% improvement over prior state-of-the-art on unsupervised classification on ImageNet-1K. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Well-written and organized**: The paper provides a comprehensive survey and insightfully extracts similarities across fields. (Reviewer 4)
- **Solid performance improvements**: The gains from transferring the proposed distribution across domains are convincing. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and innovation questioned**: Many connections between the methods have been noted in prior work; the core contribution is seen as integrating existing ideas rather than offering fundamentally new insights. (Reviewer 1) The framework is described as essentially clustering-based, not a generic representation learning framework. (Reviewer 2) The motivation for a unified framework is unconvincing: I-Con still requires choosing \(p_{\theta}\) and does not directly help select a suitable loss function for a given problem. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Scope limited**: Does not cover autoencoder-based representation learning, masked pretraining (e.g., MAE, BERT-style), or general self-supervised methods. (Reviewer 2) Data augmentation-based contrastive learning methods are not unified. (Reviewer 2) The framework is better characterized as unifying clustering methods rather than all representation learning. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing downstream evaluation**: No experiments on common downstream tasks (e.g., linear probing, k-NN classification, transfer learning) that assess representation quality beyond clustering. (Reviewer 2) The gains in Table 3 may not hold with k-NN classifiers as used in DiNO. (Reviewer 3) Only ImageNet-1K clustering is evaluated; broader generalization is underexplored. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Clarity and presentation issues**: The term ‘I-Con’ is ambiguous—used for both the unified framework (Section 3.1) and the specific integrated method (Section 3.2). (Reviewer 3) Section 3.3 (Adaptive neighborhoods) is hard to follow. (Reviewer 3) Table 2 lacks a direct link in Section 4.2 first line. (Reviewer 3) Typo: “Like in t-SNE this addition helps…” should have a comma. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient statistical rigor**: Random seeds are not considered; error bars are missing for baseline methods in Table 2 and for all scores in Table 3, making statistical significance of the reported improvements unclear. (Reviewer 3) The paper should provide results over multiple runs (e.g., 5–10) with standard deviations. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear comparison in Table 2**: It is not specified whether “I-Con (Ours)” is the integrated method from Section 3.3 or the best result from Table 3. Reporting the best among them may be an unfair comparison. (Reviewer 3)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity contradicts claimed simplicity**: The paper claims no additional regularization, yet the ablation study shows sensitivity to \(\alpha\) and propagation size (Figure 4, Table 4), which effectively act as regularizers requiring tuning. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of variational Bayesian discussion**: The impact of the choice of distribution family for \(q_{\phi}\) (e.g., unimodal vs. multimodal) on optimization and representation quality is not discussed. (Reviewer 4) The paper should compare with related surveys on mutual information maximization (e.g., Tschannen et al., 2019) and clarify how I-Con addresses limitations of MI estimation in high dimensions and sensitivity to the estimator. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing experimental extension**: Are the improvements significant beyond clustering? (Reviewer 4) Can I-Con reproduce or improve original representation learning methods (e.g., InfoNCE on ImageNet) in practical settings? (Reviewer 3)
- **Disagreement on purpose**: Clustering aims to discover clusters, while contrastive learning aims to learn transferable representations; the paper does not adequately discuss this difference. (Reviewer 2)
