## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Search4Prompt, a test-time adaptation method that uses LLM-generated descriptors (attributes) to enhance vision-language models during inference. It has two components: Retrieval-based Attribute Search (RAS) and Implicit-Explicit Attribute Injection (IEAI). Experiments across several benchmarks show performance gains, but reviewers raise significant concerns about novelty, efficiency, clarity of implementation, and experimental verification. Reviewer 4 is notably more critical (score 3) than the others (score 5), especially on the novelty and simplicity of the approach.

### Strengths
1. **Well-written and organized** (Reviewers 1,2,3): The paper is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow.
2. **Use of LLM-generated attributes is reasonable** (Reviewer 1): Leveraging descriptors for VLM inference is a meaningful direction. Reviewer 2 adds that retrieving and compositing discriminative attributes for prompt tuning is a positive contribution.
3. **Empirical effectiveness** (Reviewers 1,3,4): The method shows notable performance improvements over baselines on multiple datasets (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 states that experiments demonstrate effectiveness. Reviewer 4 acknowledges that the two‑component framework (RAS + IEAI) and the extensive experimental results support the approach.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and simplistic attribute search** (Reviewers 1,2,4): The core idea of searching relevant attributes for zero‑shot learning is not new. Reviewer 1 notes the approach is similar to DVDet [1] and lacks novelty in Discriminative Attribute Generation. Reviewer 4 points out that prior works (e.g., [a] Learning concise and descriptive attributes, ICCV’23; [b] Language in a bottle, CVPR’23) achieve similar accuracy with a smaller attribute vocabulary, and that RAS is merely a cosine similarity evaluation. Reviewer 2 calls for a clearer articulation of the innovations over standard TPT. The distinction from existing methods (especially DVDet) is not sufficiently clarified.
2. **Missing baselines and comparisons** (Reviewers 1,2,3):
   - CaF [2] should be included as a baseline (Reviewer 1).
   - Prompt learning methods that also use fine‑grained attributes, such as MAP and ArGue (CVPR’24, TCSVT’24), are not discussed or compared (Reviewer 3).
   - The paper should evaluate with multiple LLMs to test generalizability and include results with ViT‑B‑16 CLIP for consistent model‑size comparisons (Table 6, Reviewer 2).
3. **Attribute bank construction, filtering, and robustness** (Reviewers 2,3,4):
   - The details of the attribute bank (what attributes it contains, how its comprehensiveness is ensured) are unclear (Reviewer 4).
   - RAS has limitations in filtering irrelevant attributes, especially those generated by the LLM (Reviewer 2).
   - No evaluation of robustness to noisy or incorrect attribute descriptions from the LLM; the quality of generated attributes should be assessed (Reviewer 4).
   - Testing efficiency drops: inference time grows with the attribute pool and number of categories, as shown in Table 8 for ImageNet‑R with three optimization steps (Reviewer 3).
4. **Experimental results need verification and clarification** (Reviewers 2,3):
   - The same baseline model yields different results in Table 3 and Table 4 (Reviewer 2).
   - VCD results are questionable: VCD [1] reports 86.92 on Pets (zero‑shot), but this paper reports only 81.30 for VCD + TPT; CLIP+A reports 80.84 on Flowers, but this paper reports 69.23. The authors should revisit these numbers and provide detailed experimental setups to reconcile discrepancies (Reviewer 2).
   - Table 1 mixes few‑shot and unsupervised settings without clear justification; few‑shot experimental results should be provided separately (Reviewer 3).
5. **Soft voting and attribute score explanation needed** (Reviewer 2): The soft voting process (Section 3.2.1) is unclear. For the example in Figure 1, “Red velvet cake” (0.23) should be the class with highest probability, not “cup cakes” (0.22). A step‑by‑step illustration or example of how scores are computed is needed for reproducibility.
6. **Computational resource comparison missing** (Reviewer 2): Memory usage, parameter count, and FLOPS should be compared with baselines such as TPT and TDA to assess computational trade‑offs.
7. **Additional minor issues**:
   - The statement about the challenge of test‑time adaptation (lack of labels) is self‑evident and unnecessary (Reviewer 1).
   - A qualitative analysis of generated and retrieved attributes for each category, with more examples, is requested (Reviewer 3).
   - Typo on line 166: “Retrieve” (Reviewer 3).
