## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides theoretical and empirical evidence that state-space models (SSMs) augmented with local self-attention can perform in-context learning (ICL) by emulating one step of gradient descent (GD) on an implicit linear regression model. The key insight is that the diagonal linear recurrent layer acts as a gradient accumulator, enabling this emulation. The construction is validated on simple linear and nonlinear regression tasks. However, reviewers disagree on novelty: two reviewers (score 5) find the insight novel and bridging SSMs, ICL, and mechanistic interpretability, while two reviewers (score 3) argue the results are subsumed by prior work (Zucchet et al. 2023, RNNs performing GD in forward pass [1]) or are already known from approximation theory. Concerns are raised about the practical relevance to modern SSM variants (Mamba, Griffin) given the constructed architecture differs significantly, and the experiments are limited to shallow architectures and regression-style ICL.

### Strengths
- Novel insight: viewing the diagonal linear recurrent layer in SSMs as a gradient accumulator, enabling ICL via GD when combined with local self-attention (or local linear self-attention). This bridges understanding of SSMs, ICL, and mechanistic interpretability. (R1, R2)
- The theoretical construction is sound, clearly presented with step-by-step progression from simple to complex cases, and includes clear definitions. (R1, R2, R3)
- Generality is demonstrated: extensions to multi-step GD, multi-layer SSMs, and multi-dimensional data are theoretically developed. (R1)
- Empirical results on simple synthetic regression tasks (linear and nonlinear) validate that the constructed GD-SSM parameters align with theoretical predictions; the constructed circuit can be learned from scratch. (R1, R2, R3)
- The work potentially points to architectural requirements (input-dependent input/output processing, multiplicative interactions) for enabling ICL in recurrent models. (R1, R2)
- The paper is well-written, well-motivated, and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns relative to prior work** (R3, R4 high confidence; R1 moderate):
    - R3: Zucchet et al. 2023 (arXiv:2309.01775) proves that one SSM layer can implement any linear self-attention layer. This would imply that any ICL algorithm a linear self-attention layer can implement (including one-step GD) can be realized by an SSM. The experimental setup in that paper is also very similar. The authors must compare their work to Zucchet et al. and highlight what new insights are provided beyond that result.
    - R1: Section 3.1 shares structure with NeurIPS 2016 work [1] showing RNNs can perform GD during the forward pass. The authors must explicitly specify the principal differences between their approach and that earlier RNN construction to clarify novelty.
    - R4: The theoretical statement (ICL via GD) is already known from universal approximation results for transformers, CNNs, and RNNs. The authors should explain why previous approximation theory does not extend to the ICL case; otherwise the contribution appears to be a subset of known results.

- **Claims about explaining modern SSM variants are not supported** (R2, strong; R1, R4):
    - The constructed GD-SSM (a diagonal recurrent layer + local self-attention) differs significantly from modern SSM architectures (Mamba, Griffin, S6, RG-LRU). Empirical results show modern SSMs (e.g., one-layer Mamba, Griffin) perform poorly on these regression ICL tasks, while the custom GD-SSM performs well. It is therefore unclear how the paper’s findings explain the success of recent SSMs (claimed line 488) or pinpoints input-dependent processing as the key feature for ICL (claimed lines 052-053). (R2, R1, R4)
    - R2: The paper does not ablate input/output-dependent features empirically across ICL tasks, nor provides detailed theoretical analysis to substantiate the claim that these are crucial. Moreover, input-dependent processing can be implemented via various gating mechanisms, but earlier gated SSMs did not show strong ICL, making the claim ambiguous. (R1)
    - R4: The modification (local self-attention + SSM) is not commonly used; aligning with widely-used models like linear transformers would be more practically relevant.

- **Limited scope and practical interest**:
    - The analysis and experiments are restricted to regression-style ICL (linear and nonlinear). This limits impact; other ICL types exist (see e.g. arXiv:2401.12973, arXiv:2402.04248). (R2)
    - R4: The notion of “can learn” via ICL is insufficient – the same argument could apply to Newton’s or quasi-Newton’s methods. The paper should provide empirical comparisons of convergence rates or asymptotic behavior between the proposed method and alternatives like Newton’s method to clarify the practical significance.

- **Experimental details and architecture comparisons are insufficient** (R2, R4):
    - R2: The experimental section lacks detailed architectural descriptions and hyperparameters. A clear, explicit definition of GD-SSM (including learnable parameters, possibly pseudocode) is needed.
    - All experiments use shallow architectures (1 or 2 layers). This is inconsistent with typical ICL scenarios where larger models are needed for emergent ICL capabilities. Deeper architectures should be tested. (R4)
    - R2: Terminology confusion: “local self-attention” vs “local linear self-attention” must be used consistently. They are very different (nonlinear vs linear). Equation 2 introduces LSA but it is only defined later in line 119.

- **Specific empirical weaknesses and unanswered questions** (R2):
    - Q2 (merged): How do the results explain the success of recent SSM variants? If modern SSMs (Mamba, Griffin) perform poorly on these tasks, what insight does the paper provide about their ICL capabilities?
    - Q3: Why does Griffin perform so poorly compared to linear transformers and S5? Griffin combines sliding window attention and an SSM (RG-LRU) – is the model used just RG-LRU? Shouldn’t it be structurally similar to GD-SSM?
    - Q4: Why does the time-invariant S5 consistently outperform input-dependent Mamba and Griffin (even though all fail to converge)? One would expect input-dependent dynamics to help ICL.
    - Q5: Would other architectures (1-layer linear attention, S5, Griffin, Mamba) benefit from receiving the local self-attention preprocessing? Could they then learn the GD-SSM solution? If not, why?
    - Q6: Can 2 layers of these SSMs solve the task? (Note: combining local self-attention with a diagonal SSM is itself a combination of two sequence processors.)
    - Q7: Where is 1-step GD shown in Figure A? (Figure A not provided in the paper.)
    - Q8: In Figure 4B, the 1- or 2-layer GD-SSM always achieves lower loss than the corresponding number of GD steps. Why? What happens when more layers are compared to more GD steps (e.g., 5 or 10)?
    - Q9: The color scheme in Figure 4C is hard to read.
    - Q10: LSA is introduced in Equation 2 but defined only later in line 119.
    - R4 Q2: The behavior of GD and 1-D GD-SSM appears different; please explain the discrepancy.
    - R4 Q3: Font size in Figure 3 is too small.

- **Missing comparisons and extensions** (R1, R4):
    - R1 (W2): The authors overlook alternative approaches (e.g., reduction of S6 to attention, softmax omission). They should evaluate whether these could yield additional theoretical developments and state explicitly the advantages of their approach over such reductions.
    - R4: The paper should provide empirical comparisons of convergence rates/asymptotic behavior between GD-SSM and other optimization approaches (e.g., Newton/quasi-Newton).
    - R1 (W4): The claim that GD-SSM performs well on general-purpose prediction problems is unsupported by the current empirical evidence; more extensive experiments are needed.
