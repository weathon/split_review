## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Ctrl-Adapter, a framework for efficiently adapting pretrained image-based ControlNets to new image and video diffusion models (both UNet and DiT backbones). It supports diverse controls (single/multi-condition, sparse-frame, zero-shot, image/video), and tasks like video editing, style transfer, and text-guided motion control. It matches pretrained ControlNets on COCO and achieves SOTA on DAVIS 2017 with <10 GPU hours. Reviewers find the method interesting, simple, efficient, and versatile with extensive experiments. However, concerns are raised about presentation clarity (missing architectural details, MoE, multi-condition integration), unjustified design choices (1-1 layer mapping, first-frame conditioning), fairness of training cost comparison (pre-trained ControlNet cost not accounted), missing ablations (skipping latents, zero-shot quantitative results), unclear differences from X-Adapter, and incremental technical contribution.

### Strengths

- The results are interesting, convincing, and the adaptation of pretrained ControlNets to video is novel (R1, R4).
- The method is simple, efficient, and cost-effective; it significantly reduces training time (often <10 GPU hours) compared to training ControlNets from scratch (R2, R3).
- Supports a wide range of applications: image/video control, sparse-frame video control, multi-condition with fine-grained patch-level MoE routing, zero-shot adaptation to unseen conditions, video editing, video style transfer, and text-guided motion control (R2, R3).
- Comprehensive experiments across six diverse backbones (SDXL, PixArt-α, I2VGen-XL, SVD, Latte, Hotshot-XL) and tasks including single-condition, multi-condition, zero-shot, image-to-image, image-to-video (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Key technical contributions: temporal convolutions for image-to-video adaptation, use of Q-former, skipping latents for efficiency, handling noise scheduling shift (R4).
- Well-written and logically structured (R1, R2, R3) — though R1 and R4 note presentation issues (see Weaknesses).
- Code is provided (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Poor presentation of methodology**: The main paper lacks essential details about the adapter architecture, the MoE routing mechanism (how patch-level routing is implemented, how experts are chosen/combined), and the three multi-condition integration strategies. The methodology section is cluttered with engineering details and lacks mathematical formulation, making technical novelty hard to grasp (R1, R3, R4). R2 disagrees on writing quality (finds it strong), but R1 and R4 find clarity lacking.
- **Unjustified architecture choices**: The 1-1 mapping from ControlNet layers to layers of similar dimension in the new diffusion model assumes these layers learn similar features, but this choice is not justified or discussed (R1).
- **First-frame conditioning rationale unclear**: Why condition the Ctrl-Adapter on the first frame? Performance without such conditioning is not reported (R1).
- **Skipping latents**: This strategy may cause loss of fine-grained detail in frames without direct conditioning; an ablation study is needed to evaluate when skipping is beneficial vs. detrimental. It is also unclear whether skipping latents requires retraining the ControlNet, and whether it reduces the adapter's generalization across control types (R2, R4).
- **Training cost comparison not entirely fair**: The method leverages a pretrained image ControlNet, whose substantial pre-training cost is not accounted for in the reported efficiency (e.g., <10 GPU hours only covers adaptation). A complete analysis should include the pre-training resources (R3).
- **Multi-ControlNet setup unclear**: It is unclear whether the six conditions (e.g., in Table 3) are trained simultaneously in each iteration. Zero-shot results (Fig 8, Section 4.5) lack quantitative evaluation: Table 4 should include rows for models trained only on depth but applied to other conditions, and a plain diffusion baseline without ControlNet for comparison (R4).
- **Insufficient comparison with X-Adapter**: The architecture difference, training difference, and the source of the speed/performance improvement over X-Adapter are not clearly explained (R4).
- **Technical contribution seems incremental**: While the framework is versatile, the core approach extends multiple existing adapters; more elaboration on the novel technical insights is needed (R2, R4).
- **Additional inference cost for multiple conditions not discussed**: What is the inference complexity when maintaining multiple control adapters (e.g., seven conditions)? (R3)
- **Alternative baseline not explored**: Would integrating adapters directly inside each ControlNet be a simpler and efficient baseline, especially for single-condition scenarios? (R3)
- **Typo**: Line 069: “Controlnet a cannot” (R1).
