## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MAST, a sparse training framework for deep Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) that uses gradient-based topology evolution (RigL) combined with a hybrid TD(λ) target, Soft Mellowmax operator, and dual replay buffers. Experiments on SMAC benchmarks show up to 20-fold FLOPs reduction with less than 3% performance degradation. All three reviewers acknowledge the problem is important and the experiments are extensive, but raise concerns about clarity, novelty, hyperparameter sensitivity, and limited benchmarking.

### Strengths
- Using sparse training in MARL is a relatively new and important direction that can inspire the community (Reviewer 1) and makes MARL applicable to resource-limited devices (Reviewer 3).
- MAST can be applied to different CTDE methods (e.g., QMIX series) (Reviewer 3).
- A large number of experiments on SMAC with extensive analysis provide evidence of effectiveness (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 explicitly highlight the experimental breadth.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation logic** are poor. Reviewer 1 notes missing details on RigL, making it confusing for readers unfamiliar with sparse training. Reviewer 2 states the writing logic is bad (relationship between sparse models in SL, single-agent RL, and multi-agent RL unclear; too much background on MARL not clarifying the logic). In contrast, Reviewer 3 rated presentation 4 (excellent), but this minority view does not override the consensus on poor readability. Reviewer 2 additionally points out that some formulas are not numbered and Algorithm 1 should be in the main text, not supplementary.
- **Limited novelty and unclear kernel contribution.** Reviewer 3 notes that the combination of multiple techniques (RigL, hybrid TD(λ), Soft Mellowmax, dual buffers) makes it difficult to discern the specific core innovation, and the individual impact of each component is unclear. If the motivation lies in the algorithm, the contribution may seem incremental. Reviewer 1 adds that the literature review lacks closely related work (e.g., [1] and [2]), making the statement “the only existing endeavor to train sparse MARL agents” inaccurate.
- **Benchmarking and generalizability are insufficient.** Reviewer 3 strongly argues that experiments on SMAC alone do not establish generalizability; testing on more diverse environments like Google Research Football is needed. Reviewer 1 similarly notes that the limitation of many key parameters to fine-tune may make it infeasible to apply to other complex domains. Reviewer 2 asks about how topology adjustments every 200 episodes (~10,000 steps) perform under other configurations.
- **Statistical rigor concerns.** Reviewers 2 and 3 both point out that only 4 random seeds are used, which is not sufficient in RL (Reviewer 2) and ask for variance/stability information (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 additionally observes that some curves do not appear to converge at the end, possibly due to smoothing.
- **Specific technical questions and missing comparisons.**  
  - Reviewer 1 asks why larger values occur under a sparse model compared to a dense network (Section 4.1) – is this overestimation?  
  - Reviewer 1 requests a comparison of Soft Mellowmax with double Q-learning for dealing with overestimation, and asks how the values ω=5 and ω=10 were selected.  
  - Reviewer 1 questions why Table 1 values exceed 100% and how they are calculated.  
  - Reviewer 1 asks why average reward is used instead of the common SMAC success rate, and whether rewards were normalized.  
  - Reviewer 2 questions whether the discount rate can be 1 (a typo or conceptual issue).  
  - Reviewer 3 asks how hyperparameters (e.g., for hybrid TD(λ)) are decided in different scenarios and real-world applications.
- **Visualization is uninformative.** Reviewer 1 states that the visualization of latent space shows no specific patterns; projecting which connections are removed/remaining and analyzing why would be more insightful.
- **Minor issues.** Code/algorithm details are relegated to supplementary material (Reviewer 2).
