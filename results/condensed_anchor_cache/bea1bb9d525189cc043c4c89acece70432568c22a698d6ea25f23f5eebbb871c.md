## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Uni-Map, a unified HD map construction network based on MapTR that handles camera-only, LiDAR-only, and camera-LiDAR fused inputs within a single model. Key components include a Mixture Stack Modality (MSM) training scheme (sampling each batch to include all three modality variants), a projector module (MLP) to align BEV features from different modalities into a shared space, and a switching modality strategy for inference. Experiments on nuScenes evaluate performance under 13 sensor corruption scenarios, with reported gains of 4.6, 5.6, and 5.6 mAP over independently trained MapTR models.

### Strengths
- Well-written, clear structure, and easy to follow; figures (Fig. 2, 3, 6) are especially clear and aid understanding (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 3 also notes “well-written and easy to follow”).
- Strong empirical results with extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments, including ablation studies on key components and a t-SNE visualization (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- Originality: MSM batch augmentation and modality switching are new to the HD map construction literature (Reviewer 2).  
- Thorough robustness analysis with 13 sensor corruption types, covering missing inputs and corruption on camera, LiDAR, or both (Reviewer 2).  
- Problem configuration is meaningful for autonomous driving; method could benefit other fusion tasks such as RGB-D segmentation (Reviewer 3, 4).  
- The method is “simple but effective” and “notably improves” map construction across all input settings (Reviewer 3, more positive than others).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical insight** – The proposed projector module (MLP) and switching strategy lack convincing explanations or theoretical analysis; the method section consists largely of engineering details and it remains unclear *why* MLPs in Eqs. (1–3) work well for alignment and *why* the switching modality can be seamlessly adapted to arbitrary inputs (Reviewer 4; echoed by Reviewer 1, who questions how the projector verifies alignment).
- **Motivation for modality switching is questionable** – Sensor configurations in autonomous systems are typically fixed early based on cost/performance; the practical need to dynamically switch between camera-only, LiDAR-only, and fusion is not well justified, especially since the current approach requires prior knowledge of which modality is corrupted (e.g., LiDAR crosstalk is hard to detect autonomously) (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty concerns** – 
  - The MSM scheme essentially amounts to data augmentation (tripling sample count per batch), and the reported performance gains may stem from increased training data volume rather than a fundamentally new training strategy. The paper does not isolate this effect (e.g., training the baseline with comparable data) (Reviewer 2).  
  - The projector module is conceptually similar to BEV encoders like those in BEVFusion, which also align modality features; the paper does not detail how it differs architecturally or functionally from such existing encoders (Reviewer 1, 2).  
  - The claim of being “first to explore robustness in HD map construction” (line 161) overlooks prior work such as the RoboDrive challenge and subsequent studies that already address sensor corruption scenarios and include additional corruption types (Reviewer 2).  
  - The method is “straightforward and lacks new insights”; stacking BEV features and using an MLP projector are “obvious” in sensor-fusion methods. The paper does not clarify whether the stack operation is simple concatenation, nor how it differs from fusion in BEVFusion or PointAugmenting (Reviewer 1).  
- **Incorrect SOTA claim** – Table 1 shows HIMap is the previous state-of-the-art for HD map construction, contradicting the statement in line 32 and line 96 (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experimental clarifications** (questions that overlap with weaknesses/ablations):  
  - *Is the corrupted data used only for zero-shot evaluation, or was it included in training? Was the MapTR baseline trained with corrupted data as well?* (Reviewer 2)  
  - *Does the performance boost come from the increased data (since MSM triples sample count per scenario)?* (Reviewer 2)  
  - *How does the projector module differ from the BEV encoder in BEVFusion?* (Reviewer 2)  
  - *Is modality switching the best approach for handling corruptions, given that even a corrupted sensor may still provide some valuable information?* (Reviewer 2)  
  - *What training strategy (e.g., loss function) ensures the switching modality strategy works during inference?* (Reviewer 1)  
  - *How can we verify that the MLP-based projector actually aligns BEV features, especially since misalignment is mainly caused by inaccurate Camera-to-BEV View Transform? Why not apply the projector only on camera BEV features?* (Reviewer 1)
