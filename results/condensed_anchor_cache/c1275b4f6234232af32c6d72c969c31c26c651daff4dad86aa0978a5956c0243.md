## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AROS, an adversarially robust OOD detection method that combines Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs) with Lyapunov stability theory to create stable embeddings. By forcing both ID and OOD data to converge to equilibrium points in the NODE’s dynamical system, perturbed inputs are driven back to their stable equilibria. Pseudo-OOD embeddings are generated by sampling from low-likelihood regions of the ID feature space, avoiding reliance on external OOD datasets. An orthogonal binary layer further separates the equilibrium points of ID and OOD samples. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1k (and medical data) under clean and strong adversarial settings (PGD, AutoAttack, Adaptive AutoAttack) show substantial gains (e.g., robust detection from 37.8% to 80.1% on CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100). Reviewers rate the paper 5–8; one reviewer is markedly more positive (8) and one more negative (5), with the others at 6.

### Strengths
- **Solid theoretical background.** The use of the Levy-Desplanques Theorem to enforce diagonal dominance in the Jacobian is well-motivated and potentially useful for other NODE models (R1).
- **Orthogonal binary layer.** It effectively resolves practical issues like exploding/vanishing gradients (R1) and meaningfully enhances ID-OOD separation (R1, R2, R4).
- **Comprehensive evaluation.** Extensive testing across multiple datasets (CIFAR, ImageNet, medical) under both clean and strong adversarial attacks (PGD, AutoAttack, Adaptive AutoAttack) with many baselines. Reported performance is impressive (R1, R3, R4).
- **Innovative integration.** The combination of Lyapunov stability and NODEs for OOD detection is novel and well-motivated (R2, R4).
- **Clear writing.** The paper is clearly written and easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Synthetic OOD data generation** (raised by R1, R4; echoed by R3):
  - May expose the model to other vulnerabilities, e.g., an adversary poisoning ID training data with OOD samples that the pseudo-OOD generation does not cover.
  - No justification or empirical validation that sampling from low-likelihood ID feature regions produces realistic or sufficiently challenging OOD samples. The low-likelihood space may be too broad, yielding unchallenging OOD points.
  - Potential to introduce biases that hurt performance on unseen OOD data (R4). An ablation with alternative OOD synthesis strategies is suggested (R4).
  - Visual examples of generated pseudo-OOD data are missing (R3).

- **Mathematical complexity and missing details** (R2, R3, R4):
  - The Lyapunov function construction within the NODE framework and theoretical guarantees for equilibrium stability in high-dimensional spaces are insufficiently explained. Derivation steps for Theorems 1 and 2 are dense and would benefit from expanded exposition or references (R4).
  - The impact of numerical ODE solver approximations on stability guarantees is not addressed (R4).
  - The pseudo-embedding generation formula uses conditional probability notation \(p(r \mid y=j)\) where an inequality would be clearer (R2).
  - Definitions of \([\nabla h_{\phi}(X_{\text{train}})]_{ii}\) and \([\nabla h_{\phi}(X_{\text{train}})]_{ij}\) need clarification (R3).
  - The orthogonal binary layer condition \(z^T z = I\) is ambiguous: it is unclear whether \(z\) has been normalized and whether this is the intended orthogonalization (R3).

- **Loss function ambiguity and parameter tuning** (R2, R3, R4):
  - The custom loss \(\mathcal{L}_{SL}\) contains multiple hyperparameters (\(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3\)) with no clear explanation of how each term contributes to stability and robustness, nor how they interact with the cross-entropy objective (R4). Potential conflict between stability terms and classification is not discussed (R4).
  - Ablation results for \(\beta\) and \(g\) values are listed without analysis or justification for default choices; for instance, why \(\beta=0.001\) and \(\beta=0.025\) give identical results (R3). The choice of \(\gamma\) values is similarly unexplained (R2, R3).

- **Experimental concerns** (R2, R3):
  - Model architecture is not clearly described; different baselines use different architectures in comparisons without a justification for fairness (R2, R3).
  - Experiments rely predominantly on CNN-based models; evaluation with Transformers is missing (R2).
  - Only untargeted attacks are considered; the impact of targeted attacks on OOD detection is not evaluated (R2).
  - Adversarial attack methods are not the latest; adding attacks from 2023/2024 with stronger transferability would strengthen the evaluation (R3).
  - Unusual perturbation magnitudes (e.g., \(\epsilon=128/255\)) cause visible distortion; adding results for standard values like \(16/255\) and \(32/255\) is recommended (R3).
  - Code is not provided (R3).

- **Missing analyses** (R2, R3, R4):
  - No visualizations or empirical insights demonstrating the orthogonal binary layer’s effectiveness in maintaining ID-OOD separation under adversarial perturbations, nor its sensitivity to perturbation levels (R4).
  - No visualizations of generated pseudo-OOD samples (R3).
  - Applicability to backdoor attack detection is questioned but not addressed (R3).
  - Potential vulnerability to poisoning attacks (by injecting OOD into training ID data) is not discussed (R1).
