## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Conditional Activation Steering (CAST), which uses condition vectors to selectively apply refusal behavior vectors based on input context. Reviewers agreed the method is novel and well-demonstrated on models up to 8B parameters, with strong empirical results and clear presentation. Two reviewers rated 8 (R1, R3), while one (R2) rated 6, citing concerns about scalability (no improvement with data), missing baselines, and lack of qualitative understanding of condition vectors. All reviewers noted areas needing additional analysis or clarification.

### Strengths
- **Novel method**: CAST advances activation steering by enabling context-dependent refusal via condition vectors, which is valuable for content moderation and domain-specific assistants (all reviewers).
- **Condition vector concept**: Introduces and demonstrates gating behavior vectors based on input context (R2, R3).
- **Empirical validation**: Extensive experiments across multiple LLMs (≤8B) show effective refusal of harmful prompts without affecting benign responses; robust behavior modification under various categories (R1, R3).
- **Chaining conditionals**: Ability to combine conditions is an interesting contribution (R3).
- **Open-source code**: Authors plan to release code, benefiting reproducibility (R1).
- **Clear presentation**: Well-formatted figures and reproducible setup (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Scalability not shown**: CAST does not improve with data (Figure 6a) and deployment seems clunky (L202–208). One reviewer (R2) considered this a major limitation, lowering their rating.
- **Missing baselines**: No quantitative comparison to a prompting baseline (e.g., instructing the model to refuse based on harmfulness/category). A prompting baseline would help showcase CAST’s effectiveness (R2).
- **Error analysis incomplete**: Failures could stem from condition vector not triggering or behavior vector not robustly modifying behavior. An oracle ablation (applying behavior vector via ground truth harmfulness) is needed to attribute errors (R2).
- **Condition vector generalization not studied**: No experiment measuring robustness of condition vectors within categories (e.g., basic vs. hard prompts that use category language but are irrelevant). Qualitative failure modes are missing (R2).
- **Limited model scale**: All tested models ≤8B parameters. Testing on larger models is needed (R3); one reviewer (R3) noted this may be out-of-scope but would strengthen confidence.
- **No real-world attack testing**: Not tested against jailbreaks (zero-shot, few-shot) or multi-turn conversations (R3; acknowledged as potentially out-of-scope).
- **Missing quantitative summary for figures**: Pie charts (Figure 7) lack aggregated metrics like F1 or true/false positive rates (R1). Only Table 2 gives quantitative results; results for the other ~5 models referenced in Table 1 are absent (R1).
- **Figure 6a metric**: Success rate as “conditions triggered” is insufficient; F1 score would better aggregate true/false positives (R1).
- **Method justification gaps (R1)**:
  - Why PCA (first principal component) is assumed to separate positive/negative examples? Need to show bimodal distribution of activations along that direction.
  - Why tanh for similarity transformation instead of vanilla cosine similarity? No explanation.
  - For multi-conditioning, how is the steering vector chosen when multiple conditions are active? Not clarified.
- **Automatic evaluation unclear**: The paper does not explain how true/false positives for refusals are automatically assessed during hyperparameter grid search (e.g., layer, refusal threshold) (R1).
- **Multi-conditioning trade-offs**: Potential conflicts when many conditions are combined are not examined; prioritization strategies are missing (R1).
- **Obvious claim**: The duality of comparison direction (using threshold c vs. 1-c) is trivial and need not be highlighted (R1).
- **No limitations/future work section**: Missing from the paper (R3).
- **Figure/presentation issues (R3)**:
  - Figure 1 placed too early (p.2) relative to its full description (p.6).
  - t-SNE color schemes make Alpaca vs. Sorry-bench dots hard to distinguish.
  - Figure 8c text too small.
  - Figure 9: label “(c)” not in top-left like others; markers difficult to see (e.g., start marker).
- **Typo**: Prompt counts inconsistent at L365–372 (700+500=1200 per category appears off) (R2).
