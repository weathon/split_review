## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a new theoretical framework for Wasserstein GANs based on a distribution-dependent ordinary differential equation (ODE). The authors introduce W1 Forward Euler (W1-FE), a discretization that incorporates persistent training: at each substep, generator noise \(z\) and the discriminator are frozen while the generator is updated for \(K\) steps. Theorem 4.1 shows that applying a sequence of updates to the generator distribution according to the gradient of the W1 witness function (Kantorovich potential) converges to the optimal distribution in the limit. When persistent training is not used, the algorithms revert to standard WGAN methods. Numerical experiments on a 2D mixture of Gaussians and MNIST demonstrate improved convergence with increasing levels of persistent training. Two reviewers rated the paper 6, one reviewer rated it 5.

### Strengths
- Provides a novel theoretical lens for WGAN training by connecting it to gradient-flow dynamics derived from optimal transport and a distribution-dependent ODE (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Offers a solid mathematical foundation for the approach, including convergence guarantees (Reviewer 1).
- The theory gives useful insight into generator training hyperparameters (e.g., number of generator updates) which is corroborated by experiments (Reviewer 2).
- Both theoretical explanations and experiments on toy and MNIST datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of persistent training, a common practical trick (Reviewer 3).
- Numerical results at various levels of persistent training show clear performance improvement and support the proposed framework (Reviewer 1).
- Uses tools from optimal transport to provide a generalized WGAN training framework (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental validation.** All reviewers note that the experiments are limited in scope:  
  - Only simplified settings (2D mixture of Gaussians) and MNIST are used (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - More standard, larger-scale datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CelebA) should be evaluated and compared to baseline WGAN methods using metrics like FID, IS, and convergence speed (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  - Real-world applications beyond synthetic data are missing (Reviewer 1).

- **Novelty concerns and relation to prior work.**  
  - Reviewer 1 questions whether the approach is a variant of the work by Huang and Malik (2024) on generative modeling by minimizing Wasserstein-2 loss, and asks for clarification on the novelty relative to Huang and Zhang (2023).  
  - Reviewer 2 requests more discussion in the introduction about key differences from the W2-FE paper.  
  - Reviewer 3 states that discretization and persistent training are common tricks in WGAN training; specifically, according to the proof of Proposition 4.1, persistent training seems equivalent to simply increasing the number of generator iterations in the original WGAN. The paper should clarify how the proposed method differs from that simple approach.

- **Mathematical clarity and derivations need improvement.**  
  - The inequality at the top of page 4 (Proposition 3.1) is not sufficiently explained—the connection between the definition of \(J\) in (3.3), the duality formula of the W1 distance in (2.2), and the 1-Lipschitz property of the Kantorovich potential should be explicitly stated (Reviewer 1).  
  - Reviewer 1 requests a proof for the statement that “\(\mu \rightarrow W_1(\mu,\mu_d)\) is strictly convex on \(P_1(X)\).”  
  - The “Mathematical Preliminaries” section lacks citations for definitions and related concepts; explicit references would improve rigor (Reviewer 1).  
  - Notation issues: \(\mu_t, \mu^d\) not defined initially (Eq. 2.2); both \(\varphi\) and \(\phi\) used on line 087; variable \(i\) in Eq. 2.3 undefined; \(m\) in Eq. 2.4 not defined (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - More introduction of \(\nabla \frac{\delta J}{\delta m}\) is needed, along with a derivation of Eq. (3.2) from Eq. (3.1) (Reviewer 3).

- **Issues with discriminator approximation and theory-practice consistency.**  
  - Reviewer 2 questions whether the result in Theorem 4.1 still holds when the distance between the approximated and true Kantorovich potential is bounded, since in practice the discriminator is only approximately trained.  
  - Reviewer 3 notes that obtaining the Kantorovich potential remains the same as in the original WGAN, and the problems for persistent training discussed in Remark 4.2 harm the consistency between theory and practice.

- **Missing experimental details.**  
  - Reviewer 2 asks what number of discriminator steps was used in Figure 1 and whether the theory suggests when accurate \(\varphi\) approximations are more or less important.

- **Limited discussion of limitations of persistent training.**  
  - Reviewer 1 asks how the known limitations of persistent training are addressed or mitigated in the proposed approach.

- **Writing and presentation could be improved.**  
  - The contribution of the work is not conveyed clearly and concisely enough (Reviewer 1).  
  - Some typos and undefined variables exist (Reviewers 2, 3).
