## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the poor performance of locate-then-edit knowledge editing (KE) methods on multi-hop factual recall tasks. Using mechanistic interpretability tools (LogitLens, causal interventions), the authors discover that LLMs process multi-hop queries differently from single-hop queries: multi-hop knowledge is stored in deeper MLP layers, while single-hop knowledge relies on earlier layers. Based on this insight, they propose IFMET, a locate-then-edit approach that edits both shallow and deep MLP layers using supplementary sets constructed from WikiData via SPARQL queries and multi-hop editing prompts. Evaluated on MQuAKE (primarily MQuAKE-3k), IFMET outperforms existing locate-then-edit methods such as MEMIT, ROME, and Mello.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive analysis in Section 3 using LogitLens and causal interventions to identify that multi-hop knowledge is stored in deeper MLP layers, whereas single-hop knowledge is stored in earlier layers. This is a novel theoretical insight about implicit subject accumulation in middle layers.
- The work fills an important gap in KE by explicitly addressing multi-hop queries, which prior locate-then-edit methods consistently struggle with.
- The proposed IFMET is well-motivated by the mechanistic analysis, and the experimental results on MQuAKE (Tables 3 and 4) show significant improvements over strong baselines (Mello, MEMIT, ROME) for multi-hop factual recall.
- Clear writing that makes the method easy to follow.
- The use of both LogitLens and causal intervention experiments provides a thorough empirical analysis of information flow in LLMs.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and organization issues.** Tables 5 and 6 are referenced in the main text (Section 5.2) but placed in the appendix, and the term "base" in those tables is not defined; dataset details for Table 4 (e.g., whether MQuAKE is consistently used) are missing; several subfigures (e.g., Figure 6) are too small; there are typos (“cizizenship” line 387, “Mardrid” Figure 1); bibliography style is inconsistent. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Insufficient analysis of Table 3 results.** Performance after editing is often worse than the “original” setting, yet no discussion explains this drop. It is unclear whether “original” refers to accuracy on the unedited answer or on the correct answer; the metric should be clarified and explained. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing ablation studies.** The paper does not experimentally isolate the contribution of editing later layers versus merely expanding the support set. An ablation comparing different layers with and without the supplementary set is needed to show that gains come from targeting deeper layers, not from the support set alone. Also absent are ablations on the size of the supplementary dataset and the choice of specific layers to edit. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Outdated model choice.** Experiments are only conducted on GPT-J (6B). The findings and improvements may not generalize to more recent architectures like Llama-3. (Reviewer 2)
- **No evaluation of generality and locality.** The impact of edits on related facts (generality) and unrelated facts (locality) is not measured. Metrics that quantify unintended side effects on other knowledge are needed to ensure that editing does not corrupt the model’s overall knowledge base. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of efficiency discussion.** No time complexity or per-sample editing time is reported for IFMET, nor is there any analysis of how the supplementary set size affects runtime. Such information is critical for practical applicability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Dependence on external structured knowledge.** IFMET requires constructing supplementary sets using WikiData and SPARQL queries. This is not feasible for non-factual, non-encyclopedic, or unstructured data, and the SPARQL conversion may introduce biases. The method should be evaluated on unstructured facts (e.g., as in [1]) and alternatives for generating supplementary sets internally should be discussed. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Lack of discussion vs. RAG-based editing.** The paper does not address why locate-then-edit is preferable over strong retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches for multi-hop editing. A short discussion is needed, even without direct experimental comparison. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited benchmark coverage.** Only MQuAKE-3k is used; experiments on MQuAKE-T or other multi-hop KE benchmarks are missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **No explanation of batch size trends.** In Table 3, IFMET’s accuracy improves with increasing edit batch size while baselines degrade. The paper provides no analysis or hypothesis for this behavior. (Reviewer 4)
- **Method may not handle unstructured facts.** The reliance on structured templates and WikiData raises the question of whether IFMET can handle editing of unstructured facts as in “Updating Language Models with Unstructured Facts: Towards Practical Knowledge Editing”. (Reviewer 2, question)
