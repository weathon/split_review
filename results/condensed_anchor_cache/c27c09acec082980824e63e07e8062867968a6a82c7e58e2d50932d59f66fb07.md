## Merged Review

### Summary
GenLLaVA is a composite large multimodal model that unifies image understanding, generation, and editing. The authors construct a new multimodal instruction-following dataset by aggregating and selecting from existing datasets (including understanding, generation, and editing data) using GPT-4V. The model combines Mistral (language), SigLIP (vision encoder), and StableDiffusion (generation) through a single-stage instruction tuning pipeline, using a Q-former to connect LLM hidden states to the diffusion model. Evaluations show GenLLaVA outperforms LLaVA on understanding and is competitive with models like GILL, AnyGPT, and Unified-IO 2 on generative tasks. Scores from the four reviewers are 3, 5, 6, 3 – a split between positive (reviewers 2, 3) and negative (reviewers 1, 4) assessments.

### Strengths
- **New dataset curation**: The construction of a multimodal instruction-following dataset that amalgamates image understanding, generation, and editing data is innovative and addresses the need for diverse training data (R1). This dataset could be highly valuable for future research and applications (R2). The aggregation of supervised datasets across multiple tasks (text understanding, image comprehension, generation, editing) is noted (R4).
- **Single-stage training strategy**: Departing from the traditional multi-stage pipeline to a single-stage training recipe simplifies training while maintaining performance (R1).
- **Strong performance across tasks**: GenLLaVA shows competitive or superior results on understanding compared to LLaVA, and on generation/editing compared to GILL, AnyGPT, and Unified-IO 2 (R1). It demonstrates strong performance on image understanding, image editing, and image generation simultaneously (R2). The model is strong on both visual understanding and generation with relatively comprehensive evaluations (R3).
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-presented and easy to follow, with a clear goal, approach, and results (R2, R3, R4).
- **Important direction**: The work moves toward a unified MLLM capable of both image generation and understanding, which is a significant direction for MLLM development (R1).
- **Improvements over current approaches**: Several improvements on existing methods for building MLLMs are proposed and their effectiveness is demonstrated (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of methodological originality**: The model framework is essentially the same as GILL – translating LLM hidden representations into text embeddings for StableDiffusion – with only component substitution (vision encoder, LLM, diffusion model) and the choice of a Q-former as the generation head. No genuinely novel design is presented (R1, R4). The work is incremental, as similar unified models (e.g., SEED-X, Unified-IO 2) already exist (R3). The claim of being “first to achieve such capability” is unfounded (R4).
- **Insufficient methodological detail**: The methodology section lacks depth, especially on how the LLM outputs are integrated with the diffusion model for generation and editing. Specifics such as attention mask configurations, input/target output formats during generation, and the loss functions used are missing (R1).
- **Dataset novelty limited**: The dataset is composed entirely of existing datasets. The selection rationale (e.g., why certain datasets are chosen and why only a subset of IPr2Pr is used) is not discussed, reducing the claimed contribution (R3).
- **Inadequate experimental support**:
  - Table 1 shows GenLLaVA underperforms in image generation compared to peer models, and no comparison is provided against other multimodal understanding models (R1).
  - On MMB, GenLLaVA (7B LLM) scores 66.8, whereas ShareGPT scores 68.8 (R1). The model also trails recent SOTA like LLaVA-OV (80.8) significantly (R2). The authors speculate reasons (inter-task conflicts) but provide no ablation to confirm.
  - Baselines are outdated/underperforming; comparisons with Emu, Emu-2, SEED-LLaMA, SEED-X, Mini-Gemini are missing (R4).
  - No ablation studies compare single-stage vs. multi-stage training, despite single-stage being a claimed contribution (R1, R2).
  - Ablation studies on inter-task conflicts are insufficient: Table 2(a) shows a significant drop when handling all three tasks, and task tokens boost performance, but the underlying reasons for this improvement are not explored (R1). Whether explicit task format definitions are essential during inference remains unclear (R1).
- **Resources not provided**: The authors promise to open-source data, code, and weights, but none were made available for review. Since the dataset is a key contribution, this weakens verification (R2).
- **Practical limitation**: Task tokens (e.g., [T2I], [I2T]) are needed to achieve balanced performance, but real user queries are unlikely to include such tokens. Alternative, more natural approaches for user intent understanding are not discussed (R3).
- **Missing domain expert baselines**: Including reference scores from domain experts like MGIE (image editing) would give a more comprehensive picture of how the combined model compares (R3).
