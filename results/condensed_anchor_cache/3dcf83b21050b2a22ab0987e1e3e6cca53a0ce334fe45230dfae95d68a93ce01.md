## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes gradient flow training of a simplified transformer consisting of a single linear self-attention layer (no softmax, MLP, layer normalization, or positional embeddings) on a histogram-like task: given a sequence of characters, output a vector of counts of each letter. The analysis reduces the training dynamics to those of a two-layer linear neural network where the first layer is a diagonal matrix. Under one assumption (which is empirically verified), a complete gradient flow analysis is provided, and extensions to variants of the histogram problem are discussed.

### Strengths
- The writing is clear and the main result is easy to understand (Reviewer 1, confidence 4).
- The construction for solving the dynamics system in Theorem 2 is skillful (Reviewer 1).
- Multiple variants of the histogram task are considered (Reviewer 2).
- The paper explores a connection between self-attention and linear networks (Reviewer 2).
- The structure of the problem is exploited to break gradient flow analysis into multiple one‑dimensional problems (Reviewer 2).
- Proofs of theorems and lemmas are provided and detailed (Reviewer 3).
- Assumption 3.1 for the theoretical results is reasonable and experimentally justified (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-motivated (Reviewer 3). *(Note: Reviewer 1 disagrees on motivation – see Weaknesses.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and relevance.** The main setup is not well motivated. It is unclear why one should care about histogram‑like tasks: self‑attention can easily model them, but the paper does not argue how this analysis yields insights that generalize to more complex, practical scenarios (Reviewer 1, confidence 4; Reviewer 3 disagrees and considers the paper well‑motivated).  
- **Over‑simplified model.** The paper considers a very simplified setup: a single linear attention layer, no positional embeddings, no softmax, no MLP, no layer normalization, and the alphabet size equals the embedding dimension. These choices do not reflect practical transformer architectures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The absence of multi‑head attention, positional encodings, and feed‑forward networks limits the scope and relevance to understanding real‑world transformer training dynamics (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- **Limited task.** The histogram tasks are far removed from the sequence‑to‑sequence nature of common transformer applications (e.g., language modeling, machine translation) and do not explore long‑range dependencies (Reviewers 2, 3). The paper does not demonstrate whether results can be extended to such tasks (Reviewer 3 question).  
- **Lack of experiments.** The paper provides no empirical validation of its theoretical results. Experiments showing convergence behavior of the loss function, the impact of different initialization strategies (random and bad), and the effect of the simplified attention mechanism are missing (Reviewers 2, 3). Specifically, the decay behavior of the loss \(l\) in Theorem 1 under random and bad initialization should be shown (Reviewer 3 question).  
- **Insufficient comparison with prior work.** The paper criticizes prior work for stylized assumptions but then introduces its own new problem and assumptions without clearly articulating how this framework relates to or improves upon existing analyses, making it difficult to assess the significance of the results (Reviewer 1).  
- **Presentation issues.** Section 4 repeatedly mentions Eq. (2), which is only introduced later in Section 5. Theorem 2 is referenced multiple times before being formally introduced. The points in Remark 1 are not clearly explained as extensions of Theorem 2 (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unaddressed questions.** Reviewer 1’s questions are the same as the weaknesses above. Reviewer 3 asks about extension to language modeling or translation, which remains unanswered.  
- **Disagreement on contribution.** Reviewer 1 rated the contribution as poor, whereas Reviewers 2 and 3 rated it as fair. This contrast indicates that at least one reviewer found the results insufficiently significant.
