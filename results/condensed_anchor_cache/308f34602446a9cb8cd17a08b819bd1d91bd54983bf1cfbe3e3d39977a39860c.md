## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Binary Reward Labeling (BRL), which converts offline preference feedback into ±1 scalar rewards so that any reward-based offline RL algorithm can be applied. The authors provide theoretical connections to existing PBRL techniques and evaluate on D4RL-based preference datasets, reporting performance comparable to using true rewards and better than some recent PBRL baselines. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the theoretical analysis for non-overlapping trajectories is a contribution. However, the majority of reviewers (R2, R3, R4, each score 3) are critical, while one reviewer (R1, score 5) is more positive, highlighting the method’s simplicity and theoretical insights. Major concerns include poor writing, unclear motivation, missing comparisons with recent baselines (e.g., PT, OPRL), insufficient benchmarks (D4RL is known to be insensitive to reward accuracy), incomplete theoretical coverage (overlapping trajectories and inconsistent labels unaddressed), and unjustified advantage over the Bradley-Terry model.

### Strengths
- The problem of converting preference feedback into a form usable by standard offline RL algorithms is fundamental and the proposed approach is simple and novel (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The theoretical analysis establishes a connection between BRL and existing PBRL techniques, particularly for non-overlapping trajectory cases (R1, R2, R4).
- On D4RL tasks, the method achieves performance comparable to training on true rewards and outperforms some baselines, showing impressive improvement (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper includes experimental simulation for both overlapping and non-overlapping trajectory scenarios (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation quality is poor.** There are formatting errors (e.g., Definition 4.2 missing period, comma at line start; R1), confusing terminology (“link function”, “link-loss function”; R2), an unclear and incomprehensible Section 4.1 (R2), overly concise pseudocode (R2), poorly formatted citations (R3), redundant related work/theory sections, and inaccurate statements in the introduction (lines 47-49, 72-76; R4). The subtitle “Offline standard RL algorithms are model-based.” in Section 4.2 is misleading (R1).
- **Motivation is unclear.** The paper claims to bridge PbRL and standard RL, but PbRL’s core difficulty is learning accurate rewards from human preferences, not merely relabeling. The contribution is poorly justified (R3). The approach would be better motivated from the perspective of overlapping trajectories with inconsistent labels (R4).
- **Missing comparison with recent offline PbRL baselines.** The paper does not compare against Preference Transformer (PT; Kim et al. 2023), OPRL (Shin et al. 2023), or Flow to Better (Zhang et al. 2023) (R1, R2, R3, R4). The authors’ claim that no empirical study works in exactly their setting is false, as PT and OPRL are directly applicable (R3). No comparison is made against the commonly used Bradley-Terry (BT) reward model (R3).
- **Insufficient benchmarks.** Experiments are limited to D4RL Mujoco tasks, which are known to be insensitive to reward function accuracy (Li et al. 2024; R1, R3). Additional domains such as Meta-World, Antmaze, Kitchen, and Adroit are needed to validate the method (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Theoretical analysis is incomplete.** It only covers non-overlapping trajectories and does not address overlapping trajectories with inconsistent labels (R4). More rigorous analysis under function approximation is needed (R1). The paper does not justify why binary ±1 labeling outperforms the BT model theoretically (R3).
- **Soundness concerns.** Binary ±1 labeling is theoretically correct but may overfit the preference dataset (R1). Performance in scenarios with overlapping trajectories and inconsistent labels is not discussed (R4). A reviewer (R4) asks how the algorithm performs under such conditions and what the advantages are over traditional reward models.
