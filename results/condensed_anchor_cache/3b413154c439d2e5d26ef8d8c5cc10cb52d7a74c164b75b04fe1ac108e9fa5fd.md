## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SafeDreamer, a safe model-based RL algorithm built on DreamerV3. It integrates Lagrangian-based methods (PID Lagrangian, Augmented Lagrangian) with constrained cross-entropy planning (CCEM) to achieve near-zero cost in safety tasks, especially vision-only ones. Three variants are introduced: OSRP (CCEM planning), OSRP-Lag (online constrained planning with PID Lagrangian), and BSRP-Lag (policy optimization with Augmented Lagrangian, no online planning). Evaluations on Safety-Gymnasium show strong cost and reward performance compared to several model-free and model-based baselines. Reviewers agree the empirical results are impressive and the problem is important, but differ on novelty, presentation clarity, baseline fairness, and depth of analysis. One reviewer (score 8, high confidence) finds the paper well-written and technically sound; two others (score 6, high confidence) find contributions incremental and presentation or empirical rigor lacking; one (score 6, moderate confidence) sees the method as promising but poorly presented.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and significance**: The problem of safe RL, especially achieving zero cost in vision-only tasks, is well motivated (R4) and important (R2). The approach addresses a known limitation (Lagrangian methods fail to reach zero cost) by leveraging world models for internal planning, which is promising (R1, R3).
- **Novelty of combination**: Combining DreamerV3, CCEM, and Lagrangian methods into SafeDreamer is novel (R2, R4) and provides a useful perspective for solving sparse-cost safety problems (R3). The three variants offer a clear progression of planning vs. non-planning (R4).
- **Empirical results**: SafeDreamer achieves near-zero cost returns while maintaining good reward returns across multiple Safety-Gymnasium tasks (R1, R2, R3). The results are demonstrated against many relevant model-free and model-based baselines (R2, R4). The work’s robustness in vision-only scenarios is especially highlighted (R3).
- **Technical quality**: The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow according to R3 and partially R2. The algorithms, figures, and experimental results show high technical rigor (R3). The provided codebase and thorough supplementary material are valuable (R4).
- **Practical utility**: The method effectively balances long-term rewards and costs, a crucial aspect of safe RL (R3). The use of a world model and cost estimator allows high safety performance even when the cost threshold is not reached (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation**:
  - The differences between OSRP, OSRP-Lag, and BSRP-Lag are not well motivated or explained (R1). Mixing three algorithms into a single algorithm diagram is “terrible writing practice” and increases confusion (R1). The notation is misaligned between the text and Algorithm 1 (R1). In Section 3.2, the description of sampled trajectories (part from a normal action distribution, part from current policy) is confusing and poorly presented (R1). One reviewer (R1) rated presentation as 1 (poor); others rated it 3-4.
  - Figure 1 (intro) and Figure 2 are placed awkwardly (R2). Terminology such as “return and cost return” is inconsistent (R2). Grammar errors appear throughout (e.g., “proffer”, “using policy”, missing articles) (R2).
- **Novelty and contribution**:
  - SafeDreamer is a straightforward combination of DreamerV3, CCEM, PID Lagrangian, and Augmented Lagrangian (R2, R4). Little analysis is given to understand the behavior of the combined components (R2). The technical contribution is incremental, though the experimental part is solid (R3, R4). No theory or ablation study is provided to justify the specific choices (R2, R4).
  - Missing discussion of alternatives: other online planning algorithms (e.g., Bayesian methods from As et al. 2022), other policy optimization methods (e.g., beyond Augmented Lagrangian), and the possible effect of combining both online planning and Lagrangian policy optimization (R4).
- **Empirical rigor and fairness**:
  - The number of runs for experiments is not stated (R2). Key hyperparameters (model architectures, planning samples, gradient steps) for baselines are not reported, making it unclear if comparisons are fair (R2). The model-free baselines were not given a CNN or RNN for vision/partial observations, while SafeDreamer uses both; this likely explains their poor performance, which is inconsistent with prior works (R2). The model-free baselines are old, missing recent methods like Saute RL, ROSARL (R2).
  - The performance of model-free baselines (e.g., TRPO-Lag, PPO-Lag) shows reward returns decreasing to zero early in training—this does not match prior results and questions the validity of the empirical comparison (R2). The authors later (in rebuttal) note that baselines from the OmniSafe codebase performed worse than safety-starter-agents, suggesting hyperparameter differences beyond the authors’ control (R2).
- **Related work**:
  - No related work section in the main paper; relegating it to the appendix is insufficient (R2, R4). Missing relevant works: Saute RL (model-free), ROSARL (model-free), STS-MBRL (model-based) (R2); Ma et al. 2022 and He et al. 2023 on cost function design (R3). The appendix lacks a broader perspective on safe RL and does not critique the use of simple cumulative constraints (R4).
- **Missing analysis and experiments**:
  - No experiments in other domains (e.g., safe autonomous driving) to test generalizability (R1, R2). No evaluation on different training/test environments to check world model generalization (R1). The discussion of results is limited—why reward performance differs across variants is not explained (R4). Some references are wrong in Section 5.2 (R4).
  - Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms (missing experiments, ablations, or analysis):
    - Does BSRP-Lag still run planning at test time? (R1)
    - How does world model generalizability affect zero-cost claims? (R1)
    - Why are different cost thresholds used across environments (Fig 6)? Threshold 0 seems most natural; OSRP and OSRP-Lag appear overly conservative at threshold ~14 in SafetyPointButton1 (R3).
    - Why is the cost predictor head real-valued instead of a binary classifier? What is the effect of scaling costs by 10.0? (R4)
    - How scalable is SafeDreamer to complex tasks? What control frequency can be achieved in real-world deployment? (R3)
    - Can OSRP-Lag converge to zero cost? (R3)
- **Partial disagreement among reviewers**:
  - One reviewer (R3, score 8) finds the paper well-written, technically rigorous, and the contribution solid; they consider the incremental combination acceptable given strong experiments. Another reviewer (R2, score 6, high confidence) rates soundness as 2 (fair) and contribution as 3, strongly criticizing baselines and clarity. R4 (score 6, high confidence) weakly rejects due to lack of discussion and originality, though acknowledges strong results. R1 (score 6, confidence 4) is positive about the method but critical of presentation.
