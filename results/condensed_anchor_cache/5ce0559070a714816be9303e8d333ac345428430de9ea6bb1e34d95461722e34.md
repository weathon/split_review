## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Multi-modal Guided Real-World Face Restoration (MGFR), which uses a dual-control adapter and two-stage training to incorporate attribute text prompts, high-quality reference images, and identity information from a diffusion prior for face restoration. It also introduces the Reface-HQ dataset (23K+ high-resolution images, 5K identities). All reviewers agree the topic is interesting and the writing is clear. Reviewers 1 and 2 score the paper 8 (accept), while Reviewer 3 scores 6, finding the framework novel but raising implementation complexity concerns. There is disagreement on novelty: Reviewer 3 considers the multi-modal integration and adapter design novel, whereas Reviewer 1 states the main contribution is the dataset and applying existing methods to a new task. Reviewers 1 and 3 both note missing ablations; Reviewers 2 and 3 both call for a thorough computational cost analysis.

### Strengths
- Motivation: Using multi-modality to resolve ambiguity in face restoration is well-justified (reviewers 1,2).
- Diffusion prior: Its use to model a probability given the input makes sense (reviewer 1).
- Dataset: The Reface-HQ dataset (23K+ images, 5K identities) is a valuable resource for the community (reviewers 1,2,3).
- Novel framework: The dual-control adapter and two-stage training strategy that effectively combine multi-modal priors is considered a significant advancement by reviewer 3; reviewer 1 disagrees on novelty.
- Performance: MGFR achieves superior visual quality under severe degradation, with effective identity preservation and attribute correction (reviewer 3).
- Mitigation of false illusions: The method reduces generation of false facial attributes and identities (reviewer 3).
- Writing: The manuscript is clear and easy to follow (reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty disagreement: Reviewer 1 states the method is not new and the main contribution is the dataset and using an existing approach on a new task; reviewer 3 considers the framework novel.
- Missing ablation on diffusion prior effectiveness (reviewer 1).
- Missing comparison to recent works: DiffBIR, DR2, SUPIR, BFRffusion are absent from Table 1 (reviewer 2).
- Computational complexity & efficiency:
  - Running time and memory consumption are not shown to be superior to DiffBIR and DR2; a detailed analysis of FLOPs and memory per component is needed to justify practical feasibility (reviewer 2).
  - The paper lacks a breakdown of FLOPs and parameter counts in the main comparison and ablation study, making it hard to assess efficiency and trade-offs (reviewers 2,3).
  - Integration of multiple modalities and the dual-control adapter likely introduces significant overhead; memory footprint for high-resolution images should be discussed (reviewer 3).
- Evaluation metrics: A more thorough discussion of metric selection (e.g., why PSNR/SSIM are used despite limited perceptual alignment), their limitations, and sensitivity to different artifacts is needed (reviewer 3).
- Ablation studies insufficient:
  - No isolation of each modality (e.g., attribute prompts without reference images, or vice versa) (reviewer 3).
  - Impact of different types of attribute prompts (simple vs. complex) and reference images (similar vs. dissimilar) not analyzed (reviewer 3).
- Missing analyses requested (overlap with criticisms):
  - Alternative design: Can the dual-control adapter be replaced by a transformer? A comparison is missing (reviewer 1).
  - Diversity: The model can produce different results per sample; analysis of output diversity and guidance on sample selection is missing (reviewer 1).
  - Scalability: Extension to real-world video face restoration is not discussed (reviewer 2).
  - Diffusion model foundation: How does performance vary with different diffusion model versions? (reviewer 3).
- Presentation: Figures 4 and 5 have too small fonts and unclear color denotations, reducing readability (reviewer 3).
