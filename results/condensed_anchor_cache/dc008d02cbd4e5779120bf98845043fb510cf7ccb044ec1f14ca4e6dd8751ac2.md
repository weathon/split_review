## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the computational problem of estimating the statistical similarity \(s_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=1-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)\) between product distributions (Bernoulli trials). Exact computation is #P-hard. The authors propose an FPTAS by reducing the problem to #MinPMFAtLeast, then to a new multidimensional #MaskedKnapsack problem, and finally applying techniques from Gopalan et al. (2010). Reviewers are split: two find the contribution strong (scores 6,6), one moderate (5), and two critical (scores 3,3). All acknowledge the theoretical value, but concerns center on missing algorithmic detail, unclear motivation, typos in proofs, the need for polynomial-bit probability representations, lack of comparison with prior TV-distance techniques, and suitability for ICLR.

### Strengths
- **Important open problem:** Statistical similarity is a basic measure with natural interpretations; previous FPTAS for TV distance did not carry over because a multiplicative approximation of \(d_{TV}\) does not give one for \(1-d_{TV}\). The paper closes this gap for product distributions. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Novel knapsack variant:** The introduction of the multidimensional #MaskedKnapsack problem is a novel extension of the classic knapsack, and the reduction to it is “neat” and “may be of independent interest.” (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Theoretical contributions are solid:** The chain of reductions is logically sound and supported by rigorous proofs. (Reviewer 1)
- **Relevance to ML:** TV distance and statistical similarity are important in machine learning, so progress on approximation algorithms is valuable. (Reviewer 2, also implied by Reviewer 1)
- **Minority positive view on motivation and proof simplicity:** One reviewer (5) states “they provide enough motivations, the proofs seem simple,” contrasting with other reviewers who found motivation weak.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing explicit algorithm/pseudocode:** The paper claims an FPTAS but does not give explicit steps or pseudocode, making it hard to assess practicality, actual complexity, and resource requirements. (Reviewer 1)
- **No comparison with prior TV-distance techniques:** The authors do not explain why the specific algorithmic approaches (e.g., sampling, dynamic programming) used for the TV-distance FPTAS cannot be adapted for statistical similarity. A detailed discussion of inherent differences is needed. (Reviewers 2, 5)
- **Unrealistic probability representation assumption:** The algorithm requires probabilities to be representable with polynomially many bits. This assumption is not stated in the main theorem, and it raises concerns about practical applicability when probabilities have complex or infinite representations. Why is it hard to eliminate this requirement? (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Poor motivation and vague applications:** The problem motivation is weak; the paper gives only vague mentions of machine learning, hypothesis testing, etc., without concrete use cases of relative approximation of statistical similarity between product distributions. (Reviewer 3)  [Contrast: Reviewer 5 finds motivation sufficient.]
- **Many typos and errors in proofs, especially Proposition 2:**
  - Line 242: the equality for \(m_{\min}\) should be an inequality (it provides a lower bound, not the exact nonzero minimum). (Reviewers 4, 5)
  - Line 245: definition of \(V\) is problematic (should be \(\ge\) rather than \(\le\)? claims \(V\ge 1\) without restricting to \(x\) with non-zero \(\min(P(x),Q(x))\)). (Reviewer 5)
  - Line 272: \(n_u = 2^n\) stated without proper justification. (Reviewer 5)
  - Line 259: definition of \(B_i\) needs to explicitly say sum of \(Y_x\) over the interval \([(1+\epsilon)^{i-1}, (1+\epsilon)^i)\). (Reviewer 5)
  - Proposition 2 statement: “approximation of a polynomially many #MinPMFAtLeast instances” is unclear – should read “computing a \((1+\epsilon)\)-approximation of polynomially many instances of…”. (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation needs significant improvement:** multiple typos throughout (e.g., lines 56, 69, 70, 78, 287, 364, 365, 417). (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
- **No implementation or testing:** The paper would be strengthened by experiments or a proof-of-concept implementation. (Reviewers 2, 5)
- **Running time not specified:** The paper does not state the overall running time of the FPTAS. (Reviewer 5)
- **Numerical stability not discussed:** No mention of potential numerical issues in implementing the FPTAS. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing definition in Lemma 15:** The dimension parameter \(n\) should be explicitly defined. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited scope:** The approximation is only for product distributions (but reviewers acknowledge this gives a complete picture for that case). (Reviewer 4)
- **Suitability for ICLR:** Some reviewers feel the work is more suited for theory conferences like SODA or ICALP. (Reviewers 3, 5)
