- Scores: 5, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes an identifiability theory for latent variables in nonlinear causal models using only single-domain data. The authors introduce a reduction process that converts general structural causal models (SCMs) into a powerset bipartite graph (PBG) structure, and prove that latent variables can be identified up to an invertible transformation under conditions of global invertibility, independence of latent variables, and a new minimality condition. Experiments on synthetic data are provided. The reviewers noted the importance of the problem but raised significant concerns about the proof of the reduction, the restrictiveness of assumptions, insufficient novelty compared to prior work, and weak practical significance.

### Strengths

- The problem of latent variable identifiability in nonlinear causal models is fundamental and crucial for causal discovery, disentangled representation learning, domain adaptation, and generalization to distributional shifts. All reviewers acknowledged this importance, with one reviewer emphasizing it strongly.
- The identifiability analysis for the specific powerset bipartite graph structure seems robust, and the relationship between the unknown nature of latent dimensions and the minimality condition is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- Assumptions are discussed in detail with examples (Reviewer 3).
- Theoretical results are sound with detailed proofs, and the paper is well-organized and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Conversion of SCMs to PBG is unproven.** The claim that any underlying SCM can be reduced to an equivalent SCM with a powerset bipartite graph is not rigorously justified. No explicit reduction process, transformations, or assumptions are given, and the correctness is not proved (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). One reviewer noted that in latent space, multiple graph structures can generate the same observed data, making this claim seem overly strong (Reviewer 2). The paper does not clarify which graph structures can be reduced, which cannot, nor the costs of reduction.

2. **Assumptions are too restrictive or unverified.**  
   - The assumption that observable variables are children of latent variables (no direct paths among observed variables) is considered unrealistic and limits practical applicability (Reviewer 1, 4).  
   - The claim that all assumptions (invertibility, independence, minimality) are “mild and necessary” is not fully justified. Only the necessity of minimality is proven (Proposition 5.1); necessity of invertibility and independence is not shown. It is unclear whether a non-invertible generating function would necessarily lead to non-identifiability (Reviewer 4).  
   - The minimality condition is not shown to be mild; it has not been compared with assumptions in related works (Reviewer 4).  
   - Theorem 1 implicitly assumes mutual independence across dimensions of each latent variable (e.g., \(z[i] \perp z[j]\) for each latent vector \(z\)), but this is not explicitly stated in the theorem statement. All assumptions must be clearly listed to avoid ambiguity (Reviewer 2).

3. **Insufficient novelty and overlap with prior work.** The theory and methodology closely resemble a prior work (referred to as [1] by Reviewer 3):  
   - The decomposition of the SCM into basis models is nearly identical (e.g., Fig. 2 in [1] shows a similar basis model).  
   - Identifiability theorems for the basis model differ mainly by making the minimality condition explicit, while the prior work assumes known dimensions (implicitly ensuring minimality). The subspace span assumption in [1] may hold asymptotically, reducing the practical difference.  
   - Both manuscripts use an iterative identification of basis models for global identifiability.  
   The novel aspects are not sufficiently pronounced, and clearer differentiation is needed (Reviewer 3).  
   - A comparison with sparsity-based nonlinear ICA approaches (e.g., Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2022) is missing. The paper should articulate how its sparsity constraints differ and what advantages they offer (Reviewer 2).

4. **The identified latent variables lack physical meaning.** Even with the no-direct-path assumption, the PBG-SCM corresponds to many SCMs. A latent variable in the PBG combines several exogenous noises from the original SCM, so the number of latent variables differs from the original. Identifying latent variables in the PBG is far from identifying the original causal model or causal relations among latents. For comparison: a prior work [1] (unnamed) identifies a simplified SCM without the no-direct-path assumption and preserves the number of latent variables and causal relations among observed variables; another prior work [2] (unnamed) makes the no-direct-path assumption but can identify latent variables and their causal relations (Reviewer 4).

5. **Experimental verification is insufficient and questionable.**  
   - No experiments on real-world datasets are provided, weakening claims about the practical relevance of the PBG (Reviewer 2, 4).  
   - Synthetic experiments use MLPs for data generation, but MLPs are typically not invertible, contradicting the invertibility assumption required by the theorems. Thus the experiments cannot rigorously verify the theoretical claims (Reviewer 3).  
   - The algorithm based on autoencoders requires the latent dimension \(z\) to be known; in practice this is unknown, and performance degrades with inaccurate dimension. The paper does not address this practical challenge or sensitivity to this hyperparameter (Reviewer 4).  
   - A metric like the mean correlation coefficient (MCC) would be more appropriate for evaluating component-wise identifiability (Theorem 2) than \(R^2\), which is typically used for block-wise identifiability (Reviewer 3).  
   - For evaluating general PBG-SCM identification, a proof for Theorem 1 should also address the existence of edges, not just the non-existence (Reviewer 3).  
   - The claim that “multi-domain data are usually hard to acquire” is overly strong, and the cited references do not support it (Reviewer 2).

6. **Notation and clarity issues.**  
   - Notation such as \(j\&2^l\) (line 189) uses bitwise operations without explanation, making the formulation inaccessible (Reviewer 3).  
   - The definition of an SCM as a triplet (G,F,P) should cite a standard reference (e.g., Peters et al., 2017) (Reviewer 4).  
   - A proof sketch for Theorem 1 would improve readability (Reviewer 4).

7. **Questions about proofs and assumptions.**  
   - A specific step in the proof (line 777) states that \(\hat{v}_2\) does not change because the derivative of \(v_2\) w.r.t. \(s_1\) is zero; this needs explanation (Reviewer 3).  
   - The paper claims that previous methods require no connections among observed variables for identifying latent structure; clarification is needed whether this is necessary only for latent variable identification or also for structure learning (Reviewer 3).

**Minority positive view:** One reviewer (score 5, confidence 3) found the problem crucial and the proposed approach promising despite the weaknesses, while the three other reviewers (scores 3, confidence 3 and 4) were more critical, especially regarding novelty, practical significance, and insufficient justification.
