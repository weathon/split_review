## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers evaluated the paper (scores: 5, 3, 5, 5; median 5). One reviewer (score 3) was substantially more critical, finding the contributions incremental, the writing unclear, the experiments inadequate, and the related work superficial. The other three praised the novelty of the two-stage framework (AutoPrompter + LLM refinement) and its relevance for instruction-based forecasting, but all raised concerns about experimental rigor, missing comparisons, dataset details, and practical validation. The main points of consensus are a strong appreciation for the problem motivation combined with the need for more comprehensive and realistic evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Novel two-stage framework** for integrating temporal and textual data (AutoPrompter for alignment, then LLM refinement). The prompting method is novel and addresses a gap in multimodal forecasting.
- **Proof-of-concept using synthetic data** with instruction-following evaluation is a clean way to validate the idea (Reviewer 1). The data generation workflow for instruction-based forecasting is compatible with LLMs (Reviewer 2).
- **Zero-shot testing** on unseen textual descriptions helps rule out overfitting to a narrow instruction set (Reviewer 1).
- **Comprehensive ablation studies and comparative evaluations** are presented (Reviewer 2; although the same reviewer later criticizes the experiments as inadequate – contrast preserved).
- **Well-organized presentation** according to Reviewers 3 and 4: methodology is easy to follow, figures/tables are clear and convey the improvements. (Minority contrast: Reviewer 2 finds poor structure and unclear explanations – see Weaknesses.)
- **Relevance of the problem**: text-aided forecasting and expert opinion integration are timely and practical (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and benchmarks**:  
  - No comparison against **pure LLM approaches** (e.g., GPT‑4, LLaMA 3 with history+text in prompt; cite LLM Processes, Requiema 2024).  
  - Limited comparisons with **other multimodal forecasting models**; key models such as UniTime are omitted.  
  - For Table 4, results for the proposed model are not shown; only TimeLLM results are provided, making it impossible to assess whether TITSP succeeds with incorrect textual information.  
  - Insufficient **statistical significance testing** to validate performance claims.

- **Methodological concerns**:  
  - **Mismatch between Eq. 3 and Fig. 3**: Eq. 3 describes an affine function in the forecast window, but Fig. 3 shows noise in that window (Reviewer 1).  
  - **Incremental technical contribution**: cross‑attention and VQ‑VAE offer no substantial improvement over established techniques (Reviewer 2).  
  - **Data leakage risk in synthetic data generation**: no detailed explanation of how leakage between text and forecasting targets is avoided (Reviewer 3).  
  - **Circular evaluation**: datasets are tailored from existing data based on the training instructions; the order compliance rate metric is similarly tailored (Reviewer 4).  
  - **Simplistic experimental instructions**: training instructions are limited and not realistic; simpler alternatives for embedding instructions might have been equally effective (Reviewer 4).

- **Dataset and experimental details missing**:  
  - Sample counts, history length, forecasting horizon are not reported; the impact of forecasting horizon is underexplored.  
  - Hyperparameters and training curriculum for the time series portion are missing (Reviewer 1).  
  - In real-world datasets, textual information is likely constant per dataset (e.g., Section B.3), which would allow a finetuned model to ignore it (Reviewer 1).  
  - No tests on other multimodal time series tasks beyond forecasting (e.g., classification, anomaly detection) (Reviewer 3).

- **Practicality and interpretability insufficient**:  
  - Claimed interpretability from textual integration is not demonstrated with real-world examples or user studies (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - No analysis of performance when textual inputs are missing, incomplete, or noisy – a critical scenario in high-stakes domains like finance (Reviewer 2).  
  - The order compliance rate metric is tailored to the paper’s instructions; its advantage over existing forecasting metrics is not clarified (Reviewer 4).

- **Writing and presentation issues**:  
  - Poor structure, unclear explanations, and convoluted description of AutoPrompter (Reviewer 2).  
  - Frequent typos (e.g., citation mistakes line 54–55, spelling error “sucess”), inconsistent quotation marks, poorly formatted figures (Fig. 6) and tables (Reviewers 2, 4).  
  - Incomplete literature coverage: Section 2.2 misses relevant multimodal forecasting models (e.g., UniTime).  
  - Missing citations for original dataset sources; textual citations would improve readability for references lines 117–128.  
  - Parameter counts for TITSP and benchmarks are not provided, making it unclear whether improvements are due to higher computational resources (Reviewer 4).  
  - Typographical issues persist in the revised sections (e.g., incorrect quotations line 113, parenthetical citations line 127, spelling line 107).
