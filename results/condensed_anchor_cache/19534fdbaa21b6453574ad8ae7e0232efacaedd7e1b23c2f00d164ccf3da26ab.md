## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers unanimously assess FITS as a compelling lightweight time series model (≈10k parameters) that achieves competitive forecasting and anomaly detection performance through complex frequency domain interpolation. They praise its clear writing, thorough evaluation, and ingenious combination of simple components (RevIN, LPF, complex linear layer). However, they identify several limitations: the anomaly detection performance on certain datasets (SMAP, MSL) is notably weaker; the GPU inference time is slightly slower than DLinear despite far fewer parameters; probabilistic forecasting is not supported; evaluation on synthetic/benchmark data may not reflect real edge-device challenges; and the contribution of individual components (LPF, interpolation rate) is not fully disentangled. Reviewers also request more basic baselines (ARIMA, DeepAR, Random Cut Forest), a critical difference plot, and clarity on technical details (Fig. 2, RIN, back-casting, output length control). Overall, the paper is considered a solid contribution with very high parameter efficiency, but with noted gaps in generalization and practical deployment.

### Strengths
1. **Surprisingly good performance with an extremely small parameter count (~10k).** The model achieves competitive or even superior results compared to SOTA baselines while being ≈50× smaller. The error standard deviation is very small, indicating stability due to simplicity (Reviewer 1).
2. **Comprehensive ablation analysis.** Reviewers highlight the thorough exploration of hyper-parameters (e.g., cutoff frequency selection, lookback window size) and the synthetic dataset that illustrates the divide-and-conquer frequency idea (Reviewer 1, 2).
3. **Well-structured and clearly written.** The preliminary section is instructive; the paper is easy to follow and positions itself well among related work (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
4. **Addresses a critical practical need.** The compact design is ideally suited for edge devices and resource-constrained environments. The clever combination of RevIN, a low-pass filter, and a complex-valued linear layer creates a robust architecture for industrial-grade tasks (Reviewer 2).
5. **Extensive and rigorous evaluation.** The paper compares many state-of-the-art methods despite vast size differences, includes anomaly detection as a downstream task, and provides insights into why the model works better on some datasets (e.g., binary series, benchmark limitations) (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
6. **Impressive light weight with minor performance trade-off.** The reduction in model size is commendable, and the minor performance sacrifice (if any) makes it highly attractive for practitioners (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Anomaly detection performance is inconsistent.** The model performs significantly worse on SMAP and MSL compared to other datasets. The frequency domain approach may be inherently less suitable for abrupt, non-periodic anomalies or binary/categorical data. Further analysis is needed to explain this failure (Reviewer 1). Moreover, the anomaly detection experiment may be insufficiently conclusive: simple precision/recall metrics do not capture nuance (magnitudes, durations) (Reviewer 4).
2. **Inference time is slower than DLinear.** FITS takes 0.6 ms on GPU versus DLinear’s 0.4 ms, despite having far fewer parameters. The overhead of frequency domain transformations may hinder real-time applications (Reviewer 1). Experiments on real-time inference on simple hardware (not necessarily edge devices) are needed to substantiate the claimed parameter-effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
3. **No probabilistic forecasts.** The model cannot generate uncertainty intervals, which is a critical requirement in many industrial applications (Reviewer 2).
4. **Evaluation may not reflect real edge device scenarios.** Benchmarks like traffic and weather often do not require on-device processing. It is unclear how FITS would handle high-frequency noisy sensor data, irregular sampling, or signal corruption common in healthcare or industrial sensors. The linear frequency-domain interpolation may oversimplify complex non-linear dynamics in such data (Reviewer 2).
5. **Contribution of model components is unclear.** The interaction between the low-pass filter, the real-valued amplitude/phase network, and the interpolation rate is not fully explored. Ablation studies that quantify the relative importance of each component for different time series types (e.g., high vs. low frequency) are needed (Reviewer 4).
6. **Missing basic baselines and statistical rigor.** The evaluation lacks simpler methods such as ARIMA, DeepAR for forecasting, and Random Cut Forest for anomaly detection. A critical difference plot would strengthen the statistical foundation of the comparisons (Reviewer 3).
7. **Presentation of the method could be more thorough.** Several details are missing:
   - Fig. 2 caption is insufficient: it should clarify whether there is a single linear layer, what the output dimension is, the typical dimensions of \(\tilde{X}\), whether (i)RIN and (i)rFFT are performed in parallel or sequentially, and how RIN works (Reviewer 3).
   - The reasoning behind \(x(t-\tau)\) being a shift forward in time (vs. backward) requires explanation (Reviewer 3).
   - The back-casting task and its role in training are not clearly described (Reviewer 3).
   - Section 3.3 should motivate why output length needs to be controlled (Reviewer 3).
8. **Clarification on low-pass filter justification.** The statement that the LPF discards components beyond the model’s learning capabilities is vague. High-frequency components are not always noise, and trend signals (often low-frequency) are crucial for forecasting. The filtering rationale appears domain-specific and needs better justification (Reviewer 3).
9. **Heuristics that require tuning in practice.** The method shares the drawback of other approaches that rely on tunable heuristics (Reviewer 3).
10. **Questions that overlap with missing experiments or limitations:**
    - Did the authors try fitting a larger neural network for the frequency interpolation task to see if performance scales with model size? (Reviewer 2)
    - What are the challenges regarding domain shifts and refitting, and could they be addressed? (Reviewer 3)
    - How can the model be deployed on devices that do not support complex computation? (Reviewer 1)
11. **Future work directions are underexplored.** The paper could discuss further size reductions, model interpretability (which frequency components matter most), and extending with complex-valued networks or attention mechanisms (Reviewer 4).
