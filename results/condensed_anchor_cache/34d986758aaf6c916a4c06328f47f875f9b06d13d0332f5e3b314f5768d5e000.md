## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Meta MeanTeacher, which introduces a Dynamic Decay Modulation (DDM) module to meta-learn the EMA decay coefficient in the Mean Teacher framework for semi-supervised medical image segmentation. DDM takes student and teacher losses as input and updates the coefficient via pseudo-gradient descent. Experiments on CT and MRI datasets show improvements over fixed-coefficient Mean Teacher and when integrated into UA-MT, AD-MT, and PMT.

### Strengths
- **Novel meta-learning approach**: Addresses the limitation of fixed EMA coefficients by introducing the DDM module, which adaptively adjusts the decay coefficient during training (Reviewers 1, 3). This dynamic adaptation enables the teacher model to better guide the student as training progresses (Reviewer 2).
- **Plug-and-play design**: DDM integrates easily into existing Mean Teacher-based frameworks such as UA-MT, AD-MT, and PMT, demonstrating versatility (Reviewer 2).
- **Enhanced stability and robustness**: The meta-learning strategy improves upon the inherent stability of EMA by learning an optimal coefficient (Reviewer 2). The method fully taps into the potential of the MT framework (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical breadth**: Evaluation covers three datasets (including CT and MRI), showing consistent performance gains (Reviewer 1). Extensive experimental results demonstrate effectiveness (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Modifying the EMA coefficient in Mean Teacher is a well-explored direction; many similar extensions exist, and the methodological contribution may be limited unless DDM is substantially different from prior meta-learning strategies (Reviewers 1, 3). The difference between DDM and previous meta-learning approaches is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **High computational overhead**: DDM requires cloning and iterative updates of both models, adding complexity that may be infeasible for real-time or resource-limited applications, especially with large 3D medical images (Reviewer 2).
- **Narrow experimental scope**: Only a few baseline methods (e.g., UA-MT with VNet/UNet) and limited label ratios (5%, 10%, 20%) are tested. A wider range of label-scarcity scenarios (e.g., 20%, 50%, 80%, 90%) is needed to robustly assess adaptability (Reviewer 1).
- **Backbone limitation**: Exclusive use of VNet may limit generalizability; UNet or ViT-based UNets should be tested (Reviewer 1). For example, in Table 2, VNet performs significantly lower than others at 5% labeled but only slightly lower at 10%—explanation needed (Reviewer 1). In Table 3, why does VNet outperform UA-MT when 20% are labeled? (Reviewer 1).
- **Focus only on one hyperparameter**: The work only adjusts the EMA decay coefficient; other hyperparameters (learning rates, loss weights) are not explored, which may constrain overall optimization (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear motivation for dynamic α**: Why is a dynamically varying α (e.g., between 0.95-0.99) necessarily better than a fixed value like 0.97? Provide a plot of α over training iterations (Reviewer 3). The potential drawbacks of a fixed EMA decay are not clearly explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Ablation and analysis questions**:
  - In Section 4.3, why compare with the average of fixed α values? Lower α values (0.03, 0.05, 0.1) degrade performance; α=0.97 achieves higher than 0.84. The comparison seems biased (Reviewer 2). 
  - Why does α=0.01 show improvement? This contradicts the explanation in Section 3.1 (Reviewer 2).
  - What is the impact of α > 0.5? It becomes less significant; test randomly selected α > 0.5 to see if beneficial (Reviewer 2).
  - What is the initial value of α? Has an ablation study on α been conducted? (Reviewer 3).
- **Notation and formulation issues**: Confusion between Θ_s*, Θ_s, θ_s; meta data D_m vs. labeled/unlabeled; missing formula for L_m (Reviewer 3). In Equation 2, what criteria determine α_m? Is there a relationship with the teacher/student losses? For instance, if teacher loss is lower, should α_m be larger? (Reviewer 3).
- **Plug-and-play limitation**: How would DDM apply to pseudo-labeling methods like CPS (Cross Pseudo Supervision)? (Reviewer 3).
- **Data integrity concern**: In Section 4.4, experimental settings differ from those in "Alternative Diversified Teaching of Semi-Supervised Medical Image Segmentation", yet state-of-the-art results on LA and Pancreas-NIH datasets appear identical. Are these data directly borrowed? (Reviewer 2). Also, in semi-supervised setups, how is fair comparison ensured given that the feature distributions of labeled and unlabeled data may differ, even if the percentage is controlled? (Reviewer 1).
- **Figure 2 symbol**: What does "m" in Figure 2 mean? Is it redundant? (Reviewer 2).

*Note: Two reviewers gave a score of 3 (less positive), while one gave a score of 5 (more positive). The more positive reviewer still raised several methodological and motivation concerns.*
