## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes BayesDiff, a pixel-wise uncertainty estimator for diffusion models that applies a last-layer Laplace approximation to the score network and derives iterative variance propagation through the reverse sampling process. Applications include filtering low-fidelity images, rectifying artifacts, and enhancing diversity, with overhead about 1× standard sampling. Reviewers’ opinions diverged: some found the method practical, novel, and well-written (Reviewers 2, 4), while others questioned its theoretical soundness (Reviewer 1) and the conclusiveness of the evaluation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). One reviewer initially leaned rejection but later accepted after rebuttal (Reviewer 3).

### Strengths

- The methodology is sensible, naturally exploiting the probabilistic structure of diffusion models (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and clear on prior work (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The method is practical: the overhead is no more than 1× standard sampling, making it feasible for large-scale problems (Reviewer 4).
- Pixel-wise uncertainty has useful applications, particularly for correcting flawed samples, which may be valuable for practitioners (Reviewer 2).
- The approach is reasonably novel in providing uncertainty estimates for diffusion model outputs (Reviewer 2).
- The presented applications are interesting and illustrate practical potential (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- Evaluation is largely qualitative. Quantitative results (e.g., Table 1) show small differences and lack error bars, making it difficult to assess significance or compare methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- No baselines are provided. For example, a deterministic diffusion model could yield pixelwise variances from the sampling process, or non-probabilistic filtering techniques from GAN literature could be compared (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The paper does not justify why the tractable likelihood of diffusion models (Song et al.) should not be used directly to filter low-fidelity images instead of uncertainty estimates (Reviewer 1).
- The Gaussian approximation to the posterior over last-layer weights is questionable. Per-weight histograms of trained diffusion model weights should be plotted to assess Gaussianity (Reviewer 1).
- Removing low-fidelity images has been criticized for biasing generative models (Reviewer 1, citing Arora et al. 2021 and Du et al. 2023).
- Critical implementation details of the Laplace approximation are missing: whether a full or diagonal Hessian is used, how diag(γ²_θ(x_t,t)) is computed, and how the high-dimensional output (e.g., d² pixels) is handled efficiently (Reviewer 4). The Hessian size hd² × hd² and Jacobian multiplication seem prohibitive; it is unclear how BayesDiff overcomes this in practice (Reviewer 4).
- Section 4.2 (artifact correction) is handwavy. It is unclear whether bounding boxes for artifacts were determined automatically from pixel uncertainty or manually, and whether the corrected samples in Figure 8 are representative or cherry-picked (Reviewers 2, 4). No quantitative measure of improvement is provided.
- Section 4.3 (diversity enhancement) lacks quantitative evaluation; metrics such as FID or perceptual distance are absent (Reviewer 4).
- Figure 2 is hard to interpret: at skipping=3 and 4 the uncertainty clusters mix significantly, and the mean score decreases at skipping=5,6 after increasing from 1 to 4 (Reviewer 2). The caption is not descriptive and colors are hard to see (Reviewer 4). A plot of Spearman correlation with the no-skipping ranking would help (Reviewer 3).
- Figures 3 and 4 need more spacing between left/right groups; the current layout is confusing (Reviewer 4).
- Figure 5 uses indistinguishable colors; different markers or line styles are needed (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The background section on diffusion models (2.1) relies heavily on SDE terminology; an algorithmic perspective summarizing the network prediction target and training objective would be more helpful (Reviewer 3).
- Overlapping questions that are also criticisms: Is the Laplace approximation strictly necessary? Since the likelihood is Gaussian, the posterior over last-layer weights might be Gaussian without approximation (Reviewer 3). Could sampling variances from a deterministic model serve as a baseline? (Reviewer 3). Would experimenting with different covariance structures for the Laplace approximation make a difference? (Reviewer 3).
