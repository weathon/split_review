## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a general adaptive gradient (GAG) framework with quasi-hyperbolic momentum, combines it with stochastic Newton (SN-GAG) and stochastic quasi-Newton (SQN-GAG), and provides convergence analysis under strongly convex and Polyak-Łojasiewicz conditions. Experiments are on LIBSVM binary classification. Reviewer ratings are split: two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 4/3) are positive, three reviewers (scores 3, confidence 5/4/3) are critical of scope, writing, experimental comparisons, and theoretical assumptions.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea:** The QHM application in second-order stochastic optimization and the unified GAG framework are considered novel (R1, R4, R5). R4 explicitly calls the approach novel; R5 notes QHM application novelty.
- **Theoretical analysis:** Detailed convergence guarantees under both strongly convex and Polyak-Łojasiewicz conditions are provided (R1, R4). R1 highlights the PL case as a missing piece in prior work; R4 notes detailed convergence under multiple conditions; R1 and R5 mention linear convergence proofs.
- **Algorithmic clarity and performance:** Algorithms are described in detail (R3, R5). Experiments on LIBSVM show the proposed methods outperform baselines and are robust to hyperparameter settings (R3 “does well on LIBSVM datasets”; R5 “empirically faster than baselines, robust to hyper-parameter settings”).

### Weaknesses
- **Narrow experimental scope:** Experiments only cover binary classification and strongly-convex objectives (R1, R5). R2 and R5 request larger, higher-dimensional datasets (e.g., rcv1, CIFAR100). R2 and R3 note the low dimensionality of used datasets. R2 adds that wall time or effective passes are missing; R3 says second-order methods require quadratically more passes per iteration, making comparisons with adaptive gradient methods unfair without time/pass plots. R3 notes no Newton/quasi-Newton baselines are included. R2 and R3 ask for more extensive benchmarks.
- **Insufficient comparisons and missing baselines:** R1 notes missing comparison with recent SSO methods (AdaFisher, AdaHessian). R2 questions advantage over first-order methods (Katyusha, Spider, SpiderBoost) and requests convergence rate comparisons for SC, convex, and non-convex cases. R4 doubts clear advantage over SVRG given extra hyperparameters. R2 and R5 ask for running time comparisons.
- **Writing and presentation issues:** The writing is described as poor, verbose, incoherent, with unusual word choices (R3). Concrete examples: opening sentence; line 038 phrasing; acronym “AR” for AI; Equation 3 is elementary and tags ambiguous; line 068/072/079 grammar; missing Adam co-author (Jimmy Ba) in citation (R3). Formatting problems: Assumption 2 indentation/alignment (R3, R4); \mathfrak style letters (R3). Algorithm 2 is redundant (identical to Algorithm 1 except Newton vs. quasi-Newton) and wastes space (R3). Pseudocode confusion: k starts at 1 but first-order update condition k<1 is confusing (R4). No proof of Theorems 3 and 4 in appendix (R5). Abstract says experimental results are shown but they are only in appendix (R5). R5 also requests more high-level explanation of how QHM plugs into the methods.
- **Theoretical concerns:** Assumption 1 & 2 are restrictive—each individual function f(x,ξ) must be strongly convex and smooth for all ξ (R3); this also implies strong convexity should not appear in Theorem 4 (R5). The double use of preconditioners (Newton then Adam) suggests unclear understanding of adaptive gradient methods (R3). R4 questions Remark 1(1) about SARAH/SPIDER unbiasedness. R4 and R5 ask how hyperparameters are chosen.
- **Missing code/supplementary:** No anonymous repository or supplementary code was provided (R3).
