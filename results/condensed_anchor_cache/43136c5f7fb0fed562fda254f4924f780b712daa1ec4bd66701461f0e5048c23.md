## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SPO and SPO-abs, offline alignment methods that generalize DPO from binary to multiclass (listwise) settings, use soft labels from a teacher model, and add a regularization term to counteract decreasing likelihood of preferred responses. Three reviewers (ratings 3,3,3) find the problem formulation interesting but raise serious concerns about novelty, missing prior work, insufficient evaluation, and presentation. One reviewer (rating 1, confidence 5) asserts that the paper is almost identical to Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2024, "Noise Contrastive Alignment") and constitutes plagiarism, recommending desk rejection.

### Strengths
- The problems identified (limited to pairwise data, decreasing likelihood of preferred responses) are convincing.
- SPO/SPO-abs show improvements over DPO on MT-Bench and AlpacaEval (R1).
- Analysis confirms that SPO-abs regularizes and prevents likelihood decrease (R1).
- Generalization of DPO to multiclass (listwise) setting is a potentially more efficient use of multiple generations per prompt (R2).
- Evaluation across multiple settings (reward and preference data) is performed (R2).
- The goal of learning from both explicit scalar rewards and preference data is clearly stated (R3).
- The chosen benchmarks and baselines are relevant and reasonable (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Plagiarism concern (R4, rating 1, confidence 5):** The paper is nearly identical to Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2024), with the same preliminary section, identical method derivation and objective, almost identical Table 1, and exactly the same baseline numbers in Table 2. R4 recommends desk rejection. This is a minority but extremely strong negative view.
- **Missing prior work and insufficient novelty (R1, R2, R3):**
  - Soft labels from a teacher have been proposed in prior work (e.g., Fisch et al., "Robust Preference Optimization", and "Distilled DPO"); the equivalence for K=2 is not discussed (R2).
  - The problem of decreasing likelihood of preferred responses and regularization terms to counter it already appear in Pal et al. (DPO-Positive) and others (R1, R2).
  - Listwise alignment was studied in LIRE (R1).
  - The work on Noise Contrastive Alignment (Chen et al. 2024) is not cited; the method appears to derive the same objective (R4).
  - Plackett-Luce and knowledge-distillation (KD) foundations for the proposed loss (Eq. 9) are not acknowledged (R3).
- **Unclear and conflated contributions (R1, R2):** It is ambiguous whether the key contribution is the multiclass extension, the use of scalar rewards, the soft labels, the likelihood regularization, or all combined. The title stresses novelty, but each component individually is known.
- **Insufficient evaluation of individual contributions (R2):**
  - The multiclass generalization is never tested in isolation (e.g., comparing multiclass vs. binary DPO when multiple generations per prompt are available, without soft labels).
  - The soft-label variant is confounded with a much larger teacher model; it is unclear whether gains come from the objective or the teacher’s size.
  - The SPO-abs term is not compared to existing regularization methods (e.g., DPO-Positive from Pal et al.).
  - Computational efficiency (e.g., memory cost of a K>2 objective) is not addressed.
- **Problematic assumptions and claims (R1, R3):**
  - The claim that scalar rewards are more efficient/convenient than preferences (line 154) is unsupported and contradicts literature (Stiennon et al., self-play preference optimization) showing that human scalar rewards are hard to obtain and preferences do not obey transitivity. The only plausible scenario is using a teacher model, which is not stated as a limitation.
  - The claim that KTO is only applicable to pairwise preference data is incorrect (R3).
  - The convergence guarantee for SPO-abs is not substantiated (R2).
- **Experimental design concerns (R1, R3):**
  - Motivation for testing on reasoning tasks (math, coding) is unclear.
  - There is a mismatch between the training dataset (UltraFeedback) and evaluation benchmarks (e.g., AlpacaEval, MT-Bench), making it hard to isolate the effect.
  - Figure 1 y-axis is labeled “rewards” but described in prose as “likelihoods”; the x-axis is “percentage of total steps” without clarifying total number of steps (R3).
- **Presentation and errors (R1, R2, R3):**
  - Multiple typos: "a adaptive loss", "that inject listwise", "In order to draws", "We exam", "Preferecne dataset", missing comma, etc.
  - Figures 1 and 2 mislabel SPO-abs as LPO (R1, R3).
  - Table 2 underline for second-highest value appears misplaced (R2).
  - Use of “dark knowledge” is unclear (R1, R2).
  - Related work section is too broad and distracts from the core (R1, R3).
