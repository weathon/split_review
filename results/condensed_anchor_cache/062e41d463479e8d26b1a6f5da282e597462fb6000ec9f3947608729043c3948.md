## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BNCDE, a Bayesian neural controlled differential equation method for treatment effect estimation in continuous time with uncertainty quantification. It combines neural CDEs and neural SDEs in a VAE-like framework to provide posterior predictive distributions of potential outcomes, trained by maximizing an ELBO. Experiments are conducted on semi-synthetic tumor growth data. Reviewers generally agree that the topic is important and the inclusion of uncertainty quantification is valuable, but raise substantial concerns about causal assumptions, motivation for latent representations, limited experimental scope, novelty, scalability, and missing comparisons. One reviewer (R3, score 8) is more positive about clarity and motivation, while the other three (scores 6) note more significant shortcomings.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and relevant problem: estimating treatment effects over continuous time with uncertainty quantification, which is crucial for personalized medicine and goes beyond traditional causal modeling like Granger causality (R1, R2, R3).
- The method provides better uncertainty estimation compared to the non-Bayesian counterpart (MC dropout); the derivation of the ELBO appears correct (R4).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with explicit motivation and thorough experimental analysis regarding uncertainty quantification (R1, R3). Implementation details are well documented in both the paper/appendix and the provided code (R3).
- The problem setting is considered innovative and unique (R2). The paper identifies a clear gap in the literature (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for latent representations and causal concerns:** The paper does not discuss why latent representations are needed when the experimental setting uses structured data; it is unclear why not directly model an ODE over (treatment, covariates, outcome) (R1). Introducing latent variable Z may violate the unconfoundedness assumption: the causal structure (Z_t affects A_t, X_t, Y_t and Z_t → Z_{t′}) opens an unblocked path from Y_t to Y_{t′}. Given Y_t ⟂ A_t | X_t, the paper does not implement a backdoor adjustment for X_t, which is necessary to eliminate confounding bias (R1). The confounding problem is not discussed within the BNCDE framework, whereas the baseline CTDE handles confounders through a balancing term; it is unclear whether confounders can be addressed (R3).
- **Inaccurate claim about existing methods:** The abstract states that existing methods are limited to point estimates and ignore uncertainty; this is incorrect, as some methods do account for uncertainty. The authors should clarify if they refer specifically to neural methods (R3).
- **Limited experimental evaluation:** Experiments are conducted on only one synthetic dataset (tumor growth). Real-life or semi-synthetic data should be considered to demonstrate applicability (R2, R4). Comparisons are only against other neural methods; a larger body of literature may contain alternative approaches that could handle the same setup (R3).
- **Novelty concerns:** The main SDE and CDE formulations are derived from prior works, and variational inference methods are consistent with existing latent SDE literature, giving an impression of amalgamation. The work also has noticeable similarities to [2]; despite a footnote indicating divergence, a more comprehensive discussion of distinctions is needed (R4).
- **Scalability issues:** Modeling neural network weights via an SDE with a neural network drift means the input dimensionality of the SDE drift scales with the number of weights (potentially millions). This raises questions about computational cost and training stability under high-dimensional circumstances (R4).
- **Overlap assumption for treatment intensity:** Timestamps follow a point process; it is unclear how to verify that the intensity satisfies 0 < λ(t | H_t^i) < 1, especially because intensities can exceed 1, implying fewer events under that constraint (R2).
- **Potential overconfidence from variational inference:** The variance of the variational posterior is often smaller than that of the true posterior, which could lead to overconfident uncertainty predictions (R2).
- **Missing discussion of limitations for medical applications:** The paper should discuss possible downsides, required assumptions for robustness, and failure modes (R3). The claim that BNCDE is “fairly robust against noise” should be made more explicit (R3).
- **Identifiability issues:** Section 3.3 lists three assumptions but does not explicitly discuss why they lead to identifiability. Modeling potential outcomes through latent embeddings may affect identifiability (R4). The prediction of outcome uncertainty could lead to a naive model with large uncertainty; the quality of aleatoric uncertainty quantification should be examined (R4).
- **Minor typo:** Page 7: “we the maximize ELBO” (R2).
