## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the learning-augmented k-means problem, where an oracle provides predicted labels with error rate α. It proposes three sampling-based algorithms to improve running time over previous sorting-based approaches. Fast-Sampling achieves O(md log(kd)) time, Fast-Estimation achieves O(md)+~O(kd/α) time, and Fast-Filtering is a heuristic. The theoretical results remove the O(log m) factor from the prior O(md log m) state-of-the-art (Ergun et al., Nguyen et al.). Experiments on real datasets show about 6× speedup compared to prior methods, with comparable clustering quality.

### Strengths
- The paper removes the log m dependency in running time, achieving linear (or near-linear) time in the input size, a noticeable improvement over the prior O(md log m) bound (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The sampling-based technique is novel and contrasts with the sorting-based strategy used in all previous work (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 called the techniques interesting and the algorithm easy to use in practice).
- The theoretical analysis is solid and provides guarantees for Fast-Sampling and Fast-Estimation (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- The experimental evaluation is comprehensive and demonstrates practical speedups (Reviewers 1,2,4). Reviewer 1 also noted the clear explanation of ideas and results.
- The learning-augmented k-means setting is interesting (Reviewer 1).

However, one reviewer (Reviewer 4) found the technical innovations limited, stating that the algorithm “simply performs some sampling operations first and then moves on to subsequent steps, which appears to be a straightforward idea.” This contrasts with the positive novelty assessments from other reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Worse approximation ratio**: Fast-Sampling and Fast-Estimation achieve a (1+O(α)) approximation only within a constant factor, while the previous state-of-the-art achieved the exact (1+O(α)) bound. This could be significant in practice (Reviewer 2).
- **Heuristic without guarantees**: Fast-Filtering, which shows the best empirical performance, lacks theoretical guarantees, making it difficult to assess robustness and reliability across different scenarios (Reviewers 1,2). Reviewer 1 noted that comparing its runtime to theoretically guaranteed algorithms seems unfair.
- **Practical runtime not improved**: The theoretical runtime improvement of the guaranteed algorithms (Fast-Sampling, Fast-Estimation) does not translate to faster execution on the experimental datasets compared to existing algorithms (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing evaluation of Fast-Estimation**: Only Fast-Sampling and Fast-Filtering are evaluated experimentally; the performance of Fast-Estimation is not reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Discrepancy in experimental results**: In Figure 1, Nguyen et al. clearly outperforms Fast-Estimation (which uses the same sampling technique?), while in other tables the methods show similar performance. The authors did not explain this discrepancy (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons**: Several important comparisons are absent:
    - Clustering quality measured by ground-truth metrics (NMI, ARI) relative to true labels (Reviewer 1).
    - Comparison to standard k-means++ (with no prediction) in terms of cost, ARI, NMI (Reviewer 1).
    - The paper does not mention whether it is the first to use sampling in this problem; Reviewer 4 requested citation of other similar sampling-based algorithms for comparison.
- **Significance of the improvement questioned**: One reviewer (Reviewer 3) was unsure if an O(log m) improvement is super attractive given that O(md log m) already sounds fast, and asked for justification of why avoiding sorting is important.
- **Insufficient problem explanation**: The introduction could better motivate the learning-augmented setting; Reviewer 4 noted that Nguyen et al. (2022) has a nicer example.
- **Technical questions left unanswered** (turned into weaknesses as they point to missing clarifications or experiments):
    - Why can α be 0 in the Sorting and Fast-Sampling algorithms but must be positive in Fast-Estimation? (Reviewer 3)
    - Can similar results be obtained for k-median? (Reviewer 3)
    - The range of α in Fast-Estimation: should it be (0, 1/3−ε/3)? (Reviewer 3, referring to Line 153)
    - What is the quality of the clustering output w.r.t. ground truth (NMI/ARI) for all algorithms? (Reviewer 1)
