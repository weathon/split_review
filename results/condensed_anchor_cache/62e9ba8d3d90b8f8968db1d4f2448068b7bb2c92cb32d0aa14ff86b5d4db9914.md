## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Speculative RAG, a framework inspired by speculative decoding. A small, distilled specialist LM (Drafter) generates multiple answer drafts with rationales in parallel, each conditioned on a different subset of retrieved documents. A larger generalist LM (Verifier) then selects the final answer in a single verification pass. To construct the Drafter’s training data, rationales are synthesized from Gemini Ultra on an additional 40k instances. Experiments on TriviaQA, MuSiQue, PubHealth, and ARC-Challenge show state-of-the-art results and latency reductions (up to 12.97% accuracy gain and 51% latency reduction on PubHealth). The paper includes extensive ablation and analysis.

### Strengths
- The conceptual extension of speculative decoding to RAG is novel and appealing, supported by strong empirical results (Reviewer 1).
- The paper targets a timely challenge in RAG and offers promising techniques to improve efficiency (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments are comprehensive and thorough; ablation studies provide great insights (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with detailed descriptions of methods, prompts, and experimental setups (Reviewer 2).
- The method shows obvious improvements over baselines, and ablation studies verify its effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
- Training a Drafter to generate answers paired with rationale, then using a Verifier to confirm, enhances robustness (Reviewer 3).
- The lightweight specialist LM allows parallel processing of documents, reducing computation cost and increasing inference efficiency (Reviewer 4).
- The distinction between specialist and generalist LMs clarifies their roles, reducing generalization degradation risk from SFT of a generalist LM (Reviewer 4).
- Applying K-means clustering for multi-perspective sampling mitigates inaccuracies from incomplete information (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Data quality and fairness**: SFT data uses rationales synthesized by Gemini Ultra from an additional 40k instances, making comparisons with baselines without access to such resources unfair (Reviewer 1). Further ablation on drafter performance across varying data volumes is needed; acquiring substantial SFT data from a proprietary model is resource-intensive, and it is unclear whether smaller datasets suffice (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Several relevant works are not compared:
  - A related work [1] (released ~4.5 months ago) that also proposes a synthesis rationale-augmented generation approach for RAG (Reviewer 1).
  - Recent speculative decoding methods [1] and [2] for RAG (Reviewer 2).
  - Key baselines such as CRAG and Self-CRAG on the free-form datasets (TriviaQA, MuSiQue, PubHealth) are missing (Reviewer 4). It is standard to replicate missing baseline results.
- **Limited contribution and triviality**: Some reviewers view the idea as trivial and making limited contributions compared to prior two-stage RAG studies, where the differences are few (Reviewer 2). The whole framework is seen as simply clustering documents, fine-tuning a small LLM to generate answers with rationale, and prompting a verifier (Reviewer 3). In contrast, Reviewer 1 finds the concept novel and appealing.
- **Benchmark suitability**: The QA benchmarks used (TriviaQA, MuSiQue, ARC-Challenge) are primarily designed for reasoning, not specifically for RAG; testing on dedicated RAG benchmarks would be more reliable (Reviewer 3). To demonstrate effectiveness in realistic, domain-diverse settings, experiments should be extended to datasets like FreshQA and BRIGHT (Reviewer 4).
- **Verifier overhead concerns**: It is unclear whether multiple instances of the large verifier LLM are launched in parallel (Algorithm 1, lines 11–14). If so, large memory overhead may significantly offset latency gains in practice (Reviewer 1).
- **Effectiveness of P(Yes) / Self-Reflection**: The use of P(Yes) for self-reflection is noted to be uncalibrated or poorly calibrated for LLMs without target SFT or alignment (citing [1]). Although Table 3 shows improved performance with SR, there is insufficient evidence for the intrinsic effectiveness of P(Yes); an ablation study is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Improvement on strong baselines**: On TriviaQA and ARC-C, Mixtral-Instruct-8x7B already achieves high scores (73.91%, 78.41%), and Speculative RAG’s improvement is limited (74.24%, 80.55%). More explanation and analysis for these cases are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details**: 
  - For Figure 2 (average number of tokens in rationale vs. retrieved documents): What is the number of retrieved documents? Is it before or after subsetting to drafters? (Reviewer 1)
  - Is the Multi-Perspective Sampling stage considered in the latency analysis (Section 4.5)? (Reviewer 1)
  - In line 207–209, is the answer A obtained by human labeling or generated by LLMs? What if the documents do not provide evidence for generating A? (Reviewer 2)
  - Token consumption numbers of different methods, besides inference latency, are not reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalist LM’s evaluation ability**: Since the generalist LM is not fine-tuned, can it reliably evaluate the specialist’s drafts when they are inaccurate or contain hallucinations, especially if the generalist itself lacks relevant knowledge? Error analysis or boundary cases should be discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Newer model generations**: The contributions could be strengthened by adopting newer models (e.g., Gemma-2, Llama-3) in experiments (Reviewer 1).
