## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a knowledge distillation method that trains the teacher model using a conditional mutual information (CMI) regularizer (MCMI), building on the theory from [1] that a teacher should approximate the optimal Bayes classifier \(P^*_X\). The regularizer maximizes an empirical estimate of \(I(X,\hat{Y}|Y)\) during teacher training. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet with various CNN architectures show consistent student accuracy improvements when the MCMI-trained teacher is used in existing KD frameworks. The method is particularly effective in few-shot (up to +5.72%) and zero-shot (up to 84% accuracy) settings. The idea is simple to implement and complementary to student-focused distillation algorithms.

### Strengths
- **Simple and easy to implement**: adding a CMI-based regularizer to teacher training is straightforward (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Empirical improvement**: MCMI integrates with existing KD algorithms and consistently increases student accuracy across datasets and architectures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Extensive experiments**: thorough evaluation on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet with multiple architectures (VGG, ResNet, etc.) provides convincing evidence (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Strong few-shot/zero-shot results**: significant gains are demonstrated in low-data regimes (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Well-written and novel**: the paper clearly motivates the approach and provides evidence linking CMI to teacher quality and dark knowledge (Reviewer 3).
- **Focus on teacher training**: addresses a complementary aspect of KD that is often overlooked in student-oriented methods (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical connection to Bayes optimality**: the paper does not rigorously explain why maximizing \(I(X,\hat{Y}|Y)\) leads to a better approximation of the optimal Bayes classifier \(P^*_X\) beyond "capturing contextual information". It remains unclear how the regularizer ensures calibrated probability estimates rather than simply informative ones. Limitations of empirical CMI estimates in low-data or high-dimensional settings are not discussed. (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 1’s Q1)
- **Unclear role of the CMI regularizer**: it is not established whether the regularizer primarily reduces variance, increases entropy, or acts as label smoothing. Would a simple entropy regularizer or temperature scaling achieve the same effect? A comparison with standard label smoothing is needed to differentiate the method. (Reviewer 2; related to Q2, Q3)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: the number of additional training iterations for the teacher with the MCMI objective is not analyzed. This is a critical hyperparameter that may strongly influence student performance and requires guidance. (Reviewer 1 Q2)
- **Scope limitations**: the underlying theory is primarily tailored to multi-class image classification. The method is not validated on binary classification or NLP datasets. Moreover, the technique requires ground-truth labels, which precludes its use in semi-supervised distillation—a major application of KD where teachers generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled data. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing definition of temperature \(T\)**: the softmax temperature in the CMI estimation (Eq. 2) is not defined. (Reviewer 2 Q1)
- **Clarity of zero-shot experiments (Section 6.2)**: it is questionable whether the setup is truly zero-shot, as negative scores are used during training. The authors should clarify the expected mechanism and report results when different classes are dropped. (Reviewer 2)
- **Eigen-CAM visualization (Figure 3)**: the heat map for the terrier class does not focus on the animal’s body; the interpretation of the attention maps needs more careful analysis. (Reviewer 2)

*Note:* One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, praising the paper’s novelty and extensive evidence, and considered the scope limitation acceptable but noted it. Another reviewer (score 6) had stronger reservations about the theoretical depth and the method’s differentiation from existing techniques. This contrast reflects differing assessments of the paper’s contribution.
