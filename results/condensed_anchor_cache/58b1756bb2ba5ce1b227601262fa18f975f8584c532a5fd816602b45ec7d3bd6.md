## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DiffCSP++, a diffusion model building on DiffCSP (Jiao et al., 2023) that incorporates space group symmetries as inductive biases for crystal generation. It reduces space group constraints into two tractable parts: a basis constraint on the invariant logarithmic space of the lattice matrix and a Wyckoff position constraint on fractional coordinates. The model conditionally generates lattice, fractional coordinates, and atom types given the space group. Experiments on four datasets show state-of-the-art performance on crystal structure prediction and ab-initio generation, with promising results for controllable generation with customized space groups. However, several reviewers note missing model details, reporting issues, and limited novelty.

### Strengths
- First crystal generation method to explicitly consider and enforce space group symmetry constraints (Reviewer 3; also noted by Reviewer 1 as employing conditional generation rather than treating invariances separately).
- Strong experimental results: outperforms DiffCSP and other baselines (e.g., PGCGM, Zhao et al., 2023) on crystal structure prediction and ab-initio generation; DiffCSP++ is not constrained to ternary systems like PGCGM (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Well-written and well-organized; most parts are comprehensible to non-experts (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Sound and well-motivated approach with practical relevance (Reviewer 2).
- Interesting use of crystal symmetries that provides control during generation (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and citation issues (Reviewer 1):** Proposition 1 (polar decomposition) and Proposition 2 (dimension of 3×3 symmetric matrices) are standard linear algebra results; the paper presents them without citations to standard texts (e.g., Strang, Roman), giving an impression of greater novelty. Overall novelty is present but not completely unexpected.
- **Missing model details (Reviewer 3):** The description lacks clarity on: (a) how the model handles rotational symmetry of the lattice when predicting fractional coordinates; (b) how the invariant representation \( k \) is constructed and used within message passing; (c) whether the message passing module is equivariant with respect to fractional coordinates; (d) how relative fractional differences are computed and used. Also, the construction of full crystal structures from a reduced number of atoms \( N' \) is not explained: specifically, how Wyckoff positions map reduced coordinates to the full set via symmetry operations.
- **Reporting ambiguity and comparison setup (Reviewer 2):** In Table 2, it is unclear which variant is the real method. The variant using CSPML (which Reviewer 2 would call DiffCSP++) should be compared directly with competitors; the version using ground-truth space group should be presented as an upper bound on achievable performance. Similarly, in Table 3, it is not clarified whether DiffCSP++ uses ground-truth space group information (which would be unrealistic for ab-initio generation). This reporting weakens the clarity of the results and experimental fairness.
- **Relationship to DiffCSP (Reviewer 2):** The paper should better clarify which aspects are specifically contributed by the space group constraint beyond the base DiffCSP model, to more clearly highlight the incremental value.
