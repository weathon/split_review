## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces 360-InpaintR, a reference-based 360° inpainting method for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes in unbounded environments. It proceeds in three stages: (1) unseen mask generation to identify gaps after object removal, (2) depth-aware initialization of new Gaussians from a single inpainted reference view, and (3) fine-tuning the 3DGS with reference-guided multi-view inpainting. A new dataset, 360-USID, of seven object-centric scenes is also proposed. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: one finds the pipeline effective and the contributions valuable (score 6), another sees moderate novelty and important missing baselines (score 3), while a third considers the technical contribution limited and the evaluation insufficient (score 1).

### Strengths
- **Important task and framework**: The work targets the challenging problem of 3D inpainting in unbounded 360° scenes, which is relevant for AR/VR scene editing. The pipeline integrates off-the-shelf reference-guided inpainting (Left Refill) and depth estimation (Depth-Anything-V2) with 3DGS optimization.
- **Depth-aware Gaussian Spawning**: A novel component that initializes new Gaussians using inpainted depth from the reference view, ensuring geometric alignment with the surrounding scene and enabling accurate optimization. This is a key differentiator from existing methods.
- **Unseen mask generation**: The method identifies truly occluded regions requiring inpainting, reducing inconsistencies compared to naive object-mask approaches. It works accurately for many indoor and outdoor settings in the 360-USID dataset.
- **Performance**: The approach outperforms naive combinations of 2D inpainting with 3DGS optimization in both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments. Ablation studies validate the efficacy of the proposed components.
- **Dataset contribution (360-USID)**: This is the first dataset specifically designed for evaluating 360° unbounded scene inpainting for NeRF and Gaussian Splatting methods. It enables quantitative evaluation with ground-truth capture (scenes captured with and without objects to remove). This contribution is acknowledged by all reviewers, with one noting it is the primary strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The method largely combines existing techniques: (1) view-consistent mask generation is similar to SPIN-NeRF [W1] and InNeRF360 [W2] – the paper does not clearly differentiate its approach from these; (2) depth inpainting of unmasked regions is already used in InNeRF360 and DateNeRF [W3]; (3) pre-trained diffusion models for inpainting appear in DateNeRF, InFusion [W4], and InNeRF360. One reviewer states the pipeline is “engineering over novelty” aside from the depth spawning step. Another reviewer questions the claim of being the first reference-based method because Mirzaei et al. [W5] already uses a single reference image for inpainting.
- **Missing important baselines**: Several directly relevant methods are not compared: InNeRF360 (text-guided 3D-consistent inpainting on 360° scenes), SPIN-NeRF (multiview segmentation and perceptual inpainting with NeRFs), InFusion (depth completion from diffusion prior for 3DGS inpainting), Mirzaei et al. (reference-guided controllable inpainting of NeRFs), and non-generative baselines Feature 3DGS and Feature-Splatting (language-driven editing of 3DGS). This omission weakens the evaluation of the method’s advantages.
- **Confusing and inconsistent figures**: Figure 2 is misleading: (b) shows a cartoonish face despite reference-guided inpainting being claimed better; (c) changes the mask from a plant to a small hole in the ground, and views n and k are not multiview-consistent (ground texture darker in view k). Figure 7 zooms in on inpainted regions for all methods but not on the ground truth, making fair comparison impossible (e.g., Gaussian Grouping and Ours look similar but cannot be judged against GT).
- **Artifacts in supplementary videos**: Several videos (sunflower, garden, dustpan, cookie, cone, bonsai) show visible artifacts in the inpainted scenes. This raises questions about the method’s reliability and is considered a limitation that is not fully discussed.
- **3D-inconsistent unseen masks**: Despite the algorithm aiming for 3D-consistent masks, the supplementary video “cookie_compressed.mp4” exhibits inconsistencies that contradict this claim.
- **Unstable performance with single reference**: The pipeline relies heavily on the reference view’s quality; performance degrades in scenes with uneven floors when only one reference is used.
- **Dependence on off-the-shelf models**: The method’s success depends on the capabilities of external 2D depth estimation and inpainting models, which introduces failure modes not analyzed.
- **Missing experimental details and motivation**: The reason for collecting 360-USID is unclear when existing text-based 3D-consistent mask generation (e.g., InNeRF360) could provide masks without the need for “with/without object” capture. Also, it is not explained how masks are obtained for MipNeRF360 / NeRFStudio scenes, which lack ground-truth masks and have only supplementary videos, not quantitative results in the paper.
- **Minor**: The paper does not include NVS results for MipNeRF360 scenes in the main evaluation, even though they are part of the primary evaluation set.
