- Scores: 5, 6, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces BOFormer, a learning-based acquisition function for Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization (MOBO). It addresses the hypervolume identifiability issue—which stems from MOBO’s non-Markovian nature—by framing MOBO as a non-Markovian reinforcement learning problem and implementing a generalized Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework via a Transformer architecture. The method includes practical enhancements (Q-augmented observation representation, prioritized trajectory replay buffer, demo-policy-guided exploration) and is evaluated on synthetic functions and real-world hyperparameter optimization for 3D Gaussian Splatting, showing competitive performance and cross-domain transfer capabilities. Reviewer scores range from 5 to 8, with Reviewer 4 (score 8) considerably more positive than the others (scores 5, 6, 6). All reviewers find the approach novel but raise concerns about incremental contributions, theoretical depth, experimental clarity, and the choice of Transformer architecture in a small-data regime.

### Strengths
- First work to apply a learning-based acquisition function to MOBO (Reviewers 4, 1). The combination of non-Markovian RL and Transformer-based sequence modeling is a creative and fresh perspective (Reviewer 1).
- The theoretical framework connecting non-Markovian RL to MOBO is rigorously developed and mathematically sound (Reviewer 3). The Generalized DQN framework and its substantiation via sequence modeling directly tackle the hypervolume identifiability issue (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The domain-agnostic observation representation—incorporating only historical query information rather than maintaining information for all domain points—enables cross-domain transfer while being memory-efficient (Reviewer 4). The Q-augmented observation representation provides an informative indicator of prospective hypervolume improvement while remaining domain-agnostic (Reviewer 2).
- Practical enhancements (prioritized trajectory replay buffer, demo-policy-guided exploration) improve convergence, data efficiency, and search-space exploration given the limited sampling budget (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive evaluation on various synthetic and real-world tasks, with an additional ablation study on sequence length (Reviewer 4). Strong performance consistently outperforms both classical and learning-based baselines (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Well-structured related work with clear categorization and insightful comparisons to existing approaches such as OptFormer and NAP (Reviewer 4).
- The use of diagrams and examples (e.g., the hypervolume identifiability issue) aids understanding of complex concepts (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Incremental contribution and limited novelty** (Reviewers 1, 3). While the paper introduces novel elements, the core methodology builds heavily on established techniques such as OptFormer and NAP. The innovative aspects are not clearly articulated (Reviewer 1).

2. **Motivation for Transformer architecture is weak** (Reviewer 3). In the small-data regime typical of BO, Transformers have high parameter counts, raising overfitting concerns. The paper does not adequately justify why a Transformer is superior to simpler architectures (e.g., MLP) for learning the acquisition function (Reviewer 3). No stability analysis with respect to different Transformer models or initializations is provided (Reviewer 3). Moreover, the computational cost and memory requirements of Transformers may limit accessibility (Reviewer 1).

3. **Non-Markovian nature questioned** (Reviewer 4). If the state is defined as the complete set of historical queries along with the posterior distribution of candidate points, the problem becomes Markovian; the non-Markovian property appears artificially induced by the choice of a simplified state representation (Reviewer 4). The paper should address this directly.

4. **Insufficient theoretical analysis** (Reviewers 2, 3). There are no proofs of convergence or optimality for the Generalized DQN framework. The relationship between the proposed loss function and Bellman optimality equations in non-Markovian decision processes is not analyzed (Reviewer 2). The theoretical justification for computational intractability due to "curse of dimensionality" (line 244) needs more precise argumentation and supporting references (Reviewer 3).

5. **Methodology section lacks clarity and detail** (Reviewer 4). Figure 2 is not thoroughly discussed; e.g., positional encoding appears in the diagram but is never mentioned in the text, whereas Equation 5 already contains temporal information. It is unclear how `j/t` in Equation 5 is combined and encoded with other inputs (it appears to be merely an arithmetic sequence). The introduction of Off-Policy Learning and Prioritized Trajectory Replay Buffer is abrupt and lacks motivation (Reviewer 4).

6. **Temporal information in history representation raises concerns** (Reviewer 4). In BO, historical queries should be permutation-invariant; explicit temporal encoding might break this consistency. No ablation study is provided to show the impact of temporal information (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also asks whether any benefit of incorporating temporal information has been observed.

7. **Experimental setup missing key details** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The surrogate model configuration (choice of kernel, hyperparameters) is not reported (Reviewer 4). The training cost for meta-training is not reported (Reviewer 4). No information on the number of random repetitions to ensure statistical significance (Reviewer 3). The paper does not specify whether the same network architectures were used consistently across all experiments (Reviewer 3). The specific architectural details of the Transformer implementation are not provided (Reviewer 3). How the method handles continuous domains (via discretization) is not explained (Reviewer 4).

8. **Time efficiency and computational comparison missing** (Reviewer 1). The paper does not compare time efficiency of the proposed algorithm with baselines, nor does it clarify whether the non-Markovian nature consumes more time than a Markovian approach (Reviewer 1). Improvements over baseline results appear not to be significant (Reviewer 1).

9. **Scalability not addressed** (Reviewers 2, 3). The scalability of BOFormer to high-dimensional problems is not examined. The computational cost of the Transformer-based sequence model scales with input sequence length, which could increase with dimensionality (Reviewer 2). No empirical evaluation on higher-dimensional problems or theoretical analysis of computational complexity is provided (Reviewer 2). The evaluation up to 100 samples may not reflect real-world BO scenarios; more challenging problems with more samples are needed (Reviewer 3).

10. **Missing experiments and ablations** (Reviewers 3, 4). No experiment supports the idea of demo-policy-guided exploration; it is hard to see its effect (Reviewer 4). The individual contributions of the practical enhancements (Q-augmented observation, prioritized trajectory buffer, demo-policy) are not ablated (Reviewer 2). The choice of demo policy and its sensitivity are not studied (Reviewer 2). No comparison with the "direct implementation" mentioned in Section 4.1 is performed (Reviewer 4). No experiment tests the method’s effectiveness with very limited data (~10 samples), which is surprising given Transformer’s parameter count (Reviewer 3).

11. **Figure 3 is not discussed** (Reviewers 1, 4). The paper does not introduce performance profiles or explain the intended meaning of the experimental results in Figure 3 (Reviewers 1, 4).

12. **Transfer learning limitations not discussed** (Reviewer 2). The paper showcases cross-domain transfer and transfer across different numbers of objectives, but does not elaborate on scenarios where this transfer might not be applicable or effective.

13. **Mathematical formulation issues and inconsistencies** (Reviewer 4). Examples: line 345 should use `max`; line 347 should index the `i`-th objective (not `j`); inconsistent index notation (line 344 `i` indexes objectives, but line 374 `i` represents time steps); hypervolume formula (line 225) contains an unexplained `x'`; line 819 should refer to uniform distribution. Minor issues: line 201 gives incorrect appendix reference (B.2 instead of B); line 820 has an incomplete sentence.

14. **Questions that overlap with criticisms stated above** (included here when already covered). Additional specific questions from reviewers that are not yet reflected as weaknesses (but should be recorded as missing experiments/justifications):  
    - How does the Generalized DQN framework differ from the standard DQN in terms of theoretical guarantees and convergence properties? (Reviewer 2)  
    - How was OptFormer adapted for MOBO? Was it retrained from scratch or fine-tuned? (Reviewer 4)  
    - Are there potential synergies or trade-offs between the practical enhancements? (Reviewer 2)  
    - How sensitive is performance to the choice of demo policy? What characterizes a good demo policy? (Reviewer 2)  

**Minority reviewer perspective**: Reviewer 4 (score 8) is notably more positive, praising the novelty and contribution, while reviewers 1–3 (scores 5–6) are more critical of incremental contribution, theoretical depth, and experimental clarity. This contrast should be acknowledged but all criticisms are retained.
