## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SParS (Sparsity-enabled Parameter Sharing), a compression method that leverages parameter sharing, tensor decomposition, and sparsity to compress MLP layers of vision transformers. SParS uses a shared base (via SVD) and sparse factors across layers, initialized via SVD and optimized by minimizing block-wise reconstruction error. On DeiT-B and Swin-L, it compresses MLPs to 25–40% of original parameters with less than 1% accuracy loss, outperforming some baselines like GFM. Reviewers note the method is straightforward and clearly presented but raise major concerns about novelty, limited comparisons, insufficient theoretical justification, narrow scope (only vision transformers, only MLP layers, only classification), and presentation issues. One reviewer (score 6) finds it novel and well-justified; another (score 3, confidence 5) sees no novelty and considers it a straightforward combination of existing techniques.

### Strengths
- Well-organized and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).
- Straightforward method; content is rich with analysis, figures, algorithm, and experiments (R2).
- Interesting motivation and analysis, especially in Section 2 (R2).
- Empirical justification for design choices such as truncation strategy and number of grouped layers (R4).
- Achieves high compression rates (25–40%) with acceptable accuracy loss (<1%) (R1, R4).
- Outperforms some baselines (e.g., GFM) at similar compression rates (R1, R4).
- One reviewer (R4, score 6) considers the approach novel and a smart application of sparsity; another (R3, score 3) explicitly notes no strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / incremental contribution**: The method closely resembles HydraLoRA [4] (R1). All components (low-rank decomposition for FC layers, sparse training/pruning – Hoefler et al., Zhu & Gupta, Evci et al.) are existing; SParS is a straightforward combination lacking novelty (R3). The method is considered too simple without sufficient verification (R2).
- **Unjustified formulation**: Why share the same U matrix and singular values Σ across weights, with different V? The approximation error of this heuristic is not explained; empirical or theoretical studies are needed (R3). The paper lacks sufficient theoretical analysis of why SParS works and the mathematical properties of shared base and sparse factors; conditions for success/failure are not discussed (R4).
- **Limited comparisons**: Missing comparison with other parameter saving methods (R4). No comparison with structured sparsity or low-rank approximations (R4). Missing comparisons with MiniViT (Zhang et al., 2022) (R4). Missing comparisons with Distillation, NAS, and other optimization methods (R2). Differences to existing data compression methods [3,4] are not addressed (R1). Computational budget or inference time for Table 1 and Table 2 is missing (R2).
- **Narrow experimental scope**:
  - Only tested on DeiT-B (12 blocks) and Swin-L; missing Swin-B, ViT-L, and other vision transformer variants (R1, R3).
  - Only image classification; experiments on segmentation/detection benchmarks are expected (R3).
  - Not evaluated on large language models, where parameter compression is critical (R1, R2, R4). R2 notes that DeiT-B/Swin-L are not "large" compared to modern LLMs.
- **Writing/presentation issues**: Layout is chaotic, especially Section 3 (R2). Summary of contributions is too casual; high-level meaning of modules is not well mentioned (R2). Some figures lack sufficient caption explanation; hard to understand without reading text in different parts (R2). Typos: repetitive abbreviation FC (lines 103 and 107), main reference line 583 (R2). The paper does not adequately clarify the observation of Yu and Wu (2023) that activation matrices (not weight matrices) are low-rank, which is relevant to the tensor decomposition approach (R1).
- **Method limitations / open questions**:
  - Parameter sharing is only applied to MLP layers; could linear layers of QKV also use parameter sharing? (R1, R2). Is the redundancy among different blocks limited to MLP, or does the attention layer have the same phenomenon? (R1, R4).
  - Does local error minimization constrain the performance of the compressed model? (R2).
  - Why not compare with methods like MiniViT (Zhang et al., 2022) that also compress vision transformers via weight sharing? (R4).
