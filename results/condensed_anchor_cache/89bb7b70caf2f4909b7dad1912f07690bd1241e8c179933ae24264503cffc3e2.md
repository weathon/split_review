## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates distributed backdoor attacks (DBA) in decentralized federated learning (DFL), proposing a method that predicts distances between attackers to detect network topology, clusters them based on distance, and dynamically embeds local patterns from a global trigger into each cluster. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10 show improved attack success rates (ASR) over centralized attacks and naive DBA. Reviewer ratings were 6, 5, 5, 5. One reviewer was notably more positive, highlighting the novelty of studying DBA in DFL and the strong experimental results. The other three reviewers acknowledged the interesting problem and dynamic clustering approach but raised significant concerns about missing ablation studies, lack of defense evaluation, sensitivity to hyperparameters, computational cost, and practical assumptions.

### Strengths
- First work to investigate DBA in decentralized FL (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Dynamic embedding of backdoor triggers instead of fixed patterns is interesting (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Use of LSTM for distance prediction is a reasonable approach (Reviewer 2).
- The problem studied is sound and important (Reviewer 4).
- Paper structure is easy to follow (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental results show higher ASR than centralized attacks and naive DBA (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- Lacks discussion on key parameters (e.g., number of clusters, cluster size) and no sensitivity analysis of their impact on ASR (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Missing ablation studies to evaluate the contribution of each module (topology detection, clustering, dynamic embedding) (Reviewers 1, 2).
- No evaluation against common defense mechanisms (e.g., gradient clipping, robust aggregation), limiting practical relevance (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Method heavily relies on accurate distance prediction and clustering; impact of inaccuracies (e.g., in dynamic or unpredictable network environments) is not discussed, and no tolerance threshold is provided (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The trigger space is infinite; no guarantee that the dynamic selection method finds an optimal or near-optimal combination, no strategy to avoid local optima, and computational cost of exploring the trigger space is not addressed (Reviewer 2).
- Clustering and dynamic distribution may become computationally expensive with larger numbers of attackers/clients; scalability to more complex real-world topologies is unclear (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Assumes attackers can communicate and coordinate poisoned images and target labels, which is impractical in many real-world adversarial settings (Reviewer 4).
- Only focuses on DBA; does not explore possible defenses or extend to other attacks (e.g., data poisoning, model inversion), limiting contribution breadth (Reviewer 4).
- No comparison with other durable backdoor attacks (e.g., Neurotoxin) (Reviewer 2).
- Does not discuss how the choice of clustering algorithm impacts results (Reviewer 2).
- Open question whether results generalize to datasets with more than 10 classes (Reviewer 2).
