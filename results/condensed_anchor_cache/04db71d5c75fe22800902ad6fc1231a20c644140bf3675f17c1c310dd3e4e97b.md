## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents SITReg, a multi-resolution deep learning architecture for deformable medical image registration that enforces symmetry, inverse consistency, and topology preservation by construction (rather than via loss functions). It uses an implicit deformation inversion layer based on the deep equilibrium network framework. The method is evaluated on inter-subject brain MR registration (LPBA40 and Oasis datasets). The reviewers find the idea interesting but note marginal accuracy improvements, limited novelty, insufficient comparisons, and unclear presentation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written with a good introduction and clear descriptions of symmetric, inverse consistent, and topology-preserving registration (Reviewer 1). Reviewers 2 and 3 also acknowledge the clear motivation and important topic.
- The proposed architecture enforces inverse consistency and symmetry by construction, not through loss functions (Reviewer 1, 2).
- The deformation inversion layer, based on the deep equilibrium network framework, is an interesting technique (Reviewer 1).
- The work integrates existing strategies to achieve all three desired properties (symmetry, inverse consistency, topology preservation) in one end‑to‑end network (Reviewer 2).
- The method is compared against strong baselines: VoxelMorph, SYMNet, and cLapIRN. The paper provides a good summary of prior work (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Registration accuracy improvements are marginal (Table 2), and computational/memory efficiency is not improved (Table 3) (Reviewer 1, 3).
- The symmetric formulation (Section 3.1) is not entirely new; similar approaches have been used in Estienne et al. 2021 and Young et al. 2022, as the authors themselves note. The multi‑resolution formulation (Section 3.2) appears natural and is not clearly differentiated from standard multi‑resolution strategies (Reviewer 3).
- Insufficient study of related work: missing references to deep‑learning LDDMM methods (DeepFlash, NODEO, R2Net) and the closely related inverse‑consistency‑by‑construction multistep deep registration (Greer et al., MICCAI 2023). The paper should also compare with Iglesias 2023 (mentioned in the introduction) and traditional methods like SyN (ANTs) and Symmetric LDDMM (Reviewer 2).
- Unclear presentation in several places:
  – Why SYMNet does not guarantee symmetricity by construction is not explained (Reviewer 2).
  – The design of the feature extraction network h and whether it is pretrained or trained jointly is not specified (Reviewer 2).
  – Squaring‑and‑scaling alone may not guarantee diffeomorphic deformations; the paper uses the same smoothness loss as the non‑diffeomorphic VoxelMorph, which may be insufficient (Reviewer 2).
  – Figure 1 uses undefined variables x_1, x_2; the paper references a non‑existent “Figure 3.2” (Reviewer 1).
  – It is unclear whether the framework is diffeomorphic (Reviewer 1).
- Experimental evaluation is limited:
  – Only Dice overlap and Hausdorff distance on two brain datasets (LPBA40, Oasis) are reported. No landmark‑based evaluation (e.g., target registration error) is provided, and no non‑brain datasets (e.g., lung CT from EMPIRE10) are used (Reviewer 3).
  – Qualitative results (visual examples) are missing (Reviewer 2, 3).
  – Ablation studies analyzing the contribution of each design component are lacking (Reviewer 2).
- The advantage of enforcing symmetry and inverse consistency by construction (over loss‑based approaches) is not demonstrated; all compared methods achieve these properties within numerical accuracy (Reviewer 3).
- All strategies used are from prior work, raising the question of what the main technical challenge is and whether such a complicated network is necessary in practice (Reviewer 2).
