## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Smooth Training (SMOT), a training strategy for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. SMOT applies label smoothing to perturbed inputs, specifically masked images where label-relevant regions (identified by class activation maps, CAM) are removed. The soft label is softened proportionally to the masked area. This encourages the model to output lower confidence for partial inputs, widening the confidence gap between in-distribution (ID) and OOD data. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-200 benchmarks show improvements over several baselines, and the method is also applied to fine-tuning CLIP. The supplementary material provides additional justifications.

### Strengths
- The proposed SMOT strategy is intuitive, simple to implement, and technically sound for mitigating overconfidence in OOD detection (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- It is novel and makes sense to apply label smoothing to perturbed rather than original inputs (Reviewer 2).
- The paper provides theoretical analysis (Theorems 1 and 2) on how cross-entropy leads to overconfidence and how SMOT can help (Reviewer 1). However, one reviewer (Reviewer 3) found this analysis unconvincing and limited in scope (see Weaknesses).
- Comprehensive experiments across multiple base models, scoring functions, and datasets (CIFAR, ImageNet-200) demonstrate consistent OOD improvements; also shows effectiveness when fine-tuning CLIP (Reviewer 1).
- Ablation studies validate key components like the label smoothing function and masking threshold sampling distribution (Reviewer 1).
- The method does not require an auxiliary OOD dataset, making it promising when such data is unavailable (Reviewer 4).
- The supplementary materials justify many design decisions, and the paper is generally well-structured and clearly written (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 3 noted some clarity issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical analysis concerns (Reviewers 3, 4):**  
  - Theorem 1 only addresses overconfidence under cross-entropy loss, not general ERM, significantly limiting its scope (Reviewer 3).  
  - Theorem 2 provides an upper bound on OOD misclassification risk, but the connection to "overconfidence" is not made explicit (Reviewer 3).  
  - The transition from theoretical results to the SMOT masking strategy is insufficiently justified; the role of terms like \(\sqrt{C/n}\) in Eq. (4) and its relation to the theorems is unclear (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - Proofs for Theorems are missing or not referenced (Reviewer 3).  
  - One reviewer (Reviewer 3) concluded the theoretical section is hard to follow and does not clearly motivate SMOT; other reviewers found it thorough.
- **Experimental fairness and baseline selection (Reviewer 3):**  
  - Training settings differ: base models trained for 200 epochs with step-wise LR decay, while SMOT models are trained for 300 epochs with cosine decay, which could benefit OOD detection independently (Reviewer 3).  
  - Missing comparison with recent top-performing methods like ASH (OpenOOD) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Missing comparison with a highly relevant baseline that uses corrupted images with soft labels ([4]); Reviewer 3 provided a table suggesting high similarity and argued that [4] should be included as a baseline to demonstrate SMOT's advantages.
- **Missing near-OOD benchmarks (Reviewer 3):** Experiments only use far-OOD datasets; near-OOD splits (e.g., CIFAR-100 or Tiny ImageNet for CIFAR-10, SSB or NINCO for ImageNet) are omitted, limiting realism.
- **CAM reliance and alternative perturbations (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):**  
  - Using CAM to identify label-relevant regions requires a pre-trained model, adding computational cost (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  - Missing ablation comparing CAM-based masking with random masking or cropping (Reviewers 2, 3). Random masking (e.g., 30%–70% of the image) should be tested as a simpler alternative (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons with related methodological families (Reviewer 2):**  
  - The effect of data augmentation for OOD detection / calibration (e.g., [1,2]) should be discussed and compared.  
  - SMOT’s similarity to Outlier Exposure (OE) frameworks suggests that comparison with OE-based methods is needed.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity (Reviewer 1):** Further analysis needed on sensitivity to hyperparameters, e.g., how essential the CAM heatmap is, and the optimal relationship between mask size and label smoothing hyperparameter.
- **In-distribution accuracy trade-off (Reviewer 1):** SMOT shows a minor decrease in ID accuracy compared to conventional training; deeper exploration of this trade-off is desired.
- **Design choice of Eq. (9) (Reviewer 3):** The temperature-scaled exponential function (with \(t - 255\)) seems arbitrary; the value range of \(t\) and intuition behind the formulation are not explained.
- **Formatting issues (Reviewer 3):** References are inserted awkwardly (e.g., "ResNet18 He et al. (2016)") and there are mis-formatting issues like "Eq.equation 8".
- **Missing details (Reviewer 2):** The model used in Tables 2, 3, and 4 is not explicitly mentioned.
- **Design question (Reviewer 4):** Can SMOT use the updated model trained on the fly to generate the masking CAM, rather than requiring a separate pre-trained model?
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 5) was notably more critical, raising fundamental concerns about the theory, experimental fairness, baseline selection, and completeness; other reviewers (scores 6) were generally positive about the contribution and clarity.
