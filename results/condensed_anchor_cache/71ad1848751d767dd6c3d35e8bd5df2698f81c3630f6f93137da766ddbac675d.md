## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) under partially asynchronous or “hogwild” inference, where node updates occur at different times. It identifies that standard explicitly-defined GNNs fail in this setting, while implicitly-defined GNNs (fixed-point and optimization-based) are provably robust. The authors propose Energy GNN, an optimization-based implicit architecture that parameterizes a convex energy function. The paper provides theoretical convergence guarantees and evaluates on synthetic tasks (chains, counting, sums, coordinates) and benchmark datasets (MUTAG, PROTEINS, PPI). The problem is considered timely and important for distributed multi-agent systems, but reviewers have substantial concerns about the experimental scope, baselines, and real-world validation. Reviewer confidence ranges from 2 to 4 (out of 5). The most positive reviewer (score 8) still finds the experiments insufficient, while the most negative (score 5) considers the experiments too toy-like.

### Strengths
- **Timely and important problem**: Asynchronous inference in GNNs is highly relevant for distributed systems, robotic swarms, sensor networks, and temporal or agentic settings. The paper provides a principled approach where existing GNN architectures fail. (R2 S1, R4 S1, R3 S1)
- **Well-written and well-structured**: The paper clearly defines the problem, introduces complex concepts related to implicit GNNs in an accessible way, and provides a concise summary of related work. The story and motivation are easy to follow. (R1, R2 S2, R4)
- **Theoretical contributions**: The paper provides rigorous mathematical proofs for the convergence and robustness of implicitly-defined GNNs under asynchrony, drawing on distributed optimization concepts. (R1, R4 S2)
- **Novel architecture**: Energy GNN is a novel implicit GNN that uses input-convex neural networks to define a flexible and expressive energy function. This is a meaningful addition to the field. (R1, R4 S3)
- **Empirical results on synthetic tasks**: Energy GNN significantly outperforms other implicitly-defined GNNs on synthetic multi-agent tasks (chains, counting, sums) in both synchronous and asynchronous settings. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Competitive performance on benchmark datasets**: On MUTAG, PROTEINS, and PPI, Energy GNN matches or slightly outperforms synchronous baselines. (R1, R3 S1, R4)
- **Addresses a major limitation**: By focusing on implicitly-defined GNNs, the work tackles the vulnerability of conventional GNNs to asynchronous and unreliable communication, which is essential for real-world multi-agent systems. (R3 S1)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient real-world validation**: Experiments are dominated by synthetic tasks that are small and toy-like (chains, counting, sums, coordinates). Performance on real-world benchmarks is only competitive, not strongly superior; on PPI, explicitly-defined GNNs did not fail as expected, but this is not explained. Broader evaluation on real-world async scenarios is missing. (R1, R2 W2, R3, R4 W4)
- **Weak and insufficient baselines**: Comparisons are limited to basic GCN and GAT. Stronger explicitly-defined GNNs designed for long-range dependencies (e.g., Half-Hop, DRew) and graph transformers (e.g., from the Long-Range Graph Benchmark) are not included. (R2 W1, R2 Q2)
- **Lack of standard real-world async benchmarks**: The synthetic benchmarks may not correlate with real-world performance. The paper should evaluate on established benchmarks where async matters, such as the Long-Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB) or Temporal Graph Benchmarks (TGB) with many snapshots. (R2 W2, R2 Q3, R3)
- **Missing intuitive explanation for robustness of implicit GNNs**: The paper lacks an intuitive explanation of why implicitly-defined GNNs are robust to asynchrony, which is critical for reader understanding. (R1)
- **Training computationally expensive**: Implicitly-defined GNNs, including Energy GNN, require iterative forward passes, making training computationally expensive. Convergence can be sensitive to hyperparameters (step size, tolerance), and the paper does not investigate robustness to these choices. (R4 W1, R4 W3, R3 mentions training limitations)
- **Limited architectural exploration**: Experiments only use single-layer Energy GNNs. The performance of multi-layer variants and scalability to larger graphs are unclear. (R4 W2, R4 Q3)
- **Term “Energy GNN” is misleading**: The name suggests energy efficiency, which is a major AI concern, but the paper is not about energy-efficient AI. (R3)
- **Unclear separation of contributions in sections 2–4**: It is ambiguous which parts summarize existing work and which are new contributions. (R3)
- **Sparse description of Energy GNN architecture**: The main manuscript does not fully detail the computation of neuron and node states, the architecture, or training method. Properties of the model should be summarized clearly in Section 5. (R3)
- **Missing analyses and ablations**: 
  - How does the choice of staleness bound B and stagger time S affect performance and convergence? Is there a principled way to select them? (R4 Q1)
  - Have the authors explored techniques like preconditioning to address numerical instability from ill-conditioned Hessians/Jacobians? (R4 Q2)
  - How does Energy GNN scale with graph size and number of nodes? (R4 Q3)
  - How can the differences among implicit GNNs be evaluated beyond sync task performance? (R2 Q1)
- **Minor issue**: There is a typo in index notation in the target description for the Sums dataset (line 369). (R1)
- **Reviewer disagreement on experimental strength**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) praises the theoretical framing and writing but notes experiments are the main weakness. Reviewer 3 (score 5) states experiments are on toy examples that do not need machine learning and that real improvements are small. Reviewer 1 and 4 (scores 6 each) also call for broader validation. This contrast underscores that while the theoretical contribution is strong, the experimental evidence is considered insufficient by most reviewers.
