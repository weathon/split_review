## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces Cached Multi-LoRA (CMLoRA), a training-free framework for multi-concept image generation that integrates multiple LoRA modules. Based on a frequency-domain analysis, LoRAs are partitioned into high- and low-frequency sets and applied in a specific order during denoising to reduce semantic conflicts. A non-uniform caching mechanism selectively activates non-dominant LoRAs to improve efficiency. Experiments on the ComposLoRA testbed (anime and realistic styles) show improvements over training-free baselines (e.g., +2.19% CLIPScore, +11.25% MLLM win rate vs. LoraHub, LoRA Composite, LoRA Switch). The method is novel but receives mixed confidence from reviewers regarding its theoretical justification and experimental convincingness.

### Strengths

- The paper is well written and easy to follow among similar works (R1).  
- The frequency-domain analysis of LoRA behavior is an interesting and novel idea, moving beyond naive integration to a systematic sequencing strategy (R2, R3).  
- The proposed solution is easy and clear, and the experiments effectively explain the method’s advantages (R2). However, one reviewer noted that the high-level insight behind this approach is not very obvious (R2).  
- The methodology is sound, supported by rigorous experimentation with clear mathematical formulations for the caching mechanism and frequency-based ordering (R3).  
- The training-free nature of CMLoRA addresses a significant practical limitation, increasing applicability for resource-constrained scenarios (R3).  
- Claims are verified through quantitative evaluations (R1), though another reviewer found results good but not fully convincing (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Visual quality and comparisons:** The quality of figures (e.g., Fig 4, 6) can be improved (R1). There is a lack of visual comparisons for the subjective aspects of the evaluation; in particular, the claims at the end of page 9 and the beginning of page 10 are not supported by visual evidence (R1). Failure cases are not discussed or shown (R3).  
- **Mixed quantitative results with cache mechanism:** The analysis of the cache mechanism alone shows inconsistent or mixed CLIPScore results, making it difficult to identify systematic improvement or degradation (R1).  
- **Missing efficiency evaluation:** No evaluation of computational effort or efficiency is provided, despite caching being a key contribution (R1). Sensitivity of caching hyperparameters \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) and their impact on both efficiency and image quality is not explored (R3).  
- **Need for more theoretical/empirical justification:** The reason for shifting from the spatial to the frequency domain is not clearly motivated; more analysis is needed to explain why frequency-domain analysis is necessary for multi-LoRA composition (R2). The observation that some LoRAs amplify high-frequency features and others focus on low-frequency elements is based on a naïve experiment; a more comprehensive theoretical or analytical justification is required (R2). The existence of “semantic conflicts” is argued but not directly verified through experiments (R2).  
- **Assumptions and method details:** The assumption underlying Figure 1 (that meaningful amplitude differences happen at the same time steps for two LoRAs) needs justification; it is not clear if different LoRA categories are well-aligned along time steps (R2). The proposed solution in Section 2.2 (Eq. 2, 3) is introduced without a progressive high-level explanation (R2). The collective guidance term in Eq. (5) appears related to classifier-free guidance and requires further analysis (R2).  
- **Limited generalization and metrics:** Evaluations are restricted to anime and realistic styles within the ComposLoRA testbed; robustness to other domains is not demonstrated (R3). CLIPScore and the used metrics may not fully capture compositional fidelity, especially as the number of LoRAs increases (R3).  
- **Marginal gains and missing ablations:** Benchmark improvements in Table 1 appear marginal (R2). The exact impact of the frequency-based LoRA partitioning is not isolated, and alternative sequencing strategies are not compared (R3). Performance with respect to different LoRA categories (beyond the two used in the analysis) is not reported (R2).  
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R1, score 6) found the experiments and writing more convincing, while the other two (R2, R3, score 5) raised concerns about theoretical depth, experimental convincingness, and missing analyses, making the overall contribution appear less robust.
