## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers (R1, R2, R4) gave a score of 1 (very negative) with confidence 5 (absolutely certain); one reviewer (R3) gave a score of 3 (marginally positive) with confidence 4. All reviewers agree the paper is not publication‑ready. The majority finds the method lacks novelty, validation, and proper comparison; the minority (R3) considers the method understandable and functional but still insufficient.

### Strengths
- Divides controllable generation into two steps, separating controllability from quality (R1).
- Method appears to work (R3). *Note: this positive view is contradicted by multiple other reviewers who question controllability and results.*
- Paper is easy to understand and steps are clear (R3). *Contrast: R1 and R4 found the writing poor and the presentation rough.*
- Suggests the potential of harnessing multiple pre‑trained models for improved generation (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The method is seen as a trivial fusion of existing pre‑trained models (VQGAN + diffusion) without demonstrating non‑triviality (R3, R4). R3 states it “lacks novelty seriously.” R4 calls the algorithm “a fusion of two pre‑trained models.” R3 is somewhat less critical here (score 3) than the others.
- **Unclear motivation**: Multiple reviewers question the design choices. Why split controllable generation into two steps? Why use VQGAN in the first stage? Could classifier guidance directly on the diffusion model replace VQGAN? What are the specific advantages of the pre‑trained models given that the first stage still requires loss optimization (training burden)? (R1, R4)
- **Insufficient validation of controllability**: The final generated images do not consistently align with the input segmentation map, undermining the claimed controllability (R1).
- **Missing comparisons with strong baselines**: The paper does not compare with state‑of‑the‑art controllable generation methods such as ControlNet and T2I‑Adapter (R1). It also omits relevant recent works like SceneComposer (CVPR 2023) (R3).
- **No standard image quality metrics**: The paper lacks FID or CLIP scores (R1, R3). Without these, the claimed quality improvement cannot be objectively assessed.
- **Missing ablation studies**: The contribution of each component and each loss term is not validated separately (R1, R4). The selection of coefficients in Eq. (2) is not justified (R4).
- **Unexplained variance in results**: Table 1 shows notably larger variance for the proposed method; no explanation is provided (R4).
- **Lack of generation details**: Time and memory requirements are not reported (R4). More example images are needed (R3).
- **Poor presentation and insufficient detail**: The paper is “too rough” and does not meet conference standards (R2). Figures, especially Fig. 2, need more details (R3). Writing requires polishing (R1). The overall level of detail (e.g., pipeline description, results) is insufficient (multiple reviewers).
