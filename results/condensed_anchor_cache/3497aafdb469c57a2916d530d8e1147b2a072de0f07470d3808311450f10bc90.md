## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Self-Pruner, a post-training structured pruning framework for LLMs that uses an LLM (GPT-4) to guide an evolutionary search for layer-wise pruning rates. The LLM initializes populations, performs crossover and mutation, and evaluates candidate configurations via perplexity. Experiments on LLaMA and Vicuna families show reduced model size and inference speedups (e.g., LLaMA-2-70B to 49B with 0.80% accuracy drop, 1.39× speedup; to 35B with 3.80% drop, 1.70× speedup) compared to methods like LLM-Pruner and Wanda-sp.

### Strengths

- **Computational efficiency**: Avoids retraining, reducing computational overhead, which is practical for deployment.
- **Measurable performance gains**: Inference speedups (up to 1.70×) and memory reduction are demonstrated.
- **Novel use of LLM in evolutionary algorithms**: The idea of leveraging an LLM to autonomously execute population generation, selection, crossover, and mutation is novel in application, though its implementation lacks rigor.
- **Reduction of human effort**: Using the LLM for mutation and crossover minimizes manual intervention.
- **Readability**: The paper is easy to follow.
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 4, score 5)**: This reviewer found the approach interesting and noted the practical speedup (30% pruning to 49B level with 1.39× speedup). However, they still identified significant weaknesses.

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient novelty**: The use of an LLM for population initialization and mutation/crossover is a straightforward adaptation of existing evolutionary methods without significant technical innovation for LLM pruning. No detailed exploration is given of why an LLM is more suited than simpler initialization methods, nor of any unique aspects of LLM-conducted mutation/crossover.
- **Speculative assumption**: The claim that LLMs possess intrinsic knowledge of their own redundancy is not empirically validated; this makes the approach feel ad hoc rather than rigorously justified. The hypothesis was not tested.
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: Several important structured pruning methods are omitted from main comparisons, including OWL (only in an ablation study), SliceGPT, FLAP, SLEB, Shortened LLaMA, ShortGPT, LaCo, Bonsai, and Sheared LLaMA. These works were published before the ICLR deadline and reduce contextual relevance. OWL should be included in Tables 1 and 2, not just in ablation.
- **Unfair comparison with OWL**: Given OWL’s foundational role in sparsity distribution for large models, its omission from main tables weakens the evaluation.
- **No comparison with traditional evolutionary algorithms**: The paper does not compare Self-Pruner with standard genetic programming or other EA-based pruning methods without LLM involvement, making it unclear whether the LLM justifies its high computational cost.
- **Algorithm stability**: The framework relies on GPT-4 with parameters like temperature and top-k, but no analysis of result variability is provided. Specific settings (temperature, top-k) and token consumption during search are not disclosed.
- **Fitness evaluation cost**: Calculating perplexity for a large model (e.g., LLaMA-70B) is prohibitively expensive given the search space—discrete sparsity 0.1–0.9 for N layers yields N^9 configurations; for N=80, the space is ~1.34×10^17. The total time spent on these evaluations is not reported, which is a critical practical bottleneck.
- **Lack of runtime comparison**: No total pruning time, time per iteration, or scalability analysis with model size is provided. This is needed to demonstrate superiority, as previous EA-based pruning is time-consuming. Reviewer questions requesting runtime metrics are therefore relevant.
- **Number of evaluations**: The paper does not compare the number of evaluations required by Self-Pruner versus other pruning methods, nor analyze the trade-off between evaluation time and pruning performance. No techniques to mitigate evaluation time are mentioned.
- **High pruning ratio issues**: At high pruning ratios (e.g., 50% sparsity), perplexity increases sharply while accuracy on commonsense tasks is less affected. This inconsistency lacks explanation, and no generated text examples are provided to illustrate whether the model can still “speak normally” when perplexity exceeds 10.
- **Method name confusion**: “Self-Pruner” is unclear—self-pruning could apply to any pruning method. The structured pruning mechanism is not well described: the supplementary material reveals that pruning uses Wanda_sp (column masking) without making the pruned matrix dense again, raising questions about how speedup is achieved.
- **Selection of LLM**: The paper uses GPT-4 exclusively without justifying this choice. It is unclear whether smaller or different LLMs would perform equally well in the initialization role.
- **Lack of generalizability**: The technique is not tested on unstructured pruning, nor is it clarified whether the approach extends beyond the current setting.
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: While three reviewers rated the paper 3, one reviewer (Reviewer 4) gave a higher score of 5, noting the idea is interesting and speedup is achieved, but still raised the same core weaknesses regarding missing baselines, evaluation cost, and unclear methodology.
