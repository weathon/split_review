## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the loss of compositional ability in tuning-based personalization of text-to-image diffusion models. The authors identify semantic drift of the learned concept from its superclass as the core cause, supported by empirical observations and theoretical analysis. They propose ClassDiffusion, a simple semantic preservation loss that regulates the concept space during fine-tuning. The method is extended to video generation. Reviewers find the problem investigation thorough and the solution elegantly simple, with clear improvements over baselines. However, concerns cluster around insufficient ablation (especially SPL weight choice), limited multi-subject and fine-grained evaluation, underdeveloped video extension, ambiguity in figures and metrics (CLIP use, BLIP2-T novelty, Figure 3a), and redundancy in writing/figures. Reviewer 1 (score 8) is more positive, noting strong results but also a theoretical limitation; Reviewer 4 (score 5) is most critical, raising several specific technical issues.

### Strengths
- Identifies semantic drift (rather than typical overfitting) as the root cause of compositional ability loss in personalization tuning, supported by both empirical experiments and theoretical analysis (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Provides a simple yet effective solution (semantic preservation loss) that is practical for implementation (R1, R3).
- Comprehensive experimental results with clear improvements over existing baselines (R1, R2, R3); includes additional relevant baselines (R1).
- Theoretically analyzes sampling difficulty based on joint conditional probability distribution (R4).
- Well-written with logical flow from phenomenon to proof to solution (R1, R4); uses a vivid example that eases understanding (R4).
- Extends the method to video generation, demonstrating flexibility (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient ablation studies**: The paper lacks comprehensive ablation on components of the semantic preservation loss (R3). For SPL weight, only one value (1) is used in Dataset A without rigorous exploration of the trade-off between combining ability and concept fidelity (“higher SPL weights preserve combining ability better, lower SPL weights restore specific concept features more effectively”) – quantitative metrics and a wider range of weights should be examined (R4).
- **Limited multi-subject and fine-grained evaluation**: Only quantitative results for two subjects are provided; qualitative results for three or more subjects are missing from both main text and appendix (R2). It is unclear whether the method handles fine-grained distinctions (e.g., two dogs of different breeds) – request visual representation of joint embeddings for similar and diverse subjects (R2).
- **Underdeveloped video generation extension**: The video extension feels preliminary and would benefit from more detailed exploration (R3).
- **Redundancy in writing and figures**: Figure 2 adds little beyond Figure 1 and lacks quantitative analysis; the writing could be more focused (R4). Figures other than Fig. 4 are described as simplistic; Fig. 2 lacks clarity with its caption; in Table 1, every similarity is bolded, causing confusion (R1).
- **Contradiction in CLIP usage**: The paper claims “CLIP-T metric can hardly reflect the actual performance of personalized generation” but uses CLIP to calculate loss (Equation 10). The rationale for using CLIP despite its limitations for evaluation is not adequately justified – need to explain why CLIP is suitable for capturing semantic similarity in the text embedding space but not in the image-text space (R4). Evidence or references for the claim about CLIP-T metric limitations are missing (R4).
- **BLIP2-T metric novelty unclear**: It is not stated whether BLIP2-T metric is proposed by the authors or based on existing work. If novel, more details on development and validation are required (R4).
- **Figure 3a ambiguity**: It is unclear whether Figure 3a is an experimental result or a schematic diagram. If experimental, data source and processing steps should be described; if diagrammatic, rigorous notation and clearer annotations are needed (R4).
- **Theoretical limitation (minority positive reviewer)**: R1 (score 8) notes that semantic loss may not fully restore the original semantic space because the model has many parameters and fine-tuning with few samples makes minimal adjustments; theoretically, using the entire training text data could mitigate this. The method should be explained further (R1, also method could benefit from more detailed explanation).
- **Limited comparisons and diversity**: More extensive comparisons with other SOTA methods addressing similar issues are needed, along with more diverse case studies beyond the dog-headphone example (R3).
