## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Craftium, a framework based on the open-source Minetest game engine that provides customizable, computationally efficient, and rich 3D environments for open-ended and embodied AI research. It bridges the gap between simplistic 2D grid environments and computationally expensive closed-source 3D platforms like Minecraft. Craftium integrates with the Gymnasium API, uses Lua scripting for environment creation, and demonstrates substantial performance gains (over 2,000 steps per second more than Minecraft-based frameworks) across multiple benchmark tasks, including classic RL, procedural generation, and open-world simulations. The paper also includes examples using LLM-based agents and comparisons with MineDojo and VizDoom.

### Strengths
- **Computational efficiency**: Built on efficient C++ (Minetest) instead of Java (Minecraft), achieving over 2,000 steps per second more than Minecraft-based frameworks like MineDojo.
- **Addresses a real need**: Provides a flexible, fast, and customizable 3D environment platform for open-ended and embodied AI research, moving beyond simplistic 2D grids.
- **Comprehensive benchmarks**: Includes detailed comparisons against MineDojo and VizDoom, demonstrating performance and versatility.
- **Strong compatibility**: Gymnasium interface allows seamless integration with existing RL tools and algorithms.
- **Clear architecture**: Explains how Minetest’s open-source flexibility enables RL-specific modifications (reward functions, synchronous client-server interactions), and shows diverse example environments (classic RL, open-ended learning, continual learning).
- **Ease of use**: Well-written paper with detailed experiments and a straightforward Lua API for environment creation.
- **Procedural generation support**: Efficient procedural environment generation demonstrated with multiple tasks (e.g., chop tree, spiders attack, reach diamond).

### Weaknesses
- **Single-agent only**: Currently supports only single-agent scenarios; multi-agent support is acknowledged as future work. This restricts applicability to cooperative/competitive multi-agent RL research.
- **Limited novelty**: The framework is essentially a wrapper around Minetest, with modifications focused on efficiency rather than fundamentally new functionality. Some reviewers find the novelty minimal, while others consider the pragmatic contribution sufficient (reviewer disagreement: scores 3, 8, 8, 6).
- **Insufficient comparison with other 3D simulators**: Missing detailed comparisons with widely used robotic/embodied simulators such as IsaacGym, PyBullet, MuJoCo, Habitat, AI2-THOR, and ProcTHOR. The paper does not adequately address how Craftium compares in capabilities for high-level cognitive tasks.
- **Weak experimental comparisons**: Benchmarks lack clarity on baseline performance and real-time comparisons. For example, it is unclear how training in Craftium tasks compares with training in MineDojo or other simulators. Performance against real time is not reported.
- **Misleading claim about current research**: The statement that open-endedness research “usually defaults to simplistic 2D grid environments” overlooks recent advances such as OMNI-EPIC, EnvGen, and Craftax. The authors should revise this claim and better position Craftium relative to these works.
- **Procedural generation environments are effectively 2D**: The environments in Figure 12 can be represented entirely in ASCII (2D grid with 3D rendering). Evidence of genuinely rich 3D procedural worlds is lacking.
- **Figure 2 unclear**: The differences between Craftium and Minecraft are not highlighted. Which components in Figure 2 are not supported in Minecraft? This is important for understanding the contribution.
- **Motivation of experiments unclear**:
  - Section 3.5.2 (LLava-Agent vs PPO+LSTM) lacks justification: Why compare these two? Does Minecraft already support such RL/environment generation (if so, Craftium’s added value is unclear)?
  - The meaning of the rightmost icons in Figure 11 is not explained.
- **Figure 11 issues**: Why are there two maximum red lines for the LLava-Agent? Why is performance reported as percentage of steps instead of absolute number of steps?
- **Figure 12 missing analysis**: In the first two generated environments the agent obtains zero reward even though they appear simplest. Did the agent ever successfully reach the diamond in any generated environment?
- **Table 1 “active” criterion**: Whether a framework is active after some time is not a reliable or comparable characteristic; there is no guarantee of continued maintenance.
- **Gymnasium advantage not detailed**: Table 1 lists Gymnasium as an advantage, but the paper does not systematically explain how Gymnasium implementation improves RL training (e.g., through specific internal state information that Minecraft cannot provide).
- **Missing related work on open-ended 3D environments**: References [1,2,3] showing the possibility of creating 3D environments in an open-ended way are missing.
- **Minor issues**: Typo on line 1027 (“Then, the agent that the agent…”); missing reference to Evocraft.
- **Missing ablation studies**: More ablation studies on design choices (e.g., choice of Lua, scaling with complexity) are needed.
- **Unanswered questions on design and scaling**:
  - What are the key limitations (synchronization, computational cost, engine modifications) in extending to multi-agent?
  - How does computational performance scale with environment complexity?
  - What influenced the choice of Lua for environment creation?
  - Does Craftium support Minecraft Redstone components (useful for building complex structures)?
  - How does Craftium's procedural generation compare in diversity and scalability to existing continuous/open-ended RL environment generators?
  - Why is the “spiders attack” task considered challenging (denser reward signal than “chop tree”)?
  - Compare the provided APIs between Minecraft and Craftium; what disadvantages of Minecraft besides efficiency are addressed?
