## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BMLM, a method that fine-tunes an autoregressive LLM (Mistral-7B-Instruct) into a bidirectional model by removing the causal mask and adding joint intent-classification and slot-filling classifiers, trained with LoRA. Evaluated on ATIS, SNIPS, MixATIS, and MixSNIPS, BMLM outperforms both non-LLM baselines and prompted autoregressive LLM baselines in semantic accuracy and achieves a 123×–189× speedup in inference.

### Strengths
- The motivation and proposed approach are clearly explained, and the evaluation convincingly shows that BMLM outperforms baselines and is substantially faster.
- The paper investigates an under-explored scenario: adapting existing autoregressive LLMs to bidirectional processing for understanding tasks.
- Results across multiple benchmarks and varying data ratios demonstrate the effectiveness of the method.
- Addressing sequence labeling tasks with a generative framework is challenging; the authors’ effort to adapt such a framework is commendable.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** Bidirectional transformers (e.g., BERT-based) or RNNs with joint intent and slot classifiers have been used for years in SLU. The main source of the reported score improvements appears to be the larger LLM backbone, not a novel architectural or algorithmic contribution.
- **Narrow task scope.** The paper focuses only on spoken language understanding (SLU) datasets, which constrains the broader impact. Validating on more diverse semantic understanding tasks (e.g., by framing the model as a general bidirectional semantic parser) would strengthen the contribution.
- **Lack of crucial ablations.** The claim that removing the autoregressive mask is beneficial for encoding contextual information is not supported by necessary ablations. The paper should compare with: (a) the same framework but with a standard autoregressive mask (i.e., keeping the causal mask while using the same classification heads), (b) unidirectional fine-tuning approaches (e.g., causal language modelling loss without prompting, or repeating the input utterance and classifying on the second pass), and (c) a GPT2-based model at the same scale as RoBERTa to isolate the effect of model scale.
- **Insufficient analysis of BMLM itself.** The analysis is task-specific and lacks deeper investigation of the effect of bidirectional post-training. Presenting attention heatmaps after fine-tuning, exploring how different training setups or datasets affect BMLM, and testing generalization beyond SLU would improve the study.
- **Methodological clarity issues.** It is unclear how the parameter K in Equation 6 is determined or derived. The difference between BMLM and a simple mask modification is not sufficiently explained.
- **Experimental reporting gaps.** The paper does not specify whether reported results are from a single run or an average over multiple runs, and the t-test procedure (number of runs, confidence intervals) is not described. This undermines the reliability of the claims.
- **Incomplete code and documentation.** The provided code is incomplete and lacks clear instructions for running it and for implementing specific model components.
- **Missing study on intent-count prediction errors.** The model determines the number of intents per utterance, but it is unclear how a wrong prediction affects slot/intent labels; more in-depth experiments are needed.
- **Simplicity of existing datasets.** The multi-intent datasets (e.g., MixATIS/MixSNIPS) rely on explicit indicators like “and,” which may not reflect real-world utterances where intent boundaries are ambiguous.
- **Misleading terminology.** The term “spoken language understanding” is used, but the model is not specifically designed for speech input; “natural language understanding” (NLU) would be more accurate.
- **Context-dependent speedup claim.** The reported speedup (123×–189×) is highly dependent on the prompt length of the autoregressive baseline (En-Mistral). The paper should report the average context lengths used for both models and explore whether shorter prompts for En-Mistral could improve its inference speed while maintaining performance.
- **Missing recent related work.** Several relevant SLU papers are not cited, including works on multi-intent detection, co-guiding, and LLM-based SLU (e.g., Xing & Tsang, 2023; Qin et al., 2024; Zhuang et al., 2024; Pham et al., 2023; He et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; and others).
- **Relevance questioned.** With the rise of large, general-purpose language models and agent-based frameworks that handle end-to-end dialogue, the practical utility of a standalone NLU module is uncertain.
- **Presentation issues.** The paper should mark model sizes in tables (e.g., base vs. large for RoBERTa) and clarify ambiguous phrasing (e.g., L060–L062, L377–L378).
