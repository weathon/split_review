## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ZET-LLM, a method that leverages pre-trained LLMs as zero-shot feature extractors for tabular prediction tasks. It converts each tabular feature into a textual sentence (e.g., "feature is value"), uses bidirectional attention (replacing autoregressive masking) to encode each sentence into a single token via mean pooling, aggregates all feature tokens with a trainable transformer-based Feature Integrator and a CLS token, and finally uses a trainable MLP predictor. Missing values are handled by masking. Experiments are conducted on binary classification, multi-class classification, and regression tasks. Two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 4) find the idea interesting and the results promising, while three reviewers (scores 3, confidence 3-4) raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental rigor, missing baselines, and the accuracy of the "zero-shot" claim.

### Strengths

- The idea of employing LLM2Vec (bidirectional attention + mean pooling) for tabular data representation learning is interesting and novel in its combination of feature-wise serialization. (R1, R2, R5)
- Feature-wise serialization (encoding each feature as a separate token rather than the whole sample as one token) alleviates issues of feature ordering and token length limits, and ablation shows it outperforms sample-wise serialization. (R1, R2, R5)
- The masking strategy for missing values is a practical advantage, common in tabular data, and is shown to improve performance in ablations. (R1, R2)
- Performance comparisons cover diverse tasks (binary, multiclass, regression) and datasets (including medical records and e-commerce). (R1, R3, R5)
- The paper is clearly written and the illustrative figure effectively depicts the method. (R3, R5)
- The model can naturally integrate unstructured text features, a unique advantage over traditional tabular models. (R1, R5)
- The Feature Integrator provides interpretability through attention scores. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty.** Much of the approach builds on well-established concepts: the Feature Integrator closely resembles TabTransformer’s mechanism; missing value masking is widely used; text serialization for tabular data is common (citations to TabLLM, LIFT, etc.). The main novelty is feature-wise serialization. (R1, R3) One reviewer (R3) notes that an ablation study on serialization methods (e.g., manual templates, table-to-text, LLM-generated) is missing, as prior work shows serialization choice significantly affects results. (R3)
- **Misleading title and “zero-shot” claim.** The title claims “zero-shot,” but the Feature Integrator and MLP predictor are trained with labeled samples, requiring many tabular samples. This is inconsistent with the zero-shot claim. (R2, R4) Task-specific fine-tuning makes the method non-scalable; domain-specific rather than task-specific fine-tuning is suggested. (R4)
- **Comparison with LLM2Vec is unclear.** The main technical contribution over LLM2Vec is not articulated. (R2) The method directly uses LLM2Vec’s bidirectional attention and mean pooling; feeding each feature sentence individually rather than as a paragraph should be empirically justified. (R2)
- **Missing comparisons to key baselines.** Several critical baselines are absent: TabLLM (same serialization paradigm), TabTransformer, SAINT, TabPFN, InterpreTabNet, TableLlama, and TabDDPM (diffusion-based). The paper claims state-of-the-art performance but does not compare with these methods. (R3, R4, R5) Additionally, comparing with pre-trained LLM embedding models (e.g., text-embedding-3-small) that are already optimized for embeddings could determine if modifying LLMs adds value. (R1)
- **Weak ablation and experimental setup.** The ablation process for serialization is unclear: what is the baseline model? (R5) The claim that a single transformer block is sufficient for the Feature Integrator is not supported by an ablation on number of blocks or pooling alternatives (e.g., all-token mean vs. CLS). (R3) The study does not test on popular benchmark datasets (Adult, Bank, Default). (R5) Table 1 and Figure 2 are contributions from prior work and should be moved to appendix. (R3)
- **Inconsistent notation and terminology.** Equation 3 begins the combined embedding with *z*, while Figure 1 begins with *z_cls* – inconsistency. (R3) The term “token” is used incorrectly in place of “token embedding” throughout (e.g., “encoding all of them into a single token”). (R3) The notation for feature-value pairs $(k_i, v_i)$ is ambiguous regarding multiple values per feature. (R5)
- **Motivation for bidirectional attention is not empirically justified.** The paper claims autoregressive LLMs attend more to end-of-sequence tokens (without citation) and thus replace masking. However, autoregressive models (e.g., GPT) outperform BERT-like models today; an empirical comparison between the bidirectional approach and the original autoregressive approach is needed. (R3)
- **Computational cost concerns.** Encoding each feature sentence separately leads to high time cost for datasets with many features. (R2) The pretrained LLMs (Bio-Medical-LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3-8B) have billions of parameters; despite being frozen, computational costs far exceed baseline methods. A discussion of model size, FLOPs, and performance trade-offs is missing, as is a comparison with TableLlama which is more cost-efficient. (R3) No assessment of computational efficiency compared to traditional tabular models is provided. (R5)
- **Missing value masking may be suboptimal.** In cases where missing values carry implicit information (e.g., non-response bias), masking might be worse than imputation or attention-based weighting. (R5)
- **Handling of numeric precision and large tables.** The paper does not address how floating-point values are handled beyond converting them to text, nor how tables with many rows or columns are truncated. (R4)
- **Unconvincing framing.** The statement that “potential of autoregressive LLMs for tabular prediction has been explored only on a limited scale” is poorly framed given extensive existing work. (R3, R4) The claim that LLMs are underexplored due to their autoregressive nature ignores the main challenge: the heterogeneous nature of tabular feature spaces. (R3)
