## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GPT4LoRA, a training-free method for linearly combining multiple LoRA weights in text-to-image diffusion models (SDXL). It uses a multimodal large language model (GPT-4o) to generate combination coefficients, then iteratively refines them via a Generate-Feedback-Refine loop. Experiments cover 3-LoRA combinations (105 composition sets) in realistic and anime styles, evaluated with GPT-4o and CLIP scores.

### Strengths
- Training-free approach to optimize LoRA composition by leveraging MLLM self-reflection (Generate-Feedback-Refine), offering a new paradigm without additional fine-tuning or architecture changes. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Constructed a testbed of 24 LoRA models based on SDXL for evaluation. (Reviewer 3)
- Shows some qualitative improvements and potentially cheaper/easier control compared to methods requiring further training. (Reviewer 1)
- Reviewer 2 is notably more positive, emphasizing extensive experiments that demonstrate superior performance over baselines and highlighting the novelty of MLLM self-reflection for LoRA combination.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited contribution and novelty:** The method is considered low on contributions by some reviewers, bordering on a blog post rather than a full paper. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Weak quantitative results:** Table 2 results lack statistical significance tests; improvements are marginal and not clearly significant, especially compared to LoRA-Composite. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)  
- **Efficiency concerns:** GPT4LoRA incurs high computational cost—multiple GPT-4o calls (potentially >10 per image) and multiple SDXL generations for each iteration—yet yields only marginal improvements; this contradicts the paper's claim that prior methods are computationally costly. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Inconsistent claims:** The paper motivates GPT4LoRA by stating prior work is computationally expensive and evaluation is subjective, yet GPT4LoRA is even more costly and uses a similar MLLM-based evaluation. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Overlap with prior work [1] (Zhong et al.):** Sections on diffusion models and LoRA combination are nearly identical with only minor paraphrasing; Table 1 and the GPT-based evaluation approach (comparative evaluation, two dimensions, point-wise scoring, win rate, evaluation prompts) are essentially the same but without explicit citation. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Limited experiments and missing ablations:**  
  - Only 3-LoRA combinations evaluated (105 sets); prior works explore 2-5 LoRAs with more composition sets. (Reviewer 3)  
  - No human evaluation. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Missing analysis on number of iterations and per-step contributions; each iteration adds cost but the benefit is not ablated. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Missing analysis on number of LoRA weights (e.g., beyond three). (Reviewer 1)  
  - Few-shot sample selection is critical but details are sparse; k=5 chosen without justification. (Reviewers 2, 4)  
  - Lacks comparisons with comparable methods (ZipLoRA, LoRA Composite) on standard datasets (e.g., DreamBooth, StyleDrop). (Reviewer 2)  
- **Reproducibility and evaluation concerns:**  
  - The evaluation benchmark is unclear (no citation); newly created benchmark with 105 sets vs. prior work's 480 sets, and the math for 105 sets from 24 LoRAs is not explained. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
  - Position bias of GPT-4o as evaluator is not addressed (no switching/averaging of image order). (Reviewer 3)  
  - Same MLLM (GPT-4o) used for both generation and evaluation may introduce bias. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Win rates in Figure 3 show only one decimal place despite >3K comparative evaluations (105 sets × 10 repeats × 3 seeds). (Reviewer 3)  
- **Method robustness:** Relies on closed-source GPT-4o; unclear if transferable to open-source MLLMs, limiting applicability. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Ablation showing weakness:** Without few-shot demonstrations, performance drops below LoRA-Merge (Table 3), casting doubt on the actual contribution of the Generate-Feedback-Refine pipeline. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - Figure 3: legend and x-axis mismatch; legends block bars. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
  - Reference formatting errors (e.g., lines 144, 214, 215). (Reviewers 2, 4)  
  - Some results unexplained: point-wise evaluation scores in Figure 3 vary significantly when baseline changes (e.g., ~7.0 vs LoRA Merge, >9 vs LoRA Composite). (Reviewer 3)  
- **Missing implementation details:** Baselines (LoRA Composite, LoRA Switch) were reproduced with different backbone/resolution; details needed for fairness. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Missing supplementary experiments:** Claimed two-LoRA experiments with ZipLoRA are absent from supplementary material. (Reviewer 3)
