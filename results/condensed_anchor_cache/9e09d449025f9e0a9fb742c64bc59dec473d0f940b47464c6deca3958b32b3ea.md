## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies long-context linear system identification (autoregressive models of order \(p>1\)). The authors establish sample complexity bounds for constrained least squares estimators that match the i.i.d. parametric rate up to logarithmic factors, demonstrating a “learning-without-mixing” phenomenon. They extend the results to (i) low-rank representations with improved rates via rank-regularized estimators, and (ii) misspecified context lengths in strictly stable systems where shorter contexts can be statistically advantageous. Theoretical guarantees are provided for all three settings.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and exposition**: The paper is well-organized, with clearly stated definitions, assumptions, and logical progression through each theoretical component (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Novel theoretical contributions**: Non-asymptotic bounds are provided for long-context AR systems, which previous works covering only first-order dependencies had not addressed. The “learning-without-mixing” result extends prior literature on linear system identification (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Well-motivated problems**: The low-rank representation setting and misspecification scenario are introduced with clear justification for how they affect error bounds (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Low-rank extension appreciated**: One reviewer highlights the low-rank approach as a strength (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **No specific algorithm analysis**: The bounds assume an optimal solution to the optimization problems (including the non-convex rank-constrained problem). The authors do not analyze any concrete solver or its computational complexity, making it unclear how sample complexity depends on the algorithmic approach (Reviewer 3).
- **Assumption 3.9 too restrictive**: The misspecification result requires \(\| (MA^\star - MA^\star_{1:p'})L^\star \|_{\text{op}} \leq D'\), which may not hold in practice. The authors could discuss limitations or heuristics if this assumption is violated (Reviewer 1).
- **Rank tuning unclear when unknown**: There is no guidance on selecting the rank parameter in practice (e.g., on real data). One reviewer explicitly considers this a critical weakness and rates the paper low (Rating 3) for this reason (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing definition of \(E\) in main text**: The matrix \(E\) appears in Equation (14) but is only defined in the appendix; the main text should be self-contained (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Contradiction or confusion about constant dependencies**: The paper claims the constant \(C\) and logarithmic terms are independent of mixing-related quantities (line 379), yet the explicit constant \(C(\delta)\) depends on the diameter \(D\), which is a bound on the operator norm of \(M_{\mathbf{A}^\star}\) (Assumption 3.4). The authors should clarify this (Reviewer 2).
- **Condition number of \(L_\star\) not discussed**: The main results depend on this condition number, but there is no analysis of how it behaves (e.g., when \(A_i^\star\) are equal or i.i.d. normal, or its dependence on \(T\)). The dependency on \(\log(\kappa)\) should be clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Minimal experiments**: Experiments are only provided in the appendix, and the main text contains no simulation results. For a paper on system identification, this is insufficient (Reviewer 2). Additionally, details of how non-convex problems are solved in the simulations are lacking (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues**: The mathematics is dense and could be better explained with more matrix operation details (Reviewer 3). There is a typo in Equation (18) (Reviewer 2). Multiple typos appear in the appendix (e.g., “martices”, “rearrainging”) (Reviewer 2). The abbreviation OLS is used early but only defined later (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor questions left unaddressed**: Questions about slow-mixing settings when the system is marginally stable, specific applications for misspecified models, and how coordinate descent could learn \(P^\star\) in polynomial time are raised but not resolved in the review (Reviewer 1).
