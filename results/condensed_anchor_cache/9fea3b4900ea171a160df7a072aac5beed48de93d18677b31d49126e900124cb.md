## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SIDCLIP (Synthesize-Initialize-Distill CLIP) for few-shot distillation in a data-scarce setting. It augments limited labeled data using a text-to-image generation model (Kandinsky), initializes a small student network (EfficientNet B0) with a smaller CLIP model pretrained on DataComp, and then distills knowledge from a large CLIP ViT-L/14 teacher via classical logit matching. Experiments on Stanford Cars, Oxford Flowers, and Food-101 show that each component contributes to performance. The resulting compact model performs within 16% (1-shot) and 10% (8-shot) of CLIP’s linear probe while using less than 2% of CLIP’s image encoder parameters.

### Strengths
- **Task-specific focus:** Unlike general-purpose distillation, SIDCLIP targets specific downstream tasks, yielding parameter- and computationally-efficient models.
- **Novel data generation:** The synthesis component leverages CLIP’s own embeddings (linear combination of text and few-shot image embeddings) to generate synthetic data, a novel approach.
- **Ablation and analysis:** The paper provides extensive ablation studies to show the contribution of each component (S, I, D), along with evaluation metrics and visualization of synthetic images to demonstrate data quality. One reviewer specifically noted this as a strong point.
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is written in simple language and is easy to follow.
- **Practical motivation:** The method improves edge deployment by distilling large CLIP into a smaller model (EfficientNet B0) for data-scarce and budget-constrained settings.
- **Performance gains:** The combination of synthesis, initialization, and distillation yields good few-shot performance, as confirmed by ablation.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The three components (synthetic data generation, CLIP-like pretraining for initialization, logit-based distillation) are individually straightforward. While their combination is interesting, the results are not surprising given the complementary sources of information (latent diffusion, ViT-L/14 teacher, DataComp pretraining). Reviewer 3 is the most critical here (score 3), contrasting with more positive reviews (scores 6, 6, 5).
- **Narrow model scope:** All experiments use only EfficientNet B0 as the student architecture. It is unclear whether the gains are specific to this backbone. Reviewers request tests with ResNet-18, larger EfficientNets, Vision Transformers, and any model “with varying depths and widths” to assess generalizability.
- **Limited dataset scope:** Experiments cover only three fine-grained datasets (Stanford Cars, Oxford Flowers, Food-101). There are no evaluations on larger or more diverse datasets such as ImageNet, DTD, EuroSAT, FGVCAircraft, SUN397, or Caltech101. Given the claim of practical use, more datasets (especially those differing from CLIP’s pretraining distribution) are needed to assess out-of-domain robustness.
- **Unfair comparisons:** The baselines TinyCLIP and TinyViT-5M do not use a diffusion model or DataComp pretraining, making the comparison unfair. SIDCLIP’s use of a latent diffusion model and DataComp for initialization introduces additional data and resources not available to baselines, undermining the data-scarcity claim.
- **Computational overhead not quantified:** The paper does not provide a breakdown of time or compute for each stage (synthesis, initialization, distillation) compared to other methods. This makes it hard to assess the true cost or practicality.
- **Missing ablations:** The ablation in Figure 1 does not show combinations such as ENB0+S (synthetic data, no initialization, no distillation) or ENB0+S+I (synthetic data + initialization, no distillation), which would help isolate the role of each component more clearly.
- **Distillation method is basic:** Only classical logit-based knowledge distillation is used. More advanced techniques (e.g., decoupling target/non-target terms, distillation with weaker teachers) are not explored. It is unclear whether a smaller CLIP (not ViT-L/14) could serve as an equally effective teacher.
- **Linear probe training details unclear:** The method for training the linear probe (LP) is not fully described, yet LP results are used as a reference. Different fine-tuning strategies can cause large fluctuations (e.g., Task Residual, LP++).
- **Sensitivity to synthetic data:** The paper does not evaluate how the quality or quantity of generated images affects results, nor does it test simpler or different generation models (e.g., other text-to-image models) to show robustness.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not discussed:** There is no analysis of sensitivity to key hyperparameters: number of synthetic images per class, learning rate for distillation, weight given to synthetic data during training, etc.
- **Typo:** Line 339 has “intialize” instead of “initialize” (noted by Reviewer 2).
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** While Reviewers 2 and 4 rate the paper as 6 (positive, noting innovation in data generation and edge deployment), Reviewer 3 rates it 3 (negative, citing limited novelty and unfair baselines). This contrast underscores concerns about the paper’s contribution depth and experimental rigor.
