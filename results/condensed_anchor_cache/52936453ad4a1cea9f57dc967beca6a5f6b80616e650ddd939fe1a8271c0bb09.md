## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates using Text-to-Image (T2I) generators, specifically Stable Diffusion, for interventional data augmentation (IDA) to improve single-domain generalization (SDG) and reduce reliance on spurious features (RRSF). Extensive experiments are conducted across four SDG and three RRSF benchmarks, with ablations over prompting strategies, conditioning mechanisms, and post-hoc filtering. The authors argue that modern T2I generators can serve as powerful IDA mechanisms, outperforming previous augmentation techniques. However, reviewers raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental setup, interpretation of results, and practical applicability.

### Strengths
- Thorough study with four SDG and three RRSF tasks; comprehensive evaluation and ablations across key dimensions of T2I generation.
- Writing and presentation of results are easy to follow; comparisons with simple baselines are appreciated.
- Analysis comparing different editing methods (e.g., SDEdit vs. Text2Image) is insightful.
- First investigation of T2I generators as general-purpose interventional data augmentation mechanisms.
- Findings that the conditioning mechanism is the most important factor in IDA, interventional prompts are important, and post-hoc filtering is not consistently beneficial.
- Useful ablation showing that similar performance for SDG can be achieved even without generating images in the specific target domain (Table 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The paper does not present a novel model or surprising findings. The conclusions are similar to prior work (Bansal & Grover 2023), which also used predefined text prompts and showed that a combination of real and generated data improves performance on IN-Sketch and IN-R.
- **Premise undermined:** The best-performing baselines in Fig. 3 and Fig. 5 are non-interventional augmentations (Text2Image and retrieval). This questions the role of interventional conditioning, and the paper’s claim that conditioning is important appears contradicted by the evidence. The contributions should be clarified: is the paper suggesting only evaluating unconditioned generators?
- **Generalizability concerns:** The method assumes that the target domain can be easily described with a simple text prompt, which is not always possible (e.g., iwildcam with diverse camera traps). Hand-crafting prompts for RRSF tasks is expensive and not scalable; work like [1] uses captioning+GPT to automate domain descriptions, but this is not adopted here. The manual prompt engineering effort and tuning are not quantified.
- **Unclear interpretation of Table 2:** Performance on the sketch domain is better without simulating the target domain. The claim “describing the target domain is not necessary” is premature and likely dataset-specific. Why is SDEdit comparable without target domain while other target-agnostic augmentations (MixUp, CutMix) are far worse? Possible implementation differences need exploration; releasing code would help. Requests: include baselines ERM trained on all but target, on all domains, on target only; add performance with even more irrelevant prompts or prompts from OfficeHome on PACS.
- **Unfair data amount comparison:** The number of additional data points differs between generated images and baseline augmentations. The impact of data quantity (similar to He et al. 2023, Sariyildiz et al. 2023) should be studied; a runtime column would also be useful.
- **Discrepancy in Fig. 3:** The text on page 8 claims “Text2Image seems to be less effective than other techniques in reducing background and texture biases,” but the figure shows Text2Image has the least biases across all three datasets. This contradiction needs resolution.
- **Missing comparisons and evaluations:** Results should be compared with GAN-based generative methods. Show results on Cifar10-C and ImageNet-C for robustness. Include retrieval performance on DomainNet in Fig. 5. Provide retrieval description and number of generated images per original in the main paper.
- **Insufficient prompt details:** The prompts used for the three RRSF tasks are described too briefly in the main paper; more information on how they were engineered and tuned is needed.
- **Unclear takeaways:** The conclusion is confusing about whether conditioning is important or not and what the implications are for SDG/RRSF research and generator evaluation. More explicit articulation is required.

*Note: While Reviewer 3 provides a more positive assessment (highlighting the first investigation and insightful analyses), the majority express concerns about novelty, methodological rigor, and interpretation of results. The weaknesses above reflect this consensus.*
