## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GPS (Gumbel Prior Similarity), a novel set-to-set matching similarity measure motivated by Extreme Value Theory. GPS models the distribution of minimum distances (as in Chamfer Distance) using a Gumbel prior, aiming to be more robust to outliers while retaining linear computational cost. The method is validated on few-shot image classification and 3D point cloud completion, where it consistently outperforms existing metrics like Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover’s Distance. Most reviewers found the idea novel, theoretically motivated, and the experiments adequate; however, several reviewers raised concerns about theoretical rigor, missing experiments, and limited innovation.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated formulation.** The use of Gumbel distributions from Extreme Value Theory for set-to-set matching is innovative and theoretically grounded (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5). It addresses a fundamental problem and could impact other domains (Reviewer 1).
- **Empirical performance.** GPS significantly outperforms state-of-the-art loss functions on few-shot classification and 3D point cloud completion across multiple benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). The consistent improvement is seen as promising.
- **Computational efficiency.** The method maintains the same linear computational cost as Chamfer Distance, making it practical (Reviewer 5).
- **Clear narrative and reproducibility.** The paper is generally well-written, the narrative is clear, and code is provided (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 stated the paper can be reproduced with reasonable effort.
- **Extensive evaluation with transparent hyperparameter reporting.** Reviewer 1 highlighted the extensive ablation and influence of hyperparameters.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical concerns about Gumbel vs. GEV and order statistics (Reviewer 2, strong minority).** According to EVT, the correct distribution for extrema is the Generalized Extreme Value (GEV) distribution; Gumbel is only a special case. The paper does not justify why Gumbel is appropriate for all problems. Moreover, EVT only strictly applies when the number of scores is large, a condition not stated. Most critically, Gumbel cannot model order statistics, but the paper uses it to model k-th nearest neighbors via a mixture of Gumbel distributions (lines 200–203). The offset δ (line 232) is introduced but not theoretically justified. Reviewer 2 also noted missing prior work on EVT in matching, making the narrative overstate novelty.
- **Limited novelty (Reviewer 4, minority).** While most reviewers found the idea novel, Reviewer 4 argued the innovation is limited: the method remains within the scope of distance distribution fitting and does not fundamentally break from existing approaches.
- **Missing theoretical analysis of assumptions (Reviewers 2, 4).** The paper assumes independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) conditions but does not analyze the error range when this assumption is violated in practice. This affects the method’s reliability and generalizability.
- **Incomplete experiments (Reviewers 2, 3, 5).** The evaluation should include additional tasks that involve establishing explicit correspondences: point cloud registration, image matching (e.g., SuperGlue, CVPR 2020), and point cloud matching (e.g., Predator, CVPR 2021). Reviewer 2 also suggested testing whether other models (e.g., transformers, CLIP) benefit from GPS.
- **Performance improvements are modest (Reviewer 4, minority).** In most cases accuracy gains are only a few percentage points. Combined with potential additional computational cost and model complexity, the practical significance may be limited.
- **Unclear how GPS yields explicit correspondences (Reviewer 1).** The paper defines set-to-set matching as identifying correspondences but does not explain how the Gumbel-based similarity can be used to establish element-level mappings. This is crucial for applications like registration.
- **Hyperparameter tuning is nontrivial (Reviewer 1).** The need for careful hyperparameter searches may hinder wider adoption.
- **Organization and figure issues (Reviewer 5).** On page 1, Fig.2 appears before Fig.1; Fig.2(c) is confusing even after several reads. Fig.3 does not aid understanding of the method, and there is no figure showing the overall paradigm of applying GPS to a specific task.
