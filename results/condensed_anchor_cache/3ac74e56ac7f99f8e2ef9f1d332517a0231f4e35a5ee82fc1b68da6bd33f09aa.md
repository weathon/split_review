## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents ReNovo, a retrieval-augmented generation method for de novo peptide sequencing from tandem mass spectra. It constructs a datastore from the training data (storing context representations and next-token targets) and uses k-NN retrieval during inference to augment the predictions of an autoregressive transformer. Experiments on multiple widely-used datasets show substantial improvements over prior de novo methods with modest storage and time overhead.

### Strengths
- **Originality and integration of paradigms**: The work is praised for combining database-search–style retrieval with de novo sequence generation (R1, R2). It is noted as the first integration of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) into peptide sequencing (R3). *Contrast*: R4 counters that mixture methods (de novo + database search) have been documented for two decades (Spider, BICEPS).
- **Substantial performance improvement**: The method achieves significant gains over state-of-the-art de novo approaches (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Sound experimental design**: The code is provided (R1). Dataset preparation, benchmarking, and ablation studies on key hyperparameters are thorough (R2). Experiments are sound and reproducible (R1).
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and clearly defines motivation and analysis (R1, R2, R4).
- **Potential impact**: Offers a new perspective that could influence future research in de novo peptide sequencing (R2).
- **Advance beyond existing de novo methods**: Allows integration of prior knowledge (via the datastore) that standard de novo methods cannot use (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related work on RAG origins and recent de novo methods**: The paper does not discuss the KNN-LM paper (origin of storing context+target in a datastore) nor recent de novo methods such as GraphNovo, PepNet, PrimeNovo, InstaNovo, and the Bittremieux et al. survey (R3). R1 also notes no discussion of RAG in LLMs.
- **Lack of comparison with database search methods**: R1, R4: The primary motivation is overcoming database-search limitations, yet no database search baseline is provided. At minimum a simple search against the training database should be included. R4 specifically notes that error-tolerant/open database searches (e.g., MSFragger, the current gold standard) are not compared; a realistic scenario (e.g., a complete database of a closely related organism) should be tested.
- **Data splitting and potential leakage**: Several reviewers raise concerns:
    - *By-species split*: Same peptides may appear across different species; spectral and peptide similarity between train/test sets need analysis (R1). R2 and R4 also call for a stricter criterion based on sequence similarity (not just identity) to ensure generalization. R4 asks whether only identical peptides are filtered or also those with PTMs, modifications, or single-amino-acid substitutions.
    - *Datastore built from training data*: This gives ReNovo prior knowledge unavailable to standard de novo methods, making the comparison unfair (R4). R3 recommends separating the datastore from training data to better evaluate retrieval’s impact; using the same data may make retrieval redundant since the model already encodes those relationships.
- **Model capacity versus design**: R2 questions whether SOTA results are due to higher capacity rather than the retrieval design. Suggests an experiment comparing ReNovo without Retrieval of larger size (more layers, hidden dimensions) to ReNovo.
- **Clarity of key components**:
    - *Context features*: Not clearly defined (R2). Is it the embedding from the last transformer decoder layer? What does $f$ in line 213 signify?
    - *ReNovo with Residual*: Not described in the methods section and not used in the final evaluation (R1, R2). Its configuration is unclear.
    - *Formulas 9-10*: Unclear how top-K retrievals assist the model (R3). Is the probability for each amino acid derived directly from retrieved targets or after embedding into input layers? R1 also requests intuition for Equation 9.
- **Missing metrics and reproducibility details**:
    - Confidence intervals or standard deviations for main results (Table 1) missing (R1).
    - No recall-coverage curve, important for biologists who rely on model certainty (R3).
    - Only precision/recall provided; no identifications above 1% FDR threshold, a standard requirement in proteomics (R4). R4 asks for number of peptides at 1% FDR.
    - Training hyperparameters (epochs, learning rate) not stated (R2).
    - Discrepancies noted between reported Casanovo numbers and those in the original Casanovo paper (R3).
- **Minor issues**:
    - Figure 1: “identification rate” unclear – is it prediction accuracy? (R1)
    - Line 160: Charge state importance not explained (R1).
    - Line 163: “sets” → “ordered sets of the same size” for precision (R1).
    - Equation 2: typo (comma at end) (R1).
    - Line 196: Clarify how described features help attend to differences (R1).
    - Line 201: Missing citation for teacher-forcing (R1).
    - Section 4.1: Dataset references missing (R1).
    - Table 5: Meaning of “+15.99” unclear (R1). R2 asks how case study example was selected and if number of similar improvements can be quantified.
    - Appendix A: References missing (R1).
