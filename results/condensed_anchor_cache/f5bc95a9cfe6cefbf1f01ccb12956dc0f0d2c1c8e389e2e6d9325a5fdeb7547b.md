## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AnomalyTCN, a dual-branch convolution structure with contrastive representation learning for efficient time series anomaly detection. It uses dense and dilated convolutions to generate two views of the input, then detects anomalies via the discrepancy between these representations. The authors claim state-of-the-art performance while saving 83.6% running time and 20.1% memory overhead compared to attention-based methods. Reviewers acknowledge efficiency and clear illustrations, but the majority (ratings 1,3,3,5,5; minority positive 2 and 5) identify major flaws: insufficient novelty, flawed evaluation metrics (point adjustment), missing comparisons, lack of code, and limited experimental validation.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency**: The pure convolution structure achieves 83.6% running time reduction and 20.1% memory savings over attention-based models (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Clear presentation**: Figures, tables, and illustrations are well-designed and easy to follow (R4, R5).
- **Extensive datasets**: Experiments cover multiple benchmarks (R1).
- **State-of-the-art claims**: Two reviewers (R2, R5) note superior performance with low computational cost, though others contest effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The dual-branch contrastive learning idea is already proposed in DCdetector (KDD 2023); pure convolution structures for TSAD exist (e.g., Random Convolutional Kernels, DeLTA 2023). The contribution is incremental (R1, R3, R4, R5). Training framework and loss function closely follow DCdetector (R5).
- **Minor improvements on many datasets**: On SMD, SMAP, SWaT, PSM, and NIPS-TS-SWAN, gains over baselines are marginal (R1).
- **Flawed evaluation metrics**: Uses point adjustment (PA), which is known to produce misleading results—even random scores can achieve SOTA. Should use original F1, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Aff Recall, Precision, and F1 instead (R3, R4; references: [5,6,7] TKDE 2023, NeurIPS 2023, KDD 2022; [2] in R4).
- **Insufficient efficiency comparisons**: Only compared with one Transformer-based model (e.g., Anomaly Transformer). Should compare training time, inference time, memory, and parameters against other convolution-based methods (TimesNet, ModernTCN, Random Convolution) and non-learning methods (Matrix Profile variants [3,4]) (R3).
- **Missing comparisons with relevant contrastive-based methods**: TFMAE (R2), TimeAutoAD, CTAD (R2). Reported baseline results (e.g., DCdetector F1) differ from original papers (R3).
- **No code provided**: Reproducibility cannot be verified; authors must release code, explain preprocessing, and clarify discrepancies with original baseline results (R3, R4).
- **Lack of theoretical/experimental proof that dense vs. dilated convolution discrepancy captures anomalies**: Risk that abnormal points are ignored after dilated convolution. No evidence that inconsistency is due to anomalies (R2, R5). Similarly, why contrastive learning better differentiates normal vs. anomalous points is not explained (R5).
- **Missing explanation why convolution outperforms attention in effectiveness** (not just efficiency) (R5). Labeling error in Table 1: on PSM, TimesNet F1=97.47 surpasses D3R but is not underlined (R5).
- **Limited analysis of anomaly types**: Can it handle global, contextual, seasonal, trend, and shapelet exceptions as defined in [1]? (R4)
- **Incomplete experiments on some tables**: Tables 3-5 not evaluated on all datasets; univariate experiments only compared with Anomaly Transformer and DCdetector, omitting other baselines (R4, R5).
- **Robustness not validated**: No experiments with different proportions of anomalies in the training set; no case studies showing contrastive methods are more robust than reconstruction-based methods when training data contains outliers (R5).
- **Writing clarity**: Introduction spends two pages but the core challenge remains unclear (R1).
- **Minority positive reviewers (2 and 5, score 5)** still acknowledge limitations in novelty and missing comparisons; **majority (1,3,4, scores 1-3)** rate the paper low due to novelty concerns and the use of flawed evaluation metrics.
