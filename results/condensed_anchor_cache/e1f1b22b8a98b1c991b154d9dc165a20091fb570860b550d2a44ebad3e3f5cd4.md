## Merged Review

### Summary
StructRAG proposes a cognitively inspired framework for knowledge-intensive reasoning with RAG, consisting of a Hybrid Structure Router (trained via DPO) that selects among five structure types (graph, chunk, catalogue, algorithm, table), a Scattered Knowledge Structurizer to convert raw documents into that structure, and a Structured Knowledge Utilizer for multi-step reasoning. Evaluated on multiple benchmarks against long-context, standard RAG, RQ-RAG, and GraphRAG baselines, StructRAG achieves state-of-the-art performance, especially on complex, fragmented information scenarios. Reviewers found the idea novel and the experiments thorough, but raised concerns about limited model evaluation, missing analyses of structure-type distributions and multi-type tasks, unclear metrics, and a potentially unfair GraphRAG comparison.

### Strengths
- Addresses a challenging and well-recognized problem in RAG: handling scattered information in knowledge-intensive reasoning.
- The cognitive-science motivation (human information structurization) is novel, clear, and integrated naturally into the computational approach.
- The architecture (router, structurizer, utilizer) is described clearly with formulas and diagrams, making it easy to follow.
- The Hybrid Structure Router trained with DPO to choose among five structure types is innovative and practically implementable.
- Experimental evaluation is comprehensive: comparisons against long-context, RAG, RQ-RAG, GraphRAG, with multiple benchmarks and tasks.
- Ablation studies are insightful and well-documented, showing contributions of each module.
- The paper demonstrates practical value, particularly excelling in more challenging, scattered-information scenarios.
- The framework achieves both better performance and lower latency compared to GraphRAG in the tested settings.
- Writing quality is high, with logical flow and clear reasoning.
- The approach has potential for adaptation to a range of RAG-related tasks.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model evaluation (minority reviewer concern):** StructRAG is only tested on Qwen2-7B-Instruct, a relatively small model. The paper does not evaluate on other mainstream models (e.g., Llama) or larger parameter models. Since GraphRAG originally used GPT-4-turbo, it is hard to conclude StructRAG is superior without comparable backends. This reviewer explicitly questioned whether testing on other models was done.
- **Missing definition of LLM score:** The paper uses an “LLM score” metric without providing its definition, making results less reproducible and interpretable.
- **Lack of analysis on structure-type distribution:** No information is given about the distribution of structure types chosen by the hybrid router across the dataset. Some types (e.g., algorithmic pseudo code) are likely more complex to extract than others (e.g., tables or catalogues). Reporting this distribution and analyzing the structurizer’s performance on different types (especially algorithmic structuring) would clarify router efficacy.
- **Potential confounding of Table 5 results:** The performance differences when using fixed structure types (Table 5) may be influenced by the natural alignment of tasks with certain types (e.g., most tasks might favor tables). A breakdown of the router’s selected types versus human-selected types is needed to assess how well the router aligns with human judgment.
- **Unclear results on Set 4 (Table 3, Figure 3):** The Exact Match (EM) rate for Set 4 is very close across methods and sometimes equivalent. It is unclear whether this is due to dataset characteristics (e.g., limited answer variation) or coincidence. Additional context is needed.
- **No exploration of tasks requiring multiple structure types simultaneously:** The paper assumes a single structure type per task. An ablation combining two fixed structure types could reveal whether multiple types consistently improve performance across categories.
- **GraphRAG comparison may be unfair:** GraphRAG typically excels on complex relational data, not the long-context scenarios used in many experiments. A more targeted comparison on relationship-heavy datasets would better highlight relative strengths.
- **Reliance on LLM capabilities without detailed discussion:** The framework heavily depends on LLMs for structuring and utilizing raw knowledge. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of challenges encountered during knowledge processing and strategies for leveraging LLMs more effectively.
- **Missing examples for each structure type:** The paper proposes five structure types but does not provide concrete examples of tasks suitable for each, which would clarify the rationale behind the categorization.
- **Checks on LLM-generated structured knowledge:** When LLMs extract structured knowledge, it is unclear whether they can directly produce the specified formats and whether any post-hoc checks or corrections are performed.
