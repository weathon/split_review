## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper is the first to study fixed-budget best arm identification (BAI) in linear bandits under differential privacy (DP) constraints. It proposes a policy DP‑BAI based on the principle of maximum absolute determinants (MAX‑DET) rather than adapting existing fixed‑budget BAI algorithms. The authors derive an upper bound on the error probability that decays exponentially in the budget \(T\), and a minimax lower bound that matches order‑wise in sub‑optimality gaps, privacy parameter \(\varepsilon\), and a complexity measure composed of \(H_{\text{BAI}}\) (standard fixed‑budget complexity) and \(H_{\text{pri}}\) (accounting for DP). The lower bound introduces new complexity notions that incorporate DP as a policy constraint. The algorithm outperforms a naive DP adaptation of the state‑of‑the‑art non‑private algorithm. Empirical evaluation on a synthetic instance is provided. Overall, the paper provides tight theoretical results and fills a gap in the literature.

### Strengths

- First algorithm (DP‑BAI) for fixed‑budget BAI under DP constraints, using maximum absolute determinants (MAX‑DET) rather than straightforward adaptations of existing algorithms. [R1, R2, R3, R5]
- Upper bound on error probability decaying exponentially in \(T\), and a minimax lower bound matching order‑wise in sub‑optimality gaps, \(\varepsilon\), and problem complexity (expressed via \(H_{\text{BAI}}\) and \(H_{\text{pri}}\)). [R1, R2, R3, R5]
- The algorithm is novel and clearly outperforms existing benchmarks and straightforward DP adaptations in experiments. [R3]
- Empirical evaluation demonstrates effectiveness. [R1] (Note: one reviewer [R4] observed the evaluation is limited to a single synthetic instance.)
- Comprehensive theoretical details are provided. [R4]
- Lower bound is a nice technical contribution. [R5]
- Analysis includes a general analysis of a natural extension of an existing BAI algorithm (OD‑LinBAI) and benchmarks against it. [R3]

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of detailed comparison with non‑private fixed‑budget BAI**: The paper does not analyze whether the error probability of DP‑BAI converges to that of the state‑of‑the‑art non‑private counterpart when \(\varepsilon \to \infty\). Such analysis would clarify the privacy–performance trade‑off. [R2]
- **Writing clarity and intuition**: The algorithm description contains many notations without adequate explanations, making it hard to follow. High‑level insights and intuitive explanations are missing. [R2, R3] Specific unclear aspects include:
  - How the algorithm works intuitively, e.g., how the apparent dimension reduces in early rounds and how decreasing the span basis vectors of the arm space leads to convergence to the optimal arm. [R3]
  - The phrasing of Algorithm 1 lines 8 and 14 is confusing; it is unclear whether each arm in \(B_p\) is pulled \(T'/M d_p\) times or total pulls are \(T'/M\), and similarly for line 14. [R2]
  - The intuition behind the choices of \(g_i\) and \(h_i\) in equations (5) and (6) is not explained. [R2]
  - The rationale for scheme (7) is unclear. [R4]
  - Why a Max‑Det collection of \(\mathcal{A}\) is always linearly independent (Definition 3.1) is questionable; does it implicitly assume that \(\mathcal{A}\) spans \(\mathbb{R}^{d'}\)? [R2]
- **Definition of DP and neighboring datasets**: The DP definition is over length‑\(T\) sequences; some reviewers expected joint DP or a more standard online DP definition. [R3] More importantly, the definition of neighboring datasets is restrictive—only a single reward change across all time steps and arms—raising concerns about practical applicability (e.g., in clinical trials, a change could affect all arms at a given time step). The motivation and realism of this DP model need clarification. [R5] (R3 also questions the DP definition.)
- **Choice of D‑optimal design over G‑optimal design**: The paper uses D‑optimal design; however, G‑optimal design is theoretically equivalent (Proposition 3 in Soare et al. NeurIPS 2014). Since the experiments show dramatic performance improvement, an intuitive explanation for why D‑optimal design works better under DP is needed. [R3]
- **Limited empirical evaluation**: The experiments are conducted on only one synthetic data instance. [R4] (Contrast: R1 says empirical evaluation shows effectiveness, but R4 points out the limited scope.)
- **Missing discussion of connections to related settings**: The paper does not discuss potential deeper connections to (i) BAI in the fixed‑confidence regime under DP, and (ii) general MAB under DP. It is unclear whether simple adaptations of previous algorithms could apply. [R1]
- **Motivation for DP in BAI**: The motivation for applying DP to the best arm identification problem is not sufficiently addressed. A concrete real‑world scenario where privacy is critical in bandit BAI would help. [R4, also implied by R5’s practical applicability concerns]
- **Minority reviewer concern**: One reviewer [R4] rated the paper lower (score 5) citing limited empirical evaluation and fair contribution, while others rated it highly (scores 8 and 6). [R2 gave 6, also critical of clarity.]
