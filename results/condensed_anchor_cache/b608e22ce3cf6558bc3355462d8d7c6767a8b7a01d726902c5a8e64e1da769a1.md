## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TF-score, a score-based diffusion model for time series forecasting that generates the entire sequence (historical context + future predictions) simultaneously. The authors derive a continuous SDE-based loss and unify existing DDPM-based and score-matching-based methods. Experiments on six benchmark datasets show TF-score outperforms several baselines, including prior diffusion models. Guidance sampling strategies are also extended to the score-based formulation.

### Strengths
1. **Appropriate application of diffusion models to time-series forecasting** (R1, R3, R4): The work effectively adapts score-based diffusion for forecasting, a relatively novel domain, and the proposed conditional score matching view is valid.  
2. **Unified framework with theoretical foundation** (R1, R3, R4): The authors integrate existing diffusion forecasting approaches into a continuous score-based framework, which could in principle subsume prior formulations. The idea of weighing errors for history and future generations is insightful.  
3. **Experimental performance and thorough evaluation** (R1, R3, R4): TF-score consistently outperforms multiple benchmark models across diverse datasets. Experiments cover several diffusion step settings, guidance methods, and an ablation study, showing dataset-dependent behavior.  
4. **Clarity and readability** (R2, R3): The method is presented clearly and is easy to follow, despite some typos and notation issues.

### Weaknesses
1. **Excessive background limits space for contributions** (R1, R3): The paper devotes substantial space to explaining diffusion models, time-series forecasting, CRPS computation, and existing embedding/guidance methods, leaving little room for the authors’ own method. The paper feels more like a course report than a research paper.  
2. **Lack of novelty** (R1, R4): The only new element is a mask vector used as a weight when computing sequence loss, which is simplistic and described in only about one-fifth of a page. The section introducing this is even titled “ANALYSIS OF EXISTING METHOD.” Reviewer 4 notes that a similar score-matching idea was proposed earlier in ScoreGrad [Yan et al., 2106.10121v1], further limiting novelty.  
3. **Missing related work and insufficient literature review** (R2, R4): Several relevant time-series diffusion models are omitted, e.g., NIPS’22 “Generative Time Series Forecasting with Diffusion, Denoise, and Disentanglement”, ICML’23 “Non-autoregressive conditional diffusion models for time series prediction”, ICLR’24 “Interpretable Diffusion for General Time Series Generation”, and others [MG-TSD, TACTiS-2]. The paper does not properly position itself against these works.  
4. **Unconvincing claim about generating the whole sequence** (R2, R3): The authors argue that generating $x^{\text{total}}$ (history + future) captures internal structure better than conditioning on history alone. However, generating the past may introduce bias from unrelated historical information and increases computational burden, especially for long histories. Only small window sizes (<100) are evaluated, and no computational efficiency analysis is provided. Reviewer 3 notes the approach is essentially an “inpainting” interpretation, which is not new.  
5. **Mathematical rigor and presentation issues** (R3):  
   - Section 2.1 is informal, with typos (e.g., line 108 “$f$ is an affine and $w$, …”) and incorrect justification of score matching (the score term is not available analytically, not a matter of computational effort).  
   - Section 2.2 suffers notation overload ($T$ used for both diffusion time and forecast horizon), and equations (1) and (2) are unclear (e.g., whether $x^{\text{hist}}$ is used as conditioning or input).  
   - Section 3 contains mathematical mistakes: the gradient with respect to $x^{\text{pred}}$ of the conditional log-density is ill-defined because $x^{\text{pred}}_t$ cannot be obtained from $x^{\text{hist}}$ in closed form. Equations (3) and (4) are shaky; in (3) the score gradient w.r.t. $x^{\text{pred}}$ is not accessible. In (4), providing $x^{\text{hist}}$ as input to $s(\cdot)$ is redundant as $x^{\text{hist}}\subset x^{\text{total}}$.  
   - The importance of the weight $\lambda(t)$ is dismissed too casually (“ignored for computational convenience”); it determines what objective is optimized (e.g., $\lambda(t)=g(t)^2/2$ corresponds to maximum likelihood).  
   - The guidance expression at line 380 (Bayes rule) is ill-defined ($p(a|b)\sim p(b|a)p(a|b)$) and should be corrected.  
6. **Insufficient baselines and evaluation concerns** (R1, R2, R4): Most compared benchmarks are relatively outdated. The most advanced time-series diffusion models are missing from comparisons. The sole evaluation metric is CRPS, which weakens the results. The experiment part largely follows the setup of earlier work [ScoreGrad].  
7. **Guidance sampling analysis is incomplete** (R3, R4): Guidance is applied only to TF-score, not to baseline diffusion models, so the claimed performance gains cannot prove superiority over alternatives under comparable settings. The ablation shows guidance is only marginally beneficial and dataset-dependent, making it hard to find a one-size-fits-all configuration.  
8. **Missing details about method and competitor implementations** (R1, R3): Section 3.1 is titled “ANALYSIS OF EXISTING METHOD” and Section 3.2 jumps to experiments, leaving the authors’ own method undefined. The paper does not clearly explain how TF-score differs from DiffWave despite claiming so. The conditioning signal for guidance is insufficiently explained; only one variant (line 397) is well defined.  
9. **Requested missing experiments and analyses** (R2, R3, R4):  
   - Provide a detailed comparison (beyond Table 1) to clarify whether the two competitor diffusion methods (TimeGrad, CSDI) are toy models or full-fledged implementations.  
   - Show what diffusion baselines gain from applying guidance sampling; can TF-score still outperform them?  
   - Compare against newer baselines (e.g., [2–5] from R4).  
   - Justify the claim about generating $x^{\text{total}}$ with better support, including computational efficiency analysis.  
   - Provide a thorough mathematical pass (including Appendix A proof).
