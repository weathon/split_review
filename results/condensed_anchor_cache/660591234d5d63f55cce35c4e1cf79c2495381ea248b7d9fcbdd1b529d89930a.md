## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces StableSemantics, a large-scale synthetic dataset built from human-curated prompts collected from the Stable Diffusion Discord server. After filtering based on user image ratings, prompts are processed via regex and LLM rewriting to produce captions, which are used to generate images with SDXL Lightning 4-step UNet. For each noun chunk in the caption, cross-attention maps are extracted as semantic attribution maps. The dataset comprises 224K prompts, over 2 million images, and 10 million attention maps. The authors analyze semantic distributions, object co-occurrence, and benchmark captioning and open-vocabulary segmentation models.

### Strengths
- **First large-scale synthetic dataset to provide dense semantic attribution maps** at the noun-chunk level, alongside images and captions. This originality is highlighted as a potential step forward for visual semantic understanding (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Comprehensive and clearly described dataset collection pipeline**, with minimal human annotation after initial prompt curation; the process takes from a live data source, enabling periodic re-collection to maintain freshness (Reviewer 2).
- **Scale**: 224K curated prompts, 2M images, 10M attention maps, enabling robust statistical analysis (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Quality control**: prompts are manually filtered using user image rankings, and failure cases are included in the appendix to illustrate limitations (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Evaluation of captioning and segmentation models** on the dataset, providing initial benchmarks (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Clarity**: paper is easy to read, figures are well captioned, and the data analysis is thorough (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Minority (Reviewer 3) is substantially more positive**, noting the significance of synthetic media datasets (which they consider undervalued) and originality in providing semantic maps, despite acknowledged noise. Other reviewers are more critical of value-add.

### Weaknesses
- **Value-add over existing datasets (DiffusionDB, JourneyDB) is unclear.** The claimed distinction of using only human-generated prompts is weak because JourneyDB also collects user prompts from Discord. Lower perplexity of prompts/captions is not shown to be advantageous, and Figure 7 indicates captions are a subset of JourneyDB’s distribution. Additionally, the dataset only captures prompts users found visually appealing, reducing its representativeness of natural images. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Semantic maps are noisy and do not reliably capture the spatial extent of objects**, limiting their utility for tasks like semantic segmentation. Example: the “mirror” mask in Figure 5 encodes the frame rather than the mirror. Consequently, downstream evaluations (e.g., of open-set segmentation in Figure 14) are not informative. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 also notes that CLIP-based metrics like CLIP score and DAAM measure object presence but not relational accuracy.)
- **Relational and spatial inconsistencies in generated images** due to the CLIP encoder’s bag-of-words behavior. E.g., “a banana with checkered skin” may show a banana on a checkered background. The automatic metrics used (CLIP score, DAAM) fail to detect such misalignments, so human evaluation is needed to filter images—yet the dataset does not include such filtering. (Reviewer 1)
- **Single-model generation (SDXL Lightning 4-step UNet)** raises concerns about dataset staleness; it is unclear whether insights transfer to other generative models. (Reviewer 3)
- **LLM rewriting of prompts may shift distribution**: e.g., user’s “hospital gaate” becomes “hospital entrance”, which may not match the user’s intent. (Reviewer 3, question)
- **No experiments demonstrating downstream benefits** from the semantic maps. For example, does training on these maps improve performance on hand-annotated test sets (e.g., MaskCLIP as alluded to in L518)? Without such evidence, the usefulness of the dataset for model development remains doubtful. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Legal uncertainty**: releasing the dataset under CC0 1.0 may conflict with Stability AI’s Discord Terms of Service, which states Stability owns the content. (Reviewer 3, question)
- **Reproducibility limitation**: the exact seeds used for generations on Discord are unknown, so images matching the highest-rated outputs cannot be reproduced. (Reviewer 1, question)
- **Motivation for prompt-to-caption rewriting** is unclear: is it needed because misspellings hinder noun parsing? (Reviewer 2, question)
- **In Figure 10, the “Wall” semantic map is strongest at the top**, which contradicts the expectation that walls appear on the sides; this discrepancy suggests failures in spatial attribution. (Reviewer 3, question)
