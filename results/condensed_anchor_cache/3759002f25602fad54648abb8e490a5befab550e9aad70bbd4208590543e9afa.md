## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multi-task learning framework for structure-based protein function prediction. It constructs a benchmark PROMPT integrating 6 tasks from 4 public datasets (EC, MF, BP, CC, LBA, PPI) with 31,887 samples and partially/fully labeled splits. The authors introduce HeMeNet, a heterogeneous multichannel equivariant GNN that uses multi-relational message passing and a task-aware readout with learnable task vectors. Experiments show that HeMeNet outperforms several baselines (GVP, GearNet, MBP) in both single-task and multi-task settings. The benchmark is carefully curated to avoid data leakage, and the study explores cross-task influence.

### Strengths
- The problem formulation is clearly motivated and could benefit a wide range of biochemistry problems; the paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The PROMPT benchmark is a convenient and practical resource for studying protein properties; it is carefully curated to avoid data leakage, and additional efforts were made to annotate unlabeled data using UniProt (Reviewers 2, 4).
- HeMeNet is an equivariant GNN that leverages heterogeneous message passing across multiple edge types and an attention-based task-aware readout with learnable task prompts, enabling simultaneous learning of multiple tasks (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results on the fully labeled test set are strong: HeMeNet generally surpasses state-of-the-art models in single-task and multi-task settings (Table 1) (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). However, Reviewer 1 noted that gains are marginal on some tasks.
- The research topic of studying how function prediction tasks (EC, MF, BP, CC) influence affinity prediction tasks (LBA, PPI) and vice versa is interesting (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset novelty and presentation**: Several reviewers noted that PROMPT primarily amalgamates existing datasets (Enzyme Commission, Gene Ontology, etc.) without introducing new protein structures or tasks, limiting its novelty (Reviewer 2, and Reviewer 3 considered the benchmark alone insufficient for a top venue). Reviewer 1 asked for more detailed dataset statistics (e.g., ratio of missing targets) and clarification on whether EC numbers are treated as flat or hierarchical, and whether GO terms (MF, BP, CC) are simplified or used directly.
- **Missing task-interaction analysis**: Reviewers 1 and 4 criticized the lack of analysis on how tasks interact. Reviewer 4 pointed out a counter-intuitive performance decay on function prediction tasks when coupled with affinity prediction, requiring deeper analysis. Reviewer 1 requested correlation studies between tasks. Reviewer 4 suggested fine-grained ablation: coupling LBA&PPI with each individual function task (EC, MF, BP, CC) to identify beneficial combinations. Reviewer 2 proposed an experiment with zero-weight for PPI during training to probe internal transfer.
- **Equivariance claim not justified**: Reviewer 1 noted that the paper does not clearly prove or explain which components of HeMeNet are E(3)-equivariant vs. invariant, particularly the geometric relation extractor, message scaler, and task-aware readout. A rigorous architectural explanation or mathematical proof is needed.
- **Model novelty**: Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 agreed that HeMeNet lacks significant novelty. The heterogeneous message passing is essentially adding extra edge representations to existing schemes (dyMEAN). The task-aware readout with learnable task vectors resembles recent prompt/prefix-tuning works. The loss is a simple summation of task losses, which is simplistic (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 1 and 4 recommended a thorough discussion of technical contributions relative to GearNet and dyMEAN.
- **Motivation vs. pretraining**: Reviewer 3 questioned the motivation for multi-task learning given the success of self-supervised pretraining for proteins; empirical evidence or a clearer rationale is missing.
- **Incomplete baselines**: Reviewer 4 identified missing state-of-the-art encoders: CDConv and Full-atom GearNet. Reviewer 3 noted the absence of comparisons with prompting LLMs and multi-task protein sequence models.
- **Marginal results on some tasks**: Reviewer 1 stated that performance gains are marginal on certain tasks and that stronger multi-task baselines (e.g., with attention or task-specific layers) are needed. Reviewer 1 also requested error analysis and discussion of limitations.
- **Missing model details**: Reviewer 1 noted that architectural details such as φ_m, φ_x, and ε are missing. A table summarizing dataset statistics (e.g., missing target ratios) was also requested.

*Note: There is disagreement on the strength of experimental results: Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 found them strong, while Reviewer 1 considered some gains marginal. This contrast is reflected above.*
