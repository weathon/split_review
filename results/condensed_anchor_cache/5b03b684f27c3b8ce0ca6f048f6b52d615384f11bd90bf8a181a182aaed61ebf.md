## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ProULearn, a framework for source-free active domain adaptation (SFADA) that selects informative target samples for labeling before training begins. It uses homogeneity propensity estimation (HPE) with separating trees and a correlation index (similar to Pearson coefficient) to compute sample uncertainty for selection, and a central correlation loss to refine pseudo-labels during adaptation. Experiments on four benchmarks claim improvements over prior SFDA and active DA methods.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well written and easy to follow, with clear figure illustrations and algorithm procedures (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Practical scenario**: SFADA is practically meaningful due to model privacy concerns; the method requests human annotations only once, which is more practical than progressive labeling (Reviewer 4).
- **Novelty of HPE**: Using homogeneity propensity estimation to select samples is novel to this area; the idea of using trees to assess sample grouping is interesting (Reviewers 2, 4). HPE does not rely on distance calculations or density estimates, making it relatively robust (Reviewer 4).
- **Empirical performance**: ProULearn obtains better accuracies on four benchmarks with significant improvements. Experimental results show excellent sample clustering and compact class distributions, aiding accurate pseudo-label generation (Reviewer 3, 4). Table 4 demonstrates that HPE works better than traditional clustering methods (DBSCAN, K-means) (Reviewer 4).
- **Extensive experiments**: The paper includes extensive experiments to validate the claims (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of overall framework**: Several reviewers note that the core contribution is an active sample selection criterion (HPE), while the rest of the pipeline (pseudo-label refinement, information maximization, weighted cross-entropy) relies on existing techniques. One reviewer states the method appears to be a generic sampling technique not specifically tailored to SFADA (Reviewer 1, 2). Another notes that the correlation index entropy and central correlation loss have relatively small novelty, as entropy and clustering structures are widely validated (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient comparisons and baselines**:
  - Only two clustering baselines (DBSCAN, K-means) are compared in Table 4. Need comparisons with entropy-based sampling, uncertainty-based sampling, diversity-based methods, or other active learning criteria to isolate the contribution of HPE (Reviewer 1, 4).
  - Missing comparison with the recent ECCV SFADA work [A] (Reviewer 1).
  - For MiniDomainNet, Table 3 includes only 7 of 12 possible tasks; full results should be reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Results discrepancy**: The reported Office-Home result for MHPL is 78.4, while the original MHPL paper reports 79.1 under the same settings. The discrepancy needs to be explained, e.g., if results were reproduced from code (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient exploration of annotation budgets**: Experiments are conducted only with 5% labeling budget. Many prior works also test 10%; varying budgets (e.g., 3%, 10%) should be explored to show generalizability. On small datasets like Office‑31, 5% may imply very few absolute samples, raising concerns (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Complexity and stability of HPE**:
  - Building a large number of separating trees, computing homogeneity scores and relevance indices may be overly complex and computationally expensive. The process introduces instability/variance due to random feature selection and split values (Reviewer 3). The computational cost of traversing each sample through all trees and storing the trees could be a bottleneck (Reviewer 4 question).
  - The fixed maximum tree depth (log of sample size) may not be optimal across datasets, leading to underfitting or overfitting (Reviewer 3).
  - The selection of the number of neighbors K and number of trees g seems arbitrary; their impact and the need for extensive tuning across datasets need further investigation (Reviewer 3, question).
- **Unclear or insufficiently justified technical details**:
  - The correlation index needs clearer explanation: what does “k-th feature” mean? What is d in Eq. 2? The notation k is reused confusingly (Reviewer 2). It is not shown that the correlation index works better than simpler distance metrics like L2; why it is more suitable for single-domain selection when the goal is cross-domain shift is unclear (Reviewer 4).
  - The function of h(x_i) in Eq. 6 does not affect the argmax over c, yet the text claims higher homogeneity scores give more confidence in pseudo-labeling; how this is reflected needs clarification (Reviewer 4 question).
  - In Eq. 5, what is x_t? Should be a set notation (Reviewer 4 question).
  - How does the selection procedure guarantee diversity and avoid selecting duplicate near-identical pairs? (Reviewer 4 question).
  - The cost of computing the correlation matrix between all sample pairs and its storage burden during training is not addressed (Reviewer 2 question).
  - Why is the split value chosen uniformly at random rather than using an entropy criterion? Random splits may be inefficient and require larger g; would using the median value work? (Reviewer 4 question).

Note: One reviewer (Reviewer 3) rates the paper as a significant algorithmic contribution, while others find the novelty limited. This contrast in opinion is preserved.
