## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Geometric Transform Attention (GTA), a geometry-aware attention mechanism that encodes 3D geometric relationships (using the special Euclidean group SE(3)) between queries and key-value pairs via relative transformations, rather than standard absolute positional encodings. It targets sparse wide-baseline novel view synthesis (NVS). Evaluated on RealEstate10k, ACID, CLEVR-TR, and MSN-Hard, GTA consistently outperforms prior transformer-based NVS methods with negligible extra parameters/computation. Reviewers rate it 8, 6, 5, 6; one reviewer is strongly positive (8), one borderline (5), and the others cautiously positive.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and writing.** Well-structured paper with a strong intuitive justification (transforming to a canonical coordinate frame) and thorough literature review.
- **Important problem.** Tackles challenging sparse, wide-baseline NVS. Strong results on demanding benchmarks (MSN-Hard synthetic, real-world ACID and RealEstate10k) suggest a significant practical contribution.
- **Method sound and simple.** Easy to implement, integrates with minimal computational overhead, and is potentially applicable beyond NVS. The use of SE(3) to encode relative transformations is mathematically principled and interesting.
- **Thorough evaluation.** Extensive comparisons to state-of-the-art methods (both synthetic and real) and qualitative analyses convincingly demonstrate superiority. Consistent gains across all four datasets are noted.
- **Qualitative results compelling.** Visual comparisons clearly show improvements over baselines.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and conceptual overlap with prior work** *(minority view: reviewer 3 finds contribution incremental; reviewer 2 notes that geometry-aware attention and encoding camera parameters have been explored before – the specific SE(3) implementation differs but the core idea is not entirely novel, diminishing originality)*.
- **Unclear implementation and lack of intuition.**  
  - How features are transformed to a canonical space via matrix multiplication is physically unclear – the operation’s geometric meaning needs explanation.  
  - Block concatenation of multiple group representations with multiplicities to match input dimension *d* seems ad‑hoc; the authors should discuss and ablate alternative integration strategies (e.g., flattening all groups, projecting to *d*, then element‑wise multiplication with features).
- **Missing ablations and comparisons.**  
  - Need detailed ablation of each GTA component – GTA functions more like a transformer block than a pure positional encoding, so component‑wise analysis is required.  
  - No comparison to other similar geometry‑encoding methods under the same baseline and settings (e.g., feature warping approaches).  
  - Only NVS task evaluated – generalization to other 3D vision tasks (e.g., multi‑view 3D detection) is untested, reducing breadth.  
  - No comparison with recent NeRF‑based models beyond Pixel‑NeRF (2021); baselines are outdated.
- **Limited real‑world validation and sensitivity analysis.**  
  - Real‑world datasets used are modest in size and quality (videos appear limited); general SoTA claims are thus tentative.  
  - Missing sensitivity study to camera‑pose errors at training/test time (as done in e.g., SRT, Fig. 6).  
  - No experiments on high‑resolution images or with varying numbers of training views.
- **Paper organization and clarity gaps.**  
  - Related work (Sec. 5) placed after results – moving it earlier and providing a comprehensive survey of geometric positional encoding across 3D vision tasks would improve context. Background on standard transformers (first half of Sec. 2) can be moved to appendix.  
  - Definition of ρ_{g_i g_j} in Eq. 4 is missing, making the transition from Eq. 4 to Eq. 5 and the claimed quadratic complexity unclear.
- **Reviewer disagreement over contribution magnitude.** While reviewer 1 praises the method as sound and significant, reviewer 3 considers it incremental. This contrast is preserved here.
