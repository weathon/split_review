## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies SignSGD in a high-dimensional limit, deriving a limiting SDE and ODE to describe the risk. Using this framework, the authors quantify four effects of SignSGD: effective learning rate, noise compression, diagonal preconditioning, and gradient noise reshaping. They provide a comparison with vanilla SGD and conjecture how the results might extend to Adam. Reviewers overall note the clarity of presentation and the interesting theoretical perspective, but have significant concerns about the validation of the continuous approximation, the gap between the SDE models and the actual optimizers, missing comparisons with existing SDE frameworks, and insufficient experimental support.

### Strengths

*   **Clarity of presentation**: The paper presents theoretical results in a generally understandable manner. (All reviewers)
*   **Originality**: Deriving an SDE for SignSGD in a simple high-dimensional setup appears to be a new direction. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Theoretical insights**: The derivation of an ODE for the risk in linear regression, and the analysis of asymptotic expected risk under Gaussian label noise, provide interesting quantitative understanding of SignSGD's effects, though their practical relevance is limited by the assumptions. (Reviewers 1, 2)
*   **Comparison with vanilla SGD**: The paper includes a comparison with SGD, which helps contextualize the findings. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

*   **Validity of continuous approximation for long-time behavior**: The analysis relies on a continuous approximation, but the paper does not adequately discuss its quantitative accuracy, especially over long horizons.
    *   The authors should discuss theoretical or empirical evidence for how well the continuous SDE/ODE matches discrete SignSGD over long time scales. (Reviewer 1)
    *   Clarify regimes or conditions where the approximation is expected to break down for long-time dynamics. (Reviewer 1, citing Kushner (1982))
    *   Add a dedicated limitations section addressing this. (Reviewer 1)
*   **Gap between SDE models and optimizer behavior**: Insights are derived primarily about the SDEs but are framed as insights about SignSGD itself, without sufficient guarantees that the SDE closely tracks the optimizer.
    *   While Theorem 1 provides guarantees for the risk, extending such guarantees to other critical quantities (e.g., gradient norm, iterate norm) would strengthen the work. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The paper appears to compare SDE models rather than the real optimizers; experiments on the actual optimizers are needed to confirm the insights. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Missing related work and comparisons**: The paper fails to adequately discuss and compare with existing continuous-time modeling frameworks.
    *   The Weak-Approximation (WA) Framework introduced by Li et al. (2019) should be discussed and compared with the authors' approach. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The SDE of Adam has been derived by Malladi et al. (2022); given SignSGD is a special case of Adam, a comparison with these results is expected. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The relation between the derived SDE and the earlier SDE for Adam/RMSprop is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The claim about universality (line 96) needs a reference. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Insufficient experimental validation**: Numerous theoretical results (Theorems 1, 2, 3, 4) are presented with little to no empirical verification.
    *   Empirical validation of bounds on risk, the asymptotic expected risk limit, and the ODE dynamics is strongly encouraged. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Equivalent results for SGD should be provided for comparison. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Unjustified or problematic assumptions**: Several assumptions are not properly justified and may limit the framework's applicability.
    *   The need for a high-dimensional setting is not clarified, especially since the WA framework does not require it. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The focus on linear regression is not explained when the WA framework works for general loss functions. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Assumption 3: The lower bound on covariance may not hold in practice (high-dimensional data often have degenerate structure). (Reviewer 2)
    *   Assumption 4 (learning rate scaling with 1/d) is cumbersome and not needed in WA. (Reviewer 2)
    *   In overparameterized regimes (more parameters than data points), Assumption 1 may lead to non-Lipschitz SDE coefficients, hindering existence and uniqueness of solutions. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Assumption 5 (Equation 4) guarantees an upper bound on the resolvent, but Equation (6) divides by the resolvent; it is not ensured that it is non-zero. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The assumption of bounded risk in the appendix is significant and should be stated in the main paper. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Conceptual and technical inaccuracies**:
    *   Line 86: Stating that SignSGD "affects the covariance matrix K" is conceptually wrong; K is fixed and independent of the optimizer. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Line 149: Compression does not change the landscape; the optimizer moves through it without altering it. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Line 458: The phrasing conflates "optimal deterministic convex opt algorithm" with momentum; the wording is inaccurate regarding stochastic gradient algorithms. (Reviewer 3)
    *   Remark 1: The use of "interpolation" is confusing; label noise is present and interpolation can still occur. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The claim that SignSGD provides diagonal preconditioning is only true if the Hessian is diagonal; for Adam it is only in expectation. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Lack of practical guidance and quantitative characterization**: Section 4.3 does not offer clear guidance on when to use one method over the other. Section 4.4 is mostly qualitative and intuitive; a quantitative characterization of differences in noise structure would be more helpful. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Specific technical concerns**:
    *   Definition 2: The SDE is called "Homogenized" instead of the SDE of SignSGD; explanation of how the ODE of SignGD is recovered in the noiseless case is missing. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Theorem 1: Commentary needed on the strength of exponential explosion, the role of moments, and the two constants (both denoted C). The framework only provides guarantees for the loss, not for general test functions as in WA. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Theorem 2: Similar missing clarifications; the dynamics of R_t should be rephrased for clarity. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Equation (10): Not clear if it is a definition or equality. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Equation (13): The SDE for SGD is not derived in the paper; why is the high-dimensional setting needed when WA does not require it? (Reviewer 2)
    *   Equation (14): The scheduler may diverge at stationary points; it resembles Normalized SGD. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Equation (15.b): Variable ψ is not defined at this point. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Line 315: Suggesting multiplication by sqrt(risk) could negate SignSGD's advantage for unbounded variance. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Lines 316-318: Comment seems disconnected. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Line 432: The analysis could be performed via Malladi et al.'s Adam SDE; the appendix provides only an informal SDE for Adam iterates, missing equations for m_t and v_t. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Theorem 4: Could it be merged with Theorem 3? Experimental validation and SGD comparison needed. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Line 465: The phrase "when the covariance—the Hessian of the risk" needs elaboration. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Section 4.2: If noise variance approaches infinity, risk also tends to infinity; the variable ψ might not diverge. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Lines 370-377: The discussion of kurtosis should be formalized as a Proposition; kurtosis is undefined for some degrees of freedom of the t-distribution. Equation (19) is not obviously straightforward. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Equations (19)-(21) should be combined into a clear result with SGD comparison. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Scheduling SignSGD (Eq 24): The scheduler may undo the normalization that gives SignSGD its effectiveness. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Structure, clarity, and presentation issues**:
    *   The paper lacks a dedicated related work section and does not define SDE/ODE for a generic reader. (Reviewers 2, 3)
    *   Key results (ODE for risk, discussions in Sections 4.2, 4.4) should be formalized as Propositions or Theorems for rigor, not just discussed in text. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Terms like "Vanilla ODE" are undefined. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Figures (especially Figure 1) have unclear labels, are not color-blind friendly, and the caption is confusing (e.g., "usefulness of the ODE", "significant estimation of key quantities"). (Reviewer 2)
    *   Figure 1 should be extended to more epochs to show stochastic behavior near convergence. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Inconsistent notation (e.g., P_t vs P(t)). (Reviewer 2)
    *   Informal language (e.g., "apples-to-apples" in line 267). (Reviewer 2)
    *   Figure 3: Use "Student's t" not "Student-t". (Reviewer 2)
    *   Definition 1: Risk definitions do not depend on labels; list Assumption 1 first for clarity. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Assumption 3: Present assumptions immediately after the matrices' definition; the second and third assumptions are not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Lines 87-93: Could be rewritten as a cohesive paragraph. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Lines 94-96: Rephrase for clarity. (Reviewer 2)
    *   Line 248: Add comma before "respectively". (Reviewer 2)
*   **Appendix issues** (detailed by Reviewer 2):
    *   Technical lemmas and major results are not well organized; missing preliminaries and notation definitions.
    *   Equation (44) justification should be a technical lemma before usage.
    *   Lemma 8 is referenced before it is stated.
    *   Notation $\mathcal{E}_j^i$ and $\sigma_{k+1}$ are undefined.
    *   Line 1007: Mistake in direction of convergence (SDE converges to optimizer, not reverse).
    *   Term "HSGD" appears without introduction.
    *   Notation $v_i$ undefined.
*   **Extra conjecture on Adam adds little**: The conjecture about extending results to Adam is not substantiated, and the connection between SignSGD and Adam (as a normalized gradient in expectation) is already known from classic literature (e.g., Tsypkin 1971). (Reviewer 3)
