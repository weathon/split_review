## Merged Review

### Summary
This work shows that Transformers can achieve competitive performance on the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark without a separate denoising pretraining phase, using rotary positional embeddings and task-specific data augmentation (e.g., easier samples in Pathfinder, AutoAugment for CIFAR10, denoising objective as multi-task learning for text tasks). The authors further demonstrate that a fully parameterized convolution model with a small kernel (e.g., kernel size 61, receptive field ~30 tokens) can match or exceed SSM performance on LRA. They argue that LRA tasks rely heavily on positional information and short-range dependencies rather than long-range reasoning, and conclude that LRA results should be interpreted with caution. The paper includes discussions on the role of inductive biases (Section 4.3) and an ablation study of positional embeddings.

### Strengths
- Questions the representativeness of LRA as a long-range dependency benchmark, which is important given its widespread use for developing new architectures.
- Shows that Transformers with rotary embeddings and appropriate augmentation can match the performance of pretrained Transformers (Amos et al.) and SSMs on LRA without a separate pretraining stage.
- Provides insightful analysis (Section 4.3) on the importance of positional information: Table 2 shows that a convolution model with a small receptive field (kernel size 61, ~30 tokens) can achieve near-SOTA, indicating locality bias.
- Training strategies such as adding easier samples early in Pathfinder are noted as effective.
- Ablations clearly demonstrate that the choice of positional embedding significantly affects Transformer performance.
- Comprehensive analysis of factors (positional embeddings, augmentation, locality) underlying Transformer performance on LRA.
- Shows that LRA can be “solved” by very short convolution windows, effectively questioning its long-range nature.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and originality**: One reviewer (score 1, confidence 5) considers the work purely applied/engineering, applying existing techniques (rotary embeddings, data augmentation) without introducing a new model. Other reviewers (scores 5) also note the techniques are marginal improvements: Reviewer 1 states pretraining (R2) already showed the objective, and augmentations are well-known; Reviewer 2 finds broader implications unclear; Reviewer 4 notes augmentations are standard.
- **Depth of analysis**: Several reviewers call for deeper exploration:
  - Why specific training techniques are effective (e.g., theoretical explanation for how rotary embeddings improve performance).
  - More rigorous quantification of locality: e.g., measure effective receptive field per task, distribution of distances between relevant tokens, not just small-kernel experiments.
  - Broader implications of the main results (Sections 4.1, 4.2) remain unclear: if LRA is unrepresentative of real-world tasks, the connection to real-world performance is not established.
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - Evaluation is limited to LRA; missing results on other long-range benchmarks such as RULER and SCROLLS.
  - Path-X results not shown.
  - Comparisons only against MEGA, not more recent SOTA models.
  - It is unclear whether the same training setup (augmentations, rotary embeddings, etc.) was applied to baselines (MEGA, S4, S5, etc.) – if not, the claim that Transformers match performance may still reflect a gap when all models are trained identically.
  - In Figure 2, only gMLP is trained with the proposed augmentation; S4/S5 are not similarly augmented. This raises the question of whether the performance gap would persist.
  - For CIFAR10, the augmentations (AutoAugment) exploit 2D structure (translation, rotation, shearing), providing additional inductive bias not necessarily available in raw sequences. For ListOps, learning permutation invariance from data is desirable, but augmentations may introduce conflicting biases.
- **Known issues**: LRA’s positional bias is already documented (R1, R2). Reviewer 3 states “LRA has been studied a lot at this point and is not so important for long sequence modeling. SSMs solved it.”
- **Missing evidence and clarifications**:
  - No concrete evidence that representation collapse occurs in the two-stage pretraining approach (mentioned in the paper).
  - gMLP does not improve on the text-retrieval task; this is not explained.
  - The definition of the `split` operation in equation (5) and the calculation of receptive field (kernel size 61 → 30 tokens) require clarification.
  - Resource estimates for training Pathfinder vs. Path-X are not provided.
  - The paper raises the question of how representative LRA is of real-world long-range dependencies but does not present results connecting LRA performance to performance on real-world tasks.
  - Representation collapse is argued as a downside of pretraining, but it is unclear why it matters when training on a single downstream task and reaching competitive results.
  - No discussion of scenarios where the proposed augmentation techniques might fail to achieve good performance, nor suggestions for improving LRA as a benchmark for long-range modeling.
