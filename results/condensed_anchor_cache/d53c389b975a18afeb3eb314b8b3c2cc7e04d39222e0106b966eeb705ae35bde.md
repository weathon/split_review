## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FreqTST, a Transformer-based model that tokenizes continuous time series by using the discrete Fourier transform basis as a frequency dictionary. Each token corresponds to a frequency component with learned magnitude and phase. Two pretraining tasks are introduced: time series reconstruction from the frequency representation and maximum position prediction. The model is evaluated on several time series forecasting benchmarks in both supervised and zero-shot transfer settings, achieving results comparable to PatchTST and other baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novel frequency-based tokenization concept**: Using the frequency spectrum as a common dictionary for tokenizing time series is a fresh perspective (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5). This approach naturally discretizes continuous-valued data via the discrete Fourier transform, addressing a key difficulty in applying Transformers to time series (Reviewer 3). The Fourier representation captures temporal dynamics and long-time behaviors, and can help the network remove noise by masking coefficients (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation and potential for pre-training**: The idea of learning a unified frequency token dictionary opens promising directions for time series pre-training across domains (Reviewer 4). The two pretraining tasks (reconstruction and maximum position prediction) are reasonable (Reviewer 1) and contribute to generalization (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental validation**: The method achieves promising results on long-term forecasting and zero-shot transfer tasks (Reviewer 2). The experiments are fairly thorough and showcase efficacy (Reviewer 4). The transferability across different datasets is a bonus (Reviewer 5). The paper is well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewer 5). Some reviewers found the results comparable to or better than PatchTST on a subset of benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: While using frequency domain for time series is not new, the tokenization method is seen by some as a straightforward application of FFT-based feature extraction without fundamental difference from prior work (Reviewer 2). The core idea of transforming data into the frequency domain via FFT and processing it is not novel (Reviewer 2). One reviewer notes that the method is clever but that attention on fixed Fourier embeddings is unnecessary: dot products of the predetermined basis (G, H) never change, so attention re-calculates constant values, wasting computation (Reviewer 3).
- **Overembellishment and unsupported claims**: Several claims are contradicted by the reported results. The statement that “FreqTST outperforms all other supervised baseline models” is false: Table 2 shows that pretrained PatchTST outperforms FreqTST in half of the MSE/MAE metrics (Reviewer 3). Claims of “superior performance,” “full modeling capacity,” and “large margin” are not justified by the data (Reviewer 3). The abstract’s claim that transformers “fail to understand time series” because “numerical values cannot be tokenized” contradicts the paper’s own premise of tokenizing them (Reviewer 3). The claim that zero-shot transferability is proven is overstated: the transfer experiment uses Electricity (trained) to ETTh2 (tested), both hourly with a 24-hour period; the model is unlikely to work on weather data sampled every 10 minutes (Reviewer 5).
- **Tokenization length dependence and generalization limitation**: The frequency dictionary (G, H ∈ ℝ^{L×P}) depends on input sequence length L. Changing L alters both the dictionary size and the frequency corresponding to each token, so a separate dictionary is needed for each length, even on the same dataset (Reviewers 3, 5). The Fourier transform also assumes periodicity over [0, T), introducing systematic errors from endpoint mismatches that are not handled (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and datasets**: No comparison with latest frequency/Transformer methods such as TimesNet, CrossFormer, or the augmentation-based works FrAug (Chen et al. 2023) and FreDo (Sun et al. 2022) (Reviewers 1, 2). The paper uses a subset of the eight datasets from PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023); results on the remaining ETT datasets (e.g., ETTm2), illness, and M4 are missing (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). The traffic dataset used in PatchTST is also absent (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing experimental details and reproducibility**: Standard deviations are not reported, and multiple repeats are not performed, reducing persuasiveness (Reviewer 2). Architecture details are omitted: head size, embedding/hidden dimensions, activation function, dropout, optimizer, learning rate, and scheduling of hyperparameter β (Reviewer 1). It is unclear whether data augmentation was applied to baseline models (Reviewer 1). The setting of zero-shot transfer is too narrow (only one source/target pair) to support strong generalization claims (Reviewer 2). The fixed look-back window of 96 in main experiments is unrealistic; longer look-back improves PatchTST and DLinear (Reviewer 5).
- **Poor writing and presentation**: The paper contains grammar errors, invented or misused words (e.g., “cosinuid” for cosine, “discretion” for discretion), and vague statements that weaken arguments (Reviewer 3). Latex formatting issues: functions not in Roman, small parentheses, incorrect blackboard bold (ℝ), and unclear equation labels (Eqs. 4–5 use F without definition) (Reviewer 3). Figure 3 is hard to interpret and should be a table; all captions are too minimal to be self-contained (Reviewer 3). The distinction between pretraining and downstream adaptation is unclear in Figure 1 and Section 3; variables L_pre and L are not defined there (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear or unjustified design choices**: The purpose of introducing G and H for positional encoding (Eqs. 4–5) is not clearly linked to the overall method (Reviewers 3, 4). The transformation in Eq. 6 (using W_θ to combine real and imaginary parts) lacks justification; the relationship between t_k and f_k is not explained, and why Eq. 6 is optimal is unsupported (Reviewer 4). The use of MSE for the maximum position prediction (MPP) task is questionable since it is a classification problem (Reviewers 2, 5). The claim of lower computational cost than PatchTST is challenged: FreqTST applies self-attention on a longer sequence (L/2) than PatchTST (L/P) (Reviewer 2). Data augmentation is applied to frequency-domain models but its rationale is not explained (Reviewer 1). It is not clear if pretraining with a masked language modeling (MLM) loss was tried (Reviewer 1).
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: While Reviewers 4 and 5 (scores 6 and 5) find the method novel and experiments thorough, Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 3 and 3) see limited novelty and serious flaws in claims, writing, and rigor. Reviewer 1 (score 5) is mixed but notes comparable results and reasonable pretraining.
