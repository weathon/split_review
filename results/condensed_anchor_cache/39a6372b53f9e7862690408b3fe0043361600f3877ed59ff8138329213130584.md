## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CrIBo, a self-supervised learning method that extends bootstrapping from the image level to the object level by enforcing consistency between semantically similar objects across different images, using nearest neighbor retrieval with a cycle-consistency condition. The method is evaluated on dense downstream tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on dense nearest neighbor retrieval and competitive results on segmentation. Reviewers strongly disagree on overall quality: one reviewer assigns a very low score (1), citing concerns about missing backbone comparisons and clarity, while three reviewers are positive (8, 6, 8), praising the novelty, motivation, and extensive evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and writing**: The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and the figures and preliminaries help understanding; multiple reviewers appreciated the clear presentation.
- **Novelty and originality**: The core idea of cross-image object-level bootstrapping with cycle consistency is considered novel and elegant. It is the first SSL approach to explicitly enforce cross-image consistency at the object/object-part level using clustering (though a related prior work ORL uses region proposals for a similar goal).
- **Effectiveness on dense nearest neighbor retrieval**: CrIBo achieves state-of-the-art performance on dense nearest neighbor retrieval without any fine-tuning, and performs well on linear probing and fine-tuning for segmentation.
- **Empirical validation of design choices**: Ablation experiments (appendix B.2 and B.4) provide strong support for the cycle-consistency criterion, one of the less intuitive components.
- **Motivation and problem framing**: The focus on scene-centric datasets and the need for finer-grained object-level supervision over global representations is well-motivated; discussions on over-clustering and compositionality provide useful insight.
- **Improvement over prior work**: CrIBo directly improves upon the Hummingbird method by moving from pixel-level to object-level bootstrapping.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of cycle-consistency section and Figure 3**: The explanation of cycle-consistency matching (Sec 3.2.2) is convoluted and Figure 3 is unintuitive; it is difficult to determine which comparisons are being made. (Reviewer 1, low score)
- **Missing backbone comparison in linear segmentation**: Linear segmentation experiments (Table 2) do not provide direct backbone-size comparison; CrIBo (ViT-B/16) only outperforms TimeT (ViT-S/16) when using a larger backbone, which may be partly due to larger training set. This omission deserves discussion. (Reviewer 1, low score)
- **Insufficient evaluation scope**: Experiments are restricted to semantic segmentation. To assess generalizability, evaluations on object detection, instance segmentation, and panoptic segmentation are necessary. (Reviewer 3, score 6)
- **Computational cost not reported**: No comparison of GPU memory or training/inference time (e.g., time per epoch in Table 1). The object-level nearest neighbor search and cycle-consistency strategy likely add significant overhead; a breakdown of these costs is needed for practical applicability. (Reviewers 2 and 4, scores 8)
- **Fixed-K clustering limitation**: The clustering algorithm always produces exactly K objects per image, which can under-segment complex scenes or over-segment simple ones. The choice of K is dataset-specific and may be suboptimal for many images. An ablation on different clustering methods (e.g., k-means) or a discussion of adaptive K would strengthen the paper. (Reviewers 3 and 4, scores 6 and 8)
- **Early training signals from cycle-consistency**: At initialization, representations are nearly random, so very few object pairs pass the cycle-consistency test (Figure 5 supports this). This may significantly reduce training signals and slow early learning. (Reviewer 3, score 6)
- **Fine-tuning performance not competitive**: CrIBo underperforms MAE when pre-trained on IN1K (Table 3). Moreover, using a larger ViT-B/16 backbone sometimes degrades performance compared to ViT-S/16, which is inconsistent with other evaluations and requires explanation. (Reviewers 3 and 4, scores 6 and 8)
- **Inaccurate novelty claim**: The paper claims to be the first to enforce cross-image object-level consistency, but prior work (ORL, NeurIPS 2021) achieves this using region proposals rather than clustering. The differences should be discussed and the claim softened. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **Missing discussion of related cross-image SSL works**: Several closely related works on inter-image invariance (e.g., Local Aggregation, Inter-Image Invariance, Mine Your Own View) are not cited or discussed in Sec. 2. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **Unexplored global representation**: A global representation is trained but not used in any downstream task; its usefulness relative to object-centric SSL representations is not analyzed. (Reviewer 1, low score)
- **Missing ablations and design justifications**:
  - No ablation comparing grouping in feature space (clustering) vs. grouping in image space (e.g., MCG, DetCon) or using ground-truth object masks as an upper bound. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
  - Memory bank replacement strategy when full is not described. (Reviewer 3, score 6)
  - Whether CrIBo applies to CNN backbones is unclear. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
  - The use of DINO-style large-crop/small-crop protocol and smaller patch sizes (S/8, B/8) is not explored, though these are known to benefit DINO. (Reviewer 1, low score)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Table 6 has swapped headers (dataset and epochs). (Reviewer 1)
  - Typo: "Finetuning with Segmnenter" should be "Segmenter" (page 14). (Reviewer 2)
- **Memory bank size saturation uncertain**: Ablation on memory bank size (Table 4) shows performance still increasing at 25k; larger bank sizes may further improve results. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
