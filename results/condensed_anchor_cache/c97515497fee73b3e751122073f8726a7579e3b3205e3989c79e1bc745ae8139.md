## Merged Review

### Summary
VIPaint proposes a variational inference algorithm that analytically marginalizes missing features and optimizes a non-Gaussian Markov approximation of the true diffusion posterior for inpainting with pretrained pixel- and latent-domain diffusion models. The method extends Red-Diff to a noisy posterior setting. Experiments are conducted on 100 test images from ImageNet64 and LSUN-Churches256 (10 reconstructions each, 1000 total), reporting LPIPS and KID against five inference-based inpainting baselines, plus super-resolution and deblurring tasks.

### Strengths
- The variational bound provides a more principled approach than heuristic sampling procedures (R1).
- Experiments show some advantage over the compared methods (R1) and improvements over prior works in random inpainting, box inpainting, super-resolution, and Gaussian deblurring (R2).
- The method applies to both pixel-space diffusion models and latent diffusion models (LDM) (R1).
- The additional steps are of similar order to other inpainting approaches (R1).
- Extending Red-Diff to a noisy posterior is a valuable idea and provides practical benefits when critical timesteps are carefully chosen in the reverse process (R2).
- The inpainting results are visually very good (R3).
- Sections 1–3 are well written and pleasant to read (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental validation:** Only 100 test images are used, which makes the quantitative numbers unreliable; testing on larger and more diverse datasets is needed (R1). Insufficient comparison to recent methods, specifically Liu et al. (CVPR 2024) and Chen et al. (CVPR 2024) (R1). The paper does not demonstrate the method with Stable Diffusion, which would be expected given the claim of applicability to LDM (R1). Experiments should compare to state-of-the-art LDM solvers such as Rout et al. (CVPR 2024) and Chung et al. (ICML 2024), which use comparable computational resources (R2). The reported baseline results differ from those in prior works [1–3] without explanation or justification of these changes (R2).
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics:** LPIPS alone is not sufficient for inpainting, especially for large masked regions; FID (or precision/recall) for perceptual quality, CRMSE for pixel-faithfulness, and variance across samples should be reported (R3). Table 1 and Table 4 are not convincing without these metrics (R3).
- **Algorithm description and clarity:** Section 4 (the proposed algorithm) is impossible to follow; even the appendices do not clarify it (R3). The relationship of VIPaint to Red-Diff, DPS, and PiGDM is unclear from the poor description (R3). No pseudocode is provided, making the complexity of the algorithm unknown (R3). It is unclear what makes this method specifically tailored to inpainting (R3). The paper is poorly structured, spending excessive space on background (Sections 2–3) while the main contribution only appears in Section 4.2 on page 7, leaving insufficient room for experiments; results are presented as statements rather than detailed analyses (R2). The theoretical discussion in Appendix B covers well-known material and lacks new insights; contributions should be presented earlier (R2).
- **Contribution novelty concerns:** The main contribution appears to be a straightforward extension of Red-Diff under the strong assumption that the posterior is well-defined for noisy latents, which is not true (it is only defined for zeroth noise level) (R2). The claim that VIPaint captures multi-modal posteriors is questionable: for a simple Gaussian mixture, the exchange of expectation in Tweedie’s formula introduces a bias that prevents sampling the correct posterior; a rigorous proof or explanation is missing (R2). The results in Figures 18–24 look either very similar or distorted, and do not verify the claimed multi-modality; specific metrics or analyses should be provided (R2). One reviewer judges that the contribution is essentially nothing (R3). Another reviewer notes the method’s value but with high skepticism about its novelty (R2).
- **Lack of ablation and hyperparameter analysis:** The variational loss introduces many hyperparameters, yet no ablation studies are performed (R1). The choices appear arbitrary and it is unclear how to select optimal values for other datasets or how robust the method is to these parameters (R1). No analysis is provided on how to identify which timesteps in the reverse process are critical or on the impact of different timestep choices (R2).
- **Computational cost:** VIPaint uses a significantly higher computational budget than compared baselines such as DPS and PSLD, yet no comparison is made with methods that use nearly comparable resources (R2).
- **Typos:** Missing reference labels at lines 1100, 1223, and 1348, making it difficult to identify which table/figure is referenced (R2).
