## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for defining neural network layers—fully connected (FC) and attention—on symmetric spaces of noncompact type (e.g., hyperbolic spaces, SPD manifolds). The core technical contribution is a unified, closed-form expression for point-to-hyperplane distance derived via Busemann functions and Iwasawa decomposition, which generalizes to G-invariant Riemannian metrics. The authors then construct FC layers and an attention mechanism using this distance, mimicking the Euclidean affine-map viewpoint. Experiments on image classification (CIFAR-10/100), EEG signal classification, image generation, and natural language inference demonstrate competitive or improved accuracy compared to existing manifold-based and Euclidean baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novel unified theoretical framework**: Generalizes point-to-hyperplane distance to symmetric spaces of noncompact type, covering both hyperbolic spaces and SPD manifolds. This addresses limitations of prior work that focused on narrower spaces (e.g., Nguyen & Yang, 2023). (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **New architecture components**: Proposes FC layers and an attention mechanism that operate on these spaces, extending the toolbox for non-Euclidean neural networks. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Mathematical rigor**: Derivations are thorough and grounded in well-established geometry (Busemann functions, Iwasawa decomposition). (Reviewer 1,4)
- **Broad empirical validation**: Experiments span several task domains (image classification, EEG, generation, NLI) and show performance improvements, notably on EEG datasets where the model outperforms baselines like EEG-TCNet, Graph-CSPNet, and MBEEGSE. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Generality**: The approach can be instantiated on multiple manifold types (Section 4.3) and covers both point-hyperplane distance and attention weight computation in a principled way. (Reviewers 1,3)

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and organization**: Several reviewers found the paper hard to follow, especially the core practical sections (4.5.1 and 4.5.2). Section 4 mixes mathematical framework with neural network details; a clearer separation would help. Section 3.2 is highly abstract. Subsection headings like “Examples” (Section 4.3) could be more descriptive. Figure 2(b) orientation impedes readability. (Reviewers 1,2,4) [Reviewer 4 rated presentation 1/3, while others gave 2-3, indicating particular concern.]
- **Insufficient implementation detail for FC and attention layers**: It is not clearly specified what the inputs, outputs, dimensionalities, and trainable parameters are for each proposed layer. The translation of abstract definitions (e.g., exponential map, group K action in Proposition 4.12, attention weight computation) into concrete computational steps is missing. No diagram or step-by-step breakdown is provided. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Lack of backpropagation and gradient details**: There is little to no discussion of how gradients flow through the hyperbolic/SPD layers or whether the proposed operations are differentiable in practice. This makes it difficult to assess trainability and computational cost. (Reviewers 1,2) [Reviewer 2 also asks about complexity analysis.]
- **Unclear relationship to prior point-to-hyperplane formulations**: The paper would benefit from a detailed comparison explaining why the distances derived here differ from those in Ganea et al. (2018b) for hyperbolic spaces, given that the present framework subsumes such spaces. The narrative connecting affine functions to point-to-hyperplane distances (L396-404) needs elaboration. (Reviewer 3)
- **Rationale for two metrics (PEM vs. G-invariant)**: The motivation for considering both metrics and their respective strengths/weaknesses in the architecture is not sufficiently explained. (Reviewer 1)
- **Experimental design concerns**:
    - **Dataset choice for hyperbolic evaluation**: CIFAR-10/100 do not have a clear hierarchical structure; other datasets with stronger hierarchical properties (e.g., gene expression, word-document) are more typical benchmarks for hyperbolic methods. The marginal gains in Table 2 are not adequately justified. (Reviewer 4)
    - **Missing ablation study in Section 5.2**: There are multiple differences between the proposed method and baselines; it is unclear which component drives the improvements. (Reviewer 4)
    - **No runtime or complexity analysis**: Given the small accuracy gains, computational cost should be reported to assess practical utility. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Limitations statement is too narrow**: It focuses on a very specific issue and should cover scalability and broader practical applicability. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor corrections needed**: Typos in hyperbolic distance definition (Line 106), notation fix (Line 233), extraneous dot in Corollary 4.3, missing punctuation (Line 425), wFM used before defined (Line 470). Some definitions lack intuitive explanation (e.g., Definition 4.7). (Reviewers 2,3)
