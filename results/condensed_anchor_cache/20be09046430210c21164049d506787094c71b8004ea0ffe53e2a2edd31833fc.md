## Merged Review

### Summary
TapMo presents a text-driven motion generation pipeline for skeleton-free 3D characters using shape deformation-aware features, a Mesh Handle Predictor, and a Shape-aware Motion Diffusion module. It is a first step toward motion generation for generic characters and produces impressive animations across diverse shapes (walking hand, animals, etc.). Reviewers rated the paper 6,6,6,8, with one reviewer being more positive; all agreed the problem is interesting and the results are promising, but raised several concerns about clarity, experimental scope, and representation limitations.

### Strengths
- First attempt at text-driven motion synthesis for skeleton-free characters (novel and significant problem).
- Impressive animations across a variety of 3D characters, including non-humanoid ones (walking hand, animals, etc.), with well-recognized structure-motion associations.
- Sound method combining diffusion-based motion synthesis with skeleton-free mesh deformation; innovations include root handle modeling, shape-aware handle movement, and a delta term for residual motion.
- Application of shape deformation features as conditioning for motion generation is nice and effective.
- Comprehensive experiments with quantitative metrics, user study, and supplementary videos that validate naturalness.
- Approach is reasonably simple and promising for generating motions for unseen characters.
- Presentation is well done, visual illustrations are clear, paper is easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Foot-ground penetration** exists in generated animations, degrading quality (R1).
- **Unclear technical details**: mesh deformation feature (f̂) dimension (512 from appendix) and how it is obtained; mesh-specific adaptation not reflected in equations; discriminator implementation not specified; joint or separate training of the two modules unclear (R2). The shape-aware motion diffusion is presented as overcomplicated modifications to MDM, making it hard to discern key designs that improve quality (R3).
- **Insufficient evaluation**:
  - Metrics reported only on the human dataset (HumanML3D); cross-category generation ability not evaluated on non-human datasets (e.g., deforming things 4D) despite claim of handling diverse shapes (R4).
  - Handle-FID visualization only on two SMPL models; reviewers question if two are sufficient and suggest testing on more (e.g., MGN) (R2).
  - Missing comparisons to recent text-to-motion methods (MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse, T2M-GPT) (R3).
  - No direct comparison with ground truth vertices for SMPL-based models using the PMD metric (R2).
- **Representation limitations**: Fixed number of handles (K=30) may be insufficient for fine-grained motion like cloth deformation or hair, leading to artifacts (R4). Handle adjacency definition in Eq.7 needs to be done per character; it is unclear if this is required only during training or also inference, and whether it scales to shapes with large differences (R2).
- **Driving signal limitation**: Reliance on text input cannot capture nuanced motion cues (weight shifts, momentum, anticipation) that are hard to describe linguistically (R4).
- **Mesh handle predictor ambiguity**: It is unclear how the method handles characters with different topologies and semantics—how handles are selected, how the number of handles varies per character, and how training accommodates variable handle counts (R3). The claim that “each handle is dynamically assigned to vertices with the same semantics across different meshes” lacks explanation (R3).
- **Lack of important ablations and analyses**:
  - No qualitative results before and after Motion Adaptation Fine-tuning, which typically produces large differences in naturalness (R2).
  - No analysis of why Diversity metric for TapMo variants is much higher than top-rule methods; Multimodal Dist and Multimodality results are unexplained (R4).
  - No ablation of the GAN loss – is it necessary or complementary to the diffusion loss? (R4).
- **Text description transferability**: It is not clear whether the model, trained on human text-motion pairs, can generalize to quadrupeds; has it seen quadruped motion before? (R4).
- **Root handle and equation clarity**: In Eq.3, it is unclear whether the first dimension K includes the root handle (k=1) or if there are K-1 local translations/rotations (R2). The added terms τᵏₗ and hₖ in the motion representation (compared to SfPT Eq.1) are not explained (R2). The phrase “skinning weight s of the handle” in Methods may refer to vertex skinning weights, but this is ambiguous (R2).
- **Missing discussion** of related skeleton-free works: “Zero-shot Pose Transfer for Unrigged Stylized 3D Characters” (SfPT) and “HMC: Hierarchical Mesh Coarsening for Skeleton-free Motion Retargeting”. The self-supervised shape understanding in SfPT could potentially strengthen handle prediction (R2).
- *Minority reviewer note*: One reviewer (score 8) found the method sound and the results impressive, but still raised several of the above weaknesses regarding data, representation, and text limitations. The other three reviewers (score 6) were slightly less positive, particularly questioning experimental scope and clarity.
