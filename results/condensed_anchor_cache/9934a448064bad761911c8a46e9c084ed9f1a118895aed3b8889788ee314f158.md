## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper theoretically analyzes diffusion models trained in Barron spaces (spanned by single-layer neural networks), providing sample complexity bounds that depend on the intrinsic latent dimension rather than the ambient dimension, thereby showing how these models can avoid the curse of dimensionality for distributions with simple low-dimensional structure. The analysis covers two specific latent structures: linear combinations of lower-dimensional vectors (d << D) and independent components with lower-dimensional subspaces. The results connect to recent sampling guarantees for diffusion processes. However, the analysis is limited to single-layer networks, idealized linear/subspace assumptions, and lacks experiments. Reviewer 4 (score 8) is notably more positive, highlighting the step forward from requiring a specialized architecture to requiring only infinite width, while other reviewers (scores 5–6) find the setting too simplistic, the presentation unclear, and the practical relevance unsubstantiated.

### Strengths

- **Rigorous theoretical framework**: Provides a careful mathematical analysis showing how diffusion models in Barron spaces can overcome the curse of dimensionality by adapting to low-dimensional latent structures, without needing specialized architectures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The work is a meaningful step toward understanding the gap between theory and practice (Reviewer 3).

- **Barron space connection**: Using Barron spaces bridges theoretical tractability and practical relevance, as Barron norms capture essential features of networks used in practice (Reviewer 1). However, Reviewer 2 notes that this reliance may pose scalability challenges (see Weaknesses).

- **Sample complexity on intrinsic dimension**: Bounds depend on intrinsic dimension d rather than ambient D, with rates such as \(n^{-2/(d+5)}\) under certain conditions (Theorem 3.3) and similar results for independent components (Reviewers 1, 4).

- **End-to-end KL bounds**: Corollaries 3.4 and 3.8 give bounds on the KL divergence between the true data distribution and the diffusion model distribution, which is a valuable theoretical guarantee (Reviewer 4).

- **Connection to diffusion sampling**: The work links with recent progress in diffusion sampling processes, increasing its relevance (Reviewer 3).

- **Minority positive view**: Reviewer 4 (score 8) explicitly highlights that going from needing a specific architecture to “only” needing an infinitely wide network is a step forward, and that the KL bounds are interesting. This contrasts with the more critical assessments of other reviewers.

### Weaknesses

- **Restriction to single-layer networks is a major limitation**: The analysis covers only single-layer neural networks (Barron space), which do not represent the deep architectures used in modern diffusion models. Reviewer 3 strongly objects to calling this “shallow networks” as it misleadingly implies results hold for a few layers; only single-layer is proven. This limits relevance for practical deep models (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 2 adds that the Barron space norm may not be easily minimized with standard gradient-based optimization, especially as depth and width increase, and the analysis ignores the impact of architectural choices (e.g., activation functions, connectivity) on the Barron norm.

- **Idealized data assumptions limit applicability**: The theoretical results assume target distributions are concentrated on low-dimensional linear manifolds or have independent components. Real-world data often exhibit non-linear structures and complex dependencies; non-linear latent structures are noted as future work but not addressed (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 asks to what extent Lipschitz continuity and sub-Gaussianity assumptions can be relaxed, and whether the approach is robust to noise. Reviewer 2 questions if the analysis can be extended to flow models/matching (regressing vector fields instead of scores) or to discrete state spaces for language modeling — these are missing comparisons and extensions that would strengthen the work.

- **No experiments to validate assumptions or claims**: The paper lacks any experiments, either on simulations or real-world data, to demonstrate that the chosen assumptions are relevant and that the theoretical claims are supported. This makes it difficult to assess practical relevance (Reviewer 3).

- **Presentation clarity issues**: The paper is difficult to follow even for target readers (machine learning theory researchers). Key notation is not explained before use (e.g., \(\mathcal{F}_t\), \(\asymp\), \(\lor\) in \(\mu_0 \lor \sigma_t\sqrt{D}\), Law(·) in Corollary 3.4). The asymptotic statement on line 165 (risk asymptotic to \(n^{-2/(D+4)}\)) is ambiguous. The intuition for the \(F_1\)-norm (lines 175–179) is unclear. A list of notation is suggested (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 notes that formulas are overly complicated; a simpler main-body bound with a detailed version in the appendix would improve readability.

- **Title over-promises**: The title “Shallow diffusion networks provably learn hidden low-dimensional structure” sounds more general than the actual results, which only cover single-layer networks and specific linear structures. A more precise title is suggested (Reviewer 4).

- **Lack of analysis of gradient descent training**: The paper does not analyze the practical training process (gradient descent), leaving optimization abstract (Reviewer 3). This connects to Reviewer 2’s point about the difficulty of minimizing the Barron norm with gradient-based methods.

- **Lack of direct usable implications for practitioners**: The paper provides no guidelines or algorithmic innovations that practitioners could use. Reviewer 3 questions what can be taken away from the theory or what future algorithmic developments could be inspired.

- **Specific unclear claims and missing explanations** (from Reviewer 4’s questions that overlap with criticisms):
  - Lines 42–44: The phrase “these works leave open the difficulty of statistical estimation… the sampling problem’s true difficulty is hidden in the complexity of learning” is unclear and needs explanation.
  - Line 180: The assumption that neuron parameters come from the hypersphere is stated, but it is not justified whether this generalizes to parameters over the entire space; this should be stated as an explicit assumption in the contribution section.
  - Line 231: The notation \(\mu_{t,x} = \mu_0 \lor \sigma_t\sqrt{D}\) uses \(\lor\) in a non-standard way; its meaning must be defined.
  - Theorem 3.3 remarks: The paper says leaving showing a rate \(n^{2/(d+4)}\) to future work, but gives an upper bound of \(n^{2/(d+5)}\), which is smaller for \(n>1\); clarification is needed why \(n^{2/(d+4)}\) would be better. Also, the claimed dependence on \(\sigma_t\) (e.g., \(\sigma_t^{-4/(d+5)}\)) is not clearly identified.
  - Line 491: The “truncation arguments” are mentioned without explanation or reference.

- **Minor issues**: Typo on line 315: “difference” should be “different”? (Reviewer 1). Line 189: Should be \(B_2(r, d)\) (Reviewer 4). Use full names before abbreviations (e.g., DDPM, ERM, DNN, GD) (Reviewer 4).
