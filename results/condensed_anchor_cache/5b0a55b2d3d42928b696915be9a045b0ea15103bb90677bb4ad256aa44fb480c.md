## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a hyperspherical energy score for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. The method trains hyperspherical embeddings using a von Mises-Fisher distribution (MLE objective) and then uses the negative log partition function (Helmholtz free energy) of reconstructed logits as a parametric OOD score, avoiding nearest-neighbor search. Evaluations on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1k (with ResNet) report competitive FPR95/AUROC compared to CIDER and other baselines. Reviewers found the idea interesting and the method computationally efficient, but raised significant concerns about novelty, clarity, experimental thoroughness, and practical advantage over existing training-free and post-hoc methods.

### Strengths
- The method is simple, understandable, and computationally appealing because it does not require nearest-neighbor search at test time (Reviewer 4).
- The combination of hyperspherical representations with energy-based scoring is intuitively sensible (Reviewer 1).
- The paper provides solid theoretical derivations connecting the score to a log-likelihood perspective (Reviewer 2).
- The approach yields competitive performance on CIFAR and ImageNet OOD benchmarks with a ResNet (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Reviewer 3 found the paper well-presented and easy to follow; Reviewer 2 also praised its clarity.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty and limited contribution:** Several reviewers found the contribution incremental. The MLE training loss for hyperspherical embeddings is already used in prior work (SIREN, CIDER), and the test score is a proxy for negative log-likelihood (Reviewer 4). The approach is seen as a straightforward combination of existing ideas rather than a novel framework (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 2 considered the idea novel and intriguing, but this view was not shared by the most critical reviewer.
- **Insufficient comparison with recent methods:** The paper omits comparisons with several recent OOD detection approaches, including gradient-based methods (Huang et al. 2021, Behpour et al. 2023, Igoe et al. 2022), ASH (Djurisic et al. 2022), and post-hoc methods like Typical Features that achieve superior results (e.g., 94.28% AUROC on ImageNet) on classifiers trained with plain cross-entropy loss (Reviewers 2, 4). The advantages over ASH, which is training-free and fast, are not clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Practicality concerns due to required training:** The method requires special training, whereas many current OOD detectors are training-free or post-hoc, making them more practical. On ImageNet only the last residual block is fine-tuned, raising questions about training stability and generalization (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 4 argues that given many methods do well on standard cross-entropy classifiers, there is little reason to adopt a more expensive hyperspherical training procedure.
- **Insufficient empirical analysis and missing ablations:** 
  - Absolute OOD detection performance (e.g., whether an average FPR95 of 38.5 is good) is not discussed; common failure cases and dataset-dependent performance differences (e.g., why Places365 is much harder than LSUN) are not analyzed (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper does not provide density plots separating in-distribution and OOD scores (Reviewer 2).
  - Error bars and multiple random seed results are missing for all tables (Reviewers 3, 4). Experimental details for Table 2 are lacking (Reviewer 3).
  - The method is not evaluated on near-OOD benchmarks (e.g., SSB-Hard, NINCO from OpenOOD 1.5) or on common corruptions; CIDER has been shown to underperform on near-OOD compared to simpler pretraining strategies (Reviewer 4). This suggests the claimed effectiveness of hyperspherical embeddings may not hold on challenging OOD.
  - No experiments with Vision Transformers are conducted; applicability to broader architectures is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Sections 3.1 and 3.2 are hard to follow; more background on CIDER is needed before Section 3 (Reviewer 1).
  - The loss function \(\mathcal{L}\) in equation (13) is not properly defined (Reviewer 1).
  - The dot notation is confusingly used for both scalar multiplication and dot products (Reviewer 1).
  - The variable \(z\) is not clearly specified before its use (Reviewer 4).
  - The comparison to CIDER and SIREN is confusing because those methods impose additional regularization terms that are not clearly separated from the proposed loss (Reviewer 4); differences with SIREN are not discussed at all (Reviewer 3).
  - Minor grammatical issues noted (Reviewer 1).
- **Performance limitations and questions:**
  - The method shows significant improvements over the Energy baseline on SVHN and LSUN but only marginal gains on Places365; this variation is not explained (Reviewer 2).
  - Reviewer 4 notes that the reported gains over cross-entropy-based post-hoc methods are insufficient, and it is not shown whether the method can be combined with such techniques for further improvement.
  - When training the full network (vs. only last residual block), instabilities or elevated generalization error may occur (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing discussion of limitations:** The paper does not discuss limitations of the proposed method (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Questions raised by reviewers that correspond to missing experiments/analyses:**
  - Why were the first blocks of the backbone frozen? Was the network first trained with supervised contrastive loss and then with Eq. (13)? (Reviewer 3)
  - Could the approach be adapted to require minimal or no training? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is the training loss the same as CIDER? (Reviewer 4 notes identical ID accuracy in Table 5.)
  - How does the method perform on near-OOD datasets (SSB-Hard, NINCO) and on common corruptions? (Reviewer 4)
  - Can the method be extended to regression problems? (Reviewer 1)
