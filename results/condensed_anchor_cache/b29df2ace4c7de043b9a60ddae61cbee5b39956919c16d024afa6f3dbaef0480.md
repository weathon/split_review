## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies neural network training dynamics by analyzing the Jacobian of trained parameters with respect to their initial values. It reports three regions in the singular value spectrum of this Jacobian (chaotic, bulk, stable) and investigates properties of the bulk region. Reviewers find the question interesting and the presentation clear, but raise major concerns about limited experimental scope (single one-hidden-layer MLP on a simple task), insufficient novelty (the findings are arguably explained by the parameter-function Jacobian and the NTK regime), weak connections to existing literature (e.g., lazy training, low-dimensional subspace compression), and lack of theoretical grounding. One reviewer (R2, score 5) was more positive about the insights and contribution, while the others (R1, R3, R4, R5, all score 3) considered the paper not ready for publication. Soundness and contribution scores vary: R2 gave 3/2, while R3/R4/R5 gave 1/1.

### Strengths
- The question of understanding training dynamics through the training Jacobian is interesting and could have potential for accelerating training/inference (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- The paper is well written, with clear figures and engaging presentation (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- The study of the training Jacobian appears novel, with creative experimental methodologies to extract relationships between this Jacobian, eigenvector importance, and predictions (R4).
- Empirical demonstration of three distinct regions in the singular value spectrum (chaotic, bulk, stable) (R3).
- Finding that the structure of input data plays a key role and is largely independent of labels, aligning with prior results on isotropic vs. anisotropic data (R3).
- Insights are computed largely from properties of the network at initialization, which could be powerful for revealing model performance without costly training (R4).
- Reviewer R2 (minority more positive, score 5) found the paper sheds light on insightful phenomena and the subject is interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Extremely limited experimental scope:** Experiments are conducted on a single one-hidden-layer perceptron trained on a simple task achieving near-zero training loss in 25 epochs. This raises serious concerns about generalizability to modern deep learning architectures and datasets (R1, R3, R4, R5). The LeNET-5 experiment in the appendix required hundreds of CPU days, further highlighting scalability issues and limiting the approach (R3). Multiple reviewers request either theoretical guarantees or experiments on more realistic models/datasets (R1, R3, R5).
- **Insufficient novelty and weak connection to literature:** The findings appear to largely reiterate concepts already established in the literature. The paper mentions only 3 related works (10 cited in total) and does not properly situate itself within known regimes such as the NTK/lazy training (Jacot et al., Chizat et al.) or low-dimensional subspace compression (Kwon et al., Yaras et al.) (R1, R3, R5). There is a simple alternative explanation: if network predictions are indifferent to parameter changes in a given direction (as measured by the parameter-function Jacobian), gradient descent naturally will not optimize those directions—this is expected and not new (R5). The claimed three-region categorization lacks substantial novelty (R3).
- **Weak theoretical grounding:** The paper lacks rigorous definitions and theoretical justification. For example, “dimensionality of training” (ll.089-090) is not defined (R2). The claim that the bulk subspace may explain inductive bias is not sufficiently supported (R4). The assumption behind equation (1) effectively assumes gradient flow, but this is not discussed (R2). No deeper theoretical justification for the claimed low-dimensional structure is provided (R5).
- **Missing experiments and ablations:** Several important ablations and robustness checks are absent:
  - How does the spectral analysis evolve during training? Is the same phenomenon observed at intermediate steps, and do the indexes associated with the bulk singular values remain the same (without sorting) (R2)?
  - Is Figure 1 consistent across different random initializations? Does Section 3.2 suggest the bulk indexes are the same across seeds (R2)?
  - What happens without momentum? Conserved quantities exist only in the non-momentum case; comparing both would be informative (R2).
  - The phase changes in Figures 3, 5, and 6 are unclear; the empirical evidence suggests further structure beyond the three regions, but thresholds appear to be chosen to match the characterization (R4).
  - Do the small singular values in Figure 4 roughly match those in the training Jacobian? It is surprising that SGD on ‘stable’ eigenvectors yields good performance with ~300 eigenvectors while ‘chaotic’ eigenvectors are harder to optimize (R4).
  - Experimental details are insufficient: architecture (number of hidden layers, hidden dimension, nonlinearity, etc.) are not stated (R3).
- **Presentation issues:** 
  - The dashed line in Figure 2a is not clearly indicated (R1).
  - Lines 092-094 are unclear and need more precise explanation (R2).
  - Typo: “to the the Jacobian of” (l.240) (R2).
- **Code not provided** (R2).
- **Relevance to practical improvements unclear:** How the insights translate to better generalization, feature learning, or inductive bias is not demonstrated (R4).
