## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper defines “coherent factuality” for reasoning tasks and develops a conformal‑prediction‑based method that guarantees coherent factuality of LLM outputs. It constructs a deducibility graph representing claim dependencies, replacing the independent‑factuality assumption of prior work (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024). The method is evaluated on MATH and FELM mathematical reasoning datasets, showing improved calibration and stronger guarantees than baselines.

### Strengths
- Clearly identifies that existing conformal‑prediction factuality is inappropriate for reasoning tasks because it assumes independent sub‑claims; the new coherent‑factuality definition and deducibility graph properly handle claim dependencies.
- The proposed framework is simple to implement and experimentally achieves desired coverage and substantiation of claims, outperforming the baseline (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024) on MATH and FELM.
- The claim‑scoring function that incorporates graph structure and propagates confidence along descendants is a novel and effective design.
- Extending conformal prediction to reasoning problems is an important direction, and considering dependency structure among claims is straightforward and effective.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical guarantee and graph quality**: The approximate deducibility graph is generated using GPT‑4o, which provides no theoretical guarantee; this limitation is acknowledged but means the theoretical guarantee of conformal prediction is not fully utilized. The quality of the GPT‑4o‑generated graphs is unknown (Reviewer 2), and no experiments using a human‑annotated ideal graph are provided (Reviewer 1).  
  *Reviewer 1, with moderate confidence, emphasizes that if bad claims are accepted because they are consistent, the method may not help resolve hallucination; additional clarification about coherent factuality and deducibility beyond the current definition is needed.*  
- **Lack of downstream‑task evaluation**: No experiment demonstrates whether the filtered outputs improve performance on the actual downstream reasoning task, or how often the responses still contain the correct final answer after filtering (Reviewers 2, 3). For reasoning problems that require a single correct conclusion, human studies or answer‑accuracy metrics would be valuable (Reviewer 3).  
- **Experimental details missing or unclear**:  
  - It is not stated how \(C_{true}\) (ground‑truth claims) is created and used in the MATH and FELM experiments (Reviewer 2).  
  - The paper tests only one model (GPT‑4o, though the source of MATH responses is not explicitly given); testing at least one more model (preferably open‑source) is needed to assess generalizability and improve reproducibility (Reviewer 3).  
  - All qualitative results are from MATH, which contains only true claims; additional qualitative examples that include bad (incorrect) claims evaluated under coherent factuality are required (Reviewer 1).  
  - There is no experiment showing whether the method also achieves independent factuality simultaneously, or how that might affect results (Reviewer 2).  
- **Writing and presentation issues** (Reviewer 3, high confidence): The paper is often unclear and lacks rigor. Specific problems:  
  - The key property that the ideal graph uses the “minimal set of claims” is only mentioned in the appendix.  
  - “graph G” at LINE 350 is not defined.  
  - Appendix F is referenced at LINE 402 but does not contain the prompts.  
  - Figures 4(b) and 4(c) share the same title, causing confusion.  
  - Terms such as “Descendants weight boosting”, “independently filtered outputs”, and “self‑consistency scoring” are not explained.  
  - The sentence at LINES 316–318 is difficult to understand.  
  - Graph generation is critical but all details are in the appendix; a high‑level description in the main paper is needed.  
- **Unanswered implementation questions**: Why is the median used at LINE 361? How is the hyperparameter \(\beta\) selected? Is there any special design for the cases where “it is not reasonable to do so in a proof of that fact” (LINE 153)? (Reviewer 3)
