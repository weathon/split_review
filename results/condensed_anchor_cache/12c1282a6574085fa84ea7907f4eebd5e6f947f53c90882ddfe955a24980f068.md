## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Plan-Seq-Learn (PSL), a modular framework for long-horizon robotics tasks that combines LLM high-level planning, classical motion planning (AIT*), and RL for low-level control. PSL decomposes tasks into sub-sequences, uses vision and motion planning for initialization, and learns local policies via RL. Experiments on 20+ tasks from four benchmarks in simulation show success rates over 80%, outperforming prior methods.

### Strengths
- The approach is sensible and reasonable: leveraging LLMs for high-level planning, motion planners for efficient collision-free path planning, and RL for contact-rich manipulation.
- The combination of LLM task planning, motion planning, and RL is novel; using LLM guidance to enable RL online learning without a pre-defined skill library is intuitive and convincing.
- The modular design avoids cascading failures common in purely sequenced systems by allowing online improvement via RL.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written; figures (especially Figure 2) effectively illustrate the overall idea, and the method integration is described in detail.
- Related work covers plenty of relevant methods with clear descriptions.
- Extensive experiments with good results on single-stage and multi-stage benchmarks (up to 5 stages); outperforms language-based, classical, and end-to-end approaches.

### Weaknesses
- **Simulation only**: Experiments are conducted entirely in simulation; real-robot deployment and sim2real gap are not addressed.
- **Limited long-horizon definition**: The largest task has only 5 stages, which is not truly long-horizon. Success rates can be as low as .67 ± .22. Additionally, from the paper and appendix it is unclear if any sub-tasks beyond “grasp” and “place” are generated by the LLM.
- **Missing details in main paper**: LLM usage (prompting, decomposition specifics) is detailed only in Appendix D; stage termination conditions and training details (e.g., how reward functions are designed per stage, influence of sparse vs. dense rewards) are not explained in the main text.
- **Unfair comparisons**: For E2E and RAPS baselines, authors only provided a single fixed global view (O^global) while PSL uses an additional local view (O^local). The authors state they did not find improvement with extra views, but this is not demonstrated; adding O^local for baselines would have made the comparison more convincing.
- **Missing comparisons with similar LLM+RL works**: Inner Monologue and Bootstrap Your Own Skills (BOSS) share many similarities but are not included in experiments, which is a missed opportunity.
- **Method trade-offs and failure handling**: 
  - Planning without a pre-defined skill library requires relearning skills from scratch for each new task, which may be costly compared to methods with reusable skills.
  - The paper overlooks how PSL handles learning failures (e.g., when the termination condition cannot be reached) and what happens if the LLM is given unlearnable skill terms that may confuse its choices.
  - Lower-level RL cannot compensate for mistakes made at the higher planning level; this is not addressed.
- **Questionable claims about LLM planning capabilities**: The statement that LLMs are “highly capable of performing planning for long-horizon robotics tasks” is arguable (contrary evidence exists, e.g., Kambhampati et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2023). Similarly, the claim that LLMs can leverage internet-scale knowledge to break down tasks is asserted without guarantee of validity or optimality; the paper does not discuss what happens when decomposition fails or is suboptimal, or how to improve beyond the current fixed LLM (e.g., fine-tuning).
- **End-to-end vs. hierarchical tradeoffs**: The paper focuses on advantages of the hierarchical approach but does not discuss tradeoffs from the end-to-end side.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive on strengths and rated contribution as “2 fair” while two others (scores 6) rated contribution as “3 good”; the weaknesses above reflect concerns especially from the lower-scoring reviewers.
