## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a model-agnostic pipeline that uses diagnostic classifiers (linear probing) to detect which graph-theoretic and domain-specific properties (e.g., clustering, density, centrality) are encoded in GNN embeddings. Experiments are run on synthetic (Grid-House), molecular (ClinTox), and brain connectivity (fMRI) datasets across GCN, GAT, and GIN architectures. Reviewers acknowledge the topic’s importance and the pipeline’s simplicity, but raise significant concerns about methodological choices, novelty in light of prior work, incomplete analysis, and unclear presentation. One reviewer (R2, score 5) is notably more positive, calling it the first paper to address this problem and finding the pipeline effective; the other three reviewers (scores 3) are more critical.

### Strengths

- Novel task: model‑agnostic probing for structural graph properties in GNN embeddings, which is less explored than instance‑level explanations. R2 considers this the inaugural paper on the topic.
- Comprehensive dataset testing across synthetic, molecular, and fMRI datasets, demonstrating versatility (R1, R4).
- Detailed comparative analysis of GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN) and their relative strengths in capturing specific properties (R1).
- Simple yet effective pipeline (linear probing) that yields valuable insights for future research (R2, R3).
- Layer‑wise analysis approach holds potential for understanding how GNNs progressively abstract graph properties (R3).
- Exploration of both local and global graph properties (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Flawed method for pre‑pooling embeddings.** Sorting node embeddings by their norm and padding with zeros (to handle variable graph sizes) is arbitrary, lacks theoretical justification, and may introduce ordering/magnitude biases. The diagnostic classifier could exploit artificial patterns in the sorted sequence rather than genuine graph properties. Permutation‑invariant alternatives (e.g., Deep Sets, other aggregation techniques) are recommended (R1, R3).
- **Limited novelty given prior work.** R3 notes that “Probing Graph Representations” (Akhondzadeh et al., 2023) performed a very similar probing study with nearly identical conclusions; the authors do not compare their results or clearly delineate new insights. (R2, in contrast, considered the paper the first of its kind.)
- **Insufficient analysis of intermediate layers.** The study focuses on final layer embeddings, neglecting how graph properties evolve across layers. Probing deeper layers would provide a more complete understanding of feature transformation (R1). R2 also asks why certain layers perform differently (overlaps with this point).
- **Lack of thorough explanation for results.** The “findings” section emphasizes experimental settings over derived conclusions. Important questions remain unanswered: e.g., why some layers outperform others, why some substructures yield higher accuracy, whether these correlate with task labels, how the type of supervision (self‑supervised vs. supervised) influences structural encoding (R2, R4).
- **Missing key performance details.** The main paper does not report model performance on the primary task nor the correlation between task accuracy and probing accuracy; these are only in an extensive appendix, hampering self‑containment and assessment of practical relevance (R3, R4). R4 also notes that the \(R^2\) score’s mathematical definition is not clearly provided.
- **Weak presentation and organization.** Multiple reviewers criticize readability: abstract phrasing is confusing (R1); figures (especially Fig. 1, 2, 3) are poorly drawn, with unreadable text, unclear captions (e.g., meaning of \(x, x_2, x_3\) in Fig. 3 not explained); findings reference tables/figures absent from the main body; overall organization is confusing (R1, R3, R4).
- **Dataset representativity concerns.** The synthetic Grid‑House dataset may oversimplify real‑world graph structures, raising questions about generalizability to more complex, heterogeneous graphs (R1).
- **Limited GNN expressiveness covered.** Only 1‑WL‑equivalent GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN) are tested; the paper would benefit from including stronger models (e.g., 3‑WL GNNs, graph transformers) to better substantiate claims (R2).
- **Neglect of supervision signal.** The role of the supervision objective (supervised vs. self‑supervised) is not discussed, despite evidence from computer vision that supervision type affects structural information capture (R2).
