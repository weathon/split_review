## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes an iterative refinement of the reference policy in behavior-regularized offline RL. The method adds a KL regularization term between the current policy and a slowly updated target policy network to the TD3+BC objective, introduces an ensemble variant (CPI-RE), and provides a theoretical analysis for tabular settings. Two reviewers (R1, R2) rate the paper 3 and are critical; one reviewer (R3) rates it 6 and is more positive but still identifies a major flaw. All three agree the experiments are thorough and the idea is simple, but they raise overlapping concerns about clarity, novelty, the disconnect between theory and practice, and whether gains come from hyperparameter tuning rather than the proposed refinement.

### Strengths

- Comprehensive, high-quality experiments comparing against latest SOTA baselines on D4RL, clearly showing improvements over the TD3+BC backbone. (R1, R2, R3)
- Well-organized and well-written paper with good motivation (Figure 1) and theoretical analysis (Theorem 1). Section 3 effectively explains the iterative solution and connection with online learning. (R2, R3)
- The proposed method is simple and easy to implement, requiring only a few lines of code modification. (R3)
- The concept of iteratively refining the behavior policy is novel and promising (R2; though disputed by others as a weakness).

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and organization issues**: The paper’s flow is disjointed; readers must frequently navigate back and forth (e.g., Proposition 1 references an equation found in the appendix). The algorithm box is unclear regarding the reference policy in CPI-RE and the “cross-update scheme”, and the description is not self-contained. Figure 1's dashed line is not explained (R1 asks if it’s a BC trained solely on the curated dataset). (R1, confidence 5)
- **Limited originality/novelty**: The main contribution is adding a target policy network for KL regularization, a technique already used in TRPO and PPO; it is not a significant conceptual leap (R1). The idea of an iteratively refined policy constraint has already been introduced in a recent offline2online RL paper, which the paper itself cites as future work (R3). (Note: R2 found the concept novel, but overall novelty is disputed.)
- **Discrepancy between theory and practice**: The theoretical analysis assumes in-sample learning (actions drawn from the dataset), but the practical CPI implementation samples actions from the updated policy π_ω', violating the in-sample assumption. This undermines the theoretical guarantees and creates a disconnect between theory and algorithm. The paper should either reformulate the theory or modify the implementation to align. (R2, confidence 4)
- **Gains likely due to hyperparameter tuning, not the proposed iterative refinement**: CPI introduces an additional hyperparameter λ to adjust constraint strengths between π_D and \bar{π}, providing more precise conservatism adjustment than tuning τ alone. Tables 3 and 4 show hyperparameters thoroughly tuned per individual task. Table 5 shows that TD3+BC with a dynamically tuned α (careful conservatism strength sweep) is on par with CPI and CPI-RE, suggesting that careful tuning of TD3+BC alone could achieve similar results. (R3, confidence 3)
- **Narrow scope of testing**: The method is only evaluated on TD3+BC. To support universal benefits, it should be tested on other offline RL algorithms like IQL, CQL, and Diffusion-QL. (R1, confidence 5)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: ISPI introduces two hyperparameters (likely λ and the reference policy update frequency), which may be hard to tune in practice (R2). It is unclear whether CPI/CPI-RE can use one group of hyperparameters per task type (e.g., Gym-Mujoco, Antmaze, Adroit) as prior work like IQL and TD3+BC does. (R3)
- **Technical errors/typos**: Proposition 1 references equation (34) instead of (5) (R3). Equation (10) may contain an error: the term τ(1-λ)λ might be τ(1-λ) (R3). The algorithm box’s description of CPI-RE is ambiguous regarding the reference policy and the “cross-update scheme” (R1).
- **Citation error**: The claim that BCQ has the in-sample optimality equation is incorrect. (R2)

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more positive overall but still raises serious concerns about hyperparameter tuning. Reviewer 1 (score 3, confidence 5) and Reviewer 2 (score 3, confidence 4) are more critical, especially about clarity and the theory-practice mismatch.*
