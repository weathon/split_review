## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HadamRNN and Block-HadamRNN, a novel approach using Hadamard matrices to parameterize orthogonal RNNs (ORNNs) with binary or sparse ternary recurrent weights. This is the first method to achieve binarization or ternarization of vanilla RNN recurrent weights. The models are evaluated on four benchmarks: copy task (over 1000 timesteps), permuted MNIST, sequential MNIST, and IMDB. Despite binarization or sparse ternarization, they maintain performance comparable to full-precision models (ORNN, LSTM, FastGRNN). A block variant improves computational efficiency. All three reviewers rated the paper 6, indicating overall positive reception but with notable concerns. One reviewer (R3) initially expressed reservations but after rebuttal found concerns addressed and supported acceptance.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach**: All reviewers highlight the novelty of using Hadamard matrices to parameterize binary/sparse ternary orthogonal recurrent weights (R1, R2, R3). R3 specifically notes this effectively addresses instability issues in binarizing oRNNs.
- **Theoretical soundness**: R3 praises the solid theoretical foundation and consistency of the proposed method (supported by Section 3 and Appendix).
- **Clarity and organization**: R2 commends the paper’s logical flow and high clarity, especially in Sections 3 and the Appendix, which guide the reader through the formal approach with little ambiguity.
- **Promising performance**: R1 notes results in Table 2 show potential compared to ORNN, LSTM, and FastGRNN. R3 emphasizes empirical results on benchmarks (copy task, MNIST, IMDB) are comparable to full-precision models, and that HadamRNN is the first binary recurrent weight model to handle the copy task over 1000 timesteps.
- **Adaptability**: R2 points out the underlying principles have strong adaptability potential; the sparse ternary orthogonal recurrent weights could be applied across other domains with modifications.
- **Block version optimization**: R3 highlights the optimized block version of HadamRNN improves computational efficiency.
- **Post-rebuttal satisfaction (R3)**: After the rebuttal, R3 found concerns addressed and additional experiments supporting the claims, leading to support for acceptance.

### Weaknesses
- **High-level explanation of algorithms**: R1 finds the binarization/ternarization algorithms (Sections 3.3 and 3.4) explained only at a high level. A concrete example with matrix dimensions and a clear explanation of the Straight-Through Estimator’s (STE) role would improve transparency.
- **Readability of results tables**: R1 describes Tables 2 and 3 as tough to read and suggests converting some results into plots to make it easier for readers to assess contributions.
- **Unclear advantage of Hadamard approach**: R1 notes it is unclear where the advantage of using Hadamard Matrix Theory to parameterize binary orthogonal recurrent weights lies.
- **Missing edge device evaluation**: R1 states the paper does not analyze the method on any edge device; reporting overhead and speedup for HadamRNN and Block-HadamRNN would help assess the contribution.
- **Overly restrictive claim about binarized transformers (major concern, R2)**: R2 strongly criticizes the claim (lines 123-125) that binarized transformers are unsuitable for long-term dependencies on edge devices. The reviewer argues this is unsupported, as recent literature provides lightweight binary transformers and methods for long-term dependencies (citing examples [1-7]). The related work section overlooks these advancements.
- **Missing comparison with optimized transformer models (major concern, R2)**: The experimental section focuses solely on RNNs and lacks direct comparison with state-of-the-art binarized or compressed transformer models (especially those handling long-term dependencies). R2 considers this omission a significant weakness that makes it difficult to assess the true novelty and practical relevance of the proposed approach.
- **Limited real-world applicability (R3)**: The benchmarks are common but often toy datasets that do not translate well to real-world scenarios. The paper would benefit from a discussion of the limitations of oRNNs themselves (e.g., expressivity constraints on weight matrices) and how binarization interacts with these limitations.
- **Missing training time and stability analysis (R3)**: The authors mention FastGRNN which suggests oRNN training can be significantly slower. A comparison of training times for HadamRNN against baselines (full-precision and other binarized RNNs) and a discussion of training stability (sensitivity to learning rate, batch size) are needed.
- **Lack of diverse, real-world datasets (R3)**: The evaluation relies primarily on toy datasets. Including datasets like GLUE (e.g., SST-2, QQP) or SQUAD would demonstrate broader applicability, especially for tasks requiring longer-term dependencies.
- **Minor issues**: R2 notes several typos and English errors; R2 also points out that plots in the Appendix do not specify the X-axis and Y-axis.
