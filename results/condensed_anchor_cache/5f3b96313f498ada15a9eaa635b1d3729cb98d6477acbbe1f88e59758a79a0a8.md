- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies environmental generalization in RL, proposes a formal framework identifying a non-optimizable “adaption gap,” and introduces a non-parameterized randomization (NPR) method to augment training environments. The authors claim that NPR provides an optimizable lower bound for the gap and demonstrate empirical results on MuJoCo and CarRacing tasks.

### Strengths
- **Framework and formalization:** The structured framework for environmental generalization is novel (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). The modeling and theoretical analysis of the generalization error, splitting it into optimizable and non‑optimizable parts, is reasonable and could offer an interesting perspective if better explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and organization:** The paper is written clearly and well organized (Reviewer 1).
- **Motivation:** The motivation for the environmental generalization problem is intuitive and interesting (Reviewer 1).
- **Zero‑shot generalization:** The NPR method can address difficult zero‑shot generalization tasks (Reviewer 2). The experimental tasks designed for testing zero‑shot generalization are reasonable (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparisons / extra assumptions**  
  - The NPR method requires preconditions that baselines do not have: (a) the ability to change the environment during training, and (b) expert prior knowledge to label task‑relevant vs. task‑agnostic components. Both are difficult to fulfill in practice (Reviewer 2). Modifying the environment during training requires a simulator that allows such changes, introducing significant overhead (Reviewer 2).  
  - The comparison experiments are “meaningless” and “unfair” because the baselines (e.g., PPO, DRAC) are designed for fixed environments or simple parameter randomization and lack the capacity to handle the environment modifications that NPR uses (Reviewer 2).  
  - The empirical evaluation compares against methods that “all use fewer assumptions than NPR—they do not assume access to randomizing specific aspects of the environments” (Reviewer 1).  
  - Randomizing non‑parameterized task‑agnostic parts “may be even more difficult than the task‑relevant parts” and is not scalable to many real‑world applications (Reviewer 1).

- **Lack of novelty / insufficient related work**  
  - The NPR method “boils down to randomizing task‑agnostic parts of the environment to increase the diversity of the backgrounds trained on” – this is not a new or surprising conclusion. Recent works like RT‑2 (Google, 2023) have shown that increased background diversity yields zero‑shot generalization (Reviewer 1).  
  - The paper claims to be “the first to introduce a structured framework that uniformly describe the environmental generalization problem” and the “first to discuss and attempt to deal with this problem.” These claims are not true: Kirk et al. (“A Survey of Zero‑shot Generalisation in Deep Reinforcement Learning”) already provides a framework and overview. The paper also reinvents ProcGen (Cobbe) but does not cite it (Reviewer 3).  
  - Missing relevant older work: Konidaris and Barto (“Autonomous shaping”), Diuk et al. (“An Object‑oriented representation for efficient Reinforcement Learning”), Guestrin et al. (“Generalizing Plans to New Environments in Relational MDPs”), and Igl et al. (“Transient non‑stationarity and generalisation in deep reinforcement learning”) (Reviewer 3).  

- **Unclear definitions, readability, and overselling**  
  - The paper is “extremely challenging to read with many terms not being properly defined and phrasings being too vague” (Reviewer 3).  
  - The motivation oversells: the Introduction presents an extremely general “environmental generalization” problem, but later the paper introduces strong assumptions (action space shared, MDPs are goal‑conditioned, Lipschitz‑like assumptions on policies) without warning the reader (Reviewer 3).  
  - The definition of the “common part” is ambiguous: does it refer to the underlying task structure or shared environmental features? This lack of clarity hinders reproducibility and generalizability (Reviewer 2).  
  - Terms like “environmental generalization,” “intrinsically different,” and “task‑agnostic components” are not properly defined; examples are requested (Reviewer 3).

- **Theoretical / modeling weaknesses**  
  - In subsection 3.1 (“Difference with Previous Models”), both `I` and `ψ_t(I)` are defined as “similar parts of the state space” – this may be ambiguous (Reviewer 2).  
  - The MDP definition states that the MDP is induced by `I`, yet none of the MDP tuple components depend on `I`. More intuition and an example are needed (Reviewer 3).  
  - The claim that `I` “stays invariable and represents the common points of the same task in different environments” seems to be an additional assumption (Reviewer 3). What are these “common points”?  
  - Equation (1) and the “reversible function” need an example; the term “background of task” is unclear (Reviewer 3).  
  - Assumption 3.2 mentions “well‑learned policies” – are these optimal policies? Has the assumption been verified experimentally? (Reviewer 3).  
  - The discount factor is shared across environments – is this realistic? What are the consequences of this assumption (Reviewer 3)?  
  - The explanation “because compared with fixed environments, the unacceptable large variance in the dynamic learning process will disturb the gradient convergence direction” – what variance is intended? (Reviewer 3).

- **Experimental issues**  
  - In Table 3, NPR’s training results on 2d‑maze are not as good as No‑Rand, possibly indicating a flaw in the algorithm’s design (Reviewer 2).  
  - Figure 3 shows data that are too sparse, unlike a normal dense continuous training curve. More experimental details are needed. Furthermore, using only 3 seeds may produce unstable results; at least 5 seeds should be used (Reviewer 2).  
  - In subsection 5.3 (Ablation Study), the phrase “in the same environments” is ambiguous (Reviewer 2).  
  - The experiment comparing NPR to baselines is deemed “meaningless” because the baselines are not given the same opportunities (Reviewer 2; see also weakness on unfair comparisons).

- **Minor formatting issue**  
  - There are errors in the formatting of references cited in the Introduction (Reviewer 2).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 1, confidence 4) is significantly more critical than the other two (scores 3, confidence 3/4), particularly on readability, novelty, and missing related work. The above weaknesses reflect the full range of concerns, including this minority view.
