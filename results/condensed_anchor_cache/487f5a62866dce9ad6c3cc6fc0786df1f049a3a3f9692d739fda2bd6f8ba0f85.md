## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a fairness-aware regularization method that uses soft pseudo labels from an attribute classifier (instead of hard labels) and incorporates classifier uncertainty, for learning under partially annotated sensitive attributes. Experiments are conducted on Adult (tabular) and CelebA (image) datasets. Reviewers have mixed opinions on the contribution (ratings 3, 3, 5). One reviewer (rating 5) is more positive, highlighting the problem’s importance and the approach’s practical effectiveness, but still notes several shortcomings. The other two (rating 3) find the idea not sufficiently novel, the evaluation weak, and the presentation needing improvement.

### Strengths

- Addresses an important real-world problem: algorithmic fairness when only partially annotated sensitive attributes are available.
- Proposes a novel regularization using soft pseudo labels derived from attribute classifier output probabilities, a simple yet effective idea that differs from conventional hard-pseudo-label approaches.
- The algorithm is described in detail and logically clear.
- The method is evaluated on both tabular (Adult) and image (CelebA) data modalities, demonstrating applicability across data types.

### Weaknesses

1. **Novelty and related work shortcomings**:
   - Using a proxy model to predict sensitive attributes is not new; using it to obtain soft sensitive attributes has been explored in works such as [R1] (FAccT 2021) and [R2] (FAT 2019), though they focus on fairness evaluation rather than training. The paper’s related works section is brief and fails to clearly depict the limitations of prior approaches, nor does it adequately discuss whether the proposed method fundamentally advances beyond existing proxy-based methods.
   - The sensitive attributes predicted by proxy models are known to be biased (see [R3], ICML 2023), yet the paper does not sufficiently discuss the soundness of its approach under such bias.

2. **Insufficient evaluation**:
   - Only two datasets are used (Adult, CelebA), which are small and may not be convincing. Widely used fairness benchmarks (e.g., Colored-MNIST, UTKFACE, CelebA with isBlonde and HeavyMakeup targets as in Hong & Yang 2021, Barbano et al. 2022, Sarridis et al. 2023) are omitted.
   - Only one fairness metric (“Equalized Odds”) is considered, which is insufficient to fully assess fairness.
   - The number of comparison algorithms is small, and the evaluation protocol does not assess the overall accuracy–fairness trade-off (e.g., Figure 1 shows the proposed method is fairer but less accurate; it is unclear whether overall performance is higher).
   - The numbers in Table 1 are not convincing: for the “Smiling” task with only 5% labeled data, the method achieves 85% accuracy with an Equalized Odds of 0.05, raising questions about whether the proxy model can learn meaningful information from so few labels.
   - The effect of the hyperparameter λ on results is not analyzed.

3. **Insufficient analysis and missing details**:
   - Limited details are provided for the auxiliary models f_z and f_d, which are critical to the method. How can a model trained on only 5% of the data learn the desired information? Does the size of the entire dataset affect performance? Could training the auxiliary models on such limited data introduce other biases? These questions are not addressed.
   - The paper does not evaluate performance under different levels of bias in the training data.
   - The method is limited to binary classification and binary protected attributes; extension to multi-classification and multiclass protected attributes (e.g., race) is not discussed.
   - The paper emphasizes “partially” annotated sensitive attributes but does not give a specific definition of “partially.” The reasons why the soft regularization consistently yields lower bias across varying proportions of labeled/unlabeled samples are not analyzed.
   - Some references cited are outdated or not directly relevant to the proposed method.

4. **Presentation issues**:
   - The introduction is not well organized; the motivation of the paper is not clear.
   - There are several typos.
