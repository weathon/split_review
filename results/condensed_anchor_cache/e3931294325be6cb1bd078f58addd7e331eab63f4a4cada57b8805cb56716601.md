## Merged Review

### Summary
MCTBench is a large-scale (5.2k images, 8.5k QA pairs), human-annotated benchmark that evaluates the cognitive abilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in text-rich visual scenes through reasoning and content-creation tasks, while also including perception tasks for comparison. It uses GPT-4V for automated evaluation of open-ended content generation. Experiments on 18 VLMs reveal that their cognitive capabilities (reasoning and content creation) require improvement, and that text-enhanced VLMs may lose creativity in content-creation tasks.

### Strengths
- MCTBench is large-scale, human-annotated, diverse, and high-quality, providing 5.2k images and 8.5k question-answer pairs across perception, reasoning, and content-creation tasks.
- It fills an important gap by introducing content-creation tasks to evaluate cognitive abilities in text-rich scenes, broadening the scope beyond conventional OCR and current MLLM benchmarks.
- The benchmark distinguishes perception from cognitive tasks, helping identify specific areas needing improvement; the finding that larger models perform better in cognitive tasks provides useful guidance for future development.
- An automated evaluation pipeline using advanced MLLMs (GPT-4V) reduces the need for costly and subjective human assessments in content-creation tasks.
- Testing on 18 VLMs reveals that reasoning tasks still need improvement and that text-enhanced VLMs trained for different task types may lose some creative capabilities in content creation.

### Weaknesses
- Section 3.3 (Data Construction) is missing; the paper lacks details on data stratification, annotation, and preprocessing, hindering reproducibility. (R1 Q1, R3 Q1, R4 Q5)
- The division of cognitive abilities into only “reasoning” and “content creation” is not sufficiently justified. Finer-grained subcategories (e.g., logical reasoning, contextual understanding, cross-modal reasoning) are needed for a comprehensive evaluation, and the necessity/value of content creation as a cognitive component is under-explained. (R1 W2, R1 Q2, R2 W1, R3 Q3)
- The automated evaluation using GPT-4V has limited reliability: the Pearson correlation with human judgments is only 0.558, which is insufficient to fully validate the approach. Comparisons with prior evaluation methods are missing, and further validation across different generation tasks is required. (R1 W3, R1 Q3, R2 W3, R4 W2, R4 Q2)
- The claim that MLLMs need improved cognitive capabilities is not strongly supported by the results (cognitive scores are not substantially lower than perceptual scores). Moreover, perception and cognition are evaluated on different images, which is too rigid; evaluating them on the same images would better capture how MLLMs leverage perceptual understanding for reasoning. (R2 W2, R2 question)
- The content-creation task lacks sufficient explanation (e.g., prompts used are not detailed). The claim of a “broader evaluation of cognition” is only supported by a word cloud; fine-grained OCR subcategories and comparisons with existing benchmarks at that level are missing, making it unclear which aspects are novel. (R3 Q2, R3 Q3)
- Newer VLMs (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o, InternVL2, Qwen2-VL) are not tested, which is important given rapid model advancements. (R4 W1, R4 Q3)
- In Table 2, the performance difference between strong models like GPT-4V and weaker models like LLaVA1.5-13B is minimal on reasoning tasks; this unexpected result is not explained. (R4 Q1)
- The paper lacks insightful analyses, such as the impact of image resolution or different language decoders on content-creation results. (R4 W3, R4 Q4)
- Poor formatting: incomplete sections, citation errors, a figure placed in references, no appendix. (R3 W1, R3 Q1, R4 Q6)
- In Table 4, the perception score of 62.22 on “Image (text regions removed)” is surprisingly high, questioning whether some benchmark questions fully align with the intended text-rich focus. (R2 question)
