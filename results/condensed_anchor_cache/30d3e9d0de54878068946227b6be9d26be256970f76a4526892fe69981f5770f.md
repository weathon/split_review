## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PhysBench, a comprehensive benchmark with 100k interleaved video-image-text entries covering four physical-domains (object properties, relationships, scene understanding, dynamics) and 19 subclasses across 8 capability dimensions. Extensive experiments on 39 VLMs reveal that models struggle with physical world understanding despite excelling at common-sense reasoning. The authors also propose PhysAgent, a framework integrating specialist vision models (depth, segmentation, grounding) via a chain-of-thought protocol that yields significant performance gains (e.g., 18.4% on GPT-4o). Fine-tuning on PhysBench improves embodied manipulation tasks in simulation.

### Strengths
- **Scale and quality of the benchmark**: PhysBench is the most comprehensive physical reasoning benchmark to date (all four reviewers). Data sources are diverse (not mainly simulation-based), and thorough annotation and multi-round quality control processes enhance credibility (R3, R4).
- **Insight on how VLMs struggle**: The analysis in Fig. 4 reveals the gap between physical understanding and common VQA tasks, and the error-type analysis (perceptual errors, lack of knowledge) for commercial VLMs is valuable (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **PhysAgent framework**: Reuses existing foundation models (DepthAnything, SAM, DINO) and shows substantial improvement (e.g., 18.4% on GPT-4o) on the benchmark (R1).
- **Cross-task usefulness for embodied AI**: Fine-tuning on PhysBench significantly improves performance on downstream object manipulation tasks in simulation compared to zero-shot baselines, demonstrating the practical value of physical reasoning (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Extensive experiments**: 39 VLMs are evaluated, with detailed analysis of model, data, and framework scalability (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Test set contamination / out‑of‑distribution concern**: The test set of PhysBench is derived from similar images as the train set, so fine-tuning naturally yields improvement. It is unclear whether PhysAgent generalizes to out‑of‑distribution images labeled for physical understanding (R1).
- **PhysAgent design details are insufficient**: 
    - The base model for PhysAgent and how task‑specific prompts are learned (tuned vs. in‑context) are not specified (R2).
    - How depth, grounding, etc. are incorporated (e.g., JSON‑like format) and where the “knowledge memory” comes from is unclear; it is unknown whether PhysAgent can interpret such structured input without fine‑tuning (R2).
    - Whether chain‑of‑thought is used zero‑shot or taught with training examples, and how those examples are created, is not explained (R2).
    - The claim at L90 that PhysAgent “retains strong generalization abilities” and solves open‑ended problems is not supported by an explicit experiment (R1, R2).
- **Inconsistency in formatting and readability**: The paper is lengthy and has poor logical flow; task names are used inconsistently (e.g., Table 9a labels “Property/Spatial/Envir./Phe.” vs. the usual “Property/Relations/Scene/Dynamics”), tables 16‑20 have different styles, the last column of Table 20 is unclear, and the labels above Figure 4 are misaligned with ticks (R4). The appendix could be more concise (R1).
- **Correlation claim is insufficiently supported**: The correlation matrix in Figure 4a is confusing—the bottom‑right half (PhysBench splits) appears to show little correlation with other VLM benchmarks. The claim that PhysBench correlates with MMMU is not clearly tied to the figure (R2).
- **Missing discussion of related 3D spatial benchmarks**: The paper does not compare with existing 3D scene‑level VQA benchmarks such as SQA3D, OpenEQA, EmbodiedScan, MMScan, and MSQA, which directly evaluate 3D scene understanding through interleaved 2D/3D representations (R3).
- **Unfair comparison between zero‑shot and fine‑tuned models**: The comparison of zero‑shot (VLMs and PhysAgent) with fine‑tuned models on PhysBench is unfair because PhysBench may be relatively simplistic; the paper should either include more challenging tasks or explicitly note the limitations of this comparison (R3).
- **Missing comparison with VLA models**: The embodied manipulation experiments do not compare with state‑of‑the‑art Vision‑Language‑Action models (e.g., OpenVLA, Embodied‑COT, Octo) (R3).
- **Lack of detailed question design**: The paper mentions using both manual design and GPT‑4o for question generation from videos but does not provide question templates, the human design procedure, or the GPT‑4o generation pipeline (R4). It is also unclear whether reasoning steps are included in each question (R4).
- **Human evaluation and annotation details missing**: How the human evaluation in Table 3 is conducted, how many annotators are involved in each part, and the annotation procedure are insufficiently described (only GUI description is given) (R4).
- **Per‑task performance for 19 subclasses not shown**: Figure 9a does not provide the detailed performance on each of the 19 tasks for PhysAgent and baselines, making it hard to assess due to the unbalanced dataset (R4).
