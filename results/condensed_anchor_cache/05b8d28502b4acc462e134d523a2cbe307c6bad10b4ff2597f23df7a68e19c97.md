## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an input-adaptive method for allocating test-time computation in LM decoding. A model (MLP head + LoRA) predicts the distribution of rewards given a query and budget, then dynamically selects either the number of samples for reranking (adaptive best-of-\(k\)) or routes between a cheap and an expensive decoding procedure. Experiments cover code, math, and chat tasks, reporting up to 50% compute reduction or 10% quality improvement. The framework addresses both fully-observed (called “online”) and partially-observed (called “offline”) allocation settings.

### Strengths
- Addresses a timely and relevant problem: scaling test-time compute while managing cost.
- Formulates adaptive computation allocation in LM decoding comprehensively, covering binary reward, pairwise optimization in routing, and both online/offline designs.
- Empirical improvements are noticeable and span three representative subdomains: sampling (adaptive BoK), model-size routing, and decoding-method routing.
- The analysis in Figure 6 (allocation patterns across budgets) is intuitive and informative.
- The paper is clearly written and the approach is reasonable.
- Demonstrates generalizability by applying the method across three distinct domains (code, math, chat) with clear improvements over the standard best-of-\(k\) baseline.
- Provides insight via ablation of fully-observed vs. partially-observed allocations, noting the latter often performs better due to coarsening effects that hide prediction errors.

### Weaknesses
- **Generalizability of the difficulty model is insufficiently tested.** All evaluations are on the same distribution as training data (Reviewer 1). Selection of benchmarks is limited: a single dataset per domain, using less-common ones rather than widely adopted benchmarks such as HumanEval, MBPP, MATH, or GSM (Reviewers 3, 4). The choice of backbone LM varies across tasks rather than using a fixed model (e.g., Llama3-7b-Instruct), making it hard to assess method robustness and cross-domain generalization (Reviewer 1).
- **Baselines are weak and incomplete.** Comparison is limited to best-of-\(k\) with random or uniform allocation. Stronger efficient-decoding methods like Speculative Decoding or MCTS are not compared (Reviewers 1, 4). There are likely other reasonable intermediate approaches that could better demonstrate the method’s effectiveness.
- **The reward predictor is heavily relied upon but has critical issues.** Performance is sensitive to prediction errors, especially at the extremes of difficulty where success rates are very low (Reviewer 2). The correlation between predicted and actual utility in the moderate-difficulty region (Figure 3 middle) is poor, suggesting most efficiency gains come from correctly identifying unanswerable/always-correct queries rather than discriminating among moderately difficult ones (Reviewer 3). The predictor uses only the query; incorporating the initial model response \(y\) might be more informative and easier to train, but this is not explored (Reviewer 3).
- **Training overhead and data requirements are not discussed.** The cost, resource requirements, and task-specific dependence of training the difficulty estimator are omitted, which is crucial for practical deployment (Reviewer 3).
- **Naming of “online” vs “offline” allocation is misleading.** In the optimization literature, “online” refers to problems with incomplete future knowledge, which does not match the usage here (Reviewer 2).
- **Error propagation under sequential utility predictions is not analyzed.** The potential for compounding mispredictions in a multi-step allocation is unexplored (Reviewer 2).
- **Related work section is too concise.** It lacks comprehensiveness, especially in covering relevant adaptive-computation research beyond one recently published paper (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing clarifications and analyses.** The definition of \(N\) in Equation (10) is not given (Reviewer 1). The Spearman correlation in Figure 3 (b, middle column) is not reported (Reviewer 3). The computing budget metric is not clearly defined (e.g., is it based on inference calls?) (Reviewer 3). If a more complex decoding method like MCTS were used, retraining of the probe would likely be required, raising questions about probe generalizability to varying prompts or decoding methods (Reviewer 4).
