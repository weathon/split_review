## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a defense that trains a robust classifier using only clean images and standard training, without knowledge of attacks. It adds a hand-designed input layer that performs randomized feature squeezing: during training, the input is corrupted with dependent Gaussian noise and also convolved with a 3×3 kernel, ReLU applied, then inverted; the two branches are multiplied and passed through a sigmoid. The loss adds a term to keep the convolution output small. At inference, the convolution branch is dropped and sigmoid is replaced with a sign function. Experiments on CIFAR-10 (100 epochs) and ImageNet (50 epochs) claim robustness against AA attacks under ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞ norms, and also defend against One-Pixel Shortcut (unlearnable examples). One reviewer (Rating 8) finds the idea interesting and educational, but three reviewers (Ratings 3, 3, 5) have major concerns about obfuscated gradients, insufficient evaluation, lack of theoretical justification, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The topic of defending against adversarial examples using only clean training samples is meaningful and challenging (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The designed input layer can be easily integrated into different networks (WideResNet, ConvNeXt) (Reviewer 3).
- The paper provides intuition for design choices, discusses the “last move” strategy to avoid wrong robust accuracy, and clearly states limitations (Reviewer 2).
- The method shows less impact on unlearnable examples (One-Pixel Shortcut) (Reviewer 2).
- Results appear good and computational cost is low (100/50 epochs); the approach does not require prior knowledge of attackers (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The paper offers a new perspective on randomization in adversarial defense (Reviewer 2). One reviewer (Rating 8) finds it overall interesting and educational, while others are less convinced.

### Weaknesses
- **Obfuscated gradients and gradient landscape**  
  Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3) suspect the results are influenced by obfuscated gradients. The paper cites Athalye et al. only for the training phase but fails to address that the model itself may not be robust. The reliance on a non-differentiable sign function at inference and specific Gaussian noise injection raises concerns about gradient masking. A thorough analysis of the gradient landscape is missing (Reviewers 1, 2).

- **Lack of theoretical justification**  
  No explanation is provided for why randomized feature squeezing leads to robustness. The loss function does not highlight adversarial defense principles. Reviewers request theoretical analysis (e.g., modification of the loss landscape, Rademacher complexity is mentioned but not connected to mechanism) or empirical evidence showing how the squeezing operation affects internal representations and decision boundaries (Reviewers 3, 4).

- **Incomplete threat model and attack evaluation**  
  - The paper does not explicitly define a threat model; the noise bound that the training simulation covers is unclear. There is no analysis of how the Gaussian noise magnitude relates to attack budgets (e.g., what happens if attack noise budget exceeds 8/255 or 16/255?) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Attacks evaluated are limited to AA (standard or random version not specified), FAB-attack, and Square attack, which are considered outdated. The paper should include more recent ℓₚ-norm attacks from top conferences (e.g., AutoAttack is already used but need recent variants) and also black-box attacks such as SPSA, N-Attack, and one-pixel attack (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - The paper does not evaluate against attacks beyond ℓₚ norms, e.g., JPEG corruption, ReColorAdv, LPA, StAdv, FSA, GAN-based attacks, diffusion-model-based attacks, or attacks that manipulate semantic content (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  - Low-frequency attacks are not considered (Reviewer 2).  
  - The defense’s performance against masked perturbations (e.g., PGD only on a region) is not tested (Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient adaptive attacks**  
  Only EoT is considered. Adaptive attacks such as parametric bypass approximation (PBA) from LINAC are necessary. The use of BPDA with sigmoid is mentioned but not sufficiently analyzed; the approximation effectiveness and limitations are not discussed (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing comparisons with related attack-agnostic defenses**  
  The paper claims to be the only method not requiring prior knowledge of attacks with standard clean-image training, but BaRT (random transforms) and LINAC (implicit neural representation with secret key) achieve similar goals without adversarial training. These should be compared (Reviewer 2). Also, image purification/denoising methods like DiffPure and NRP, which also do not train the classifier, are omitted (Reviewer 4).

- **Parameter selection and ablation**  
  The selection of parameters for the input layer (Sections 4.1, 4.2) is not explained, and no ablation studies show how they affect performance. More ablation experiments are needed (Reviewer 4). The relation between the Gaussian noise simulation in training and the attack noise budget is unclear (Reviewer 2).

- **OPS (One-Pixel Shortcut) defense** lacks detailed analysis and explanation (Reviewer 4).  

- **Minor issue**: Missing full stop in Section 5.1.1 (Reviewer 2).
