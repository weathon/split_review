## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Selective Concept Bottleneck Models (UCBMs) that convert any pre-trained black-box model into a CBM without requiring predefined or human-annotated concepts. It uses unsupervised non-negative matrix factorization (inspired by CRAFT) to decompose feature vectors into concept vectors, applies an input-dependent alignment score selection to retain only a sparse subset of concepts, and learns a sparse linear layer for class prediction. Experiments are conducted on ImageNet, CUB, and Places365 using CNN architectures. The paper also demonstrates error correction by updating the sparse linear layer weights using a vision-language model.

### Strengths
- **Unsupervised concept discovery**: Unlike previous CBMs, UCBMs do not assume a predefined set of concepts; concepts are discovered automatically via non-negative matrix factorization (R2, R3). This is considered a novel contribution in the CBM field (R3). (R1 acknowledges it builds on known methods but includes it as a feature.)
- **Input-dependent sparsity mechanism**: The idea of an input-dependent concept selection constraint is interesting and non-trivial; the paper explores multiple candidate functions (R2). Empirical results show improved prediction accuracy with sparse activated concepts (R3).
- **Clear presentation and user study**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow; the human user-study design is well-documented and validates qualitative claims about discovered concepts (R1).
- **Code availability**: Code is provided to replicate results (R1).
- **Application for model editing**: The paper demonstrates how to correct model errors by updating the sparse linear layer using a vision-language model (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Several reviewers note that components of UCBM are largely based on existing work.
  * The assumption that features can be linearly decomposed into concepts using a sparse matrix is well-established (e.g., Zhou et al. ECCV, Grazini et al. TMLR) (R1).
  * The input-dependent concept selection resembles prior work using auxiliary branches or binary latent indicators to predict important concepts per input (Wang et al. CVPR, Sarkar et al. CVPR, Sinha et al. IJCAI, Panousis et al. ICCV 2023) (R1, R3). R1 states this “severely limits the novelty”; R2 notes novelty is limited to the input selection step; R3 states the idea is “not novel.”
  * The feature generation step uses CRAFT, an existing matrix decomposition method (R2).
- **Missing comparisons with relevant baselines**:
  * Comparisons needed with newer CBMs that do not require predefined concepts: LaBo (Language in a Bottle, CVPR 2023) which trains a learnable dictionary to approximate VLM embeddings, VLG-CBM (arXiv 2024), and concise/descriptive attribute methods (Yan et al. ICCV 2023) (R2).
  * Comparisons with earlier interpretable models like SENN (Alvarez-Melis et al. NeurIPS) (R1) and with the specific input selection method of Panousis et al. (R3).
- **Limited evaluation scope**:
  * Only three datasets (ImageNet, CUB, Places365) using CNN architectures; experiments on ViT-based architectures are missing (R1).
  * Should include additional datasets to demonstrate broad applicability: CIFAR-10/100, UCF-101 (actions), DTD (textures), HAM10000 (skin tumors) as done in LaBo (R2).
- **Interpretability concerns with discovered concepts**:
  * The concepts are represented visually, not textually. Textual concept representation is considered an important feature of CBMs for conciseness and user-friendliness; its absence limits novelty and practical utility (R3).
  * R1 questions whether discovered concepts **C** are truly interpretable: without pre-selected prototypes (unlike Post-hoc CBMs), interpretation is ambiguous and user-dependent; even feature activations can be effective if tuned (citing Fig 6 in [7]). This skepticism casts doubt on the “w/o concept selection” row in Table 1, which amounts to a black-box with sparsity regularization (R1).
  * No quantitative evaluation of the quality of descriptions assigned by VLMs (R2).
- **Questions about the score measure**: Using cosine similarity between activations and concept vectors as alignment scores is not well-justified. It is unclear what a high or low score implies (e.g., does a similarity of 1 mean the activation is interpretable?) and why this measure is used instead of learning the scores automatically (R1).
- **Missing analysis of CBM fragility**: CBMs are known to suffer from concept leakage; the paper does not investigate whether UCBM faces similar issues or provide any experiments in this direction (R1).
- **User study scalability and labeling**: In the user study, the most activating image crops for UCBM concepts may have been labeled by humans; this is not scalable. The paper should include experiments with (a) no text labels and (b) text labels generated by VLMs to assess dependency on human labeling (R3).
- **Missing quantitative evaluation of model editing**: The grid search for weighting factors (line 431) needs clarification; the paper should provide quantitative metrics such as edit success rate to validate the editing method (R3).
- **Title inconsistency**: The paper title uses “Selective” but this term does not appear in the main text; the acronym UCBM does not align with the title (R1).

*Note: Reviewer 1 (score 3) is more critical compared to Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 5), particularly regarding novelty and interpretability. The above weaknesses reflect the majority concerns while preserving the stronger criticisms from R1.*
