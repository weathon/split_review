## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to use task-specific adapters (parameter-efficient fine-tuning modules) in a neural network for incremental learning, aiming to balance stability and plasticity. The backbone is co-trained with adapters to learn invariant features while adapters capture task-specific details. The method is integrated with regularization-based approaches like EWC and LwF and evaluated on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet under task-incremental and class-incremental settings. The paper is generally well organized and easy to follow.

### Strengths
- The paper is well organized and easy to follow, with visualizations of results (Reviewer 1).
- Addresses a critical issue in incremental learning: the stability‑plasticity dilemma. Task‑specific adapters are a promising approach (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments are sufficiently done, including evaluation on ImageNet at higher resolution (224×224) and different task sizes (5, 10, 20 classes per task) (Reviewer 3).
- The approach includes two distinct components: a backbone for invariant features and adapters for task‑specific information. Also provides insights into compression with KD‑based incremental learning (Reviewer 4).
- Evaluation protocols cover both task‑incremental learning and class‑incremental learning (the latter being much harder). The authors analyze the impact of task order (coarse and iCaRL orderings) and run experiments with 10 random seeds for consistency (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work.**  
  The claimed novelty is weak. The idea of using adapters (or expansion‑based methods) for continual learning has been explored many times before, e.g., Random Path Selection (NeurIPS 2019), Side‑Tuning (ECCV 2020), TAMiL, DualNet, L2P, DualPrompt, InfLoRA, and others. The paper does not cite, discuss, or compare with these highly relevant works, and states “among the first to investigate the role of adapters in incremental learning” (line 085) which is not appropriate. One reviewer (score 1) considered the approach naive and lacking any real contribution beyond a small modification. Another reviewer (score 5) agreed novelty is lacking and requested comparisons with side‑tuning and random path selection.

- **Outdated baselines and insufficient comparisons.**  
  The only baselines used are EWC and LwF from 2017—far outdated for a practical component proposal. Recent incremental learning methods (especially other adapter‑based approaches) are missing. The paper claims “superior performance among …” (line 328‑330) which is debatable and unsupported by contemporary literature. The absence of a comparison with modern adaptation methods reduces the paper’s relevance.

- **Soundness of the co‑training design.**  
  It is unclear how adapters are co‑trained with the entire network without causing catastrophic forgetting in the backbone. The method appears to rely entirely on the base regularization method (e.g., LwF) to prevent forgetting, rather than being an independent continual learning strategy. The crucial choice of not freezing the backbone is not ablated—no comparison with frozen‑backbone adapter methods. Without such an ablation, it is hard to attribute any improvements to the co‑training design.

- **Unclear inference for class‑incremental learning (class‑IL).**  
  The paper does not explain how task‑IDs are inferred at test time when they are not given (as in class‑IL). Without correct task‑specific information, it is unclear how the correct adapter is selected. This undermines the feasibility of the method in the class‑IL setting where the authors claim applicability.

- **Inconsistent and often negative results.**  
  Adapter‑based methods frequently *worsen* performance compared to the base method. Examples: EWC and MAS in Task 2 (Table 1), LwF and LwM (Table 1), LWF‑A for every task after Task 6, and multiple cases in Figures 6, 7, 8 (middle plot). Improvements are not clear‑cut; the paper admits diminishing returns with more classes or harder task orders. On class‑IL, gains are marginal or negative, contradicting the claim of broader applicability. The method reaches only ~68% accuracy on CIFAR‑100 (10‑class increments), whereas other methods (cited in [1]) achieve ~78%. This performance gap limits generalizability.

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity.**  
  Table 3 shows high sensitivity of results to λ (only one λ reported per dataset) and to the rank of the adapter, with different rank sensitivities on CIFAR‑100 vs ImageNet (and possibly on different architectures). The rank hyperparameter itself exhibits considerable sensitivity to the dataset and architecture.

- **Architecture choices and parameter scaling.**  
  A deeper network (ResNet‑34) is used for CIFAR‑100 while a shallower one (ResNet‑18) for ImageNet, which is unusual and could affect comparisons. The paper does not characterize how many additional parameters adapters introduce per task or how model size grows with the number of tasks. The statement “When applied to large networks, the number of parameters introduced by adapters becomes negligible” (line 226‑227) is never demonstrated. Only up to 10 tasks are evaluated, which is small.

- **Missing analyses and presentation issues.**  
  - Figure 3 would benefit from showing the width on the x‑axis to visualize the optimal middle point.  
  - It is not discussed whether adapters must be added for every task or if conditions could trigger adaptive initialization only when needed.  
  - The paper does not show whether the method works on other architectures like Vision Transformers (it claims it should, but no evidence).  
  - Figure titles in Figures 5 and 8 are incorrect (refer to tasks instead of classes).  
  - Several typos and notation issues: “cosine similarities” → “cosine similarity”, missing definitions for `ℓ_t` and `Θ` in Eq. 1, “intput” (line 292), backwards double parentheses (line 469).  
  - The experimental results section begins with a hyperparameter sweep that would fit better as an ablation.  
  - Colors in Figs 4+5 are too similar and may be problematic for color‑blind readers.  
  - Table 1 would be more digestible if deltas from baselines were shown.  
  - Line 395‑6 suggests capacity issues but a larger adapter size is not tried.  
  - The paper exceeds the page limit without justification; it could be condensed to 9 pages.

- **Contrasting reviewer opinions.**  
  One reviewer (score 1, high confidence) strongly criticized the lack of literature search, calling the approach naive and the experimental design insufficient. Another reviewer (score 5) praised the writing and experiments but still noted missing comparisons and limited novelty. The majority (scores 3) agree that the baselines are outdated, comparisons are missing, and the improvements are inconsistent. The most positive reviewer still expects comparisons with side‑tuning and consideration of ViT architectures.
