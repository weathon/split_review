## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies negative transfer as a prevalent issue in Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) that existing methods—which focus on mitigating plasticity loss or promoting positive transfer—fail to address. To counteract negative transfer, authors propose Reset & Distill (R&D), which resets the online actor-critic networks upon each new task and distills knowledge from the online actor and prior expert policies into an offline model. Experiments on long sequences of Meta World tasks (and negative transfer analysis on DeepMind Control Suite and Atari) show R&D consistently outperforms prior CRL approaches in task success rates.

### Strengths
- **Addresses an important yet overlooked problem**: Negative transfer in CRL is rarely studied; prior work concentrated on preventing forgetting or encouraging positive transfer. The paper convincingly demonstrates that negative transfer is frequent and cannot be mitigated by these existing techniques (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Strong empirical evidence across diverse domains**: Negative transfer is demonstrated on three domains—Meta World, DeepMind Control Suite, and Atari—providing solid evidence of its prevalence (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Effective baseline**: R&D is simple, plausible, and consistently outperforms multiple recent CRL methods on success rates (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Comprehensive comparisons**: The paper compares R&D against a wide range of techniques aimed at promoting positive transfer, showing they cannot address negative transfer (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter ablation study**: Performed on Meta World to understand the impact of key R&D components (Reviewer 2).
- **Thorough literature review**: Well-structured positioning of the work relative to plasticity loss and negative transfer literature (Reviewer 4).
- **Solid experimental design**: Experiments on recognized RL benchmarks effectively test the hypothesis that task order impacts performance (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and exposition**: The paper is difficult to follow for readers not deeply familiar with the CRL literature; concepts like negative/positive transfer and baseline descriptions are too verbal. Improvements in presentation would benefit a general RL audience (Reviewer 1).
- **Figure 2 missing R&D**: Including R&D’s performance in Figure 2 would visually clarify its effectiveness alongside the negative transfer analysis (Reviewer 1).
- **R&D evaluated only on Meta World (main results)**: While negative transfer is analyzed on three domains, the main R&D experiments (including comparisons and ablations) are limited to Meta World. Performance on DM Control and Atari would strengthen the claims (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Forgetting trade-off**: R&D sometimes suffers more forgetting than other approaches, even while reducing negative transfer (Reviewer 2).
- **High expert rollout requirement with SAC**: When applied with SAC, R&D needs a very large number of expert rollouts to maintain good performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of theoretical depth**: There is no deep theoretical analysis explaining why R&D effectively mitigates negative transfer. Fundamental mechanisms—such as how periodic resets and distillation interact with learning dynamics—are not explored (Reviewer 3; echoed by Reviewer 4 as “contradictory hypothesis”).
- **Novelty concerns**: R&D builds on Progress & Compress (P&C); the differences (e.g., the “reset” operation) are not clearly explained or distinguished from P&C’s “adaptor.” More detailed comparison is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Contradictory assumption**: The paper argues that all prior knowledge should be erased when learning a new task, but retaining certain knowledge could be beneficial for similar tasks. This assumption may limit positive transfer (Reviewer 4).
- **Task order not addressed**: The paper identifies task arrival order as impactful on negative transfer, but R&D does not directly address this issue; it merely applies known techniques (distillation + resetting) (Reviewer 4).
- **Computational overhead**: The dual-network architecture with periodic resets and distillation may introduce significant computational cost. The paper does not discuss strategies to mitigate this for resource-constrained settings (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing benchmark standardization**: It is unclear whether the sequential Meta World evaluation is a standard CRL benchmark or customized. This affects generalizability assessment (Reviewer 1).
- **Baseline storage and budget**: It is not specified whether baselines store copies of policies and data, and how their budget compares to R&D’s storage of offline policies and data (Reviewer 1).
- **Why PPO more susceptible than SAC**: No explanation is given for why PPO exhibits more negative transfer than SAC; such analysis could guide more robust CRL design (Reviewer 4).
- **Relationship between negative and positive transfer**: The paper states that mitigating negative transfer does not imply facilitating positive transfer, but the reasoning is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential for curriculum learning**: Since task order affects negative transfer, could curriculum learning further enhance R&D? This is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
