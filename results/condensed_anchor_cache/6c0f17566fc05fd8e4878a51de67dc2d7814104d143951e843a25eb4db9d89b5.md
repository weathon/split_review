## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviewers collectively find the paper to be an ambitious exploration of the relationship between representation covariance spectra, manifold geometry, and adversarial robustness, bridging neuroscience-inspired local learning (Krotov-Hopfield model) with spectral and Jacobian regularization. Three reviewers rate it low (3–3–3) due to lack of a clear, impactful result, preliminary experiments, and poor organization; one reviewer gives a 6, noting the analysis provides useful information about KH robustness. All agree the topic is important but the paper does not yet deliver a compelling story. Major reservations center on the exploratory and premature nature of the analyses, the small scale (2-layer MLPs on CIFAR), insufficient connection to prior work, and difficulties in following the presentation. The paper itself acknowledges limitations (can’t reproduce Nassar et al., suboptimal choices), which further weakens conclusions.

### Strengths

- The detailed dissections of the models (KH, backprop with L2/Jacobian/spectral regularization) are applaudable. (R1)
- The latent geometry analysis linking manifold smoothness to decision boundaries is illuminating, especially the similarity between KH and L2/Jacobian regularization models. (R1)
- Figures are well made, clean, and easy to understand. (R1)
- Provides evidence that the 1/n power law spectrum is not necessary for robustness (though few data points). (R1)
- Understanding robustness of neural representations is an important problem. (R2)
- The attempt to leverage neuroscience insights (Stringer et al. 2019) to improve ML algorithms is praiseworthy. (R2)
- The experiments bridge lines of work in machine learning and neuroscience; the question of power-law spectra in biological and artificial networks is topical. (R3)
- One reviewer (R4) finds the study provides useful information on the robustness of the Krotov–Hopfield model specifically. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of a central, impactful result.** The paper is largely explorative data analysis in many directions; no clear key conclusion emerges. The connection between 1/n spectrum, geometry, decision boundary, and robustness is not woven into an overall story. (R1, R2, R3) 
- **Preliminary experiments on a small scale.** Results are based on 2-layer MLPs; generalization to deeper networks or CNNs is needed for broader impact. (R1, implicitly R2, R3)
- **Poor organization and unclear writing.** Method section jumps between method, results, and interpretations; many descriptions are messy. Better headings and streamlined organization are needed. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Misinterpretation of prior work (Stringer et al. 2019).** The paper states Stringer et al. *proved* a link between 1/n spectrum and robustness, but it was only a conjecture in the last paragraph. Stringer et al. also predict different power laws for different stimulus sets; it is unclear whether the current results are consistent. (R2)
- **Unclear scientific rigor.** Statements like “biological neurons adjust connectivity based on neighboring cell activity” lack evidence; the brain also has abundant long-range connections. (R2)
- **Lack of general theory; over-focus on KH model.** The study would be stronger with analysis of multiple unsupervised learning rules beyond KH, and more general theoretical understanding (R4). Reviewer 4 notes the paper has some analysis of multiple rules in Fig. 5 but little general theory.
- **Reproducibility and suboptimal choices.** The paper states it could not reproduce Nassar et al. (2020) and that suboptimal choices may explain weak results with spectral regularizers. This leaves more questions than answers, making results hard to interpret. (R3)
- **Insufficient connection to related literature.** Motivation with cancer diagnosis is too far; more relevant would be NeuroAI robustness comparisons (e.g., Geirhos et al. 2018/2021, Guo et al. 2022, Harrington et al. 2022, Feather et al. 2023). (R3)
- **Missing details and quantification.** 
  - Decision landscape analysis (Fig. 4) is qualitative and on one example; systematic population statistics are needed. (R1, R2, R3)
  - Fig. 4’s statements about “smoother” decision landscapes should be quantified to compare with adversarial robustness curves. (R3)
  - Non-normalized robustness plots should be shown because clean performance differs significantly. (R3)
  - Fig. 2 and Fig. 3: what do the different color shades represent? (R1, R3) A legend is needed. (R3)
  - Fig. 2: which points/scales are used for fitting the alpha value? How is fitting done (Stringer et al. ignore first/last eigenvalues)? (R3)
  - What is the point of the left panel of Fig. 1c? The pruned version mainly differs in the first few components, not the tail that concerns the scaling law. (R2)
- **Notation and clarity issues.**
  - Equation 9 (appendix): D is unclear. (R1)
  - Equation 10 (appendix): confusing/wrong, e.g., σ’(Wx) seems a vector; how is absolute value and L2 norm of W used? (R1)
  - λ denotes learning rate in eq. (4) but eigenvalue in Fig. 1c. (R4)
  - Matrix S (synaptic adjacency) not well defined; what is ||S||? (R4)
  - Two Krotov and Hopfield papers are cited but not distinguished by year. (R4)
  - “BNN” (used for models) not a common acronym; unclear if referring to biological systems or models. (R3)
  - “SHLP” undefined. (R1)
  - Missing parentheses around text citations. (R3)
  - Section 3.2 says Figure 3 shows KH layer spectrum, but that appears in Figure 2, not Figure 3. (R3)
- **Missing comparisons and ablations requested by reviewers.**
  - How do local learning rules other than KH behave? (R4)
  - The comment that covariance spectrum is global while Jacobian norm/adversarial properties are local (R1 citing [3]), suggesting controlling spectrum alone is insufficient for robustness – this point should be discussed.
  - Decision boundary analysis should reference previous work on Jacobian norms (e.g., [1] on volume elements, [2] on Jacobian eigenframe homogeneity). (R1)
