## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces GeoReg, a graph neural network-based deformable image registration method that abandons grid constraints by adopting a Lagrangian reference frame. Image features are modeled as nodes in a dynamically updating graph, avoiding intermediate resampling. A multi-resolution strategy progressively refines deformations. Experiments on synthetic brain deformations and three public benchmarks (brain MR, lung CT) show on-par or better results compared to several learning-based baselines. Reviewer scores are 3, 6, 5, 5, with one reviewer (score 6) particularly positive about the novelty and synthetic performance, while another (score 3) raises significant concerns about methodology soundness and comparisons.

### Strengths

1. Novel framing and motivation: The use of a Lagrangian framework and geometric deep learning to circumvent grid resampling artifacts is a fresh, innovative approach in medical image registration. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
2. Clear and easy-to-follow presentation. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
3. Strong performance on synthetic large deformations (rotation, scaling, translation) and low folding ratios reported – a strength highlighted by the most positive reviewer (score 6). The method handles large geometric shifts that challenge grid-based methods like VoxelMorph.
4. Multi-resolution coarse-to-fine deformation design reduces computational load at higher resolutions and requires only minimal adjustments per layer, improving efficiency and accuracy. (Reviewer 2)
5. Integration of attention as a data-driven interpolation scheme is a nice contribution. (Reviewer 3)
6. The model is less parameter‑intense, though memory footprint remains a concern. (Reviewer 3)
7. Experiments cover two common registration tasks (brain, lung) with relevant SOTA baselines. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses

1. **Methodology soundness and topological integrity**  
   - The paper does not explain how the graph structure inherently prevents self‑intersections, volume inversions, or folding/surface structure misconstruction – common issues in deformable registration. The well‑posedness of the learned graph is questionable. (Reviewer 1)  
   - There is a lack of explicit diffeomorphic constraints, leading to high folding ratios in real‑world results (e.g., brain and lung datasets). This is a significant limitation; the authors should discuss why the regularization is insufficient and how diffeomorphism could be achieved. (Reviewer 3)  
   *Disagreement: Reviewer 2 reports low folding ratios as a strength, whereas Reviewer 3 finds the number of foldings in real‑world results “rather high and concerning.”*

2. **Insufficient and outdated comparisons**  
   - Missing comparisons with traditional diffeomorphic methods (e.g., LDDMM, ANTs) and recent learning‑based approaches: SynthMorph, GradICON, LapIRNv2, FourierNet, and state‑of‑the‑art methods from the Learn2Reg 2024 challenge. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
   - Specifically, methods designed for large deformations (e.g., LapIRN, FourierNet) are not included, making it hard to assess the claimed advantage on large deformations. (Reviewer 4)

3. **Lack of discussion and comparison with point cloud / surface registration**  
   - The paper does not adequately relate GeoReg to the large body of point‑cloud and surface registration literature (e.g., Hansen et al.). It is unclear whether graph edges are fixed or dynamically updated and how this affects accuracy. (Reviewer 1)

4. **Ambiguity in experimental design**  
   - The distinction between “Ours (feat. warp)” and GeoReg in Table 1 is not explained (e.g., architecture or training differences, warping algorithm used). (Reviewer 2)

5. **Limited generalizability to multi‑modal and realistic datasets**  
   - The model is tested only on single‑organ datasets (brain, lung) with synthetic or relatively simple deformations. Multi‑modal registration (e.g., T1‑to‑T2) and datasets with multiple organs, sliding deformations, or organ‑internal non‑uniform deformations (e.g., Abdomen CT‑CT from Learn2Reg) are not evaluated. (Reviewers 2, 4)  
   - The Lagrangian framework’s handling of varying image intensities/contrasts in multi‑modal data is not discussed. (Reviewer 2)

6. **Missing analysis on deformation sensitivity and practical constraints**  
   - The effect of deformation magnitude (e.g., Brownian noise parameter) on performance is not studied; the paper does not show limits where the model begins to fail. (Reviewer 2)  
   - Memory footprint and training time compared to grid‑based methods are not reported, even though the method appears memory‑hungry due to dynamic neighborhoods. (Reviewers 2, 3)

7. **Applicability to truly sparse/irregular data not demonstrated**  
   - The Lagrangian frame is most beneficial for sparse or irregularly‑sampled input (e.g., point clouds, intra‑interventional problems), but the paper only applies it to dense grid‑like medical images, undermining the claimed advantages. (Reviewer 3)

8. **Minor issues**  
   - Typo in Table 2: “VoxelMorph” likely misspelled.  
   - Table 2, Brain CamCAN T1T2 column: bold font may be misplaced (should be on 2.95±1.16 rather than 2.98±0.89). (Reviewer 4)
