## Merged Review

### Summary
MM-SAM extends the Segment Anything Model (SAM) from single-modal RGB to multi-modal sensor data (e.g., depth, thermal, LiDAR, hyperspectral). It introduces two modules: Unsupervised Cross-Modal Transfer (UCMT) and Weakly-supervised Multi-Modal Fusion (WMMF). UCMT uses a modal-specific patch embedding and LoRA-based parameter-efficient tuning to adapt SAM’s image encoder to non-RGB modalities, with an embedding unification loss to align features across modalities. WMMF employs a selective fusion gate to adaptively combine multi-modal embeddings. Training relies on pseudo-labels generated from SAM’s own masks, enabling mask-free adaptation. Experiments on seven datasets show improved segmentation performance over SAM.

### Strengths
- Pioneer work: first to extend a visual foundation model (SAM) to multi-modal sensor suites.
- Simple and efficient design: UCMT and WMMF are technically straightforward and easy to follow.
- Label-free adaptation: training without manual mask annotations, reducing annotation cost (reviewers 2,3).
- Robustness and versatility: effective across diverse sensor modalities (RGB, depth, thermal, LiDAR, hyperspectral) and scene types.
- Shared latent space insight: the output latent space of SAM’s RGB encoder is effectively shared across modalities, enabling compatible multi-modal embeddings.
- Strong empirical results: consistent improvement over SAM on multiple benchmarks, with promising zero-shot potential.
- Multi-modality inference support: segmentation results from combined modalities are visually good (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Lack of novelty in individual techniques: LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) and selective gating (similar to Li et al. AAAI 2020, Chen et al. ECCV 2020) are well-established; the pseudo-label training is a typical unsupervised/weakly-supervised approach (reviewers 1,2,3). The paper does not detail differences from prior works (reviewer 1).
- Missing ablation study on the selective fusion gate (SFG): unclear impact of removing this module (reviewer 1).
- Missing training details: how is the prompt input obtained during training and inference? Is MM-SAM an all-in-one model or does each modality maintain an independent LoRA? Can it generalize directly to multi-modality input after training on single or paired modalities? What is the relation to ImageBind (reviewer 3)?
- Limited comparison to modality-specific segmentation methods: e.g., on SemanticKITTI, 2DPASS achieves 72.9 mIoU on single-modal LiDAR, while MM-SAM achieves 66.4; lack of comparisons to state-of-the-art methods for each modality (reviewer 2).
- Potential limitation of pseudo-label quality on uncommon data distributions (e.g., remote sensing): relying solely on SAM-generated pseudo-labels without manual annotation may constrain performance; additional experiments on such data are requested (reviewer 1).
- Methodology unclear on how single-modal mask predictions \(M_I\) and \(M_X\) are fused; details about the fusion algorithm are missing (reviewer 1).
