## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Swift-FedGNN, a federated GNN training framework that reduces communication and sampling overhead by performing primarily local training and periodic cross-client updates. It provides a theoretical convergence rate of O(1/√T) and reports experiments on OGBN and Reddit. Reviews are mixed (scores 3, 5, 5, 6). Reviewer 4 (score 3) is the most negative, citing limited novelty and unclear methodological details; Reviewer 3 (score 6) is the most positive, finding the theory convincing and experiments showing a clear edge. The other reviewers (1, 2) raise substantial concerns about missing comparisons, narrow experiments, privacy analysis, and theoretical rigor.

### Strengths
- Writing is good and clear; the paper is well-organized with rigorous definitions, assumptions, problem statement, algorithm descriptions, and experiment settings (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Related works are comprehensive and appropriately positioned (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical assumptions (e.g., bounded gradients/Lipschitz) are mild; derivations appear correct up to Lemma D.5 and likely beyond; the convergence rate matches the SOTA for sampling-based GNN methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The method achieves a clear empirical edge in wall-clock convergence (Figure 4) and outperforms comparable SOTA federated GNN approaches in efficiency (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The algorithm and analysis are novel for vertical FL on graph data, providing original convergence guarantees (Reviewer 2).
- The insights on the relationship between approximation errors and the structure of GNNs are insightful (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern:** Swift-FedGNN is described as a combination of FedGCN and [1]; the core idea of periodic updates is not novel. The paper does not clearly articulate the novelty beyond combining existing techniques; specific differences in client selection strategy and two-stage aggregation need highlighting and justification for why they lead to reduced costs (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 states that periodic updates alone are insufficient innovation.
- **Missing comparisons with recent and classic baselines:** The paper omits comparisons with FedGCN and FedSage+ (which may have lower communication cost due to one-time neighbor communication), FedAvg, FedSage, and other recent works (e.g., LLCG). Baselines appear 2–3 years old; the authors should include more recent methods to justify impact and ensure apples-to-apples comparison (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 noted a potential discrepancy in reported memory usage with LLCG (Table 2 vs. LLCG’s Table 1) that needs clarification.
- **Narrow experiments:** Only two datasets (OGBN and Reddit) are used. This raises doubts about generalizability. Authors should include additional datasets such as Flickr, OGB-Products, OGB-Arxiv, and others used in baselines (Reviewers 2, 3). Experiments on models other than GraphSAGE (e.g., GCN, GAT) are also missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation study and theoretical validation:** The paper does not ablate key hyperparameters \(K\) and \(I\) (affecting residual error) nor estimate the values of \(B^f, B^l, B^r\) as they relate to assumptions. These are needed to fully support theoretical outcomes (Reviewer 2). Additionally, there is no convergence plot versus iterations (only wall-clock time); the fast convergence in wall-clock may be due solely to reduced communication, not faster iteration convergence (Reviewer 4).
- **Inaccurate theoretical statement:** Theorem 5.6 shows convergence only to a neighborhood of the exact solution; claiming O(1/√T) convergence rate is misleading without clarifying that the residual error remains. The paper should compare its residual error with SOTA algorithms (e.g., Chen et al., 2018; Cong et al., 2021; Ramezani et al., 2022; Du & Wu, 2022) and prove Remark (3) (lines 448–449) (Reviewer 2).
- **Privacy analysis is insufficient:** Authors claim that communicating aggregated embeddings is more private than node features, but this claim is not rigorously defended. No discussion of whether node features, edges, or both are protected, nor how it links to real applications (e.g., hospitals). Homomorphic encryption was removed from FedGCN; simply sending aggregated results may still leak information without careful design. A detailed analysis of privacy risks and trade-offs between privacy and communication cost is needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Unclear methodological details:** The basis for neighbor sampling is not explained: how are nodes chosen and verified as true neighbors? How are cross-client neighbors verified between clients? The paper should clarify assumptions here (Reviewer 4). Additionally, the statement about why cross-client neighbors differ across mini-batches (line 182) is not fully justified—why can't cross-client neighbors be pre-computed for a sampled batch? (Reviewer 1, Q2).
- **Final test set performance not reported:** Figure 4 only shows validation loss over wall-clock time; final test accuracy, ROC-AUC, or F1 scores are not provided. A table comparing final test metrics across methods is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure readability:** Figures 4, 5, 6 are hard to read and require zooming to 200%; this disrupts flow (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issue:** A statement appears to be repeated twice (lines 104–109) (Reviewer 1).
