## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MaskFeat3D, a self-supervised pretraining method for point clouds. Instead of reconstructing masked point positions (as in Point-MAE), it recovers high-order geometric features (surface normals and surface variations) at masked points using an attention-based decoder that is independent of the encoder design. The method is evaluated on shape classification (ScanObjectNN), part segmentation (ShapeNetPart), and object detection (ScanNet, SUN-RGBD).

### Strengths
- Provides an alternative to point coordinate prediction in masked autoencoders for point clouds; fixing point coordinates avoids decoder design difficulties. (R1)
- Addresses the importance of geometric measurements (surface normal, surface variation) for 3D pretraining, supported by ablation studies. (R2)
- Extensive experiments with various backbone architectures, loss designs, and downstream tasks; achieves SOTA results. (R2, R3)
- Idea is interesting and results are promising. (R3 – one reviewer rated this paper 8, finding it more compelling than others)
- Evidence shows recovering high-order features is more effective than predicting point positions. (R4)
- Proposes a novel encoder-agnostic attention-based decoder. (R4)
- Paper is clearly written and well-organized. (R3, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Experiments are largely on relatively controlled datasets (ModelNet40, ShapeNet). While object detection on ScanNet/SUN-RGBD is included, additional results on challenging real-world tasks (e.g., semantic/instance segmentation on S3DIS) are needed to demonstrate generalization and avoid overfitting concerns. (R1, R2) One reviewer specifically asked why S3DIS semantic segmentation was not included. (R2)
- **Novelty concerns**: The approach may not be the first to recover high-order features in 3D masked signal modeling; prior work MaskSurpel (Zhang et al.) also recovers surface normals, diminishing the novelty. (R4)
- **Unfair ablation comparison (Table 4)**: The claim that disregarding point position recovery is essential is not fairly supported because the decoder architecture differs when comparing MaskFeat3D to the PointMAE baseline. A fair comparison would use the same decoder to predict point positions. (R3) The reviewer further questioned why predicting point positions still enhances performance in PointMAE if the claim is that it is inessential.
- **Missing baseline**: Results on ScanObjectNN show that Point-MA2E outperforms MaskFeat3D significantly, but this comparison is not included in the paper. (R4)
- **Insufficient analysis of 2D/3D MAE difference**: The paper’s discussion on why 3D MAE differs from 2D MAE (masked pixel location not predicted in 2D) could be deepened; given the sparse, irregular nature of point clouds, a clearer justification for extending MAE to 3D is needed. (R2)
- **Lack of geometric insight**: The work can be seen as a naive extension of Point-MAE that simply replaces the prediction target with geometric measurements; the authors do not provide geometric analysis explaining why this leads to improvement. (R2)
- **Real-world noise handling**: Computing surface normals and variation via PCA on noisy real-world scans (e.g., ScanNet) is problematic; the paper lacks discussion on how this issue is addressed. (R2)
- **Suggested additional decoder analysis**: To better assess the decoder, results should be presented where the decoder combines block features and masked queries into self-attention blocks of the same depth (as in MAE), using points as positional embeddings. (R4)
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (rating 8) was substantially more positive about the approach and results, while the other three (rating 6) expressed stronger reservations regarding novelty, evaluation scope, and fair comparisons.
