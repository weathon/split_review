## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Generalizable Neural Point Field (GPF), a novel paradigm for generalizable NeRF that shifts from image-based to point-based neural rendering. It explicitly models visibilities by combining geometric priors (depth from PatchmatchMVS) with neural features to guide feature fetching and handle occlusions. A nonuniform log sampling strategy, derived from point density, accelerates rendering and improves geometry. A learnable kernel spatially augmented with features is used for feature aggregation. Experiments on three datasets (DTU, BlendedMVS, NeRF Synthetic) demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in both generalization and fine-tuning settings.

### Strengths
- **Novel paradigm**: First generalizable NeRF based on point-based neural rendering (as opposed to image-based methods like MVSNeRF, NeuRay, GeoNeRF). Achieves state-of-the-art quantitative results.
- **Explicit visibility modeling**: Integrates geometric priors and neural features to explicitly handle occlusions during feature fetching, a novel contribution.
- **Nonuniform log sampling**: A sampling strategy based on point density that simultaneously improves reconstruction quality and rendering speed.
- **Learnable feature-augmented kernel**: A spatially augmented learnable kernel for feature aggregation that mitigates distortions in geometrically discontinuous regions.
- **Ablation study**: Proper evaluation of several components; many proposed modules are pluggable and could benefit other point-based rendering systems.
- **Solid quantitative improvements**: Consistent outperformance of image-based counterparts across multiple datasets in both generalization and fine-tuning settings.

### Weaknesses
- **Flawed visibility score (Eq4)**: The visibility score decays more quickly for points ahead of the depth (\(P_z < D_{i,xy}\)) than for points behind; e.g., for \(P_z = D_{i,xy} - \epsilon\), the score is \(\frac{D_{i,xy} - 2\epsilon}{D_{i,xy} - \epsilon}\) while for \(P_z = D_{i,xy} + \epsilon\) it is \(\frac{D_{i,xy}}{D_{i,xy} + \epsilon}\), meaning points in front actually get lower scores. The score can also become negative when \(P_z < 0.5 D_{i,xy}\), contradicting the claim that it is naturally bounded in [0,1].
- **Vague convergence speed metric (Table 2)**: The term “Convergence Speed” is not defined; no PSNR curve is shown. This makes the results in Table 2 difficult to interpret. A visualization of the test PSNR curve over training would be more convincing.
- **Lack of quantitative geometry/occlusion evaluation**: The paper claims “better geometry” and “occlusion awareness” but provides no quantitative metric (e.g., depth error) compared with existing methods like MVSNeRF and NeuRay. Depth error or other geometric metrics are needed.
- **Separation of low-level and high-level features not validated**: The paper separates features into low-level (color/edges) and high-level (semantic) but provides no ablation study justifying this separation. A single feature encoding might suffice; an ablation comparing strategies is necessary.
- **Qualitative regression on some baselines**: In Fig3, the ship’s head, sea, and durian table are better recovered by ENeRF. In Fig4, the ground dirt in BlendedMVS is overly smooth while ENeRF retains detail. The reason (e.g., dependency on the MVS point cloud) is not analyzed, and no explanation is given for these failures.
- **Sensitivity to initial point cloud**: The method depends on PatchmatchMVS for the initial point scaffold. No study investigates how the quality or density of the initial point cloud affects GPF performance, and whether the method is robust to varying point cloud quality.
- **Comparison with PointNeRF unclear**: The table under Figure 17 includes an entry “Ours” – it is unclear whether this is the fine-tuned model. If so, what are the results without fine-tuning? The paper states improvement is due to hierarchical fine-tuning, but does not report fine-tuning runtime or clarify whether pretraining of the generalizable NeRF is necessary. PointNeRF optimizes from scratch in ~40 minutes; GPF’s advantage appears limited to the fine-tuning strategy.
- **Log sampling is handcrafted and not a significant technical contribution**: The strategy is a manually designed sampling distribution around the surface; it is only applicable when a depth prior is available. The contribution is incremental.
- **Limited datasets and lack of scalability discussion**: Only three datasets are tested. The complexity of the approach (e.g., U-Net feature extraction, KNN query, visibility computation) may pose scalability and efficiency challenges that are not addressed. Compare rendering speed with ENeRF.
- **Missing comparison with Gaussian-splatting**: The recently introduced efficient Gaussian-splatting representation is not discussed or compared; the paper should clarify the advantages of GPF over it.
- **Related work incomplete**: The related work section on generalizable NeRF is not extensive enough; it should specifically discuss other point-based neural rendering methods (e.g., PointNeRF, 3D-GS). The claim that all generalizable NeRF methods are “image-based neural rendering” is inaccurate (e.g., fine-tuned MVSNeRF/GeoNeRF/NeRFusion aggregate multi-view information and do not require original images at inference).
- **Proofreading issues**: “Convergeuce” in Table 2; missing underline under “1.04s”; missing parentheses for \(\exp\) in Sec3.3 last paragraph; missing reference to Figure in Sec4.2’s fifth sentence; citation format needs fixing.
- **Minority reviewer note**: One reviewer (score 8) was more positive overall, but still raised several of the above concerns including vague convergence speed, lack of feature encoding ablation, and missing geometry metrics. The other three reviewers (score 6) were more critical, especially regarding the flawed visibility score, qualitative regressions, dependence on MVS points, and limited novelty of the log sampling.
