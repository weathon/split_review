## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PLNL for Complementary Label Learning (CLL), decomposing the inverse problem into Positive Label Guessing (PLG) for highly-confident instances and Negative Label Enhancement (NLE) for moderately-confident and under-confident instances, based on a confidence evaluation of model outputs. A unified framework views PLG and NLE as negative label recovery. Theoretical upper bounds for PLG and NLE error rates are provided, and experiments show strong improvements (e.g., on STL-10 from 34.96% to 55.25%). Reviewers agree the method is novel and well-organized, but raise concerns about the triviality of the bounds, limited dataset variety, missing ablations, and an unclear distinction from partial label learning. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, emphasizing the theoretical contribution and clarity; the other four (score 5) are more critical of the theoretical depth, incremental nature, and experimental scope.

### Strengths
- Novel decomposition of CLL into PLG and NLE subtasks, transforming the problem into an inverse problem on output space information. (R2, R3, R5; one reviewer called it a "novel solution" – R2)
- Comprehensive theoretical analysis including generalization error bound and upper bounds for PLG and NLE error rates, which enhances credibility. (R1, R2, R4; R1 particularly values the theoretical contribution)
- Extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvement over state-of-the-art CLL methods, e.g., on STL-10 accuracy increases from 34.96% to 55.25%. (R1, R3, R4; R4 notes "significant" empirical improvement)
- Paper is well-organized, clear, and easy to understand. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical bounds are too loose/trivial**: The upper bound in Eq.(21) ranges from 0 to 1, offering no practical insight; the bound in Eq.(22) is not easily estimated. Theorems 2 and 3 essentially state error rates ≤ 1, which is meaningless. Reviewers request tighter bounds or methods to estimate them, and a deeper discussion showing practical implications. (R1, R5; also R4 notes the bounds are not tight)
- **Insufficient and narrow experimental validation**:
  - Only one SOTA method from 2023/2024 is compared; more recent methods are needed to validate effectiveness. (R3)
  - Datasets used (Tables 1, 2) are relatively easy; missing challenging text datasets like 20 Newsgroups, and missing CIFAR100 results in Table 1. (R1, R3)
  - No results under biased complementary label learning settings or on manually annotated datasets (e.g., CLCIFAR10, CLCIFAR20) that assess instance-dependent CLL. (R4)
  - No standard deviations or confidence intervals reported in Tables 3 and 4; add deviation to assess statistical reliability. (R4)
  - Generalization ability across different data modalities and tasks remains unvalidated. (R2)
- **Parameter sensitivity and ablation shortcomings**:
  - Performance is sensitive to k-NN parameter k and confidence threshold λ; detailed tuning needed. (R2)
  - The separation of moderately-confident vs. under-confident instances is not ablated – compare treating them the same versus separately to evaluate the design's contribution. (R4)
  - Ablation study shows weak-strong data augmentation is more effective than the proposed confidence-based instance selection strategy, questioning the core contribution. (R3)
- **Motivation and relation to partial label learning unclear**:
  - The paper claims CL can involve multiple class labels the instance does not belong to (lines 48, 121), which conflates CLL with partial label learning (PLL). The fundamental difference is not clarified, and the method does not address inherent PLL ambiguity. (R5)
  - Advantages over existing CLL and PLL algorithms are not clearly motivated; conditions for success/failure are not provided. (R5)
  - Contribution is incremental, relying on a commonly used confidence-based instance selection strategy. (R3)
- **Computational cost**: k-NN computation may incur high cost on large-scale datasets, limiting applicability in resource-constrained environments. (R2)
- **Specific concerns and questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Assumption 1 (variety of negative labels in k-NN instances is large enough) is considered too strict. (R4)
  - Figure 2(b): PLG precision decreases over training while NLE precision stays stable – needs explanation. (R1)
  - Why report the POCR method in Table 4? (R1)
  - Derivation of Eq.(30), especially the first inequality, should be detailed. (R1)
  - Is there a difference between supervised and complementary learning losses for high-confident instances (e.g., gradient perspective)? (R4)
  - The negative label set size τ_i in Eq.(15) is fixed; if top-τ_i and the complementary label overlap largely in multiple complementary label settings, the benefit of the complementary label sharing mechanism is weakened. (R4)
- **Typos**: In Eq.(32), "B" is missing; line 134: "show" should be "shown"; line 169: "p" should be "f"; line 216: "are" should be "is"; Figures 2(b),(c): "Precison" → "Precision". (R1)
