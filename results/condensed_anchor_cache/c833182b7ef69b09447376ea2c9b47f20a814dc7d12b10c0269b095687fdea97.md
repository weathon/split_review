## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes CLIPure, a purification-based defense for adversarially robust zero-shot classification using CLIP. It introduces two variants: CLIPure-Diff, which purifies in CLIP’s latent space via a diffusion prior, and CLIPure-Cos, which uses cosine similarity to a blank template without a generative model. Experiments show large gains over prior SOTA across multiple datasets with modest inference overhead. All six reviewers rated the paper 6 (accept), but several raised concerns about baselines, evaluation scope, ablation, and presentation clarity. One reviewer (R3) was notably more critical, questioning the motivation, theoretical justification, and missing experiments.

### Strengths

- **Novel purification in CLIP’s multi-modal latent space.** The paper is the first to perform adversarial purification in latent space rather than pixel space (R1, R2, R5, R6). The analysis shows that this yields larger KL divergence between adversarial and benign distributions compared to pixel-space and uni-modal latent-space methods (R4).
- **CLIPure-Cos achieves defense without generative models.** This variant avoids external diffusion models, substantially improving inference efficiency (R1, R4, R5). R1 notes it is “much more efficient regarding inference cost.”
- **Strong empirical performance.** CLIPure significantly outperforms prior SOTA: e.g., from 71.7% to 91.1% on CIFAR-10, 59.6% to 72.6% on ImageNet, and 108% relative improvement on 13 datasets (R3, R4, R6). R3 calls it “good performance” and R6 says it “exceeds the comparison methods.”
- **Well-motivated by KL divergence formulation.** The paper derives a purification risk lower bound that inspires latent-space purification (R2). R2 finds the motivation “well motivated.”
- **Comprehensive experiments.** The evaluation spans multiple datasets and includes detailed analysis (R2, R6). R2 calls them “comprehensive.”
- **Clear figures with bright colors** (R3) and overall reasonable organization and good writing (R6).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing important baselines and comparisons.** R1 points out that “[1] Choi et al., Adversarial Robustification via Text-to-Image Diffusion Models, ECCV 2024” is missing as a comparison (vs. TeCoA). R4 recommends comparing with “Understanding zero-shot adversarial robustness for large-scale models” (ICLR 2023) and “Pre-trained Model Guided Fine-Tuning for Zero-Shot Adversarial Robustness” (CVPR 2024). R5 notes that baseline methods are not clearly specified (pre-trained vs. trained by authors) and that base generative models should be unified; specifically, a comparison to DiffPure based on DALLE-2 is needed. R4 also notes the adversarial training baseline in Table 2 defends attacks on CLIP text functionality, not zero-shot defense.
- **Ablation studies are missing.** R3 directly asks: “Where is the ablation study?” R4 and R3 ask for ablation on the number of purification steps (10 steps chosen, but R4 asks for experimental evidence and whether this is optimal). R2 requests an ablation on different VLMs to see if gains generalize beyond CLIP (e.g., models with different attention mechanisms or pretraining datasets). R3 and R4 note missing analysis under different threat levels (ϵ values). R4 questions how the 80 description templates were selected and whether an automated selection is possible. R6 asks for more hyperparameter details.
- **Motivation is verbose and not sufficiently strong.** R3 says the motivation and contribution are “too verbose” (e.g., the phrase “by matching an image with text prompts ‘a photo of <class-name>’” in the abstract should be removed). R5 states: “The motivation is not strong enough. It seems that the key motivation is simply that performing purification in the latent space of CLIP is promising… However, there are only a few discussions about why it is necessary to perform purification methods for CLIP in its latent space and what issues exist with previous methods for CLIP.”
- **Theoretical analysis is unclear.** R3 finds the theoretical analysis (purification risk, SDE) “difficult to understand” and asks: “how can you extend it to diffusion model?” R6 notes that $p_{ben}(X)$ is not mentioned in Eq. (3) and asks why the KL divergence in Eq. (6) can excel at detecting out-of-distribution adversarial examples, requiring further explanation.
- **Evaluation is limited in model diversity.** R2 points out that ImageNet results are only on WideResNet-50 and suggests Transformer-based models (e.g., from “Diffusion Models for Adversarial Purification”) for a more comprehensive assessment. R3 similarly notes only one model is tested. R5 calls for unification of base models across purification comparisons.
- **Efficiency concerns and missing FARE comparison.** R3 observes that CLIPure-Diff increases inference time significantly (Table 3) and that the efficiency comparison misses the result of FARE.
- **Lack of a method illustration figure.** R3 states: “Lack a figure to illustrate the process of the method.” R6 suggests “Providing more visual results about the Purification process like Figure 5 would be better.”
- **Incorrect definition of zero-shot learning.** R3 notes the paper’s definition is not correct: “In the original paper of CLIP, it has clearly claimed they changed the definition of ZSL from class-level to dataset-level.”
- **No detailed analysis under different threat levels (ϵ).** R4 asks for defensive effectiveness under different ϵ values with detailed analysis.
- **Missing training/implementation details.** R6 requests more training and testing details and hyperparameter settings.
