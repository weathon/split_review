## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents two contributions for visually-rich document retrieval: (1) ViDoRe, a benchmark covering multiple domains, languages, and page-level retrieval tasks; (2) ColPali, a model that uses Vision Language Models (VLMs, specifically PaliGemma-3B) with a late interaction matching mechanism (inspired by ColBERT) to produce multi-vector embeddings directly from document page images. The main claim is that ColPali outperforms existing document retrieval pipelines (including those using Unstructured + OCR/captioning) while being faster and end-to-end trainable. Reviewers agree that the problem is important and the benchmark is a useful contribution, but they have mixed assessments on novelty, experimental rigor, and presentation. Scores range from 3 (high confidence, missing details and limited evaluation) to 8 (lower confidence, sees strong innovation). One reviewer (Reviewer 4) is notably more critical, pointing out missing model details, no experiments on public datasets beyond ViDoRe, and unclear retrieval candidate space.

### Strengths
1. **ViDoRe benchmark fills a gap** – Covers an important gap in evaluating document retrieval systems, especially those emphasizing visual information (Reviewer 1, also Reviewer 2: multiple domains and languages, Reviewer 3: good contribution for page-level retrieval with wide range domains).
2. **ColPali significantly outperforms existing pipelines** – Even compared to methods that require fine-tuning, ColPali is superior (Reviewer 1: see Table 2; Reviewer 2: outperforms existing methods in several visually complex tasks; Reviewer 3: effectiveness and efficiency demonstrated; Reviewer 4: superior on ViDoRe and significantly faster).
3. **Extensive ablation studies** – Justify key design decisions (Reviewer 1; also noted by Reviewer 3 as clearly presented).
4. **End-to-end trainable** – Simplifies optimization for the retrieval task (Reviewer 4).
5. **Open-source release** – All resources (models, data, code) released under open licenses, fostering community research (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
6. **Multilingual capabilities** – ColPali shows generalization to non-English languages (Reviewer 4).
7. **Hyperparameter tuning avoided direct ViDoRe contamination** – Tuning was done on a 2% split from training set, not on ViDoRe itself (Reviewer 1, though notes training set issues).

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing model details for reproducibility** – The paper lacks essential definitions: N_q, N_d, and how the document representation is generated (e.g., from [BOS], [EOS], or average of features). These are vital for replicating the study (Reviewer 4). Additionally, the iterative building from SigLIP/ColBERT/PaliGemma is not well detailed; readers unfamiliar with those methods may struggle to understand Section 5.1 (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 3: needs more explanations and analyses to prove novelty).
2. **Training data is too in-distribution with ViDoRe** – The training set (120k query-page pairs) has 63% of its samples from the same academic distributions used in ViDoRe. Although no overlapping queries/pages, this may bias both hyperparameter tuning and final performance. The authors should present results using only the synthetic training data to isolate architecture vs. fine-tuning contributions (Reviewer 1).
3. **Evaluation limited to ViDoRe** – No experiments on publicly available datasets, such as the retrieval task in VLM2Vec or page prediction in MPdocVQA. Testing on those would strengthen experimental results (Reviewer 4). A separate reviewer (Reviewer 2) also notes that generality and adaptability in broader scenarios need further validation.
4. **Lack of latency/throughput quantitative results** – The paper emphasizes efficiency for industrial applications, but does not provide detailed quantitative latency figures for different models/systems (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 echoes that speed comparisons are needed.
5. **Benchmark description incomplete** – The manuscript should include sample pages from ViDoRe to showcase diversity in modalities, domains, and languages. The retrieval candidate space should also be clearly defined (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 also notes that ViDoRe is composed mainly of existing academic VQA datasets plus some collected PDFs, but the exact composition could be better explained.
6. **Questions about capabilities (page-level retrieval within a PDF, text retrieval)** – Reviewers ask whether ColPali supports page-level retrieval within a PDF (e.g., DUDE, MPdocVQA) and whether it can perform conventional text-based retrieval or is limited to visual features. These are relevant for understanding the method's scope (Reviewer 4).
7. **Novelty concerns** – The pipeline is largely inspired by ColBERT and PaliGemma; the paper does not sufficiently explain its novelty beyond combining existing components (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 agrees more detail needed).
