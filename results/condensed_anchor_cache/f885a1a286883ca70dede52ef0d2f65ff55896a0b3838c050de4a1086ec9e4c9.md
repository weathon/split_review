## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SD3, an unsupervised RL method that maximizes skill density deviation (inter-skill state diversity) via a conditional autoencoder with soft modularization and uses a KL-based intrinsic reward for intra-skill exploration. Extensive experiments on state- and pixel-based URLB demonstrate diverse skills and downstream task performance.

### Strengths
1. **Clear writing and presentation.** The paper is well structured and easy to follow.
2. **Novel skill-density perspective of mutual information.** Reformulating MI as a soft version using the skill density ratio \(p(s|z)\) is an interesting contribution.
3. **Innovative use of soft modularization.** Introducing a routing-network-based CVAE to estimate skill-conditioned state densities is novel and addresses a challenging density estimation problem.
4. **Solid theoretical analysis.** Detailed proofs accompany the objectives, and the connection to count-based exploration in tabular MDPs is provided.
5. **Extensive experiments.** The method is evaluated on both state- and pixel-based URLB against multiple baselines, with additional ablation studies and visualizations (e.g., maze trajectories).

### Weaknesses
1. **Evaluation protocol and metric concerns.**
   - URLB’s downstream evaluation (pre-train DDPG with intrinsic reward, then fine-tune with extrinsic reward) is questionable because the value function learned during pre-training mismatches the fine-tuning reward distribution, and both policy and critic are largely re-learned. “Random selection” or “regress-meta” do not convincingly isolate skill discovery quality [R1].
   - A better metric would be using a high-level controller with **frozen skills** (as in [1]) or measuring total state coverage [R1].
   - The paper does not clarify how baselines with both continuous and discrete skill spaces were configured, how skill dimensionality was chosen, or whether fine-tuning was over all skills or a single best \(z^*\) (e.g., METRA supports zero-shot \(z^*\)) [R3, R4].
   - Results in Table 2 and 3 are partially highlighted, making interpretation unclear [R1].

2. **Missing strong baselines and unfair comparisons.**
   - Recent SOTA methods such as MOSS (NeurIPS’22), EUCLID (ICLR’22), CeSD (ICML’24), and BeCL (ICML’23) are not compared in state-based URLB [R2]. CeSD and BeCL are also omitted in the maze environment (Figure 6), where existing results show they outperform CIC [R2, R3].
   - The MI-based baselines do not use soft modularization, while SD3 does. SD3’s performance **without** the soft modularization CVAE should be reported in the main experiments to enable fair comparison [R1].
   - In the maze (Figure 2/6), only CIC is shown as a baseline; including CeSD and BeCL would provide a more complete picture [R2, R3]. SD3’s gains over CIC are marginal, and CIC is a weaker baseline than the missing methods [R2].

3. **Intra-skill exploration reward is insufficiently justified for continuous spaces.**
   - The exploration bonus \(r_z^{\text{exp}} = D_{\text{KL}}[Q(h|s,z) \,\|\, r(h)]\) (with a standard Gaussian prior) can be expressed analytically, but its connection to state novelty or surprise is unclear. Established methods use pseudo-counts \(\approx 1/p(s)\) or \(-\log p(s)\); \(r_z^{\text{exp}}\) does not exhibit similar properties in continuous domains [R2].
   - Theorem 3.2 only addresses tabular MDPs, while all experiments are continuous. The components \(\mu^T\mu\), \(\operatorname{tr}(\sigma)\), and \(-\log|\sigma|\) are not analyzed for their role in encouraging visitation of novel states [R2].
   - Because the CVAE is trained jointly with RL, the KL may plateau as the encoder learns to reconstruct frequent states, reducing the incentive to explore frontier regions and potentially leading to local optima [R3].
   - There is a typo on Line 238: \(D_{\text{KL}}[Q(h|s,z) \,\|\, P(h)]\) should be the upper bound of \(I(S;H|Z) = D_{\text{KL}}[Q(h|s,z) \,\|\, P(h|z)]\) [R2].

4. **Lack of details about the routing-based CVAE.**
   - The number of layers, activation functions, and the specific routing mechanism within the CVAE are not described [R2]. The number of modules \(m\) is not reported in the main text or appendix, and no ablation on \(m\) is provided [R1].
   - The visualization of soft modularization in Figure 1-a could be more intuitive [R3].

5. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and theoretical grounding.**
   - The motivation for introducing \(\lambda\) in \(I_{\text{SD3}}(S;Z)\) is unclear and lacks theoretical grounding. The claim that “increasing \(\lambda\) weakens the gradient and prevents skill collapse” is not rigorously justified; gradient analysis and a precise definition of “skill collapse” are needed [R2].
   - Softmax temperature (Figure 10a) appears to have a large impact on performance, yet the default choice (close to 1) is not justified, and sensitivity to \(\lambda\) is not discussed [R1, R2].
   - The algorithm is limited to discrete skill spaces due to the soft modularization architecture, which restricts the richness of learned behaviors compared to methods that use continuous skill manifolds [R3].

6. **Visualization and interpretability gaps.**
   - Only maze examples are shown; visualizations of learned skill trajectories or state distributions in more complex environments (both state- and pixel-based) are missing [R1, R4].
   - For state-based observations, it is unclear whether different skills activate distinct sub-networks or share similar representations; visualizing network activations or skill-specific trajectories would help [R4].
   - The left side of Figure 2 shows skill discovery for pixel-based tasks, but the corresponding state-based visualizations are not provided [R4].

7. **Robustness experiments lack rigor.**
   - The claim of robustness is not well supported. The exploration bonus \(r_z^{\text{exp}}\) is not obviously related to pseudo-counts in continuous spaces, undermining the premise for robustness [R2].
   - Injecting noise only during pre-training and measuring downstream task performance does not reflect policy robustness; a better design would inject noise during fine-tuning (adversarial setting) [R2].
   - A parameter count comparison between SD3 and CIC is needed, as robustness may be affected by model size [R2].
   - It is unclear whether robustness and ablation experiments use state- or pixel-based environments [R4].

8. **Network size and computational overhead.**
   - SD3’s use of soft modularization likely requires larger networks than baseline methods. Experiments with matched parameter counts for baselines are not provided, and the computational cost (training time, memory) of the routing network is not discussed [R4].

9. **Other missing details.**
   - Why is PPO used for maze visualization (Figure 2) while DDPG is used for main URLB results? [R1]
   - Line 210: “of length \(l+1\)” should likely be “of layer \(l+1\)” or “of shape \(m \times m\)”; softmax temperature is not mentioned in Line 212 [R1].
   - In robustness experiments, is noise added to states or to transitions (states and actions)? [R1]
   - The discrete skill space size is a hyperparameter; it is unclear whether all baselines use the same number of skills [R4].
