## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents MolGen, a 700M-parameter BART-based language model pretrained on over 100 million SELFIES strings, with a two-stage pretraining (syntax learning + domain-agnostic prefix tuning) and a self-feedback fine-tuning paradigm to align generation with chemical properties. Reviewers are split: two (R2, R4) rate it 8, praising clear writing, strong benchmark performance, and insightful discussions; two (R1, R3) rate it 6, raising major concerns about concept misuse, missing ablations, insufficient baselines, and unclear figures. R3 considers the paper not suitable for publication in its current form due to conceptual misalignment. All reviewers agree the paper is well-written and illustrated, but disagree on the validity of the core contributions and experimental completeness.

### Strengths
- **Clear writing and rich illustrations** – The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and supported by good figures and tables (R1, R2, R4).
- **Use of SELFIES and reconstruction of 100M molecules** – MolGen internalizes structural and grammatical insights; SELFIES is more concise than SMILES and ensures 100% validity in generated molecules (R1, R2, R4).
- **Discussion of “Molecular Hallucinations”** – The paper introduces and attempts to address this concept, contributing to the field’s understanding (R1). The self-feedback paradigm is a plausible alignment method (R3, R4).
- **Strong benchmark performance** – Significant improvements in distribution learning, targeted molecule discovery, and constrained optimization on penalized logP, QED, and docking scores (R2, R3). Baselines are shown to be outperformed, though some reviewers question their recency (R4).
- **Simple and plausible pretraining method** – The two-stage pretraining approach is straightforward and effective (R3). The domain-agnostic prefix tuning is designed for knowledge transfer across diverse domains (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Concept misuse: “Molecular Hallucination”** – The term is misaligned with the NLP definition (generation of nonexistent entities). MolGen’s “hallucinated” molecules are chemically valid, so the term is misleading. Related works on hallucination are missing (R3). (Minority: R1 and R2 found the discussion beneficial; R1 notes the paper “attempts to address it”.)
- **Concept misuse: “Self-feedback”** – The fine-tuning method depends on an external property model, making “external-feedback” a more accurate name. The feedback signal is not intrinsic to the model (R3).
- **Missing ablations**  
  – No direct ablation comparing SELFIES vs SMILES on the same BART architecture (only a comparison between Chemformer and MolGen). This is critical to justify the choice of representation (R1).  
  – No ablation on the second pretraining phase (domain-agnostic prefix tuning): its contribution to downstream tasks is unclear (R1, R3).  
  – No comparison against other parameter-efficient tuning methods (e.g., LoRA) (R3).  
  – No ablation on the “soft” label smoothing (Eq. 8) and hyperparameter α; the effect of different α values is not shown (R4).  
  – No ablation on the impact of the prefix vectors themselves on model performance (R4).  
- **Incomplete baseline comparisons** – Most baselines are pre-2022 (JT-VAE 2018, GCPN 2018, GraphAF 2020, GraphDF 2021). Missing comparisons with recent transformer-based models and SOTA sequence generation methods. The current baselines do not represent the state-of-the-art (R4).
- **Unclear figures and notation**  
  – In Figure 7 (right subfigures): x-axis is not labeled (R3).  
  – In Figure 8 (right subfigures): x-axis and y-axis are not labeled (R3).  
  – Appendix Figure 2: x-axis not labeled (R3).  
  – Figure 8 and Appendix Figure 5: correspondence between attention scores and molecular structures is not intuitive; does not convincingly show SMILES-based PLM focusing on less relevant positions (R1).  
  – Figures 2 and 3 have opposite locations (typo) (R4).  
  – Masking/start tokens: “[MASK]”/“[S]” in text vs “[Mask]”/“[s]” in Figure 3 – inconsistency (R4).  
- **Model description clarity** – The meaning of “l” is ambiguous: does it denote the total number of SELFIES or the prefix vector length? The two tunable prefix vectors (keys and values) are not explained: how they are initialized, how they interact with attention, why two separate vectors are needed, and proof of improvement (R4).  
- **Dubious claims**  
  – Claim that previous works “rely heavily on task-specific tuning” is undermined because MolGen also requires fine-tuning (R3).  
  – Claim that “almost all previous studies … neglected natural products” lacks literature support (R3).  
  – Section 2.2 describes a substructure counteraction problem but does not explain how the proposed method solves it (R3).  
  – Claim about “brittleness of SMILES” leading to high invalidity lacks a citation (R3).  
- **Limited evaluation scope** – Only three properties (penalized logP, QED, binding affinity for two proteins). More diverse property metrics would strengthen the model’s applicability and robustness (R1).  
- **Open questions that reflect missing analysis**  
  – What potential limitations does the self-feedback mechanism introduce? (R2).  
  – Is the synthetic dataset in Section 3.1 ZINC250K? Using the explicit name would improve clarity (R3).  
  – In the sentence before Eq. (1), should “SMILES” be “SELFIES”? (R4).  
  – How are \((S_i, S_j)\) pairs in \(\mathbf{S}^{*}\) selected? (R4).  
- **Naming inconsistency** – “MolGen” and “MOLGEN” are used interchangeably; tables use “ours” instead of “MOLGEN” (R4).
