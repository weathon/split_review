## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MMIE, a large-scale benchmark (20K queries) for evaluating interleaved multimodal comprehension and generation in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). It spans 12 fields (including math, coding, physics, literature, health, arts) and supports interleaved text and multi-image inputs and outputs in both multiple-choice and open-ended formats. The authors also propose an automated scoring model fine-tuned with human-annotated data. Experiments on eight LVLMs reveal significant room for improvement; the benchmark and code are publicly released.

### Strengths

- **Novel and comprehensive benchmark** for interleaved multimodal comprehension and generation, an underexplored but increasingly important area. (R1, R2)
- **Large scale (~20K)** with detailed categorization (project-based learning, situational analysis, multi-step reasoning) and rigorous dataset curation and quality control. (R1, R2)
- **Automated evaluation metric** using a finetuned LLM that captures multiple facets (image-text alignment, image quality, text quality) and is more reliable/unbiased than using a raw GPT model; comparison with human annotations highlights its strength. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Detailed experiments**, including the creation of integrated LLMs (combining state-of-the-art LLMs with text-to-image generation models) and an error analysis (Sec 5) identifying typical error types. (R1)
- **Benchmark design** supports interleaved multi-image inputs and outputs alongside multiple-choice and open-ended formats, capturing complexities of real-world multimodal interactions. (R3)
- **Public release** of the benchmark and the finetuned scoring model. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing details on scoring‑model training data and human annotation process.** The paper does not describe the construction of the dataset used to finetune the scoring model (finetuning method, dataset composition). The claim (L110) that the scoring model is “comparable to human evaluation” is insufficiently justified: further details on how human annotations for Table 5 were obtained are needed. (R1)
- **Potential biases in the scoring model.** Reliance on a LLM may introduce biases (e.g., preference for verbosity, writing style, sensitivity to minor variations). The paper should analyze these biases across domains/tasks and examine the impact of the training data on evaluation outcomes. (R2)
- **Content warning lacking transparency.** The paper includes a content warning but does not elaborate on the specific types of content flagged or the measures taken to ensure ethical soundness; this raises concerns about dataset creation transparency. (R2)
- **Lack of fine‑grained results across fields.** The evaluation results are not fully broken down by the 12 fields, making it difficult to analyze performance gaps and derive targeted improvements for specific models and domains. (R3)
- **Potential overfitting of the scoring model to MMIE‑specific tasks and response styles.** Because the scoring model is finetuned on data from MMIE’s 12 domains, its generalizability to other benchmarks (e.g., MMLU with 30 domains) is unclear and should be tested. (R3)
- **Practicality of full‑set validation.** With 20K samples, validating the entire benchmark is costly; the authors should consider designating a smaller “mini” subset (e.g., 800 samples) for quicker evaluation. (R2)
- **Role of visual content in some examples.** Several examples in Figure 1 use images for illustration only, not as a necessary input for the task. The paper should analyze how often the visual content is critical vs. ancillary, and compare text‑only generation with generation that includes images. (R2)
- **Qualitative examples of scoring model responses** corresponding to figures in the appendix (Fig. 7, 9–12) would better illustrate the metric’s behavior. (R1)
