## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ULoRA-MoE, a fine-tuning approach for time-series foundation models (GPT4TS, UniTS) in anomaly detection. It integrates LoRA and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with a Gumbel-Softmax router to sample multiple reconstructions, and uses a negative log-likelihood (NLL) anomaly score to handle contamination in fine-tuning data. Experiments on 8 real-world benchmarks compare against traditional (IForest, LOF, HBOS), classical, and deep learning baselines, using adjusted F1 score.

### Strengths

- **Comprehensive experimental setup**: Extensive comparisons across 8 real-world datasets and a broad set of baselines, including non-learning (e.g., IForest, LOF, HBOS), classical, and deep learning methods. The use of adjusted F1 (instead of point-adjust) is appreciated. [Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 5]
- **Innovative uncertainty mechanism**: The combination of MoE LoRA with Gumbel-Softmax gates to generate multiple reconstruction samples and an NLL-based anomaly score is a clever idea for capturing epistemic uncertainty. Ablation against LoRA-MoE is convincing. [Reviewer 2, 3]
- **Clear motivation and structure**: The paper is well-structured, with a clear explanation of each component (MoE, LoRA, Gumbel-Softmax, NLL score). [Reviewer 2]
- **Performance improvements**: The reported gains over existing fine-tuning methods and baselines are large. [Reviewer 3]
- **Addressing anomaly contamination**: The paper explicitly considers the impact of anomalies in fine-tuning data and proposes a mechanism to mitigate it. [Reviewer 4, 5]

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: Much of the methodology follows standard approaches (LoRA, MoE, Gumbel-Softmax) without substantial novel component. The paper directly combines existing techniques; no new algorithm or theoretical insight is offered. *Contrast: Reviewer 2 found the routing mechanism innovative, but Reviewers 4 and 5 (score 1) considered the contribution very limited.* [Reviewer 2, 4, 5]
- **Claims insufficiently supported**:
  - **Boundaries of normal/abnormal patterns**: The paper claims each MoE expert learns different types of anomalies and boundaries, but provides no evidence or visualization. Reviewers question what these boundaries are, how they are traced, and how MoE/LoRA relate to them. [Reviewer 1, 4, 5]
  - **Epistemic uncertainty quantification**: The NLL score with 5 samples is claimed to quantify epistemic uncertainty, but no confidence bands, distribution analysis, or deeper investigation is provided. The mechanism mainly penalizes high-uncertainty predictions; alternative distributions or confidence-interval approaches are not explored. [Reviewer 2, 4]
  - **Contamination mitigation**: The claim that training data contamination harms performance is not directly validated. The effect of contamination percentage is untested; SWaT is known to be uncontaminated yet still shows improvement. Reviewers request an ablation with varying contamination levels (e.g., on Epilespy/DSADS following Xu et al.) and estimated contamination per dataset. [Reviewer 1, 3, 5]
- **Missing ablations and analyses**:
  - Number of LoRA experts (fixed at 5) – no ablation on varying this number. [Reviewer 1, 3]
  - Hyperparameter analysis (e.g., rank of LoRA expert, number of generated samples). [Reviewer 1, 3, 4]
  - Effect of contamination in fine-tuning data: systematic study with synthetic or real datasets at different anomaly percentages. [Reviewer 1, 3]
  - Efficiency analysis: MoE and multiple reconstructions increase complexity; no comparison of time/space tradeoff with baselines. [Reviewer 5]
  - Threshold selection for other methods unclear; low results for many baselines raise concerns (e.g., random predictions would give ~0.66 affiliation F1). [Reviewer 3]
- **Limited foundation models tested**: Only GPT4TS and UniTS are fine-tuned. Important missing models: Lag-Llama, TimesFM, TimesGPT, Moment, Timer, which are widely used. This limits generalizability claims. [Reviewer 2, 4]
- **Missing comparisons and references**:
  - HypAD and BAE (related to anomaly detection with uncertainty). [Reviewer 1]
  - Probabilistic time-series models such as CSDI and Diffusion-TS (probability-based reconstruction). [Reviewer 5]
  - Known dataset issues for PSM, SMAP, SMD, SWaT (see Wu & Keogh 2021, TimeSeAD by Wagner et al. 2023) not discussed. [Reviewer 3]
- **Code unreleased and reproducibility concerns**:
  - No code provided. [Reviewer 3, 4, 5]
  - Discrepancy with baseline D3R results: original D3R reports much higher F1 (e.g., PSM 0.7609, SMD 0.8682, SWaT 0.7812) than in this paper, calling the reliability of reported numbers into question. [Reviewer 5]
  - Precomputed anomaly scores for all methods/datasets should be provided for threshold and metric verification. [Reviewer 3]
- **Writing and presentation issues**:
  - Poorly written in parts, lacking intuitions and justifications (e.g., why MoE/LoRA solve the challenges, unclear symbol definitions). [Reviewer 4]
  - Typos: “It incorporate ...”, “boundries”; line 88: $X$ should be $\hat{X}$. [Reviewer 3, 5]
  - Title differs between PDF and submission page. [Reviewer 5]
  - Acronyms undefined: PLoRA-MoE (line 197). [Reviewer 3]
- **Equation 6 confusion**: $x$ appears as a single value rather than a vector; likelihood for Gaussian distribution is not justified for multi-feature time series with only 5 samples; variance-covariance estimation unclear for high-dimensional data (e.g., SWaT). [Reviewer 3]
- **Anomaly score only applied at inference**: The NLL score cannot address contamination during fine-tuning; the model is still trained on potentially anomalous data. [Reviewer 4]
- **Metric and benchmark selection**: Additional metrics (PR-AUC, ROC-AUC) and broader benchmarks (e.g., UCR) are recommended. Computation of overall F1 for SMD (multiple sub-datasets) not explained. [Reviewer 1, 3, 5]
- **Error bars missing**: Figure 2 lacks error bars. [Reviewer 1]
- **Adjusted AUCPR not shown**: Requested by Reviewer 1.
