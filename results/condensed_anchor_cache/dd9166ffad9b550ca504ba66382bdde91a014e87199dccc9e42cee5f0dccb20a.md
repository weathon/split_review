## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a quantum algorithm for (approximate) \(D^2\)-sampling, a key component of \(k\)-means++, and a dequantized (quantum-inspired) classical implementation (QI-\(k\)-means++). The quantum algorithm runs in \(\tilde{O}(\zeta^2 k^2)\) time (where \(\zeta\) is the aspect ratio) and preserves the \(O(\log k)\) approximation guarantee of classical \(k\)-means++. The dequantized version runs in \(O(Nd) + \tilde{O}(\zeta^2 k^2 d)\) time after setting up a sample-query access data structure. The authors also provide a quantum approximation scheme for the \(k\)-means problem with polylogarithmic dependence on \(N\). Experiments on large datasets with bounded aspect ratio compare QI-\(k\)-means++ against classical \(k\)-means++. The results are incomparable with prior work, asymptotically improving on existing algorithms only when \(\zeta\) and \(k\) are sufficiently small.

### Strengths
- **Novel algorithms for a fundamental ML problem**: The paper gives new quantum and classical algorithms for \(k\)-means, which is widely used in machine learning (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Extends quantum-inspired techniques**: It connects Tang’s sample-query access model to the classical \(k\)-means problem, providing novel theoretical insights (Reviewer 3).
- **Clear presentation and experimental validation**: The problem formulation, motivations, related work, and algorithm design are clearly stated. Experiments verify performance on large datasets (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Interesting trade-offs in runtime**: The algorithm achieves better dependence on \(N\) than classical \(k\)-means++ at the cost of worse dependence on \(\zeta\) and \(k\); this is a good contribution (Reviewer 3).
- **First quantum approximation scheme for \(k\)-means** with polylogarithmic \(N\)-dependence (Reviewer 1, from abstract).
- **Broad applicability**: The work will interest the ICLR audience and the broader ML and data science community (Reviewers 2, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Aspect ratio dependency is a major concern** – The algorithm has quadratic dependence on \(\zeta\) (\(\tilde{O}(\zeta^2 k^2)\)). Reviewers 2 and 3 question whether it will be useful in practice, as large \(\zeta\) (e.g., from well-separated clusters or very close point pairs) degrades performance counterintuitively. Reviewer 2 asks for justification of why this dependence is needed, whether a lower bound exists, and a more thorough comparison with Cohen-Addad et al. (which achieves \(\log \zeta\) dependence at the cost of an \(N^{\theta(1)}\) factor). Reviewer 3 suggests the counterintuitive behavior might indicate the bound can be tightened. All three main reviewers (1, 2, 3) note the algorithm is only better in narrow parameter regimes (small \(\zeta\) and \(k\)).
- **Preprocessing cost omitted in comparisons** – The \(O(Nd)\) term for setting up the sample-query data structure is ignored when comparing to classical algorithms. Adding it makes both new and classical methods linear in \(N\). Reviewer 3 suggests experiments should show runtime *after preprocessing* to better reflect the claimed advantage.
- **Related work discussion is unclear and incomplete** – Reviewer 2 requests a table of relevant prior quantum/classical algorithms, their runtimes, approximation guarantees, and key parameters (e.g., aspect ratio, condition numbers). Reviewer 4 asks for expanded connections to computational complexity.
- **Missing background and notation explanations** – Reviewer 4 asks for: an introduction to quantum computing and Dirac bra-ket notation; elaboration on dequantization with examples; justification of why states (1)–(4) are correct in the quantum \(D^2\)-sampling algorithm; and clarification of the term “pseudo approximate” (lines 412–417).
- **Conclusion and Future Work omitted** – The paper ends abruptly without a concluding section (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor issues** – Tilde notation is used incorrectly (hiding logarithmic factors in parameters not shown, e.g., Theorem 1) (Reviewer 1). Line 94 should say “result” instead of “observation” (Reviewer 1). The abstract contains too much low-level detail (Reviewer 4).

**Note on disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8) found the paper significant and well-presented, with only requests for clarifications, while the other three reviewers (score 6) emphasized the limitations due to aspect ratio dependency and the omission of preprocessing cost. These latter concerns are the core weaknesses of the paper.
