## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LLM-Boost, a method that fuses large language models (LLMs) with gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) for tabular classification. The approach uses LLM logits per row as predictions, then has the GBDT learn residuals to the true labels. The authors claim that LLM-Boost outperforms standalone LLMs and GBDTs on a range of dataset sizes, with the largest gains at intermediate sizes, while matching each at extremes. Experiments are conducted on multiple tabular datasets, with Flan-T5 as the primary LLM and XGBoost as the GBDT.

### Strengths

- **Enhanced performance:** LLM-Boost shows superior classification performance compared to traditional GBDTs and other ensemble methods (Reviewer 1). However, some reviewers note that at maximum training set sizes the improvement is very small or absent (Reviewer 4, Table 3 in Appendix). (Contrast: Reviewer 1 scores 5 and emphasizes gains; Reviewer 4 scores 3 and highlights limited improvement at large sizes.)
- **Model agnostic:** The boosting mechanism can be applied to other high-performing tabular architectures beyond LLMs (e.g., TabPFN) (Reviewer 1).
- **Efficient training after pre‑computation:** Once LLM outputs are pre‑computed, training cost aligns with standard GBDT training (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty:** Combining LLMs with GBDTs in such a simple manner is a valuable contribution (Reviewers 2, 3). The method is intuitive – replacing the first tree in a GBDT with an LLM prediction (Reviewer 4).
- **Motivation:** Injecting linguistic context into tabular models is an important research direction (Reviewer 4).
- **Extensive experiments:** The authors conduct experiments on a variety of datasets (Reviewer 4). A thorough discussion of limitations and future work is appreciated (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Dependence on interpretable column headers:** The method requires meaningful text descriptors and may require prompt engineering; performance could degrade significantly if headers are encoded or anonymized (Reviewer 1). This point is echoed in a question about semantically ambiguous or incorrect headers (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational overhead:**
  - **Pre‑computation cost for large datasets:** The initial LLM inference can become a bottleneck; a detailed analysis of time and resources for different dataset sizes and LLM models is missing (Reviewer 1).
  - **Prediction‑time cost:** LLM inference is required for every prediction, introducing significant latency and cost vs. standalone GBDTs; the paper lacks a clear trade‑off analysis between accuracy gains and computational cost (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited practical alternative comparison:** An alternative, arguably simpler use of LLMs is automatic feature engineering followed by GBDT training on augmented features (Reviewer 3) – this baseline is not compared.
- **Missing baselines and experiments:**
  - The major baseline “Select” mixes LLM and GBDT results in a confusing way; it would be clearer to show standalone LLM and GBDT baselines separately (Reviewer 2).
  - No comparison against simply increasing the number of trees in the GBDT (Reviewer 3).
  - Hyperparameter tuning procedure for GBDT vs. LLM‑Boost is unclear; it is not shown whether the same tuning is applied to both (Reviewer 3).
  - Lacks error analysis and case studies showing when the method excels (Reviewer 2).
  - Scaling to datasets with larger feature sets (where LLM context window becomes a bottleneck) is not evaluated (Reviewer 3).
  - Performance on very large datasets (e.g., millions of samples) is not demonstrated (Reviewers 1, 4; Table 3 in appendix shows that at maximum size LLM‑Boost is nearly identical to XGBoost).
  - No experiments with closed‑source / commercial LLMs such as GPT‑4 (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - Missing comparisons to prior tabular representation learning methods: Tabbie and HYTREL are not cited or compared (Reviewer 1).
- **Choice of LLM:**
  - The selection of Flan‑T5 for the main experiments is not convincingly justified (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - Llama‑3‑8B performed generally worse when used in LLM‑Boost, raising concerns about robustness across LLMs (Reviewer 4). The rationale for choosing Flan‑T5 over other models is not explained (Reviewer 2’s question).
- **Scope and clarity:**
  - The claim that the method is strongest at “intermediate dataset sizes” is vague and needs a clear definition (Reviewer 4).
  - Visualizations for the main results are too difficult to interpret (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor issues:**
  - Typos: line 201 “weather” → “whether” (Reviewer 1); Section 3.2 “int he” → “in the” (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing citations for Tabbie and HYTREL (Reviewer 1).
