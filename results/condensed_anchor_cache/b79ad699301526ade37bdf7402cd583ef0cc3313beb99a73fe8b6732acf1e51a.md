## Merged Review

### Summary
DocGenome is a large-scale multimodal academic document understanding dataset constructed by annotating 500K scientific documents from 153 disciplines in the arXiv open-access community, using an automatic pipeline that parses LaTeX sources. The dataset provides structured data with 13 layout attributes and 6 logical relationships between entities, and supports seven tasks: document classification, visual grounding, layout detection, document transformation, open-ended single-page QA and multi-page QA. The test set comprises 1,004 sampled papers with QA pairs generated by GPT-4V and verified by human faculty members. The authors also provide a training set and conduct experiments showing that training on DocGenome improves performance on downstream tasks relative to selected baselines. The dataset and pipeline are intended to be released as a resource for the community.

### Strengths
- **Large-scale and comprehensive:** The dataset is superior to existing collections on multiple aspects (Table 1). It is the first dataset to structure data from all modalities (13 layout attributes plus LaTeX source codes) and covers a wide diversity of disciplines (153) and tasks. The collection fills a scarcity of such resources (Reviewer 4).
- **Carefully constructed and documented:** The automatic labeling pipeline and quality control process (dual-file validation) are described clearly and pragmatically. The provided GitHub repository includes documentation and download links, supporting reproducibility and accessibility (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Training set provided and verified effective:** The inclusion of a training set, along with experiments demonstrating that it improves performance on downstream tasks, is a valuable contribution (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **High-quality test benchmark:** The annotated test set with human-verified QA pairs is shown to be a useful benchmark for recent large multimodal models (Reviewer 1). The experiments are extensive and cover multiple models (Reviewer 3).
- **Resource for community:** The pipeline and processed data can serve as a foundation for further annotations and new tasks (Reviewer 1, 3). Reviewer 4 considered the contribution solid, while others acknowledged the dataset as a helpful resource.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty in methodology and tasks:** The method of creating the dataset via parsing LaTeX sources is not novel (e.g., GROTOAP, PubLayNet, DocBank use similar approaches), and the tasks have been studied before. The paper feels like a resubmission to a dataset/benchmark track rather than a research contribution (Reviewer 2). Several reviewers noted the lack of research contributions (Reviewer 1, also Reviewer 3: contribution score 2).
- **Heavy reliance on GPT-4 for QA generation:** All QA pairs are generated by GPT-4V, which can introduce biases regarding question type, difficulty, and diversity. Questions may favor the model’s own strengths and neglect more challenging or nuanced questions. Further examination of potential biases is needed (Reviewers 3, 4). Additionally, GPT-4V itself does not score highly on the benchmark (Table 3), raising questions about the quality of generated QAs (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation metric deficiencies:** For Equation-to-LaTeX and Table-to-LaTeX tasks, edit distance and BLEU do not account for semantic equivalence of different LaTeX expressions; syntactic equivalence metrics are missing. Grammatical correctness of generated LaTeX is not evaluated. For open-ended QA, GPT-4 is used as the evaluator, which introduces potential model bias; human evaluation to verify reliability would be beneficial (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Potential dataset limitations from LaTeX source parsing:** The dataset is curated from LaTeX-formatted documents, which is a subclass of all documents. This may constrain the variance in formatting and structure (e.g., LaTeX denotes missing references with “??”), and models trained on it may not transfer well to other types of papers (Reviewers 1, 2). The authors acknowledge this limitation in line 458.
- **Logical relationships not actively used in experiments:** Although the dataset provides 6 types of logical relationships between components, the empirical studies focus on individual components and do not demonstrate the utility of these relationships (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing details and unanswered questions:** 
    - Number of QA pairs created and kept is unclear: 4 QA pairs per sample are created but only ~3k total pairs are kept? Filtering process not specified (Reviewer 1).
    - Acceptance/editing rate of GPT-4-generated QA pairs not reported (Reviewer 4).
    - OOD experiment details not fully described; reproducibility concerns (Reviewer 1).
    - Inter-annotator agreement or process for the cross-verification of QA pairs (including how two manual results and one automatic result are reconciled) is not provided (Reviewer 3).
    - Faculty member selection criteria for QA review not clarified (Reviewer 1).
    - Whether multimodal models (e.g., GPT-4V, QWen-VL) are benchmarked on Equation-to-LaTeX and Table-to-LaTeX tasks is not shown (Reviewer 4).
    - The role of `\ref` commands is ambiguous: line 161 says they are removed, but Table 2 implies they are used (Reviewer 1).
