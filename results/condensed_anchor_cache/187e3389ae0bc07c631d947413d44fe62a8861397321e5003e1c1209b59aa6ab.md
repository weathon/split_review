## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reexamines GNN expressive power through the lens of probabilistic graphical models (PGMs), specifically Markov random fields (MRFs). It establishes a hierarchy of GNN variants (MPNNs, higher-order GNNs, subgraph GNNs, labeling tricks) based on their ability to estimate node and edge marginals derived from clique-based MRFs, framing expressivity beyond traditional WL tests. To enhance inference capacity, the authors propose phantom nodes (for node classification) and phantom edges (for link prediction) that augment the graph structure. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets (PPI, Planetoid, link prediction benchmarks) show improvements, though several limitations in scope, scalability, and experimental thoroughness are noted. The paper is well-written and provides a novel theoretical perspective, but the practical novelty and completeness of the empirical evaluation are debated across reviewers.

### Strengths
- **Novel probabilistic perspective** for measuring GNN expressivity, moving beyond WL tests and providing new insights into node-level and link-level behaviors (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5).
- **Solid, principled theoretical analysis** that considers many GNN variants (MPNN, higher-order, subgraph, labeling tricks) and progressively elaborates connections to PGM inference (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5). The analysis overcomes permutation invariance issues and shows surprising correspondences (e.g., between k-WL and clique orders) (R3).
- **Identifies that GNNs can perform probabilistic inference independently**, rather than requiring explicit integration with graphical models, which is a novel contribution (R4).
- **Proposed phantom nodes/edges methods** empirically improve node classification (e.g., GCN+LiftedNet outperforms baselines on PPI) and link prediction across standard datasets (R1,R4). The framework offers a systematic way to extend GNN inference capacity (R1,R4).
- **Well-written and clearly structured**, with thorough background, problem setup, and extensive appendix details (R2,R3,R5).
- **Recasting expressivity via probability is natural and lucrative** in the ML setting (R1). The results are sensible and the analysis covers many inference tasks (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical setup limitations**: Defining discriminative power via WL test still reduces to graph isomorphism and may lack practical relevance; the connection to existing WL-based works needs more thorough discussion (R5). The analysis does not incorporate node features, whereas PGM potentials depend on them, potentially deviating from realistic PGM inference scenarios (R5).
- **Novelty and scalability of proposed methods**: Phantom nodes/edges closely resemble CIN and possibly DropGNN; the approach is not entirely novel (R3,R1). The method requires identifying maximal cliques (exponential complexity) or cliques of size k (O(n^k) complexity), but this limitation is not discussed, and experiments are only on small datasets (R3,R5). No discussion of how to scale to large graphs (R2,R4,R5).
- **Experimental evaluation is weak and incomplete**:
  - Missing tasks: No graph classification experiments (R2). Only node and link prediction are tested.
  - Missing large-scale and diverse datasets: No large link prediction datasets (R4,R5); no homogeneous or heterogeneous node classification datasets beyond PPI and Planetoid (which is outdated) (R4,R5). Impact of graph heterophily on phantom nodes not examined (R4).
  - Limited baselines: Many GNN variants analyzed in theory (e.g., higher-order, subgraph GNNs) are not compared experimentally (R4,R5). Competitors like DropGNN, BUDDY baselines, and other data augmentation methods are omitted (R1,R4,R5). On PPI, the best performance in 4/6 columns is achieved by GCNII+SPN, not the proposed method (R5).
  - Improvements on strong backbones (e.g., GCNII) are marginal (R4). Synthetic dataset results show limited improvement (R5).
  - Lack of experimental details: Hyperparameter selection is not described (R4). For link prediction, why not use HITS@10 as in BUDDY? (R4). For node classification, why not traditional benchmarks? (R4).
- **Missing related work**: Recent Factor Graph Neural Networks (FGNN) are highly relevant and should be discussed (R3). Comparison with Zhen et al. (2023) on higher-order LDPC codes would strengthen experiments (R3).
- **Exposition and motivation gaps**: The motivation for phantom nodes/edges is insufficient; the paper would benefit from a longer discussion of how the methodology changes graph properties and what its limitations are (R1). A direct comparison to prior node labeling tricks is needed (R1).
- **Inaccuracies and typos**: The first paragraph misrepresents prior GNN works by claiming they ignore node dependencies – message passing itself captures dependency akin to loopy BP (R3). Invalid references in appendix A and E.2; repeated citations (Bergen et al., Cai et al.) (R5). Typo “a successful ? for minimizing” in Section 3.2 (R5).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer lowered their score from 6 to 5 due to lingering concerns about novelty, experimental coverage, and practicality (R5). Another reviewer noted that while many theoretical results are natural, some are surprising (R3).
