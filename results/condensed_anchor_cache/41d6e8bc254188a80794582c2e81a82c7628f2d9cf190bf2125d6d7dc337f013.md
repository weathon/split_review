- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 3, 6, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates whether learned spatial convolution filters are necessary in CNNs. The authors propose freezing random spatial convolutional filters and only learning linear combinations via 1×1 convolutions (“LC blocks”). They show that on ResNet-based models trained on ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, and Fashion-MNIST, this approach can match or occasionally exceed the generalization of fully learned spatial filters, particularly for small kernels. They attribute the success to the expressivity of random high‑dimensional bases (Lemma 4.1) and to an implicit regularisation effect that improves robustness. They find that learning gains increase with kernel size due to non‑idealities of i.i.d. initialization. All reviewers agree the paper is well‑written and systematic, but there is strong disagreement on novelty, practical utility, and the conclusiveness of the evidence.

### Strengths
- **Well‑written and organized.** All reviewers praise the clarity, structure, and digestible presentation of findings (R1, R2, R3, R4). R1: “an interesting, intriguing and enjoyable read.”
- **Systematic empirical study.** The paper thoroughly investigates the hypothesis across multiple architectures, datasets (including ImageNet), kernel sizes, and expansion rates, with relevant ablations (R1, R2, R3). R2: “experimental setup is overall quite good.”
- **Robustness analysis is insightful.** R2: “perhaps the most novel/insightful part of the paper.” R3 and R4 also note the regularisation and robustness findings.
- **Thorough literature review.** R3 appreciates the clear context positioning against prior work.
- **Qualitative analysis of kernel distributions** for large kernel sizes provides a plausible explanation for performance gaps (R3).
- **Explicitly questions a core assumption** about the necessity of learned spatial filters in modern CNNs (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Practical impact and motivation are unclear.** Multiple reviewers note that the proposed frozen‑spatial LC models underperform or require much larger width/expansion rates to match baselines, yet no compute, memory, or VRAM comparison is provided (R2, R3). R2: “It’s obvious that … frozen LC models … are much more expensive in terms of compute and … working memory.” R3: “the trade‑off in computational complexity is unclear.”
- **Triviality / well‑known nature of the finding.** R2: “The hypothesis and results are not surprising at all given how many linear combinations the authors learn.” R4: “The idea is trivial and well‑known.” Specifically, random vectors in high dimensions are nearly orthogonal, so learning linear combinations is akin to reconstructing from an orthogonal basis – the paper does not analyze this (R2). R2 asks for an orthogonality analysis.
- **Limited scope of experiments.** All experiments are on image classification; segmentation results are only in the appendix with limited detail (R3). R3 suggests moving segmentation to the main body. R4: “experiments are done on toy models and datasets, which are not convincing.” R1: “experiments in Section 5.2 are not conclusive” – ImageNet results are not robust; the authors attribute failure to “granularity of tested expansion rates” and “fluctuations of a single run”, which is insufficiently convincing.
- **Incomplete comparison with fair hyperparameters.** R2: different model sizes (number of trainable parameters) are compared with identical training hyperparameters; no evidence that the chosen hyperparameters (learning rate, schedule, etc.) are appropriate for each model, so the generalisation comparison may be unfair. R2 also asks whether any hyperparameter tuning was done for the frozen vs. learnable LC models.
- **Theoretical analysis is limited and potentially contradictory.** R3: the paper claims both (i) that random filters plus learned combinations can represent any learned filter (expressivity), and (ii) that the method has a regularising effect – these seem contrasting and no formal reconciliation is provided. R3 asks for clarification of where the “theoretical explanation” resides.
- **Robustness results further undermine motivation.** R2: even when frozen LC matches generalisation, robustness degrades (“another reason why … they are poorly motivated”).
- **Lack of comparison to state‑of‑the‑art models.** R4 questions whether the findings hold beyond small architectures, explicitly asking for experiments on SOTA models.

**Reviewer disagreement**: R1 considers the paper “very strong with a little flaw”; R3 is positive but with clear reservations; R2 and R4 are critical, finding the idea trivial and the evaluation insufficient. The positive reviewers still agree that the ImageNet evidence is weak and the practical/compute trade‑off is not addressed. The negative reviewers stress that the paper does not demonstrate utility beyond empirical validation of an already known fact.
