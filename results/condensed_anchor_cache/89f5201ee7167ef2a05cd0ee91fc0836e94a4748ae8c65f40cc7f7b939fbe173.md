## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper curates a novel multi-modal visual-tactile dataset of paired full-hand force pressure maps and manipulation videos, and proposes a cross-modal manifold learning method for representation learning. The approach represents each modality in a shared latent space and then projects the global manifold to local submanifolds for each signal, enabling cross-modal prediction. Experiments demonstrate effectiveness on retrieval, prediction, and latent structure discovery. (Reviews 1–4)

### Strengths
- **Novel large-scale paired visual-tactile dataset.** The dataset is a significant contribution given the scarcity of such resources, and if open-sourced it could accelerate cross-modal research. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Clear and well-organized writing.** The paper is easy to follow even for readers unfamiliar with the topic. (R1, R4)
- **Innovative yet straightforward method.** The manifold learning approach (global → local submanifold projection with test-time optimization) is simple and does not impose heavy computational load, and has potential to advance subsequent research. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Tackles a more challenging visual-tactile prediction task** compared to prior work. (R2)
- **Comprehensive experiments with meaningful visualizations.** t-SNE results show that the model captures semantically meaningful information. (R2)
- **Potential for downstream robotics applications.** The representation learning method is practical for visuotactile manipulation tasks. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset limitations in diversity and generalization.** Training:test set ratio is around 12:1 with only 4 categories. It is unclear whether the method truly generalizes to unseen objects; the diversity of training and testing sets should be clarified. (R2) Video prediction results for new instances and new categories appear not promising. (R4) Further, the dataset would benefit from more detailed visualization (e.g., histograms of force magnitudes, examples of sensor readings across manipulation phases) and a breakdown of object types, manipulations, and sensor data to assess potential biases. (R1)
- **Method assumptions and modeling of multimodality.**  
  - The assumption that visual information equals a linear combination of shape and tactile information is strong; tactile data also encodes geometric information (e.g., surface curvature, texture) and the method does not account for complex non-linear relationships between modalities. (R3)  
  - The study does not account for the inherent multimodality of the data distribution: a single video observation can correspond to many possible tactile signals (e.g., varying force when touching dough). (R4)  
  - The paper claims many-to-many mapping between force maps and RGB; it is not clear what advantage force maps offer over other tactile signals, nor how the spatial scale disparity challenge is addressed even under one-to-one mapping. (R2)
- **Missing technical details and reproducibility.** Key training details (learning rate, batch size, model architecture) are absent. (R4) The meaning of symbol γ in Equation 2 is not defined. The distance quantification and subtraction operation for tuples (I_i, I_j) in Eq3 need clarification. (R3)
- **Lack of downstream task validation.** The approach is only evaluated on cross-modal prediction; there is no verification on actual manipulation tasks, which would be needed to demonstrate practical value. (R4)
- **Computational cost and practicality.** Test-time optimization is required for cross-modality queries; its time cost is not reported, which is critical for real-time robotic applications. (R2, R3) Similarly, the robustness and calibration needs of the tactile glove are not discussed. (R2)
- **Visualization and presentation issues.**  
  - Figures 3 and 4 show minimal variation across frames; it is unclear if this is a dataset or visualization limitation. (R1) A baseline using only the initial frame (vs. the full sequence) would help assess the value of temporal information. (R1)  
  - Figure 1 omits the process of obtaining the shared latent space and the projection layer for modality x; the figure should be referenced in Section 4.2 and better explained to show how signals of different dimensions are mapped. (R2, R3)  
  - The paper lacks video examples of the tasks and generated sequences, making it hard to judge the quality of the results. (R3)  
  - Minor errors: typo “Additioanlly” on page 5 after equation 2 (R1); empty citation parentheses in Dataset: Data Collection Setup (R3); use “Chen et al.” instead of “Peter et al.” (R3)
- **Disagreement on soundness.** One reviewer rated soundness as fair (2) due to limited validation, while three rated it as good (3).
