## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TREELGNN, combining LightGBM features with static GNNs for relational database prediction. It uses a “time-then-graph” approximation (treating the graph at each time step as static) and distills tabular model predictions into GNN node features. Evaluated on four RelBench tasks, it reports up to 33% performance improvement and up to 1050× inference speedup over a temporal GNN baseline (RDL). Two reviewers (scores 5,5) find the method effective and practical; two reviewers (scores 3,3) question its novelty, fairness of the speedup claim, baseline implementation, and limited experimental scope.

### Strengths
- The studied problem (efficient prediction on relational databases) is practically important. (R1, R4)
- The method is intuitive and easy to understand. (R2, R1)
- Combining feature-engineered tabular predictions (LightGBM) with a static GNN backbone yields significant speed improvements over temporal GNNs (RDL) while maintaining or slightly improving predictive performance on the evaluated tasks. (R1, R2, R3)
- The framework is flexible and extendable: it supports a wide selection of tabular models and GNN layers. (R1)
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work.** The idea of combining tree-based models (e.g., XGBoost) with GNNs is not new; the paper fails to discuss prior work such as “Boost then Convolve: Gradient Boosting Meets Graph Neural Networks” (ICLR 2021). (R2) The main architectural change from RDL is replacing a deep-learning node feature encoder with a LightGBM encoder, while the rest follows the RDL design. (R3)
- **Questionable premise about TGNNs.** The paper claims that prior relational deep learning methods used temporal GNNs, but many cited works (including the main baseline, Robinson et al., 2024) actually use static GNNs. (R2)
- **Lack of clarity and fairness in runtime comparison.** The “100× to 1050× speedup” claim is disputed: LightGBM’s own training/inference time is not reported; the definition of an “epoch” differs between static and temporal GNN mini-batches; end-to-end training/inference times are not given; and in some cases (e.g., rel-hm) RDL has ~100× more parameters, making the comparison unfair. (R2) Using pre-trained LightGBM embeddings while the baseline trains its encoder jointly also inflates speed gains. (R3)
- **Missing details on baseline implementation.** The paper does not specify which variant of RDL (e.g., GraphSAGE or ID-GNN) is used, and the reported numbers differ from those in the original RDL paper. (R2)
- **Limited experimental evaluation.** Only 4 out of 30 RelBench tasks are tested; link prediction is omitted. (R3, R4) The tested datasets are small; larger-scale experiments are needed. (R3) Only one tabular model (LightGBM) and one temporal GNN baseline (RDL) are compared; XGBoost, static GNNs, and other temporal GNN methods should be included. (R4, R3)
- **Unclear distillation mechanism.** The paper does not explain why distilled soft labels outperform directly using the tabular model’s raw predictions or why some baselines (e.g., RDL w/ LightGBM predictions) perform worse than LightGBM alone. (R1) Appendix C’s distillation experiments need more elaboration. (R1) It is unclear if the improvement comes from distillation or from reweighting tabular features. (R1)
- **Marginal performance in some tasks.** On rel-hm and rel-stack, LightGBM outperforms TREELGNN, questioning whether the GNN component is necessary if efficiency is the main goal. (R2) The paper should report runtime comparisons with LightGBM itself. (R2, R4)
- **Limited applicability to non-temporal relational databases.** The method explicitly models time steps; for static RDBs, the approach would be slower (additional LightGBM preprocessing) and could be compared against a vanilla static GNN. (R2)
- **Minor presentation issues.** Typos in tables (commas/decimal points); “articles” used instead of “products” in Figure 1 and Section 2.2; Figure 2 is unclear and needs better explanation. (R1, R4) A pipeline diagram would improve overview, and results should separate main experiments from ablations. (R1) The meaning of “TREELGNN” is not explained. (R4)
