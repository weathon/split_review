## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes G2D2, a method for solving linear inverse problems using discrete diffusion models as priors. It overcomes the non-differentiability of discrete codes by approximating the posterior with a variational categorical distribution and continuous relaxation (Gumbel trick). A star-shaped noise process is introduced to mitigate limitations of standard absorbing-state Markov chains, improving error correction during sampling. Experiments on super-resolution and Gaussian deblurring (ImageNet, FFHQ) show G2D2 achieving competitive results against continuous diffusion solvers (DPS, DDRM, PSLD, ReSample). A motion path-following task is demonstrated qualitatively using a MaskGIT prior. The paper is the first to apply discrete diffusion priors to image inverse problems.

### Strengths
- **Novel direction**: First work to target discrete diffusion models for inverse problems, opening new opportunities for discrete priors. (All reviewers, though one reviewer assigned contribution score 1 due to unclear motivation, while others gave 3.)
- **Sound theory**: The variational derivation and resulting algorithm are straightforward and parallel common continuous DIS practice (using predicted $x_0$ for likelihood loss and DDIM-like sampling). (Reviewer 3)
- **Star-shaped noise process**: Well motivated and shows substantial downstream performance gain over the standard Markov noise process. (Reviewer 2)
- **Paper well written** overall, though some presentation aspects need improvement (see Weaknesses). (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear advantage of discrete diffusion in image domain**: The paper does not convincingly justify why discrete representations are beneficial over continuous models (e.g., low-precision floating point, lighter architectures). The authors themselves note “G2D2 does not significantly surpass its continuous counterparts in terms of computational speed or performance.” Experiments in domains where discrete representations are essential (language, molecules) are missing. Direct comparisons with lightweight/low-precision continuous models are needed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Weak results and insufficient analysis**: Results are underwhelming, partly due to suboptimal pre-trained VQ-Diffusion prior and inherent information loss from the VQ-VAE decoder. A thorough analysis of how decoder artifacts impact inverse problem solving, including comparison with pixel-based diffusion models, is missing. Comparisons with the latest latent diffusion and score-based models are lacking. For the motion task, only qualitative results are shown without any quantitative evaluation against state-of-the-art methods. (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Presentation and clarity deficits**:
  - The star-shaped noise process is introduced too late (Section 3.1); a brief review earlier would help. The introduction does not adequately explain why the standard Markov chain is insufficient nor how the star-shaped process addresses it. (Reviewer 3)
  - Insufficient connection to continuous counterparts: the sampling procedure and the optimization of the variational reverse categorical distribution should be linked to the denoising process in continuous diffusion models. (Reviewer 3)
  - Computational trade-offs are not discussed: number of optimization steps per time step, wall-clock time, memory usage, and potential for adaptive scheduling are all unaddressed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Fairness in baselines**: Text prompts were used for VQ-Diffusion in image experiments; it is unclear if competing methods had analogous conditioning, which could give G2D2 an unfair advantage. Hyperparameters for baselines (DPS, DDRM, etc.) are either taken from original papers or found via an undocumented search; the paper should describe the search range and validation samples, or recommend tuning specifically for this setup. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing experiments**: MaskGIT prior is only used for motion, not for image inverse problems, despite being a stronger prior than VQ-diffusion. The paper also limits evaluation to super-resolution and deblurring; broader applicability (e.g., 3D medical imaging) is not explored. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Potential errors in proofs and text**: Lemma B.3 proof (lines 975–980) appears incorrect: exact equalities should be replaced with equality in distribution (the lemma’s conclusion is unaffected). Typo on line 265: $z_t$ should be $z_{t-1}$. (Reviewer 1)
- **Star-shaped noise process limitations not explored**: There is no analysis of failure modes (e.g., when the initial estimate is far from the true solution) or of how the re-masking operation enables error correction in difficult cases. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing related work**: Should cite “Diffusion modeling with domain-conditioned prior guidance for accelerated mri and qmri reconstruction.” (Reviewer 4)
