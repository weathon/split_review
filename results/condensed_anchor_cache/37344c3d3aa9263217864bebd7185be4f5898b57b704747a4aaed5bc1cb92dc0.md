## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MACPO, a multi-agent contrastive preference optimization framework for weak-to-strong alignment. It iteratively reinforces unfamiliar positive behaviors and penalizes familiar negative behaviors, using mutual positive behavior augmentation and hard negative behavior construction. Experiments on HH-RLHF and PKU-SafeRLHF datasets show improved alignment for both weak teachers and strong students, with better performance as the number of weak teachers grows. Reviewers agree the problem is timely and the approach innovative, but raise concerns about terminology clarity, the perplexity-based assumption for noise detection, experimental setup (dataset size, model size fairness, limited tasks), pipeline ordering, and missing efficiency analysis. Scores: 8, 8, 6, 6.

### Strengths
- **Important and timely problem**: weak-to-strong alignment as LLMs surpass human performance (R1, R2).
- **Innovative approach**: multi-agent contrastive preference optimization with iterative improvement from weak teachers; enhances alignment by reinforcing unfamiliar positive and penalizing familiar negative behaviors; performance improves with more teachers (R2, R4).
- **Clear problem definition and structured writing** (R1, R3); opens avenues for future research with discussion of limitations (R3).
- **Well-structured workflow** combining contrastive preference optimization with multi-agent setup, encouraging behavior diversity and robustness (R2).
- **Promising experimental results** with extensive evaluation, detailed analysis, scalability across model sizes (8B and 70B) and number of weak teachers (R1, R2).
- **Comprehensive ablation study** validating benefits of unfamiliar positive and familiar negative behaviors (R4).
- **Acknowledgment of limitations and future directions** (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology and definition issues**: Terms “familiar”/“unfamiliar” not defined before use (R1, R2, R4). R4 asks whether familiar means self-generated and what measures determine familiarity. R1 notes framework introduction uses these terms before definition and contains repetitive text (lines 053 and 196). R4 criticizes heavy notation in section 4, making it difficult to follow.
- **Foundational perplexity assumption without evidence** (R3, R4): Assumption (line 224) that high perplexity of strong student on weak labels indicates negative noise is unsupported. R3 calls this a fundamental weakness—no reference or experiment shows a positive correlation between perplexity and answer quality. R4 points out a contradiction: selecting weak labels with low perplexity (as done for positive behavior construction) makes those labels likely familiar to the strong student, undermining the goal of unfamiliar positive behavior. R3 emphasizes at least a correlation analysis is needed.
- **Pipeline ordering confusion** (R3): Algorithm 1 derives Strong Student before Weak Teacher training, contrary to typical teacher-student paradigms; rationale unclear.
- **Experimental fairness and scope limitations**:
  - Only two alignment datasets used (HH-RLHF, PKU-SafeRLHF). R2 suggests extending to toxicity detection or complex reasoning; R3 suggests tasks like Reddit TL;DR, noting even negative results would be informative.
  - For HH-RLHF, only 10K samples (<10% of dataset) are used; R3 questions whether results heavily depend on this subset and calls for generalizability validation.
  - Model size inconsistency in Table 1: MACPO uses both 8B and 70B models, while other baselines only use 8B, making comparisons potentially unfair (R3).
  - The third-party reward model used for evaluation is not justified; R3 asks why this specific model was chosen over other gold models in RewardBench.
- **Computation efficiency unclear** (R1): Method requires multiple iterations optimizing multiple models. R1 asks whether strong-to-weak and self-alignment baselines would benefit from comparable computation and whether the comparisons control for effective compute.
- **Agent interaction and dynamic adjustment details missing**:
  - Section 4.2 lacks explanation of how agent interactions are managed and behaviors tracked across agents (R2).
  - R2 asks what criteria define a “weak teacher” and how its effectiveness is measured versus using strong agents from the start.
  - R2 also asks if reinforcement strategies are adjusted dynamically based on agent performance; no mechanisms are described.
- **Problem formulation alignment with motivation** (R1): Section 3.1 uses an analogy with fine-tuned small models as weak teachers and large models initialized on weak labels as strong students. It is unclear whether the strong student is truly strong and how this relates to the motivating scenario of humans providing weak supervision.
