## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SPLAVU, which shifts privacy anonymization from input pixel space to latent feature space using an Anonymizing Adapter Module (AAM). It employs a self-supervised privacy budget by minimizing mutual information between static clip features and uses a latent consistency loss to maintain multitask generalization. The method is computationally efficient as it does not require full fine-tuning of the utility video encoder and supports multiple downstream tasks including action recognition (Kinetics400, UCF101, HMDB51), temporal action detection (THUMOS14), and anomaly detection (UCF-Crime). Additionally, the paper introduces new protocols for assessing gender bias. Reviewers overall find the method effective for privacy protection with maintained performance, but raise concerns about novelty, clarity of privacy objectives, insufficient experimental comparisons, and practicality issues regarding latent space anonymization.

### Strengths
- Effectively achieves privacy protection while maintaining near-peak multitask generalization capability across diverse video understanding tasks (R1, R2, R3, R4). Specifically, a ≈29% reduction in privacy leakage with within 1% utility drop (R1, R4).
- Computational efficiency: no full fine-tuning of the utility video encoder; does not require training a video model from scratch (R2, R3).
- The self-supervised static clip privacy budget objective is considered novel and intriguing by one reviewer (R4), while other reviewers find the overall method limited in novelty (R1, R3) — a disagreement in assessment of novelty.
- Comprehensive experimental design with thorough analysis covering multiple tasks, datasets, and bias assessment (R1, R4). The paper addresses bias issues related to human attributes (R4).
- The motivation and design are clear and intuitive (R4). The method shows generalization among tested tasks (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and technical contribution**: Several reviewers find the method lacking uniqueness, relying on common techniques and combining existing loss functions without clear insight (R1, R3). The Anonymizing Adapter Module (AAM) is considered not novel (R3). One reviewer (R4) appreciates the novelty of the static clip privacy budget objective, but this is not universally shared.
- **Unclear privacy definition and leakage concerns**: The definition of privacy and privacy evaluation metrics are unclear, and there is insufficient discussion of prior work on face de-identification in videos (R2). More critically, the motivation is questioned: since raw frames with visible private information are still encoded by the utility video model in Fig. 1(c), privacy leakage occurs before the latent anonymization, contradicting the claim in lines 47-49 that previous methods suffer from privacy leakage in the encoder itself (R3). Thus, the practical advantage of latent-space anonymization over input-level anonymization is unclear; scenarios where latent anonymization would be preferable need to be discussed (R4).
- **Insufficient explanation of the Budget Privacy Objective**: The role of θ in Eq. 6 and how the objective achieves data anonymization and privacy optimization require clearer theoretical analysis (R1, R3). The purpose of the Budget Privacy Objective is not clearly explained in the presentation (R3).
- **Insufficient experimental comparisons**: The paper compares only two existing methods in Table 1, which is insufficient (R3). Missing comparisons with STPrivacy, Privacy-Preserving Action Recognition via Motion Difference Quantization (R1), and Joint Attribute and Model Generalization Learning for Privacy-Preserving Action Recognition (NeurIPS 2023) (R4). This weakens the persuasiveness of the results.
- **Generalization vs. multi-task learning**: The model is trained and evaluated on the same set of tasks, making it multi-task learning rather than generalized learning to unseen tasks. Prior methods could also handle multiple tasks, so the claim of generalization is overstated (R4). Furthermore, the utility loss in Eq. 5 is task-specific, raising the question of how the method would handle different or more tasks (R2).
- **Presentation and clarity**: The presentation can be improved; the purpose of the Budget Privacy Objective is not clearly explained (R3). Questions remain about model initialization: does the video encoder use only pretrained parameters and receive no updates? What is the specific process for initializing and updating the adapter, and what is the loss? (R1).
