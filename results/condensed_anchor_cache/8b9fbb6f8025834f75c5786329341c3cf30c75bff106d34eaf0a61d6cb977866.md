## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes g-MotorCortex, an agentic robot policy grounding framework that uses multiple Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate action-scoring guidance at runtime, shifting the action distribution of a base policy online. The framework consists of conversational agents (high-level advisor, visual grounding, monitoring, robotic) and a multi-granular object search for robust visuomotor grounding. Experiments on RLBench and real-world tasks demonstrate significant performance improvements for adapting existing policies (Act3D, 3D Diffuser Actor) and for learning new skills from scratch, including zero-shot capabilities. Code, models, and system prompts are open-sourced.

### Strengths
- The high-level idea of extracting VLM knowledge to guide low-level behavior is interesting and timely.
- The paper is clearly written and well structured; experimental results are presented across multiple evaluation axes and are interesting/significant for the field (nicely demonstrates integration of VLMs with robot control).
- The framework self-improves by generating action-scoring guidance functions to update the action distribution of a base policy online.
- Experiments in simulation (RLBench) and real-world demonstrate significant improvements in adapting existing policies and zero-shot capabilities.
- Robustness against cluttered and unseen environments is shown, attributed to multi-granular object search.
- Code, models, and system prompts are open-sourced.
- One reviewer (score 6) found the paper clearly written and results well presented, while two other reviewers (score 5) had significant clarity concerns and requested additional comparisons.

### Weaknesses
**Clarity and Presentation**
- Figure 2 shows many arrows connecting agents (Robotic, Advisory, Grounding) but these agents are absent from Algorithm 1; the overall inference procedure is not clearly described. A clearer, more detailed explanation of the full inference process is needed.
- Algorithm 1: steps "Generate_Guidance" and "Infer future state" (Line 9) are unclear in implementation.
- It appears actions are sampled from a product distribution π(a|o) * G(a|o,…). The parameterization of π (mostly diffusion-based) and G is not specified, nor how the score guides diffusion sampling or whether G is represented as a vector.
- "Iterative improvement" (Line 300 and Figure 4) is not clearly explained.
- Figures 3 and 5 are difficult to interpret; this may stem from the unclear score representation.
- The definitions of "1% and 10% guidance" are not explained; it is counterintuitive why ACT3D with 1% guidance outperforms 10% guidance.
- Figures (especially Figure 2) need more detailed descriptions to clarify design principles and system flow.

**Experimental Methodology and Comparisons**
- Only three base algorithms are evaluated (Act3D, 3D Diffuser Actor, RandomPolicy). The rationale for this choice is not given; results from other algorithm types would help understand generality.
- Claim of generalizability across different tasks/environments is not supported: simulation experiments are only on a test set within the same domain distribution as training, with no evidence on different tasks or environmental perturbations.
- Claim of zero-shot capability lacks comparisons to existing zero-shot methods such as Manipulate-Anything, VoxPoser, and Code-as-Policies, many of which have RLBench implementations.
- The core contribution appears to be failure detection and guidance, but no comparisons are made to relevant approaches like AHA (failure detection/reasoning), RACER (language-guided failure recovery), or REFLECT (iterative failure explanation) which have public code and similar guidance mechanisms.
- Real-robot evaluation is limited to a reaching task with no physical interaction; real-robot challenges often involve physical interaction. More complex cluttered real-world experiments would better showcase the multi-granular object search strength.

**Limitations and Open Questions**
- What are the limitations of learning from scratch? Can the system effectively incorporate human preferences or demonstrations?
- Why do the self-improvement experiments plateau?
