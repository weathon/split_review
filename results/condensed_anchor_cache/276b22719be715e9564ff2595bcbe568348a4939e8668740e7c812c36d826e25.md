## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RapidDock, a transformer-based model for blind molecular docking that predicts interatomic distances and reconstructs the docked pose via L-BFGS. It reports a 100× speed advantage (average 0.04 s per protein-ligand pair on a single GPU) over existing deep-learning methods, with 52.1% and 44.0% RMSD<2Å success rates on PoseBusters and DockGen, respectively. All reviewers acknowledge the speed improvement, but raise concerns about missing baselines, insufficient novelty, lack of proteome-scale validation despite the title’s emphasis, and incomplete technical description of critical components.

### Strengths
- **Clear presentation and well-motivated problem statement** (R1, R4). The paper clearly explains how ligand and protein modalities are fed into the transformer.
- **Novel application of a transformer architecture to docking**, resulting in dramatically faster inference (100× over DiffDock, NeuralPLexer, etc.) (R1, R2, R3). This is the first use of a non-autoregressive full-attention transformer for blind docking.
- **Reasonable design choices**: ligand atom charge embeddings, pretrained ESM-2 protein embeddings, distance-based attention scaling, and a hybrid physics-based rigid distance matrix (RDKit MMFF/EDKG) (R1, R3).
- **Strong results on two benchmarks**: outperforms DiffDock-L and NeuralPLexer in RMSD<2Å on PoseBusters and DockGen while being much faster (R1, R2, R3). Achieves best performance among open-source methods on PoseBusters.
- **Ablation studies** confirm the benefit of several design choices (e.g., charge embeddings, pretrained embeddings, distance scaling) (R1, R4).
- **Scalability to proteome-scale studies** (R3, R4). The reported speed could enable use as an oracle function in drug design.
- **Detailed hyperparameter tables** and training details are provided (R3).
- **Originality in preprocessing** (rigid distance matrix construction, charge discretization) and embedding steps (R3). (R1 also notes the approach is novel; R4 disagrees on ML novelty – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation for proteome-scale docking and lack of corresponding results.** The authors argue off-target detection requires docking against the whole proteome, but pharmacologists often screen only safety-relevant proteins (hundreds) (Bendels 2019, Peters 2012); the paper does not justify why more targets would reduce adverse effects (R1). Despite the title and introduction emphasizing proteome-wide docking, the work provides no substantial proteome-scale results or analysis beyond estimated timings (R2, R4). The claim that the method “will revolutionize medicine” (l.30) is unsupported hyperbole (R4).
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** AutoDock Vina, the current standard for applied large-scale docking, is missing (R1). Also missing are TANKBind and earlier transformer-based docking methods (ETDock, FeatureDock) (R3). The paper only compares to a few popular deep-learning methods.
- **Unfair parameter and method comparisons.**
    - DiffDock-L uses 30M parameters, RapidDock uses 60M; parameter efficiency not discussed (R3).
    - AlphaFold3 performs rigid docking followed by energy minimization (giving an unfair advantage) and is not open-source; NeuralPLexer and AlphaFold3 predict from sequence/graph only, while RapidDock uses known protein structure, so direct comparison less meaningful (R1, R2).
    - DiffDock-L is generative and evaluated on top-n poses; RapidDock is deterministic (single output) – a fair comparison should discuss the impact of multi-pose evaluation (R2).
- **Insufficient technical detail on key components.**
    - **Pose reconstruction from predicted distances** is not clearly explained; it is unclear whether post-processing (L-BFGS) is always required and how robust the reconstruction is when predicted distances are inconsistent (R2).
    - **Time accounting unclear.** Reported inference time does not clarify whether it includes the conformational search pre-processing (96 conformations per molecule) or only the model forward pass (R2, R4). A breakdown of pre-processing, prediction, reconstruction, and post-processing time is needed (R4).
    - **Choice of 257 distance buckets** and charge embedding discretization is not motivated or ablated; effect on performance/inference tradeoff not studied (R4).
    - **Use of -1 for non-fixed distances** in the rigid distance matrix: rationale not explained; could bias learning since distances are inherently positive (R3).
    - **Attention scaler** applied only to known-distance pairs; unclear whether it applies to protein-ligand pairs (R3).
    - **Distance bias matrices**: role and mathematical form insufficiently explained; no mechanistic justification (R1, R3).
    - **Equivariance claim (l.113f) is incorrect**: since the model operates on interatomic distances, it is invariant, not equivariant (R4). Paper lacks sufficient explanation on equivariance (R3).
- **Missing metrics beyond RMSD.** RMSD alone does not guarantee biologically active or physically plausible poses; chemical/physicochemical metrics (PoseBuster, PoseCheck, PoseBench, CompassDock) should be included (R3). The claim of “strong understanding of physicochemical principles” (l.419f) is unsupported (R3, R4).
- **Problematic experiment in Section 4.2** using holostructures: it evaluates distance predictions within known complexes, not de novo blind pose prediction, thus doesn’t validate real-world utility (R2).
- **Data handling details missing.** Splitting strategy for training/validation/test not described; potential data leakage not assessed (R3). Ligand filtering criteria during preparation not discussed (R3).
- **Contrast in novelty assessment.** While R1 and R3 see novelty in the transformer application to docking, R4 considers the ML contribution insufficient (standard transformer + ESM2 + cross-attention) and recommends submission to a domain-specific journal. (R2 does not directly comment on novelty but notes several limitations in methodology.)
- **Presentation issues.**
    - Related Work placed after Experiments disrupts flow (R3).
    - Numerous grammatical errors (R3).
    - Permutation loss citation is wrong (should cite Zhu et al. 2022) (R4).
- **Code not shared**, hindering reproducibility (R3). (R3 states they would reassess if code were provided.)
- **Selective visual examples in Appendix A.6** show only cases where RapidDock outperforms AlphaFold3; missing examples where it fails and AlphaFold3 succeeds, as well as comparisons with DiffDock and NeuralPLexer (R2, R3).
- **Questions raised that overlap with criticisms:**
    - How to better determine rigid/flexible atoms (e.g., using rotatable bond definitions from Vina) without searching many conformations? (R1)
    - Not discussing applicability to target fishing/drug repurposing, a natural use case for the method (R1).
    - Are the same time-splitting approaches used as in previous works? (R3)
    - Is a single conformation generated per runtime? (R3)
