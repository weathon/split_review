## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a metric-based detection framework for LLM-generated texts that uses triplet loss to learn embeddings that cluster LLM responses close together while separating them from human responses. The detection is performed by thresholding the distance between an input text and an LLM-generated example of the same semantic content. The framework includes a text embedding model (pretrained MPNet) and a metric model trained on triplets (one human response, two LLM generations from the same prompt). The authors introduce four datasets totaling over 85,000 prompts and response triplets from GPT-3.5 Turbo. Experiments report F1 scores between 0.87–0.95 in both same-corpus and out-of-corpus settings, with and without paraphrasing.

### Strengths

* The approach is novel: detection without requiring access to LLM internals or logits, and without watermarking (noted by R3, R4). One reviewer (R4) specifically found the approach new and valuable.
* The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4; R3 noted major presentation issues – see Weaknesses).
* The focus on short texts (3–5 sentences) is considered a strength by R3 (though others see it as a limitation).
* The four new datasets (85k+ prompts and triplets) will be useful for downstream tasks and are being released publicly (R3, R4).
* Experimental results are impressive (R4: F1 0.87–0.95 across settings) and the emphasis on computational cost and public accessibility is relevant (R4).

### Weaknesses

* **Insufficient baselines:** Only a distance-threshold on standalone MPNet embeddings is used as baseline (R2). Many suggested baselines are missing: simple classifier on two sets of texts (R1), OpenAI Detector, LLMDet, a RoBERTa detector fine-tuned on context and LLM/human answer pairs (R3). The key idea of using same-meaning sentences needs empirical validation against a simpler pair-based classifier (R1).
* **Short text limitation:** The study focuses exclusively on responses averaging 5 sentences (minimum 3), which limits generalizability to longer documents (R2, R4). R3 listed this as a strength, creating a disagreement.
* **Context requirement overly restrictive:** The method requires knowing the exact prompt/context given to the LLM. This is often unrealistic; an alternative using a fixed set of known ChatGPT generations without context was suggested (R3), but not implemented.
* **LLM scope too narrow:** Only ChatGPT (GPT-3.5 Turbo) was tested. Experiments with broader LLMs (LlaMA v1/v2, OPT, GPT-2, GPT-4, Cohere, Dollyv2) are needed to assess robustness to unseen generators (R3).
* **Paraphraser experiment design flaw:** In the “same corpus with paraphraser” setting, the model was trained on paraphrased LLM outputs, so all test data remains in-domain. Suggested fixes: use a different paraphraser on the test set or do not train on paraphrased text (R3).
* **Missing evaluation on standard benchmark:** The method is not evaluated on the HC3 (Human ChatGPT Comparison Corpus) dataset (R2).
* **Parameter mismatch confound:** It is unclear whether the sentence-level framework outperforms the full-text framework simply because it has more parameters; a parameter-matched comparison is needed (R3).
* **Evaluation metric issue:** Reporting F1 at the best validation threshold obscures behavior across false positive rates; ROC curves with median and percentiles would be more informative (R3).
* **Metric-learning losses outdated:** Only a basic triplet loss is used. More recent losses such as Supervised Contrastive Loss, InfoNCE, and Tuplet Margin Loss were suggested for exploration (R3).
* **Typographic errors:** Many typographic errors made the paper difficult to understand (R3). Other reviewers found presentation good (R1, R2, R4), indicating a contrast.
* **Reliance on LLMs:** The approach still relies heavily on the LLM itself to generate the comparison response (R4).
* **Missing robustness analysis:** Questions remain about robustness to distribution shifts (new LLMs, domains, decoding methods) and whether the method is more robust than simple supervised classifiers (R3). Inference-time cost comparison with baselines was also requested (R3).
* **Prompt variety not described:** One reviewer (R4) asked for elaboration on the types/variations of prompts used.
* **Lack of literature:** The paper lacks previous studies in the literature (R2) – the scope of related work is insufficient.
