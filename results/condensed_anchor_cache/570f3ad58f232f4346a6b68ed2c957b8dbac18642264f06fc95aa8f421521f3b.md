## Merged Review

### Summary
DUALFormer is a graph transformer that addresses scalability and the local-global information trade-off by using local graph convolution on the node dimension and a modified self-attention on the feature dimension. The approach is simple, efficient, and supported by theoretical analysis. Experiments on eleven datasets show competitive performance and scalability. Reviewer 3 (score 8) finds the work strong and clear; Reviewer 1 (6), Reviewer 2 (6), and Reviewer 4 (6) have substantive concerns about the interpretation of global attention, the complexity analysis, the efficiency evaluation, and the clarity of contributions.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated and simple design: the dual dimensional placement of local GNN and global attention is clear and interesting (R1, R2, R3).
- Solid theoretical foundation: the paper provides analysis of intra-class variance reduction (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Extensive and diverse experimental validation: node classification, node property prediction, ablation studies, and parameter sensitivity across eleven datasets, showing effectiveness, scalability, and robustness (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The writing is clear, well-organized, and easy to follow; the method is understandable and implementable (R1, R2, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Interpretation of “global” attention** (R1, R2, R4): multiple reviewers question whether the proposed feature‑dimension attention (Eq. 13) truly captures global node‑level information.  
  – R1 asks how it differs from standard self‑attention and when it should perform better/worse.  
  – R2 notes that for a 1‑D feature the operation reduces to V·α (a scalar), and asks how this constitutes global perspective; also questions the replacement of linearized attention’s ϕ(K)ᵀV (which can have negative entries) with softmax(KᵀV) (all non‑negative).  
  – R4 states the method “only captures feature‑to‑feature correlations” and cannot capture node‑node relations, contradicting the claim of global graph information.  
  Additional analysis (case studies, semantic interpretation) is requested (R1, R2).
- **Efficiency and scalability evaluation** (R2, R4): the experimental evidence for scalability is considered insufficient or flawed.  
  – R2 recommends testing on larger graphs (Roman‑Empire, Question, Wiki, ogbn‑papers100M).  
  – R4: the claimed O(n) complexity is incorrect; Eq. 14 involves the adjacency matrix, giving O(|E|) which is typically ≥ |N|; per‑epoch time is not a valid efficiency metric – total training cost should be reported, and GPU memory comparisons need controlled batch sizes.  
  – R2 suggests comparing with recent strong baselines (ChebNetII, OptBasis) to verify whether GTs consistently outperform GNNs.  
  – R4 suggests comparing with NAGphormer on Aminer, Reddit, and Amazon2M.
- **Outdated GNN baselines** (R2): Tables 2 and 3 use old GNN models, making the statement that GTs beat GNNs less convincing; newer methods (ChebNetII, OptBasis) should be included.
- **Hyperparameter α sensitivity** (R3, R4): the effect of α (Eq. 13) is not analyzed, yet Table 5 indicates sensitivity; a parameter study is missing.
- **Motivation and contribution clarity** (R2, R4): R2 finds the motivation not fully convincing (detailed in questions); R4 says the key contributions are not clear.
- **Architecture design rationale** (R3): why are GNN layers placed after the attention layers? Would reversing the order be possible? Provide justification.
- **Performance difference in backbones** (R3): Figure 4 shows APPNP outperforms SGC on Cora and Pubmed – explain why.
- **Minor issues** (R2, R3): typo “abov” (line 182); inconsistent notation for multiplication (line 168 vs 216, line 191 vs 167); Table 2 missing mean value for GraphGPS on Citeseer; Eq. 13 could be split for readability; no open‑source code plan (R3).  
  Additional requested analysis: empirical evidence that GNNs after the graph transform learn localities (R1).
