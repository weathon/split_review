## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) method that learns a world model online without full retraining on all past data. The model is a linear regression on high-dimensional nonlinear features, with a locality sensitive sparse encoding to enable efficient incremental (Follow-The-Leader) updates. The architecture is based on Dyna. Experiments compare feature encodings on MNIST denoising, test handling of covariate shift, and evaluate in a Dyna-MBRL setting against continual learning baselines. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the approach is original, but have mixed confidence (scores: 6, 8, 6); one reviewer (score 8) found the method well-motivated and the experiments comprehensive, while the others raised significant methodological and experimental concerns.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and under-explored problem: learning a world model online in RL without catastrophic forgetting, which is critical for lifelong agents.
- The idea of using a non-trainable encoder (sparse features) with a linear model for efficient incremental updates is novel and interesting; few prior works build sparse representation-based world models.
- The mathematical formulation (algorithms, definitions) is rigorous and clearly presented, and the background section is sufficient for non-expert readers.
- The method is simple but appears effective in the reported settings.
- A comprehensive set of experiments demonstrates the efficacy of the proposed Losse-FTL approach, including comparisons to other encoding methods and continual learning strategies.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baseline comparisons:** No comparison to state-of-the-art MBRL world model methods (e.g., Dreamer, TD-MPC). Without such comparisons, the paper does not isolate the issue of non-stationary data for world model training or show that existing NN-based world models fail due to catastrophic interference. In particular, FTA (Follow-The-All-Leader) should also be included as a baseline in the RL setting, not just in supervised learning.
- **Unclear source of benefit:** It is not justified whether the reported benefits come from the sparse representation for *model learning* or for *policy learning*. The full-replay baseline (which should be best against forgetting) is sometimes outperformed, raising the concern that the advantage stems from the policy learning side rather than the proposed model learning.
- **Simple environments and unclear scalability:** All RL experiments are on simple tasks; there is no discussion of limitations (e.g., can the method handle high-dimensional image observations or complex environments like Humanoid?). The term “world model” may mislead readers expecting more challenging benchmarks.
- **Questions about neural network training details:** The full-replay baseline’s implementation is underspecified. Was the neural network reset at each episode? What initialization was used? When was training stopped – was data split into train/validation sets to avoid overfitting early data? These details are critical for interpreting results; the limited performance of full-replay may be due to primacy bias rather than non-stationary data.
- **Surprising sample efficiency of full-replay vs. model-free:** The full-replay (model-based) baseline does not appear more sample-efficient than the model-free experiment, which is unusual and could indicate that high-dimensional features sacrifice sample efficiency. This is not discussed.
- **Missing ablation for primacy bias:** A simple baseline where the NN is reset at each episode would remove primacy bias and act as an upper bound for MBRL. This is feasible for the environments used but not included.
- **Notation and presentation issues:**
    - In Figure 1: \(d(s_{t+1}, f(s_t, a_t))\) and \(\delta\) are not defined in the caption or main text.
    - In Equation (3), \(\|\cdot\|_F^2\) is not defined as the Frobenius norm until after Equation (5); it should be introduced earlier, especially since \(F\) also denotes feature dimension.
    - Algorithm 1 should be titled “Dyna architecture” rather than “MBRL”, as not all MBRL algorithms follow that pattern.
    - Algorithm 2, lines 4–5: should the update be an outer product? Dimensions of matrices \(\Phi\) and \(\varphi\) should be specified.
    - “Example 3.1” formatting is unnecessary; it is the only example in the paper.
    - The abstract is too long and the final section title “Summary” should be “Conclusion”.
    - Multiple minor inconsistencies: reward function notation \(R(s,a,s')\) vs \(R(s,a)\); initial state distribution \(\rho\) vs \(\rho_0\); “We firstly formulate” grammar; incomplete sentence “When the input is a convex set \(\mathcal{S}\), the prediction a vector…”; \(\rho\) used for both initial state distribution and grid dimension (Section 3.2); undefined \(\pi_0, \pi_t, \pi_{t'}\) and \(\delta\) in Figure 1.
    - Catastrophic forgetting is discussed in the introduction but not elaborated in the main text (deferred to appendix).
    - Sections 4/4.1, 5/5.1, and appendix subsections lack introductory text before subheadings.
