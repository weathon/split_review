## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Gaussian Loss Smoothing (GLS) to address the discontinuity, non-smoothness, and perturbation sensitivity that cause tighter convex relaxations (e.g., DeepPoly) to underperform looser ones (e.g., IBP) in certified training. GLS is instantiated via two algorithms: PGPE (zeroth-order, gradient-free) and RGS (first-order, gradient-based, more efficient). Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and TinyImageNet combine GLS with IBP and DeepPoly.

### Strengths
- The certified training paradox (tighter relaxations performing worse) is an important and well-motivated subject to study (Reviewer 1).
- GLS is theoretically grounded; the paper provides a theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) linking Gaussian smoothing to reduced loss-landscape problems (Reviewer 1).
- The empirical results on small perturbation settings (e.g., MNIST ε=0.1, CIFAR-10 ε=2/255) in Table 3 are impressive, showing clear gains over prior methods (Reviewer 1).
- GLS introduces novelty in using Gaussian smoothing to mitigate a key problem in certified training (Reviewer 2).
- The writing is fine; there is little difficulty understanding the paper (Reviewer 3; note: Reviewer 2 disagrees on writing quality, see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
**Contribution**:
- The only novelty is combining existing methods (IBP, DeepPoly, GLS, PGPE, RGS). Theorem 3.1 is a direct application of an existing result (Reviewer 3). Some also question the practical value given marginal gains (Reviewer 2).

**Theoretical Rigor**:
- Theorem 3.1 is presented informally and is not mathematically precise; the connection to Stein’s Lemma is not made explicit, weakening the theoretical foundation (Reviewer 2).
- The proofs of Lemmas B.1 and B.2 are flawed: undefined symbols (δθ, P_ε1, P_ε2, P_N(0,σ²)), the limit δθ→0 is never stated, the integral of derivative L' should be multi-dimensional, and there are excessive simplifications (Reviewer 2). This undermines credibility of theoretical claims (Reviewer 2).
- Terms such as “soundness” and “sensitivity” are never formally defined, making it difficult to assess the validity of the theoretical claims (Reviewer 2).

**Experimental Results and Presentation**:
- Large perturbation settings show no significant gain or even worse performance:
  - MNIST ε=0.3: DP-RGS vs MTL-IBP (88.69 vs 88.68) essentially tied (Reviewer 1).
  - CIFAR-10 ε=8/255: DP-RGS (29.25) worse than GRAD-IBP (29.62) (Reviewer 1). This should be discussed in the main text, not only the appendix (Reviewer 1).
- In Table 1 (CNN3), the claimed “DeepPoly bounds now yield the best certified accuracy across all settings” is misleading: on large perturbation radii, DeepPoly-PGPE is worse than IBP (MNIST ε=0.3: 77.23 vs 74.28; CIFAR-10 ε=8/255: 25.72 vs 22.19) (Reviewer 1).
- In Table 1, PGPE does not improve over GRAD meaningfully; IBP-PGPE is always worse than IBP-GRAD (Reviewer 3). On MNIST (0.3) and CIFAR-10 (8/255), the best PGPE method is worse than the best GRAD method (Reviewer 3).
- Contradiction between Tables 1 and 3: standard IBP on CIFAR-10 (2/255) reports natural acc 48.05% and certified acc 37.69% in Table 1, but 54.92% and 45.36% in Table 3 (Reviewer 3). The authors attribute this to model size (CNN3 vs CNN5), but then the purpose of Table 1 is unclear—why not always use a bigger model (CNN7)? It also casts doubt on the trustworthiness of the results (Reviewer 3).
- DeepPoly-GRAD certified accuracy on MNIST (0.1) drops from 90.04% (Table 1, CNN3) to 68.47% (Table 2, CNN5). That cannot be explained solely by model size; only DeepPoly-GRAD has such a huge drop (Reviewer 3).
- Missing comparisons: CROWN-IBP is not compared in Table 3 (Reviewer 1). RGS is not compared in Table 1 despite being the computationally cheaper alternative (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Since scaling DeepPoly to CNN7 is infeasible, why not apply RGS to CROWN-IBP (cheaper) (Reviewer 1)? 
- Figure 1 does not specify the dataset or architecture used (Reviewer 2). Standard deviations over multiple runs are missing for all experiments; given marginal gains, this is essential for robustness (Reviewer 2).
- Connection to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) is not discussed; both methods aim to smooth the loss landscape (Reviewer 2).
- Undefined notation: italic numbers in Table 3 are never explained (Reviewer 1); “DP-RGS” appears first at line 480 without definition (Reviewer 1).
- The paper needs clear definitions for “adversarial attack” vs “adversarial robustness” vs “certified robustness” (Reviewer 2).
- Section 2.1 (“Training for Robustness” and “Adversarial Training”) feels redundant and poorly structured (Reviewer 2).
- Overall, transitions between sections are missing, making the paper hard to follow; statements like “While CROWN-IBP is not strictly more or less precise than either IBP or DeepPoly” lack references or explanation (Reviewer 2).
- It is unclear whether only the training loss is changed, not the certification method (Reviewer 3; confirmed in text).
