## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GRformer, combining GNN (mix-hop propagation) with RNN-based position encoding inside a Transformer for multivariate long-term time series forecasting. Two reviewers (rating 3) found novelty limited and motivation insufficient, while one reviewer (rating 6) praised the originality and validation. Common strengths include some empirical improvements and a clear presentation, but all reviewers raised significant concerns about claims, ablation significance, graph construction choices, and justification.

### Strengths
- The model achieves improvements over 7 real-world benchmarks across 4 metrics (Reviewer 1).
- Ablation study on different positional encoding strategies is provided (Reviewer 1).  
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 2).
- Using Pearson correlation for graph construction is reasonable and efficient (Reviewer 2).
- Original fusion of GNN and RNN-based position encoding within a Transformer, addressing cross-channel dependencies (Reviewer 3).
- Rigorous empirical validation on eight real-world datasets (Reviewer 3).
- Clear articulation of distinctive features and advantages over existing models (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- The claim of a 5.7% MSE and 6.1% MAE average improvement is misleading: averaging across benchmarks without considering scale differences lets the ILI dataset (much higher errors) dominate. The average *relative* improvement per benchmark is 2.55% MSE and 4.96% MAE (Reviewer 1).
- Figure 1(b) and the analysis of Transformer weight matrices are unclear; the claim that cross-channel interactions are “chaotic” is not rigorously justified (e.g., no distribution or correlation comparison) (Reviewer 1). The visualization only reflects hidden-state correlation, not the intended claim (Reviewer 2).
- The motivation for RNN-based position encoding is not well-supported. The ablation (Table 3) shows marginal improvement over learnable position embedding, and statistical significance is unclear (Reviewers 1, 2). RNNs operate recursively and are not parallelizable, offsetting the Transformer’s efficiency advantage (Reviewer 2). Enhancing Transformer with RNN is not new (Reviewer 2, citing [1]).
- The RNN-based position encoding description is ambiguous—it is not clearly distinguished from using the RNN to directly model temporal dependencies (Reviewer 1). The paper mentions multi-layer RNNs but the appendix says 1-layer RNN; the choice and the claim that “deep RNNs can properly capture temporal dependencies while Transformers can’t” are not justified (Reviewer 1).
- The mix-hop propagation layer is **exactly the same** as in [2] and is not explicitly referenced in Section 3.2.3 (Reviewer 2). Overall the model appears to be a direct combination of PatchTST and “Connecting the dots” (Reviewer 2).
- Graph construction via Pearson correlation is static and captures only linear relationships; making GNN parameters learnable could offer more flexibility (Reviewer 3). The assumption of homoscedasticity is not validated across datasets, and outlier impact is not considered (Reviewer 3). Alternative metrics (e.g., TLCC, DTW) are not explored or justified (Reviewer 3).
- Mathematical notation is inconsistent (e.g., function names in italic) (Reviewer 3).
- Hyperparameter choices (threshold 0.8 for graph construction, top-k value \(k\), EMA parameter \(\alpha\) in mix-hop propagation) are not justified or analyzed for their impact on performance (Reviewers 2, 3).
- No comparison of computational efficiency between RNN-based position embedding and learnable position embedding at varying sequence lengths (Reviewer 2).
- The evidence that Transformer-based models are ineffective at capturing cross-channel dependencies and temporal orders is not convincingly demonstrated (Reviewer 1).
