## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces FairMT-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating fairness in multi-turn dialogues, addressing a gap since prior benchmarks focus on single-turn. It constructs datasets (FairMT-10K and a challenging subset FairMT-1K) using GPT-4 with data from HolisticBias, RedditBias, and Jigsaw, covering stereotype and toxicity across seven demographic groups. Six tasks are designed across three stages of dialogue complexity. Fairness is evaluated using GPT-4 as a judge, Llama-Guard-3, and human validation on 15 LLMs. Reviewers rated the paper between 6 and 8 (two 8s, two 6s). The higher ratings highlight the novel multi-turn focus, extensive experiments, and clear analysis; the lower ratings raise concerns about reliance on GPT-4 (both for data generation and evaluation), insufficient human annotation, missing formal task definitions, and limited exploration of mitigation strategies.

### Strengths
- **Novel multi-turn fairness benchmark**: The first benchmark specifically targeting fairness in multi-turn dialogues, reflecting real-world conversational complexity and addressing a clear gap compared to single-turn evaluations.
- **Comprehensive and detailed evaluation**: Extensive benchmarking on 15 prominent LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Llama variants) across six tasks, two bias types (stereotype, toxicity), and six bias attributes (gender, race, religion, etc.), with analysis across tasks, turns, bias types, and attributes. Results show fairness degrades in multi-turn compared to single-turn.
- **Well-defined tasks and taxonomy**: Two taxonomies are presented: comprehension-focused vs. bias-resistance tasks and implicit vs. explicit biases. Six multi-turn dialogue tasks allow fine-grained fairness assessment.
- **Reliable and reproducible evaluation methodology**: Uses GPT-4 as a judge alongside Llama-Guard-3 bias classifiers and human validation (though small-scale). Detailed experimental setup enhances reproducibility.
- **Valuable insights**: Reveals two distinct bias defense mechanisms in current LLMs (targeting implicit vs. explicit biases). Identifies specific challenging areas (e.g., gender and race attributes).
- **Resource contribution**: Datasets FairMT-10K (10K items) and FairMT-1K (challenging subset) provide a rich resource for future fairness research. Presentation is clear with illustrative figures (e.g., per-turn bias trends).

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on GPT-4 as both data generator and evaluator**: This raises concerns about bias amplification—GPT-4 is known to have its own biases, and the paper does not sufficiently address how this confounds the fairness assessments. The synthetic data may not generalize to real-world scenarios. (Raised by multiple reviewers; R2 and R4 were especially critical.)
- **Lack of formal task definitions and missing details**: The main paper lacks specific formal definitions for each of the six tasks. The taxonomy in Section 2.1 is unclear, especially the distinction between “Interaction Fairness” and “Fairness Trade-off”; the boundaries are not well explained. Missing details include the distribution of examples per task in FairMT-10K (e.g., numbers not provided). (R1, R3, R4; R3 explicitly questions the clarification of task boundaries.)
- **Insufficient human annotation**: Only a small-scale human validation test is presented in the appendix. More human annotation during dataset construction and evaluation would increase confidence. (R4)
- **No discussion of mitigation strategies or future directions**: The paper focuses solely on evaluation; it does not offer even preliminary solutions or suggestions for improving fairness, such as training or alignment techniques. (R1, R4)
- **No analysis of fairness vs. quality trade-off**: Models that perform well on fairness might sacrifice response quality. This dimension is absent from the analysis, limiting insights into practical deployment trade-offs. (R1)
- **Insufficient analysis of why certain attributes (gender, race) perform poorly**: The paper does not explore whether performance discrepancies stem from data distribution biases or fundamental model limitations. Deeper error analysis is missing. (R2)
- **Lack of comparison with existing bias evaluation work**: No comparison with related benchmarks like S-Eval or other recent fairness evaluation frameworks. (R2)
- **Evaluation cost not discussed**: The combination of GPT-4 as evaluator, multi-turn dialogue, and large dataset size (10K items) is computationally expensive; this is not addressed. (R1)
- **Minor issues**: Typos in line 368 ("dialogues" likely correct) and Figure 4 with potentially identical subfigures (R1). Line 130 has a double colon, and Figure reference missing in Appendix (R4). The method for selecting the FairMT-1K subset (using scores of six models that may diverge) needs clarification (R1).
