## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of Single Frame supervision for VAD and the construction of three annotated datasets, but express significant concerns about methodological rigor, parameter sensitivity, and the practical sufficiency of a single anomalous frame. Scores range from 3 to 5 (three 5s, two 3s), with the lower scores emphasizing insufficient validation of hyperparameters, lack of generalization analysis, and unclear writing.

### Strengths
- The idea of using a single timestamp (first anomalous frame) as supervision is novel and reduces annotation effort compared to fully-supervised VAD, while providing more precise labels than video-level MIL (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- The method is intuitive, with clear structure and complete experiments; SAPM and GNPM are interesting contributions that merit further investigation (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The release of three SF-VAD datasets (UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, ShanghaiTech) is a valuable resource for future research (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Experimental results show the proposed method outperforms existing weakly-supervised approaches, demonstrating effectiveness with minimal supervision (Reviewer 1).
- The annotation cost is similar to weakly-supervised video-level labeling, offering a better annotation-cost trade-off (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **SAPM reliance on similarity across time**: SAPM assumes anomalous frames remain similar to the annotated one, which may fail for sudden/drastic changes (e.g., object appearance, camera angle change) leading to missed detections. The phenomenon is not addressed by the inference-time Boundary Refining Module (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewers 3 and 4 explicitly duplicate this point.
- **Parameter sensitivity and potential overfitting**: The method uses seven fine-tuned hyperparameters (θ for SAPM, η, σ, buffer g for GNPM, w for TD, θ′ and θ′′ for BR). Figure 6(b) shows AP decreases as θ increases, and each parameter has a conspicuous impact on performance (Tables 4–7, Figure 6). No cross‑validation strategy or analysis of generalizability across datasets is provided, raising concerns that parameters may be tuned on the test set (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 1 also notes the fixed buffer g does not adapt to unpredictable anomaly durations.
- **Insufficiency of single-frame annotation for multi-event videos**: Many anomalous videos (especially in XD-Violence) contain multiple anomalous events or frames; the single annotated frame only covers one event, yielding fixed abnormal intervals that are suboptimal. For example, the "Shoplifting" class in UCF-Crime has visually subtle anomalies that may be ignored (Reviewer 3). The assumption that the annotated frame is near the beginning of the anomaly (used by GNPM) is not validated—many videos have anomalies starting early (as shown in Figure 3a), making the normal buffer effective only if the first annotated frame is truly early (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Unclear writing and missing definitions**: Terms "obscure noise" (line 295) and "obscure temporal features" (line 316) are vague. In Equation 9, the symbol *w* is not defined in the main paper (only in Appendix F). Algorithm 1 contains repeated lines (lines 2 and 7) and ambiguous notation for *l* and *r* that is not clearly connected to the *l*, *r* in Eq. (9) (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Incomplete comparisons and outdated qualitative results**: The claim of superiority over SOTA needs comparisons with a broader range of baselines (Reviewer 2). Qualitative results compare against a 2018 method rather than more recent work (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient analysis of inference modules**: The Temporal Decoupling (TD) module masks attention queries based on variance—reviewers ask why queries, not keys, and why keys are used for feature masking in the Boundary Refining (BR) module (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The authors should evaluate whether TD/BR would benefit other transformer-based models (e.g., URDMU), and provide ablation studies on multiple datasets (Reviewer 3, 4).
- **Annotation release not guaranteed**: It is unclear whether the single-frame annotations will be released (Reviewer 5).
- **Alternative direction**: While interesting, the single‑frame paradigm is less compelling than fully unsupervised VAD which requires no annotation cost at all; the paper should explicitly compare annotation cost vs. performance, possibly side-by-side with unsupervised methods (Reviewer 5, citing Zaheer et al. 2022).
- **Missing discussion of limitations**: The paper does not discuss scenarios where single‑frame annotations may be insufficient or ambiguous, e.g., when there are multiple anomaly types or when the anomaly onset is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **GNPM weighting clarification**: Equation 7 and line 279: the weighting seems more based on distance to the first frame than to the annotated frame. An inverse Gaussian centered on the annotated frame would be more interpretable (Reviewer 5).
