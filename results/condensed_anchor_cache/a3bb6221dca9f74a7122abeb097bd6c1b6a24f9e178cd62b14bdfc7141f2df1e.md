## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes VanTA (Vision-language model guided Temporal Abstraction), a hierarchical offline RL method that uses VLMs to extract semantically meaningful skills from offline datasets. Skills are learned via a VQ-VAE whose latent codebook is iteratively refined using VLM annotations on image pairs. The high-level policy selects skills via Q-learning, and the low-level policy outputs actions via behavior cloning. Experiments on Franka Kitchen, Minigrid, and Crafter show improvements over existing offline RL and skill-based methods. The paper also provides a theoretical analysis of suboptimality reduction.

### Strengths
- Novel use of VLMs to annotate skills in discrete latent space, improving both codebook learning and representation quality without extensive manual labeling.
- The hierarchical policy design is interesting: discretization via VQ-VAE enables Q-learning for the high-level skill policy while retaining a continuous low-level action policy.
- The paper offers a theoretical analysis to guide algorithm design.
- Extensive empirical evaluations across multiple environments (Franka Kitchen, Minigrid, Crafter) show robust improvements over existing methods.
- The research direction (grounding VLMs to manipulation and beyond) is significant and timely.
- The paper is well-organized and uses consistent terminology.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation issues (Reviewer 2, confidence 3):** The text is often hard to parse. Key terms are not clearly defined: what is meant by “skill” in the introduction? Abbreviations like MDP and sg() are never defined. Equation references are incorrect (Line 210 cites Eq. 3 but means Eq. 1). Section 5 appears without stated goal or context. The experiment section ordering (6.5 → 6.2 → 6.1 → 6.4 → 6.3) is confusing, and Figure 5 is in the late appendix. These issues significantly hurt readability; Reviewer 2 tends toward reject without improvements.
- **Insufficient comparison with existing skill learning baselines (Reviewer 3, soundness 2):** The experiments lack comparisons to prior skill-learning methods such as [1] (VQ-VAE-based skill extraction) and [2] (LLM-guided skill learning). Without these, it is unclear whether VanTA’s performance gains come from the skill learning framework itself or from the specific VLM guidance. The ablation study only compares against a non-VLM variant, which does not isolate the impact of the VLM. A comparison with language-guided methods like [2] is needed to assess novelty and effectiveness.
- **Novelty concerns (Reviewers 3, 4):** The core idea of extracting semantic skills via VLMs and grounding them to action policies has been explored before (e.g., [1]). The authors need to clearly differentiate their VQ-VAE implementation from [1] and justify why automation (VLM annotation versus human labeling) is a significant enough departure to constitute a novel contribution. Given recent VLMs, the labeling process in existing methods could likely be automated, undermining the claimed distinction.
- **Limited generalization and skill reuse (Reviewer 4, confidence 4):** The paper focuses on single-task performance and does not explore how learned skills can be reused or transferred to new tasks. Rapid or zero-shot adaptation is a key motivation for skill-based RL; the paper should provide empirical evidence for generalization beyond the offline dataset. The reliance on task-specific reward structures for policy learning may limit the domain-agnostic benefits VLMs could provide.
- **Missing implementation details and ablations:**
  - *VLM usage scope (Reviewer 2):* Is the VLM used only during training or also during rollout? In the non-VLM ablation of Section 6.3, at which stages is the VLM omitted?
  - *Codebook requirement (Reviewer 2):* Figure 1 suggests the policy extraction process requires the codebook model, but the high-level policy outputs a skill (codebook entry). Why does it need the codebook?
  - *Skill segmentation mechanics (Reviewer 2):* How does the VQ model receive input—single frames or multiple frames? How are the initial and terminal states of a primitive skill $\bar{s}$ determined? How many skills were identified with VLM guidance compared to without?
  - *Policy architecture and alternatives (Reviewers 2, 4):* Ablations on the VAE policy decoder are missing; the architecture of the policy decoder is unclear. Why train separate high-level and low-level policies instead of leveraging the VQ-VAE’s encoder/decoder structure as policies (as suggested by the description in Line 159)?
  - *Low-level policy learning (Reviewer 4):* The low-level policy uses behavior cloning (BC) on expert data. How does BC perform with mixed-quality datasets in Minigrid?
  - *Baseline fairness (Reviewer 4):* Did the baseline methods use models of comparable size to the VLM? The VLM’s large size may give an unfair advantage.
  - *VLM adaptation (Reviewers 1, 4):* Was any fine-tuning or prompt engineering applied to the VLM? The paper notes limited reasoning abilities of VLMs (Line 104) but uses them as-is. Is it cost-effective or possible to fine-tune VLMs for this task?
- **Other questions (converted to weaknesses where overlapping with criticisms):** 
  - (Reviewer 2) Do you have experiments in a purely imitation learning (IL) setting? This would clarify the need for Q-learning dependent on a reward function.
  - (Reviewer 2) For Section 6.4, could a graphical visualization of the table be provided to make the claimed slower degradation more apparent?
  - (Reviewer 2) The main limitation mentioned is “proper initialization”—please explain what this means, as it is barely discussed in the main text.
