## Merged Review

### Summary
YOQO is a novel label-only membership inference attack (MIA) that reduces the query budget from >1000 to exactly one query. It identifies an “improvement area” around the target sample – the region where the decision boundary changes due to the target’s inclusion in the training set – and crafts a single query sample \(x'\) by optimizing to maximize the error of OUT shadow models and minimize the error of IN shadow models, solved via gradient descent. Experiments show YOQO matches or exceeds existing label-only MIAs on multiple datasets and architectures, and demonstrates strong robustness against various defenses. An offline variant that avoids retraining shadow models is also presented and is noted as practical.

### Strengths
- **Novel reduction in query budget**: From >1000 queries to only one query. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Well-written and easy to follow**. (R1, R3)
- **Well-designed experiments** that clearly demonstrate attack efficacy. (R1)
- **Effectiveness on par with state-of-the-art MIA methods**, indicating practical relevance. (R2)
- **Greater robustness to many sophisticated defenses**, including multiple defense experiments. (R2, R3)
- **Extensive ablation studies**, especially under diverse defense mechanisms. (R3)
- **Offline attack variant** (no shadow model retraining) is advantageous for real-world use. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Overfitting concerns due to small datasets**: All experiments use very small datasets and training sizes (e.g., 2500 samples). The target model is trained until >98% training accuracy, leading to a >40% overfitting gap (inferred from GAP attack). This may artificially inflate MIA performance and limit generalizability. (R1, R2)
- **Lack of experiments on larger, more realistic settings**: No tests on ImageNet, fine-tuned pre-trained models, or other real-world scenarios. Unclear how the attack would perform in practice. (R1)
- **Evaluation metric concern**: Accuracy alone may not capture worst-case performance. Suggestion to focus on samples farthest from the decision boundary. (R2)
- **Missing training details**: No explicit mention of regularization (L2, adversarial training) or dataset sizes for experiments with/without defenses. (R1)
- **Equations 1→2 transformation**: The justification is present, but could be deepened. Reviewer 2 asks whether adding a weight term \(\lambda\) to Equation 2 could achieve a similar result. (R2)
- **Missing reference and comparison to prior work**: [1] (ICLR 2023) uses a similar loss to craft queries for MIA (though extracts logits). The paper neither cites nor discusses the main difference from this closely related method. (R3)
- **Transferability concern with different training algorithms**: If the target model’s training algorithm (e.g., learning rate, optimizer) differs from the shadow models, attack potency may degrade. (R3)
- **Reviewer disagreement on overall assessment**: R2 is notably more positive (score 8, contribution 3) than R1 and R3 (both score 6, and R3 rates contribution as 2 fair). (R1, R2, R3)
