## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes COSTAR (COntrastive Safe TAsk Representation), a meta-learning safe RL framework for generalizing to dynamic safety constraints (varying cost functions and safety thresholds). It uses a transformer-based task encoder with contrastive learning to extract safety representations from trajectories, feeding them as augmented input to the policy. The framework is evaluated on two modified Safety-Gymnasium tasks (Velocity and Hazard) using CRPO as the base safe RL algorithm. Scores are 1, 3, 5, 3. Reviewer 3 was notably more positive (score 5), while the other three reviewers expressed serious concerns about novelty, evaluation, and theoretical support.

### Strengths

- The setting of CMDP with time-varying cost functions and thresholds is novel and practically important (acknowledged by all reviewers; Reviewer 2 calls it “new”).
- Motivation is clearly justified: zero-shot adaptation to safety constraints is valuable in safety-critical scenarios (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- The proposed architecture is simple to use in practice; the two key components (task encoder and Safe Residual Block) are reasonably designed (Reviewer 1, 4).
- Reviewer 3 (score 5) found the motivation strong and the zero-shot adaptability demonstration promising.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty is limited.** The method is a simple combination of an encoder, contrastive learning, and augmented policy input; all techniques are from existing literature (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Insufficient comparison.** Only two safe RL baselines are compared (variants of CRPO and CUP). Missing comparisons with methods that handle adaptive constraints, such as MESA (2021) and LUSR (2021) (Reviewer 1), and with constraint-conditioned offline methods like CDT (Liu et al., ICML 2023) and TREBI (Lin et al., ICML 2023) (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation scope is narrow.**
  - Only four environments from Safety-Gymnasium are used, which is a small subset compared to current safe RL papers (Reviewer 1, 4).
  - Only one constraint per environment is tested; the paper claims COSTAR handles multiple constraints (Eq. 6) but never evaluates multi-constraint scenarios (Reviewer 4).
  - Even within each environment, the obstacle layout is fixed, oversimplifying the evaluation; random re-layout would provide a more rigorous test (Reviewer 4).
- **Large variance in cost.** In Table 1, the variance for cost often exceeds the mean, indicating instability (Reviewer 3).
- **No theoretical analysis.** There is no result showing that the method learns the optimal feasible policy for target tasks, and the mutual information analysis is not connected to optimality (Reviewer 2).
- **Formulation ambiguity and limitations.**
  - The problem setting is restricted to a **finite** set of cost functions and thresholds ($\mathcal{M}^c$ finite). If $\mathcal{M}^c$ is large or infinite, efficiency and generalization are unclear (Reviewer 4).
  - Equation (6) assumes a fixed threshold per episode, but Eq. (7) appears to use a per-transition threshold, making the actual CMDP ambiguous (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper says the policy is applied to tasks from the same distribution during meta-testing (lines 138–141), but the abstract claims “out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks” – this is inconsistent (Reviewer 4).
  - The training task distribution is not defined; if $|\mathcal{M}^c|$ is large, how is sampling efficient? (Reviewer 4).
- **Constraint update rule may be insufficient.** Algorithm 1 minimizes cost for only one of the violated constraints per iteration; when constraints conflict, this may not ensure overall satisfaction. Reference: Huang et al., ICML 2022 (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing oracle baseline and ablation informativeness.**
  - Figure 4 (velocity threshold adaptation) would be more meaningful if compared to an oracle trained on each single threshold; COSTAR fails to maintain safety when the threshold is lower than training levels (e.g., threshold=10) (Reviewer 1, 4 – both request additional results for stricter thresholds).
  - Figure 3 (ablation) shows that removing components does not lead to constraint violations, making the ablation inconclusive (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 2 should include thresholds as dashed lines in the cost plots for clarity (Reviewer 3).
- **Only two main meta-training tasks** are evaluated, leaving broader generalizability unclear (Reviewer 4).
