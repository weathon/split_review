## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FOLK, a frequency-based SSL method that addresses two limitations of prior masked frequency modeling (MFM): (1) pre-defined frequency filters ignore per-image frequency response variability, and (2) pre-training with only filtered images hinders downstream adaptation. FOLK adaptively selects masked frequencies using Com/RCom filters (inspired by image compression) and employs a two-branch knowledge distillation framework to include original images. Experiments on classification, few-shot learning, and semantic segmentation show competitive performance. Three reviewers rated 6 (confidence 3–5), and one rated 8 (confidence 5), with the higher-score reviewer emphasizing strong motivation and extensive analysis, while others raised concerns about novelty, computational cost, and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated design.** The paper identifies two fundamental limitations in MFM (fixed filters and absent original-image access) and proposes two targeted solutions: adaptive frequency selection based on image frequency responses, and a self-knowledge distillation framework that processes both filtered and original images.
- **Clear and thorough presentation.** The method is clearly described, and the experimental setup is easy to follow.
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation.** The method is tested across multiple datasets and tasks (image classification, few-shot learning, semantic segmentation) with promising results, especially in few-shot settings. All three reviewers agree that the experiments demonstrate the method’s effectiveness.
- **Novel combination of techniques.** The paper presents a new way to combine frequency-domain masking with self-distillation, addressing known gaps in SSL for vision. One reviewer noted the framework is straightforward and applicable.
- **Extensive analysis in the main paper and supplementary.** The high-score reviewer highlighted the comprehensive analysis and the strong motivation as key strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost is insufficiently analyzed.** Only GPU memory usage is reported (Table 12, Sec. B.6). A comparison of training time per epoch, FLOPs, and how costs scale with batch size and image resolution is missing. The computational overhead of the adaptive frequency masking and the dual-stream architecture (additional forward pass through the teacher) is not quantified relative to MFM or other SSL methods. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Filter formulation and hyperparameter selection are underspecified.** The Com and RCom masking filters lack precise mathematical equations or pseudocode, making reproduction difficult. The thresholds used for adaptively selecting frequencies ([0.005, 0.01, 0.05]) are pre-defined, and no methodology is provided for choosing them based on image characteristics; optimal thresholds may vary across datasets, limiting generalizability. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Missing ablation on the knowledge distillation component.** The contribution of self-knowledge distillation versus the adaptive masking alone is not isolated. It is unclear how much the distillation branch adds to overall performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **No direct verification that the two stated limitations are addressed.** The paper claims to overcome the fixed-filter and adaptation problems but provides only performance comparisons; there is no diagnostic experiment showing, e.g., that the adaptive filters produce more suitable SSL tasks or that the two-branch framework reduces the fine-tuning burden. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited novelty differentiation.** The combination of dual-stream processing (student-teacher) and frequency-domain masking is relatively common in SSL. The paper does not sufficiently clarify how this particular combination yields a unique and significant improvement over existing approaches. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparisons to other filtering techniques and low-level vision tasks.** The paper only evaluates Com/RCom filters; comparisons with Gabor filters, wavelet transforms, or other frequency-domain masks are absent. Since MFM has been applied to low-level vision, FOLK’s performance on such tasks (e.g., denoising, super-resolution) should be reported to establish generalizability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Robustness evaluation is incomplete.** The supplementary material shows only simple loss-value changes under a few degradations. A rigorous evaluation against adversarial attacks, common image corruptions, and out-of-distribution scenarios is lacking. (Reviewer 3)
- **No analysis of how data augmentations interact with the frequency masking process.** The paper uses random cropping, color jittering, etc., to generate the two views (u, v) but does not study how these augmentations alter frequency content and affect masking effectiveness. Ablation over augmentation combinations is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **CNN performance does not improve over MFM in full fine-tuning.** For CNN architectures, FOLK shows no gain compared to MFM in the full fine-tuning setting (Table 4, Sec. B.2). The paper provides no justification for this, raising concerns about the method’s applicability to convolutional backbones. (Reviewer 4)
- **Optimal view selection is not investigated.** The method relies on two views (u and v) obtained via different augmentations, but the choice of which views to use is not explored; the effect of different view selection strategies on performance is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
- **No discussion of ways to reduce computational complexity.** The paper does not address strategies for lowering the overhead of the adaptive masking and dual-stream framework, which could hinder practical adoption. (Reviewer 2)
