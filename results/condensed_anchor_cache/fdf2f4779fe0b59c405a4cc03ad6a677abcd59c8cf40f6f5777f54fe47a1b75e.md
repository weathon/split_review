## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies in-context learning (ICL) for image classification in the presence of spurious correlations. The authors show that conventional ICL approaches can lead to task memorization and poor worst-group performance. They propose techniques: permuting input embeddings (+P), inserting intermediate queries (+I), using previous examples as queries (-), and passing group values (+G). Training on diverse synthetic ICL instances is also explored. Experiments on Waterbirds, CelebA, iNaturalist, and a synthetic Waterbirds-Severe dataset show that the proposed methods can match or outperform ERM and GroupDRO. Scores: 6, 5, 5, 5. One reviewer (score 6) found the method generally sound and validated, while the other three (score 5) raised more concerns about limited experiments, theoretical foundation, and assumptions.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and underexplored problem: ICL under spurious correlations.
- Novel approach with interesting ideas (embedding permutation, intermediate queries, group-aware context) to mitigate spurious correlations.
- Thorough experimental analysis across Waterbirds, CelebA, iNaturalist, and Waterbirds-Severe, with clear presentation and well-organized structure.
- Investigates not only single-task ICL but also generalization to unseen tasks by training on diverse synthetic datasets, a critical real-world aspect.
- Demonstrates that the proposed in-context learner can match or outperform strong baselines like ERM and GroupDRO on certain tasks.
- Includes experiments with varying spurious correlations and tests on unseen tasks, adding depth.

### Weaknesses
1. **Heavy reliance on spurious feature/group annotations**: The methods require knowledge of spurious features (e.g., for uniform group distribution sampling, +G technique) both at training and in some cases at inference. This is a strong assumption rarely available in practice. The paper does not address performance with noisy or imperfect annotations. Moreover, if uniform group distribution is available, it is unclear why not simply use those examples as in-context samples instead of the proposed pipeline (R1, R4). One reviewer (score 6) acknowledged this limitation but still considered it a major issue.

2. **Limited experimental validation and need for more benchmarks**: Experiments are confined to three datasets (Waterbirds, CelebA, iNaturalist) and a single model architecture (depth, width). The Waterbirds-Severe dataset is artificially constructed with an unrealistically strong spurious correlation; the paper lacks justification for the generation parameters and does not demonstrate generalizability to more diverse or complex tasks, such as vision-LMs or LLM setups. More benchmarks (including natural/realistic spurious correlations) and varying architectural hyperparameters are needed (R1, R2, R3).

3. **Lack of theoretical foundation and in-depth analysis**: The proposed techniques (especially +I for induction heads and +G) lack strong theoretical support. There is no evidence that the +I method actually forms induction heads; the query pairs $(x_i,\tilde{y}_i)$ do not contribute to the loss and the label of $q_{i-1}$ does not appear in the sequence. Analysis of results in Section 2.4 (Waterbirds-severe, background prediction, group-balanced context) is vague and lacks rigorous interpretation. The paper would benefit from theoretical analysis or simulations (R2, R3, R4).

4. **Data leakage concerns**: The paper does not address potential data leakage that could inflate reported performance. A thorough analysis of leakage sources and mitigation is necessary (R3).

5. **Clarity and presentation issues**: The data generation procedure in Section 3 is hard to parse; an illustration would be helpful. Overall, more intuitive figures explaining the settings and proposed methods would improve understanding (R1, R4). Additionally, it is unclear how the modified spurious feature $\tilde s$ is computed for Waterbirds-Severe and whether it is added before or after permutation when using +P (R4).

6. **Missing comparisons and ablations**: Several experiments are missing:
   - Sampling all in-context examples with uniform group distribution as a simple baseline (Section 2.2) (R1).
   - Ablation on the +I technique to verify if performance gain is from induction heads (R4).
   - Exploring performance with varying model architectures (R3).
   - The claim that the approach does not require spurious feature annotations at inference time is contradicted by the +G method, as $\tilde{y}$ represents group and requires group labels at inference (R4). This needs clarification.
