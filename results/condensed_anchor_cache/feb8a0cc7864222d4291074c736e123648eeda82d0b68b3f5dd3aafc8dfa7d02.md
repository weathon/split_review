## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes TFCounter, a training-free, class-agnostic object counter based on a count-by-detection approach using SAM. It introduces two modules: a background-enhanced similarity module to boost precision and a prompt-aware counting module with coarse and fine point prompts (iterative counting framework with dual prompt system) to enhance recall. The method supports both few-shot and zero-shot counting. The authors also collect a new counting dataset, BIKE-1000 (1000 images of shared bicycles from Meituan). Experiments on FSC-147, CARPK, and BIKE-1000 show that TFCounter outperforms the SAM-Free baseline and is competitive with trained methods.

### Strengths

- The proposed background-enhanced similarity module and iterative (prompt-aware) counting framework are reasonable and improve upon the SAM-Free baseline.
- The new dataset BIKE-1000 for object counting is a useful contribution.
- Experiments are adequate, covering multiple datasets and including ablation studies.
- The method shows effective results compared to SAM-Free and achieves competitive results against previous methods.
- The paper is generally clear and well-organized (according to one reviewer, R4), with interesting figures (e.g., Figure 5 qualitative comparison, ablation studies).
- The community is encouraged to work in training-free approaches due to resource efficiency.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and motivation are insufficient**: The first claimed contribution (integrating SAM into counting) is not novel because the baseline SAM-Free already applied SAM to object counting (R1). The paper lacks a convincing statement of novelty; the design appears complicated without clear insight into what is new (R2). The introduction is generic, spending paragraphs on prior categories without explaining the specific problem or how the method solves it (R2). One reviewer (R4) found the paper clear, but this does not resolve the novelty concerns.

- **Ablation results do not convincingly support the modules**: Table 5 shows that using the background-enhanced similarity module or the multi-round counting module individually leads to performance degradation (R1, R3). The authors claim that background similarity enhances accuracy and prompt-aware counting improves recall, but no quantitative evidence is provided to substantiate these claims (R3). The design of background similarity and multi-round counting are not strongly correlated, and the reasons for performance decline when used alone need further discussion (R1).

- **Performance gains over baseline are marginal and inconsistent**: On FSC-147, TFCounter achieves MAE 18.41 vs. baseline SAM-Free 19.95 (R3). The improvement in NAE is significant only on BIKE-1000 and CARPK; on FSC-147 the SAE is weaker than SAM-Free (R1). Moreover, Figure 7 shows that SAM-Free outperforms TFCounter on BIKE-1000 when the number of objects exceeds 15 (R3). TFCounter does not consistently outperform SAM-Free.

- **Missing experiments and comparisons**: Quantitative results of GroundingDINO on few-shot/one-shot counting are absent; only visualization is provided (R1). No comparison with similar or updated architectures (e.g., CellSAM, SAM2) is included; without these, it is difficult to claim state-of-the-art for training-free counting (R4). Including training-based methods in the experiments is unhelpful because they follow a different strategy and are not directly comparable (R2). The related work section lacks discussion of few-shot object counting, even though a large portion of experiments (including ablation) focus on few-shot (R1).

- **Writing, presentation, and clarity issues**: The text contains multiple ambiguities and errors (R1): line 047 "former"/"latter" references are unclear; lines 049-086 list four points that are confusing and sometimes irrelevant (e.g., zero-shot discussion not relevant to this paper; critique of models that generate masks losing precision applies to TFCounter as well); line 107 incorrectly states "The presentation of dataset BIKE-1000 validates the superior performance of TFCounter" instead of experiments on three datasets; line 478 claims "Background Similarity filters out irrelevant masks" but it appears to enhance relevant masks. Citation formatting and English need improvement (R2). Figures should be more professional (R2). Table 4 and 5 are challenging to parse; a plot would be more effective (R4). The paper lists many evaluation metrics without explaining why they are used or how they complement each other (R4). Terminology like Sim^B, M^B should be included in figure captions (R4).

- **Inference efficiency and hyperparameter tuning are unaddressed**: The multi-round counting during inference could be computationally expensive, but no analysis of inference speed is provided (R3). One reviewer explicitly asks about inference speed (R3). The method requires careful hyperparameter tuning: the selection of lambda (0.5, 0.7) varies by dataset without explanation (R3, R4). There is no clarification on how the number k (k foreground masks) was decided (R4).

- **Figure 2 iterative process is unclear**: When new bounding boxes exist, the red dotted line points to Boxes Prompt Stacks; it is unclear whether iteration starts from scratch or from similarity computation, and which variables change each round (R1).

- **Questions requiring clarification**: Is all of SAM frozen (just used for inference)? (R4). Does FSC-147 include bicycles? (R4). Why is the Hadamard product used instead of an inner product or other product? (R4). The naming "residual point prompts" suggests smaller objects, but the intent should be clarified (R4).
