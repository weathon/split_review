## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers note that the paper proposes a novel framework integrating Causal Representation Learning (CRL) with Large Language Models (LLMs) for causally-aware reasoning and planning. A causal world model is learned from sequences of image states and text action descriptions, with causal variables mapped to natural language to provide an interface for LLMs to query and simulate futures. Experiments on causal inference and planning tasks (GridWorld, iTHOR) show that the causally-aware approach outperforms a baseline pure LLM (RAP with Llama 3), especially on longer planning horizons. The framework builds on BISCUIT and extends it with natural language action representations. The paper also analyzes different action representations (CB, TB, HB) and their sample efficiency. Reviewers find the idea interesting and relevant, but raise concerns about reliance on annotated data, simplicity of environments, experimental comparisons, and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- The integration of CRL with LLM planning is novel and intuitive, providing a solid foundation for future work on causal world models for System 2 reasoning. It is straightforward to extend beyond RAP to other LLM-based search algorithms.
- The paper investigates different text-based action representations (CB, TB, HB) with detailed results, showing potential advantages in low-data regimes. The connection to the RL decision-making problem is appreciated.
- The approach is technically sound: the underlying math principles are analyzed, and experiments demonstrate large performance increases over a baseline pure language model on state-variable recovery (R² scores) and multi-step next-state prediction accuracy, as well as consistent superior performance in planning, especially for longer horizons.
- Presentation is clear and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1: “novel and interesting”; Reviewer 2: “clear presentation”; Reviewer 3: “particularly interesting”; Reviewer 4: “solid future approach”)
- The minority reviewer (R4, score 8) is notably more positive, emphasizing the potential of the approach and its strong experimental results against the baseline.

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on unrealistic annotations.** The framework requires a set of annotated images with ground-truth causal variables and a rule-based state description generator. In more realistic environments these may not be available, and causal factors may be unidentifiable or relationships highly complex. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons.** 
  - The Reasoning via Planning (RAP) baseline is not state of the art; literature [1] combines LLM as world model and policy model with MCTS, which should be better. 
  - The baseline LM (RAP + Llama 3) is a general LLM not fine-tuned on the domain. The proposed method is trained on the domain, making the comparison unfair. A supervised fine-tuned version of the baseline LM using the same annotated data should be compared. (R1, R2)
  - A comparison with RAP using the real simulator (oracle) is missing, which would clarify if the causal world model’s look-ahead is actually needed in complex visual environments. (R1)
- **Missing analysis of how decoded text helps LLM reasoning.** It is unclear whether the performance gain comes from (1) the text being easier for the LLM to reason about, or (2) the CRL model’s higher accuracy in predicting future states. Examples of decoded text and ablation experiments separating these two aspects are needed. (R4)
- **Action representation choice inconsistency.** The experiments compare CB, TB, and HB in low-data scenarios, but the main planning experiments use TB at 100% data. It is unclear which representation is best for the full dataset; HB performed better at 100% in Table 1, so why not use HB? Also, the claim of “better sample efficiency” for TB is not clearly supported by the results (TB does not outperform HB consistently at low subsample rates). (R2, R4)
- **Scalability and environment simplicity.** All experiments are in synthetic or simple environments (GridWorld, iTHOR). There are doubts about whether the method can scale to more complex, real-world environments, especially when the causal mapper’s autoencoder may fail to identify all objects. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Overhead vs. increased LLM calls.** The paper does not compare the overhead of learning the causal world model against simply increasing the number of LLM calls in a tree‑search baseline (e.g., iterative prompting or more MCTS rollouts). (R1)
- **How objects are identified in images.** It is not explained how the autoencoder ensures that all objects are identified in the image; if objects are missing, the causal model cannot run properly. (R1)
- **Confusion about causal mapper training (L285).** The description of training the causal mapper using image input is confusing. (R1)
- **Parameter sensitivity and selection.** The paper does not discuss how parameters for the various components were chosen or how sensitive the results are to these choices. (R3)
- **Missing planning metric.** The planning results should include SNA (success weighted by number of actions), a common metric that combines success rate and number of steps. (R4)
- **Typos and minor issues.**
  - L269: should read \( p_\phi(E_t | z_t) \), not \( p_\phi(X_t | z_t) \). (R1)
  - Page 4 footnote: “prioi” should be “prior”. (R4)
  - Figure 1: missing an arrow from \(\mathbf{C}^1\) to Text Decoder. (R4)
  - “Baseline LM” is a confusing name; the paper should simply say “RAP” in tables and clarify that it uses Llama 3. (R1)
  - Provide examples of coordinate-based action representation (e.g., 2-D vector vs. text “(2,3)”) and clarify how HB differs from TB. (R2)
