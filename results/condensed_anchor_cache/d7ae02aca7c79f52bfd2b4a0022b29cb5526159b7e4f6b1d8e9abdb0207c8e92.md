## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes the Scalar Interaction Network (SINet), built on a unified equivariant basis decomposition framework that treats both tensor product and scalarization operations as basis construction with invariant coefficients. The key idea, REBIF (Replace Equivariant Basis with Input Features), uses input feature fragments directly as the equivariant basis, enabling interactions between different type‑\(l\) features with \(\mathcal{O}(L^2)\) complexity instead of the \(\mathcal{O}(L^6)\) of standard CG tensor products. Scalar Interaction computes inner products between fragments and passes the resulting scalars through an MLP to obtain invariant coefficients. Experiments on QM9 and N‑body dynamics show competitive performance and computational efficiency gains.

### Strengths
- The efficiency problem of tensor‑product equivariant operations is of broad interest (R1).
- Scalar Interaction reduces complexity from \(\mathcal{O}(L^6)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(L^2)\), a significant theoretical improvement (R1, R3).
- The equivariant basis decomposition perspective provides a clear theoretical unification of tensor product and scalarization methods (R3).
- REBIF is a simple, intuitive, and easy‑to‑implement idea (R1, R2, R3).
- The architecture generalizes scalarization beyond type‑1 vectors to handle higher‑order representations (R2, R3).
- Despite its simplicity, SINet yields strong empirical results on standard geometric graph datasets; one reviewer highlighted competitive performance against baselines (R2).
- The paper is well‑written and presents the ideas in a clear, understandable way (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Expressiveness limitation**: Directly using input feature fragments as the equivariant basis (instead of computing new bases as in tensor products) may limit expressive power and universal approximation capability, especially for tasks requiring richer basis functions (R1, R3).
- **Acceleration not orders of magnitude**: From Figure 3, the speedup ratio degrades with more channels, and the empirical acceleration does not achieve the order‑of‑magnitude gain suggested by the \(\mathcal{O}(L^6) \to \mathcal{O}(L^2)\) theory (R1).
- **No consistent benefit from higher \(L\)**: Table 3(b) shows that \(L=1\) (type‑1 only) achieves the best performance on N‑body dynamics, implying that scalarization alone may be sufficient and the added expressiveness of SINet’s higher‑order interactions is not beneficial (R1, R2). More generally, SINet with larger steerable features does not consistently improve performance (R1).
- **Hybrid design lacks motivation**: The method first uses tensor products to generate high‑order input features, then applies scalar‑interaction blocks that resemble scalarization. The paper does not clearly justify why this hybrid is necessary over a pure scalarization approach (R3).
- **Unsupported assumption**: The premise that tensor‑product methods are more accurate than scalarization is contradicted by Table 1, where EGNN (a scalarization method) outperforms several tensor‑product baselines (R3).
- **Missing comparisons**: Efficiency of Scalar Interaction versus pure scalarization methods (e.g., EGNN) is not provided, despite the hybrid nature (R1, Q2). Authors should report time and MSE for both on the N‑body system, as done in EGNN/SEGNN papers.
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation studies are conducted to isolate the contribution of the proposed Scalar Interaction blocks versus other components (R1, R3; Q3).
- **Unclear comparison budget**: It is unclear whether SINet’s improved performance over TFN (Table 1) comes from architectural innovation or simply from having more parameters/computation; a fair comparison would control computational budget (R2).
- **Performance not appealing**: One reviewer found Table 1 results uncompetitive compared to previous baselines (R1). (Another reviewer considered them good—this disagreement is noted.)
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: One reviewer (score 6) was significantly more positive, praising the simplicity and generality, but still identified the TFN comparison and experimental details as weaknesses. The other two reviewers (score 3) were more critical, focusing on expressiveness, motivation, and empirical limitations.
