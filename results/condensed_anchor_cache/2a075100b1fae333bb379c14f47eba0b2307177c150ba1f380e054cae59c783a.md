- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 5, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
TinyTrain proposes an on-device training approach for IoT and MCU devices that combines meta-learning-based offline pre-training with a task-adaptive sparse-update method that dynamically selects layers/channels based on a multi-objective criterion (jointly capturing user data, memory, and compute capabilities). It aims to address data scarcity and resource constraints during few-shot learning on edge devices. The authors report 3.6–5.0% accuracy improvement over vanilla fine-tuning, 9.5× faster training, 3.5× more energy-efficient, and up to 1,098× backward-pass memory reduction, with deployment on Jetson Nano and Pi Zero. However, the work’s novelty and alignment with the “extreme edge” claim are debated across reviewers.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: on-device training under data scarcity and tight resource constraints (all reviewers).
- The dynamic sparse-update (task-adaptive, layer/channel selection) is superior to static sparse-update methods (Reviewer 1).
- Incorporates a few-shot learning scheme to improve sample efficiency during on-device training (Reviewer 1).
- Provides a practical solution for resource-constrained edge devices (Reviewer 2).
- Well-motivated by real-world privacy and personalization needs; extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks (Reviewer 3).
- Real deployment on Pi Zero and Jetson Nano with measured latency and energy consumption – a strong empirical contribution (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Novel contributions include meta-learning-based offline pre-training and a channel selection strategy that jointly considers importance (Fisher information), memory footprint, and computational cost (Reviewer 4).
- Solid empirical results: TinyTrain mostly outperforms SOTA frameworks (TinyTL, MCUNet) on 9 datasets (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 4 finds the novelty and contributions stronger (score 8) than other reviewers, who raise novelty concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and similar prior work** (Reviewers 1, 3 disagree with Reviewer 4): 
  - Reviewer 1 notes that E2-Train (Wang et al., 2019) already jointly considers layer importance and computation cost for selective updates, raising concerns about the paper’s novelty.
  - Reviewer 3 points out that p-Meta (a prior work) studies the same problem setting (few-shot learning on edge devices with limited data and resources) and also uses a two-stage pipeline (meta-training in cloud, few-shot on device).
- **Figure quality and presentation issues** (Reviewers 1, 4): 
  - Figures are too small and hard to read (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 3 is problematic: it shows layer indices across the whole model rather than per block, making it impossible to verify the authors’ claims about first/second layers inside a block; one claimed pattern is acknowledged as “not clear” but presented without further justification; terms “accuracy gain per parameter” and “accuracy gain per MAC” are used without definition (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper feels unfocused and dense, especially Section 2 (Reviewer 4); Fig. 5a is missing (Reviewer 4); using ‘§’ instead of ‘Sec.’/‘Section’ is distracting (Reviewer 4).
- **Disconnect between observations and design** (Reviewer 1): The positional observations about layer importance within blocks (Figure 3) do not motivate the design, which uses a gradient-based metric for layer/channel importance that does not consider layer position.
- **Limited task scope** (Reviewer 2): Experiments are only on image classification with few epochs; missing evaluation on segmentation or detection tasks, which are more complex and would better test the approach’s generality.
- **Missing training/inference speed or time cost** (Reviewer 2): The paper should report actual training/inference time on downstream tasks to allow assessment of computational efficiency and scalability for time-sensitive applications.
- **Memory footprint calculation concerns** (Reviewer 3): 
  - The peak memory during backpropagation must be clarified. Since the forward pass of 8-bit MCUNet already requires 0.49 MB (as reported in MCUNet paper), training MCUNet in 32-bit floating point would require >1.96 MB, unless low-precision training or a different architecture was used. The authors should elaborate how peak memory was computed (which layer during backprop).
  - The use of momentum SGD during on-device learning adds memory for momentum buffers; the paper should either justify this choice or compare using vanilla SGD for a fair memory comparison.
- **Hardware scope not “extreme edge”** (Reviewer 4): The paper claims “extreme edge” but uses Pi Zero and Jetson Nano, which are much larger than Cortex-M based MCUs (e.g., with RTOS, limited floating-point support). Applicability to such truly resource-constrained MCUs (like those targeted by MCUNet) is not demonstrated.
- **Remaining reviewer questions** that overlap with missing experiments or clarifications:
  - Is meta-learning used only for offline pre-training or also during on-device training? It appears online learning is standard transfer learning; could on-device meta-learning (few-shot) be feasible? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does the channel selection determine how many channels to remove/update at a given time? (Reviewer 4)
  - How was energy consumption measured? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why does “FullTrain” (updating the entire network) not act as an accuracy upper bound? (Reviewer 4)
