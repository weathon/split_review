## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ‘dynamic sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics’ (dynamic SINDy), a deep learning framework for identifying governing equations in noisy, non-stationary, and nonlinear dynamical systems. By combining time-series VAEs with SINDy, it enables unsupervised inference of time-varying ODE coefficients from data and provides uncertainty quantification. The method is validated on three synthetic datasets (including switching, sigmoid, Fourier motifs) and a calcium imaging dataset of C. elegans, compared against baselines, and shown to recover latent variables (e.g., Lotka-Volterra).

### Strengths
- Important and highly relevant problem: learning parsimonious, interpretable models for non-autonomous dynamical systems (R1, R3).
- Unsupervised inference of ODE parameters with fidelity – no direct reconstruction loss for the ODE parameter time series in the loss function (7) (R1).
- Uncertainty quantification via VAEs allows calibrated estimation of ODE coefficient uncertainty (R1, R4).
- Comprehensive toy experiments across several time-varying motifs (switching, sigmoid, switch, Fourier) with calibrated uncertainty (R2).
- Latent variable discovery demonstrated on Lotka-Volterra where only prey data are observed (R4).
- Tested against baselines and on a real-world dataset (C. elegans) (R1, R4).
- Problem and method presented clearly and easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Scalability not addressed**: All benchmark datasets are low-dimensional (2–3D). The method is not evaluated on high-dimensional systems that cannot be sufficiently described by a few PCA components, which is a major drawback for real-world applications (R1, R3). Reviewer 2 asks whether the method has been applied to higher-dimensional latent spaces.
- **Unclear novelty and contribution separation**: The exact technical advances over prior work (e.g., HyperSINDy) are not clearly stated – more precise statements about differences with HyperSINDy (around line 096) are needed (R2). Reviewer 3 describes the method as a straightforward combination of two existing approaches, with limited experiments and lack of significant technical innovations, making the overall contribution limited (R3, minority more negative).
- **Real-world impact not convincingly demonstrated**: Experiments focus on synthetic datasets that avoid many real-world challenges (high dimensionality, non-Gaussian noise, large space of possible coefficients). The C. elegans dataset is simple; the method is only applied to low-dimensional PCA representations; results do not add scientific insights, and identifiability is not discussed (R3). Reviewer 4 adds that the assumed form for C. elegans depends heavily on prior knowledge (dimensionality, input), and it is unclear what scientific insights the method offers for systems where little is known a priori.
- **Uncertainty quantification usefulness unclear**: Section 4.2 shows that the estimated standard deviation follows the truth, but it is unclear what practical use the uncertainty provides or whether it is necessary for accurate estimates (R4). Reviewer 3 notes that variational inference tends to produce overconfident uncertainty estimates due to the KL term’s mode-covering behavior, and asks for discussion of this impact.
- **Method scope limitations**: The method only deals with non-stationarity arising from separable time-varying variables; the authors should elaborate why this is the case (R3). The examples mostly use systems driven by mean/input processes; performance for noise-driven dynamics (e.g., systems with multiple meta-stable points or line attractors) is not addressed (R4).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
  - No comparison with methods for smooth switching of dynamics (e.g., Kurle et al. 2020) (R2).
  - Comparison with reservoir computing (Köglmayr & Räth 2024) and benchmarks from Patel & Ott 2023 (different bifurcations) is missing (R1).
  - Section 4.6 & Fig. 7 do not clearly compare dynamic SINDy to SLDS and SINDy with group sparsity; Fig. 7 would be clearer if all comparison methods were shown side-by-side (R1). Similarly, Reviewer 4 suggests putting dynamic SINDy reconstruction together with the SLDS result in Fig. 7.
  - Robustness of identified parameters to library size and noise level is not quantitatively studied (R3).
- **Technical and presentation issues**:
  - Explanation of dynamic HyperSINDy in main text is insufficient; the supplement’s description almost forces the reader to read Section 1.2.2 (R1).
  - Figures: Fig 1B labels are very small and font sizes vary; centre box is hard to read (R1, R2). Fig 2d green/blue lines and their ordering are unclear (R2). The zoomed-out view of timeVAE in Fig 1B does not help (R2). R1 suggests reducing Fig 1A to enlarge Fig 1B and moving suptitles to captions.
  - Typos: Fig 3B y-axis says “approxiate std” (R1); lines 353 and 360 reference wrong figures (6A/B/C instead of 5A/B/C) (R1); supplement line 262 says weight decay of 1e5 (likely 1e-5) (R1); line 461 typo “rLDS” (R4).
  - Reference style: should use author-year (e.g., APA) instead of numerical numbers to improve readability (R1).
- **Specific results/observations needing explanation**:
  - Inferred ODE parameter time series for switch signals (Fig 2a–c, Fig 3A low noise) show high-frequency oscillations on top of correct switching dynamics – the authors should explain why the encoder-decoder struggles and how to address it (R1).
  - Results in Fig 3B look weak; the authors should report Pearson’s r of noise level vs. standard deviation (R1).
  - References for studies mentioned in lines 409–411 are missing (R1).
