## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses the novel problem of generating first-order ambisonics (FOA) directly from silent videos. The authors introduce the YT-Ambigen dataset (102K 5-second clips with FOA), new evaluation metrics based on audio energy maps and saliency, and the ViSAGe framework, which uses CLIP visual features, patchwise energy maps, and an autoregressive neural audio codec with directional and visual guidance. ViSAGe outperforms two-stage baselines (mono generation then spatialization) in quantitative metrics. One reviewer is notably more positive (score 8) and calls the approach groundbreaking; the other four (score 6) acknowledge its novelty and contributions but raise several concerns.

### Strengths

- The problem of generating spatial audio from silent video is novel, interesting, and important for immersive media (VR, AR, film). One reviewer calls it “groundbreaking” and a “significant advancement” (R1).
- An end-to-end framework (ViSAGe) integrating CLIP visual features with neural audio codec and directional guidance, avoiding the error accumulation of two-stage approaches (R1, R4, R5).
- The introduction of YT-Ambigen (102K clips) and new evaluation metrics (audio energy maps, saliency) provides essential resources and benchmarks for the community (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- Careful design components: FOA encoding, rotation augmentation, patchwise energy maps, and sequence generation (R3). The dataset curation pipeline addresses gaps in existing datasets (R2).
- The paper is clearly written and well-structured (R1, R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Computational cost not analyzed:** Model parameters, inference time, and per-module breakdown are missing. This limits assessment of practical applicability, especially for real-time or resource-constrained deployment (R1).
- **Missing subjective evaluations:** No MOS or similar tests for audio quality and directionality/localization accuracy. Comparisons with baselines on subjective metrics are needed (R3).
- **Dataset quality and coverage concerns:**
  - The dataset was collected automatically; human verification may be needed to ensure test sets are clean and challenging (R2).
  - Most video content appears centered, making the spatial synthesis task similar to mono generation. The distribution, event types, categories, and acoustic environments are not detailed. This limits the diversity compared to real-world scenarios (R2, R5).
  - Lack of moving-object samples, off-center sources, or fast-motion scenes that require time-synced spatial audio (R2, R5).
- **Generated audio issues:**
  - Sounds are often too diffuse, making precise source localization difficult (R3). The method may not adhere to physical sound propagation principles, leading to unnatural spatialization or inaccurate reflections, especially in complex environments (R5).
  - The method may not fully capture temporal evolution of sound events for fast motion or abrupt visual transitions (R5).
- **Model design details unclear:**
  - No explanation of how the camera orientation parameter enhances spatial perception (R4).
  - CLIP visual representation lacks spatial information; could a representation with stronger local perception (e.g., DINOv2) improve quality? (R4)
  - Generation of the 9 RVQ codes per timestep per audio channel is not clearly described; figures illustrate single-code generation but the codec uses 9 codes (R2).
- **Visualization and presentation issues:**
  - In Fig. 3(a), generated spectrograms differ from ground truth but areas of interest are not highlighted (R2).
  - In Fig. 3(b), overlaying the heatmap on the video would improve clarity; the predicted heatmap still does not fully align with visual events (R2).
  - Check bold numbers in Table 3, and fix figure/table references (L476 references Figure D) (R2).
- **Missing relevant references:** Heydari et al. (Immersediffusion) and Kushwaha et al. (Diff-SAGe) on conditional spatial audio generation should be cited (R3).
- **Suggested improvements:**
  - Create a clean, single-source static-sound subset for DoA evaluation and clearer demos (R3).
  - Explore cross-modal contrastive learning from sound source localization (e.g., Chen et al. 2021) to impose additional constraints (R1).
