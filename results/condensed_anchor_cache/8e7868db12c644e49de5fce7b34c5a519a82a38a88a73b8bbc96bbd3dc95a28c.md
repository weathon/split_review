## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Mambular, an adaptation of the Mamba architecture for tabular data, treating features as ordered sequences. Benchmarks against neural network and tree-based models across 12 datasets are presented, along with ablations on pooling strategies, feature interaction, and bi-directional processing. Reviewers note that the paper is clearly written and the ablation study is thorough. However, the novelty is contested: one reviewer (score 8) sees a novel sequential approach to capture feature interactions, while three others (scores 3,3,3) find it little more than applying Mamba to tabular data with minor modifications, similar to prior work like MambaTab. Performance is described as competitive but not consistently superior, and the experimental evaluation is considered limited (12 datasets, no CatBoost or recent deep learning baselines like TabPFN, GANDALF, TabR, T2G-Former, SAINT). Concerns about hyperparameter tuning (not performed, yet defaults may affect rankings), significance testing (10% level vs. standard 5%), and feature order sensitivity are raised.

### Strengths
- Clearly written and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- Comprehensive ablation study exploring pooling strategies, feature interaction mechanisms, and bi-directional processing (R1, R3).
- Model achieves competitive performance across both regression and classification tasks (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Novel sequential approach: treating tabular features as ordered sequences to capture feature interactions, extending Mamba to a new domain (R3; R4 acknowledges exploration, though less novel).
- Architecture incorporates feature interaction mechanisms and various pooling strategies, providing flexibility (R4).
- Solid benchmarks against state-of-the-art models including FT-Transformer, TabTransformer, XGBoost, LightGBM (R1, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / insufficient contribution**: Several reviewers find the adaptation minor and similar to prior work MambaTab; the modifications are mainly hyperparameter tuning and slight architectural changes, not a fundamentally new contribution (R2, R4). One reviewer (R3) considers the sequential approach novel, creating a contrast in assessment.
- **No hyperparameter tuning**: The authors do not perform hyperparameter tuning and claim it does not impact rankings (citing Grinsztajn et al. 2022, Gorishniy et al. 2021). This is questioned: Table 3 shows Random Forest outperforming XGBoost, which is unusual; model sensitivity to kernel size in permutation experiments suggests hyperparameters matter (R1, R3). A comparison using a benchmark like TabZilla is recommended (R3).
- **Limited datasets and missing standard benchmarks**: Only 12 datasets are used, and well-established benchmarks from the community (e.g., McElfresh et al. 2023, Kadra et al. 2021) are not included (R1, R4).
- **Missing baselines**: CatBoost, which handles categorical variables natively and is often top-performing, is absent (R1). Recent deep learning tabular models (TabPFN, GANDALF, TabR, T2G-Former, SAINT) are not compared (R3, R4).
- **Performance not consistently superior**: Mambular does not show significant improvement over XGBoost or LightGBM across datasets (R2, R4). Results are not statistically significant at the standard 5% level; the authors use a 10% level (R1).
- **No theoretical or empirical justification for sequential treatment**: The paper does not explain why treating tabular data (which lacks natural feature order) as a sequence is advantageous, nor provides evidence for a distinct advantage (R4). Reviewer R3 suggests an experiment with random feature permutations (Definition 2 of Borisov et al. 2022) to test robustness.
- **Lack of runtime/inference time analysis**: No detailed comparison of training or inference time with baselines (R1).
- **Related work gaps**: Missing core references, e.g., Kadra et al. 2021 (line 47) and CatBoost (Prokhorenkova et al. 2018, line 252) (R1).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - How were the set of defaults devised? Are they from baseline papers? (R1)
  - Why was CatBoost not included? (R1)
  - Could significance tests be applied over all datasets (using mean per dataset) and a critical difference diagram provided? (R1)
  - What is the runtime and inference time of Mambular vs. baselines? (R1)
  - Could an experiment with random feature permutations be conducted to address feature order sensitivity? (R3)
  - What is the rationale for treating tabular data as sequences? Is there theoretical or empirical evidence for advantage? (R4)
