## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes D3AD, a diffusion-based anomaly detection framework that introduces dynamic step-size computation (via KNN-based dynamic implicit conditioning), noiseless input scaling, a latent diffusion model, and a domain adaptation fine-tuning mechanism. Evaluated on VisA and BTAD, it reports strong performance on anomaly localization (especially PRO metric) but shows mixed results on image-level AUROC compared to other diffusion methods.

### Strengths
- The idea of dynamic noise injection / dynamic time stepping for anomaly detection is interesting and makes intuitive sense.
- The method achieves good localization gains, particularly on the PRO metric (e.g., 94.1 on VisA).
- The combination of dynamic conditioning, noiseless input scaling, and latent diffusion is presented as a novel contribution, with state-of-the-art results claimed on VisA and BTAD.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing critical baseline dataset:** D3AD has not been evaluated on MVTec, the most widely used anomaly detection benchmark. Evaluating on VisA and BTAD alone is insufficient to claim state-of-the-art performance without MVTec comparison.
- **Limited novelty and overclaiming:** Several components closely resemble prior work. The conditioned denoising and domain adaptation fine-tuning are similar to DDAD (Anomaly detection with conditioned denoising diffusion models, arXiv 2023), and the anomaly scoring paradigm resembles RD4AD. While the authors and one reviewer consider the contributions novel, two reviewers find the framework a combination of existing ideas with limited originality.
- **Poor clarity and insufficient support for key components:**
  - No rigorous justification for using a noiseless, only-scaled latent (\(z_{\hat{T}}\)) as guidance. It is unclear why this choice is optimal for faithful reconstruction, and the paper lacks theoretical or experimental backing.
  - The dynamic implicit conditioning (DIC) method and the binary search function \(\psi\) used to select the timestep are described only briefly, leaving their operation and rationale obscure.
  - The “predefined histogram” in Figure 3 is not explained; how it is constructed and used is unclear.
  - The impact of the dynamic noise amount on reconstruction quality is not analyzed, and visual results in Figure 3 appear to show poor reconstruction.
  - Missing quantitative and qualitative analyses linking the proposed components (e.g., DIC, noiseless scaling) to the final anomaly detection performance.
- **Performance concerns and questionable state-of-the-art claim:**
  - On image-level AUROC, D3AD underperforms other diffusion methods such as DDAD and DiffusionAD (numeric comparison not fully provided, but noted as weaker).
  - The paper’s claim of state-of-the-art is disputed: For example, method [2] (Remembering normality, ICCV 2023) achieves I-AUC 97.6, P-AUC 98.4, PRO 94.9 on VisA, while D3AD reports 96.0/97.9/94.1. Moreover, DDAD reportedly scores 99.3 I-AUC on VisA, a result not included in the comparison table. Baselines from recent top conferences (ICML, CVPR, ICCV) are missing.
  - The paper does not report all three standard metrics (I-AUROC, P-AUROC, p_PRO) together in a single main table, making comparison difficult.
  - The use of a latent diffusion model (rather than pixel-space) makes the gain over other diffusion methods unclear.
- **Missing details and unanswered questions:**
  - Why was the batch size |B| set to 10? No ablation or reasoning provided.
  - Term “unnoised” is non-standard; should be “noiseless” for clarity.
  - No theoretical or empirical explanation for why noise is not needed during inference.
  - How does D3AD specifically localize anomalies of varying scales? The main body lacks a description and corresponding experiments for this claim.
  - Computational cost (slower sampling due to U-Net and multiple steps) is not reported, nor compared to competitive methods.
- **Presentation quality:** The paper contains grammatical errors, unclear phrasing, and lacks polish. It is described as imprecise and would benefit from careful revision.
- **Low reviewer confidence in soundness and contribution:** Soundness ratings of 2, 2, and 1; contribution ratings of 2, 2, and 1.
