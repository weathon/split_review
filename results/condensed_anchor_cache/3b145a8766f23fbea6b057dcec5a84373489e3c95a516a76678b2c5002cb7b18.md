## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Federated Dual Extrapolation (FeDualEx), a primal-dual algorithm with local updates for distributed composite saddle point problems (SPP). It introduces a new Bregman divergence for saddle functions and a corresponding proximal operator. Convergence analysis and communication complexity are provided for the homogeneous setting. Experiments on synthetic and real datasets show FeDualEx outperforms Federated Mirror Prox and yields better sparse solutions.

### Strengths
- The paper studies federated learning of composite SPP, a largely unexplored setting, and proposes a novel Bregman divergence for saddle functions with an associated proximal operator. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Theoretical contributions: convergence analysis and communication complexity for the homogeneous setting, with rates matching known sequential results when the number of clients is one. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Experimental evaluation on synthetic and real datasets demonstrates that FeDualEx outperforms Federated Mirror Prox, and that the dual extrapolation approach benefits learning sparse solutions. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The proposed method has its own interest and comes with convergence analysis. (Reviewer 2)
- Paper is well written, ideas are clearly explained, well-motivated, and backed by strong theoretical guarantees. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- The paper does not sufficiently explain why existing Bregman divergences and proximal operators are inadequate for composite SPP in the distributed setting, nor the specific technical hurdles that arise in dual extrapolation that necessitate the new definitions. The similarity to Federated Dual Averaging is not clearly differentiated. (Reviewer 1)
- The claim after Definition 3 that previous approaches adding the composite term to the Bregman divergence “may not work for dual extrapolation” needs more specificity – which parts of the analysis break down? (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The claim that the sequential version of FeDualEx yields “the first convergence rate for stochastic optimization of composite SPP in non-Euclidean settings” is incorrect; existing work on operator splitting (e.g., https://epubs.siam.org/doi/epdf/10.1137/20M1381678) should be acknowledged. (Reviewer 2)
- Missed references: [R1] (nonconvex composite, Douglas–Rachford), [R2] and [R3] (non-convex composite, decentralized, without local updates, primal algorithms). The paper should justify why a dual approach is needed despite the success of primal algorithms. (Reviewer 3)
- The analysis relies on bounded stochastic gradient and homogeneity assumptions, which many FL papers avoid; providing analysis without these assumptions would give more generality. (Reviewer 2)
- Mistakes in complexity derivation: in Lemma 3, Lemma 17, and Theorem 3, the condition for η should be η ≤ 1/(√3 β) to ensure 3η²β² – 1 ≤ 0, which changes the final complexity. In Lemma 13, the last two inequalities should involve √... √B, also affecting the final complexity. (Reviewer 2; note: this reviewer had lower confidence, rating soundness 2 and presentation 2, while Reviewers 1 and 3 rated soundness and presentation 3.)
- Table 1 presents complexities strangely: for the strongly-convex–concave case, comparison with previous work should use a standard regularization trick; complexity terms should be added explicitly when there are several terms. (Reviewer 2)
- Theoretical guarantees are independent of the number of clients; the effect of the number of clients and whether FeDualEx achieves linear speed-up should be discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- Inconsistent terminology: distance-generating function is first called strictly convex but later assumed strongly convex; it should be strongly convex from the beginning. (Reviewer 3)
- Definition 3 should define h₁ and h₂ explicitly. (Reviewer 3)
- Appendix is hard to read because lemma numbering does not follow a strict order (e.g., consecutive numbering). (Reviewer 2)
- Several questions indicate unclear aspects: (a) Can a similar algorithm be obtained using only the generalized Bregman divergence on x? (b) The relation between z = (x, y) and ς needs discussion. (c) The convexity assumptions on functions in problem (1) should be stated explicitly. (Reviewer 1)
- Typos: bottom of page 5, second argmin missing a bracket; Definition 4, tη is missing in the subgradient formula. (Reviewer 2)
- Unclear: In Section 4, the term ℓ_{r,k} should be given an exact expression in the main text. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper would benefit from more detailed examples of practical setups requiring distributed SPP (e.g., GANs, multi-agent RL) and how FeDualEx addresses their specific challenges (non-convexity, non-stationarity). (Reviewer 1)
