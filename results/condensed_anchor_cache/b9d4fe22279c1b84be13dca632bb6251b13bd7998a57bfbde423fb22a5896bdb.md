## Merged Review

### Summary

This analysis paper investigates the modality gap and object bias in contrastive vision-language models (VLMs), arguing that information imbalance between images and captions is the root cause of both phenomena. Through experiments on 98 VLMs (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/16, SigLIP ViT-B/16), synthetic data (MAD), and real-world datasets, the authors introduce metrics RMG (to measure modality gap) and MOAD (to measure object bias). They find that the gap is driven by few embedding dimensions, linked to entropy, and that controlling for confounders suggests a smaller gap benefits performance. Experiments with controlled information imbalance (e.g., half/quarter captions) support the hypothesis that reducing imbalance improves attribute recognition.

### Strengths

- Clear introduction of the problem, motivation, and seven concise takeaways. The paper is well-written and provides rich insight into the modality gap from a new information-imbalance perspective.
- Proposed metrics RMG and MOAD are well-designed to intuitively measure modality gap and object bias, addressing limitations of previous measures like L2M.
- Extensive experimental analysis validates the information imbalance hypothesis using fully-controlled synthetic data and covers a large set of models (98 VLMs) and datasets. The connection between modality gap and entropy is intriguing.
- Section on Takeaway 1 effectively demonstrates how controlling for confounding factors (e.g., model size) reveals that a smaller modality gap correlates with better downstream performance (Reviewer 1 finds this convincing).
- The provided appendix is sufficient to follow experimental details and settings.

### Weaknesses

- The main evidence for the claim that information imbalance causes both phenomena and that reducing it improves attribute recognition relies heavily on the simple synthetic dataset MAD. Results on real-world datasets (e.g., RMG) are noticeably weaker and less compelling, raising concerns about generalizability beyond controlled settings. More experiments with diverse architectures, datasets, and better ways to control the number of attributes are needed.
- The claim that “when we control for these factors, we observe the expected negative correlation, i.e., a smaller gap seems to correlate with better performance” (L245) is not well-supported by the presented data: some training datasets (e.g., DataComp-1B, OpenAI) show positive correlations. The phrasing “seems to correlate” is too weak, and a more rigorous statistical analysis is required. (Note: Reviewer 1 found this evidence convincing, creating tension with the insufficient support noted by Reviewer 4.)
- There is a mismatch in the level of information imbalance between synthetic and real data settings. In real data, half/quarter captions are created by randomly dropping parts of captions, which may cause incompleteness rather than pure information imbalance. The paper would benefit from metrics verifying that truncated captions are grammatically perfect but carry less information.
- The paper lacks analysis on actual pretrained VLMs (e.g., pretrained CLIP, SigLIP with large modality gaps). It is unclear whether fine-tuning on an enriched dataset can reverse the process — i.e., reduce the gap and improve performance in such models.
- The paper spends excessive detail on experiments that fail to close the modality gap (Section 4.2). These could be briefly summarized and moved to the appendix, as they distract from the core claim about information imbalance.
- There is insufficient discussion of limitations of the findings and of strategies to mitigate information imbalance (e.g., caption enrichment examples). One reviewer asks for concrete examples of caption enrichment that could effectively reduce imbalance.
- The presentation is marred by excessive use of question-answer patterns (e.g., “Is object bias explained by the global word frequencies of the dataset?”). These could be converted to statements to improve readability.
- Minor issues: typo (“Extend details for Section 5” should be “Extended details for Section 5”), and a request for elaboration on what “the modalities have different local neighborhoods” means (Takeaway 3).
