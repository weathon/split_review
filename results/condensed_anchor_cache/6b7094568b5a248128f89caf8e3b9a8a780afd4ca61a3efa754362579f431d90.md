## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the iteration complexity of sampling from a distributed diffusion model in resource-constrained settings. It derives a generation error bound that is linear in the data dimension \(d\), matching the best known bound in the single-worker setting. The analysis does not assume perfect score approximation, instead showing how arbitrary pruning affects score estimation convergence. The authors show that \(Q = \operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)\) rounds of distributed training suffice to achieve a given total variation sampling error.

### Strengths
- **Novel setting**: This is the first paper to propose a theoretical analysis for distributed diffusion models with arbitrary pruning. The bound matches the best known bound in the single-worker setting, and the work could open the door to further analyses that lead to practical impact. (R1)
- **Practical relevance**: The analysis highlights how resource constraints such as arbitrary pruning affect generation performance, which is valuable for real-world applications. (R2)
- **Bridge with federated diffusion**: The theoretical results align with recent advances in federated diffusion and distributed learning, helping to close gaps in understanding error bounds across distributed workers. (R2)
- **Proof sketch**: The proof sketch is well-presented. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation**: The paper is extremely difficult to read. Theorem statements and corollaries are hard to parse, and the writing is convoluted and unclear. (R1, R2) Specific issues include: lack of equation numbers for important equations, non-descriptive notation for terms \(B_1,\ldots,B_5\) (e.g., should be \(B_1^{(q)}\)) (R3), and an unclear derivation from equation (24) to (25) (R2). The main text needs a complete rewrite focusing on core intuition, with simplified theorem statements. (R1)
- **Incremental / insufficient novelty**: The combination of federated local sparse training with diffusion models appears incremental compared to prior works, in particular Zhou et al. 2024 and Benton et al. 2024. (R1, R2, R3) The paper does not clearly differentiate its contributions or demonstrate substantial technical advancements beyond these results. (R1, R2, R3) Because the convergence of federated local sparse training is already established in Zhou et al. 2024 and is orthogonal to the diffusion model aspect, the overall contribution is ambiguous. (R3) The analysis also focuses on a simplified scenario that does not address heterogeneous data distributions or client drift, which are core challenges in real federated settings. (R2)
- **No empirical validation**: The paper contains no experiments to validate the theoretical error bounds or show practical performance. (R2, R3) Experiments demonstrating how pruning and convergence rates affect actual image generation quality under different distributed configurations would be necessary to assess practical relevance. (R2, R3)
- **Proof clarity / self-containedness**: The proof is not self-contained; readers are left to fill in gaps. (R2) For instance:
  - The derivation of equation (25) from (24) is insufficiently explained. (R2)
  - The proof of Corollary 2 relies heavily on Theorem 1 of Benton et al. 2024 without explaining the required modifications for the distributed setting. (R2)
  - The claimed dependence in Theorem 1 (e.g., \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/(\eta^2 Q^2) \mathbb{E}[\|\theta_0\|^2]\)) is not clearly justified; a direct summation of the given bounds leads to a different dependence, so the derivation needs clarification. (R3)
- **Lack of discussion on practical implications and open problems**: The paper does not explain whether the bound could be improved under Lipschitz score assumptions, what direct practical implications the analysis has, or what open problems it exposes. (R1) 
- **Low practical contribution**: Unless the authors provide additional algorithmic design for training diffusion models using the sparsified update rule (Equation (7)) beyond what is already in the classification-oriented analysis of Zhou et al. 2024, the analysis will have limited impact on advancing distributed diffusion model training. (R3)

**Reviewer disagreement**: There is a strong contrast: one reviewer (R1, score 5) found the paper creative and promising, emphasizing the new setting and matching bound. Two reviewers (R2 and R3, scores 3) considered the contribution incremental, lacking empirical validation, and limited in practical impact. This division highlights the uncertainty about the significance of the theoretical results.
