## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MetaGFN, an exploration strategy for continuous GFlowNets that incorporates Adapted Metadynamics, a variant of metadynamics applicable to arbitrary black-box reward functions on continuous domains. The authors provide theoretical consistency proofs and empirical evaluations on three continuous domains (line, grid, and a low-dimensional environment) showing accelerated convergence and improved mode discovery compared to prior off-policy exploration strategies. Reviewers agree that the problem is important and the cross-disciplinary connection is novel, but concerns about limited experimental scope, unclear selection of collective variables, missing high-dimensional evaluation, high variance in results, and insufficient novelty relative to the combination of existing methods lead to a split in scores (6, 5, 3, 6, 3). Two reviewers found the paper strong (scores 6) while two found it weak (scores 3) and one gave 5.

### Strengths
- Theoretical results are sound and comprehensive, including proof that Adapted Metadynamics reduces to standard metadynamics in a limit (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The research problem—mode exploration in continuous GFlowNets—is fundamental and has received little attention in the literature; the cross-disciplinary adaptation of metadynamics from molecular modelling is novel (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The experimental evaluation is thorough, with multiple baseline techniques adapted from discrete GFlowNets to the continuous case, a faithful comparison, and clear visualization in Figure 5 (Reviewer 4). The proposed method works on the provided environments (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written, clear, and well-structured, with methodology explained in sufficient detail (Reviewers 2, 5).
- MetaGFN empirically outperforms existing GFlowNet exploration strategies on continuous domains (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Selection of collective variables (CVs)**: The process for choosing the low-dimensional CVs $z(x)=(z_1(x),\dots,z_d(x))$ is unclear. No example is given. If CVs rely on expert knowledge, this would limit applicability to domains without such insight (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Experimental details on how CVs are chosen for the line and grid environments are missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited dimensionality and scalability**: Experiments only reach a maximum dimension of 4 in the grid environment, which is not representative of high-dimensional cases. No evaluation on higher-dimensional tasks (e.g., molecule generation, biological sequence design from (Bengio et al., 2021) and (Jain et al., 2022)) is provided. Missing discussion of potential limitations in high-dimensional settings (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Without such evaluation, the method’s significance is diminished (Reviewer 3).
- **High variance and unconvincing results**: The results exhibit high variance (particularly column 2 row 2, column 3 rows 1–2, column 4 rows 1–2, column 5 rows 1–2 in Fig. 4) and high L1 loss values, contradicting the paper’s claims about robustness. The claim that MetaGFN “is the most robust” is unsupported (Reviewer 5). Results are not significantly better than baselines (Reviewer 3). In contrast, Reviewer 4 found the experimental evaluation thorough.
- **Missing experimental details**: The choice of forward and backward kernels is not described; the grid environment is insufficiently detailed (is it the same as [1]?); the condition for terminating states in the hypergrid is unclear (what is $\mathcal{X} \approx \mathbb{R}^d$ and to what extent does the approximation hold?); runtime analysis of baselines is missing (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Intuitive explanation lacking**: Despite theoretical motivation, the paper lacks a high-level intuition of how the method works and why it improves exploration in continuous GFlowNets, making it hard to understand for readers without prior knowledge of metadynamics. Appendix D and Algorithm 3 should be moved to the main text (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **KDE hyperparameters**: The choice of Gaussian width $\sigma$ and Gaussian height $w$ for KDE is not fully explained (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Terminology inconsistency**: The terms “Adaptive Metadynamics” and “Adapted Metadynamics” are used interchangeably; consistency should be maintained (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Missing comparisons and discussions**: The paper does not discuss the MALA-based approach from [2] (Sendera et al.) which shares similarities (off-policy, storing points in a replay buffer). Clarification that MALA requires reward gradients, whereas MetaGFN does not, is needed (Reviewer 4). The method’s applicability to diffusion sampling tasks is not discussed (Reviewer 4). A discussion of how the method works for flow matching is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty concerns**: The paper primarily combines two existing methods (metadynamics and continuous GFlowNets) and the experimental results do not convincingly demonstrate advantages over simpler alternatives; the contribution seems incremental without strong empirical support (Reviewers 3, 5). Some reviewers note that the method works on the provided environments and the connection is novel (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Definition of acyclicity in Measurable pointed graph**: The condition in line 122 might need clarification—specifically, whether bounding the number of steps to reach any measurable set guarantees acyclicity as per [1] (Reviewer 4).
- **Figure quality**: Figure 4 (pdf) loads slowly; reducing grid density could help (Reviewer 4).
