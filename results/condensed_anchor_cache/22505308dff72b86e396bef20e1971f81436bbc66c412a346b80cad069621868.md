## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes robust GNNs (SoftMedian, TWIRLS, ElasticGNN) from a unified robust estimation perspective, identifies estimation bias in ℓ1-based graph smoothing, and proposes an unbiased estimator using a minimax concave penalty (MCP). To solve the resulting non-smooth objective, it develops a Quasi-Newton Iterative Reweighted Least Squares (QN-IRLS) algorithm, which is unfolded as feature aggregation layers (RUNG). Empirical evaluation on Cora-ML and Citeseer with adaptive PGD attacks shows strong robustness, especially under large perturbation budgets.

### Strengths
- Provides a unified view of ℓ1-based robust graph signal smoothing for several established robust GNNs (R1, R2, R3, R4), offering insights into their performance under adversarial attacks (R1). Also considers perturbation transferability (R2).
- Introduces the Quasi-Newton IRLS algorithm (R1, R2, R5) as a novel and practical optimization approach (R1, R2) for an ElasticGNN derivative (R2). Using the MCP penalty helps alleviate estimation bias prevalent in typical GNN models (R5).
- Demonstrates strong empirical performance, especially for large attack budgets (R2), and evaluates the method with adaptive attacks (R2, R4), which strengthens the robustness analysis (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited and insufficient empirical evaluation.**  
  - **Datasets:** Only tested on Cora-ML and Citeseer, both small and homophilic. Larger graphs (e.g., ogbn-arxiv) and heterophilic graphs are needed (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The claim that robust GNNs on heterophilic graphs is largely unexplored is inaccurate, as works like GNNGuard and GARNET address this setting (R4).  
  - **Attack types:** Only adaptive PGD (evasion) is used. Missing poison attacks, white/black-box scenarios, injection/modification types (R1), and attacks like TDGIA, MetaGIA (R5). A comprehensive evaluation with diverse attacks is necessary (R3, R4, R5).  
  - **Statistical reliability:** For small attack budgets, accuracy gaps are often smaller than the standard deviation, and only 5 random splits are used; more splits or significance tests are needed to show robustness (R4).  
- **Missing computational complexity and scalability analysis.**  
  No discussion of time or memory complexity (R1, R2, R3, R4). Without this, the practical feasibility and scalability to large graphs remain unclear (R2, R3, R4). The QN-IRLS approach for scalability is not validated on any large dataset (R4).  
- **Insufficient theoretical support and unclear claims.**  
  - The central claim that ℓ1-based estimation introduces significant bias under heterophilic edge attacks lacks rigorous proof (R1). The numerical simulation in Figure 2 has issues: unclear outlier definition, unexplained behavior in the third plot, and a simplified single-step approximation that does not convincingly demonstrate robustness in a full GNN (R1).  
  - The statement that the estimator “recovers the true mean regardless of the outlier ratio” is misleading; the breakdown point of a location estimator is at most 50% (R2).  
  - Unbiasedness of the proposed RUNG estimator is not rigorously proven (R5, also questioned by R2).  
  - The derivation of \(W\) in eq(7) is unclear (R1); R3 also asks for details on computing the derivative. The MCP function is not defined in eq(4) and (7) (R3). Notations are inconsistent: superscript (0) in eq(1) is undefined, \(\ell_2\) is defined as the square of the 2-norm (technically not a norm), and the use of \(\ell_1\) is ambiguous (R5).  
  - Overstatement: the claim that the framework “covers many classic GNNs as special cases” is inaccurate; it offers only an approximate perspective (R1).  
- **Missing ablation and hyperparameter sensitivity.**  
  No ablation study on design choices such as penalty function, choice of optimization algorithm (IRLS vs QN-IRLS), or hyperparameters \(\lambda\) and \(\gamma\) (R2, R3, R4). Their impact on performance is unclear, and guidance for selection across datasets/attack scenarios is lacking (R4). Also absent: a demonstration of where the method fails (e.g., extending Figure 2 beyond the breakdown point) (R2).  
- **Incomplete baselines.**  
  Several recent robust GNNs are not compared, e.g., “On the robustness of graph neural diffusion” (NeurIPS 2022) and “Graph-coupled oscillator networks” (ICLR 2022) (R5); GNNGuard, GARNET (R4). SVD-GCN is mentioned as a baseline but its results are not shown (R4). Most citations are from 2021 or earlier (R5).  
- **Inadequate discussion of limitations.**  
  The method relies on a strong homophily assumption; its performance on heterophilic graphs is not addressed, and the paper should explicitly discuss this limitation (R1, R4). R1 also notes that the method likely prunes heterophilic edges like other defenses. R2 asks for a failure analysis. R4 questions the practical relevance of very large attack budgets.  
- **Presentation and notation issues.**  
  R5: The paper is not well-written; key notations are undefined (superscript (0), \(\ell_1\)/\(\ell_2\) norms). R1: citation style is inconsistent. R3: MCP function not defined. R4: typo in Table 2 (runner-up under 5% attack on CoraML is SoftMedian, not RUNG-\(l_1\)). R4: notation \(\beta\) used twice in different contexts. Code not provided (R3).

**Additional minor points:**  
- R2: It would be interesting to see how RUNG perturbations transfer to other defenses, and to add notes on composing multiple GNN layers in Section 2.2.  
- R1: The method’s applicability may be limited to homophilic datasets; this should be acknowledged.  
- R4: Questions about adaptive PGD on Jaccard-GCN and the practical scenario for large-budget attacks.  
- R5: Asking if the quasi-Newton approach is related to BFGS/Broyden family.
