## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces sampling methods to accelerate attention computation in LLMs, providing theoretical analysis for distribution-specific samplers and sampling scenarios. All four reviewers note a severe lack of experimental validation (R1, R2, R3, R4). Three reviewers (score 3) consider this a critical deficiency that prevents verification of claimed efficiency and performance gains; one reviewer (score 5) is more positive about the theoretical contributions but also asks for practical demonstration.

### Strengths
- **Important and timely topic**: Accelerating attention in LLMs is a key problem (R1, R3, R4).  
- **Comprehensive theoretical analysis**: The paper offers extensive theoretical results for different distribution-specific samplers and sampling scenarios, demonstrating deep expertise (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Clear and precise notation and logical writing**: Notations are well-defined and consistently applied, and the theoretical framework is robustly presented (R2, R3).  
- **Potential impact on modern AI systems**: Speeding up attention could accelerate a vast number of systems (R4).  
- **Minority reviewer (score 5) is more positive**: R4 rates the paper higher, citing strong theoretical results and potential impact, while the other three reviewers (score 3) are far more critical due to missing experiments.

### Weaknesses
- **No empirical experiments**: The paper provides zero experimental validation of the claimed computational cost reduction, speedup, or performance maintenance/enhancement (R1, R2, R3, R4). R1 notes this contradicts the stated "detailing of implementation strategies and resultant gains." R2 highlights that the claim to "maintain or enhance model performance" is unproven even though the paper includes a limitation section. R3 states that the absence of computational experiments fundamentally undermines the contribution and falls below ICLR quality standards. R4 asks for example scenarios showing significant speedup with reasonable precision drop to corroborate theoretical bounds.
- **Practical applicability to current LLM architectures unexplored**: No experiments or analysis on popular models like LLaMA or Mistral, nor comparative performance/cost with and without the proposed methods (R3, Q1 by R3).
- **No comparison to existing attention-acceleration frameworks**: The paper does not benchmark against prior work or clarify what specific challenges it solves that were previously overlooked (R2, Q2 by R2).
- **Novelty and impact not clearly articulated**: The community impact is unclear; which problems does the theory address that were difficult or unsolved? (R2).
- **Lacks robustness analysis**: No discussion of method behavior under failure scenarios, adversarial conditions, stress, or corrupted data, which is important for real-world deployment (R3, Q3 by R3).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms** (retained as specific missing experiments/comparisons):  
  - How do the proposed samplers behave in practice regarding speedup/scalability of the attention operation itself and of training/testing with a transformer model that implements them? (R4, Q1).  
  - Can the methods be integrated into current LLMs, and what are the performance and costs? (R3, Q1).  
  - In what cases do the methods work or fail? More discussion on limitations is needed (R3, Q3).
