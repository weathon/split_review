## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses bias and unexpected performance degradation in LLMs during prompting and in-context learning (ICL). It first provides a systematic analysis of existing calibration methods, unifying them by examining decision boundaries. The authors then propose Batch Calibration (BC), a zero-shot, inference-only method that models contextual bias from the batched input. An extension BCL introduces a learnable parameter for few-shot black-box adaptation. Experiments on more than 10 NLU and image classification tasks with PaLM 2 (S, M, L) and CLIP demonstrate state-of-the-art performance over previous calibration baselines.

### Strengths
- The proposed Batch Calibration (BC) method is simple and empirically effective (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper provides a thorough, systematic examination of existing calibration methods, filling a gap in the literature and offering a unified view of their decision boundaries. The revisiting of previous methods is considered novel to the community (R2, R3, R4).
- BC is zero-shot, inference-only, computationally efficient, and reduces prompt sensitivity, facilitating easier prompt engineering (R1, R2).
- The scope is broadened compared to prior work such as Contextual Calibration (CB), which only studied GPT-2 biases; BC is evaluated on multiple model families and tasks (R2).
- Extensive experiments on over 10 NLU and image classification tasks with PaLM 2 (S, M, L) and CLIP demonstrate state-of-the-art performance over previous calibration baselines on most tasks (R1, R3).
- The paper is technically sound and well-written according to most reviewers, though one reviewer found the presentation only fair (R1 vs. R2,R3,R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient theoretical justification and analysis (R1, R3, R4):** The method is not thoroughly justified. The argument that linear decision boundaries from calibration methods are more robust/generalizable is not well supported theoretically or empirically. The advantage of BC over existing methods needs more intuitive descriptions and theoretical analysis. The derivation details of Table 1 are not easy to follow. One reviewer noted that the improvement from BC to BCL appears marginal in Figure 3, raising questions about the practical significance of the added learnable parameter γ; analysis of when BCL provides substantial improvement vs. not is missing and the added complexity may not always be justified.

2. **Insufficient exploration of the BCL strength parameter γ (R3, R4):** Only two tasks (CB, SST-2) are shown with γ variation; it is unclear if the strong performance at γ=1 generalizes to other tasks. The optimal γ per task is not reported (e.g., not included in Table 2). The range [0,5] for γ is not justified; a wider range (e.g., [0,5]) and more comprehensive sensitivity analysis across tasks and datasets are needed to understand the parameter's impact and whether γ=1 is a consistent finding or an artifact of chosen tasks. The examples provided are insufficient to fully understand the impact of this hyperparameter.

3. **Unsubstantiated claim about emoji verbalizers (R3):** In Section 4.3, the claim that BC retains performance even when using emoji pairs as verbalizers is not supported by any empirical evidence, example, or result. Lack of detailed analysis across different emoji pairs weakens the argument.

4. **Writing and presentation issues (R1, R2, R4):**  
   - The analysis of existing methods and the proposed method could be better written (R1).  
   - Derivation of Table 1 is not easy to understand (R1).  
   - The literature review of test-time tuning is considered a bit unnecessary because ICL is not a tuning method (R2).  
   - Subfigure margins in Fig. 2 are overlapped and need adjustment (R2).  
   - The paper should cite "Survival of the Most Influential Prompts: Efficient Black-Box Prompt Search via Clustering and Pruning" correctly as accepted by EMNLP-2023 (R4).  
   - Related work is placed in Section 5; introducing it in Section 2 would give better background (R4).  
   - Unify the usage of abbreviated words (e.g., "Tab." → "Table") (R4).

5. **Confusion over the term "calibration" (R2):** The term calibration is overloaded; another widely used notion relates to uncertainty, explainability, and reliability. The authors should either use a different term (e.g., "bias") or add a footnote in the Introduction to clarify.

6. **Missing model details and reproducibility concerns (R2, R3):** Model sizes and details should be included, as ICL and prompting performance is highly irreproducible and context-dependent. One reviewer noted high variance across model architectures in the 1-shot MNLI setting: 75.12/60.02/81.34 for PaLM-2-S/M/L (PaLM-2-M from a single run). Such variance needs explanation. Additionally, the question of how significant the bias/miscalibration is for larger models (e.g., 65B Llama2, GPT-3.5, GPT-4) is raised, as the method does not require tuning (R2).

7. **Minor overhead concern (R2):** In highly resource-constrained environments, even the small computational overhead of BC might be significant.

8. **No broader impact or limitation discussion (R4):** The paper does not include any broader impact or limitation discussion.

9. **Questions that also serve as criticisms (R1, R2, R3):**  
   - (R3) How to obtain the contextual prior p̂ in Eq. (3)?  
   - (R3) How would BC work on tasks other than classification?  
   - (R2) In Fig. 5, why does a strength greater than 1 consistently decrease accuracy?  
   - (R1) The advantage of BC over existing methods is not thoroughly justified; more intuitive descriptions and theoretical analysis are needed.
