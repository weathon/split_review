## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ST-GCond, a self-supervised and transferable graph dataset condensation method for cross-dataset and cross-task scenarios. It uses two components: task-disentangled meta optimization to adapt condensed graphs to task relevance, and multi-teacher self-supervised optimization to inject universal knowledge. The method leverages multiple pre-trained models as teachers. Evaluations on node- and graph-level datasets show gains of 2.5%–18.7% over existing methods in cross-task and cross-dataset settings, and state-of-the-art results on 5 out of 6 datasets in the single-task/single-dataset setting. Reviewers found the transferability topic important, the performance improvements nontrivial, and the writing clear. One reviewer (score 8) was more positive about the method’s cross-task/dataset performance and its ability to overcome traditional condensation limitations, while the other two (score 6 each) noted concerns about fairness and computational overhead but acknowledged promising results.

### Strengths
- The transferability of condensed graphs is an important and under-explored topic; this is the first effort to address cross-dataset and cross-task graph condensation.
- The proposed method achieves nontrivial performance gains in both cross-task/dataset scenarios and the single-dataset/task scenario (2.5%–18.7% over baselines).
- ST-GCond is applicable to both node-level and graph-level tasks, making it more general.
- The combination of multi-task learning and self-supervised learning in dataset condensation is novel and may inspire further research.
- The use of multiple pre-trained models as teachers captures a wide range of features and knowledge, improving generalization.
- The motivation is clear and the introduction is well-written.

### Weaknesses
- **Mutual Information Guided Joint Condensation**: It is unclear why performance drops when using both the hard label from supervised condensation and the soft label from self-supervised condensation. The authors argue this is due to conflict but give no further explanation.
- **Overlap with model pre-training**: The cross-task and cross-dataset settings overlap with graph model pre-training (which also enables fast adaptation). A more detailed discussion of differences is needed.
- **Ablation contradiction**: Ablation results show that using only the “self” part or only the “meta” part achieves the best performance on some datasets, yet the paper states “ST-GCond w/o self and ST-GCond w/o meta perform poorly on both datasets.” This discrepancy requires clarification.
- **Fairness of comparisons**: The proposed method uses more information during condensation (multi-label meta-training data and multiple self-supervised teachers) than baselines, making the comparison potentially unfair.
- **Dependence on multi-task information**: What if the dataset to be condensed does not have multi-task or auxiliary self-supervised task information?
- **Computational overhead**: The method introduces substantial overhead due to task-disentangled meta optimization (iterative updates per sub-task) and multi-teacher self-supervised optimization (multiple models). A detailed analysis of training time and GPU memory usage compared to multiple baselines is needed—the appendix currently only compares one baseline.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and values**: Sensitivity analysis and guidelines for hyperparameters (e.g., number of sub-tasks, learning rates) are needed. Additionally, the actual values of α and β (and their change during training) in the final loss function are not provided, only the search space.
- **Task disparity limits**: Significant disparities between tasks may limit the condensed graph’s ability to capture all relevant information, reducing effectiveness.
- **Quality of pre-trained models**: The multi-teacher strategy’s effectiveness depends on the quality of pre-trained models. It is unclear whether low-quality or irrelevant models impair performance.
- **Teacher weight optimization**: The paper does not explain how the weights assigned to each teacher model’s output are optimized during training.
- **Visualization and statistics**: Visualization of condensed graphs and discussion of their statistical characteristics would make the results more credible.
- **Finetune step in cross settings**: In cross-dataset/task scenarios, a finetune step on the original downstream data is used (i.e., actual training data plus condensed data). It is unclear what happens without this finetune step, and using actual data may reduce the contribution.
- **Ablation on loss terms**: The ablation study on each loss term shows results, but the reasons for these results are not explained.
- **Justification of “most important step”**: In Line 222, the paper claims “The most important step is to utilize fast adaptation in Gs.” It is unclear what makes this step the most important.
- **Unexplained term in Appendix G.1**: The final matrix is subtracted by a δ term that appears without explanation.
- **Typos**: Missing period at the end of the caption (Line 396); “global minimum” should be “global minimum” (Line 214).
