## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes InterAct, an interaction-based framework for temporal action segmentation using RGB and skeleton data. It introduces Multi-scale Frame-Action Interaction (MFAI) for multi-scale temporal dynamics and Decoupled Spatial Modality Interaction (DSMI) to better align spatial features with temporal modules. The method is evaluated on six large-scale benchmarks and reports performance improvements over existing methods.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and clearly outlines its motivations. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- The idea of using a shared backbone for both skeleton-based and RGB-feature action segmentation is promising. (Reviewer 3)
- The method achieves good performance and shows substantial improvements on six diverse datasets over competitive benchmarks. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- Reviewer 2 noted a certain degree of originality in the proposed MFAI and DSMI components, though this was contested by other reviewers (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unsupported claim about over-smoothing and blurred action boundaries** (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3): The core motivation—that frame-action interactions at a fixed temporal resolution cause over-smoothing—lacks rigorous experimental support.  
   - Reviewer 2 suggests concrete ablations: compare t-SNE visualizations of InterAct with and without the boundary prediction module (BPM); compare InterAct with a variant where the encoder-decoder is replaced by MS-TCN (which operates at fixed full temporal resolution); and replace label smoothing with the Average used by FACT to isolate effects.  
   - Reviewer 3 requests a quantitative measure of feature smoothness correlated with boundary detection performance, clarification of how Figure 4 supports a causal link, and an explanation of why **three stages** of frame-action modeling (Table 6) inherently prevent over-smoothing rather than merely being an empirical optimum.  
   - Without these experiments, the motivation remains speculative and the analysis lacks persuasiveness.

2. **Limited novelty and borrowed ideas** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3):  
   - Reviewer 1 states that multi-scale temporal modeling has been well studied by many prior works, so novelty is limited.  
   - Reviewer 3 details that multi-scale temporal resolution and multi-scale outputs within an encoder-decoder model have already been explored in **Singhania et al. (2023)**, and that the segment-level learnable queries with cross-attention closely resemble techniques in **Behrmann et al. (2022)**. The paper must clearly articulate how MFAI differs from or improves upon these specific prior works.  
   - Reviewer 2 also notes that **BIT** uses frame-action interaction and **ASPnet** uses multimodal fusion similar to DSMI, further underscoring the need for explicit differentiation.

3. **Missing recent SOTA comparisons and benchmark results** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3):  
   - All three reviewers note that the paper omits comparisons with several strong recent methods: **Semantic2Graph, ASPnet, and BIT**.  
   - Reviewer 3 additionally points out that results for **DiffAct, UVAST, and LTContext** are missing on the **MCFS-130** dataset in Table 1, even though the code for these methods is publicly available.  
   - Including these comparisons is essential to assess relative performance, especially since some of these baselines also adopt frame-action interactions or modality fusion strategies.

4. **Inadequate ablation study and experimental scope** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3):  
   - Reviewer 2 requests an ablation that replaces the encoder-decoder with **MS-TCN** or **ASFormer** (which operate at fixed full resolution) to test the fixed-resolution over-smoothing claim.  
   - Reviewer 3 notes that ablations in Tables 4 and 5 are conducted on different datasets, and no RGB-specific ablation is provided on the **Breakfast** dataset—a large RGB action segmentation benchmark—raising concerns about generalizability.  
   - Reviewer 3 also identifies the absence of an ablation on the number of encoder and decoder layers, which directly influences temporal resolution granularity.  
   - Reviewer 1 questions whether early fusion of RGB and skeleton data (i.e., using both modalities as input to a single model) has been tested; no experiment addressing this is provided.

5. **Insufficient explanation of spatial-temporal misalignment correction** (Reviewer 3): The paper claims DSMI addresses misalignment between spatial features and temporal modules in skeleton-based TAS, but it does not elaborate on the specific mechanisms within DSMI that ensure better alignment beyond general fusion claims. More detailed explanation is needed.

6. **Language and presentation issues** (Reviewer 2):  
   - Specific grammar errors: (a) "suffers from" should be "suffer from" (page 4); (b) "frame and action level" should be "frame and action levels" (Section 2.1); (c) "as follow" should be "as follows" (before eq. 8); (d) "D is the feature dimensions" needs revision.  
   - Inconsistent acronyms: both **MFAI** and **MAFI** appear; the paper should use a single acronym consistently and include a list of abbreviations.  
   - A professional proofread or review by a native English speaker is recommended.

7. **Missing ablation for encoder and decoder layers** (Reviewer 3): As noted in point 4, an ablation on layer depth is missing; this is critical because layer depth directly affects temporal resolution granularity and the multi-scale modeling claims.
