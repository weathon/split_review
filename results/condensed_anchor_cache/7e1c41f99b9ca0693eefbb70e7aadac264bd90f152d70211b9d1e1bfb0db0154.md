## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an autoencoder architecture using cross attention to embed and reconstruct “oddly shaped” Total Electron Content (TEC) data for ionospheric modeling. The goal is to obtain a global embedding and produce completed TEC maps. Reviewers generally found the application domain interesting (ionospheric prediction) but raised major concerns about clarity, methodological novelty, comparisons, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- The problem setting—ionospheric modeling using TEC data—is important and well motivated. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The paper is well structured and uses proper language; the experimentation section is thorough. (Reviewer 3).
- The application of an autoencoder to this uncommon problem is novel and achieves results of reasonable quality (e.g., R² of 0.93 in Figure 6). (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **No methodological innovation.** The approach applies existing cross-attention and autoencoder techniques to a new dataset without proposing new architecture or learning principles. (Reviewer 3: “does not pass the bar for methodological contributions”; Reviewer 2: motivation unclear, data is essentially sparse/missing data; Reviewer 1: claim of “oddly shaped data” is fuzzy, data is like images with missing values.)
- **Arbitrary sequence representation of 2D spatial data.** The method flattens lat/lon coordinates into a 1D sequence, which is arbitrary because there is no natural order among measurement points. Transformers cannot learn meaningful spatial co-occurrences from a random ordering. Image-based approaches (masked autoencoders, inpainting) are more appropriate. (Reviewer 1, citing Pathak et al. 2016, He et al. 2021, Li et al. 2021, Kakogeorgiou et al. 2022; Reviewer 2 notes no clear mathematical description; Reviewer 4 suggests comparing with CNN/ViT.)
- **Insufficient comparison to competing methods.** The paper dismisses prior GAN-based TEC completion work (SNP-GAN, VISTA) as introducing bias, but does not compare against them. Modern GANs also have latent spaces; GAN inversion could be used to obtain latent codes for direct comparison in the downstream regression task. (Reviewer 1). Only input variants are compared, not different ML algorithms. (Reviewer 4). The literature review omits many relevant ML-ionospheric physics papers. (Reviewer 3).
- **Marginal empirical improvement without significance analysis.** R² improves from 0.915 to 0.93 (Figure 6). Figure 7 shows no significant correlation improvement for “DINN_eTEC”. Figure 8 (top) shows no significant MAE reduction for “hmf2”. The magnitude of change considered significant in ionospheric modeling is not discussed. (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing implementation details and reproducibility concerns.** No code is provided. Key details not described: (a) what is the “learnable starting vector” in Figure 2? (b) how is the latent space constructed (random or deterministic)? (c) what is the loss function—does it reconstruct all channels (e.g., lon/lat)? (d) how exactly are TEC maps completed for missing pixels? (e) hyperparameters (optimizer, learning rate, layer sizes) are absent. (Reviewers 1, 4). Variable names like Kp index, ap index, Dst are introduced but not defined. (Reviewer 4). The paper lacks a clear mathematical description of the data and method. (Reviewer 2).
- **Weak and unclear recurrent processing attempt.** The paragraph in line 131 describes using the autoencoder output as input for a recurrent process, but this is poorly described and technically weak. (Reviewer 4).
- **Confounding factors not thoroughly explored.** The paper hints at controlling for time and place, but a deeper investigation of other confounders is missing. (Reviewer 3; question: what are the main confounding factors beyond time and place?).
- **Minority reviewer notes experimentation as thorough, but others disagree.** Reviewer 3 praises thorough experimentation, while Reviewers 1 and 4 find comparisons insufficient and significance unreported. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Definition of “oddly shaped data” remains unclear** and is not distinguished from standard “missing data” or “irregularly sampled data” problems. Examples beyond TEC are not provided. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4; questions: “How is oddly shaped data different from missing data?”, “Can you provide a definition and more examples?”).
