## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SCALE, a framework that integrates compact Specialized Translation Models (STMs) with large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4. For each translation example, a triplet is formed: source sentence, STM-generated translation, and ground-truth reference. Ten such triplets are provided as in-context demonstrations, enabling the LLM to refine the STM output, perform pivoting, or update its translations without fine-tuning. Experiments focus on X→En low-resource settings, showing improvements over both few-shot GPT-4 and dedicated models such as NLLB. The method is also applied to pivoting (e.g., LAO→En) and updating (Xhosa→En). Ablation studies verify the contribution of each component.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The idea is simple yet effective; SCALE shows substantial improvement for low-resource X→En translation (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The framework is useful across three different scenarios (refinement, pivoting, updating) in nearly the same way (Reviewer 2).
- According to evaluations, SCALE outperforms both the STM and few-shot GPT-4 (Reviewer 2).
- The ablation study convincingly demonstrates the essential role of each component (Reviewer 3).
- The work is comprehensive, with main experiments and analysis clearly conveying the authors’ perspective (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Engineering-oriented, limited scientific insight**: The idea is straightforward and the experiments are more engineering-focused than scientifically insightful (Reviewer 1).
- **Weak baselines**: Comparison baselines are not strong. Prior techniques like back-translation, pretraining, and other low-resource language improvements may require fewer resources and perform better (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: SCALE involves both STM decoding and LLM decoding; the inference cost is not examined (Reviewer 1).
- **Restricted to X→En and low-resource languages**: (a) Only translation into English is tested; the paper does not demonstrate En→X translation, where refinement may be ineffective because GPT-4 is weak in low-resource languages (Reviewer 3). (b) High-resource language pairs (e.g., German, French, Chinese ↔ English) are not evaluated, leaving unclear whether SCALE offers any improvement there (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Limited validation of pivoting and updating**: The pivoting experiment is restricted to LAO→En, and the updating experiment only to Xhosa→En. More comprehensive experiments across additional directions are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Perplexity and fluency evaluation concerns**: The analysis in §5.1 uses perplexity of GPT2-XL to claim fluency improvement. However, lower perplexity on GPT2-XL does not guarantee that larger LMs (e.g., GPT-4) prefer the refined translations (Reviewer 2). Using GPT-2 to evaluate GPT-4’s fluency is questionable; a more suitable model should be considered (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear interpretation of NM and USW metrics**: It is not obvious why STM achieves higher NM (likely more literal) than GPT-4, yet SCALE produces even lower NM than GPT-4. Also, it is unclear whether higher or lower NM/USW is desirable; GPT-4 might better understand the source, making its translations more figurative—contradicting the claim that STM is more figurative (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of analysis on demonstration selection**: Since the LLM learns from the triplet examples, the choice of demonstrations could be critical, but its effect is not studied (Reviewer 2).
- **Unsubstantiated claim about bias mitigation**: The paper states that SCALE “mitigates both language bias and parallel data bias,” but this claim feels intuitive rather than factual. It should be supported by clear definitions and quantitative analysis (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 3) assigns lower contribution rating (2/4 vs. 3/4 from others)** and scores the paper 5 vs. 6, indicating stronger reservations about the limited scope and the lack of solid evidence for some claims.
