## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the LUOS model (DELUCA neural architecture) for high-rank matrix completion. It extends the union-of-subspaces (UoS) model by learning a UoS in a nonlinear latent embedding, using an auto-encoder with a novel pseudo-completion layer (two trainable fully connected layers) to impute missing entries before embedding, and a self-expressive layer in the latent space for clustering. Experiments are performed on synthetic data and three real datasets (COIL20, Extended Yale B, ORL) under uniform random missingness, evaluating clustering accuracy and reconstruction error. Reviewers note the idea is simple and the method shows strong absolute performance, but raise major concerns about novelty, baseline selection, theoretical grounding, clarity, and evaluation scope.

### Strengths
- Unlike standard linear subspace models, the method leverages a nonlinear latent space via deep learning, which appears effective in capturing non-linear relationships in incomplete data (R1, R4).
- Substantial empirical improvements are reported: ~40% higher clustering accuracy than the nearest baseline across real datasets, and strong reconstruction even at high missing rates (R1).
- The method is simple and easy to follow; the motivation is adequate (R2, R3).
- Compared to linear subspace clustering methods, the deep auto-encoder approach performs very well in an absolute sense (R3).
- Figure 4 (architecture diagram) is nicely presented (R3).
- The authors honestly admit that synthetic data results are not strong, showing some objectivity (R3).
- Extending HRMC to nonlinear structures is a reasonable direction (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis / empirical validation:** There is no analysis of how the latent UoS transformation impacts performance across different data distributions, nor discussion of potential observational bias from using existing entries to impute missing values in the pseudo-completion layer. Choices of regularizations, initialization, step size, and stopping criteria are not justified theoretically or empirically (R1, R2).
- **Extremely limited originality:** The core novelty is adding two fully connected layers (pseudo-completion layer) at the beginning of the architecture from [1] (Deep Subspace Clustering Networks). The authors state this is where they mostly contribute, but the layer is not properly explained. The description (“two flattened, partially linked layers… normalize observed elements… replace missing entries”) lacks a clear mathematical formulation; lines 260–267 add little and include an overleaf comment (“For the earlier version… these layers were fully connected”) suggesting incomplete editing (R3). (Minority view: Reviewer 1 found the latent space approach novel.)
- **Cherrypicked / incomplete baselines:** Missing critical comparisons:
    - Traditional and deep matrix factorization: PMF, RPCA, NCF (R1).
    - The baseline from [1] (Deep Subspace Clustering Networks) *without* the pseudo-completion layer – a natural ablation not performed (R3).
    - Autoencoders with Intrinsic Dimension Constraints [4] (results on ImageNet for the same task) (R3).
    - Correntropy-Induced L2 Graph for subspace clustering with missing entries [5] (R3).
    - Tensor decomposition methods (R4).
    - VAE-based methods for missing data, e.g., Mattei & Frellsen (2019) MIWAE which shows zero-imputation works well for VAEs – not compared, yet the pseudo-completion layer’s advantage over simple imputation is unsubstantiated (R4).
    - Most baselines are too old to represent the state of the art (R1).
- **Inadequate experiments:**
    - Experiments are on small datasets (COIL20, Extended Yale B, ORL); results on larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) are needed (R3).
    - Only uniform random missingness is studied – other missing patterns (e.g., block missing, structural missing) are not considered (R3, R4).
    - Synthetic experiments are inconclusive: the proposed method is outperformed by some baselines in several settings (R3; note R1 described the method as “robust” on synthetic data, indicating a contrast).
- **Scalability issue:** Self-expressive layers require access to the full dataset for similarity calculations, hindering scalability to extremely large datasets that do not fit in memory (R1).
- **Poor presentation / clarity:**
    - The pseudo-completion layer is not clearly explained – it lacks equations, and it is unclear whether the layers are fully connected or not; the description is insufficient for reproducibility (R3, R4). The vague statement “we can concurrently do both goals” and missing article in title at line 268 further harm clarity (R3).
    - The abstract mentions “exponential memory requirements” without any subsequent discussion (R4).
    - The introduction rambles with unrelated references (DDPM, GAN) and does not emphasize the core problems of learning nonlinear embeddings and handling highly sparse observations (R4).
    - The title is not informative and does not represent the contribution (R1).
    - Multiple typos and formatting issues: Line 92 citation format, missing space after period (Line 119), “Architectural novelty” as a standalone sentence (Line 131), “psuedo” in Figure 4, “enconder” in Figure 4 caption, Figure 6 axis label “completion accuracy” is misleading (it reports error norm, not accuracy), inconsistent keyword highlighting, and poor section structure (COIL20/Extended Yale B/ORL listed at same level as “Real Data”) (R2, R3, R4).
    - Figure 3 resolution is poor (R3).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms:** Clarify notation: what do ℓ₁ and ℓ₂ norms refer to when θ is a matrix? (R2) – this is a missing explanation that weakens soundness.
