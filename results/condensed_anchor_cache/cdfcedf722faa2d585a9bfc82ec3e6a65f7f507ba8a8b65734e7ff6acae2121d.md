## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers largely acknowledge the paper's novel contribution: ProLIP, a probabilistic vision-language model pre-trained at billion-scale using an uncertainty token and inclusion loss to capture many-to-many image-text relationships. The model demonstrates strong zero-shot classification (e.g., 74.6% ImageNet accuracy with ViT-B/16), and uncertainty estimates align with human intuitions. However, most reviewers (ratings 6) note that improvements over deterministic CLIP are modest (≈0.9% on ImageNet), scalability gains are marginal, and the evaluation lacks depth on complex queries and downstream tasks. One reviewer (rating 8) found the originality and significance substantially stronger, particularly the probabilistic formulation and efficient uncertainty estimation, while still identifying areas for improvement in interpretability and task scope.

### Strengths
- **Novel probabilistic VLM formulation**: First fully probabilistic VLM pre-trained on a billion-scale dataset; uses a multivariate Gaussian for each embedding to model many-to-many relationships (R1, R3, R4).
- **Efficient uncertainty estimation via uncertainty token ([UNC])**: No extra parameters; simply increases input length; computationally lightweight (R1, R4; R5 questions efficiency but acknowledges the design).
- **Novel inclusion loss**: Enforces distributional inclusion (e.g., general captions cover specific ones) and between original/masked inputs; enhances interpretability (R1, R3).
- **Well-motivated problem**: Addresses real-world ambiguity that CLIP’s deterministic one-to-one assumption oversimplifies; many-to-many matching within a batch is clearly explained (R2, R5).
- **Solid mathematical foundation**: Loss derivations and theoretical support are strong (R2).
- **Extensive experiments**: Pre-training on billions of image-text pairs; zero-shot evaluation across multiple datasets; comparisons with CLIP and probabilistic baselines (PCME++); ablation studies (R1, R2).
- **Clear writing and good structure**: Well-organized, definitions, and figures aid understanding (R1, R2, R5).
- **Insightful uncertainty analysis**: Shows shorter texts have higher uncertainty, general inputs include specific ones, and image uncertainty is lower than text; aligns with human expectations (R1, R3).
- **Practical applications demonstrated**: Uncertainty used for image caption traversal and prompt enhancement (zero-shot classification improvement from 74.6% to 75.8%), showing real-world utility (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited improvement over deterministic CLIP**: On ImageNet zero-shot, ProLIP achieves only 0.9% absolute gain (ViT-B) and average 0.7% across datasets (Table 1); ViT-L results (Table C.1) show similarly marginal gains, suggesting scalability challenges (R2, R5). One reviewer (R5) notes this limits the contribution compared to PCME++.
2. **Computational efficiency concerns not quantified**: The uncertainty token increases sequence length, impacting transformer quadratic attention cost; zero-shot prediction requires Complex Singular Detour (CSD) rather than simple cosine similarity, adding complexity. No empirical comparison of training time, inference latency, or FLOPs vs. CLIP or PCME++ is provided (R5). This includes a request for quantitative efficiency metrics (R5 question).
3. **Narrow evaluation on single-object, simple queries**: Main results focus on ImageNet (single-object); Table C.1 shows ProLIP underperforms CLIP on Flickr and other datasets with longer, complex queries. The paper lacks analysis of why this drop occurs and does not include these results in the main table (R4). This overlaps with limited task diversity: only zero-shot classification, image traversal, and prompt rewriting are explored; important downstream tasks (captioning, VQA, data filtering, error estimation, retrieval diversity) are missing (R1, R3). A detailed breakdown of performance on multi-object or complex scene datasets is needed (R4 question).
4. **Interpretability and hierarchy alignment not rigorously demonstrated**: Visualizations are abstract; no direct comparison with deterministic models on hierarchical embedding structures (e.g., using datasets with explicit hierarchies). The claim that uncertainty estimates align with human intuition lacks a user study or quantitative human evaluation (R1). Additionally, the observation that single-object images have higher uncertainty may be a dataset artifact (R4 question; also R3 question about smaller image variance being a product of loss design).
5. **Motivation overclaims**: The paper states that deterministic VLMs assume a 1‑1 mapping and that VL tasks suffer from uncertainty (Sec 2.1–2.2). However, contrastive learning mathematically samples negatives from independent distributions, not forcing 1‑1 mapping; the claim about suffering from uncertainty lacks direct experimental evidence in Sec 2.1 (R3). This overclaim is considered a writing issue but should be corrected (R3). (Other reviewers did not raise this concern.)
6. **Gaussian single-mean cannot capture multi-modes**: The many-to-many intuition implies multiple distinct captions per image (e.g., “a dog” vs. “a man looking at a building”), but the probabilistic model uses a unimodal Gaussian with a single mean, limiting its ability to cover multiple modes (R3).
7. **Missing human evaluation for uncertainty alignment**: No user study or human rating comparison is conducted to validate that model uncertainty scores match human perception (R1). This is requested as a future work (R1 question), but the paper’s claims would be strengthened by including such evaluation.
8. **No exploration of few-shot learning**: The paper only evaluates zero-shot; questions about whether ProLIP can be adapted for few-shot settings and what modifications would be needed are not addressed (R1 question).
9. **Loss design justification incomplete**: The Probabilistic Pairwise Contrastive Loss (PPCL) is used; comparison with simpler alternatives like KL divergence or Wasserstein distance is not provided in terms of training stability or final performance (R1 question). The inclusion loss formulation (e.g., direction in Eq. (6) and whether it should be \(L(t,v)\) vs \(L(v,t)\)) and comparison to KL(p1||p2)–KL(p2||p1) are not clarified (R3 questions). The Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) implementation details are missing from the main text (R3 question).
10. **Uncertainty usage in applications unclear**: The image traversal task does not specify how the uncertainty information is utilized (R3 question). The prompt enhancement approach lacks an explanation of how estimated variances contribute to rewriting (R3 question).
11. **Open question on “golden uncertainty”**: What distribution should a probabilistic VLM converge to for an optimal uncertainty estimate? This remains unaddressed (R3). While not strictly a weakness, it highlights the need for further theoretical grounding.
