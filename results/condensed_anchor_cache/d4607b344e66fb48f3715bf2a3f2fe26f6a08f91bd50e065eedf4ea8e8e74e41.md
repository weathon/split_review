## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviews are split. One reviewer (score 5, confidence 2) finds CtrlMol interesting, with strong empirical results on ZINC250K and good conditional generation. The other three reviewers (scores 3) share substantial concerns about limited novelty, reliance on the existing Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN) framework without sufficient new contributions, insufficient experimental evaluation (only ZINC‑250K, no code, no repeated trials), missing details in the paper presentation, and lack of theoretical support or ablation studies. All reviewers note that the method is clearly written and well‑organized, but the overall assessment is that the technical contribution is incremental.

### Strengths
- Interesting application of Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) to 2D molecular graph generation.
- Strong empirical results on the ZINC‑250K dataset; the method outperforms competing baselines in both unconditional and property‑conditional generation.
- The background introduction is well‑organized, clearly written, and easy to read.
- The paper leverages a recent innovative architecture (BFN) for discrete topological graph generation via continuous parameters.
- Good performance in conditional generation with property‑guided sampling.
- The proposed K‑regular graph sampling strategy reduces edge sampling complexity from O(N²) to O(KN), which is a practical contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The core formulation is a straightforward application of BFNs (Graves et al., 2023). The improvements largely stem from the BFN framework itself rather than new concepts. The main adaptation – adding a conditioning parameter c to the neural network – is standard in other controllable generative models (e.g., diffusion models). The paper does not sufficiently justify why BFNs are uniquely suited for molecular graph generation or analyze the specific challenges BFNs address.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: Experiments are restricted to ZINC‑250K. Missing evaluation on commonly used datasets such as QM9 and MOSES, and missing additional metrics like FCD. No repeated trials are reported, and the code is not provided, raising reproducibility concerns. Results for baselines (e.g., GLDM) are not cited or explained, further undermining validity.
- **Missing comparison**: The paper does not compare to SyCo‑GeoBFN (Ketata et al., 2024), which uses synthetic coordinates to apply 3D models to 2D data. A targeted discussion or small experiment on when CtrlMol outperforms that approach is needed.
- **Paper writing lacking detail**: The description of BFN is insufficient for readers unfamiliar with the framework. Missing: pseudocode for training and sampling, a clearer derivation of the loss function (Eq. 7, cf. Eqs. 189‑190 of the BFN paper), explanation of how the flow is parameterized and how the Jacobian is computed, and details on how the edge sampling strategy interacts with the BFN framework.
- **Missing references**: Line 119‑120 (BFNs) and line 372 (graph attention network) lack citations. GLDM is mentioned without a citation.
- **Theoretical support and ablations lacking**: Performance improvements have no theoretical justification or ablation studies. The paper does not analyze whether the K‑regular graph sampling strategy (Theorem 1) complicates convergence compared to starting from a complete graph, nor does it report time costs with and without Theorem 1.
- **Technical contribution limited by denoising core**: Despite using Bayesian optimization, the core framework remains a denoising process, similar to diffusion models. The claimed distinction is not significant enough to be considered a major technical advance.
