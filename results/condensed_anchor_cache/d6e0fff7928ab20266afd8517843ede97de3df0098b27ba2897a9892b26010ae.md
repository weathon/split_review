## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers rated the paper (scores: 6, 5, 6, 3; confidence all 3). The paper proposes SupCBM, a Concept Bottleneck Model that uses label supervision in concept prediction, a hierarchical (perceptual/descriptive) concept set, and an intervention matrix to reduce information leakage. Reviewers agree the idea is interesting, the design is well-justified, and experiments are extensive across CUB-BIRD, CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and HAM10000. Information leakage metrics (concept removal, locality, perturbation) confirm improved performance over P-CBM, Label-free CBM, LaBo, and Yan. Strengths include enhanced interpretability, post-hoc conversion capability, and a human study (AMT). However, concerns center on reliance on GPT-generated concepts (trustworthiness, process clarity, domain suitability), lack of precise definitions (information leakage, perceptual concepts), missing baselines (random intervention matrix, fine-tuned VLM), unclear handling of no-shared-concepts cases, equal weighting of concepts per class, and scalability discussion. Reviewer 4 (score 3) is notably more critical about clarity, statistical rigor, and operational definitions.

### Strengths
- **Interesting and well-justified approach**: SupCBM’s structured intervention matrix and hierarchical concept learning offers a fresh perspective on information leakage prevention. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Design directly targets information leakage**: Method is clear and focused. (R2)
- **Extensive experiments across diverse datasets**: Evaluated on CUB-BIRD, CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and HAM10000. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Empirical evidence favors SupCBM**: Outperforms existing CBMs (P-CBM, Label-free CBM, LaBo, Yan). (R1)
- **Rigorous information leakage evaluation**: Multiple metrics (concept removal, locality, perturbation) confirm success. (R2, R3)
- **Enhanced interpretability**: Focuses on relevant concepts. (R3)
- **Practical advantage**: Allows post-hoc conversion of existing models into CBMs with minimal overhead. (R3)
- **Well-explained annotation and GPT concept generation/pooling**: Method for annotating images with only relevant concepts is clear. (R3)
- **Reproducibility**: Provided code is easy to understand and replicates results. (R3)
- **Human study**: Amazon Mechanical Turk evaluation of concept quality. (R4)
- **Case study (Figure 3) shows reasonable concepts**: Helpful for interpretability, though Reviewer 4 notes that without seeing outputs of other CBM algorithms, it is impossible to judge superiority. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of precise definitions** (Reviewer 4): “Information leakage” (Definition 2.1) uses “unintended” and “additional” information – subjective; needs an operational definition. “Perceptual concepts” (directly observable without reasoning) is ambiguous because humans have different levels of reasoning. (R4)
- **Inadequate statistical rigor** (Reviewer 4): The statement “p-value < 0.05 usually indicates statistical significance … should be statistically significant” is imprecise; the test used to obtain the p-value (≈0.0003) is not clarified. (R4)
- **Missing evaluation metric** (Reviewer 4): Table 1 does not specify the metric. If accuracy, the authors should explain why the vanilla pre-trained backbone achieves such low accuracy on CIFAR datasets and consider other metrics. (R4)
- **GPT-based concept generation concerns** (R1, R2, R4): Relying on LLMs for concept sets and intervention matrix raises trustworthiness and robustness issues, especially in safety-critical domains (e.g., medical). Different GPT versions yield different performance; which LLM to use in practice is unclear. (R1) The generation process is not clearly defined, making reliability assessment difficult. (R1, R4) No experiments on diverse datasets where off-the-shelf LLMs are insufficient. (R2) Section 3.3’s validity relies on oracle concept sets, but in reality concepts come from ChatGPT. (R4) Questions: How to ensure SupCBM “only focuses on concepts that should be involved” when concept sets are ChatGPT-generated? (R4) How to ensure intended concepts match human intentions? (R4)
- **Scalability not discussed** (Reviewer 1): No analysis of computational cost (memory, training/inference time) with respect to number of concepts. Performance and runtime scaling with larger concept sets should be examined. (R1)
- **Equal weighting of concepts per class** (Reviewer 1): All relevant concepts contribute equally; a weighting mechanism could capture nuanced relationships between concepts and classes. (R1)
- **Unclear handling of A = B = ∅ (no shared concepts)** (Reviewer 1): Need more practical treatment and concrete examples showing how the model distinguishes classes when no concepts overlap. (R1)
- **Meaningless concepts in hard CBMs** (Reviewer 1): The observation that adding meaningless hard concepts can improve hard CBM performance suggests the concept set may be insufficiently informative. Experiments should test if a more informative concept set eliminates this effect and whether SupCBM also benefits from meaningless concepts. (R1)
- **Role of intervention matrix** (Reviewer 2): If the number of concepts is large enough to unambiguously encode each label, reaching upper-bound accuracy is unsurprising. A baseline with a random binary intervention matrix is needed to understand strengths/weaknesses. (R2)
- **Overemphasis on information leakage, under-discussion of explainability** (Reviewer 2): The paper emphasizes information leakage at the expense of clarifying what aspects of explainability the proposed method improves compared to existing CBMs. (R2)
- **Insensitivity to hyperparameters** (Reviewer 2): Results are surprisingly not sensitive to LLM type or number of concepts; a deeper discussion is needed (e.g., is it because even weak LLMs can identify concepts from dataset class labels?). (R2)
- **Missing ablation experiments** (Reviewer 3): Need ablation on the two-level concept set (perceptual vs. descriptive) to see its impact on performance. Impact of parameters p and q: increasing p=7, q=8 improves CIFAR10 (89.26%) but decreases CIFAR100 (69.57%). Whether injecting domain knowledge into the intervention matrix could improve performance. (R3)
- **Missing comparison to fine-tuned VLM** (Reviewer 4): The case study (Figure 3) should compare SupCBM’s concept outputs to those from a fine-tuned vision-language model to demonstrate advantage. (R4)
- **Extendability not tested** (Reviewer 4): No experiment to support the claim that the method extends; would performance drop if the backbone does not support classes in the concept set? (R4)
