## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the inconsistency of perturbation-based fidelity metrics used to evaluate saliency maps. It proposes two conformity measures, DROP and PSim, to assess metric consistency across different perturbation schemes. Using multiple perturbation types (nine) and deep learning models on several datasets, the authors argue that assumptions underlying traditional fidelity metrics (e.g., AOPC, Average Drop) do not always hold, causing unreliability. They recommend cautious interpretation and specifying the perturbation type when reporting fidelity. One reviewer was notably more positive, praising the comprehensive analysis and actionable recommendations, while the other three reviewers found significant issues with novelty, methodology, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The problem of understanding inconsistencies in perturbation-based fidelity metrics is interesting and important (R1, R4).
- The proposed DROP and PSim conformity measures are simple and offer a standardized way to examine consistency, potentially useful for the XAI community (R1, R2, R3).
- Extensive empirical evaluation covers nine perturbation types, multiple models, and datasets, strengthening the validity of findings (R2, R3).
- Performance evaluation on CNNs shows that fidelity metrics are inconsistent with respect to perturbations as measured by DROP and PSim (R1).
- The paper provides clear actionable recommendations, such as specifying the perturbation type when using fidelity metrics (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Practical utility unclear**: There is no guidance on how DROP and PSim scores should be interpreted or used when working with saliency maps, limiting their usefulness (R1; also R2 asks for theoretical justification for desiring PSim=1).
- **Writing quality and notational errors**: Notational inconsistencies and typos are present (e.g., Equation (9) missing summation index; “varaiances”; Equation (2) subscripts with inconsistent meanings; unclear meaning of ρ in `R_ρ` on Line 148). The paper needs careful proofreading and standard equation formatting (R1, R4).
- **Misalignment with traditional fidelity metrics**: DROP and PSim use fixed performance drops at predetermined steps, while established metrics like AOPC and ROAR/ROAD intentionally accumulate effects across perturbation steps to account for spatial interdependencies among pixels. This gap suggests a fundamental misalignment between the criticism and the proposed design (R2, major).
- **Lack of justification for the consistency requirement**: The paper claims fidelity metrics should be consistent across different perturbations (desiring PSim=1), but provides no theoretical or empirical basis for why this consistency is necessary. Other metrics typically fix a single perturbation to report performance; the reasoning for requiring uniformity is unsupported (R2, major).
- **Overlooks OOD effects in fidelity metrics**: Recent work (Hooker 2019, Rong 2023) distinguishes performance drops from feature perturbations versus out-of-distribution (OOD) effects. The paper does not address this crucial distinction, which may affect the interpretation of DROP and PSim (R2, major).
- **Segment-wise perturbation scheme insufficiently detailed**: The implementation of the segment-wise approach is not clearly described, hindering replicability (R2, minor).
- **Problematic visualizations**: Figures 3 and 5 have discontinuities along the axes; using surface plots with disconnected axes reduces clarity (R2, minor).
- **Limited analysis on robustness of DROP and PSim**: The paper does not explore the sensitivity of the proposed conformity measures themselves to various perturbations and model types, leaving their reliability across broader conditions unclear (R3; also R1 asks for visual examples).
- **Narrow scope of adversarial analysis**: Only adversarially trained models within the ResNet50 architecture are used due to availability constraints. This limits generalizability, and transformer models are omitted entirely despite widespread use (R1, R3; also R1 asks about ViT).
- **Theoretical framework is dense**: The mathematical formulations and theoretical background are complex, limiting accessibility. Simplification, visualizations, and intuitive examples would help (R3; notation issues also raised by R1, R4).
- **Limited technical novelty**: The two proposed measures are already widely used in related fields, and the paper overlooks the essential task of establishing the accuracy of the underlying saliency maps (e.g., referencing the FunnyBirds dataset). The necessity of assumptions [P1] and [P2] when the saliency map itself may be inaccurate is questionable (R4).
- **Reasons behind inconsistency remain unclear**: After reading the manuscript, the root causes of why perturbation-based fidelity metrics are inconsistent are not clearly itemized or explained (R4).
- **No principled approach to perturbation selection**: The paper does not offer guidance on how to choose perturbations when assessing fidelity metric consistency (R4; also R1 questions model choice justification).
- **Gaussian blur behavior could be better explained** (R1).
- **DROP and PSim scores not labeled in Table 2** (R1).
