## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an active scene reconstruction architecture that uses foveal and peripheral sampling from multiple glances (sequential saccades) to reconstruct the original image, guided by a reinforcement learning-based saccade controller (actor-critic). The authors claim a >90% reduction in input pixels per frame while maintaining image recognition performance, 5% higher accuracy than state-of-the-art foveal models on GTSRB and ImageNet, and higher data efficiency during training. Reviewers found the task novel and methods innovative (R1, R3, R4), but raised concerns about clarity of architectural details, support for the 90% claim, experimental fairness, missing literature, and real-world applicability. Reviewer scores diverged: two reviewers gave high scores (8) with moderate to high confidence, while two gave lower scores (6) with lower to moderate confidence, indicating disagreement about the paper’s overall impact.

### Strengths
- Novel application of foveal-peripheral vision to image reconstruction, and the first to reconstruct from continuous foveal subsampled images (R1, R3).
- Innovative use of an actor-critic (reinforcement learning) model for saccade control, achieving near-original classification accuracy in five scans (R3).
- Significant reduction in required input pixels (over 90%), with practical implications for real-time processing (R2).
- The method is simple, useful, and can work with existing classification models without retraining (R4).
- Experiments support the claimed contributions of higher accuracy and data efficiency compared to existing foveal models (R1, R4).
- The paper is easy to read and understand (R2, R3).
- Includes references to perceptual psychology and neuroscience (R1, though noted as incomplete – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Architecture details missing:** The paper lacks a detailed explanation of how sequential glimpses are integrated in the predictive reconstruction model. The ConvLSTM structure is not described: input/output dimensions, hidden state initialization and update across timesteps, number of channels, kernel sizes, and activation functions. This hinders assessment of novelty and effectiveness (R4).
- **Unclear support for the 90% reduction claim:** It is ambiguous whether the claim refers to total pixels across all glimpses or per glimpse. In Figure 3, all subsampled models perform worse than the original; Figure 4 comes closest but the baseline is not clearly stated. The paper should explicitly state which experiment supports the 90% reduction (R4).
- **Unclear training setup and state initialization:** Is the system trained and tested on the same images? What is the initial state t0 – blank, corrupted, or a partial glimpse? If testing on the training set, the contribution would be limited to compression (R1).
- **Missing critical references:** Key prior work on foveated perceptual systems and saccade modeling is omitted, potentially limiting novelty. Missing references include: Deza & Konkle (2021), Wang & Cottrell (2017), Cheung, Weiss & Olshausen (2017), Freeman & Simoncelli (2011) on texture-based periphery, and Wang et al. (2004) on SSIM. Also missing saccade modeling literature (e.g., [1],[2],[3] from R2, [2] uses reconstruction as a guiding task). The paper should better position itself within this broader literature (R1, R2, R4).
- **Unfair experimental comparisons:** The uniform control group uses an 8% sampling probability while the 1/16+2% group differs by 0.25%, leading to unequal information that may affect performance (R3).
- **Questionable reconstruction evaluation:** Using classification model metrics to assess reconstruction quality is problematic because classification tasks do not focus on texture details. The paper should compare to a simple downsampling of the original image using the same number of sampled pixels (R3).
- **Lack of convincing real-world application:** The simulation of multiple eye samplings does not address practical problems; the motivation for reconstruction from foveated glances is not well-founded (R3).
- **No discussion of computational overhead:** The paper does not provide any insights into hardware, energy, or computational overhead of implementing the model in practice (R2).
- **Missing experiments:** Show how the hybrid reconstruction loss changes over timesteps (not just classification accuracy) (R4). Compare learned scanpath strategies to human scanpaths (e.g., [3,4] from R4). Consider more plausible peripheral sampling (e.g., [6] from R4) instead of per-pixel sampling with small probability (R4).
- **Minor issues:** “Continuous saccades” should be “sequential saccades” or “scanpaths” (R4). The paper states there are two types of photoreceptors but there are three (rods, cones, ipRGCs) (R4). SSIM citation is missing (R4). Heading 3.1 contains a typo: “Periphrl” (R4).
