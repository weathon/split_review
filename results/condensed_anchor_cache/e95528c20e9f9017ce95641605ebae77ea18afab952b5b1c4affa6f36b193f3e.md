## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies performance limitations of Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) in Split Federated Learning (SFL) and proposes PromptSFL, which transmits client prompts to the server via a linear layer ("skip prompts") and uses an adaptive client learning rate. Experiments are conducted on Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC) tasks. Reviewers acknowledge the interesting extension of VPT to SFL but raise concerns about novelty, scope, experimental validation, privacy implications, and the overall motivation for using VPT in SFL when it initially underperforms.

### Strengths
- Extending VPT to SFL is a relatively novel attempt within SFL, and the overall research question of improving training performance with pre-trained large models in split federated learning is important. (R1, R2, R4)
- Prompt fine-tuning is an important topic for FL applications due to its efficiency in computation and communication. (R3)
- The proposed framework is simple and effective. (R3)
- Extensive experiments on FGVC tasks show benefits over baselines. (R1)
- The manuscript has a complete structure with rich descriptions of methods and experimental setups. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Scope and task limitation**: The experiments solely focus on visual classification (FGVC), leaving open how PromptSFL would transfer to other domains (e.g., NLP) or to more diverse tasks. (R1, R4 – one reviewer (R4) notes that image classification is already well-explored in FL/SFL, and the authors are improving a solution that is initially inferior rather than building on existing SOTA.)
- **Novelty concerns**: Integrating VPT with SFL is straightforward; the additional design elements (skip prompts, adaptive LR) are considered trivial and easy to implement, leading to limited novelty. (R3) In contrast, another reviewer (R2) views the application as a novel attempt, but both agree that the novelty is not strong.
- **Missing computational overhead analysis**: The paper should compare the additional computation and communication costs of PromptSFL against baselines. (R1)
- **Unclear skip prompt design**: The meaning of "skip" is insufficiently explained – what is being skipped, and what is the intuition behind sending client prompts to the server? (R3) Additionally, the selection of skip prompts and how they represent "public knowledge" is not clarified. (R1)
- **Insufficient validation of convergence speed claim**: The claimed improvement in convergence speed is not clearly validated in the experiments. (R2)
- **Adaptive learning rate concerns**: The adaptive LR mechanism based on loss values requires sharing loss values between server and clients, which raises suitability issues in the FL context. (R2)
- **Ambiguity in method implementation**: It is unclear whether the training of the learnable weight and linear layer (Equation 8) occurs on the server or on the clients. (R1)
- **Privacy violation**: Sending prompts to the centralized server completely breaks the privacy protocol of split learning, FL, or split federated learning – the prompt belongs to user data and should not be transmitted as-is. (R4 – this reviewer expresses strong concern with high confidence.)
- **Weak motivation for VPT in SFL**: The paper does not adequately justify why one should stick with VPT in SFL given that VPT performs badly in that setting. The rationale for improving an inferior solution rather than optimizing more promising SFL approaches is not elaborated. (R4 – a minority reviewer strongly emphasizes this point, with confidence 5.)
- **Confounding factor in preliminary experiment**: In Figure 2a, using a fixed dataset means per-client data decreases as client count increases. The observed lower performance of VPT vs. FFT may be due to reduced data volume, not inherent inferiority of VPT. More experimentation is needed. (R2)
- **Marginal gains over existing methods**: According to Table 1, the performance improvement of PromptSFL over VPF-SFL is marginal; a more detailed analysis would be needed to convince. (R3)
- **Missing experiments under varying number of clients**: Performance for larger client scales (e.g., 50, 100, 200) is not reported. (R3)
- **Unclear preliminary experiment settings**: For Figure 5 (or other motivation experiments), the datasets, data distribution, and model settings are not specified, making the results hard to interpret. (R3)
- **Lack of experiments beyond image classification**: No results are provided for other types of tasks. (R4)
- **Irrelevant selection of "unsupervised learning" as primary area**: The paper appears unrelated to unsupervised learning; the justification is missing. (R4)
- **Notation inconsistency**: The manuscript uses inconsistent indexing (e.g., Section 2.1 uses \(i\) as client index and \(L_i\) as layer index, while Section 3 uses \(k\) as client index and \(i\) as layer index). (R1)
- **The baseline "Full Fine-Tuning" is not clearly explained**: It is unclear whether it is an FT setup where all clients tune the model and why the server also needs to train. (R1)
- **Lack of analysis on challenges specific to SFL fine-tuning**: The paper could benefit from a deeper discussion of what new difficulties arise in SFL compared to standard FL. (R2)
