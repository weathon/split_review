## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends RoPE to higher-dimensional inputs (2D, 3D) by learning a dense rotation matrix (LieRE) via the exponential map of a linear combination of skew-symmetric matrices. LieRE replaces block-diagonal 2D rotations with a single high-dimensional rotation from absolute position information. Shared parameters across heads reduce overhead. Evaluated on 2D/3D image classification tasks (CIFAR100, ImageNet, UCF101) against DeiT III, RoPE-Mixed, Vision-Llama. Reported relative accuracy improvements (10.0% 2D, 15.1% 3D), 3.9x training time reduction, 30% less data needed. However, absolute performance on standard benchmarks remains <70% (CIFAR100, ImageNet) and ~50% (UCF101), far from reported SOTA >80%. The writing and clarity need significant improvement; method details are incomplete or deferred, making reproducibility and intuition difficult.

### Strengths
- Novel use of Lie groups for position-aware attention is interesting and theoretically motivated (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Treats spatial and embedding dimensions equally, leading to a unified understanding of embedding space as a tensor field. Generalizes RoPE from dimension 2 to n; LieRE-Commute subsumes RoPE-Mixed as a commutative special case (Reviewer 2).
- Sharing parameters across layers (LieRE-Commute) improves over learnable RoPE variants (Reviewer 2).
- Empirically shows improvements over baselines in predictive performance, training speed (3.9x faster to same accuracy), and data efficiency (30% less data) for 2D/3D tasks (Reviewer 1). The approach is applicable to multidimensional data (Reviewer 1).
- Parameter sharing across attention heads mitigates the additional parameter cost (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and organization** (All reviewers):
  - Writing needs proofreading: repeated “equation equation” (Lines 169,173), missing capitalization of “Figure”/“Table” (Lines 187,397), inconsistent notation for updated keys/queries (Lines 188,189,209), broken figure reference (Line 400), “Rope” instead of “RoPE” in Table 2 line 381, improper \citet/\citep usage.
  - The method section (3.1) should include the attention equation explicitly; details like parameter sharing appear first in Results section instead of Method. Figures are often far from their references.
  - Subsubsections in related work are unnecessary and take space that could clarify the method.
  - Code in appendix is not explained in connection with main text (Reviewer 3).
- **Mathematical description incomplete** (Reviewers 1,3):
  - The “generator space” is not clearly identified as the Lie algebra; suggest using A(x) as argument to exponential map (Reviewer 1).
  - Notation n and d in Section 3.2 is inconsistent; the A matrix is not defined in detail (Reviewer 3).
  - Equation (2): the final term exp(U)^{-1} exp(V) appears reversed; should it be exp(V)^{-1} exp(U)? (Reviewers 1,2).
  - It is not intuitive why a single high-dimensional rotation is better than multiple 2D rotations (Reviewer 3). The benefit of capturing different distance effects with multiple rotations in RoPE is lost without justification (Reviewer 3). The relation between LieRE-Commute, learnable RoPE, and RoPE-Mixed should be clarified in the Method section, not deferred to 5.3.3. A reminder that SO(d) is not abelian for d>2 with one-line intuition would help (Reviewer 2).
- **Performance concerns** (Reviewers 2,3):
  - Although relative gains are significant, absolute accuracy is low: <70% on CIFAR100 and ImageNet, ~50% on UCF101. The cited baseline (Heo et al. 2024) reports >80%. This raises questions about practical applicability in high-accuracy scenarios (Reviewer 2).
  - The 3.5x training time reduction is measured only as wall time to reach fixed performance at 200 epochs. It is unclear if LieRE converges to a better final loss or if running longer would close the gap. The comparison should report best achievable performance (e.g., converged training/test loss after 200 epochs) (Reviewer 2).
  - FLOPs analysis is less informative than learning curves; the overall convergence behavior and best possible performance should be the focus (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experiments/ablation** (Reviewers 2,3):
  - No experiments on datasets with varying image sizes/resolutions to test robustness to spatial scale changes (Reviewer 3).
  - The word “stem” in Table 2 is confusing; clarify in text rather than only in Figure 3a (Reviewer 2).
  - The order of Algorithm 1 and 2 in Line 201 might be reversed (Reviewer 2).
  - Need more intuition for why the stem option outperforms layer or head heterogeneous implementations. Could the O(n) symmetry in the dot product be exploited to unify representations by rotationally aligning subspaces? (Reviewer 2).
