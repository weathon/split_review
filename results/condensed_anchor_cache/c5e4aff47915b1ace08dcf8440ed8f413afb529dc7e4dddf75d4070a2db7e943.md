## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Point-PQAE, a cross-reconstruction generative paradigm for self-supervised point cloud learning. It first generates two decoupled views via a crop mechanism, then reconstructs one view from the other using a relative positional encoding (RPE) and cross-attention. The approach combines contrastive learning with MAE-style completion. Experiments on classification (ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40) and segmentation (S3DIS, ShapeNetPart) show gains over single-view reconstruction baselines like Point-MAE. Two reviewers (R3, R5) found no major flaws; three others (R1, R2, R4) raised significant concerns about novelty, explanation, and benchmarking.

### Strengths
- Writing is clear, well-organized, and easy to follow (R1, R4).
- Experiments are thorough and convincing, covering multiple downstream tasks and ablations (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- The cross-reconstruction idea is novel for 3D point clouds and combines contrastive learning with masked autoencoding in an interesting way (R2, R3, R5).
- Performance gains over previous SSL methods are meaningful, especially on classification tasks (R3, R5; R2 notes limited segmentation improvement).
- The decoupled-view crop mechanism and relative positional encoding are an inspiring attempt that may motivate future work (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Overblown claims**: Claiming to “surpass previous methods by a large margin” is too bold given that Point‑PQAE performs similarly to PointM2AE on some metrics (R1).
- **Limited novelty of cross‑reconstruction**: Core idea of cross‑view reconstruction already exists in prior works (e.g., [1,2,3] in image domain; [1,2] in point cloud context) (R1, R2, R4). The training framework adds only an RPE module and cross‑attention, both standard techniques, and the overall architecture is largely based on existing methods (R4).
- **Unclear why cross‑reconstruction works**: No explanation why structural information of view 2 can recover view 1 if they might not overlap; the “crop” mechanism is essentially a different form of masking, not a true view construction (R1, R2, R4). The overlap ratio between the two views is not provided (R1).
- **Information leakage suspicion**: During decoding, features from view 2 likely leak into view 1, making the approach not fundamentally different from single‑view reconstruction (R2). Experimental results do not convincingly demonstrate a unique benefit over simpler alternatives (R2).
- **Relative Position Encoding (RPE) is not new**: RPE is a known concept and is not specifically adapted to 3D point clouds; no details on how it differs from prior positional encoding methods for 3D data (R4).
- **Results questionable vs. stronger baselines**: Point‑DAE with augmentation achieves 88.69 on PB‑T50‑RS, while Point‑PQAE’s ablation using absolute position encoding only gets 87.7. This raises doubt about whether the claimed “multi‑view” and cross‑attention are the real drivers of improvement (R4).
- **Classification vs. segmentation gap**: The method gives large gains on classification (Table 2) but only modest improvements on segmentation (Table 3). No explanation for this discrepancy is provided (R2).
- **Missing comparisons/discussion**: Similar self‑supervised cross‑reconstruction paradigms exist in images; the paper should discuss differences in the learning framework (R2).
- **Questions about generality**: Could the approach be extended to images/text? What about a triple‑view reconstruction? (R3).
- **Minor**: Line 247 missing a period after “RPE” (R3).
