## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Safe RLHF, a framework that decouples human preferences for helpfulness and harmlessness to separate reward and cost models. It formalizes LLM safety as a constrained optimization problem and uses Lagrangian methods to dynamically balance the objectives during fine-tuning. Three rounds of Safe RLHF on Alpaca-7B improve both helpfulness and harmlessness compared to existing value-aligned algorithms. Reviewers highlight it as the first integration of Safe RL and RLHF, with a two-dimensional annotation scheme and released data/code.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and presents a clear methodology with careful ablations and robust evaluation (R1, R3, R4).
- Decoupling rewards and costs resolves the optimization contradiction in RLHF, enabling cleaner human annotations and separate training of objective models (R1, R2).
- Concrete experimental results on Alpaca-7B demonstrate significant improvements in both helpfulness and harmlessness (R2, R4).
- The framework is extensible to other human values beyond safety (R1).
- It addresses the important societal issue of balancing LLM performance and safety (R1, R3).
- Safe RLHF is the first integration of Safe RL and RLHF frameworks, employing a two-dimensional human annotation scheme and a safe training mechanism (R4).
- Release of all data and training codes enhances reproducibility (R4).
- Note: One reviewer (R3, confidence 5) considers the technique contributions incremental (standard CMDP + Lagrangian), while other reviewers view the decoupling and first integration as innovative.

### Weaknesses
- Missing comparison with more safe RLHF baselines such as Constitutional AI; only ablations of fixed lambda (reward shaping) and the Sparrow approach are included (R1).
- Stronger evidence is needed to show Lagrangian methods are superior to reward shaping. The paper does not test multiple cost thresholds for Lagrangian or multiple coefficients for reward shaping (R1, R3).
- Technique contributions are incremental: decoupling rewards/costs is standard in CMDP, and Lagrangian methods with RL are not new (R3, high confidence). Reviewer 4 agrees that the contribution in Safe RL is limited.
- The choice of cost threshold and cost-reward trade-off in LLM alignment is not well justified. In safety-critical scenarios the cost limit should be 0, and a simple ranking function (A > B if preference holds and A passes safety check) might suffice, questioning the necessity of the CMDP formulation (R3). The paper uses negative cost thresholds for some experiments; the number of threshold values tried and the robustness of Lagrangian to different thresholds are not reported (R3).
- The connection between the learned reward/cost models (from human preferences) and traditional reward/cost signals in Safe RL (often pre-defined or environmental) is not clearly established. The mapping from subjective human-defined safety to objective safety constraints is unclear (R4).
- No theoretical convergence guarantees or analysis of training stability. The interplay between reward/cost models and fine-tuned LLM may cause oscillations or divergence; no mechanisms for ensuring stable learning are discussed (R4). Stability of the reward and cost signals should be analyzed.
- The role of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is not isolated. Safety could be incorporated into SFT data, and using off-the-shelf Alpaca-7B conflates RLHF gains with potential gains from safety-focused SFT (R1). The paper does not train stronger SFT models using the safety data collected (R1).
- Model selection procedures for the reward/cost model and RLHF training are not clearly explained, despite model selection often having a larger impact than the algorithm (R1).
- Figures require polish: Fig.2(a) axis indexes are absent (R2); Fig.6(a)(b) should add scatter points for Beaver-v2 and Beaver-v3 (R2); Fig.1 font is too small (R4); some figure labels and text are hard to read at 100% resolution (R1).
- The paper lacks discussion of computational resources, time, scalability, and limitations/potential risks (R3).
- The cost model explanation in Section 3.2 (loss with both Bradley-Terry and classification-like terms) could be clearer (R1).
- Related work is relegated to the appendix; it should be moved to the main paper to better position Safe RLHF among other works (R1).
