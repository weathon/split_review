## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces computational methods for entropic optimal transport (OT) under martingale-type conditions, which include discrete martingale optimal transport (MOT) as row-wise (in-)equality constraints. The authors adopt entropic regularization (Equation 6) to obtain a dual problem (Equation 7) that is solved by a block coordinate ascent scheme. Two blocks are updated via standard Sinkhorn iterations; the additional block due to martingale constraints is optimized using Newton’s method. The Hessian’s sparsity is exploited via sparse Newton iterations. The paper provides an approximation error bound (Theorem 1) and compares the algorithm to APDAGD (first-order method) on several examples. The focus is on empirical efficiency rather than theoretical convergence.

### Strengths
- The paper provides and discusses multiple examples that well justify the underlying problem in various fields (Reviewer 1).
- It proves that the effect of entropic regularization vanishes at an exponential speed with respect to the growth of the regularization parameter (Reviewer 1).
- The resulting algorithm is compared to a state-of-the-art first-order method (APDAGD) and shows remarkable improvement in speed of convergence (Reviewer 1). [Note: Reviewer 1 gave a score of 6, indicating a more positive view than the other reviewers.]
- The literature on numerically solving OT problems is well presented (Reviewer 4).
- Focusing on optimal transport with martingale conditions is interesting, with applications such as model-free optimal pricing (Reviewer 2).
- The article is well organized, and the overall motivation and story is clear; developing an efficient algorithm for MOT is helpful (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical convergence analysis lacking**: The paper does not provide a theoretical convergence analysis for the proposed algorithm (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The algorithm’s convergence speed scaling with the regularization parameter (e.g., linear as in Altschuler 2017) is not analyzed (Reviewer 1). There is no rigorous analysis of convergence rate or computational complexity (Reviewer 4).
- **Novelty concerns**: The paper is very similar to existing numerical considerations for OT problems; the martingale constraint did not require new mathematical analysis, and entropic regularization smooths away the specific structure (Reviewer 4). The sparse Newton algorithm largely overlaps with previous work [2] and appears to be a direct application to entropic MOT (Reviewer 3). The theoretical result (Theorem 1) is a straightforward generalization of Corollary 9 of [1] (Weed 2018) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: Only APDAGD is benchmarked; existing MOT solvers such as Guo (2019) should be compared (Reviewer 2).
- **Rationale for algorithmic choices**: The necessity of a Sinkhorn-type algorithm is questioned—since the g variables require Newton updates, why not simply use L-BFGS to solve the dual problem (7) directly? (Reviewer 3). The abstract incorrectly states that entropic regularization addresses infeasibility; in fact the relaxation (4) handles infeasibility, while entropy smooths the LP (Reviewer 3). The choice of entropy vs. a logarithmic barrier (which provides self-concordance) is not justified (Reviewer 1).
- **Sparse Newton claims unsubstantiated**: The paper claims super-exponential convergence but provides no theoretical guarantee—only empirical observation (Reviewer 3). It is unclear whether the sparsified Hessian is guaranteed invertible, and if not, how that is addressed. The claim that the sparsified Hessian is the Hessian of a concave function plus a non-positive diagonal matrix is not justified; the dual for MOT is more complicated than standard entropic OT, and the truncation method from [2] may not preserve concavity properties (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: The paper lacks clarity in structure; sections should begin with main messages (Reviewer 2). The connection between the dual variables in (7) and the primal variables in (6) is not explained (Reviewer 4). The numerical algorithm description is thin, mostly spanning from the middle of page 5 to the middle of page 6, while many pages cover background (Reviewer 4). The claim of “efficient” is not fully supported (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details and comparisons**: The switching criteria for transitioning to Newton’s method are not described or justified (Reviewer 2). The runtime of warm initialization is not included in the numerical experiments (Reviewer 2). The scalability gap between current experiments and large ML problems is acknowledged but noted as a general limitation (Reviewer 1).
- **Unaddressed technical challenges**: It is not clarified what technical challenges arise when extending Sinkhorn algorithms to MOT (Reviewer 2). Theorem 1 shows that the regularization parameter must grow proportionally to the inverse of the duality gap; for large problems the gap may be extremely small, leading to very large regularization parameters, and how this affects overall efficiency is unexplored (Reviewer 1).
