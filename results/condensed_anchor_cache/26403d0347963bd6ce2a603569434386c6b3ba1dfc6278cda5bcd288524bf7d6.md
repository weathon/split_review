## Merged Review

### Summary
This work addresses the ill-posedness of function‑space variational inference in BNNs, where the KL divergence between stochastic processes evaluated on a finite set of inputs can be infinite. The authors replace the standard KL with the regularized KL divergence (Quang, 2019) to obtain a finite objective, leading to a generalized function‑space VI method. Experiments on synthetic and UCI regression datasets show good performance.

### Strengths
- Well‑written, well‑organized, and clearly motivated with sufficient background.
- Bringing the regularized KL divergence to function‑space variational inference is a good idea that may benefit the community.
- Empirical results on UCI regression benchmarks are good.
- One reviewer found the principled justification for adding jitter to the covariance (via regularized KL) to be a strength, as it addresses the numerical issue of infinite KL divergence.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution.** The proposed objective (Eq. 9) differs from Tractable Function‑Space VI (TFVI; Rudner et al., 2022) only by adding a jitter term (γ M I_M) to each covariance term in Eq. 11. Adding a diagonal identity matrix for numerical stability is common practice in implementation, and the combination of regularized KL with TFVI is straightforward. The claim that “VI is too restrictive for BNNs with informative function‑space priors” largely repeats existing work (discussion in Sec. 2.2 and Appendix 1.1).
- **Limited experimental scope.** Experiments cover only regression tasks (synthetic and small UCI datasets) with small MLP architectures. No classification experiments (e.g., CIFAR‑10/100 with ResNet) are shown, even though TFVI was originally demonstrated on both classification and regression. This makes it hard to judge the method’s effectiveness at scale.
- **Lack of theoretical justification for the new objective.** Replacing the KL with a regularized KL yields an objective that is no longer an ELBO. There is no analysis from a generalization perspective or explanation of what optimum the alternative objective corresponds to.
- **Unclear differentiation from TFVI.** It is not clear which specific differences in the proposed method lead to the improved performance reported in Table 1. Additionally, the categorization of TFSVI (Rudner et al., 2022) under weight‑space priors in Table 1 is questionable.
- **Missing ablation on the jitter effect.** No experiments demonstrate cases where a non‑singular covariance matrix (the added jitter) greatly impacts the results, i.e., where the original KL would be infinite and the proposed regularization makes a concrete difference.
- *Note:* One reviewer viewed the jitter justification as a principled strength; other reviewers considered it a minor technical contribution.
