## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReKV, a training-free approach that extends Video-LLMs to streaming video question-answering (StreamingVQA). It uses a sliding-window attention mechanism for short-term context and stores processed video KV-Caches in RAM/disk, retrieving only query-relevant caches via either an external retriever (e.g., CLIP) or internal parameters of the Video-LLM. Experiments on MLVU, QAEGO4DMC, EgoSchema, ActivityNet-QA, and StreamingVQA (RSV-Ego, RSV-Movie) datasets show improved accuracy and efficiency over standard Video-LLMs. The method is evaluated only on LLaVA-OV (0.5B, 7B). Reviewer 2 is notably more positive (score 8, confidence 4) than the other reviewers (scores 6, confidence 3–4), praising the novelty and thorough explanation, while Reviewer 1 is most critical about novelty and generality.

### Strengths
- Outperforms existing Video-LLMs on long-form benchmarks (Reviewer 1). Improvements against standard Video-LLMs are substantial (Reviewer 3).
- Novel, simple, training-free method that extends existing Video-LLMs for StreamingVQA by integrating sliding-window attention and efficient KV-Cache retrieval (Reviewer 2). The approach is well-motivated and clearly explained with supportive figures (Reviewer 2).
- Efficiency: sliding-window attention and KV-Cache retrieval significantly reduce computational overhead and memory usage (Reviewer 4). Ablation study shows the validity and impact of the retrieval methods (Reviewer 1). ReKV improves accuracy while maintaining stable inference latency and memory usage as frames increase (Reviewer 2).
- Real-time processing capability enables prompt responses, practical for surveillance and live broadcasts (Reviewer 4). The training-free nature facilitates integration across different Video-LLMs (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive evaluation across multiple benchmarks (Reviewer 4). Adequate number of evaluation benchmarks (Reviewer 3). Seamless integration with existing Video-LLMs without extra training (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern (Reviewer 1, minority strongly critical):** Many LLM systems already reduce context-processing delay using KV-caches; the paper is built on LLMs coupled with a video encoder but lacks specialty for video streaming. Causal attention and retrieval with cosine similarity are not new. Reviewer 1 also questions whether the proposed attention/search is more effective than adaptive KV cache (ICLR'24) or Keyformer (arXiv'24).
- **Limited model generality (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4):** ReKV is only evaluated on LLaVA-OV (0.5B, 7B). Reviewer 2 explicitly says evaluating on other Video-LLMs (e.g., VideoChatGPT) would strengthen claims of versatility and would increase their score. Reviewer 3 and 4 also ask about integration with other architectures, including 70B-scale models.
- **Missing fair comparison with existing memory-based models (Reviewer 3):** No controlled comparison against VideoStreaming, Flash-VStream, etc., using the same backbone (visual encoder, LLM, training data). This makes it difficult to isolate the impact of the proposed retrieval mechanism.
- **Missing related works discussion (Reviewer 3):** Should clarify novelty relative to VideoLLM-online, MC-ViT in terms of training-free vs. fine-tuning, amount of visual information retained, and handling of long videos (token pruning, merging, memory-based approaches).
- **Confusion about "External Video KV-Cache Retrieval" (Reviewer 3):** This seems to be keyframe selection using CLIP-like cross-modal matching, already investigated by ATP, SeViLA, etc. The authors should clarify whether this is a core contribution or a baseline, and why chosen over existing methods.
- **Writing and organizational issues (Reviewer 4):** Ablation study placed before main experiments (should be after). Section 2.1 (Task definition) is repetitive of introduction. Poor citation format: Table 4, line 391 may misleadingly cite Video-LLaVA-7B as Video-ChatGPT-7B (Reviewer 4). Inconsistent citation format throughout (Reviewer 3).
- **Unexplained terms and metrics (Reviewer 4):** "Oracle retrieval" in Table 2 and Line 305 is unclear. How is "recall" calculated and how can it be 100? Missing hyperparameters for external retrieval (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis for StreamingVQA efficiency (Reviewer 1):** How about GFLOPs on streaming VQA? Reviewer 3 asks for efficiency comparison of ReKV vs. original LLaVA-OV in Table 4.
- **Scalability and practical concerns (Reviewers 2, 4):** How does ReKV manage KV-Cache storage for extremely long streams (e.g., continuous surveillance)? Are there mechanisms to prevent unsustainable cache growth? How does it scale with increasing video length, complexity, high resolution, or high frame rate? (Reviewer 2 asks this as minor weakness; Reviewer 4 as a question.)
- **Minor citation issue (Reviewer 3):** Citation format inconsistent over the paper; unify.
