## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents ReSimAD, a reconstruction-simulation-perception pipeline for zero-shot 3D domain transfer in autonomous driving. It reconstructs source-domain 3D meshes using implicit fields (LiNR) and then simulates target-domain LiDAR point clouds in CARLA, adding foreground assets guided by source ground truth. Experiments on Waymo→KITTI, Waymo→nuScenes, Waymo→ONCE show improvements over a UDA baseline and benefits for 3D pre-training. Reviewers acknowledge the potential value but raise major concerns about presentation clarity, technical novelty, evaluation breadth, mesh/simulation quality validation, and practicality. Reviewer opinions are mixed: two rate 6, one 5, one 6; two reviewers (R2, R3) find presentation poor and novelty limited, while others see merit but list significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated and easy-to-follow pipeline with clear three-component structure (R1).
- Cross-domain experiments on multiple setups (Waymo→KITTI, Waymo→nuScenes, Waymo→ONCE) show effectiveness and meaningful margins over UDA baseline (R1, R2).
- Large-scale benchmarking with multiple datasets (R2).
- The dataset generation/simulation pipeline can enrich and adjust old annotated data for new target domains (R3).
- The paradigm addresses cross-domain degradation from a data-source perspective and has significant research value (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity are poor** (R2, R3, R4). The mesh reconstruction and LiDAR rendering algorithms lack formal definitions (input, output, losses). Only a depth rendering equation is given; it is unclear whether RGB rendering losses (NeuS) are used (R2). Figures and tables are poorly placed, making the paper difficult to follow (R3, R4). Fig.2 is confusing (R2). Table.1 provides no additional information beyond text (R2). Formatting needs adjustment to align figures with text (R4).
- **Technical novelty is limited** (R3). The methodology is largely a step-by-step pipeline description without theoretical insights or verification. The contribution is not strong enough to offset the lack of novelty (R3). More elaboration and supporting evidence are needed (R4).
- **Missing evaluation on larger/modern detection models** (R1). Only PointRCNN is used; benefits may not scale to more data-hungry architectures (e.g., ViT, DETR). This also applies to the pre-training experiment in Table 3 (R1).
- **Only zero-shot performance is reported** (R1). The paper does not explore settings with increasing target-domain information (few-shot or more samples), which is a common practical scenario. This applies equally to Table 3 (R1).
- **Insufficient comparison to state-of-the-art DA/UDA methods** (R1). The comparison is limited and does not fully contextualize performance against recent domain adaptation approaches.
- **Generalizability and practicality concerns** (R1). Zero-shot performance likely relies on similar driving scenarios and massive geometric observations; it is unclear how the method generalizes to more diverse environments. Acquiring 3D meshes requires high computational cost (per-scene optimization of LiNR) and multi-LiDAR sensor specs; scalability and practicality are questionable (R1).
- **Mesh quality and simulation realism are not adequately validated** (R2). Reconstructed meshes appear poor quality; it is unclear why simulated points from them are meaningful for detection. Systematic evaluation of mesh quality (Table 6 is unclear) and rendered point cloud quality is missing. Comparisons to simpler simulation methods (e.g., VDBF) are needed (R2).
- **Questionable dataset selection** (R3). The pipeline simulates from a richer dataset (Waymo) to sparser ones (KITTI, nuScenes), but the paper’s motivation would be better supported by simulating from a less rich dataset to a richer one. The pipeline appears to rely on interchanging sensor configuration using the richest point cloud; this needs elaboration (R3).
- **Missing explanations and supporting details** (R4). Specifically: (a) how point cloud registration was performed to consolidate frames for reconstruction; (b) interpretation and analysis of “Closed Gap” in Table 2; (c) significance and specific impact of “matching vehicle traffic flow density” in mesh-to-point rendering; (d) reason for the large gap between zero-shot and oracle results in Table 5, with missing details on sample quantity and other experimental settings.
- **Minor issue**: The paper may be more suited to computer vision/robotics than machine learning (R3).
