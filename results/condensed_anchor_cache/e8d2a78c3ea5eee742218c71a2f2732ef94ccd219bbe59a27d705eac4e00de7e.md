## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a pipeline to discover hyperparameter-free symbolic optimizers for reinforcement learning, using an LLM-based “thinker” and “coder” to iteratively refine optimizers from an archive. It compares the out-of-distribution generalization of these symbolic optimizers with the black-box optimizer OPEN (Goldie et al., 2024) and Adam. Experiments cover variations in training length, model size, activation function, and environment. Results indicate that while the black-box optimizer performs better in-distribution, the discovered symbolic optimizers often generalize better out-of-distribution. Reviewers note that the study is limited by comparing only a single black‑box baseline (OPEN), relying on OPEN‑specific prompt engineering, depending on a single LLM (GPT‑4o), and using only four RL environments. Presentation and clarity issues are also raised.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a crucial and timely question in learned RL optimization: whether symbolic or black‑box representations generalize better, providing an empirical comparison (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The LLM‑based discovery pipeline is clever and well‑developed; the use of separate “thinker” and “coder” LLMs is interesting and hot (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The pipeline requires less manual hyperparameter tuning and human expertise (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer found the presentation clear (Reviewer 3).
- The background and related work sections provide a well‑rounded introduction to learned optimization in RL (Reviewer 4).
- The experiments in Section 8 (testing under various conditions) are helpful and valuable (Reviewer 2).
- The discussion of results and future directions is thoughtful (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baselines and narrow scope.** Only OPEN (a single black‑box optimizer) and Adam are compared; many other learned optimizers reviewed in the paper, as well as standard optimizers like SGD, Lion, and AdamW, are omitted. This limits the generalizability of the conclusions (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).   The study also uses only four RL environments and a single discovery run (acknowledged by the authors) (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Dependence on LLM and OPEN‑specific prompts.** The method relies heavily on GPT‑4o, making results model‑specific and reducing robustness/reproducibility (Reviewer 4). Substantial domain knowledge from OPEN is embedded into the prompts (Appendix C), making the approach hand‑crafted and diverging from the stated goal of reducing human input (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** Several reviewers note poor writing: key terms (e.g., OPEN, discopt1/2/3) are introduced without explanation; the research question and the definition of “generalizability” are not stated early; typos (e.g., “usesw,” missing periods) and grammatical errors appear; technical details (e.g., the meaning of “small” weight changes, justification of p=0.8, multi‑task learning setup) are insufficiently explained or appear in the wrong sections (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). One reviewer, however, found the presentation clear (Reviewer 3).
- **Significance and practical value.** Even though symbolic optimizers show better out‑of‑distribution generalization, Adam often outperforms them in new environments overall. This raises the question of whether the extra meta‑learning cost is justified, especially when the pipeline is time‑ and resource‑consuming (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Generalization weaknesses.** The symbolic optimizers do not significantly outperform Adam in out‑of‑distribution environments, and they struggle on some transfer tasks (Reviewers 3, 4). Additionally, it is unclear whether the discovered optimizers generalize to other RL methods (e.g., DQN, REINFORCE) or non‑RL paradigms (e.g., supervised learning) (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablations and analyses.** The paper does not compare the performance of the discovered optimizers against the optimizers in the initial archive (Reviewer 3). The selection of the four archive optimizers is not justified, nor is the impact of archive size or different implementations studied (Reviewer 3). The thinker‑coder split has been explored in prior work and its advantage over a single LLM is not demonstrated (Reviewer 2). The sensitivity of results to prompt design and to the meta‑training task is not examined (Reviewer 2).
- **Technical concerns.** The discovery pipeline requires a large number of LLM conversations and RL inner training loops, making it computationally expensive (Reviewer 3). The parameter “m=0.9” in discovered optimizers is not clarified (is it a fixed hyperparameter?) (Reviewer 2). There is no code review or error‑recovery mechanism for LLM‑generated code (Reviewer 3). The method may overfit to the limited training environments, and its failure to transfer to new tasks suggests possible overfitting (Reviewers 2, 4).
