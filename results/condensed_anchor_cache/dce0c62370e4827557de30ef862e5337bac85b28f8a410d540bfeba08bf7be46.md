## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates circuit reuse between the Indirect Object Identification (IOI) task and the Colored Objects (CO) task in GPT-2 Medium. It reproduces the IOI circuit analysis on a larger model, finds 78% overlap in in-circuit attention heads, and performs an intervention that boosts CO accuracy from ~50% to ~94% by adjusting four heads to match IOI behavior. The work aims to address the task-specificity criticism of mechanistic interpretability by showing that circuit components and algorithms can generalize across tasks. One reviewer notes the paper addresses an important blindspot in the field; others acknowledge the importance but highlight significant limitations in scope, experimental rigor, and clarity.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important blindspot in mechanistic interpretability: the transferability and generalizability of circuit findings across tasks (R2, R4). This is a significant and original contribution (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper presents a clear and well-structured analysis of two tasks, with high-quality use of path patching, logit attribution, and attention pattern analysis (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The intervention experiment (Section 5) is a novel and promising proof-of-concept: by adjusting four attention heads to mimic IOI behavior, the authors boost CO accuracy from 49.6% to 93.7%, demonstrating a practical application of circuit understanding to correct model errors (R1, R3, R4). This is the most interesting contribution according to multiple reviewers (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper provides evidence that the IOI circuit is more than a task-specific mechanism; it functions as a general high-level copying mechanism, reused in the CO task (R2). Specific component roles (mover heads, induction heads, duplicate-token heads) are shown to have similar functionality across tasks (R3).
- The authors show good understanding of related prior work and frame their contribution well in context (R3).
- The paper openly reports a negative finding for GPT-2 Large/XL, showing honesty (R1), though this also raises concerns about generalizability.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope and lack of generalizability evidence:** The analysis is restricted to two toy tasks (IOI and CO) that are similar to each other, and only on GPT-2 Medium. The claim of circuit reuse is not tested on less similar tasks or on a broader distribution of text (R2, R3). Furthermore, Appendix J and the authors' own report that GPT-2 Large and GPT-2 XL do not show significant overlap raise serious doubts about generalizability. The paper fails to explain whether the lack of overlap is due to different heads performing the same functional roles or entirely different algorithms (R1). This limitation is acknowledged but strongly weakens the core argument (R2).
- **Overlap measurement is insufficiently justified:** The paper heavily relies on the 78% overlap figure, but the metric used is not described in sufficient detail. There is no quantification of uncertainty or control baselines to contextualize the significance of 78% (e.g., comparison to overlap with a random circuit or on random tasks). The authors mention an experiment in Appendix I, but this should be expanded and included in the main paper (R2). One reviewer explicitly asks for a more detailed description of the overlap metric, uncertainty quantification, and reference baselines to assess significance (R2 Q1).
- **Intervention experiment lacks necessary controls:** To prove that specific heads function as gathered heads (e.g., content gatherers), the authors perform an intervention (blocking attention) and observe a decrease in accuracy. However, they do not include control baselines, such as performing the same intervention on similar heads not expected to perform the gathered-head role. Without such controls, the conclusion is not definitively supported (R2). Additionally, the intervention focuses only on the inhibition-mover subcircuit and ignores the roles of induction heads, duplicate token heads, and content gatherer heads. A more thorough analysis of how the entire circuit interacts would strengthen the claims (R3). For example, can an intervention on induction heads reliably produce the attention pattern artificially induced in the inhibition heads? (R3)
- **Clarity and presentation issues hinder accessibility:** The paper uses heavy jargon without clear definitions, making it difficult for readers outside mechanistic interpretability to follow. Terms such as "circuit", "residual stream", "write in the residual stream", "inhibition", "noisy signal", and "[end]" are not adequately defined (R4). Figures (especially Figure 4) have unclear legends (e.g., "Dot w/token embed", "attn prob on token") and the significance of color coding is not commented upon (R2, R4). Section 4.3 is particularly difficult to follow (R2, R3). Appendix B and J are also hard to follow (R2). More formalism (e.g., equations for operations like dot with token embed) and more visualizations/examples would improve clarity (R4). Minor errors include "deterimine", "other" repeated, "to make to make", and misreferencing "Appendix 4.3" (R3). One reviewer suggests using different typesets for named heads (R4).
- **Conceptual and methodological novelty is limited:** The finding that circuits are reused is not new, as induction heads are known to be reused across contexts (R1). The techniques used (path patching, logit attribution, attention pattern visualization) are all previously established methods (R3). The analysis style largely reproduces the IOI paper approach (R1). The paper does not introduce new methods for quantifying circuit similarity or for performing interventions (R3).
- **Related work section is too concise:** The paper does not sufficiently discuss prior work addressing the task-specificity of interpretability findings. Specific connections to induction and duplicate heads (Olsson et al. 2022) and their relationship to the present findings are not explored (R2). Expanding this section would better position the contribution (R2).
- **Incomplete explanation of the "noisy" inhibition signal:** The paper's explanation for why the inhibition signal is "noisy" in the CO task is unconvincing. If the IOI view of inhibition is correct, the inhibition heads need input from induction heads to attend to the wrong color tokens. It is unclear how inhibition heads identify incorrect colors if the induction heads are not providing correct signals (R3). One reviewer hypothesizes that more in-context learning examples might improve the inhibition heads' performance and suggests testing this (R3 Q).
- **Analysis ignores MLPs:** The paper does not consider the role of MLPs in the circuit, and one reviewer questions whether MLPs are even considered in the analysis (R4). This omission is a significant gap in the circuit analysis.
- **Model failure on CO task not fully explored:** The paper does not deeply investigate why GPT-2 Medium fails on the CO task. It would be informative to vary the number of distractors and analyze the effect on overlap and accuracy (R2 Q5). The role of the [end] position is not clearly explained (R2 Q6). The definition of "top 2% important heads" (Section 4.5) needs more detail (R2 Q7).
- **Appendix J appears to contradict main claims:** Appendix J raises questions about the generalizability of the small-model/toy-task results to larger models on more complex tasks, which seems inconsistent with the paper's optimistic narrative. Clarification is needed (R2 Q8).
