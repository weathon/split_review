## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces MM-GRAPH, a multimodal graph benchmark that integrates both text and image features at the node level. It provides seven datasets spanning node classification, link prediction, and knowledge graph completion, along with standardized evaluation of GNN architectures, knowledge graph embeddings, feature encoders, data loaders, and evaluators. The study tests conventional GNNs alongside multimodal models such as MMGCN and MGAT, finding that multimodal GNNs do not consistently outperform traditional models and that aligned embeddings (e.g., CLIP, ImageBind) generally perform better than unaligned ones. The benchmark aims to facilitate more rigorous evaluation and development of multimodal graph learning methods.

### Strengths

- **Novel and necessary contribution.** MM-GRAPH fills a unique gap in graph learning benchmarks by including both textual and visual node attributes, moving beyond prior text-attributed graph benchmarks. The topic is practical and the datasets are necessary for real-world applications.
- **Comprehensive coverage of tasks and datasets.** The benchmark includes seven datasets at various scales across three tasks (node classification, link prediction, knowledge graph completion), enabling broad evaluation. The tasks are sufficient to assess multimodal graph learning methods.
- **Clear motivation and construction.** The motivation for multimodal attributed graphs is clearly stated, and the data construction process is easy to understand. The evaluation framework is detailed and the overall pipeline straightforward.
- **Extensive experimental analysis.** The paper provides extensive studies on the performance of various graph learning methods and feature encoders, including state-of-the-art techniques. The experiments lead to several meaningful conclusions about multimodal fusion.
- **Well-structured framework.** The benchmark standardizes evaluation across models, datasets, and feature encoders, facilitating consistent and reproducible comparisons. This structured approach is a strength for the community.
- **Significance for research.** By enabling researchers to focus on graphs with rich node attributes in realistic scenarios, MM-GRAPH helps develop graph algorithms better suited for practical applications.

### Weaknesses

- **Reproducibility and availability.** Dataset and code are not available on anonymous GitHub, limiting reproducibility and the ability to build upon the benchmark.
- **Missing tasks and task design limitations.**
  - Unsupervised graph tasks such as node clustering (e.g., Dink-Net) and graph-level clustering (e.g., GLCC) are omitted, and a comprehensive survey on these topics is missing.
  - The benchmark primarily addresses link prediction and node classification, but overlooks other common multimodal graph tasks like recommendation and retrieval, which are often more relevant in real-world applications.
  - Goodreads and Amazon datasets are each split into separate datasets solely based on task type (e.g., Goodreads-NC and Goodreads-LP), rather than being used as single datasets for both tasks. This construction approach is not ideal.
  - Graph-level tasks (e.g., graph classification) are not included; the authors should consider whether introducing such tasks is appropriate.
- **Scalability concerns.** The largest datasets (Goodreads-NC, Goodreads-LP) contain only about 0.6M nodes, which is modest compared to large graph benchmarks like ogbn-papers100M (~100M nodes). The benchmark's scalability is therefore limited.
- **Limited comparison and positioning relative to existing datasets.** The paper does not clearly distinguish the proposed benchmark from existing multimodal knowledge graph datasets or multimodal graph datasets. The relationship among MM-GRAPH, multimodal recommendation systems, and multimodal knowledge graphs is not adequately discussed.
- **Image quality issues in e-commerce datasets.** Images in Amazon-Sports and Amazon-Cloth are of lower quality (potentially low resolution). This could impact feature extraction quality and introduce bias, limiting the benchmark's representativeness for high-resolution multimodal data. The authors should consider whether image quality can be improved and quantify its impact on performance.
- **Insufficient analysis of experimental results and conclusions.**
  - The claim that aligned features are superior is not fully substantiated. For example, on Goodreads-LP, aligned features achieve only 5 out of 18 best metrics, losing the predominant superiority seen on Amazon datasets. The distinction between Goodreads-LP and the Amazon datasets and the reasons for underperformance deserve detailed discussion.
  - In KGC results (Table 6), aligned features are clearly inferior; T5-ViT and T5-Dinov2 significantly outperform them. This contradiction is overlooked.
  - The effect of switching KGE methods (e.g., MoSE to VISTA) differs across datasets: MM-CoDEx-m sees large improvements, while MM-CoDEx-s mostly degrades. The correlation between KGE method and dataset scale needs investigation.
  - The MLP model performs better than others on Ele-fashion but shows a marked decline on Goodreads-NC. The reasons for this discrepancy (e.g., dataset characteristics, handling of multimodal features) are not explored.
  - The paper claims that visual information improves understanding for complex tasks, yet Image-only models are mostly inferior to Text-only models, which contradicts this judgment.
  - Limited analysis is provided for each experimental section; deeper observations and findings are needed throughout.
- **Experimental design flaws regarding modality fusion.**
  - The conclusion that aligned features are superior is not controlled: it cannot be ruled out that the superior performance stems from CLIP/ImageBind text encoders being better than T5, or their image encoders outperforming ViT and Dinov2, rather than alignment itself. More comprehensive pairings are needed.
  - The paper does not explain how traditional unimodal models (GCN, MLP, SAGE) utilize multimodal features in the experiments. The strategy for standard GNNs to handle multimodal input is not introduced, and the difference between multimodal GNNs and standard GNNs requires further elaboration.
  - Multimodal feature fusion should be a key focus but is not adequately addressed.
- **Model selection limitations.**
  - Only T5, CLIP, and ImageBind are considered as text encoders; large language models (LLMs) that could serve as powerful text encoders are omitted.
  - MMGCN and MGAT were originally developed for multimodal recommendation tasks. The paper does not clarify how these methods are directly applied to the constructed datasets or describe any necessary adaptations.
  - The GNN baselines are relatively simple and do not incorporate many newer models. Evaluating recent multimodal large language models (MLLMs) on the benchmark could be more significant.
- **Insufficient innovation.** Beyond the benchmark itself, the methodological novelty is limited. The baselines compared are simple, and the GNN models evaluated are not state-of-the-art in the broader graph learning context.
- **Writing and presentation issues.**
  - The introduction contains multiple similar statements repeatedly emphasizing potential contributions; it should be more concise.
  - Section 3 (dataset descriptions) is structured ineffectively: dataset statistics are redundantly presented in both text and tables, and descriptions of homologous datasets (Amazon-Sports/Amazon-Cloth, Goodreads-LP/Goodreads-NC, MM-CoDEx-s/MM-CoDEx-m) contain repetitive phrases that could be streamlined.
  - Table formatting is chaotic: alignment is inconsistent in Table 1 (left, center, right mixed; "Proposed Task" and "Metrics" nearly overlap). In Table 5, text and image encoders do not align correctly with the models. The best performance highlighting varies between architecture-wise (Table 4) and architecture-agnostic (Table 5). The bar chart in Figure 4 is visually unappealing.
  - A formatting error appears (extra space before first word in Section 5.2).
- **Lack of domain-specific insights.** The benchmark does not explore how different domains (e.g., e-commerce vs. literature) exhibit distinct effects from integrating text and image features. This limits interpretability and adaptability across diverse data types, especially for applications with unique modality relationships (e.g., scientific texts with figures).
- **Insufficient references for key claims.** Some arguments, such as the added value of visual information, lack adequate references or citations from relevant studies to substantiate them.
