## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviews are split: two reviewers rate the paper highly (8) for its novel meta-pruning approach, comprehensive evaluation, and clear writing; one gives a medium score (6) noting solid experiments but minor presentation issues; two reviewers give low scores (3) citing insufficient theoretical motivation for the meta metric, non-negligible search overhead, lack of efficiency testing (latency/memory), limited compression-rate and dataset scope, and marginal performance gains that do not justify the added complexity. One high-scoring reviewer found no obvious weaknesses, while others raised multiple concerns.

### Strengths

1. **Novel and thought-provoking problem framing.** Casting LLM pruning as a meta-problem via evolutionary optimization to adaptively search pruning metrics and layerwise sparsity ratios is innovative (Reviewer 3). The paper provides insights into weight-distribution diversity across models, motivating adaptive strategies (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).

2. **Solid and comprehensive evaluation protocol.** Experiments cover four 7B-level models (LLaMA-1/2/3, Mistral) across 9 tasks (e.g., WikiText, GSM8K, MMLU), with analysis including end-to-end speedup evaluation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5). An ablation study justifies the choice of NSGA-III over other evolutionary algorithms (Reviewer 5).

3. **Methodology is logically structured and well-written.** The paper progressively deepens the discussion, clearly presenting the logical flow, experimental importance, and distinctions from related work (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). The writing is clear and free of typos (Reviewer 5).

4. **Effective search space and use of NSGA-III.** The meta-pruning metric search space is designed to mitigate diverse weight distributions (Reviewer 4). Employing NSGA-III for both single- and multi-objective search is a well-justified algorithmic choice (Reviewers 3, 4).

5. **Addresses a significant problem.** Efficient post-training pruning remains important, and the paper provides rigorous validation of the suboptimal generalization of fixed strategies (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

1. **Unclear motivation and insufficient theoretical backing.** The paper lacks a rigorous analysis of *why* existing pruning metrics fail to generalize across models (e.g., mathematical properties of weight distributions, activation patterns). Figure 1 only shows performance differences without underlying explanation (Reviewer 4). The design of the meta metric and the candidate options in the search space is confusing and lacks theoretical support (Reviewer 2). Consequently, the gains observed are not explained from a theoretical perspective (Reviewers 2, 4). *Minority contrast: One reviewer (score 8) found no obvious weaknesses and considered the evaluation thorough; however, this view is outweighed by the calls for deeper analysis from others.*

2. **Neglected efficiency and trade-off analysis.** No concrete measurements of inference time, memory footprint reduction, or energy consumption are provided, especially for unstructured pruning which suffers from irregular memory access (Reviewer 5). The search algorithm overhead is non-negligible: the paper restricts the search space to a limited discrete set but does not analyze the trade-offs between search space size, computational cost, and solution optimality, nor the sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., population size, number of generations) (Reviewer 4). One reviewer questions whether simple coefficient tuning within the meta metric could achieve similar results with less resource consumption (Reviewer 2). *Minority contrast: The high-scoring reviewers did not flag efficiency as a weakness, but the low-scoring reviewers (both with confidence 5) stress its criticality.*

3. **Limited experimental scope.** Testing is confined to a single 50% compression rate; no exploration of more aggressive rates (e.g., below 50%) (Reviewer 5). Only three datasets (GSM8K, MMLU, WikiText) are used; it would be beneficial to evaluate on a broader range (e.g., from lm-evaluation-harness) to demonstrate generalizability (Reviewer 5). Another reviewer questions why experiments are limited to LLaMA and Mistral models, suggesting testing on additional architectures (Reviewer 4). The paper shows negligible improvements on 30B/70B models without explanation (Reviewer 1’s question).

4. **Marginal performance gains versus added complexity.** On some models, e.g., Mistral in Table-2, MECON achieves 63.51 while simple Magnitude pruning achieves 63.34; such minor gains do not justify the significantly higher time and search complexity (Reviewer 5).

5. **Insufficient novelty and differentiation from prior work.** The proposed method is seen as a combination of existing ideas. It is not adequately distinguished from Pruner-Zero (both use search-based approaches); extending the search to layerwise sparsity ratios has been explored in computer vision (Reviewer 4). Several relevant works on NAS-based LLM compression are not compared or cited, e.g., “Pruning Large Language Models via Accuracy Predictor” and “Tune As You Scale” (Reviewer 5). A reference for NSGA-III is missing in the introduction (Reviewer 1).

6. **Missing interpretation of searched configurations.** The paper does not analyze which pruning metrics are most important, nor does it explain why methods like SparseGPT/Wanda perform poorly on some models (Reviewer 3’s questions). These omissions undermine confidence in the method's general insights.
