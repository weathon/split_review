## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents ClimaQA, a question-answering benchmark for evaluating climate foundation models. The authors develop ClimaGen, an automated framework that generates QA pairs from graduate-level textbooks with climate scientists in the loop. Two datasets are produced: ClimaQA-Gold (502 expert-annotated questions) and ClimaQA-Silver (3000 synthetic questions). The benchmark includes three task formats (multiple-choice, freeform, cloze) across three complexity levels (base fact recall, reasoning, hypothetical scenario). Several large language models are evaluated under default, few-shot, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) settings, using metrics such as Factual Accuracy, Phrase Similarity, BLEU, and BERTScore. Reviewer scores range from 6 to 8, with high confidence from some and lower confidence from others; overall there is disagreement about the strength of validation and the impact of proprietary biases.

### Strengths
- **Originality and Relevance**: ClimaQA is the first benchmark specifically tailored for climate science QA, addressing a critical gap in expert-annotated evaluation resources (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). The expert-in-the-loop pipeline (ClimaGen) is an innovative solution for generating domain-specific QA pairs (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Quality of Datasets**: The rigorous development process, including expert validation of ClimaQA-Gold and systematic generation of ClimaQA-Silver, ensures high-quality and scientifically grounded QA pairs (Reviewer 1, 4). The combination of a human-curated gold set and a large-scale synthetic set supports both high-quality benchmarking and scalability (Reviewer 3, 4).
- **Task and Complexity Diversity**: The three task types (MCQ, freeform, cloze) reduce biases from model familiarity with a single format (Reviewer 2). The three clearly defined complexity levels (base, reasoning, hypothetical scenario) enable comprehensive assessment of factual recall, reasoning, and application, allowing precise identification of model weaknesses (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Evaluation Capabilities**: The benchmark effectively stratifies models, demonstrating its challenge and utility as a reference for future climate AI models (Reviewer 3). Dedicated metrics (Factual Accuracy for freeform, Phrase Similarity for cloze) provide nuanced evaluation of factual precision and contextual understanding (Reviewer 4). RAG is shown to improve performance on reasoning questions, highlighting a promising adaptation technique (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability and Maintainability**: The synthetic scaling approach enables continuous updates to the benchmark, accommodating new findings in climate science research (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset Construction and Source Limitations**:
  - Exclusive reliance on 18 graduate-level textbooks may limit diversity of contextual information and scientific perspectives; details such as specific textbook titles, editions, and selection criteria are missing, undermining reproducibility and representativeness (Reviewer 2, 4, 5). The set of 5 books used for QA generation and 13 for RAG may not fully overlap; it is unclear whether the 13 books contain all necessary information to support questions from the 5 books (Reviewer 3). Coverage analysis across climate sub-disciplines and statistics on question distribution are absent (Reviewer 3, 4, 5).
  - ClimaQA-Gold is small (502 questions), which may restrict generalizability and scalability of automated annotations to other scientific domains (Reviewer 1, 4). The expert validation process is resource-intensive and may introduce inconsistencies due to subjective judgment; limited analysis of inter-annotator agreement or common generation errors is provided (Reviewer 1, 2).
  - ClimaQA-Silver questions often focus on single-hop fact retrieval and may lack the complexity needed to thoroughly test advanced models; the 85% validity rate for multiple-choice questions in Silver (Section 4.4) means that some models already exceed that performance on Gold, potentially compromising the synthetic dataset’s reliability (Reviewer 1, 2). No comparative performance analysis similar to Table 3 is provided for ClimaQA-Silver (Reviewer 2).
- **Evaluation Methodology Concerns**:
  - Exclusive use of proprietary models (GPT-3.5-turbo for question generation, GPT-4o-mini for freeform judgment) introduces potential bias favoring models from the same provider or those trained on similar outputs (Reviewer 2, 4). BLEU and BERTScore metrics show inconsistent correlation with expected model capability (e.g., GPT-3.5 performing comparably to GPT-4o, Figure 5) and may favor the generation model, raising validity concerns (Reviewer 2, 4). The study lacks quantitative comparisons with existing climate benchmarks such as Climate Crisis or SciQAG-24D (Reviewer 2).
  - Additional metrics for freeform evaluation (e.g., coherence, logical flow) are not considered, and reliance solely on GPT-4o-mini for judging freeform answers could be improved by a mixture-of-experts approach (Reviewer 1, 2). A tiered evaluation emphasizing reasoning/hypothetical questions separately would provide deeper analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing Validation and Experimental Depth**:
  - The paper does not experimentally demonstrate that the ClimaQA benchmark improves the scientific validity of climate foundation model outputs or that QA pairs are scientifically meaningful beyond factual recall (Reviewer 5). A case study visually comparing model outputs to groundtruth is lacking, which would clarify the benchmark’s ability to differentiate models (Reviewer 5). The motivation for selecting only three task types is not explained; other formats (e.g., judgment questions) could be considered (Reviewer 5).
- **Presentation and Clarity Issues**:
  - The term “foundation models” is used loosely; most evaluated models are large language models or QA models (Reviewer 3). Formulas in Section 3 use undefined symbols (e.g., \( p_s, p_r \); letters in factuality/phrase similarity equations) (Reviewer 3, 5). Figure 4’s conclusion that a context window of 4 is optimal is not clearly supported by the upward-trending plot (Reviewer 3). Writing flaws include missing citations (lines 32–35) and inconsistent font sizes in illustrations (Figure 6) (Reviewer 5).
- **Domain Justification**: The paper could more strongly argue why climate science requires specialized LLM evaluation and what applications are unlocked by better climate QA models (Reviewer 3).

**Note on Reviewer Disagreement**: One set of reviewers (1, 3; scores 8) emphasize the benchmark’s high quality, originality, and ability to stratify models, while another set (2, 4, 5; scores 6) raise significant concerns about evaluation biases, dataset validation, and missing experimental or source details. Reviewer 5 (confidence 5, highest) particularly questions whether the benchmark has been shown to improve scientific validity of model outputs. These contrasting perspectives highlight both the contribution’s promise and its current limitations in rigor and generalizability.
