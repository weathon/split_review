## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EMS (Adaptive Evict-then-Merge Strategy) for head-wise KV cache compression. It introduces a Global-Local score to identify token importance, combining global (across all layers) and local (within a layer) attention accumulation to reduce biases. The compression strategy first evicts irrelevant tokens, then merges remaining tokens based on similarity, using a zero-class center for dissimilar tokens. The method is head-wise and adaptive, adjusting compression ratios per head. Experiments on LongBench (budget 256) and Needle-in-a-Haystack tasks show improved perplexity and retrieval accuracy under extreme compression ratios.

### Strengths
- The global-local attention scoring mechanism provides a balanced token importance measure, reducing bias from accumulated attention scores or positional encoding.
- The head-wise Evict-then-Merge strategy adapts to sparsity and redundancy across different heads, improving storage density.
- Efficient parallel compression using a zero-class center enables merging without expanding memory for dissimilar tokens.
- The observation on KV similarity across heads is interesting and motivates the redundancy-based merging.
- The combination of eviction and merging achieves high compression rates while preserving context information, as demonstrated by 95% retrieval accuracy with <2% of context length on NIAH.

### Weaknesses
1. **Budget restriction and insufficient evaluation**: Only a single budget of 256 tokens is used on LongBench. No rationale is given for this choice. The method should be evaluated across multiple budgets (e.g., 128, 256, 512) to show trade-offs and robustness. Results for varying budgets in Table 5 lack baseline comparisons—all compression methods converge to full cache with larger budgets, so comparisons with baselines at each budget are needed. Reviewer 3 (rating 3) highlights this as a key missing analysis.
2. **Doubts about experimental pipeline**: Reported full-cache performance on several datasets deviates significantly from prior work. For Mistral-7B on MF-en, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, SAMSum, the paper reports 29.62, 36.42, 21.77, 71.00, whereas prior works [1,2] report ~49.34, 42.77, 27.35, 42.96 respectively. Similarly, on the synthetic PRe dataset, all models score near 0 in this paper, but prior work reports 86.98/89.3 for Mistral-7B and 30.5 for LongChat-7B. These discrepancies suggest potential implementation or pipeline errors. Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 5 both raise this, making it a serious concern.
3. **Redundancy estimation may mis-identify dissimilar tokens**: The method defines two tokens as redundant if cos_sim(K_x,K_y) * cos_sim(V_x,V_y) is high (product near 1). However, if both K and V are highly dissimilar (cos_sim = -1), the product is +1, incorrectly labeling them as redundant when they should both be kept. This needs clarification.
4. **Efficiency and deployment overhead**: 
   - The head-wise irregular pattern (non-uniform N_tbm, N_irr, N_imp across heads) requires the KV cache to exceed the target budget and may cause memory spikes. The claimed latency/efficiency analysis in Section 5.4 may be misleading without a per-head/layer memory breakdown.
   - The “shared entry expansion” mechanism has a non-zero probability of expanding to the full context length; the overhead of look-up tables and expansion logic (e.g., merging operations) is not quantified.
   - Global score calculation recomputes attention weights over the full context, which is impractical with FlashAttention due to quadratic computation and storage. The local score avoids this burden. Unless global scores provide significant benefits, the local-only variant is more practical.
   - Real-time merging adds computational cost; its overhead should be quantified and compared to alternatives across sequence lengths.
5. **Ablation experiments are insufficient**: 
   - The ablation study references a baseline value of 31.43 that does not appear in Table 1; its derivation must be explained.
   - No ablation varies the relative weights of global vs. local scores, nor are results provided under different budget settings or across different model sizes.
   - More ablation results (e.g., comparing eviction-only, merge-only, full EMS) under multiple budgets and models would strengthen the analysis.
6. **Similarity to existing methods and lack of differentiation**: Some sub-methods resemble StreamingLLM (local attention) and H2O (eviction based on accumulated attention). The head-wise adaptivity, similarity-based merging, and refined scoring have been explored in prior works [3-5]; the paper should clearly distinguish its contributions from those.
7. **Undocumented details and missing explanations**: 
   - The “expansion” step in Evict-then-Merge is only briefly mentioned in the appendix; its purpose and implementation (why and how) need elaboration in the main text.
   - The three categories of KV cache (important, irrelevant, mergeable) are not clearly distinguished.
   - The specific operations for “using and not using position information” (lines 369-371) are unclear.
   - The benefit of mean-alignment for sGlo and sLoc is not justified.
   - The exact setting of the NIAH task (e.g., haystack from repetitive sentences vs. long corpus) should be specified.

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 3) is notably more critical, particularly on experimental validity and redundancy estimation; these points are retained as minority perspectives. Other reviewers (scores 5) are positive overall but raise substantial concerns about evaluation scope, efficiency, and practical deployment.*
