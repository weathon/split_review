## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dynamic Negative Guidance (DNG), a technique that adapts the guidance scale in diffusion models based on time and state, improving upon conventional Negative Prompting (NP) which assumes a constant scale. DNG requires no additional training and estimates the posterior via tracking the discrete Markov chain. The method is evaluated on MNIST and CIFAR-10 for class-removal and on Stable Diffusion for text-to-image tasks, showing improvements in safety, class balance, and image quality over NP and Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD).

### Strengths
- DNG solves the suboptimal results or complete failures that can occur with conventional NP by dynamically modulating guidance, avoiding the problem of blindly inverting the force field toward the positive prompt. This is reflected in the factor \(p_t(c_-|x)/(1-p_t(c_-|x))\) in Eq. 10, which allows the method to adaptively determine whether a negative prompt is even relevant.
- The method is novel, requires no additional training, and has strong practical significance for text-to-image generation.
- The structure of the paper is clear and easy to follow, with thoughtful touches (e.g., color highlights in algorithms and formulas) that improve readability.
- The evaluation on MNIST and CIFAR-10 is comprehensive, covering safety, class balance, and image quality metrics (FID, KL divergence) and comparing against NP and SLD.
- Preliminary results with Stable Diffusion are promising, suggesting the method can improve prompt accuracy in more sophisticated generative models.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation on complex tasks:** The experiments are primarily on relatively simple class-conditional datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10). The paper lacks rigorous evaluation on high-variability and high-complexity datasets such as ImageNet. Given that text-to-image (T2I) is the primary application area, the evaluation on T2I generation is insufficient and lacks a systematic approach across different prompt types and image styles. One reviewer (rating 5) specifically notes this as a significant limitation, while another (rating 8) also raises the need for more diverse semantic tests.
- **Missing quantitative metrics for T2I:** The T2I evaluation provides only qualitative examples (Figures 6, and images starting on page 22) and does not include standard quantitative metrics such as FID or CLIP score. Deferring these metrics to future work greatly reduces the rigor and objectivity of the assessment, making it difficult to compare with existing methods. A reviewer (rating 5) explicitly identifies this as a notable gap.
- **Insufficient comparison with leading methods:** The paper compares DNG only with NP and SLD. It lacks a comprehensive comparison with other contemporary negative prompting techniques, which would better clarify the unique contributions and limitations of DNG.
- **Lack of parameter sensitivity analysis:** The paper does not explore how hyperparameters (e.g., guidance scale in NP, threshold in SLD) affect performance across methods. Without such an analysis, it is hard to draw definitive conclusions about the superiority of DNG over baselines.
- **Unclear experimental setup and illustrative examples:**  
  - The purpose of removing one class from training in Section 4.1 (MNIST and CIFAR-10) is not clearly motivated. If the goal is to avoid undesirable features, one reviewer questions why the model is not simply trained on all classes and then provided with a negative prompt at inference time.  
  - Example images (Figures 6, and the figures starting on page 22) are too small, and the differences between NP and DNG results are difficult to appreciate. In many cases NP does not produce obviously visible undesirable features (e.g., “view of skyline” in Figure 6), making the advantage of DNG unclear.  
  - The paper does not provide full example prompts (in English) as they would be fed to the T2I model, making it hard to reproduce or assess.  
  - For Figure 10(a) and (b), NP shows a decreasing pattern in KL divergence and FID while DNG performance picks up at higher percentages of wrong images; the cause of this inversion is not discussed. Similarly, in Figure 10(b), why NP achieves a lower FID than DNG in some regimes (though overall worse) needs explanation.  
  - Figure 11 shows larger guidance scales at later diffusion times for illogical prompts; intermediate generative results corresponding to this change would be helpful to understand the dynamics.
- **Writing and presentation issues:**  
  - Inconsistent notation (e.g., \(c_-\) vs. \(c-\) should be unified).  
  - Terms like “Dynamic Negative Guidance” and “guidance scale” could benefit from brief contextual definitions for readers unfamiliar with the domain.  
  - The flow and conciseness could be improved, and jargon (e.g., “non-stationarity and state-dependence”) should be explained when first introduced.
