## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Smart-Placement, a scene-aware augmentation method for monocular 3D detection. It uses a Scene-Aware Placement Network (SA-PlaceNet) to learn a distribution of plausible 3D bounding boxes from scene context, then renders synthetic cars using a diffusion model (ControlNet) and places them accordingly. Evaluated on KITTI and NuScenes, the method improves detection performance and data efficiency. The approach is modular but relies on multiple pre-trained components. Reviewers note the idea of learning placement distributions is interesting, but raise concerns about novelty, realism, missing ablations, insufficient evaluation, and overclaimed contributions.

### Strengths
- Novel distribution-based placement network (SA-PlaceNet) that goes beyond simple heuristics, producing plausible object positions (Reviews 1, 2).
- Clear motivation and problem decomposition into object placement and appearance; pipeline is well-structured and easy to follow (Reviews 3, 4).
- Extensive evaluation on KITTI val set shows consistent performance gains across multiple detectors; method shows training data efficiency (Reviews 1, 2, 3).
- Ablation studies and extensive visualizations of intermediate results (Review 4).
- Evaluated on two standard benchmarks (KITTI and NuScenes) (Review 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Learning training distribution, not edge cases**: The placement network learns to mimic the training distribution (e.g., orientation distribution nearly identical in Figure 5), so the method may not cover rare or novel scenarios. Source of improvements is unclear; potential overfitting to test distribution is speculated. This undermines the core motivation for data augmentation (Review 3).
- **Rendering realism is insufficient**: (a) Light inconsistencies – predefined lighting does not align with scene (Figure 4). (b) No shadows. (c) Car image is conditioned only on 3D asset edge images, not the surrounding scene, leading to artificial appearance (Figure 9). (d) The claim that “cars match scene lighting” is unsupported because the background is never used in the ControlNet process (Review 1, 3, 4).
- **Lack of novelty and overclaimed contributions**: The geometric-aware augmentation is similar to rule-based placement, and many technical details are incremental engineering rather than learning theory. The premise that 3D-aware placement is important for augmentation is described as common sense, not a novel contribution. Recent BEV detection and multi-view reconstruction (NeRF, 3DGS [1, 2] in review) could offer more realistic placements, making the approach appear less advanced (Reviews 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Missing evaluations and ablations**: (a) KITTI test set results are absent (Review 2); (b) Only two datasets – insufficient diversity (Review 4). (c) Missing fine-grained ablations, e.g., isolating the effect of location distribution vs. rendering choice (Review 2). (d) Core loss function (Eq. 5) is not defined clearly; it is unclear why that loss solves the bounding-box distribution learning problem (Review 4).
- **Dependence on multiple pre-trained networks**: The pipeline requires inpainting, depth prediction, and style transfer models, each introducing potential errors and uncertainties (Review 1).
- **Missing discussion of related work**: Highly related data augmentation methods, e.g., multi-modality augmentation [1] in Review 2, are not discussed (Review 2).
- **Formatting issues**: Excessive use of `\vspace` creates a disjointed layout (Review 1).
