## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the impact of prior distribution mismatch in PnP-ADMM, providing a theoretical error bound under nonconvex data-fidelity terms and expansive denoisers, and empirically demonstrates a simple domain adaptation strategy on image super-resolution and deblurring tasks. The reviews acknowledge the timeliness and theoretical contribution but raise concerns about experimental scope, clarity of presentation, and practical implications.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical analysis of prior mismatch**: Provides an explicit error bound for PnP-ADMM under mismatched denoisers, without requiring convex data-fidelity terms or non-expansive denoiser assumptions (Reviewers 1,3,4).  
- **Timely and relevant topic**: The study of prior mismatch is a relatively underexplored area in PnP literature (Reviewer 1).  
- **Empirical validation**: Numerical experiments on image super-resolution are well-done and suggest that the performance gap due to mismatch can be partially mitigated (Reviewer 1).  
- **Domain adaptation efficacy**: Demonstrates that fine-tuning a denoiser with as few as 4 images from the target distribution significantly improves performance; this result is considered surprising and valuable (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 similarly highlights the significance of domain adaptation in mitigating distribution shifts.  
- **Contrast in novelty assessment**: While one reviewer (Reviewer 2) considers the prior mismatch problem well-known and finds the comparisons missing, Reviewer 3 notes the theoretical error bound as novel and finds the domain adaptation role pivotal.  

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of connection between theory and experiments**: The paper does not explicitly relate the derived error bound (Theorem 1) to observed reconstruction quality in the numerical experiments, nor does it discuss how metrics like PSNR and SSIM relate to the theoretical bound (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing comparisons to existing methods addressing denoiser gap**: Several existing works (e.g., Shocher et al. 2018; Tirer & Giryes 2019) are not compared or discussed. No single existing method for handling mismatched priors is included in the experiments (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient details on domain adaptation**: The domain adaptation method lacks description of the loss function, training time, and computational resources (e.g., GPU memory); these are important since additional data/training may conflict with PnP’s non‑supervised nature (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 adds that the term “domain adaptation” should be defined early and different strategies (adversarial training, transfer learning, meta-learning) should be mentioned to justify the chosen “distribution alignment” approach.  
- **Limited empirical scope**:  
  - Experiments focus only on super-resolution and deblurring with convex ℓ₂ data fidelity and negligible measurement noise (level 0.01). No evaluation on nonconvex data-fidelity terms (e.g., ℓ₁ norm, total variation) or on problems like compressive sensing or phase retrieval (Reviewers 2,3).  
  - The low noise level (0.01) is considered near noise‑free; more realistic noisy conditions are needed to assess robustness (Reviewer 3).  
- **Theoretical concerns**:  
  - Theorem 2 contains an apparent typo: the starting point condition \((x^0,z^0,s^0)\) cannot be guaranteed; clarification is needed (Reviewer 4).  
  - The convergence analysis appears similar to existing nonconvex ADMM literature (e.g., “Global Convergence of ADMM in Nonconvex Nonsmooth Optimization”) but the comparison is missing. It is unclear whether the results simply instantiate known analyses with summable‑error assumptions (Reviewer 4).  
  - Theorems 1 and 2 are described as proving convergence to a stationary point, but the stated results do not directly convey this; the actual proof in the supplement may show it, but the main text should be explicit (Reviewer 4).  
  - Assumption 6 (involving all stationary points of the augmented Lagrangian) needs clarification: does the theorem assume existence of a stationary point? Is it guaranteed by the other assumptions? (Reviewer 4).  
  - Assumption 1 (full‑space support of the image distribution) is questionable: realistic images often lie in a lower‑dimensional set (e.g., union of subspaces), which might violate the assumption (Reviewer 4).  
- **Inadequate literature review**: The related work section lacks depth, especially in domain adaptation and PnP theoretical analyses. A table comparing assumptions of prior convergence proofs is missing, making it hard to assess the contribution relative to existing work (Reviewer 3).  
- **Presentation issues**:  
  - PnP is incorrectly described as a class of deep learning algorithms; it should be clarified as an iterative framework that can incorporate a deep denoiser, not a DL class itself (Reviewer 3, Q1).  
  - The introduction needs a brief explanation of PnP and early definition of “domain adaptation” (it appears five times before Section 4.2) (Reviewer 3, Q2, Q6).  
  - Some references are irrelevant to the sentence they support (Reviewer 3, Q8).  
  - The derivations of \(\nabla\hat{h}(z^k)\) on Page 15 and Eq.(16) on Page 16 are unclear and need further explanation (Reviewer 3, Q14, Q15).  
- **Practical implications limited**: The theoretical results rely on assumptions that are rarely verifiable in practice, and knowing a bound on the gradient deviation does not easily translate into convergence to a meaningful point (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing ablation and robustness tests**: The paper does not explore varying levels of measurement noise, nor does it include nonconvex data-fidelity scenarios to support the theoretical claims (Reviewer 3, Q10, Q11).
