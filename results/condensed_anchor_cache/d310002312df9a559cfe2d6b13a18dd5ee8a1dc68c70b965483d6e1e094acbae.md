## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sketched Adaptive Federated Learning (SAFL), combining gradient sketching (e.g., CountSketch) with adaptive optimizers (Adam, AMSGrad) for communication-efficient federated learning. It provides convergence analyses achieving logarithmic dependence on the ambient dimension \(d\) (as opposed to linear in prior work) under a novel assumption of fast decaying Hessian eigenspectrum. Two variants are presented: SAFL for i.i.d. clients (asymptotic \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) convergence with faster initial rates) and SACFL (SAFL with clipping) for non-i.i.d. heavy-tailed noise. Experiments on CIFAR-10 (ResNet, ViT) and SST2 (BERT) show the framework is competitive with existing communication-efficient methods. Reviewers disagreed on the significance: some (R2, R4, score 6) found the theoretical extension important and the analysis technically involved; others (R1, R3, score 3) questioned the novelty given the stronger assumptions, the experimental rigor, and the motivation for heavy-tailed noise.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical analysis of adaptive optimizers + gradient sketching.** The combination is relatively unexplored in federated learning. The analysis handles sketching error, local steps, and client data heterogeneity, using a martingale-based error decomposition (R4).
- **Logarithmic dependence on dimension \(d\).** Convergence rates scale as \(\text{poly}\log d\) instead of the linear in \(d\) seen in prior work (e.g., FetchSGD, Song et al. 2023). This is achieved by leveraging the assumption of fast decaying Hessian eigenspectrum (anisotropic curvature), which is observed in many deep networks (R1, R2, R4).
- **Flexible framework.** Compatible with various optimizers and sketching algorithms; dual versions for i.i.d. (SAFL) and non-i.i.d. with heavy-tailed noise (SACFL) increase applicability (R1, R2).
- **Empirical validation across domains and architectures.** Tested on CIFAR-10 (ResNet, ViT) and SST2 (BERT) in both fine-tuning and training-from-scratch regimes (R1, R2).
- **Clear and well-motivated presentation.** Theoretical motivation and contributions are clearly stated; the paper reads well (R2, R3).
- **Extends prior theoretical work.** The analysis generalizes [Rothchild et al., 2020; Song et al., 2023] by allowing elementwise second moments of sketched gradients and handling bounded \(\alpha\)-moments for \(\alpha\in(1,2]\) (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Improvement in dimension dependence is a direct consequence of stronger assumptions, not fundamentally new techniques.** The logarithmic \(d\) dependence follows from Assumption 4 (power-law decay of Hessian eigenspectrum); prior works only assumed smoothness and bounded gradient. Without this assumption, the worst-case nuclear norm \(\hat{L}\) could be \(dL\), recovering the linear dependence (R1, R3, R4). Some reviewers (R1, R3) viewed this as low novelty, while others (R2, R4) acknowledged it as a valid use of an empirically observed property.
- **Assumptions are restrictive and may not hold simultaneously.** Assumptions 1 (bounded gradients) and 4 (Hessian eigenspectrum decay) are hard to ensure together: deep networks may satisfy Assumption 4 but not bounded gradients; classic convex objectives (e.g., logistic regression) satisfy bounded gradients but do not exhibit the Hessian decay typical of deep learning. The paper does not provide a natural example satisfying both (R3). Moreover, Assumption 4 is only shown to hold after a few epochs of SGD; its validity throughout the entire training process is unclear (R2).
- **Potential hidden dependence on \(d\) via constant \(\hat{L}\).** Even though the rate is \(\text{poly}\log d\) in the main term, the constant \(\hat{L}\) (nuclear norm of Hessian) may itself scale with \(d\) in practice, reducing the practical benefit (R3).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons and ablations.** Experiments compare mostly against older baselines (1-bit Adam, FetchSGD) and omit recent algorithms (Efficient-Adam, Compressed AMSGrad, CocktailSGD, etc.). Comparisons with non-adaptive FL methods (e.g., FedAvg) are missing, as are ablation studies isolating the contributions of sketching versus adaptivity (R1, R3). The question of whether SAFL outperforms “FetchAdam” (FetchSGD with Adam) is not addressed (R2).
- **Weak motivation for heavy-tailed noise in federated learning.** The paper cites Yang et al. 2022, but that work observes heavy tails in the distribution of aggregated local client updates, not in the stochastic gradient noise at a single point. The justification for modeling heterogeneous FL with heavy-tailed gradient noise is therefore questionable (R3).
- **Experimental methodology concerns.** Hyperparameter tuning is described only as “tuned to achieve the best performance,” with no strict protocol; it is possible some algorithms were tuned more than others. Results are reported over a single random seed, and the sketching algorithm used in experiments is not specified (R2, R3). Communication cost of FetchSGD relative to the other methods is not controlled (R3).
- **Missing convergence rate comparison with prior work.** The paper does not explicitly write out the convergence rates of previous works for a direct comparison; the claim of linear dependence relies on an interpretation of per-round constant communication cost (R3).
- **Scalability and compression-quality trade-offs not explored.** Experiments involve small numbers of clients under controlled conditions; real-world FL with thousands of heterogeneous clients, variable network conditions, and diverse hardware is not examined. The impact of different compression rates on convergence speed, accuracy, and robustness is not systematically studied (R1).
- **Related work discussion incomplete.** Discussion of heavy-tailed noise literature misattributes claims (Charles et al. 2021 may not discuss heavy tails) and incorrectly claims optimality in distributed settings (R3).
- **Assumptions 1 and 2 are strong and hard to ensure in practice** (R2). The claim that SAFL enjoys faster convergence near initialization due to adaptivity is not well motivated; it is unclear whether performance gains come from Adam over SGD (which is used in FetchSGD) (R2).
- **Formatting issues:** Abstract should be a single paragraph; typos in Lines 081, 133; ambiguous expectation in Property 2; Figure 1 uses inconsistent colors across subplots (R3).
