## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper shows that standard binary activation approaches fail to train when applied to recurrent binary activation networks (BARNNs) with explicit recurrent weights, and presents a theoretical argument for the necessity of temporal continuity. By systematically incorporating mechanisms from spiking neural networks (SNNs), the authors find that integrative state (stateful accumulation) enables recurrent binary activation networks to reach similar performance as floating-point approaches, while explicit reset and leakage terms do not affect performance. Reviewers were divided: one found the work highly significant, original, and clear (score 8), while others were critical (scores 1, 3, 3), citing narrow scope, flawed assumptions, insufficient temporal tasks, unconvincing analysis, and poor exposition.

### Strengths
- **Innovative application of SNN concepts**: Introducing pre-activation state, leakage, and reset mechanisms is an innovative approach to handling binary activations in RNNs.
- **Exploration of multiple training methods**: The paper systematically compares surrogate gradient descent, probabilistic surrogates, and progressive sharpening.
- **Comprehensive experimental setup** across three distinct tasks (image classification, keyword spotting, small object tracking) demonstrates versatility.
- **Potential for hardware implementations**: Valuable for real-world deployment in energy-efficient temporal modeling (embedded/neuromorphic systems).
- **Significance**: Makes a valuable connection between conventional binarized networks and SNNs, carefully uncovering that stateful accumulation (integration) is the key ingredient for training BARNNs.
- **Originality**: First systematic comparison of conventional BARNN training methods for RNNs to SNNs on relevant spatiotemporal tasks.
- **Quality and clarity**: Approach generally clear, line of reasoning lucid, clear narrative.
- **The results are new**.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of basic SNN understanding**: The claim that "Binary activation NNs have only been reported for feedforward topologies" is false because every SNN network is a binary activation recurrent network.
- **Obviousness**: The claim that state allows training is trivial; state is responsible for recurrent behavior to begin with.
- **Temporal instability discussion (Section 4.1)** is unconvincing:
  - It is just about the smoothness of gradients, not a clear technical analysis of instability.
  - The term "instability" is ambiguous (does not mean unbounded dynamics; gradients may still propagate).
  - The edge case of a constant input does not justify the claim.
  - The logic from lines 313–341 is hard to follow: the oscillatory example (Eq 10) would still have non-zero surrogate gradients; the phrase "real valued BARNN" is confusing; "dense discontinuities in the input" is unclear.
- **Experiments are not temporal**: Inputs are provided in a single timestep (only step response observed), so the tasks are essentially spatial. This makes it impossible to assess temporal integration or the effect of decay/refractoriness.
- **Results do not decisively support conclusions**: Different datasets favor different strategies; statefulness does not consistently achieve the best results.
- **Conclusion about decay/refractoriness is invalid** because with no temporal integration, no effect would be expected.
- **Narrow scope**: The paper only tests SNN-like architectures (with/without integration), but recurrent BANNs are a broader class (e.g., binarized GRU and LSTM exist, not addressed).
- **Accuracy is well below SOTA** (e.g., ~80% on GSC vs 95% in recent work).
- **Activation function choice not discussed**: SNN community uses {0,1} while BANN community uses {-1,1}; restricting to {0,1} limits scope.
- **Confounding factor**: SNN Eq (7) uses an infinite-extent surrogate derivative, while BARNNs (Eqs 2–4) use finite-extent; this could be a reason SNNs perform better beyond integrative state.
- **Equation nomenclature and legibility**: Eq 8 unclear if the layer is a stacked ConvRNN; variable ‘y’ is used in both SNN and BARNN contexts, conflating distinct mechanisms.
- **Figure 4.1 requires more explanation**: It seems to show binary activation reconstructing the input unlike the SNN – unclear.
- **Lack of focus**: Contributions are listed but lack clarity; pre-activation states, leak, and reset are discussed without a strong unified narrative explaining necessity.
- **Missing justification and explanations**:
  - L-099: justification for centering activation functions missing.
  - L-100: “literature standards” not explained.
  - Table 2: difference between CNN-RNN and CRNN not explained.
  - SOT benchmark not explained clearly.
- **Minor presentation issues**:
  - Eq 9 should be dx_L / dt.
  - Eq 12 corresponds to the non-leaky Integrate and Fire (IF) neuron.
  - Eq 13 bottom should be dx_L, not dx_L / dt.
  - g_L is redundant with tau_x (both control decay).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**:
  - Are all weights and integrative states floating point? (Only activations are binary.)
  - Why are dense layers for SC and SOT not recurrent?
  - Hyperparameter selection process not justified; could conclusions be artifacts of hyperparameter choices? (E.g., sharpening might work with different settings.)
  - What is an autapse?
  - Did the authors use a distribution of leaks? Were leaks trainable parameters?
  - For output, why only the final step activity was taken instead of mean/max or all timesteps? (Common practice in SNN papers.)

*Note: One reviewer (R3) was highly positive about the significance and originality, but other reviewers found substantial flaws as listed above.*
