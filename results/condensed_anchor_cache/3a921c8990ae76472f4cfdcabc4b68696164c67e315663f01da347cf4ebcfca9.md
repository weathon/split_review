## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TUAP, a targeted universal adversarial perturbation method against CLIP models in a black-box setting, focusing on transferability. The adversary specifies a target text description and generates a universal \(L_\infty\)-norm or \(L_2\)-norm perturbation or an unrestricted patch using an ensemble of surrogate CLIP encoders. Experiments across 9 datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR, Food101, etc.) and multiple downstream tasks (image-text retrieval, zero-shot classification, image description generation) demonstrate transferability to unseen CLIP models and large VLMs (OpenFlamingo, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, BLIP2). Most reviewers (scores 3,3,3) find limited novelty and a lack of technical innovation or in-depth insight; one reviewer (score 8) is more positive about the experimental scope but still notes multiple limitations.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well organized, and provides comprehensive background knowledge.
- Universal transferability is demonstrated across diverse datasets and models, including unseen CLIP variants and several large VLMs (OpenFlamingo, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, BLIP2).
- The attack is targeted: adversaries can specify precise text descriptions for the adversarial output.
- The method offers flexibility through three perturbation forms (\(L_\infty\)-bounded, \(L_2\)-bounded, unrestricted patch), suitable for different scenarios.
- The ensemble of surrogate CLIP encoders improves attack transferability.
- Experiments are extensive: 9 datasets (e.g., ImageNet, CIFAR, Food101) and multiple tasks (image-text retrieval, zero-shot classification, image caption generation).
- Visualizations (e.g., Figure 1) intuitively show that the attack succeeds.
- Tables and presentation format are good; experimental analysis is reasonable and convincing.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The idea is a straightforward migration of existing UAP methods from image classification to VLM models. No specific design for the CLIP architecture is introduced (all reviewers note this; reviewer 2 calls it “an experimental report”).
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison with prior universal adversarial attack methods for VLP models (e.g., [1][2]) or with black-box transferability-based attacks (e.g., [a][b]). Query-based black-box attacks are also not compared (reviewers 1, 3).
- **No in-depth analysis**: The paper reports attack success but lacks mechanistic insight into why the attack transfers or how CLIP’s representation space is exploited (reviewers 2, 3).
- **Perceptibility of perturbations**: \(L_\infty\) and \(L_2\) perturbations may produce visible artifacts; no quantitative evaluation of structural similarity or perceptual distance. Figure 1 shows visible shark textures that VLMs could (correctly) identify as sharks, undermining stealth (reviewers 1, 4).
- **Limited target text diversity**: Only 10 target sentences are tested, with no exploration of complex or nuanced semantics (negation, conditionals, emotional tone) that occur in real applications (reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient ablation and sensitivity analysis**: The effects of perturbation intensity and spatial location (edge vs. main area) on attack success, imperceptibility, and transferability are not systematically studied (reviewer 4).
- **Dataset scope**: Experiments rely on typical datasets like ImageNet; the attack is not evaluated on domain-specific images (e.g., medical, remote sensing, surveillance) where CLIP feature representations may differ significantly (reviewer 4).
- **Lack of results on closed-source models**: No experiments on models like ChatGPT‑4 (reviewer 1).
- **Training details unclear**: It is not specified whether the universal perturbation is trained on the whole training set of the surrogate model (reviewer 2).
- **Missing exploration of universal adversarial texts**: The paper only considers image perturbations; the possibility of generating universal adversarial texts for the text encoder is not discussed (reviewer 3).
- **Introduction needs reformulation**: The introduction uses unnecessary space (reviewer 2).
