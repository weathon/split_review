## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Add-it, a training-free method for inserting objects into images based on text instructions. It extends the multi-modal attention mechanism of a pretrained diffusion model (FLUX) to incorporate information from three sources: the source image, text prompt, and generated image. A weighted extended self-attention, structure transfer (via noise injection and blending), and a subject-guided latent blending (using SAM2 for mask refinement) are combined to maintain structural consistency and fine details while achieving natural object placement. The method also introduces a new “Additing Affordance Benchmark” (200 images) for evaluating placement plausibility. Without task-specific fine-tuning, Add-it achieves state-of-the-art results on both real and generated image insertion benchmarks, outperforming supervised methods, and is preferred in over 80% of human evaluations.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and presentation:** The paper is well-written and easy to follow; figures are thoughtfully designed and effectively illustrate the method (all reviewers). Hyperparameters and implementation settings are provided, aiding reproducibility (R4).
- **Training-free and simple method:** The approach requires no fine-tuning or additional training, directly leveraging pretrained diffusion models (R2). The method is considered straightforward and innovative (R3).
- **Visual quality:** Results are visually compelling, with objects integrated seamlessly and original details preserved (R1). Experimental results substantiate effectiveness (R3) and achieve state-of-the-art results (R4).
- **Attention analysis:** The analysis of MM-DiT attention distribution is interesting, and the weighted extended self-attention mechanism is an effective way to control contribution from different inputs (R4).
- **Novel components:** The Subject-Guided Latent Blending mechanism preserves fine details while enabling adjustments like shadows and reflections (R2). The introduction of a benchmark to evaluate object placement plausibility is a contribution (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty and lack of comparative analysis:** Several core techniques have been used in prior U-Net-based training-free editing works, and the modifications here are seen as incremental (R4). Specifically:
  - *Weighted Extended Self-Attention:* Applying a coefficient to keys is similar to scaling text embeddings [1] without a clear justification or comparative analysis against other weighting methods (R4).
  - *Structure Transfer:* Shows only minor improvements over SDEdit [2]; no detailed analysis of limitations overcome and no ablation to isolate its impact (R4).
  - *Subject-Guided Latent Blending:* Similar blending operations in [3,4,5]; using SAM2 is a straightforward extension that lacks originality (R4).
- **Inadequate baselines and missing comparisons:** The paper lacks comparison with existing methods for adding objects [1,2] whose codes have been released (R2). The set of tuning-free editing baselines is insufficient; methods such as Masactrl [3], Proximal Guidance [4], and InfEdit [5] provide more sophisticated control mechanisms and should be included (R4). Also missing comparisons on traditional image quality metrics like FID and IS (R3).
- **Reliance on SAM2:** The method requires SAM2 for mask estimation, which may not always accurately capture desired object boundaries (R2), and using SAM2 impacts processing speed compared to the original FLUX (R1). Alternative approaches using self-attention/cross-attention maps could relieve this requirement (R2). No experiment with using a coarse mask as input for SAM2 vs. point-based prompts is reported (R1).
- **Prompt complexity and user experience:** Add-it requires text prompts for target images, while prior methods like InstructPix2Pix or MagicBrush may offer a more straightforward user experience (R1).
- **Evaluation benchmark limitations:** The Additing Affordance Benchmark contains only 200 images, limiting generalizability (R1). The advantages of this benchmark over existing benchmarks like MagicBrush are not clearly articulated, and details about the benchmark itself are scarce (R3).
- **Potential biases not fully discussed:** The paper only briefly mentions biases inherited from pretrained models, which could impact object placement based on demographic or contextual biases, leading to unfair outcomes (R2).
- **Ablation claim inconsistency:** The paper claims setting gamma=auto yields best results, but Figure 7(a) appears to show a peak at ~1.07 surpassing the auto setting (R1). This discrepancy undermines the claim.
- **Minor error:** Incorrect quotation marks on line 237 (the word ‘choose’ is improperly quoted) (R4).
- **Missing or insufficient analysis:** The paper does not discuss the underlying reasons for the phenomenon observed in lines 210-212 (whether it arises from text encoder or diffusion model architecture) (R4). More details about Structure Transfer would be useful (R3). It is unclear if the approach can be adapted to perform removal of objects (R3). The method’s applicability to multiple target objects or editing beyond insertion (e.g., changing a prompt to “The man is holding a shopping bag” or “wears a red shirt”) has not been experimented with (R3).
