## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces Chunk-Distilled Language Modeling (CD-LM), a decoding technique that uses a chunk proposal model (CPM) to retrieve fine-grained multi-token chunks from a structured datastore (trie with entry tokens) and integrate them into generation. Like speculative decoding, it skips decoding steps; unlike speculative decoding, it can change the output distribution and enables novel applications. The datastore can be built from the model itself (self-distillation), a larger model (knowledge distillation), or human-annotated/expert corpora (expert distillation). The method is evaluated on domain adaptation, inference speed, and grounding to external knowledge.

### Strengths

- **Technically sound and well-designed**: The algorithm marginalizes the joint distribution \(p(x,z)\) for accepting/rejecting chunks, and the trie structure reduces storage space compared to naïve vector search (Reviewer 1). The method is novel, combining phrase-level grounding with decoding speedup without increasing context length like RAG does; it acts as a fine-grained phrase-level cache with fuzzy matching (Reviewer 2). 
- **Flexible and plug-and-play**: It extends chunk-level generation to three important settings—self-distillation (inference speedup), knowledge distillation (improving smaller models via larger-model chunk scoring), and expert distillation (injecting private or curated knowledge)—without additional training (Reviewer 3). 
- **Extends \(k\)NN-LM to chunk retrieval**: Overcomes the limitation of retrieving only one token per step, which leads to higher information density and faster inference (Reviewer 4).
- **Extensive experiments and clear writing**: The paper includes many experimental results and detailed descriptions, inspiring future work (Reviewers 1, 2). 
- **One reviewer is more positive (rating 8)**: Emphasizes that “even if there are short-comings … this efficient, granular grounding technique will be of value to the community,” and notes the strong demonstration of the flexibility to fill the cache with anything (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Distribution shift vs. speculative decoding**: The decoding algorithm (Section 3.2) does not guarantee equivalence to sampling from the base model’s distribution, unlike speculative decoding. This can reduce performance; e.g., in self-distillation experiments (Figure 6), saving about 20% mean token time causes a significant BLEURT score drop across many LLMs (Reviewer 1). A minority reviewer (Reviewer 2) agrees this is a limitation but notes it can be used “potentially for the better” in some applications.
- **High cost of datastore construction**: Knowledge distillation requires two full passes over the corpus (scoring chunks with the larger model, then building the vector datastore with the base model) – a hidden offline cost (Reviewer 1). In general, LLM scoring to build a sufficiently covering datastore may be expensive (Reviewer 2). Cost may not be cheaper than fine-tuning a small model, yet Table 1 shows KCD-LM does not outperform domain fine-tuned models (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental limitations and missing coverage realism**:
  - The chunk datastores are built from contexts that align with the evaluation data; results thus represent a best-case scenario for a well-built/updated cache. It is unclear how retrieval would perform with very large collections of tries or on tail queries (Reviewer 2).
  - Decoding time savings depend on cache coverage; experiments only show good-scenario results. If coverage is low, the cost of unnecessary nearest-neighbor search may cause speed loss (Reviewer 2).
  - The chunk datastores in the paper are small; scalability of storage (huge key-value pairs) is a major concern, and the method is not tested at scale (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons**:
  - No comparison with other chunk-retrieval methods like “Nearest Neighbor Speculative Decoding for LLM Generation and Attribution” (2402.17532). This related work also applies chunk-level generation to speculative decoding, and the authors should clarify differences in inference speed and generation quality (Reviewer 3). 
  - KCD-LM relies on large-model distillation to improve small models, which is an unfair comparison to small models that do not receive such external information. There is also no self-distillation baseline for KCD-LM (Reviewer 4).
  - Need to include more QA and closed-set tasks to demonstrate effectiveness more convincingly (Reviewer 3). 
- **Insufficient human evaluation and metric concerns**:
  - Experiments in 6.1 and 6.2 rely only on automated metrics (PPL, MAUVE), which correlate poorly with human judgment. Human evaluation or LLM-as-a-judge should be used to assess generation quality, as already done in 6.3 (Reviewer 3). 
  - For “injecting factual knowledge,” the evaluation focuses on entity distributions rather than factuality or response quality; mis-retrieved entities could harm factuality, which is not captured (Reviewer 2).
- **Table 1 issue**: In medical and law domains, KCD-LM’s PPL is not better than the base model. Does this mean chunk-based generation can reduce quality? The authors should discuss possible reasons (Reviewer 3). 
- **Machine learning content**: The method is largely statistical (chunk mining via probability thresholds), with ML only used to tune acceptance probabilities. The paper lacks insights into machine learning or representation learning (Reviewer 4). 
- **Novelty concerns**: CD-LM heavily follows the Copy Generator framework (“Copy is all you need”), though the extension of application scenarios is appreciated (Reviewer 3). 
- **Clarity issues**: 
  - Section 5 (the dynamic decoding marginalization) is difficult to follow (Reviewer 4). 
  - The definition of \(f_\theta\) and pooling from context to context vector is not given (Reviewer 2). 
  - Calculation of perplexity (PPL) for chunk-level generation is unclear; the method does not use a fixed vocabulary, so how is PPL defined? (Reviewer 3). 
  - In the self-distillation setting, only inference speed is compared; why is there no PPL comparison? (Reviewer 4).
