## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Riemannian Optimization for Hyperbolic Prototypical Networks (RHPN), which leverages hyperbolic geometry within a Prototype Learning framework. The method optimizes prototypes on the Poincaré ball using Riemannian optimization and adds a regularization term to push embeddings away from the boundary. Experiments on four image classification datasets show improvements over state-of-the-art prototype learning methods.

### Strengths
- Applying Riemannian optimization in hyperbolic spaces to prototype learning is interesting and the paper is the first to use learnable prototypes in this setting (Reviewer 1; also noted by Reviewer 2).
- Extensive experimental evaluation across multiple datasets and embedding dimensions demonstrates the effectiveness of RHPN (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). In particular, the analysis of δ-hyperbolicity of the learned embeddings offers insights into how hyperbolic geometry aligns with data structure (Reviewer 2).
- The framework’s flexibility to be extended to other geometries is a strength, suggesting potential future applications (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is clearly structured, with easy-to-understand presentation of the method and results, and provides many visualizations of training behavior (Reviewer 3). The comparison between learnable prototypes and fixed prototype methods is interesting (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The contribution is limited. Learning prototypes in hyperbolic spaces has been explored before (e.g., Hyperbolic Image Embeddings, curvature generation in curved spaces for few-shot learning, curvature-adaptive meta-learning; see references [a,b,c,d] in Reviewer 4). The paper applies existing concepts (Riemannian optimization, clipping, regularization) without introducing new ideas (Reviewer 3). The specific novelty for image classification versus other applications or optimization methods is not clearly articulated (Reviewer 2). Reviewers disagree on this point: one reviewer (score 6) finds the approach compelling, while three others (scores 3,3,5) argue that the novelty is low and the motivation for hyperbolic geometry is insufficiently justified.
- **Unfair comparison and weak motivation for hyperbolic geometry**: The main comparison uses RHPN* (with regularization) against ECL (without regularization), which is not a fair ablation. The fair comparison (RHPN without regularization vs. ECL) shows hyperbolic is considerably worse on CUB for large embedding dimensions. Adding regularization that pushes representations towards the origin makes them less hyperbolic, undermining the argument for using hyperbolic space (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental scope**: Only four image classification tasks are evaluated. Missing evaluations on few-shot learning, incremental learning, and zero-shot learning, which are common benchmarks for hyperbolic prototype methods (Reviewer 4, also mentioned by Reviewer 1 as a question about few-shot learning performance). The trend of increasing performance with higher embedding dimensions warrants exploration of bottlenecks, computational cost, and diminishing returns (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The slope parameter λ requires separate tuning per dataset and shows significant performance variance across dimensions (Reviewer 1, 2). Figure 4(a) and 4(b) indicate model collapse for λ=0.1 and λ=0.3 in low dimensions, which is not explained (Reviewer 2). The reasons behind these fluctuations and collapse need deeper analysis (Reviewer 2).
- **Methodological clarity gaps**: The vanishing gradient problem in Riemannian SGD is not described explicitly. The paper should illustrate how the Poincaré ball’s curvature and distance metric cause gradient magnitudes to shrink near the boundary, and why standard Euclidean optimization is insufficient. Figure 3 should clarify this issue (Reviewer 2).
- **Regularization term insufficiently motivated**: The regularization that pushes embeddings away from the boundary is an important component, but its motivation and effects are not well explored (Reviewer 3, 4). The fact that it reduces hyperbolicity contradicts the original choice of hyperbolic space (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational complexity**: Riemannian optimization adds extra computational cost, which is not investigated or discussed (Reviewer 1, 3). The cost and memory requirements for larger embedding dimensions are not analyzed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related work**: The paper should cite Riemannian optimization methods and clarify differences, and also discuss existing works on hyperbolic prototype learning in more depth (Reviewer 4).
