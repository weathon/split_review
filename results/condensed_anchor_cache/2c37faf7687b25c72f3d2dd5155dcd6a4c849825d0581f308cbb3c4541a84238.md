## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SURE (Scalable Uncertainty and Reconstruction Estimation), a framework for handling missing modalities in pretrained multimodal models. It reconstructs missing modalities in latent space and estimates uncertainty for both reconstructions and predictions using a Pearson Correlation Coefficient loss and statistical error propagation. The method is architecture-agnostic and validated on sentiment analysis, genre classification, and action recognition. While the idea of incorporating uncertainty estimation is interesting, the motivation, novelty, and evaluation have several issues.

### Strengths
- The theoretical proofs and methodology are solid and valid (Reviewer 1).
- The Error Propagation and Uncertainty Estimation approach is interesting and unique (Reviewer 1).
- The framework provides both reconstruction and output uncertainty estimates, helping assess prediction reliability (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The approach is validated across multiple benchmarks (sentiment analysis, genre classification, action recognition) and compared against previous methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- SURE is designed to be compatible with a wide range of pretrained multimodal models, making it adaptable across diverse architectures and tasks (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The method achieves good results on multiple datasets (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing prior work:** Several multimodal works on modality absence [1,2,3,4,5] are neglected; these represent current SOTA in robust joint representations and reconstructions. The authors should clarify differences and compare with these works (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and clarity:** The paper lacks depth in explaining introduced concepts (e.g., reconstruction module, MMML framework, Figure 3 symbols and flow). A full caption for Figure 3 and clear definitions are needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Limited missing modality scenarios:** The paper considers only a limited set of missing modalities, omitting intramodal and intermodal absence that occur in real-world scenarios. Results for all missing cases should be reported, and different missing ratios should be investigated (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Unclear benefit of uncertainty estimation:** It is not explained how uncertainty estimation improves predictive performance (accuracy). The paper does not detail how uncertainty is integrated into the objective or decision-making to directly improve accuracy beyond providing an estimate (Reviewer 2).
- **Gaussian assumption vs. “distribution-free” claim:** The Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) loss is claimed to be distribution-free, but Equation (4) and the derivation rely on a Gaussian assumption. The authors need to clarify this contradiction and justify how the loss remains effective when the true error distribution is non-Gaussian (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Questionable novelty:** Latent-space reconstruction and Bayesian uncertainty estimation are well-established techniques; the combination appears trivial without clear explanation of how it uniquely enhances adaptation of pretrained models for downstream tasks (Reviewer 3).
- **Inconsistency in baseline results:** The reported F1 score for IMDer on CMU-MOSI with full inputs is 62.0, while the original IMDer paper reports 85.1. This discrepancy raises concerns about experimental setup and result comparability (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited evaluation of uncertainty estimates:** Only correlation between uncertainty and error is reported. Additional metrics (e.g., calibration error) are needed to assess quality. The paper should clarify why high correlation is a good proxy for reliable uncertainty (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure 1 insufficiently motivates the approach:** Figure 1 suggests that pretraining alone might handle incomplete inputs, so it is unclear why SURE is needed. The figure does not demonstrate that missing modalities hurt pretrained model adaptation (Reviewer 3).
- **Pretraining accounts for most performance gain:** In Tables 3 and 4, SURE without pretrained weights performs worse than prior methods, while with pretrained weights it surpasses them. This suggests pretraining is the main source of improvement, not the proposed method (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons to prompt-based methods:** The pipeline (pretrained model, fine-tune reconstruction and head) is similar to prompt-based methods that also perform latent space reconstruction (e.g., references [1,2] in Reviewer 4). These baselines should be compared, and differences in handling reconstruction reliability should be explained (Reviewer 4).
- **Weak justification for PCC loss:** Why use Pearson correlation instead of nonlinear metrics (e.g., Spearman, Kendall)? The relationship between uncertainty and error may not be linear, and this assumption is not justified. Other correlation coefficients should be explored (Reviewer 4).
- **Two-stage training not justified:** The reconstruction module and classifier are trained separately. The authors should explain why joint training is not used and provide empirical support for the chosen approach (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability and computational overhead not addressed:** The number of reconstructors \( r_i \) may scale with the number of modalities, and the added modules could increase computational cost. No quantitative analysis of computational cost or experimental evidence for scalability is provided, despite “Scalable” in the name (Reviewers 2, 3).
