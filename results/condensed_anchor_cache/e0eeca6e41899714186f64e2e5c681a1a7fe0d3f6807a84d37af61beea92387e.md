## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper empirically studies neural collapse (NC) in intermediate layers of deep neural networks, extending analysis to larger models (e.g., ResNet-152, Swin Transformer) and datasets beyond CIFAR-10 (e.g., ImageNet, Mini-ImageNet). It challenges the idea of constant geometric progression of NC across layers, identifying a phase transition (plateau in middle layers, then improvement in last layers). It connects NC to generalization, robustness under noise (input/label corruption), and domain adaptation. Two reviewers (R2, R3) find the direction novel and the connection to OOD robustness actionable; R1 finds the premise well-motivated; R4 considers the motivation insufficiently novel given prior work on intermediate-layer NC. Overall, the paper is seen as offering interesting empirical observations but lacking clarity, rigor, and actionable insights.

### Strengths
- The premise of validating neural collapse on a larger scale (deeper networks, more complex datasets) is well-motivated. [R1]
- Connecting neural collapse properties to generalization, robustness, and domain adaptation is promising and a novel direction. [R1, R3]
- The analysis of intermediate features as a function of layer depth (rather than training steps) is a novel and interesting angle, especially for models larger than those typically studied. [R2, R3]
- Explaining trends of out-of-distribution robustness using neural collapse is actionable. [R2]
- The paper presents a nice overview of existing neural collapse literature. [R2]
- Deriving connections between neural collapse and memorization is a novel contribution. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and scope**: One reviewer (R4) argues that the motivation is not substantially novel, as paper(s) like “Neural Collapse in the Intermediate Hidden Layers of Classification Neural Networks” already studied intermediate layers, and the current findings do not clearly improve understanding. [R4]  
- **Limited evaluation of “complex” settings**: Only larger models are considered (ResNets up to 152, Swin), but dataset complexity is sparse (Swin not evaluated on ImageNet). Deeper investigation comparing CNN and transformer families is missing. [R1]  
- **Unclear relationship between NC₁ and CDNV**: Figure 4 shows NC₁ behaving differently on ImageNet vs. CIFAR, while CDNV patterns are similar. The discrepancy is not adequately explained; reliance on one metric could lead to different conclusions. [R1]  
- **Plateau vs. performance saturation link is vague**: For Swin-T on CIFAR-100, no plateau is observed, yet performance still improves with larger models. A correlation metric between plateau definitions and accuracy could quantify this. The insight is not actionable: to know if a large model saturates, one must first train a large model. [R2]  
- **Intuition missing**: No hypothesis for why only larger models exhibit the plateau phenomenon, or why the correlation between NC plateau and performance saturation exists. [R2]  
- **Unsubstantiated claim about linear probing**: Line 474–475 claims intermediate features with better neural collapse induce better linear probing accuracy, but this is not clearly supported by Figure 3. [R2]  
- **Definition of memorization is non-standard and unjustified**: The definition (Definition 1) is non-trivial; its rationale is not provided, making the claim that memorization occurs mostly in the last layers unconvincing. One reviewer (R3) notes this contradicts findings that memorization is localized across all layers (e.g., arXiv:2307.09542). The memorization ratio should be separately considered for input vs. label corruption. [R1, R2, R3]  
- **Experimental settings unclear**: It is not explicitly stated whether base models are trained from scratch. The use of ImageNet vs. Mini-ImageNet is inconsistent, and exact noise percentages (clean, aggre, randn1, worse) in Figure 2 are missing. [R3]  
- **Presentation and organization issues**:  
  - Sections present many points in rapid succession, feeling like a collection of facts; significance is hard to gauge. [R1]  
  - Background on deep learning is unnecessary for the audience. [R2]  
  - Transformers results are pushed to the appendix despite being a major contribution. [R2]  
  - Figures are far from the text; captions could include key takeaways. [R2]  
  - The term “progressive” is confusing (normally refers to training steps, not depth). [R3]  
  - “Improvement” of NC values is vague (should specify direction). [R3]  
  - Order of presentation: Section 3.3 (explaining NC) should come earlier. [R3]  
  - Only two result figures; more interest in results than reasoning. [R4]  
  - Proofreading needed: typos (“dataset”→“datasets”, “h_{L+1}”→“b_{L+1}”, missing spaces, repeated phrases, incorrect equation reference “P^T≠P^T”). [R3]  
  - Appendix should be split into sections with specific references (not just “see appendix”). [R3]  
- **Applicability questionable**: Using NC for model-depth selection is not a practical problem. [R1]  
- **Definitions non-standard**: Domain adaptation settings should use standard names (label shift, covariate shift, supervised domain adaptation). [R2]  
- **Questions raised that overlap with weaknesses** (criticisms, missing experiments, ablations):  
  - Does the NC rate depend on fraction of layer depth rather than absolute index? [R1]  
  - What is the role of constant \(c\) in Eq. (8)? [R1]  
  - Would scaling width (e.g., Wide ResNets) show a similar pattern? [R1]  
  - How to quantify the correlation between NC plateau and accuracy? [R2]  
  - Why does the 2-step phase transition not hold for Swin-T on CIFAR-100 (Figure 5)? Discussion needed. [R3]  
  - Is there a contradiction between “adding layers after transition yields marginal gains” (second contribution) and “plateau benefits generalization” (third)? [R3]
