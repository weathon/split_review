## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces T2V2, a unified non-autoregressive model that performs both ASR and TTS using a shared Conformer backbone with RoPE operating on discrete speech tokens (from HuBERT). ASR uses CTC loss, TTS uses conditional masked LM, with auxiliary tasks: CTC error correction (Lcorr) and unconditional speech MLM (SMLM) for classifier-free guidance. Alignment for TTS uses Monotonic Alignment Search (MAS) with intermediate CTC outputs. Experiments on LibriHeavy show state-of-the-art TTS and competitive discrete ASR. Scores: 8, 6, 5, 6. One reviewer (score 5) considers the work early-stage and not ICLR-ready; other reviewers are more positive (6,8).

### Strengths
* First unified framework for ASR and TTS operating on discrete speech tokens, allowing parameter sharing and multitask learning.
* Novel auxiliary tasks: CTC error correction to refine raw CTC outputs (potentially addressing CTC independence assumption) and unconditional speech MLM for classifier-free guidance.
* MAS-based alignment using intermediate CTC outputs is innovative, enabling TTS alignment without external alignment tools.
* Comprehensive experiments and ablation studies (Tables 1,6,7,8) justify design choices and demonstrate effectiveness.
* Claims tested: TTS improvements demonstrated, ASR achieves competitive discrete performance.

### Weaknesses
* **Clarity and presentation issues.** Figure 1 is too complicated (interlocking arrows, difficult to follow). Mixed terminology (“CTC token”, “text token”, “content token”, “acoustic token”) needs clear definition. Losses for each head in Figure 1, especially conditional TTS vs unconditional MLM, should be differentiated (e.g., different colors). Hyperparameter tuning experiments should be in appendix.
* **Missing inference efficiency comparison.** The motivation for non-autoregressive models is superior decoding speed, but no latency/RTF comparison with autoregressive counterparts is provided. The iterative generation during inference (e.g., 16 iterations in Table 7) makes the model more semi-autoregressive than non-autoregressive; this distinction should be clarified.
* **Insufficient discussion/comparison with other multi-task ASR & TTS models.** No comparison with prior multi-task models (e.g., [1], [2] cited by Reviewer 2). Related work coverage is insufficient.
* **CTC error correction novelty and in-depth analysis lacking.** Only substitution errors in CTC logits are corrected; deletion and insertion errors are not considered (cf. Improved Mask-CTC, ICASSP 2021). It is unclear how this method fundamentally breaks the CTC independence assumption (logits are detached). The ablation shows that Lcorr impact on TTS is low (Tables 1,6). The effect of iterative correction in Table 7 shows minimal CER change across thresholds/iterations; no analysis of why more iterations do not yield significant gains and no discussion of the computational overhead (e.g., time increase for 16 vs. 1 iteration).
* **Dependency on external components for speech synthesis.** Content tokens are converted to acoustic tokens via SoundStorm and then to speech via a codec decoder. The paper does not analyze how the choice of SoundStorm impacts overall TTS performance relative to alternatives, nor the limitations introduced by this dependency.
* **TTS baseline comparisons do not isolate T2V2 contribution.** Compared models (e.g., Hierspeech++, WhisperSpeech) use different overall pipelines; missing comparisons against models that predict only content tokens + SoundStorm (the most similar setting). True contribution of T2V2 architecture is hard to assess.
* **Ablation suggests SMLM can harm both tasks.** In Tables 1 and 6, comparing (SMLM, no CORR) vs (no SMLM, no CORR), SMLM degrades both TTS and ASR. The paper lacks analysis of why SMLM is detrimental in single-pass scenarios.
* **Questions on multitask loss optimization and contributions.** How were individual losses weighted in eq. 16? How did each loss contribute to each task? The length prediction loss is not included in the final objective (eqs 16); how is the length prediction head trained? The rational of length prediction by feeding text embedding through the Conformer backbone is unclear—this may confuse the model.
* **Dataset training stages unclear.** Confusion between 509-hour subset (lines 311-315) and the 50K-hour subset for discrete ASR (lines 484-485). Is the larger model initialized from the first stage or trained separately?
* **ASR performance not competitive with non-discrete ASR.** WER on test-clean is almost twice that of non-discrete ASR. The advantage of discrete ASR is not stated. Motivation for using CER for English ASR is unclear.
* **Need for additional analysis and ablations.**  
  - For TTS, missing TTS-only training to evaluate whether joint ASR/TTS modeling is beneficial (ablation shows low impact of MLM and CORR).  
  - MAS explanation is insufficient: how is MAS applied? How does it compare to external alignment? How does it compare to CTC alignment (cf. CASS-NAT, ICASSP 2021)?  
  - Missing evaluation of TTS attributes like pitch, volume, speed.  
  - Missing performance of the large model on TTS.  
  - Table 8 should include baseline: discrete Zipformer-CTC.
* **One reviewer (score 5) finds the work still in an early stage and not yet suitable for ICLR publication**, citing lack of competitive ASR, insufficient clarity, and missing comparisons. Another reviewer (score 8) finds the problem well-motivated and claims tested. This disagreement highlights differing opinions on the contribution depth and readiness.
