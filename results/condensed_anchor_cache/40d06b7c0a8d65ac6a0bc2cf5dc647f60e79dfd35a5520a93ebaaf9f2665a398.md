## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MissTSM, an imputation-free method for time series with missing values, using Time-Feature Independent (TFI) embedding (each time-step and feature pair as a distinct token) and a Missing Feature-Aware Attention (MFAA) layer. It is evaluated on forecasting and classification tasks under MCAR and periodic masking, plus one real-world dataset. All reviewers acknowledge clear presentation and the inclusion of multiple imputation baselines. However, there is strong and near-unanimous concern about limited novelty (TFI + missingness bias is not new), marginal and inconsistent performance gains (often worse than simpler methods), insufficient baseline comparisons (missing GRU-D, ODE/SDE methods, robust modeling approaches such as [m1], SOTA imputation [m2], and simple imputers like mean/mode/LOCF), narrow experimental setup (only 3 datasets, MCAR and periodic missing only, 60-70% missing range, no MAR/MNAR), weak ablation, and incomplete reporting of hyperparameters and hardware. One reviewer (score 8) found the intuition clear and the experiments comprehensive, but still agreed that improvements are not significant and the contribution is limited. The other three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5) are substantially more critical.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, clearly presented with intuitive figures, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Source code is provided for reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments cover both time series forecasting and classification tasks, and include an ablation study (Reviewers 3, 4).
- A diversity of imputation baselines are considered in the experiments (Reviewer 3).
- The core idea—circumventing the imputation step—is conceptually clear (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited experimental scope and missing details**  
   - Only three datasets per task; the selection is not diverse (ETTh2 and ETTm2 come from the same ETT family; datasets from different application domains would be more reasonable) (Reviewer 1).  
   - Only MCAR and periodic masking are tested; MAR and MNAR are widely used in the missing data literature and should be included (Reviewer 3).  
   - The 60–70% missing ranges tested are unusually high; 10–40% is more common and would provide more practical insight (Reviewer 4).  
   - Hardware information for the computational cost comparison is omitted, and hyperparameter selection methods for MissTSM and all baselines are not disclosed (Reviewer 1).  
   - Baseline models are missing from the code repository, making reproducibility and fairness verification difficult (Reviewer 1).

2. **Incomplete and potentially unfair baseline comparisons**  
   - Several relevant imputation-free methods are omitted: GRU-D, ODE/SDE-based approaches for irregular time series, and robust modeling methods (e.g., [m1]) that handle missing values without a separate imputation stage (Reviewers 2, 3).  
   - State-of-the-art imputation methods (e.g., [m2]) are not compared (Reviewer 3).  
   - Simple imputation baselines such as mean/mode imputation and last observation carried forward (LOCF) are missing, which would provide a lower bound for expected performance (Reviewer 2).  
   - It is unclear whether baselines were reproduced using official code or a unified library (e.g., Time-Series-Library, PyPOTS); inconsistent data processing could bias results (Reviewer 1).

3. **Limited novelty and insufficient technical contribution**  
   - Treating each time-feature combination as a separate embedding and using the missingness indicator as a bias term in attention is not technically novel (Reviewer 2).  
   - The claim that all existing works are two-stage is inaccurate; [m1] directly handles missing values and anomalies with lower importance, not via imputation (Reviewer 3).  
   - Performance gains over baselines are marginal and often dataset-dependent; MissTSM sometimes performs worse than benchmarks (Reviewers 2, 4). Improvements are neither statistically nor practically significant.  
   - The paper is primarily an empirical study without theoretical analysis or formal guarantees (Reviewer 4).

4. **Insufficient ablation and analysis**  
   - The ablation study does not isolate the contributions of TFI and MFAA; results for MissTSM without TFI but with MFAA are not provided, leaving the necessity of TFI unclear (Reviewer 4).  
   - The method only addresses missing values, ignoring other common data quality problems (anomalies, staleness, errors), which limits its practical applicability (Reviewer 3).

5. **Technical and presentation issues**  
   - The introduction reads more like a related work section and includes an inaccurate claim about the two-stage paradigm, weakening the motivation (Reviewer 3).  
   - Several technical details need clarification:  
     * The justification for the 2D positional encodings (Equations 2 and 3) is not explained, including why they provide an effective representation (Reviewer 4).  
     * Why do the queries not depend on the time point, while keys and values do? (Reviewer 2)  
     * What is the benefit of using missingness indicators as bias terms rather than explicitly removing masked tokens from self-attention computation? (Reviewer 2)  
   - The paper does not adequately explain how MissTSM circumvents the two claimed drawbacks of imputation (error propagation, trade-off between efficacy and complexity): errors in TFI could still cascade into MFAA, and the method itself has a complexity-efficiency trade-off (Reviewer 4).
