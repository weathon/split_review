## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers describe CypST as a deep learning model that combines ESM-2 (protein language model) with masked graph attention networks (GATs) and an XGBoost classifier to predict whether a compound is a substrate of five major human CYP450 isoforms. The model is trained on 51,753 enzyme-substrate and 27,857 enzyme-non-substrate pairs, achieving 0.861 accuracy and 0.909 AUROC. The architecture fuses protein and molecular graph embeddings. While the problem is biologically important, reviewers strongly disagree on the paper’s quality and novelty: one reviewer (R2, score 3) finds the combination of two powerful encoders rational and appreciates the five-fold cross-validation and clarity of Figure 1; two reviewers (R1 and R3, score 1) and one reviewer (R4, score 3) judge the method as a straightforward, non-novel application of existing techniques with significant presentation and reproducibility issues.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a biologically important problem (CYP450 substrate prediction) relevant to drug metabolism (R1,R2,R3,R4).
- The intuition of combining sequence-based (ESM-2) and graph-based (GAT) representations for this task is rational (R1,R2).
- Five-fold cross-validation was performed to select hyperparameters (R2).
- Different encoder backbones were tested, and the best-performing one was selected (R2).
- Figure 1 depicts the model architecture in a clear way that helps understanding (R2).
- The paper describes limitations of the current approach (R2).
- Experimental outcomes show satisfactory performance (R4).
- The paper demonstrates that encoding both ligand and enzyme structure improves substrate prediction (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation are poor.** The paper appears unfinished, lacks logical flow, and contains many grammar mistakes (R1,R2,R3,R4). Figures have very small fonts, low resolution, and excessive blank space (R1,R2,R4). Figure 4 should be a bar plot instead of a line plot (R2). The Related Work section is placed within the Introduction (R4). There are formula errors (e.g., “{p” on page 4, “*mathbf*” on page 5) and an atom notation error: “for each atom $(i,j)\in \nu$” should be $i \in \nu$ (R2). Equations 5 and 6 are unclear: it is not specified whether Equation 6 describes a second layer or a different attention head, and Equation 5 should use $\mathbf{h}_j^{(0)}$ and $\mathbf{h}_j^{(1)}$ (R2). The scaffold diversity curve in Figure 3 is confusing; its interpretation depends on compound ordering, and the same message could be conveyed by reporting counts of unique molecules and scaffolds (R2).
- **Method lacks novelty.** The approach is a straightforward combination of two existing encoders (ESM-2 and GAT) without any novel modifications to the GAT itself (R1,R2,R3,R4). The enzyme-substrate prediction task is essentially similar to Drug-Target Interaction (DTI) prediction, for which combining ESM and GAT is already known (R4). Substantial prior work exists (e.g., ESP model by Kroll et al., leaderboard on Therapeutics Data Commons) and is not adequately discussed (R3).
- **Experiments are insufficient.** Table 1 only compares older backbone models, not recent SOTA methods (R1). The reported results appear to come from a single run; multiple independent runs with confidence intervals are needed to assess statistical significance (R2). Simple baselines like XGBoost using ECFPs are missing from Table 1 (R2). The proposed method is outperformed by DeepP450 on several isoforms (R1,R3), and the authors’ claim that DeepP450 might generalize worse because it was trained on a smaller dataset is unsupported by evidence (R3). A comparison with other general enzyme-substrate models (e.g., ESP) on this task is missing (R3). Performance should be reported with error bars in Table 1 and Figure 4 (R2).
- **Method description is incomplete/irreproducible.** The code is not published (R2). How negative samples were selected (“three structurally similar molecules … that are not true substrates”) lacks details on similarity computation and selection criteria (R2). The training procedure is unclear: XGBoost is used as the final classifier, but the loss function and how gradients propagate to the GAT layers are not explained (R2). Several method description errors make the text difficult to read and hinder reproducibility (R2).
- **Motivation is weak.** It is unclear what specific issue in enzyme-substrate prediction CypST is intended to solve that existing methods do not address (R4).
- **Reviewer disagreement preserved:** While Reviewer 2 (score 3) found the architecture clear and the approach rational, Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 1) and Reviewer 4 (score 3) strongly critiqued the lack of novelty, poor presentation, and insufficient experimental rigor.
