## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes State Chrono Representation (SCR), which augments bisimulation-based metric learning (specifically MICo) with long-term temporal information by using chrono embeddings of state pairs and a temporal measurement of cumulative rewards. The method is tested on DeepMind Control and Meta-World, aiming to improve generalization and sample efficiency. Reviewers note a clear motivation and some performance gains over DrQ and SimSR baselines, but raise substantial concerns about theoretical correctness, missing implementation details, limited evaluation diversity, and mixed experimental evidence.

### Strengths
- The problem of capturing long-term behavior in state representations is well motivated and interesting. (R1, R2)
- The idea of integrating temporal information into metric learning is straightforward and intuitive. (R2)
- The modified MICo distance (diffuse metric) may simplify implementation and improve numerical stability. (R3)
- Ablation studies are provided to isolate the contributions of different components. (R2, R3)
- SCR shows better sample efficiency and generalization than some prior methods (SimSR, DrQ) on a few tasks in DeepMind Control, including distractor settings, despite no explicit mechanism to handle distractions. (R2, R3, R4)
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow for most reviewers, with sound motivation behind each introduced component. (R3, R4) *However, Reviewer 1 found the writing overly grandiose and some theorems unclear.*
- The paper proposes a variety of losses for temporal representation learning. (R1)
- Experimental evidence supports performance improvements on different variations of the DeepMind Control suite. (R4)

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical and Conceptual Issues**
- The claim that “behavioral metrics only capture immediate, one-step information” is incorrect; bisimulation considers long-term behavioral differences. Poor performance in sparse rewards is due to limited supervision, not a lack of long-term information. (R4)
- The statement that SimSR’s cosine distance does not satisfy zero self-distance is false; cosine distance is zero at self. (R4)
- The term “one-step update mechanism” should be “temporal-difference update mechanism.” (R4)
- Several statements labeled as “Theorems” are actually definitions and should be renamed (e.g., Theorem 1 is a definition; A is never defined). (R1)
- The proof of Theorem 5 appears invalid: the algorithm treats states as not fixed, unlike Theorem 3. Using an auxiliary state space would be dimensionality-increasing, so validity is uncertain. (R4)
- Equation 9 is questionable: the right-hand-side policy for $d$ is unclear (current policy vs. optimal policy). If current, the equation may never update; if optimal, it is unknown and Eq. 10 would not hold. (R4)

**Writing and Presentation**
- Writing is overly grandiose (e.g., “beacon in this realm”). (R1)
- Theorems are not clearly written (e.g., Theorem 1’s notation undefined). (R1)
- Terminology inconsistency: DBC is mentioned between definitions 3.1 and 3.2 without prior introduction. (R3)
- Typo after Eq. 4: “TThe encoder…” (R3)
- Legend in Figure 5: “CR” should be “SCR”. (R3, R4)
- Figure 6 should be Table 3. (R4)

**Missing Implementation Details**
- The Łukaszyk-Karmowski distance lacks a description of how probability density functions $f(x)$ and $g(y)$ are estimated in practice. (R3)
- The Interval Quasimetric Embedding (IQE) description is high-level; it is not specified how vector segments are chosen or how the ‘max’ and ‘mean’ operations are applied across component distances. (R3)
- For chrono embeddings $\psi(x_i, x_j)$, the method does not specify how the number of steps between states $i$ and $j$ is determined, which is a significant hyper-parameter. (R3)
- The role of the encoder $\phi$ EMA parameter is unclear: is it used for target calculations in $L_\phi$ and $L_\psi$? Gradient stopping vs. target encoder for these losses is not stated. (R3)
- “Future state” sampling is undefined (e.g., uniform random or something else?). (R4)
- It is unclear whether “500K steps” refers to environment steps or gradient steps. (R4)
- Number of random seeds used in Table 2 and Figure 6 is not reported. (R4)
- Experimental setup for distractor settings is vague: are training and evaluation environments the same or different? (R4)

**Missing Ablations and Analyses**
- No analysis of the impact of the number of steps between $i$ and $j$ in chrono embeddings. (R3)
- No ablation isolating the effect of the introduced diffused metric on MICo. (R3)
- Not shown how performance is with only $\mathcal{L}_\phi(\phi)$. (R4)
- Lack of analysis about long-term behavior captured by the representations; experiments only view sample efficiency and generalization. (R2)
- Insufficient analysis explaining why performance varies substantially across tasks. (R2)
- Losses appear ad-hoc with insufficient justification for chosen forms. (R1)

**Limited Evaluation**
- Evaluation is restricted to variations of Mujoco/DeepMind Control and Meta-World; results on more diverse environments (e.g., procgen, RL-Bench, Minecraft, Antmaze) are missing. (R1, R3)
- In stochastic environments (e.g., Mario with different levels, or partial observability), the chronological metric might incorrectly treat similar states as highly dissimilar, but no such experiments are conducted. (R3)
- Many confidence intervals in the results overlap, making the claimed improvements unconvincing. (R1)
- Performance comparisons are inconsistent across tasks (some better, some worse). (R2)

**Missing Differentiation from Related Work**
- The difference from prior representation learning works (beyond the references) needs elaboration. (R1)
