## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RecurFormer, which identifies “recency-aware” attention heads (where attention concentrates on nearby tokens) in pre-trained LLMs and replaces them with Mamba-based linear RNN blocks, reducing cache size without token eviction. The remaining heads retain long-range dependencies, and the model is up-trained with continual training. Experiments on Qwen2 and LLaMA2 variants report reduced cache and reasonable perplexity, but evaluations are limited to HashHop and MQAR tasks. One reviewer (score 5) considered the motivation solid and the approach intriguing and novel, while three reviewers (score 3) found the experimental validation insufficient, baselines incomplete, and novelty limited.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to understand and clearly presented (R1, R3).
- The idea of selectively replacing recency-aware attention heads with linear RNNs (Mamba) instead of all heads is worth exploring and has some innovation (R1, R3).
- The method provides a practical, non-eviction cache reduction solution that reduces inference cache size while aiming to maintain generation quality (R2, R4).
- The methodology is well-documented, including the recency ratio calculation and the selection/replacement process (R2).
- The motivation is solid: token-eviction-based cache reduction can be problematic, and linearizing recency-aware heads is an intriguing and novel non-eviction approach (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Experimental validation is very limited; evaluations are conducted only on HashHop and MQAR (two synthetic datasets). Standard language benchmarks (HellaSwag, MMLU, C-Eval, lm-evaluation-harness) are missing, so the extent of NLU performance degradation cannot be assessed (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Long-context reasoning evaluations are absent. Standard datasets such as LongBench, LongEval, InfiniteBench, SCROLLS (e.g., Qasper, NarrativeQA), and passkey retrieval should be included (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Missing comparisons with important baselines:
  - Multi-Query Attention (MQA) (R2).
  - Sliding window attention (e.g., Razor Attention, arXiv:2407.15891) (R1, R3).
  - Linear attention focusing on long-term tokens (e.g., LESS, arXiv:2402.09398) (R3).
  - Within-layer hybrid (Mamba+attention in same layer) vs. between-layer hybrid (e.g., Jamba, Lieber et al., 2024; Samba, Ren et al., 2024) (R1, R2).
- The comparison with PyramidInfer is considered unfair because PyramidInfer is a training-free method; for a fair comparison it should be integrated during training (R3).
- Perplexity gap is noticeable: in Table 3 for the 0.5B parameter model with β=0.5, PPL increased by more than 2 compared to the original structure (R2).
- The overall novelty is limited: the observation that different heads focus on different ranges and the conversion approach are not highly innovative (R2, and implied by R3’s request for baseline comparisons).
- Missing references to prior work that takes pre-trained Transformers and up-trains portions into RNNGs/linear models, including:
  - Kasai et al., "Finetuning pretrained transformers into rnns," EMNLP 2021.
  - Zhang et al., "The hedgehog & the porcupine: Expressive linear attentions with softmax mimicry," ICLR 2024.
  - Mercat et al., "Linearizing Large Language Models," COLM 2024 (R4).
- Several reviewer questions point to missing comparisons and ablations: comparison with sliding window attention under similar compute (R1), comparison with MQA, eviction-based methods, and hybrid approaches (R2), evaluation on academic/long-sequence benchmarks (R2), parameter count change after replacing heads with Mamba (R2), and details on continual training tokens (e.g., how many tokens, which pre-training data like RedPajama) (R4).
