## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies errors in the prior work of Dubey & Pentland (2020) on differentially private federated linear contextual bandits (LCB)—specifically a failure of claimed privacy protection, incorrect regret bound due to noise miscalculation, and ungrounded communication cost. It proposes the Private-FedLinUCB algorithmic framework, incorporating LinUCB exploration, a distributed binary-tree-based mechanism, privacy amplification, and fixed batch updates. The paper provides theoretical guarantees for silo-level local differential privacy (LDP) and shuffle differential privacy (SDP). Under SDP, via two schemes (privacy amplification via shuffling and integration of a vector-sum shuffle protocol into the tree-based mechanism), the algorithm approaches the regret of a centralized server. The analysis includes regret bounds, communication cost, and numeric evaluations on synthetic and real-life data.

### Strengths

- **Identification of a significant gap.** The paper succinctly identifies and corrects errors in the highly cited work of Dubey & Pentland (2020), which is valuable for setting the record straight. All reviewers acknowledge this as a key contribution. (Reviewer 1: “important to highlight the potential risk of privacy leaking from adaptive communication”; Reviewer 3: “important to set the record straight if errors are not well known”; Reviewer 4: “meticulous analysis, identification of mistake … compellingly highlights the necessity of a more robust silo-level LDP setting.”)
- **Thorough combination of DP with federated contextual bandits.** The two approaches for achieving shuffle DP and the use of privacy amplification are well-executed and may be of independent interest. (Reviewer 1: “nicely combines DP … may be of interest for future studies”; Reviewer 3: “thorough treatment, including shuffle DP setting”; Reviewer 4: “comprehensive narrative encompassing regret and communication guarantees under varying privacy constraints … novel extension of the current amplification analysis.”)
- **Clear and detailed comparison with related work.** The paper provides a thorough discussion of related results and a detailed comparison of theoretical guarantees. (Reviewer 2: “detailed and clear”; Reviewer 4: “thorough and detailed comparison.”)
- **Intuitive algorithm design based on well-studied baselines.** The algorithms build on LinUCB and tree-based mechanisms in a reasonable way, with theoretical results that align with design. (Reviewer 2: “algorithm design appears reasonable”; Reviewer 3: “intuitive algorithms that build on well studied baselines.”)
- **Overall presentation quality.** Writing is generally good, and the study is considered comprehensive, with sound theoretical results by most reviewers. (Reviewer 1: “satisfactory, study is thorough and complete”; Reviewer 3: “good flow and writing”; Reviewer 4: “presentation commendable.”)

*Note on reviewer disagreements:* Two reviewers (R2, R3) gave scores of 8 and expressed no substantial weaknesses (R2: “no obvious weakness”; R3: only minor presentation issues). Two reviewers (R1, R4) gave scores of 6 and were more critical, raising concerns about novelty, presentation clarity, and missing justifications. The summary above reflects the overall positive valuation of the error identification and thoroughness, while the weaknesses below capture the critical points.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and inspiration of the problem are limited.** The core components (LinUCB exploration, tree-based mechanism, amplification) are well-established; the solutions largely draw on existing results without introducing fundamentally new insights or modifications. Silo-level LDP, while rigorous, is considered relatively straightforward to formulate. The privacy amplification techniques are standard applications, not novel derivations. (Reviewer 4; contribution rated “fair” by Reviewer 1.)
- **Lack of justification for some algorithmic choices and analysis gaps.**
  - *Transition from LDP to SDP:* The discussion is unsmooth; the paper does not explain why the \(M^{1/4}\) gap from centralized performance arises in LDP, why it is hard to remove, or whether it is inherent. A more detailed explanation of the difficulty and a discussion of potential lower bounds or fundamental challenges would be beneficial. The practical implications of using SDP (which is weaker than LDP) in this context should be clarified, e.g., via a concrete scenario. (Reviewer 1)
  - *DP mechanism choice:* The paper does not clarify why the chosen mechanisms (Gaussian noise, specific aggregation protocols) are necessary or if alternatives could be used. A discussion of trade-offs between different DP mechanisms would strengthen the work. (Reviewer 1)
  - *Fixed batch size:* The fixed batch size selection lacks justification compared to an adaptively growing batch size (as used in many low-switching bandit studies). The potential benefits of exponential growth for regret or privacy-utility trade-offs should be considered. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing discussion of related work.** A recent work [R1] studying a different DP notion in federated contextual bandits should be included and discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Generalization to nonlinear models.** The algorithm design relies heavily on the binary-tree-based mechanism, which appears limited to linear models. The paper does not discuss whether the algorithm or analysis can be generalized to nonlinear models such as Generalized Linear Models (GLM). (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation and clarity issues.**
  - *Summary table:* The results across different privacy settings (regret bounds, communication cost, algorithms) would be much clearer if tabulated. (Reviewer 4)
  - *Structural choices:* The concluding remarks are placed under simulation results; Section 3 is described as “prose heavy” by Reviewer 3; there is a spacing issue (likely from a `\vspace` command) in the tree-based mechanism section. (Reviewers 3 and 4)
  - *Related work placement:* For a 9-page paper, the related work section should be in the main body rather than an appendix. (Reviewer 3)
  - *Abstract inclusion:* Reviewer 3 notes that some information regarding the errors in Dubey & Pentland (2020) could be included in the abstract to motivate the paper. (Not a major criticism, but a suggestion.)
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms.** (Listed above: questions about the \(M^{1/4}\) gap, DP mechanism choice, batch size, and GLM generalization are treated as missing comparisons/ablation requests and are included under the respective weaknesses.)
