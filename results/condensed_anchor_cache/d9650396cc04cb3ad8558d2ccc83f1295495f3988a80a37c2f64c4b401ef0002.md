## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RED (Robust Environmental Design), an attacker-agnostic method that redesigns road signs by adding class-specific background patterns and uses a majority-voting scheme over masked inputs to improve robustness against patch-based adversarial attacks. Experiments in digital and physical settings compare favorably to existing defenses.

### Strengths
- The approach offers a novel perspective on adversarial robustness by modifying the appearance of objects rather than only the classifier.
- The method is attack-agnostic, making no assumptions about the specific patch attack.
- Physical experiments are conducted beyond digital simulations.
- The attack vector (patch attacks on road signs) is realistic and easy to understand.
- The method is simple and easy to follow.
- Results appear promising.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution**:
  - Training a DNN on patterns per class is highly similar to existing work and does not advance understanding of machine learning security (R1).
  - The masking-plus-majority-voting classifier design is not novel and heavily based on previous defenses (e.g., PatchCleanser) (R2, R3, R4).
  - The classifier was also changed; ablations are missing to measure how much robustness comes from sign redesign vs. classifier redesign. Table 1 suggests most improvement comes from the classifier alone (R3).
- **Missing related work**:
  - Previous work on modifying inputs for adversarial robustness (e.g., Wang et al. "Defensive Patches" CVPR 2022; Frosio and Kautz "Adversarial Augmentation" CVPR 2023) is not acknowledged or compared (R3).
  - A closely related recent work (Shua & Sharif 2024 "Adversarial Robustness Through Artifact Design") should be discussed (R4).
- **Insufficient evaluation against strong threats**:
  - Only PGD attacks are considered; other important attacks like CW are not evaluated (R1).
  - The threat model assumes single contiguous patches (rectangular, triangular, circular). Stronger attacks (e.g., adaptive attacks aware of RED, thin patch grids, splitting patches into multiple pieces, manipulating background pattern order, Lp-norm perturbations) are not tested (R3, R4).
  - Baseline defenses were not tested in the physical experiments (R3).
- **Limited practicality and real-world applicability**:
  - Replacing all traffic signs in the wild would be extremely expensive (R3, R4).
  - The generated patterns make signs very hard to read for humans, even in paper examples; human readability (including for color-blind people) is not addressed (R3, R4). This relegates the work to a toy scenario unless only automated vehicles are assumed (R4).
  - Expected robustness gains over PatchZero (which requires no sign changes) are sometimes only 4–10% (Table 3) (R3).
  - Domain transferability of patterns is not explored (R2).
- **Methodological details missing**:
  - How attacks were adapted for RED is not described (R3).
  - How backgrounds are adjusted on real-world images (where signs include background environment) is not explained; only synthetic examples are shown (R3, R4).
  - Model architecture, training details, hyperparameters (e.g., grid and mask sizes) are not reported, hindering reproducibility (R3, R4).
  - The appendix describing physical experiment setup appears missing (R3).
  - No explanation why masks are not optimized or why not train a separate model per masking function (R3).
- **Unclear inference workflow**:
  - For an unknown-category image, how does the system automatically select which class-specific pattern to apply? This is not described (R2).
  - Figure 4 may not demonstrate validity: if the pattern was manually chosen as a "stop" sign pattern, the seven sub-images predicting "stop" might just capture domain differences. An experiment using patterns directly as input could reveal if a strong association is learned (R2).
- **Lack of threat model specification**: Assumptions underlying attack and defense are not clearly stated (R1).
- **Imprecise presentation**: Citation titles often mismatched with authors (R3). Writing needs proofreading for typos and grammar (R1).
- **Generality unclear**: Whether RED can transfer to other application domains is not shown (R3).
