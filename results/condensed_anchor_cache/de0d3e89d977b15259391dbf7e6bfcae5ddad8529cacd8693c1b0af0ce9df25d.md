- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 8, 8, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
SiReRAG proposes a RAG indexing method that explicitly incorporates both semantic similarity (via a similarity tree built on recursive summarization, following RAPTOR) and entity relatedness (via a relatedness tree built by extracting propositions and entities, grouping by shared entities, and recursively summarizing). The two trees are flattened into a unified retrieval pool. Experiments on MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA show consistent F1 improvements over baselines such as RAPTOR, HippoRAG, and GraphRAG. Reviewers generally find the idea well-motivated (Table 1 coverage study) and the ablation experiments thorough. However, one reviewer (score 5) is notably more critical, citing ad‑hoc algorithmic choices, overspecialization to entity‑centric multi‑hop QA, and insufficient generality. Other points of concern include lack of novelty (incremental over RAPTOR), missing baselines and recent datasets, unclear merging of the two trees, inadequate handling of structured data (2Wiki), high computational cost, and missing details/provoked prompts.

### Strengths
+ **Clear motivation and preliminary evidence.** The coverage study in Table 1 provides hard evidence that similarity only or relatedness only each miss different correct connections, motivating the need for both. (Reviewers 2, 3)
+ **Consistent improvements on three multi‑hop QA datasets.** SiReRAG outperforms RAPTOR, HippoRAG, and GraphRAG in F1/EM across MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA (Tables 4, 6). The improvement is significant (average 1.9% F1; up to 7.8% with reranking). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
+ **Thorough ablation analysis.** The paper examines alternative design choices (e.g., Sections 4.1 and 4.3) and shows that the relatedness tree is necessary for the gains (Table 5). (Reviewers 1, 3)
+ **Original combination of two existing indexing philosophies.** Merging similarity‑based (RAPTOR) and entity‑relatedness‑based (proposition‑grouped) indexing into one solution is considered original and intuitive. (Reviewer 2)
+ **Acknowledges alternative approaches and provides negative results.** The paper tries and rejects several variants, strengthening the conclusions. (Reviewer 3)
+ **Includes inference time analysis.** The experiment shows that inference time grows with retrieval pool size. (Reviewer 3)
+ **Generally well‑organized and easy to follow** (with some exceptions noted below). (Reviewers 2, 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty and ad‑hoc contributions.** The similarity tree is directly from RAPTOR; the core novelty is the relatedness tree. Several reviewers question whether this is enough for a top‑tier venue—one calls the paper “incremental over RAPTOR” (Reviewer 1), while another finds the relatedness‑indexing algorithm “ad‑hoc” with many heuristic decisions (how to filter propositions, resolve entity references) and over‑specialized to entity‑entity association patterns in multi‑hop QA (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 considers it an original combination but still an incremental addition.
- **Lack of details on merging the two trees.** It is unclear how nodes from the similarity tree and the relatedness tree are combined into the unified retrieval pool—whether retrieval scores are weighted, reranked, or simply unioned. Figure 2 merely shows a “+” without technical specification. This lack of precision makes the method hard to reproduce. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing important baselines.** Several baselines are omitted, which weakens the empirical comparison (Reviewer 1, 4):
  - Closed‑book (LLM without retrieval).
  - Iterative retrieval methods such as Self‑Ask and DSPy.
  - Recent state‑of‑the‑art multi‑hop reasoning systems like Open‑RAG.
  - Agent‑based or iterative RAG‑based multi‑hop QA systems (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing recent/leakage‑safe datasets.** All three evaluated datasets are pre‑2022, raising concerns about LLM pretraining leakage. The paper should also evaluate on more recent multi‑hop QA datasets (e.g., FanOutQA, FreshQA). (Reviewer 1)
- **Poor performance on structured datasets (2WikiMultiHopQA).** SiReRAG performs worse than HippoRAG on 2Wiki, indicating that its relatedness encoding (treating propositions as flat text) does not capture the Subject‑Predicate‑Object structure of triples. A reviewer hypothesizes that the RAPTOR summarization pipeline destroys the triple structure and suggests encoding SPO features separately. (Reviewer 2)
- **Computational cost is not adequately addressed.** The “time per retrieval pool size” metric is considered contrived because it reflects the denominator (number of candidates) rather than actual query latency. The retrieval pool can be an order of magnitude larger than baselines, which could be prohibitive in practice. The paper does not provide the average number of candidates per query compared to baselines, nor does it quantify the overhead. (Reviewer 2; supported by Reviewer 3’s mention of time increase with pool size)
- **Missing prompts and implementation details.** Several key prompts are not provided (summary prompt, topic extraction prompt, hierarchy prompt), hindering reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient detail on baseline reproduction.** It is unclear whether baseline numbers were re‑run or taken from original papers; the Table 4 numbers are not found in those papers, and the retrieval size of baselines is omitted. (Reviewer 3)
- **No analysis by number of hops or for single‑hop queries.** The paper does not show whether the method benefits short (single‑hop) queries or how performance varies with reasoning hops. (Reviewer 1)
- **Surprising similar performance for GPT‑3.5‑Turbo and GPT‑4o.** The authors should provide insight/analysis, and report results with an open‑source model (e.g., Llama‑3 8B). (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of qualitative examples.** The paper includes only two examples. Adding walkthrough examples for the relatedness tree and “why” SiReRAG beats RAPTOR (which specific component helps) would improve clarity. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Figure 1 motivation example is misleading.** The question “Who is the father of the artist who painted Head I?” actually requires two reasoning hops, not three as stated. (Reviewer 1)
- **No evaluation on other complex reasoning tasks** (e.g., scientific QA, table reasoning) to demonstrate generality beyond entity‑centric multi‑hop QA. (Reviewer 4)
- **No comparison with other relatedness‑based tree constructions** (e.g., entity‑based or entity‑pair‑based trees) as an ablation. (Reviewer 4)
- **The “time per retrieval pool size” (TPRS) metric is questionable**—it may simply reflect document length effects. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing quality needs improvement** in Sections 1 and 3 (Reviewer 1, 4).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 5) is substantially more critical, emphasizing lack of algorithmic rigor, overspecialization, and missing generality. The other three reviewers (final scores 8,8,8) view the work as well‑motivated, solidly incremental, and empirically strong, though they also note several weaknesses listed above. The largest methodological disagreement is on the degree of novelty and the suitability of the relatedness indexing approach for tasks beyond multi‑hop QA.
