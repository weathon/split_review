## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FedPALS, a federated learning aggregation method that addresses label shift by assuming the server knows the label distributions of clients and the target domain. Reviewers generally agree that the problem setting is realistic and interesting. However, the paper received mixed evaluations (scores: 3,5,3,6,3). While one reviewer (score 6) praised the novelty, theoretical soundness, and clear presentation, other reviewers found significant weaknesses including strong assumptions, hyperparameter sensitivity, use of outdated baselines, limited client scalability, and presentation inaccuracies. The lack of comparison with related work (e.g., Agnostic Federated Learning) and insufficient justification for privacy-critical assumptions were widely noted.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem formulation and method**: The paper is the first to tackle the realistic federated learning scenario of generating a global model for an arbitrary target distribution that differs from aggregated client training datasets. FedPALS introduces a novel aggregation strategy that optimizes a convex combination of client models to align with the target label distribution, balancing bias (target distribution match) and variance (effective sample size). This is well-justified and addresses a specific yet important gap.
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides theoretical proofs (Proposition 1, Proposition 2) supporting the algorithm, and the method ensures unbiased updates under SGD.
- **Extensive empirical evaluation**: Experiments on multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, PACS) with varying degrees of label sparsity demonstrate FedPALS’s superiority over standard baselines FedAvg, FedProx, and SCAFFOLD.
- **Applicability to real-world non-i.i.d. settings**: The method is well-positioned for the common challenge of label distribution heterogeneity in federated learning.

### Weaknesses
- **Strong, insufficiently justified assumptions**: Multiple reviewers criticize the core assumptions.
  - The server is assumed to know the target label distribution \(T(Y)\) and all client label distributions. Privacy justification is weak (only one reference cited, L113-115). No realistic examples are given of how to obtain this information without compromising privacy.
  - The target label distribution is known but no target examples are available—this scenario lacks motivating examples.
  - The assumption that client distributions are known to the server also raises privacy concerns.
- **Hyperparameter \(\lambda\) selection is unclear and critical**: The hyperparameter \(\lambda\) balances bias and variance but lacks practical guidance for selection.
  - No concrete method is provided for choosing \(\lambda\) in Figure 3(a)(b) or in general.
  - \(\lambda\) significantly affects performance, and its optimal value likely varies across settings (different data heterogeneity, number of clients, etc.). This limits practical deployment.
  - One reviewer notes that the training procedure based on equation (7) is confusing, and \(\lambda\) selection is not explained.
- **Outdated and unfair baseline comparisons**: 
  - Baselines are limited to FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD (from 2020 or earlier). Recent works (2023-2024) on label shift in FL (e.g., [1,2,3]) are missing, making it unconvincing that FedPALS is state-of-the-art.
  - The method uses prior information about \(T(Y)\) not available to baselines, creating an unfair advantage.
  - The paper does not compare with Agnostic Federated Learning (Mohri et al., ICML 2019), which also addresses unknown target distributions and should be a strong baseline.
- **Limited scalability and experimental setup**:
  - Experiments use very few clients: 3 for PACS, 10 for CIFAR-10/Fashion-MNIST. This does not represent large-scale FL environments.
  - The evaluation with only 10 clients and \(\alpha=0.1\) heterogeneity leaves unclear how performance changes with more clients or extreme label shift (e.g., \(C=1\), each client has one class).
  - The optimization to determine client aggregation weights could become computationally expensive with many clients or labels—this scalability concern is not addressed.
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - One reviewer (score 6) found the writing clear and well-organized, but other reviewers cite multiple inaccuracies:
    - L87-88: Incorrect claim that FL assumes all client distributions identical (federated learning generally allows heterogeneity).
    - L161: Table 1 reference appears many pages later; vector \(\alpha\) and \(\mathcal{S}\) are not defined.
    - L98: The specific loss function is not mentioned.
    - L300: The validation set dataset type is undefined; if identical to target, this would be cheating.
    - L444-445: Confusing use of \(C\) to denote number of clients.
    - L359: "this benchmark" unclear.
  - The proof of Proposition 2 is confusing: the derivation from \(\mu = 2\sum_i \alpha_i^*/n_i\) to \(\mu = 2/\sum_i n_i\) using primal feasibility is not clear.
- **Empirical convergence concerns**:
  - In Figure 3(b), the approaches do not appear to converge after 80 communication rounds as their target F1-scores keep increasing.
- **Limited scope and novelty**:
  - The method focuses exclusively on label shift, ignoring covariate shift that often co-occurs in real FL.
  - Two reviewers question the novelty, noting the method builds on existing aggregation schemes without a fundamentally new approach.
- **Proposition 1 insufficient for practical FL**: Establishing equivalence between aggregated training distribution and target distribution does not address client data heterogeneity when multiple local iterations are performed (only a single local step case is discussed).
- **Missing discussion of limitations**: The paper does not explore how FedPALS performs when label shift assumptions are violated (e.g., covariate shift not induced by label shift, or client label distributions not well-represented in the target).
