## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces the Mineral Fertilizer Dataset (MFD), a segmentation dataset of 1,608 images and 125,648 instances of KCl, NH₄NO₃, DAP, and NPK fertilizer granules. It benchmarks several semantic (FPN, UNet, MANet) and instance (Mask R-CNN, YOLOv8, YOLOv9, Mask2Former) segmentation models. Scores range from 3 to 6 (3, 6, 3, 3); three reviewers rate it low (3) while one is substantially more positive (6). Majority critiques: lack of technical innovation, missing connections to quality‑control metrics, insufficient dataset documentation, vague generalization claims, and poor presentation. The positive reviewer (score 6) values the dataset’s novelty and thorough benchmark but also notes missing comparative analysis and lack of real‑time validation.

### Strengths

1. **Novel dataset filling a gap** – MFD is the first annotated segmentation dataset specifically for mineral fertilizer granules, addressing a real‑world quality‑control need in the fertilizer industry. (All reviewers)  
2. **Real‑world granular variety** – Contains images of four fertilizer types (KCl, NH₄NO₃, DAP, NPK) with different colors in realistic scenes, supporting both semantic and instance segmentation. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
3. **Logical and compelling annotation process** – The granule annotation strategy, including individual grain isolation, is well‑designed and industry‑friendly. (Reviewer 1)  
4. **Comprehensive benchmark evaluations** – Multiple modern architectures are tested: FPN, UNet, MANet for semantic segmentation; Mask R‑CNN, YOLOv8, YOLOv9, Mask2Former for instance segmentation. Results provide guidance for future work. (Reviewers 1, 2)  
5. **Detailed dataset construction** – Image capture, annotation, and preprocessing steps are clearly described; experimental setup, model selection, and evaluation metrics are transparent and reproducible. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of technical novelty** – The paper applies only off‑the‑shelf architectures without any task‑specific modification (e.g., exploiting granule texture, shape, or color). No novel loss function, model design, or technical optimization is proposed, making the work resemble an experimental report. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; reviewer 2 does not raise this point, reflecting the minority positive view)  
2. **Missing link to quality‑control metrics** – The paper claims MFD supports industrial quality control but does not demonstrate how segmentation outputs (IoU, accuracy) translate to practical parameters such as area‑equivalent diameter, circularity, aspect ratio, or size distribution. The derivation of these quality indicators from masks is not explained. (Reviewer 1)  
3. **Insufficient detail on generalization experiments** – Tests on other granular objects (PET pellets, corn, beans, pharmaceutical tablets) lack specifics: which models were used, which training hyperparameters, and what performance metrics were achieved per object. Figures 9–12 do not identify the segmentation models, and challenges like lighting, background, or density variation are not discussed. (Reviewer 1, Q6)  
4. **No comparative analysis with prior datasets** – The paper does not position MFD against existing datasets for granular material analysis or industrial quality control, nor discuss how it differs from related work in terms of uniqueness or advantage. (Reviewers 1, 2)  
5. **Incomplete dataset diversity and documentation** –  
   * Whether the four granule types cover the full range of common fertilizer granules is not addressed; missing types and their impact on applicability are not discussed. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
   * Detailed usage guide, composition of each data element, annotation standards, and a clear attribute breakdown are lacking, hindering dataset adoption. (Reviewer 2)  
6. **Unsubstantiated robustness and real‑time claims** –  
   * Abstract claims robustness to granules of colors not in the dataset, but no experiment verifies this. (Reviewer 1, Q1)  
   * Claims of real‑time application are not backed by inference speed tests or device‑dependent measurements. (Reviewer 2, Q4)  
   * No experimental results using a consistent image resolution across models. (Reviewer 2, Q5)  
7. **Vague or unclear technical explanations** –  
   * The “self‑developed algorithm” and “some OpenCV operations” for annotation are not specified (e.g., how block uniqueness is achieved, what operations are applied). (Reviewer 3, Q2)  
   * The x‑axis of the violin plot (Figure 3) is not labeled for each fertilizer type. (Reviewer 1, Q3)  
   * Figure 4 shows only a few KCl granules annotated without justification. (Reviewer 1, Q4)  
   * Lines 262–268 contain an intended explanation that is unclear. (Reviewer 1, Q5)  
   * Criteria for selecting the specific segmentation models are not justified. (Reviewer 2, Q6)  
8. **Poor writing and organization** – The paper contains unclear expressions, uses “first, second, at last” within single paragraphs, and has severe presentation flaws (reviewer 4 calls writing “very catastrophic”). This raises concerns about AI‑generated content. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
9. **Limited evaluation** – No additional metrics beyond standard ones are provided to more comprehensively assess dataset quality. (Reviewer 3, Q3)  
*Note: The positive reviewer (score 6) agrees with weaknesses 1, 4, 5, and 6, while expressing that overall the dataset construction and benchmarks are valuable.*
