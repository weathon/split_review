## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the trade-off between enhancing factual accuracy and maintaining context-faithfulness in LLMs. The authors evaluate several factuality enhancement methods (contrastive decoding and representation editing) and find that while these methods yield at best inconsistent improvements in factuality, they cause severe declines in context-faithfulness—up to 69.7%. Through analysis of hidden states and logit distributions, they attempt to explain the underlying mechanisms. All reviewers agree the paper brings attention to an important conflict, though one reviewer (R3, confidence 4) notes that the framing may make the finding unsurprising.

### Strengths
- Valuable insight that factuality enhancement can reduce context-faithfulness, raising awareness of this trade-off in the community.
- Comprehensive experimental setup testing a variety of methods (contrastive decoding, representation editing) across multiple models and datasets.
- Rich analysis, including hidden states and logit distributions, which provides some explanatory depth.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, facilitating understanding of the experimental details.

### Weaknesses
- **Contested framing of “factuality enhancement” (R3, confidence 4):** The term is used too broadly. The investigated approaches (representation editing, contrastive decoding) specifically steer models toward parametric knowledge, making the decline in context-faithfulness a natural consequence and not a surprising finding. The paper excludes retrieval-augmented generation, prompting-based factuality methods (e.g., [1-2]), and parameter-modifying methods such as factuality SFT [3] or DPO [4], which do not fit the claim that “current methods for enhancing factuality do not modify the underlying structure or parameters” (lines 188–189). This narrow definition may twist the conclusion to fit a pre‑defined narrative.
- **Algorithm 1 limitation (R1, R2):** The algorithm assumes only one token difference between S_new and S_para. The `None` checks stop at the first differing token, ignoring subsequent tokens. This limits the analysis to single‑token differences and raises concerns about generalizing findings (e.g., in Section 5) to cases with multiple token differences. Additionally, it is unclear how S_new and S_para were obtained and whether they come from the MQUAKE-STUBBORN dataset.
- **Lack of forward-looking recommendations or actionable conclusions (R2, R3):** The paper identifies the trade-off but does not propose practical strategies or research directions to mitigate it. Model internal analysis (hidden states, logits) is presented but lacks crisp, actionable takeaways. The abstract and conclusion vaguely cite “limitations of current approaches” and “underlying causes” without specifying what they are or how to address them. No potential fixes are validated (e.g., combining with inference‑time context‑faithfulness methods [5]).
- **Missing baselines (R3):** Earlier prompting-based factuality enhancement approaches (e.g., [1-2]) are not included. Their inclusion might alter the conclusion about context-faithfulness.
- **Trivial section (R3):** Section 2.1 is considered trivial and could be omitted from the main paper without loss.
- **Typographical errors (R1, R3):** Missing closing parenthesis in Equation 2; Figure 3 legend: green bar should read “Factuality” (not “Faithfulness”); typo “Knowledge Conficts” on line 499; repeated words elsewhere.
