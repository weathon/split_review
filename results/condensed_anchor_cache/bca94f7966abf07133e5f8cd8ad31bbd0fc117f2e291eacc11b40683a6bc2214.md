- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 5, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies the effect of batch size on the convergence speed of SGD for training wide neural networks. It proves a linear scaling and saturation phenomenon, with a "critical batch size" beyond which further increases give negligible improvement. The reviewers are split: two found the paper clear and promising (scores 5), one found it lacking novelty and limited (score 3), and one had major concerns about theoretical foundations, notation, and experimental validation (score 1). Reviewers agree the topic is motivated, but disagree on novelty, rigor, and empirical support.

### Strengths

- The large-batch training problem is practically important and well-motivated, especially in the era of large models.
- The idea of relating second moments of gradient distances to network width and then applying existing convergence analysis is interesting and promising.
- The paper is clear and to the point, and constitutes a first step in understanding the existence of a critical batch size for neural networks that has been observed empirically.
- The theoretical treatment shows how the width \(m\) and conditions on the radius \(r\) relate to \(\beta\) and the assumptions (Theorems 4.2–4.4). Even a critical reviewer (score 1) found these theoretical results interesting.

### Weaknesses

#### Novelty and scope

- The main result (Theorem 1.1) – that the effective noise level reduces with batch size – is considered trivial and provides little novelty.
- The paper’s contribution is limited to Theorem 3.3 (a bound on the stochastic gradient norm for non-linear least squares); almost everything else comes from prior work [1] (Liu et al. 2023) and could be obtained simply by plugging in the computed \(\beta\).
- Wide neural networks are effectively linear models with interpolating solutions close to initialization; thus the result is essentially for linear least squares with small \(O(1/\sqrt{m})\) error terms. This does not support the abstract’s claim of handling “a broader class of nonconvex problems”.
- The introduction is very short and does not clearly state the contributions; the comparison with related work is brief.

#### Theoretical gaps and errors

- **Proofs missing:** Theorems 4.1–4.4 lack proofs in the main body, making it difficult to verify their correctness. The authors could move proofs of Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2, and Theorem 3.3 to an appendix to make space.
- **Derivation unclear:** The step from Theorem 4.1 (linear-form upper bound) to Theorem 4.2 (maximum-form bound) is stated as a “direct application” without derivation. Additionally, the paper writes “Theorems 4.2 and 4.2 directly imply…” – likely a typo for Theorem 4.3.
- **Notation overload:** \(S\) is used both for a batch of indices and for the set of interpolating solutions. Later \(m\) (width) is reused elsewhere confusingly. Many symbols are undefined: \(\lambda_{\min}\), \(c\), \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) in Theorem 1.1; \(\mathbb{E}_S\) in (2.2); \(f\) in (4.1).
- **Critical batchsize definition:** The expressions for \(b^*\) in the linear and non-linear cases are presented without derivation. It is not shown why these expressions follow from the definition, nor is the definition of “critical batchsize” given mathematically. The reference [1] (Shallue et al.) defines it as the batch size beyond which doubling brings less than halving of steps; the paper should clarify if its definition matches.
- **Assumption justification:** The moment bound (Assumption 3.1) is not naturally shown to hold except when inputs \(x_i\) are bounded. The high-probability convergence bound requires existence of a solution in a ball whose radius scales with the failure probability – it is not explained how the width \(m\) is chosen to guarantee this for given failure probabilities.
- **Convergence speed:** No explicit rate of convergence is given. The term “iteration complexity” is used without a precise mathematical definition.
- **Limited problem class:** The paper only considers MSE minimization (Equation (2.1)). It does not address whether the analysis extends to a more general nonconvex objective \(\frac{1}{n}\sum f_i(w)\) with nonconvex differentiable \(f_i\).

#### Empirical experiments

- **Mismatch with theory:** In Figure 2(f) (and potentially elsewhere) the predicted convergence rate from the analytical formula is far from the true empirical rate. No explanation is provided for this discrepancy.
- **No testing results:** Only training loss/error is shown; testing accuracy/loss and its dependence on batch size are absent. The paper does not connect the convergence bound to generalization performance.
- **Wall-clock time missing:** Results are given only as a function of iterations; the practical goal of large-batch training is to reduce total runtime, which is not measured.
- **Hyperparameter choices:** The experiments fix the same step size for all batch sizes. The theoretical step size in Theorem 2.1 depends on \(\beta\), and empirical studies (e.g., Shallue et al. 2018) show that batch size effects are largely explained by metaparameter tuning. The choice to not adjust step size per batch is not justified.
- **Figure 2, column 2:** It is unclear whether the predicted rate is obtained by computing the first terms of (4.3) on the datasets. The plotted range is very small (e.g., \(3\times10^{-4}\) to \(4\times10^{-4}\) on MNIST), suggesting the effect of batch size on convergence speed may be negligible in practice.
- **No evidence that experimental settings satisfy theoretical conditions:** The paper does not show how the parameters \(r\), \(m\), \(\beta\), and \(\eta\) used in Section 5 are set in accordance with Theorems 4.2–4.4. Without this, the experiments do not corroborate the theory.
- **Small-scale networks:** The neural networks tested are small. Experiments on wide ResNets on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet are needed. The paper should also plot the number of iterations to achieve high test/training accuracy versus batch size to reveal the critical batch size directly.
- **No comparison with other optimizers:** The experiments only test SGD with a constant step-size (with \(\eta\) from a grid search). The authors should compare with momentum, Adam, and their variants, and show whether using \(\eta = 1/\beta\) is competitive.
- **NanoGPT experiment:** The plot shows NanoGPT does not converge linearly nor approach zero training error, so it may not fit the paper’s theoretical setting (wide networks with interpolation). Reviewers question its inclusion.

#### Presentation issues

- The paper is hard to understand in its current form, with missing steps and several mistakes (e.g., typo “opt” on page 7, “in each.” in Figure 1 caption, and a missing phrase in Theorem 1.1). One reviewer found it clear, but the mathematical preliminaries are widely considered insufficient.
