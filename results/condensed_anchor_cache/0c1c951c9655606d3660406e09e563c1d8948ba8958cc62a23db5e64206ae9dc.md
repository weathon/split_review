## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a video prediction method that reduces latency by modeling frames as a continuous process in latent space, using the last predicted frame as a starting point instead of Gaussian noise. It evaluates on KTH, BAIR, Human3.6M, and UCF101. Reviewers are split: two rate 5 (positive about motivation, novelty, latent space), two rate 3 (find formulation unconvincing, poorly explained, contributions incremental). All agree the problem is important, but clarity and technical justification are major concerns.

### Strengths
- The work addresses an important and hard problem of efficient video prediction, with well-motivated latency reduction (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Using latent space for generation is a promising direction to reduce complexity and runtime of diffusion models for video prediction (R1, R3, R4).
- The method is different from prior works using GANs or pixel-space diffusion (R2).
- The model is concise and technically sound in principle (R4).
- Detailed experimental results are presented on multiple challenging benchmarks (R1, R2, R3).
- The method outperforms relevant baselines on those benchmarks (R3).
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to follow and understand (R4). [Note: this contrasts with other reviewers’ clarity complaints; see weaknesses.]

### Weaknesses
- The formulation lacks clear justification.
    - Equation (1) is directly written without intuition, reference, or justification of why it is the optimal modeling choice (R2).
    - It subsumes strong assumptions about motion modeling and seems restrictive for large motion, shot changes, occlusions, and pixel-space variations; the paper does not validate the assumption in these challenging scenarios (R2).
    - How the error coefficient in Eq. (1) is obtained is unclear (R1).
    - Eq. (2) term \((z_y - z_x)\Delta t\) is not clearly derived (R1).
    - How \(\tilde{\mu}\) in Eq. (192) is obtained is unclear (R1).
    - In Eq. (3), \(g(t) = -t\log t\) might imply negative variance when \(t>1\) (R2).
    - In Eq. (8), it seems the random variable is \(z_{t-1}\) but the RHS contains a distribution over \(z_t\) (R2).
- Notation is unclear or undefined, making the method hard to follow.
    - Lines 165–166: how does the approach allow for a larger context window? (R1)
    - Line 170: define \(t\) (R1).
    - Line 177: “Once the frames are encoded” – are they encoded all together? If so, how is that handled in the reverse process? (R1)
    - \(z^x\) and \(z^y\) are not defined; how do they differ from \(z^j\) and \(z^{j+1}\)? (R1, R3)
    - Line 187: “with latent \(z_y\)” – should it be \(z^T\)? (R1)
    - Notation \(\mathcal{N}(a;b,c)\) is nonstandard (R3).
    - The concrete problem definition is unclear: the introduction suggests interpolation (“two consecutive frames … interpolate between these endpoints”) while the results section says prediction (R3). The paper should clarify which task is being solved (R3).
    - Section 3.2 is titled “Forward and Reverse Process” but only describes the forward process (R3).
    - Scalar vs bold notation for zero and identity in Normal distributions is inconsistent (R3).
- The evaluation lacks insight and analysis.
    - Very little insight is derived; we do not learn why the method works better than baselines (R3).
    - No ablation or impact of method details is provided; fewer datasets with more analysis would be better (R3).
    - Experiments do not showcase performance on challenging special cases like occlusions and large motions to test the validity of Eq. (1) (R2).
- The contributions are incremental.
    - Using latent visual representation to reduce computation is a recognized practice (already in PVDM [1] and Seer [2]) (R4).
    - Representing videos as multi-dimensional continuous processes is a well-established framework used in CVP [3], the previous state-of-the-art compared in the paper (R4). Given these precedents, the novelty is limited (R4).
- Several implementation details are missing or questionable.
    - How does the method guarantee that the generated latent vector stays on the manifold of in-distribution images to decode a reasonable frame? (R3)
    - Are two context frames enough? Qualitative results suggest they may not capture the rate of motion in some scenes (R3).
    - Figure 1 seems misleading: the noise distribution is based on the adjacent frame, similar to conditional diffusion; the paper should better differentiate from conditional diffusion (R3).
