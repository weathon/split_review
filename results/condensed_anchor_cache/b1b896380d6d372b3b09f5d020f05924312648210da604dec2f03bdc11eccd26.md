## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Octavius, a framework combining LoRA and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with a sparse gating network to mitigate task interference in multimodal LLMs, along with a point cloud encoder (Object-As-Scene) for 3D tasks. Experiments on a diverse set of 2D and 3D tasks show ~20% improvement over baselines like LAMM and LLaVA-LoRA. Reviewers generally agree the idea is novel and the framework is extensible, but strong concerns are raised about the utility of the routing mechanism, missing baselines and ablations, insufficient analysis of expert selection, lack of comparison with state‑of‑the‑art methods, and unclear generalization beyond the evaluated settings.

### Strengths
- The combination of MoE with LoRA for task‑ and modality‑specific sample routing in MLLMs is novel and well‑motivated (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The method is straightforward yet powerful, integrating multiple vision tasks and modalities (2D images and 3D point clouds) with few trainable parameters (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- Thorough experiments on a diverse set of 2D and 3D tasks show substantial gains (~20%) over strong baselines (LAMM, LLaVA‑LoRA); the architecture is modular and extensible (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The paper provides a thorough discussion of related works (Reviewer 4).
- Some reviewers ( 1, 2 ) find the efficacy and versatility convincingly demonstrated, while others are more critical, as noted below.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines and ablations**: A key missing baseline is a dedicated LoRA per task individually (Reviewer 1). The non‑MoE baseline in Tables 2‑3 is not clearly defined (Reviewer 3). Ablation on the impact of top‑k is requested (Reviewers 1, 5). One reviewer ( 3 ) strongly suggests running the ablation in Table 5 with exactly two experts to test whether MoE routing is necessary, because Figure 5 shows all 2D tasks use only two experts, casting fundamental doubt on the utility of the routing mechanism.
- **Analysis of gating/routing mechanism**: Multiple reviewers request a qualitative or quantitative analysis of the gating network: a study of the predictions from the sample‑based gating network to understand how routing works (Reviewer 2); an analysis of how expert selection is distributed between 2D and 3D tasks in the combined dataset (Reviewer 3); and an explanation for why LoRA 2 has zero probability on six tasks in Figure 5, along with more visualizations of expert usage (Reviewer 5). The concern is that the gate may simply act as a task classifier (Reviewer 2).
- **Comparison with state‑of‑the‑art methods**: The paper lacks comparison with strong 2D baselines such as InstructBLIP, Shikra, MiniGPT4, mPLUG‑Owl, LLaVA‑1.5, and Qwen‑VL (Reviewers 4, 5). The 2D results in Table 4 show a significant performance gap to these methods (e.g., on ScienceQA and Flickr30K captioning) (Reviewer 4). Evaluation on recent MLLM chat benchmarks (MMBench, MME, SEED‑Bench) is missing (Reviewer 5).
- **Generalization and scaling**: The paper does not study scaling behavior as more modalities and tasks are added (Reviewer 2). Generalization to unseen tasks (not just novel datasets of the training tasks) is questionable, and robustness to prompt‑template changes during zero‑shot evaluation is unclear (Reviewer 4). Applicability to pre‑training on massive data is not addressed (Reviewer 5).
- **Tug‑of‑war problem demonstration**: The phenomenon is only demonstrated for the combination of 2D detection and VQA; a multi‑modality preliminary (e.g., 2D & 3D captioning) is needed to fully motivate the problem (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear implementation details**: The router input construction (system prompt, modality embedding, question embedding) appears to conflict with Table 5 and needs clarification; it is also unclear how the question embedding is obtained (on‑the‑fly or from an external model) (Reviewer 4). The actual LLM decoder used is never mentioned (Reviewer 3). It is not stated whether LoRA‑MoE is applied in every Transformer block and only to the MLP, as in standard MoE (Reviewer 4). In Figure 5, it is unclear whether a single layer or an average over layers is visualized (Reviewer 4). The loading‑balancing loss is reported as hurting performance, but it is not specified whether it is used thereafter; without it, how collapse to a single expert is prevented is unclear (Reviewer 4).
