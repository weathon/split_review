## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LAMDA, a two-phase multi-fidelity hyperparameter optimization (HPO) framework. In the first phase, LAMDA searches the low-fidelity (LF) landscape (using TPE to model PDFs of promising and inferior solutions and the Overlapping Coefficient OVL to measure convergence) to identify promising regions. In the second phase, these regions are leveraged to guide the high-fidelity (HF) search by modifying the sampling distribution. The framework claims to reduce computational waste by focusing HF evaluations on promising areas and can be integrated with existing HPO methods (PriorBand, BOHB, MUMBO, BO, random search). The paper provides empirical validation on 56 HPO tasks (tabular, surrogate, real) and theoretical analysis for Bayesian optimization and Hyperband.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency and cost reduction**: LAMDA reduces the computational cost and time of HPO by focusing HF evaluations on promising regions identified from LF evaluations, avoiding exhaustive exploration of the whole search space (R1, R3).  
- **Versatility and integration**: The framework can be integrated with various existing HPO techniques, including prior-based, bandit-based, and multi-fidelity BO methods (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Empirical validation across diverse tasks**: Extensive experiments on 56 benchmarks (including 36 explicitly mentioned by R2, covering tabular, surrogate, and real tasks) demonstrate substantial improvements over baselines (R1, R2).  
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides theoretical results for the integrated BO and Hyperband variants (R2).  
- **Data-driven approach**: LAMDA learns promising regions from data rather than relying on expert knowledge, allowing it to adapt to diverse scenarios (R3).  
- **Hyperparameter impact minor**: Although LAMDA introduces additional hyperparameters (e.g., weight \(w\), threshold \(\gamma\)), the paper shows that their impact on performance is minor in the tested settings (R2).  
- **Novelty**: One reviewer (R4) found the idea to be novel, while others (R1, R2) considered the novelty limited. (Contrast preserved: R1 and R2 argue the approach combines existing techniques and that the overlap observation is well-established; R4 states the original idea seems novel.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and overstated claims**:  
  - The core idea of using LF evaluations to focus HF search is not new; existing methods like BOCA already propose this (R2). The observation that promising regions overlap between LF and HF is well-known in multi-fidelity HPO and is not a new key insight (R1, R2).  
  - The paper claims to overcome the need for exhaustive search of the entire search space (Table 1), but LAMDA itself requires an exhaustive search of the LF landscape (R2).  
- **Dependence on overlapping regions**: LAMDA’s performance hinges on the assumption that promising regions in LF and HF landscapes significantly overlap. The paper does not provide a clear analysis of performance degradation as overlap decreases, nor does it offer adaptive strategies to mitigate poor overlap (R1, R3). This limits applicability in scenarios where the LF approximation is a poor proxy (R3).  
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**:  
  - The paper lacks experiments comparing LAMDA with existing multi-fidelity HPO frameworks, such as BOCA or the method by Song et al. (2019) (R2, R4).  
  - The experimental setup does not clearly specify the fidelity levels used (R2; Appendix C provides ranges but not exact levels).  
  - More recent benchmarks (e.g., DYHPO, Quicktune, DPL) are not used; using a decision tree benchmark (rpart) for a multi-fidelity HPO method at a top venue appears out of place (R2).  
- **Unclear integration details**:  
  - How LAMDA is integrated with existing multi-fidelity methods like BOHB is not clearly explained. BOHB itself is a multi-fidelity method that uses LF and HF evaluations – it is unclear why LAMDA’s additional phase would not be redundant or how it modifies BOHB’s internal procedure (R2).  
  - The paper claims LAMDA acts as a "booster," but practical integration may require significant modifications to sampling strategies, acquisition functions, or exploration-exploitation balances, introducing new hyperparameters (R3).  
- **Computational overhead and parameter sensitivity**:  
  - The computational cost of the first-phase LF search and the subsequent HF refinement is not thoroughly analyzed. There is no discussion of settings where the overhead might outweigh rewards (R2, R3).  
  - The method’s sensitivity to parameters such as the overlapping coefficient (OVL) and the weight \(w\) is not fully addressed; while R2 states the impact is minor, R1 and R3 call for more thorough sensitivity analysis and adaptive guidelines (R1, R3).  
- **Experimental details missing or unclear**:  
  - The paper does not specify which BO library is used; a state-of-the-art library (e.g., HEBO) should be employed (R2).  
  - It is unclear how much the lower fidelity is explored in practice and when the method transitions from LF to HF (R2).  
  - The failure modes of the probability distribution in Equation 6 are not explored; e.g., in complex HF landscapes with many local minima, the prior from LF might shift sampling away from good solutions, deteriorating anytime performance (R2).  
  - The overlap coefficient used is not compared to the one proposed in arxiv.org/abs/2212.06751 (R2).  
- **Minor presentation issues and typos**:  
  - Typo on line 103: "quirky" (R2).  
  - Figure 10 is small with overlapping curves, hard to read (R2).  
  - Lines 204–205 are ambiguously phrased, implying OVL could only be 0 or 1 (R2).  
  - Reference to Swersky et al. (2013) may be incorrect; should be Swersky et al. (2014) (Freeze-Thaw Bayesian Optimization) (R2).  
  - Typos: line 289 \(\sigma_f^2(x)\) should be \(\sigma_f^2(\tilde{x})\); line 298 unnecessary parentheses; line 291/296 "equation 7" should be formatted as (7); line 322 in Theorem 2, what is \(2_B^k\)? (R3).  
  - Line 186: definitions of \(\mathcal{S}_{pro/inf}\) should clarify inclusion of \(f_l(x)=y^*\) (R3).  
  - Line 294: definition of \(f_\mathcal{D}^*\) and its contribution to the framework is unclear (R3).  
  - Table 2’s results require a more detailed explanation (R3).  
- **Additional open questions**:  
  - How does LAMDA perform when LF and HF promising regions do not overlap? (R1)  
  - Why does LAMDA outperform existing multi-fidelity methods? (R2)  
  - Could the framework be applied to gradient-based HPO? (R4)  
  - Is it sufficient to only update the PDF of promising regions? (R2)
