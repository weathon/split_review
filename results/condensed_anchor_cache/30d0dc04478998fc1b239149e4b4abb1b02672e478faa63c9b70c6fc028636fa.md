## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes KLay, a new data structure for representing arithmetic circuits used in neurosymbolic AI that enables efficient parallelization on GPUs. The approach organizes d-DNNF circuits into layers and uses Merkle hashing for node deduplication, reducing evaluation to indexing and scattering operations. Empirical results show speedups of multiple orders of magnitude over prior methods. One reviewer (score 8) found the work very promising, while three others (score 6) noted limitations in scope and justification. One reviewer reported no weaknesses.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important challenge: scalability is a major roadblock for neurosymbolic AI, and the achieved speed-ups on both CPU and GPU are very promising (R1, R2, R4). Speedups of multiple orders of magnitude over state-of-the-art (R2, R1).
- The problem formulation is clear (R3).
- The paper is well-written, with intuitive explanations via appropriate figures and examples despite the involved methodology (R1).
- Tackles a difficult problem that other authors had claimed impossible to solve (R4).
- Paves the way for broader deployment of neurosymbolic AI models on standard deep learning hardware (GPU/TPU) by efficiently exploiting their parallel potential (R2, R4).
- Empirical results are displayed clearly and show effective superiority over existing methods (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient focus in title/abstract:** The paper focuses on a particular flavor of neurosymbolic AI (neural network feeding into probabilistic inference based on arithmetic circuits). This should be reflected explicitly in the abstract and a more specific title (e.g., "Accelerating Arithmetic Circuits in Neurosymbolic AI") to attract the right audience (R1).
- **Missing background context:** The introduction to neurosymbolic AI is lacking. Section 2 ("A Brief Primer") does not adequately explain the concept, focusing mainly on Boolean circuits and GPU execution challenges without emphasizing the significance and value of the work (R2). The motivation needs further justification (R3).
- **Limited experimental comparisons:** Comparisons with state-of-the-art methods appear limited relative to those discussed in related works (R2). Additionally, the batch size used in Table 1 is 1; this may disadvantage non-parallel methods due to lower resource utilization and is not standard measurement practice (R2).
- **Unclear end-to-end impact:** Only the MNIST addition dataset tests the combined neural+symbolic timing. The contributions of individual blocks are not quantified. The neural part in that task is lightweight (simple digit recognition); it is unclear how the relation between neural and symbolic parts would look in other tasks (R1). The workload and time consumption of the arithmetic circuit compared to the upstream neural network are not provided; what is the typical percentage? (R3).
- **Node explosion on Sudoku/HMLC:** The number of KLay nodes increases by 1.5–2.7× on these datasets. The reason for this increase is not explained (R1).
- **Insufficient dataset documentation:** Datasets should be introduced in the appendix for self-containment. In Figure 6, the number of instances (variables, clauses, or combination?) is not explained, and the claimed range over 5 orders of magnitude is not visible in the figure (R1). The slope increase when instances exceed 100 in Figure 6 is not discussed; does it align with the expected linear time complexity? (R2).
- **Missing explanation of neural network interface:** The details of how a neural network interfaces with the arithmetic circuits (assumptions, domain knowledge) remain unclear. This should be explained at least for one task (e.g., MNIST addition) in an appendix (R1). Are the neural networks indeed separate from the arithmetic circuit? If leaf node values represent probabilities from neural networks, could the probability data be transferred to CPUs for post-order tree traversal after neural network computation on GPUs? (R3).
- **Lack of discussion on alternative AI accelerator platforms:** The paper states arithmetic circuits are ill-suited for AI accelerators, but ASICs and FPGAs are known to handle irregular operations. The authors should discuss why arithmetic circuits are not suitable on those platforms (R3).
- **No comparison to efficient parallel post-order tree traversal:** If this approach is an efficient method for parallel post-order tree traversal, how does it compare to other efficient parallel algorithms for that purpose? (R3).
- **Missing justification of advantages over modern AI models:** What capabilities does neurosymbolic AI offer that current AI models lack? Providing examples of anticipated advantages would be helpful (R3).
- **Unclear optimization process:** How are KLay optimized? Is optimization performed on the arithmetic circuits (which are differentiable) before representing them as KLay? A more explicit answer would complete the analysis (R4).
