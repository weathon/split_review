## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes GIST, a method to generate image-specific fine-grained text descriptions from image-only datasets to improve fine-grained classification. The workflow has three steps: (1) prompting a pretrained LLM (GPT-3) with domain-specific prompts to generate diverse fine-grained candidate text descriptions per class; (2) using pretrained CLIP to match each training image to label-preserving descriptions that capture relevant visual features; (3) summarizing matched texts via LLM to construct image-text pairs. The pairs are then used to fine-tune a CLIP image encoder and train a classifier for downstream tasks. Experiments are conducted on four fine-grained datasets in full-shot and few-shot settings, showing an average improvement of 4.1% accuracy over CLIP linear probes and 1.1% over the previous state-of-the-art image-text method.

### Strengths
- The motivation of using LLM-generated text for fine-grained visual classification is well presented and the paper is clearly written and easy to follow.
- The three-step workflow (LLM candidate generation, CLIP matching, LLM summarization) is simple, straightforward, and sound; its explanations and illustrations are well-formulated.
- GIST achieves good results on several fine-grained classification benchmarks in both full-shot and few-shot settings, outperforming comparable methods including CLIP linear probes and prior image-text methods.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Several reviewers noted that using LLMs to generate/augment text for CLIP training has been explored in prior works (e.g., [1,2], Maniparambil et al., 2023). One reviewer (score 6) considered the technical contribution limited; others (score 5) noted the core idea is the same as typical data-labeling workflows using LLMs and that the community is already familiar with prompting LLMs for visual classification.
- **Prompt engineering burden**: The method requires domain-specific designs for LLM prompts, which are not free and encode human knowledge/preference prior. In few-shot settings, it is hard to determine the designs due to scarcity of validation/test sets, making the method less general to real-world few-shot tasks. There is no discussion of the difficulty of prompt preparation compared to simple prompts like “a photo of class name”.
- **Missing ablations and analyses**:
  - No ablation on the matching step (CLIP matching of images to generated texts). It is unclear whether the matching influences downstream performance or how many captions are matched per image and whether more captions help.
  - No study on the impact of fine-tuning CLIP with the generated pairs, nor on whether more training images further improve performance.
  - No proper explanation for why a summary step is required or beneficial.
  - No comparison with other fine-tuning families such as prompt-tuning approaches.
- **Incomplete comparisons**:
  - The most directly conflicting method (Maniparambil et al., 2023) is not compared.
  - In Table 1, the proposed workflow using GPT-3, CLIP, and intricate prompts does not achieve significant improvement over FLYP (which uses CLIP and a simple “a photo of class name” prompt), even though the comparison setting is described as unfair by a reviewer.
- **Data and cost concerns**:
  - The method requires full images for the target domain, which may be hard to obtain in real-world settings (e.g., medical images).
  - Manual cleaning mentioned in Section 5 is expensive, especially in expert-driven fields; the same effort could be spent on labeling more images, which is likely to yield larger improvements.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms/missing experiments**:
  - What are the text inputs to GIST – standard prompts with labels or the text descriptions? Is performance improved by fine-tuning CLIP with both short and original texts, or by fine-tuning with longer texts while classifying with shorter ones?
  - How would using a trained vision-LLM (e.g., BLIP-2, Flamingo) replace the caption generation and matching pipeline?
  - Are the generated explanations exclusively matched to a single image or can the same caption match multiple images? Please provide statistics on matching frequency.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) was more positive about the method’s effectiveness, while the other two (score 5) found the contribution limited and the improvements non-significant compared to simpler baselines.
