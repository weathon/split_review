## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a test-time training (TTT) pipeline for out-of-distribution industrial anomaly detection (IAD) that uses a memory bank and a rectified Sinkhorn algorithm for distribution alignment. It claims to avoid retraining or access to training data during inference. The reviewers diverge sharply: Reviewer 3 (rating 8) finds the approach novel, technically sound, and extensively benchmarked; Reviewers 1 and 2 (ratings 3, 3) are moderately positive about the idea but raise significant concerns about motivation and practical relevance; Reviewer 4 (rating 1) sees the work as lacking novelty, poorly justified, and containing technical errors.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive experiments (Reviewer 1).
- First to introduce test-time training in traditional IAD; the idea is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- Rectified Sinkhorn algorithm is a technically interesting modification (Reviewer 2).
- Addresses the critical issue of distribution shifts during inference (Reviewer 3).
- Use of Sinkhorn distance is well-suited to the problem (Reviewer 3).
- Clever augmentation in the target domain at test time (Reviewer 3).
- Extensive benchmarking against SOTA anomaly detection and TTT methods on multiple datasets (MVTec, RealIAD) (Reviewer 3).
- Effectively applies advanced techniques to handle distributional shifts (Reviewer 4, though this praise is generic).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and novelty**: Two reviewers question the need for TTT in traditional IAD. Reviewer 2 argues that imaging equipment in real production lines is high-quality, so noisy inputs as simulated in the paper are unrealistic; TTT is more appropriate for few-shot IAD (citing recent work FOCT). Reviewer 4 states the problems (needing prior knowledge of target distribution, access to training data at inference) have already been addressed by prior works such as Invariant Risk Minimization, and the paper does not justify why a memory bank is the solution. Reviewer 4 also calls the approach an unnatural combination of existing techniques lacking originality. In contrast, Reviewer 3 considers the problem critical and the solution clever.
- **Theoretical foundations**: The methodology is described as ad-hoc (Reviewer 1). There is no theoretical analysis of convergence for the rectified Sinkhorn algorithm (Reviewer 2). Mathematical formulations are unclear or erroneous: e.g., backbone network denoted as \(z_i\), \(C(\cdot, K)\) with \(C\) and \(K\) undefined, \(M \in D_s\) and \(z_j \in D_s\) with no clear set relationship (Reviewer 4). Reviewers call for stronger theoretical grounding.
- **Insufficient explanation of the method**: The paper does not clearly explain why a memory bank helps address distribution shifts at test time (line 76; Reviewer 4). Logical flow has gaps (lines 82, 84, 92, 94; Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also notes that mixing Gaussian KL-divergence is dismissed without adequate justification, and the framing as an optimal transport problem is not motivated.
- **Scalability and efficiency**: Scalability concerns as memory bank size increases (Reviewer 3). No discussion of trade-offs between memory bank size, computational cost, and performance; a complexity analysis is requested (Reviewer 3). Lack of real-time efficiency comparisons with competitors, which is crucial for practical deployment (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**: The paper does not compare with few-shot IAD methods where Reviewer 2 believes TTT is more natural. No ablation isolating the effect of the rectified Sinkhorn algorithm versus standard Sinkhorn.
- **Presentation and reproducibility**: Editorial issues: citation formatting and incorrect quotation marks (Reviewer 1). Poor writing quality with many logical gaps (Reviewer 4). Code is not provided; Reviewer 3 recommends releasing an anonymous GitHub implementation to support reproducibility.
