## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SimCAS (Chunk, Align, Select), a three-step framework to enable pre-trained encoder-decoder transformers (e.g., BART) to process long sequences without additional pre-training. It first divides the input into non-overlapping chunks and applies local self-attention within chunks. After each encoder layer, inter-chunk communication is performed by updating each chunk's bos/eos tokens with the average of the newly computed bos/eos tokens across all chunks. For decoding, a randomly initialized 8M-parameter feedforward selector network, trained via PPO with rewards based on language modeling likelihood and a penalty for selecting too many tokens, selects the most representative encoder hidden states. Experiments on long-text summarization and reading comprehension (NarrativeQA) show improvements over baselines; the main gain comes from chunking and selection.

### Strengths
- The framework is simple, intuitive, and can be applied directly to existing pre-trained PLMs like BART without any additional pre-training or architectural changes.
- The PPO-based training of the selector network is a novel and interesting approach to focus attention on crucial encoded hidden states; it provides insights beyond simpler selection methods.
- Experiments are extensive across many long-context datasets (Reviewer 3: “excellent coverage” and “robust and strong results”; Reviewer 2: “better results over strong baselines”; Reviewer 1: ~20% improvement over baselines). NarrativeQA, a recognized hard benchmark, is included.
- Sparsification of cross-attention via selection shows surprising potential for performance gains.

### Weaknesses
- **Experiment coverage and baselines:** Reviewer 1 notes that experiments focus mainly on summarization and omissions of CNN/DailyMail and XSum (the most standard datasets) and only one other task (NarrativeQA). More tasks (document translation, classification) would strengthen the work. Reviewer 2 adds that only BART models are tested; it is unclear if the method works on recent large models like LLaMA. Conversely, Reviewer 3 considers the evaluation comprehensive.
- **Missing baseline and ablations:**
  - Table 4 should include a row for the pure baseline (w/o chunk and w/o select) to show the individual contribution of each component (Reviewer 1).
  - No ablation study for the RL objective components (reward formulations, necessity vs. MLE). The paper lacks justification for why RL is needed over simpler selection methods, does not discuss training instability or sensitivity to reward design (Reviewer 2).
  - The effect of chunk length is not ablated: smaller chunks give finer granularity, larger chunks accelerate inference. Is 512 the optimal chunk length? (Reviewer 3)
- **Latency and computational cost:** None of the reviewers found a latency measurement or per-iteration speed-up comparison with baselines. Selection/retrieval-based methods typically incur overhead, and the tradeoff between performance gain and inference time must be quantified (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Applicability to decoder-only models:** The method relies on cross-attention in encoder-decoder models; its applicability to encoder-only or decoder-only transformers (standard LLMs) is unclear, especially with causality constraints on the averaging step for chunk alignment (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Related work omissions:** Missing discussion of length extrapolation methods (e.g., ALiBi, “A Length-Extrapolatable Transformer”, “Train Short, Test Long”) (Reviewer 2). Also missing related works on chunking and memorization: TRIME (Zhong et al., 2023), LongMem (Wang et al., 2023), and Landmark Attention (Mohtashami and Jaggi, 2023) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 notes the method is a form of dynamic token pruning (e.g., Transkimmer [1], [2]) and needs better literature contextualization.
- **Training stability and selector details:** The selector FFN is randomly initialized while the backbone is pre-trained; the first ~10k iterations may be unstable. The paper provides limited details on training dynamics and no analysis of sensitivity to the random initialization (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 also notes the absence of discussion of RL variance issues.
- **Probing/PPO motivation and analysis:**
  - No PPL scores (standard for long-text language modeling) are reported (Reviewer 2).
  - The reward function design appears arbitrary; no sensitivity analysis to different reward formulations (Reviewer 2).
  - No exploration of scaling the selector (e.g., to a RoBERTa with binary classification head) or pre-training it on the same corpus (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarifications needed:**
  - Do baseline methods use sparse self-attention? (Reviewer 1)
  - How are sentences longer than a chunk handled when sentence segmentation fails? (Reviewer 3)
  - Figure 3 has a typo: the blue line should be labeled “selected length” (Reviewer 1).
  - Minor presentation issue: inconsistent line spacing in the background section (Reviewer 2).
