## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SCIP-Net, a neural method using controlled differential equations to predict potential outcomes in continuous time, adjusting for time-varying confounding via inverse propensity weights (IPW) and stabilized weights. The method is evaluated on a synthetic tumor growth model and a semi-synthetic MIMIC-III dataset. Reviewer opinions are mixed: one reviewer is highly positive, praising novelty, rigor, and clarity; the other three are more critical (scores 5–6), citing concerns that the contribution is an incremental A+B combination of TE-CDE and existing IPW theory, that the experiments lack crucial details and do not convincingly demonstrate benefits, and that presentation and claims have inaccuracies. Reviewers 2 and 4 have high confidence (4), while Reviewers 1 and 3 have moderate confidence (3). Overall, the paper addresses an important problem but has notable shortcomings in experimental rigor, justification of novelty, and presentation.

### Strengths
- The problem of predicting counterfactual outcomes in continuous time from EHR data is practically important, with relevance to healthcare and other domains (e.g., banking, fraud detection) – all reviewers.
- To the best of knowledge, the use of IPW and stabilized weights for conditional average potential outcome estimation in continuous time is novel, and the paper presents the first neural method that performs proper adjustment for time-varying confounding in continuous time – Reviewers 1, 2.
- The paper is generally well-written, rigorous, and provides a thorough explanation of the problem formulation and model architecture, with clear visual emphasis and coherent organization – Reviewers 1, 2, 3.
- Experimental results on MIMIC-III (one-hour ahead) and one-day ahead tumor growth prediction show noticeable improvements over baselines, and the ablation studies comparing CIP-Net and SCIP-Net effectively demonstrate the benefit of stabilized weights – Reviewers 2, 3.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution and inaccurate claims** (Reviewer 2): The contribution is essentially a combination of TE-CDE and the IPW framework from Lok, Roysland, Rytgaard; this is an A+B style contribution without high significance. The paper claims balancing is a "heuristic" and does not "properly" adjust for time-varying confounding, but provides no evidence that it is more biased than IPW for conditional estimation. The parameter of interest should be correctly stated as the conditional average potential outcome, not simply "potential outcome". The column name in Table 1 should be "propensity weight adjustment for time-varying covariates" rather than "adjustment for time-varying covariates".
- **Insufficient experimental details and evaluation** (Reviewers 3, 4): 
  - The experiment setup is unclear: the sampling procedure, metric definition ("RMSE of what"?), and dataset generation motivation are not sufficiently explained in the main paper.
  - Simulated data (tumor growth) is not reliable for concluding effectiveness; the paper should demonstrate benefits under covariate shift by re-sampling interventions to test robustness (Reviewer 4).
  - The effect of forecast horizon on irregularity is not analyzed; distributions of timestamp lengths should be reported to contextualize when SCIP-Net provides gains (Reviewer 3).
  - Performance on regularly sampled timestamps is not shown, which would help isolate the advantage of modeling irregularity (Reviewer 3).
  - The choice of baselines is not justified (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing related work** (Reviewers 2, 4): 
  - The temporal-difference learning approach (Shirakawa et al., 2024) that adjusts for time-varying covariates by constructing targets with weighted averages of neural pseudo-outcomes should be discussed in related work.
  - More literature on irregular time series modeling should be included (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation and readability issues** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4): 
  - Training difficulty is not discussed; the network has multiple moving parts (stabilized weight estimation, outcome model) and may be hard to train – more training details and potential difficulties should be provided (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 3 and Table 2 have small fonts and low readability; fonts and legends should be improved (Reviewer 3).
  - Some portions of the manuscript are too casual (e.g., "=>Takeaway" at L509) and expressions are unclear (e.g., "unconditional of" at L272) (Reviewer 4).
- **Inconsistency in baseline performance** (Reviewer 3): In Figure 3, TE-CDE is outperformed by RMSN and CRN, which differs from the original TE-CDE publication and from the MIMIC experiment; an explanation is needed.
- **Sensitivity to confounding strength** (Reviewer 4 question, overlapping with missing ablation): It is unclear how sensitive SCIP-Net is to the level of confounding (parameter $\gamma$); the presented analysis is hard to relate to realistic datasets.
- **Additional missing ablation** (Reviewer 3): The paper should show performance on regularly sampled timestamps and more detailed analyses of irregularity distributions to clarify when the method is beneficial.
