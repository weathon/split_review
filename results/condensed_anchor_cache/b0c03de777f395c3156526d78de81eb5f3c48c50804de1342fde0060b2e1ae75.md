## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates how synthetic context extension (fine-tuning LLMs on synthetically generated long-context data) imparts abilities for long-context retrieval and reasoning tasks. Across three tasks, the authors vary concept expression and context diversity in synthetic data, and find models trained on synthetic data underperform those trained on real data. They interpret this gap through analysis of "retrieval heads" (attention heads responsible for retrieving tokens from context). They show that retrieval heads learned on synthetic data are mostly subsets of those learned on real data, and there is a strong correlation between the head recall and downstream performance. Attention knockout and activation patching experiments demonstrate that retrieval heads are necessary (though not sufficient) for task performance.

### Strengths
- Presents a principled framework for constructing synthetic long-context examples from existing databases with controlled similarity to real data (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Retrieval heads analysis provides interpretable insights into behavioral differences between models trained on different datasets, helping explain both effectiveness and limitations of synthetic data (Reviewer 1).
- The quality analysis of synthesized datasets is reasonable; the authors show that there are no preset principles for synthesizing data, and they find similarity of retrieval heads to be a highly-correlated metric with performance. Sufficient experiments support this (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is clearly written, readily comprehensible, with clear motivation and compelling discussion on the impact of synthetic data in training LLMs (Reviewer 4).
- Clear and coherent experimental procedures with well-organized technical results (Reviewer 4).
- The introduced dataset similarity measure (cosine similarity between retrieval-score vectors) is promising for guiding synthetic data generation; while requiring further investigation, it offers a new perspective (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Visualization quality and clarity** (Reviewer 1, 3): Figure 1 axis labels are tiny and occluded; the retrieval heads heatmap lacks a color scale. Figures 4 and 5 would benefit from larger fonts. Axes in Figure 1 are nearly impossible to read. Figure 3 caption does not explain what the figure shows. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Subset relationship of retrieval heads**: The assertion (line 361) that synthetic data retrieval-scoring heads are "strict subsets" of real-data-trained heads appears contradicted by Figure 1 (e.g., head #0, layer #21 shows high score in synthetic but not real). The characterization (line 428) "nearly" a subset needs clarification. The authors should specify for which tasks and conditions the strict subset holds versus approximate (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of clarity on retrieval head definition and matching** (Reviewer 3): It is not clearly defined how retrieval heads are formally selected (top-k by retrieval score? Not stated in text). It is also not explained how common subsets (intersections) are detected between models; no matching algorithm is described, and simple index matching may be insufficient due to functional symmetry (permuting heads does not change output).
- **Patching operation not explained** (Reviewer 3): Section 5's patching procedure is referenced only as "following Prakash et al."; a proper definition is needed as it is a key part of the analysis.
- **Missing baseline: short-context fine-tuning** (Reviewer 3): It is not shown that long-context fine-tuning is actually required for these tasks; results for base models fine-tuned only on short-context data are needed to justify long-context necessity.
- **Contribution concerns** (Reviewer 3, minority more negative): One reviewer finds the main takeaways (retrieval heads influence performance; models share subset; patching improves weaker model) were already discovered in prior work ([1] Wu et al. 2024, [2] Prakash et al.). The fourth point (similarity metric) is promising but alone insufficient for a conference paper, especially because it requires access to realistic data to compute similarity, which is a major limitation. Other reviewers (1,2,4) found the analysis insightful and valuable as guidance for future work.
- **Treating real data as ceiling may be incomplete** (Reviewer 2): The paper assumes real data performance is the upper bound, but synthetic data could outperform real data in some conditions (e.g., larger quantity). If so, the similarity metric with real data may not be universally valid. Preconditions for using this metric (e.g., dataset size) should be discussed.
- **Representativeness of synthetic data generation principles** (Reviewer 2, 4): The three principles (concept expression, context diversity, symbolic tasks) may not cover all possible synthetic data variations; the results appear somewhat random and no unified configuration/pattern emerges, affecting generalizability.
- **Low-diversity setting confounded with quality** (Reviewer 2): In the low-diversity version (line 202), the "haystack" consists of repeated trivial sentences ("The grass is green…"); this fuses diversity with quality, making it hard to ablate their separate effects. Repeated patterns should be meaningful text related to the task.
- **Unclear definitions and typos** (Reviewer 3): EM (exact match) in line 357 is not defined. Table 1 has duplicate column headers ("concept exp" and "context div"). Theta and RoPE embeddings (line 154) are not defined. The three tasks are hard to understand; examples of samples and needles (at least in appendix) are needed. Where do numbers 0.35 EM, 0.32, 0.20, and 129, 112, 39 heads come from? Typos: line 280 "a sparse of heads" (should be "sparse set"), line 309 "given AN evaluation example", line 351 "H_synth reflects", line 481 "is THE greatest", line 101 "\mathcal{M}" not defined, line 126 missing dot.
- **Missing ablation and inconsistency** (Reviewer 1): (a) "Limited" real data condition (line 137) should be quantitatively defined. (b) MuSiQue context extension: criteria for selecting padding paragraphs? How to ensure added context does not introduce extra information implying the answer? (c) Table 4 appears to have duplicate values from Table 3 in columns Compl., Inter., Rand for SummHay row; the "Orig." value for (Real, Real) setting is inconsistent with the addition of "Orig." and "delta" in following rows.
- **Generalization to full parameter fine-tuning** (Reviewer 1): The paper uses LoRA; it is unclear how patterns in retrieval head behavior and dataset relationships would generalize to full fine-tuning. Authors' predictions/explanation would be valuable even if empirical verification is impractical.
- **Random head dropping** (Reviewer 4): The authors note that randomly dropping attention heads sometimes improves performance; it should be explored whether selectively dropping the least important heads could enhance performance further.
- **Evaluation may be single and subjective** (Reviewer 4): The evaluation metric may not fully support the conclusions.
- **Hybrid training with limited real + synthetic** (Reviewer 1): Request to discuss whether combining limited real data with synthetic data could improve performance, especially in tasks where synthetic retrieval heads are not a strict subset of real data.
- **Short-context generalization** (Reviewer 4): The influence of synthetic data for short-context learning was not addressed; this could affect the generalizability of conclusions. (Also related to Reviewer 3's missing baseline.)
