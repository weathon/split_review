## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CtrLoRA, a two-stage parameter-efficient fine-tuning pipeline for controllable image generation. It first trains a Base ControlNet on multiple conditions to learn common knowledge, then uses condition-specific LoRAs to adapt to new conditions with few data (e.g., 1,000 pairs) and low GPU time (<1 hour). Experiments demonstrate training efficiency and the ability to unify various visual conditions.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: reducing the high cost of training separate ControlNets per condition.
- Well-organized, easy to follow structure.
- Extensive ablation studies and analyses of the proposed modules.
- Impressive experimental results, especially in convergence speed and training efficiency (reduced data/time per new condition, 90% fewer learnable parameters).
- Sufficient qualitative and quantitative results covering more than a dozen conditions.
- Capability to unify multiple condition types in a single framework.

### Weaknesses
- **Justification for training a new Base ControlNet**: Not clearly justified why a pre-trained ControlNet or Uni-ControlNet cannot be directly fine-tuned instead. Missing comparison of training time/resource usage between these options (R1, R2, R3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons to existing efficient methods**:
  - No comparison to ControlNet++ (R1), UniControl (R1, R2, R4), T2I-Adapter, ControlLoRA, SCEdit, or controlnet-union (R3).
  - Missing fully trained single-condition ControlNet baselines for each base condition and for new conditions (R4).
  - Comparisons should include quantitative metrics, adaptation speed, data efficiency, training time, and resource consumption (R1, R3, R4).
- **Computational cost not significantly reduced**: Training Base ControlNet uses millions of images and ~6000 GPU hours for 9 conditions, while a single ControlNet condition requires ~600 GPU hours. The claimed reduction in burden is not clearly supported (R2).
- **Training mechanism of Base ControlNet unclear**: All 9 conditions given equal training iterations despite different sparsity levels; continuous shifting between conditions may cause training difficulty (R2).
- **Motivation for not training new conditions via the same shifting mechanism**: Unclear why new conditions are not added to Base ControlNet training rather than using LoRA (R2).
- **Limited evidence for transfer to community models** (Figure 11a): Only a few results shown; insufficient to convince that CtrlLoRA is effective when transferring to other community models (R2).
- **Pretrained-VAE trick**: Seems to be only an interesting trick; its necessity and effectiveness are not justified (R2).
- **Prompts all in appendix**: Inconvenient for reading (R2).
- **Limited innovativeness**: Using LoRA for fine-tuning is a straightforward idea already implemented in community (e.g., ControlLoRA). The paper does not clearly articulate advantages of Base+LoRA over direct LoRA fine-tuning or over unified models like controlnet-union (R3).
- **Lack of guidance on selecting base conditions**: No discussion of how many or which conditions to use for Base ControlNet to maximize knowledge transfer to new conditions. Missing ablation on the effect of base condition set size/composition (R3, R4).
- **Generalizability to other diffusion models**: Not explored (e.g., SDXL, Pixart). The paper should at least discuss challenges and opportunities (R1).
- **Missing experiments on specific methodological details**:
  - Figure 11b: Why is a direct summation module effective for disentangling conditions? Provide results with/without summation (R2).
  - Table 2: Why not present results for all 9 base conditions compared to UniControl? (R2)
  - Adaptation to new condition: Comparison between fine-tuning a pre-trained ControlNet (or similar) vs the proposed Base+LoRA under the same conditions (R3).
  - Number of conditions in Base ControlNet: How does it affect learning new conditions? (R4)
  - Low-light image enhancement: Demonstrate CtrLoRA’s performance on this task compared to other diffusion models (R4).
