## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Neural Additive Tensor Decomposition (NeAT), which extends CP decomposition by replacing the outer product with component-wise MLPs to capture nonlinear interactions while preserving additive interpretability. The method is evaluated on link prediction for several sparse real-world tensors and a downstream classification task. NeAT is reported to outperform CP and other neural tensor models in accuracy, and its component-wise structure is used for interpretability analysis.

### Strengths
- The model provides a novel and reasonable combination of interpretable CPD with separate nonlinear modules per component, capturing nonlinear interactions while maintaining an additive structure (R1, R3, R4).
- The decomposition yields an interpretable form that is advantageous for understanding latent patterns (R1, R3).
- Extensive experiments on six large-scale real-world datasets show SOTA link prediction performance, outperforming CP by 10% and the second-best neural tensor model by 4% in AUC (R1, R3, R4).
- The evaluation includes both tensor completion accuracy and a downstream fine-tuning task, which is an interesting design (R3, R4).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Many prior works (some cited) replace the outer product with neural networks; NeAT’s main distinction is the absence of cross-component interactions, but this difference is not sufficiently clarified or justified (R2). The dismissal of related methods as having “entangled associations” is overly broad without specific justification for each method (R4).
- **Interpretability claims not fully convincing**: It is unclear whether meaningful patterns reside in the component-wise MLP or the latent factors; more discussion or experiments on consistency are needed (R3). The interpretability advantage over component-cross methods (e.g., Tucker core) is not demonstrated (R3). The interpretability results are not strong enough to prove outstanding performance (R2).
- **Weak empirical evaluation**: The datasets are not challenging enough to demonstrate significant advantages; accuracy improvements over baselines are minor (R2, R4). Missing comparisons with other nonlinear tensor methods (e.g., Gaussian-Process-based) (R3). MovieLens appears to be an easy dataset for neural methods, as all achieve high accuracy (R1). Concerns about baselines: Were they implemented or existing code? Adam may not be efficient for some baselines; why is CPD not trained with cross-entropy? (R4). Parameter count relative to CP is not compared; overfitting risk when parameters exceed observed entries (R2). No evaluation of accuracy relative to model size (R4).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper provides no theoretical analysis beyond a simple cost quantification; the optimization problem is non-unique and difficult to solve for good minima (R1, R4).
- **Computational cost**: The method may be expensive for large tensors (R1).
- **Applicability**: It is unclear whether NeAT works for non-sparse tensors (R2).
- **Missing justification for design choices**: No discussion on when NeAT should be preferred over CPD or more complex models (R2). The downstream fine-tuning setup lacks analysis on why it improves generalization (R3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (score 8, confidence 4) is very positive, praising the contribution and presentation, while Reviewers 2 and 4 (scores 3, confidence 4) are critical of novelty and evaluation strength; Reviewer 3 (score 5, confidence 4) is moderately positive but raises concerns about interpretability and experiments.
