## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies weak supervision with image-level labels to achieve segmentation by establishing formal parallels between differentiable heatmap architectures (unrolled LRP) and conventional encoder-decoder architectures (e.g., U‑Net). It trains these architectures with a combined classification and segmentation loss and evaluates segmentation accuracy. Empirical results show competitive performance against comparable U‑Nets, especially in semi‑supervised settings with few pixel‑level labels. The goal is to convert image classifiers directly into segmentation models, leveraging weak image‑level supervision.

### Strengths
- The idea of turning image classifiers into segmentation models via unrolled LRP is novel and interesting; it draws formal parallels between heatmap architectures and encoder‑decoder architectures (R1, R2, R4).
- The combined classification and segmentation loss yields competitive segmentation accuracy without degrading classifier performance (R2, R4).
- In semi‑supervised settings, the proposed method outperforms comparable U‑Nets, with the margin increasing as the level of pixel‑level supervision decreases (R4).
- The approach uses standard training objectives, making it simple and easy to plug into existing task‑specific architectures (R2).
- The weak‑supervision setting studied helps understand CNN learning (R1).
- The research direction is promising, especially given the availability of well‑performed pre‑trained (vision‑language) classification models (R3).
- **Contrasting view:** One reviewer (score 8) found the paper well written and structured, and felt the experiments support the claims convincingly (R2). Other reviewers (scores 3, 5, 6) strongly disagreed with this assessment (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity:** The paper’s organization and language are poor; LRP is used many times without a concrete definition (R1, R4). Grammatical errors and vague writing are present (R4). Conversely, R2 (score 8) considered the paper well written.
- **Insufficient comparisons:** The work lacks comparisons with other weakly‑supervised semantic segmentation methods that extract pseudo masks from image‑level labels (R3). The only contrast is with U‑Net; relevant variants are ignored (R4).
- **Weak experimental results:** One reviewer (score 3) found the reported segmentation results unacceptably poor and noted the authors state they do not aim for best semi‑supervised performance, yet the numbers are too low (R3). No ablation studies are provided (R3).
- **Limited datasets and validation:** Experiments are run on few datasets; extensive datasets are not used (R4). The data selection description is insufficient and more validation on wider datasets is needed (R4). The claim that “ResNet18 U‑Net is outperformed by ResNet50 U‑Net” lacks direct experimental support (R4).
- **Restricted architectural scope:** The method is only compared with U‑Net; it is unclear whether unrolled heatmap architectures can be extended to other segmentation models (e.g., beyond U‑Net) and, if so, how they would compare (R4).
- **Missing ablations and experimental support:**
  - Algorithm 1 in Section 2.1 needs a clearer explanation (R4).
  - The role of the 1×1 convolution in the “Final classifier layers” (Section 2.2) is not clarified (R4).
  - Different weight combinations for the loss function (Section 2.3) require experiments to justify the choices (R4).
  - The claim in Section 2.4 that “the gradient of the classification loss backpropagates solely through the encoder, while the gradient of the heatmap loss backpropagates solely through the decoder” should be validated through additional experiments or theoretical analysis (R4).
  - More comparative analysis with improved U‑Net models is needed in the Quantitative Results section (R4).
- **Robustness concerns:** Segmentation performance may be tied to heatmap quality, which depends on data quantity and class distribution. The method’s robustness to real‑world class imbalance is not addressed (R2). How does the method perform on larger multi‑label datasets with many instances per image that create uncertain heatmap regions? (R2)
- **Impact of orthogonal semi‑supervised losses:** How would losses from other semi‑supervised segmentation approaches affect training with unrolled LRP? (R2)
- **Reproducibility:** Code is not currently available (R2).
