## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses Source-Free Video Domain Adaptation (SFVDA) with multiple pre-trained source models (model zoo). It proposes the Multi-Source-Video-Model Aggregation (MSVMA) framework, consisting of Multi-level Instance Transferability Calibration (MITC) – which enhances uncertainty-based transferability metrics with group- and dataset-level calibration – and Instance-level Multi Video Model Aggregation (IMVMA), which uses instance-specific weights to aggregate source models via a path generation network. The method is evaluated on three video action recognition datasets (Daily-DA, Sports-DA, UCF-HMDB) and achieves state-of-the-art results compared to adapted baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novel task.** The work is the first to explore multi-source SFVDA (model zoo), an underexplored area in video domain adaptation. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Clear motivation for multi-level calibration.** MITC leverages instance-, group-, and dataset-level information to estimate transferability, providing a principled approach. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Strong empirical performance.** Extensive experiments on three diverse video benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over both single-source and multi-source baselines. Detailed ablation studies dissect the contribution of each calibration level and weighting strategy. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Comprehensive baseline comparisons.** The paper includes comparisons with adapted versions of conventional source-free DA methods, and the framework is generally reproducible (despite typos). (Reviewer 1)

*Note: Reviewer 2 rated the originality and experimental thoroughness most highly; Reviewers 1 and 3 were less convinced about methodological novelty but acknowledged empirical strength.*

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of video-specific innovation.** The method heavily relies on image-domain concepts (SUTE, entropy) without addressing video-specific spatiotemporal complexities. The same approach could be applied to any domain (point cloud, time-series, NLP) by swapping the model zoo. Prior SFVDA works (e.g., [15], [22]) explicitly design video-specific strategies that are missing here. (Reviewers 1, 3)
2. **Limited novelty – more engineering than research.** Many components are direct adaptations of existing methods: MITC is a combination of SUTE and entropy; \(L_{SHTC}\) and the path generation network are from prior literature. While the task is new for video, the core ideas are not. (Reviewers 1, 3)
3. **Attribution of effectiveness unclear.** It is not shown whether gains come from the proposed method or simply from using multiple pretrained models, which inherently improve generalization. (Reviewer 2)
4. **Missing most relevant baselines.** The experiments do not include a direct comparison to the most related multi-model methods SUTE [16] and [17] extended to video with the same feature extraction scheme. Such a baseline would better isolate the contribution of the proposed aggregation. (Reviewer 2; also raised as a question)
5. **No hyperparameter sensitivity for \(\theta_1\).** Despite \(L_{cor}\) being a primary contribution, a sensitivity analysis for the tradeoff parameter \(\theta_1\) in the overall objective is absent. (Reviewers 1, 3)
6. **Presentation and writing issues.** Several critical points confuse or mislead: the abstract states “multiple source domains” but only a single source domain with multiple architectures is used; notations such as \(\mathcal{H}\) in SUTE and \(d\) in Eq. 4 are undefined; there are typos (e.g., Line 709 should reference equation 9); the motivation for selecting the 15 models (C3D, SlowFast, I3D variants) is not given. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
7. **Unsatisfactory literature review.** Wrong citation: Line 64 points to [14] which does not discuss SFVDA. Missing relevant SFVDA works (e.g., on source-free multimodal video DA, large language-vision models for SFVDA, spatio-temporal contrastive learning) and transferability estimation methods (e.g., for active or universal DA). (Reviewer 1)
8. **Dataset diversity and generalizability.** The three datasets (Daily-DA, Sports-DA, UCF-HMDB) may not test adaptability to drastically different video types (low-light, surveillance). The method requires dataset-specific tuning for hyperparameters, reducing its utility in adaptive scenarios. (Reviewer 2)
9. **Lack of interpretability for model aggregation weights.** The path generation network assigns instance-specific weights but provides no explanation of why certain models or instances are favored. Analysis of how weights vary across tasks (e.g., large improvement on MIT→ARID vs. marginal on H→A) is missing. There is no discussion of how the network handles models with similar transferability scores, or whether an optimal aggregation threshold exists across datasets. (Reviewer 2, also from questions; Reviewer 1)
10. **Sensitivity to noise/outliers.** Heavy reliance on uncertainty metrics for transferability estimation may make the method vulnerable to noise or outliers in diverse video content. (Reviewer 2)
11. **Code anonymization issue.** The provided code is not properly anonymized and contains personal information, violating double-blind submission guidelines. (Reviewer 1)
