- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 6, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
GLoRA adaptively selects LoRA ranks per layer by computing the intrinsic dimensionality of hidden state representations, aiming to balance efficiency and expressivity. The paper provides theoretical analysis linking intrinsic dimension to a lower bound on optimal LoRA rank, and reports experiments on DeBERTaV3 across 6 GLUE tasks, comparing against LoRA and SLoRA.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical foundation:** The paper presents a series of definitions, theorems, and corollaries (Section 3) that connect the intrinsic dimensionality of data representation manifolds with the rank of transformer block weight updates. This provides a principled basis for rank selection. (Reviewer 1, score 5, confidence 4; Reviewer 2, score 5, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, score 6, confidence 4; Reviewer 4, score 3, confidence 3)
- **Empirical promise:** Experimental results (Table 1) show that GLoRA outperforms LoRA and SLoRA under the same parameter budget, demonstrating potential for resource-constrained scenarios. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- **Adaptive ranks for NLU:** The proposed method dynamically adjusts LoRA ranks based on hidden representation geometry, addressing the expressivity–efficiency trade-off across multiple natural language understanding tasks. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Bridge between geometry and optimization:** The paper studies the lower bound of the optimal fine-tuning rank, linking the geometry of hidden representations to the parameter manifold, which is a novel angle. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing algorithm description:** The paper does not provide a clear, step‑by‑step description, pseudo‑code, or algorithmic overview of GLoRA. After the theoretical Section 3, it jumps directly to experiments. All four reviewers consider this a critical omission that hampers reproducibility and understanding. (Reviewer 1: “I haven't found any description about the main algorithm”; Reviewer 3: “lack of an overall summary description … an algorithm description or pseudo code can be added”; Reviewer 4: “did not provide a formal definition or introduction of the proposed GLoRA algorithm”) – **Severe**.
- **Theory–practice gap:** The connection between the theoretical lower bounds (Theorems 3.1, 3.2) and the actual selection of LoRA ranks is not articulated. It is unclear how the intrinsic dimension is estimated, how the lower bound is used to choose ranks, and how ranks are adapted during training. Reviewer 4 notes the lower bound can be 0 (trivial) and that the bound on \(d_i - d_{i-1}\) is insufficient to inform rank selection. Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 also emphasize this gap. (Reviewer 4: “lower bound … can take the value 0, which is the trivial lower bound”; “does not reflect how the LoRA rank can be adaptively adjusted based on data representations that might change during fine‑tuning”) – **Severe**.
- **Limited empirical scope:** Experiments are conducted only on DeBERTaV3 (2021), an encoder‑only model, and only on 6 of the 8 GLUE tasks (MNLI and QQP are missing). There are no results on decoder‑only LLMs or other architectures. (Reviewer 1: “a bit outdated and of limited scope”; Reviewer 2: “only 6 of the 8 tasks from the original LoRA paper”; Reviewer 3: “Why is there a lack of testing for MNLI and QQP?”; Reviewer 4: “confined on the DeBERTaV3 architecture and GLUE dataset”)
- **Insufficient baselines:** Comparison is limited to naive LoRA and SLoRA. Adapt‑LoRA, ALoRA, and SaLoRA, which are mentioned in the related work, are not included. Specifically, AdaLoRA is missing despite the paper stating an intention to compare it (Section 4.1). (Reviewer 1: “I wonder why the comparison is only conducted on the naive LoRA and SLoRA”; Reviewer 3: “Why are there no experiments on Adalora?”; Reviewer 4: “did not provide compare their method against AdaLoRA, SaLoRA, and ALoRA in Table 1 and Table 3”)
- **Experimental explanation issues:** The mean ranks are reported as integers (1 or 2) rather than real numbers. The method outperforms LoRA with ranks 1 or 2 even though the mean rank across datasets is consistently 1 or 2, raising questions about how the adaptivity contributes beyond a fixed low rank. (Reviewer 2: “Mean Rank is given as either an integer (1 or 2), rather than a real number”; “proposed method performs significantly better than LoRA with ranks of 1 or 2, but the mean rank … remains either 1 or 2, which raises questions about the method’s effectiveness”)
- **Notation and rigor problems:** Several undefined notations and typos compromise the mathematical presentation. Examples: \(\mathrm{dim} \mathfrak{J}mf\) (Page 3, line 157) is not defined; the Fisher Information Matrix subscript \(\phi\) may be missing (Page 4, lines 181‑184); the Riemannian metric formula is imprecise; the term “intrinsic dimension” itself lacks a formal definition (lines 240‑242). Reviewer 4 (score 3, confidence 3) is the most critical of this, but the issue is objective. (Reviewer 4: “the solidness of the notations and the derivations … is compromised by some typo and undefined notations”)
- **Lack of adaptivity analysis:** It is unclear whether GLoRA uses fixed or adaptive ranks over the course of training, what the rank increasing/decreasing policy is, and where in the transformer the method is applied (all layers or a subset). No analysis of the resulting rank patterns is provided. (Reviewer 3: “lacks experiments exploring the application position of GLoRA in the transformer model”; Reviewer 4: “Does GLoRA use a fixed rank across the training process? … What is the rank increasing and decreasing policy?”)
- **Missing sensitivity and cost analysis:** The paper does not examine sensitivity to the choice of intrinsic dimension estimator, hyperparameter sensitivity, or the additional computational overhead of the rank estimation process. (Reviewer 3: “lacks experiments exploring the sensitivity … to different choices of the intrinsic dimension estimator”; Reviewer 1: “Estimating the intrinsic dimension … should be non‑trivial and time‑consuming. How does GLoRA achieve … even faster training speed?”; Reviewer 4: “What is the algorithmic complexity of the rank estimation process?”)
- **Missing prior citation:** The paper omits a key reference on intrinsic dimension: Li et al. “Measuring the intrinsic dimension of objective landscapes” (2018). (Reviewer 1)
