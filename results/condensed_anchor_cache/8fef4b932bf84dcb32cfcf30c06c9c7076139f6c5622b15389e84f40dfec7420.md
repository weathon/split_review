## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a framework of *natural counterfactuals* to address limitations in Judea Pearl’s standard approach, which often produces counterfactuals that deviate too far from observed data. The method allows controlled backtracking – changes in causally preceding variables – guided by a “naturalness” criterion, and is formulated as an optimization problem. Empirical experiments on simulated and real-world data are provided.

### Strengths

- **Well‐written and structured.** (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Clear rationale** for why traditional nonbacktracking counterfactuals can be impractical. (Reviewer 2)
- **Novel and interesting approach** – a new take on counterfactual reasoning that addresses limitations of the Pearlian framework. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Actionable goal** of producing more realistic counterfactuals; the problem is formulated as a simple optimization. (Reviewer 2)
- **Empirical results** on both simulated and real-world data demonstrate the method’s applicability. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

- **Missing differentiation of reasoning goals.** The introduction’s example conflates reasoning about an “actual cause” (identifying causal chains) with “constructive” reasoning (finding plausible alternative events to change an outcome). These are different but both useful; the proposed approach is an elaboration, not a replacement. (Reviewer 1)
- **Ambiguity and data‐dependency of “naturalness”.**  
  - “Naturalness” is unlikely to be defined unambiguously and universally agreed upon.  
  - The *mechanisms* of intervention are external to an SCM, so the SCM itself does not contain the information needed to decide naturalness. Example: “Tom is restrained from hitting Jerry” may be unnatural in a bus but natural in a car; not all such intervention variables can be represented in the SCM.  
  - The notion conflates the *likelihood of an event* with the *plausibility of an intervention* – an intervention can be unlikely yet perfectly plausible. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation.**  
  - The experiments are more like **demonstrations** than severe tests; the underlying logic is not explained, and they do not convincingly show the method would work well in general. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The claim that the method yields **more plausible interventions** is **not validated in the case studies** – the evaluation is unclear. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparison to related work.** How does this work differ from “Backtracking Counterfactuals” (arXiv:2211.00472)? (Reviewer 2; note: Reviewer 2 stated low confidence, rating 6 vs. 5 from others)
- **Strong assumption of causal graph knowledge.** While common in causality, the paper lacks a clear relation to machine learning and how the method would apply to arbitrary ML systems. Appendix D touches this, but the main paper should elaborate more. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor issues.**  
  - Broken reference in Section 2 (second line).  
  - Inconsistent spelling: “non-backtracking” vs. “nonbacktracking”.  
  - Broken V‑SCM reference in Appendix A.2. (Reviewer 3)

**Disagreement note:** Reviewer 2 (rating 6) is more positive than Reviewers 1 and 3 (both rating 5), but Reviewer 2 expressed low confidence (1) and did not examine the paper deeply. The other reviewers raise more substantial concerns about the evaluation and assumptions.
