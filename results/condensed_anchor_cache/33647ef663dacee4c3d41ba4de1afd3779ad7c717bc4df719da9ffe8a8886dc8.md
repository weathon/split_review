## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DP-LSGD, a generalization of DP-SGD that performs multiple local gradient descent steps per example before clipping, averaging, and noising the aggregated update. It provides convergence bounds for convex and non-convex smooth losses without a Lipschitz assumption and analyzes how local iterations can reduce clipping bias and improve the utility–privacy trade‑off. Experiments on CIFAR10, SVHN, and EMNIST with ResNet20 and WRN‑40‑4 show that DP‑LSGD (typically K=10) outperforms standard DP‑SGD at various privacy levels. Reviewers disagreed on the overall contribution: two (R2, R4) were positive, praising the strong empirical results and interesting theoretical analysis, while the other two (R1, R3) raised serious concerns about theoretical interpretability, experimental validity, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The problem of understanding how clipping affects model training is important and challenging, especially for local SGD, which strictly generalizes regular SGD.
- Empirical results are strong, going beyond accuracy comparisons: the paper studies the incremental norm (clipping bias) in detail to validate the intuition behind local updates, and consistently shows better accuracy than DP‑SGD under the same privacy budget.
- The algorithm draws on ideas from federated learning (local SGD) and provides both theoretical convergence guarantees and improved empirical performance.
- Experiments are conducted on multiple datasets (CIFAR10, SVHN, EMNIST), and confidence intervals are reported for most of them.
- The theoretical analysis provides convergence results for both convex and non‑convex cases; the non‑convex case is especially interesting because it does not require a Lipschitz assumption on the loss, making it more general than prior work.
- The paper includes a convenient comparison because DP‑SGD is a special case (K=1) of DP‑LSGD, so the theorems (Theorems 1 and 2) directly contrast the two algorithms.
- Empirical results are very impressive, with DP‑LSGD outperforming DP‑SGD in every experiment, often by a substantial margin in the low‑epsilon regime.
- Source code is provided, which helps clarify the implementation details.

### Weaknesses
- **Theory clarity and parameter consistency:** The theoretical section lacks clarity on several parameter choices and bound terms. It is difficult to interpret the results or assess how meaningful the claimed improvements are.
- **Questionable assumption B = O(c):** Both Theorem 1 and its discussion assume B = O(c). If B = Θ(c), the clipping bias term γ becomes a lower bound, raising the question of why understanding clipping bias is necessary. The authors need to justify why a regime where B = o(c) is practical or why the bound is not already dominated by γ.
- **Inconsistent parameter settings in Theorem 1:** The paper gives two different settings for K: K² = O(nq) and later K = O(T d / n² ε²). For small dimensions, these can conflict (q ≈ o(1/n) unless T = Ω(n²)), leaving the intended parameter regime unclear.
- **Privacy term in Theorem 2 does not grow with T:** The bound contains d²/(ε² n²) independent of T, whereas in Theorem 1 the privacy term grows with T. Without strong convexity, the DP noise should increase with T, so the setting appears to avoid paying a privacy cost for more iterations.
- **Questionable assumption B = B₀·η after Theorem 2:** Setting B to decay with K and T is not consistent with the expectation that B should be independent of T and possibly increase with K (more local steps on non‑convex losses increase variance). This assumption may artificially suppress the privacy cost.
- **Batch sampling and accounting mismatch (major issue):** The privacy accounting uses Poisson subsampling (prv_accountant), but the implementation uses a standard `torch.utils.data.DataLoader` with `shuffle=True`, not Poisson sampling. This mismatch can lead to different (ε,δ) guarantees (see Chua et al. ICML 2024). The paper does not mention this discrepancy and it may affect the reported privacy levels.
- **Misleading comparison to De et al. (2022):** (a) Table 2 lacks error bars, so it is unclear whether the gap between DP‑LSGD and De et al. is statistically significant. (b) The numbers reported for De et al. (e.g., 80.3% at ε=8) are lower than the original paper (81.4 ± 0.2) for the same setting. The authors should clarify why their reproduced numbers differ, or rerun the experiments under consistent conditions.
- **Accounting method differs from De et al.:** The authors use the PRV accountant, while De et al. used the RDP accountant, which typically yields a higher noise multiplier for the same (ε,δ). Any reported accuracy gap could be partially due to this accounting difference. A fair comparison would use the same accounting, and the paper should explicitly state which accountant is used.
- **Computational complexity and fairness:** DP‑LSGD requires K times more runtime than DP‑SGD (K=10 in most experiments). This is a significant limitation that should be highlighted. Since more compute often improves results, it is unclear whether DP‑LSGD is truly better or just benefits from more computation. The paper would benefit from experiments that fix the total compute budget (e.g., run DP‑SGD for K T iterations).
- **Unclear choice of K:** The paper always uses K=10 but does not explain why this value is optimal or how it interacts with the clipping threshold. Experiments varying K under different clipping thresholds are missing.
- **Impact of very small clipping thresholds:** When clipping thresholds are very small (and most gradients are clipped), the benefit of local updates is unclear. The paper should discuss how the clipping threshold was chosen and whether it affects the advantages of DP‑LSGD.
- **Practicality for large models:** For very large models with per‑core batch sizes of 1 or very small, the memory overhead of storing per‑example local updates may be prohibitive. The paper mentions this only briefly.
- **Missing error bars in Table 2:** The paper should include confidence intervals (or standard deviations) for the test accuracies in Table 2 to assess statistical significance.
- **Questionable claim about clipping bias in Fig. 1(c):** The faster convergence observed in Fig. 1(c) may not directly demonstrate reduced clipping bias; additional experiments would be needed to support that claim.
- **Minor issues:** (a) In Fig. 2, the symbol B should likely be 𝒷 (the incremental norm). (b) The `get_std` function sets the noise multiplier to 100 as an exception handler, which could silently hide bugs if the required σ exceeds 100.
- **Limited algorithmic novelty:** The proposed algorithm is essentially DP‑FedAvg (McMahan et al. 2018) with individual samples in place of clients. While applying it in this context appears new, the core idea is not novel.
- **Writing could be improved,** especially in the theoretical sections, to make the assumptions and results more accessible.
