## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies a drawback of the standard Multiple Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA) for multi-objective optimization: MGDA can converge to weakly Pareto-optimal points and fail to reach strict Pareto optimality, especially when objective functions have partial variable dependencies. To address this, the authors propose RP-MGDA, which partitions the variables into groups based on known variable structure (using three merging rules: dense-block and loop) and runs separate MGDA steps per group. Toy examples and a synthetic bi-objective non-convex problem illustrate the improvement, and a case study on personalized federated learning (in the appendix) is discussed. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow. Two reviewers found the ideas novel and valuable, while two others were more critical, particularly about the assumptions, experimental scope, and relationship to existing practice.

### Strengths
1. **Timely and relevant topic**: Multi-objective optimization is important and underexplored in the learning community; the paper provides useful insights into the behavior of MGDA.
2. **Clear illustrative examples**: Toy examples throughout the paper help explain the weak optimality issue and the motivation for refined partitioning.
3. **Novel problem setting**: The analysis of MOO with variable dependency structures is rarely studied; considering parameter structure is an interesting direction.
4. **Non-trivial partitioning rules**: The three merging rules (dense block, loop) are a reasonable first step toward exploiting variable structure.
5. **Weakly Pareto analysis**: The paper highlights a concrete failure mode of vanilla MGDA and proposes a targeted remedy.
6. **Real-world relevance**: The personalized federated learning case study demonstrates a scenario where avoiding weak Pareto points is beneficial.
7. **Well-written**: The paper is organized and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
1. **Strong assumption of known variable structure**: The proposed method assumes that all variable relations among objectives are completely known, which is a much stronger requirement than the black-box first-order oracle used by MGDA. This key difference is not adequately discussed or justified.
2. **Limited experimental scope**: All experiments are on toy/synthetic problems (one bi-objective non-convex, one random 5×5 dependency matrix) and the personalized FL example is a prototype without actual experiments. Comprehensive evaluations on real-world applications (e.g., deep multi-task learning, vision, language) are missing, as expected for a methodology paper. One reviewer noted that algorithms that work on toys may need substantial modification to work on real datasets.
3. **Novelty concerns**: A reviewer pointed out that separately optimizing objective-specific variables and then handling the remaining shared variables with MGDA is already standard practice (e.g., Sener & Koltun NeurIPS 2018). The examples in the paper all follow this pattern, and it is unclear when RP‑MGDA would lead to a different grouping. Two other reviewers considered the problem setting novel, so there is disagreement on this point.
4. **Lack of theoretical depth**: The paper is mostly empirical; the theoretical result (Theorem 2) states only that there exists a convex problem where RP‑MGDA achieves Pareto optimality while MGDA does not, which does not establish general superiority and was judged “odd” or “not very meaningful.”
5. **Insufficient discussion of non-convex cases**: Most examples assume convex objectives; the single non-convex example is still synthetic. Whether the advantage persists for typical non-convex neural network losses remains unclear.
6. **Unclear intuition and design principles for partitioning rules**: Beyond specific showcases, it is not explained why the proposed rules help escape weak Pareto points, nor is there a general design principle for constructing partitions.
7. **Missing literature discussion**: Several relevant MGDA variants and applications are not cited (e.g., Momma et al. ICML 2022, federated MOO arXiv:2310.09866, and many multi-task learning references such as Sener & Koltun, PCGrad, CAGrad, Impartial MTL, Bargaining Game, etc.).
8. **Claims about applicability to other methods**: The paper states that refined partitioning could be applied to PCGrad, CAGrad, etc., but provides no justification or analysis.
9. **Inappropriate illustrative example**: The example in Equation 8 is criticized because once the variable structure is known, it trivially reduces to two independent single-objective problems, which does not meaningfully demonstrate the need for RP‑MGDA.
10. **Citation style issues**: The paper has instances where \citet and \citep are used improperly, which should be corrected.
