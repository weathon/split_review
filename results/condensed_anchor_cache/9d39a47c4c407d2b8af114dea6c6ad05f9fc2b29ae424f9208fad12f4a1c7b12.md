## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a new ML4Sci task paradigm – “PDE Interpretation” – that selects physical hypotheses (laws) aligning with observed data, rather than discovering PDEs purely via symbolic regression. The approach is formulated as a mixed-integer programming (MIP) bi-level optimization (outer layer selects hypotheses, inner layer recovers expressions) and solved using a Monte Carlo Tree Search-inspired Hypothesis Selection Tree Search (HSTS) and Sequential Threshold Optimization (STOP) algorithm. Two new datasets in fluid mechanics (Newtonian and non-Newtonian) and laser fusion are designed to test the framework. Experiments show PhysPDE outperforms baselines (including PDE-FIND and GPT-based pipelines) in recall, precision, and computational efficiency.

### Strengths
- **Novel, physically motivated task.** The paper reconceptualizes PDE discovery as a physically guided interpretation/hypothesis selection problem, addressing the gap between mathematical expressions and physical meaning. This is an original contribution to ML4Sci and a promising direction for making PDE learning interpretable. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Clear articulation of the approach.** The mixed-integer programming formulation and two-layer optimization (hypothesis selection + expression recovery) are explained with adequate rigor. (Reviewer 2)
- **New, domain-significant datasets.** The fluid mechanics (Newtonian and non-Newtonian) and laser fusion datasets are representative of real physical laws and provide valuable benchmarks for the community. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Robust experimental design.** The evaluation uses cross-validation, varying boundary conditions, and multiple baselines, and the method demonstrates better recall, precision, and efficiency than existing approaches. (Reviewer 2)
- **Well-motivated and timely direction.** The motivation for incorporating prior physical knowledge into PDE discovery is well described, and the idea of constructing PDEs under the current physical framework (rather than pure symbolic regression) is interesting and useful. (Reviewers 1,4)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient detail on the method and key figures.** The algorithm needs more explanation (e.g., with a concrete example). Figure 2’s caption does not clarify the difference between “learned PDE” and “discovered new expression”; Figure 3’s caption does not explain whether the shown PDE formulations are obtained from the algorithm or from domain knowledge. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing comparisons with recent interpretability-focused PDE discovery approaches.** The paper does not compare against methods that incorporate physical biases (e.g., Champion et al. 2019; Chen, Liu, Sun et al. 2021) or sparse regression with physically-informed basis functions. It also does not discuss how explicit hypothesis selection in PhysPDE provides advantages over SINDy-like approaches where physical knowledge is implicitly included via basis function selection. (Reviewer 2)
- **Scalability and computational complexity unclear.** The framework’s performance on high-dimensional real-world systems (e.g., climate modeling, biomedical engineering) is not discussed. The hypothesis space can grow exponentially, and the MCTS/STOP methods may struggle. No analysis of computational complexity is provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Robustness to real-world noise not adequately tested.** Experiments use synthetically generated data with controlled Gaussian noise. Real-world data may have systematic errors, non-Gaussian noise, or missing points. The reliance on a structured hypothesis decision forest could lead to inaccuracies if the true physics is not fully represented. (Reviewer 2)
- **Hypothesis decision forest construction is under-explained.** The criteria for selecting hypotheses and organizing them hierarchically are not detailed; it is unclear whether the forest must be manually created for each new domain (e.g., biomechanics). No guidelines or automation/semi-supervised methods are shared. (Reviewer 2)
- **Decision process when multiple hypotheses fit similarly well is not addressed.** It is unclear if PhysPDE allows probabilistic ranking or ensemble approaches to indicate uncertainty, or if it is a hard-selection process. (Reviewer 2)
- **Interpretability evaluation metrics are limited.** Beyond recall, precision, and MSR, there is no explicit metric for measuring physical interpretability or alignment; it remains unclear whether domain expert opinion is still required. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Reproducibility concerns (originally).** The datasets and code were not shared during the review process, limiting reproducibility and impact. (Reviewer 3; note: this was partially addressed during the discussion period, and the score was increased, but the initial lack of access remains a concern.)
- **Clarity on how physical conditions map to PDE structure.** For readers (especially ML researchers less familiar with physics), it is unclear how decisions like “is_compressible=yes” or “is_newtonian=false” directly affect the PDE system. Figure 3 shows PDE formulations under different conditions but does not explain the connection between the conditions and the appearance of PDE terms. (Reviewer 4)
- **Use of LLMs (GPT-4o) for hypothesis extraction is not elaborated.** The paper should explain why LLMs are specifically appropriate for extracting physics hypotheses from data and how they are integrated into the pipeline. (Reviewer 3)
- **Comparison with classical empirical law discovery (e.g., BACON) is missing.** The paper does not mention how PhysPDE relates to or improves upon older symbolic discovery approaches. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minority reviewer perspective:** One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 8) considered the weaknesses to be primarily clarity issues that are critical for understanding but not fundamental flaws, while other reviewers (scores 6) raised additional substantial concerns about scalability, noise robustness, missing comparisons, and construction of the hypothesis forest.
