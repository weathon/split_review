## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends explainable k-means to kernel k-means, proposing algorithms (Kernel IMM, Kernel ExKMC, Kernel Expand) that build decision trees with interval cuts to approximate kernel k-means clusters. It introduces surrogate feature maps to retain interpretability in the original space despite non-linear kernel mappings, and provides theoretical upper bounds on the price of explainability: O(k²) for additive kernels, O(dk²) for interpretable Taylor kernels, and O(Cdk²) for distance-based product kernels. Experiments on five datasets demonstrate the trade-off. Two reviewers rated 8 (positive), two rated 6 (more critical).

### Strengths
- First work to address explainable kernel clustering, a natural generalization of the well-studied explainable k-means problem (R1, R3, R4).
- Well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with concise structure, helpful figures (especially Figure 2), and detailed appendix (R2, R3, R4).
- The relaxation of explainability to interval/complement-of-interval cuts is motivated by Theorems 1 and 2, which prove that axis-aligned splits in the transformed feature space do not correspond to axis-aligned splits in the original space for the Gaussian kernel (R2).
- Novel approximations to kernel functions yield interpretable feature maps, enabling adaptation of existing explainable k-means algorithms (R1).
- Theoretical upper bounds for several popular kernels represent a significant contribution to the theory (R2, R4). A reviewer who checked the proofs found them correct (R2).
- Experimental results on five datasets show practical performance (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and scope**: One reviewer questions the practical need for interpretable kernel k-means, as kernel k-means is less widely used than k-means, spectral clustering, or DBSCAN (R1). In contrast, another reviewer strongly motivates the problem (R4). The paper focuses only on kernel k‑means without discussing implications for k‑median or k‑center objectives; a broader discussion would strengthen the work (R2).
- **Two‑step approach and potential suboptimality**: The algorithms first compute kernel k‑means, then fit a tree on the resulting clusters. Joint optimization of clustering objective and tree parameters is not addressed, and the paper does not discuss drawbacks of fitting a tree to a suboptimal clustering solution (R1).
- **Inadequate comparison to supervised tree methods**: The paper dismisses fitting a supervised decision tree (e.g., CART) on cluster labels, citing only a rare toy example from prior work. No empirical evidence supports this dismissal. One reviewer asks for a comparison of the price of explainability with training a CART tree on the cluster assignments (R1). Another reviewer asks whether the improvement in clustering cost stems from the generalized interval-cut structure rather than better clustering, and suggests converting interval trees back to regular threshold trees for comparison with expanded IMM at the same number of leaves (R4).
- **Theoretical bounds and practical utility**: The bounds lose a factor of d (e.g., O(dk²) for interpretable Taylor kernels), and d may be very large in practice. One reviewer questions the practical value of asymptotic bounds like O(dk²) (e.g., on MNIST: 784 × 100 times worse), noting experiments show trees within a constant factor; the paper should discuss practical implications and the relationship of bounds to observed performance (R1). Another reviewer notes the O(k²) bound generalizes Dasgupta et al. (2020) baseline, and later work improved to Õ(k); asks whether that improvement was tried and whether it could help (R2).
- **Missing lower bound discussion**: The paper provides upper bounds but no lower bounds, leaving tightness unaddressed (R2).
- **Limited experimental analysis**: Only five datasets; the interpretability of resulting trees (number of leaves, depth, rule complexity) is not analyzed. Kernel ExKMC and Kernel Expand achieve good accuracy relative to kernel k‑means, but the cost in interpretability (e.g., tree size) is not quantified. A case study showing the actual tree structure on one dataset would be valuable (R3). One reviewer asks whether codes will be made public and for an explanation of the rand index metric (R2).
- **Missing related work**: The paper omits references on the impossibility of depth reduction in explainable clustering (R2) and other interpretable clustering methods not based on k‑means (e.g., Carrizosa et al. 2023, Lawless & Günlük 2023) (R3).
- **Minor issues**: The introduction could be shortened to free space for experiments (R3). Reference to Appendix C appears before Appendix B (R3). There is a typo ("of of") in the explainable k‑means paragraph (R3). Contributions could be listed for conciseness (R3).
