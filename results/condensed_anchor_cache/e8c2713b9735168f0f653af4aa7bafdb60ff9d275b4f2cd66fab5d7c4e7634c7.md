## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a cross-modal collaborative knowledge transfer method to enhance video understanding (focused on action recognition, though the title/abstract do not explicitly state that task). It uses a large VLM to improve spatial and semantic understanding of video models through three loss components: video adaptation (classification), attentive spatial knowledge transfer (L1 alignment between video tokens and image tokens from the VLM’s frozen image encoder with a gating mechanism), and contrastive textual knowledge transfer (using a decompose-expand prompting method to generate fine-grained descriptions). Experiments are conducted on several video datasets with multiple backbones. Reviewers find the writing and motivation strong but have mixed opinions on novelty, significance of improvements, experimental rigor, and presentation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written, easy to follow, and Figure 2 effectively captures the overall pipeline. (R1, R2)
- The motivation to leverage large VLMs to enhance spatial understanding in video models is strong and meaningful. (R1, R2, R4)
- The proposed attentive spatial knowledge transfer (distilling spatial knowledge from the VLM’s image encoder) and contrastive textual knowledge transfer (fine-grained text-video alignment) are reasonable. (R3)
- The gating mechanism to focus distillation on action-relevant regions and the decompose-expand prompting method to generate diverse, fine-grained descriptions are interesting design choices. (R3)
- The method yields consistent improvements across multiple baselines and datasets (R3), though some reviewers note the gains are marginal (R4) and not state-of-the-art compared to recent works (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution unclear.** The classification and contrastive losses are common in video pretraining and classification; L1 alignment between image and video tokens is not novel. The overall contribution is incremental. (R1, R4)
- **Insufficient evidence for core motivation.** The claim that video models lack spatial cues is supported by only one heatmap (Fig. 1c), which is insufficient. It remains unclear how the proposed method specifically addresses this issue and what the underlying principle is. (R2)
- **Performance not state‑of‑the‑art.** The reported Top‑1 accuracy (e.g., 86.9%) is lower than existing methods (88.5% [1], 89.7% [2]), so claiming SOTA is imprecise. Top‑5 results are missing. (R2)
- **Marginal improvements and sensitive ablation.** Gains over baselines are small (e.g., +0.24% from the gate mechanism in Table 3). The negative text sampling ablation shows a non‑monotonic trend (drop then rise) with no clear explanation; the authors attribute this to “easy negatives” but do not provide evidence or measure easy negatives. Variance of results is not reported, making it unclear if gains are statistically significant. (R1, R3)
- **Weak analysis of key components.**  
  - The cross‑attention variant (Eq. 6‑8) underperforms; the explanation (L258‑260) is unsound and lacks in‑depth discussion. (R1)  
  - For the gate mechanism, the improvement is only +0.24%; per‑class analysis and attention map comparisons are needed to verify the hypothesis. (R3)  
  - Decompose‑expand prompting: how is the accuracy of generated descriptions ensured? Errors could mislead the contrastive loss. The number of descriptions per category and the distribution of positive text scores (some too low) are not analyzed. (R2, R3)
- **Missing experiments and comparisons.**  
  - Why not experiment on the mainstream action recognition dataset Something‑Something V2 (SSV2)? (R4)  
  - Training cost (e.g., time, memory) is not analyzed. (R2)
- **Implementation details unclear or inconsistent.**  
  - The learning rate reported in the implementation details differs from the appendix. (R4)  
  - How are the weight coefficients for the three loss functions obtained? (R4)  
  - The negative text sampling experiment is limited to k=1200; higher k values would be informative. (R3)
- **Writing and formatting issues.**  
  - The title/abstract do not specify that the task is action recognition. (R4)  
  - Line 72 should be “(e.g., CLIP)” not “e.g., CLIP”. (R4)  
  - Equations (6) and (7) are missing symbols; bold/italic formatting is inconsistent. (R4)  
  - Line 238 missing punctuation; Line 313 “Eq. equation 2” is poor. (R4)  
  - Title capitalization inconsistent. (R4)

*Note:* One reviewer (R3) found no obvious weaknesses in the paper, while the other three reviewers identified multiple significant issues as listed above. This contrast highlights the divergence in assessment of the paper’s soundness and contribution.
