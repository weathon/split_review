## Merged Review

### Summary
This work extends prior theoretical results (Braun et al., 2022) on exact learning dynamics in two-layer linear networks to λ-balanced initializations, where λ is a tunable parameter controlling the relative scale of weights across layers. The authors derive exact solutions (Theorem 4.3) for the Neural Tangent Kernel, representation similarity, gradient flow, and loss, showing that the dynamics span a continuum from rich (λ→0) to lazy (λ→±∞) regimes (Theorem C.4). The paper also discusses implications for neuroscience, continual learning, reversal learning, and transfer learning.

### Strengths
- Novel and timely idea: introducing a tunable balance parameter λ that allows systematic exploration of the full spectrum from rich to lazy learning regimes.
- Strong theoretical foundation: exact solutions for NTK, representations, and loss dynamics (Theorem 4.3, Theorem C.4) built on and extending the framework of Braun et al. (2022). Solid derivations backed by simulation results.
- Insightful experiments on semantic task examples demonstrating how λ interpolates between learning regimes, with implications for neuroscience and machine learning.
- The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with a comprehensive literature review.
- The λ-balanced framework covers architecture shapes, initialization schemes, and structural properties relevant to a broad audience (neuroscience, continual learning, transfer learning).

### Weaknesses
- Limited technical novelty: the method largely follows Braun et al. (2022); the extension to λ-balanced initializations is achieved under a stricter assumption (whitened inputs), which reduces applicability. Reviewer 3, however, found the theoretical extension strong and timely, noting that assumptions are already more relaxed than prior work.
- Heavy reliance on strong assumptions (task-aligned initialization, whitened inputs, linear two-layer network, finite width where Nh > min(Ni, No)). Authors should provide at least empirical evidence that main results hold qualitatively when these assumptions are relaxed (e.g., on benchmark datasets without whitening). This includes explicitly showing performance gains for the claimed transfer learning advantage (D3) – it would be better to show a performance increase rather than just mentioning the possibility.
- Application demonstrations are weak and only briefly discussed in the main text. Reviewer 2 suggests moving some application results (e.g., transfer learning) to the main text to strengthen the paper; Reviewer 1 notes that the demonstration of applications is the only weakness.
- Insufficient intuitive explanation of how λ interpolates between regimes. The mechanism by which λ→∞ leads to a task-agnostic identity representation is not clearly explained beyond Theorem 5.1 (convergence to sigmoidal vs. exponential transition functions). The connection between mathematical limits and resulting representational properties needs elaboration.
- The interaction of relative scale (λ) and absolute scale of weights is not decoupled. For λ→∞, the weight norm becomes large, and large initialization is known to cause lazy learning. Without control experiments using random unbalanced initialization but matched W1 and W2 norms, it is unclear whether observed lazy behavior is due to the balancing constraint or the large weight magnitude. A reviewer asks for more discussion on how these two factors interact.
- Several specific issues raised by reviewers that overlap with missing experiments, comparisons, or errors:
  - The definition of over-parameterization (Nh > min(Ni, No)) may differ from the standard meaning (capacity vs. parameters needed for zero training loss). Clarification needed.
  - Typo in Fig. 2b caption: states λ=0.001 but figure shows λ=0.
  - Fig. 4b: simulation labels for w1w1 and w2w2 appear reversed compared to theory.
  - Fig. 5: role of the ratio Nh/Ni or Nh/No is not addressed.
  - Previous work on lazy/rich regimes in infinite-width networks often involves N→∞ and scaling of initialization; here there is no large-N assumption but the NTK becomes fixed in the lazy regime. Is this specific to linear networks? How do results scale to larger or infinite-dimensional networks?
  - Reviewer 3 did not have bandwidth to verify proofs in the appendix, affecting confidence.
- Reviewer questions that overlap with the above criticisms (included as weaknesses):
  - Generalization to more complex tasks requested.
  - How λ interacts with other knobs (e.g., absolute scale, width)?
  - Empirical demonstration needed for whitening assumption.
  - More intuition on λ→∞ leading to identity representation.
  - Clarification on the role of width ratios in Fig. 5.

A note on reviewer disagreement: one reviewer (score 8) rated the work as novel, timely, and theoretically strong, while two other reviewers (score 6) stressed the limited technical novelty, strict assumptions, and weak application demonstration. This contrast is reflected above where both perspectives are included.
