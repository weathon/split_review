## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a method for multi-source few-shot domain adaptation (MFDA) using vision-language models. It introduces a prompt structure with domain-specific and shared context prompts, plus a “domain-aware mixup” technique intended to improve domain sensitivity. Reviewers acknowledged that MFDA is a challenging and important problem and that the method shows empirical gains on DomainNet and OfficeHome. However, the majority of reviewers found the paper poorly written, the novelty limited, the claims unsubstantiated, and the experimental presentation confusing. One reviewer gave a score of 6 and considered the domain-aware mixup novel, while others (scores 3, 3, and 1) raised major concerns about novelty, clarity, and scientific rigor. There was broad agreement that the paper needs substantial rewriting, clearer problem setting, stronger justification of claims, and additional experiments.

### Strengths

*   The paper addresses an important and challenging problem: multi-source few-shot domain adaptation (MFDA). (All reviewers)
*   The proposed “domain-aware mixup” is a novel approach to handle domain adaptation in the few-shot setting (per one reviewer, score 6).
*   Prompting techniques are utilized to better learn domain knowledge via a foundation model. (Reviewer 4)
*   Empirical validation was conducted on standard benchmark datasets (DomainNet, OfficeHome). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
*   Superior performance is reported compared to CLIP and CoOp. (Reviewer 4)
*   The author conducted adequate evaluation to support the proposed approach. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

*   **Limited Novelty:** The method is very similar to DAPL (an unreviewed work) extended to multi-source scenarios; the only difference is adding a [DOM] token. (Reviewer 4, high confidence). Prepending/learning domain- or instance-specific prompt tokens to handle distribution shifts is not a new idea; prior works condition prompt vectors on image inputs and partition prompts into domain-specific and domain-agnostic components (e.g., [4]). (Reviewer 2). The paper lacks a clear demarcation of differences from existing works in terms of mathematical formulations and mechanisms leading to performance gains. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Insufficient Related Work Review:** Previous works on domain adaptation with prompt learning from 2022–2023 are missed, and the differences between the proposed method and prior works are not highlighted. (Reviewer 2). The paper does not reference or discuss the relevant literature on feature-space mixup. (Reviewer 4)
*   **Unclear Problem Setting and Motivation:**
    *   The first sentence of the abstract (“large vision-language models … strong performance in MFDA”) is incorrect; there is no such reference applying large VL models to MFDA. (Reviewer 4, high confidence)
    *   The MFDA problem setting is not clearly explained at the beginning, and the description in the Method section is confusing and inconsistent with the original MFDA paper (Yue et al., 2021a). For example, the text mentions a target domain with “sparse labels” but then states the target domain is unlabeled; the use of unlabeled source data is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
    *   The paper does not clearly delineate the inherent challenges of the few-shot setting, such as how limited labels influence training, modeling multi-source domains, or transferring knowledge to the target domain. (Reviewer 2)
*   **Unsupported Claims and Weak Justification:**
    *   Claim: “Using learnable prompts increases the risk of overfitting on training samples, which reduces the ability of domain prompt models to extract common semantic features.” This claim is not justified and is likely wrong; overfitting risk depends on prompt complexity relative to data, not simply on having multiple prompts. (Reviewer 1)
    *   Claim: “Large-scale pre-trained vision-language models lack domain awareness during training, which hinders the acquisition of domain-specific tendencies.” The general pre-training process aims to learn generalizable features; the paper does not clarify why this is a specific problem for the proposed method. (Reviewer 1)
    *   Claim: “These models often misinterpret source domain-specific semantic features as belonging to the target domain.” Standard prompt tuning is designed to align the model with the target domain; the paper does not justify why it is insufficient or how the proposed method mitigates this misinterpretation. (Reviewer 1)
    *   Claims about the complexity of extracting domain information in multi-domain settings and the overfitting risk of learnable prompts are neither supported by references nor empirical results. (Reviewer 2)
    *   The motivation for the domain-aware mixup is confusing; it is unclear how it enforces learning of domain-specific knowledge. (Reviewer 4)
*   **Poor Writing, Inconsistent Notation, and Confusing Presentation:**
    *   The introduction is difficult to follow, with many undefined terms introduced abruptly (e.g., context prompts, domain vs. semantics, context variables, cross-domain mixed feature information). (Reviewer 2)
    *   The term “prompt” is used ambiguously (single vector, set of vectors?) and “mixed-up feature” is unclear. (Reviewer 1)
    *   There is significant text overlapping with DAPL (only with rewording), which should be strictly prohibited. (Reviewer 4)
    *   Figure 1 is not referred to in the text. (Reviewer 4)
    *   Related work does not include sentences distinguishing prior works from the proposed method. (Reviewer 4)
    *   Notation inconsistencies: [v] is used for context prompts in equations but in Figure 3 [v] represents domain features; letters “f_t” and “f_s” are used inconsistently for target and source features. (Reviewer 1)
    *   Subscript “d” in Eq. 4 and superscript “d” in Eq. 5 are not explained; “d*k categories” after Eq. 5 is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
    *   The letter “t” is used to refer to both text and target domain, causing confusion. (Reviewer 4)
    *   The feature mix-up formulation in Figure 3 differs from Eq. 8 (one uses γ, another uses λ; weighting differs). (Reviewer 4)
    *   In Eq. 10, “D^s” and “D^u” are never defined; the equation is copied from DAPL and is inconsistent with the problem setting. (Reviewer 4)
    *   Loss function notations in Eq. 7, 9, and 10 are inconsistent. (Reviewer 4)
    *   Table 5 in text should be Figure 5 (typo). (Reviewer 4)
*   **Unclear Optimization and Training Objectives:**
    *   It is unclear what the training objectives of [d] and [v] are, and which prompt is optimized to minimize Eq. 7. The optimization process and gradient calculation are not clearly defined. (Reviewer 1)
    *   Eq. 6 is confusing: the outer summation over d ∈ {s, u} computes similarity between target domain prompt and source image features, and it is unclear how learning on unlabeled target data is realized. (Reviewer 4)
    *   The role of “inter-source domain mixup” and why maintaining it harms target domain representation learning is not motivated. (Reviewer 4)
*   **Insufficient Ablation and Sensitivity Analysis:**
    *   No ablation studies analyzing the effects of different prompt types (handcrafted vs. learned), prompt initialization, or number of prompts per domain. (Reviewer 3)
    *   No sensitivity analysis on the number of prompts per domain. (Reviewer 3)
    *   The paper does not test on more downstream tasks to evaluate broader applicability. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Missing Implementation Details:**
    *   The exact architecture of the vision-language model, pre-training dataset, and fine-tuning parameters are not specified, making reproduction difficult. (Reviewer 3)
    *   The construction of the prompt bank (“diverse prompts are achieved by constructing a prompt bank that contains various configurations specific to each domain”) is not explained. (Reviewer 4)
*   **Confusing Experimental Presentation:**
    *   Terms like “single best” and “source combined” are unclear (setting or method?). The rows referring to MFDA in Tables 1 and 2 are not labeled. “Large model” is listed as a setting rather than a model architecture. (Reviewer 4)
    *   Figures 6 and 7 are hard to read; the numerical values should be reported in a table. (Reviewer 4)
*   **Missing Information in Equations:**
    *   In Eq. 1, functions g() and f() are not defined (presumably text and image encoders, but not stated). (Reviewer 4)
    *   The relationship between f^i\_mixup and f\_u is unclear; neither is used in subsequent text. (Reviewer 4)
    *   In Eq. 9, the loss requires source data, but Eq. 10 only passes D^u (target domain) to L\_u. (Reviewer 4)
