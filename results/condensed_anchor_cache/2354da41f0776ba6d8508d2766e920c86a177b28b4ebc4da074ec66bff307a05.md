## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes sample-level differential privacy (DP) accounting in federated learning (FL) with multiple local steps and secure aggregation (SecAgg). The authors first motivate the problem by showing that a naive privacy analysis leads to large noise per communication round (Section 3.2). They then introduce the concept of sum-dominating mechanisms (Definition 4.2) and prove that the privacy leakage of one communication round is bounded by the leakage of a composition of local steps (Theorem 4.7). Experiments on several ML tasks in a cross-silo setting (up to ~50 clients) show that multiple local steps improve utility over a single local step under fixed communication.

### Strengths
- The research question is relevant and well-motivated (Section 3.2), as communication is a frequent bottleneck in FL.
- The paper is well-written and clearly explains key concepts.
- Defines a new notion of sum-dominating mechanism for DP methods that are compatible with secure aggregation.
- The privacy budget is thoroughly discussed and analyzed.
- Exploring DP machine learning without a trusted central party is a strong, realistic model with high potential impact.

### Weaknesses
- **Disagreement on novelty**: Reviewer 1 considered the sample-level DP analysis novel and clear; reviewers 2 and 3 found the theoretical contribution trivial. Specifically, Lemma 4.6 (sum of DP sums is DP) appears to be a simple consequence of post-processing, and Lemmas 4.3, 4.5, 4.6 and Theorem 4.7 are described as direct instantiations of existing theory that could be derived without difficulty.
- **Missing comparison with naive accounting**: Theorem 4.7 is not compared to the naive approach from Section 3.2, so the claimed benefit is not directly validated.
- **Experimental shortcomings**:
  - The experiments conflate two variables (number of seen examples and privacy accounting) and do not isolate the benefit of Theorem 4.7. Repeating experiments with the naive privacy accounting (from Section 3.2) is needed.
  - The empirical results only show that multiple local steps outperform a single step, which is already expected from prior FL studies and does not provide new research insights.
  - An experimental privacy audit to measure actual privacy spent and support the theoretical analysis is missing.
- **Lacks broader comparisons**: To demonstrate the full privacy potential of FL+SecAgg, the paper should compare to techniques based on shuffling (e.g., Ghazi et al., ICML 2021) or FTRL (e.g., McMahan et al., 2024; Kairouz et al., ICML 2021) in terms of communication, computation, and accuracy.
- **Practical significance unclear**: The difference between client-level and sample-level DP guarantees (e.g., against membership inference and reconstruction attacks) is hard to judge.
- **Technical questions that also serve as criticisms**:
  - Why does the sum-dominating mechanism allow joint DP noise via SecAgg when the mechanism output must be discrete? (Reviewer 3)
  - What is the value of the communication budget used in the experiments? Can total communication cost under a fixed privacy budget be provided? (Reviewer 3)
  - Can the authors explain why Theorem 3.2 of Malekmohammadi et al. (2024) does not show the benefits of more local steps? (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor presentation issues**: Page 3 has a confusing parenthetical remark; page 10 has a typo (“different states have very number of samples” → “different states have very *different* number”).
