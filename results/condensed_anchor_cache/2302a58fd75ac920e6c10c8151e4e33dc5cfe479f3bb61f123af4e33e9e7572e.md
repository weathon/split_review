## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a distributionally robust optimization (DRO) framework for learning Signed Distance Functions (SDF) from sparse and noisy 3D point clouds without ground-truth supervision. Building on Neural-Pull, it introduces two variants (SDRO and WDRO) that adversarially sample query points from a worst-case distribution using Wasserstein distance, with dual reformulation and entropic regularization. Experiments on synthetic and real datasets show reconstruction improvements over baselines. However, multiple reviewers raise serious concerns about the method’s extreme similarity to the prior work Ouasfi & Boukhayma (ICML 2024) [1], including copied text and figures, which raises ethical issues. Additionally, the experimental validation is considered incomplete (missing key baselines, efficiency analysis, varying point densities, and comparisons to [1] itself), and the theoretical motivation for DRO in an optimization (not learning) context is unclear.

### Strengths
- The idea of integrating distributionally robust optimization into neural implicit surface reconstruction is considered “exciting” and “new” (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). It explores a different direction from typical smoothness priors (Reviewer 5).
- The empirical results on sparse and noisy point clouds are “impressive” (Reviewer 1) and the method is benchmarked on several public datasets (ShapeNet, D-FAUST, etc.) and real-world data (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5).
- The two DRO variants include a dual reformulation and entropic regularization, which Reviewer 3 considers “novel theoretical contributions.”
- The approach works effectively on sparse, noisy, and unoriented point clouds without requiring oriented normals (Reviewer 5).
- The paper includes an analysis of runtime for the two variants (Reviewer 1) and qualitative results that show recovery of fine details and removal of outliers (Reviewer 1).
- The method is shown to be effective on the selected datasets, with stable and efficient optimization (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Extreme similarity to prior work [1] (Ouasfi & Boukhayma, ICML 2024).** Multiple reviewers (Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 5) identify extensive textual and formulaic overlap. Specific evidence includes:
   - The abstract sentence “Within the realm of 3D shape representation, Neural Signed Distance Functions (SDF) have demonstrated remarkable potential in faithfully encoding intricate shape geometry” is copied verbatim from [1] (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
   - Figure 1 is identical to that in [1] with only color changes (Reviewer 4).
   - Many sentences in Section 2 are copied from [1] (Reviewer 4).
   - Equations (1)–(4) are the same as in [1], only symbols changed (Reviewer 4).
   - Algorithm 1 text, Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.5, and figure/table captions are identical to [1] (Reviewer 4).
   - Reviewer 5 expresses disappointment and suspects possible LLM use due to unacknowledged similarities.
   - Reviewer 2 and 3 require a careful explanation of differences; Reviewer 3 will change rating if no explanation is provided.
   
   *Minority contrast: Reviewer 1 does not mention this similarity.*

2. **Incomplete validation and missing baselines.**
   - Key recent methods are not compared: StEik, Neural-Singular-Hessian (Reviewer 2), OPS (Ma et al. CVPR 2022) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4), CAP-UDF (Reviewer 4), DiffCD (ECCV 2024) (Reviewer 3), and the closely related method [1] itself (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
   - Results for DIGS and OG-INR are included but are not state-of-the-art (Reviewer 2).
   - Some baseline results in Table 1 differ from those reported in [1], raising doubts about experimental reliability (Reviewer 2).
   - The paper lacks efficiency comparisons with other baselines (running time and GPU memory) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
   - Performance on dense point clouds is worse than baselines, but the density scale is not specified, making it hard to assess effectiveness across varying sparsities (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 requests experiments with 1024, 4096, 8192, 16384 points.
   - The method is only tested on 1024-point clouds; generalizability to other sizes is unclear (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
   - No ablation on the noise level or visualization of noisy inputs; Reviewer 3 suggests exploring very large noise.
   - No comparison to UDF-based methods for open boundaries (Reviewer 4).
   - The method is only applied to Neural-Pull; it is unclear if the DRO sampling strategy generalizes to other SDF methods like SIREN, DIGS, StEik (Reviewer 2).

3. **Unclear theoretical motivation.**
   - It is not clear why simulating a worst-case sampling scenario benefits an *optimization* (not learning) problem (Reviewer 1). The link to prior work is not analytically justified.
   - The improvement over Neural-Pull in the noise-free setting is puzzling (Reviewer 1).
   - The geometric interpretation of the DRO approach is missing (Reviewer 1).
   - The formulas in Eqs. (6)–(10) are hard to follow and their motivation for surface reconstruction is unclear (Reviewer 4).
   - The dependency on the hyperparameter λ (ball radius) and its sensitivity is noted, with performance instability shown in Figures 9 and 10 (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 5 questions how the radius is defined to avoid either inaccurate SDF or negligible effect.

4. **Presentation issues.**
   - Qualitative figures (Figs. 2, 3, 5, 6) are too small and low-resolution; thumbnails make details unreadable (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
   - Introduction is long and mixed with related work; should be restructured (Reviewer 3).
   - Math font issues: subset E of ℝ³ and Q in Eq. (1) look odd (Reviewer 3).
   - Overloaded use of λ (Reviewer 1).
   - Many typos: l.077, l.361 (Reviewer 1); l.167 uses “Fig” vs “Figure”, extra “the” in l.77, missing figure number in l.475–476 (Reviewer 5).
   - Table 5 does not explain which metric is being compared (Reviewer 2).

5. **Specific method concerns.**
   - Neural-Pull as a foundation: nearest neighbor search fails for very sparse clouds, and planar surfaces are problematic (Reviewer 3). The paper shows recovery of planar surfaces in Fig. 4, but robustness to non-uniform sampling or missing areas is not evaluated (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5).
   - The method’s sensitivity to input point distribution: if points are non-uniformly sampled, the reconstruction may degrade (Reviewer 3).
   - The improvement in noise-free setting is not explained (Reviewer 1).
   - Post-processing: Reviewer 1 asks if the method uses any post-processing to avoid off-surface elements (qualitatively absent in results).

6. **Missing discussions in related work.**
   - Recent developments in DRO are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
   - The literature review is too short and mostly mixed in the introduction (Reviewer 3).

7. **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (missing experiments, ablation requests, generalizability):
   - Reviewer 1: Visualize distributions Q and Q’/Q_{x,ρ} in a toy/2D example; ablation interpolating from Q’ to Q to verify source of improvement.
   - Reviewer 1: How does the method scale with different point counts (increase/decrease from 1024)?
   - Reviewer 3: Provide results with different noise levels (e.g., very large noise) and visualize noisy inputs.
   - Reviewer 3: Test with non-uniformly sampled points or missing areas.
   - Reviewer 5: Can the method handle partial/occluded point clouds?
   - Reviewer 4: Comparison to UDF-based methods for shapes with open boundaries.
