## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a one-for-all few-shot anomaly detection method leveraging vision-language models (CLIP). It introduces an instance-specific prompt generator that adaptively generates prompts for each instance, trained via alignment with visual space and guidance from textual normality/abnormality descriptions. A class-aware memory bank is used to address mismatch in the one-for-all paradigm. The task is novel and practical, addressing multi-class few-shot anomaly detection. Experimental results on MVTec and VisA show superior performance.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a new and practical task: multi-class few-shot anomaly detection under the one-for-all paradigm, meeting industrial needs.
- The proposed method is innovative, combining prompt tuning, contrastive learning, and a class-aware memory bank.
- The methodology appears sound and introduces several novel steps (instance-specific prompt generator, synthetic visual guidance, class-aware memory bank).
- Experimental results show consistent improvement over baselines on MVTec and VisA, achieving highest performance in the one-for-all task.
- The method is practical and has strong potential for real-world applications.
- The paper tests numerous outstanding anomaly detection algorithms on the new task, providing comprehensive comparisons.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation issues:** The introduction is too broad and not concise; the core insights are hard to extract. Figure 1 does not fully illustrate the methodology, especially the "Guidance of Prompt Learning" section and the visual guidance process described in Section 3.2.1. The representation of "P" is omitted from Figure 1.
- **Grammatical errors:** Abstract: "learn" should be "learns" (third person singular). Line 203: second 'S' error (unspecified). Line 236: "an the embedding" should be "and the embedding".
- **Ablation study is insufficient:** The method contains too many modules, and ablation experiments are not thorough enough to demonstrate individual contributions. Specifically:
  - The roles of Projection Network and Cross-Attention in the prompt generator are not clearly shown; separate ablations are needed.
  - The two losses of Synthetic Visual Guidance for Anomalous Prompt require separate ablation.
  - The contribution of the semantic-level alignment loss is not discussed in Table 5; it should be ablated separately.
  - The effect of the Q-Former and instance-specific prompt generator is unexplored (e.g., why not use CLIP directly? Q-Former and ViT are frozen and unaligned without BLIP2 pre-training).
  - Table 4: shift from OVB to CAMV when adding M2 confounds results; an ablation isolating Prompt Generator and Class-aware Memory Bank is needed.
- **Missing comparisons:** AnomalyClip and InCTRL are representative works in the same field but not compared in experiments.
- **Inconsistent results and missing explanation:** Tables 1 and 2 show identical performance for "one-for-one" (class-wise) and "one-for-all" settings, but no interpretation is provided. Table 6 shows differing AUROC for VisA at image level, requiring verification of experimental accuracy.
- **Undefined symbols and errors:** Equation 12 has undefined loss terms \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^n_f\) and \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^a_f\). Table 5 lacks definitions and contains errors in loss expressions (e.g., "L_c" vs "L_s").
- **Method details unclear:**
  - How is the subset of F chosen for prompt learning (line 218)?
  - How do normal and anomalous object tokens \(O\) from the same MLP and same learnable token become distinct? Architecture explanation needed.
  - What are the output tokens of Q-Former and how are they processed to construct prompts?
  - Why is building a category-aware memory bank using both image patch tokens and category tokens better than prior memory banks? Is cross-category query mismatch actually likely?
  - The term "mismatch problem" of the memory bank is ambiguous and not clearly explained in Section 3.
- **Missing quantitative analysis and runtime:** The paper does not analyze computation complexity or runtime compared to base methods, nor does it provide an experiment with Gaussian noise to validate the claim that it is not needed.
- **Lack of detailed comparative analysis:** While comparisons with PromptAD and WinCLIP are mentioned, a detailed analysis of specific scenarios where these methods fail and the proposed method succeeds is missing (e.g., could be added to appendix).
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis:** The ablation sections lack theoretical intuition for why proposed modules are effective. More explanation of the core insights would strengthen the paper.
