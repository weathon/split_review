## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes I-LoRA, an iterative LoRA merging method for multi-task learning in vision-language models. It uses routing-tuning to minimize activation of LoRA matrices on general tasks and a merging mechanism (SVD-based) to combine adapters trained on different tasks, aiming to preserve original model capabilities while learning new tasks. Experiments are conducted on Atari games and general VQA tasks.

### Strengths
- The idea of iterative merging of LoRA adapters is interesting and straightforward.
- The method achieves competitive results on Atari games, with performance comparable to single-task models while maintaining reasonable general vision-language understanding (Table 1, Table 3).
- Multi-task learning for vision-language models is a significant and intriguing area of study.
- The paper is clearly written, well-structured, and provides a strong motivation for the proposed method.
- Empirical results show considerable improvement in different games compared to selected baselines.

### Weaknesses
- **Problem Formulation Ambiguity:** The paper conflates continual learning/lifelong learning (learning new tasks while maintaining old task performance, old tasks often inaccessible) with multi-task learning (learning multiple tasks simultaneously). Section 3.1 needs clearer definition.
- **Unconvincing Assumption:** The constraint to push LoRA activations to zero on general tasks assumes new tasks never benefit the model's general ability. This ignores forward transfer in lifelong learning and task conflict in multi-task learning (e.g., [1], [3] missing).
- **Inappropriate Baselines & Comparisons:** The Atari dataset is derived from a pre-trained APPO algorithm (expert dataset for distillation), making the method align with multi-task distillation. Comparing directly to RL methods like DreamerV3 and DART (trained from scratch) is questionable. The paper should present original APPO scores and clarify if VLM weights are randomly initialized or pre-trained before distillation. A multi-task baseline (training all game tasks jointly with general data over more iterations) is missing. Direct multi-task fine-tuning with LoRA should be assessed as an upper bound to evaluate SVD-based merging trade-offs.
- **Missing Baselines & Ablations:** No ablation of proposed losses (KL-divergence loss, norm loss). Values of ε1, ε2, ε3 are not specified. No ablation of task weighting in Equation 2 (balance between general and fine-tuning data, loss weights for KL and norm). Ablation studies on data distribution and weighting strategies are needed. The method is only evaluated on VLA (Atari games); applicability to general LLM/VLM multi-task/continual learning is not shown. Missing comparisons with PEFT-based methods, LoRA-merging methods, and their variations (e.g., AdaMerging, Task Arithmetic, DARE, etc. – see missing literature below). Table 4 baselines (SVD method, DARE variants) are poorly described; unclear if SVD is applied to LoRA weights or full weight matrices, or how DARE is adapted to continual learning.
- **Literature Review Gaps:** Missing relevant works on model merging: AdaMerging (Yang et al., ICLR 2024), Task Vectors with anisotropic scaling (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024), Model merging survey (Yang et al., 2024), Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023). Also missing on adapters: Adaptive Budget Allocation (Zhang et al., ICLR 2023).
- **Performance Inconsistencies:** In Table 2, routing tuning causes substantial variance: e.g., 'Breakout' improves significantly while 'Pong' drops noticeably. No explanation is provided. In Table 3, performance on most general tasks _worsens_ after Routing Tuning, contradicting the claim that "both single-task fine-tuning and Routing Tuning improve the model’s performance on general datasets." This suggests negative transfer.
- **Missing Details:** No visualization of activations (Equation 4) for target and base test sets. The "Maximize Merge" concept is unusual; why not simply retain task-specific adapters? The ratio of kept singular values is not explained. Input to the VLM (text input for game rules and COT, but visual input and action interaction with Atari environment are not detailed). The general dataset (30000 samples, Line 431) – how it is sampled and what it contains – lacks specifics.
- **Presentation Issues:** Figures and table captions are very poor/not informative. Typographical errors: "Llava" (L161, L347, L430) and errors at lines 259, 261, 433. Table 1 could use color to distinguish better-than-human or better-than-SOTA performance. Performance drops should be provided for clearer comparison in Table 3. Metrics like average ranking or normalized average score could be more intuitive for Table 1.
