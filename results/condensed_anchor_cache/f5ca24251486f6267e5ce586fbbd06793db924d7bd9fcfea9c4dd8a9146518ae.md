## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SupReMix (Supervised Contrastive Learning for Regression with Mixup), a method that creates hard contrastive pairs via mixup in embedding space for regression tasks. Anchor-inclusive mixtures (anchor + negative) serve as hard negatives; anchor-exclusive mixtures (two negatives) serve as hard positives, chosen such that the mixed regression target equals the anchor’s target. Experiments on six datasets (2D images, volumetric images, text, tabular data, time-series) show consistent improvements over the vanilla baseline. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the idea is intuitive, but raise concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, limited applicability, and clarity.

### Strengths
- **Important problem & good motivation:** Representation learning for regression is understudied; the paper clearly argues for ordinality-awareness and hardness (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Clever, intuitive formulation:** Using mixup to create harder positives/negatives without relying on data augmentation is a significant advantage, especially for domains like time series where augmentations are nontrivial (R2).
- **Consistent empirical gains:** SupReMix outperforms the vanilla baseline across all six datasets and modalities, and is superior to other contrastive baselines on most benchmarks (R1, R2, R3). (Note: R4 describes the gains as incremental and notes they are reported w.r.t. vanilla, not second best.)
- **Supportive empirical analyses:** Visualizations of logit distributions and latent spaces provide insight into the method’s behavior (R1).
- **Clear presentation (minority positive):** R4 finds the paper clearly written and organized; R1 also states it is well-ranged and easy to follow. However, other reviewers note significant readability issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with key related methods:**
  - No comparisons with mixup-based methods for regression: Vanilla Mixup, C-Mixup (NeurIPS 2022), ManifoldMix, local-Mixup, manifold intrusion (R1, R2, R4).
  - No comparisons with general hard-sample-mining contrastive methods using mixup: MoCHi, i-Mix (R1, R4).
  - No comparisons with non-contrastive regression frameworks (e.g., Gaussian Process Regression, Support Vector Regression, Moving Window Regression) (R4).
  - C-Mixup is especially relevant (selected pairs close in function value) and should be compared both empirically and methodologically (R2).
  - Training-from-scratch evaluation is missing; only self-supervised pre-train+finetune is reported (R1, R2).
- **Limited novelty:** Hard sample mining for contrastive learning has been explored since 2020; the proposed mixup strategy is similar to existing methods (MoCHi, i-Mix, C-Mixup) and uses analogous anchor/negative selection (R1). The paper does not adequately justify why contrastive learning is suitable for regression, beyond clustering intuitions (R4).
- **Unclear theoretical contribution:** The theoretical results in Section 3.4 are presented without clear practical implications; the connection to the specific loss design and empirical gains is not well-established (R3, R1 weakly).
- **Limited applicability (1D & discrete targets):**
  - The method is only applied to regression problems with 1D targets (R2, R3).
  - All tested datasets have targets that take a small set of discrete values (e.g., age with bin size 1). It is unclear whether the method works for truly continuous or high-variability targets, and whether it requires that exactly matching targets exist within a batch (R3).
  - The method does not generalize to multi-dimensional regression outputs (R2).
- **Implementation ambiguities:**
  - How is the ordering of training samples by function value implemented? Per mini-batch? (R2).
  - Are backbone networks trained from scratch or fine-tuned from pre-trained checkpoints? (R2).
- **Hyper-parameter & ablation concerns:**
  - SupReMix introduces many hyper-parameters compared to SupCon and standard mixup. No thorough ablation of mixing ratios, anchor choice (inclusive vs exclusive), or contribution of each component (R1, R4).
  - The impact of different choices is not rigorously investigated; improvement over SupCon may stem from mixup itself rather than the specific design (R4).
- **Conflicting evaluation quality:**
  - R2 states SupReMix shows superior performance across all baselines, while R4 argues the gains are only incremental and reported relative to vanilla rather than the second-best method. This disagreement is not resolved by the presented results.
- **Writing and presentation issues (majority view):**
  - Equations (1) and (3) are dense and hard to parse; notation is not clearly defined (R2, R3).
  - Section 2 (analysis of contrastive learning with permuted labels) seems out of place and its connection to the proposed method is not explicitly stated (R3).
  - Figures 1A/B are confusing and do not clearly convey the method (R2).
  - Theoretical section (3.4) lacks clear takeaway and connection to empirical results (R3).
  - Typos: “input”→“inputs” in Figure 1 caption and Section 3; Table 9/10 missing dataset labels (R3).
  - Linear probing used for only 4/6 datasets with no explanation (R3).
- **Minority positive on presentation:** R4 rates presentation as “excellent” (score 4), contrasting with the majority criticism.
