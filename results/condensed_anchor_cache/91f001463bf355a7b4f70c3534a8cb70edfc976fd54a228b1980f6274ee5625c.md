## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Adaptive Decomposed Prompt Tuning (ADePT), an extension of DePT that replaces fixed token embedding offsets with a token-shared shallow feedforward neural network to dynamically generate content-based offsets. This aims to improve generalization without adding inference time or trainable parameters. The method is tested on many NLU and NLG tasks with T5-220M and T5-3B models, against multiple baselines (PT, DePT, Adapters, LoRA, full finetuning). It often achieves state-of-the-art results, sometimes surpassing full finetuning in low-data scenarios (e.g., RTE, CoLA). The paper is well-contextualized within the PeFT literature, with pilot experiments motivating the approach. However, concerns exist about generality, scalability, and analysis depth.

### Strengths
- **Innovative and well-motivated fix to DePT limitations**: The paper clearly identifies DePT's fixed (position-based) offset as a limitation and proposes a simple content-based alternative via a shared MLP. Pilot experiments convincingly demonstrate this weakness. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Thorough experiments on many tasks and models**: Evaluations cover 23 tasks across NLU and NLG, using T5-220M and T5-3B, with comparisons to multiple PeFT baselines (including LoRA, Adapters, PT variants). This increases trust in the method's general applicability. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Strong performance in data-scarce settings**: At the 220M scale, ADePT surpasses full finetuning on tasks like RTE and CoLA, showing remarkable efficacy. (R2)
- **No added inference cost or extra parameters**: ADePT maintains the inference speed advantages of DePT without increasing the parameter budget over vanilla PT. (R1, R2)
- **Clear writing and effective contextualization**: The paper provides a comprehensive overview of PeFT and situates ADePT well within prior work. (R1, R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited analysis of hyperparameters and design choices**: The effect of the bottleneck dimension and prompt length is not explored; it is unclear if relocating all parameters to the projection (prompt length=0) would work. (R4)
- **Scalability concerns**: Performance gains over PT at the 3B scale are only 0.1–0.2 points on a selected set of tasks (Table 5). This tiny margin and missing baselines (Adapters, LoRA) weaken confidence that DePT-style methods generalize to larger models. (R2, also noted by R1)
- **Missing training cost comparison**: Despite being a key appeal of PeFT, the paper does not compare training latency/throughput among PT, DePT, and ADePT, leaving a gap in understanding practical benefits. (R2)
- **Generality for large language models and long text**: The method is only tested on T5 (encoder-decoder) models; evaluation on decoder-only models (e.g., LLaMA series) is absent. Additionally, effectiveness on long-text problems remains unconfirmed. (R1, R4)
- **Possible baseline mismatch**: Baseline performance numbers for SPoT in Table 3 are taken from other papers and appear lower than PT, raising concerns about fair comparison. (R3)
- **Clarity issues in introduction and framing**: Descriptions like "violate uniqueness of token embeddings" are confusing without Section 3 context. The weakness of DePT would be clearer if framed as "position-based" vs. "content-based" offsets. The prose is repetitive in places. (R2, R3)
- **Unclear implementation details**: It is not stated whether the optimization stabilization from Razdaibiedina et al. (2023) is used. When measuring latency, it is unclear if token offsets were precomputed. The metric for offset analysis (mean/variance) may be less informative than norm or direct ablation. (R3)
- **Latency-accuracy trade-off and few-shot drop**: Table 8 shows ADePT trades accuracy for latency compared to DePT; the added complexity also hurts few-shot performance relative to DePT. (R4)
- **Minority concerns about semantic capability of FFNN**: Reviewer 1 questions whether a shallow feedforward network has sufficient semantic understanding for long text and LLMs, and doubts the method's general robustness. (R1)
