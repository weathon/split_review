## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TGC, a general framework for deep temporal graph clustering that incorporates clustering assignment distribution and adjacency matrix reconstruction into interaction sequence-based batch processing. Reviewers find the motivation clear and the topic timely, but opinions diverge on novelty and experimental rigor: two reviewers rate it highly (8/8) for novelty and insights, while one reviewer (6) criticises the technical contribution as limited and comparisons as unfair.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, structured, and easy to follow, with sufficient implementation details in the appendix (R1, R3).
- The focus on temporal graph clustering is novel and meaningful, potentially shedding new light on the graph learning community (R1, R2, R3).
- The core idea of mining temporal information via a temporal loss and improving clustering with node-level distribution and batch-level reconstruction is easy to follow (R3).
- Experiments are extensive, using both large and small datasets, with clear presentation of results (R1, R2, R3).
- Memory consumption and running time are reported, demonstrating the efficiency of the framework (R2).
- Ablation studies are provided to show the effectiveness of the proposed components (R2, but see weakness on individual ablation).
- The analyses offer significant insights for temporal graph clustering (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Technical contribution**: One reviewer finds the contribution very limited, noting that the two tricks (clustering assignment distribution, adjacency matrix reconstruction) are widely used in existing static graph clustering models (e.g., SDCN, DEC; Bo et al. 2020, Xie et al. 2016) and that no new models are proposed (R2). Another reviewer considers the core idea novel and the temporal loss and clustering loss improvements easy to follow (R3). This disagreement should be noted.
- **Datasets and temporal nature**: The selected datasets have very limited timestamps (e.g., DBLP, arXivAI, arXivCS with only publication year), making them more like discrete-time dynamic graphs rather than continuous-time dynamic graphs as claimed (R2). The two self-developed datasets are not well presented in the paper or appendix (R1). Missing middle-scale datasets; the authors should explore the size boundaries of static deep graph clustering methods (R3).
- **Experimental comparisons**: GPU memory usage comparisons (Figure 2) are unfair because TGC is compared only to static deep clustering baselines that feed whole graphs, not to existing temporal baselines (e.g., HTNE, TGAT, JODIE). This also applies to running time comparisons (Figure 3) (R2). The low consumption of TGC largely depends on its backbone HTNE, making it unclear that this is an inherent advantage of TGC (R2).
- **Ablation and analysis**: In Figure 4, it is unclear what “TGN + TGC” means; the authors likely mean applying the two components to TGN. Individual ablation studies on each component (clustering assignment distribution, adjacency matrix reconstruction) should be provided (R2). Ablation studies should be placed in the main text (R3). The performance of TGC on the Brain dataset is not promising and requires explanation (R3). Clustering algorithms are run only once; most clustering algorithms are not robust and are sensitive to random seeds (R3).
- **Training restrictions**: The chronological order training is a strong restriction that may limit performance on some datasets; some methods can disrupt the graph structure for training (R3).
- **Related work**: The related work section is limited; the authors need to survey and compare more papers published in 2022 and 2023 (R3).
- **Presentation and writing**: There are many typos and grammatical errors (e.g., “A sample general framework” should be “A simple general framework”, wrong quotation marks above Eq (7), “which we do not discuss here” should be “that we do not discuss here”, “Then We conduct” should be “Then, we conduct”, issues with commas) (R1, R2). Figure 2 is too small (R3). In Figure 11, indicate which sub-figure corresponds to which dataset (R3).
- **Practical significance**: The authors should further discuss the practical application of temporal graph clustering in real-world scenarios to demonstrate the significance of this new task (R1).
- **Additional questions/considerations**: The authors mention processing data in batches; is each batch a subgraph obtained by sampling? How does this compare to training with subgraphs (R1)? Consider explaining the relationship between the number of nodes in the static graph and the number of edges in the temporal graph, i.e., the difference in thinking when the focus shifts from nodes to edges (R3).
