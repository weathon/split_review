## Merged Review

### Summary
Vevo proposes a two-stage zero-shot voice imitation framework (content-style AR transformer + acoustic flow-matching) that uses VQ-VAE on HuBERT features with different codebook sizes to progressively disentangle timbre, style, and content. Trained on 60K hours of audiobook data (tokenizers on 100-hour subset), it handles timbre/style imitation, accent/emotion conversion, and text-to-speech. Reviewers praise the novel information-bottleneck disentanglement idea and demo quality, but raise serious concerns about: the lack of strict disentanglement evidence, insufficient metrics gaps, limited innovation relative to existing cascaded frameworks (e.g., SeedTTS, CosyVoice), weak baselines, potential information leakage in the acoustic stage, and reliance on meticulous hyperparameter tuning. One reviewer (score 8) sees no major flaws, while another (score 3) finds the framework insufficiently novel and the results unconvincing.

### Strengths
1. Unified framework covering zero-shot timbre, style, and voice imitation (including accent, emotion, VC, TTS), with clear definitions of timbre and style.
2. Novel self-supervised approach: using VQ-VAE codebook size as an information bottleneck to progressively decouple content, style and timbre—simple yet effective.
3. Study on the order in which information (content, style, timbre) is filtered out as codebook shrinks is highly meaningful.
4. Experimental results are detailed and promising; demo sounds impressive.
5. Vevo matches or exceeds comparable SOTA models across zero-shot scenarios (accent, emotion, VC, TTS), demonstrating strong generalization.
6. Well-structured, easy to read, and can control both timbre and style simultaneously.

### Weaknesses
1. **Disentanglement evidence is weak.** Metrics gaps between codebook sizes (e.g., S-SIM from 0.398 to 0.441; SIM “40 to 44”) are minimal and insufficient to claim successful or strict disentanglement. Results in Table 2 show substantial gaps compared to ground truth.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and lack of strict proof.** The self-supervised VQ-VAE tokenizer requires meticulous tuning of codebook sizes, with trade-offs between content and content-style tokenizers (suboptimal and risky). The disentanglement may be biased by hyperparameter tuning in downstream tasks.
3. **Missing comparisons.** Table 2 should include other disentanglement methods (e.g., label-based). Baselines are weak—should compare with open-source CosyVoice; comparison with FaCodec is unfair; NaturalSpeech3 would be more appropriate. Unknown if conclusions hold for other SSL representations (e.g., wav2vec2, w2vbert).
4. **Potential information leakage in acoustic stage.** The conditional flow matching with masking may learn speaker context from unmasked mel portions during training; during inference, aiming to replicate timbre reference may inadvertently learn consistent style information.
5. **Limited novelty.** The cascaded AR+flow-matching framework is not highly innovative (already seen in SeedTTS, CosyVoice). The unified definition of zero-shot timbre/style/voice tasks is not new (ControlSpeech, StyleFusion TTS). Overall, relatively little innovation beyond the representation comparison.
6. **Insufficient performance gains.** Experimental results do not show significant improvements over strong baselines.
7. **Tokenizer training data concerns.** Both tokenizers are trained on only a 100-hour subset of 60K hours. Is this sufficient for generalization and robustness? No comparison with different data amounts provided.
8. **Ambiguity in mask ratio.** How is the “random mask ratio” (reported as 70%-100%) determined in the conditional flow matching model?
9. **Vague style removal evidence.** When K reduces to 32, FPC drops from 0.764 to 0.706—a small difference. How is it proved that style has been removed?
10. **Implementation details missing.** Unclear specifics of Vevo-Timbre and Vevo-Style versions. Will code/weights be released?
11. **Testing domain limitation.** Has the model been tested on datasets beyond audiobooks to evaluate zero-shot voice generation?
12. **Minority positive view (score 8):** One reviewer saw no significant flaws in the paper.
