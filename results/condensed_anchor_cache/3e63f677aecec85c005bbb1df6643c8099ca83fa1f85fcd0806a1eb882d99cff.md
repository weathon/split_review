## Merged Review

### Summary
LLM-VTP is a training-free visual token pruning method that leverages the attention scores between question tokens and visual tokens in early LLM layers to select semantically relevant video tokens. By retaining only the top-ranked tokens and compressing the KV cache, it prunes 80–90% of visual tokens while maintaining competitive performance. Experiments integrate LLM-VTP with two Video LLMs (PLLaVA and Tarsier) and evaluate on MVBench, MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, TGIF-QA, and VideoChatGPT-Bench. The method achieves up to 1.4× inference speedup.

### Strengths
- The motivation is intuitive and straightforward: prune question-irrelevant visual tokens to reduce redundancy in video data. The approach is training-free and incurs a small computational burden.
- The method generalizes across different LLMs (PLLaVA and Tarsier), enhancing applicability in real-world settings.
- Writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow.
- Extensive experimental results and ablation studies are provided, demonstrating effectiveness across multiple benchmarks. On MVBench, the pruned model even outperforms the original in some cases.
- The research topic—video LLM pruning—has practical value for deployment.
- **Contrasting reviewer opinion:** One reviewer (score 8) finds the method “simple yet effective” and “superior across multiple benchmarks,” while other reviewers note mixed performance-efficiency tradeoffs compared to baselines like PruMerge and Look-M.

### Weaknesses
- **Sensitivity of the layer selection hyperparameter M:** Figure 3 shows that the optimal M varies significantly across datasets and models, requiring a computationally expensive grid search. No principled method for choosing M is given, undermining the “training-free” claim and practical utility.
- **Lack of evaluation on long-form videos:** Despite motivating the method for long video understanding, experiments are conducted on relatively short clips. No analysis is provided for long videos (e.g., a subset of EgoSchema) or scenes with drastic temporal changes, leaving uncertainty about the method’s effectiveness on extended temporal dependencies.
- **Impaired relative position understanding:** On MV-Bench, both PLLaVA and Tarsier with LLM-VTP show performance drops on Object Spatial (OS) and Motion Direction (MD) metrics, indicating that pruning may discard tokens encoding positional cues crucial for spatial awareness and motion reasoning.
- **Overclaim of “LLM-reasoned”:** The title implies explicit reasoning by the LLM, but the paper only uses attention scores without demonstrating or analyzing any reasoning process. Visualizations in Figures 1b and 4 are vague and insufficient to support this claim.
- **Limited technical contribution:** Token pruning and KV compression via attention-score top-α% selection are widely used in (multimodal) LLMs. The approach is essentially a simple ranking strategy, sensitive to hyperparameter α, and may fail on videos with dense information.
- **Mixed comparative results in Table 2:** The method does not consistently achieve a better performance-efficiency tradeoff than PruMerge and Look-M. No intuitive summary of the comparison is provided, and the explanation in lines 362–371 does not fully address concerns.
- **Retained ratio is an inadequate efficiency metric:** Retained ratio ignores token merging as a cost-reduction strategy. The only argument against merging is a single comparison (line 485), which is not solid. Additional metrics like FLOPs should be included for a comprehensive computational analysis.
- **Unfair comparisons in Table 3:** LLM-VTP on Tarsier is compared against other methods on PLLaVA. This makes the comparison inconsistent, as hyperparameters (α, M) and model architectures differ. Results on the same backbone are needed.
- **Missing 10% retained ratio results:** In Tables 1 and 2, the method uses only 20% retained ratio for Tarsier. The corresponding results for 10% retained ratio are not reported, making it hard to assess the effect of compression rate.
- **Ablation on α only for PLLaVA:** The α sensitivity study is conducted only on PLLaVA; it is unclear whether similar trends hold for Tarsier.
- **Limited backbone verification:** The method is tested only on PLLaVA and Tarsier. Its applicability to other models (e.g., ST-LLM, VideoChat) is unknown, and the need to tune α and M per model may restrict generalization.
- **Inefficiency of post-encoding pruning:** The method first encodes all video tokens and then prunes at later layers, which is computationally heavier than early-stage token reduction. Pruning after attention is largely ineffective because features are already captured by other tokens.
- **Lack of analysis for similar attention activations:** Figures 4 and 1(b) show that activation patterns are similar across layers, yet pruning different layers yields varying performance. No explanation is given for this discrepancy.
- **Missing visualizations of pruned tokens:** No side-by-side comparison of original and pruned attention heatmaps is provided to illustrate which tokens are discarded.
