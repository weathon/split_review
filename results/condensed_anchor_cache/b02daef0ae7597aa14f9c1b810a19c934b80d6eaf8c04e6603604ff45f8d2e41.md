## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper demonstrates that LMs can be well-calibrated on average over a broad distribution yet systematically miscalibrated within narrower slices (e.g., overconfident in math, underconfident in history). The authors propose a few-shot recalibrator that, for a given slice, approximates the precision-threshold curve using a few unlabeled samples from that slice. This curve is used to identify slice-specific confidence thresholds for a target precision and can be mapped back to a calibration curve to adjust confidence estimates. Experiments on PaLM2-Large with MMLU and XNLI show the method consistently outperforms temperature scaling (e.g., 16% calibration error improvement on MMLU).

### Strengths
- The problem is well motivated and clearly illustrated (Figures 1, 2, discussion of domain slices).  
- The observation that average calibration can mask per-slice miscalibration is demonstrated effectively.  
- The proposed method is novel in using precision curves for slice-specific recalibration and is few-shot, requiring no additional labeled data or retraining.  
- The approach is simple and easy to follow.  
- Experimental results show consistent improvements over temperature scaling across evaluation settings.  
- Additional ablation studies explore the inner workings of the recalibrator.  
- The solution is consistent with findings in the calibration literature.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and prior work**:
  - One reviewer (score 3) notes that the observation that classifiers can be “calibrated on average but not within a slice” is not novel; it is well-understood in the community (e.g., https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08353). The standard solution—recalibrating for finer granularity using a small supervised set—is also known. Using the precision curve instead of the reliability curve is also not new (e.g., https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12800). Another reviewer (score 5) criticizes the lack of prior works for comparison, making it unclear how effective the recalibrator is relative to state-of-the-art calibration methods.
  - *Contrast*: Other reviewers (score 6) find the method novel, while the overall assessment varies.

- **Experimental evaluation**:
  - Only temperature scaling (TS) is compared, which is far from state-of-the-art calibration. Many other calibration methods exist and should be included.
  - It is unclear how TS is trained: is a separate temperature learned per slice or globally? If globally, the underperformance is unsurprising.
  - The paper should report standard metrics like accuracy, classwise-ECE, and NLL in addition to conf-ECE, since improved precision or ECE may come at the cost of accuracy or sharpness.
  - The reported improvement in utility (Sec 5.3) is quite small.
  - Results lack variance or interquartile range; statistical significance is not assessed.
  - Simulations on synthetic data are requested to better understand the behavior of the method in controlled settings.

- **Clarity and missing technical details**:
  - Section 3 (method) is insufficiently detailed. Needed clarifications:
    - *Slice construction*: How are slices defined for MMLU and XNLI? Are they pre-existing categories, clustering-based, or something else? What criteria determine slice boundaries?
    - *Algorithm block*: A step-by-step algorithm listing inputs, outputs, and intermediate steps (including how few-shot samples are selected and used) is needed.
    - *Precision curve definition*: A precise mathematical expression (step function, continuous, etc.) and how it is estimated per slice should be provided. The impact of sample size on estimation should be discussed.
    - *Loss function*: In Sec 3.2, the loss function is written as \(\mathcal{L}(\theta)\) but the data points used are missing. What data is used to compute the precision curve? The loss function should be clearly defined with its arguments.
  - The final method remains unclear: does it require ground-truth labels to compute the true precision curve? In Sec 4.3, labeled queries are said to give an unfair advantage, yet the TS few-shot baseline also uses labels.
  - The acronym “FSC” (Few-Shot Calibrator) is not defined before use, making the paper harder to follow.
  - One reviewer asks: what do the learned recalibrator parameters look like? Is it just a temperature scaling term?

- **Theoretical support and limitations**:
  - The paper lacks theoretical analysis or guarantees. Reviewers ask about the boundaries/limitations of the method and whether the problem would vanish as data and model size increase.
  - Robustness concern: if the initial set of precision curves \(p_i\) is not representative of the true distribution of slices, the method may not correct miscalibration effectively. How does it handle cases where the predicted precision curve diverges significantly from the true curve?
  - The loss function uses a hand-coded “if” condition with \(\beta\); alternatives (e.g., using an increasing function of the difference, like \(\exp(prec_\theta(c)-prec(c))\)) are suggested to make it smooth.

- **Minor issues**:
  - Section 5.1’s goal is unclear: “We first experiment with measuring the success rate of selecting a confidence threshold that achieves a given target precision on the slice’s ground-truth precision curve.” This needs paraphrasing.
