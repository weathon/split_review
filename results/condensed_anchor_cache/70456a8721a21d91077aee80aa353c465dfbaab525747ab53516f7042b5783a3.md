## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper questions the necessity of locality as an inductive bias in Vision Transformers by evaluating vanilla Transformers that treat each pixel as a separate token (1×1 patches) instead of conventional 16×16 patches. Experiments cover supervised classification (CIFAR-100, ImageNet), self-supervised masked autoencoding, and image generation with diffusion models. The findings show that pixel-based Transformers can achieve competitive performance, challenging the belief that patch-level locality is essential. However, the approach incurs a quadratic increase in token count and thus severe computational cost. Reviewers generally find the topic interesting and the experiments extensive, but disagree on the novelty, practical value, and completeness of the evaluations. Reviewer 2 is most positive (score 8), finding the work a valuable re-check of patchification; Reviewer 4 is most critical (score 5), questioning the marginal gains and lack of practical use case definition.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and clearly motivated**: The paper is organized, explains the rationale behind pixel-as-tokens, and is upfront about trade-offs.
- **Thorough experimental coverage**: Extensive evaluations across three diverse tasks (supervised learning, self-supervised learning, image generation) that support the general conclusions.
- **Interesting question for the community**: The locality-free concept is relevant to bridging computer vision with NLP models, and the work explicitly re‑evaluates a core design choice in ViTs.
- **Compelling observations**: The experiments consistently show that smaller patch sizes (down to 1×1) yield competitive or better performance, challenging the necessity of strong locality biases.

### Weaknesses
- **Practical value is unclear**: The quadratic increase in token count (e.g. 262k tokens for a 512×512 image) makes the 1×1 approach impractical for real-world use. The paper acknowledges this but does not explore mitigation strategies (e.g., efficient attention, alternative architectures). The overall takeaway is mainly feasibility, with no demonstrated benefit that outweighs the cost.
- **Marginal gains and missing baselines**: 
  - In Table 3 (ImageNet), 1×1 tokens yield only 0.1–0.4% improvement over 2×2 tokens, which is insufficient to justify the computation.
  - Table 5 lacks comparisons with other patch sizes (e.g., 4×4, 8×8) and shows no clear advantage of 1×1 over 2×2.
  - No direct comparison with the standard 16×16 patch baseline is provided for the main classification results.
- **Incomplete model‑size analysis**: Experiments focus on ViT‑S and smaller. It is unclear whether the trend holds for larger models (ViT‑L, ViT‑H), where increased capacity might reduce the importance of patch size. Scaling experiments are needed.
- **Case Study #2 is underdeveloped**: Lack of detailed training parameters, architecture description, evaluation metrics, and a clear motivation for why this case adds value beyond Case Study #1.
- **Qualitative comparisons missing**: Figure 3 shows generated images only for the 1×1 approach, without corresponding examples from baseline methods (same prompts/seeds), hindering visual assessment.
- **The finding is not entirely surprising**: Many researchers expected that removing locality could work to some degree, and similar observations may have been considered during ViT design. The paper does not demonstrate a new training scheme or effective pixel tokenization/dictionary that would advance locality‑free models.
- **Downsampling introduces locality bias**: The use of bicubic resampling (e.g., to 28×28 on ImageNet) acts as a fixed convolutional kernel, reintroducing a locality bias that may confound the reported gains.
- **Missing related work**: The paper should discuss emerging lines of work that use grouping/slot attention (e.g., Images as Sets of Points) or cluster‑based tokenization, as these offer alternative ways to achieve flexible patches with lower computation.
- **Conditions of “locality‑free” need clarification**: The concept is conditional (still preserves pixel spatial ordering via positional embeddings) and should be clearly distinguished from random pixel‑level training. Overly broad statements may be misleading.
- **Minor inaccuracies**: L201: vocab size for [0,255] is 256³, not 255³; L411: typo “Intrestingly” → “Interestingly”. Also, the claim about translation equivariance in ViTs (L44–46) requires more precise explanation.
