## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes AdaDiag, an optimizer that rotates gradients via periodic SVD of the gradient matrix so that the Adam/Adafactor preconditioner becomes approximately diagonal in the transformed space. Reviewers found the core idea interesting and the writing mostly clear, but raised major concerns about novelty due to unaddressed prior work, limited experimental evaluation (no wall-clock time, few benchmarks, no second-order baselines), and theoretical weaknesses. Two reviewers (5, 5) see the idea as promising but the paper as preliminary; two reviewers (3, 3) find the contribution unclear without proper comparisons and with flawed theoretical justification.

### Strengths

- The topic of improving adaptive optimization is important and interesting. The core idea of rotating the preconditioner space is reasonable and promising. (R1, R2, R3)
- Explicitly spelling out the motivation via SVD of the gradient matrix (equivalent to eigenvectors of Shampoo’s preconditioner without accumulation) is a good contribution. (R2)
- Experimental results on ResNet, ViT, and LLaMA pretraining are promising and suggest potential for significant impact if they hold consistently. (R2)
- Figures 1, 2, and 9 showing off-diagonal/sparsity behavior of projected gradients are the most interesting aspects of the paper. (R4)
- Writing is mostly clear and easy to follow. (R1, R2)

### Weaknesses

**1. Missing prior work and overclaimed novelty.** The paper ignores highly relevant prior work that also rotates the parameter space to approximately diagonalize a preconditioner: Liu et al. (2018), George et al. (2018) proposing EK-FAC (using K-FAC eigenbasis), and Anil et al. (2020) (Appendix B) describing how to adopt EK-FAC using Shampoo eigenvectors as the transformation matrix. Anil et al. also target the adaptive-moment preconditioner, making their approach directly comparable. (R2) The paper’s claim that SOAP’s approach “lacks an interpretable framework” (lines 371-372) is incorrect; SOAP inherits the framework from this prior work. (R2) The proposed method may be exactly the same as SOAP without accumulation. (R3) The claim in Section 3.3 that SOAP lacks theoretical basis is also false—SOAP uses the Shampoo basis, for which optimal Kronecker approximation theory has been established. (R3) R1 also asks for clarification on the difference between AdaDiag and full-rank GaLore.

**2. No second-order baselines.** Despite lying within the class of second-order methods, AdaDiag is not compared to Shampoo, KFAC, or SOAP. Without these baselines, it is unclear if there is any significant contribution. (R3, R4) R4 specifically notes that the optimizer has connections to Shampoo and should be compared against the distributed Shampoo implementation.

**3. No wall-clock time comparison.** The claim that SVD overhead is “less than 10%” is hand-waving and informal. No wall-clock running time experiments are shown, despite wall-clock time being crucial for optimizer evaluation. (R1, R2) R1 “kindly disagrees” that <10% is negligible and requests rigorous wall-clock comparison with AdamW.

**4. Insufficient memory analysis.** The exact memory requirement of AdaDiag compared to AdamW is not provided (e.g., 50% extra over Adam’s optimizer state?). Table 3 is too abstract; exact memory footprint in GB for a concrete LLM architecture like LLaMA is needed. (R1)

**5. Narrow and insufficiently controlled experiments.**  
- Only two datasets (ImageNet, LLaMA-like) and three architectures are tested; more benchmarks are needed. (R2)  
- No wall-clock time evaluation. (R2)  
- No multiple random seeds are reported. (R2)  
- Hyperparameter tuning details for both AdaDiag and baselines are not provided; it is unclear if reported improvements come from the method or insufficient baseline tuning. (R4)  
- A simple experiment on quadratic functions with dense Hessian is needed to motivate the method. (R1)  
- Ablation studies on SVD frequency \(T\) are missing; it would be interesting to see how \(T\) affects performance. (R1, R2)

**6. Theoretical issues.** The claim that “\(\text{vec}(\Sigma_\tau)\text{vec}(\Sigma_\tau)^\top\) is almost diagonal” is not correct: this is a rank-1 matrix, and the only rank-1 diagonal matrix has a single non-zero entry. The authors’ real point relies on strong decay along \(\Sigma_\tau\)’s diagonal, not diagonalization. (R4) Furthermore, the original matrix \(C(G_\tau) = \text{vec}(G_\tau)\text{vec}(G_\tau)^\top\) is itself rank-1; there is a trivial rotation to make it exactly diagonal (aligning one basis element with \(\text{vec}(G_\tau)\)), under which Adam would just produce per-layer gradient normalization, undermining the claim that diagonalization is the key motivation. (R4)

**7. Convergence analysis not novel.** The convergence results in Section 4 appear to be a direct corollary of Liang et al. (2024) without significant change—the case of AdaDiag corresponds to their Sections 4.1–4.2, and AdaDiag++ to their Section 4.3. The choice of projection matrices does not affect the analysis. (R2)

**8. Distributed implementation not addressed.** Challenges of implementing SVD when the weight matrix is sharded across ≥2 GPUs are not discussed, nor are potential solutions. (R1)

**9. Minor issues.** Missing \(\nabla\) symbol inside the EMA in the first two equations in Section 2.2. (R2) Figure 4 cuts the x-axis at 80 epochs for ViT experiments despite training for 300 epochs, which is visually misleading until clarified in the text. (R2)
