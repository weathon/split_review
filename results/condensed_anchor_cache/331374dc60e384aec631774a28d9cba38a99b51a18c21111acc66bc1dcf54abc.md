## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LoRA Recycle, a data-free meta-learning framework that reuses diverse pre-tuned LoRAs to improve few-shot adaptability of visual foundation models without fine-tuning. It distills a meta-LoRA from pre-tuned LoRAs using synthetic data generated via LoRA Inversion, and introduces a double-efficient mechanism that prunes background tokens. Experiments on eight datasets in in-domain and cross-domain settings are reported.

### Strengths
- The idea of reusing pre-tuned LoRAs without original data is intriguing and expands the utility of LoRA modules beyond task-specific applications (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations and well-made visualizations (Figures 2 and 3) that aid understanding (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The 5-way 1-shot accuracy improvement is impressive, demonstrating the utility of the proposed method (Reviewer 4). The method performs well on several datasets (Reviewer 2).
- The double-efficient mechanism using token pruning and sparse tokens accelerates meta-training effectively (Reviewer 1). Masking images and using self-attention weights for pruning tokens is an interesting idea (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution concerns across reviewers:**
  - The major contribution is not sufficiently interesting; meta-learning is widely used for generalization, and token pruning appears in other lightweight designs. The paper does not clearly highlight why the method is distinctive, especially given that it uses generated data rather than private data (Reviewer 1).
  - The techniques employed are basic and lack innovation: LoRA inversion is standard in data-free KD, and meta-learning with ProtoNet is widely used. The method could be generalized to recycle other models without significant modification, raising questions about uniqueness and specificity (Reviewer 2; see also arXiv:2110.04545).
  - The paper did not use a wide range of pre-finetuned LoRAs from the community; instead, LoRAs were constructed from existing datasets, which may introduce biases and limit diversity (Reviewer 3).

- **Limited evaluation and missing comparisons:**
  - Evaluation uses relatively simple, toy datasets that lack scale and complexity. Results on cross-domain datasets ISIC and CHESTX-RAY were unsatisfactory, possibly due to limited category diversity. More challenging datasets such as WILDS or DomainNet should be tested (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Missing comparisons with established methods: CooP, CoCOOP, PromptSRC (Reviewer 3); Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al. 2022), Balanced-Pairwise-Affinities Feature Transform (Shalam & Korman 2024), Test-time prompt tuning (Shu et al. 2022) (Reviewer 4). Without these, it is difficult to assess relative strengths.
  - Baselines such as LoRAHub, MOLE, and LoRAs Avg+Linear achieve similar 5-way 5-shot performance (inferior by ~1%) while being far more computationally efficient (no synthetic data generation or distillation). The method's standout 1-shot performance lacks explanation of the special technique giving that benefit, and cross-domain results (Table 3) are less convincing (Reviewer 4).

- **Unclear motivation and missing ablations:**
  - The necessity of meta-learning is unclear; an ablation study focusing on its role should be provided (Reviewer 2).
  - The motivation for using synthetic data ("data-free") is not justified. What happens if a standard dataset like MiniImageNet (on which LoRAs are already pre-trained) is used instead? The overhead of synthetic data generation is significant; why is it better than using a large-scale dataset like Laion-2b (as in CAML)? An ablation comparing synthetic vs. real data is needed (Reviewer 4).
  - The method is claimed to be "parameter-lightweight" and "computation-efficient" (Line 084-085), but generating synthetic data involves training trainable pixels during LoRA inversion, contradicting this claim (Reviewer 4).
  - The claim of being "architecture agnostic" (Line 086) is questionable because synthetic data generation depends on the gradient of the chosen VFM, so it is not truly agnostic like adapters or prompts (Reviewer 4).

- **Potential issues with token pruning:**
  - Sparse tokens may break the potential correlation between foreground objects and background; the paper cannot dismiss this adverse effect without further analysis (Reviewer 1). *(In contrast, Reviewer 4 finds the masking idea interesting, highlighting a disagreement on this point.)*

- **Missing related work discussion:**
  - The paper lacks a thorough discussion on data-free knowledge distillation (Reviewer 2).

- **Clarity and presentation:**
  - Several typos: Line 074 "LoRs" should be "LoRAs" (Reviewer 1); Line 090 "re-quiring" should be "requiring" (Reviewer 2).
  - Terms like "meta-LoRA" and "LoRA Inversion" are mentioned without definition; they should be emphasized (e.g., italics) (Reviewer 4).
  - The rationale for using patch masking over token reduction in synthetic data generation should be explained (Reviewer 2).
  - The retrieval-based technique for synthetic few-shot task construction (Line 228) is not clearly tied to the claimed benefit of solving few-shot tasks without fine-tuning (Reviewer 4).

- **Additional concerns:**
  - How does domain generalization perform on this task? The relationship between this setting and cross-domain generalization needs discussion (Reviewer 1).
  - The use of synthetic datasets should have a clear upper bound; the authors should discuss the relationship with using sufficient private datasets, and the trade-off in extreme cases where few-shot samples are available (Reviewer 1).
