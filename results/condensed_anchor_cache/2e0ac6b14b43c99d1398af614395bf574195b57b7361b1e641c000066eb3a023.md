## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Tree-Planner, a three-phase framework (plan sampling, action tree construction, grounded deciding) designed to improve token and correction efficiency in LLM-based close-loop task planning. Reviewers generally agree that the problem is important and the efficiency gains are empirically demonstrated, but they raise significant concerns about experimental scope (single domain, 35 tasks), the grounding of sampled plans, handling of compounding errors, and the originality of the approach relative to prior tree-based planning methods (e.g., Tree-of-Thoughts, Hao et al., Hazra et al.). One reviewer (score 8) is strongly positive about the technical soundness, detail, and well-justified improvements; the others (scores 3, 5, 5) are more critical, citing limited novelty and evaluation breadth.

### Strengths

- **Good idea:** offline plan sampling for LLM-based planning may avoid the distraction of incoming observations and allow more systematic execution (Reviewer 1).
- **Reasonable baselines** (zero-shot, iterative replanning) are used; oracle analysis shows more samples lead to smaller improvement in plan coverage (Reviewer 1).
- **Well-written and well-motivated** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The paper presents a clear rationale for addressing token consumption and redundant error correction.
- **Novel decoupling** of tree construction from grounded deciding for token efficiency; the two‑step approach is intuitively clear (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Significant efficiency gains:** 92.2% reduction in token consumption and 40.5% reduction in error corrections over the best prior model, without performance loss (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Excellent technical presentation:** formalization of token consumption, error type analysis, detailed results using multiple criteria (success rate, GCR, token cost, corrections) – convincing (Reviewer 3).
- **Impactful framework** for the planning-and-decision-making audience using LLMs (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 3):** the improvement is well‑justified, not incremental, and the approach has originality.

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear grounding:** Plan sampling does not include the full initial observation in the prompt, so action sequences may be drawn from the LLM’s implicit distribution rather than grounded in the environment. This weakens the claim of dealing with a POMDP (Reviewer 1).
- **Fundamental limitation:** A flexible plan that must react to observations cannot be guaranteed by a fixed number of samples (N=50); planning inherently requires search, making the approach brittle when items appear in rare locations (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental scope too narrow:** Only one domain (VirtualHome) with 35 tasks – tasks are not even listed. This limits evaluation of broader utility (Reviewers 1, 2). No results on other common environments (e.g., Jericho, ToolBench) or justification for their exclusion (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **GCR metric is misleading:** Subgoals may have different difficulty; the distribution of goal conditions is not reported. The gap between GCR and success rate (SR) is larger for Tree‑Planner than for baselines, indicating GCR overestimates success (Reviewer 1).
- **POMDP emphasis overstated:** VirtualHome has high visibility; the policy includes action history but not observation history, so it is not a true POMDP framework (Reviewer 1).
- **Non‑comparable baseline:** Global replan teleports to the initial state, fundamentally changing the problem and making the comparison unfair (Reviewer 1).
- **Inverse actions as symbolic knowledge:** Relying on predefined inverses goes beyond a purist LLM-only approach; the paper does not discuss how realistic it is to have such knowledge available (Reviewer 1).
- **Algorithm 1 is trivial** (building a trie) and adds no insight (Reviewer 1).
- **Originality limited:** Multi‑plan sampling and tree construction have already been explored in offline/open‑loop planning (Hao et al. 2023, Hazra et al. 2023) and backtracking appears in Tree‑of‑Thoughts. The main novelty is decoupling tree construction from grounding for token efficiency – an engineering tweak (Reviewer 4). *(Contrast with Reviewer 3’s positive view on originality.)*
- **Compounding errors not addressed:** Sampling plans non‑iteratively means a single infeasible action step can cause subsequent steps to be invalid; the paper does not explain how this is handled (Reviewer 4).
- **Ambiguous “corrections”:** Definition (action insertion, deletion, replacement?) and whether measured only for successful plans or all plans is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **No prompt sensitivity analysis:** Errors in plan sampling are catastrophic, yet the paper does not study how performance depends on the plan-sampling prompt (Reviewer 2). A related question: how would the method perform with a different LLM (GPT‑4, LLaMA)?
- **Incomplete prompt details:** The history of error information included in the grounded‑deciding prompt is not fully described (Reviewer 2). It is also unclear whether failed actions are local (current root‑to‑node path) or global.
- **LLM role in grounded deciding unjustified:** It is not explained why the LLM is needed in the second stage – classical search techniques (e.g., UCB, best‑first heuristic search) might suffice. The paper should clarify where exactly the LLM is required (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related‑work discussion:** Faithful CoT (Lyu et al. 2023) – which uses LLM to generate PDDL and then a classical planner – is not mentioned; the paper should justify its exclusion (e.g., on partial observability grounds) (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing citation:** SayCanPay (Hazra et al. 2023) (Reviewer 4).
- **Typographical error:** “Leave node” should be “leaf node” in Figure 4 (Reviewer 4).
- **Many missing experimental details** requested by reviewers (Reviewer 1): list of the 35 tasks, scenes used, whether gold plans are per task or per (task, scene), distribution of plan steps, number of branches in action trees per task, whether observations are monotonic, whether the same prompt is used across four scenes, GCR distribution, randomization for Table 2, etc. These gaps reduce reproducibility.
