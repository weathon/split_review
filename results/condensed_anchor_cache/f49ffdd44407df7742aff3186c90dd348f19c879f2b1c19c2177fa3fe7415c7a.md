## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MovieDreamer, a hierarchical framework for long-duration video (movie) generation that combines autoregressive models (VLM) with diffusion-based rendering. A multimodal MLLM (fine-tuned LLaVA) predicts compressed visual tokens of keyframes, conditioned on a multimodal script (description, face embedding, style). A diffusion decoder (fine-tuned SDXL) renders keyframes, and SVD (I2V) generates the video. A few-shot training method enables customized generation. Quantitative experiments compare to story keyframe generation methods.

### Strengths
+ **Effective integration of autoregressive and diffusion models.** The combination leverages the arbitrary-length generation capability of AR models and the powerful rendering ability of diffusion models for long video generation (R1, R2, R3, R4). This is a product-level solution (R1) and a promising direction (R3).
+ **Novel technical components.** Training a MLLM to output visual features given scripts (rather than an agent framework) (R1). The Gaussian mixture model for continuous visual token prediction is reasonable and inspiring (R2). Additional novel techniques include continuous token supervision, anti-overfitting strategies, and ID-preserving (R4).
+ **ID preserving is effective.** The method achieves strong character-consistent story keyframes (R1, R3). Experimental results show superiority to state-of-the-art methods (R3, R4).
+ **Few-shot capability.** The proposed few-shot training method enables customized generation (R3).
+ **High visual and narrative quality.** The framework produces long videos with high visual fidelity (R4), and the quality is described as “GREAT” (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ID consistency evaluation.** The paper proposes many techniques for identity consistency but does not provide any ID-related evaluation (e.g., face similarity metrics) on the generation results (R2). In the few-shot results (Fig 8), there is still an obvious gap between target ID and generated image; the potential reason is not discussed (R4).
- **Limited technical novelty.** Many multimodal generative models can perform similar key frame generation tasks, though not directly for movie generation (R2). *Minority dissent: R1 finds the scientific contribution (MLLM outputting visual features) novel, but other reviewers rate novelty lower (contributions scores: R2:2, R3:3, R4:3).*
- **Unclear descriptions of key components and equations.** Several technical details are insufficient or misleading:
    - Line 242: The GMM parameters are stated as “2kd means and 2kd variances”; according to the reference, each GMM parameter should contain *kd* means, *kd* variances, and *k* coefficients for each compressed token (R3).
    - Eq. 1 and the DAE (de-noising autoencoder) are not clearly defined: the DAE architecture, training procedure, and integration within the pipeline are missing; Fig 2 does not show the DAE (R4). It is unclear whether Eq. 1 is the real training loss, as the inputs described in Sec 3.3 and Fig 2 include face and description embeddings, which Eq. 1 does not reflect (R4).
    - Fig 2 is confusing: the multimodal script in the top-left corner appears to generate a single keyframe, but three keyframes are shown under the VLM section, misleading readers into thinking they come from the same script (R3). The inference process at the bottom of Fig 2 does not fully display complete inputs and outputs (no noise latent shown, despite Eq. 1 requiring one) (R3, R4).
    - The random masking strategy is not explained: masking ratio, type (token/patch level), which parts are masked, and why it is not used during inference (R4).
- **Missing analysis of method boundaries and limitations.** 
    - No analysis of when the model will fail at ID consistency (e.g., using only one face embedding) (R2). 
    - The reliance on the first frame’s feature (line 323) may limit motion and content diversity; for large rotations/movements performance may drop (R4).
    - Even under zero-shot setting, face embedding is used; for few-shot generation, it is unclear what reference images are used and how they help (R3). *Minority: R1 also asks about generalization to non-human/general domain long-video generation.*
- **Missing analysis of Eq. 2 and loss components.** There is no ablation analyzing whether using only Eq. 2 as the objective function is sufficient, or if the L2 and l2 terms are necessary (R3).
- **High dropout rate (50%) is unusual.** It is unclear whether all VLM parameters are updated; only tuning part of the network is not explored. Ablation studies on dropout rate and parameter tuning are needed (R4).
- **Training cost and inference details are missing.** The paper does not report training cost, inference time, or GPU memory requirements (R4). The number of frames for full-length video is not specified (R4). The amount of cherry-picking needed per long video is not disclosed (R1).
- **Quantitative evaluation is only image-level.** Reported metrics (keyframe generation) cannot evaluate the quality of spatio-temporal modeling; video-level metrics are needed (R4).
- **Minor technical questions not fully addressed.** (R1) How does changing LLaMA input to CLIP sentence embeddings affect LLM adaptation? (R3) Is the covariance matrix in Eq. 7 of the supplementary material diagonal? (R3) What are “episode’s visual tokens” in the few-shot training section (line 302)?
