## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the generality-locality trade-off in multi-modal model editing by proposing BalancEdit, which uses positive and negative samples to dynamically determine each fact’s influence radius, and introduces the OKEDIT dataset for evaluation. All reviewers agree the motivation is good, the approach is simple (no extra training for influence radius), and the method shows good efficiency in time and data usage. Reviewer 1 additionally highlights the innovation of using positive/negative samples to estimate the radius.

### Strengths
- **Significance and clear articulation**: The issue of influence scope in editing is important, and the paper clearly explains the problem and solution (Reviewer 1).
- **Innovative use of positive/negative samples**: Estimating the influence radius of each edit via positive and negative samples is a commendable approach (Reviewer 1).
- **Simple influence-radius determination**: The method requires no extra training, enhancing usability (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Good efficiency**: BalancEdit is efficient in both time and data usage (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Improved generality evaluation**: Introducing multiple generality tests per editing case is beneficial (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ablation study**: The paper does not ablate the two main contributions (positive/negative sample radius estimation and fine-tuning of the transformation layer) to isolate their effects (Reviewer 1).
- **Questionable use of the harmonic mean (HM)**: Because a minimum in either component can lower the HM, the metric is questionable. In Table 3, FT on BLIP2-OPT achieves less than 1% locality, yet the HM still appears (Reviewer 1).
- **Base model accuracy inconsistent**: In Table 3, the Base model’s accuracy is not 0. Per prior work (Can We Edit Multimodal Large Language Models?), the expected values are Acc=0 and Loc=100 for the base model (Reviewer 1).
- **Sequential editing experiments on wrong dataset**: Sequential editing experiments are performed on OKVQA instead of the paper’s own MMEdit and OKEDIT datasets (Reviewer 1). Also, MEND is reported without NaN values in Table 4, but prior work (VLKEB) notes NaN issues during sequential editing—possible differences in settings? (Reviewer 1).
- **No text locality measurement**: If language-module weights are edited, text locality should be measured and compared with other methods (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of comparison with SERAC**: The paper does not present results for the SERAC method (Reviewer 3).
- **Visual reasoning goal limited**: The approach offers little differentiation from MMEdit; test data is also in QA format (Reviewer 2).
- **Black/white image as negative sample is too simplistic**: Using a black image as the negative sample (a) lacks citations for its use across visual recognition tasks (Reviewer 1), (b) may not achieve an “optimal balance” between generality and locality (Reviewers 2, 3), and (c) leads to a fixed hyperparameter α, limiting flexibility (Reviewer 2).
- **Generated image quality concerns for OKEDIT**: The dataset uses GPT-4 and a diffusion model to generate rephrased images for generality/locality tests. All reviewers question the quality and consistency:
  - Reviewer 1: Prior work (VLKEB) notes generated images may shift content, causing inconsistencies.
  - Reviewer 2: The approach offers limited advancement over MMEdit due to inconsistent image quality; human verification is needed to confirm generated images are usable for testing.
  - Reviewer 3: In Figure 4, the second generated image is totally different from the first; in Figure 6, the first and third generality test examples are entirely different from the editing sample, making test results questionable. Also, how many locality test images are used per editing case? If only one, it is imbalanced because generality has 10 images per case (Reviewer 3). No image quality verification procedure is reported (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Outdated baseline models**: The experiments use Blip2-OPT and MiniGPT-4, which are relatively old and limited. Newer architectures such as the LLaVA series should be tested and may yield different results (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues**:
  - Duplicate paragraphs on page 10, lines 489–514 (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Typo “labelis” on line 723 (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing reference on line 862 (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Table 3: some bolded values do not correspond to the best results (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Figure 4 is confusing: the first image differs significantly from the rest, and the main subject appears to be two people rather than a church (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Inconsistent use of proprietary terms such as MiniGPT-4 and BLIP-2 OPT (Reviewer 1).
