## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes BaDExpert, a backdoor defense that extracts the backdoor functionality from a potentially backdoored model by fine-tuning it on a small set of mislabeled clean samples to obtain a “backdoor expert” model that recognizes only trigger-carrying inputs. Test instances that yield different predictions between the original model and the backdoor expert are considered benign; matching predictions indicate a triggered input. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10, GTSRB, and ImageNet across ResNet, VGG, MobileNetV2, and Vision Transformer architectures, covering 17 SOTA attacks and including adaptive attacks. Reviewer 3 (score 8, confidence 5) was most positive, praising novelty and thoroughness, while Reviewer 2 (score 5) emphasized practicality issues.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach**: Extracting backdoor functionality into a separate “backdoor expert” model is a new idea in backdoor defense (R1, R3, R4). The method is simple, straightforward, and effective (R2, R4).
- **Clear presentation**: The methodology is well-structured and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3). Excellent presentation overall (R3).
- **Extensive experimentation**: Solid experiments across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, GTSRB, ImageNet), model architectures (ResNet, VGG, MobileNetV2, Vision Transformer), and 17 SOTA attacks (R1, R2, R3, R4). Includes adaptive attack evaluation and shows limits under those cases (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No in-depth theoretical justification for why fine-tuning on mislabeled data isolates backdoor functionality. Rigorous theory would bolster credibility (R1).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and practicality**: Optimal learning rate varies significantly across datasets (e.g., η=10⁻⁴ for CIFAR-10, η=2.5·10⁻⁵ for GTSRB) and architectures (e.g., η=10⁻⁴ for ResNet18, η=10⁻⁶ for pretrained ViT-B/16). The fine-tuning and unlearning rates (η′) are sensitive and impractical to tune when the defender does not know whether a backdoor exists and cannot observe the clean accuracy trajectory to verify when it first drops to ~0% then recovers. The ablation studies provide no overall guidelines for real-world application (R2, R4).
- **Dependence on attack strength**: BaDExpert relies on a strong mapping from trigger to predefined behavior; weak attacks (low poison rate, small trigger size, low blend rate) may cause failure. Only partial results are shown in Table 10; further analysis is needed (R2). The method is relatively weak against Blend and Dynamic attacks – explanation is required (R2). For non-backdoored models, disagreement between the original model and backdoor expert may degrade performance (R1).
- **Metric concerns**: AUROC may overestimate defense quality. For Blend attack, ASR is 11.4% but AUROC is 99.2%; ASR/CA are more appropriate metrics for evaluating BaDExpert (R2).
- **Missing comparisons with recent works**:  
  – Reconstructive Neuron Pruning [Li et al. 2023] (reports ASR 5.03, CA 92.18 on CIFAR-10) (R1).  
  – AWM [1] and ABL [2] [One-shot Neural Backdoor Erasing via Adversarial Weight Masking; Anti-Backdoor Learning] (R2).  
  – ASSET [2] and cognitive backdoor pattern distillation [3] (R4).  
  – Test-time detection methods [Li et al. 2022] (R3).  
  Current comparisons are insufficient to establish superiority or parity (R4).
- **Unclear handling of detected samples**: The paper focuses on detection but does not clearly state how detected backdoor inputs are handled (discarded or label-corrected) to produce the CA/ASR results in Table 1. This ambiguity affects interpretation (R4).
- **Other missing details**: Time complexity comparison with I-BAU is not provided (R1). Code is not released (R1). Relationship to the honeypot defense [Shan et al. 2019] is not discussed (R3).
