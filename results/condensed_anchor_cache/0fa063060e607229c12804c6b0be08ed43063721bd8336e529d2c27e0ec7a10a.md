## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q-score matching (QSM), a new off-policy reinforcement learning method that links the score of a diffusion model policy to the action gradient of the Q-function. Theoretical analysis connects QSM to soft policy iteration. Experiments on six DeepMind Control Suite tasks against SAC and TD3 show competitive performance in some environments.

### Strengths
1. **Novel perspective**: Conceptualizing RL as a dynamic process and using a diffusion model to learn the score of the policy by leveraging the action gradient of the Q-function. This offers a fresh angle for policy optimization that may inspire future research. [Reviewers 1, 2, 3]
2. **Comprehensive theoretical analysis**: Rigorous mathematical derivations and theoretical support for the proposed method, establishing a solid foundation. [Reviewers 1, 2, 3]
3. **Clear writing and motivation**: The paper is well-written, motivation is clearly presented, and Figure 1 gives an effective illustration of QSM’s effect. [Reviewer 2]
4. **Promising initial results**: Empirical evaluation shows competitive performance in several environments (e.g., Hopper Hop). [Reviewer 2]

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient experimental scope**: Only two baselines (SAC, TD3) on six simple DMC tasks; no comparisons to other diffusion-based RL methods (Diffusion-QL, QGPO) and no offline setting evaluation. More challenging environments (Ant, Humanoid) and discrete action space tests are needed. [Reviewers 1, 2, 3]
2. **Inconsistent performance**: QSM only best in 3 of 6 environments; baselines often converge faster or achieve higher final returns. Notably on Cheetah Run and Walker Run, QSM shows clear saturation and inferior results with no explanation provided. [Reviewers 2, 3]
3. **Theory-practice gap**: Theorem states that the score of the optimal policy aligns with the gradient of the optimal Q-function (∇_a Q^*), but the algorithm uses the current Q-function (∇_a Q). The theoretical justification may not directly support the practical algorithm’s efficacy. [Reviewer 1]
4. **Implementation inconsistency**: The paper describes learning a discrete action sequence from a_{t-1} to a_t (Section 2.3), but the algorithm samples a_t by iterative denoising from random noise, not starting from a_{t-1}. The algorithm box lists π_φ and the score network Ψ(s,a); it is unclear why sampling a_t does not depend on a_{t-1}. [Reviewer 1]
5. **Methodological similarity to prior work**: QSM minimizes the L2 norm between the score and the Q-function gradient; this is conceptually similar to Diffusion-QL (which maximizes Q via score) and QGPO. The distinctiveness and advantages of QSM over these methods are not demonstrated. [Reviewers 1, 3]
6. **Lack of analytical comparison**: Figure 4 visualizes the learned action distribution for QSM but includes no baseline for comparison. SAC also has built-in exploration; how QSM’s exploration strategy compares is unaddressed. [Reviewer 2]
7. **Limited ablation and analysis**: No hyperparameter sensitivity, no analysis of why QSM fails on certain tasks, and no experiments testing the method in more challenging environments. [Reviewers 2, 3]
