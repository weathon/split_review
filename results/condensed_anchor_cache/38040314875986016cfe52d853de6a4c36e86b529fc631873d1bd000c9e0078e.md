## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **TimeRAF**, a Retrieval-Augmented Forecasting model that enhances zero-shot time series forecasting using customized knowledge bases, an end-to-end learnable retriever, and a Channel Prompting mechanism. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and the experiments comprehensive, but express significant concerns about novelty, methodological rigor (especially the claim of zero-shot learning), the fairness and completeness of experimental comparisons, and the practical efficiency of the approach. One reviewer (score 3) is notably more negative, questioning the marginal improvement and the necessity of the retrieval process.

### Strengths
- **Writing and presentation**: All four reviewers note the paper is well-structured, well-written, and easy to understand (R1, R2, R3, R4). Figures are clear and help comprehension (R2).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The paper includes thorough ablation studies, interpretable analysis, and evaluations across multiple datasets (R1, R2). Some experiments are meaningful (R4).
- **Novelty**: One reviewer (R2) considers the research novel and unresearched in the field. Another reviewer (R3) acknowledges that combining retrieval-augmented models with time series contributes to efficient model adaptation. *However, a counterpoint (R1) notes that while applying RAF to time series is new, the RAF technique itself is not novel—this contrast is preserved in Weaknesses.*
- **Performance**: R1 reports that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance. *But R3 finds the improvement marginal (0.00x in most datasets), raising a minority negative view (see Weaknesses).*

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal improvement and necessity of RAF** *(minority reviewer, R3)*: The improvement shown by RAF is minimal (only 0.00x in most datasets, e.g., Fig. 3). This raises concern that the pre-trained backbone is already too powerful, reducing the necessity of the retrieval process. The authors should explore replacing the backbone with alternative foundation models to better understand RAF’s effectiveness.
- **Novelty and positioning** *(R1, echoed by R4)*: The major novelty is the application of RAF to time series, but RAF itself is well-known. It is unclear how this work differs from existing time series RAG methods (R1 asks for elaboration on the claimed gap: "existing methods are either limited to historical data or do not support foundation models"). The paper gives two citations but does not clearly delineate the difference.
- **Zero-shot setup is flawed** *(R4, strong criticism)*: The authors claim zero-shot forecasting, but the setup appears to fine-tune both the retriever and forecaster on parts of the test data, violating standard zero-shot principles (e.g., true zero-shot would be training on ETTh1 and testing on ETTh2). The authors should refer to rigorous zero-shot setups like GPT4TS or TimeLLM. This directly undermines the claimed contribution of zero-shot generalization.
- **Unfair experimental comparisons** *(R4)*:
  - **Input length mismatch**: TimeRAF uses input length 512, but baseline results (e.g., iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear) are reported with different input lengths. For example, with input length 512, PatchTST on ETTm1 (96-step forecast) yields 0.292, not the value in the paper. This invalidates the reported improvements.
  - **Source of results**: The paper mixes results from other papers (full-shot methods) with the authors’ own implementation of TTM, without clearly stating this.
  - **Only forecast horizon 96**: Results are shown only for one forecast length (96). To demonstrate generalization, results for 192, 336, 720 are needed. The provided results for other lengths are not convincing because full scripts are missing and results without RAF do not match the original TTM paper.
- **Incomplete and missing experimental details** *(R4, R2)*:
  - Pre-training details of the foundation model (TSFM) are omitted: datasets used, training procedure, loss functions. This raises reproducibility concerns. The reported results also differ from the original TTM paper for the same pre-trained model (R4).
  - Code is not provided (R2, R4). This hinders reproducibility verification.
- **Efficiency and scalability concerns** *(R2, R3, R4)*:
  - **Channel Prompting overhead** (R4): For high-dimensional datasets (e.g., traffic), each channel queries k retrieved time series, so multivariate data requires channel * k external series. Without channel-independence, only k series would be needed. The paper’s claim of efficiency is not valid; scalability analysis is missing.
  - **General efficiency** (R2, R3): The retrieval process inevitably increases time cost. Theoretical analysis and empirical memory/time costs (e.g., storing retrieval data vs. a foundation model) are needed.
- **Label leakage risk** *(R2)*: Different datasets may have very similar or identical temporal patterns, leading to potential label leakage through the RAG knowledge base. The authors should discuss this.
- **Knowledge base domain dependency** *(R1)*: The knowledge base is a curated multi-domain dataset. If a new domain emerges (e.g., stock) that is not in the base, performance is unclear. Cross-domain evaluation beyond the original base is also requested (R3).
- **Technical ambiguities** *(R1, R4)*:
  - Line 192: "retrieve information solely from datasets distinct from the input source"—what defines "distinct"? (R1)
  - Line 226: "weighing the importance of different components" but the method appears to compute an average; how are weights applied? (R1)
  - Line 244: why is it challenging to guarantee retrieved candidates? (R1)
  - Section 3.3: non-similar data used for training, similar data for inference—implementation details for different datasets are unclear (R4).
  - Scaling: how does TimeRAF handle magnitude mismatches between retrieved data and input? Normalization techniques are not elaborated (R4).
- **Minor presentation issues** *(R4)*:
  - Typos and grammar: e.g., lines 53-70 ("Retrieval-Augmented for time series Forecasting"), line 301 (extra comma). Figure 5 lacks a y-axis label.
- **Performance on Electricity dataset** *(R2)*: TimeRAF does not achieve best performance on Electricity. The authors should analyze why.
- **Retrieval data amount** *(R3)*: How much data is used for forecasting after retrieval? Memory and time costs are needed.
