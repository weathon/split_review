## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes V‑FactER, a training‑free corrector‑based method for detailed image captioning that decomposes captions into atomic propositions, verifies each with an MLLM, and revises accordingly. It also introduces an evaluation framework and benchmark for factuality and coverage. Reviewers generally found the paper clear and the method effective but raised concerns about novelty (similar to prior decomposition‑verification works like FactScore, FaithScore, RLAIF‑V, HalluciDocter, etc.), missing baseline comparisons (LURE, SPICE, CHAIR, POPE, MMBench), evaluation limitations (synthetic captions, lack of human‑judgment correlation, only object hallucination tested), insufficient ablation, and lack of analysis of atomic proposition quality and computational cost. Two reviewers rated 5 (positive), three rated 3 (moderate).

### Strengths

- Clear motivation and coherent story: converting detailed captioning into VQA for both generation and evaluation. (R1, R2, R5)
- Proposed method is simple, training‑free, plug‑and‑play, and effective in reducing hallucinations. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
- Introduces an evaluation framework and benchmark dataset for detailed captioning focusing on factuality and coverage, which is beneficial to the field. (R1, R4, R5)
- Method can be applied to any captioning model and improves factuality even for GPT‑4V. (R1, R2)
- Reveals limitations of existing hallucination detection methods on long captions, justifying the VQA‑based approach. (R1, R5)
- Well‑organized, easy to follow. (R2, R3, R5)
- Addresses measurement of factuality and coverage, a topic not extensively explored. (R4)
- Coverage evaluation transformed into detailed VQA dataset allows quantitative assessment. (R4)
- Extensive experiments support claims. (R5)

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns**: The method of decomposing captions into atomic propositions for individual verification is similar to prior works: FactScore (Min et al., 2023), FaithScore (Jing et al., 2023), RLAIF‑V (Yu et al., 2024), HalluciDocter (Lee et al., 2024), “Visual factor checker,” Zhou et al. (2024), and the POPE paper (Li et al., 2023). The concept of problem decomposition and self‑criticism is common in the LLM agent field. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
- **Missing baseline comparisons**:
  - SPICE (scene‑graph based) not compared; SPICE also has precision/recall for factuality/coverage and attribute/color breakdowns. (R1)
  - LURE (a correction‑based method) not included. (R5)
  - CHAIR and POPE for hallucination evaluation on captions not used. (R3)
  - Results on question‑answering benchmarks like MMBench missing. (R3)
  - Baselines like VCD, OPERA, Volcano do not use external knowledge, unlike V‑FactER, making the comparison unequal. (R5)
- **Evaluation limitations**:
  - The evaluation of the proposed factuality metric uses synthetic captions; patterns may not match real hallucinations. (R1)
  - No human evaluation or correlation with human judgment for the proposed metrics. (R1, R4)
  - Hallucination detection evaluation only considers object hallucinations, not attribute or relation. (R1)
  - Insufficient qualitative comparisons and analyses of generated captions. (R3)
  - No breakdown of factuality/coverage metrics by hallucination type (object, attribute, relation). (R1)
- **Ablation studies limited**: only the choice of different pi value tested. (R1)
- **No analysis of the quality of atomic proposition generation**. (R1)
- **No analysis of potential information loss or hallucination when converting caption to atomic propositions and merging back**; an encoding‑plus‑decoding without hallucination check could reveal this. (R1)
- **Computational cost of the proposed method not analyzed**. (R2)
- **Table 5 conclusion not convincing**: detailed captioning may be too ambiguous compared to deterministic VQA tasks; lower performance could be due to the model not being trained for that caption style, not inability. (R1)
- **Scores in Table 5 do not match scores in Table 3 (w/ VFactER rows)**. (R1)
- **Motivation for using the IIW‑400 dataset not clear**; other datasets like MSCOCO and DOCCI exist. (R4)
- **Missing broader results across various MLLMs on proposed metrics** (CLAIR, Factuality, Coverage). (R4)
- **Questions (overlapping with criticisms)**:
  - How does the factuality metric change if only the image or only the reference caption is provided as input? (R1)
  - Can the authors provide performance on the coverage VQA dataset without feeding input to assess chance guessing? (R1)
  - Will there be information loss or hallucination when decomposing and merging captions? (R1)
  - How does V‑FactER affect performance on general VQA datasets (e.g., VQAv2, GQA, OKVQA)? (R4)
  - Is there correlation between the proposed detailed VQA dataset and general VQA datasets? (R4)
- **Minority contrast**: Two reviewers (R1, R3) rated 5 and found the method effective and well‑motivated, while three rated 3 due to novelty and evaluation concerns.
