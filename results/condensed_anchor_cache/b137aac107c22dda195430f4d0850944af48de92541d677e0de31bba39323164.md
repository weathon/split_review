## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DIG-MILP, a deep generative framework based on a variational auto-encoder (VAE) for generating Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) instances with a feasibility and boundedness guarantee obtained by leveraging MILP duality. The VAE is trained to reconstruct instances after masking a random node and its connections. Experiments on several benchmarks are conducted to demonstrate similarity to original data and utility for solver hyperparameter tuning and data augmentation.

### Strengths
- The use of VAE sampling in dual space to directly guarantee feasibility and boundedness of generated instances is a strong point (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
- The paper addresses a real need for high-quality MILP instance generation in both industry and academia (Reviewer 4).  
- Experimental results are reported on multiple datasets and show that the generated instances can facilitate solver enhancement and business scenarios (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
- The VAE framework is described as self-contained and reasonable for instance generation (Reviewer 4).  
- The proof that generated instances are feasible and bounded is provided (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work**: The technical novelty is considered minor. The method is a direct combination of an existing VAE-based approach (NeurIPS 2023, Geng et al.) and a dual method for feasibility (Bowly et al.), without significant modifications or adaptations (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The paper lacks a detailed explanation of the differences from the cited NeurIPS 2023 work and does not compare against it experimentally (Reviewers 2, 3). The literature review misses that most related paper and also omits prior SAT instance generation methods such as G2SAT, HardSATGEN, and others that use similar bipartite graph structures (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **Ambiguity: MILP vs IP**: The formulation in Eq. (1) constrains all variables to be integers, suggesting an Integer Program (IP), yet the paper claims to generate MILPs. Table 1 lacks features indicating whether variables are integer or continuous, creating confusion about the actual problem class addressed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Feasibility guarantee necessity and infeasibility limitation**:  
  - An ablation study removing the feasibility component is needed to show whether it is necessary when training data contains only feasible instances (Reviewer 1).  
  - The framework cannot generate infeasible MILP instances, which are common in real-world applications, limiting its applicability (Reviewer 4).  
- **Experimental insufficiency**:  
  - Experiments lack large-scale benchmarks (e.g., MIPLIB instances with over 100,000 variables and constraints) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Missing evaluations on downstream tasks such as learning to cut and learning to branch (Reviewer 2).  
  - Baselines are insufficient; the method should be compared to G2SAT and other generative models for SAT/CO problems (Reviewer 2, 4).  
  - Datasets: small datasets (SC, CA) have 1000 instances but large datasets (CVS, IIS) have fewer than 10 instances; training and testing on only 3 instances raises overfitting concerns (Reviewer 4). The authors should consider the NeurIPS 2021 ML4CO datasets as an alternative (Reviewer 4).  
  - Hyperparameter configurations and their impact on performance are not reported, making it difficult to assess robustness (Reviewer 1).  
- **Metric confusion**: The paper states similarity score is derived from JS divergence (lower is better), but Table 1 uses “similarity score ↑” implying higher is better. The contradictory description makes interpretation unclear; the trade-off between authenticity and diversity is not addressed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Performance relative to random**: One reviewer notes the method does not perform significantly better than a random baseline, raising concerns about its practical utility (Reviewer 1). Another reviewer states it outperforms baselines on benchmarks (Reviewer 2) — this disagreement should be resolved with clearer evidence.  
- **One-node masking too incremental**: Masking only one node and its connections may be too small a change for large instances (e.g., thousands of nodes/constraints), potentially leading to generated instances that are only marginally different from training data (Reviewer 4).  
- **Motivation for VAE over other generative models**: The paper does not explain why VAE was chosen over GAN, diffusion models, or other alternatives; one reviewer finds the VAE framework reasonable while another demands justification (Reviewer 2 vs. Reviewer 4).  
- **Theory-to-pipeline relationship**: The connection between Theorem 1 (theoretical derivation) and the actual VAE generation pipeline is unclear (Reviewer 2).  
- **Usefulness of optimal value prediction task**: The paper does not clarify whether the proposed method aids in solving MILPs or only predicts optimal values; the practical relevance of that task is questionable (Reviewer 1).  
- **Figure readability**: Several figures have too small axis fonts, making them hard to read (Reviewer 3).  
- **Table 1 feature clarification needed**: The all-zero values for y,r and all-one values for x,s need further explanation (Reviewer 3).  
- **Authenticity claim**: The claim that boundedness and feasibility guarantee authenticity is not dogmatic; the authors should provide more evidence (Reviewer 3).
