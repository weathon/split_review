## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GPST, a hierarchical transformer for speech language modeling. Audio is quantized into two discrete representations: semantic tokens (from HuBERT/wav2vec2/w2v-BERT clustering) and acoustic tokens (from SoundStream/Encodec residual quantizers). A global transformer processes the coarse sequence at 75 Hz (summed embedding over the stack of codes per time step), and a local transformer predicts the stack of codes within each time step autoregressively. This one-stage architecture jointly models semantic and acoustic tokens, in contrast to prior multi-stage approaches (e.g., AudioLM, VALL-E). The model is evaluated on semantic-to-acoustic generation, voice conversion, and acoustic continuation tasks using LibriSpeech clean test and Aishell-2, with metrics including WER, speaker similarity (SIM), and DNSMOS.

### Strengths
- The paper studies joint training of global and local transformers for speech language modeling, mitigating the long-sequence problem (R1, R2).
- A single model optimizes the joint probability of semantic and acoustic tokens; the hierarchical transformer facilitates efficient modeling of long sequences (R2).
- Empirical results demonstrate superiority over evaluated baselines, including outperforming AudioLM (R1, R2).
- The one-stage architecture is novel, absorbing cascaded representation generation into the transformer itself, and is of interest to audio/multimodality audiences (R3, R4).
- The approach offers theoretical efficiency improvements over two-stage models; the memory/time cost is reduced by using a relatively small transformer where top layers are not as large as the bottom layers (R3, R4).
- Objective metrics show the effectiveness of the proposed method (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Global and local transformers were introduced earlier in NLP (MegaByte style). The contribution appears incremental compared to AudioGen, VALL-E, AudioLM, and SoundStorm, particularly as full NAR approaches (SoundStorm) already address efficiency (R1, R4). One reviewer (R3) considers the one-stage architecture novel but gave only a fair contribution score. Another reviewer (R4) notes that the primary contribution is the unified stage, yet the title/writing may be misleadingly focused on efficiency.
- **Unsupported claims**: Several assertions lack backing references or experiments, e.g., “which limits the performance of fine acoustic token generation,” “multi-stage generative models induce significant error propagation,” “obstructing information flow among hierarchical quantizers degrades performance” (R1).
- **Efficiency analysis disagreement**: The paper’s efficiency analysis is disputed; the size \(D\) is typically 4/8/12 (small), while local sequence length \(m_l\) is likely much larger. A reviewer argues that if \(m_l\) is ignored, \(D\) should also be ignored. Actual speed/latency measurements are missing and should be provided in Table 3 (R1, R3, R4).
- **Unfair comparison on speaker identity transfer**: GPST and AudioLM use semantic tokens inferred from speech, which may leak speaker information, unlike zero-shot TTS models (YourTTS, SPEAR-TTS, VALL-E) that take text and a speaker prompt. For a fair comparison, reviewers request a GPST-TTS setup where semantic tokens are inferred from text (R2).
- **Missing direct comparison with flat baseline**: No comparison between hierarchical and non-hierarchical (flatten audio tokens, vanilla LM) models trained with the same objective. Comparison with AudioLM is confounded by other factors (R2).
- **Missing DNSMOS scores**: DNSMOS is not reported for the *Semantic to Acoustic* and *Acoustic Continuation* tasks, only for *Speaker Identity Transfer* (R2).
- **GPST-Hi-Res degrades on key metrics**: GPST-Hi-Res (higher resolution) shows worse WER and speaker similarity than GPST despite better audio quality; the reason is not explained (R2).
- **Evaluation methodology questions**: In Table 1, for the semantic-to-acoustic condition, it is unclear why WER is used rather than human evaluation of acoustic quality, whether the box refers to unconditional generation, and why HuBERT-Large/XLSR-53 are used as ground truth (R3).
- **Confusion on GSLM WER computation**: GSLM is a decoder-only unconditional model operating on HuBERT units; more details are needed on how WER was computed for GSLM (R1).
- **Subjective evaluation needed**: Objective metrics (including DNSMOS) are insufficient; subjective listening tests are required to assess speech quality. A demo page for reviewers is advisable (R4).
- **Missing comparisons/ablations**: The paper should explore/compare to a simple delay pattern (Kharitonov et al., Copet et al.) which could simplify the model and remove need for local transformer (R1). Also, information on vocab size for acoustic tokens (1024 vs. 8192), whether \(E_a\) is shared between global and local transformers, the meaning of “local drops,” and the number of layers (\(N_g, N_l\)) is not provided (R2).
- **Potential submission format issue**: The REPRODUCIBILITY AND ETHICS STATEMENT on page 10 may break the 9-page limit (R1).
- **Memory issues**: The reviewer questions whether long sequence prediction causes memory issues and requests training/inference time details (R4).
