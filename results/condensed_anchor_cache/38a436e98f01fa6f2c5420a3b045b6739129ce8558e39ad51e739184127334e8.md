## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes YOLO-MARL, which uses a single, pre-training interaction with an LLM to generate a strategy, state interpretation, and planning functions that shape rewards for MARL agents, thereby avoiding frequent LLM calls during training. There is disagreement among reviewers: Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 are more critical (scores 3,3), questioning novelty and experimental rigor, while Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 4 are more positive (scores 5,5), praising the idea and presentation but still noting significant concerns.

### Strengths
- The core idea—leveraging LLMs for high-level task planning in MARL while querying the LLM only once per environment to avoid costly online inference—is novel, interesting, and well-motivated. It addresses a real computational inefficiency.
- The paper is easy to read and follow, with clear descriptions and ample supplementary material (examples, code, appendices).
- The experimental setup includes multiple environments (Level-Based Foraging, MPE, SMAC), which helps validate the approach against established baselines like MADDPG and Qmix.
- The approach demonstrates the potential of using LLMs to design or modify the MDP description in multi-agent settings, a contemporary and promising direction.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and depth of literature review**  
  - The methodology is not convincingly novel; similar studies integrating LLMs with multi-agent systems exist but are not discussed (e.g., [1] Hu et al., 2024; [2] Gong et al., 2023; [3] Wu et al., 2024; [4] Zhang et al., 2023). This omission weakens the claimed novelty.  
  - The necessity of the MARL setting specifically (vs. single-agent RL) is not justified; potential benefits might also be realized in single-agent scenarios without the added complexity.

- **Experimental rigor, reproducibility, and significance of results**  
  - The result figures appear unprocessed (e.g., directly from WANDB) and many show only one run, lacking statistical significance. At least five independent trials are needed.  
  - Table 1 (averaged results) shows high overlap with Figure 2 curves, and standard deviations are missing; this raises concerns about whether the improvements from using the LLM are quantifiably significant.  
  - The number of independent trials for experiments in Section 6 is not stated.  
  - On SMAC (provided maps are easy), performance improvements are minimal; results almost overlap with the baseline. This suggests YOLO-MARL may not be beneficial for more challenging cooperative scenarios. Was Qmix run with YOLO-MARL on SMAC maps? Were more complex maps (e.g., SMACv2) considered?  
  - In MPE, as the number of agents increases, results start overlapping, raising scalability concerns.  
  - A dip in performance around 450K episodes for MADDPG and YOLO-MARL+MADDPG is uncommented.

- **Insufficient training length and convergence analysis**  
  - In Level-Based Foraging, both YOLO-MARL and Qmix seem not to have converged at 2M steps; longer training (e.g., 10M steps) would help compare final performance and stability.  
  - The experiments show faster convergence but not necessarily higher final performance, especially in SMAC. Long-term training results in MPE and LBF would clarify whether YOLO-MARL yields lasting improvements.

- **Domain knowledge and human effort required**  
  - A great deal of domain knowledge is still needed to format the environment description for the LLM (State Interpretation, Strategy Generation). This effort is on par with, or even harder than, classical feature engineering (e.g., OO-MDPs), making the comparison to baselines arguably unfair.  
  - The reliance on human expert and LLM-generated planning functions may limit applicability in highly strategic or domain-specific scenarios (e.g., Go, Dota2).  
  - The approach is essentially a straightforward application: using human-crafted “State Interpretation” prompts to let the LLM generate a base strategy that shapes RL rewards. This does not fully exploit the potential of LLMs in dynamic settings.

- **Missing details and unclear components**  
  - Some parts are over-explained (e.g., State Interpretation) while others (e.g., Planning Function Generation) are under-elaborated.  
  - The reward generation mechanism is unclear: does the LLM generate a reward function? When (before training, like strategy generation)?  
  - More details are needed on how the planning function is generated for SMAC, where actions are more complex than in MPE.  
  - Minor: Figures lack axis labels.

- **Generalizability and impact**  
  - The approach requires significant manual effort to describe each environment to the LLM; a more impactful and general solution would use a multimodal foundation model to automatically analyze raw observations (e.g., images) via fixed general prompts.  
  - The paper does not reach the impact level expected for a main-track ICLR paper.  
  - The name “YOLO-MARL” conflicts with the widely known YOLO object detection model.

- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**  
  - Clarify if the only LLM interaction occurs once at the beginning of the game.  
  - Provide experimental results on more challenging tasks (e.g., higher-difficulty SMAC maps).  
  - Extend experiments to show long-term training results in MPE and LBF.  
  - Test in environments with higher strategic depth to validate utility in complex scenarios.  
  - Discuss how dependence on pre-trained LLMs affects generalizability and reliability of planning functions in domain-specific environments (e.g., chess).  
  - Compare YOLO-MARL with the additional related works, especially regarding reward assignment and policy generation in multi-agent contexts.
