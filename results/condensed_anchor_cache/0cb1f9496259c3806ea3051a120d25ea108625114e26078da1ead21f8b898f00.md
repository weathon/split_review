## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a point cloud generation framework using the Euler Characteristic Transform (ECT). The ECT provides a permutation-invariant representation; a VAE operates on ECT maps, and a learned MLP (ECT-MLP) inverts the ECT back to a point cloud, trained with Chamfer loss and a differentiable ECT-based loss. The method is evaluated on ShapeNet15K and MNIST point clouds, achieving performance close to some prior works but with inference that is “orders of magnitude faster.” Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of applying ECT to generation and the speed advantage, but raise serious concerns about limited novelty (the inversion step is seen as trivial, the ECT formulation is directly adopted from Röell & Rieck, 2024), insufficient comparisons (missing several recent diffusion/PVCNN-based baselines), mixed quantitative results (lagging behind in most settings), writing flaws, and the lack of an inherently equivariant network. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, highlighting thorough theoretical foundations and comprehensive experiments, while the other three (scores 3) find the contribution and performance insufficient.

### Strengths
- Novelty of applying ECT to generative tasks (point cloud generation). The paper is the first to demonstrate learning the inversion of the ECT for this purpose. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The idea of using topological concepts (ECT) for point clouds, which lack inherent topology, is seen as a good direction. (Reviewer 2)
- The model achieves significantly faster inference times (at least an order of magnitude) compared to previous methods, likely due to a simpler neural network. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The paper provides a thorough explanation of the ECT and its theoretical foundations (Reviewer 3 – minority positive opinion).
- Shows the possibility of learning an approximately equivariant representation via rotation augmentation (Figure 4). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- The framework is used for reconstruction, generation, and interpolation tasks. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and reliance on prior work:** The ECT formulation is directly adopted from (Röell & Rieck, 2024) without modification, and the writing/description is highly similar. The main contribution – an additional encoder to invert the ECT representation into a point cloud – is considered trivial. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 also notes the application is not extremely novel.)
- **Inadequate evaluation and missing baselines:** The paper omits several important recent baselines: LION (NeurIPS 2022), Autoregressive 3D Shape Generation via Canonical Mapping (ECCV 2022), Shape Generation and Completion Through Point-Voxel Diffusion (ICCV 2021), and Diffusion Probabilistic Models for 3D Point Cloud Generation (CVPR 2021). Some of these (e.g., LION) use diffusion models and should be compared. The authors claim that existing processing networks like PVCNN are unsuitable but provide no comparison, despite several baselines using PVCNN for point cloud generation. (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 3 notes that LION should be considered as a baseline.)
- **Mixed quantitative performance:** The proposed method lags behind prior works in almost all settings. Performance does not achieve the best on quantitative evaluations, and results do not show significant improvement over a saturated state-of-the-art. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4 – though Reviewer 3 calls experiments “comprehensive,” they also note the performance is not the best.)
- **Lack of inherent equivariance:** The ECT-MLP decoder is not permutation-equivariant, despite the ECT being permutation-invariant. This weakens the discussion on learning equivariant representations. (Reviewers 1, 4) Additionally, the rotation augmentation experiment (Figure 4) adds little value because any method can acquire equivariance through data augmentation, and no comparisons are provided. (Reviewer 1)
- **Mathematical rigor and writing issues:**
    - Over-generalization: the writing is inconsistent about dimensions (e.g., “spherical coordinate,” “voxel grid,” “scale cubically” are 3D-specific but later implies arbitrary dimensions). (Reviewer 1)
    - Factual errors: L161 says “We realise both of these steps using an MLP,” which is false – encoding uses the ECT algorithm. (Reviewer 1)
    - Inconsistencies: the role of the “encoder” in Figure 1 does not match the text in L161; broken sentence at L178. (Reviewer 1)
    - Lack of mathematical rigor: authors first describe ECT as an intrinsic property but then embed the simplicial complex in 3D coordinates, violating that notion. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reproducibility and missing details:** The resolution of the ECT map is not specified, creating confusion about the expected input shape of the ECT-MLP. (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 3 wants more insight on encoding/decoding mechanisms.)
- **Concerns about ECT’s effectiveness for point clouds:** Point clouds have no underlying topology beyond a set; the Euler Characteristic reduces to a trivial formula. Without an actual underlying simplicial complex, the power of ECT is significantly reduced – the method effectively classifies points based on depth with respect to view directions. This raises the question of how ECT differs from simply using the z-axis in arbitrary coordinate systems. (Reviewer 2)
- **Scale sensitivity and limited datasets:** The ECT is susceptible to scale differences; no ablation addresses this. Experiments are limited to three categories (chair, airplane, car) and MNIST; more diverse shapes with various topologies are needed to demonstrate generalization. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing discussion of failure cases:** The paper does not present limitations or failure cases. (Reviewer 3)
- **Large number of parameters:** The model has a comparatively large number of parameters, which could be a drawback; optimization is not explored. (Reviewer 3)
- **Only VAE tested:** The framework only uses a VAE as the generative model; other generative models (e.g., diffusion, autoregressive) could be applied but are not explored. (Reviewer 4)
- **Reproducibility questions:** Can the ECT-to-point cloud inversion be replaced by a non-parametric method (gradient descent using differentiable ECT)? How does the resolution of the ECT affect quality? Would other angle parameterizations (e.g., a spiral on the unit sphere) work? (Reviewer 1) What is the exact difference between using ECT and using simple depth-based features? (Reviewer 2)
