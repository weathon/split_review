## Merged Review

### Summary
ADVPAINT generates adversarial perturbations that degrade diffusion-model inpainting by disrupting self- and cross-attention blocks. It uses SAM to segment an image into foreground and background, then applies a two-stage perturbation strategy with an enlarged bounding box to improve robustness across mask shapes and sizes. Results show novel protection effects (foreground: “nothing in the masked regions”; background: a repetitive “blind spot” effect) and quantitative improvements in FID/LPIPS over prior methods.

### Strengths
- The writing is clear and easy to follow, with well‑presented method and good coverage of the literature.
- The protection effects are novel: foreground inpainting produces a “nothing” effect (instead of chaotic/noisy artifacts), and background inpainting creates a “blind spot” pattern that is repetitive and lacks harmony.  
- The problem of inpainting abuse is practically significant and timely; most prior work focused on image‑to‑image or text‑to‑image tasks, making the inpainting focus a valuable contribution.  
- The two‑stage perturbation strategy (separate target and background regions) enhances robustness across diverse mask shapes and sizes, a point especially emphasized by one reviewer (score 8) as a key strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited architecture evaluation.** The method was tested only on Stable Diffusion Inpainting. Its effectiveness on Transformer‑based diffusion architectures (e.g., SD3, Flux) – which use different patchify and norm mechanisms – is unknown. Even if crafting perturbations for these models requires modifications, testing the transfer of SD‑crafted perturbations would indicate robustness. (R1, R4)  
- **Sensitivity to prompt variations.** The protection appears to be effective only when the foreground inpainting prompt is a noun phrase and when the background prompt explicitly includes the foreground object. An attacker can easily modify the prompt (e.g., replace “sunflower” with “person”, or omit the object from the background prompt). Such variations may cause the “nothing” or “blind spot” effects to disappear, possibly making simpler noisy artifacts from prior methods more advantageous. Experiments with a wide range of prompts are needed. (R1)  
- **Assumptions about the inpainting target.** The method assumes the SAM‑segmented object is exactly the region the adversary will inpaint. In multi‑object images, users might target different objects or combinations. Experiments where the inpainting mask does not overlap or only partially overlaps with the SAM‑defined target are missing. (R2, R4)  
- **Limited robustness against countermeasures.** The paper does not test whether the perturbations survive common preprocessing such as Gaussian noise, JPEG compression, super‑resolution, or more advanced purification methods (e.g., from works [2,3]). Since prior work shows such noise can be easily disturbed, resilience against purification is more critical than raw effectiveness. (R2, R3)  
- **Insufficient detail about experimental setup.**  
  - It is unclear how `m^{out}` (Section 5.6) relates to the masks used during optimization – does it exceed all training masks? (R3)  
  - Section A.2.2 tests different models for optimizing perturbations, but the model used to actually perform the inpainting is not specified. (R3)  
  - The paper does not specify whether Figures 7 and 8 evaluate image‑to‑image tasks, text‑to‑image tasks, or both, making interpretation confusing. (R3)  
- **Lack of analysis for key parameters.** The experiments use a fixed noise budget and fixed PGD iteration count. How robustness changes with different noise magnitudes and iteration counts is not explored, which limits understanding of the protection’s operating range. (R4)  
- **Predominantly white‑box evaluation.** Most experiments appear to use the same diffusion model for both optimizing perturbations and performing inpainting. No comparison is provided when the models differ (e.g., SD v1.4 vs. v1.5), which would be more realistic since an adversary can choose arbitrary models. (R3)  
- **Complex pre‑processing steps not robustly analyzed.** The method relies on ChatGPT for prompt generation and SAM for segmentation. The paper does not assess how different prompt‑generation models or segmentation algorithms affect the quality and consistency of the adversarial noise. A sensitivity analysis is missing. (R2)  
- **Insufficient related‑work comparison on attention‑disruption.** There is a similar prior work [1] that also disrupts attention layers. The precise technical differences – which layers are targeted (cross‑ vs. self‑attention) and the optimization strategy – are not clearly discussed. (R2)  
- **Memory and runtime not reported.** No information is given on the computational cost (time and memory) of generating ADVPAINT perturbations. (R1)
