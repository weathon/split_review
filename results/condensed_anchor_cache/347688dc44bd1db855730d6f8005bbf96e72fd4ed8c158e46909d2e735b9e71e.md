## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes the reconstruction error of sparse autoencoders (SAEs) applied to language model activations. It proposes a decomposition of the SAE error into unlearned sparse linear features, residual dense features, and a nonlinear error component that persists as SAEs scale. The authors measure these components empirically on Gemma-2 9B (using GemmaScope SAEs) via linear prediction of the error from activations, study per-token scaling behavior, evaluate downstream cross-entropy loss impact, and test two methods to reduce the nonlinear error: inference-time gradient pursuit and linear transformations from earlier-layer SAE outputs.

### Strengths
- The core idea of decomposing SAE reconstruction error into interpretable components (unlearned features, dense features, nonlinear error) is novel and could inform the debate on the linear representation hypothesis and guide training of better SAEs. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The analysis is thorough: the theoretical decomposition is sensible, scaling laws are well described, and the methodology is sound. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper examines SAE error from multiple aspects: automated interpretability, FVU, loss measurements, per-token scaling behavior, and downstream effects. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The investigation into methods to reduce nonlinear error (gradient pursuit, cross-layer SAE reconstructions) is a useful addition. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 2, score 5):** This reviewer considered the paper solid progress on the faithfulness problem (i) because it asks why SAEs are limited, and praised the range of analyses.

### Weaknesses
- **Only one model and one layer tested.** The empirical analysis is limited to Gemma-2 9B and a single layer, making the findings a single data point. It is unclear whether the results generalize to other models, layers, or SAE training algorithms. (Reviewer 1)  
  – A related missing ablations: testing different SAE architectures/sparsity penalties (e.g., SAE with no sparsity penalty to capture dense features in superposition) is not done. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Central definitions and exposition are unclear, making the paper very hard to follow.** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)  
  * Notation issues: signs in definitions inconsistent (e.g., `SaeError(x)` uses `-Sae(x)`, `NonlinearError` uses `+Sae(x)`); vector `a` vs matrix `A`; summation indices in Equations (3) and (4) are incorrect; `Wx` is introduced with poor motivation and later dropped without explanation. (Reviewers 2, 4)  
  * The decomposition in Section 3 is not clearly delineated: naming of components is inconsistent; the relationship between "linear component" and `Dense(x)` (which may be nonlinear) is confusing; the grouping of dense features and linear error in Figure 1 is questionable. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)  
  * The experiment description in the first paragraph of page 4 (norm prediction test) lacks intuition and clarity. (Reviewer 4)  
  * Ambiguous phrasing: "most common" (line 146), "its existence is not guaranteed", "likely" (line 240). (Reviewers 2, 3)  
  * The synthetic setup in Section 4.1 is verbose, the Gaussian noise simulation is unrealistic (activations may lie on a curved manifold; Dense and NonlinearError could be correlated; Gaussian support is inappropriate), and the claim about variance explained being an upper bound lacks rigorous derivation or assumption hedging. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
  * The justification for the sanity check in Section 4.1 (correlation between estimated linear and nonlinear errors) is unclear; the paper does not explain why this test is necessary. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Conclusions are drawn too strongly without proper hedging.**  
  * The paper states that nonlinear error norm is constant per token level as SAE width scales, but this definition of `NonlinearError` depends on the estimation method and may not capture true nonlinear error. No hedging is included in the conclusion. (Reviewer 2)  
  * The methods may be picking up on shrinkage artifacts (appendix suggests ~10% shrinkage, which could be boosted 10× by predictors), so the estimated linear/nonlinear error components may not be faithful. The paper does not address this or state assumptions (e.g., that the SAE is sufficiently good at reconstructing). (Reviewer 2)  
- **Missing details about the SAE training and setup.** The paper cites GemmaScope but does not describe the SAE training process, sparsity penalty, architecture choices, or whether the SAE was trained from scratch. These details are essential for reproducibility and assessing generality. (Reviewer 1)
- **Several presentation issues and missing information:**  
  * Figure 1: labels and equations in the figure are unexplained; what part is “dark matter”? Where did the data come from? (Reviewer 1)  
  * Equation (1): is `w` random or a coordinate of `x` in the basis? Is `||w||_1 << d` correct? Couldn’t one component be much larger? (Reviewer 1)  
  * Line 196: synthetic setup details missing. (Reviewer 1)  
  * What is `L_0`? (Reviewer 1)  
  * Figure 3b: what is it supposed to show? (Reviewer 1)  
  * Figure 4 caption is incomplete and unclear. (Reviewer 1)  
  * Last equation on line 490: what does it say? (Reviewer 1)  
  * Appendix A: needs introduction (what is the proof trying to show, what does it imply); why is `λ=1` special, and what does `ρ=0.73` conclude? Is `d > m`? Define WLOG. (Reviewer 1)  
  * Appendix B: report results quantitatively instead of just qualitative description. (Reviewer 1)  
  * Does Appendix C have a reference in the main text? (Reviewer 1)  
  * Citation formatting errors (e.g., broken author name “Team” in Gemma reference, missing `\citep`/`\citet`). (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
  * Stray commas and typographical errors. (Reviewer 4)
