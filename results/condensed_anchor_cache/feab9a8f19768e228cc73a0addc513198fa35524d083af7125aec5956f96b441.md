## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Consistency Verification (ConV), a training-free detection framework that identifies AI-generated images by checking consistency between two functions designed to be consistent for natural images but inconsistent for AI-generated ones. ConV avoids training on generated images by leveraging a self-supervised model (DinoV2) pre-trained on natural images. Reviewers agree on the potential of the training-free setting but raise major concerns about novelty relative to baseline RIGID, theoretical justification, missing implementation details, limited backbone evaluation, and insufficient experimental superiority.

### Strengths
- The underlying mathematical intuition is well-presented (Reviewer 1).
- The method is novel to Reviewer 1 and its effectiveness is supported by a collection of experiments (Reviewer 1).
- It eliminates the need for large datasets of generated images and the computational cost of training a binary classifier (Reviewer 2).
- The orthogonality principle provides some theoretical intuition (Reviewer 2).
- Using a pre-trained foundation model for prediction instead of exhaustive training on AI-generated or real data is appealing (Reviewer 3).
- The experiment on the SORA benchmark is interesting (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The method is very close to the baseline RIGID. It only replaces cosine similarity with L2 distance and Gaussian augmentation with training augmentations (Gaussian blur, color jitter, etc.), without insightful design beyond that change (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 3 explicitly states this feels like an ablation study of RIGID, and contributions should go to the original paper. Reviewer 2 adds that the paper does not explain why these augmentations are better than Gaussian noise.
- **Insufficient theoretical justification**: A formal proof of convergence of ConV’s generalization risk is missing (Reviewer 1). The orthogonality principle provides intuition but lacks rigorous analysis: there is no explanation for why DinoV2’s training augmentations are orthogonal to its standard output, nor guidance for choosing augmentations. The concept of ‘local tangent space’ is not clearly defined, and it is unclear whether the chosen augmentations move data *along* or *off* the manifold, which could invalidate the orthogonality argument (Reviewer 2). The impact of different augmentation strengths or combinations is not discussed (Reviewer 2). Section 2 (2.1–2.3) overexplains general manifold hypothesis without deeper explanation of why the consistency form is specifically necessary (Reviewer 3).
- **Sensitivity to initialization / backbone choice**: ConV shows a large performance gap between CLIP and DinoV2 instantiations (Table 6), raising robustness concerns (Reviewer 1). The method is only tested on DinoV2 and not on other self-supervised models, leaving generalizability unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing implementation details for augmentations**: The paper does not specify parameters for data augmentations (e.g., color jitter ranges, blur kernel size, probability of each augmentation), nor whether single or multiple augmentations are applied, making reproduction difficult (Reviewer 2).
- **Weak experimental results**: The proposed method does not outperform RIGID in all cases; it should at least outperform RIGID across all AI-generated data, not only on average (Reviewer 3).
- **Discrepancy in RIGID’s reported performance**: The results for RIGID, especially on ImageNet, are deteriorated compared to the original paper. This is puzzling because using larger \( n=20 \) should improve performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity issue**: Line 135’s phrasing (“if a natural image \(x_n\) captures the same content as \(x_g\), they are distinguishable”) is confusing; it should clarify that subtle differences still make them distinguishable (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority contrast**: Reviewer 1 rated the paper 6 (positive overall), while Reviewers 2 and 3 rated it 3 (weak). The latter two emphasize limited novelty and weak experiments, whereas Reviewer 1 finds the method novel and well-presented but acknowledges sensitivity and theoretical gaps.
- **Outstanding questions that overlap with weaknesses or missing experiments**: How to overcome sensitivity to initialization function (Reviewer 1)? Can the method be generalized to other self-supervised models beyond DinoV2 (Reviewer 2)? Why are training augmentations better than Gaussian noise (Reviewer 2)? Why do RIGID’s results differ from the original paper (Reviewer 3)? Why is the data processing different for GenImage (cropping only) vs. ImageNet (resize then crop) (Reviewer 3)? How does AEROBLADE perform on the GenImage dataset (table has typos) (Reviewer 3)? What is the runtime of the ensemble-heavy method (Reviewer 3)?
