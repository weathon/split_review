- Decision: Reject

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DreamFuser, a trajectory-based diffusion policy for offline RL that integrates the diffusion denoising process into the MDP as hidden transitions (GNMDP), jointly training a conditional diffusion model and a Q-function. Three reviewers assessed the paper: two scored it 3 (fair/poor), citing clarity issues, insufficient analysis, and incremental novelty; one scored it 6 (good), finding the method novel and algorithmically sound but still noting that the advance feels incremental.

### Strengths
- The idea of incorporating the diffusion process into the MDP as hidden transitions is creative and enables more efficient policy learning (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The method is algorithmically sound and supported by comparisons to multiple prior methods (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical results are strong: DreamFuser outperforms baselines on D4RL and robotic manipulation tasks with consistent gains (Reviewer 2) and matches or outperforms contemporary methods (Reviewer 1).
- Ablation studies clearly demonstrate the value of Q-learning, sequence modeling, and dynamics modeling (Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 3 specifically noted the algorithm is novel to their knowledge, and Reviewer 1 found the concept interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation**:
  - Inference is not clearly described: no pseudocode in the main paper; it is unclear whether the Q-value is used at test time or only the diffusion policy is sampled (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 1 is placed in Section 4.1 but explained in Section 4.2, causing layout confusion (Reviewer 1).
  - Section 4.2 is confusing: the described dynamics imply corrected formulas for the consistency loss (should use $\mathbb{E}_{A_t^{k-2}\sim \pi(\cdot|\hat{s}')}[Q_\phi(\hat{s}', A_t^{k-2})]$) and for the MSBE loss (should use $Q_\phi(\hat{s}, \epsilon)$) (Reviewer 1).
  - The last term in equation (9) is unclear; $\epsilon_{t,k}$ in equation (10) is not defined (Reviewer 1).
  - TD3 is mentioned but the losses do not reflect it; details are only in Appendix B.1 (Reviewer 1).
  - Counting down from $K$ to $0$ for diffusion steps conflicts with typical timestep notation; both should increment in the same direction (Reviewer 3).
  - Grammatical errors appear after Section 1 (Reviewer 3).
  - Equation (15) is missing $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$ (Reviewer 1).
  - The introduction mentions a learned dynamic model, but this is only briefly treated in Section 5; Appendices B.2–3 unexpectedly introduce GRU, transformers, and $K_{inference}=4$ (used independently of chosen $K$), causing confusion (Reviewer 1).

- **Methodological concerns**:
  - Loss formulas do not reconcile with the GNMDP formalism presented (Reviewer 1).
  - How does the MDP formulation in Section 4.1 differ from a POMDP? Why is it not described as a particular subtype of POMDP? (Reviewer 3)
  - What are the downsides of the GNMDP formulation? It seems likely to make action/Q-value gradients more noisy and slow learning due to temporal extension/noisy credit assignment (Reviewer 3).
  - The justification for multi-action prediction is insufficient: why does predicting a whole trajectory improve forward action prediction accuracy at test time without forward dynamics? Does it benefit supervised training stability? (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper combines elements from prior work (Diffusion Policy, Diffuser); the conceptual advance appears incremental without deeper analysis (Reviewers 2, 3). One positive reviewer (Reviewer 3) specifically noted that the paper does not clearly justify why the algorithm is radically better than prior methods or how it moves the field forward.

- **Experimental shortcomings**:
  - Results are computed with only 3 seeds (found only in Appendix) and no confidence intervals are reported, making relative performance hard to assess (Reviewer 1).
  - No analysis of increased computational costs and inference times from modeling longer action sequences; trade-offs should be quantified (Reviewer 2).
  - Optimal sequence length likely varies across tasks; more ablation on diverse tasks is needed. Adaptive adjustment of sequence length could reduce manual tuning burden (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing essential comparisons: DreamFuser is not compared to Diffusion Policy on D4RL mujoco and antmaze tasks (Reviewer 2). In-depth analysis against prior diffusion methods (DD, Diffuser) using similar-length sequences is lacking (Reviewer 2).
  - Figures 2(a) and (b) only show results for sequence lengths 1 and 7; values in between are not examined (Reviewer 1).
  - Figures 2(a) and (c) exhibit strange behavior in learning curves that is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
  - The claim in Section 5.2 that DreamFuser “converges to optimal actions” is not justified (Reviewer 1).
  - The method’s success on “umaze” tasks in particular (Section 5.2) is not explained; what is the connection to the diffusion process? (Reviewer 1)
  - In Figure 2(c), the overfitting issue without the dynamics model is not compared to other offline RL methods like CQL; it is unclear whether most offline RL methods also suffer overfitting late in training (Reviewer 3).
  - The name “DreamFuser” is not explained; the “dream” part is unclear (Reviewer 3).
