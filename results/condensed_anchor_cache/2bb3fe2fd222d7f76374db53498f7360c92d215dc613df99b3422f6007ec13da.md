## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the joint problem of graph matching and clustering under a heterogeneous set of graphs (mixture graph matching and clustering, MGMC). The authors propose M3C, a learning-free algorithm based on the Minorize-Maximization (MM) framework with theoretical convergence guarantees and relaxed clustering. They extend this to UM3C, an unsupervised model that learns edge-wise affinities and uses pseudo-label selection. Experiments on public benchmarks show accuracy and efficiency gains over prior graph matching and MGMC methods. The framework is general and uses both node and edge attributes.

### Strengths
- The problem of jointly matching and clustering graphs is sound, novel, and addresses a practical scenario where graphs exhibit multiple modes, making the work important for real-world applications.
- The MM-based optimization algorithm enforces convergence (objective non-decreasing) and achieves fast convergence in practice; the theoretical analysis is well written (though largely classical).
- The framework is general: it combines supervised and unsupervised components, can use both node and edge attributes, and relaxes hard cluster assignments (which can capture inter-cluster confusion).
- Numerical experiments are well conducted and show adequate performance on the two datasets used (all graphs <20 nodes). A large variety of experiments is provided, both in the main text and appendices, demonstrating efficacy over state-of-the-art methods.
- The paper is mostly clear and easy to read; the exposition of the joint formulation and the MM derivation is logical.

### Weaknesses
- **Need for joint approach is not convincingly justified.** Several reviewers questioned why solving matching and clustering jointly is necessary compared to a sequential pipeline (e.g., cluster then match, or use optimal transport). No ablation experiment is provided to verify the advantage of joint optimization over such baselines, and the paper does not discuss situations where the joint approach is essential or beneficial. The examples are limited to image graphs; the necessity for more complex graphs (larger, non-image) is not demonstrated.
- **Comparison with state-of-the-art is incomplete.** The most recent competitor in the main comparisons is MGM-Floyd (2021); many recent deep-learning methods (SIGMA, Universe Points Representation Learning, DGMC) and alternative pairwise solvers (KerGM, GWL) are missing. All compared methods are from the same research group. This raises concerns about whether the claimed improvements hold against a broader and more modern SOTA.
- **Scalability concern due to Lawler’s QAP formulation.** The pairwise matching relies on QAP, which does not scale beyond small graphs (the tested graphs have <20 nodes). No discussion or evaluation on larger graphs is provided, limiting applicability to real-world settings.
- **Unsupervised claim is partially contradicted.** UM3C still requires the hand-crafted affinity matrix \(K^{raw}\) as input, which is a form of supervision. How \(K^{raw}\) is derived and its impact on the unsupervised nature is not explained. A reviewer also noted that the method is not fully end-to-end and that the deep learning pipeline (VGG-16 features, edge feature construction) is not clearly described.
- **Theoretical convergence analysis needs more rigor.** The statement that the objective is non-decreasing (Eq. 3) alone does not guarantee convergence; the finite solution space of \((X,C)\) may allow multiple optimal solutions and oscillation. Proposition 4.1 uses \(N_{g_i}\) without definition and assumes cluster sizes remain constant between iterations – a strict condition. The claim of "quick convergence" (Appendix C.3) lacks a convergence rate analysis.
- **Heuristic/underexplained components.**
  - The parameter \(r\) for selecting pseudo-labels (Eq. 6) is not systematically chosen; its selection is heuristic and can significantly influence performance.
  - The greedy search for top-\(rN^2\) values (Section 4.2) may break transitive constraints (e.g., \(c_{ik}=1,c_{kj}=1\) but \(c_{ij}\neq1\)), and no justification is given for why disregarding transitivity is beneficial.
  - The formulation in Eq. (2) appears ad hoc, lacking a derivation or discussion of alternatives.
  - The supergraph construction and the methods for solving the two hard subproblems (Eqs. 6 and 7) are not described in sufficient detail; it is unclear whether heuristics are needed.
- **Initialization sensitivity not discussed.** The framework requires initialization with a classical pairwise matching method (e.g., RRWM). No analysis is provided on how the choice of initialization (or its quality) affects final performance, nor are alternatives (KerGM, GWL) considered.
- **Notation and presentation issues.**
  - In Definition 2, variables \(k_1,k_2,\dots\) and \(N\) are used without definition; the multiplication of matrices \(X_{ik_1}\) and \(X_{k_1k_2}\) is given without verifying shape compatibility.
  - The vertex set \(\mathcal{V}\) is defined as a set of graphs, which is unconventional.
  - Some equations are numbered and referenced but not used in the main text (e.g., Eq. (18) uses inconsistent notation for iteration).
  - Figures 1 and 2 contain too many elements, making them unclear.
- **Minor/other.**
  - The literature review lacks works from the last two years.
  - The link between image graphs and general graphs is not established; it is unclear if the method extends beyond the image domain.
  - \(K\) (the affinity matrix) is not defined on page 1; \(\Lambda\) is not defined in Section 5.1.

### Reviewer Disagreement
Two reviewers rated the paper 6 (fair) and two rated it 8 (good). The lower-score reviewers emphasized the lack of compelling justification for a joint approach, incomplete comparisons, and presentation/notation flaws. The higher-score reviewers found the framework general and the contributions solid despite some missing SOTA comparisons, and they rated presentation higher (one rated 4 excellent, the other 3 good). The merged review reflects both perspectives, conservatively retaining criticisms from the minority more critical reviews.
