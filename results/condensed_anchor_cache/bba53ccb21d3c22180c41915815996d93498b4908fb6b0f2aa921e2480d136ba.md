## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses privacy leakage in in-context learning (ICL) for LLMs by introducing AdaDPSyn, a data-adaptive differentially private algorithm that generates synthetic examples from a private dataset. Building on the DP ICL framework of Tang et al. (2023), AdaDPSyn adaptively adjusts noise during data synthesis based on the statistical properties of the data, using a Precision-Focused Iterative Radius Reduction technique to refine the aggregation radius. Experiments show that AdaDPSyn outperforms the DP-few-shot generation baseline and achieves accuracy close to non-private ICL baselines. Reviewers concur the problem is important and the approach is technically interesting, but raise concerns about limited baselines, insufficient ablation, heavy reliance on prior work, computational cost, and unclear impact of privacy parameters.

### Strengths
- **Important problem**: Mitigating privacy risks from jailbreak attacks that leak prompt examples in ICL is a timely and significant challenge (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Novel data-adaptive technique**: AdaDPSyn improves over Tang et al. (2023) by making synthetic token generation data-adaptive rather than applying uniform noise, leading to substantially better private ICL performance (Reviewer 1). The dynamic radius update for private aggregation is interesting (Reviewer 4).
- **Strong empirical results**: AdaDPSyn demonstrates significant improvements over the DP-few-shot generation baseline across a wide range of benchmarks and approaches non-private ICL performance (Reviewers 1, 2 [S1]).
- **Sound theoretical analysis**: The paper correctly uses amplification by subsampling (Wang et al., 2019) instead of Poisson subsampling used by Tang et al. (2023) (Reviewer 3).
- **Good presentation**: The paper is well organized, well-written, and ideas are easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2 [S2], 3).
- **Technical interest**: The direction of data-adaptive techniques to improve privacy-utility tradeoffs is important for broader DP adoption (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baseline comparison**: Only one baseline (“DP few-shot generation” from Tang et al. 2023) is used; the paper would benefit from comparisons with other DP ICL or DP fine-tuning approaches (Reviewer 2 [W1], Reviewer 1 suggests investigating direct DP fine-tuning vs. synthetic ICL samples as future work).
- **Iterative aspect underutilized**: The iterative parameter \(\hat{T}\) is frequently set to 1, limiting the significance of the iterative radius reduction (Reviewer 2 [W2]).
- **Heavy reliance on prior work**: The overall contribution is a modification to the existing Tang et al. (2023) framework by adding a data-adaptive step to private aggregation; novelty is thus incremental (Reviewer 3 [W1]). Minority opinion: Reviewer 1 rated contribution 3 and novelty higher (score 8), while others rated contribution 2–3 (scores 6), indicating disagreement on the extent of originality.
- **Many new hyperparameters without ablation**: The method introduces four parameters (\(\hat{T}, \lambda, \sigma_0, \sigma_2\)) beyond Tang et al. (2023), requiring additional tuning, yet no ablation studies are provided for \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_2\), making their effect on the privacy-utility tradeoff unclear (Reviewer 3 [W2]).
- **Insufficient ablation studies**:
  - For varying LLMs, only one instruction-tuned closed-source model (GPT-3.5/4o mini) is included alongside pre-trained Llama-2-7b; results for instruction-tuned Llama-2-7b are missing (Reviewer 3 [W3]).
  - When testing different LLMs, models of similar scale are not compared (e.g., Llama-2-7b vs. Gemma-7b). GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4o mini may not be of comparable parameter scale to Llama-2-7b (Reviewer 3 [W5]).
  - No ablation varying model size, as done by Tang et al. (2023) (Reviewer 3 [W5]).
  - Zero-shot performance for GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4o mini in the varying LLMs ablation is not reported (Reviewer 3 [W4]).
- **Missing details on center values**: The choice of center values and how they are selected for experiments in Table 1 is not clarified (Reviewer 3).
- **No guidance on privacy budget allocation**: It is unclear how to optimally split the overall privacy budget across the noise-consuming steps in Algorithm 1 (Reviewer 1).
- **High computational cost**: Synthesizing one DP in-context sample requires \(M\) queries to the LLM, which is expensive; the paper lacks any computational cost analysis or time comparison with baselines (Reviewer 4 [W1]).
- **Inadequate comparison of generated vs. real private samples**: Only DP samples at \(\epsilon=4\) are shown without direct comparison to the original private samples. Results for other \(\epsilon\) values and their comparison with real samples should be included (Reviewer 4 [W2]).
- **Lack of exploration of epsilon impact**: The paper does not investigate how varying \(\epsilon\) affects the quality of generated DP samples and downstream ICL performance. It is unclear whether the chosen \(\epsilon\) is optimal or whether smaller \(\epsilon\) values would significantly degrade utility (Reviewer 4 [W3]).
- **Conceptual justification unclear**: The rationale for why privacy of ICL examples should be considered when the examples are already sent to the LLM (i.e., who is being protected from whom) is not clearly addressed (Reviewer 4).
