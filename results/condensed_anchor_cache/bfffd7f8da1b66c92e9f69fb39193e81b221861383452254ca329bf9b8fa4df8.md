## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes NormIntSleep, a two-stage interpretable sleep staging method that first trains a deep neural network (CNN+LSTM) on sleep stage classification, learns a linear projector from its embeddings onto a set of hand-crafted clinical features (FeatShort), and then trains an interpretable glass-box model on the projected features. The authors also introduce a metric, Alignment_DT, to quantify how well a decision tree’s nodes align with clinical domain knowledge (AASM). Results on two public datasets show NormIntSleep achieves 0.814–0.847 accuracy, 0.787–0.793 F1, 0.759–0.788 κ, and the highest Alignment_DT score. Reviewers generally agree on the importance of the problem and the extensive experimental effort, but have major concerns about interpretability guarantees, clarity, generalisability of the metric, and missing details. One reviewer (R1, score 5) found the paper well written and the results valuable, while the other three (R2, R3, R4, score 3 each) raised substantial weaknesses, particularly regarding the method’s interpretability soundness and presentation.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses a relevant and important problem: providing clinically interpretable sleep stage classification to foster trust in machine learning for healthcare. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Extensive experiments were performed on two public datasets with many baselines, and the authors adapted prior methods for fair comparison. (R1, R3, R4)
- The Interpretable Decision Tree (Section 5.1, Figure 2) offers a practical way to explain sleep stages using domain knowledge. (R2)
- The paper is generally well motivated, and the results clearly show a trade-off between performance and interpretability. (R1, R3)
- The work represents an interesting line of research, and reporting such results is valuable for the community. (R1)

### Weaknesses

- **Interpretability–performance trade‑off is ill‑posed.** If the linear projector perfectly reconstructs the clinical features, downstream performance equals feature‑based models (and is worse than black‑box). If regression error increases, performance may improve, but the projections no longer faithfully represent the intended features, undermining interpretability guarantees. (R3)
- This concern is echoed: it is not clear why a glass‑box model trained on *predicted* FeatShort features (NormIntSleep) outperforms the same model trained on *ground‑truth* FeatShort features. Requested: cross‑prediction experiments (train on ground truth, test on prediction and vice versa) and the missing FeatShort‑LogisticRegression baseline in Table 3. (R1)
- **The Alignment_DT metric is only applicable to decision trees**, limiting its utility when the best‑performing glass‑box model is not a tree. (R2, R4)
- **Fairness of comparing Alignment_DT across methods** is questionable: NormIntSleep’s features are constructed from the same domain‑knowledge set used in the metric, while FeatLong features are not, creating a potential double‑dipping effect. (R1)
- **Insufficient evidence that interpretations from NormIntSleep are clinically meaningful.** Only the decision tree (not the best performer) was shown to a clinician; only SHAP was used for feature importance; comparisons with other feature‑based interpretable methods are missing. (R2)
- **Clarity and presentation issues** in the Methods section: the ordering of sections (dataset introduced before method), the term “epoch” confusing with neural network training, “glass‑box model” not defined, notations overloaded, and Figure 1 hard to follow (multiple steps unclear, colour coding ambiguous). (R1, R3, R4)
- **The new metric (Equation 1) is poorly defined.** It remains unclear what “aligned with clinical domain knowledge” means concretely (how are thresholds used? compared to AASM Manual?). (R1, R3)
- **Missing details on the hypnogram:** how it is used alongside interpretable representations (Section 3.2, Figure 1). (R2, R4)
- **No confidence intervals or standard errors** reported for any results, making it hard to assess statistical significance. (R1, R3)
- **Lack of related work** – clear connections to partial concept bottleneck models are not discussed, and the context in the field is insufficient. (R3)
- **Limited technical contribution:** the DNN architecture (CNN+LSTM) is standard; the FeatShort features are adapted from prior work (Al‑Hussaini & Mitchell, 2022). (R4)
- **Missing code** to reproduce experiments. (R1)
- **Minor issues:** In Algorithm 2, line 9, \(R'\) vs \(R\) is unexplained (similarly \(T'\) in Algorithm 1); Table 3 would be easier with features/glass‑box model split; the best method is not consistently bolded in tables; in the ablation study, differences between Least Squares and Linear Regression (sklearn) are unclear. (R1, R3)
- **Questions about experimental choices:** How was the ridge regression regularisation parameter chosen? How were final learning/scaling schemas selected? How is the cross‑entropy loss combined with regression loss in pre‑training (e.g., simple average)? Did a single clinician manually compute the Alignment metric, or was there consensus? (R1, R4)
- **Generalisation** of the approach beyond sleep staging is questionable, especially since Alignment_DT cannot be used when the best glass‑box model is not a decision tree. (R2, R3)
