## Merged Review

### Summary
CircuitNet 2.0 is a large-scale public dataset for ML in EDA, extending the earlier CircuitNet. It contains over 10,000 samples from CPU, GPU, and AI chip designs, fabricated using advanced 14 nm FinFET technology. The dataset provides multi-modal features and labels for routability, timing, and power prediction tasks, and includes realistic ML tasks (imbalanced data, transfer learning) to demonstrate its potential. Reviewers agree that the dataset is valuable for advancing ML in EDA, but raise concerns about limited design diversity (only eight base designs), potential overfitting, missing end‑to‑end performance evaluation, and the fact that prediction tasks are not final chip‑design decisions.

### Strengths
- **More design types** than CircuitNet (CPU, GPU, AI chip) – enriches diversity and better approximates the chip‑design distribution (R1, R2).
- Samples are **absolutely different** from CircuitNet, allowing simultaneous use to train better models (R1).
- Uses **advanced 14 nm FinFET** technology (R1, R2).
- Supports **three prediction tasks** (routability, IR‑drop, timing); timing is new compared to CircuitNet (R1, R2).
- Provides a **complete dataset-generation flow** along with EDA tool flows, inspiring future ML‑EDA dataset work (R2).
- **Realistic ML tasks** (imbalanced data, transfer learning) are reasonable, sound, and verify the dataset’s potential (R2, R3).
- **Rich design types** (CPU, GPU, AI chip) for practical prediction tasks (R3).
- **Dataset files are very complete**, helping researchers use them (R4).
- Includes **analysis of the dataset** (e.g., data imbalance, possible usage) (R4).
- Data acquisition is **expensive** (some designs take over a week) – the dataset saves researchers time and money (R4).
- Development of a large-scale public dataset is **crucial for ML advancement in EDA** (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Overfitting risk**: each sample in the same design has identical numbers of cells, nets, macros, pins, and IOs, making the dataset unsuitable for many ML approaches; authors lack discussion of overfitting and its negative impacts (R1).
- **MLP vs. GNN comparison doubt**: in Section 4.1.2, MLP performance is lower than GNN, but the MLP design appears arbitrary – this is a minor weakness (R1).
- **Typo**: line 12 in Section 4.3 has two “introduce” (R1).
- **Missing experiment details** (reviewer questions overlapping criticisms):
  - What is the total generation time and what machines were used? (R1)
  - What are the congestion‑prediction results on the training set (e.g., NRMSE and SSIM for CPU cases when using CPUs as training data)? (R1)
  - What do “details” in Table A.2 mean? What are the numbers below? (R1)
  - Is the learning rate the same in the transfer‑learning experiment (Figure 6)? (R1)
  - In the congestion and IR‑drop experiments comparing CircuitNet 2.0 vs. CircuitNet, what are the “unseen designs in the test set”? (R1)
  - Will the trained parameters of timing, routing, and IR‑drop prediction models be open‑sourced to reproduce Table 3, B1, B2? (R4)
- **Only pure ML predictions evaluated** – the paper does not show overall performance when the ML model is combined with classical heuristic optimization methods; training ML models is costly, and it may not be beneficial at every P&R stage (R2). (Also request for examples of overall performance benefit – R2.)
- **Limited design diversity**: the 10,000+ samples come from only eight open‑source designs (especially three smaller CPU designs); samples from the same design likely share similar features, so the dataset may not be as diverse as claimed. Models may overfit to characteristics of the few base designs, limiting real‑world applicability (R3). *(Minority reviewer R3, score 5, is more critical on this point.)*
- **Unrealistic design‑flow settings**: most adjustments were made in the floorplan and power‑plan stages; some parameter combinations may be unreasonable compared to industrial designs, potentially yielding unrealistic scenarios and reducing practical usefulness (R3).
- **Missing t‑SNE information**: the t‑SNE plot in Section 4.2.2 does not show where the GPU and AI Chip samples are (R3).
- **Transfer‑learning training time**: how long does 20,000 iterations take? (R3)
- **Multi‑modal advantage unverified**: in Section 4.1, only graph‑based input is used – the advantage of multi‑modal training is not shown (R3).
- **Incremental extension of CircuitNet**: CircuitNet 2.0 can be seen as an extension with few new features/labels easily obtained from Synopsys tools; both versions have ≈10,000 designs, and doubling the amount of data typically yields limited improvement in prediction accuracy (R4).
- **Prediction tasks ≠ final chip‑design decisions**: the most significant EDA tasks are decision tasks, so the dataset’s impact on real chip design remains limited (R4).
