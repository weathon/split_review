## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes RL4DViT, a multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework using MAPPO for dynamic token selection in Vision Transformers (ViT). Each token is treated as an agent that decides to keep or discard itself. Experiments on ImageNet-1K with DeiT-B report a 39% reduction in GFLOPs while losing only 0.17% top-1 accuracy. Reviewers’ scores range from 1 to 5 (mean 3), reflecting strong disagreement on novelty, experimental thoroughness, and justification of the MARL formulation.

### Strengths
- The idea of modeling token selection as a multi-agent RL problem (Markov Game) is interesting, reasonable, and novel. This is the first work to integrate RL/MARL for dynamic token reduction in ViTs.
- The motivation for pruning tokens to reduce ViT computational cost is clear, and an RL-based decision-making approach is a plausible solution.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the MARL method (MAPPO) and the MDP formulation.
- Results on ImageNet-1K show that the proposed method reduces computational cost (39% GFLOPs reduction) with only a small accuracy drop (0.17%), demonstrating effectiveness.
- Compared with DynamicViT and A-ViT, the proposed method performs slightly better.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing justification for multi-agent RL vs. single-agent RL**: The paper does not explore or discuss why a multi-agent formulation is needed. With parameter sharing, a single-agent PPO predicting a binary mask could be a simpler alternative. Reviewers ask for an explicit comparison (including IPPO as a baseline) to justify the multi-agent framework. This is the most common concern.
- **Insufficient experiments across backbones and datasets**: Only DeiT-B on ImageNet-1K is tested. No results on DeiT-S, DeiT-T, other ViT architectures (Swin, LV-ViT), or other datasets (CIFAR-10/100). This raises doubts about generalizability and whether the method exploits ImageNet-specific simplicity.
- **Missing important related works and baselines**: Several key token reduction methods are not cited or compared: EViT, ATS, SPViT, ToMe, and many token pruning/merging works. The paper should discuss differences and advantages over these methods, especially on token selection strategy.
- **Lack of real runtime measurements**: Only theoretical GFLOPs are reported. Actual inference time (including overhead from reshaping, in-memory selection, etc.) should be provided, as GFLOPs can misrepresent efficiency.
- **Trivial performance gain over existing methods**: DynamicViT (2021) achieves 81.3% top-1 with 11.2 GFLOPs, while the proposed method (81.38%, 11.6 GFLOPs) is not clearly superior. More recent methods are even stronger, so the advantage of using RL is not demonstrated.
- **Insufficient qualitative and quantitative analysis**: Figure 2 does not show token selection for baseline methods, making it hard to assess the quality of selection. No quantitative analysis (e.g., selection patterns, ablation of MARL components) is provided to explain why MARL outperforms other selection strategies.
- **Unclear and overly complex reward function design**: The definition of an agent being “alive” and the notion of agents being both competitive and cooperative are not well motivated. The reward structure appears unnecessarily complicated. Suggested improvement: tokens discarded at earlier layers should receive higher rewards to encourage early pruning.
- **Presentation issues**: Several typos (“specified”, “zeroed-out tokens”, etc.), spacing/formatting errors, and incorrect use of `\citep{}` for ICLR style. Reference format and missing citations for claims (e.g., why RL methods were previously avoided) and for MAPPO.
- **Potential concern about task difficulty**: The token selection may be effective mainly because ImageNet classification is relatively easy. Effectiveness on more challenging tasks (e.g., fine-grained classification, detection) is unclear.
- **Underperformance relative to base model**: In Table 1, the base DeiT-B has higher GFLOPs and better Top-1 accuracy than the proposed method, raising questions about overall effectiveness.
- **Repetitive rows in tables** (noted by Reviewer 1, though not elaborated).
- **Unnecessary RL implementation details**: Standard PPO actor/critic loss equations (Eq. 1 and 2) and verbose descriptions of the RL algorithm do not provide novel insights and could be shortened.
