## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes FlexCAD, a unified model for controllable CAD generation across all construction hierarchies (sketch, extrusion, face, loop, curve) by fine-tuning large language models (LLMs). CAD models are represented as structured text, with each hierarchy abstracted as token sequences. A hierarchy-aware masking strategy is introduced: during training, a hierarchy-aware field is masked and the LLM predicts it; at inference, user intent is converted into a CAD text with a mask token replacing the part to modify. Experiments on the DeepCAD dataset demonstrate improved generation quality and controllability over baselines like SkexGen and Hnc-CAD, with extensive ablation studies (e.g., comparing foundation models, pre-training vs. fine-tuning, LoRA vs. full fine-tuning). Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 6,6) found the work novel and well-executed; Reviewer 1 (score 3) considered the novelty limited and the empirical margins small, noting low confidence (2). All reviewers agree the paper is well-organized.

### Strengths
- Unified controllable generation across all CAD hierarchies (sketch, extrusion, face, loop, curve) in a single framework, addressing a key gap in prior work that required separate models (Reviewers 1–3).
- Novel use of LLMs for CAD generation, bridging LLMs and CAD models through hierarchical text representation and a mask-and-prediction mechanism (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 1 acknowledges “first to leverage LLMs” but questions novelty of the approach).
- Hierarchy-aware masking strategy effectively enables control based on user intent, demonstrated via sketch-level and extrusion-level tasks (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Experimental results on the DeepCAD dataset show superior performance over previous baselines (SkexGen, Hnc-CAD) in generation quality and controllability (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 1 notes improvement only in some metrics and not with a large margin).
- Extensive ablation studies (e.g., Table 3) analyze the impact of LLM pre-training, hierarchy-aware tokenization, and fine-tuning strategies. Fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs with LoRA yields the best results, providing a promising insight for CAD and design automation (Reviewer 2).
- Paper is well-organized and clearly written, making methodology and contributions easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty (Minority view – Reviewer 1):** The training methods are standard LLM fine-tuning; previous works (SkexGen, Hnc-CAD, ICML 2023) already use transformer architectures, making the contribution incremental. This view is contrasted by Reviewers 2 and 3 who highlight the novelty of using LLMs and the masking strategy.
- **Evaluation only on DeepCAD dataset (Reviewers 2, 3):** Generalizability is not tested on other CAD datasets with different structural complexities or design paradigms. Real-world applicability is thus not fully demonstrated (Reviewer 2). Relatedly, the evaluation for prompt-result alignment (intent understanding, instruction following) is unclear and under-analyzed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and references to recent work (Reviewer 2):** The paper does not cite relevant recent methods, such as “OpenECAD: An Efficient Visual Language Model for Editable 3D-CAD Design” and “CadVLM: Bridging Language and Vision in the Generation of Parametric CAD Sketches”, which address similar CAD generation/infilling tasks. Including these would better position FlexCAD and facilitate assessment of its novelty and impact.
- **Motivation for using LLMs insufficiently justified (Reviewer 3):** Why LLMs are a good choice for CAD generation is not clearly argued. Additionally, it is unclear whether FlexCAD inherits typical LLM advantages like instruction following and in-context learning, and whether the model can handle natural language intent directly or assumes users are skilled in converting intent into mask-based instructions (Reviewer 3, question).
- **Missing experimental details and analyses:**
    - Why full model fine-tuning performs worse than LoRA on some metrics (Reviewer 1).
    - Whether LLaMA-3 can perform CAD generation in a zero-shot manner, and how much fine-tuning improves over that (Reviewer 3).
    - The average mask ratio per training sample and its relationship to performance; which output is selected from multiple possible generations (e.g., Figure 5) for final evaluation (Reviewer 2).
    - The instructions used to prompt GPT-4o (Reviewer 3).
    - Whether the fine-tuned model strictly follows CAD structured text grammar; existence and percentage of failure cases that cannot be parsed into valid CAD models (Reviewer 3).
    - Efficiency or any drawbacks compared to prior CAD generation methods (Reviewer 3, question).
- **Missing discussion of ethics and potential risks** (Reviewer 1).
