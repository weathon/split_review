## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RaDAgent, an LLM-agent framework for sequential decision-making that replaces reliance on potentially unreliable external performance metrics with an internal utility judgment learned from posterior experiences. It combines Experience Exploration and Utility Learning, using Elo scores assigned via pairwise comparisons to judge each decision step’s utility. Experiments on ToolBench (and also Game of 24 and WebShop) show over 10% improvement in Pass Rate over baselines, with reduced ChatGPT API costs.

### Strengths
- The proposal to use Elo scores to judge decision utilities is reasonable and points to an interesting direction for rethinking how evaluation metrics can guide LLM-agent decision-making (R1).
- The paper presents a novel idea of shifting from external performance measures to an internal utility judgment approach for LLM-based agents (R2).
- The framework provides a practical solution for developing internal rationality in LLM-based agents (R2).
- Experimental results are good and extensive, demonstrating promising performance across multiple datasets (ToolBench, Game of 24, WebShop) and significantly outperforming baselines on most tasks (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper is well-written and well-presented (R3).
- The approach also shows complementarity between LLMs and decision-making approaches, mutually enhancing performance (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Minority reviewer (R1, score 5, confidence 2) is more critical: the paper is rather empirical and heuristic, combining two heuristic techniques (Experience Exploration and Utility Learning); the black-box use of Elo without significant extensions or detailed discussion of its known limitations (e.g., reference [1] notes it may provide no useful signals sometimes) limits novelty. The other two reviewers (R2, R3, both score 6, confidence 3) are more positive overall but still raise related concerns.
- The Elo rating system might oversimplify the complexity of decision utilities, and the pairwise comparison approach could be time-consuming for complex tasks (R2).
- The Elo-based utility learning lacks theoretical convergence analysis. While empirical effectiveness is shown, no rigorous guarantees about whether and how quickly Elo scores converge to stable values are provided (R2).
- The computational overhead of conducting multiple pairwise comparisons and updating Elo scores is not compared to baseline methods; this additional cost needs evaluation (R2 question, overlaps with missing experiment).
- The paper only experiments with GPT-series LLMs. It is unclear if RaDAgent works with other state-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., Claude, Llama). The authors should explain why GPT-series was chosen and discuss possible reasons if the method fails on other models (R3 question and weakness, missing comparison).
- Presentation and writing need improvement: move Related Work after Introduction to give readers better background; fix typos and grammar issues (e.g., line 96-97 “take action”, line 139 “Elo score” should be plural); the MDP introduction on page 2 should use standard tuple notation (S, A, R, P, μ) and denote the policy as π (R3).
