## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a sub-one-shot NAS paradigm that bridges zero-shot and one-shot NAS by using a small subset of training data to warm up the supernet. Within this framework, the authors introduce SiGeo, a proxy that combines the average gradient estimate divided by gradient standard deviation (from ZiCo) with a Fisher-Rao norm and training loss. The proxy is motivated by theoretical analysis connecting training loss, convergence, and generalization. Empirical evaluations are performed on image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, NAS-Bench-201) and recommendation system datasets, showing comparable or better performance than zero-cost proxies and reduced computational cost relative to one-shot methods.

### Strengths
- The research motivation is clear and well-defined: reducing the cost of one-shot NAS while improving the reliability of zero-shot proxies.
- The theoretical motivation for proposing SiGeo (jointly considering minimum achievable training loss and generalization error) is valid, and the use of theorems to guide proxy design is sensible.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The application of the proposed method to recommendation systems is commendable and addresses a practical need.
- The sub-one-shot framework can achieve better performance than zero-shot NAS and consumes less search cost than one-shot NAS.
- Empirical results show comparable or better performance than previous zero-cost proxies on standard benchmarks.
- The authors provide theoretical justification (convergence and generalization) and experimental validation in the recommendation system domain.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited.** The proxy is essentially ZiCo with the addition of a Fisher-Rao norm and training loss. Several reviewers noted that this is a marginal combination of existing components, and the paper does not clearly articulate unique properties or advantages beyond combining existing methods. The improvement over ZiCo is inconspicuous; in Table 2 results are very similar to ZiCo. The concept of sub-one-shot has already been introduced in PreNAS, and the paper offers few additional insights.
- **Theoretical assumptions are too strong or incorrect.** Reviewer 3 (confidence 5) points out that Assumptions A3 and A4 are rarely satisfied for DNNs: differentiability is violated by ReLU, max-pooling, and argmax operations; positive-definite Hessian implies local convexity, which is not true for deep learning; the use of ℓ1-norm to bound points in a ball is mathematically flawed. These assumptions undermine the theoretical foundation. Other reviewers also questioned the validity of the theory when applied to weight-inherited NAS supernets.
- **Empirical improvements are marginal and insufficiently analyzed.** The performance gains over ZiCo on CV tasks are small, and the experimental results often show no significant advantage. The paper does not report standard deviations over multiple runs (reviewer 2 question). Moreover, the sub-one-shot aspect (warm-up) is not thoroughly investigated: experiments on NAS-Bench-201 and CIFAR-10/100 treat SiGeo as a zero-shot proxy (no warm-up), so the claimed benefit of warm-up remains unsubstantiated. The paper should have systematically varied warm-up duration (e.g., 0%, 10%, 40%, 60%, 80%) and analyzed its effect on correlation with actual performance.
- **Missing comparisons with important baselines.** The paper does not compare with PreNAS (which also proposes a sub-one-shot approach) in the experimental evaluation. It also does not evaluate on NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero, a diverse benchmark for zero-cost proxies. Several reviewers noted the lack of experiments on NAS-Bench-201 and ImageNet, which limits generalizability. For CV tasks, the paper only considers zero-shot settings; the sub-one-shot setting with SiGeo should be evaluated as well.
- **Proxy design may not follow from the theory.** The theorems provide bounds involving gradient variance and training loss, but the final proxy also includes gradient mean. There is an inconsistency: Theorem 1 only gives gradient variance, yet SiGeo uses both mean and variance (reviewer 5 question). The theoretical justification does not lead to a unique or non-obvious design; the proxy could have been derived without the theorems under strong assumptions.
- **Ablation and extensions are lacking.** When λ₂ and λ₃ in Formula (7) are set to zero, SiGeo reduces to ZiCo. It is unclear whether other zero-shot proxies could also benefit from adding the two additional terms (reviewer 4 question). Additionally, the theoretical verification in Figure 2 is only on a two-layer MLP with ReLU; it is unclear if the insights extend to more complex architectures (e.g., NAS-Bench-201) under different warm-up percentages (0%, 10%, 40%).
- **No code provided.** The absence of code hinders reproducibility.
- **Title overclaims use of information theory.** The paper uses the Fisher information matrix, which is a common alternative to the Hessian, but does not significantly leverage information-theoretic concepts beyond that.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* While most reviewers agree on limited novelty and marginal improvements, one reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) saw the theoretical analysis and application to RecSys as strengths, and another (score 5, confidence 3) valued the connection between zero-shot and one-shot. However, two reviewers (score 3) and the most critical reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) raised strong concerns about assumptions and contribution, indicating disagreement over the paper’s significance.
