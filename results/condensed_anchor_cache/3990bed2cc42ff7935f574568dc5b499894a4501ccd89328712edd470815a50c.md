## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Distribution-aware Group Quantization (DGQ) for text-to-image diffusion models. It analyzes activation outliers and cross-attention score distributions, introducing outlier-preserving group quantization and prompt-specific logarithmic quantization to maintain image quality and text-image alignment without fine-tuning. Experiments on MS-COCO and PartiPrompts using FID, IS, and CLIP metrics show improvements over prior methods and claim the first successful <8-bit quantization for T2I diffusion models.

### Strengths
- Clear writing and well-organized presentation (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- In-depth analysis of activation outliers and cross-attention score distributions, with valuable insights (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). Specifically, the analysis of attention scores for the <start> token is convincing and well-addressed (R5).
- Experiments are thorough and solid, with significant improvement over state-of-the-art methods (R2, R1). The exploratory part is rich (R4).
- The proposed group-wise quantization strategy based on activation distribution is promising and innovative (R3). The method effectively identifies and targets outlier issues from prior approaches (R5).
- Maintaining text-image alignment without additional fine-tuning of weight quantization parameters is an interesting contribution (R4).
- Motivation is clear and straightforward; the idea is reasonable and easy to follow (R2, R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited base model and generality**: Validation is only on SDv1.4, which is outdated. Additional experiments on more recent T2I models (e.g., SDv2+, SDXL, PixArt, SD3, Flux, EDM, Imagen) are needed to assess generality (R2, R3, R5). The paper lacks comparisons with latest research such as MixDQ (R3).
- **Performance degradation at low activation bits**: Figure A.3 shows a significant performance drop when activation quantization bits are below 8 (R1). Why does reducing activation bits cause such loss? (R3) – This calls for further analysis.
- **Missing inference performance evaluation**: Inference speed, FLOPs, or latency comparisons are not provided (R1). New matrix computations (including D_d) are introduced without addressing the extra time overhead (R4).
- **Incomplete experimental details**: The number of generated samples to compute FID and IS is not reported (R2). Notation W8A8 (and WXAXGX in supplementary) is not explained upon first use (R3). Statistical results (e.g., error bars) for conclusions in Figure 5(b) are missing (R4).
- **Questions about implementation and mechanisms**:
  - How does setting all attention score quantizer bits to match activation bits (line 417) impact performance? What is the FLOP cost of setting them to 16? (R1)
  - In Table 2, the quantized model with groups=16 underperforms groups=8 on some metrics (IS, FID); reasons should be analyzed (R4).
  - How can a quantized model outperform the full-precision model in Table 2? What mechanisms cause this? (R3)
  - Does excluding the <start> token mean dropping/zeroing it? Why does the peak near value 1 in Figure 5(a) correspond to the <start> token while other regions do not? (R4)
  - What are the attention score distributions for other special tokens (e.g., <end>, <pad>) and how do they compare to <start>? (R5)
  - The Attention-aware quantization applies full precision to <start> token but Table 2 shows identical memory and BOPs values to baselines; clarify the difference (R5).
- **Figure clarity**: Figures are confusing and hard to interpret. Specific issues: (a) How are “drops outlier”, “high quantization error”, and “high overhead” reflected in Fig. 3? (b) What does the horizontal axis in Fig. 4(b) represent? In channel-wise boxplot, why does case1 range 0-900 while case2 1100-1190? What do “specific pixel” and “specific channel” refer to? (R3). The distinctions between group-wise, layer-wise, and channel-wise quantization are abstract; a figure illustrating these differences would help (R3).
- **Novelty concerns**: While the analysis is insightful, the method heavily relies on existing techniques (Group Quantization, logarithmic quantization), which may limit novelty compared to the analysis itself (R5). The claim of being “first to achieve low-bit quantization (<8-bit) on T2I diffusion models” (lines 127-129) may conflict with concurrent work (e.g., [A]); a discussion of recent works is needed (R5).
- **Missing comparisons**: Lack of qualitative and quantitative comparisons with the latest relevant work (e.g., MixDQ) (R3). Evaluation could be strengthened by including no-reference image quality assessment (IQA) metrics (e.g., MANIQA, PromptIQA) against full-precision and baseline models (R5).
- **Minor issues**: Repeated sentences at lines 326-327 (R2). Recommend adding brackets around feature indices in Formula 9 for clarity (R3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewers R2 and R5 (score 8) are more positive, emphasizing the method’s effectiveness and insightful analysis. R1, R3, R4 (score 6) raise concerns about limited generality, missing details, unclear figures, and unaddressed overheads. The novelty is debated: R3 finds the group-wise strategy innovative, while R5 worries about reliance on existing methods.
