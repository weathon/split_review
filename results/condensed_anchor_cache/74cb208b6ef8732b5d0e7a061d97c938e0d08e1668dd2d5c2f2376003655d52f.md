## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes instruction-finetuning of a text-only LLM to gain vision-language capabilities for chest X-ray (CXR) understanding and generation. It uses VQ-GAN for image tokenization and fine-tunes the LLM on diverse tasks: CXR-to-report generation, report-to-CXR generation, and CXR visual question answering (VQA) augmented via GPT-3.5. The authors claim superior performance over models like XrayGPT and UniXGen on these tasks with a smaller model size.

### Strengths
- Demonstrates potential of LLMs in radiological diagnostics, offering greater flexibility than previous methods (R1).
- Encodes images into tokens using VQ-GAN and integrates them into the LLM fine-tuning to align language and image features in feature space; efficient use of existing resources by expanding the pretrained LLM’s embedding space (R1, R4).
- Defines four tasks, especially using the ChatGPT API for VQA, which strengthens image-text association; interesting design (R1).
- Training strategy includes dataset cleansing: initial learning of latent features from low-quality data, then fine-tuning with cleansed data (R1).
- Instruction-finetuning effectively elevates the LLM’s capability to map vision-image modalities; markedly excels in CXR-to-Report, CXR-VQA, and Report-to-CXR generation, outshining open-sourced versions of XrayGPT and UniXGen (R2).
- Novel “instruction-finetuning” method, a significant departure from adapter network techniques, allowing more intimate integration of visual and language features (R3).
- Smartly leverages the inherent instruction-following abilities of pretrained LLMs; approach is efficient and maximizes the potential of existing models (R3).
- Versatility: handles CXR-to-report, report-to-CXR, and CXR-specific VQA; breadth of application showcases potential in real-world medical scenarios (R3).
- Outperforms other specialized models on these tasks (R3).
- Smooth integration of image and text token spaces without structural changes to the base LLM via VQ-GAN; crucial for practical implementation (R3).
- Bidirectional instruction fine-tuning maintains integrity of the LLM’s structure and objectives while expanding capabilities, with many applications in the field (R4).
- Tackles image-text alignment in medical imaging, which is not yet well-researched (R5).
- Manuscript overall easy-to-follow (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and differentiation are contested.**  
  Reviewer 2 considered the approach not particularly novel, as many large-scale medical models adopt similar instruction-tuning strategies but with broader functional range; both methodological and applicative contribution were viewed as lacking.  
  Reviewer 3 described the method as a novel departure from adapter networks.  
  Reviewer 5 argued that the claimed difference from prior bidirectional LLMs is minor, because the VQGAN encoder/decoder is essentially equivalent to a vision adapter and image generative model; the distinction between Figure 1(a) and (c) is marginal, and the novelty is overclaimed.

- **Incomplete and potentially unfair comparisons.**  
  * Fairness: The comparison with open-sourced versions of XrayGPT and UniXGen may be unfair because those models were not trained on the same instruction-tuning dataset; a uniform dataset should be used (R2).  
  * Missing baselines: No comparison with existing LLM+instruction-tuning models such as Med-PaLM and Med-PaLM 2 (R2, R5). Missing comparison with RadFM (R4).  
  * Missing contemporary methods in report generation, e.g., “Dynamic Graph Enhanced Contrastive Learning for Chest X-ray Report Generation, CVPR 2023” (R2, R5).  
  * Evaluation on only one dataset (MIMIC-CXR), with limited quality; cross-domain evaluation on IU X-ray is missing (R1, R5).  
  * No demonstration of downstream clinical tasks (e.g., disease classification/localization) that would justify the pretraining; the experiments only test the instruction-tuning tasks themselves (R5).

- **Evaluation metrics are insufficient or non-standard.**  
  * Report generation: Only AUROC scores for six categories reported, whereas prior work typically reports average Precision/Recall/F1 over all 14 categories (R2). Missing AUCROC/F1, Jaccard similarity index (R5).  
  * Image generation: Only FID reported, but other common metrics (e.g., visual fidelity measures) are not provided (R5).  
  * VQA: Only accuracy reported (R5).  
  * No qualitative evaluations or user studies with medical professionals to assess clinical relevance and accuracy (R3).  
  * No comparisons with human diagnostic capabilities (R3).

- **Methodological concerns and lack of ablation analyses.**  
  * Catastrophic forgetting: The paper does not discuss measures (regularization, replay buffers, parameter isolation) to prevent degradation of the LLM’s original language skills, nor does it provide empirical analysis demonstrating their preservation (R3).  
  * Adapter network dismissal: The critique of adapter-based approaches lacks a comprehensive empirical comparison; a side-by-side evaluation of training time, parameter efficiency, and downstream performance is needed to substantiate the claim of superiority (R3).  
  * VQ-GAN limitations: Potential artifacts or unfaithful representations from VQ-GAN are not discussed; details on architecture, training process, impact of quantization errors, and an analysis of reconstructed image fidelity are missing (R3).  
  * Clinical information preservation: Using only L2 reconstruction loss may be insufficient to prevent loss of clinically important information (e.g., microscopic lesions); the paper does not show experimental results before/after introducing the L2 loss (R1).  
  * The upsampling process for image data (Section 3.1) is not justified given that X-ray images are much larger than the target resolution; its effect on results is unclear (R5).  
  * The size of the image token embedding table (K_img) is not specified (R5).  
  * The term “txv-all-1024” is not defined; it is presumed to refer to 1024-dimensional outputs from a DenseNet-121 trained on chest X-ray classification, but this needs confirmation (R3).

- **Interpretability and scalability not addressed.**  
  Scalability of the tokenization and fine-tuning process to larger medical images (e.g., 3D CT/MR scans) is not discussed (R4).  
  How interpretable the model’s decisions are in a clinical context, where explanations for predictions are crucial, is not addressed (R4).

- **Missing visualization of image-text alignment.**  
  When using VQA for data augmentation, GPT-generated questions focus text on key lesion areas; appropriate visualization is needed to demonstrate the effectiveness of incorporating VQA (R1).

- **Discrepancies and minor issues.**  
  * RoentGen AUC and FID numbers: The authors need to clarify whether these were reproduced or cited, and explain any differences (R3).  
  * Writing style is not fluid and contains multiple errors (e.g., repetitive phrases, grammatical mistakes) that hinder readability (R2, R3).  
  * Grammar needs attention across the text (R3).
