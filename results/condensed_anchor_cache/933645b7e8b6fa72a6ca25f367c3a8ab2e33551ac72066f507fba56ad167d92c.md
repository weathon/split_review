## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SlotMIM, a self-supervised visual pre-training method designed for non-object-centric (NOC) data. It introduces a semantic bottleneck to Masked Image Modeling (MIM) by reducing the number of prototypes to encourage objectness at patch-level, and applies cross-view consistency regularization to promote multiview invariance. The method is validated on ImageNet, COCO, CC12M, and Ego4D, with evaluations on image recognition (ImageNet), scene understanding (ADE20K), and robot learning. The paper also studies the impact of pre-training data on existing SSL methods.

### Strengths

- Visual representation learning from non-object-centric and ego-centric data is a fundamental and interesting research topic.
- The paper provides a study on the impact of pre-training data to existing SSL techniques across three NOC datasets, yielding insights (though some are unsurprising).
- The proposed SlotMIM leads to better performance than compared competitors on several benchmarks, demonstrating effectiveness.
- Evaluations span a diversity of tasks: classification, segmentation, and robot learning; this allows assessing generalization and the role of data in different contexts.
- One reviewer (score 5) found the paper clearly written and effectively expressing its findings, and highlighted the thorough evaluation across multiple datasets and tasks, as well as the practical significance of the robot learning application.
- The paper experiments with a variety of scenarios (ImageNet, COCO, CC12M, Ego4D).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: Most reviewers agree the approach primarily combines slot attention and iBOT, closely following SlotCon (prototype assignment pooling, contrastive loss for slots) and MoCo. It lacks comparison to VicRegL, which uses a similar cross-view patch alignment loss. The paper does not clarify novel technical components that set SlotMIM apart from previous works. One reviewer further notes that many claimed insights (e.g., NOC data benefits similar-domain downstream tasks, features from web data are linearly separable on ImageNet) have been previously demonstrated by SEER, DINOv2, Tolan et al., etc.
- **Insufficient and incomplete empirical results**: 
    - Performance is reported as approximately 84% ImageNet top-1 accuracy and ~49% ADE20K mIoU from plots (Fig. 4), which is poor relative to state-of-the-art (EVA, EVA-02, InternImage not compared).
    - Only two main benchmarks (ImageNet classification, ADE20K segmentation) are used to draw conclusions in Secs 3.2–3.4; fine-grained, retrieval, or OOD benchmarks are missing.
    - Missing data points in Figures 5, 6, 8: performance of DINO, DINOv2, and MAE on the largest-scale datasets should be included.
    - Lacks comparison to SlotCon; ablation of masking and within-patch loss is missing.
    - Unsupervised object segmentation results should be reported if the paper emphasizes object clustering.
    - The gap of ~0.1% in fine-tuning results between COCO+ and ImageNet (L310-312) is within random fluctuation; experiments need repeats with confidence intervals.
- **Evaluation protocol issues**:
    - Pre-training datasets are limited to 241k images, a small fraction of what these models are normally trained on, making all models undertrained (evident from ImageNet linear probe results in Fig. 3) and comparisons potentially invalid.
    - For ImageNet fine-tuning, average pooling of patches is used instead of the CLS token, which is non-standard.
    - Linear probing is not used for ADE20K, which would better reveal pre-trained feature quality.
- **Motivation and claims**:
    - The paper claims to focus on data efficiency yet uses 241k images, which is still large; visual representation learning is winner-take-all.
    - Several claims are vague or unsupported:
        * L182-185: iBOT loss lacks cross-view invariance guidance – contradicts I-JEPA which shows that patch prediction in latent space yields semantically meaningful representations.
        * L224-226: stating that ResNet architecture provided strong inductive bias for objectness is opposite to prior work (Slot Attention) that argues CNNs lack such biases.
        * L533-535: formalization into scene decomposition and object-centric representation learning is not demonstrated by the experiments.
- **Terminology and clarity**:
    - The term "object-centric" is used to describe single-object datasets (ImageNet) and contrasted with "non-object-centric" (COCO, etc.), which is non-standard and confusing; previous works often describe COCO as "object-centric". Suggestion: use "single-object" / "multi-object".
    - Other phrasing issues: "soft one-hot encoding" (cannot be both), "normalizes attention scores on the query side" (unclear axis).
    - The phrase "cross-view consistency regularization" is unclear – what does "cross-view" mean?
    - The paper fails to clearly illustrate differences with DINOv2 and iBOT; a framework comparison figure would help.
    - Figure 1 lacks informative caption (how are visualizations obtained? what are the queries for retrieved images?).
    - DINO loss description (L109) is incorrect, missing sharpening and centering for teacher logits.
    - L201: definition of p and \mathbb{1}_i is confusing.
- **Dataset categorization**: Categorizing datasets as object-centric vs. non-object-centric may be oversimplified; e.g., COCO (scene-centric) can contain simple scenes with clear foregrounds. It is difficult to determine whether performance differences stem from object-centricity or other distribution factors (object class distribution, image quality, resolution, etc.). Validations are on previous datasets, making it unclear how training data distribution affects test data.
- One reviewer questions how SlotMIM would work on other NOC data (satellite images, PDF documents) where the assumption that images decompose into objects may not hold.
