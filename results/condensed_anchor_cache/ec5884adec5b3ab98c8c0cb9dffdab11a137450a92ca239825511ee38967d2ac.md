- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes AIME, a protocol that uses multiple LLMs each independently evaluating separate criteria (e.g., logic, correctness, clarity) and concatenating the evaluations to improve iterative text-based optimization for code generation. The theoretical claim is that a linear combination of multiple evaluators can approximate the optimal one-shot evaluation policy. Empirically, AIME achieves up to 62% higher error detection rate and up to 16% higher success rate over a single-LLM evaluator on LeetCodeHard and HumanEval. Reviewers broadly agree that the approach is practical, modular, and supported by extensive ablations (including a role-wise analysis in Table 1). However, major concerns are raised about novelty (multiple-LLM evaluators have been proposed before, and ensemble methods are well-known), the theoretical justification (idealized, contains errors, and inconsistencies between “addable” and “concatable” representations), missing baselines (especially majority voting with a single prompt and multiple sampled responses), writing quality issues, and limited validation to code generation only.

### Strengths

- The approach is general, conceptually simple, and could be applied to other domains (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 finds the approach novel.
- Figure 1 clearly shows AIME’s advantages over single-LLM evaluation and strongly motivates the work (Reviewer 1).
- The evaluation is extensive, with non-trivial improvements over baselines: up to 62% higher error detection rate and up to 16% higher success rate on LeetCodeHard and HumanEval (Reviewers 1, 2, 3; abstract).
- The role-wise analysis in Table 1 provides insight into which criteria (e.g., logic, correctness, clarity) contribute most to performance (Reviewer 1).
- The approach is modular, very practical, and easily extendable (Reviewer 2).
- The ablations are insightful; the finding that diversity of roles helps evaluation is a useful takeaway, similar to prior work on diverse rewards for training (Reviewer 3, citing Rame et al. 2024).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing baseline**: A single prompt containing all criteria (as in SingleEval) but with *k* independent sampled responses using temperature>0 (i.e., majority voting / sampling ensembles). This would be a fairer comparison, since AIME uses *k* separate evaluators each with fewer tokens (3600/*k* vs 3600 for SingleEval), and literature shows sampling outperforms greedy selection (Reviewer 1, citing Stiennon et al. 2020 and Wang et al. 2023). Figure 5’s result that increasing evaluators even with the same role helps further motivates this baseline.
- **Novelty and contribution are questioned**: 
  - The biggest issue is that combining multiple models (ensembles) is well-known; the paper does not discuss ensemble methods or frame itself as one, and it feels like “just another paper saying ensembles work” (Reviewer 2).
  - Prior works (Verga et al. 2024, Kim et al. 2024) have already proposed using multiple LLMs as evaluators, diminishing the contribution (Reviewer 3).
  - *Minority contrast*: Reviewer 4 finds the approach novel, and Reviewer 1 considers it general and simple, but the majority (2, 3) raise substantial novelty concerns.
- **Theoretical justification is problematic**:
  - The bounds are for one-shot evaluation, not the iterative setup used in the paper (Reviewer 3).
  - The proof contains errors and undefined terms: Equation (4) should have |e_max| instead of |e*|; line 204 introduces an undefined *l*; the last equation in the block for Δ2 randomly introduces e* (Reviewer 3). The additivity assumption on the aggregation function is not stated in the theorem statement nor before it is used (Reviewer 4). The proof only covers two evaluators, not more (Reviewer 4).
  - Inconsistency: Section 3.2 changes from “addable” to “concatable” representations without clear motivation (Reviewer 4). The assumptions for the claim that increasing mixture components improves approximation are not stated (Reviewer 3). It is also unclear whether Theorem 1 only applies when the mixture constants α_k are the same for the mixture evaluator distribution and the linearly combined evaluator (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing quality is below standard**: 
  - Unaddressed comment “cite ipm” appears in parentheses at line 159 (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Line 185 says “combine the losses” instead of “combine the evaluations” (Reviewer 3).
  - Table 3 third row incorrectly highlights AIME as more performant when the numbers for SingleEval are higher (Reviewer 3).
  - Section 3.2 needs improvement for clarity (Reviewer 1). The font size in Figure 4 is too small to read (Reviewer 1).
  - The Rumelhart citation for backprop should be accompanied by earlier work that preceded it (Reviewer 2).
- **Error detection metric (EDR) issues**: 
  - The definition appears erroneous: $1/|Z_\text{fail}| \sum_{z \in Z_\text{fail}} q_z$ would evaluate to 1 since $Z_\text{fail}$ is defined as “set of iterations with at least one failed test case” (Reviewer 3). Why is EDR so significantly favorable for AIME relative to SR and CR? (Reviewer 2).
  - EDR only evaluates cases with failed test cases; it does not test whether the evaluator correctly identifies when there are no errors (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited domain validation**: Only code generation (HumanEval, LeetCodeHard) is tested; the paper claims general-purpose evaluation but does not test other domains like summarization or dialogue (Reviewer 4). Moreover, gains on HumanEval in success rate and completion rate appear within noise (Reviewer 1, Appendix Tables 3 and 4). The amount of performance improvement differs significantly across benchmarks, but no explanation is given (Reviewer 3, also a question).
- **Qualitative claim unsupported**: “AIME is more thorough” is qualitative; the paper should clarify what constitutes thoroughness (length vs. something else) and provide quantitative evidence. The appendix examples suggest longer responses are considered more thorough, which may not always be valid (Reviewer 1).
- **Title too broad**: “AI System Optimization” should be replaced with “Text-based Optimization” or similar (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablations and clarifications**:
  - The paper does not explore the effect of chain-of-thought prompting on evaluators (Reviewer 2, from Figure 3).
  - Temperature settings (0 and 1) are not consistently explained in the main text (Reviewer 1).
  - Deduplication of error detection phrases is not clarified: if the LLM outputs “This code is correct, it has to be incorrect because it does not pass the first test”, does that count as one error or two? (Reviewer 1).
  - The invariance to temperature on LeetCodeHard vs. HumanEval is not explained; examples would help (Reviewer 1).
  - The low SingleEval performance on HumanEval (Figure 2 left) is not explained with examples (Reviewer 1).
