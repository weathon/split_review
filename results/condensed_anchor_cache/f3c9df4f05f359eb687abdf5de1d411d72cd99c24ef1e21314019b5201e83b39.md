## Merged Review

### Summary
RTDiff is a diffusion-based data augmentation method for offline RL that synthesizes trajectories in reverse (moving from unknown to known states) to mitigate distribution shift and overestimation. It also includes an OOD detector to truncate OOD segments, flexible trajectory length control, and noise management for efficiency. Empirical results on both proprioceptive and visual observation tasks demonstrate improved performance over baselines.

### Strengths
- The idea of generating reverse trajectories to address distribution shift is interesting and, to my knowledge, the first work to use this concept for that purpose (Reviewer 1). (Minority contrast: some reviewers consider this not novel – see Weaknesses.)
- Clearly written, well-motivated, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Experiments are comprehensive, covering a range of environments (both vector-based and pixel-based tasks) and including ablation studies that validate each component (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The reverse generation scheme is empirically shown to reduce augmentation in risky/overestimated regions, and the intuitive diagram effectively illustrates the idea (Reviewer 4).
- The approach (reverse generation + OOD detector + noise control) is intuitively sound and demonstrates consistent improvements over forward generation baselines (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** Reverse trajectory generation is not new to offline RL; prior work ROMI [1] proposed a similar concept using a transition model. The paper lacks discussion and experimental comparison with ROMI, and does not clearly differentiate its dynamics learning or rollout strategy from that work (Reviewers 2,3,4). Consequently, the overall contribution is limited – the paper is neither the first to propose reverse trajectory generation in offline RL nor the first to use diffusion for data augmentation (Reviewer 2). (Minority contrast: Reviewer 1 viewed the idea as novel.)
- **Missing clarity on OOD detector usage with baselines:** It is unclear whether the OOD detector (and noise management) is incorporated with other data augmentation baselines such as SynthER and ATraDiff. Without this, the better performance of RTDiff may be due to the OOD detector rather than reverse synthesis (Reviewer 1). Additionally, if the OOD detector were used with normal synthesis, could dangerous areas also be avoided? (Reviewer 1)
- **Section 6 analysis is inadequate:** The analysis uses a simple illustrative environment rather than one from the main experiments. The explanation of why reverse synthesis avoids issues is confusing: reverse trajectories move from a dangerous area to upper/lower areas while normal trajectories start from lower area and enter the middle dangerous area. Clarification is needed on the starting states for both approaches (Reviewer 1). Also, why does normal synthesis + OOD detector produce significantly more In2Out than normal synthesis without OOD detector (18.2% vs 11.2%)? (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing comparisons with model-based offline RL methods:** Since diffusion-based data augmentation is conceptually similar to model-based rollout (e.g., MOPO, RAMBO), these methods should be compared (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of hyperparameter details:** It is not mentioned whether hyperparameters of the underlying algorithms (IQL, TD3BC, CQL) were adjusted after data augmentation (Reviewer 2).
- **No quantitative evaluation of generated trajectory quality:** The paper does not assess the fidelity of generated samples (e.g., model error, FID). Improved RL performance alone is insufficient; sample quality must be demonstrated (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Insufficient justification of the core claim:** The paper states that forward trajectory generation suffers from distribution shift and value overestimation. However, since offline RL algorithms already handle distribution shift, data augmentation is only beneficial when it generates novel samples. The paper needs to clearly articulate why forward generation is problematic and how reverse generation addresses this beyond merely generating different samples (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor error:** Line 156: "dat" should be "data" (Reviewer 4).
- **Disagreement on visual environments:** Reviewer 4 states that environments based on visual data should be included in the analysis. However, Reviewer 2 confirms that visual observations are used in the experiments. This discrepancy should be resolved (Reviewers 2,4).
