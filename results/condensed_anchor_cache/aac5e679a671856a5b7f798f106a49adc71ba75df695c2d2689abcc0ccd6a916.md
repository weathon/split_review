## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedInverse to evaluate privacy leakage in federated learning (FL) via model inversion (MI) attacks, where a malicious client uses the global model to reconstruct other clients’ training data. It applies existing MI methods (GMI, KED-MI, VMI) with a GAN and an HSIC-based diversity regularizer. Evaluations on CelebA, MNIST, and CIFAR-10 show successful data recovery, though the experimental setup and metrics have limitations.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important, understudied threat model: a malicious client extracts sensitive data from other participants via the global model without being detected by the server.
- Proposes FedInverse, a novel method that adapts existing MI attacks (GMI, KED-MI, VMI) to FL and introduces an HSIC regularizer to increase output diversity.
- Research question is well-defined and valuable to the community.
- The paper is easy to follow; the underlying attacks are clearly presented and perform well in the evaluation.
- Comprehensive evaluation covering multiple factors (datasets, attack methods, defenses) makes results understandable.
- The HSIC regularizer demonstrably improves attack success and image quality.

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic public-data assumption**: The attack assumes the attacker has access to public data closely matching the target clients’ distribution (e.g., using a split of the FL training set as public data). This is often unwarranted in practical FL (e.g., medical images). Experiments with out-of-distribution public data are needed.
- **Limited dataset diversity and scale**: Only small vision datasets (CelebA, MNIST, CIFAR-10) are used; the real-world impact is unclear. Larger datasets (e.g., iNaturalist), more clients, larger batch sizes, and non-vision tasks (e.g., NLP) should be evaluated.
- **Unclear or insufficient evaluation metrics**:
  - The evaluation classifier’s architecture, training data, and hyperparameters are not specified, raising concerns about metric reliability.
  - Attack accuracy using a classifier may be misleading (a flawed image can be classified correctly); top-5 accuracy is too weak for MNIST (10 labels).
  - FID lacks baseline comparisons, e.g., applying MI attacks directly to local models rather than the global model.
  - No rigorous similarity analysis between inverted images and original training data (e.g., nearest-neighbor or visual similarity metrics). Figure 4(a) shows inverted images that do not closely match the target data, suggesting the attack may not provably recover training data.
- **Experiment setting lacks realism**: The disjoint-label scenario (attacker holds labels 5-9, other clients 0-4) is not representative of typical FL where clients have similar data distributions. The attack may fail when many clients share overlapping distributions. More realistic settings with multiple clients having similar distributions should be tested.
- **Inability to attribute recovered data**: The method cannot tell whether an image originates from the attacker’s own local dataset or a specific other participant.
- **Need for evaluation against stronger defenses**: The paper tests only two common defenses (including BiDO, which is not tailored for FL). Stronger, FL-specific defenses should be considered, e.g., Huang et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and Li et al. (CVPR 2022).
- **Modest improvement from HSIC regularizer**: The proposed diversity optimization does not show impressive improvements over baseline attacks (GMI, KED-MI, VMI).
- **Missing related-work discussion in main body**: The paper claims to be the first to study data leakage from a malicious client pretending to be benign, but does not discuss related data-extraction attacks from a malicious aggregator (e.g., Boenisch et al., Pasquini et al., Zhao et al.) in the main text (only in appendix).
- **Unclear positioning and algorithm details**: It is ambiguous whether FedInverse is intended as an attack or an evaluation framework for FL operators. Algorithm 1 is unclear: Q_t is not passed as a parameter but assumed global, yet the function returns it. Section 3 lacks sufficient detail on the attack procedure.
- **Need for more visual results**: Additional visualizations (e.g., on CIFAR-10, across different attack success rates) and comparisons with attacker’s own local dataset would help assess the attack’s effectiveness.
