## Merged Review

### Summary

This work presents DNABERT-2, a genome foundation model that replaces the k-mer tokenizer with Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) and incorporates ALiBi, FlashAttention, and LoRA to improve efficiency and context length. The authors argue that k-mer tokenization introduces computational and sample inefficiencies, which BPE overcomes via non‑overlapping tokenization. DNABERT-2 is pre-trained on a multi-species corpus and fine-tuned on the newly introduced Genome Understanding Evaluation (GUE) benchmark (36 datasets, 9 tasks, sequence lengths 70–10,000 bp). Results show that DNABERT-2 achieves comparable performance to the state‑of‑the‑art Nucleotide Transformer (NT) while using 21× fewer parameters and ≈92× less GPU time; it outperforms DNABERT on 23/28 datasets with an average +6 absolute score on GUE, while being 3× more efficient. The code, data, and pre-trained model were not publicly released at the time of review.

### Strengths

- **Adoption of recent LLM techniques**: The model successfully integrates ALiBi, FlashAttention, and LoRA, enhancing speed, context length, and memory efficiency.  
- **Significant computational and sample efficiency**: Compared to the original DNABERT, DNABERT-2 requires 3× fewer FLOPs; vs. Nucleotide Transformer it uses 21× fewer parameters and ≈92× less GPU time while achieving comparable performance.  
- **Comprehensive and challenging benchmark (GUE)**: The authors curated a collection of 36 datasets across 9 tasks, covering diverse genome‑understanding problems and providing good discrimination between methods. The benchmark is a valuable resource for the community.  
- **Clear motivation and well‑defined challenges**: The paper clearly articulates the limitations of k‑mer tokenization and the need for standardized evaluation, and the proposed solutions (BPE, ALiBi, etc.) are intuitive and well‑motivated.  
- **Solid and extensive experiments**: The evaluation is thorough, comparing against DNABERT and Nucleotide Transformer, and the results convincingly demonstrate the benefits of the proposed approach.  
- **Potential impact on computational biology**: With few open‑source DNA foundation models available, DNABERT-2’s reduced computational cost and improved accuracy make it a welcome addition for the research community.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns**: BPE, FlashAttention, LoRA, and ALiBi are all well‑known techniques. Their combination into a DNA language model is an engineering contribution rather than a methodological novelty. (Reviewers 2, 4 note reduced novelty; Reviewer 3 finds the integration of recent techniques a strength – this contrast is preserved.)  
- **Disagreement over attributing efficiency solely to BPE**: Reviewers 3 and 2 point out that the claimed advantage of BPE over k‑mer tokenization is not disentangled from other model differences. An ablation study controlling for tokenizer type is needed. (The authors later performed such an ablation; nonetheless the original criticism stands.)  
- **Missing ablation studies**:  
  - The individual contributions of BPE, ALiBi, FlashAttention, and LoRA have not been independently quantified.  
  - No ablation on FlashAttention, ALiBi, and LoRA is provided (requested by Reviewer 4).  
- **GUE benchmark limitations**:  
  - The benchmark predominantly focuses on short to moderate‑length sequences (70–10,000 bp). Reviewer 1 notes that sequences ≤1,000 bp may not capture long‑range dependencies critical in genomics (e.g., enhancer‑promoter interactions, structural variations).  
  - Missing baselines: No CNN‑based models (e.g., DeepBind, DeepSEA) are included for comparison; no language model trained from scratch is evaluated. These would provide important comparative insights.  
  - Reviewer 3 recommends comparing with the recently published “Genomic benchmarks” (Grešová et al., 2023).  
- **Interpretability concerns**: For many biological applications, interpretability is crucial. Models using one‑hot encoded sequences often offer better interpretability than those relying on k‑mer or BPE tokenization. BPE likely reduces interpretability further. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Potential data leakage and generalization issues**:  
  - Cross‑species information leakage: Given genome overlaps (e.g., human vs. primate), the model might easily predict masked tokens, inflating performance. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Further pre‑training on GUE sequences may risk overfitting and limit generalization to novel sequences; a fair comparison would avoid additional pre‑training. (Reviewer 4)  
  - No evaluation of sequence statistics between GUE and the pre‑training corpus. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Missing comparisons**:  
  - Comparison with the HyenaDNA model is not provided. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Performance with larger model sizes not explored; if scaling does not yield gains, this should be noted. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Inconsistency in LoRA usage**: The authors note they use LoRA for NT but full fine‑tuning for DNABERT/DNABERT‑2, yet LoRA is described as an integral part of the approach – this is confusing. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Lack of biological depth**: The paper is suitable for a machine‑learning audience but provides limited biological insights. Better downstream tasks (e.g., CAGE‑seq prediction with longer context) are suggested. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:  
  - The meaning of “genome language modeling” should be clarified. (Reviewer 1)  
  - No citation or reference to models like DeepBind/DeepSEA; the citation for TF‑DNA binding prediction (Wang et al., 2022) is noted as not representative of genome language modeling. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Add number of sequences for each dataset in Table 1. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Explain why MCC and F1 are used as metrics for different tasks. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Clarify the sentence “This results in 0.41B training tokens...” in Section 5.3 and the benefit of further pre‑training. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Typos: “BENCKMARK” in the section title. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Code/data availability**: The code, data, and pre‑trained model were not made public at the time of review; the authors should explain this restriction. (Reviewer 2)
