## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes an online learning framework for Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT) that supports both incremental and decremental learning via in-place updates of existing tree nodes (rather than adding/removing entire trees). It detects changes in best split and retrains subtrees accordingly, with optimizations including split candidate sampling and robust LogitBoost. Experiments evaluate running time, memory, error, batch addition/deletion, backdoor attacks, high-dimensional data, and ablation. Reviewers are divided: two (R1, R2, scores 8 and 8) consider the work novel, well-organized, and experimentally thorough; one (R3, score 3) views the methodology as a minor improvement with limited insight and finds the presentation lacking.

### Strengths

1. **Novel in-place update GBDT framework** supporting incremental and decremental learning. R1 finds the research topic interesting and the method novel. R2 emphasizes that the in-place update of decision trees is a significant departure from traditional adding/removing entire trees, offering more granular and efficient adaptation.

2. **Detailed theoretical analysis and formulation.** R1 highlights that the theoretical analysis is well expressed and proofed.

3. **Extensive and complete experimental evaluation.** R1 confirms sufficient experiments. R2 notes the evaluation covers running time, memory, test error, batch addition/deletion, addition with more classes, backdoor attack verification, and more, demonstrating effectiveness and efficiency. R3 acknowledges an extensive set of experiments.

4. **Paper is well-written and easy to follow** (R2). R1 also notes the paper is well organized.

### Weaknesses

* **Novelty and contribution (disagreement).** One reviewer (R3) considers the framework a minor improvement on existing research with limited inspiration. R3 states the core difference is using the additivity of Formula 5 for node updates on incremental/decremental data (Section 2.3), and optimizations (Section 3.1 uses incremental/decremental data for node updates, Section 3.2 changes gradient accumulation frequency, Sections 3.3 and 3.4 reduce time via split sampling parameter α) are incremental changes with limited novelty. The other two reviewers (R1, R2) assess the in-place update idea as novel and interesting, and R2 calls it a significant departure. R3 further finds the motivation unclear: the only stated reason is that no one has studied it (line 75), and claims that GBDT outperforms deep learning on accuracy and provides interpretability lack supporting evidence.

* **Insufficient parameter guidance and sensitivity analysis.** Multiple reviewers note missing discussion of important hyperparameters.
    * Split candidate sampling parameter α and robust LogitBoost parameter σ are not analyzed for their impact on accuracy or data-dependence (R3). R2 states these heuristics require careful tuning and the paper provides insufficient guidance for different datasets or problem settings.
    * R1 requests parameter sensitivity analysis and analysis of the impact of the number of base learners on learning performance.
    * R3 notes that the main text lacks necessary conclusions from ablation studies (e.g., impact of sampling method on accuracy); only a brief introduction appears in line 505.

* **Missing runtime complexity analysis.** R1 requests a detailed runtime complexity analysis of the proposed method.

* **Experimental design limitations.**
    * R2: No real-world datasets with varying data distributions are used; performance under rapidly changing distributions remains unknown. R2 also asks whether the training in Section 4.1 is offline.
    * R1: The proposed method is not the best on some datasets – a detailed explanation is needed.
    * R1 asks for a comparison with existing online boosting methods (OnlineBoost, OnlineAdaC2Classifier, OnlineRUSBoost from [1] by Wang and Pineau).
    * R3 states that the experimental section lacks internal progressive connection and conveys repetitive information between sections (e.g., backdoor attacks in 4.6, membership inference in 4.7, high-dimensional data in line 498 vs. Sections 4.3 and 4.4). R3 suggests the stability and rationality of the method should be discussed in the main text rather than the appendix.

* **Writing and presentation issues.**
    * R2: Font sizes in Tables 1 and 2 are too small; minor language corrections (lines 59, 199: “Eq. equation” → “Eq.” or “Equation”; line 77: Bagging samples with replacement, not disjoint subsets; line 87: challenges not specific to in-place online learning; line 199: “with ascending depths” → “layer by layer from the root to the leaves”; lines 271–274: phrasing improvements).
    * R3: Citation issues (line 29, 34, 39 – incorrect format); typo in Algorithm 3, line 4; line 533: should be “a novel”; experimental setup in Section 3.4 lacks details (e.g., repeated times, why 1% was chosen as upper limit).
