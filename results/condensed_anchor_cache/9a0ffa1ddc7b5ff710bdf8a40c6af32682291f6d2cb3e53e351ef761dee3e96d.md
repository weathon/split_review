## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Node-MoE, a GNN framework that uses a mixture of experts (MoE) to apply node-specific filters, addressing the limitation of traditional GNNs that use a single global filter. Theoretical and empirical analysis (including CSBM-based motivation) shows that a global filter optimized for one pattern can harm performance on nodes with different patterns. Node-MoE uses a GIN-based gating model to assign weights to multiple ChebNetII expert models with fixed filters (low-pass, high-pass, constant). A filter smoothing loss is added for stability. Experiments on homophilic and heterophilic datasets demonstrate effectiveness, with ablation studies and time complexity analysis.

### Strengths
- Strong theoretical and empirical analysis using CSBM to validate the limitation of a single global filter and motivate node-specific filtering.
- Novel use of MoE for node-wise filter selection, with new insights such as the filter smoothing loss and node-wise filtering. Experimentally outperforms other graph MoE techniques.
- Extensive experiments across homophilic and heterophilic datasets, including ablation studies on design choices (gating variants), time complexity, and hyperparameter choices.
- Good set of experiments on analysis of the method, detailed hyperparameter analysis, and efficiency considerations.
- Well-structured and generally easy to follow; technically sound theoretical analysis.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The observation that real-world graphs contain nodes with varying homophily is well-known. The core MoE idea is not entirely novel—comparable frameworks exist (e.g., Link-MoE for link prediction is neither cited nor discussed). The implementation relies heavily on existing MoE techniques (Top K, gating), making the adaptation appear incremental (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- **Performance gains unclear**: In Table 1, many results overlap in standard deviation with non-MoE baselines. Additionally, stronger results exist in prior work using ACM-GCN+ (76.08% on Chameleon, 69.98% on Squirrel) and structural features ([5] achieves 77.48% on Chameleon, 74.17% on Squirrel), questioning whether the MoE complexity is necessary. Heterophilic datasets chosen are known to be problematic; additional datasets from [4] are needed (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Theoretical analysis limited**: Theorem 1 only proves linear separability for linear models (lower expressivity than typical GNNs). Part 2 relies on the strong assumption that every homophilic/heterophilic node can be perfectly classified and assigned the appropriate filter (Reviewer 1).
- **Model complexity and fairness**: Node-MoE introduces additional learnable parameters and hyperparameters (γ, K). A parameter count comparison with baselines is missing. The broader hyperparameter search space, combined with longer per-epoch training time, likely increases total training time significantly. The claim of better computational complexity is not rigorously analyzed; prior post-fusion architectures may be cheaper (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Assumption about heterophilic patterns limits applicability**: The proposed method assumes heterophilic pattern when node features differ from neighbors. This may not hold when feature-label alignment is weak, reducing effectiveness. Prior works allow filters to adaptively learn without such assumption, making the distinction a trade-off rather than a clear improvement (Reviewer 4).
- **Figures 1, 2, 6, 15 problematic**: 
    - Figure 1 is confusing: it contradicts the description of homophily (a node with mixed labels is used as homophilic example), lacks clear labeling of nodes after global filter, and does not clearly delineate node-level homophily determination (Reviewers 3, 4).
    - Figure 2 is unclear: relevance to key contributions is not clear, message of node homophily density distribution lacks quantitative measure, and the y-tick (density) values appear incorrect (not in 0-1 range) (Reviewers 1, 3).
    - Figures 6 and 15 are inconsistent with the motivation: in Figure 6, even at homophily level 1 the high-pass filter average weight exceeds the low-pass weight, and the difference between weights is not significant across the homophily range; in Figure 15, even that trend is absent (Reviewer 1).
    - Figures 6 and 7 have font sizes too small (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation overload**: The notation h(v_i) is used for both node homophily level (Section 1) and node classifier (Section 3.1), causing confusion (Reviewer 3).
- **Choice of gating and expert models not justified**: Why GIN (claimed to have strong community detection capabilities) and ChebNetII? What alternatives were considered and why does this combination perform best? How does similar expert selection for neighbors work in heterophilic graphs? (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Missing details and comparisons**:
    - No clear algorithm detailing the techniques (Reviewer 2).
    - No comparison with methods using higher-order polynomial filters, piecewise polynomial filters, or graph transformers that could achieve similar node-wise filtering without MoE complexity (Reviewer 5).
    - End-to-end training time comparison versus other methods is missing (Reviewer 5).
    - The paper does not discuss limitations (Reviewer 5).
- **Unanswered questions from reviewers**:
    - What were the winning hyperparameters from the search space for the main results (especially Top K setting)? (Reviewer 2)
    - How would random initialization of filter coefficients impact performance? (Reviewer 5)
