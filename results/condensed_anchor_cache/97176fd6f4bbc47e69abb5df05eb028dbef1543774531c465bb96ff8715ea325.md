- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 1, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a “transform coefficient” method to solve Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) and Partial Unbalanced Optimal Transport (PUOT) with KL divergence without entropy regularization. The approach uses KKT optimality conditions to derive iterative updates for coefficients that yield the marginal distributions. Then, with those marginals and Lagrange multipliers that encode sparsity, a Cost-Reweighted OT (CROT) problem with a modified cost matrix is solved to obtain the transport plan. Numerical experiments on small synthetic datasets and a domain adaptation task are presented.

### Strengths
* The specific approach of deriving and using transform coefficients to first obtain the marginals, then solving a reweighted OT problem, is new. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
* The method yields a sparse transport plan (since it avoids entropy regularization), which may be beneficial in settings requiring sparsity. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5)
* The exploitation of the Lagrange multipliers **s** (from the KKT conditions) to extract the sparsity pattern and to define the CROT cost matrix is a conceptually interesting step. (Reviewer 2)
* The method is unregularized (no entropy term) so it does not produce dense solutions by default. (Reviewer 5)
* Empirical results indicate computation time gains over a Majorization-Minimization (MM) algorithm and the regularization‑path approach, and the method is applied to a domain adaptation task with comparisons to DeepJDOT and JUMBOT. (Reviewer 2)
* One reviewer (Reviewer 3) noted the reweighting idea is “somewhat new and a bit interesting” and could be a potential future direction with more careful analysis.

### Weaknesses
* **Lack of convergence guarantees.** Algorithm 1 (and the PUOT analogue) have no convergence analysis; the stop criterion is not stated or related to the KKT conditions. This is a major concern raised by all reviewers. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
* **Absence of rigorous theoretical justification.** The key results (e.g., Proposition 1, the equivalence claimed between UOT and the CROT problem, and the derivation of transform coefficients) are presented informally, without proofs or error bounds. Several reviewers doubt that the conversion is exact. (Reviewers 3, 4, 5)
  * Reviewer 3 states the conversion is only approximate and “the whole framework and idea, though interesting, are invalid” because one cannot retrieve the true UOT solution from an approximate OT conversion.
* **Insufficient literature review and positioning.** The paper omits relevant prior work:
  * L2‑regularized UOT/OT (Nguyen et al., 2022; Essid & Solomon, 2017) and the fact that these also induce sparsity with accelerated convergence. (Reviewers 3, 5)
  * Prior results showing that UOT converges to OT as the penalty (τ) grows. (Reviewer 3)
  * The distinction between “partial unbalanced OT” and other notions of partial OT (semi‑constrained vs. fractional mass transportation) and related references (Le et al., 2021; Chapel et al., 2020). (Reviewer 4)
* **Weak and sometimes unfair empirical evaluation.**
  * All experiments use very small datasets. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  * The quality of the estimated coupling **π*** is assessed by comparing to a plain (balanced) OT solution, not to an existing UOT solver. This comparison is unfair and does not measure how well the UOT problem is solved. (Reviewer 2)
  * Figure 4 shows a UOT solution with τ=10⁵ (very strong marginal penalty) but compares to Sinkhorn instead of non‑regularized OT, which is inconsistent. (Reviewer 2)
  * In the domain adaptation experiments, the JUMBOT baseline is used, but JUMBOT jointly with an existing UOT solver (to provide a fair comparison) is not included. (Reviewer 2)
  * Only synthetic data are used, which limits the evaluation. (Reviewer 3)
* **Scalability concerns and lack of complexity analysis.** The online computational complexity of Algorithm 1 is not given; each iteration is O(NM), similar to Sinkhorn, and then an additional OT solver must be run. This suggests the method may be slower than Sinkhorn or gradient‑based alternatives. (Reviewers 1, 5)
* **Theoretical novelty is limited.** The derivation of transform coefficients follows directly from KKT conditions; Corollary 4 (asymptotic convergence) is a weaker version of the non‑asymptotic result in Nguyen et al., 2022. (Reviewer 5)
* **Assumptions and notation issues.**
  * The derivation assumes ‖**a**‖₁ = ‖**b**‖₁; the paper does not explain how the method behaves when this does not hold. (Reviewer 2, 4)
  * Many notations are undefined (e.g., KL divergence, Ĉ*ᵢⱼ in Proposition 1). (Reviewer 4)
  * The term “partial unbalanced optimal transport” conflicts with established usage (semi‑constrained vs. partial transport). (Reviewer 4)
* **CROT motivation and parameter tuning are hazy.** It is unclear why one would solve the reweighted OT with Sinkhorn, L2‑norm, or sparse OT solvers (each with its own hyper‑parameters) instead of directly using the sparsity pattern from **s** to fix zero entries in **π**. (Reviewer 2) Also, the effect of the extra hyper‑parameter η (the scaling factor for the Lagrange multipliers in the modified cost matrix) on the solution quality is not analyzed. (Reviewer 4)
* **Unsupported claims.** The paper asserts (without evidence or references) that the optimal **π*** should be sparse in most cases. (Reviewer 4)
* **Presentation quality.** The text contains numerous grammatical errors and inconsistent usage (e.g., “KL divergence” vs. “KL Divergence”). Figure 3 is nearly unreadable in black and white. (Reviewers 1, 4)
* **Minority contrasting opinion:** Reviewer 3 rated the paper 1 (Reject), stating that the framework is invalid because the conversion from UOT to OT is approximate, not exact, and that the experiments are only on synthetic data. All other reviewers gave a score of 3 (Weak Reject/Neutral). This strong negative view should not be ignored.
