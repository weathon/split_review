## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Grounded Point Colorization (GPC), a pre-training method for LiDAR-based 3D object detectors that teaches the model to colorize point clouds using RGB images. To handle color variance, GPC provides ground-truth colors to 20% of points as “hints.” Reviewers largely find the idea novel and the seed-point design reasonable. Two reviewers (R1, R2) are distinctly more positive (scores 8), praising the strong empirical results and clear writing. Two reviewers (R3, R4) are more cautious (scores 6), raising concerns about unfair multimodal advantages, outdated baselines, insufficient ablations, and limited downstream performance on Waymo. There is broad consensus on several weaknesses, but disagreement on the overall impact.

### Strengths
- **Novel pre-training objective**: Introducing colorization as a pretext task for LiDAR point clouds is a new and orthogonal approach to existing self-supervised methods (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- **Seed-point design**: Providing ground-truth colors to a subset of points is a simple yet effective fix for the color variance problem (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Strong empirical gains**: GPC achieves significant fine-tuning improvements; on 20% of KITTI data it outperforms full training from scratch (R1). Results are validated on both KITTI and Waymo (R1, R2).  
- **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper explains the failure of naive colorization, then logically builds toward the proposed solution, making it easy to follow (R1, R4).  
- **Thoughtful ablation**: The experiment showing that naive colorization hurts performance is especially illuminating (R2).  
- **Completeness**: Despite focusing on KITTI, the experiments cover key ablations and two datasets (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Outdated detection baselines**: Experiments rely on PointRCNN and IA-SSD, which are not state-of-the-art. Reviewers stress the need to compare with current top detectors such as PV-RCNN and FSD (R2, R4). Results on KITTI and Waymo are thus not fully convincing; the Waymo performance in particular is described as “weak” (R4).  
- **Multimodal vs. unimodal comparison**: GPC uses camera images during pre-training, yet all compared methods (e.g., ProposalContrast, DepthContrast) are LiDAR-only. This may give GPC an unfair advantage; the authors should discuss or control for this (R3).  
- **Missing linear probing**: Without linear probing experiments, it is unclear how much of the fine-tuning improvement comes from pre-training itself versus other factors (R3).  
- **Insufficient loss ablation**: The comparison of loss variants is not fair because different losses have vastly different magnitudes and were likely not tuned separately. SL1 regression sometimes outperforms CE; a balanced SL1 could be considered (R2). The reweighted loss (Eq. 5) may also reduce transferability across datasets with different class distributions (R1).  
- **Lack of ablation on color bins**: The number of color bins (128) is not systematically studied; the paper should include an ablation on this hyperparameter (R1).  
- **Seed-point and 20% data selection not detailed**: The paper does not specify whether seed points are sampled randomly or informed (e.g., ensuring per-cluster coverage). An informed strategy could reduce required seeds and boost results (R2). Similarly, the selection of the 20% data subset is not clarified; if random, prior work (LaserMix) already achieves nearly the same level (R3).  
- **Camera-projection inaccuracies**: Color assignment depends on projecting LiDAR points onto 2D images, which can be inaccurate for points outside the camera FOV or where multiple points map to the same pixel (R1). KITTI only has a front-facing camera, so many points lack color ground truth; the authors should explain why pre-training nonetheless remains beneficial (R3).  
- **Per-class performance discussion lacking**: In Table 2, GPC outperforms ProposalContrast and DepthContrast overall, but those baselines are better on the cyclist and car classes. This pattern deserves explicit discussion (R2).  
- **Downstream task mismatch**: Colorization primarily learns semantic cues, but object detection also requires accurate location/pose. Segmentation might be a better downstream task; the authors should consider evaluating on benchmarks like nuScenes or on point/range segmentation (R4).  
- **Missing recent related work**: The paper omits key references: image colorization as a pretext task (Zhang et al.), point cloud colorization (Point2color), and seed-based image colorization (Wan et al.). These are highly relevant and should be discussed (R2).  
- **Minor presentation issues**: Some sentences contain grammatical errors or missing articles (R2). The decorative math on page 4 is unnecessary (R4). A sentence in the introduction claiming “no explicit connection between input data and labels” is confusing (R2).
