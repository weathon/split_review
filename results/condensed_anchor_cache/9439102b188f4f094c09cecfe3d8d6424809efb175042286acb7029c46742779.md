## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ProtoLLM, an example-free framework that prompts an LLM to generate oracle feature values per class, building a prototype for a non‑parametric classifier. The prototype can be combined with few‑shot samples. The method requires no training and is evaluated on several tabular datasets in zero‑shot and few‑shot settings. Reviewers note strong performance in some settings and some novelty, but raise major concerns about clarity, dataset choice, reproducibility, scalability, missing comparisons, and applicability beyond binary classification.

### Strengths
- Strong overall performance (R1).
- Novelty: generating features with LLMs is known (FeatLLM, ICML 2024), but using them to build prototypes for classification is relatively new (R1, R3).
- The method requires no training and is applicable to both zero‑shot and few‑shot learning (R3).
- Intriguing evidence that LLMs can solve tabular classification by predicting feature values per class in a zero‑shot manner (R2).
- Comprehensive ablation studies and helpful appendix material, including a complete results table (R2, R3).
- Choice of few‑shot baseline methods is comprehensive and strong (R2).
- Work is reproducible (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and formatting.** The paper is hard to follow and lacks mathematical formulation. For example, the feature value *z* is not defined; Figures 3 and 4 lack color legends. The term “Oracle” is misleading – “LLM‑generated feature” is more appropriate (R1, R3). Many typos and odd phrasing appear throughout (e.g., lines 283, 300, 337, 356, 381, Table 1, Sec. 4.1 header). Line numbers do not align with lines (R2).
- **Dataset selection and evaluation scope.**
    - Most datasets are binary classification; multi‑class datasets (e.g., those from STUNT) are missing, and regression tasks, a key tabular application, are not addressed (R1, R3). The paper does not report full‑shot accuracy (R1).
    - The set of datasets is small and non‑standard; a large established benchmark (e.g., arXiv:2305.02997) should have been used. Datasets appear cherry‑picked; some baselines are missing in Fig. 5 (R2). The performance of a simple logistic regression baseline with only 64 samples beats ProtoLLM on NHANES and Cultivars (the two datasets where contamination is less likely), and the declining relative performance on NHANES after 16 samples suggests a scaling limitation (R2). A Limitations section is needed (R2).
- **Reproducibility and LLM choice.**
    - Main results are demonstrated only on ChatGPT (closed‑source), which is not reproducible and biases future research. Results should be shown on open‑source LLMs (e.g., T0, LLAMA‑3‑8B) while controlling for model size, data, and hyperparameters (R2).
    - In zero‑shot, TabLLM (GPT‑3.5) already beats ProtoLLM on 4 of 9 datasets, undermining the claimed zero‑shot contribution. Few‑shot comparisons with TabLLM are unfair because they use a different backbone (T0 vs. GPT). The authors should provide TabLLM GPT‑3.5 results in few‑shot and compare using the same backbone (R2).
    - Missing comparisons with other LLM‑based tabular learning frameworks (R1: refs [6,7,8]; R2: e.g., DSPy). More zero‑shot baselines are needed (R2).
- **Cost and scalability.** The method requires prompting the LLM *d × K × C* times (number of features, iterations, classes), which restricts applicability to datasets with few features and classes and can be computationally expensive (R3). The token consumption per average iteration should be reported (R2). A comparison of the time to construct golden prototypes vs. training baselines would be informative (R3).
- **Variance and statistical significance.** Few‑shot experiments lack variance estimates (R1). Figure 5 shows averages but should include standard deviations or confidence intervals; statistical tests (e.g., Wilcoxon signed‑rank test) should be performed to assess significance of performance gains (R3).
- **Ablation for added value of LLM prototype in few‑shot.** The paper should assess the benefit of the LLM prototype in the few‑shot setting by constructing the prototype using only the few‑shot samples (excluding LLM generation) and comparing performance, similar to the zero‑shot ablation (R3).
- **Data contamination and novelty.** Most datasets are old; the method may not generalize to truly novel tabular problems (e.g., factory settings, biology). Even on the two newer datasets (NHANES, Cultivars) performance is weak. It is unclear whether the approach works when LLMs lack relevant knowledge (R1, R2, R3). Investigating whether humans could generate equally relevant prototypes would be useful (R3).
- **Handling of continuous features, distance function, and hyperparameters.**
    - How does the method deal with continuous features? Is it similar to FeatLLM? (R2)
    - The distance function and the meaning of “logistics” in the sentence “All we need to calculate the logistics is the Euclidean distance between the input samples and obtained prototype” need clarification (R2).
    - The temperature τ in Equation 3 appears critical; how is it selected? (R3)
- **Additional missing ablations and questions.**
    - Impact of the number of LLM parameters on performance (R3).
    - Effect of noisy samples when generating prototypes in the few‑shot setting (R3).
    - Performance on high‑dimensional datasets (e.g., Arrhythmia) (R3).
    - Modifications needed to extend the method to regression tasks (R1, R3).
