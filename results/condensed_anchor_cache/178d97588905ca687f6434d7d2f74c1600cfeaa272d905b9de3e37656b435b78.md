## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Augmented Emotional Semantics Learning (AESL) framework for multi-label class incremental learning (MLCIL) in emotion decoding. AESL addresses past- and future-missing partial label problems using an augmented Emotional Relation Graph (ERG) for soft label generation, semantic-guided feature decoupling via cross-attention, and relation-based knowledge distillation grounded in affective dimensions (arousal, valence). Evaluations on three datasets (video, audio, brain images) under multiple incremental protocols show significant gains over baselines and progress toward an oracle upper bound, with ablation and qualitative analyses.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem and framework**: MLCIL for emotion decoding is introduced as a new setting; the integration of ERG, graph autoencoder (GIN-based), label propagation, and affective-dimension distillation is a novel end-to-end contribution applicable beyond emotions (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- **ERG and label disambiguation**: The augmented ERG with graph-based label disambiguation is interesting and intuitive (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Relation-based knowledge distillation**: Integrates domain knowledge from affective space via distillation from two teachers, shown to be reasonable and effective (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Rigorously tested on three distinct datasets with multiple CIL protocols, including an oracle upper bound; ablation studies show incremental benefit of each component (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Qualitative analyses**: T-SNE visualizations, ERG visualizations, and parameter sensitivity help build intuition (Reviewer 2).
- **Task meaningful**: The practical importance for HCI and robotics is recognized (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear problem definition**: The explanation of how the model evolves across tasks while maintaining a single parameter set is insufficient; Figure 1 does not clearly show the technical challenges of parameter sharing and knowledge retention (Reviewer 1). In contrast, Reviewer 2 found the formalism comprehensive but noted confusing component ordering and the need for a big-picture view aligned with the figure.
- **Missing baselines**:
  - Building emotion embeddings from descriptions (name, definition, examples) or directly from arousal-valence vectors is not compared; such a baseline could simplify the pipeline and question the need for graph-based label propagation (Reviewer 2, Questions 1–2).
  - For relation-based knowledge distillation, a simpler baseline using SoTA embedding models that already model arousal and valence dimensions is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Inadequate comparison with recent methods**: The paper lacks comparison with recent MLCIL methods and a cited relevant work (e.g., “Multi-View Multi-Label Fine-Grained Emotion Decoding From Human Brain Activity”) (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues**:
  - The introductory paragraphs on HCI are verbose and misaligned with the paper’s emotion classification focus (Reviewer 4).
  - The related work section is deliberately removed from the main text, which is inadvisable (Reviewer 4).
  - Multiple citation format errors (e.g., line 080) and disconnection of the “Parameter Sensitivity” title (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 3 lacks explanation: what is the V-A teacher? Is it frozen or tuned? How does the left distillation framework relate to the right discrete emotion categories? (Reviewer 3).
  - Tables/descriptions in the method are unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient analysis of why the framework works**: No experiments or discussions explore the internal mechanisms that lead to success; case studies or badcases are needed to illustrate strengths and limitations (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the accuracy of the unsupervised ERG construction is not justified beyond quantitative comparisons; in-depth analysis is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Imprecise claims**: Some contribution statements are overstated (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing complexity analysis**: No computational complexity analysis is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - L159–160 claim that emotion embeddings require graph complexity is unsubstantiated (Reviewer 2).
  - Could emotions be encoded from the start as a combination of arousal and valence vectors, and would that avoid the relational knowledge distillation component? (Reviewer 2, Question 2).
  - How can the accuracy of the constructed ERG be proven? (Reviewer 4).
