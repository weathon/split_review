## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses private inference on fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) by proposing a layerwise polynomial-degree optimization for activation functions (ReLU) in pre-trained CNNs (ResNet-20/32 on CIFAR-10). Two main contributions: (1) replacing the conventional minimax approximation with a weighted least squares method that incorporates the input distribution (assumed normal) to minimize mean squared error, tying this to minimizing the variance of the loss; and (2) using a dynamic programming algorithm to select the polynomial degree per layer, accounting for each layer’s impact on overall accuracy. Additionally, the paper introduces layerwise ciphertext moduli-chain management to reduce bootstrapping operations. On CIFAR-10, the method achieves 3.44× and 3.16× inference time reductions over prior uniform-degree approaches for ResNet-20 and ResNet-32, respectively, with negligible accuracy loss.

### Strengths
- **Importance and relevance**: The paper targets a critical problem – accelerating FHE-based private inference via polynomial approximation of activation functions (reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **No retraining required**: The approach works with pre-trained models, avoiding accuracy recovery steps common in retraining-based methods (reviewer 2).
- **Layer-specific optimization**: Exploiting layerwise characteristics to reduce polynomial degrees significantly, using input distributions and dynamic programming (reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Methodological clarity**: The theoretical analysis of the weighted least squares approximation, the dynamic programming formulation, and the moduli-chain management are well presented and mathematically detailed (reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Inference time reduction**: Demonstrated 3–4× speedups over previous uniform-degree baselines on ResNet-20/32 (reviewers 1, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited empirical scope** (multiple reviewers): Evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10 on ResNet-20/32. Standard practice in private inference requires validation on larger datasets (CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet) and deeper networks (ResNet-18, ResNet-101/152) to assess scalability and the impact of different activation distributions (reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 2 notes that scalability of the dynamic programming approach itself may become impractical for very deep networks, as complexity scales with network depth (unlike manual ReLU pruning which is stage-independent).
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - With MPC-based ReLU reduction methods (e.g., AutoReP, ICCV 2023) that selectively approximate at the neuron level (reviewer 1).
  - With hybrid HE+MPC approaches for end-to-end runtime (reviewer 2).
  - With low-degree polynomial approximations (e.g., 2-degree polynomials) that achieve negligible accuracy loss; such low-degree approximations might outperform the proposed higher-degree ones (reviewers 2, 3).
  - With prior work on low-degree polynomial substitution that has shown effectiveness on complex datasets (reviewer 2).
- **Unvalidated core assumptions** (reviewer 1):
  1. *Normality of ReLU inputs*: The paper assumes activations follow a normal distribution without empirical evidence or theoretical justification. This may not hold for every layer or network architecture.
  2. *Variance of loss as accuracy surrogate*: The justification that minimizing the variance of the loss (via weighted MSE) is a suitable proxy for classification accuracy is insufficient. In FHE inference, a single large unbounded approximation error can corrupt the result, so the minimax bound is typically preferred. A rigorous connection to accuracy is needed.
- **Ablation and attribution of improvements** (reviewer 4): It is unclear how much each technique contributes (weighted MSE over minimax, layerwise over uniform, dynamic programming over simple greedy). The large reduction in polynomial degree seems driven primarily by the weighted MSE choice; ablation studies are required.
- **Impact of polynomial degree reduction on inference time** (reviewer 1): Table 3 shows orders-of-magnitude decrease in max polynomial degree, but this is not proportionally reflected in max depth or overall inference time. A detailed breakdown of computational costs across degrees and depths is needed to explain the discrepancy.
- **Only ReLU evaluated** (reviewer 4): Other activation functions (Swish, Sigmoid, GeLU) are not tested, limiting generality.
- **Practicality concerns** (minority reviewer 2): The minority reviewer (score 3) questions the practicality of using higher-degree polynomials in FHE, as they require extensive bootstrapping compared to hybrid approaches. The same reviewer states that the paper offers no new insights to enhance the overall feasibility of private inference.
- **Missing visualization** (reviewer 4): A plot comparing the ReLU function, the proposed approximation, and other baselines would help illustrate approximation quality.
- **Question on initial layer identity** (reviewer 2): Table 1 shows the first layer requires a high-degree polynomial. The reviewer asks if identity can be used instead, since prior PI-specific ReLU optimization shows early ReLUs are often unimportant. This represents a potential missing experiment or alternative that could simplify the solution.
