## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a pixel-aware diffusion schedule where each pixel’s drift and diffusion rate depends on its initial value (SNR), inspired by the water‑pouring algorithm. This accelerates the forward process (200–500 steps vs. ~1000) and enables parallel one‑shot reverse trajectory generation via an auxiliary model that predicts per‑pixel noise schedules. Evaluated on CelebA and CIFAR‑10, the method is claimed to be 4× faster while maintaining quality.

### Strengths
- The idea of making the diffusion schedule pixel‑dependent (based on per‑pixel SNR) is novel and not a straightforward extension of existing work.
- The theoretical link between pixel values and the PSNR trajectory, as well as the use of the water‑pouring analogy, is interesting.
- The forward process under this paradigm is well formulated.
- Enabling parallelized one‑shot inference (single forward pass of the model) is a valuable extension that reduces sampling time.
- The model achieves lower FID scores on CIFAR‑10 and CelebA compared to DDIM/DDPM baselines with faster sampling.
- *Minority view:* One reviewer found no strengths in the manuscript, citing lack of novelty and poor execution.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison to state‑of‑the‑art acceleration methods:** The paper only compares to DDIM, DDPM, and SDE‑based sampling – all outdated. No experiments or direct comparisons with DPM‑Solver (the default sampler for Stable Diffusion), consistency models (Song et al. ICML 2023), improved consistency models, or distribution‑matching distillation (Yin et al. CVPR 2024). DPM‑Solver is only mentioned in a brief sentence but not evaluated, allegedly due to “space limitations.” This is a critical omission.
- **Limited scope to pixel‑space diffusion:** The method requires per‑pixel SNR, which prohibits application to latent diffusion models (LDMs) – the current standard for efficiency and high‑resolution generation. The computational overhead of pixel‑level SNR calculations grows strongly with resolution, and the method cannot leverage the efficiency gains of latent space. No extension to LDMs is discussed.
- **Poor justification and analysis of key components:**
  - No proof or sufficient empirical evidence that the method truly requires fewer steps; the claim is not substantiated with theoretical convergence analysis or a step‑count comparison versus standard diffusion.
  - The derivation of the forward process (especially Equation 8 and how \(\overline{\alpha} = \prod \alpha_t\) is obtained when \(\alpha\) depends on the pixel value) is unclear and not adequately explained.
  - The SNR definition in Equation 11 deviates from the standard GDM definition by making SNR dependent on \(x_t\). The rationale for this deviation is not justified.
  - The reverse process (Section 3.3) is poorly described; it is not clear how Algorithm 1 is derived or why the model can output all 500 noise predictions at once. The motivation for predicting \(x_\delta\) (structural information only) rather than \(x_0\) is not adequately justified, and the impact of VAE approximation error is not analyzed.
- **Experimental concerns:**
  - Only two small, low‑resolution datasets (CIFAR‑10 and CelebA) are used; no high‑resolution or complex benchmarks are included.
  - Tables 1 and 2 are presented unprofessionally: DDIM (a sampler) is compared as if it were a model, the number of parameters and steps differ across methods without explanation, and the proposed model has ~2× the parameters of baselines, raising fairness concerns.
  - It is not shown how DDIM performs at the same low number of steps (e.g., 200) as the proposed method; this would be a necessary ablation.
  - No ablation study is provided for the key hyperparameters \(\gamma\) and \(T\), which are set differently for the two datasets without a systematic procedure.
  - No visualization of the intermediate diffusion steps or a clear demonstration that the pixel‑aware scheduling visually changes the process in a beneficial way.
- **Poor writing and presentation quality:**
  - Figures are of low quality: blurred, stretched, inconsistent fonts, small text, and poor color choices. Figure 1 (main concept) is not impactful; Figure 2 is distorted; Figure 3 adds little; Figure 4 is unclear; Figure 5a has tiny images.
  - Many typos and formatting errors: “offering” → “offer”, “over-all” → “overall”, “atleast” → “at least”, “dissusion” → “diffusion”, repeated citations, incorrect use of “\citep”.
  - The continuous‑time version (Eq. 10) is introduced but not used later, creating confusion.
  - References are mostly pre‑2023, missing several recent key works (e.g., Common Diffusion Noise Schedules and Sample Steps are Flawed, which discusses zero‑SNR and is highly relevant).
- **Motivation and consistency issues:**
  - The water‑pouring analogy from communications is not clearly justified for image generation; the term “dual” in Figure 1 is inappropriate.
  - In Figure 2, the starting points at t=0 are shown at different values; the reviewer questions why they are not identical.
  - The transition from discrete DDPM formalism (background) to continuous SDEs (Appendix B) is inconsistent and confusing.
