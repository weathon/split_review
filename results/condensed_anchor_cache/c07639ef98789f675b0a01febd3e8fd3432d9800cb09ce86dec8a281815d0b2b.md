## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SF(DA)², a source-free domain adaptation method that leverages data augmentation in the feature space via three losses: Spectral Neighborhood Clustering (SNC), Implicit Feature Augmentation (IFA), and Feature Disentanglement (FD). The approach constructs an augmentation graph from neighbor relationships and uses spectral clustering to form tighter clusters, while IFA and FD regularizers simulate unlimited augmented features without heavy computation or memory. Experiments on 2D image datasets (VisDA, DomainNet) and 3D point cloud (PointDA-10) show state-of-the-art results. The reviewers’ assessments range from very positive (praising originality, theoretical grounding, and thorough experiments) to more critical (questioning novelty compared to AaD, experimental completeness, and the interplay of losses). Three of four reviewers recommend acceptance (scores 6,8,6); one rates 5.

### Strengths
- **Novel perspective and motivation**: The paper explores data augmentation in SFDA via implicit feature augmentation, reducing computational cost compared to explicit augmentation methods. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Well-motivated with theoretical analysis**: In-depth discussion and mathematical foundations provide insight into why the method works. (R1, R2)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive comparisons on multiple benchmarks (2D image and 3D point cloud) demonstrate significant improvements over prior methods; running time analysis supports efficiency claims. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-structured, with proper notation and a logical flow. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Practical value**: The method addresses practical issues like handling similar categories via feature disentanglement. (R2)
- **Originality**: The implicit augmentation approach broadens the scope of SFDA research. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and similarity to AaD**: The SNC loss is very similar to the loss in AaD [1] in both motivation and objective form; the paper does not clearly distinguish its contribution (e.g., whether the graph structure introduces a different learning dynamic or is simply a re-parameterization). (R3, R4)
- **Insufficient baselines and missing comparisons**:  
  – Missing comparisons with SHOT++ and recent source-available DA methods from 2022-2023. (R2)  
  – Results for NRC and AaD are not reported on DomainNet, nor AaD on PointDA-10, making it hard to contextualize performance. (R3)  
  – Several results for other methods in the main tables do not match the original papers; the paper does not note that these are reproduced results. (R4)
- **Ablation study shortcomings**:  
  – SNC alone outperforms SNC+IFA or SNC+FD, and the performance drop when using only IFA or FD individually suggests potential conflict; the interplay between losses and why IFA and FD must be applied simultaneously is not thoroughly explained. (R1, R4)  
  – No ablation study on hyperparameters α1 and α2; they are set differently per dataset, which may be sensitive and hard to tune in an unsupervised way. (R4)  
  – IFA loss weight is very small (1e-4) compared to FD loss (10) in many datasets, making its contribution unclear; a more detailed analysis or ablation with varying weights is needed. (R3)
- **Covariance matrix estimation concerns**: The online estimation of class-wise covariance matrices lacks detail on how accuracy is ensured; early in adaptation, target predictions are unreliable and features are entangled, which could bias the estimates and degrade performance, especially for classes with low probability. (R1, R3)
- **Relationship between augmentation graph and section 3.4**: The graph Ĝ is not explicitly used in section 3.4; it is unclear how equation (4) (which only considers cosine-similarity neighborhoods within mini-batches) influences Ĝ and the overall optimization. (R2)
- **Potential negative effect of spectral clustering**: If two groups of features belonging to the same ground-truth category are erroneously assigned to separate partitions, the method may push them apart, degrading performance. (R2)
- **Missing related work citations**: The Introduction lacks relevant DA surveys and recent works (e.g., [2], [3] for data augmentation in SFDA). (R2, R3)
- **Unclear impact of proposed method on SHOT**: Figure 2 shows the method does not improve SHOT, while AaD indicates SHOT is already similar to NRC/AaD; no explanation is provided. (R4)
- **Non-informative figure**: Figure 3(b) is neither consistent with the corresponding accuracy nor useful for hyperparameter tuning; more datasets are needed to support hypotheses about the performance drop when using only FD or IFA. (R4)
- **Lack of evaluation for IFA on other tasks**: The paper claims IFA is a general-purpose augmentation technique but provides no examples or discussion of its applicability to other unsupervised tasks, nor its limitations. (R3)
- **Unanswered question about using only IFA and FD**: It is unclear whether adaptation could work using only IFA and FD losses, without SNC. (R4)
