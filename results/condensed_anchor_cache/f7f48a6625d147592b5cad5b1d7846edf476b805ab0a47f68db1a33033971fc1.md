## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Simple-TTS, a latent diffusion model for TTS that uses a pre-trained text encoder (ByT5) and audio autoencoder (EnCodec) to avoid explicit text-speech alignment. It adapts the U-ViT architecture into a U-Audio Transformer for speech synthesis, trains with v-prediction and cross-attention, and pads all utterances to a fixed 20-second length during training.

### Strengths
- Training pipeline is simplified by leveraging pre-trained components (ByT5, EnCodec) and removing the duration predictor; only the diffusion weights are trained (Reviewer 1). This design facilitates training (Reviewer 3).
- The U-Audio Transformer architecture is potentially useful for speech generation (Reviewer 2).
- Some samples in the supplementary material sound expressive (Reviewer 1).
- Reported experimental results are encouraging (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Not end-to-end / overclaiming simplicity**: The model relies on separately pre-trained components (ByT5, EnCodec) and involves multiple training stages, contradicting the “end-to-end” and “simple” claims. Reviewer 1 questions the end-to-end definition; Reviewer 2 notes YourTTS is simpler because it trains everything from scratch; Reviewer 3 calls the claim an overstatement.
- **Poor audio quality**: The generated speech quality is substantially worse than recent SOTA models (NaturalSpeech2, VoiceBox, VALL-E, VITS). Reviewer 1 says the samples are “too bad” compared to NaturalSpeech2’s continuous latent generation; Reviewer 2 rates the audios below VITS and NaturalSpeech; Reviewer 3 attributes the low quality to the EnCodec autoencoder and recommends using DAC, HiFi-Codec, or re-training with a codec-based vocoder (e.g., Vocos).
- **Insufficient comparisons and missing experiments**: Only YourTTS (an easy baseline) is compared. Missing direct comparisons to NaturalSpeech2, VoiceBox, VALL-E, or VITS (trained on the same data). Missing MOS evaluation for text-only TTS (Reviewer 2, 3). Missing ablation study for the model architecture (Reviewer 3). Missing synthesis speed and sample rate (Reviewer 2). Missing robustness evaluation for varying text lengths (Reviewer 1, 3); Reviewer 1 specifically requests ASR metrics on the hard sentences from FastSpeech Appendix B.
- **Fixed-length training limitations**: Training with a fixed 20-second maximum raises concerns about GPU efficiency and the model’s ability to control or generate speech beyond that duration (Reviewer 1, 3). Reviewer 1 asks how the model would handle generating 30+ seconds of speech for long sentences.
- **Alignment robustness concerns**: Using cross-attention for text-speech alignment without explicit monotonic constraints may reintroduce robustness issues seen in Tacotron and Transformer TTS (Reviewer 1). The fixed-length padding and reliance on the diffusion model to learn alignment internally are suspected to be dataset-length-dependent.
- **Lack of controllability and similarity to prior work**: Removing the duration predictor reduces controllability over speech timing. The approach is essentially the same as NaturalSpeech2 except for the removal of the duration predictor, yet no comparison or ablation against that method is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Overstatement in abstract**: Claiming to release “the strongest publicly available system” based on outperforming YourTTS is an overstatement given the absence of comparisons with stronger SOTA models (Reviewer 1).
