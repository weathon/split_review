## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an imitation learning framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation that autonomously discovers manipulation concepts from unlabeled demonstrations (using only proprioceptive states) and uses a closed-loop concept-guided policy for task execution. The framework consists of an automatic concept discovery module (VQ-VAE-based) and a concept-aware policy learning module (Concept Selection Transformer + Concept-Guided Diffusion Policy). Experiments on six Robosuite (MimicGen) tasks with 950 demonstrations each show the method outperforms several baselines, especially under randomized initial conditions and noise.

### Strengths
- The overall idea of using self-discovered concepts from proprioceptive states to mitigate human annotation effort, semantic misalignment, and dynamic environmental unpredictability is a good catch (Reviewer 2). The design avoids reliance on predefined skills or human labels and adapts dynamically (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The three tricks for stable concept discovery from trajectories (Reviewer 1) and the self-supervised discovery mechanism are innovative (Reviewer 4).
- The approach is a novel combination of modern techniques (VQ-VAE, transformers, diffusion policy) applied to the open problem of concept-guided policy learning; it expands upon prior work like InfoCon (Reviewer 3).
- The paper compares to a set of highly relevant, SOTA baselines in a fairly direct head-to-head comparison and the proposed method nearly universally outperforms them by a good margin (Table 1) (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The methodology is well-designed and robust, with a clear pipeline, thorough experimental setup including an ablation study (Reviewer 4). The paper is written clearly with detailed explanations of architectures and loss functions (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related literature.** The paper does not contrast with works on learning abstract actions via clustering (BeT) or VQ-VAE, nor with self-supervised / mutual information based skill discovery works (e.g., Li et al. 2023, Peng et al. 2022, Li et al. 2024) (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Evaluation domain is relatively easy.** The Robosuite (MimicGen) tasks are inherently highly compositional, making concept discovery easier. Validation on an “in-the-wild” dataset (e.g., learning from play) is missing (Reviewer 1). Evaluation is limited to simulated tabletop manipulation; no real-world validation or roadmap for sim-to-real transfer (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear presentation details.**
  - Figures are messy and lack connections (e.g., Fig. 2 closed-loop policy part, Fig. 3 connections) (Reviewer 2).
  - It is unclear whether the discovered concepts are in a unified, task-agnostic space for all demonstrations (Reviewer 2).
  - The training data description is ambiguous: 950 demos per task — does that mean a single unified policy is trained on all 6 tasks at once (2.1) or one policy per task (2.2)? If the latter, performance may be close to overfitting (Reviewer 2).
  - It is unclear how the 950 demonstrations were generated (over all 6 tasks or a subset) (Reviewer 3).
- **Concept interpretability and quality are underexplored.** Only a brief evaluation with a single visual example; no mean similarity score, 2D embedding visualization, or analysis of whether all discovered concepts are utilized by the CST (Reviewers 3, 4). Characterization of XSkill via K-means clustering is not a charitable interpretation (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical problem setting is less practical.** The expensive robot demonstration collection reduces the significance of avoiding annotation, as raw demo collection is typically more costly than annotation (Reviewer 2).
- **Concerns about overfitting and over-parameterization.** The large variety of approaches (multiple transformer sizes, diffusion policy, hypernetworks) runs the risk of over-parameterization, especially since demonstrations are collected on the same tasks used for evaluation (Reviewers 3). The need for a hypernetwork is questioned (Reviewer 3).
- **Generalizability and scalability concerns.** What if demonstrations contain concept inconsistency (same manipulation done in different ways)? With growing demo amount, will the number of concepts converge or grow fast? (Reviewer 2)
- **Only proprioception used for concept discovery.** It seems natural to include observations; similar proprioception (e.g., robot pose) may correspond to different observations (e.g., obstacles), and it is unclear how to generalize to observation-conditioned concepts (Reviewer 1).
- **How to decide the number of concepts / codebook size K?** Robustness with respect to K is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Diversity collapse issue.** In skill discovery, a downstream selector can collapse onto one or two useful concepts, canceling discovered diversity. The paper does not address how to retain and demonstrate diversity across concepts (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing an IL SOTA baseline not limited to Diffusion Policy basis** (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewer 4’s request for mutual-information-based methods).
