## Merged Review

### Summary

This work proposes an end-to-end LLM-based method to construct knowledge graphs (KGs) from electronic medical record (EMR) notes, focusing on diseases, factors, treatments, and manifestations. The approach uses guided instruction prompting and evaluates 12 LLMs across encoder-only, decoder-only, and encoder-decoder architectures, demonstrating the methodology on age-related macular degeneration (AMD). While the problem is important and the analysis of multiple LLMs is useful, the paper suffers from a lack of comparison to prior work, unclear evaluation of KG construction quality, significant writing and presentation issues, and insufficient ablation studies. One reviewer (R4, rating 5) was more positive, praising the technique framework and clear prompts; another (R5, rating 1) strongly criticized the scientific rigor and contribution, calling the experimental design incomplete and the KG construction process not clearly articulated.

### Strengths
*   The problem of automatic knowledge graph construction from medical text is important and practically significant for drug discovery and clinical trial design (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5).
*   The study provides a thorough evaluation of 12 LLMs of different architectures, offering a useful comparative analysis (R2,R3,R5).
*   The instruction-based prompting approach is clearly demonstrated and may assist future researchers (R2,R4). R4 positively notes that the prompts make most LLMs work.
*   The methodology is applied to a real-world medical domain (AMD) (all reviewers).

### Weaknesses
*   **Insufficient comparison to prior work and baselines.** The paper does not adequately contextualize its contributions within the existing body of literature on LLM-based KG construction (R2). There is no comparison to standard results or other published methods (R1,R5). R1 states that the reported precision (0.98) and recall (1.0) are hard to believe without such comparisons. R5 notes no references for the prompt design process.
*   **Unclear evaluation of constructed KG.** How the ground truth labels are defined and how precision/recall are computed is not clearly explained (R3). R1 and R5 question whether the metrics actually evaluate QA performance rather than KG construction quality. R5 explicitly asks: “Why use QA performance to demonstrate KG construction performance?” and “How to evaluate the quality of KG construction?”
*   **Missing ablations and experimental details.** Several design choices are not justified or ablated:
    *   The preprocessing of clinical notes is not compared with using raw data (R4).
    *   The accuracy of the BioBERT NER model used to extract disease lists is not evaluated (R3).
    *   Similarity thresholds (e.g., 0.8) are given without explanation of how they are determined or varied (R3).
    *   Arbitrary hyperparameters are introduced without studying their effect (R3).
    *   The effectiveness of guided instruction-based prompting is not isolated; results without the guide are missing (e.g., "Vicuna-33B w/o guide") (R4).
    *   GPT-4 is not evaluated (R4).
    *   The difference between “open-book QA” and “in-context learning” is unclear; both seem to include question and context (R3).
*   **Poor writing and presentation quality.** The paper contains numerous typos, formatting errors, and inconsistencies (R3,R4). Examples: "Knowdledge" typo; cross-references to non-existent "Section ?0.8"; Table numbering wrong (says Tab.5 should be Tab.2); inconsistent notation for disease variables (\(d\) vs. \(c_{input}\)); undefined variable \(e\) in Section 4.6; Algorithm 2 symbol error; “hallucination” analysis relegated to appendix (R4). These issues reduce clarity and reproducibility.
*   **Unclear scope and purpose of the KG.** It is not stated whether the constructed KG is per-patient or general, nor why clinical point-of-care notes are used rather than scientific literature for general knowledge (R2). There is no plan to release the KG (R2). The overall KG construction process is not clearly articulated (R5).
*   **Limited methodological novelty for the venue.** Several reviewers note that the work is primarily an application and evaluation, lacking significant methodological innovation (R2, R5). R2 suggests the paper may be better suited for an NLP-oriented venue.
*   **Dataset specificity.** The evaluation is only on AMD data; testing on other existing datasets (e.g., i2b2) is requested (R1, R5).
