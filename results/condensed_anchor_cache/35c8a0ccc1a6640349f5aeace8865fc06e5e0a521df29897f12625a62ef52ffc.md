## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes InstructCV, a vision generalist that casts multiple computer vision tasks as instruction-guided image-to-image generation using a text-to-image diffusion model. It fine-tunes InstructPix2Pix on a multi-task dataset (segmentation, detection, depth, classification) with instructions generated via LLM paraphrases. Experiments show competitive performance on several benchmarks and generalization to unseen categories and datasets.

### Strengths

1. **Novel use of T2I models for vision tasks:** The paper pioneers instruction-tuning of a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model to perform multiple computer vision tasks, providing a human-intuitive language interface (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 notes the novel integration of dense prediction tasks with a T2I model as an editing-like paradigm, which seems reasonable for segmentation and depth compared to seq2seq.

2. **Generalization to unseen categories:** The T2I backbone enables open-vocabulary capabilities, leading to good generalization to unseen categories and datasets (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 1). However, Reviewer 2 notes this is not unique, as models like X-Decoder also demonstrate open-vocabulary generalization.

3. **Presentation:** The paper is well-organized with polished figures and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).

4. **Relevance:** The problem of generalist visual models is timely and relevant (Reviewer 3).

5. **Competitive performance on most tasks:** Reviewer 3 finds quantitative performance (except Image Classification) impressive; Reviewer 1 states results validate the method's effectiveness on a variety of tasks.

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty and strong resemblance to prior work:** The overall pipeline highly resembles InstructPix2Pix (Reviewer 4). Formulating CV tasks as generative problems is not new (Reviewer 4). The paper does not demonstrate the unique advantage of instruction tuning (generalization to unseen tasks), instead reducing to multi-task learning on a fixed set of tasks (Reviewer 2). A very similar concurrent work, InstructDiffusion [3*], is not compared or cited (Reviewer 1).

2. **Performance concerns and incomplete baselines:** 
   - Image classification performance is weak compared to baselines; it is unclear if diffusion models are well-suited for this spatially ungrounded task (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 considers the inference pipeline for classification (Figure 4) weird and hard to evaluate on 1000-class ImageNet.
   - Overall results lag far behind SOTA methods in Table 1 (Reviewer 4). SOTA depth estimation methods are not compared; bold VOC results are on old benchmarks (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 disagrees on non-classification tasks, finding them impressive, but Reviewer 4 and the results in Table 1 show a significant gap to task-specific models.
   - Missing comparisons with other strong generalist models: Painter [2*] (currently best in prediction tasks), X-Decoder (which also shows open-vocabulary generalization), and Prompt Diffusion [1*] for generation tasks (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
   - The method has only been validated on a subset of vision tasks; low-level tasks (denoise, deblur, derain) and generation are not addressed (Reviewer 1).

3. **Segmentation and classification inference issues:**
   - Semantic segmentation derivation of all class masks is unclear; predicting one class at a time reduces to referring segmentation rather than full semantic segmentation (Reviewer 1).
   - Both segmentation and classification require multiple inferences per image (N×), leading to significant computational overhead (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The reported inference time is inconsistent: 5 seconds per image in the main text vs. 10 seconds in the Appendix (Reviewer 1).
   - For object detection, the phrase “cross-reference the bounding boxes with the dataset annotations” in Appendix A.3 needs clarification (Reviewer 1).

4. **Instruction prompts and training choices:**
   - The use of rephrased prompts (InstructCV-RP) leads to worse performance than fixed templates (InstructCV-FP) when both use template prompts at inference (Table 3). The benefit of training with rephrased prompts is unclear, especially given only 7 variations per task and the ability to manually create such templates without an LLM (Reviewer 1). More ablations are needed, e.g., reporting InstructCV-FP results in Table 1 (Reviewer 1).
   - The paper claims that inpainting-based methods use prompts with “uninterpretable numerical values” – this claim is not clearly justified (Reviewer 1).

5. **Justification for single-model architecture:**
   - The paper does not justify why a single diffusion model should be preferred over an ensemble of task-specific experts (e.g., Mask R-CNN for segmentation, ResNet for classification) (Reviewer 3). The benefits of a unified model are not convincingly shown given the performance gaps.

6. **Minor issues:**
   - Section 2.2 contains an extensive introduction to diffusion models that could be moved to an appendix (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 suggests moving Related Work earlier for better flow.
   - Reference formatting is messy and inconsistent, with many missing conference/journal titles (Reviewer 1).
   - The ability to generalize to new datasets is largely attributed to the T2I backbone's open vocabulary, which is also available in other unified models; thus this is not a unique contribution (Reviewer 2).
