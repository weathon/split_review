## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose LLM-ABBA, which uses the ABBA symbolic time-series approximation to convert time series into token sequences, then fine-tunes LLMs (RoBERTa, Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B) via QLoRA for classification, regression, and forecasting tasks. A “fixed-polygonal chain trick” is introduced to mitigate error propagation from symbol misuse. The paper evaluates on many datasets across three tasks, with mixed results compared to state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths
1. The paper evaluates on a large variety of time series datasets across three distinct tasks (classification, regression, forecasting) and three different LLMs, providing breadth.
2. The technical content is easy to follow, with a detailed description of the ABBA algorithm.
3. The idea of connecting conventional symbolic aggregation methods (ABBA) with LLMs is a potentially helpful approach for tokenizing time series to be processed by LLMs.

### Weaknesses
1. **Incomplete and outdated baselines**:  
   - No comparison with other LLM-based time-series methods that are mentioned in the introduction, such as Time-LLM (Jin et al. 2024), One Fits All (Zhou et al. 2023), or the zero-shot text-based method of Gruver et al. (2023).  
   - For classification, *only TS2Vec* is used as baseline (Table 1), and many SOTA values are missing; some results are inconsistent with the TS2Vec paper. The separate set of baselines in Table 2 ignores TS2Vec, creating confusion.  
   - For regression, the “SOTA” column is not specified and omits advanced classification methods that are applicable (e.g., TS2Vec, TimesNet, T-Rep).  
   - For forecasting (Table 4), baselines include outdated methods like Informer; many recent methods (e.g., TimesNet, iTransformer, PatchTST, MOMENT) are missing, and many values are left blank without explanation.  

2. **Missing comparison with other symbolic approximations** (e.g., SAX) – the motivation for choosing ABBA over alternatives is not empirically supported.

3. **Lack of statistical rigor**:  
   - No standard deviations or confidence intervals are reported for any results, despite stochastic optimization and variable train/test splits.  
   - No significance tests or critical difference plots (e.g., Demšar 2006) are provided.

4. **Missing ablation studies**:  
   - No experiment that uses ABBA features *without* the LLM (e.g., training a classifier directly on the ABBA symbols) to justify the benefit of the LLM component.  
   - No comparison with zero-shot or few-shot LLM forecasting as in Gruver et al. (2023) – the paper jumps directly to expensive QLoRA fine-tuning.

5. **Limited novelty**:  
   - Sections 3.1–3.2 largely describe existing work (ABBA and standard LLM fine-tuning). The only technical contribution is Section 3.3 (fixed-polygonal chain trick), which is a minor modification.  
   - The overall pipeline (symbolize time series → feed to LLM) is similar to prior art (e.g., Jin et al. 2024).

6. **Soundness concerns**:  
   - ABBA cluster centers are translated into characters that may include special symbols; LLMs trained on natural language may not produce meaningful outputs given such sequences. This claim is not validated.

7. **Presentation issues**:  
   - A large portion of the paper is devoted to background methods, while the actual contribution (Sections 3.2–3.3) spans only one page. Important experimental details are pushed to appendices due to space constraints.

8. **Parameter sensitivity and calibration undocumented**:  
   - How ABBA’s parameters (e.g., number of clusters, tolerance) were chosen is not explained, nor is their impact on downstream tasks studied.

9. **Missing values and inconsistencies in result tables**:  
   - Tables 1 and 9 have many missing “SOTA” entries; some reported results differ from the TS2Vec paper. Table 4 (forecasting) has many missing values with no discussion.

10. **Lack of systematic analysis**:  
    - No analysis of when LLM-ABBA works or fails (e.g., sensitivity to time-series length, noise, time-warping; why the method appears better on regression tasks).

11. **Unclarified experimental details**:  
    - Figure 3 does not clearly indicate which panel (right) is the final approach used.  
    - The source of reported SOTA scores is not specified.  
    - For multivariate time series, it is not stated whether symbols are defined per variate or shared.  
    - The average number of symbols needed per time series sample is not reported.
