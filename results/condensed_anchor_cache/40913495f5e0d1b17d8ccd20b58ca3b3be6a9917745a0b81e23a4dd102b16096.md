## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a structured pruning framework that does not require retraining, using only a small amount of unlabeled data. It first groups coupled structures across layers based on dependencies and measures channel importance. A two-phase layer reconstruction strategy is then applied: (1) a sparsity penalty pushes information from less important channels into the remaining ones before pruning; (2) after pruning, the output discrepancy between the pruned and original models is calibrated. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet with ResNet and VGG models show improved accuracy over other calibration-based methods and match retraining-based approaches. The framework prunes in minutes on a single GPU.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- Improves group-based structured pruning with a holistic approach (R1).
- Achieves fairly good results compared with baselines (R2).
- Natural extension of previous methods to induce structured sparsity while maintaining accuracy (R3).
- Compacting information across filters by considering the action of a group prior to pruning is a favorable direction (R3).
- Minimizing the amount of data required makes the method attractive (R3).
- Clever strategy for transitioning loss functions to account for structural connectedness and encourage sparsity (R3).
- Balanced updating strategy to distribute gradient updates across layers (R3).
- Results for ResNet and VGG models demonstrate notable improvement over competing implementations (R3).
- The problem of recovering accuracy without finetuning is important and challenging (R4).
- Method seems technically sound and straightforward in principle (R4).
- Empirical results demonstrate the strength of this approach (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and significance**: The core idea of using weight magnitude for importance is not new; the formulation is similar to prior unstructured work and the paper does not sufficiently explore why this specific group importance provides a significant advantage over other structured or unstructured methods (R2). The grouping of coupled structures is proposed by DepGraph, and the remaining contribution is not significant given limited performance improvement (R1).
- **Weak baselines**: Retraining-based baselines (e.g., naïve L1-norm) are outdated and weak; more recent methods should be used (R1, R4). It is unclear how the L1-norm pruned model in Table 2 is generated, and other works (e.g., [a],[b],[c] cited by R4) show much superior retraining-based performance (R4). The L1-norm accuracy in Tables 1 and 2 seems low – does it use all training data? (R1, Question).
- **Data setting and “no retraining” claim**: The unlabeled data setting is questionable because the data still comes from the original dataset (still violates data privacy) and a small labeled calibration set is already a realistic setting; the paper does not clearly articulate the practical scenarios that justify this specific unlabeled data choice (R2). The claim of “no retraining” is exaggerated compared to genuine data-free methods (e.g., RED++) since this method still employs training data (R1).
- **Performance relative to data-free methods**: The performance is mediocre compared to state-of-the-art data-free algorithms such as RED++ (R1).
- **Limited experimental diversity**: Experiments are limited in diversity of datasets and models (R1).
- **Lack of clarity and justification in methodology**:
  - Equation (2): unclear indexing (i) and how filter groups are formed and how reconstruction loss is computed across groups (R3).
  - “Imbalanced updates and over-optimization” of earlier layers: not clearly explained what negative consequences this imbalance has and why it necessitates a balanced update strategy; how magnitude of updates in earlier layers affects reconstruction quality in later layers (R3). What causes Equation 2 to over-optimize earlier layers? (R1, Question).
  - Equation (4): similar notational issues; relationship between sparse penalty and filter groups not clearly defined (R3).
  - “Side effect of the sparse penalty”: needs explanation of the specific negative impact and how the method mitigates it (R3).
  - Section 3.2 claims direct layer-wise reconstruction is unsatisfactory for structured pruning, but no experiments or citations justify this claim, weakening the motivation (R4).
  - The phrase “sparsity of different layers is inconsistent in a pruning group” (Section 3.2) is unclear (R4, Question).
- **Algorithm and implementation details**:
  - Algorithm 1: the pretrained model input is not used in the algorithm body; should show how its weights are used in the filter reconstruction and pruning process (R3). Also, how are the layer-wise pruning ratios selected? (R4, Question). Typo in the last weight update (R4).
  - Table 2: missing column headers or visual separators to delineate the first set of columns (Top-1, RP, RF) from the second set (R3).
  - Why is VGG-19 used instead of VGG-16? (R1, Question).
- **Missing analysis and comparisons**:
  - No detailed analysis of the interaction between the group importance metric and the two-phase reconstruction; unclear if reconstruction simply compensates for information loss or has a more fundamental reason (R2). Suggested: comprehensive experiment report comparing with other baselines (R2).
  - PSP yields similar RP/RF factors to AdaPrune but much better accuracy; is the gain due to the calibration step? Do other non-training methods also perform post-pruning calibration? (R3). Compare accuracy of calibration vs. fine-tuning under same group-wise pruning ratio to show effectiveness of calibration (R1, Question).
  - Figure 5: ResNet18 on CIFAR100 shows a sharp drop-off for “w/o REC” compared to other configurations – is this due to architecture, dataset, or both? (R3).
- **Missing resource reporting**: FLOPs and wall-clock time of the framework are not reported, and a comparison of compute cost with finetuning methods is missing (R4).
- **Sensitivity**: For Figure 4, how were calibration samples selected? How sensitive are the results to different samples? (R4, Question).
- **Minor writing issues**: The presentation of formulas could be polished (R3); font inconsistency between equations (1)/(2) and main text (R3).
