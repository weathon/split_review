- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 1, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses generalization in anomaly detection under test-time distribution shifts by proposing Knowledge Exposure (KE), which uses a pretrained CLIP model to select semantically preserving augmentations per class. KE then trains a contrastive learning based anomaly detector (ResNet-18 + SVM) to be invariant to those augmentations. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN in a one-vs-all setting, showing improvements over baselines in covariate shift scenarios. However, reviewers raised substantial concerns about the problem formulation, method justification, experimental rigor, and writing quality. There is strong disagreement: one reviewer (score 1) considered the contribution very limited and the paper poorly written, while three reviewers (scores 3) found the task important and the method promising but identified major weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a critical and underexplored challenge in anomaly detection: generalization under distribution shifts at test time (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The misalignment augmentation problem is clearly described (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed method is effective in covariate shift settings (Reviewer 1).
- Using CLIP to automatically select augmentations in a data-driven manner offers a compelling solution to the limitation of hand-crafted augmentations (Reviewer 3).
- The observation that CLIP alone cannot reliably classify instance-level augmentation invariance is interesting, though the solution has limitations (Reviewer 4).
- Some reviewers found the approach interesting (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Very limited novelty**: The method is a straightforward application of pretrained CLIP to select augmentations, with no explanation of why CLIP is helpful or what alternative methods exist. The anomaly score assignment (SVM) is also simple (Reviewer 2). One reviewer noted the contribution is insufficient even empirically (Reviewer 2, score 1), while others saw more value but still flagged concerns about contribution depth (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Poor writing and presentation**: All reviewers noted severe writing issues: missing/broken references (no citep, bibliography only in appendix), typos, low-quality figures, missing figure reference on page 6 (L272), missing Appendix A, no explanation of what “CLIP” is, and a long hard-to-read paragraph (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Problem setup questioned**: 
  - The notion of “realistic” distribution shifts is unclear; CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN are toy datasets insufficient to demonstrate realistic shifts (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewers suggest using ImageNet30, natural domain shifts (Reviewer 3), or the Hendrycks & Dietterich (ICLR 2019) robustness benchmark with realistic corruptions like JPEG compression (Reviewer 1).
  - The one-vs-all setting is the easiest and not representative of multi-class normal data (Reviewer 4).
- **Method details insufficient and not well motivated**:
  - Why the Wasserstein distance is chosen over simpler alternatives (e.g., cosine or Euclidean distance, or averaging per-sample CLIP distances) is not justified (Reviewers 3, 4). The WD is also computationally expensive (Reviewer 4).
  - The specific set of augmentations used is not described (Reviewer 3).
  - The approach assumes all normal data belong to a single class; it is unsuitable for the common multi-class normal scenario (Reviewer 4).
  - The claim (line 53) that CSI places the decision boundary extremely close to in-distribution samples is unsupported, and the proposed method’s use of one-class SVM also creates a tight boundary (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical weaknesses**:
  - Performance on the standard (non-shifted) detection protocol is weak; there is still a large gap to CSI (Reviewer 1). The improvement in the new shifted setting may be self-serving because the same procedure selects augmentations for both training and evaluation (Reviewer 4).
  - The evaluation only compares to contrastive methods on small ResNet-18; larger pretrained encoders (e.g., ResNet152, DinoV2) are known to work much better on CIFAR-10/100 and could easily incorporate augmented samples, but these are not compared (Reviewer 4).
  - Results for the “Alternative Encoders for Knowledge Exposure” experiment are missing (Reviewer 3), and the paper does not explain why the method fails with other pretrained models (Reviewer 3).
  - The method underperforms baselines in standard evaluation settings, and the description of the new evaluation setting is unclear, making it difficult to assess whether gains are simply due to adaptation to that setting (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
  - No direct comparison to existing CLIP-based anomaly detection (e.g., Liznerski et al., TMLR, “Exposing Outlier Exposure”) (Reviewer 1).
  - No comparison of the proposed augmentation selection method against simpler alternative: running CLIP per sample before and after augmentation and averaging the distances (Reviewer 4).
  - The paper does not test whether KE can be a plug-in for other contrastive methods like CSI or MSDA (Reviewer 1).
  - No comparison to CSI with different augmentation strategies to isolate the effect (Reviewer 1).
- **Unaddressed related work**: The paper omits discussion of zero-shot anomaly detection (e.g., Li et al., NeurIPS 2023), invariant anomaly detection under distribution shifts (Carvalho et al., NeurIPS 2024), and semi-supervised approaches using only normal data (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
