## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents FlashAttention-2, a set of optimizations to the original FlashAttention algorithm for GPU execution, focusing on better work partitioning to increase matrix-multiply FLOPs utilization. Key changes include deferring scaling in online softmax, saving logsumexp for backward, parallelizing over sequence dimension across thread blocks, splitting KV cache across thread blocks, and better warp partitioning. The approach shows speedups up to 2× over FlashAttention and reaches 50‑73% of theoretical peak FLOPs/s on A100. Reviewers widely praise the engineering contributions, clarity, and potential impact, but raise concerns about insufficient ablation studies, limited baseline comparisons (no compiler baselines, missing FlashAttention v1 in some figures, unclear use of CUDA Graphs), reliance on specialized hardware (A100/H100), lack of performance counter validation, and missing discussion of compatibility with positional encodings and sparse masks. Reviewer scores range from 1 to 8: one reviewer (score 1) was highly critical of hardware dependence and custom‑kernel complexity but still acknowledged the strengths; another (score 8) was very positive about the engineering contributions.

### Strengths

- **Well‑written and accessible**: The paper is clearly written and well‑organized, making it accessible to readers without a low‑level background in GPU programming. The improvements are explained in a clear, intuitive manner (R2, R3).
- **Clever and sound platform‑specific optimizations**: The proposed tweaks to work partitioning and parallelism are technically sound and carefully analysed (R1, R3).
- **Symmetric applicability to training and inference**: The approach is useful for both phases, with broad applications in research and production (R2, R3).
- **Promising performance improvements**: FlashAttention‑2 achieves up to 2× speedup over FlashAttention and reaches 72% model FLOPs utilization in end‑to‑end GPT training, addressing a performance‑critical bottleneck (R2, R3, R4).
- **Useful artifact with potential for widespread adoption**: The resulting software is expected to benefit both researchers and practitioners. Even the most critical reviewer (score 1) notes “there are many things to like” and expects widespread adoption (R1, R2, R3).
- **Builds on a strong existing baseline**: The work improves upon the already impactful FlashAttention (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of ablation study**: The paper provides no breakdown of the contribution of each individual optimization (e.g., deferring scaling, parallelization over sequence dimension, warp partitioning, block size tuning). This makes it difficult to discern which tweak has the highest impact and whether gains stem from algorithmic changes vs. extra tuning (R1, R4). Specific missing analyses: (i) Is causal‑masking skipping (Section 3.1) also applied to FlashAttention? If not, how does that affect the performance gap? (R1) (ii) What is the speedup from just modifying the work‑partitioning scheme? (R1) (iii) How much does improved parallelism contribute independently? (R1) (iv) In Section 3.3, was the same level of block‑size tuning applied to FlashAttention? Could some gains be from extra tuning effort? (R1) (v) The backward‑pass paragraph on MQA/GQA (Section 3.1.2) is unclear in its relevance to changes from FlashAttention (R4).
- **Limited and weak baselines**: The main benchmarks (Figure 5, Section 4) compare only to FasterTransformer and PyTorch; they do not include compiler‑based frameworks (e.g., XLA) that can fuse ops and reduce memory I/O. It is unclear whether the PyTorch baselines use CUDA Graphs, which can provide large speedups for latency‑sensitive workloads. FlashAttention v1 is missing from Figure 5 (latency benchmarks). The comparison in Section 4.1 uses Cutlass and Triton but not other code‑generation frameworks. Additionally, the difference in gaps between FA/FA‑2 under Cutlass vs. Triton is not explained (R3, R4).
- **Hardware dependence and custom‑kernel complexity**: FlashAttention‑2 relies on recent specialized GPU architectures (A100, H100) and custom CUDA kernels, limiting applicability to older hardware. The paper does not specify minimum hardware requirements or which GPU architectures are supported, nor does it discuss modifications needed for relative positional encoding (RoPE, ALiBi) or compatibility with sparse block masks (as in v1). It is also unclear how the algorithm functions when there is no explicit sequence‑length dimension (e.g., tokens padded without sequence boundaries) (R2, R3).
- **Insufficient validation of claimed improvements**: The paper lacks performance‑counter data (e.g., from NVIDIA Nsight) to substantiate claims about increased occupancy and reduced shared‑memory accesses. The definition of “occupancy” is vague (number of concurrent warps vs. maximum supported by hardware) and no measurement confirms the claimed occupancy improvement (R4). For Figure 3, it is unclear whether the speedup sources are fewer shared‑memory accesses or fewer synchronizations (__syncthreads), and why either matters given that shared‑memory accesses are fast and intra‑block synchronization is low‑overhead (R4). It is also unclear how Figure 3 relates to Algorithm 1 (which lines it illustrates) and the caption is insufficient (R2, R4). In Figure 2, axes are unlabeled; it is unclear whether uncolored squares mean no computation, whether backward workers synchronize between forward and backward passes, and how >50% peak performance can be achieved if half the cycles are spent waiting for the longest‑running thread block (R3, R4).
- **Marginal theoretical contribution**: The optimizations are mostly engineering “tweaks” rather than novel algorithmic ideas. The paper lacks analysis of how these modifications affect fundamental attention properties (e.g., model expressiveness, convergence behavior). The 2× speedup is relatively marginal compared to the original FlashAttention’s gains over the baseline; the real‑world significance (e.g., training time reduction, cost savings) is not clearly justified (R1).
- **Clarity issues in equations, text, and notation**: (i) The equation at the top of Page 5 contains ambiguous notation: “diag(l^{(1})^{-1}” – should the exponent be -1 or 1? (R4) (ii) The six equations on Page 5 (online softmax trick) are not explicitly compared to the earlier six on Page 3; a summary of changes would help (R4). (iii) How the tweaks in Section 4.1.1 reduce non‑matmul FLOPs is not obvious (R4). (iv) Much of Algorithm 1 is similar to the original FlashAttention; highlighting which lines are new and which reduce non‑matmul FLOPs or improve occupancy would help (R4). (v) Typo: “Only at the every end of the” (R4). (vi) Figure 3 would benefit from a more descriptive caption (R2). (vii) The constant and equation in Sections 4.1 (i.e., 4) are not adequately explained (why is sequence length squared? why 4?) (R3). (viii) The first sentence of the abstract (“major problem”) is ambiguous – is the problem difficult, significant, or both? (R3) (ix) Missing word: “**as** context length increases” (Section 1) (R3). (x) Text in all diagrams could be rendered more clearly (R3). (xi) Section 3.2 could better explain “prefill” and “KV cache” for accessibility (R3).
- **Missing discussion of future hardware trends**: The paper does not discuss how the approach’s relevance might change as GPU hardware evolves (e.g., HBM bandwidth not improving as quickly as SM arithmetic) (R3).
- **Unaddressed compiler‑feasibility**: It is not explored whether a compiler could realistically generate FlashAttention‑2 or what cost models would be required (R3). This is noted as a missing discussion point.
