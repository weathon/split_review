## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Class-Relational Label Smoothing (CRLS) for visual place recognition (VPR). CRLS transforms one-hot labels into soft labels based on inter-class visual similarities, computed from the classifier weight vectors, to bridge the classification-retrieval gap. A Class Stability Weighting (CSW) further adjusts the smoothing strength per class using the magnitude of the class weight vector as a stability indicator. The method is evaluated on multiple VPR benchmarks and shows improvements over prior approaches, particularly for lifelong variations.

### Strengths
- The idea of using inter-class visual relations for label smoothing is novel within VPR, well-motivated, and simple (only two hyper-parameters). (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewer 2, 3, 4)
- The work directly addresses the challenge of lifelong appearance variations, which had not been explicitly tackled in prior VPR research. (Reviewer 2)
- Derivative analysis is provided to support the proposed approach (Reviewer 3).  
- Extensive experiments are conducted on diverse benchmark datasets; the method shows competitive results and outperforms competing methods in lifelong VPR settings. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4)
- One reviewer (score 6) finds the approach novel and showing consistent improvements over prior methods on many benchmarks; three reviewers (score 5) are more critical about the significance of improvements and the fairness of comparisons.

### Weaknesses
- The claim of “outperforming SoTA across 17 benchmarks” is overstated. The method achieves SoTA on only a few benchmarks, and current SoTA often uses foundation models (SELA, SALAD, CricaVPR, BoQ) that are not compared on all benchmarks. Comparisons with foundation models should be extended to all benchmarks. (Reviewer 2)
- Comparisons with DINOv2-based methods are unfair because training datasets differ (SF-XL is the largest) and the method adds extra layers (two-layer MLP + FC for token embeddings) without ablation to attribute gains. Results are worse than EffoVPR (DINOv2-large) on some datasets. Suggestion: compare with EigenPlaces on DINOv2-base backbone. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- Advantages over EigenPlaces on Pitts30k, Pitts250k, and MSLS val are marginal; improvements are not obvious. (Reviewer 3)
- Training uses larger batch size and learning rate than EigenPlaces. The authors claim these do not enhance baseline performance, but it is unclear whether the benefit would persist under exactly the same settings. (Reviewer 3)
- The method is only evaluated with CosFace loss (as in EigenPlaces/CosPlace); many recent VPR works use multi-similarity loss (e.g., MixVPR), limiting the contribution to the community. (Reviewer 3)
- The relation to lifelong variation is not clearly justified: why should inter-class similarity smoothing improve generalization across lifelong changes? (Reviewer 1, 2)
- The second contribution (CSW min-max normalization) lacks clarity: the step-by-step procedure, motivation, and link to derivative analysis (Eq. 9) are not described. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper does not compare with the standard label smoothing (LS) baseline in the main ablation (Table 6). More broadly, related work and competitors are insufficient. The method should be positioned against approaches that handle label inconsistency, e.g., learning with noisy labels via distillation, bootstrapping, or knowledge graph methods (Reed et al. 2014, Sukhbaatar et al. 2014, Lee et al. 2017, Li et al. 2017, Sharma et al. 2020, Han et al. 2019, Iscen et al. 2022). These should be presented as background and compared as strong competitors. (Reviewer 4)
- The CSW weighting strategy also lacks comparison with confidence-based weighting methods from semi-supervised or active learning. (Reviewer 4)
- On MSLS, the foundation-model version shows worse R@5 (Table 11). In single-view category, the method lags behind MixVPR; the authors note feature size differences but add layers to foundation models – an ablation matching feature size is needed. (Reviewer 2)
- Notation: \(l\) in Eq. 1 is not defined. (Reviewer 2)
- It is not stated that AnyLoc is a zero-shot method; this should be clarified in comparisons. (Reviewer 2)
- Minor error: “EigenPlace” is misspelled as “EigenPlace” in Lines 939, 940, 1019. (Reviewer 3)
- Results for two-stage methods (TransVPR, StructVPR, R²Former, SelaVPR) are much lower than in their original papers; it is unclear if only the first stage was used, which would be unfair because global features from two-stage methods are more low-dimensional. (Reviewer 3)
- The method is not evaluated on seasonal changes (e.g., Nordland dataset). (Reviewer 2)
- The training dataset is not clearly specified. (Reviewer 2)
- Error bars / confidence intervals are not reported (optional, but would strengthen comparisons). (Reviewer 1)
