## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RefineCLIP, a two-phase model editing framework to correct CLIP-ViT errors caused by unknown spurious correlations with limited data. Phase 1 identifies problematic attention heads via data-driven metrics (DE_A, DE_B, DE_C, DE_D) that compare feature similarity changes after ablating head outputs. Phase 2 learns a diagonal projection matrix using a loss combining success (align with spurious-feature-ablated model on misclassified samples), locality (preserve original predictions on unrelated samples), and cross-entropy. Experiments are on Waterbirds (synthetic) and CelebA, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A. One reviewer (R2, rating 6) is more positive than the others (rating 5), noting strong practical motivation and innovative metrics. All others (R1, R3, R4, all rating 5) have substantial concerns about novelty, comparisons, analysis, and presentation.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses the important and practical scenario of correcting CLIP prediction errors with limited data and without prior knowledge of biases (R1, R2).
- The proposed causal perspective on error analysis, using data-driven metrics to identify problematic attention heads, is novel and innovative (R1, R2, R4).
- The experimental design using Waterbirds with known spurious correlations provides good validation of feature identification ability (R2).
- The paper is clearly structured and experimental results appear good (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Title mismatch**: The title refers to "CLIP" but the method only addresses CLIP-ViT; scope is inconsistent (R1).
- **Methodology cohesion and presentation**: The flow from attention head analysis to feature editing is hard to follow (R1). Readability is poor; a schematic diagram of the overall framework is missing (R4). Visualizations of the W, C, A sets and DE metric calculations are lacking (R2). Redundant explanations of symmetric metrics make the paper verbose (R2). Alternative heatmap visualizations (e.g., for each method separately) are needed to better show how attention regions change after editing (R3, R4).
- **Theoretical foundation and justification**: Proposition 1 (in appendix) is not properly discussed in the main text; the connection between the proposed metrics and actual error correction is not clearly established (R2). The choice of a diagonal projection matrix over full matrix or LoRA lacks theoretical justification; tradeoffs are not discussed (R2). The claim that the method is “data-driven” is insufficient to establish novelty, because other methods (e.g., Tip-Adapter) are also data-driven and the two phases resemble prior work (Gandelsman et al., Santurkar et al.) (R3).
- **Insufficient comparisons**: The only compared baseline is Tip-Adapter (training-free version) and TextSpan; no comparison with a trainable version of Tip-Adapter (R3). Missing comparisons with many recent few-shot CLIP adaptation methods such as prompt learning approaches (CoOp, PLOT, CLAP) and other debiasing techniques for CLIP (R2, R4). The relationship and differences from prompt learning are not discussed (R2).
- **Limited experimental scope and inconsistent design**: Edit success is evaluated only on Waterbirds, while edit locality is evaluated on different datasets (CelebA, ImageNet-R/A) without justification (R3). Tip-Adapter has been evaluated on 10 datasets, yet this work tests on very few (R3). Experiments would benefit from broader scope across multiple datasets for success edits and generalization (R3).
- **Model editing harms generalization**: The statement that locality is preserved is contradicted by results in Fig. 2, showing a performance drop on unrelated data; the ablation may inadvertently remove features important for generalization (R2 minor, R4). The tradeoff between edit success and locality deserves more detailed analysis, including effect of number of samples, choice of target classes (R2). There is a risk that post-deployment edits overfit to deployment-specific noise rather than improving generalizability (R1).
- **Requires training and labeled data**: The method cannot be applied to unlabeled images, limiting its use in low-label scenarios (R4). Computational cost of the two-phase framework for large-scale models is not measured (R1).
- **Inadequate analysis and ablation studies**: Hyperparameter sensitivity (especially α and β in the combined loss) is not adequately analyzed (R2). Ablation experiments are insufficient: the roles of the three loss components (success, locality, cross-entropy) are not separately analyzed, and the weights are not clearly defined (R4). The initialization of the diagonal matrix for fine-tuning is unclear; when all diagonal elements are 1, the loss in Eq. 11 and 12 becomes 0 (R4 question). The impact of the number of MSA heads (T) on performance is not discussed (R3). Error identification handling differs between synthetic and real-world datasets (R1).
- **Entanglement of spurious and causal features**: The framework may struggle when spurious features are not clear-cut or are entangled with causal features; handling multiple interacting spurious correlations is not addressed (R1).
- **Missing discussion of limitations and future directions**: The conclusion lacks discussion of limitations and future work (R2).
