## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a refined study of learning rate schedules for SGD, focusing on last-iterate convergence. It proposes a problem-adaptive schedule that automatically yields warm-up and rapid annealing, and predicts linear decay when gradient information is unavailable. The method extends to coordinate-wise algorithms. An extensive empirical evaluation across 10 deep learning problems, LLMs, and logistic regression shows linear decay outperforms cosine annealing, and the schedule refinement gives further improvements.

### Strengths
- **Refined theoretical characterization** that captures warm-up and annealing, recovering practical heuristics that previously lacked theoretical support (Reviewer 1, very positive; also Reviewer 5).
- **Thorough theoretical analysis** of the problem-adaptive schedule (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 finds proofs correct).
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation** comparing many schedules on diverse benchmarks (deep learning, LLMs, logistic regression), establishing a clear hierarchy (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5).
- **Interesting theoretical framework** that explains linear decay and can be extended to element-wise algorithms like Adam (Reviewer 4).
- **Novel practical techniques**: median filter, ℓ₂ norm inverse weighting, ℓ₁ norm inverse weighting (Reviewer 4).
- **Novel schedule refinement method** using a prior run to produce an improved schedule, with last-iterate convergence guarantees and generalizability beyond SGD (Reviewer 5).
- **Well-organized and easy to follow** with correct proofs (Reviewer 3).
- Investigates **last-iterate convergence** rather than average iterate; automatically yields warm-up and annealing (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of non-convex theoretical analysis**—the theory is restricted to convex functions, limiting relevance to deep learning (Reviewer 2, 5; also Reviewer 4 notes convexity assumption should be stated clearly in theorem statements).
- **Limited theoretical novelty**—the \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\) convergence rate is not new; the novelty and challenges of the analysis need clearer emphasis (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 5 calls the theory “heuristic”).
- **Claim that linear decay is “best” not fully theoretically supported**—the reduction to linear decay does not prove it outperforms other schedules; it is not evident how the proposed method theoretically outperforms classical schedules (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Unclear writing about anytime vs. fixed horizon**—the paper assumes a known end time \(T\); missing discussion and analysis for the unknown end time case (e.g., using the doubling trick) and challenges in that setting (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing theoretical or empirical comparison to adaptive methods like Adagrad** (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear applicability to optimizers like LAMB** (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient experimental details**—it is not explicitly stated which algorithm (SGD with ℓ₂ weighting vs. Adam with ℓ₁ weighting) is used for each experiment (e.g., ImageNet, RCNN, CIFAR) (Reviewer 4; also training from scratch for GPT, RoBERTa, ViT not specified—Reviewer 2).
- **Definition of the “Cosine” schedule unclear**—classic cosine decay or cosine annealing? (Reviewer 2).
- **Refined schedule requires a sweep over learning rates on a grid**—what parameters are swept and the rationale are not explained (Reviewer 4).
- **High computational cost of schedule refinement**—requires twice the compute resources; no improvement suggested (Reviewer 4).
- **In Figure 1, the definition of “warm-up” is unclear**—why the refined schedule starts from nearly zero, causing slow updates? Could this be improved? (Reviewer 5).
- **Theorem 3’s assumption of conditional independence between \(w_t\) and \(g_t\) is violated by the chosen \(w_t\)**—making equation (2) potentially invalid; the impact is not discussed (Reviewer 5).
- **Definition of `median_filter` is missing**—how it ensures the gradient norm sequence does not change significantly after refinement (Reviewer 5).
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 1, score 8) is notably more positive** about the contribution and thoroughness, while other reviewers (scores 5,5,5,6) are less convinced, citing the above concerns.
