## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper examines language model post-training when SFT demonstrations and preference comparisons are unreliable (contain mistakes). The authors find that SFT retains some effectiveness under unreliable data, but DPO fails to improve beyond SFT due to overoptimization. They propose Iterative Label Refinement (ILR), which uses comparison feedback to decide whether unreliable human demonstrations should be replaced by model-generated alternatives, then retrains via SFT on the updated data. SFT+ILR outperforms SFT+DPO on math, coding, and safe instruction-following under both LM-simulated and time-constrained human supervision.

### Strengths
- Novel and significant approach to the key challenge of unreliable human supervision in LM post-training, which is increasingly crucial as LMs are applied to harder-to-supervise tasks.
- Comprehensive evaluation across multiple tasks (math, coding, safe instruction-following), using both simulated unreliable feedback (small LMs) and real time-constrained human annotators.
- Clear analysis of why DPO fails under unreliable feedback: it overfits to unreliable preferences, and the paper provides reasonable evidence for this overoptimization.
- ILR performs clearly better than DPO under unreliable settings, and the gains are robust across tasks and models tested.
- Effective insight: directing comparison feedback towards improving the training data rather than continually training the model is a better use of feedback under weak supervision.
- Validation on a human labeling experiment increases confidence that the synthetic results are reasonable.
- The method is simple and clearly explained.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to other preference optimization methods.** The paper focuses on DPO but does not compare to PPO, conservative DPO (label smoothing), IPO, MMPO, or other methods designed to be robust to noisy preference feedback. It is unclear whether the overoptimization issues are unique to DPO or extend to other RLHF algorithms.
- **Missing baseline: SFT on chosen responses from unreliable DPO pairs.** The reviewers note that simply using the chosen response from the DPO dataset for SFT (or similar) is not tested, making the comparison between DPO and SFT+ILR potentially unfair.
- **Unreliable supervision is not well-defined.** The paper lacks a quantitative definition of unreliability (e.g., error rate or disagreement with ground truth) for both the LM-simulated and time-constrained human settings. This makes it difficult to assess generalizability.
- **Questionable assumptions underlying ILR.** ILR assumes that (a) the SFT model can generate responses better than those in the SFT dataset at least some of the time, and (b) the annotator can reliably identify the better response. The paper does not analyze how ILR’s performance varies with dataset size, proportion of unreliable data, or model size. The conditions under which the model generates better responses are not examined.
- **Potential ensembling effect.** The ILR procedure (Section 5.1) trains one model on each half of the SFT data and uses these to cross-label. An ablation against a two-model ensemble (as opposed to a single model) is missing.
- **Need to decouple filtering from addition of synthetic data.** The 1:1 replacement of data points (one removed, one added) may conflate the benefits of filtering low-quality demonstrations and adding synthetic ones. The paper does not isolate these effects.
- **Limited task diversity.** The evaluation covers math, coding, and safe instruction-following, but does not include more subjective or open-ended tasks (e.g., creative writing, opinion-based QA) where human feedback is highly unreliable or nuanced.
- **Computational cost and scalability.** ILR requires multiple rounds of retraining and cross-evaluation, which may be more expensive than DPO. The paper lacks a quantitative analysis of the computational overhead compared to baselines.
- **Theoretical depth lacking.** The paper does not analyze the long-term convergence of the iterative refinement process or the risk of introducing new biases or errors into the dataset over multiple rounds.
- **Figure 6b is hard to parse** due to overlapping labels and small differences between rounds.
- **Clarity question:** It is unclear how the unreliable DPO dataset is constructed (whether the classifier used to pick the chosen/rejected pair is the same across rounds).
- **Could DPO be improved with longer training and higher beta?** The paper shows a higher beta helps somewhat but does not explore whether longer DPO training with a high beta alleviates the overoptimization further.
- **Minority reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R2, rating 5, low confidence) was significantly more critical than the others (all rating 8), raising concerns about the definition of unreliability, the absence of many relevant robust baselines, and the untested assumptions underlying ILR's effectiveness. This contrast should be weighed when interpreting the results.
