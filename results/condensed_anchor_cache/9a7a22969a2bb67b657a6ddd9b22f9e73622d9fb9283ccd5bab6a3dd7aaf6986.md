## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes using the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of the most likely output sequence as a single-sample uncertainty estimate for LLMs, replacing traditional multi-sample Monte-Carlo methods. The approach is derived from proper scoring rules and decomposes uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic components. Experiments on free-form QA tasks across several LLMs claim state-of-the-art AUROC in distinguishing correct from incorrect generations.

### Strengths
- **Computational efficiency**: The metric requires only a single generation (the MAP sequence), avoiding the cost of multiple sampling passes. This is highlighted as a practical advantage by all reviewers.
- **Theoretical foundation**: The derivation of aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty using proper scoring rules (Eq. 7‑8) is considered valid and helpful (R1: “well‑established within the framework of proper scoring rules”; R2: “theoretical soundness”; R3: “derivation…is helpful”; R5: “derivation of aleatoric and epistemic entropy is valid”).
- **Empirical performance**: The method achieves strong AUROC scores across multiple model sizes, architectures, and training stages (R1: “achieves or surpasses existing state‑of‑the‑art”; R4: “experiment results are good, surprising given the simplicity”).  Note: R5 and others question the evaluation methodology (see Weaknesses).
- **Broad applicability**: R1 notes the method works “across various model architectures, sizes, and training stages”.
- **Relevance**: The topic of uncertainty quantification in LLMs is judged important by R4 and R5.

### Weaknesses
- **MAP approximation is non‑trivial and unstudied**: Obtaining the true argmax sequence via beam search (or greedy decoding) itself requires multiple candidate sequences, undermining the claimed efficiency. No analysis is provided on how close the generated sequence is to the true mode, nor on the variance of log‑likelihoods across different sequences. Worst‑case behavior under poor approximation is unexplored. (R2: “obtaining the MAP sequence…could be no computationally cheaper than sampling”; R4: “no analysis on the distribution of log‑likelihoods…unclear if the greedy approach consistently finds a sequence close to the mode”; also R4 points to Appendix A, Figure 2 showing beam search requires multiple samples.)
- **Limited novelty**: The proposed metric (zero‑one score = 1 − p(most likely sequence)) is essentially confidence estimation familiar in machine learning. Several reviewers find the technical contribution weak. (R3: “equivalent to propose p(y=y’|x) as confidence…lacks technical novelty”; R4: “contribution is somewhat limited to empirical findings without any theoretical guarantees”; R5: “writing is very blurred…unclear which part is the original contribution”.)
- **Lacks justification why NLL outperforms sampling‑based methods**: The paper does not explain how NLL captures different aspects of uncertainty (e.g., prediction variance) compared to multi‑sample baselines, making the performance differences hard to interpret. (R3: “lacks justification and insights on why the NLL is a better metric than the variants using sampling”; R4: “lacks a theoretical justification for why the log‑likelihood of a single, greedily generated sequence should correlate with the model’s uncertainty”.)
- **Missing empirical comparison to verbal confidence baselines**: The paper mentions but does not test LLM‑generated verbalized confidence estimates (R3). Also experiments are limited to free‑form QA; other NLG tasks (dialogue, story generation) are not evaluated (R1).
- **Evaluation methodology is questionable**:
    - Correctness is judged by LLM‑based evaluator (LLaMA 70B) without fine‑tuning or discussion of biases/limitations (R5).
    - The use of beam‑search with size 20 to obtain the “most likely answer” is not justified; no guarantee exists that this sequence has near‑maximal log‑probability (R4, R5).
    - The F1‑based threshold for correctness and how AUROC is computed are poorly described; details of the actual estimation algorithm (Eq. 8) are missing (R5: “actual estimation algorithm is not described…unclear how to estimate this part”).
- **Theoretical gaps**:
    - The definition of uncertainty in Eq. (1) assumes a ground‑truth y and a posterior over parameters; it is unclear how this applies when the model parameters are fixed (R5).
    - It is ambiguous which equations are prior work and which are contributions (R5: “Eq(1‑6) are unclear”).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper is difficult to follow (R4: “not very well written, making it difficult to understand”; R5: “blurred”). The paper uses only 7 of 10 allotted pages, missing opportunities for additional experiments, analyses, or clarifications (R1). Notation is introduced without definition (e.g., vocabulary 𝒱, set 𝒴, line 083‑094; R4, R3). The relation of the proper‑scoring‑rules discussion to the proposed method is not made explicit (R4: “I am not sure how…advances your main claims”).
- **Semantic uncertainty ignored**: The metric captures only statistical uncertainty from model probabilities and does not account for semantic correctness or meaning mismatch (R1).

*Note: There is clear disagreement among reviewers. One reviewer (R1, score 6) is strongly positive, emphasizing the method’s simplicity, strong results, and practical value. The other four reviewers (scores 5, 3, 3, 3) find the paper lacking in novelty, theoretical support, and methodological rigor, with major concerns about the MAP approximation and evaluation design.*
