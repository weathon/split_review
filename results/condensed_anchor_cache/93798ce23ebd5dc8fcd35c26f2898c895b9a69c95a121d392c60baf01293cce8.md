## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes Nesterov acceleration (NAG) under benign non‑convexity assumptions inspired by overparameterized deep learning, covering continuous‑time, discrete‑time, and stochastic variants. Reviewers agree the topic is interesting and the paper is clearly written, but there is significant disagreement on novelty, correctness of related‑work claims, and experimental support. Two reviewers are enthusiastic (scores 8), one is moderately positive (6 after rebuttal), and one is critical (5). Main weaknesses include confusion between NAG and heavy ball, insufficient differentiation from prior work (especially Aujol et al. 2022), overly strong assumptions, limited technical novelty, weak experiments, and an incomplete literature review.

### Strengths
- Clear writing and organization (Reviewers 1,2,3). The paper is well-structured with concise introduction and discussions of related works.
- The convergence rates improve upon previous work, achieving accelerated rates under weaker geometric assumptions than strong convexity or quasar‑convexity (Reviewer 1).
- The authors are straightforward about limitations, e.g., the global assumption only holds locally (Reviewer 1).
- The assumptions are motivated by overparameterized deep learning and supported by some empirical evidence (Reviewer 2).
- The direction of benign convexity is interesting for understanding why gradient methods work in practice (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Confusion between Nesterov acceleration and heavy ball method (Polyak momentum).** The paper claims to study NAG but focuses on heavy ball. Several references cited for NAG motivation are about heavy ball, and the distinction is not clarified (Reviewers 3,4).
2. **Incorrect characterization of prior work.** The introduction claims Aujol et al. (2022) use “strong quasar‑convexity,” but that work uses strong convexity with respect to the projection onto the minimizing set (Assumption 4 in this paper). Theorem 7 therefore replicates existing convergence rates from Aujol et al. The literature review also mischaracterizes Guminov et al. (Reviewer 4).
3. **Assumptions are not as novel or weak as claimed.** Assumption 4 (strong convexity w.r.t. closest minimizer) implies the PL inequality and has been used in prior work. Recent works (e.g., Josz et al., 2023) require only the KL inequality, which holds for a significantly larger class of functions (Reviewers 3,4).
4. **Limited technical novelty.** The overall proof strategy is standard, with the main contribution being Lemma 1. The continuous‑time analysis does not directly connect to the discrete‑time results, and its purpose beyond additional motivation is unclear (Reviewers 2,3,1).
5. **Incomplete and weak experiments.** The numerical experiments are too simple (only one minimizer \(w\); toy nonconvex examples). They do not provide sufficient evidence that the assumed geometry holds in realistic deep‑learning settings (Reviewers 3,4).
6. **\(C^1\) smoothness assumption is restrictive.** Many common activation functions (e.g., ReLU) are not continuously differentiable, limiting applicability to deep learning. The paper does not address whether almost‑everywhere differentiability could be handled with similar techniques (Reviewer 1).
7. **Motivation from deep learning is poorly supported.** Momentum methods in deep learning typically use heavy ball, not NAG. The connection to overparameterized neural networks is mentioned but not adequately referenced (Reviewer 3).
8. **Disorganized introduction and overblown language.** The introduction jumps between many topics (accelerated methods, PL condition, NTK, Hessian eigenvalues, gradient method, nonsmooth optimization, heavy ball, KL inequality, time discretization, strong quasar convexity, implicit bias) without a clear structure. Claims such as “much more general setting than convex optimization” and “realistic loss landscapes” are overstatements (Reviewer 4).
9. **Lack of insight into proof techniques.** The paper mentions controlling “tangential motion” but does not explain how this is achieved or how it connects to the assumptions. The presentation of proof techniques is not accessible to the optimization community (Reviewer 4).
10. **Literature review is incomplete.** Missing important references, e.g., Josz et al. (2023) on unified convergence analysis for momentum algorithms. The paper does not clearly differentiate its contributions from existing works (Reviewers 3,4).

*Note on disagreement:* Reviewers 1 and 2 are highly positive (score 8), considering the contribution worthy of publication despite the weaknesses. Reviewer 3 is critical (score 5) and questions the novelty, assumptions, and experiments. Reviewer 4’s score (6) acknowledges interesting ideas but calls for strong revisions, especially in redaction and positioning relative to prior work.
