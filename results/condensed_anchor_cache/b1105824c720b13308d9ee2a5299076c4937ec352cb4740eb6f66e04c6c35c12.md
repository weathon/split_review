## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes "Parameters Fusing," a post-training approach that merges parameter deltas from existing instruct-tuned checkpoints into a new base model (continually pre-trained on domain data). The goal is to replicate traditional post-training effects (instruction following, domain knowledge) with reduced time and resource costs. The method leverages open-weight models like Llama and is validated across multiple benchmarks. Reviewers generally find the idea innovative and the method cost-effective, but raise concerns about experimental transparency, scalability, missing comparisons, and limited evaluation of knowledge retention. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive, highlighting the theoretical hypothesis linking performance gains to parameter changes; another reviewer (score 6) finds the empirical improvements marginal and presentation confusing.

### Strengths
- **Innovation**: The "Parameters Fusing" approach is creative and resource-efficient, representing a novel alternative to conventional post-training (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). It builds on prior work but offers fresh insights (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical grounding**: The paper clearly explains post-training challenges and presents an intriguing hypothesis—a concave relationship between performance and parameter changes—supported by experiments (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Cost effectiveness**: The method significantly reduces post-training costs, making model customization more economical (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Flexibility**: Parameter delta operations allow customization across characteristics like coding ability and tool usage within homologous models (Reviewer 2). The approach is demonstrated using open-weight Llama models, promising broad applicability (Reviewer 1).
- **Empirical support**: Rigorous experiments across multiple benchmarks show fused models approach or exceed traditional post-trained performance (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). The simple weight-averaging operation (f + (g2 − g1)) is straightforward and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical relevance**: The method offers a practical solution when continual pretraining is needed—parameter fusing provides a resource-efficient alternative to post-training, with potential wide-reaching impact (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with parameter-efficient methods**: The study lacks comparisons with adapter-based or LoRA methods, needed to contextualize performance and efficiency relative to existing approaches (Reviewer 1, 4). Reviewer 4 notes technical novelty is limited since the method uses simple addition/subtraction on entire model parameters, and asks how it differs from or improves upon existing parameter fusion techniques in LLMs.
- **Insufficient experimental transparency and reproducibility**: Details on how parameter deltas were extracted, which layers were included, and how fusion was performed at the tensor level are missing, affecting reproducibility (Reviewer 2). The criteria for choosing parameter delta combinations is not explained (Reviewer 2, 3). The method relies on manual tuning of delta combinations, which is not scalable and may not lead to optimal performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of scalability analysis**: The study focuses on Llama models but does not assess whether the approach scales to larger models or if adjustments are needed for different architectures (Reviewer 1). It remains unclear whether Parameter Fusing is model-agnostic or requires architectural modifications.
- **Potential performance limitations**: In some benchmarks, fused models slightly underperform traditional post-trained models. The paper does not include statistical significance tests or a breakdown of performance across task types (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 characterizes empirical improvements as marginal (Figs 1, 2; Tabs 1, 2) and recommends showing scenarios where the method easily outperforms existing ones.
- **Lack of quantitative evaluation of knowledge retention**: Only a single example (Table 4) is provided to show that knowledge from continual pretraining is preserved after fusion. A systematic evaluation across diverse tasks/datasets is missing, and it is unclear how the fused model performs on tasks requiring a combination of base and specialized knowledge (Reviewer 3).
- **Risk of overfitting and model degradation**: The approach may risk overfitting in highly specialized domains. There is limited discussion of interpretability and potential performance decline, domain mismatches, or conflicting knowledge when fusing deltas from multiple sources (Reviewer 1). Mitigation strategies are not discussed.
- **Presentation quality**: The writing is confusing, with overly long subscripts in equations (e.g., \(\theta_{model_i-pretrain}, \theta_{post-train-llama3.1-8b}\)). Figures are too small, with x/y labels hard to see. Presentation needs substantial improvement—simpler equations, a straightforward illustration of the method via figures (Reviewer 4).
- **Domain assumption**: The method assumes that the base model (f) and the pretrained source model (g1) share common knowledge, given both are pre-trained on a variety of data. It is unclear how the method would perform if f and g1 are pre-trained on different domains (Reviewer 4).
