## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces PDDFormer, which uses Pairwise Distance Distribution (PDD) as an invariant representation for crystal property prediction, and enhances it with atom-specific weights (WPDD) and intra-unit cell structures (UPDD) to capture atomic interactions and improve computational efficiency. The authors provide theoretical guarantees on continuity and completeness of the PDD-based graphs, and experiments on Materials Project and JARVIS-DFT datasets show that WPDDFormer outperforms existing methods.

### Strengths
1. **Strong empirical performance**: WPDDFormer consistently outperforms other state-of-the-art methods across multiple tasks on JARVIS and Materials Project datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
2. **Theoretical guarantees**: The work provides theoretical justification for the continuity and completeness of the WPDD-based graphs, ensuring robustness under minor structural perturbations (Reviewer 1).
3. **Novelty in combining pairwise distance distribution with ML**: The idea of efficiently integrating pairwise distance distribution into a transformer model is insightful and significant for the AI-for-science community (Reviewer 2).
4. **Good clarity and presentation**: The paper is overall well-written with nice flow and illustrations (Reviewer 4).
5. **Incorporation of atomic information into PDD**: The proposed (W/U)PDDs address the high computational cost of original PDD and include atomic information (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Problematic completeness claims**: The completeness proof relies on the assumption that "the PDD is a generally complete invariant" (citing Widdowson & Kurlin, 2022). However, this claim is inaccurate: PDD by design disregards atomic types, cannot distinguish unstable crystal structures that may have subtle differences not captured by a fixed set of k-values, and cannot differentiate between chiral crystal structures (Reviewer 3). This undermines the claimed theoretical foundation. The authors should reorganize the proof or reorganize the completeness claims (Reviewer 3).  
   *Minority reviewer more negative* (Reviewer 3 strongly criticizes this; Reviewer 1 also flags concerns about robustness of proofs raised by other reviewers).

2. **Limited novelty in crystal graph construction**: The UPDD crystal graph construction method is not novel and has been previously introduced in "Zeoformer: Coarse-Grained Periodic Graph Transformer for OSDA-Zeolite Affinity Prediction" (Reviewers 1, 3). The method name (UPDD) and exact methodology appear identical to those in Zeoformer. Although the ICLR policy on concurrent submissions is acknowledged, the overlap is substantial. The authors should discuss similarities and differences with Zeoformer (Reviewers 1, 3).

3. **Message-passing architecture largely derived from ComFormer**: The message-passing mechanism and implementation code appear to greatly overlap with ComFormer (Reviewer 1). The paper does not clearly articulate novel aspects or improvements beyond a simple adaptation (Reviewer 3). The authors should highlight key differences and consider moving overlapping content to background or appendix (Reviewer 1).

4. **Insufficient ablation studies**: The current evaluation does not isolate the impact of each contribution. Needed ablations include:  
   - Applying pairwise distance distribution features to existing crystal graph models (e.g., ComFormer) and removing PDD from PDDFormer to see if gains come from the PDD integration or the new transformer architecture (Reviewer 2).  
   - Varying the radius in WPDDFormer to study its effect on performance and scalability (Reviewer 1).  
   - Time comparison between WPDDFormer and UPDDFormer to support UPDDFormer’s efficiency claims (Reviewer 1).  
   - Exploring different methods of integrating PDD into the graph representation (Reviewer 2).

5. **Missing clarification on physical motivation**: The abstract claims that atomic vibrations cause continuous fluctuations, and PDD resolves this, but the connection is not clearly established. It is unclear how representing interatomic distances as a distribution inherently accounts for dynamic vibrations. The authors should explain whether the distribution captures the range of motion or probability of distances due to thermal vibrations (Reviewer 2).

6. **Limited scope and interpretability**: The experimentation focuses on relatively simple scalar properties, some of which do not physically depend on crystal periodicity (Reviewer 4). Additionally, although incorporating PDD makes the data physics-informed, the model does not consider interpretability; the authors should consider evaluating the interpretability of (W/U)PDD crystal graphs and the proposed transformer (Reviewer 4).

7. **Drawbacks of UPDD**: UPDD has multiple drawbacks compared to WPDD and shows inferior performance. Its advantage and when it is preferred over WPDD are unclear (Reviewer 4). The source of improvement from PDD is also unclear—whether it is due to accounting for periodicity or simply because the input is more informative in other ways (Reviewer 4).

8. **Minor issues**:  
   - In Definition 1, use boldface or other methods to distinguish scalar, vector, and matrix (Reviewer 4).  
   - Vague language in definitions: Line 133, specify the range of what “crystal graph” refers to; Line 137, description of geometrical completeness is unclear (Reviewer 4).  
   - Typos: Line 129 “and If”, Line 512 “However” (Reviewer 4).
