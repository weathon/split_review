## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FreeDyG, a GNN for continuous-time dynamic graphs, incorporating a Node Interaction Frequency (NIF) Encoding and a frequency-enhanced MLP-Mixer layer (using discrete Fourier transform) to capture periodic patterns and the “shift” phenomenon. Experiments on seven real-world datasets for transductive and inductive link prediction show improvements over nine baselines, often with competitive training time and model size. Reviewers note the novelty of combining NIF with frequency-domain learning but raise concerns about the insufficient validation of frequency representations, the greater importance of NIF over the frequency component, computational cost and scalability analysis, unclear design choices and inconsistencies, and the limited scope (edge addition only, lack of evaluation on minority nodes).

### Strengths
- **Novel and effective architecture**: The Node Interaction Frequency (NIF) Encoding and the frequency-enhanced MLP-Mixer layer (inspired by discrete Fourier transform) are novel elements that together improve link prediction accuracy. NIF simplifies SEAL-like encoding for dynamic graphs.
- **Strong empirical performance**: FreeDyG outperforms 9 baselines on 7 datasets in both transductive and inductive settings, across three negative sampling strategies and multiple metrics (AUC-ROC, AP, MRR). Ablation study (Figure 3) confirms each component contributes.
- **Efficiency**: Figure 2 compares accuracy, training time, and model size, showing that accuracy gains do not come from significantly longer training or larger models; high efficiency is demonstrated.
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluation covers 9 methods, 7 real-world datasets, transductive/inductive splits, and three negative sampling strategies, with detailed hyperparameter and sensitivity studies.
- **Clear presentation**: The overall organization, problem formulation, and methodology are well written and easy to follow; key details are kept in the main paper.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient justification of the frequency domain**: The paper does not adequately explain why temporal shifts in dynamic graphs would manifest as specific patterns in the frequency domain or how these patterns differ from other temporal dynamics. No evidence is provided that the frequency-enhanced MLP-Mixer actually captures meaningful periodic patterns (as opposed to adding parameters). The claim that self-attention acts as a persistent low-pass filter and that DFT overcomes this limitation is not validated; an ablation comparing with/without DFT or a theoretical analysis is missing. The authors’ claim of being “the first work” to consider frequency information for dynamic graph embedding is overclaimed.
- **NIF more important than the frequency contribution**: The ablation study (Figure 3) shows that removing the NIF encoding causes a much larger performance drop than removing the frequency-enhanced MLP-Mixer layer, contradicting the paper’s positioning of frequency as the main novelty. Design decisions for NIF are unclear: why sum pooling in Equation (3) is sufficient to combine common neighbor count and interaction frequency (alternatives not explored). The transformation involving \( w_k^{(t)} \) in Equation (9) is not clearly explained.
- **Computational cost and scalability not properly analyzed**: The paper argues that RW/TPP/ODE methods are expensive, but sampling \( L \) first-hop historical neighbors (for NIF and FFT) likely has similar complexity. No theoretical or empirical time complexity analysis is provided. The sampling strategy for the Fourier transform and its potential bias are not detailed. The mean of the circle sizes in Figure 2 is not defined. Providing pseudocode for neighbor sampling, FFT, and NIF extraction (even in the appendix) is recommended.
- **Limited to edge addition and link prediction**: The model only handles continuous-time dynamic graphs with edge additions; edge deletion events are not considered. Applicability to other tasks (e.g., node classification) is not discussed. Extension to weighted dynamic graphs (predicting edge weights) is not addressed.
- **Inconsistent and unclear statements**: 
    - In Equation (1) indexing (\( n \) from 0 vs. \( n=1..N \)) is inconsistent with the text.
    - In Section 3.1, definitions of \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are missing.
    - In Equation (11), the dimensionality of \( W^{agg} \) is not specified, the variable \( t \) is missing from the right-hand side, and it is unclear how a vector \( h_*^t \) is derived from a matrix \( Z_*^l \).
    - Table 3 (hyperparameter study for \( L \)) is not presented as a trend plot, making it hard to see patterns.
    - Table 4 “Duration” meaning is ambiguous (are time steps regularly spaced?).
    - Typos: “neigh encoding” (p.7), capitalization error (“We specifically encode”).
- **Need for more analysis per dataset**: The model’s effectiveness varies across datasets; which datasets exhibit periodic patterns and how the frequency layer correlates with these characteristics is not discussed. A comparison with RNN- or Transformer-based temporal models to explain why FreeDyG outperforms them would be valuable.
- **Missing evaluation on minority nodes**: AUC-ROC may not capture link prediction performance for minority (low-degree) nodes; reporting Micro- and Macro-F1 scores is suggested.
- **Minor unanswered questions**: Inductive setting definition (observed+new vs. two new nodes) is not clarified; whether inductive inference is feasible without node/edge attributes is not addressed; training details regarding LastFM sampling summit are not explained.
