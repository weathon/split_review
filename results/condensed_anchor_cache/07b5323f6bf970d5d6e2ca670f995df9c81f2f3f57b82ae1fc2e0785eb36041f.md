## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Profiler, a black-box method for detecting the origin LLM of AI-generated text by extracting context-level inference patterns via novel context losses between a surrogate model’s output logits and adjacent input context. It is evaluated against 10 baselines on natural language and code datasets. Reviewer scores are split: two reviewers (1, 4) rate 6 (positive) with confidence 4–5, praising thorough experiments and clear presentation; two reviewers (2, 3) rate 3 (negative) with confidence 4–5, citing major omissions in design explanation, data construction details, and mathematical clarity. The following strengths and weaknesses merge all distinct points, preserving the disagreement.

### Strengths
- Thorough experiments comparing Profiler with 10 state-of-the-art baselines, including ablation studies on context window size and surrogate model selection, under both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings.
- Design of a contextual loss between output logits and adjacent input tokens to capture distinct inference patterns across different LLMs (GPT-4-Turbo, Claude3 Sonnet, Gemini-1.0 Pro, etc.).
- Consistently outperforms baselines with over 25% average increase in AUC score across natural language and code datasets.
- Addresses the nuanced task of identifying the specific source LLM, not just human vs. AI discrimination.
- Paper is well-written and clearly presented, making it accessible to both technical and non-technical readers.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of intuition for core design choices**: The rationale for using cross-entropy loss between surrogate output logits and one-hot encodings of the context is unclear. Why is this loss a good indicator of AI-generated text? The motivation for subtracting neighboring cross-entropy losses to enhance distinguishability is not explained; what properties of AI-generated text does this operation exploit? No experimental analysis links loss values to specific LLMs or explains what high/low loss represents.
- **Missing details on data generation and collection**: How were AI-generated texts from GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4-Turbo, Claude-3-Sonnet, Claude-3-Opus, and Gemini-1.0-Pro constructed? The number of samples, prompts used, and how generated samples differ from original datasets are not provided. Paraphrased versions of the six datasets also lack construction details. Without these, it is unclear why Profiler achieves near 100% AUC on some datasets (e.g., Essay for GPT-4 Turbo).
- **Ambiguity in mathematical notation and definitions**: (a) Equation 1 uses an unexplained black dot. (b) Lines 235–240: the definition of input token sequence X (X=x_{1:n} or X=x_{1:i}) needs a subscript if it depends on i. (c) Line 256: is C a set or a vector? (d) Lines 259–260: why does an even W guarantee the context matrix L^C to be symmetric, given its dimension depends on n? (e) Equation 2: the dot product is unclear—element-wise (if o^v is a scalar) or inner product (if o^v is a vector). (f) Line 279: including both standard deviation and variance provides redundant information. (g) Lines 291–293: vectors s^j (6x1), d^j ((n-W-1)x1), and g^j ((n-W-2)x1) have different dimensions; how are they combined into a matrix?
- **Unclear feature representation and visualization**: (a) Figure 2: why do GPT-3.5 Turbo features not follow a 2D Gaussian distribution and look very different from GPT-4 Turbo? (b) Do Profiler features have only 2 dimensions? How are these two dimensions selected? (c) Feature 1 appears to provide most separable information while feature 2 ranges heavily overlap; can texts be separated with one feature?
- **Surrogate model selection and training details**: (a) Surrogate models (LLaMA2-7B/13B, LLaMA3-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2B/7B) may not have comparable capabilities to detect closed-source LLMs (GPT-4-Turbo, Claude-3-Opus). (b) How do authors ensure surrogate models have not seen the evaluation datasets (Essay, Arxiv, Yelp, etc.) during pretraining? (c) Is Profiler fine-tuned? Provide training details.
- **Generalization and experimental concerns**: (a) The terms “in-distribution” and “out-of-distribution” settings are not clearly explained; the difference between these two experiment setups needs definition. (b) “Zero-shot pattern” (line 340) requires explanation or reference. (c) Table 1 shows large variation in baseline performance (0.01 to 0.8 AUC); why is Profiler more robust across models? (d) Table 2: Claude-3-Sonnet and Claude-3-Opus have relatively lower scores on Normal Dataset; why is performance better on paraphrased datasets? (e) Potential data contamination: samples from Yelp may contain GPT-generated texts, and Arxiv may contain human-written and GPT-moderated samples. (f) Generalization to marginal cases (e.g., text first human-written then later modified or translated by an LLM) is not addressed—would these be considered human-written or LLM-generated?
