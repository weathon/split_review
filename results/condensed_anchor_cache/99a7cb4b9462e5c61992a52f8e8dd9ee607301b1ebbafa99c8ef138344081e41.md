## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces novel hypersphere-based anomaly detection methods: Deep Orthogonal Hypersphere Compression (DOHC) and Deep Orthogonal Bi‑Hypersphere Compression (DO2HSC). DOHC uses an orthogonal projection layer to align the training data distribution with a hypersphere hypothesis, improving accuracy and reducing false negatives. DO2HSC employs two concentric hyperspheres to form a more compact hyperspherical shell decision region. The methods are supported by theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–3) and extended to graph‑level anomaly detection via maximum mutual information between substructure and global features. Extensive experiments on image (e.g., CIFAR10), tabular, and six graph datasets demonstrate significant superiority over state‑of‑the‑art methods (DROCC, PLAD, GLocalKD, OCGTL), with impressive visualizations.

### Strengths
- **Novel and interesting ideas**: The orthogonal projection layer for standard hypersphere learning and the bi‑hypersphere compression (concentric hyperspheres) are innovative. The paper addresses existing limitations: decision surface often a hyperellipsoid (rather than a standard hypersphere), regular data lying far from the center, and hypersphere sparsity. The regularization term in the objective function avoids feature correlation and variance issues.
- **Solid theoretical foundation**: Propositions 1, 2, and 3 provide rigorous analysis; the paper includes mathematical proofs and theoretical justification for the proposed methods.
- **Extensive and comprehensive experiments** across three data modalities: image (CIFAR10), multiple tabular datasets, and six graph datasets. The proposed methods (DOHSC and DO2HSC) significantly outperform state‑of‑the‑art baselines (Deep SVDD, DROCC 2020, PLAD 2022, GLocalKD 2022, OCGTL 2022), with remarkable improvements.
- **Clear motivations and technique details**: Well‑structured, clearly written, with logical flow. Visualizations (Figures 1–4, 12, 13) are distinctive, intuitive, and effectively convey the superiority of the proposed methods.
- **Broad applicability**: The framework extends to challenging graph‑level anomaly detection via graph representation learning with maximum mutual information, demonstrating versatility across data types.
- **Sound research quality**: Rigorous validation on benchmark datasets, reproducible design, and well‑presented contributions.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical justification for hypersphere vs. ellipsoid**: The assumption that data will conform to a hyperspherical shape in latent space after neural network transformation needs more rigorous mathematical support, especially given that NN transformations can introduce correlations and non‑uniform variances. The paper should elaborate on how the orthogonal projection layer mitigates these issues and specifically *guarantees* a standard hypersphere. (Multiple reviewers)
- **Fundamental reason for orthogonal projection improvement not fully explained**: Why does the orthogonal projection layer improve performance (Figure 2)? Further analysis and references are requested. (Reviewer 2)
- **“Soap bubble phenomenon” needs clarification**: Does it indicate incomplete optimization? A clearer explanation is required. (Reviewer 3)
- **Why anomalies appear inside the decision boundary under Deep SVDD**: Given that Deep SVDD forces normal data near the center, what are the key differences and similarities between normal data and those anomalous points that fall inside? Discussion needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Advantage of DO2HSC over DOHSC for data compactness**: The paper claims DO2HSC makes training data more compact, but complete optimization of DOHSC might achieve the same goal. The specific benefit of bi‑hypersphere for compactness should be clarified. (Reviewer 3)
- **Motivation for outer hypersphere (Proposition 2)**: In high‑dimensional space, normal data are already far from the origin. Why is it necessary to push them further to the outer hypersphere (i.e., reduce shell thickness)? The connection to the new anomaly score (Equation 9) should be made explicit. (Reviewer 2)
- **Parameters r_max and r_min**: Are they fixed or adaptively determined? (Reviewer 2)
- **Proposition 3 edge case**: When r_min = r_max, κ becomes infinity. Does this case make physical sense? (Reviewer 2)
- **Scalability concerns**: The computational and memory costs of singular value decomposition (SVD) for high‑dimensional and large datasets are not addressed. Potential optimizations, approximations, and memory requirements for projection matrices and intermediate results should be discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- **What makes graph anomaly detection special?** A more explicit explanation of the differences compared to image and tabular data is needed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparison result**: FCDD is mentioned in Section 3.1 but is absent from Table 2. (Reviewer 2)
- **Imbalanced vs. balanced experiments in graph data**: Appendix K shows imbalanced graph results. Are the main paper graph results from balanced experiments? What is the difference between the two settings? (Reviewer 2)
- **Table 13 bolding inconsistency**: Three results are bolded for Class 1 of MUTAG, but the caption specifies marking only the best two results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Typo/grammar issue**: In Section 2.1.2, “cannot be avoided by solving equation 1” – it is an optimization problem, not an equation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Clarity of Proposition 2 proof in supplementary**: The proof should be made clearer. (Reviewer 1)
- **Equation (3) guarantee**: How does singular value decomposition guarantee that the projected embeddings are orthogonal? (Reviewer 1)
- **Table 1 averaging details**: Were scores averaged over multiple runs (as in Tables 2 and 3)? (Reviewer 4)
- **Choice of evaluation metric for tabular data**: Why was F1 used instead of AUC ROC (used for graph data)? (Reviewer 4)
- **Visualization processing**: For visualizations, the projection dimension was set to 3. Was any additional dimensionality reduction technique applied? If so, the reported AUC ROC for those experiments may differ from Table 3 scores. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor weakness**: The problem is formulated as one‑class anomaly detection, but the authors clarified this choice. (Reviewer 4)
