## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Spatio-Temporal Dependency-Aware Neuron Optimization (ST-DANO) method for SNNs, which introduces two neuron variants—Long-LIF (LLIF) and Short-LIF (SLIF)—designed to capture long-term and short-term temporal dependencies, respectively. The authors argue that existing SNNs use uniform temporal dependencies across layers, leading to missing or redundant gradients. Their method includes a layer-wise neuron search to automatically select the optimal neuron type per layer. Experiments on static and neuromorphic datasets (e.g., 83.90% on DVS-CIFAR-10, >5% improvement) demonstrate effectiveness. The paper is clearly written and the storyline is complete.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important issue in SNNs by optimizing neuron behavior based on varying temporal dependencies across layers.
- Introduction of Long-LIF and Short-LIF neurons is a valuable attempt to balance long-term and short-term dependencies.
- Significant performance improvements over baseline models on both static and neuromorphic datasets, with a notable 5%+ gain on DVS-CIFAR-10.
- The whole storyline is complete and reasonable.
- Conducted experiments show the effectiveness of LLIF and SLIF.
- The paper is written clearly and easy to understand.
- Experiments and implementation details are abundant.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related work comparison:** The authors did not review or compare against extensive existing work on dynamic neuron parameters (e.g., biologically inspired dynamic thresholds, synapse-threshold synergistic learning, adaptive threshold plasticity, GLIF, etc.). Over 12 relevant references are listed, and the claimed advantages over these methods are not demonstrated.
- **Limited flexibility:** The method relies on two predefined neuron types (LLIF and SLIF) rather than a generalized strategy that could dynamically balance temporal dependencies. This limits adaptability compared to evolved or self-adaptive neuron designs.
- **Insufficient comparisons:** Table 2 lacks comparisons with state-of-the-art methods. The study does not validate on complex architectures (e.g., RNNs, Transformers) or large-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet), limiting generalizability.
- **Ablation studies incomplete:** Only limited ablation on neuron combinations is provided (e.g., only LIF+SLIF, LIF+LLIF, SLIF+LLIF). Missing ablations on using only Long-LIF or only Short-LIF, or verifying on other encoding paradigms.
- **Performance concerns:** Performance on CIFAR-100 is relatively low. In Figure 2 a1/a2, SLIF-4 fails to converge with ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10; explanation is needed.
- **Mechanism clarity for SLIF and LLIF:** 
  - The claim that SLIF captures short-term dependencies is questionable. In Eq.(9), τ[t] decreases when a spike fires, which may overlook correlations between time steps rather than capture them.
  - For LLIF, the explanation that increasing threshold increases gradient contribution from early time steps (lines 232-247) is weak and ambiguous. A quantitative analysis (e.g., derivative of Eq.4 w.r.t. V_th) is needed.
  - The motivation for Eq.9 (SLIF design) is not introduced; it is unclear how short-term dependency is reflected in the neuronal dynamics.
- **Redundancy concern:** One reviewer argues the distinction between LLIF and SLIF is redundant, as adjusting the threshold in LLIF could also achieve short-term dependency (SLIF). The necessity of separating them should be justified with experiments or analysis.
- **Hardware implementation issues:** The use of tanh activation in neuronal dynamics (Eq.7 and Eq.9) involves exponential operations and is not hardware-friendly. Previous methods (e.g., Temporal-wise Attention SNN) avoid this by using different train/inference behaviors (e.g., sigmoid in training, Heaviside in inference).
- **Missing experiments on temporal tasks:** The method focuses on long/short-term dependency but is not tested on memory or continuous time-series tasks (e.g., sequential/pixel classification on SMNIST, or adaptive spiking RNN benchmarks) that would directly evaluate the claim.
- **Extra cost not quantified:** Training/inference cost (e.g., parameters, FLOPs, search cost ratio to training time) compared to baseline LIF networks is not provided. Table 3 reports search cost in GPU days but no ratio.
- **Table formatting:** Table 2 is too wide.
