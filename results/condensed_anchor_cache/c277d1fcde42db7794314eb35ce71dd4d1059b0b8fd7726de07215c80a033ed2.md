## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Improved Integration Approximation (IIA) for ODE-based diffusion sampling (EDM, DDIM, DPM-Solver). Instead of using analytically determined coefficients, IIA optimizes coefficients at each time step by minimizing MSE against a high-accuracy integration computed with a fine-grained grid (M steps). This accelerates sampling without modifying the pre-trained model. Experiments show improved FID for NFE ≤ 25.

### Strengths
- The proposed method is easy to understand, with clear writing and theoretical formulations (R1, R2, R4).
- The technique can be applied to any ODE-based sampler (EDM, DDIM, DPM-Solver) and improves them without requiring model changes (R1, R4).
- Extensive numerical experiments demonstrate improvements in FID, especially for small NFEs (R2, R3).
- The paper addresses an important and timely topic, and its contributions are clearly described (R3).
- The idea is original and has potential to be integrated into SOTA diffusion models (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Design principle is not well-motivated; e.g., for IIA-EDM, why choose $z_i - D_\theta$ as a "first gradient" rather than $D_\theta$? For IIA-DDIM, both data-prediction and noise-prediction models are used despite their mathematical equivalence, reducing to a linear combination of $z_i$ and $\epsilon_\theta(z_i,t_i)$ (R1). The common design principle across different solvers is unclear (R1).
- No theoretical analysis of convergence, stability, or error reduction of the IIA-based algorithms; it is unclear if the optimization guarantees reduced error compared to standard methods (R2).
- Improvement is only observed for small NFE (≤25); the paper lacks discussion on why improvement diminishes at larger NFE and does not analyze error accumulation (R2).
- Hyper-parameter choices are not sufficiently justified: selection of coarse/fine grids (number and location of points), $r$ (number of past gradients used), and $M$ (fine-grained steps). In experiments, $r=1$ is mostly used (except BIIA-EDM uses $r=0$), but no ablation study demonstrates the impact of different $r$ or $M$ on FID and computational overhead (R3, R4). The choice of $M$ and fine-grained time-step may also depend on the pre-trained model; how to select them is unclear (R2).
- No comparison with other numerical integration methods (e.g., Anderson Acceleration, which resembles IIA) or discussion of why existing methods are inappropriate (R3, R4).
- The difference between IIA and BIIA is not clearly illustrated; IIA treats coefficients of two gradients as free parameters, but it is unclear how IIA compares to BIIA with larger $r$ (R4).
- Minor issues: typo in Table 1 column 6 ("IIA-PDM-Solver" should be "IIA-DPM-Solver") (R1); for SD v2 the variant is 2nd-order multi-step DPM-Solver++, not DPM-Solver (R1); typo in equation (10) (R2). The derivation ignoring stepsize factors in (13) compared to preserving 1/2 in (9) needs clarification (R2).

Note: One reviewer initially had major concerns about lack of principle but after revision found them addressed and raised score to 6 (R1). All reviewers assigned overall score 6.
