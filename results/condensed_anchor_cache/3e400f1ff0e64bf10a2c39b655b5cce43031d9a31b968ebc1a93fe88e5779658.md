## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two graph-based diffusion models (DGN and LDGN) for sampling equilibrium states of fluid dynamical systems from short, partial trajectories. It combines multi-scale Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), optionally using a Variational Graph Auto-Encoder (VGAE) for latent-space diffusion. The method is evaluated on three tasks: pressure on an ellipse in 2D laminar flow, velocity and pressure around an ellipse, and pressure on a 3D wing. Baselines include a Bayesian GNN, Gaussian mixture model, Gaussian regression model, and a VAE. The approach shows strong quantitative results but raises concerns about methodological originality, baseline strength, reproducibility, and theoretical formulation.

### Strengths
- **Clear writing and presentation** (R1, R2, R3, R5): The paper is well-written, with thorough mathematical and architectural details. The pipeline, input/output structure, and experimental setup are clearly described, aiding potential reproducibility.
- **Strong results relative to baselines** (R1, R3, R5): Both DGN and LDGN consistently outperform the tested baselines, with impressive results on realistic test cases (e.g., 3D wing) and a robust evaluation.
- **Interesting and relevant application** (R1, R4, R5): The problem of predicting full distributions of equilibrium states from short simulations is highly relevant to the physics-ML community. The integration of GNNs with diffusion models is novel in this space (R1, R4) and enables handling unstructured meshes, a key advantage for complex geometries.
- **Novelty highlighted by one reviewer** (R3): “Novelty and Innovation: The paper presents a novel and compelling approach by combining multi-scale GNNs, DDPM, and VGAEs, pushing boundaries in fluid flow modeling.”
- **Ability to learn from incomplete data** (R1, R5): The method learns full distributions from short trajectories that do not reach equilibrium, which is a valuable practical feature.
- **Well-reasoned design choices** (R2): The use of multi-scale GNN as an encoder for latent diffusion is motivated and makes sense. The downsampling strategy aligns with numerical PDE practices (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Disagreement on novelty / limited originality** (R2, R5 vs. R3): R2 states “the primary limitation lies in the methodology’s originality. The work largely assembles existing methods.” R5 notes “the building blocks are unsurprising and reflective of larger trends.” In contrast, R3 explicitly sees novelty. This contrast should be noted.
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons** (R1, R2): The chosen baselines are not widely used or state-of-the-art (R1). Missing baseline: “Direct Prediction of Steady-State Flow Fields in Meshed Domain with Graph Networks” (R2). R2 strongly encourages adding this baseline and discussing when deterministic vs. generative approaches are preferable. R1 questions whether improvements come from the diffusion model or GNN design, and suggests considering latent DiT or DDIM.
- **Missing related work and citations** (R1, R2, R3):
    - R2: Missing references to “Direct Prediction of Steady-State Flow Fields in Meshed Domain with Graph Networks” (steady-state GNN baseline) and “Efficient Learning of Mesh-Based Physical Simulation with Bi-Stride Multi-Scale Graph Neural Network” (automatic graph hierarchy construction, closer to this paper’s pipeline than Fortunato 2022).
    - R1: The related work on DDPMs for graphs needs to differentiate from DiffSTG and Lino et al. 2022.
    - R3: Missing citation for the original GAN paper (Goodfellow 2014) when discussing generative models.
- **Lack of code and reproducibility** (R3): No code is provided; specific hyperparameters and implementation details of the multi-scale GNN and diffusion process are unavailable. Only datasets are released, hindering independent replication.
- **Insufficient problem formulation and theoretical grounding** (R4, R5):
    - R4: The underlying physics (PDEs) and the concept of “statistical equilibrium” need rigorous definition. The learning problem and its generality (across trajectories, physical parameters, domain shapes) are unclear. R4 asks how complex/chaotic systems (e.g., Lorenz system) can be handled.
    - R5: Questions the well-posedness of training on short trajectories to predict converged states (Line 142). Asks for intuitive explanation. Also asks about the meaning of “interpolates between the bounds of the process’ entropy” (Line 169) and applicability of (Ho et al., 2020) to graph-based models. Questions the receptive field of vanilla DGN and its impact (Line 180). Asks what “perceptually equivariant” means and how it is verified (Line 207). Inquires about the accuracy of W₂^graph estimation given high-dimensional optimal transport and number of samples used (Line 384). Notes that the “3x faster” claim (Line 451) likely excludes training time of the diffusion model.
- **Formatting and presentation issues** (R1, R3, R5): Acronyms not consistently introduced (e.g., DDPM on line 76, GNN) (R3). Typo: “defined their discretization mesh” (Line 107) (R5). Unnecessary hyphen in “bottleneck” (Line 249) (R5). R1 rated presentation 2 due to lack of contextualization.
- **Missing ablation and design justification** (R1, R5): R1 asks whether gains come from diffusion or GNN design, and whether DiT or DDIM was considered. R5 asks about receptive field limitations of the vanilla DGN. R4 asks about generality to chaotic systems.
