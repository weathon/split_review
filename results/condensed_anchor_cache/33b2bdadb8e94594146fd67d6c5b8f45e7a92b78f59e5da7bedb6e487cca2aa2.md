## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for hybrid analog/digital large-neighborhood local search (LNLS) by reducing it to block Langevin Diffusion (BLD) algorithms. It proves exponential KL-divergence convergence for randomized and cyclic block selection under ideal analog accelerators, and gives explicit 2-Wasserstein bias bounds for finite device variation. Reviewers find the work novel, timely, and well-structured, with explicit constants and hyperparameter simplification. However, concerns are raised about clarity for non-experts, heavy reliance on references, missing discussion of analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) bottlenecks, the realism of key assumptions (LSI, Assumptions 3 and 6), a bound–use tension in Theorem 3, and insufficient experimental analysis in appendices.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, with remarkably few typos; exposition, motivation, and limitations are presented clearly (R3, R1).
- The reduction to block Langevin diffusion is natural, promising, and yields significantly softer assumptions compared to previous results (R3).
- Results include valuable practical properties: explicit constants, hyperparameter simplification, handling of device variation (R3).
- The paper provides a good literature review and contextualization of results; contributions are objectively and clearly identified (R1).
- The authors are upfront and honest about the limitations of their work (R1, R3).
- The topic is interesting, timely, and the unusual approach is nontrivial (R3, R5).
- The paper is clearly structured, with each theorem building on previous results to form a coherent narrative; findings are supported by clear numerical experiments (R4).
- Strong novelty: first explicit probabilistic convergence guarantees for hybrid LNLS (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- The paper is challenging to read for a broad audience; many uncommon terms (LNLS, etc.) are not explained mathematically; Figure 1 is too abstract; introduction needs simplification (R5).
- Despite overall clarity, the paper relies too heavily on references; essential details about the Dynamical System (DX) baseline architecture and parameters are missing (e.g., Section 4), hindering self-containedness (R3).
- Appendices lack discussion and analysis: parameter choices (Gaussian means, variances, number of iterations, block sizes) are not justified; impact on convergence rates not discussed; experimental results are insufficiently analyzed (R3).
- Claim in lines 051‑059 that device variation requires retraining or hyperparameter adjustment lacks references or substantiation; should be supported by literature or further discussion of problematic device variation types (R3).
- No discussion of analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) bottlenecks, which are crucial for accuracy, latency, power efficiency, and die footprint of hybrid analog/digital accelerators; experiments do not take ADCs into account; previous works on the topic also not addressed (R3).
- No guidance for interpreting 2‑Wasserstein distance values in experiments (e.g., is \(W_2=1\) large?); quantitative reasoning would benefit from general guidance (R3).
- Lack of discussion on performance differences between Random and Cyclic block selection strategies; motivation for choosing one over another is missing (R1).
- Figure 2(e) shows a discrepancy: the curve for \(\delta=0\) does not match the ideal curve; explanation needed (R1).
- Need more discussion on the plausibility of the log‑Sobolev inequality (LSI) in applications and examples of distributions that satisfy it (R1).
- Why consider Block Langevin Diffusion? Why not optimize w.r.t. all variables? (R5)
- Lines 345‑347: likely a typo, should be \(\|x-y\|^2\) instead of \(\|x^2 - y^2\|\) (R5).
- Assumptions 3 and 6 are very uncommon; Assumption 5: how does the function inside the integral depend on \(t\)? (R5)
- Theorem 3 yields convergence rate \(\log(1/\varepsilon) + \varepsilon\), which is \(\geq 1\); unclear how to achieve Wasserstein distance \(\leq 0.001\) with such a bound (R5).
- Equations throughout the paper lack punctuation and should be corrected (R2).
