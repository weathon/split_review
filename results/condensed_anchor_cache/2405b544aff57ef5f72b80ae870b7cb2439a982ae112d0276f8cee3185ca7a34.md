## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a content-style disentanglement method for controllable artistic stylization using a new multimodal dataset WikiStyle+, Q-Formers to learn separated representations, and multi-step cross-attention layers injected into a pre-trained diffusion model. Reviewers acknowledge the dataset contribution and the modal flexibility but express major concerns about method clarity, insufficient comparisons, questionable evaluation metrics, and overclaimed results. One reviewer (score 6) found the qualitative style transfer more nuanced (e.g., capturing broad brushstroke techniques rather than mere pattern copying), while the other three (score 5) judged the work as incremental and the evidence incomplete.

### Strengths
- **WikiStyle+ dataset**: Provides artwork images with paired textual descriptions of style and content. Multiple reviewers note this is a meaningful resource for the community. [R1,R2,R4]
- **Multimodal input flexibility**: The method accepts content and style from different modalities (text or image). [R2]
- **Use of Q-Former for disentanglement**: A creative way to decouple content and style features before injection into the diffusion model. [R3]
- **Thorough qualitative comparisons**: The paper includes extensive visual analyses and surpasses several baselines on reported metrics. [R3]
- **Nuanced style transfer (minority positive view)**: One reviewer noted that in examples like Van Gogh’s Starry Night, the method captures broader brushstroke techniques rather than replicating signature swirling patterns, suggesting better general adaptation to artistic style. [R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Method description is unclear and inconsistent**:
  - The outputs and training objectives of the CSDN (Content and Style Disentangled Network) and the specific architecture of the image encoder and Q-Former are not explained. [R2]
  - The “image-grounded text generation loss” and the “two-class linear classifier” in the image-text matching loss (ITM) are not defined; which model generates the text and how the embeddings are used in the contrastive tasks (ITC, ITM, ITG) is unspecified. [R2]
  - The “Mlti-step Cross-attention Layers” (MCL) in Line 251 are not elaborated: how they are implemented and how style embeddings are injected during the denoising process remains vague. [R3]
  - Eq. 3 lacks details on the binary classification network (inputs, how cosine similarity is transformed into a matching probability, specific layers). The distinction between content and style inputs ($(I_c,T_c)$ vs $(I_s,T_s)$) in the equation is unclear. [R3]
  - The text generation decoder in Eq. 4 is not introduced; its architecture (layers, activation functions, hidden-to-vocab mapping) is completely absent. [R3]
  - The pipeline in Fig. 4 contradicts the text description (Sec. 4.2): the figure shows style embeddings injected only into the middle block rather than via multi-step cross-attention; the time embedding is missing from the multi-step cross-attention layers; content embeddings are shown fed into cross-attention layers but are described as concatenated with text encoder outputs. [R1]
  - Symbols are overloaded: T represents forward steps, text features, and text length; t denotes both timestep and text sequence index; image features are called $z$ in Fig. 4 but $F_I$ in the text. [R3]
  - Typos: “Mlti-step” (should be “Multi-step”), learning rate “51e-5” (likely a typo), Fig. 4 “Detangle Loss” (should be “Disentangle Loss”). [R3]
- **Dataset concerns**:
  - The overlap between WikiStyle+ and the existing WikiArt dataset [A] is not discussed; it is unclear whether the dataset will be released. [R1]
  - Style descriptions rely on existing WikiArt tags, which may be too simplistic for lesser-known styles, while famous styles like Van Gogh may benefit from prior knowledge in the pre-trained model. [R4]
  - No out-of-distribution evaluation (where the reference style/artist is not in the pre-training dataset) was conducted to test generalization of learned style representations. [R1]
- **Missing comparisons and incomplete evaluation**:
  - Recent strong baselines such as StyleDrop (NeurIPS 2023) and DreamStyler (AAAI 2024) are not compared. [R2,R4]
  - In qualitative results (Fig. 1 and Fig. 5), methods like DeaDiff and InstantStyle better follow the input style than the proposed method, raising doubts about the model’s style replication ability. [R1]
  - Quantitative experiments are only provided for the text-to-image stylization task; the stylized text-to-image generation and collection-based stylization tasks lack any quantitative evaluation, limiting assessment of generalizability. [R2]
- **Style similarity metric is not persuasive**:
  - The metric generates descriptive text from the reference image and compares it with the generated image, introducing an unnecessary layer of abstraction and potential information loss. It is unclear how the descriptive text is generated and what ensures its faithfulness to the original style. [R2]
  - Alternatives such as CLIP direct similarity or Gram loss (widely used) are not employed. [R2]
  - The metric does not disentangle style aspects like tones, color schemes, or contrast, making it insufficient for meaningful comparison. [R1]
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:
  - The effect of each component in the disentanglement loss (Eq. 1) is not studied. [R1,R3]
  - No analysis of how the level of detail in content and style descriptions affects disentanglement performance (e.g., whether minimal descriptions lead to poorer control). [R4]
  - The paper does not examine how style replication varies with the granularity of style-content prompts. [R4]
- **Overclaimed performance and style accuracy**:
  - The stylized images consistently show noticeable deviations in color and fine-grained details compared to the reference style, often producing a generalized style rather than precise replication. Multiple reviewer observations support this. [R1,R2]
  - One reviewer noted that while the method may capture broader stylistic elements, it does not convincingly outperform prior work in terms of style fidelity. [R2]
- **Computational overhead not addressed**:
  - The inference speed and computational overhead of the CSDN and the diffusion process are not reported, making it unclear how the method compares to previous work in efficiency. [R2]
