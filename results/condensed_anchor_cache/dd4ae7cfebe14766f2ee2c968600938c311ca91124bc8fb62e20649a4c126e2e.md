## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces SambaMOTR, a tracking-by-propagation framework that uses Samba, a linear-time set-of-sequences model designed to jointly process multiple tracklets by synchronizing multiple selective state-spaces. It addresses long-range dependencies within tracklets, interdependencies among tracklets, and temporal occlusions. The method is evaluated on DanceTrack, BFT, and SportsMOT, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths

- Extensive experiments with different modeling alterations provided in the main paper and appendix (R1).
- Strong performance on DanceTrack, BFT, and SportsMOT (R2, R3).
- The reason to integrate SSMs into a tracking-by-propagation framework is well motivated (R2).
- Ablation studies verified the introduced modifications (R2).
- The paper is well written, with provided illustrative examples showcasing strong results (R2).
- Introduces a novel linear-time set-of-sequences model that synchronizes multiple selective state-spaces, enabling the joint processing of tracklets (R3).
- Performance gains are achieved on challenging multi-object tracking benchmarks (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of qualitative comparisons:** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3) noted the absence of visual comparisons between SambaMOTR and prior works, especially for occlusions, missed detections, ID switches, and complex motion patterns. Specific tracking cases demonstrating superiority in trajectory modeling are needed (R2, R3). R1 requested direct visual comparisons to substantiate the claim that the autoregressive model outperforms memory/motion models.
- **Unclear notation and motivation:** The interpretations of the system **A** and control **B** matrices in the context of object tracking are unclear; clearer definitions would help (R1). One reviewer (R1) found the innovation limited, characterizing the method as a straightforward combination of MOTR and Mamba with a not-fully-compelling motivation. In contrast, other reviewers (R2, R3) considered the contributions novel and well-motivated.
- **Tracking-by-propagation vs. tracking-by-detection:** The distinction between TBP and TBD is subtle because TBP still requires a detector to initiate states and recognize new objects; propagation alone is not practical for multiple object tracking (R1). It was noted that performance still falls short behind classic motion models as reported in Table A (R1). R2 requested an apple-to-apples comparison with TBD methods by evaluating SambaMOTR on top of YOLOX-X.
- **Efficiency claims lack measurements:** The paper states (L137) that SambaMOTR has "the same GPU memory requirements", but no measurements back this claim. Efficiency data (peak memory, FLOPs, parameter counts, hidden state size) are missing, and a breakdown of memory usage by components (Mamba blocks, attention layers) would provide granular understanding (R2).
- **Computational complexity analysis missing:** Time and space complexity of the Samba module during training and inference, and how the linear-time claim scales with number of tracklets and sequence length, need analysis and comparison with other tracking methods (R3).
- **Need for comparison with alternative sequence models:** An in-depth analysis justifying Samba for trajectory modeling is required, including comparison with xLSTM, other SSMs, and RRNs, and a discussion of representational power and inductive biases (R3).
- **Uncertainty about SSM domain:** It is unclear whether the state space model operates on the coordinate domain (bounding boxes) or the visual domain (pixel values). If it operates on coordinates, how are auto-regressed output boxes refined to fit subjects without visual features? (R1).
