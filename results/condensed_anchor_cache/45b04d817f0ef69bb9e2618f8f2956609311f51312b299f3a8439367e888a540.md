- Scores: 5, 6, 8, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a unified framework for general 3D shape restoration, handling multiple defect types (noise, incompleteness, low-resolution) within a single model. The method uses a hierarchical VAE and a hierarchical latent diffusion model operating on high-resolution (256³) TSDF grids with sparse convolutions. A large-scale dataset is constructed from Objaverse, ShapeNet, GSO, and ABO, containing diverse defect types. A two-stage training strategy is introduced: first train on clean shapes, then refine the VAE on corrupted shapes with a latent guidance loss. The model is validated on the proposed dataset and on 3DQD and PatchComplete benchmarks, showing state-of-the-art results. Reviewers disagree on the significance and demonstration of contributions (scores: 5,6,8,8). Some reviewers find the method sensible and the dataset valuable, while others note insufficient comparisons, unclear architectural details, and weak evidence for claimed improvements.

### Strengths
- The problem (unified shape restoration for noisy, incomplete, low-resolution 3D data) is useful for preprocessing real scans.
- The proposed large-scale dataset is an improvement over existing ones: it is larger, includes diverse shape sources, and models realistic combined defects (e.g., noise + incompleteness) rather than a single defect type.
- The new benchmark using TSDFs could be very useful to the community, given the fragmented state of current evaluation (e.g., PatchComplete, 3DQD).
- The method is reasonably justified step-by-step; the hierarchical VAE + LDM design using sparse convolutions is sensible.
- The diffusion-based approach can produce multiple plausible completions, allowing user selection.
- Qualitative results suggest the proposed approach better preserves fine geometric details and produces higher-quality completions than prior methods.
- The attempt at a unified framework for multiple restoration tasks is interesting and shows good performance across tasks.
- The paper provides good writing and substantial appendix details for reproducibility.
- The dataset and method outperform state-of-the-art on multiple tasks (Tables 1, 2, 3) when pre-trained on the large-scale dataset.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing visual comparisons**: No visual comparisons with other methods are provided, making it hard to assess the claimed improvements (Reviewer 1).
- **No real-world validation**: All data is from the synthetic proposed dataset; results on real noisy 3D scans are absent (Reviewer 1).
- **No overview figure**: Figures 3–4 are insufficient; a high-level pipeline overview is missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Architecture lack of detail**: The actual architectures of the H-VAE encoder, decoder, and H-LDM are not described, even in the appendix (Reviewer 2).
- **Scalable training strategy not validated**: No evidence (e.g., training time table) is given that the two-stage strategy is more efficient. The claim that the second stage reduces loading costs is unclear: loading defective shapes still occurs, and the first stage also loads intact shapes (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Generalization issues**: On unseen categories (ABO, GSO), performance degrades substantially. Table 1 shows 2–5× worse MMD/AMD on ABO and ~100× worse on GSO across tasks, even for the easier noisy refinement task (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair quantitative comparisons**: In Table 2, the proposed method is the only one pre-trained on the large-scale dataset, which is not a fair comparison. The method performs similarly or worse than NeuSDFusion until extra task-specific data (noise-free completions) is added. A comparison without pre-training is needed (Reviewer 2). Same concern for Table 3 vs SC-Diff (Reviewer 2).
- **Shape representation not communicated early**: The full paper uses TSDF grids (resolution 256), but this is stated only on page 4. The title, abstract, and introduction do not clarify the representation, potentially misleading readers who assume different 3D formats (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing time and memory information**: No training time, inference time, or memory requirements are reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Diffusion method unsuitable for real-time**: The approach is not applicable to online use during 3D scanning (Reviewer 3).
- **Undemonstrated conditioning claim**: The paper claims the denoiser can be conditioned on “text or images” (Eq. 1), but this is never demonstrated or evaluated. The claim must be removed or proven in context (Reviewer 3).
- **Key challenges unclear**: (1) The dataset creation seems straightforward (random perturbations on existing data); why was it not done before? (2) The method’s advantage over simply merging existing task datasets and joint training is not ablated. Is improvement due to dataset scale, defect synthesis pipeline, or method design? (Reviewer 4).
- **Dataset ambiguity**: It is unclear whether each shape receives one defect type or multiple simultaneously. In Table 4 ablation, does “joint training” have twice the data of “noisy completion only” and “noisy refinement only”, confounding data scale with joint learning benefit? (Reviewer 4).
- **Open-sourcing**: The future availability of the dataset is not stated (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor errors**: Table 6 (appendix) has incorrectly bolded best CD for Lamp and best IoUs for Bed and Bench; Table 7 has incorrect IoU boldings for Bathtub, Basket, Printer (Reviewer 2). Typo: “tsdf” should be “TSDF” (line 429) (Reviewer 3).
- **Technical clarifications needed**: (a) How is feature alignment loss computed when the sparse voxel grids of defective and intact shapes differ? Is it only over shared voxel indices? (Reviewer 2). (b) How does the decoder generate missing geometry from a sparse feature grid? Is a structure prediction module used? (Reviewer 2).
