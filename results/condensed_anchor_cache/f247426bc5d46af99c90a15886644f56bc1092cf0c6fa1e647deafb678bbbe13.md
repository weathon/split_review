## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper revisits CNN architectures for DNA foundation models, proposing ConvNova with dilated convolutions, gated convolutions, and a dual-branch gating mechanism. Extensive empirical results show ConvNova outperforms recent Transformer- and SSM-based models on more than half of tasks across several benchmarks while using fewer parameters and enabling faster computation, demonstrating that CNNs remain strong competitors in DNA sequence modeling.

### Strengths
- Addresses a critical and timely question: whether CNNs are truly surpassed by Transformers and SSMs in DNA foundation models, with a novel application of classical convolution that yields significant improvements, especially on species-aware tasks.
- The overall presentation is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow, providing step-by-step design decisions that improve performance and efficiency.
- The experimental evaluation is strong, rigorous, and detailed, including comparisons against several strong baselines across a comprehensive set of tasks.
- The significance is high: a pure convolutional model outperforming Caduceus (a leading SSM) is an important result in the context of the field, adding to evidence that SSM complexities may not be necessary.
- The model is evaluated on multiple benchmarks covering short-range (Nucleotide Transformer Benchmark, Genomic Benchmark) and long-range tasks (Bend Gene Finding, Chromatin Profile Prediction), with carefully controlled experiments using different random seeds and robust ablation studies.
- The proposal improves the state of the art and may stimulate further research on CNN-based architectures for sequence modeling.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and connection to prior work**: The design choices (dilated convolutions with gating branches) are not entirely new; similar ideas have been explored in prior vision models (MogaNet, StarNet, MambaOut). The paper lacks a clear discussion of these connections and does not adequately motivate why this specific architecture is particularly useful for DNA tasks.
- **Missing comparisons**: The paper overlooks several recent DNA models (GPN, VQDNA, DNABERT-S) and important benchmarks (GUANinE v1.0, BEND, GUE, and long-range benchmarks where SSMs excel). There is also no comparison with a more standard CNN architecture (e.g., vanilla CNN or LegNet) to isolate the contributions of ConvNova’s specific design. This weakens the claim that the proposed design is superior to all prior CNN models.
- **Lack of qualitative and receptive field analysis**: The paper does not analyze the types of errors made by different model classes (e.g., whether a smaller receptive field causes mistakes on tasks requiring longer-range reasoning). It also lacks a clear explanation of how the receptive field is determined via dilated convolutions and how this choice impacts capturing long-range dependencies. Visualization of the receptive field or learned patterns is needed to support the claim that dilated convolutions are capable and efficient compared to self-attention or SSMs.
- **Ambiguity about novelty of training and architecture**: The training procedure appears similar to that of HyenaDNA, and the gated convolution design has been proposed elsewhere. The paper does not clearly articulate why such a CNN model was not discovered earlier and what specifically is novel about ConvNova compared to prior CNN-based DNA models (e.g., is it just better pretraining data, inclusion of complement sequences, or the specific combination of components?).
- **Missing experimental setup clarity and ablations**: The organization of the paper is somewhat confusing; for instance, the comparison of downsampling vs. dilation in Section 3.3/Table 1 belongs in the experiments. A dedicated section in the main text with objectives, baseline descriptions, and configurations per task is missing. Additional ablation studies on the gating branches (as in Eq. 1 and Appendix A.3), hyperparameter sensitivity (kernel size, dilation ratio), and the role of the dual-branch mechanism are absent.
- **Foundation model terminology**: Calling the model a “foundation model” is misleading given its relatively small size (7M parameters) and short pretraining (14 hours on a single GPU).
- **No pretraining data description in main text**: The details of the pretraining dataset are not provided in the main paper, which is essential for reproducibility and understanding the foundation model claim.
- **Lack of intuitive rationale for design choices**: The paper does not explain the intuition behind using dilation over self-attention in a DNA foundation model, why dilation outperforms self-attention, or what factors cause the dual-branch design to outperform a single-branch approach. A thorough analysis connecting design to task characteristics is missing.
