## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NeighborViT, an adaptive defense framework for Vision Transformers (ViTs) against adversarial patch attacks. It detects and categorizes attacks (catastrophic vs. non-catastrophic based on patch location) and applies tailored mitigation: for non-essential areas, neighboring patches fill masked adversarial patches; for essential areas, attention weights for adversarial tokens are reweighted. The method uses a Sobel operator with dynamic windows for detection and average predictions from reconstruction for classification. Experiments on ImageNet with multiple ViT models show improved robust accuracy while preserving clean accuracy. Two reviewers gave score 5 (positive), two gave score 3 (negative); the main disagreements concern experimental rigor, clarity, and generalizability.

### Strengths
- The core idea of distinguishing different types of adversarial patch attacks and adopting corresponding tailored defense mechanisms is interesting, logical, and validated by Table 1.
- The framework is presented in detail; the method analysis is clear and understandable, with helpful visualizations.
- The method is simple yet effective, achieving high clean accuracy with minimal reduction.
- Extensive experiments are conducted across multiple ViT models and attack methods (e.g., several state-of-the-art ViTs, various attack approaches), demonstrating robustness improvements.
- The proposed attack detection (AD) and classification are lightweight, requiring no auxiliary models or multiple queries, reducing computational overhead.
- The observation that location-based classification (catastrophic vs. non-catastrophic) improves defense performance is a sensible contribution (supported by Table 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited attack scenarios and comparisons:**
  - Experiments only use single-patch attacks (e.g., Figure 5); the claim of applying different defenses for various attack types requires validation with multiple adversarial patches per image.
  - Comparisons are only against robust ViT frameworks; classical input processing defenses (smoothing, quantization, JPEG compression, diffusion-based purification like "Diffusion Models for Adversarial Purification") are omitted, weakening the evaluation.
  - Experiments focus on specific attack types; a broader range (e.g., naturalistic adversarial patches with low pixel continuity like TnT Attacks, or attacks with L_p constraints) is needed to test generalizability.
- **Missing ablations and computational analysis:**
  - No ablation study on different operators (e.g., Sobel vs. other edge detectors); the Sobel choice appears empirical.
  - Additional time cost (seconds per epoch), FLOPs, and GPU memory usage are not reported; training costs are important for practical application.
  - Lack of experiments with varying attack strengths (e.g., patch size, perturbation budget) to understand robustness limits.
- **Clarity and technical details:**
  - The definitions of "catastrophic" and "non-catastrophic" attacks (L151-153) are unclear and introduced without proper explanation or reference. "Essential" and "non-essential" parts are vaguely defined; the paper overemphasizes how the area detector works rather than defining these terms precisely.
  - Technical aspects like the definition of similarity in Section 3.2 and the parameter `cl` in Table 10 are not explained in sufficient detail.
  - The paper lacks a discussion of limitations of the proposed method.
- **Practical deployment concerns:**
  - The method is designed for ViTs only; applying it to CNNs presents challenges, limiting its technical contribution and general applicability.
  - Three hyperparameters (including `gamma` in Table 6) require tuning; the optimal `gamma` varies with attack patch size, indicating sensitivity and practical difficulty.
  - The strategy may require fine-tuning for different datasets or attack scenarios, potentially affecting generalizability. The ratio of catastrophic to non-catastrophic attacks in experiments is not specified, nor which attack methods fall into each category.
- **Minor presentation issues:** Bar graphs can be improved in color and layout.

- **Reviewer questions overlapping with criticisms:**
  - Would naturalistic adversarial patches (non-noise, low pixel continuity adversarial patches) defeat the ENED module? (Reference: TnT Attacks, Universal Naturalistic Adversarial Patches.)
  - Performance improvement is only ~2% over the second-best defense in most settings; Jedi supports multitasking and naturalistic patches, suggesting more scenario limitations.
  - Can hyperparameters be adaptively tuned? Do they need adjustment for different ViT architectures and datasets? If so, how?
  - Could a more fine-grained classification of attack types further enhance detection and defense effectiveness?
  - Is it possible to dynamically scan patches and replace with more similar patches rather than just neighbors?
