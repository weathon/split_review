## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the first single model for sentence-level multilingual Visual Speech Recognition (VSR). To reduce computational costs, the authors introduce visual speech units obtained by discretizing features from a self-supervised audio-visual model (AV-HuBERT). They train a multilingual AV-HuBERT (mAV-HuBERT) on 5,512 hours of multilingual data and then pre-train a text prediction model using these discrete units as inputs, reducing input data size to 0.016% of original video. A curriculum learning strategy gradually transitions from audio-visual speech units to purely visual speech units during pre-training. After fine-tuning on continuous features, the model achieves state-of-the-art multilingual VSR, performing comparably to language-specific models.

### Strengths
- First sentence-level multilingual VSR with a single model, a novel and important problem. (R1, R3, R5)
- Clear improvement in training efficiency: discrete units reduce data storage and allow larger batch sizes, significantly cutting training time. (R1, R2, R5)
- The multilingual mAV-HuBERT achieves best WER on 3 out of 5 languages and second-best on 2 out of 5, with especially encouraging gains on under-resourced languages. (R5)
- Good ablation studies demonstrating efficacy of system components. (R4, R3)
- Thorough analysis of visual speech units confirms they capture viseme information while suppressing non-linguistic features (e.g., speaker identity). (R1, R5)
- Solid engineering with sufficient details to support reproducibility; code, models, and samples to be made publicly available. (R4)
- The general structure is clear, the method is simple, and it is easy to follow. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The core components (AV-HuBERT, HuBERT, curriculum learning, discrete units) are pre-existing with minimal architectural modification. While one reviewer considered the framework novel (R3), others noted that the work largely applies existing models to a new task without new insights, and the concept of visual speech units was already introduced in AV-HuBERT. (R1, R2, R4, R5)
- **Lack of architectural innovation**: The paper does not detail any changes to the AV-HuBERT architecture (e.g., transformer layers, feature extraction) when extending to multiple languages, making it difficult to assess the true contribution beyond scaling data and model. (R4, R5)
- **Unclear or insufficient justification of design choices**: 
  - Why the visual front-end is ResNet-18 and the transformer architecture is used; alternatives not considered. (R4)
  - The hyperparameter choices: target size 1,000, 350k steps, one iteration. (R4)
  - The curriculum learning schedule: why is audio mix probability p set to 100% after 70% of training? (R4)
  - The effect of visual token size (1,000) on final performance is not varied. (R5)
  - The statement “reduce training speed by removing the visual front-end” is ambiguous (perhaps “reduce training time” was intended). (R4)
- **Issues with comparisons and experimental fairness**:
  - In Table 2, the proposed mAV-HuBERT performs worse on English than the English-only AV-HuBERT, attributed to “curse of multilinguality.” But the English data constitutes a large portion of the training set (specific ratio not given), and if visemes are shared across languages, the larger-scale training should help; this needs explanation. (R2, R4)
  - The comparison in Section 4.3.1 (Table 6) may be unfair: AV-HuBERT is pre-trained on English only and then fine-tuned separately for each language, while mAV-HuBERT is a single model that benefits from multilingual pre-training. The paper should clarify whether mAV-HuBERT is fine-tuned on the combined multilingual set or per language. (R5)
  - The comparison to existing multilingual VSR approaches is weak. Recent works such as Cheng et al. (MixSpeech, ICCV 2023) and Anwar et al. (Muavic, 2023) are not discussed. (R5)
  - In Table 3, it is unclear whether the “standard VSR” baseline is trained from scratch or fine-tuned from pretrained models, and whether the reported training duration includes the pretrained model loading time. (R2)
- **Curriculum learning impact is unclear**: From Table 5, curriculum learning does not seem to have a significant effect. (R1) The paper does not report results using unit pre-training only (without CL and without FT) for comparison, which would show whether the method degenerates to the AV-HuBERT baseline of Table 6. (R2)
- **Missing experiments/ablations**:
  - How would performance differ if continuous features were used instead of discrete units (computational cost aside)? (R3)
  - Why use audio speech units rather than complete speech features during joint training? Is it only for computational efficiency, or does discretization affect performance? (R3)
  - The advantage of sentence-level (vs. word-level) models is not explained. (R3)
- **Reproducibility concerns**: The method is complex and might be difficult to reproduce without very careful implementation. (R3)
- **Presentation issues**: The reference list and title are misleading—the term “neural network” or “deep learning” should be added, or the related work section should include historical VSR work prior to deep learning. (R4) The paper contains grammatical errors and requires proofreading. (R1) Section 3.2 is hard to follow and not self-contained; results justifying modeling choices should be brought into that section. (R4)
