## Merged Review

### Summary

MF-LAL proposes a multi-fidelity generative framework for designing molecules with high binding affinity to a protein target, addressing the translational gap between docking (low-fidelity, cheap) and absolute binding free energy (ABFE) oracles (high-fidelity, expensive). The method uses hierarchical latent spaces with separate encoder-decoder pairs per fidelity level, stochastic variational Gaussian processes (SVGPs) as surrogate models, and active learning to query the most informative oracles. A likelihood-based generation objective encourages high-fidelity candidates to also score well at lower fidelities. Experiments on BRD4 and c-MET generate 15 compounds per method and evaluate ABFE; MF-LAL reportedly outperforms single- and multi-fidelity baselines in top-3 mean binding free energy.

### Strengths

- Addresses a critical problem in de novo drug design: improving the translational efficiency of oracles from docking to more accurate (but expensive) methods like ABFE.
- The hierarchical latent space design (separate latent representation \(z_k\) per fidelity) is clever, enabling specialized surrogate modeling and improved generation quality.
- Using SELFIES as the molecular representation is appropriate for this generative setup.
- The likelihood-based term in the generation objective (a molecule generated at level \(k\) should also have high property score at level \(k-1\)) is a smart technical trick, and its necessity is supported by ablation studies (Table 2).
- Integrating SVGPs as surrogate models directly on the latent spaces introduces mathematical robustness; joint minimization of the ELBO and the marginal log-likelihood ensures well-structured latent representations.
- Active learning for querying samples is sensibly extended from supervised learning to generative optimization in latent space with gradient-based methods.
- The use of ABFE for evaluation, despite its high cost, is a notable achievement enabled by the efficiency of the multi-fidelity approach.
- The paper is well-written, the problem is well-motivated, and the presentation is clear.
- Diverse baselines are implemented, and source code is provided for reproducibility.
- A new criterion for candidate molecule generation during active learning is proposed.

### Weaknesses

- **Input fidelity assumption not validated**: The claim that “low-fidelity → high-fidelity” methods represent an increasing accuracy hierarchy is not demonstrated; the authors should show correlation between all fidelity levels and experimental binding affinities for known binders. Without this, the multi-fidelity framework rests on shaky ground.
- **Evaluation with only 15 compounds is insufficient**: The small sample size (15 generated molecules per method) and high variance (large gap between mean and top-3 values) make the claims of “significantly better” unconvincing. Statistical significance tests are missing. The top-3 values are outliers and need careful chemical-motif analysis; for example, the best BRD4 molecule contains a pan-reactive SO2 group, and the best c-MET molecule contains an aldehyde group (known to promiscuously bind). Such artifacts may be rewarded by scoring functions. Benchmarking against known binders with docking and ABFE is lacking.
- **Termination criterion is not principled**: Training at the highest fidelity is stopped after 7 days of wall time rather than by a convergence threshold. This yields unsystematic results (e.g., top-2/3 c-MET values are much worse than top-1, while BRD4 values are similar). The effect of this termination rule on performance is not justified.
- **Likelihood constraint may limit exploration**: Maximizing the likelihood that a molecule would also be generated at the previous fidelity with high score (Equation 3) biases search toward regions that score well at lower, less accurate fidelities. The low-fidelity oracle may misguide the search, contributing to high variance. Ablation results (Table 2) show that the top-3 values without this constraint are not significantly lower than with it, questioning its necessity.
- **Method novelty and soundness are questionable**:
    - The hierarchical latent construction \(q(z_k|x)= \mathcal{N}(\mu_k(z_{k-1}),\sigma_k(z_{k-1}))\) with noise added at each step is conceptually similar to stacking deterministic networks and adding noise only at the final step. No theoretical or empirical evidence is provided to show that adding noise at each step is advantageous over the alternative. If the alternative is equivalent, the claimed “better inter-fidelity information passing” is trivial.
    - The claim that MF-LAL “combines generative and surrogate models into a single framework” is similar to prior work (e.g., [1]); optimizing the generative and surrogate terms jointly vs sequentially makes little difference since they are sequential. Furthermore, GPs are non-parametric; the second term in Equation (1) for fitting GP hyperparameters may not exist if hyperparameters are fixed.
    - Equation (1) is technically incorrect: the ELBO for the generative part should include \(P(z_1)\), i.e., \(\mathbb{E}_{z_1}[\log P(x|z_1) - \log q(z_1|x) + \log P(z_1)]\). Also, if \(f_k\) is a GP, the definition of \(p(y|f_k)\) (observation likelihood) is unclear.
- **Missing multi-fidelity baselines and fair comparisons**:
    - A standard multi-fidelity active learning baseline (e.g., autoregressive GP or co-kriging with EI/UCB) is not included. The comparison to Pocket2Mol is unfair because no optimization for binding affinity was applied, and Pocket2Mol is not state-of-the-art among 3D generative models. Newer 3D models (e.g., DecompDiff, PILOT) are not compared.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not explored**: Key hyperparameters – UCB trade-off \(\beta\), query threshold \(\gamma_k\), KL weight (very low ~0.08, which may create “holes” in the latent space), and inducing point locations for SVGPs – are not ablated. SVGPs are known to be sensitive to inducing points and can overestimate variance far from them, making line 7 of Algorithm 1 (acquisition using UCB) unreliable. The initial dataset distribution strongly influences inducing point locations and hence the entire active learning trajectory.
- **Reconstruction quality and latent space validity not reported**: The reconstruction accuracy of the four separate encoder-decoder networks is not provided, nor are learning curves. As fidelity increases, fewer training points are available, making the latent spaces sparse; it is unclear how often an optimized latent \(z_k\) can be decoded to a valid SELFIES string.
- **Insufficient evidence for the multi-fidelity trade-off**: The authors only present final aggregate results (Table 1), not the cost–performance curves that would demonstrate the claimed trade-off between fidelity and computational budget. The appendix figure shows curves for always choosing a single fidelity, which does not validate the multi-fidelity active learning component.
