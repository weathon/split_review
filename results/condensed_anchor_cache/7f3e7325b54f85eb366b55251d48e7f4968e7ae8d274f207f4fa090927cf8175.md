## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers agree that LMO-DP (score range: 3–6) proposes a novel non‑Gaussian noise mixture for DP fine‑tuning and reports very strong empirical accuracy at extremely low ε (e.g., 92.20% at ε=0.3, δ=0, outperforming prior work by an order of magnitude). However, opinions diverge sharply: one reviewer (R1, score 3, soundness 2) strongly questions the soundness of the results and the privacy mechanism, while two others (R2, R4, scores 5,5) and one (R3, score 6) find the results impressive but point out major presentation gaps, missing analyses, and potential issues with the problem setting. All reviewers note that the manuscript needs substantial clarification and additional validation.

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical results**: LMO‑DP consistently outperforms SOTA DP‑SGD baselines, especially in the high‑privacy regime (ε<3). For example, Table 3 shows LMO‑DP at ε=0.16 beating all prior work at ε=1.4. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Novel approach**: Minimizes added noise while satisfying a finite RDP guarantee by building on Mohammady (2020). The LMO subspace provides universal RDP guarantees. [R1, R3]
- **Simple implementation**: The method is relatively straightforward to add to DP training libraries such as Opacus. [R2, R3]
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Covers both classification (RoBERTa‑large on SST‑2) and generation (GPT‑2) tasks, with a provided reproducibility repo. [R2]
- **Timely and relevant**: Addresses privacy‑preserving fine‑tuning of LLMs, a problem of high practical importance. [R1, R4]
- **Potential generality**: One reviewer (R1) notes the framework may be independent of LMs (optimizes over cross‑entropy loss) and could have broader impact.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation is seriously unclear**: Key algorithm (Algorithm 3) appears only in the supplement, making the main‑text argument hard to follow. Terminology is imprecise (e.g., “corresponding parameters, including θ and others”, “secondary optimization”). The acronym “LMO” is defined differently in the abstract vs. Appendix C. Numerical inconsistencies occur (e.g., α range 2…129 in Eq.(2) vs. 3…130 in Algorithm 1). [R1, R2, R3]
- **Sections 3.3 and 4 are vague and hard to follow**: The description of “LM geometry” lacks precise definitions; the connection between the noise mixture and the optimization process is not clearly explained. Figure 1’s demonstration of coverage by the mixture of distributions is insufficient. [R2]
- **Privacy mechanism appears to protect outputs, not gradients, which departs from standard DP‑SGD**: Theorem 4.1 shows only the loss (cross‑entropy) without defining the query – this seems to release the loss rather than the gradients, leading to potential privacy leakage. R4 further states the core mechanism protects output labels/probabilities, making direct comparisons with DP‑SGD work (e.g., Bu et al. ICML 2023) potentially misleading. The problem setting may be different (protecting final query output vs. intermediate updates). [R1, R4]
- **Noise reduction analysis is insufficient**: Figure 2’s scale makes the difference from Gaussian noise unclear; noise frequently appears near zero. A log‑scale plot, average reduction statistics, and per‑component analysis are needed. R1 suspects the surprising accuracy gains may stem from this noise behavior. [R1]
- **Privacy‑utility trade‑off seems inverted**: The model achieves very high accuracy at extremely low ε (e.g., 92.20% at ε=0.3) without the expected utility drop, which requires explanation (e.g., does the noise actually vanish?). [R1, R3]
- **Missing discussion of tighter accountants**: The paper uses RDP but does not discuss state‑of‑the‑art PRV accountants, which could handle multiple noise distributions and might give better utility‑privacy trade‑offs. [R2, R4]
- **Theoretical gaps**: No theoretical convergence guarantee is provided; only empirical observations support the claim of fast convergence. [R1]
- **Misleading claims**: Table 1 reports “low memory” for LMO‑DP, but the method does not optimize memory (only noise scale). [R1]
- **Empirical details omitted**: Dataset sizes and number of classes (important for DP learning) are not reported. [R1]
- **Surprising training dynamics**: Figure 5 shows training completes in <10 steps; non‑private training steps are not reported for comparison. [R1]
- **Worst‑case over neighboring datasets not formalized**: Section 3.3’s global optimization appears to be formulated for a fixed pair (D, D′), but DP requires a max over all pairs. The worst‑case consideration is missing. [R4]
- **Unexplained parameter and noise behavior**: Why is α restricted to 2…129? Could the optimal α lie outside this range for some datasets? The noise level at ε=2,3 is slightly larger than at ε=0.2 – this needs explanation. [R4]
- **Missing ablations**: How would results change if models were trained from scratch instead of fine‑tuned? Extension to vision tasks (e.g., NAR generative models) is not discussed. [R3]
- **Reviewer disagreement on overall quality**: One reviewer (R1, score 3, soundness 2) finds the results likely unsound and the privacy mechanism flawed, while others (R2 score 5, R3 score 6, R4 score 5) acknowledge the potential but require major clarification and further analysis. This contrast is not resolved in the paper.
