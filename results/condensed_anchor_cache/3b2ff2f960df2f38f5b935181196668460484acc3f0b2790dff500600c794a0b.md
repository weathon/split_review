## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper presents a theoretical analysis of Barlow Twins (BT), arguing that the success of the method stems from the normalization of the cross‑correlation matrix rather than the off‑diagonal redundancy‑reduction term. The authors show that the cross‑correlation matrix of distorted images is symmetric independent of the backbone, implying that a linear layer can always diagonalize it, and that minimizing the off‑diagonal terms alone (e.g., with random Gaussian embeddings) is trivial. They conclude that the normalization forces the same minima as contrastive methods and is therefore essential for learning informative representations.

All four reviewers agree the paper is well‑written and that analyzing the role of normalization is a welcome goal. However, the majority (scores 1,1,1) find the work severely lacking in experimental support, novelty, and theoretical convincingness, while the more positive reviewer (score 3) also raises major concerns about assumptions and real‑world relevance.

### Strengths

- The paper aims to theoretically clarify why normalization matters in Barlow Twins, a component often treated as an implementation detail.
- It provides a clear explanation of the proposed theoretical perspective (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The insight that Barlow Twins does **not** work when used with a non‑normalized cross‑correlation matrix is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- The underlying analysis of feature‑contrastive methods tackles an important and under‑explored area (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **No experimental validation.** The paper provides no empirical study to support its claims. Key missing experiments include: how often does the model collapse without normalization in practice? Are there alternative ways beyond simple normalization? (Reviewers 2,3,4; echoed by Reviewer 1’s request to reconcile with BT’s Table 5 where raw cross‑correlation still yields 53.7% on ImageNet).
- **Unrealistic theoretical assumptions.** The proof that the cross‑correlation matrix is symmetric (Equation 6) assumes that all possible pairs of augmented images are used. In practice, only a single pair is seen per batch, with **T^A ≠ T^B** almost surely; symmetry is not guaranteed, and trivial solutions may not arise in training (Reviewer 1).
- **Trivial trace property.** Equation (8) is an immediate consequence of **Tr(AB) = Tr(BA)**—it holds regardless of normalization. The trace simply measures the similarity of augmented pairs, and normalization is only tied to the target of 1 in the BT loss (Reviewer 1).
- **Existing ablations already cover the finding.** The Barlow Twins paper (Table 5) already demonstrates that different normalization choices are all effective, and the same insight appears in prior work (Garrido et al., ICLR 2023, Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.4.1). The conclusion “Without normalization, the BT loss would not yield meaningful solutions” (≈line 247) is too strong and should be nuanced (Reviewer 1).
- **Paper addresses a non‑existent problem** (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewers 3,4). Barlow Twins is an established method whose formulation already uses Pearson correlation (normalized) – differentiating “cross‑correlation” from the actual implementation is a semantic nuance with no practical relevance.
- **Low novelty.** Roughly 50% of the paper consists of abstract, introduction, and the description of Barlow Twins. No new experiments, baselines, or downstream tasks are presented (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete / too short.** At 5 pages the paper feels unfinished. It does not convincingly prove the effectiveness of the theoretical framework, nor does it deliver actionable insights for improving BT or other SSL methods (e.g., VicReg variants) (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Confusing theoretical exposition** (Reviewer 4). Section 3 does not clearly explain **how** normalization influences the dynamics of the BT objective or why it is crucial.
- **Missing reference / justification** for the claim that an MLP projector can diagonalize outputs of a random backbone (line 186‑187; Reviewer 1).
- **Baseline comparison.** Barlow Twins raw off‑diagonal loss still achieves 53.7% on ImageNet – far from trivial. The paper’s Section 2 arguments should be reconciled with this empirical evidence (Reviewer 1).
- **Common intuition** that normalization prevents blow‑up or degenerate norms is well known; the paper does not distinguish its mechanism from that standard view (Reviewer 1, citing Garrido et al.).
