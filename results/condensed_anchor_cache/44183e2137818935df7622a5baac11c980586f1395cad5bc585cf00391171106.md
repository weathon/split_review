## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FastTF, a lightweight long-term time series forecasting model operating in the time-frequency domain. Its architecture combines patch-wise downsampling (weight sharing), a Sparse Frequency Mixer (SFM) that captures correlations between frequency points, and a patch predictor to model temporal variations. The design draws on the Nyquist sampling theorem and spectral properties to achieve extreme parameter reduction (as low as 4 parameters on ETTh1). FastTF is evaluated on up to five public datasets, compared against multiple baselines (Transformers, CNNs, MLPs), and deployed on an FPGA (Zynq UltraScale+ RFSoC ZCU208) to demonstrate low resource usage. Reviewers are split: two rate it highly (5) praising novelty and thoroughness, two rate it moderately (3) noting promise but significant concerns, and one rates it low (1) questioning dataset dependency and missing analysis.

### Strengths
- FastTF is an extremely lightweight model (as few as 4 parameters) that can be deployed on resource-constrained devices, with some configurations using 46,400× fewer parameters than certain baselines.
- The combination of global frequency-domain information and compressibility is well-motivated and novel—extreme compression while maintaining competitive performance.
- Architectural choices (e.g., SFM) are motivated by empirical observations of weight matrix sparseness, adding theoretical grounding.
- Experiments are fairly extensive: ablation studies, hyperparameter analyses, and FPGA deployment validate practicality and robustness.
- The paper includes honest reporting of failure cases (e.g., Figures 15 and 16 in the appendix) and a detailed analysis of algorithm complexity and resource usage.
- Comparisons cover many baseline families (Transformer, CNN, MLP); the model achieves competitive or SOTA performance on several datasets.
- (Minority positive reviewers 4 and 5 particularly highlight the novelty and thorough experimental validation.)

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset dependency and limited datasets**: The headline 4-parameter result only holds for ETTh1; other datasets require orders-of-magnitude more parameters (e.g., 1928 for ETTh2, 4329 for Electricity). This variation is not explained, and no analysis is given of what dataset characteristics (e.g., periodicity, stationarity) lead to optimal performance.
- **Limited evaluation**: Only four datasets appear in the main results; the appendix adds only three more, which is insufficient to demonstrate generalizability. The model underperforms on Electricity and Traffic compared to several SOTA methods, and the reasons for this gap are not explored.
- **Patch size dependency**: Patch size \(P\) is always an integral multiple of the fundamental frequency (e.g., 24h for ETTh). It is unclear whether the strong performance stems from this stack-by-frequency patching or from the architecture itself. A stress test with non-multiple patch sizes (to test spectral-leakage handling) is missing.
- **No statistical variation**: The paper does not report metrics across multiple random seeds, raising the possibility of cherry-picked results.
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - Lightweight approaches SparseTSF and FITS (arXiv:2307.03756) are not included in the experiments. A detailed comparison with FITS is needed to highlight FastTF’s contribution.
  - TimeMixer appears in Table 3 but is missing from Table 2 without explanation; consistency across all datasets is required.
- **Practical motivation**: The justification for extreme parameter reduction (4 parameters) is weak given modern hardware capabilities and potential accuracy trade-offs. The authors should provide compelling use cases beyond the generic edge-device argument.
- **Case study issue (Section 5.4)**: The demonstration on ETTh1 using only local means does not leverage the Fourier representation or SFM; it is a finding about the dataset, not the architecture, and should not be in the main text.
- **Reproducibility concerns**: The reported performance for PatchTST differs significantly from the original; the authors claim a single `drop_last` bug causes this, but this explanation is questioned.
- **Related work gaps**: The paper omits relevant works such as RobustPeriod (Wen et al. 2021), the Transformers in Time Series survey (Wen et al. 2023), and ReCycle (Weyrauch et al. 2024).
- **Ambiguities and errors**:
  - The description of the Exchange dataset in Appendix A.5 appears mixed up with another dataset.
  - "Division into patches" and "downsampling" are used interchangeably; only one term should be used.
  - Parameter counts (Section 5.3) are given for complex degrees of freedom; for fair comparison with real-valued models, the number of real parameters should be reported (factor of 2).
  - Error magnitudes imply metrics are still normalized; either provide denormalized metrics or explicitly state normalization.
  - Transformers section: description of Informer/Autoformer/FEDformer is inaccurate and non-informative.
  - Use of "Theorem" (Theorems 1 and 2) is inappropriate—Theorem 1 is not novel, Theorem 2 is a simple observation.
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Tables need centering; figure colors should be legible in black/white; font sizes are too small; lime green highlighting in tables is distracting.
  - The quote at the beginning of the text seems out of place for a conference paper.
  - Grammar: "Natual Correlation" (should be "Natural"), inconsistent capitalization.
- **Missing analyses**:
  - Provide baseline hyperparameter search results (e.g., e_layers, d_models, n_heads) to contextualize the trade-off between performance and efficiency.
  - Report energy consumption during training: does faster convergence of FastTF translate into energy savings?
  - Test on out-of-distribution data with strong noise or shifts, since the model may only learn a single template pattern shifted by local mean (as hinted by the ETTh1 result).
