## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a targeted behavior manipulation attack (BIA) and a defense (TDRT) for deep reinforcement learning. BIA uses imitation learning to force a victim policy toward a target behavior in black-box/no-box settings without environment heuristics. TDRT applies time-discounted regularization to improve robustness against such attacks. Experiments are conducted on MetaWorld and MuJoCo. Reviewers generally find the problem timely, the dual attack/defense contribution original, and the behavioral imitation formulation novel. However, concerns are raised about insufficient motivation distinguishing behavior manipulation from reward-minimization, missing baselines and related work, limited theoretical guarantees, questionable generalizability of the defense, and mixed or unclear empirical results.

### Strengths

*   **Novel problem and complete contribution:** The paper is the first to formalize and solve behavior-manipulating attacks in DRL, providing both a black-box/no-box targeted attack (BIA) and a dedicated defense (TDRT). It studies adversarial and robust angles, making a thorough contribution.
*   **Original attack formulation:** BIA shifts from action matching to overall behavioral imitation, which enables effective attacks even when perfect action matching is impossible under limited intervention.
*   **Theoretical insight for defense:** The paper identifies the critical role of early-trajectory stability and action-state sensitivity in defending against behavior-targeted attacks, motivating the time-discounted regularization.
*   **Clarity and simplicity:** The paper is considered well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 4). The proposed methods are seen as simple and easy to implement, which adds to practical value (Reviewer 1).
*   **Promising baseline comparison:** Baselines are reasonably chosen, and the attack and defense show strong improvements over compared methods (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 also notes the novelty of the problem setup and theoretical contributions.
*   **Potential broader impact:** The area of adversarial manipulation and defense for RL is niche but important; advancements could transfer to other RL robustness problems (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

*   **Unclear real-world motivation and distinction from reward-minimization:** The claim that behavior manipulation differs from reward-minimization is not convincingly justified. Reviewers question how such attacks would happen in the real world without reducing reward in practice. Concrete examples and evidence that behavior manipulation already threatens systems are missing (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The relationship to classic reward-minimization attacks needs formal demarcation (Reviewer 3).
*   **Missing challenges explanation:** The paper does not explicitly clarify why the problem is hard or what the key challenges are (Reviewer 1).
*   **Weak theoretical guarantees:** The theoretical analysis provides insight but lacks guarantees on the defense algorithm (Reviewer 1). In contrast, other reviewers view the theoretical contributions as a strength (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
*   **Unconvincing baseline attack results:** The PGD attack performs almost identically to a random attack. More details on its implementation and tuning effort are needed; a deeper investigation is required rather than a simple explanation (Reviewer 1).
*   **Attack setting not clearly stated:** The paper needs to clarify that the attack occurs during testing time (no training during attack) and should discuss related training-time DRL attacks to complete the related work (Reviewer 1).
*   **No performance gap between BIA variants:** BIA-ILfD and BIA-ILfO yield nearly identical performance in relatively easy MetaWorld tasks, contradicting the established understanding that ILfO is more challenging than ILfD. This weakens empirical validation and raises concerns about the method's ability to capture nuances of different imitation learning paradigms (Reviewer 2).
*   **Missing analysis of demonstration quantity/quality and expert requirement:** The paper does not study how the number and quality of demonstrations affect attack performance. Using 20 demonstrations for MetaWorld tasks seems excessive. The assumption of requiring expert-level demonstrations severely limits practical applicability and is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
*   **Lack of limitations and efficiency analysis:** No analysis of computational cost, scalability, or the amount of trajectory needed for a successful attack is provided (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4 Q4).
*   **Overlooked related work and missing baselines:** The paper overlooks recent defense work (Liang et al., ICLR 2024) and other defense methods ([1], [2], [3]) that should be compared against TDRT. For attacks, the PA-AD component of [3] should be compared to BIA-ILfD/O (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
*   **Empirical presentation issues:** Table 1 is confusing: attack rewards appear higher-is-better but boldface scores are lower than the target reward, inconsistent with Table 2 (lower-is-better). The description of experimental tasks (Section 7.1) lacks details on reward distribution for each goal and how it correlates to agent performance (Reviewer 3).
*   **Unclear adaptation of untargeted defenses:** The paper uses untargeted defense methods as baselines without explaining how they are adapted to the targeted setting (e.g., is the target reward used for PA-ATLA?). The selection process for the target policy $\pi_{tgt}$ is not formalized; the only well-defined $\pi_{tgt}$ appears to be reward-minimizing, which is the existing attack paradigm (Reviewer 3 Q2, Q4).
*   **Simpler optimization than claimed:** Since the victim policy $\pi$ is fixed, the optimization in equations 8 and 11 may be solvable directly, making the problem simpler than if $\pi$ were dynamic (Reviewer 3 Q3).
*   **Limited generalizability of TDRT:** The defense's effectiveness heavily depends on critical actions occurring early in the episode. If crucial actions happen later, time-discounted regularization may provide insufficient protection. Applicability to non-episodic or infinite-horizon problems is unclear (Reviewer 4).
*   **Mixed and limited experimental results:** Main paper results are convincing but limited. MuJoCo results in the appendix are mixed, weakening claims of general robustness. The method's effectiveness may be highly sensitive to environment characteristics (Reviewer 4). Additional results on more MetaWorld/MuJoCo tasks would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 4 Q7).
*   **Unclear definition of clean rewards and worst-case adversarial policy:** The defense results section uses "clean rewards" representing the victim trained to achieve the adversarial task without attacks, but it should compare nominal task performance. In Equation 12, the "worst-case adversarial policy" is not defined, and it seems to contradict the attack premise of steering toward a target behavior rather than simply minimizing reward (Reviewer 4 Q5, Q6).
*   **Minor writing issues:** $\hat{p}$ is defined in Eq. 10 but unused in Eq. 9; misspellings such as "Notaion" header (Reviewer 3).
