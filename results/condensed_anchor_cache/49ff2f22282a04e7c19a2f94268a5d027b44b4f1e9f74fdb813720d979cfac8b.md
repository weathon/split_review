## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers describe Bitune as a method to add bidirectional attention to decoder-only LLMs by learning new Key-Value matrices (via sparse LoRA adaptors) that are used without causal masking. The prompt is processed twice—once with the original causal weights and once with the new bidirectional weights—and the resulting KV caches are mixed using a learnable weight. This mixed cache is then used for autoregressive generation. Evaluation on instruction-tuning and question-answering tasks (commonsense reasoning, arithmetic, language understanding) shows gains over baselines. Extensive ablation studies analyze the contribution of each component.

### Strengths
- The idea is simple but effective, addressing an innate limitation of decoder-only LLMs while preserving generation efficiency.
- Experiments are comprehensive, covering various LLMs (multiple architectures) and multiple downstream benchmarks; concrete performance gains are shown in Tables 1 and 2 compared to baseline pretrained and LoRA fine-tuned models.
- The paper includes a large number of ablations that are very convincing of the efficacy: each time a reviewer considered an alternative explanation (e.g., effect of more parameters, fusion effect, different initialization), the authors had a corresponding ablation covering that possibility.
- The ablation study helps identify the contribution of different design choices (e.g., necessity of the bidirectional weights, fusion mechanism, mixing ratio). Reasonable baselines are included.
- The paper discusses the computational cost of the two-pass inference, provides a reasonable solution using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), and highlights that Bitune is not tied to a specific weight-updating method.
- The computational cost analysis (Appendix 6.4) shows that while training time increases more noticeably, inference time increases to a much lesser extent for a fixed 200-token generation.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient differentiation from prior work:** Several reviewers note that prefix language models (e.g., U-PaLM, UniLM) already use bidirectional attention for processing prefixes. The core difference of Bitune—fusing causal and bidirectional features—is seen as an incremental change rather than a fundamentally new approach. One reviewer (R3, score 5) is particularly critical, stating this may not warrant significant attention. The paper does not adequately discuss how Bitune differs from these models or include experiments comparing instruction tuning on prefix LMs versus bidirectional instruction tuning after causal pretraining. A more detailed analysis of the advantages over existing methods is requested.
- **Missing analysis of why causal attention is still limiting after extensive pretraining:** One reviewer questions the argument that causal attention prevents the model from “seeing” the full context, given that LLMs have been trained extensively on causal modeling. Concrete examples or a hidden-state analysis showing where causal attention fails to capture necessary context would strengthen the motivation.
- **Lack of multi-turn/chat evaluation and practical concerns:** Experiments focus on single-turn input/output tasks. Given that many LLMs are used for chat or dialogue, reviewers (R2, R3) note missing experiments on multi-turn settings. Questions include: Does the effect of initial bidirectional attention decrease over longer chat histories? Does it help model follow the initial prompt better? Can the approach be applied to multi-turn dialogue (e.g., only user text receives bidirectional attention, while model-generated text stays causal)? One reviewer (R3) further points out that the need to recompute the KV-cache for each new turn makes the method less practical for long conversational histories, and the computational overhead may outweigh benefits. A scalability analysis for longer sequences is also missing; one reviewer (R4) specifically asks how inference time scales for generation lengths beyond 200 tokens (e.g., 2000 tokens).
- **Missing deep dive on mixing ratio initialization:** Figure 2 shows that the initial value of the mixing ratio causes large differences in the final average ratio, and Table 6 provides some experiments, but the scale is small and results are inconclusive. A deeper investigation into how this hyperparameter affects performance is desired.
- **Lack of visualization of bidirectional attention:** One reviewer suggests visualizing attention maps on examples to highlight cases where bidirectional attention behaves differently from causal attention and brings benefit.
- **Missing evaluation on pure generation tasks:** A reviewer asks whether bidirectional instruction tuning affects quality for tasks like open-ended text generation.
- **Missing experiments on alternative training objectives and data requirements:** A reviewer suggests training the Bitune attention matrices on a prefix language modeling task (randomly splitting sequences) and asks how much data is needed to learn the matrices—this is mentioned but not answered.
- **One-pass inference overhead:** Two inference passes are required per prompt, incurring additional time and memory costs. While the paper acknowledges this, the time analysis is limited to a fixed generation length. (Reviewer 4 notes this may be acceptable if the performance gain justifies it, but scaling is uncertain.)
