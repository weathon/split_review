## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to reduce the bit-width of accumulators in DNN inference, specifically using 12-bit floating-point accumulation with minimal accuracy loss. The method involves ignoring underflow effects ("floor" rounding) and applying scaling via chunked summation. Experiments on ResNet (ImageNet) and BERT (SQuAD) show effectiveness. The paper also explores low-bitwidth accumulation during training using modified straight-through estimators (STE). Most reviewers find the problem timely and the approach novel.

### Strengths
- Well-written and well-organized (R1).
- Unique backpropagation through quantized accumulation, not studied before (R1).
- Solid empirical results on diverse benchmarks: ResNet models on ImageNet and BERT on SQuAD (R1, R4).
- Reducing accumulator bitwidth is a practical way to further reduce matmul cost (R2).
- Experimental design in Section 3.1 is straightforward and appears easy to reproduce (R2).
- Quantization optimization of accumulators is an interesting, overlooked viewpoint (R3).
- STE discussion in Section 4 is original and achieves better results in backpropagation of accumulated errors (R3).
- Good presentation pinpointing the accumulation problem in GEMM (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of hardware analysis**: No estimation of gate count, computational energy, latency, power, or silicon area improvements compared to FP16/BF16 accumulators (R2, R3). Without concrete hardware evaluation, the practical value is unclear (R2, R3). The overhead of additional logic for quantization and potential benefits in memory bandwidth/complexity are not quantified (R3).
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis**: The solution in Section 3 is too qualitative (e.g., "hard to escape" underflow region) and lacks mathematical formalism (R4). The paper does not provide a rigorous framework to evaluate error propagation and accuracy requirements for low-bit accumulators (R3). The choice of exponent bias offset (\(b_{acc}=b_{prod}-\frac{1}{2}\log_2(\text{chunk size})\)) is not formally justified and does not guarantee overflow prevention under all conditions (R3). The impact of accumulation distribution (e.g., normal) on performance is not explored (R1).
- **Missing comparison with related works and alternative quantization schemes**: No detailed comparison with prior work Wrapnet (key difference unclear) (R2). No comparison of computational complexity or quantization overhead with previous works [1,2,3] (R1). Integer/fixed-point quantization is mentioned in Section 2.2 but not evaluated in the main experiments; a comparison with fixed-point methods at the same hardware cost is needed to justify the choice of 12-bit floating-point (R2, R3). The paper does not address the practical challenges of integrating a non-standard 12-bit FP accumulator into existing hardware ecosystems (R3).
- **Limited scope**: The method only applies to forward pass accumulations; accumulations in backpropagation GEMMs are not addressed (R4). Benchmarks are limited to ResNets and BERTs; experiments on larger models such as GPTs are missing (R4). The impact of chunk size on reducing bit-width needs further exploration (R1).
- **Training with low-bit accumulators**: The proposed STE method may be impractical because it requires unfolding all FMA operations in sequence, conflicting with highly optimized parallel GEMM (R3). Hyperparameter choices for two-stage fine-tuning (e.g., 10 epochs, learning rate) lack justification (R3).
- **Additional concerns/suggestions**: Whether overflows can be allowed in a controlled manner (like magnitude clipping) to further lower complexity while maintaining accuracy (R4). The distribution of accumulations could affect quantization performance (R1). Note: Reviewer 2 (score 5) was less confident and raised stronger concerns about the lack of hardware evaluation and related-work context compared to the other reviewers (scores 6).
