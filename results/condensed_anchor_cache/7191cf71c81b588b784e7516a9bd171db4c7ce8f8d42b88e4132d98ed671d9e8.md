## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses expert-level sparsification in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. It proposes the MoE Experts Compression Suite (MC-Suite), a collection of expert importance estimation criteria spanning weight-guided, inference behavior, activation-guided, and gradient-guided perspectives. The authors also introduce iterative pruning with task-agnostic fine-tuning (termed MoE Lottery Subnetworks) and investigate the impact of expert pruning on model capabilities, finding that instruction-following is predominantly affected. The evaluation is primarily on Mixtral 8x7B models.

### Strengths

- Addresses the practical challenge of reducing memory requirements in MoE models.
- Proposes a variety of new expert pruning criteria (part of MC-Suite) and provides a comprehensive empirical study comparing many criteria on Mixtral 8x7B Base.
- The MoE Lottery Pruning method (iterative pruning with task-agnostic fine-tuning) is straightforward and shows empirical effectiveness.
- Provides valuable empirical insights about expert characteristics, particularly that instruction-following capabilities are most harmed during expert pruning.
- Experimental breadth includes evaluation across multiple tasks and detailed ablation studies examining different components.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing experimental details for reproducibility:**
  - The dataset used for task-agnostic fine-tuning is not clearly stated (appendix suggests C4 validation set, but ambiguity remains).
  - It is unclear whether Table 3 and Figure 6 experiments use the base or instruct model.
  - The instruction-tuning datasets for Figure 6 supervised fine-tuning are not specified (asked if those listed in Table 6 of appendix and if models are fine-tuned on training splits).
  - The source of the comparison in line 412 (between Table 1 and Table 2) is ambiguous regarding which model is used.
  - Key hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, batch size) and computational infrastructure are missing.
  - Calibration process details for different criteria are insufficient.
- **Limited empirical validation:**
  - The most effective criteria identification (e.g., Table 2) is conducted only on a single pre-trained MoE model (Mixtral 8x7B Base), undermining generalizability.
  - Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art expert pruning methods such as ExpertPrune (Lu et al., 2024), DynamicMoE (Huang et al., 2024), and SEER-MoE.
  - No evaluation on larger MoE models (>8x7B parameters) or examination of zero-shot generalization, load balancing, or changes in expert specialization patterns.
  - Task-agnostic fine-tuning effectiveness for instruct models: perplexity reported on C4 for an instruction-tuned model may be unfair if the fine-tuning used C4; downstream performance for instruct models is not demonstrated (e.g., similar to Table 3 or with in-context learning).
- **Methodological justification and novelty:**
  - Most criteria in MC-Suite lack originality and theoretical grounding. EWS, EAN, and others are direct adaptations from traditional pruning without convincing evidence for expert importance. Activation-based (EAS, EAE) and weight-based (EWS, RWN) metrics have unclear connection to expert importance; gradient-based measures introduce overhead without clear benefits.
  - Iterative pruning–retraining is not a new technique (known from lottery ticket hypothesis), and its application here may be straightforward. The authors did not clearly differentiate from prior iterative pruning or discuss unique challenges in the MoE context, such as suboptimal routers and pruned experts.
  - The choice of iterative pruning (k rounds) over one-shot is not theoretically motivated; pruning schedule (e.g., 32 experts per round) appears arbitrary.
  - The linear combination assumption in reconstruction compensation oversimplifies complex expert relationships.
- **Issues with iterative pruning and fine-tuning:**
  - No stopping criteria for iterative pruning.
  - Task-agnostic fine-tuning may harm expert specialization and cause catastrophic forgetting; no measures are discussed to prevent it.
  - The relationship between pruning rounds and fine-tuning steps is not justified.
  - How iterative pruning affects the router's ability to dispatch tokens effectively is not examined.
- **Presentation and clarity problems:**
  - Figures: Figure 1 has poor resolution and lacks a proper caption; Figure 2 has inconsistent fonts and sizes; Figure 4 contains too much content with poor organization.
  - Writing is verbose and hard to read; concepts like "MoE Lottery Subnetworks" have vague meanings.
  - The methodology section (Section 2) is scattered and lacks clear separation between methodology and experiments; equations 3-18 lack explanations.
  - No dedicated Related Work section in the main body; the introduction is long and unfocused.
  - Language errors: typo "idetified" (should be "identified"), misplaced comma, missing articles ("by gating function" should be "by the gating function"), missing punctuation after equations 1-18.
- **Other specific concerns:**
  - Expert pruning criteria only support independent layer pruning; non-uniform dropping per layer (global estimation) leads to poorer performance (line 375).
  - It is unclear if the claim about expert usage frequency (EUF) being insensitive to calibration dataset (line 216) applies to other criteria (gradient/activation based) and what evidence supports it.
  - How to handle cases where different criteria give conflicting importance scores is not addressed.
  - Computational overhead of iterative pruning and fine-tuning is not reported.
- **Notable disagreement between reviewers:** Reviewer 3 was more critical, rating the paper lower (score 3) and particularly questioning methodological foundations, novelty, and experimental completeness, while Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 5) found the approach promising but with significant concerns about details and comparisons.
