## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes STD-Former, a spatio-temporal (mislabeled "diffusion") transformer for video action recognition on Something Something V1/V2. It uses a two-branch network (spatiotemporal and temporal) with several plug-and-play modules: Parallel Transformer Module (PTM), Cross Transformer Module (CTM), Spatiotemporal Diffusion Module (SDM), and Salient Motion Excitation Module (SMEM). Experiments show small improvements, but reviewers find the approach conceptually flawed, poorly motivated, lacking novelty (most blocks already published), and insufficiently benchmarked.

### Strengths
- Experiments on Something Something V1 and V2 show effectiveness of proposed modules (Reviewer 1; note: this is a minor strength, as other reviewers find results insignificant).
- Idea is simple and straightforward (Reviewer 2; but unclear motivation undermines simplicity).
- Applying diffusion model for action recognition is a new try (Reviewer 2; however, the "diffusion" term is misused – Reviewer 1, 2, 3 all agree the method has no connection to diffusion; it is essentially factorized 3D convolution).
- The writing is neat (Reviewer 3; contrasted by Reviewer 2 who finds writing poor and motivation unclear).

### Weaknesses
1. **Misuse/absence of diffusion concept**: The title and paper claim a "Spatio-temporal Diffusion Transformer" but no actual diffusion process (e.g., denoising, forward/reverse) is used. Reviewers suggest "Fusion" would be more appropriate. The "diffusion" module is merely a factorized 3D convolution (citing Tran et al., CVP 2018). The module does not make a significant performance difference (Table 2), so the title is misleading.
2. **Outdated paradigm and lack of novelty**:
   - The approach of designing hand-crafted spatial-temporal blocks for action recognition is pre-2021 (e.g., TDN). Modern paradigms use general visual models (CLIP, large multi-modal) with parameter-efficient tuning (e.g., ST-Adapter).
   - Each proposed block already exists in prior art:
     - PTM (mixed conv + attention) → Pan et al., CVPR 2022.
     - CTM (cross transformer) → Chen et al., ICCV 2021 (CrossViT).
     - SDM (factorized 3D conv) → Tran et al., CVPR 2018.
     - SMEM (motion excitation from adjacent frames) → Wang et al., CVPR 2020 (Correlation networks) and Li et al., CVPR 2020 (TEA).
   - No new insight or unique contribution beyond trivial engineering combinations.
3. **Unclear motivation and writing quality**:
   - Abstract claims "high similarity of video backgrounds and long time span" are challenges, but existing methods are not discussed nor shortcomings analyzed. No explanation of how the proposed design solves these issues.
   - Introduction reads like a Related Work; no formulation of problem or motivation from L82 onward.
   - Method: Why two branches? What is the difference between "video features" and "action features"? Are spatiotemporal and temporal features redundant? No ablation study on branch redundancy.
   - Why 12 modules in PTM? Is it a hyperparameter? No ablation.
   - SMEM motivation and importance are unclear.
   - Figures and tables lack detailed captions, making reading difficult (Reviewer 2).
4. **Insufficient experiments**:
   - Ablation results (Tables 2, 4) show negligible differences between module inclusion/exclusion, undercutting necessity of each component.
   - Models tested only on Something Something V1/V2; no evaluation on large-scale, challenging datasets (e.g., Kinetics700). This limits generalizability and comparison to modern works.
   - Performance is subpar (Reviewer 3; no explicit numbers given in reviews, but statement stands).
5. **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (retained as weaknesses):
   - Why stick to outdated "network block" game when modern paradigms (CLIP, ST-Adapter) exist?
   - Clearly highlight any unique contributions over existing methods (e.g., difference from CrossViT, TEA).
   - Why not benchmark on Kinetics700?
