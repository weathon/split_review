## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a concept bottleneck (CB) layer into generative models (GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) to constrain the generative latent to human-understandable concepts. Key novelty: an “unknown” concept embedding, trained to be orthogonal to known concepts, acts as a side channel for preserved information. The CB layer is trained with a concept loss and orthogonality loss, enabling steering by controlling concept presence/absence probabilities and interpretability by inspecting those probabilities. Experiments on several datasets with ground-truth concepts show steerability improvements over InfoGAN and other baselines, and limited demonstrations of debugging and generation quality.

### Strengths
- **Important and under-explored problem**: Interpreting generative models is a significant challenge, and the paper addresses it with a structured approach (R1, R3, R4).
- **Novelty of the unknown-concept and integration with generative models**: The idea of an orthogonal unknown embedding to capture residual information is interesting and novel; applying concept bottlenecks to generation (not just classification) is a clear gap filled (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Model-agnostic applicability**: The CB layer is demonstrated on three diverse families (VAEs, GANs, diffusion models), showing generality (R1, R2, R3).
- **Partial scalability evidence**: Experiments on a LAION subset with a diffusion model follow contemporary practices (R1).
- **Better quantitative steerability**: CB-GAN achieves higher concept classifier accuracy than InfoGAN and other baselines (Table 1); in some cases the improvement is 10× (R2).
- **Clear writing and good motivation**: The paper is largely well-structured and enjoyable to read (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Claim of model agnosticism is not fully validated**: Applying the CB layer to normalizing flows or invertible models would be non-trivial (R1). More modern generative architectures (e.g., StyleGAN-2) are not tested; recent GAN inversion methods are missing as baselines (R1, R2). For steering, comparisons to GANSpace, StyleGAN direction-based control, and text-image diffusion (e.g., Stable Diffusion) are absent (R2).
- **Heavy reliance on dense concept annotations**: The model requires ground-truth concept labels for all concepts, which is impractical for large-scale datasets; this limits real-world usefulness (R3, R4). Scalability with the number of concepts is not studied—each concept needs a separate network, raising concerns about resource cost (R3). Performance of the generative model as the number of concepts grows is unexamined (R3).
- **Supervised nature limits concept discovery**: The model can only represent concepts provided through labels; unsupervised discovery of unknown underlying factors is not possible (R3). Whether the method can handle unseen (open-world) concepts is unclear (R2).
- **Experimental evaluation is weak and incomplete**:
    - Table 1, the main quantitative result, is poorly explained. Steerability is measured as the accuracy of a pre-trained concept detector after turning a concept *on*, but never *off* (R4). The accuracy of the concept detectors themselves is not reported, so their reliability is unknown (R3, R4). The absolute accuracies are far from 100%, raising questions about the practical effectiveness of steering (R3, R4).
    - Interpretability is assessed only qualitatively (Section 4.3.1, Figure 4) (R4).
    - Generation quality (FID) is measured on only one dataset (R4).
    - Debugging is shown only for a single example (R4).
    - Overall, the choice of research questions is sensible, but each experiment lacks breadth or depth, making it hard to assess true limitations (R4). More datasets and systematic ablations are needed (R4).
- **Concept orthogonality and the unknown concept**: The orthogonality loss (Eq. 5) is written confusingly (index j unused) (R1). Minimal orthogonality is crucial to avoid overlap between known and unknown concepts, yet no ablation checks whether strictly projecting the unknown vector to be orthogonal (e.g., via a penalty) harms performance—or whether such a procedure could be integrated during training to guarantee orthogonality (R1). The role of the unknown concept in generation is not explored (e.g., what happens when steering it to “presence”?) (R3).
- **Missing training details**: The paper mentions Platt & Barr (1987) for hyperparameter optimization but does not describe its use (R1). All models are presumably trained from scratch; training time for the diffusion model is not reported (R2).
- **Limited justification relative to alternatives**: While the paper argues that VLM-based steering (e.g., via CLIP) can already provide concept-level control, it does not clearly position CBGMs against such approaches, which also avoid the need for human annotations (R4).
