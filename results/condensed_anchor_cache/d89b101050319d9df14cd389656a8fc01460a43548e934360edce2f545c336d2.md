- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 8, 8, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
This work extends the path-norm toolkit to general DAG ReLU networks with biases, skip connections, and any operation based on order statistics (max pooling, GroupSort, etc.). The embedding from “Stock & Gribonval, 2022” is generalized, using the fact that such networks are locally affine. The paper provides generalization bounds that recover or beat the sharpest previous path-norm based bounds. New peeling and contraction lemmas for Rademacher complexity are proved. The toolkit is applied numerically on pre-trained ResNets of various sizes trained on ImageNet. Experimental results show that the bound is tight and meaningful when the network is sparse, but vacuous (L¹ path-norms ≥ 10³⁰) for deep dense networks. Despite this drawback, the work takes theory a step closer to practice (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).

### Strengths
- The paper is very well written, with accessible proofs, consistent notation, and precise mathematical statements (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The contribution is important for understanding generalization bounds (Reviewer 1).
- The result is elegant, unifying several previous path-norm results. The core new peeling argument relating Rademacher complexity over a set of nodes to that of its incoming nodes is a key contribution (Reviewer 2).
- The theoretical bound is applied to real architectures and datasets (ResNets on ImageNet) for the first time. The observation that sparse networks greatly reduce the bound may guide future research (Reviewer 2).
- The new peeling and contraction lemmas for computing Rademacher complexities for more complex networks are novel and interesting (Reviewer 3).
- The notion of path-norms introduced handles very general ReLU networks (biases, skip connections, max pooling), and the generalization bounds improve or recover existing path-norm based bounds (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Numerical experiments are partial; a comparison of the theoretical bound with an empirical estimate of generalization using a validation dataset would be expected (Reviewer 1).
- The “Proof of the Lipschitz Property” (page 14) ends with an incorrect statement: the boundaries of the constant regions must be examined, and the proof holds only because there is a finite number of such regions (Reviewer 1).
- Lemma C.1, Setting 1, can be obtained by a simple application of the well-known scalar contraction inequality (Ledoux & Talagrand, 1991) and is therefore not a new result (Reviewer 2).
- The relevance of the 2‑norm of Φ(θ) is questioned: it could be small simply because the number of paths is huge; it does not seem to be a valid generalization bound even for a simple two‑layer linear network, and there is no real evidence that this quantity indicates generalization (Reviewer 2).
- Minor typos: (i) \(\overline N_{in}\) is not defined in Appendix A; (ii) the third row of the first equation on page 29 is missing a factor of \(5^d\) (Reviewer 2).
- Linear activations can be “ignored” by linking incoming nodes of a current node directly to its outgoing nodes: this would not change the path norm, and then one should count not the number of Conv layers but the maximum number of ReLU and max pooling activations along a path, which is smaller than the number of Conv layers in ResNets (Reviewer 2).
- The presentation could benefit from first stating the result without biases (making the notation much simpler) and then pointing out that biases are handled by appending a constant‑1 dimension; this would clearly explain why Theorem 3.1 uses \(d_{in}+1\) and the max in the definition of \(\sigma\) (Reviewer 2).
- The statement of Lemma D.2 is confusing because \(\Theta\) is not arbitrary but preprocessed by Algorithm 1; it would be clearer to define \(r\) as the sup of the 1‑norm of the last layer of \(\Theta\). A similar issue occurs for Lemma D.3 (Reviewer 2).
- Generalization bounds are proven only for \(L^1\) path‑norms, not for general \(L^p\) norms. In experiments, the \(L^1\) path‑norm is extremely large (\(\ge 10^{30}\)) for dense networks, making the bounds vacuous (Reviewer 3). (Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 acknowledge the vacuousness but view the experimental findings as interesting; Reviewer 3 considers the enormous magnitude a major weakness.)
- Can similar bounds be proved for general \(L^p\) path‑norms without incurring a large dimension dependence? (Reviewer 3, overlaps with the criticism of only \(L^1\) being considered.)
- (Minor) The measurable condition in Lemma D.1 appears unnecessary (Reviewer 2).
