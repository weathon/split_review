## Merged Review

### Summary

SSLAM proposes a self-supervised learning (SSL) method for audio that incorporates synthetic audio mixtures during pre-training to improve performance on polyphonic soundscapes. It uses a two-stage masked latent bootstrapping framework with a student–teacher architecture, an element-wise maximum mixing inspired by the Ideal Binary Mask, and a source retention loss. Evaluated on standard benchmarks (AS-2M, AS-20K, ESC-50, KS1, KS2) and polyphonic datasets (SPASS, DCASE, URBAN-SED, DESED, etc.), the method achieves up to 3.9% mAP improvement on AS-2M (50.2 mAP) and up to 9.1% mAP on polyphonic datasets. Two reviewers rated it 8 (positive; well-written, effective, important) and two rated it 6 (more critical; novelty overlap with MixIT, missing baselines, limited task diversity, reliance on supervised teacher). The review contrast is preserved below.

### Strengths

- Addresses the important and under-explored challenge of polyphonic sound processing in self-supervised audio learning; real-world audio is typically multi-source and models trained on monophonic data may generalize poorly.
- The two-stage training approach (unmixed then partially mixed) and the source retention loss are well motivated by principles of auditory scene analysis (Ideal Binary Mask) and show effectiveness in ablations.
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on both standard audio SSL benchmarks and multiple polyphonic datasets; the 3.9% improvement on AS-2M (50.2 mAP) and up to 9.1% mAP gains on polyphonic datasets are convincing.
- Component-wise ablations and analysis of mixing/aggregation strategies provide insight into the contribution of each objective.
- The paper is generally well written, clear, and logically structured (R3: “clarity: paper is well written”; R4: “well-written, with clear explanations and logical flow”).
- The partial mixing strategy demonstrates a nuanced understanding of balancing novel polyphonic information with preservation of monophonic characteristics (R2: “nuanced understanding”).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and relation to MixIT**: The core idea (training on mixtures of mixtures) closely resembles the MixIT approach for unsupervised sound separation. The paper insufficiently justifies why this adaptation is novel within SSL representation learning; the distinction between learning representations of mixtures versus separating sources is not rigorously defined. ([R2])
- **Missing baseline**: No comparison with a straightforward baseline: applying an unsupervised sound separation method (e.g., MixIT) to separate mixed datasets, then feeding the separated sources to the self-distillation pipeline. Without this baseline it is unclear whether gains stem from the proposed synthetic mixing or simply from having cleaner separated inputs. ([R2])
- **Questionable “self-supervised” claim**: The teacher model is a pre-trained network that was trained on labeled data (e.g., AS-2M). This dependency undermines the fully self-supervised claim; the paper should acknowledge this and possibly use “teacher–student training” instead. ([R2]; R2’s question: “How many labeled audio clips were used to train the teacher model?” – overlaps with this criticism.)
- **Limited task diversity**: Evaluation is restricted to sound event detection (multi-label classification) and keyword spotting (speech). The paper does not explore other polyphonic domains such as multi-speaker recognition, instrument recognition, or full sound event detection with temporal localization. Performance on speech/music tasks beyond keyword spotting is not shown, limiting evidence of generalizability. ([R1, R2, R3])
- **No confidence intervals or standard deviations**: Results are reported without mean/std over multiple runs; it is unclear whether reported numbers are from a single test run. This is especially important for polyphonic evaluations where variance may be higher. ([R3])
- **Figure 4 quality**: The figure illustrating the mixing process is of extremely low quality and uses an unconventional orientation (high frequencies at the bottom), hindering understanding. Should be replaced with a lossless image with correct frequency axis. ([R2])
- **Inconsistent gains on URBAN-SED**: The method sometimes underperforms on URBAN-SED compared to other datasets. The paper does not systematically analyze why the mixing strategy hurts performance in some polyphonic scenarios (e.g., sensitivity to dataset characteristics or mixing parameters). ([R1]; R1’s question: “why mixing boosts performance in some cases but not others.”)
- **Baseline setup dominates results**: In Table 4, Stage 1 (unmixed audio) already achieves 40.2 mAP on AS-20K, matching or exceeding prior work (EAT: 40.2, ASiT: 38.6). This raises concern that the baseline itself (e.g., training schedule, hyperparameters, model size) may be responsible for improvements, not the proposed mixing objectives. ([R4])
- **Need for both unmixed and mixed objectives**: The final model uses a composite of 5 losses. In Tables 2–3, the MB-UA-PMA variant (unmixed + mixed objectives) does not significantly outperform MB-PMA (mixed only). The necessity of including the unmixed objectives is unclear; a more principled motivation for each loss component is needed. ([R3, R4]; R3’s question “Why does SSLAM require both unmixed and mixed versions?” and R4’s question “What would be the impact of using only mixed objectives and SRL?”)
- **Figure/text inconsistencies**: Figure 1(C) appears to show two teacher models, but the text says one teacher handles two inputs separately. Figure 2 shows layer averaging after the teacher, but Section 3.1.2 states that only the last layer (global loss) or all 12 layers (local loss) are used, inconsistent with the diagram. ([R4])
- **Writing errors**: Citation format issues in Tables 1, 7, 8. Numerical error in Table 1: A-JEPA score for ESC-50 listed as 06.3 instead of correct 96.3? (needs correction). ([R4])
- **Missing ablation variants in Table 1**: Table 1 does not include the intermediate variants MB-UA, MB-PMA, or MB-UA-PMA that are evaluated later, making it hard to compare with prior work. ([R3])
- **Inconsistent highlighting in Table 2**: The MB-PMA variant works best for IDMT DESED linear evaluation and SPASS-Market fine-tuning but is not highlighted. ([R3])
- **No evaluation on sound event detection with temporal localization**: The polyphonic datasets are treated as multi-label classification; the paper does not evaluate on SED tasks that require time-stamped predictions (e.g., with an RNN decoder). ([R4’s question])
