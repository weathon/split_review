## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ToRL, a topology-preserving representation learning model that captures object deformations from images by learning transformation groups in the latent space. A decoder with a smooth composition module preserves topological properties, and the method eliminates the need for a reference image during inference. Experiments on synthetic 2D images, real 3D brain MRI, 3D adrenal CT shapes, and real 2D facial expressions show improved classification performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. Reviewer scores are mixed (5,6,5,6), reflecting appreciation for the novel concept and practical advantage, but also substantial concerns about insufficient explanations, experimental gaps, factual errors, and lack of real-world applicability.

### Strengths

- The technical contribution is novel and well-formulated: learning explicit transformation groups in the latent space to preserve object topology, with a clear mathematical foundation (Reviewers 1,2,4). The approach is inspiring, essentially treating diffeomorphisms as stationary velocity fields (SVF) in the feature space (Reviewer 3).
- Eliminating the need for a reference image during inference is a practical improvement over existing methods (Reviewers 2,4).
- Experimental validation is comprehensive, covering multiple datasets (synthetic 2D, real 3D brain MRI, real 3D adrenal CT, real 2D facial expressions) with classification tasks and ablation studies (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The paper is well-written (Reviewer 1) and the ablation study is considered comprehensive (Reviewer 4).
- The decoder’s design with smooth transformations ensures structural continuity, addressing issues in traditional feature fusion methods (Reviewer 2,4).
- Reviewer 3 increased his score from 3 to 5 after the author response, acknowledging improved quality, though still noting persistent issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Mathematical and technical clarity:** The descriptions and proofs of formulas (2), (5), (6) are insufficient; readers cannot fully understand the design rationale (Reviewer 1). The connection between transformation groups and actual image deformations is not intuitive—visualizations showing how group operations affect the latent space and image changes would help (Reviewer 2). The paper spends too much space on background about diffeomorphisms without clearly highlighting the contributions, while the design of TGM (a key component) is barely mentioned (Reviewer 3). More intuitive explanations or diagrams are needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Factual error:** The paper incorrectly describes AdrenalMNIST3D as “3D Abdominal MRIs,” but it is a binary shape dataset derived from CT scans, not MRI. This raises concerns about other potential factual errors (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental gaps:**
    - No qualitative results are provided for the 3D Brains dataset (Reviewer 1).
    - No quantitative metrics for topology preservation (e.g., persistent homology measuring connected components, holes, handles) are reported, despite claims of superiority in that aspect (Reviewer 2).
    - The paper lacks a robustness analysis to input perturbations (noise, varying contrast, artifacts), critical for medical imaging applications (Reviewer 2).
    - Only classification tasks are demonstrated; no experiments on real applications related to diffeomorphisms (e.g., registration, shape matching) are performed, limiting assessment of topology-preservation capabilities (Reviewers 2,3). The classification tasks are considered overly simplistic (Reviewer 3).
    - Computational cost is not reported: parameter counts and FLOPS are not compared with state-of-the-art methods, nor are before/after comparisons for the smooth composition module given. Hence performance gains might stem from added parameters rather than the method itself (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also raises concerns about computational overhead for large-scale/real-time use.
- **Ablation and integration concerns:**
    - The individual contributions of different components (e.g., choice of transformation groups) are not analyzed; no ablation on using different Lie groups or other group types (Reviewer 2).
    - The custom decoder and latent transformation modules may make integration with standard architectures (e.g., ResNet, U-Net) difficult without extensive modification (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential overfitting:** The strong focus on topology preservation might lead to overfitting when topological consistency is not essential, reducing flexibility (Reviewer 4). Due to discrete errors in stationary velocity fields, the model does not theoretically guarantee a true diffeomorphism, yet no analysis of this discrepancy is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and structure:** The paper has poor writing structure and unclear contributions; irrelevant network architecture details (similar to U-Net, U-Net++) are overly emphasized while key parts like TGM lack description (Reviewer 3). The claim that “our approach can be easily applied to other parameterizations of diffeomorphisms” is unsupported (Reviewer 3).

*Note:* Reviewer 3 is notably more critical about the paper’s foundation and writing than other reviewers, even after raising his score; he maintains that the work is neither theoretical nor practical due to the artificial settings and lack of real-world validation. Other reviewers (2,4) are more positive but still share key concerns about clarity, evaluation, and generalizability.
