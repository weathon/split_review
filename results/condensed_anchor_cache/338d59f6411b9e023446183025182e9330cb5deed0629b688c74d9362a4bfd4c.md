## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detector (NCI) inspired by Neural Collapse (NC). Using observations that ID features cluster closer to weight vectors while OOD features reside near the origin, NCI combines feature–weight proximity (p-score: angle scaled by weight norm) with feature norms to filter OOD samples. Experiments on ResNet-18/50, ViT B/16, and Swin v2 across ImageNet and CIFAR-10 benchmarks show the method is lightweight (matching softmax latency) and performs comparably to prior SOTA.

### Strengths
1. **Sound motivation and unified perspective**. Linking OOD detection to Neural Collapse provides a coherent view that connects and extends prior methods (R1, R2, R3, R4). The theoretical inspiration is interesting even though partly explored (R4).
2. **Simple, efficient, and lightweight**. NCI matches the latency of vanilla softmax-confidence detectors (R1, R2, R3, R4). The method is easy to implement (R2, R3).
3. **Broad experimental validation**. Extensive evaluation across multiple architectures (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, ViT B/16, Swin v2) and two benchmarks (ImageNet, CIFAR-10) demonstrates versatility and robustness (R1, R3). The paper is well-written and clear (R2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty of core observation**. The finding that ID features cluster closer to weight vectors than OOD features is well-established in prior work, e.g., the familiarity hypothesis [5] and works on feature-norm OOD detection [2,3,4] (R1, R2). The neural collapse view for OOD detection has also been explored in [6] (NECO), but comparison with [1–6] is missing (R2). One reviewer (R1) further notes the logical connection from Figure 2 (“early low cosine similarity equals no need for NC convergence”) is weak and not well-argued.
2. **Marginal empirical gains and missing ablations**. NCI ranks in the top three on two benchmarks, but improvements over existing SOTA are marginal; prior leading methods showed significantly larger gains (R3). The authors need to justify why “CIFAR-10 Strong” and “ImageNet Strong” baselines were chosen and why other OpenOOD methods were omitted (R3). Transformer results are incomplete (e.g., some comparison methods missing), and a full table with transformer architectures should be included (R4). The OpenOOD benchmark includes covariate shift datasets that were excluded; NCI’s performance under covariate shift should be tested (R4).
3. **Lack of theoretical rigor and proof issues**. There is a possible mistake in Theorem 3.1: assuming Σ_W → 0 does not necessarily imply (h_{i,c} – μ_c)(h_{i,c} – μ_c)^T → 0 (R4). The paper would benefit from a stronger theoretical foundation relating the geometric structure of ID vs. OOD samples (R1).
4. **Overclaimed and redundant statements**. The claim in the Introduction that the work “explain[s], connect[s], and complete[s] prior methods under a holistic view” is too broad given the current scope of discussion (R3). Section 3 partially repeats content from the Introduction (e.g., “Where do features of ID samples form clusters?”) and should be reorganized (R3).
5. **Insufficient analysis of use cases and limitations**. The paper should include analysis of success and failure cases of NCI, especially since many models may not be well-trained and could violate neural convergence (R3). It is essential to evaluate the method on detecting misclassified ID data, as prior work [7,8,9] shows that many OOD detectors harm other failure detection tasks (R2). The distinction between ID and OOD data used in experiments should be clearly explained, particularly for scenarios where classes are similar or identical (R3).
6. **Visualization and reproducibility concerns**. UMAP visualizations are used to support interpretive arguments, but UMAP is nonlinear and does not preserve global structure. The authors should replicate visualizations with PCA and across diverse model/dataset pairs (R4: e.g., ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100, ImageNet vs. ImageNet-O, Textures, iNaturalist, SUN, Places365; similarly for ResNet-50 and ViT). Hyperparameter choices for all comparison methods (including the authors’ own model) are needed for reproducibility; a table in the appendix listing all hyperparameters should be provided (R4).
7. **Missing ablation on Neural Collapse convergence**. If NCI relies on NC, would further training toward stronger NC improve its performance? The authors should save pscore, NCI, and NC metrics (NC1–NC4) during training for multiple DNNs to see how they evolve over long training (R4). Additionally, the relation between Neuron Collapse metrics and NCI performance should be explored (R3).

*Minority reviewer note:* While three reviewers rated the paper 5, one reviewer (R1) rated 3, expressing stronger concerns about the weak logical argument connecting early cosine similarity to Neural Collapse, the lack of theoretical foundation, and the basic nature of the central observation. This contrast should be kept in mind.
