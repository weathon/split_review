## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Litee, a lightweight exploration algorithm for deep RL that leverages linear bandit techniques (UCB, Thompson Sampling) to compute an uncertainty bonus from the value network’s state embeddings without adding extra parameters. Litee+ extends Litee with a small auxiliary Inverse Dynamics Network (IDN) to handle sparse rewards. The authors provide theoretical regret bounds and experiments on MiniHack and MuJoCo. Reviewers are split: three rate the paper low (3/10) due to insufficient novelty, limited experiments, and incremental theory; one rates it higher (6/10) praising the motivation and combination of theory and empirics but noting missing experiments and ablations.

### Strengths
- **Bridging theory and practice**: The paper explicitly tries to connect theoretical RL exploration guarantees with practical deep RL algorithms, addressing a recognized gap in the literature (R3).
- **Theoretical guarantees**: Provides regret bounds for the proposed methods, giving a formal sample-efficiency guarantee alongside empirical results (R1, R2, R4). R4 highlights that both theoretical and empirical analysis are presented.
- **Parameter efficiency**: Litee adds no new parameters; Litee+ adds less than 1% extra parameters (≈10 lines of code). The method is substantially lighter than E3B (R1, R2).
- **Inverse Dynamics Network**: The use of an IDN in Litee+ is recognized as a practical solution for sparse reward environments (R1).
- **Clear motivation**: The opening motivation (lines 72-79) is clear and straightforward (R4). R3 also agrees the paper addresses a common issue.
- **Minority positive view (R4)**: R4 found the method sound overall, with no major concerns about the approach itself, only suggestions for additional experiments and ablations.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The algorithm is nearly identical to existing methods in linear MDP RL, specifically LSVI-UCB (Jin et al., 2020), with the main difference being that features φ are learned and change over time. Litee’s UCB bonus (Eq. 3) and Thompson sampling (Algorithm 2) are standard, with Algorithm 2 closely resembling LSVI-PHE (Ishfaq et al., 2021), which is not cited. Litee+ is closely related to ICM, and the relations to these prior works are insufficiently discussed (R1, R2, R3). The paper’s claim (lines 72-79, 134-136) that no prior algorithm is both theoretically grounded and empirically efficient is false—counterexamples exist (e.g., Ishfaq et al., 2024a,b) (R3).
- **Insufficient experimental validation**:
    - No table of results; only plots are shown, making it difficult to assess statistical significance. The number of repetitions varies (3 or 7) without explanation (R1).
    - MuJoCo experiments miss standard environments: HalfCheetah, Ant, and Humanoid are omitted. The swimmer task where SAC-Litee outperforms SAC/PPO is too easy to be convincing (R2).
    - MiniHack uses only a small subset of tasks; Litee fails to solve all selected tasks. Comprehensive testing would require more tasks to evaluate different RL aspects (R1, R2).
    - Baselines are not state-of-the-art. On MuJoCo, stronger recent algorithms (e.g., DSAC-T, Duan et al., 2023) are not compared. For MiniHack, comparisons with SAC+RND, SAC+ICM, and other strong exploration baselines from the E3B paper are missing (R2, R3).
    - No ablation study comparing Litee vs. Litee+ on sparse reward tasks (e.g., MiniHack) to quantify the IDN contribution (R4).
    - No experiments with DQN+Litee on classic sparse reward tasks like Montezuma’s Revenge, despite the theoretical analysis covering this setting (R4).
- **Limited theoretical contribution**: The proof is short (<5 pages), uses standard techniques without new insights, and most lemmas are direct adaptations of existing papers (e.g., Lemma 7). The theoretical framework is largely reorganized from [1,2] (R1, R2, R3). The neural tangent kernel H appears without proper introduction (Theorem 4.2), forcing readers to consult [2] to understand it (R2). The positive-definite assumption of the NTK (rather than positive-semidefinite) is suspect (R2).
- **Computational concerns**: The argument that parameter overhead is a major bottleneck in DRL is questionable—many successful DRL models use shallow networks where even a 100% parameter increase is negligible on GPUs (R1). For Thompson sampling, inverting the high-dimensional noise variance matrix A is computationally non-trivial (R3).
- **Organization and presentation**: The theoretical part is poorly organized (R2). The paper does not adequately differentiate its contributions from LSVI-UCB, LSVI-PHE, and ICM. The implementation snippets (Listing 1, 2) are insufficient—full code is requested (R3).
