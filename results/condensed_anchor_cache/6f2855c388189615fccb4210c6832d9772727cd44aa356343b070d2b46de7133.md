## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Improved Stochastic Controlled Averaging (ISCA) to address the practical performance inconsistency of SCAFFOLD across homogeneous and heterogeneous federated learning settings. ISCA modifies SCAFFOLD by updating control variables more frequently and adjusting the momentum term. An extension ISCAM incorporates unbiased communication compression. The authors provide convergence guarantees and experiments on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST with a fully connected network.

### Strengths

- **Attempt to improve SCAFFOLD in homogeneous settings** (Reviewer 1): ISCA and ISCAM aim to bridge the gap between SCAFFOLD’s theory and practice, particularly in homogeneous data.
- **Interesting motivation** (Reviewer 2): Interrogating why SCAFFOLD’s practical performance is affected by heterogeneity despite theory predicting otherwise is a useful and rarely carried out investigation.
- **Addresses a limitation** (Reviewer 4): ISCA provides more consistent performance across homogeneous and heterogeneous settings by improving the local control variable update process.
- **Extension with compression** (Reviewer 4): ISCAM extends ISCA to communication compression while maintaining theoretical guarantees.
- **Theoretical guarantees** (Reviewers 1, 4, 5): The paper provides convergence results for both ISCA and ISCAM, comparable to SCAFFOLD.
- **Clarity and thoroughness** (Reviewer 5): The paper is well-written and the theoretical analysis is thorough.

**Contrast**: Reviewers 4 and 5 are more positive about the strengths; Reviewer 3 found no specific strength; Reviewers 1 and 2 noted only limited strengths.

### Weaknesses

**1. Clarity and correctness of algorithm and methodology**  
- The differences between ISCA and SCAFFOLD are hard to discern beyond minor adjustments to local control variables (Reviewer 1).  
- Equations 1 and 2 appear erroneous compared to Karimireddy et al. (2020); the discussion in Section 3.2 is unconvincing and the claim about “ancient local control variable” seems inaccurate (Reviewer 3).  
- Algorithm 1 Line 12 seems impossible to compute on clients because it involves the average function $f$; likely a typo (Reviewer 2).  
- The update of $u_i^{t+1,0}$ in Algorithm 1 Line 14 is unclear; it is not obvious how Lemma 8 in the appendix follows (Reviewer 5).  
- Algorithm 1 requires computing a full gradient (Line 1), which is impractical in large-scale settings (Reviewer 5).  
- Typos: Line 222 should read $-\alpha_{\text{in}}$ instead of $+\alpha_{\text{in}}$; Line 225 superscripts should be $t$ and $k$ not $i$ and $k$ (Reviewer 5).

**2. Theoretical issues**  
- No theoretical evidence that ISCA outperforms SCAFFOLD in homogeneous settings; both have identical convergence rates in Table 1, yet the paper claims better performance (Reviewer 1).  
- The inner stepsize requirement from the theorem forces an infinitesimally small stepsize, effectively negating local updates. The dimensional scaling is wrong (should scale with $1/L$, not $1/L^2$). The proofs are disorganized; the condition on $\alpha$ only allows arbitrarily small stepsizes, causing the term $4R_1/(\alpha T)$ to explode, and no proper stepsize specification is given (Reviewer 2).  
- Lemma 4 is derived for a different algorithm in Huang et al. (2024) and its applicability to Algorithm 1 is not justified (Reviewer 2).  
- Theoretically, ISCA offers no improvement over SCAFFOLD (Reviewer 5).

**3. Insufficient and questionable experimental validation**  
- Experiments are limited to toy datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST with a fully connected network); what works on MNIST rarely transfers to ImageNet or modern architectures (Reviewer 2).  
- Missing comparisons with other FL algorithms that address heterogeneity, such as FedProx and FedNova (Reviewer 1).  
- No experiments on larger, more realistic datasets like FEMNIST, Shakespeare, or Reddit (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- In Figure 1 (MNIST IID), SCAFFOLD initially outperforms ISCA but then shows an unusual decline that never reappears elsewhere (Reviewer 1).  
- Results in Figure 1 appear inconsistent with Figure 1 of Huang et al. (2024) for SCAFFOLD and FedAvg (Reviewer 3).  
- ISCA underperforms SCAFFOLD in the non-IID setting, contradicting the claim that ISCA is unaffected by data heterogeneity (Reviewer 5).  
- Numerical experiment results should be reported with numbers, not just plots, to better assess improvements (Reviewer 5).  
- No evidence that communication compression works in practice (e.g., wall-clock time comparisons) (Reviewer 5).

**4. Significance and justification of the homogeneous setting**  
- The practical significance of improving performance in homogeneous settings is not justified, especially since most real FL scenarios are heterogeneous (Reviewer 1).  
- The claim that SCAFFOLD performs worse in homogeneous settings contradicts the idea of control variates; an independent corroborating paper is needed (Reviewer 3).  
- The statement that $f_1 = \dots = f_N$ in homogeneous settings is not universally valid; if objectives were equal, why consider a finite sum? (Reviewer 4)  
- In a homogeneous setting with full gradients, local control variables in SCAFFOLD would be identical and thus should not affect convergence; the authors should explain why SCAFFOLD might then perform poorly and provide experiments under such conditions (Reviewer 4).  
- Besides the first subfigure of Figure 1, are there other references or experiments supporting the claim that SCAFFOLD performs poorly in IID settings? (Reviewer 4)

**5. Missing related work**  
- The paper omits important references to newer methods that use more advanced approximate control variates (e.g., STORM-based methods), such as “Faster Non-Convex Federated Learning via Global and Local Momentum” (UAI 2022) and “Mime: Mimicking Centralized Stochastic Algorithms in Federated Learning” (NeurIPS 2021) (Reviewer 3).

**Contrast**: Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 are more critical of the experiments, theory, and significance; Reviewers 4 and 5, while more positive overall, also raise substantial concerns about theory and experiments.
