## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces COPRA, a language-agent approach that uses GPT-4 in a stateful backtracking search for formal theorem-proving. The policy selects tactics, retrieves lemmas and definitions from an external database, and uses execution feedback and search history to refine prompts. It is evaluated on miniF2F (Lean) and CompCert (Coq) benchmarks. Reviewers acknowledge the problem is interesting and the MDP formalization is rigorous, but raise concerns about novelty, the fairness of comparisons (data contamination, inference-cost disparity), missing ablations, overclaimed “state-of-the-art” and “first LLM-agent” statements, and clarity of the algorithm and the RL framing.

### Strengths
- The problem is rigorously formalised as a Markov decision process (MDP) (Reviewer 4).
- The method incorporates execution feedback and Sanchez-Stern’s pruning method in a backtracking search, which is effective (Reviewer 1).
- COPRA achieves positive results on challenging benchmarks (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The paper is well organized, with good language and detailed implementation that ensures reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- Formal theorem-proving is a less explored application domain; the paper provides encouraging results for it (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Data contamination and SOTA claims.** The results look “fishy” – GPT-4 may have been trained on these datasets (the passage on page 7 strongly suggests this), making absolute comparisons hard to interpret (Reviewer 1). ‘Decomposing the Enigma’ (Zhao et al., May 2023) achieves 45.5% on miniF2F (ChatGPT-3.5) vs. COPRA’s 23.36% (GPT-4), and Proverbot outperforms COPRA on CompCert; the paper does not adequately explain this gap or why a more powerful LLM underperforms (Reviewer 3). The claims of “state-of-the-art” and “first LLM-agent approach” are not fully supported, given prior works like ReProver and ‘Decomposing the Enigma’ (Reviewer 3).  
- **Unfair inference-count comparisons.** Figures 5 and 6 compare pass@k inferences between COPRA (GPT-4) and ReProver (much smaller LLM); one GPT-4 query is far more powerful, making the metric misleading without accounting for computational cost or wall-clock time (Reviewers 1, 3). Why not use pass@k tactic applications or wall-clock time? (Reviewer 1). Also, in Fig 6 Proverbot has y > 0 at x = 0 (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing ablations and controls.** The paper includes one-shot prompting in COPRA, which dilutes what can be attributed to the method; an ablation without one-shot is needed (Reviewer 1). No ablation isolates the search: how does running COPRA with k=1 repeatedly (i.i.d. LLM samples) compare to COPRA with k>1, normalized for number of tactic applications? (Reviewer 1). The “w/o backtracking” baseline in Table 3 needs precise description and control for computational work (e.g., number of tactic calls) (Reviewer 1). Ablation studies are also needed to understand the role of the RL component (Reviewer 2).  
- **RL formalization not used in practice.** The problem is formalised as an MDP, but then the authors say “we do not take on this problem” and treat the policy as a fixed wrapper around GPT-4; the scalar reward r_i and the RL formulation appear unused (Reviewers 1, 4). What RL algorithm is actually employed, and how is exploration–exploitation handled? Can it be replaced by tree search or ReAct? (Reviewer 2).  
- **Clarity and presentation issues.** The discussion of *Rewards* and *Histories and Policies* is confusing: why are both scalar rewards and text feedback part of the reward? Where is the scalar reward used? What is the point of the trajectory definition? (Reviewer 1). The algorithm in Fig. 3 is high-level; calls and interactions with the LLM are not detailed (Reviewer 4). Fig. 4 is helpful but confusing: is the entire protocol repeated up to k times, or is this within one value of j in the pseudocode? (Reviewer 1). The claim of a “more sophisticated search algorithm” is not explained (Reviewer 4).  
- **Method limitations and comparisons.** Only allowing the model to choose tactics seems highly restrictive; why not also prompt for the most promising partial proof to attack next? (Reviewer 1). The removal of ReProver’s retrieval mechanism is unclear – the lemma repository should be crucial, and BM25-based retrieval from ReProver’s code for miniF2F could have been integrated (Reviewer 1). The method is similar to retrieval-based LLMs; detailed comparison with ReProver is needed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Minor weaknesses.** Text in Fig. 1 is too small/blurry (Reviewer 1). Fig. 3 indentation needs fixing (Reviewer 1). Execution feedback is nice but the program synthesis literature could be cited (Reviewer 1). Reference page should start on a new page (Reviewer 3).  
- **Open questions that overlap with criticisms.** Why does GPT-3.5 outperform GPT-4 in Table 2? Could this indicate prompt overfitting for different LLMs? (Reviewer 3). How can it be verified that theorems are proved sufficiently? (Reviewer 3). What is the average API cost per proven theorem? (Reviewer 3).  
- **Typos.** “correlated [with the] number of correct proofs”; “if only 60 inferences [are] allowed” (Reviewer 1).
