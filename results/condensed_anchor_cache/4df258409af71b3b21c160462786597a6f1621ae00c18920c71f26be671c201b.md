## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles a well-motivated problem in internal-coordinate density modeling of proteins: small fluctuations in internal coordinates can produce large 3D atom displacements. The authors propose constraining the variance of Cartesian positions and solving a Lagrangian optimization to find a multivariate Gaussian over internal coordinates that is close (in KL) to an unconstrained estimate while satisfying those constraints. The method is implemented as a VAE where a U-net predicts Lagrange multipliers from the conditional mean in 3D. Experiments on MD simulation data cover two regimes: small fluctuations (unimodal) and larger conformational changes (multimodal). The goal is to scale internal-coordinate density models to full protein backbones.

### Strengths
- The problem is well-motivated: the coupling between internal and Cartesian coordinates is a key challenge (all reviewers).
- Using 3D variance constraints to induce covariance in internal coordinates is conceptually appealing and sound under the multivariate Gaussian assumption (R1).
- The description of the number of parameters to be estimated (Section 2.1) is clear and helpful for intuition (R2).
- The “Variance along the atom chain” figures effectively illustrate where fluctuations accumulate (R2).
- The main idea of deriving how angles and coordinates are coupled through a Lagrangian framework is outstanding; the paper is well written up to that point (R3).
- Results show relatively good generation of variations relative to the reference, and the approach is promising (R3).
- The authors explicitly define limitations of the method with respect to data availability for a given protein (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons:** Experiments lack comparisons to related ML methods mentioned in the related work (R1, R3). No scalar metrics are provided for quantitative comparison to other models (R2). The ML significance is light; the paper takes too narrow a perspective and does not position itself in the broader conformer generation literature (R3). R1 explicitly desires a more thorough comparison despite the focus on internal-coordinate modeling.
- **Mathematical rigor and clarity concerns:** Reviewer 3 raised substantial concerns about the derivation. Specific issues: eq5 is presented as a Lagrangian but not a KL divergence; functional derivatives are undefined and missing steps; eq7 approximation is unjustified; eq8 uses the same index for angles and coordinates; eq9 derivation is missing; the integral=1 constraint is redundant for normalized Gaussians; predicting Lagrange multipliers with a U-net conflicts with Lagrangian theory (multipliers should be at optimality, not predicted); the method appears to be a penalty method rather than true Lagrangian constraints; many terms are left undefined (e.g., “local/global constraints”, “full covariance matrix”, “standard estimator”, “valid Ramachandran distribution”). The reviewer noted during discussion that these mathematical concerns were addressed, but the paper still lacks full derivations and clarity in presentation.
- **Unclear experimental demonstration:** It is unclear what the VAE and U-net are intended to achieve; the pipeline from constraints to final model is confusing (R3). The Ramachandran plots (Fig 4) show that samples do not capture the actual variance in the data, raising questions about the suitability of a Gaussian prior (R2). The TIC plots in Fig 5 are not well explained and appear to have points cut off (R2). The figures (Fig 4, Fig 5) lack clear labels and descriptions (R3). The experiments do not directly discuss how the constraints prevent large Cartesian fluctuations, deviating from the original problem statement (R3).
- **Missing analyses and ablations:** Remaining questions include: any supervision or auxiliary loss on the predicted Lagrange multipliers (R1); how to balance fluctuation restriction vs. representation power beyond hand-tuning (R1); comparison of models learned in unimodal vs. multimodal scenarios (R1); generalization to new proteins (R1). The relationship between the auxiliary regularizing loss (MAE on λ⁻¹) and introducing a Laplace prior is not explored (R2).
- **Motivation for using ML on simulation data:** It is unclear what the ML model improves over directly running the simulation; the paper would benefit from explaining how this method advances beyond the simulation framework (R2).
- **Unsubstantiated claims:** The claim that “samples are guaranteed to fulfil physical constraints” is not justified given the numerous approximations in the derivation (R3).

*Note:* Reviewers 2 and 3 scored 6, while Reviewer 1 scored 5, indicating somewhat lower confidence in the contribution. Reviewer 3’s initial soundness rating was 1 (poor) due to math issues, though these were partially resolved during discussion; the reviewer still considers the ML significance weak. All reviewers noted the need for better baselines and comparisons.
