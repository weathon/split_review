## Merged Review

### Summary

PSI-KT is a hierarchical generative model for knowledge tracing that jointly models individual cognitive traits and a shared prerequisite knowledge graph using Bayesian inference. It aims to achieve predictive accuracy, scalability, and interpretability, and is evaluated on three public educational datasets. Reviewers agree that the motivation is meaningful, the methodology is technically sound, and the experiments show favorable results. However, concerns are raised about the experimental setup (only a small fraction of data used), missing ablation studies and baselines (GKT, SKT, IKT, ICKT, QIKT), insufficient clarity in method description and cognitive trait mapping, and the strength of the interpretability evaluation. One reviewer is notably more critical (score 5) regarding these issues, while the other reviewers are more positive (scores 8, 8, 6).

### Strengths

- The model is designed based on psychological principles and combines knowledge tracing and knowledge mapping into one framework, offering originality (R1, R4). The integration of cognitive science and AI is significant for personalized education (R1).
- The paper is well-written and provides clear intuitive explanations of the model components, with good mathematical notation and derivations (R1, R2, R3).
- The method is technically strong in probabilistic modeling and uses a three-level hierarchical state-space model with approximate Bayesian inference (ELBO) rather than cross-entropy, which is novel and reasonable (R1, R2).
- Experiments demonstrate predictive accuracy, scalability, and interpretability across multiple datasets and settings (within-learner and between-learner), with detailed confirmatory experiments on cognitive traits and prerequisite graphs yielding favorable results (R1, R2).
- The tables and graphs are helpful in following the results (R4). The data used is relevant and allows reasonable evaluation (R4). The interpretable representations of learners and knowledge structure provide an important basis for personalized education (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Experimental setup uses only a limited portion of datasets (100–1000 learners)** despite Table 1 showing datasets have over 10,000 learners. This may unfairly disadvantage deep learning baselines and limit generalizability. The justification (“to simulate real-world data constraints”) is questioned, and full-dataset experiments are needed (R3). A reviewer notes that for a binary prediction problem, accuracy of 55–80% is not strong, only better than baselines (R4).
- **Missing essential ablation studies** to isolate the contributions of the cognitive traits and the prerequisite graph. Current experiments remove whole components but do not provide granular insight into which specific parameters or functional forms drive accuracy gains (R1, R2). Ablations on prerequisite graph complexity and temporal dynamics of learner traits are also requested (R1).
- **Missing important baselines**: no comparison to explicit knowledge graph models like GKT (graph-based knowledge tracing) or SKT (structure-based knowledge tracing) (R2), and no inclusion of recent interpretable KT methods such as IKT, ICKT, or QIKT (R3). A more thorough positioning within the interpretable KT landscape is needed (R3).
- **Interpretability assessment is not entirely convincing.** The paper uses mutual information between PSI-KT’s learned parameters and hidden vectors of baselines (e.g., DKT, AKT), but without evidence that they capture the same underlying features, direct comparisons are questionable (R3). The limited dimensionality (16) of deep learning representations may constrain them, making comparison uneven (R3). Interpretability and scalability claims rely heavily on “correct by construction” rather than rigorous evaluation (R4). The correctness of the inferred prerequisite graph is not well quantified (R4).
- **The four dimensions of cognitive traits lack explicit mapping** to established psychological constructs (e.g., working memory capacity, attention span, learning styles), reducing interpretability for readers familiar with cognitive science (R2, R1). The paper should clarify which specific traits each dimension represents.
- **Long-term retention modeling uses exponential decay**, considered too simplistic given memory research suggesting power-law decay or models with consolidation and interference effects. Individual differences in memory capacity and learning styles are not considered (R1).
- **Method description is insufficiently clear** regarding the neural network used to generate cognitive parameters. The main text only briefly mentions it, leading to the misconception that PSI-KT is not a deep learning approach; details on input features, number of layers, activation functions, and optimization algorithm are missing (R3).
- **Evaluations are limited to three datasets**; more diverse domains (varying noise, subject matter, temporal granularity) would better reveal capabilities and limitations (R1). Claims about educational effectiveness and knowledge graphs need a sufficient cross-section of data (R4).
- **No case studies are provided** to intuitively demonstrate interpretability (e.g., visualizing knowledge mastery trends over time), as done in prior KT research (R3).

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 5) is notably more critical than the other reviewers (scores 8, 8, 6), particularly regarding the experimental setup, missing baselines, clarity, and interpretability evaluation. This contrast should be considered when assessing the paper’s strengths and weaknesses.*
