## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses spatial reasoning failures in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and proposes ADAPTVIS, a test-time decoding method that adjusts attention to image tokens based on model confidence. The authors analyze attention patterns, noting that errors often correspond to attention being misallocated to irrelevant objects and that attention differs between familiar and unfamiliar spatial relationships. They propose two variants: sharpening attention when confidence is high and smoothing it when confidence is low. Evaluations on WhatsUp and VSR benchmarks show improvements (up to 50 points on WhatsUp) with minimal computational overhead.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and challenging problem: spatial reasoning in VLMs.
- The idea of intervening on attention weights using model confidence as a proxy for correctness is clever, interesting, and conceptually novel.
- The test-time per-sample attention adjustment using output probability is a simple yet effective approach for spatial reasoning.
- The method shows promising results on controlled spatial reasoning benchmarks, especially on balanced, synthetic datasets like WhatsUp.
- The hypothesis that "generation confidence is a reliable indicator of attention correctness" opens an interesting direction for further exploration in the vision-language domain.
- The paper provides a thorough analysis with visualizations (though some reviewers disagree on quality and relevance).
- The method is clearly structured and easy to understand (noted by Reviewer 3, though others critique writing clarity).

### Weaknesses
- **Analysis is not comprehensive.** Sections 3 and 4 are overly lengthy yet insufficiently rigorous. Strong conclusions (e.g., that models primarily process information in intermediate layers) are drawn from a single dataset. Section 4.1 lacks quantitative evidence for the claim that incorrect answers correlate with incorrect attention patterns; only four qualitative examples are provided, and at least one attention map (third example) appears reasonable. The authors should use COCO bounding box annotations to quantify attention overlap with relevant entities and report the correlation with spatial reasoning accuracy.
- **Section 3.2 is highly unclear.** The caption and main text do not adequately explain Figure 5; it is unspecified whether left or right plot is referenced. The calculation of AUROC is not given. The variables Cont_A and Cont_B are used throughout but never defined. The experiment "incrementally increasing attention weights across the entire image" is not linked to a particular subplot.
- **Figure 7 is unclear and contains discrepancies.** How is "model average confidence" calculated (e.g., using ground-truth and averaging only correct predictions)? What is the "golden" label—ground truth? In the left panel of Table 7, COCO_two shows non-zero values for *behind* and *front*, but Appendix Table 6 reports zero such relationships. The impact of incorrect model predictions on this analysis is not discussed.
- **Claims about ScalingVis are not justified.** The earlier setup (L223–L225) that augments image attention logits with a constant coefficient does not improve performance; it is unclear why ScalingVis (which uses a constant sharpening factor) behaves differently. The hyperparameters (α₁, α₂, β) are tuned per test dataset via grid search, raising concerns about overfitting to each specific test set. No reasoning is provided for why sharpening helps on synthetic datasets (WhatsUp) while smoothing helps on real datasets (VSR).
- **Limited experimental validation.** (a) Only two small datasets are evaluated; most images and possibly all captions are synthetic, leaving generalizability unclear. (b) The evaluation splits differ from prior works (e.g., [3] and [4]), making direct comparison impossible. Prior works report 60+% accuracy on VSR, whereas the baseline here achieves only 35–40%, suggesting either suboptimal baseline implementation or different splits. (c) In Table 1, LLaVA 1.6 underperforms LLaVA 1.5 on most splits, which is unexpected; some splits show near-0% baseline accuracy, indicating possible implementation errors. (d) No comparisons are made with prior spatial reasoning methods or on common benchmarks (GQA, VQA-v2, MMBench, SEED-Bench). Reviewers suggest evaluating on generic VQA datasets or a spatial-reasoning subset thereof.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity.** The method uses multiple thresholds (α₁, α₂, β) that require grid search and do not transfer across datasets or VLMs. Table 4 shows performance dropping significantly compared to Tables 2 and 3 when thresholds are not selectively chosen. This limits practical applicability; an adaptive algorithm for setting thresholds would be needed.
- **Conceptual concerns about the method.** If the model is confidently wrong, sharpening attention may exacerbate the error; if the model is less confident, smoothing attention may further reduce confidence. Moreover, α (or β) is applied uniformly across all layers, forcing diffused attention from start to finish. The method seems to rely on a specific attention behavior (as in Fig. 1) that may not hold generally.
- **Assumption about attention magnitude.** The paper initially assumes that larger attention weights imply more information usage, but later claims that "location of attention on image tokens is more crucial than quantity." It is unclear if any non-zero weight suffices to use information; there is no citation to literature supporting the assumption.
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Several terms are undefined: VDC, DoLa (Dola) appear in Tables 2 and 3 without citation or explanation in the main text. Related Work is jargon-heavy and lacks elaboration on cited papers. There are typos: L296-L298 incorrectly reference Sec 5.1/5.2; L395 Equation 5.2 appears to be a typo; LaTeX formatting for inverted commas is incorrect (L401 and elsewhere). Figure 10 is incorrectly referenced at line 399. Figure 12 is unclear—it claims to show three data types but only two curves are visible, and the visualized phenomenon is overly simplistic.
- **Missing experimental details.** The VLM used in Sections 4 and 5 is not specified. It is not stated whether the VLM was fine-tuned on the Controlled Image and VSR datasets. Compute and inference time for the method (which may require two passes) are not reported.
- **Lack of generality assessment.** The method is only evaluated on LLaVA series; it is unclear how it would perform on other VLMs (e.g., BLIP-2, InstructBLIP). Its effectiveness on general multimodal capabilities beyond spatial reasoning is unverified, despite the method being generic.
