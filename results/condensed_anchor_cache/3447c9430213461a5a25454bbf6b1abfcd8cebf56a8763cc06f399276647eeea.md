## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SLiMe, a one-shot image segmentation method that leverages pretrained Stable Diffusion. Given a single image and its segmentation mask, the method optimizes text embeddings using cross-attention maps and a novel weighted accumulated self-attention (WAS) map so that the embeddings highlight mask foregrounds. The optimized embeddings can then segment unseen images. The method is evaluated on car/horse/face part segmentation benchmarks and outperforms existing one-shot and few-shot methods.

### Strengths
- **Novel and interesting idea**: Retargeting the text branch of a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model for few-shot segmentation by optimizing text embeddings is novel. This approach avoids overfitting to input features common in adaptor-based methods and generalizes well. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Superior performance**: SLiMe significantly outperforms existing one-shot and few-shot segmentation methods on the ReGAN benchmarks (horse, car, face datasets), both quantitatively and qualitatively. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Practical benefits**: Requires only a single annotated sample and works at arbitrary granularity levels, reducing the need for large supervised training while maintaining competitive performance. [R1, R4]
- **Thorough ablations and analysis**: The paper provides extensive ablation studies showing the effectiveness of design choices, includes a detailed supplementary appendix, discusses limitations, and open-sources code for reproducibility. [R2, R3, R4]
- **Clear presentation**: Well-written and easy to follow, with good theoretical background and sufficient implementation details in the appendix. [R2, R3, R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear role and construction of text prompts**: The introduction of the text prompt is abrupt. It is not explained how text embeddings are defined, how they are obtained, or whether different granularities use the same embedding. No evidence is provided that the optimized text embeddings correspond to individual segmented regions, especially for arbitrary granularity. A main-text clarification (currently only in appendix) is needed. [R1, R4]
- **Unconvincing importance of WAS-attention**: The contribution of the weighted accumulated self-attention map appears marginal in Table 5; without it, performance might be boostable by simple post-processing like GrabCut. There is a numerical inconsistency: Table 5 (setting α=0) reports 68.0 average performance, while Appendix Table 9 reports 62.7 without WAS. This discrepancy should be resolved, and the incremental benefit of WAS should be presented in the main text. [R2, R4]
- **Limited evaluation and comparisons**: Quantitative results are only on horse/car/face datasets from ReGAN. Missing comparisons on broader benchmarks: ADE-Bedroom-30 (used by segDDPM) and FSS-1000 (used by segGPT). Also, few competing methods are compared relative to related work. The reason for omitting SegDDPM results from Table 1 is not explained. [R2, R3]
- **Missing citations and related context**: The method is closely related to Hedlin et al. on unsupervised semantic correspondence but does not discuss that connection. Recent zero-shot segmentation works using diffusion models (Peekaboo by Burgert et al., Diffuse, Attend, and Segment by Tian et al.) are not cited and should be acknowledged or differentiated. [R3]
- **Confusing figure**: Figure 3 is unclear; it should explicitly place the UNet inside a box and show the attention extraction module selecting layers and combining attention maps. [R2]
- **Interpretability not studied**: It would be valuable to analyze whether the learned text embeddings are interpretable (e.g., by finding closest tokens), but this is not done. [R2]
- **Minor readability**: Best results in ablation tables should be bolded for clarity. [R3]

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: Two reviewers (R3, R4, score 8) are highly positive about novelty and experiments; two reviewers (R1, R2, score 6) raise substantial concerns about prompt clarity, WAS-attention significance, and evaluation scope. These contrasts are reflected in the weaknesses above.
