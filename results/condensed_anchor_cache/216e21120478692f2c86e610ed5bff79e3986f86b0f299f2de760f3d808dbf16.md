## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper re-evaluates compressed LLMs (pruning and quantization) and introduces LLM-KICK, a benchmark of knowledge-intensive tasks to go beyond perplexity. It benchmarks current methods (e.g., SparseGPT, GPTQ) on Vicuna models and finds that perplexity often masks performance degradation, especially for pruning at low sparsity (25-30%), while quantization (e.g., 8-bit) is more successful. The paper shows compressed models can still be robust for in-context retrieval/summarization.

### Strengths
- Timely and important topic given recent proliferation of training-free/data-free compression papers (Reviewers 1,3).
- Reveals new and widely unknown gaps between compressed and uncompressed LLMs, especially that perplexity fails to capture subtle capability changes (Reviewers 1,3).
- Provides a decent array of curated benchmark datasets (LLM-KICK) that will be useful for future research (Reviewer 1).
- Clearly shows the discrepancy between perplexity and performance on more realistic tasks (Reviewer 1).
- Demonstrates that compressed models may offer better performance on some tasks (e.g., In-Context Text Summarization) than others (e.g., Factoid-based QA), offering practical insights (Reviewers 3,4).
- Authors plan to release code, aiding development of future compression techniques (Reviewer 3).
- Paper is well-written with clear and comprehensive plots; information is easily digestible (Reviewer 4).
- Highlights that pruning methods (e.g., SparseGPT) often underperform quantization methods (e.g., GPTQ), which is an actionable insight for practitioners (Reviewer 4).
- The work is important for enabling researchers with better evaluation tools (Reviewer 1) and for designing new algorithms that address current weaknesses (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model family**: Only Vicuna (7B and 13B) is evaluated. Multiple reviewers (3,4) stress that conclusions may not generalize to other architectures (e.g., LLaMA 2, Falcon) or larger models (e.g., 70B+). This is a significant missing experiment that reduces robustness.
- **Limited compression methods**: Only GPTQ for quantization and SparseGPT for pruning are benchmarked. Reviewers (3,4) request additional quantization methods: LLM.int8() (Reviewer 3), AWQ, SmoothQuant (Reviewer 4). Evaluating these would provide a more complete picture of state-of-the-art.
- **Subjective threshold choice**: The 5% degradation threshold is arbitrary; changing to 10% would qualitatively change conclusions (e.g., 4-bit quantization might be acceptable, and 50% sparsity could be "competitive") (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of inference time context**: The accuracy loss from compression is not contextualized with potential speedups; e.g., a 5% accuracy drop yielding 10% faster inference could be considered successful (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing prompt designs**: The main paper lacks the exact prompts used for each task; adding them would clarify task difficulty and improve reproducibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing summary table**: A table listing dataset statistics (Reviewer 1) and summarizing tasks' performance across architectures, compression techniques, and perplexity (Reviewer 3) would aid reader understanding.
- **Question about SparseGPT being data-free**: Reviewer 2 questions whether SparseGPT is truly data-free; the paper should clarify.
- **Lack of explanation for performance degradation**: Reviewers (3,4) ask why quantization works better than pruning, and more generally why performance degrades, and whether mitigation strategies exist. The paper provides no analysis or countermeasures, which is a missing explanation.
- **Minority view on contribution**: Reviewer 2 (score 5) considers the paper essentially a benchmarking effort without new model, data, or algorithm, and suggests it is more suitable for a workshop than a full conference paper. This contrasts with other reviewers' higher scores. Reviewer 4 also rates contribution as fair (2). The paper does not offer novel methods, only evaluation insights.
