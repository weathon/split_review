- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 6, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
X-Diffusion is a cross-sectional diffusion model that generates 3D MRI volumes from a single or few 2D slices by conditioning on arbitrary viewing directions. It is evaluated on brain tumour (BraTS), whole-body (UK Biobank), and out-of-domain knee MRI. Two reviewers (6,6) consider the approach novel, the experiments thorough, and the writing clear; two others (5,5) find the experimental setup confusing, the clinical scenario impractical, and the evaluation metrics insufficient. Major concerns include lack of data consistency, unclear conditioning inputs, over-claiming of clinical readiness, and insufficient justification for out-of-domain generalisation.

### Strengths
- **Novelty:** The cross-sectional conditioning on arbitrary viewing directions (not just axial, coronal, sagittal) is a unique contribution to the medical imaging community. (R1, R2)
- **Comprehensive experiments:** Multiple datasets (brain, whole-body, knee) are used with extensive ablations and supplementary material. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Clear problem statement:** The motivation, contributions, and related work are well articulated. (R1)
- **Appropriate baselines:** Diffusion-based benchmarks (ScoreMRI, TPDM) are chosen from recent literature. (R4)
- **Demonstrated generalisation:** The method shows out-of-domain synthesis (knee MRI) and preserves tumour profiles, spine curvature, and brain volume. (R1, R2)
- **Effective ablations:** Key components (e.g., Stable Diffusion encoder) are ablated to demonstrate their contribution. (R2)
- **Well-written and illustrated:** Figures, mathematical descriptions, and tables support understanding. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Clinical realism and practical utility:** Several reviewers question the scenario of reconstructing a full 3D volume from one or a few high‑quality 2D slices. It is considered unrealistic and not aligned with clinical workflows where multiple undersampled slices are standard. (R2: “dangerous hallucination”, especially small tumours; R3: not cost‑efficient; R4: impractical). R2 recommends framing the work as an exploratory/proof‑of‑concept study rather than claiming clinical readiness.
- **Lack of data consistency:** The method has no mechanism to enforce agreement between synthesized slices and acquired k‑space data, a fundamental aspect of established reconstruction techniques. This raises concerns about fidelity and clinical applicability. (R4)
- **Insufficient and poorly justified evaluation metrics:**
  - PSNR is used as the primary metric but is deemed insufficient; SSIM should be reported in all tables (R2, R4). Additional metrics (e.g., LPIPS, medical LPIPS) are recommended (R2).
  - No estimates of variation (confidence intervals) are provided in tables (R3).
  - Difference maps lack a scale and use a poor colormap, making them uninterpretable (R3).
  - Total brain volume is an oversimplified downstream metric; cortical volumes, white/grey matter analysis (e.g., Freesurfer) would be more meaningful (R2, R4). One reviewer notes that regressing total brain volume from a single slice may be trivial (R4).
  - For spine curvature, Bland‑Altman or regression plots are needed instead of simple reporting (R4).
  - In Figure 6, up to 10–13 conditioning slices show significant hallucinations in structure/contrast, which reduces confidence in all 1‑5 slice results and shows that PSNR is a poor discriminator (R4).
- **Ambiguity in experimental setup:**
  - The number and location of input conditioning slices are not specified in Sections 5.1–5.4 (R3).
  - BraTS dataset split is unclear: the numbers (5880 scans from 1470 patients, split into 4704/588/588) do not clarify whether the split is at subject level; data leakage is possible. If only FLAIR is used, the counts do not add up (R3).
  - For Figure 3, it is unknown how many input slices were used and whether tumour information was present in the conditioning (R2).
  - For Figure 6 and Appendix Figure II/II, the number of conditioning slices and presence of tumour in the conditioning are not stated (R2).
  - A better test would be to condition on a slice without tumour from a tumour patient and check if the model correctly hallucinates the tumour (R4). The paper’s brief mention of this is insufficient.
- **Baseline comparisons and prior work:**
  - Baselines (ScoreMRI, TPDM) were not designed for the single‑slice‑to‑3D task, making comparisons difficult; this should be discussed more prominently (R2).
  - The related work section is incomplete: it misses super‑resolution approaches and a clear definition of “classical” methods (R3, R4). The Full‑Body MRI Analysis paragraph is generic and lacks key references (e.g., Tunariu et al., Küstner et al.) (R3).
  - Some references in the introduction do not match the claims (Tran et al., Sohl‑Dickstein et al., Kawar et al.) (R3).
  - When 31 of 155 slices are used, why not simply undersample 5× and apply variational reconstruction methods (which routinely achieve 8–10× acceleration with higher metrics)? This is not justified (R4).
- **Speed and inference cost:** No timing measurements are provided. Figure 3 suggests ~1000 diffusion steps are needed; the paper dismisses studying whether T can be reduced without quality loss (R1). Adapting acceleration ideas from text‑to‑image models is not explored (R1).
- **Out‑of‑domain generalisation not justified:** It is unclear why a model trained on brain MRI (UKB IDEAL) should generate knee MRI (fastMRI TSE, different contrast/resolution). Claiming Stable Diffusion training is insufficient (R4). R1 suggests augmentations (blurring, color space) to boost OOD performance.
- **Frozen VAE from Stable Diffusion:** No discussion of why a pretrained VAE is used without any MRI‑specific finetuning (R1). Integration with existing text‑to‑3D models is suggested (R1).
- **Figure and formatting issues:**
  - Figure 4 incorrectly depicts acquisition as coronal; the actual slice direction is axial, and evaluation should be in that direction (R4).
  - Ablation sections A1 and A3 are nearly identical and should be merged (R2).
  - Hyperref links in the appendix point to wrong figures (R3).
- **Missing analyses:**
  - No evaluation of performance on anisotropic data (study assumes H=W=D) (R4).
  - No progression of PSNR as a function of distance from the conditioning slice; such a plot would assess the model’s ability to generalise across the volume (R4).
  - The k‑space reconstruction experiments show that X‑Diffusion is not on par with SOTA (R2).
