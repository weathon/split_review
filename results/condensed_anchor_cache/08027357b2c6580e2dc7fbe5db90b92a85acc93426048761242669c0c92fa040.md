## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MMAR, a joint image-text probabilistic model that combines MAR-style masked autoregressive image generation (with per-patch latent diffusion) with autoregressive text modeling. It uses continuous image tokens to avoid vector quantization information loss, applies a diffusion head on top of the LLM, and introduces v-prediction for low-precision training stability plus a two-stage training strategy. The model is evaluated on 18 image understanding benchmarks and COCO FID-30k for generation.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of MAR to multimodal joint generation and understanding** (Reviewers 2, 4). This combination had not been explored before.
- **Analysis of v-prediction for numerical error and low-precision training** is interesting and practically important (Reviewers 1, 4). The finding that v-prediction stabilizes low-bit training inspires future work.
- **Interesting ablations**: comparison of v vs. n parametrization, and a VQ-based variant of MMAR (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong understanding performance**: MMAR achieves competitive results against strong baselines (LLaVA, Chameleon, DreamLMM) on multiple benchmarks (Reviewer 2). It also complements existing MLLMs by explicitly modeling both P(I) and P(T|I) (Reviewer 3).
- **Low-precision training of diffusion models** is significant for deployment on resource-limited edge devices (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty** (Reviewers 1, 3). The main contribution is a straightforward extension of MAR (Reviewer 1, 3). The deduction of the optimal diffusion parameterization (Eq. 5 & 6) is seen as straightforward and not a major contribution (Reviewer 3).
- **Fundamental justification unclear**. The paper claims discretization is a bottleneck, but Reviewer 1 is unconvinced because (i) discrete representations (e.g., EMU3) achieve SOTA, (ii) data is inherently discrete, (iii) unified discrete representations are simpler. Reviewer 2 also finds the argument that full-image diffusion hurts understanding weak: masked modeling introduces its own noise (dropped tokens) and both approaches can operate on noiseless images for P(text|image). The claim that v-prediction is the “only” way to fix low-precision error (Line 113, 258) is questionable; other solutions (optimizer, architecture, noise schedules) exist (Reviewer 4).
- **Text-to-image quality is weak** compared to understanding quality (Reviewer 2). Generation results in the appendix appear low quality (Reviewer 1). Evaluation is insufficient: only zero-shot FID-30k is reported; more metrics (Geneval, T2I-bench) are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing experimental support** for key claims. No training curves are shown to demonstrate that low-precision training actually causes instability (Reviewer 2). A pure image-generation baseline (potentially trained from scratch) is missing to explain the T2I weakness (Reviewer 2). The two-stage training strategy does not outperform existing SOTA joint models; MMAR often ranks second best among joint probabilistic models (Reviewer 3). The paper claims “scaling laws” but only shows scaling behavior, not quantitative power-law relationships (Reviewer 4).
- **Serious citation and presentation issues** (Reviewer 4). Many works (MARS, LlamaGen, Chameleon, VQ-VAE, Transfusion, ROPE, SiLU, ImageGen) are not cited on first mention. Formats are wrong (abuse of \citet/\citep, missing spaces). The paper is poorly proofread (e.g., “PRABABILISTIC” in title, “has be focused”). These issues are pervasive.
- **Logic and notation confusion** (Reviewer 4). EmbeddingViT and VisProjector are introduced without explanation or citation. CFG is used extensively but never defined (even in Section 3.3.4 on Classifier-Free Guidance). Notation for autoregressive modeling uses the same symbol for both text and images, yet images use masked modeling, not left-to-right next-token prediction.
- **Unreasonable assumption in Section 3.2** (Reviewer 4). The authors assume ϵ_θ(x^t, t, z_i) follows a standard normal distribution without justification. In fact, ϵ_θ models E[ϵ|x^t], which approaches 0 as t→0 (Bao et al., ICLR 2022). The assumption is invalid.
- **Joint probability P(T,I) never properly defined** (Reviewer 4). Eq. (4) is claimed to be the overall loss for the joint probability, but it is not proven to be an exact likelihood or a bound; it appears to model marginals separately without interaction.
- **Missing experimental details** (Reviewers 2, 4). Results for 13 of the claimed 18 image understanding benchmarks are not shown in main text or appendix. Ablation on positional encoding for image tokens is missing. Details on the masking schedule and sampling procedure are absent. The conditioning of the per-patch Diffusion MLP is not described.
- **Speed issue not addressed** (Reviewer 2). MMAR is reported as slow, yet speed was a selling point of MAR. The role of the MAR decoder (which predicts mask locations) is unclear; it is not used in MMAR but could potentially speed inference.
- **Minor questions**: Whether EDM precondition (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022) can be analyzed for numerical error similarly (Reviewer 1). Derivation of the limit in L253-256 needs formal justification (Reviewer 2).
