## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the infinite-width limit of Neural ODEs (and their discretized ResNet approximations), showing convergence to Gaussian Processes (GPs) with kernels that differ depending on whether weights are shared across layers (autonomous) or independent (non-autonomous). The authors derive recursion equations for limiting covariance kernels, provide a dynamic programming algorithm to compute them, and prove that the resulting GP kernels are strictly positive definite under non-polynomial activations on the unit sphere.

### Strengths
- The topic of understanding infinite-depth and infinite-width residual models (e.g., Neural ODEs) is timely and interesting (R1, R2, R3).  
- The perspective of analyzing Neural ODEs as infinitely deep ResNets with shared weights, and showing that the limiting GP depends on whether parameters are shared, is technically correct and provides a useful distinction (R1, R3).  
- The derivation of the covariance kernel recursions and the dynamic programming algorithm appear mathematically sound; the proof techniques from random matrix theory are valid (R2, R4).  
- Numerical simulations demonstrate Gaussianity of preactivations and convergence of covariance kernels to their limits, supporting the theoretical claims (R2).  
- The proof that GP kernels are strictly positive definite (for non-polynomial activations on the unit sphere) is a specific contribution (R4).  
- **Contrast:** While most reviewers rated the contribution as fair (2), Reviewer 4 rated it good (3) and overall score 6 (vs. 3, 3, 5), noting that the analysis is beneficial for understanding Neural ODEs (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and missing literature:** The work is incremental over prior analyses of Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs), following a similar outline with only minor adjustments (e.g., summation term∑zⁱ instead of z^l) (R1). The shared-weight (temporally constant) case is essentially a randomly connected recurrent RNN, identical to the Cristanti–Sompolinsky model (without leak term), which has been analyzed for decades using dynamical mean field theory (DMFT). The authors do not cite or compare with that literature (e.g., Helias & Dahmen book; NeurIPS 2022 paper solving non-stationary covariance equations). This significantly reduces the claimed novelty (R2, R3).
- **Unclear or missing assumptions:**
  - Equation (4): It is not clear what assumptions on matrix A make this equation hold. Requiring the expectation of the outer product of A to be the identity is a strong condition; no distribution for the entries is stated (R2, R4).
  - The relationship between number of layers L, time horizon T, and step size β is not precisely specified. The claim that ResNet approximates the Neural ODE as L→∞ holds only if T is fixed (β→0). This needs explicit clarification (R4).
  - The controllability assumption on activation functions is introduced before Definition 3.1 but not referenced in Theorem 3.2. It is unclear whether this assumption is required for all results or only specific theorems (R4).
  - The scaling of β: For temporally independent weights, one would expect β ~ O(1/√L) rather than O(1/L), which affects the connection to SDE limits (Li et al., Hayou & Yang). This should be stated clearly (R2).
- **Presentation issues:** The paper is often hard to follow. Formatting problems: “width is infinity..” in text; Figure 1 legend does not match the plot labels (e.g., “Neural ODE, Shared ResNet, Indep. ResNet” vs. legend “Neural ODE, Shared ResNet, Indep. ResNet”); equation references missing parentheses; unknown label in Lemma C.2 (R3, R4). Some claims lack sufficient evidence (e.g., “infinite-depth residual networks with shared weights” is not adequately justified) (R3).
- **Missing experiments/analyses raised as questions:**
  - Figure 2: Shared-weight networks converge to the GP limit, but non-shared networks do not. No analysis of finite-size errors is provided to explain this discrepancy (R2).
  - Figure 3: Using the smallest eigenvalue as a metric may be misleading; numerical stability often depends on condition number (ratio of largest to smallest eigenvalue). The superiority of shared weights might be due to better conditioning, not larger minimum eigenvalues (R2).
  - Why is the model referred to as a Neural ODE rather than an RNN, given that weight sharing across layers is what distinguishes RNNs from feedforward networks? (R2)
  - How is parameter sharing achieved in ResNets where different layers may have different numbers of channels? If a simplified version is used, that should be clarified (R3).
  - Missing references in the first sentence of the second paragraph of the introduction (R3).
- **Overall novelty assessment:** While Reviewer 4 rated contribution higher, three of four reviewers (R1, R2, R3) found the contribution insufficient—the primary distinction (different GPs for shared vs. independent weights) is mathematically sound but does not represent a significant conceptual leap beyond existing understanding, especially given the prior DMFT results for random RNNs.
