## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Explainable Operations Research (EOR), a framework that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate actionable and understandable explanations for Operations Research (OR) decision-making. It introduces the concept of “Decision Information” derived from what-if analysis and uses bipartite graphs to quantify changes in OR models. The framework employs multiple LLM agents to answer user queries and produce explanations. The authors also introduce a new industrial benchmark for evaluating explanation quality in OR. Experiments compare EOR against OptiGuide, using both automated (LLM-based) and human expert evaluations.

### Strengths
- Tackles a relevant real-world problem at the intersection of explainability for OR and LLMs, a timely and hot topic.
- The integration of LLMs with OR is novel and interesting; the experimental results suggest the approach is effective as per ratings by LLMs and human OR experts.
- Example 3 is particularly useful for understanding the task and output.
- The paper is well‑presented and easy to follow, with a convincing and thorough experimental setup.
- The focused contribution addresses a niche but important problem—explainability in OR—and the proposed agentic workflow could be practically useful for practitioners.
- The framework provides users with insights into critical aspects of OR problems that have been often overlooked, enhancing their understanding and decision-making.

### Weaknesses
- The benchmark, listed as a key contribution, is not publicly available and is poorly described. Details are missing on how data were gathered, how tasks and queries were created, what metrics are used and their rationale, whether it reflects real‑world complexity, and whether it will be released. One reviewer notes that the paper states queries were “developed from scratch and managed in‑house” without clarifying whether this means manual creation.
- The paper compares EOR against only one baseline (OptiGuide). Additional baselines—including other explainability frameworks outside OR—are needed. It does not compare modeling accuracy on other public OR benchmarks.
- The central concept of Decision Information and its quantification via bipartite graphs is discussed but no results are reported for it. Its connection to the LLM workflow (e.g., how LLMs “sense” the process) is unclear.
- The evaluation of explanation quality focuses only on attribution and justification; actionability—stated as a key goal—is not assessed, and the benchmark does not include actionability metrics.
- Explanation quality assessment relies solely on LLM evaluation; no manual validation by the authors is performed to verify these judgments. It is also unclear whether ground‑truth expert ratings on explanation quality are part of the benchmark.
- The proprietary LLM used in experiments is not specified, introducing ambiguity.
- Failure ratios for the baseline method (OptiGuide) and a typification of errors are not reported. With only a small number of executions (e.g., two failures in 14 runs), the statistical reliability is questionable.
- The problem formulation lacks concrete examples to clarify what constitutes a “problem” and a “query” in the OR context. The focus on coding tasks is not well explained.
- The role of the bipartite graph in the framework is mentioned but not clearly explained; it is unclear why it is relevant or how it contributes.
- The paper does not cite related literature on agentic workflows or discuss how EOR’s agentic design compares to existing approaches.
- Table 3 has missing cells that require explanation. Table 2 and Table 3 formatting issues: the meaning of bolding is not indicated, best results are not highlighted in all tables, and metric arrows (indicating whether higher/lower is better) are missing. Figure 3 highlighted explanations are not labeled (attribution vs. justification).
- The paper does not analyze computational complexity or scalability of the framework—a concern given the use of LLMs and graph‑based methods.
- It does not discuss how decision-makers would interact with the system in real time, how mistakes are handled, or whether human supervision is possible.
- The literature review on explainability for OR is thin; relevant works such as De Bock et al. (2024) and Thuy & Benoit (2024) should be referenced, and explainability metrics from the literature should be discussed.
- **Minority contrast:** Reviewer ratings range from 5 to 8, with one reviewer (score 5, confidence 2) being considerably more critical. That reviewer found the novelty insufficient for ICLR, the related work shallow, and the evaluation unconvincing due to the lack of a public benchmark and manual validation, while also noting that the workflow appears simple.
