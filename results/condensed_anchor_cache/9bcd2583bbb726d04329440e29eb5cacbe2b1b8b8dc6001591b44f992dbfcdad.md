## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MotionBase, a million-level motion generation benchmark (15× the previous largest dataset) with multimodal data and hierarchically detailed text descriptions. The authors leverage this dataset to train large motion models and systematically study scaling of data and model size, synthetic data, and pseudo labels. They propose a 2D lookup-free motion tokenization method to expand codebook capacity and enhance motion representation. Evaluation is performed on multiple datasets with strong baselines, including OOD behavior analysis and ablation of motion quantization.

### Strengths

- **Large-scale dataset**: MotionBase is the first million-level motion generation benchmark, offering 15× the data volume of prior largest datasets, with multimodal data and hierarchically detailed text descriptions. This is a valuable addition to the community. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Identifies scaling importance**: The work systematically underscores the importance of scaling both data and model size for motion generation, and shows that synthetic data and pseudo labels can mitigate data acquisition costs. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Novel motion quantization**: A 2D lookup-free (LFQ-based) motion quantization approach is proposed to enhance motion information retention and expand codebook capacity, building on prior LFQ (Mentzer et al., 2023). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Evaluation on multiple datasets and models, discussion of OOD behavior, ablation of motion quantization. The dataset’s statistics are clearly presented. (Reviewers 1, 5)
- **Motivation and gap analysis**: Well motivated by showing the gap in prior work, lack of domain generalization, and limitations of existing metrics. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

- **Motion data quality concerns (fatal for some reviewers)**: 
  - The dataset includes one-frame pose data from datasets designed for pose estimation (Agora, MSCOCO, MuCo-3DHP) that lack temporal dynamics, potentially biasing the model toward static or jerky motion. The method for converting single-frame poses to multi-frame motions is insufficiently detailed. (Reviewer 4)
  - The quality of video mocap data is not evaluated; prior dataset Motion-X++ suffers from significant jittering and foot sliding. Without ground truth quality checks, the generated motion results are not solid or convincing. (Reviewer 5)
  - No demo video is provided, which is unacceptable in the animation community. The FID in Table 5 is extremely large, confirming motion quality concerns. (Reviewer 5)

- **Insufficient validation of dataset quality**:
  - Neither the estimated motions nor the LLM-generated text annotations (from Gemini-1.5-pro) have been manually or algorithmically checked for quality (e.g., "sorry..." responses). The claim that >1M texts were verified is not supported. (Reviewers 4, 5)
  - The video collection process lacks details: which shot boundary detection algorithm was used, and how many frames were input to the LLM for text generation? This affects reproducibility. (Reviewer 4)
  - The proposed hierarchical text annotation is not discussed as being used in model training; its contribution is unclear. (Reviewer 5)

- **Incomplete comparative analysis**:
  - The paper lacks a thorough comparison of varied methods trained on MotionBase to demonstrate its impact. It does not explore how different model architectures (varying parameters, transformer layers) respond to the dataset’s scale compared to smaller datasets. (Reviewer 2)
  - Missing baselines: no comparison with a standard transformer baseline (e.g., T2M-GPT, CVPR-23) to justify the LLM-based approach. It is unclear whether pre-trained LLM parameters are used and whether fine-tuning is LoRA. (Reviewer 5)
  - The LFQ method is borrowed from prior work (Mentzer et al., 2023) without new understanding; H2VQ from Humantomato (ICML-24) is missing for discussion or comparison. (Reviewer 5)

- **Issues with evaluation metrics and experimental fairness**:
  - No visual comparisons between motions generated from Motion-X vs. MotionBase; static images are insufficient—dynamic visualizations (video clips or interactive 3D renderings) are needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - Ground truth R-Precision and FID scores are missing from relevant tables, making it hard to contextualize performance. (Reviewer 2)
  - The FID in Table 6 (reconstruction 1.76) is higher than generation FID (0.166 in Table 3), which seems impossible; this may indicate poor reconstruction quality (e.g., inaccurate translations). (Reviewer 4)
  - The ablation study on static data is not fair: it is unclear whether the validation set contains static or synthetic data, which could skew results. A validation set free of static/synthetic data is needed. (Reviewer 4)
  - R-precision scores on MotionBase are relatively low, potentially weakening benchmarking. Implementing TMR (text-motion retrieval) may provide more accurate evaluation. (Reviewer 2)

- **Motivation and technical clarity**:
  - The motivation for introducing LLMs (rather than a standard transformer) is not clear. The paper misses a basic transformer baseline and does not clarify whether pre-trained LLM parameters are used or how the vocabulary mismatch between LLM and motion codebooks is handled. Fine-tuning method (LoRA or full) is not discussed. (Reviewer 5)
  - The contribution of the dataset is limited because video data originates from InternVid and WebVid and the collection process is from Motion-X and other methods. (Reviewer 5)
  - The paper’s layout is challenging: tables/figures are mid-page, many explanations are in the Appendix, and the flow is disrupted. (Reviewer 3)

- **Minor issues and missing references**:
  - Appendix D: "quantitative results" should be "qualitative results". Figure 4 lacks a y-axis label. (Reviewer 3)
  - Prior work on body part partitioning and 2D quantization (Pi et al., 2023, ICCV) is not cited, even though the paper claims to be the first to introduce these concepts. (Reviewer 3)
  - The ratio between synthetic, static, and real data in Table 4 is not explained in the caption. (Reviewer 3)
  - For occlusion/blurred cases (common in multi-person settings), how motion quality is ensured is not clarified. (Reviewer 3)
  - One reviewer strongly questions the necessity of citing a specific paper ([1] Steve-eye) and requests justification. (Reviewer 5)

- **Missing experiments and unanswered questions**:
  - Scalability of different architectures: have the authors fine-tuned existing methods (MotionGPT, MoMask) with larger parameter settings on MotionBase? (Reviewer 2)
  - What do the authors gain from scaling experiments? Is there any sign of emergence? The shown examples are common cases. (Reviewer 4)
  - Do supervised labels contain only motion tokens or both text and motion tokens? (Reviewer 4)
  - Has zero-shot text testing been performed (e.g., "The old man with a broken leg is walking forward slowly with a crane")? The largest model's performance on such prompts is not shown. (Reviewer 4)
  - How do the authors verify the correctness/accuracy of pose estimation? What are the desired properties of a new metric? (Reviewer 1)
  - How is the vocabulary mismatch between the LLM tokenizer and motion codebook handled? What is the efficiency compared to the fastest method, MotionLCM (ECCV-24)? (Reviewer 5)
