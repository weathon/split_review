## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **eSpark**, a framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate exploration functions in a zero-shot manner for pruning the action space in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). Using only a basic task description, eSpark iteratively refines exploration functions via policy feedback and evolutionary search. It is evaluated across 15 scenarios in inventory management and traffic light control, achieving average performance gains of 34.4% and 9.9% respectively, and a 29.7% improvement in scalability for environments with over 500 agents.

### Strengths
- **Innovative use of LLMs**: The paper presents a novel integration of LLMs into MARL for action space pruning, generating exploration functions without manual intervention or complex prompt engineering.
- **Scalability and generalization**: eSpark handles scenarios with over 500 agents and generalizes across two different domains (traffic control and inventory management), demonstrating wide applicability.
- **Performance gains**: eSpark consistently outperforms baseline MARL methods (e.g., IPPO) and heuristic/random pruning approaches, with substantial improvements in both tasks.
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-structured, with effective figures and tables illustrating the improvements. One reviewer particularly noted the clarity and good ablation studies.
- **Ablation studies**: The paper provides solid ablation studies supporting the proposed method. (One reviewer – rating 6 – emphasized this as a strength.)
- **Ease of combination**: eSpark requires no complex prompt engineering and can be easily combined with existing MARL algorithms.

### Weaknesses
- **High training costs**: eSpark requires multiple iterations, each generating *k* exploration functions and evaluating all state-action pairs, substantially increasing GPU memory and training time. The paper does not analyze how the number of iterations impacts final performance, nor does it quantify the computational overhead relative to standard MARL baselines.
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees**: There is no guarantee that LLM-generated exploration functions preserve optimal actions; stochastic LLM outputs could lead to suboptimal or detrimental pruning and poor local optima. The convergence properties are not analyzed.
- **Limited experimental environments**: Experiments are confined to two specific tasks (logistics and traffic control) with homogeneous agents. The paper does not test on classical MARL benchmarks (e.g., SMAC), sparse reward settings, or heterogeneous agents, raising questions about generalizability. One reviewer (rating 3) doubted whether experiments truly represent complex real-world environments.
- **Missing baselines**: Comparisons are limited to MARL methods and heuristic/random pruning; the paper omits comparisons with existing LLM-based methods for action pruning (e.g., [1-4]) and straightforward rule-based action space pruning techniques.
- **No analysis of generated action masks**: The paper focuses on overall performance without providing detailed case studies, analysis of the action masks produced by the LLM, or investigation of learned pruning patterns.
- **Lack of iterative performance analysis**: Training curves and the effect of the number of eSpark iterations on performance are not shown. (Reviewer 1 requested these visualizations.)
- **Only IPPO as base MARL algorithm**: The paper tests eSpark only with IPPO; it does not evaluate performance with other underlying MARL algorithms.
- **Prompt sensitivity unexamined**: The paper states no complex prompt engineering is needed, but does not experiment with different prompts or analyze their effect on exploration function quality.
- **LLM error handling**: The LLM checker can generate flawed exploration functions (e.g., variable misuse, misaligned task logic). The paper provides limited discussion on how such errors are detected or mitigated, and lacks fallback mechanisms to prevent performance degradation.
- **Reward feedback may be insufficient**: Relying solely on scalar rewards may not capture policy state; more informative feedback (e.g., reward components) could improve refinement.
- **Independent action masks without collaboration**: Each agent generates its action mask separately; no mechanism enforces inter-agent coordination, potentially limiting utility in strongly cooperative tasks.
- **Unfair computational efficiency comparison**: Comparing LLM-based methods with simpler algorithms may be unfair due to inherently higher complexity and resource demands of LLMs.
- **Continuous action spaces**: eSpark is not designed for continuous action spaces; the paper does not discuss this limitation or possible extensions.
- **Choice of benchmarks**: The selected benchmarks come primarily from operations research, not classical MARL. The rationale for this choice is not fully justified.
