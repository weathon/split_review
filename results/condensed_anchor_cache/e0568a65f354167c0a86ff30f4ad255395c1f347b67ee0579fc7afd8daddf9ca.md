## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes pFedRLLA, a reinforcement learning-based layer-wise aggregation method for personalized federated learning (PFL). It separates neural network layers into body (shared knowledge, aggregated by data size) and head (personalized knowledge, aggregated by a DRL agent using DDPG). A multi-head autoencoder reduces state space. Experiments on benchmark datasets show up to +3.1% accuracy improvement and up to 20.5% reduction in global rounds. The three reviewers give scores 6, 3, 6. One reviewer (score 3, high confidence) is strongly negative about novelty, feasibility of DRL in FL, and reward design; the other two (score 6, fair confidence) find the approach novel, well-organized, and empirically strong. There is disagreement on whether the contribution is sufficient.

### Strengths
1. **Novel use of RL for aggregation weights.** Applying deep reinforcement learning (DDPG) to learn personalized aggregation weights for classifier heads is a fresh perspective (Reviewers 1 and 3). One reviewer (Reviewer 2) disagrees, stating the concept is not novel and contribution marginal compared to existing RL-based FL methods and multi-armed bandits.
2. **State-of-the-art empirical results.** The method consistently outperforms baselines in accuracy (up to +3.1%) and convergence speed (global rounds reduced by up to 20.5%) across benchmark datasets with varying heterogeneity (Reviewers 1 and 3; Reviewer 2 does not comment on performance but questions its significance).
3. **Practical dimension reduction choice.** The use of a multi-head autoencoder instead of PCA is more practical (Reviewer 2).
4. **Code provided.** Source code is available (Reviewer 2).
5. **Well-organized and clear.** The paper is easy to understand with clear figures (Reviewers 1 and 3). Note: Reviewer 1 also notes clarity issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty in layer-wise aggregation.** The paper claims layer-wise design as novelty, but prior work exists: pFedLA achieves finer-grained layer-wise aggregation via hypernetworks; FedPAC also separates body and head with more detailed mathematical logic; weighted averaging by data size for body aggregation is trivial (Reviewer 1). One reviewer (Reviewer 2) further argues the entire concept is not sound or novel given plenty of PFL works using RL and other methods.
2. **Lack of theoretical analysis.** No theoretical justification why RL-based methods work better—only intuitive reasoning (Reviewer 1). For a limited number of FL rounds, it is doubted whether accuracy-related rewards (r1, r2) can be effective; an ablation study of r1, r2, r3 is requested (Reviewer 2).
3. **DRL convergence and training impracticality.** DRL requires many experiences; is the number of FL rounds sufficient for the DRL policy to converge, or must it be trained in advance? (Reviewer 2). Similarly, training the multi-head autoencoder in advance is unclear; if either is pre-trained, how is the dataset obtained before FL? (Reviewer 2). These feasibility concerns make the approach suspect for realistic FL scenarios.
4. **Reward design issues.** The hyperparameter β for weighting rewards r1, r2, r3 is not explained; in the code it is set to {1, 1, -2} with no deeper consideration (Reviewers 1 and 3). The reward in Eq. (1d) seems structured so that weights closer to the similarity vector give smaller reward, which encourages smaller weight for similar clients—contradicting the intended goal of promoting personalization from similar clients (Reviewer 2).
5. **Missing constraint handling.** It is unclear how the weights p_k from DDPG are constrained to sum to 1 (Reviewer 2).
6. **Missing baselines and references.** Some similar work (e.g., [1]) should be discussed in the main paper (Reviewer 3). Also, only DDPG is considered; other RL methods should be discussed or tried (Reviewer 1).
7. **Clarity and completeness.** Descriptions of figures and tables (Figure 1, Table 2) are excessively long in captions while main text is weak; figure description includes redundant content (Reviewer 1). The name of Algorithm 2 is missing (Reviewer 3).
