## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a parameterization for residual networks that combines \(\mu\)P parameterization with a \(1/\sqrt{\text{depth}}\) scaling on residual branches, aiming to achieve hyperparameter transfer across both width and depth. Theoretical motivation comes from DMFT analysis showing stable feature learning in the infinite width and depth limit. Experiments on ResNets and Vision Transformers on CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet demonstrate transfer of optimal learning rates across scales.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important practical problem: reducing the cost of hyperparameter tuning by enabling transfer across both width and depth.
- Well-written, with a clear combination of theory and experiment.
- Provides convincing empirical evidence on realistic architectures (ResNets, Vision Transformers) and datasets (CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, ImageNet) for hyperparameter transfer.
- Theory using DMFT and NTK motivates the parameterization and establishes a stable infinite-width/depth limit for feature learning.
- Future directions highlight the potential for studying depth-width trade-offs.

### Weaknesses
- One reviewer (rating 6) was more critical, particularly on the limited novelty of the \(1/\sqrt{\text{depth}}\) scaling and the short training duration; another reviewer conditioned acceptance on clarification of the performance degradation observed in Figure 1.
- **Limited experimental evaluation**: Most experiments are run for only 10–20 epochs, leaving it unclear whether hyperparameter transfer holds for longer training. One reviewer argued experiments are confined to a single task (CIFAR-10), though other reviewers note results on Tiny ImageNet and ImageNet, suggesting the presentation could be clearer. On CIFAR-10, the proposed parameterization underperforms compared to \(\mu\)P scaling (Fig. 1), raising questions about its practical utility.
- **Gap between theory and practice**: The theoretical derivation relies on infinite width/depth limits, simplified models (linear networks, gradient flow, time-invariant kernels under \(\gamma_0\to 0\)), and continuous-time dynamics. It is unclear how these assumptions transfer to finite-size networks trained with discrete SGD, especially in the regime where increasing scale improves performance (e.g., training loss decreases with depth in Fig. 1b). The DMFT is based on gradient flow, yet is used to transfer learning rates that typically lie far from the asymptotically low learning rate regime. The paper lacks a discussion of these limitations.
- **Performance concerns**: In Figure 1, the proposed parameterization reaches higher loss levels compared to alternatives. Whether the parameterization can achieve optimal hyperparameter values in practice needs clarification, especially given the poorer performance on CIFAR-10 relative to \(\mu\)P.
- **Innovation and related work**: The \(1/\sqrt{\text{depth}}\) scaling has been extensively studied; a minority reviewer questioned the novelty of the proposed method. The paper does not compare with alternative approaches such as “rezero” initialization or justify why \(1/\sqrt{\text{depth}}\) is used instead of \(1/\text{depth}\) or 0. It is also unclear whether the observed transferability is due to the \(1/\sqrt{\text{depth}}\) scaling or the \(\mu\)P parameterization. Broader references to \(\mu\)P’s maximal updates property and adaptive kernel methods would strengthen the literature context.
- **Need for better exposition of \(\mu\)P**: The paper depends heavily on \(\mu\)P parameterization, which is not widely used. Multiple reviewers requested a more comprehensive introduction, including how it differs from standard initialization, what mathematical properties lead to transferability, and its practical implementation overhead.
- **Applicability and cost**: The limited range of architectures (primarily ResNets, with one Vision Transformer experiment) leaves generality unclear. The paper does not discuss computational costs compared to alternatives, nor the applicability to other architectures like DenseNets.
- **Transferability of theoretical conclusions**: The exact DMFT solutions are derived for a deep linear network acting on a single data point. It is unclear to what extent these conclusions transfer to realistic nonlinear networks with multiple data points.
