## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents an extensive empirical comparison between pre-trained (PT) models and Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) in few-shot learning, using effect size (Cohen’s d) as a statistical measure and a “diversity coefficient” to characterize datasets. It finds that for low-diversity datasets, PT slightly outperforms MAML; for high-diversity datasets, MAML tends to be better—but the average effect size difference is below 0.2. The paper concludes that PT is not always better than meta-learning and that task diversity is a driving factor.

### Strengths
- **Extensive empirical study**: The paper considers 21 few-shot learning benchmarks, including Meta-Dataset, and reports many results. (All reviewers)
- **Novel use of effect size**: Cohen’s d is applied to compare PT and MAML, providing a more robust measure than standard p-values when sample sizes are large. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Diversity analysis**: The introduction and empirical validation of task diversity as a key factor distinguishing PT and MAML is insightful and intuitive. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Well-written and clearly framed**: The methodology is clearly described. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Robust statistical methodology**: Using effect size and a variety of datasets elevates the evaluation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Problem value**: Studying whether PT always beats meta-learning is timely and important. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines and limited generalizability**:
  - Only vanilla MAML is used; more recent meta-learning variants (e.g., MAML++ with adaptive learning rates or improved gradient estimation) are missing. This may significantly affect conclusions about meta-learning’s performance. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - PT fine-tuning is restricted to the head only; modern approaches (e.g., BiT with full fine-tuning, FiT with selective parameter adjustment) are not compared. The paper’s conclusions might shift when evaluated against these state-of-the-art techniques. (Reviewer 4)
  - **Question**: Do the authors believe a comparison with MAML++, BiT, or FiT is feasible, and would it yield consistent conclusions? (Reviewer 4)
- **Poor presentation of results**:
  - All results are in tables with no visual aids (e.g., scatter plots, figures), making trends very hard to grasp. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4) Specifically, Reviewer 2 found the presentation “very hard to follow”; Reviewer 3 called it “very poor”; Reviewer 4 said “hinders quick understanding”. Tables should be moved to an appendix and replaced with comprehensible figures. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
  - Table formatting is inconsistent: captions lack clarity (e.g., “MAML5” not defined), seeds in Table 3 are unclear, model architectures vary across tables (ResNet12 vs ResNet50 vs 5-layer CNNs), and standard deviations/confidence intervals are missing. (Reviewer 2)
  - The abbreviation “Meta-Data set” appears in the text; should be “Meta-Dataset”. (Reviewer 4)
  - Table 6 should be in the main body, not elsewhere. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of explanation and justification for the statistical test**:
  - The paper does not explain why p-values and confidence intervals become zero with large sample sizes, which motivated the effect-size approach. (Reviewer 1)
  - The principle of the new test is unclear: what distributional assumptions are made about performance differences, and how can we assess confidence when rejecting the null hypothesis? (Reviewer 1)
  - The choice of 1% as a threshold for “significant” effect size is arbitrary and not justified; its relation to standard thresholds (0.2, 0.5, 0.8) is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
  - The discussion of effect size drawbacks is missing: it can be misleading when sample sizes differ or underlying distributions are non-normal. (Reviewer 2)
- **Training to convergence** may cause overfitting; why not use early stopping? Learning curves for PT and MAML are not provided to verify convergence. (Reviewer 2)
- **Which diversity matters?**: It is ambiguous whether Cohen’s d results are caused by diversity of *meta-training* datasets, *meta-test* datasets, or both. The paper should clarify which factor drives PT vs MAML performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing experimental details in main paper**:
  - Data splits (train/validation/test), number of samples per class, optimization algorithms, hyperparameters, and hardware details are not summarized in the main text. (Reviewer 2)
  - The averaging method for overall effect sizes is not described (e.g., is it an average over cases where the hypothesis holds?). Variance or distribution of effect sizes across datasets is not reported. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing citation**: Section 5 cites “feature reuse vs rapid learning” without a reference. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited novelty**: The paper introduces no new techniques or analyses beyond the empirical study; this is acceptable only if the results are intriguing, but the presentation undermines the contribution. (Reviewers 3, 4)  Reviewers 1 and 4 consider the statistical test novel, but others view the overall contribution as limited. (Reviewer 3 gives contribution “1 poor”, Reviewer 4 gives “3 good”)
