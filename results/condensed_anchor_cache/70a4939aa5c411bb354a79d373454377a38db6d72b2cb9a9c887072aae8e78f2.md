## Merged Review

### Summary
AlignCLIP reduces the modality gap in CLIP via two modifications: sharing all parameters between vision and text transformers (SharedCLIP) and an Intra-Modality Separation (IMSep) loss that pushes apart semantically dissimilar uni-modal embeddings while preserving similar pairs. Evaluated on zero-shot classification, linear probing, robustness to distribution shifts, and multi-modal retrieval, AlignCLIP shows consistent gains over vanilla CLIP. Reviewers praised the clear writing, well-motivated problem, comprehensive ablation studies (testing SharedCLIP and IMSep separately, varying which modality gets IMSep, and testing the rescaling mechanism), effective visualizations (DOSNES projections, alignment score plots), and tangible downstream improvements. One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, particularly highlighting the IMSep idea and the breadth of validation, while other reviewers (score 6) raised substantive concerns about novelty, unfair baselines, incomplete ablations, and architectural misrepresentations.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated and clearly written**: The paper addresses a meaningful challenge (modality gap in CLIP) with a clear flow of ideas and clear motivation (R1, R2, R7). Writing is easy to follow (R1, R7).
- **Comprehensive ablation studies**: The effects of SharedCLIP, IMSep (on images only, text only, their combination), and the cosine-similarity rescaling mechanism are tested individually (R1, R2, R6). The ablation for the rescaling effect is explicit (R2).
- **Broad evaluation across tasks**: Performance is measured on zero-shot classification, linear probing, robustness to distribution shifts, and multi-modal retrieval, providing a thorough scope (R4, R5). Gains are meaningful in both zero-shot classification and retrieval (R5).
- **Effective visualizations**: DOSNES projections and alignment score plots clearly show improvements in modality alignment and embedding spread (R4, R6). Figure 5 is particularly appreciated for offering clear insights (R6).
- **Novelty of the IMSep module**: The IMSep objective introduces a new way to handle intra-modality representations, ensuring semantically dissimilar image embeddings are spread apart without affecting similar pairs (R6, R7). It is simple to implement yet effective (R3).
- **Significant performance gains over CLIP**: AlignCLIP surpasses the baseline CLIP in most settings, with measurable improvements in downstream tasks (R6, R7). The method is technically solid with well-organized content (R7).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**:
  - Sharing parameters between vision and language encoders is a known approach (prior work by You et al. and others); extending it to all parameters (including the projection layer) is an incremental change without sufficient justification that sharing all parameters consistently outperforms sharing a subset (R3, R7). The paper does not discuss why the extension to the projection layer is necessary or how it would apply to non‑transformer backbones (e.g., ResNet) (R7).
  - The IMSep loss largely repurposes InfoNCE (pulling positive pairs apart rather than together) without substantial modifications (R3, R4). The overall contribution is thus limited (R3, R4), and the paper could be strengthened by connecting the approach more clearly to prior contrastive learning work (R5).
- **Unfair baseline comparisons and insufficient empirical support**:
  - Comparisons in Table 7 with CyCLIP and DFSep are taken from the DFSep paper, but training hyperparameters differ significantly: DFSep used 64 epochs, batch size 128, 1024‑dim features, ResNet-50 backbone, while AlignCLIP uses 30 epochs, batch size 512, 768‑dim features, ViT-B-16 backbone. These differences make the comparison unfair; either the baselines should be re‑implemented under the same settings, or the differences must be clearly stated (R2). Additionally, several methods compared in Table 7 were not designed to mitigate the modality gap, weakening the relevance (R4).
  - The paper repeatedly claims that “naive” alignment approaches (e.g., Liang et al.) harm downstream zero-shot performance but provides no empirical evidence for this claim (R4). No qualitative or quantitative results are shown to support it.
  - Missing comparisons with more recent state-of-the-art methods in each task setting (R1). Only vanilla CLIP and the authors’ own SharedCLIP are directly compared (R4).
- **Ablation and analysis gaps**:
  - It is unclear whether the improvements come mainly from SharedCLIP or from IMSep. The paper lacks an ablation of a standard CLIP trained with IMSep only (without SharedCLIP) (R6, R7). Such an experiment would isolate the contributions and show the expansibility of IMSep to non‑transformer models (R2, R7). Similarly, an ablation of original CLIP with SharedCLIP (no IMSep) is required (R7).
  - The improvement from IMSep may partly stem from using the additional pre-trained sentence encoder (SBERT all-mpnet-base-v2) at training time. An ablation without SBERT would clarify the real benefit of the loss (R6).
  - Hyperparameters α and β (weights of IMSep in the total loss) are reported inconsistently: R1 says in the rebuttal that L269 states α=1, β=0.5, but β is never introduced, and Appendix A.2 states α=0.5. No sensitivity analysis is provided (R4).
  - The paper does not analyze why AlignCLIP’s fine-tuned retrieval performance shows only marginal gains over SharedCLIP, and why SharedCLIP sometimes outperforms AlignCLIP (e.g., COCO T→I in Table 5) (R6, R7). No discussion or analysis of this degradation is given (R7).
  - The effect of the modality gap on downstream performance remains an open question; a larger gap can improve performance on some datasets (R3). The paper lacks a nuanced discussion of when reducing the gap is beneficial and what potential trade‑offs exist (R3).
- **Methodological errors and presentation issues**:
  - Figure 2 contains two errors: it depicts a Conv2d applied to image patches (which is a misunderstanding – CLIP uses a Conv2d with stride equal to patch size, equivalent to a linear projection) and shows max‑pooling applied to text embeddings (where the original CLIP uses an argmax operation to select the EOS token) (R6). These errors affect the clarity and accuracy of the architecture description (R6).
  - There is a contradiction in Sec. 3.1 (L198‑202): one sentence claims the denominator is strongly minimized, but the next says it is not strongly minimized (R4).
  - The method uses a single‑encoder framework, which differs fundamentally from CLIP’s two‑tower architecture. The paper does not analyze how the learned single‑encoder space aligns with or relates to the original CLIP space or how structural differences affect the modality gap measurement (R3).
- **Limited applicability and scalability concerns**:
  - The approach relies on transformer architectures for both encoders (shared parameters). It is unclear how it would apply to CLIP with ResNet or other non‑transformer backbones (R2, R7). Testing IMSep alone (without SharedCLIP) on non‑transformer architectures would strengthen the claim of broad applicability (R2).
  - The paper does not address scalability to larger backbones (e.g., ViT-L, ViT-H) or larger datasets (e.g., LAION-400M) (R4, R1). Similarly, the robustness of the SBERT-based semantic distance calculation across diverse domains and vocabularies is not evaluated (R4).
- **Other reviewer concerns and missing experiments**:
  - Sharing all parameters may degrade modality‑specific learning; a parameter comparison between CLIP and AlignCLIP (number of learnable parameters) is missing (R1). The objective may favor image‑based tasks; evidence for balanced learning is lacking (R1).
  - No hyperparameter sensitivity analysis is reported (R4). The paper does not track how the learnable temperature changes during AlignCLIP training, even though low temperature is known to be an important factor in modality gap emergence (R2).
  - The claim that better alignment directly translates to downstream gains needs explicit demonstration (R4). The paper should discuss specific cases where improved alignment correlates with task gains (R4).
  - The use of the “photo of {caption}” prompt in multi‑modal retrieval instead of just the caption itself is not motivated (R2).
