## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a block coordinate descent (BCD) algorithm for training deep neural networks and proves global convergence to global minima (i.e., interpolating solutions) under strictly monotonically increasing activation functions. It extends the result to ReLU activations by introducing skip connections (ResNet-style). Generalization bounds are derived using Rademacher complexity. The algorithm introduces auxiliary parameters \(V_{l,i}\) to approximate layer outputs and performs layerwise updates. The analysis assumes the overparameterized regime \(d_{in} \ge n\) (input dimension at least number of samples). Empirical experiments on toy data are provided.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important problem (global convergence guarantees for neural network optimization) and offers a novel BCD-based perspective.
- Writing is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow; algorithms are presented clearly and proofs are generally parseable.
- The work generalizes prior results that only guarantee convergence to stationary points to actual global minima.
- Studying BCD convergence is well-motivated from existing optimization literature; Assumptions 1 and 2 are reasonable.
- Remarks are included to connect results with related work.
- One reviewer found the proofs to appear sound (though another reviewer disputes specific steps — see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
**1. Circular argument and strong assumptions on the auxiliary parameters \(V\)**  
- Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 5.3 assume that \(\lambda_{\min}(V_j V_j^\top)\) is bounded by a constant \(C_V\) throughout training, yet this same \(C_V\) is used to select the step-size \(\eta_W^{(1)}\). This creates a circular argument: an assumption on future training behavior is used to set a parameter that controls that same training. Remark 4.1 mentions that \(C_V\) could be bounded differently, but no concrete derivation is provided, and it is unclear whether such a bound would work in the rest of the proof.  
- The analogous assumption that \(\lambda_{\max}(V_j V_j^\top)\) is bounded throughout training is strong and not justified (e.g., it might scale with \(d_{in}\) or \(r\)).  
- Remark 4.2 claims that \(C_V\) can be expressed in terms of \(\eta_V\), \(K\), and \(K_V\), but the reasoning is not clear and no explicit bound is given.  
- These issues are raised by multiple reviewers.

**2. Loose generalization bound and missing bound for ReLU case**  
- The generalization bound in Theorem 4.3 depends **exponentially** on the depth \(L\) of the network. Consequently, to obtain a non-vacuous bound one needs sample size \(n\) larger than an exponential in \(L\), which is impractical. Such loose spectral bounds already exist in the literature.  
- The paper claims “favorable generalization performance” (Abstract), but the only support is Theorem 4.3, and the bound is too weak to explain the empirical success of deep networks.  
- No generalization bound is provided for the ReLU / skip-connection variant, which makes the analysis less complete.  
- One reviewer suggests using techniques from Golowich et al. (2018) to avoid the exponential dependence.

**3. Alleged proof errors**  
One reviewer identifies three specific mistakes in the appendix:  
- **Line 796:** The equality \(\|\sigma(X w') - Y\|^2 = \|\sigma(X w) - X\nabla - Y\|^2\) holds only for linear \(\sigma\), but the paper uses a general nonlinear activation satisfying Assumption 2. This error is fundamental to the proof of the main convergence result.  
- **Lines 802‑803:** The claim \(\|I - \eta \Xi X X^\top D\|_{\text{op}} \le 1\) for small \(\eta\) is not justified; if \(A = \eta \Xi X X^\top D\) is not PSD, the operator norm of \(I-A\) may exceed 1.  
- **Line 871:** The inequality \(\|N\| \|N^{-1}\| < 1\) for small \(\eta\) is asserted without proof.  

A second reviewer, in contrast, found the proofs to appear sound. The existence of these alleged errors seriously undermines the reliability of the theoretical claims.

**4. Questionable necessity of BCD when activation is bijective**  
If the activation \(\sigma\) is bijective (Assumption 2), one could simply solve a series of linear equations by repeatedly applying \(\sigma^{-1}\) to obtain \(W_L, W_{L-1}, \dots, W_1\) that fit the data exactly. Why is the complicated BCD algorithm needed? This concern casts doubt on the novelty and practical value of the algorithmic framework.

**5. Conflicts and restrictions in the setting**  
- The paper states (line 098) that its analysis “does not require any overparameterization,” but later (line 273) it says it works in “the overparameterized setting” because \(d_{in} \ge n\). The authors should clarify and correct this inconsistency.  
- The requirement \(n \le d_{in}\) is not satisfied in many standard deep learning problems.  
- Additional restrictions: activation must be bijective (or ReLU with skip connections), layers must have equal widths (square weight matrices), and the algorithm uses a specific singular value bounding initialization whose benefits are not verified.

**6. Weak empirical validation**  
- Figure 1 (left) is missing the curve for layer 1; it is also hard to see that the last layer’s loss decreases exponentially to zero — more epochs should be shown.  
- The training loss in Figure 1 does not appear to converge to zero; longer runs are needed to confirm global optimality.  
- No support is given for the claim that the initialization (line 235) leads to “faster convergence.”

**7. Missing comparisons and justifications**  
- The paper does not compare BCD with other BCD variants in the literature, nor with standard gradient descent or NTK-based training. No experiments contrast test error or inductive bias.  
- The choice of different learning rates and different numbers of steps for each layer appears arbitrary and is not explained.  
- For the ReLU extension, the necessity of skip connections is not intuitively motivated; one reviewer asks whether the goal is to make ReLU bijective.  
- In Section 6, the weights of the last layer are fixed after initialization. How can a global minimum be achieved without training the last layer? An explanation is needed.  
- The paper claims BCD “has more accessible landscapes to optimize” (line 049) but provides no intuition or evidence for this claim.  
- No discussion of the computational cost of BCD compared to backpropagation.

**8. Minor issues**  
- Missing citation: Banerjee et al., ICLR 2023 (related approach not using NTK).  
- Typo in line 162: “filed” instead of “field”.  
- “Positive and negative components” in Lemma 5.1 should be “entries”.  
- Theorems 4.1 and 5.3 should explicitly note that \(\mathbf{W}\) and \(\mathbf{V}\) are the parameters at the last iteration \(k\).  
- Page 21 contains a proof without a corresponding theorem (likely a typo).  
- Assumption 2 uses the subdifferential, which is defined only for convex activations; a clearer condition (e.g., \(\sigma(u_2)-\sigma(u_1) \ge \alpha |u_2-u_1|\)) or explicitly assuming convexity would be preferable.
