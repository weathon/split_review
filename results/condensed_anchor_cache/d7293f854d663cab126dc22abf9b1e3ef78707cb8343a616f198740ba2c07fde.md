## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes two differentiable correction layers (rounding-classification and learnable-threshold) for learning-to-optimize applied to Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programs (MINLPs). A neural network first predicts a relaxed solution; then another network provides update directions for continuous variables and rounding rules for integer variables. Straight-Through Estimator (STE) and Gumbel-Sigmoid are used to handle non-differentiability. The method is evaluated on parametric MINLPs against exact solvers and heuristics under a 60-second time limit, showing speed and solution quality.

### Strengths
- Paper is well written and organized, clearly presenting the approach.
- MINLP is an important and interesting topic in learning-to-optimize, with real-world relevance (energy systems, transportation).
- The idea of integrating rounding operations into model training is sound. The proposed differentiable correction layers are a novel way to handle non-differentiability of integer outputs in deep learning models (Reviewer 4).
- Significant computational speed advantages over traditional exact solvers, especially as problem size increases.
- The combination of gradient information during optimization is a useful design choice (Reviewer 2).
- Authors claim to be the first to introduce STE and Gumbel-Sigmoid techniques in the context of learning-to-optimize for MINLPs (Reviewer 3). *Contrast: Reviewer 2 notes STE is not novel in ML; see Weaknesses.*

### Weaknesses
- **Constraint satisfaction and feasibility**: The method uses a soft constraint approach and cannot guarantee feasibility; integer variables make projection to restore feasibility difficult. While the percentage of infeasible solutions is low, the impact of different constraint functions (type, shape) is not systematically evaluated. The Lagrangian-inspired method (Table 5) may improve feasibility but needs more thorough analysis. The penalty parameter λ is crucial for balancing feasibility and optimality; its effects should be studied more carefully. Penalty methods in L2O often show weak generalization – the high infeasibility ratio in Table 4 supports this concern. Reviewers suggest designing special algorithms to handle nonlinear constraints rather than just penalizing them.
- **Novelty of core techniques**: While application to MINLP L2O is new, the Straight-Through Estimator is well-known in ML (Reviewer 2). The loss function essentially applies the Lagrangian multiplier method, which is a well-established technique; the paper should more clearly differentiate its application from existing uses of Lagrangian methods (Reviewer 3).
- **Scalability to varying problem sizes**: The network uses MLP with fixed-size inputs; it is unclear how it handles problems with different numbers of variables/constraints. Generalization to large instances (thousands of constraints/variables) is not demonstrated.
- **Computational experiment limitations**:
  - Time limit of 60 seconds for exact solvers is far too short; existing L2O works use at least 1000 seconds for challenging instances. A plot of %Infeasible vs. Time is needed.
  - Datasets perturb only right-hand sides of constraints; authors should perturb all parameters (objective coefficients, etc.) and include equality constraints to test generalization.
  - Modifications to Donti et al. (2021) problems (adding integrality, removing equality constraints) may inadvertently simplify the problem in favor of the learning method; analysis of how modifications affect difficulty is missing.
  - Experiments are only on simple nonconvex problems. Real-world instances from MINLPLIB should be tested; current instances are toy-like.
- **Fairness of comparisons and metrics**:
  - Selection of solvers (Gurobi, SCIP) lacks justification; need explanation of appropriateness for the specific MINLP structures.
  - %Unsolved metric is unconventional: claiming a “solved” problem when the solution is infeasible is problematic. Metrics should reflect finding feasible solutions within tolerance. Degree of infeasibility is not reported.
  - The time to convert an infeasible solution into a feasible one can be substantial, diminishing the significance of reported speed.
- **Algorithm 1 detail**: Only round-down direction is considered for integer variables; justification for excluding round-up is missing.
- **Presentation**: Several typographical errors exist; thorough proofreading needed.
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: One reviewer (score 6) is more positive overall, viewing the integration of rounding as a clear advantage over relaxed+rounding baselines, but still flags the feasibility concern. Another reviewer (score 5) is moderately supportive but questions scalability and parameter analysis. Two reviewers (score 3) are more critical of novelty, experimental fairness, and practicality.
