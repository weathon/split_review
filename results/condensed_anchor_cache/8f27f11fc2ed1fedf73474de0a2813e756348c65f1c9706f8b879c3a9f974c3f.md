## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DiffEqFormer, a transformer architecture reformulated as a non-autonomous neural ODE. It parameterizes attention and feed-forward weights as continuous functions of layer depth via hyper-networks, overcoming prior weight-sharing assumptions. The model is analyzed through spectral dynamics of QK/OV pairs and Lyapunov exponents for token-level sensitivity. Adaptive fine-tuning is demonstrated, enabling architectural changes (e.g., layer count) post-pretraining. Language modeling perplexity is evaluated on OpenWebText and WikiText-103, matching GPT-2 (Small/Medium/Large) performance, with a slight degradation at the largest scale.

### Strengths
- **Elegant original formulation:** The time-dependent weight implementation via hyper-networks is both elegant and original, effectively overcoming the limitations of weight-sharing approaches used in prior neural ODE transformers (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 4 finds it “extremely natural under the DiffEqFormer formulation.”
- **Adaptive fine-tuning capability:** The ODE formulation enables flexible fine-tuning (e.g., varying layer count by adjusting ODE solver steps) post-pretraining, which is a promising direction for model adaptation (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 highlights this as “particularly promising for post-pretraining adjustments.”
- **Lyapunov exponent analysis for interpretability:** The use of Lyapunov exponents to analyze token-level sensitivity is innovative and well-adapted to the dynamical system framework, providing new interpretability tools beyond attention visualization (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 notes similarity to Chefer et al. (2021) for better faithfulness; Reviewer 3 calls it “highly innovative.”
- **Spectral analysis insights:** Spectral analysis reveals increasing eigenvalue magnitudes across layers, which inhibits cluster formation compared to weight-sharing-based theoretical studies, challenging existing assumptions (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Comprehensive interpretability experiments:** The experimental setup for spectral dynamics and Lyapunov exponent analysis is very complete, with detailed visualizations that help understand model behavior (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-organized, with clear progression from theory to implementation; complex mathematical concepts are presented accessibly with effective visualizations (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 notes that the mathematical notation is “easy to follow,” which is rare for ODE papers.
- **Research potential:** The approach has strong potential to serve as a starting point for further research, e.g., on adaptive fine-tuning and time-dependent weights (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty:** The core ODE formulation of transformers is directly borrowed from Lu et al. (2019) without significant theoretical advancement. The analysis methodology closely follows Geshkovski et al. (2023a,b) without introducing new analytical tools. The specific design of time-dependent weight parametrization lacks theoretical justification; choices appear arbitrary without clear connection to model dynamics (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 also notes the paper lacks a rigorous justification for why this formulation is superior to discrete-layer models given the computational overhead.
- **Insufficient analysis rigor:** Spectral analysis lacks formal theoretical guarantees and a clear relationship to model performance; it remains descriptive without a predictive framework. The Lyapunov exponent analysis is essentially equivalent to analyzing the Jacobian of transformer blocks, which is input-dependent and does not provide generalizable insights. Many claims are supported by cherry-picked empirical examples rather than a broader theoretical framework or statistical validation (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing downstream evaluation:** No evaluation of zero-shot, few-shot, or downstream task performance (e.g., reasoning, reading comprehension, scientific knowledge) is conducted, leaving a significant gap in understanding the model's practical capabilities (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 suggests zero-shot and 5-shot evaluation on benchmarks from the Pythia paper (Biderman et al., 2023). Reviewer 4 specifically misses comparisons with modern SOTA LLMs like Llama-3.2-1b or Gemma-2-2b.
- **Scaling and performance concerns:** Performance degrades at GPT-large scale, and there are no experiments at billion-parameter scales, raising doubts about viability for large, state-of-the-art models (Reviewer 3). Training is computationally and memory-intensive: for GPT-medium, DiffEqFormer requires 1.8B parameters (with d_emb=144) compared to the original 345M (Reviewer 2). The memory overhead during training and inference is not clarified, nor how it scales with model size and d_emb (Reviewer 3 question). No section compares training time, FLOPs, or GPU memory with vanilla transformers (Reviewer 4 question).
- **Inadequate ablation and sensitivity analysis:** The paper does not sufficiently justify architectural choices, particularly the dimension of time embeddings (d_emb). Performance appears sensitive to this hyperparameter, but limited guidance is given for optimal selection across model scales (Reviewers 1, 3). Ablation studies are not comprehensive enough to validate design choices (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues in Section 5.1:** The discussion of attractive/repulsive scenarios (Section 5.1) is disconnected and hard to parse. The example of V=−I_d leading to model collapse is unclear. The conclusion (L336) that “positive values from QK pairs and copying behavior in OV pairs hint at induction heads” seems mismatched with the explanation that QK magnitude and OV positiveness are what matter. This section needs clearer explanation (Reviewer 4). Additionally, the paper structure is somewhat disjointed, with early sections largely reiterating existing work (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor clarity issues:** Figure 1-b could have arrows in blue to connect with Figure 1-a (Reviewer 4). Typos: L304 “has” → “have”, L322 “model collapse” → “collapses”, Figure 6 legend “cratch” → “scratch” (Reviewer 4).
- **Connectivity between theory and practice:** The relationship between the theoretical analysis (spectral, Lyapunov) and practical benefits (e.g., performance improvements) is not clearly established. Reviewers 1 and 4 question how insights from the analysis inform or explain observed empirical results.
- **Other requested experiments:** Reviewer 4 asks about interpretability results shown alongside attention matrices in Section 5.2 to make differences evident. Another question is whether training at randomized time steps (as in diffusion models) is feasible and could enable more flexible adaptive fine-tuning.

**Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 is significantly more negative (rating 3, contribution 2), emphasizing limited novelty and insufficient analysis rigor. Reviewers 2 and 3 are moderately positive (rating 6, contribution 3), and Reviewer 4 is very positive (rating 8, contribution 3), praising originality and potential. The paper is stronger as an analysis and framework contribution than as an empirical one, and most reviewers agree that adoption of current SOTA models and downstream evaluation would greatly strengthen the work.
