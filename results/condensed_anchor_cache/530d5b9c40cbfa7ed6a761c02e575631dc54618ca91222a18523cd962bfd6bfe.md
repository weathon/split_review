## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a learnable invisible backdoor attack for diffusion models. It uses a bi-level optimization framework to generate imperceptible triggers for both unconditional and conditional diffusion models, including text-guided inpainting. Experiments show effectiveness and stealthiness.

### Strengths
- First work to demonstrate backdoor attack in text-guided image editing/inpainting pipelines (conditional diffusion models), and also works on inpainting tasks (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- The proposed attack is applicable to both unconditional and conditional diffusion models, exposing backdoor risks in training (Reviewer 1,4).
- The optimization framework learns invisible triggers, improving stealthiness and robustness over prior manual visible patterns (Reviewer 1,4). One reviewer (4) highlights this as an innovative approach that opens a fresh domain.
- Extensive experiments across various samplers and datasets confirm effectiveness and imperceptibility (Reviewer 1,4). Reviewer 4 praises clarity and supplementary materials.

### Weaknesses
- **Practical motivation and threat scenario**: The benefit of backdooring a diffusion model is unclear—the adversary and the end user may be the same person. A more compelling attacker scenario is needed (Reviewer 1). For unconditional cases, the need for an invisible trigger is poorly motivated: the input is already random noise, so subtle manipulation is unnoticeable (Reviewer 1). Moreover, the assumption that an attacker controls both the bi-level training and the noise used at inference is unrealistic; if the attacker can dictate the inference noise, trigger visibility becomes less significant (Reviewer 3).
- **Trigger visibility**: Despite claims of invisibility, the backdoor is clearly visible in some figures (e.g., Fig. 7 shows tampered images). This raises questions about the meaning of “invisible” in this context (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of comparison with prior works**: No comparison with existing backdoor attacks on diffusion models (e.g., previous visible-pattern attacks) is provided (Reviewer 2). Additionally, the technical novelty is limited: the bi-level optimization approach is a direct application of prior classification backdoor methods (Doan et al., ICCV 2021; Doan et al., NeurIPS 2021), which are not cited. The adaptation to diffusion models is not thoroughly justified (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient experiments**: Missing an ablation study for the norm bound of the trigger, a crucial hyperparameter (Reviewer 3). The actual value of the norm bound C used in experiments is not stated (Reviewer 3). Training details are omitted, including overall training time, hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes, optimization algorithms for inner/outer loops), and computational resources needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing defense evaluation**: The attack is not evaluated against existing defense mechanisms that rely on input perturbation or other countermeasures. Discussion of defensive strategies and their practical challenges is lacking (Reviewer 1,3,4). The interaction between the proposed invisible trigger and existing watermarking/fingerprinting techniques is not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **Convergence concerns**: The two-level optimization lacks rigorous convergence analysis. There is ambiguity about ensuring convergence if inner/outer problems fail to converge. Only empirical observation (“generally converges within 50 epochs”) is given; no theoretical guarantees or study of how different norm bounds affect convergence speed and stability (Reviewer 4).
