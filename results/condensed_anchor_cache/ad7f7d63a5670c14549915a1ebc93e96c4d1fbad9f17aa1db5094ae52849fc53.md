## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MOESR, a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm for image super-resolution that optimizes multiple metrics (PSNR, SSIM, perceptual losses) by decomposing the problem into sub-problems and using an improved differential evolution algorithm. It is evaluated on five benchmarks and two multi-objective tasks. Reviewers find the paper easy to follow and note the novelty of applying evolutionary strategies to SR, but they raise serious concerns about the necessity of multi-objective optimization, overclaimed contributions, limited gains, weak justification for gradient-free optimization, missing comparisons, and poor visualization.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and has clear motivation (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- First use of an evolutionary strategy for multi-objective super-resolution (Reviewer 4, confidence 5).
- Sufficient ablation study (Reviewer 4).
- Aims at a novel approach and provides results over various datasets and models (Reviewer 3, despite overall negative assessment).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of necessity for multi-objective optimization:** Reviewers 1 and 3 question whether the objectives (PSNR, SSIM, perceptual losses) are truly conflicting. Reviewer 1 states no conflicts are observed between metrics. Reviewer 3 argues objectives aim at similar goals, so a weighted loss combination is more natural than Pareto MO. Pareto MO typically requires contradictory objectives, which is not justified here. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Overclaim of novelty:** The claim of being the first method to simultaneously address objective and perceptual metrics is contested. Reviewer 2 cites existing works (C1: perception-distortion tradeoff, C2: wavelet domain style transfer, C3: ADMM optimization). Reviewer 4 also states the first contribution is overclaimed and needs reorganization. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Small improvements and potential statistical flukes:** Reviewer 1 notes experiments show little advantage over baseline. Reviewer 3 states improvements in PSNR and SSIM (Fig. 3) are very small and likely within statistical error margins; gains may be flukes from population-based optimization. Reviewer 3 also questions how solutions in Tables 3 and 4 were selected—if chosen based on test set performance, that is unfair. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Weak justification for gradient-free optimization:** Reviewer 3 argues gradient-free methods are weaker and require more computation; since the model and losses are differentiable, the justifications (avoiding gradient vanishing/local optima) are unconvincing. Reviewer 1 notes computational overhead with eight 3090 GPUs. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Missing comparisons with recent methods:** Reviewer 4 asks about performance on transformer-based methods like SwinIR, as Table 3 uses only classic methods. (Reviewer 4)
- **Visualization issues:** Reviewer 3 finds Fig. 3 unclear and difficult to discern differences; suggests following the example from “A Deep Journey into Super-resolution: A Survey” for clearer figures. Reviewer 4 cannot tell differences in Figs. 1 and 4 and suggests using other images. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Unexplained results:** Reviewer 2 asks for more explanation of why IMDN on Manga109 and EDSR on Urban100 simultaneously achieve higher PSNR and SSIM in Table 1. (Reviewer 2)
- **Typos:** “an extensively” in Sec.1 and “table 2 It” in Sec.4.1 (Reviewer 2).
- **Evolutionary algorithm justification depends on Pareto MO:** Reviewer 3 notes that if Pareto MO is not needed, the evolutionary approach becomes unmotivated. (Reviewer 3)
