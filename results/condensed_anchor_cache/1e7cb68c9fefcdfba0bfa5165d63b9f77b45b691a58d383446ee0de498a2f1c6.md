## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method to train GFlowNets in stochastic environments by replacing transition dynamics with an entropy-ratio term to handle exploration-exploitation. The approach is evaluated on Hypergrid and TFBind-8 with added artificial stochasticity. However, the paper suffers from fundamental theoretical gaps, lack of clarity, missing experimental rigor, and poor presentation.

### Strengths
- The problem of training GFlowNets in stochastic environments is important, as deterministic assumptions are restrictive and GFlowNets are a promising method.
- The approach looks novel in addressing this challenging problem.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical justification is missing.** The core equation (3) introduces undefined quantities \(H_{\text{high}}\) and \(H_{\text{low}}\) with no definition or explanation. No derivation or intuitive justification is given for why replacing stochastic transition dynamics with an entropy ratio is valid, how it preserves MDP properties (e.g., probability mass conservation), or why the marginal distribution over terminal states matches the reward function. The paper does not address potential bias or instability from this substitution. The claim that equation (3) holds is unsupported; it lacks dependence on \((s_t, a_t)\) on the right-hand side, and it is unclear whether it defines a valid probability distribution over \(s_{t+1}\). Equations (3) and (7) appear identical, yet (3) includes \(s_t, a_t\) on the left but not on the right. Equation (4) is missing the distribution with respect to which expectation is taken. Equation (9) is ambiguous about what is being summed over. The abstract promises "detailed proofs" but none are provided.
- **Presentation is extremely poor and disorganized.** Key terms and symbols are left undefined (e.g., \(H_{\text{high}}, H_{\text{low}}\), \(\alpha\) is not concretely defined until Section 8.1.1 while Section 7's title refers to \(\alpha\)). Variables are used before introduction. The algorithm figure is confusing: \(s_{t+1}\) on line 10 is out of scope, reward \(R(s_{t+1})\) is unused, and line 15 ("adjust gamma to balance exploration and exploitation") is imprecise. The abstract frames the work as handling inherently stochastic environments, but Section 7 treats stochasticity as a tunable parameter. The alpha-randomness policy is misattributed to Machado et al., who introduced sticky actions (a different concept). There is no information on learning architectures, hyperparameters, or any details required to reproduce the experiments.
- **Experiments are too simplistic and lack realism.** Both environments (Hypergrid, TFBind-8) have state spaces that can be enumerated (TFBind-8 terminal space is \(20^8\)) and can be solved without functional approximation. Stochasticity is artificially added via \(\alpha\)-greedy exploration rather than arising naturally. The paper does not provide a compelling real-world motivational example where stochasticity is essential. The small-scale experiments raise concerns about scalability and generalizability to high-dimensional problems where function approximation is needed.
- **Plots are illegible and inconsistent.** Y-axis labels are missing. The same color does not always correspond to the same method across figures. Some methods (e.g., PPO) appear in some figures but are missing in others without explanation. Text in graphs is too small to read without zooming. Colors change between plots, making comparison difficult.
- **Missing baseline comparison.** The paper does not compare against the stochastic GFlowNet setting of Jiralerspong et al. (2024), which is the most relevant prior work.
