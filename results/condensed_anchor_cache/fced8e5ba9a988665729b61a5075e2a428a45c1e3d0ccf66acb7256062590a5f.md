## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers broadly agree that the paper addresses emergent language in a multi-agent RL framework, but they find the core contribution unclear, the writing poor, and the presentation of results inadequate. One reviewer (score 1) is significantly more negative, arguing the paper lacks novelty and impact entirely, while two reviewers (scores 3) see some value but note major presentation and clarity issues.

### Strengths
- The review of previous works on language emergence is well organized (R1).
- The overall analysis and statement of assumptions are sound; the visualization of the guessing game is nice, and there is formal grounding with technical detail (R2).
- The proposed multi-agent guessing game has greater complexity than existing signaling games, which could be useful for future work. The proposed properties and objective function show alignment with natural languages, e.g., Zipf’s law (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear contribution:** The original contribution on top of prior work is not clear (R1, R2). One reviewer (score 1) states that after reading the paper, they do not know what the actual contribution is; the paper essentially introduces a specific game that may not be novel and shows it works, without comparison, ablation, or demonstration of impact. The other two reviewers (scores 3) also find the contribution unclear, though less extremely (R2, R3).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations:** There is no comparison against a prior (weaker) environment or baseline to show that the proposed game or learning method enables new abilities or outperforms existing approaches (R2). The RL method discussion falls short of pointing out what makes it special, and no good ablation study is performed to understand what makes it work (R2).
- **All results in appendix, none in main paper:** No result figures, tables, or quantitative findings appear in the first 10 pages. The main paper should present at least key results, with method details or additional analyses in the supplement (R1, R2, R3).
- **Poor writing and clarity:** The abstract is vague and confusing; the introduction includes a long chain of citations without a coherent message (R3). The paper is not concise and goes on tangents (e.g., section 6.2) (R2). It needs significant revision to meet ICLR standards (R3).
- **Missing related work section:** The paper would greatly benefit from a dedicated related works section that clearly states its contribution and differences from prior work (R2).
- **Insufficient captions and explanations:** Figure 1 has an insufficiently elaborate caption (R3). Several sentences are vague or unclear, e.g., line 74 “self-organization in language games,” line 92-93, line 257 “effective guidance” (R3).
- **Unclear assumptions about the environment:** What is meant by “ontology”? Is it assumed that all agents discretize the continuous environment exactly the same way even before any language evolves? (R1). When communication fails, how is the topic node disclosed to the listener without language? (R1).
- **Unclear properties of the learning method:** How does mirror learning ensure continual learning (line 67)? (R3). How does the mentioned property (line 510) mirror patterns observed in human interactions? Please provide support and an example (R3).
- **Limited impact:** The paper shows that a language emerges, but it is unclear what significant problem is solved or what new capability is enabled. Without further discussion on impact, it is unclear how the work would interest other researchers (R2).
