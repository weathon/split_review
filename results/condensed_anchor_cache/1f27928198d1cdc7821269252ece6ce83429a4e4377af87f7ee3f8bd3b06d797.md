## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a physics-constrained diffusion model (PCDM) that solves inverse problems by combining a pre-trained diffusion prior with a likelihood term enforcing physics constraints. The method uses variable splitting (ADMM-like) to alternate between a diffusion regularization step and an optimization step that enforces data/physics consistency. It is validated on three tasks: full-waveform inversion, data assimilation, and topology optimization. Reviewers agree the applications are relevant but raise major concerns about novelty, missing comparisons to existing plug-and-play diffusion methods, insufficient implementation details, and questionable baseline fairness. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive about the intuitive framework, while another (score 1) finds the paper incomprehensible. Most reviewers rate the technical contribution as low.

### Strengths

- The method is simple and intuitive: alternates between a diffusion prior update and a gradient-based likelihood optimization, making it accessible for physics-constrained inverse problems.
- Validation is done on three very different and challenging tasks (full-waveform inversion, data assimilation, topology optimization), each compared against task-specific baselines and DPS.
- Applying plug-and-play diffusion model methods to physics-constrained inverse problems is relatively new and relevant for the diffusion model community.
- The paper is generally easy to follow (Reviewer 4).
- Numerical applications are relevant and results look interesting (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5).
- The variable splitting scheme for solving physics-constrained inverse problems with diffusion priors is a reasonable approach (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Marginal technical novelty.** The variable splitting idea for diffusion-based inverse solving is not new; it closely resembles prior works (e.g., Algorithm 1 of [1], Equation 8 of Song and Shen et al. 2022, and the plug-and-play framework). The only novelty appears to be the physical applications themselves. Several reviewers note that PCDM is mathematically equivalent to a special case of Li et al. [1] (Tweedie’s formula) and other existing PnP-diffusion methods (DiffPIR [2], DAPS [3], RED-diff [4], [5]).
- **Insufficient coverage of related work.** The paper does not discuss a wealth of methods for inverse problems with diffusion priors, especially those leveraging variable splitting / Split Gibbs sampling (e.g., Dou and Song 2024, Cardoso et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2024). No comparison is made with DiffPIR, DAPS, RED-diff, or the corresponding Related Work sections.
- **Missing comparisons with standard plug-and-play diffusion baselines.** The experiments omit DiffPIR, DAPS, RED-diff, and other recent PnP-diffusion methods that can incorporate the physics loss. SDA is excluded from Experiments 4.1 and 4.3, and DPS is omitted from Experiment 4.3, despite both being applicable.
- **Fairness of DPS comparison is questionable.** In Figure 3(b) the DPS reconstruction does not appear visually plausible (does not match Kolmogorov flow statistics), unlike in prior DPS applications (Rozet and Louppe 2023, Feng et al. 2024). The lack of detail on hyperparameter selection for DPS and other baselines raises concerns about reproducibility and whether baselines were properly optimized. Default parameters may have been used without adaptation, which could unfairly disadvantage DPS.
- **Opt w/o diff baseline performance is suspicious.** In Table 1, DPS and Opt w/o diff have nearly identical performance, which is surprising because pure optimization for severely ill-posed inverse problems typically yields inconsistent solutions. The authors attribute this to local minima, but adding a diffusion regularizer should complicate the landscape, not improve data residual (Reviewer 2). Moreover, Opt w/o diff recovers large structures surprisingly well, contradicting typical FWI literature (e.g., Virieux and Operto 2009) and the reviewer’s own experiments. Implementation details (optimizer, initialization, hyperparameters) are insufficient. Opt w/o diff was not included as a baseline for the other tasks, making the comparison incomplete.
- **Missing residuals for InversionNet and VelocityGAN in Table 1.** Without measurement consistency residuals for these baselines, a fair comparison is impossible. Given that Deepwave provides gradient computation, these residuals should be straightforward to compute.
- **Reproducibility concerns.** Important experimental and implementation details are missing: hyperparameters for DPS and SDA, specifications of pre-trained diffusion models, hyperparameter selection criteria for all baselines, and criteria for the early stopping of the likelihood optimization. The code and supplementary material were not provided.
- **Lack of ablation studies.** The paper does not analyze sensitivity to key algorithm design parameters: number of likelihood steps per iteration, the optimization threshold \(t_s\) (parameter that triggers optimization), and optimizer configurations (e.g., learning rate, convergence tolerance).
- **Methodological ambiguities.**
    - The regularization step in Section 3.3 lacks clear justification for its specific formulation. Two regularization hyperparameters \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) are introduced, but only \(\mu\) appears in update equations and the regularization step is independent of both.
    - The relationship between the Langevin dynamics (Eq. 8–9) and the optimization problem (Eq. 11–12) is unclear.
    - The ADMM formulation in Eq. (12) omits the Lagrange multiplier term, making the solution different from the true constrained problem.
- **Terminological unclarity.** The phrase “physics constrained” is not defined mathematically. It is unclear what constraints are being enforced, on which variables, and how (Lagrange multipliers, penalty, elimination?). The paper does not connect to the large body of PDE-constrained optimization literature.
- **Questions about experimental setup.**
    - Experiment 4.1: is the operator \(A\) a discretization of the d’Alembert operator? Is \(s(r,t)\) provided in the dataset?
    - Experiment 4.3: given it is a constrained optimization problem, how does the operator \(A\) map \(x\) to \(y\)?
    - Equation (6): the left-hand side should likely be \(x_{t-1}\) to represent a sampling process over time.
    - Equation (15): what is \(\hat{\epsilon}_t\)? Is it from Eq. (7) with \(x_{t_k}\)? The indexing between \(t_k\) and \(t\) is mixed.
- **Computational cost is misleading.** Tables 1 and 2 advertise fewer reverse steps for PCDM, but the method also uses 1000 likelihood iterations (each requiring iterative gradient-based optimization). For expensive forward models, these likelihood steps could dominate the total cost. The authors must clarify what constitutes one likelihood iteration and provide a detailed breakdown of computational cost.
- **Broader applicability questions.** Could the method be applied to image restoration, and how would it compare? For problems of moderate dimensionality (~5000), why not use Sequential Monte Carlo methods (which offer better theoretical guarantees and can avoid mode collapse by propagating multiple particles in parallel)?
- **Hyperparameter selection criteria for all compared methods are not defined.** Using values from prior work without adaptation is inappropriate given different experimental setups.
