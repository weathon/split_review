## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes replacing automatic differentiation (AD) with numerical differentiation to compute PDE residuals in physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). The authors claim that this hybrid approach is faster (up to two orders of magnitude), can handle tabulated coefficients, enables exact imposition of Dirichlet boundary conditions, and supports enriched inputs. Numerical experiments are presented. The manuscript is easy to read and reports honest timing comparisons. However, the reviewers strongly disagree on the contribution and soundness (scores: 1, 3, 6). Minority Reviewer 3 is more positive (rating 6, confidence 2) while Reviewers 1 and 2 are negative (ratings 1 and 3, confidence 5 and 4).

### Strengths

- **Addresses a fundamental pipeline limitation.** The paper attempts to find an alternative differentiation mechanism for PINNs, going beyond the usual focus on architecture or loss design (Reviewer 1).
- **Readability and clear motivation.** The article is easy to follow, and the experiments are clearly explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Honesty in reporting runtime.** The authors include time comparisons for PINN and preconditioned conjugate gradient, which is refreshing (Reviewer 2).
- **Handling of non-analytic coefficients and enriched inputs.** The method addresses cases where AD fails, e.g., PDE coefficients without an analytic form or when enriched input data are fed to the model (Reviewer 3).
- **Exact Dirichlet BC imposition.** The method allows strong constraint of Dirichlet boundary conditions (Reviewer 3).
- **Scalability and efficiency.** The proposed approach shows significant speedups; the numerical cost is independent of the complexity of the trained model (Reviewer 3).
- **Flexibility.** The approach can be incorporated into any physics-informed model (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Overclaimed novelty: The alleged AD limitations are solvable by existing methods/trivial workarounds.**  
  *Tabulated coefficients:* One can fit a smooth function (e.g., cubic splines) to the tabulated data and use AD on that function, or rewrite the PDE in non-conservative form and estimate derivatives separately. The paper does not compare against these straightforward baselines (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  *Scalar-field input:* Defining a JVP/VJP function for the scalar field is available in most AD frameworks and solves the problem (Reviewer 1).  
  *Dirichlet BCs:* There is a well-known technique using distance functions and an ansatz (Sukumar & Srivastava 2022, “Exact imposition of boundary conditions with distance functions in physics-informed deep neural networks”) that exactly enforces BCs without numerical differentiation. The paper does not compare with this method (Reviewer 2).  
  *Enriched input / neural operators:* DeepONet can be trained on tabular data with interpolation or non-conservative formulations; FNO does not use AD because its functions are explicitly discretized, not because AD fails. The authors’ claim that AD prevents physics-informed neural operators is problematic and undercuts the motivation (Reviewer 2).  
  **Severity:** High. All three examples are deemed “highly artificial” by Reviewer 2; any practitioner with basic AD knowledge would avoid the naïve setup presented.

- **Major literature omissions.** The paper fails to cite and compare with several works that combine classical discretization with PINNs:  
  a. Finite element method-enhanced neural network (AMSEA 2023) – uses FEM with a neural network on a discretized grid.  
  b. Physics Informed Neural Network using Finite Difference Method (IEEE 2022).  
  c. Hybrid Finite Difference with PINN for complex geometries (arXiv 2022).  
  d. hp-VPINNs (Kharazmi et al., 2020) – neural network trial space with polynomial test space.  
  These works are directly relevant and should be discussed and compared numerically (Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons.** The experiments do not thoroughly compare with state-of-the-art neural operators (e.g., FNO, GNO), which are important baselines for PINN-based neural methods. Without this, it is unclear if the claimed speedups are unique to the proposed method or achievable with existing approaches (Reviewer 3).

- **Limited generalization and lack of evidence.** The method is demonstrated only on 1D and 2D PDEs with Dirichlet BCs. It is unclear whether it extends to:  
  - 3D geometries or larger-scale problems (Reviewer 3).  
  - Non-rectangular domains (Reviewer 3).  
  - More complex boundary types (Neumann, Robin) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Nonlinear PDEs (Reviewer 3).  
  The current experiments do not provide sufficient evidence for broad applicability (Reviewer 3).

- **Dependency on external numerical solvers.** Relying on external solvers adds complexity and limits flexibility. The need to interface with such solvers may not be practical in all scenarios (Reviewer 3).

- **Presentation issues.** There are typos (e.g., Eq. (19) not properly aligned). The paper lacks a clear explanation of the limitations of the proposed method and how it compares to existing approaches. The experimental setup and results could be presented more clearly and thoroughly (Reviewer 3).
