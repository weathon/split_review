## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **Learning2Rewrite (L2R)**, a framework that detects AI-generated text by fine-tuning an LLM (LLaMA 8B) to rewrite input: the model is trained to make few edits to AI-generated text and substantial edits to human-written text. At inference, the normalized Levenshtein edit distance between original and rewritten text is thresholded to classify. Experiments across 21 domains using GPT‑3.5, GPT‑4, Gemini, and Llama‑3 show L2R outperforming RAIDAR and Fast‑DetectGPT by up to 23.04% AUROC in‑distribution, 37.26% out‑of‑distribution, and 48.66% under adversarial attacks. The paper also introduces a calibration loss to prevent overfitting. **Reviewers disagree on the paper’s contribution**: two reviewers (scores 6 and 6) praise its novelty and strong experimental evidence; two reviewers (scores 5 and 5) raise serious concerns about dataset construction, baseline fairness, missing details, and computational cost.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Using the LLM’s own rewriting behavior as a detection signal is innovative and simple. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Experiments cover 21 domains and four major LLMs (GPT‑3.5, GPT‑4, Gemini, Llama‑3). The method outperforms RAIDAR and Fast‑DetectGPT on both in‑distribution and out‑of‑distribution tasks. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Robustness**: Demonstrated strong performance under adversarial attacks (decoherence and rewriting attacks). (Reviewers 2,3)
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well‑organized, easy to follow, and includes helpful illustrations (Figure 1) and qualitative examples (Figure 2). Reviewer 3 also notes the methodology and training objectives are thoroughly documented. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Generalization**: The method shows good cross‑domain generalization and interpretability via edit distance. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Ablation studies**: Several ablation experiments (parameter impacts, training configurations, data diversity) are reported. (Reviewer 3)
- **Reproducibility**: The description is sufficiently detailed to reproduce the main ideas. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Dataset construction and relevance**  
   - The dataset contains only text that was originally human‑written and then rewritten by an LLM. This is analogous to a non‑native speaker using AI to polish writing, which reviewer 2 considers one of the less risky applications. It is unclear that this benchmark adds substantial value for detecting general LLM‑generated content. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The paper claims superiority of its dataset only by comparing to a less diverse version of itself, not to existing public benchmarks such as SQuAD, XSum, WritingPrompts, RAID, RuATD, or MAGE. A more meaningful comparison is needed. (Reviewers 2,4)  
   - Reviewer 4 additionally notes that the Datasets section is vague (“datasets … such as others”) and omits key detection benchmarks that also target real‑world scenarios.

2. **Baseline comparison unfair**  
   - For the “Llama Logits” baseline, the authors used the same LoRA hyperparameters found for L2R. This is not a fair comparison—equal effort should have been spent to tune hyperparameters for each baseline. The large gap between in‑distribution and out‑of‑distribution performance for Llama Logits (Table 2) suggests overfitting due to poor hyperparameter selection. (Reviewer 4)

3. **Missing experimental details**  
   - The decoding strategy (temperature, top‑p, etc.) used to generate LLM examples is not reported. (Reviewer 4)  
   - The prompts used to generate LLM examples are not specified. (Reviewer 4)  
   - No justification is given for choosing an average sequence length of 120 words. (Reviewer 4)  
   - The method relies on LLaMA 8B generating hundreds of tokens per prediction, which is computationally expensive. A plot of FLOPs vs. performance across methods is needed. (Reviewer 4)

4. **Ablation and validation gaps**  
   - An ablation study on the calibration loss is missing. Reviewer 1 asks how useful fine‑tuning is compared to no fine‑tuning (the “Llama Rewrite” baseline without training). The paper does not provide clear details of the Llama Rewrite variant.  
   - Reviewer 3 questions how the calibration loss prevents overfitting: Figure 4 shows training loss but does not compare OOD test performance with/without the loss.  
   - Why was edit distance chosen over similarity measures like MAUVE? (Reviewer 1)

5. **Limited scope of OOD evaluation**  
   - The paper only considers OOD by domain/writing style. Other OOD dimensions (different source models, decoding strategies, text lengths) are not explored. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Reviewer 2 notes the large performance gap of Llama Logits between ID and OOD confirms a disconnect between the dataset and real‑world scenarios.  
   - Reviewer 1 asks how OOD performance varies across individual domains.

6. **Hard cases not addressed**  
   - L2R appears unable to handle mixed human‑AI text (e.g., identifying which segments are AI‑generated). This is a growing real‑world scenario. (Reviewer 3)  
   - The robustness evaluation could be extended to include advanced paraphrasing tools and other adversarial strategies. (Reviewer 3)

7. **Gaps in related work and literature review**  
   - The comparison to RAIDAR is vague (“rewrite amount still varies across distributions…”) and does not clearly explain what L2R does differently. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Several seminal pre‑2020 works are missing: GLTR (arXiv:1906.04043), “Automatic Detection is Easiest when Humans are Fooled” (arXiv:1911.00650), and more recent benchmarks RAID, RuATD, MAGE. The paper should expand its literature review and possibly add these as baselines. (Reviewer 4)

8. **Other specific issues**  
   - Equation 3 could be simplified using labels +1/‑1. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Equation 4 is hard to parse; pseudocode or English would be clearer. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Table 1 shows a mis‑bolded value (“EducationalMaterial” should be “Llama Rewrite,” not “Llama L2R”). Also, statistical significance is not reported. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Multiple references have incorrectly formatted (missing capitalization) paper titles. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Is the method model‑agnostic? (Reviewer 1)  
   - Reviewer 2 asks why Fast‑DetectGPT drops so much on OOD (since it requires no training) and whether the paper’s findings agree or contradict the DetectGPT hypothesis (minor rewrites of model‑generated text have lower log‑probability).
