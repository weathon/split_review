## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes applying the Pyramidal Recursive learning (PyRv) method to compose word embeddings into multi-word unit representations, switching PyRv’s original subword units for word vectors. It evaluates on Croatian UPOS and DEPREL, reporting 77% accuracy vs. 34% for averaging and 71% for a single fastText word embedding. Reviewers gave scores of 1, 1, 3 (confidence 4–5). Two reviewers consider the work far below ICLR standards due to lack of novelty, weak baselines, and insufficient explanation. One reviewer (score 3) is slightly more positive about the empirical results but still finds the contribution limited.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, the problem is well motivated, and experimental results are presented in an accessible manner (R2).
- Empirical results on various ways to compose FastText word embeddings for UPOS and DEPREL are provided (R3).
One reviewer (R1) listed no strengths; the more positive reviewer (R3, score 3) highlighted these aspects while still noting limited novelty. The other two reviewers (scores 1) did not see a positive contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The approach is a variant of CNNs (cf. Kim 2014; Kalchbrenner et al. 2014) and does not adequately differentiate itself from recursive models without syntactic structure (Cho 2014; Le & Zuidema 2014; Drozdov et al. 2019) or from Socher’s recursive models. The paper mostly just applies an existing method (PyRv) to word embedding composition, a task of limited scope that is unlikely to be of broad interest to ICLR (R1, R2, R3; R2 asks for elaboration on differences to Socher’s work).
- **Missing baselines**: Only averaging and single fastText embedding are compared. Stronger baselines such as max-pooling, concatenation, or BERT-style pooling are omitted; some of these may preserve the single-word fastText performance (R2). No comparison against the cited recursive models (R1).  
- **Mixed experimental results**: The proposed method does not show clear improvement over the simple single fastText word embedding baseline (R1: “mixed results”; R2 notes that better baselines could match single-word performance).
- **Incomplete method description**: Parameters and the training loss (e.g., next word prediction?) are not specified; Figures 1–2 remain purely visual without referencing composition functions or their parameters (R2, R3).
- **Limited dataset scope**: Evaluation is conducted only on Croatian datasets. Benchmarking on existing multilingual or standard English datasets is needed to demonstrate generality (R2).  
- **Presentation issue**: Croatian phrases in Tables 3 and 4 should be provided in the original language alongside the English translation (R2).
- **Outdated context**: Word embedding research peaked around 2010–2015; the paper does not argue why this approach is relevant given current large language models, nor does it discuss potential integration into modern architectures (R2).  
- **Contributions too narrow**: All reviewers agree that the contribution is minimal (contribution scores: 1, 1, 1). While one reviewer (R3) rated the paper slightly higher overall (3 vs. 1), that reviewer still considered the scope limited and the paper not novel.
