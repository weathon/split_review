- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Hypernetwork-based Equivariant CNNs (HE-CNN), which use a dynamic hypernetwork to generate convolutional filters and linear coefficients from rotated inputs, aiming to achieve rotation/reflection equivariance without modifying the main CNN structure. The hypernetwork is trained to produce equivariant parameters by composing outputs from a non-equivariant parameter-pieces generator. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, and STL10 show competitive or better accuracy compared to original G-CNN (Cohen & Welling 2016) and some steerable methods, but below recent steerable approaches like E2CNN. All four reviewers assign a score of 5; three raise major concerns about scalability, outdated baselines, missing analyses, and methodological limitations, while one reviewer praises the clarity and novelty but also notes significant computational overhead.

### Strengths
- The idea of using a dynamic hypernetwork to generate rotation-equivariant filters and coefficients is novel and interesting (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The method allows any non-equivariant base model to be turned into an equivariant framework, differing from canonicalization-based approaches and retaining equivariance throughout the network (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is well-written with illustrative figures, making complex concepts accessible (Reviewer 4).
- The design is validated in small-scale experiments and achieves better or competitive results on CIFAR-10/100 and STL10 compared to original G-CNN and some baselines (Reviewers 3,4; reviewer 4 notes strong performance; reviewer 3 says performance is comparable; reviewer 1 says outperforms original G-CNN but not recent methods).

### Weaknesses
1. **Outdated and insufficient baselines / limited comparisons**  
   - The main baselines are the original G-CNN (2016) and only one steerable method (E2CNN) is tested on rotated MNIST, but not on CIFAR-10/100 (Reviewer 1). More advanced steerable methods exist and outperform HE-CNN (e.g., E2CNN, Steerable-CNN, LieConv); outperforming only the 2016 G-CNN is not convincing (Reviewers 1,3).  
   - The experimental validation is limited; results in Sec 5.1 use an extremely weak baseline (96% on MNIST can be achieved by simple MLPs), making it hard to judge informativeness (Reviewer 4).  
   - No experiments on non-invariant tasks (e.g., segmentation) are included (Reviewer 4).

2. **Scalability and computational cost**  
   - The method requires passing each input sample through the hypernetwork and main network \( |G| \) times; batch-parallelism is not possible because parameters are sample-specific unless averaged over the batch, severely limiting scalability to large groups, large networks, or large data (e.g., ImageNet) (Reviewers 2,4).  
   - The hypernetwork must generate weights for the whole main network; LoRA partially alleviates this but does not solve the fundamental scaling problem (Reviewer 2).  
   - The paper lacks a limitations section and does not discuss runtime or memory compared to baseline CNNs and G-CNNs (Reviewers 2,4).  
   - The method assumes all inputs in a batch have the same orientation; if not, different weights must be generated per input, which does not scale (Reviewer 2).

3. **Missing parameter counts and ablation analyses**  
   - Parameter counts are not reported, making it impossible to assess whether performance gains come from increased capacity rather than the equivariance mechanism (Reviewer 1).  
   - No ablation separates the effect of (a) relaxing kernel constraints (as in G-CNNs) versus (b) input-dependent weight generation. Gains could be entirely due to input dependency (Reviewer 2). Without such analysis, conclusions about why HE-CNN outperforms G-CNN are unsupported (Reviewer 2).

4. **Assumptions and theoretical concerns**  
   - The method assumes the transformation \( g \) applied to the input is known beforehand (Reviewer 2). If known, the equivariance problem is drastically simplified; in practice, this is unrealistic.  
   - Generating filters from rotated inputs raises issues because the translation-rotation group does not commute (rotation then translation ≠ translation then rotation). G-CNNs rotate filters, not inputs; HE-CNN’s approach could introduce inconsistencies, and standard CNNs are not perfectly translation-equivariant, potentially impacting HE-CNN more than other G-CNNs (Reviewer 1).  
   - The method uses hard-coded 90-degree rotations and permutation operations (Figures 2,3), which limits generalizability to continuous transformations like scaling or shear (Reviewer 1).  
   - Extending to larger groups like \( D_{4n} \) requires many forward passes through hypernetwork and main network, reducing novelty and making the approach similar to feeding multiple rotated images to a static CNN (Reviewer 3).  
   - The hypernetwork’s own kernels are also constructed by transforming a canonical kernel under \( g \), imposing the same gradient constraints as in G-CNNs; the claimed advantage of removing constraints is thus questionable (Reviewer 4).  
   - Steerable G-CNNs have a theoretical foundation (Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem), while HE-CNN is purely empirical (Reviewer 1).

5. **Presentation and clarity**  
   - Equations and figures are sometimes hard to interpret; e.g., the dimension of output \( a^i \) in Line 204 is unclear, and the connection between Figure 2 and the filter decomposition text is not clear (Reviewer 3). Notation is inconsistent and imprecise (Reviewer 3).  
   - The paper lacks a dedicated limitations section (Reviewer 2).

6. **Minor disagreement on performance**  
   - Reviewer 4 states that performance on CIFAR-10/100 and STL10 is “strong” and “better or competitive”, while Reviewers 1 and 3 note that the method underperforms compared to recent steerable methods and that the results in Table 3 (e.g., Z4-HE-CNN outperforming continuous-rotation models like LieConv, Steerable-CNN) are questionable without matched parameter counts (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 says the method is not competitive on deeper CNNs for CIFAR-10/100.

**Reviewer questions presented as criticisms**  
- What is the train/test split for MNIST? (Reviewer 1)  
- Is the orientation of the input assumed known? (Reviewer 2)  
- Could you elaborate on hypernetwork design? (Reviewer 3)  
- How does this relate to Zhdanov et al. (2024) on hypernetwork-parametrized steerable kernels? (Reviewer 4)  
- Could you provide computational complexity details (runtime, memory) relative to base CNN and G-CNN models? (Reviewer 4)
