## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PromptDiff, a prompt-based 3D molecular diffusion model for structure-based drug design. It uses a pre-trained geometric protein-molecule interaction network (PMINet) to predict binding affinity, retrieve high-affinity ligand prompts from a database, and steer generation via exemplar prompting and self-prompting. On CrossDocked2020, PromptDiff shows improved binding affinities and molecular properties. Reviewer ratings range from 3 to 6, with one reviewer (score 6) considerably more positive about the work than the other three (score 3).

### Strengths
- First adoption of prompting techniques for 3D molecule drug design (R1).
- Two novel prompting mechanisms: exemplar prompting and self-prompting (R1, R3).
- The idea of using exemplary molecules as prompts is well-motivated, reasonable, and makes the approach target-aware (R2, R4).
- Clear and logical presentation of motivations and implementation (R2).
- Figure 6 nicely illustrates the prompting idea (R4).
- Empirical results show generation of more realistic 3D structures and state-of-the-art binding affinities towards protein targets (R1, R2, R3).
- Enhanced drug-likeness, diversity, and synthetic accessibility compared to other diffusion-based methods (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: prompting and diffusion are common techniques from NLP and image domains; deeper insights for their choice are missing (R1).
- **Limited contribution and unclear scope of application**: unclear if PromptDiff can be used for pockets without known binders; the scenario of application is not clearly defined (R4).
- **Inference efficiency**: need to retrieve ligand prompts from database before generation, inheriting diffusion model inefficiency and making it even less efficient (R3).
- **Pipeline analysis insufficient**: the effect of each step (retrieval, prompting) is unclear. The claim that the model is robust to top-1 retrieval yet easily affected by selection is inconsistent and lacks empirical evidence (R1).
- **Missing baselines**: comparisons with recent methods are absent: Zhang et al., ICLR2023; Zhang & Liu, ICML2023; ResGen, Nature Machine Intelligence 2023 (R3).
- **Writing and clarity issues**:
  - Method description unclear, confusing use of symbols (superscripts, subscripts) (R3).
  - Notations \(\mathcal{M}\) and \(\boldsymbol{M}=[\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{V}]\) used interchangeably without reason (R4).
  - Sampling procedure in Algorithm 1 line 1 not explained (R4).
  - How are initial atom types sampled? (R4)
- **PMINet motivation and design**:
  - Why create a new binding affinity prediction model? It does not outperform GraphDTA; if not, why not use better methods? (R4)
  - What is the advantage of PMINet over existing binding-affinity prediction methods? (R3)
  - Relationship between ligand prompts and ground truth ligands not clarified; why not directly use the ground truth as prompt? (R3)
  - Why enhance protein atom features? (R3)
- **Focus mismatch**: abstract and introduction claim focus on optimizing multiple molecular properties (e.g., synthesizability), but actual focus is only on binding affinity; SA scores are mediocre (R4). The synthesizability aspect is not supported by experiments (R4).
- **Self-prompting degradation**: adding self-prompting surprisingly degrades the average Vina score in Table 3; this warrants investigation (R4).
- **Disentangling prompting vs. PMINet**: ablation discusses prompting without PMINet, but relative importance of good exemplars vs. learned interactions in PMINet is not disentangled (R2). Also, how to prompt without PMINet is not explained clearly (R3).
- **Diversity concerns**: while PromptDiff enhances diversity, it is not intuitive why using high-affinity prompts maintains diversity; further investigation needed (R2). Also, performance when database lacks high-affinity molecules needs study (R2).
- **Hyperparameter analysis incomplete**:
  - Table 4 settings unclear; n-k pair information missing (R3).
  - No discussion of the impact of T (R2).
  - Impact of hyperparameters on overall computational cost not discussed (R2).
- **Evaluation details missing**:
  - How were “reference molecules” selected? (R2)
  - QED, SA, Diversity evaluation: which schemes/tools were used? Provide definitions and citations (R2).
  - Standard deviations of Vina scores are needed (R4).
- **Multi-objective optimization**: extension to optimizing multiple molecular properties simultaneously is important for drug design but not discussed (R2).
- **Generalizability of property improvements**: are the enhancements in drug-likeness and other properties a byproduct of the diffusion scheme, or would similar outcomes be expected for other optimization scenarios? (R2)
- **Train/test correspondence**: how do the train/test sets for PMINet and PromptDiff correspond? (R4)
- **Difference between self-conditioning and self-prompting**: not clarified; cite Chen et al. (2022) and explain the distinction (R4).
- **Retrieval procedure ambiguous**: “_We randomly choose 128 ligands from training set for retrieval, and select the ligand of top-1 predicted binding affinity as prompt for each protein_” – is it 128 random different ligands used for all samples, or 128 ligands bound to the same pocket? Please clarify (R4).
- **Additional suggestions** (indicating missing analyses): case studies with active compounds would be interesting; the scoring model could be used to produce a ranked list of generated samples (R4).
