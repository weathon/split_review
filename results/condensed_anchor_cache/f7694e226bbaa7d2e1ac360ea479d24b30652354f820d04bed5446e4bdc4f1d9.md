## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a method for single-channel speech separation that combines a deterministic (discriminative) separation model with a pretrained diffusion-based generative model (DiffWave). The discriminative model produces initial source estimates; the generative model refines them conditioned on the discriminative output; and a learned alignment network fuses the two outputs in the frequency domain via per-frequency mixing weights. The authors derive an upper bound on SDR for the combined approach, arguing it can exceed the previously derived bound for deterministic methods. Experiments on WSJ0-2mix, WSJ0-3mix, LibriMix (2/3/5/10/20 speakers) across several separation architectures (SepFormer, DPRNN, ConvTasNet, SudoRM-RF, BLSTM, Transformer) show improvements over the discriminative models alone. Ablation studies confirm the benefit of the mixing network.

### Strengths
- Novel theoretical contribution: generalizes the SDR upper bound from deterministic models to random generative models, providing a mathematical justification for combining discriminative and generative outputs.
- Innovative integration of deterministic and generative models for speech separation, with a learned alignment network to handle phase mismatches between the two estimates.
- Broad experimental validation across multiple separation architectures (SepFormer, DPRNN, ConvTasNet, SudoRM-RF, BLSTM, Transformer), two datasets (WSJ0-mix, LibriMix), and varying numbers of speakers (2, 3, 5, 10, 20).
- Ablation study confirms the mixing network is beneficial compared to naïvely using the generative output directly.
- Audio samples (provided as supplementary) demonstrate perceptual improvement.
- The approach is general and can be applied to any existing discriminative separation model.

### Weaknesses
- **Practical relevance of theoretical bounds:** The analysis builds on Lutati et al., which assumes a chunking approach with uncorrelated chunks. This assumption does not align with the time-frequency processing of models like TF-GridNet and does not account for non-stationarity or temporal dependencies in speech. Consequently, the derived bounds may not reflect real-world performance. The paper does not discuss this limitation sufficiently or acknowledge that TF-GridNet already surpasses the claimed deterministic upper bound (SI-SDRi 23.4 dB on WSJ0-2mix, close to the SepFormer+DiffWave result). This undermines the practical necessity of the diffusion post-processing, especially given its computational cost. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Generative model selection:** Only one diffusion-based generative model (DiffWave) is used to demonstrate the benefit of noise introduction. HiFiGAN (a deterministic generative model) did not improve performance, but the reasons are unclear—whether due to its deterministic nature or lower generation quality. The authors should test other diffusion models (e.g., FastDiff) and higher-quality deterministic models (e.g., UnivNet) to isolate the impact of generative model characteristics. (Reviewer 3)
- **Alignment network analysis:** The alignment network uses the relative phase difference between \(V_g\) and \(V_d\) and the phase of \(V_d\) as inputs. It is unclear whether the observed improvement is due to phase alignment or simply the introduction of additional parameters. The authors should perform ablations with different input combinations (e.g., directly using the phases of \(V_g\) and \(V_d\)) to isolate the contribution of each component. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing noisy dataset evaluation:** All experiments are on clean datasets. The method should be evaluated on noisy datasets (e.g., WHAM!, noisy LibriMix) to assess robustness to environmental noise, which may significantly impact the generative model’s performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing ablation:** An ablation where only the generative output is used after conditioning on the discriminative output (not a simple average or learned mix) is requested. (Reviewer 2)
- **Training specification:** The manuscript does not clearly state whether the separation model (discriminative) and the generative model (DiffWave) are frozen or fine-tuned during training of the alignment network \(F\). (Reviewer 3)
- **Clarity and notation issues:** Multiple reviewers noted poor clarity. Specific issues include: undefined acronyms (SDE, GM), inconsistent notation between \(\alpha_i,\beta_i = F(\bar{v}_d^i, \bar{v}_g^i)\) and \(F(\bar{V}_d^i, \bar{V}_g^i)\) in (4), missing semicolon in \(I(m_r,v_r)\) in (6), typographical errors (\(p(v_{d}r)\) instead of \(p(v_{dr})\)), inconsistent use of \(v_{gr},\bar{v}_{gr},v_{dr},\bar{v}_{dr}\) across Section 3, overloading of \(p(v_{gr})\) in (10), italics misuse in Figure 4 axis labels (function names should be roman), and transposed axes in Figure 5 (frequency on horizontal, time on vertical, contrary to convention). (Reviewer 1)
- **Derivation details missing/clarification needed:** The derivation of inequalities in (11) is unclear: how \(I(v_r; v_{gr})\) relates to \(I(v_r; v_{dr}, v_{gr})\) in (9), and the necessary condition for the second inequality (which requires \(I(v_r; v_{gr}, v_{dr}) \leq I(v_r; m_r)\) but is not implied by (7) or (9)). Also, the origin of the “3.0 dB” gap in (21) is not explained. (Reviewer 1)
- **Symbol representation:** Vectors should be represented in boldface consistently throughout the paper. (Reviewer 3)
