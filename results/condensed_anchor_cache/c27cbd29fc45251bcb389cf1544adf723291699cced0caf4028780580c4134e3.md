## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a skinning-free 3D garment deformation method that decouples low-frequency vertex positions and high-frequency vertex normals, and represents them as 2D texture images from front/back views to leverage pretrained image encoders. An optimization fuses both frequency modalities to reconstruct the deformed mesh. Reviewers recognize the novelty of the 2D image representation and the technical soundness of the fusion algorithm, and note that quantitative/qualitative results are superior to several baselines. However, major concerns are raised: the practical motivation (no efficiency or differentiability advantage), heavy reliance on simulation data with limited generalization, missing comparisons (DeepDraper, HOOD, Zhang et al. 2022), handling of dynamics (jittering, collisions), and several ablations and failure case analyses omitted.

### Strengths
- The idea of representing garment deformation as 2D images (displacement and normal maps) is novel and inspiring, making learning more structured than direct 3D prediction.
- The 3D fusion optimization that deforms a mesh from displacement and normal renderings is reasonable and achieves good results (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The paper is technically sound and clearly presented, with two levels of detail (coarse vertex positions and fine normals) (Reviewer 2).
- Results appear quantitatively and qualitatively superior to existing methods (Reviewer 2).
- The ablation studies effectively demonstrate the contribution of each component (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and practical benefits:** The method does not offer computational efficiency or differentiability, which are the main advantages of neural approaches over classical simulators. It is trained exclusively on simulation data and shows no generalization beyond that data, so it does not alleviate the setup cost of simulators (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalization limits:**
    - Only a single body shape and single-layer, simple-topology garments are considered. It is unclear how the method scales to multiple body shapes within one model or to garments with very different sizes (e.g., a long ankle-length skirt vs. a short top) because the fixed image resolution may not capture the geometry of both accurately (Reviewers 1, 2).
    - Garments are trained separately per type; a single model for all garment types is not demonstrated (Reviewer 1).
- **Handling of dynamics and collisions:**
    - The method models deformation as a static function of pose, ignoring cloth inertia and temporal history. This causes jittering artifacts visible in the supplementary video, where garment vibrations are much more pronounced than in prior methods (Reviewer 3).
    - Despite claiming collision-free results, the supplementary video shows more cloth-body penetrations than previous methods. The collision loss is standard yet no collisions appear in static figures, raising concerns about selective examples or post-processing. If post-processing is applied, results before and after should be shown (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:**
    - The paper does not discuss or compare with DeepDraper (ICCV 2021), which also predicts normals from rendered texture images (front/back/sides) and learns vertex offsets – and improved over TailorNet without separate high/low-frequency modules (Reviewer 1).
    - HOOD, the closest best method in tables, should be compared in the video (Reviewer 1).
    - Motion Guided Deep Dynamic 3D Garments (Zhang et al. 2022, SIGGRAPH Asia) is also missing from comparisons (Reviewer 4).
    - Video comparisons are very limited (only a few examples, no HOOD, no DeepDraper) (Reviewer 1).
- **Self-occlusion and viewpoint limitation:** Only two views (front and back) are used; self-occluded regions under perspective projection may not be encoded accurately. The authors should discuss the trade-offs (cost vs. accuracy) of using more views (e.g., side views) and whether experiments with different numbers of views were performed (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Failure cases:** The paper lacks specific broken or failing examples, such as extreme poses or unusual garment types (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablations / alternative representations:** The choice of encoding position and normal into RGB is not ablated against directly using 2D projections of the 3D shape (Reviewer 4).
- **Temporal consistency:** No discussion of how the method handles temporal coherence across successive frames (Reviewer 2).
