## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses a limitation of current Vision Mambas: patch-based tokenization flattens 2D images into 1D sequences, ignoring spatial structure and hindering global information extraction. GlobalMamba uses Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to convert an image from spatial to frequency domain, group pixels by frequency band, transform each band back to spatial, then tokenize these as a causal sequence from low to high frequency. The resulting Mamba model is evaluated on ImageNet-1K classification, COCO detection, and ADE20K segmentation. All five reviewers rate the paper 5 (borderline reject) with confidence 5,4,3,4,4.

### Strengths
- The frequency-based tokenization orders tokens from low to high frequency, mimicking human visual processing (contours first, then details) (R1, R3, R5).
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow; motivation is clear (R2, R4).
- The proposed method is straightforward and can be readily reimplemented (R4).
- GlobalMamba achieves stronger results compared to other Mamba-based SSMs (R5). One reviewer notes the approach has merit as a pyramidal tokenizer for both SSMs and ViTs (R5).
- The method leverages classic image processing techniques (DCT, low-pass pyramids); using more tokens leans into the proposed benefits of longer sequences that SSNs can provide (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited performance gain vs. increased cost** (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5): Accuracy improvements over V-Mamba are marginal (often 0.2%–0.3%, <1%), while FLOPs and computational cost increase substantially. For example, GlobalMamba-M (Mini) has lower accuracy than PlainMamba-L1, EffVMamba-T, and EffVMamba-S, which are also more efficient in parameters and FLOPs (R2). The long-sequence version of Vim-T improves top-1 accuracy from 76.1 to 78.3, but GlobalMamba improves a reproduced Vim-T from 75.8 to 76.4 (R4). The increase in FLOPs outweighs the accuracy gain, raising questions about practical benefit (R3, R4).
- **Lack of inference time/throughput reporting** (R1, R5): The paper only reports FLOPs, not throughput (images/sec). Lower FLOPs do not always translate to faster inference, especially for Mamba models (R5). One reviewer asks explicitly for throughput (Q4, R5).
- **Source of performance gains unclear** (R5): The method increases the number of tokens compared to baselines, and the performance gains could stem from the longer sequence rather than the claimed causal ordering of frequency bands. The reviewer argues this is a central issue that must be ablated (R5). Another reviewer notes that using replicated/augmented token sequences also improves performance, but GlobalMamba is better; the paper does not adequately explain why (R4). A reviewer asks to ablate the effect of low-pass DCT filtering – i.e., show that gains come from the ordering and not simply from downscaling patches without low-pass filtering (Q1, R5).
- **Methodological concerns** (R5): The tokenization method mimics classic Gaussian pyramids (Adelson et al., 1984) and the scan ordering resembles JPEG scanning (Wallace, 1992), but these are not cited. The claim of “causal tokens” is opaque because each pyramid level still relies on an arbitrary raster scan order. The role of frequency subsampling in DCT domain is not clearly supported; the reviewer suspects the gains come from more tokens, not from the frequency argument (R5).
- **Missing experiments and ablations** (R1, R4, R5): The paper does not study the effect of dropping low/high frequency information (R1). It does not compare against more recent baselines like DEiTv3 instead of DEiT (R5). The analysis of why GlobalMamba outperforms simple sequence replication (Table 5) is insufficient (R4). One reviewer asks for a detailed computational cost vs. benefit analysis (R3).
- **Ahistoricity and presentation issues** (R5): The paper spends excessive space on basic DCT background that could be moved to supplemental. There are minor typos and missing references to figures (R5). The paper omits classical references to image pyramids, JPEG scanning, etc.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Can the authors report inference time/throughput? (R1, R5)
  - What happens if low/high frequency components are dropped? (R1)
  - Why is EffVMamba more efficient in the mini size? (R2)
  - Can the authors provide a more detailed analysis of computational costs relative to baseline improvements? (R3)
  - Is the benefit more pronounced for certain image types or tasks? (R4)
  - Did you ablate the effect of the low-pass DCT filtering? Can you show that gains come from the ordering rather than more tokens? (R5)
  - Why omit references to Gaussian pyramids and JPEG scanning? (R5)
  - Why use DEiT instead of DEiTv3? (R5)
