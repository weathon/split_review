## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a self-supervised video face clustering method that adapts a generic face ID model to a target video using coarse track matching and fine-tuning, then applies a parameter-free clustering algorithm. A new movie face clustering dataset (MovieFaceCluster) is introduced. The method achieves competitive results on existing TV series benchmarks and on the new dataset. All three reviewers rated the paper 5 (weak reject) with confidence 4. Two reviewers gave soundness/presentation 2 (fair); one gave both 3 (good). All rated contribution 2 (fair). The main concerns center on insufficient justification for the new benchmark, unclear novelty relative to prior joint adaptation/ clustering work, missing ablation and experimental details, and unfair comparisons due to more advanced pre-processing.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-motivated and easy to follow; automatic face clustering for video is an important and practical problem that lacks sufficient attention (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The proposed method is reasonable and feasible, with novelty in eliminating the need for manual selection of a clustering threshold (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The annotation effort for the movie dataset is substantial, and the dataset contributes toward addressing the lack of good benchmarks (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- The pre-processing pipeline uses cutting-edge building blocks: PySceneDetect (2022), RetinaFace (2020), and SER-FIQ (2020) (Reviewer 2).
- Commendable effort to replicate baselines in PyTorch (Reviewer 2).
- Limitations and future works are discussed (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments are detailed and the method shows good performance compared to current methods (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset justification and comparison**: The paper does not provide clear motivation for a new benchmark. There is no quantitative/qualitative comparison with existing movie person identification datasets (e.g., MovieNet) in terms of size, diversity, complexity, or limitations. The dataset description lacks visualizations and statistical analyses that demonstrate its uniqueness. The meaning of “Varying Parameter” in Fig. 1 is unclear regarding annotations (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity of novelty vs. prior work**: The method is similar to prior works that jointly perform face ID model adaptation and clustering (e.g., Sharma et al. (2019), Zhang et al. (2016b)). The paper does not clearly state what is unique. It is unclear whether performance gains come from better face ID adaptation (and if so, whether from the teacher-student branch) or from the clustering method. A granular ablation isolating the self-supervised learning component from the clustering algorithm is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing fine-tuning details**: The fine-tuning process lacks crucial information: how data is split into train/validation sets, how many tracks are required in a video, how track quantity affects convergence and final clustering performance, and sensitivity to track quality/diversity (e.g., if a video has few or very similar tracks). The claim that positive track pair construction is independent of ground truth/temporal constraints is contradictory because the coarse matching implicitly uses temporal information; this description needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair comparisons due to advanced pre-processing**: The method uses recent pre-processing tools (PySceneDetect 2022, RetinaFace 2020, SER-FIQ 2020), while most baselines in Table 1 were established before 2020. Without an ablation study that isolates the impact of each pre-processing step, it is impossible to attribute observed improvements to the proposed method rather than to more accurate scene/face detection. Also, most compared methods are outdated (only one from the last three years) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Parameter-free claim vs. fixed values**: The paper claims a parameter-free clustering algorithm, yet fixed values are used in the quality assessment and coarse track matching modules to generate adaptive thresholds. These fixed values lack empirical or theoretical support (Reviewer 1).
- **t-SNE visualization and failure analysis**: The t-SNE embeddings in Fig. 5 show that the proposed method’s clusters approximate ground truth, but the ground truth itself exhibits anomalous clusters. The paper should explore limitations and present failure cases to provide deeper insights (Reviewer 1).
- **Reproducibility**: Data, training code, and baseline re-implementations are not promised to be open-sourced (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor**: Citation format inconsistency (both “Table 1” and “Tab. 1” in Section 4.1) (Reviewer 1). While Reviewer 1 rated presentation and soundness as 3, the other two reviewers rated them 2, indicating that clarity and experimental rigor need improvement to meet community standards.
