## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IW-GAE, an importance-weighted group accuracy estimator for model calibration and model selection in unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA). The method splits data into groups, estimates an importance weight for each group by solving an optimization problem (using the Clopper-Pearson interval), and uses the resulting group accuracy estimate for calibration and selection. Theoretical analysis and experiments with one UDA method (MDD) on one dataset (Office-Home) are provided; the paper claims 22% improvement in calibration and 14% in model selection over state-of-the-art.

### Strengths
- The problems of model calibration and model selection under distribution shift are important and not yet fully solved.
- The use of importance weighting is a sound principle, supported by prior work (You et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2020; Park et al., 2020, 2022).
- The derivation of an optimization problem (based on the Clopper-Pearson interval) to determine the importance weight is a novel technical element.
- The paper includes theoretical analysis bounding target group accuracy estimation error via source group accuracy estimation error (e.g., Eq. 5), and attempts to tighten the bound by bounding the maximum/minimum of importance weights.
- Multiple reviewers noted the paper is well-motivated and appears theoretically solid.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited empirical evaluation**: Experiments only use one UDA method (MDD) on a single dataset (Office-Home). Reviewers unanimously found this insufficient to demonstrate generalizability. Additional datasets (e.g., DomainNet) and more baselines (e.g., CDAN, state-of-the-art UDA techniques) are needed. The appendix shows that IW-GAE does **not** outperform TransCal (Wang et al., 2020) when combined with CDAN, raising doubts about effectiveness.
- **Novelty concerns**: The core idea of using importance weighting for calibration and selection has been explored in prior works (You et al., 2019 for selection; Wang et al., 2020; Park et al., 2020 for calibration). The main difference is the adoption of bin-wise importance weighting from Park et al. (2022), which is an incremental advance.
- **Complexity and practicality**: IW-GAE requires at least three sensitive hyperparameters (temperature, number of accuracy groups, and number of bins), whereas TransCal and DEV (You et al., 2019) are hyperparameter-free. This complexity makes it less suitable for model selection, and the current evidence does not show a clear advantage.
- **Poor clarity and organization**: Multiple reviewers criticized the presentation. Figure 1 has unclear legend and overly long annotation. An algorithm box is missing ([Reviewers 1, 4]). Important analyses (e.g., optimization error correlation with group accuracy estimation error) are relegated to the appendix and should be moved to the main text ([Reviewer 2]). Excessive background and proofs crowd out essential algorithmic and implementation details.
- **Unclear core justification (minority Reviewer 4 – score 3)**: Reviewer 4 strongly questions why minimizing the difference between the direct Monte-Carlo source accuracy estimate and the importance-weighted source accuracy estimate (Eq. 8 / Eq. 34) leads to improved calibration on the target domain. The temperature \(t\) only affects group assignments of target data, and the paper does not explain why a temperature that makes two source-domain estimates agree would give better calibration under shift. This is a fundamental methodological question.
- **Coarse granularity concern**: Reviewer 3 notes that group accuracy is intrinsically coarser than individual accuracy, and asks whether this direction inherently limits performance compared to pointwise calibration methods.
- **Tightness of theoretical bounds**: Reviewer 3 points out that the bound in Eq. 5 multiplies the source error by both a factor \(M\) and another term; if these are large, the upper bound may be loose, making the optimization over the bound less meaningful. The paper’s brief mention of “bounding the maximum and minimum values of IW” to tighten the bound needs elaboration, including its impact on the theoretical flow.
- **Issues with large language models (LLMs)**: The paper claims its method does not improve when applied to fixed LLMs, attributing this to LLMs being less subject to distribution shifts. Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 both counter that an alternative explanation could be poor scalability to large models; the paper lacks discussion or experiments on scalability.
- **Clarity of group definition**: Reviewer 4 notes that the groups defined in Section 4.2 are exactly the confidence-based groups used in ECE – this could simplify presentation but is not leveraged.
- **Missing details**: The papers lacks concrete steps of the algorithm, and the dependence of the optimization on temperature \(t\) is not clearly explained ([Reviewer 4]).
