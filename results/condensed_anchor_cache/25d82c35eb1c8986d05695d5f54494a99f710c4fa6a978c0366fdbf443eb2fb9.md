## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies privacy accounting for DP-SGD with random batching and correlated noise (matrix mechanism). It proposes a “balls-in-bins” batching scheme that simplifies privacy analysis, and uses Monte Carlo accounting to estimate privacy parameters for any lower-triangular, non-negative correlation matrix **C**, bypassing composition. The framework allows optimizing **C** with amplification. Experiments show improved RMSE on prefix sums and better deep learning performance compared to prior work.

### Strengths
- Proposes a practical, novel minibatch sampling scheme (“balls-in-bins”) that enables easier privacy analysis for matrix mechanisms and achieves better privacy amplification.
- Uses Monte Carlo accounting (Wang et al., 2023) to bypass composition; considered a non-trivial and interesting application of that framework.
- The paper is well-written and the theoretical results appear correct at a high level (though one reviewer notes theory is weak; see Weaknesses).
- Provides convincing experimental results indicating the approach is viable and can improve over state-of-the-art on both prefix sums and deep learning tasks.
- Addresses an important problem: privacy amplification with correlated noise and arbitrary correlation matrices, whereas prior methods were limited to banded matrices or gave loose bounds.
- The simulation method allows optimization of the noise form for arbitrary **C**.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of formal guarantees for accuracy of estimates**: The paper lacks high-probability or confidence bounds for the computed DP security parameter (ε, δ). It is unclear whether the method provides provable guarantees or only heuristic estimates. No theoretical convergence guarantees are given for the Monte Carlo estimates, despite Theorem 2.1 being stated (materialization missing). The term “near-exact” needs a precise definition, and the potential looseness of the dominating pair *P* should be discussed.
- **Missing justification for the “closer to practical random batching” claim**: The abstract claims the balls-in-bins scheme is closer to practical random batching than Poisson sampling, but no theoretical explanation is provided.
- **Missing proof of monotonicity for bisection (Algorithm 2)**: The bisection method for computing σ requires monotonicity between σ and δ; this is not proved.
- **No closed-form or asymptotic bound for amplification**: The paper does not provide analytic bounds on the amplification rate or the amount of noise saved, making it hard to gauge improvement beyond empirical optimization.
- **Applicability to adaptive DP-SGD is unclear**: The analysis assumes a static linear aggregation model (input matrix x fixed). In DP-SGD, gradients depend adaptively on previous noisy outputs; the paper does not clarify how the sensitivity analysis of the matrix mechanism applies in this adaptive setting.
- **Missing ablation and parameter studies**: The choice of batch size *b* (from {1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}) is not analyzed with respect to key parameters like ε. The impact of suboptimal *b* on performance is not shown. The noise variance (σ) is not reported for Figure 4, making it hard to interpret the amplification effect.
- **Caveat about practical batching variant**: The practical variant of balls-in-bins batching (Appendix H) may complicate the privacy analysis and reintroduce difficulties similar to Poisson sampling; this is not addressed.
- **Missing details in theory and presentation**: The neighborhood relation (assumed add/remove) is not stated in Section 2. The proof of Lemma 3.2 is too brief and could be elaborated (e.g., by adding required results from Choquette-Choo et al., 2024b to the appendix). The paper should discuss whether results change for the substitution neighborhood. Figure 1 would benefit from explicit labeling of epochs. The bisection complexity is given as O(log(1/δ)) but should be O(log(1/err)) where err is the error tolerance on σ*.
- **Unexplained behavior at large ε**: For large ε, the proposed method performs much worse than subsampling amplification; the paper does not comment on why.
- **Minor**: The padding technique in Appendix H is introduced to avoid wasted computational resources, but the paper does not explain why wasted resources are a problem.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8) finds the theory weak and lacking guarantees, while another (score 6) considers the theoretical results correct; this contrast is not resolved in the paper.
