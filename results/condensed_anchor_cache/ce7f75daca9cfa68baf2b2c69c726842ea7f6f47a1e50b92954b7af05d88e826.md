## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a new approach for SE(3)-invariant crystal structure modeling by replacing static, structure-wide frames with *dynamic frames* that are computed per atom based on its local neighborhood and adapted through the network’s attention mechanism. This concept is instantiated in the **CrystalFramer** architecture, a transformer-based encoder. Dynamic frames are constructed either via PCA from weighted atoms or by greedily selecting the highest-weighted atoms, where weights are attention scores. Experiments on large-scale datasets (JARVIS, MP, OQMD) show that CrystalFramer with dynamic frames consistently outperforms models using static frames and achieves state-of-the-art results in most property prediction tasks. The paper also provides an efficiency analysis and a visual study of frame evolution, noting that earlier layers attend to closer atoms and later layers to further atoms.

### Strengths

- **Novel and effective idea**: The concept of dynamic frames is a novel and powerful extension of stochastic frame averaging for crystals. It makes the model more expressive by giving each atom a local, task-dependent view of the structure (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Clear presentation** (majority view): The method is straightforward and presented with commendable clarity; the paper includes relevant details, mathematical definitions, and a candid discussion of advantages and limitations (Reviewers 1, 3). *Note: Reviewer 4 raises counterpoints on writing quality – see Weaknesses.*
- **Strong experimental validation**: Extensive evaluations on JARVIS, MP, and OQMD demonstrate that dynamic frames, especially the “max frames” variation, yield significant improvements over the base Crystalformer and other state-of-the-art models. The results are convincing and the analysis is thorough (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Useful analysis**: The visual analysis shows intuitive behavior – frames evolve from focusing on close atoms in early layers to more distant atoms in later layers – and the paper honestly discusses both benefits and limitations (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Significant contribution** (reviewer 4): The contribution is considered significant and novel; the improvement over static frame averaging in crystal property prediction is large and believable (Reviewer 4). Other reviewers rate the contribution as solid but note room for further extensions (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparison with prior frame-based equivariant methods** (Major, Reviewer 1): The paper should clarify how the “max frame” construction differs from those in [1], [2], [3], [4]. In particular, [1] and [4] use atomic local frames for equivariance in molecular structures. A comparative discussion is needed to establish whether the approach is a direct adaptation from molecular modeling or contains novel insights.
- **Lack of equivariant extension and evaluation** (Major, Reviewer 2): Previous work (ComFormer) provides an SE(3)-equivariant version. Can CrystalFramer be easily extended to equivariant predictions (e.g., forces, high-order tensors)? Even for invariant properties, it would be interesting to see whether an equivariant variant improves performance. The paper should discuss or provide initial results for this direction.
- **Continuity and generalization concerns** (Minor, Reviewer 1, citing [5]): Frame-based methods can hinder model continuity, potentially affecting generalization. The paper should discuss whether the proposed dynamic frames address this issue and provide an empirical analysis of continuity versus generalization.
- **Writing quality** (Minor, Reviewer 4): The paper contains numerous grammatical errors and awkward sentences (examples: lines 121, 198, 234). The writing needs thorough proofreading (e.g., using grammar tools). This is a distinct criticism; other reviewers found the paper well-written.
- **Unexplained runtime discrepancy in Table 4** (Question, Reviewer 4): PotNet takes 43s/epoch to train and 313ms for inference, while CrystalFramer takes 74s/epoch to train but is nearly 10× faster during inference. The paper should explain this large discrepancy.
- **Angle feature expressiveness** (Question, Reviewer 4): The model uses only the cosine of angles (line 298), which maps π/2 and –π/2 to the same value 0. Consider concatenating both sine and cosine for a more expressive representation.
- **Limited discussion of related applications** (Minor, Reviewer 3): The paper could be strengthened by connecting to machine learning potentials and applications involving crystal surfaces (e.g., catalysts). While acknowledged as potential future work, more explicit discussion would broaden impact.
- **Missing analysis of frame evolution across materials and training stages** (Question, Reviewer 3): The dynamic frames offer a new analytic dimension. The paper could discuss how frames differ across material types and how they evolve during training. Reviewers 3 also asks about scalability to larger supercells with more atoms.
- **Interpretability of learned frames** (Minor, Reviewer 4): It is hard to gain an intuitive understanding of what the angles between learned frames and direction vectors represent or why they would be useful features.
