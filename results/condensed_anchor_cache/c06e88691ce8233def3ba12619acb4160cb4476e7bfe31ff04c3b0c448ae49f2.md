## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper explores design choices for building vision-language models (VLMs) for GUI element grounding. It presents four experimental findings: (1) a warm-up fine-tuning stage with general visual grounding tasks improves UI grounding; (2) a simple-to-complex fine-tuning curriculum maximizes data utility; (3) scaling up both warm-up and UI grounding data enhances accuracy; (4) a convolution-based image feature compressor improves performance on high-resolution UI images. Based on these findings, the authors develop UI-Pro, a VLM that achieves strong grounding accuracy with fewer parameters than many competing models.

### Strengths
- The work presents a systematic exploration of design choices (data scaling, feature compression, fine-tuning schedules) for GUI grounding. All four proposed findings are well-ablated through comprehensive experiments, providing a useful reference for future research on GUI-based multimodal models. [R2, R4]
- Validation across multiple benchmarks demonstrates that the proposed methods lead to substantial improvements in grounding accuracy compared to existing models. [R1]
- The paper is organized in a clear and coherent manner. [R3]
- The experimental results effectively validate the conclusions drawn. [R3]
- UI-Pro achieves strong performance using significantly fewer parameters than many leading models. [R1, R4]
- Detailed comparisons of different data scaling and compression methods provide clear evidence of their impact on performance. [R1]
- The use of a warm-up phase with general visual grounding tasks before UI-specific fine-tuning is a strategic innovation that enhances adaptability and performance. [R1]
- All findings are backed by ablative experiments that directly support the claims. [R3, R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and insufficient insight.** The paper reads more like an experimental report that lists methods proposed by others and reports their best results. Curriculum learning, the benefits of scaling data, and convolution-based connectors are all well-established. The work fails to provide meaningful discussion or deeper insights into why certain strategies work, leaving the reader with little new knowledge. [R4] In particular:
  - *Finding 1*: The conclusion that grounding on natural images is the best warm-up data is presented without analysis of why grounding data from a different domain helps, or why warm-up with same-domain data is not optimal. Many unexplored combinations (e.g., adding QA on natural images, text localizations) could perform even better, so guidance on data curation is missing. [R4]
  - *Finding 2* (simple-to-complex curriculum): The experiments in Table 2 confound data type with data size—when using 2 or 3 SFT stages, both factors change. It is unclear if the gains come from the data ordering or merely from using different data sizes. For example, what happens if SeeClick data is also used for SFT-1 rather than only for SFT-2? [R4]
  - *Finding 3* (scaling data and overfitting caution): The complex UI grounding data is an order of magnitude smaller than other datasets. Without controlling for data size, the caution about overfitting is unreliable. Intuitively, same-domain fine-tuning should yield the best performance, but the experiments do not isolate this effect. [R4]
- **Limited scope and evaluation.** The paper focuses only on grounding tasks and does not evaluate grounding in the context of an autonomous UI agent (which requires both grounding and action selection). Improvements in a single component may not translate to overall system performance, limiting the paper’s broader applicability. [R2, R3]
- **Generalizability concerns.** All experiments are based on a specific VLM (LLaVA). It is unclear whether the findings (warm-up, feature compression, etc.) hold for other architectures (e.g., InternLM, Qwen-VL2, Phi-3.5-V). Comparisons with other base models are needed. [R2, R3] Also, the paper does not cite or discuss Ferret-UI, which uses local patches in a conceptually similar way. [R2]
- **Missing comparisons.** There is a need to compare against other specialized UI grounding models beyond the general ones tested. [R3]
- **Model performance not state-of-the-art.** Despite claims, the model does not achieve state-of-the-art results on all benchmarks. [R2]
- **Warm-up may be unnecessary.** Most current state-of-the-art VLMs already have strong text-rich visual perception capabilities, suggesting the proposed warm-up strategy might be redundant. [R2]
- **Insufficient analysis of stability and errors.** The paper cautions against overfitting when scaling data but lacks in-depth analysis or metrics on model stability across different data scales. There is also no detailed error analysis to understand where the model’s grounding falls short. [R1]
- **Limited UI diversity.** The evaluation datasets are primarily focused on GUI scenarios without covering diverse UI types such as industrial control interfaces, mobile applications, or accessibility-focused UIs. [R1]
- **No inference efficiency analysis.** The paper does not discuss computational requirements or inference time/resource costs for deploying UI-Pro in real-world applications with limited processing power or memory. [R1]
- **Unclear results in specific ablations.** In Table 2, focusing on the ScreenSpot zero-shot benchmark, there appears to be no significant difference among r4, r6, and r8, which undermines the claimed benefit of the curriculum ordering. [R2] In Figure 4, the description suggests that the 5M results in Stage 1 should match the 212k results in Stage 2 and the 625k results in Stage 3, but the figure shows mismatched values. This requires clarification. [R4]
- **Contrasting reviewer opinion**: One reviewer found the paper clearly organized and well-validated (score 6), while three reviewers rated it much lower (scores 3, 3, 5), citing the lack of novelty, depth, and generalizability as major flaws. [R1,R2,R3,R4]
