- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies online assortment selection and pricing under a contextual multinomial logit (MNL) choice model with unknown parameters. It proposes a UCB-based algorithm that achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$ revenue regret and provides a matching $\Omega(d\sqrt{T})$ lower bound (up to log factors). The algorithm has two phases: an initialization phase with uniformly random assortments/prices to ensure covariance matrix invertibility, and a UCB phase that selects assortments and prices to maximize optimistic revenue. Experiments show improved performance over baselines.

### Strengths
- The problem of joint dynamic assortment selection and pricing in a contextual setting is important and, according to **Reviewer 1**, looks new. **Reviewer 4** also notes the authors are the first to formalize this specific joint problem in a contextual bandit setup (though **Reviewer 3** points to prior non-contextual work by Miao & Chao and limited comparison).
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented (**Reviewers 1,2,4**).
- The algorithms are intuitive and the analysis is solid, with both upper and lower regret bounds that are nearly matching, showing near-optimality (**Reviewers 1,2,4**).
- Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm and better empirical performance compared to previous algorithms (**Reviewers 1,4**).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty in algorithm design and analysis.** Several reviewers note that the algorithmic approach (two-phase design with uniform exploration followed by UCB) and the proof techniques (MLE consistency, standard regret decomposition) are very similar to existing works on contextual MNL bandits (e.g., Oh & Iyengar 2021, Chen et al. 2020). **Reviewer 2** states “the proof skills are standard” and **Reviewer 4** says “the analysis does not introduce much challenge.” **Reviewer 3** asks for a clear statement of the main technical challenges that go beyond simply applying previous techniques.
- **Computational concerns.** Algorithm 1 (solving the revenue-maximization subproblem) appears computationally expensive; the paper does not discuss its running time or whether the required optimization (Assortment optimization (5) and Algorithm 1 line 5) is NP-hard (**Reviewers 1,2**).
- **Strong assumptions limit practical applicability.**  
  - The algorithm requires knowledge of a lower bound $L_0$ on the price sensitivity $\langle\phi^\star,x\rangle$, which is typically unknown in practice (**Reviewers 1,4**). **Reviewer 1** asks whether a modified algorithm could work without this knowledge.  
  - Assumption 3 (context stochasticity) is used only to guarantee eigenvalue lower bounds during initialization; **Reviewers 2 and 3** suggest that L2-regularized/ridge MLE could avoid the need for this assumption and the random initialization phase. **Reviewer 3** asks what happens without Assumption 3 if regularized MLE is used instead.
- **Inconsistency between algorithm and proof.** **Reviewer 1** points out that Algorithm 2 chooses $S_t$ and $p_t$ based on an *estimated* algorithm (Algorithm 1), while the proof assumes the chosen assortment and prices are optimal. More elaboration on this mismatch is needed.
- **Incomplete comparison with related work.**  
  - **Reviewer 3** notes that Perivier & Goyal (2022) also study assortment selection and pricing under MNL (though not jointly), and the comparison given is insufficient—neither theoretical nor experimental.  
  - **Reviewer 3** and **Reviewer 4** (implicitly) note that Miao & Chao (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3173267) address assortment pricing and selection jointly in a non-contextual setting; the paper should cite and compare with this work.  
  - The claim of being “first to address the problem of dynamic contextual assortment selection and pricing” is therefore questionable.
- **Regret bound dependencies unclear and potentially weak.**  
  - **Reviewer 3** criticizes the omission of the dependence on $\kappa$ (the minimum MNL choice probability parameter) from the main-text regret bound; this assumption is standard (Chen et al. 2020, Oh & Iyengar, Perivier & Goyal) and its dependence should be discussed, especially given recent improvements (Perivier & Goyal, 2022).  
  - **Reviewer 4** raises a more specific concern: the lower bound has $\Omega(1/L_0)$ dependence, but the upper bound involves $P = \Theta(1/L_0)$, $\kappa = \Theta(e^{-1/L_0})$, and $T_0 = \Theta(\frac{1}{L_0^2} e^{2/L_0})$, leading to a multiplicative factor $\Theta(\frac{1}{L_0^3} e^{2/L_0})$ in the regret—much larger than the lower bound. The paper should examine whether this gap is fundamental or can be narrowed.  
  - **Reviewer 3** also questions the absence of any dependence on $K$ (assortment size), while prior works (Chen et al., Oh & Iyengar, Perivier & Goyal) have at least logarithmic $K$ dependence. How is this independence achieved?
- **Relevance to ICLR audience** is questioned by **Reviewer 2**, who notes the paper’s contribution is mainly on the modeling side.
- **Minor issues:** Proposition 2 is considered too obvious for the main text (**Reviewer 3**).  
- **Specific technical question:** **Reviewer 4** asks for clarification on Lemma 6: how $|\langle\theta_t,\tilde{x}_t\rangle - \langle\theta^\star,\tilde{x}_t\rangle| \leq (1+p)g_t$ is derived, given that $||\hat{\theta}_t-\theta^\star||_{V_t} \leq \alpha_t$ but $||(x_t,-p x_t)||_M \leq (1+p)||(x_t,x_t)||_M$ does not hold for a general PSD matrix $M$.
- **Uniform random initialization phase** may cause undesirable fluctuations in prices and assortments in practice (**Reviewer 2**). The suggestion to use regularized MLE to avoid this phase is echoed by **Reviewer 3**.
