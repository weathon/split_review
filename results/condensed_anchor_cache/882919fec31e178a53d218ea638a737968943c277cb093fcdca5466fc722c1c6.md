## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two random mechanisms—Layer Shuffling and Task Vector Superposition—to reduce interference when merging multiple fine-tuned models via task vector arithmetic. Layer Shuffling randomly reorders layers of each model to reduce alignment between task vectors, and Task Vector Superposition applies random orthogonal transformations to further decorrelate them. Both techniques are data-independent, require no training, and allow on-the-fly addition/removal of models (hot-swapping) without recomputation. Experiments on CLIP-ViT, Flan-T5, and GPT-2 across image classification, text generation, and text classification benchmarks show improved performance over vanilla task arithmetic and several baselines, while reducing storage costs by up to 4× compared to keeping individual models.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and effectiveness:** Both methods are intuitive, straightforward, and require no additional training, optimization, or complex configurations, making them easy to implement and adapt.
- **Effective interference reduction:** The combination of Layer Shuffling and Task Vector Superposition successfully increases orthogonality among task vectors, leading to better merging performance. The paper makes the key observation that individual task vectors are too similar and exploits it to reduce interference.
- **Data/model independence and hot-swapping:** The approach operates without any data, enabling seamless addition or removal of models (hot-swapping) without retraining or re-computation, a valuable feature for dynamic deployment.
- **Comprehensive evaluation:** Experiments cover multiple benchmarks (CLIP, Flan-T5, GPT-2) and diverse domains (image classification, text generation, text classification), demonstrating generalizability across tasks and architectures.
- **Scalable and memory efficient:** Only random seeds need to be stored to retrieve the final model, and the method achieves storage reduction compared to keeping individual fine-tuned models.
- **Proper ablation and motivation:** The motivation for both components is well written and properly ablated.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with closely related works:** The paper lacks comparison with recent works on task vector manipulation (e.g., Guillermo Ortiz-Jimenez et al.) and compression algorithms (e.g., [1] from reviewer 2). The related work section is extremely limited and does not place the method in the broader context of model merging and compression.
- **Requires task ID during inference:** This limitation is not adequately discussed. It restricts applicability compared to task-agnostic merging methods (e.g., TIES, Task Arithmetic). A fair comparison under the same inference constraints is missing.
- **No forward pass time complexity analysis:** The method introduces overhead: layers must be reshuffled into the correct order, signs restored, and the residual added to pre-trained weights. A study of latency and computational cost per step is needed, especially for real-time applications.
- **Experiment scale and model size bias:** The evaluation focuses on small models (base variants of ViT and Flan-T5), not larger ones (ViT-L/14, T5-XXL) commonly used in the literature. Performance on larger models and with more tasks (e.g., 14 and 20 task benchmarks from [1]) is not shown. Additionally, the proposed method uses a larger effective model size than many baselines (due to parameter duplication), making comparisons unfair—performance gains may simply come from higher capacity rather than the method itself.
- **Memory overhead may not justify gains:** Although the paper claims minimal memory overhead, storing context matrices and shuffled task vectors nearly doubles the memory requirement. Results with LoRA show that SMILE achieves a better accuracy–memory tradeoff than the proposed method.
- **Lack of detailed analysis on shuffle/superposition levels:** The paper does not systematically explore how varying the degree of layer shuffling or the number of superposed task vectors affects performance (e.g., diminishing returns, optimal number for different tasks or architectures).
- **Clarity issues in method description:** Equation (8) and related description are unclear: does shuffling cause mixing of task vectors across layers (e.g., between k-1 and k+1)? It should be explicitly stated whether shuffling is confined within each layer or allows cross-layer mixing, and how that affects layer-specific alignment.
- **Unexplained task-dependent effectiveness:** Performance of TA+Shuffle vs. STA varies across tasks (e.g., image classification vs. text generation) without discussion. The paper does not analyze why certain tasks benefit more from specific strategies or how task characteristics (modality, complexity, dataset size) correlate with method effectiveness.
- **Unclear complementarity of the two techniques:** The interaction between Layer Shuffling and Task Vector Superposition is not thoroughly analyzed. It is unclear whether they are truly complementary or one dominates; a rigorous ablation study isolating each component is missing. Theoretical justification for why Layer Shuffling specifically enhances orthogonality more than other random transformations is lacking.
- **Minor formatting and presentation issues:** Incorrect equation references (e.g., referencing equation 3 in context of equation 4), missing parentheses in citations, unclear meaning of values in parentheses in tables (e.g., average (%) and Bits (Gb) columns requiring text reading). Vision benchmark reference (L268-269) is attributed to FusionBench but comes from Ilharco et al. Baselines (e.g., PSP, WEMoE, SMILE) are not explained or categorized; the reader cannot understand why they are included or their relative strengths.
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with missing analysis:** 
  - How does varying the degree of shuffling or number of superposed task vectors affect performance? (Reviewer 1)
  - Would a more systematic (non-random) orthogonalization further improve performance? (Reviewer 3)
  - Is there consistent/inconsistent performance variance due to randomness in shuffling and superposition? (Reviewer 3)
  - Need a clear ablation to separate the effects of Layer Shuffling and Task Vector Superposition. (Reviewer 4)
- **Weak theoretical foundation:** The mathematical analysis is overly simplistic and lacks a rigorous proof showing why Layer Shuffling enhances orthogonality more effectively than other random transformations.
