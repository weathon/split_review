## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the statistical performance of kernel classifiers in Sobolev spaces. It derives an upper bound (Theorem 2) and a minimax lower bound (Theorem 1) on the classification excess risk, together establishing the optimality of a kernel classifier trained via gradient flow on the square loss. The theory is extended to overparameterized neural network classifiers via the neural tangent kernel (Corollary 1). The paper also proposes a simple method for estimating the smoothness parameter of the conditional probability function \(f^* = 2\eta - 1\) and applies it to standard image classification datasets.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear message (Reviewers 1, 3). (Reviewer 4 found it less accessible — see Weaknesses.)
- Studying the optimality of kernel classifiers is a fundamental problem, and the results improve upon earlier work such as Kerkyacharian & Picard (1992) (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The upper bound (Theorem 2) generalizes beyond ridge regularization to a broad class of spectral algorithms including gradient flow, and the lower bound (Theorem 1) nicely complements it (Reviewer 3; also noted by Reviewer 4 as showing minimax optimality).
- It is noteworthy that optimal classification rates can be achieved with any loss, specifically the square loss, which is less common for classification (Reviewer 3).
- The association of estimated smoothness to dataset difficulty is interesting (Reviewer 1).
- The theory has potential to guide practice in kernel learning and neural networks (Reviewer 2).
- The connection to NTK and deep neural networks (Corollary 1) is interesting (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Gap between theory and practice**: The minimax optimality result relies on gradient flow with the \(L_2\) loss, which is not commonly used for classification (SVM, logistic regression are more typical). The same concern applies to the neural network extension. The optimality is only proven in an asymptotic sense, limiting practical significance (Reviewer 2).
- **Reliance on unpublished work**: The main theoretical analysis depends on an unpublished preprint (arXiv:2305.07241) whose validity has not been verified by the reviewers (Reviewer 2).
- **NTK assumptions not fully justified**: For Corollary 1, it is not clear that Assumption 3 can be verified with a meaningful \(\alpha_0\) (Reviewer 3). The NTK is a dot-product kernel on the sphere but not outside it (e.g., \(K_{ntk}(0,0)=1\)), and the paper’s assumption of a dot-product kernel is questionable; the need for this assumption is also unclear (Reviewer 3). The connection between the “min kernel” and the NTK is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **Implicit assumption in Theorem 2**: Theorem 2 implicitly assumes \(\alpha_0 = 1/\beta\); this should be stated explicitly. It is not clear whether this holds for general domains in Corollary 1 (Reviewer 3).
- **Smoothness estimator not theoretically justified and potentially unreliable**: The proposed smoothness estimation method lacks theoretical analysis (Reviewers 1, 3). Concerns include: (1) if the target function has different smoothness in different dimensions, eigenvalue decay may not follow a single slope, and linear regression would estimate an intermediate smoothness while the function only lies in the interpolation space with the lower smoothness; (2) under the manifold assumption (common for image data) the estimator’s behavior is unclear; (3) for well-separated, non-noisy data (e.g., MNIST with \(\eta(x)\in\{1,-1\}\)), it is unclear whether the estimator can detect arbitrarily high smoothness (Reviewer 3). The experiments only estimate smoothness and do not validate whether the predicted rates hold in practice (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Missing comparisons to existing NTK results**: The paper does not discuss how its results compare to existing NTK generalization theory (Reviewers 1, 4). The statement in Corollary 1 that “width \(m\) and sample size \(n\) are sufficiently large” is vague; in NTK literature one typically requires \(m \gg n\), and the asymptotic regime should be clarified (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of practical insights and further experiments**: The experiments only estimate smoothness; the paper does not show how to enforce a smoothness constraint in learning or use the estimate to guide classifier design (Reviewer 1). It was also asked whether the smoothness estimate could give insight into optimal steps in gradient flow, and whether numerical studies could support this (Reviewer 2).
- **Assumptions not fully explained**: Assumption 3 and its notation are difficult to understand (Reviewers 3, 4). The “min kernel” and its relation to the NTK also need clarification (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues**: While overall well-written (Reviewers 1, 3), Reviewer 4 found the paper difficult to digest and requested improvements in presentation, including reorganizing the proofs and appendix for better readability. Several minor typos and formatting errors were noted (Reviewer 3): “spectra algorithm” should be “spectral algorithm” (also in Reviewer 4), missing whitespace, missing constant in a bound on p. 1, inconsistent citation formatting (all-caps author names), incorrect notation in Eq. (14) (last layer uses \(W^L\), not \(W^{(L,p)}\)), missing author in Hui (2020) (M. Belkin), missing period before Proposition 1, and a blank after \(\in\) on p. 8.
- **Other concerns**: The paper does not discuss generalization to multi-class classification or other loss functions (e.g., log-loss) (Reviewer 3).
