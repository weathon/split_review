## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces ToolTalk, a benchmark for evaluating tool usage in multi-turn conversational settings. It includes 28 tools across 7 plugins (e.g., Calendar, Email, Weather) with simulated implementations, 50 complex human-authored conversations (averaging 3 tool calls each) plus 28 single-tool easy conversations, and automated metrics (tool precision/recall, success rate, incorrect action rate). GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 achieve success rates of 26% and 50% respectively. Error analysis identifies premature tool calls, faulty reasoning, and incorrect tool invocations. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (fair) with confidence 3-4, while one reviewer gave a 6 (good) with confidence 4, disagreeing on the benchmark's overall contribution. The positive reviewer sees the benchmark as a valuable contribution with careful design; the others raise major concerns about scope, evaluation diversity, dataset size, and presentation.

### Strengths

1. **Important problem** – addresses the under-explored intersection of conversational AI and multi-step tool/API usage (R1, R2, R4).

2. **Careful benchmark design** – includes a complete simulated implementation for each tool, realistic variety across action/non-action tools, multiple plugins, and detailed argument structures (R1, R2, R4). Emphasizes world-affecting tools (R3, R4). The equality metrics for both action and non-action tool calls enable automated evaluation with a focus on both correctness and incorrect actions (R2).

3. **Insightful error analysis** – identifies three failure categories (premature calls, faulty reasoning, incorrect invocations) and shows that tool documentation matters (R1, R2, R4 via summary).

4. **Potential community resource** – the benchmark could aid future research on tool-using assistants (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Narrow scope and limited novelty** – the benchmark’s conversational focus is insufficiently differentiated from recent benchmarks (e.g., ToolLLM, ToolAlpaca, APIBank, MINT) that also involve multi-turn interactions and real-world tasks. The specific novelty of the conversational aspect needs clearer articulation (R3, R4). The number of tools (28) and conversations (50 hard + 28 easy) is too small to serve as a robust test set; it resembles a development set more than a benchmark (R1, R2).

2. **Dataset detail and construction lack clarity** – no statistics are given on average turns, number of tool calls per conversation, or tool usage distribution across plugins. The ground-truth dialog creation process (human-authored from GPT-4-generated scenarios) is not validated, and potential bias from GPT-4 scenarios is not addressed. It is unclear whether the user always explicitly requests tool calls or whether the assistant must infer them from passive statements (e.g., “I forgot who emailed me” → search_email). The exact difference between “easy” and “hard” conversations (one tool vs. multi-tool?) is not specified (R1, questions). Manual evaluation for success rates and multiple possible plans are not discussed (R1, question 4).

3. **Severely limited model evaluation** – only GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 are tested. Experiments on open-source models (Llama, Llama Chat, Code Llama, Vicuna, Alpaca, etc.) are missing, which limits the benchmark’s generalizability and utility for the research community. The evaluation does not compare alternative prompting strategies (e.g., those that induce better/worse tool usage) to demonstrate the benchmark’s discriminative power (R1, R2, R3, R4). No attempts to enhance model ability on the benchmark are presented (R2).

4. **Evaluation metrics insufficiently justified** – success rate and tool-call metrics are used, but turn-wise evaluation (e.g., ROUGE variants) is common for conversational systems and is not addressed. The paper does not argue why the proposed metrics suffice or how the generated text quality (not just tool calls) is assessed (R1, question c). The methodology’s generalizability to search engines or general-purpose retrieval tools is unclear (R1, weakness).

5. **Presentation issues** – The two algorithm blocks on page 5 lack sufficient detail and clarity (e.g., missing data structures, control flow, error handling) to be informative (R3, R4). Section 3.2 may be misplaced (R1, question 1).
