## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper evaluates the robustness of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to adversarial visual transformations (e.g., rotations, flips) rather than traditional perturbation-based attacks. The authors propose combining harmful transformations manually and via adversarial learning with gradient approximation to attack models. Evaluated on LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, BLIP-2, and InstructBLIP across VQA, Classification, and Captioning tasks. The results show that simple, easy-to-implement visual transformations can significantly degrade LVLM performance.

### Strengths
- **Original perspective**: focuses on visual transformations instead of perturbations, a seldom-explored direction in adversarial attack studies.  
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: tests multiple models (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP) and tasks (VQA, Classification, Captioning).  
- **Thorough empirical evidence**: uses appropriate metrics (semantic similarity, attack success rate) and a broad spectrum of transformations (spatial and spectral).  
- **Clear presentation**: well-structured prose with intuitive tables and figures.  
- **Significance**: addresses security and reliability of LVLMs, relevant for real-world deployment.  
- **Self-explanatory hypothesis**: the argument that straightforward visual transformations degrade performance similarly to adversarial noise is not thoroughly explored before, and the paper provides strong empirical support (though one reviewer (score 1) finds this hypothesis conceptually flawed—see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Conceptual flaw (minority more negative)**: One reviewer (score 1) argues the paper relies on visually obvious image transformations that drastically alter appearance, neither simulating natural variations nor aligning with the imperceptibility constraint of adversarial attacks. Example: Figure 2 shows an image so distorted that it is unrecognizable to both the VLM and humans; the main takeaway (“more extreme transformation → less capable model”) is predictable and trivial. The authors do not adhere to the conventional adversarial robustness requirement of imperceptible perturbations.  
- **Unclear motivation and problem framing**: If visual transformations already produce large perturbations, why not directly attack the raw image via end-to-end gradient (instead of targeting the transformed image as in Fig. 3)? (Reviewer 1)  
- **Lack of comparison with other adversarial attacks**:  
  - No direct comparison with end-to-end direct attacks on LVLMs (white-box). The authors’ method (using white-box gradients plus transformations) is expected to be weaker than end-to-end attacks.  
  - No comparison with black-box attacks like Attack-Bard (NeurIPS 2023 Workshop). The claim of black-box practicality (line 414) is unsupported; no evidence that transformation-based transfer attacks outperform end-to-end black-box attacks or can attack commercial black-box models. (Reviewer 1)  
  - No comparison with other forms of adversarial attacks to elucidate comparative strengths of transformation-based attacks. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Dataset construction ambiguity**: The description of dataset construction (line 153) does not specify whether samples were randomly selected or manually chosen. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Incomplete comparison with CroPA (Table 9)**: CroPA attacks aim to force output to a target word (e.g., “None”) across many prompts. It is unclear whether the authors’ attack in Table 9 is tested on many prompts or only a fixed prompt. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Limited scalability and generalizability**: The experiments focus on a specific set of models and tasks; there is little discussion on complex, real-time, or dynamic environments. The paper would benefit from testing on a wider range of models and tasks. (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewer 3)  
- **No discussion of defenses**: The paper does not explore potential countermeasures against the proposed transformation-based attacks. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
- **Overall contribution questioned**: One reviewer (score 3) found the paper a simple exploration without new insights or approaches. Another (score 1) stated that the lack of transparent practical applications leaves the findings uninsightful; even slight changes in `transform.Normalize` can drop performance, so the pronounced transformations here are not surprising. (Reviewers 3, 4)
