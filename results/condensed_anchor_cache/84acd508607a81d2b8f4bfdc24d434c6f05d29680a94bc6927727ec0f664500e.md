## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes GeVLM, a geometry-enhanced vision-language model for 3D object grounding. It introduces Viewpoint-Consistent Position Encoding (VCPE) to incorporate observer viewpoint into positional embeddings, and Distance-Aware Cross-Entropy (DACE) loss to penalize predictions based on spatial distance to the target. The authors also create the DetailedScanRefer dataset using GPT-4 and Mask3D, providing fine-grained grounding annotations for each described object. Experiments on ScanRefer, Scan2Cap, ScanQA, and SQA show improvements over a Chat-3D v2 baseline (e.g., +4.0% Acc@0.25, +2.7% Acc@0.50 on ScanRefer).

### Strengths
- The motivation—enhancing 3D LLM grounding by explicitly modeling viewpoint and relative spatial relationships—is clear and aligns with human intuition (R1, R2, R4). This addresses an interesting aspect overlooked by many prior 3D LLMs (R3).
- The VCPE and DACE modules are tailored to the identified problems and successfully tackle viewpoint ambiguity and spatial distance neglect, as demonstrated by ablation studies (R1, R3).
- The DetailedScanRefer dataset, with identifiers and spatial annotations per described object, is a valuable contribution to the community for future research in 3D object grounding (R1, R2).
- The method achieves significant improvements over the Chat-3D v2 baseline (R1, R3), and is orthogonal to existing approaches, offering potential to enhance other methods (R3).
- Focusing on positional relationships is a worthy research problem for 3D tasks (R4). Experiments validate the performance of the proposed method against some advanced algorithms (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution of VCPE is limited.** One reviewer (R4, score 3) notes that VCPE simply applies a rotation transformation to object coordinates based on viewpoint, then uses standard positional embeddings and self-attention—an incremental step with no clear departure from existing methods. The rotation parameterization is not clearly explained, and it is unclear whether it introduces learnable parameters or is a fixed transformation. No qualitative analysis (e.g., attention maps, embedding visualizations) convincingly shows that VCPE and DACE learn the intended spatial relationships (R4). Other reviewers also raise concerns about the small gains from VCPE in the ablation (R1, R2, R4).
- **Real-world applicability and reliance on camera pose.** The method requires camera viewpoint (rotation and translation) as input, which may be difficult to acquire in real-world scenarios. The paper does not address how errors in camera pose estimation would affect VCPE performance (R2). A reviewer also asks whether viewpoint information is only used during training (R3), implying an additional limitation for inference.
- **Insufficient comparisons.** The experimental section lacks comparison with non-LLM-based models specifically designed for 3D visual grounding (R1). Notable missing baselines include ConcreteNet (which also explores viewpoint; R1), LEO, and LL3DA (R3). The paper should clarify whether other compared models also incorporate viewpoint information to ensure fair comparison (R1).
- **Weak improvements on Scan2Cap and SQA.** On Scan2Cap, the improvement is weak despite incorporating extra caption information; some baselines (LEO, LL3DA) are not compared, and caption ability is claimed but no results provided (R3). On SQA, a viewpoint-focused benchmark, the improvement is unconvincing given the paper’s focus on viewpoint. The ScanQA dataset lacks explicit viewpoint annotations, and the paper should clarify the number of viewpoint-related questions in SQA (R3). The claim that DACE loss is less effective in multiple-target scenarios (line 418) lacks evidence; no analysis shows how the spatial emphasis conflicts with semantic focus (R3).
- **Ablation and dataset contributions are inconclusive.** Ablation studies (Table 4) show that most performance gains come from DACE; the contributions of VCPE and DetailedScanRefer are marginal (R1, R2, R4). The paper does not provide an ablation using only “world + DACE” without DetailedScanRefer, nor does it test DACE without the new dataset, making it difficult to attribute gains (R1, R3). The utility of DetailedScanRefer for grounding performance is questionable given the small improvements, and the types of errors introduced by GPT-4o in object ID assignments are not analyzed (R2). No quantitative metric is provided to assess the dataset quality (R1).
- **DACE overlooks semantic similarity.** The distance-aware loss penalizes all incorrect predictions equally based on spatial distance, but objects that are spatially close yet semantically different from the target should be penalized more than semantically similar but spatially distant objects. A combination of spatial and semantic distances would be more effective (R2).
- **Missing metrics and inconsistency.** Metrics such as ROUGE-L and Exact Match on ScanQA are not reported (R3). Chat-3D v2 is marked with * in Tables 1, 3, and 4 but not in Table 2—needs clarification whether those are reproduced results (R3).
- **A reviewer (R3) asks about ambiguity in “near”/“far” questions:** distance between objects remains constant from a given viewpoint, so these may not be viewpoint-ambiguous. Clarification needed.
