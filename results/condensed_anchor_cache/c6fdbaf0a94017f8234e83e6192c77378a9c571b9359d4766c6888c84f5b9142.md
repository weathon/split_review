## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates which diffusion timesteps present harder denoising tasks. Using convergence and KL divergence analyses on FFHQ256, the authors conclude that lower timesteps (less noise, higher SNR) are harder. Based on this, they propose a curriculum learning training scheme: timesteps are clustered by SNR, and the model is trained on easier (high-noise) clusters first, then progressively on harder ones, with an adaptive pacing function based on loss plateau. Experiments on DiT, SiT, and other baselines on FFHQ and ImageNet show improved FID and faster convergence without extra training cost, and ablations suggest orthogonality with loss-weighting techniques like MinSNR.

### Strengths

- **Novelty of idea:** One reviewer (R4, score 8) finds dividing training into difficulty phases by timestep clusters novel and well-motivated. Others (R1, R3, R5) consider the curriculum learning idea generally known but agree the specific application to diffusion timesteps is new and interesting.
- **Clear motivation and analysis:** The observation study (Sec 4, Figs 1–2) convincingly shows that lower timesteps have slower convergence and higher relative entropy, establishing task difficulty direction (R1, R3, R4, R5). The experiment with separate models trained on partial trajectories is particularly appreciated (R5).
- **Consistent improvements:** The method improves FID and convergence across multiple architectures (DiT, SiT, etc.), datasets (FFHQ, ImageNet), and training settings (unconditional, class-conditional, text-to-image) (R1, R2, R4, R5). Gains are achieved with no additional training overhead (R4).
- **Thorough ablations:** Experiments explore larger models, interaction with MinSNR weighting, anti-curriculum training, and number of clusters (R1, R2, R4, R5). The anti-curriculum ablation (hard-to-easy) shows no improvement, supporting the curriculum direction (R2).
- **Well-structured presentation:** The paper flows logically from observations to method to experiments, making the contributions easy to follow (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and theoretical depth:** The clustering is adopted from [1], the pacing function is trivial, and curriculum learning is a general idea (R1, R3, R4). There is insufficient explanation of why the easy-to-hard scheme works for diffusion models, especially given that time-step conditioning changes across phases and parameters are shared (R3). A theoretical justification or discussion linking to curriculum learning literature is missing (R3). One reviewer (R4) notes the observation that low-noise tasks are harder is already well-known.
- **Insufficient comparison to state-of-the-art:** The reported FIDs are significantly worse than current SoTA (e.g., ImageNet 256 FID <5 as of 2022). The paper does not discuss this gap or demonstrate improvement on a very high-performing model (e.g., EDM2) (R3). R1 specifically requests applying the method to EDM2, which proposes dynamic loss weighting and outperforms DiT/EDM. R2 notes the main effect is faster convergence, not final performance improvement, as the gap shrinks at longer training (400k vs 2M steps).
- **Missing experiments and generalizability concerns:**
  - Analysis is only on FFHQ256; dataset and resolution may affect difficulty distribution (R1 cites [3,4] showing different optimal intervals per dataset). Pixel vs. latent space behavior should be discussed (R1).
  - Only DiT and EDM are tested; EDM2 and flow-based/consistency models should be tried (R1, R4).
  - Anti-curriculum comparison is only shown for class-conditional generation; unconditional should be shown too (R2).
  - The experiment where model is first trained on only the hardest cluster (C_N) then standard training is missing; this would test if the benefit is truly from curriculum or just from early focus on the initial noise-to-data mapping (R5).
  - Training steps equalization: How is the total number of steps kept constant when each phase uses a patience threshold (R5)? Are all models compared at the same number of training iterations (R5)?
  - Statistical significance is unclear for close FID values; confidence intervals and number of samples should be reported (R5).
  - The proposed method may not be totally orthogonal to noise-weighting techniques (MinSNR): a discussion is needed (R3).
- **Method details and clarity issues:** 
  - Section 4.2: It is not clearly explained why high KL between marginals of two timesteps implies higher task difficulty. One interpretation is that larger changes are required, but this should be explicitly stated (R1, R4). Also, the expression in line 294 seems to contradict Fig.2 (R4).
  - The SNR-based clustering is insufficiently described; why is it better than uniform clustering (R2)? Should gradient-based clustering [1,2] be considered (R1)?
  - Figure 4 is puzzling: results across different numbers of splits (5,10,20,30) are nearly identical except for the chosen 20 splits. The lack of clear explanation and the precision drop at 30 clusters undermines robustness (R5).
- **Missing citations:** General idea of easy-to-hard diffusion training relates to progressive distillation [1] and cascaded diffusion [2] (R4). Supplementary material has improperly cited references (R3).
- **Minor presentation issues:** Table 2, Figure 4 exceed specified margins (R5). Section 5 is labeled "Method" but should be "Method" or "Methodology" (R5).
