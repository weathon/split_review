- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper provides a non-asymptotic analysis of DP-SGD (and variants with Polyak-Ruppert averaging) under local differential privacy (using GDP notation) for both strongly convex and non-strongly convex loss functions, aiming to give practitioners hyperparameter guidelines. Numerical simulations are included. One reviewer (score 6) found the theoretical analysis showing the benefit of weight averaging in private optimization to be solid and novel, stating “the paper just makes the cut for me because of this.” The three other reviewers (scores 3) found the technical contribution weak, the presentation difficult, and the results similar to prior non-private work. There is strong disagreement on the overall contribution.

### Strengths

- Provides a comprehensive non-asymptotic convergence analysis of DP-SGD, including tedious calculations for the convergence rate (Reviewer 1).
- Detailed proofs and several numerical simulations are given (Reviewer 2).
- Covers both strongly convex and non-strongly convex settings with fine analysis (Reviewer 3); aims to help practitioners choose hyperparameters (Reviewer 3).
- Solid theoretical analysis showing that the Polyak-Ruppert averaged iterate improves over the last iterate in private optimization. According to one reviewer, results like this (benefits of weight averaging in private optimization) do not exist in the literature (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and clarity of theoretical results**:  
  The theorem statements are extremely hard to parse; it is not clear how practitioners could extract hyperparameter guidelines from the complicated expressions (Reviewer 1: “I really (like really) do not see how one can figure out these hyperparameters”). Theorem 4 is overloaded with symbols; the authors should defer full versions to the appendix and present simplified, leading-order asymptotic forms (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The paper lacks an explicit remark comparing the convergence dependence of the averaged iterate and the last iterate on \(n\), and does not highlight which terms (e.g., reduced variance) lead to the improved bound (Reviewer 4). Equations 3 and 4 appear to have a compiling error (Reviewer 3). Typographical errors: e.g., citing the same authors on page 2; missing cross‑reference on the first page of supplementary material; referencing “(3)” instead of “Theorem 3” in Remarks (Reviewer 1). These show a lack of diligence (Reviewer 1).

- **Assumptions and motivation**:  
  The Hessian smoothness assumption (Condition 3) is too strong (Reviewer 1). The motivation for using GDP notation instead of standard LDP is poorly explained; since each user’s data passes only once, the composition guarantee of GDP is not relevant (Reviewer 2). It is unclear whether the results can be extended to regular \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-DP (Reviewer 4). The paper’s claim that convergence is not assured when \(\alpha=1\) is questioned: convergence for strongly convex losses with step size \(O(1/i)\) may be readily attainable by adapting the proof of Rakhlin et al. (2011) (Reviewer 2).

- **Comparison with prior work**:  
  The results and proof techniques are very similar to the non‑private setting (Rakhlin et al. 2011); the addition of Gaussian noise does not pose significant challenges (Reviewer 2). The paper does not explicitly compare its bounds to non‑private ones, leaving unclear how privacy noise affects the constants and convergence speed (Reviewer 2). More broadly, there is no comprehensive comparison with existing LDP‑SGD papers (e.g., Wang et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2020); a summary table of previous upper bounds and the new ones is missing (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (score 6) does not share this assessment and considers the analysis novel—this reviewer believes no prior results on weight averaging in private optimization exist (Reviewer 4).

- **Technical contribution and novelty**:  
  The proof techniques lack sufficient novelty; the technical contribution is relatively weak (Reviewer 3). The use of Polyak-Ruppert averaging is not motivated, and the authors do not explore other SGD variants that might give better performance (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing lower bounds and tightness**:  
  No lower bounds are provided, so the tightness of the upper bounds cannot be evaluated (Reviewer 3).

- **Issues with specific terms in the bounds**:  
  Theorem 4 contains a quartic term \(\|\theta_0 - \theta^*\|^4\), whereas Theorem 3 has only a quadratic term. If the initialization is far from the optimum, this could make the averaged iterate worse than the last iterate; the paper should discuss this penalty and the conditions under which averaging helps (Reviewer 4). It is unclear why expectations \(\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_0 - \theta^*\|^2]\) and \(\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_0 - \theta^*\|^4]\) appear, since these are deterministic; the random source for the expectation is not specified (Reviewer 4). The constants \(C_{3,0}\) and \(C_{4,0}\) in Theorem 4 are not defined (Reviewer 4). Condition 3 seems unused in Theorem 3 because no \(C_1\) term appears (Reviewer 4).

- **Experiments**:  
  The experiments are too small; they should be larger (beyond linear/logistic regression, with larger dimension \(d\)) and include different privacy budgets to evaluate performance across privacy regimes. One reviewer found the current experiments insufficient to show practical relevance (Reviewer 4).

- **Intuitive explanation**:  
  The paper provides only mathematics; an intuitive explanation of why averaging helps would improve understanding (Reviewer 4).
