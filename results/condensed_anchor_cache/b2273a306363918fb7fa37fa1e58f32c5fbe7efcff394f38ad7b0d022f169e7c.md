## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose EquiformerV2, which extends Equiformer to higher-degree irreducible representations by replacing SO(3) convolutions with eSCN convolutions (reducing complexity from O(L^6) to O(L^3)) and introducing three architectural improvements: attention re-normalization, separable S^2 activation, and separable layer normalization. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on OC20 (up to 9% improvement on forces, 4% on energies) and OC22, offers better speed-accuracy trade-offs (Figure 4), and reduces DFT calculations by 2× on AdsorbML. EquiformerV2 trained on only OC22 outperforms GemNet-OC trained on both OC20 and OC22, demonstrating data efficiency. On QM9, performance is similar to Equiformer.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive experiments across OC20, OC22, QM9, and AdsorbML, with state-of-the-art results on OC20 S2EF, IS2RS, and IS2RE tasks, especially in force prediction.
- Scaling to higher-degree representations is verified to be useful for large-scale DFT benchmarks, leveraging eSCN’s efficient SO(2) convolution to enable higher maximum degrees.
- The three architectural modifications (attention re-normalization, separable S^2 activation, separable layer normalization) are presented as novel and show empirical gains in ablation studies on OC20.
- Better speed-accuracy trade-off than eSCN (Figure 4) and strong data efficiency (OC22 only outperforms GemNet-OC trained on OC20+OC22).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is limited: the efficient SO(2) convolution and S^2 activation are from eSCN; attention re-normalization and separable layer normalization are engineering tricks. The overall architecture is an incremental improvement over Equiformer and eSCN.
- Ablation study for the three proposed modules is only provided on OC20; a similar ablation on QM9 is missing to test generalizability (Reviewer 1).
- On QM9, performance is similar to Equiformer, suggesting limited benefit for smaller datasets (Reviewer 2).
- Minor issues: Equation (2) in Appendix A.1 should use \ddots; Equation (4) in Appendix A.3 should not use a new notation assignment on the left (Reviewer 1). There is a double citation for the stochastic depth paper (Reviewer 2).
