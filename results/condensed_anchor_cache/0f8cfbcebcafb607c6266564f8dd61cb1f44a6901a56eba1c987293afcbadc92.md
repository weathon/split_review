## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel LLM-guided spatio-temporal framework for modeling disease progression in neurodegenerative diseases, specifically tau-pathology propagation in Alzheimer’s Disease. The method simultaneously estimates temporal positioning of patient data along a common disease trajectory and discovers a graph structure capturing spatiotemporal relationships between brain regions. It uses prompts to query multiple LLMs (mixture) as expert guides to inform graph initialization and regularization, aiming to bridge mechanistic models and data-driven approaches for better convergence, stability, and interpretability. The approach is tested on ADNI data with a limited sample (254 samples, 185 subjects; mostly baseline and one follow-up).

### Strengths
- **Innovative use of LLMs in disease progression modeling**: The paper introduces a creative and potentially impactful approach by leveraging LLMs for graph-based disease progression modeling. (Reviewer 1)
- **First attempt at using LLM for understanding AD progression** (Reviewer 2)
- **Exploration of LLMs as expert guides to enhance graph inference by integrating expert knowledge** (Reviewer 3)
- **Well-written and engaging**: The paper is well-written and engaging; the idea of leveraging LLM knowledge for graph initialization is intriguing and effectively demonstrated. (Reviewer 4)
- **Solid background information** provided to help readers grasp the context; sufficient detail on critical components including prompting strategies, clear mathematical definitions, and experimental details. (Reviewer 4)
- **Evaluation through critical edge numbers** is interesting and effective, showcasing advantages of LLM-based graphs. (Reviewer 4)
- **Extensive analysis**: Covers prompt ablation, synthetic data, both data-driven and mechanistic contexts, and model convergence. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Vague language and disorganized manuscript** (Reviewer 1; notable contrast with Reviewer 4’s “well-written”):
    - Terms like “causal interactions” used without justification; graph learning alone does not establish causation (associative, not causal).
    - “Long-term” not clearly defined (timeframe missing).
    - Equation 9 referenced before it is introduced (Section 2.2 vs 2.5.1); evaluation measure SSE used before being defined (also noted by Reviewer 4: “SSE calculation lacks clear definition”).
    - Numerous typos throughout (e.g., “consder”, “knowlegdge”) (Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of comparisons with baselines and limited experiments**:
    - Baselines cited (e.g., Bellot et al. 2021) but not empirically compared. (Reviewer 1)
    - Insufficient comparison with many disease progression modeling models. (Reviewer 3)
    - No qualitative results showing predicted trajectories in test examples. (Reviewer 1)
    - Experiments conducted on a single data split (train-test-validation), no cross-validation or random splits; small variances reported in Table 2 make it difficult to evaluate significance/reproducibility. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Inconsistency in LLM usage**: Results in sections 3.3 and 3.4 use only Claude3.5, not the proposed mixture of LLMs; weakens coherence and validity of the claimed method. (Reviewer 1)
- **Questionable prompt strategy for LLMs** (Reviewer 1):
    - **Scientific validity**: General-purpose LLMs lack domain-specific scientific knowledge, prone to hallucinations; generated weights/connections may be inaccurate or fabricated.
    - **Scalability**: Creating prompts for each brain region does not scale to fine-grained ROI representations, computationally intensive and potentially inconsistent.
    - **Stability/consistency**: LLM responses vary with prompt phrasing; no analysis of prompt sensitivity or consistency checks provided.
- **Limited longitudinal data for temporal modeling**: Only 254 samples from 185 subjects; most have baseline and one follow-up. Doubts about model’s ability to learn meaningful temporal dynamics; the paper does not address how it captures progression trends with such limited data. (Reviewer 1, also Reviewer 2: small sample size; Reviewer 3 asks sample size and number of time points per subject.)
- **Unclear data preprocessing** (Reviewer 2):
    - Which brain atlas used? Appears to be Desikan-Killiany atlas, which excludes subcortical regions – not a perfect parcellation for studying AD.
- **Lack of biological underpinnings in result interpretation**: Results lack supporting evidence from current AD literature. (Reviewer 2, also Reviewer 3: “Can authors provide the related propagation pattern in the brain over time?”)
- **Unaddressed disease heterogeneity**: Model assumes a single typical progression path, overlooking significant heterogeneity in AD (e.g., amyloid and tau spreads). (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited discussion of practical application and real-world feasibility**: Reliance on LLM prompts, access, and prompt engineering may hinder clinical adoption. (Reviewer 1)
- **Technical details unclear**:
    - In Eq. (5), why p is calculated from the 99th percentile of tau distribution? (Reviewer 3)
    - In Eq. (8), what does D_LLM represent? Clarify how structural connectivity is addressed in Eqs (7-9) (the diffusion process along white matter bundles is not clearly accounted for). (Reviewer 3)
    - In Eq. (10), what does X stand for? Relationship of this section to previous sections unclear. (Reviewer 3)
    - Ratio for mixing two LLMs (0.865:0.135) not justified or empirically supported. (Reviewer 1)
    - Handling of repeated measures in training not specified. (Reviewer 1)
    - Performance on other diseases? (Reviewer 3)
- **Figure 4 / faster convergence clarity** (Reviewer 4):
    - Decreasing critical edge number via “7-factor” prompt is unclear; the other factors provided by LLM do not show much advantage.
    - Representation of faster convergence (Appendix A.3) between the second and third figures could be clearer; adjusting scale might help.
- **Missing comparison clarification** (Reviewer 4): When comparing Model 1 and Model 2, are the authors comparing the two methods directly, or the graphs used in those models within the context of Method 3?
- **Why summation limited to four types of connectivity in Section A.3, not five?** (Reviewer 4)
