## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes WAPITI, a watermarking method for open‑source fine‑tuned LLMs. It embeds a watermark into the base model via distillation, then transfers it to a fine‑tuned model by interpolating between the fine‑tuned weights and the watermark weight delta (Δθ). The approach aims to maintain fine‑tuned capabilities while providing detectability and resistance to fine‑tuning attacks, with controllable watermark strength via a hyperparameter λ. Reviewers highlighted the importance of the problem and the simplicity of the idea, but raised substantial concerns about theoretical rigor, experimental scope, missing threat model, insufficient baselines, and absence of robustness and ablation studies. One reviewer (rating 6) was significantly more positive, praising the parameter‑integration idea and structure; the other three (ratings 3) found the contribution limited and the evaluation inadequate.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important challenge: watermarking fine‑tuned open‑source LLMs is hard, and the observation that fine‑tuned models are difficult to watermark is valuable.
- The core idea of using parameter integration (adding Δθ from the base model to the fine‑tuned model) is novel as a parameter‑based watermarking method that avoids the performance degradation and high computational cost of watermark distillation.
- The method is relatively simple and provides controllable watermark detectability with a trade‑off in generation quality.
- The approach is presented clearly and the experiments (using KGW and AAR watermarks) appear sound to some reviewers.
- A minority reviewer (rating 6) specifically commended the well‑structured paper and the logical flow that effectively conveys the main content.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical grounding.** The method relies on multiple approximations and heuristic observations (especially the orthogonality of parameter differences) without rigorous justification. The sensitivity of these assumptions in high‑dimensional parameter spaces is not analyzed.
- **Missing threat model.** If the base model is accessible (a reasonable assumption for open‑source models), an adversary can invoke Algorithm 1, obtain Δθ_base, and undo the watermark. This is a significant oversight that renders the watermark easily removable.
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons.** Only AAR and KGW are compared. The evaluation needs to include a wider range of watermarking techniques, especially parameter‑based methods, to establish competitiveness. Additional attacks (model pruning, quantization, adversarial fine‑tuning) should be tested.
- **Lack of robustness analysis.** No evaluation against common attacks such as text editing, paraphrasing, or changes in decoding parameters (e.g., temperature scaling).
- **No ablation study on λ.** The hyperparameter λ controls watermark strength, yet its effect on both detectability (p‑values) and performance (MMLU, GSM8k) is not systematically ablated. The paper refers to Appendix F, but only partial analysis is found in Appendix E.1, and the selection method for λ is not explained.
- **Cherry‑picked dataset for baseline.** Figure 2 uses mathematical data (low entropy) to show that watermark distillation degrades model performance. Mathematics is notoriously difficult to watermark; the authors should have used watermarks designed for low‑entropy text (e.g., [1]). The baseline should instead demonstrate that a pre‑trained watermarked model, when fine‑tuned on mathematical data, does not exhibit detectability. Gu et al. [2] show that watermarks can be resilient to fine‑tuning.
- **Misleading novelty claims.** The inability of watermark distillation to withstand fine‑tuning was already identified in Gu et al. The paper’s claim of being “first to distil watermarks” is confusing because Gu et al. already do that. The notion of preserving “fine‑tuned capabilities” is not rigorously defined or quantitatively compared.
- **“Train‑free” claim is inaccurate.** The method requires invoking the watermark distillation algorithm (Algorithm 1) as a subroutine, which involves training. It should be described as requiring a single distillation step for the base model.
- **Missing experimental details.** Table 2 lacks information on the scoring method (binomial or z‑score for KGW), number of tokens scored, and deduplication procedure. The p‑values of 0.5 appear to be expected rather than computed; evidence that tests yield random p‑values under H0 is needed.
- **Distillation process insufficiently documented.** The authors claim distillation impacts math capability (Llama‑2‑7B) but do not provide the specific datasets, hyperparameters, or training procedures used, nor explore alternative distillation strategies that might mitigate the degradation. The need for 1.3M samples for distillation is not justified (what constitutes a “sample”? for which method and window size?).
- **Contribution relative to prior work.** The paper is an improvement on the watermark distillation scheme of Gu et al., which diminishes its originality. While one reviewer (rating 6) sees it as the first parameter‑integration method, three others find the contribution limited, noting that parameter interpolation (e.g., LoRA) is a known technique.
- **Lack of efficiency quantification.** The claim of efficiency (line 415) lacks supporting execution time data; time cost experiments under various models and watermark methods are needed.
- **Ambiguity regarding fine‑tuning attacks.** The paper claims resistance to fine‑tuning attacks (line 78) but does not define what constitutes a “fine‑tuning attack” versus standard fine‑tuning. Appendix E.2 attempts to argue synchronous decline in detectability and usability, but comparative experiments against Gu et al. are missing to demonstrate advantage. The selection of fine‑tuned models (Llama‑Math, Llama‑QA) omits Llama‑chat and Pythia‑chat used elsewhere, and omits the AAR watermark.
- **Visual and typographical errors.** Figure 2 lacks clear annotations; Table 1 has a missing checkmark and inappropriate parentheses; the appendix contains duplicated figures (F.3 and E.1 identical, Figures 2 and E.2 same), incorrect references (Figure 14 referred to as Figure 6), missing captions for Figures 9‑14, and misspelling (“coefffcient.S”). These errors detract from credibility.
