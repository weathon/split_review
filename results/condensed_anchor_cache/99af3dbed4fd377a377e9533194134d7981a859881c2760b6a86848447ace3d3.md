## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a three-stage pipeline for contextually harmonious local video editing, a task focused on replacing a local moving subject in multi-subject videos while preserving its original motion and making its size harmonious with the scene. The stages are: (1) using a pretrained VLM (Vision-Language Model) to estimate the size/shape ratio between original and replacement subjects, (2) erasing non-target moving subjects to extract the target's motion and dynamically choose the editing method, and (3) a coarse-to-fine subject transfer that aligns bottom centers (best for grounded objects) and refines the edit. The method is evaluated on a new dataset derived from TGVE (40 out of 76 videos). Quantitative and qualitative results are compared against baselines including FateZero, with a user study on harmony score. Reviewers are mostly negative (scores 3,3,3) with one positive (score 6) due to the claimed performance and reasonable use of world knowledge.

### Strengths

- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 1, score 6, confidence 4):** The paper interestingly targets the size harmonization of the edited subject with the original video context. Using pretrained LLMs/VLMs to obtain world knowledge of subject size is a reasonable and clever approach (also highlighted by Reviewer 2). The proposed method achieves "much better performance than previous methods" according to R1.
- **Reviewer 2 (score 3, confidence 5):** The approach of leveraging a large multi-modal pretrained model for shape/size ratios is feasible and easily applicable. The user study on harmony score demonstrates improvement on the claimed size-effect problem over previous methods. Visual comparisons via the provided Google Sites link are valuable, especially when large size changes are needed. The bottom-center alignment offers a simple solution for grounded objects.
- **Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 4):** The divide-and-conquer design (using world model for source-target relationship, pipeline to address misalignment between edited and non-edited areas) recognizes limitations and proposes reasonable techniques.
- **Reviewer 4 (score 3, confidence 5):** Figure 1 is illustrative. Experiments are sufficient. Writing is easy to follow (though Reviewer 2 strongly disagrees on writing quality, noting major issues; Reviewer 3 finds it fair). R4 considers experiments sufficient, while R3 finds temporal consistency evaluation insufficient.
- **General:** The method directly tackles the underexplored challenge of maintaining subject size harmony in multi-subject videos.

### Weaknesses

- **Claim of novelty is questionable:** The paper states it is "the first to focus on local video editing" (line 100). Reviewer 2 notes that AVID (CVPR 2024), Videoshop (ECCV 2024), and VideoSwap (CVPR 2024) all address local editing. The paper should clarify its distinctiveness, especially compared to using one of these methods with an object mask. (R2)
- **Contextually harmonious editing is not new:** Reviewer 4 argues that traditional video editing always strives for harmonious integration without altering unintended attributes. The paper does not clearly distinguish its definition from standard video editing. (R4)
- **Heavy reliance on external models with insufficient robustness evaluation:** The pipeline depends on pre-trained VLMs for mask extraction, shape/size ratio estimation, and object detection. Failure cases arise if the VLM produces inaccurate masks or incorrect size ratios. No experiments evaluate robustness to mask inaccuracies (R1, R2, R3). The VLM's reliability for rare/unusual objects is not discussed (R2). The choice of using 40 videos out of the 76 TGVE dataset is unexplained (R3).
- **Temporal consistency is weak:** Table 1 shows the proposed method underperforms in temporal consistency metrics. Reviewer 3 questions whether the inter-frame DIFT similarity matrix can fully address pixel-level flickering. Reviewer 4 notes the sample appears to have noticeable flickering. No edited videos are provided to assess temporal performance (R1, R3, R4); only two static frames are shown. R1 explicitly requires evaluation of temporal performance via videos. Reviewer 2's Google Sites link provides some results but not full temporal evaluation.
- **Attribute preservation and quality issues:** In Figure 1, the man's face appears noticeably altered, indicating below-standard consistency and attribute preservation (R4). The quantitative results table (Table 1) shows that FateZero achieves better scores on masked LPIPS and BM-preserve metrics, but these are not bolded (R2, minor formatting issue). The method's harmony metric is not objectively defined; Reviewer 4 asks how harmony is measured, noting it is highly abstract and subjective.
- **Pipeline complexity and usability:** The three-stage pipeline (including a coarse-to-fine final stage) is tedious and may not be easy to use (R1). Figure 2 is difficult to follow; it should be split into three modules or restructured with better labels (R2).
- **Writing and presentation issues:** Significant grammatical errors and language unclearness (R2). There are typos (e.g., "DreamMotion" should be corrected; "DMT" undefined; missing spaces). These hinder readability. (R2, partially contradicted by R3 and R4 who find writing easy to follow, but R2's confidence is high (5) and provides specific examples.)
- **Lack of computational cost analysis:** No runtime or GPU requirements are provided; such details are critical for practical feasibility and scalability (R2).
- **Applicability limitations and failure cases:** The bottom-center alignment works best for grounded objects (e.g., walking person, moving car); for mid-air objects or those without a floor reference, the position may be inaccurate. The authors should clarify this and discuss failure cases (R2, also R1 asks about failure cases). Multi-subject editing support is not addressed (R1 asks: could the method support multi-subject editing?). The paper focuses on single-subject replacement.
