## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GeoGS3D, a two-stage framework for single-view 3D object reconstruction. The first stage uses a diffusion model conditioned on a proposed orthogonal-plane decomposition of the input image to generate multi-view consistent images. The second stage reconstructs a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation from those views, using epipolar attention and a novel metric called Gaussian Divergence Significance (GDS) to prune unnecessary optimization operations. The method is evaluated on Objaverse and Google Scanned Objects datasets, with quantitative and qualitative comparisons against several baselines.

### Strengths
- The problem of single-view 3D reconstruction is of interest to the research community. (R1, R2)
- The paper is easy to follow. (R1)
- The experimental evaluation shows strong performance: multi-view generation is quantitatively closer to ground truth (Table 1) and qualitatively more reasonable (Figure 4); 3D reconstruction achieves better metric scores (Table 2) and more detailed results (Figure 5). (R2)
- The evaluation comprises both synthetic (Objaverse) and real (Google Scanned Objects) datasets, the latter being out of training distribution. (R2)
- Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the proposed components (geometric conditioning, epipolar attention, GDS). (R2, R4)
- The proposed GDS metric significantly improves the reconstruction speed of 3DGS while enhancing quality. (R3, R5)
- The method performs remarkably well both in quantitative metrics and visual quality. (R3)
- The mechanism of epipolar attention is clearly explained, and its application in reconstruction is innovative. (R5)
- Good demonstration of combining Zero123, epipolar attention, and 3DGS as components. (R4)

### Weaknesses
**1. Lack of clarity about the reconstruction stage (feed-forward vs. per-object optimization).** Multiple reviewers found the description confusing. The paper seems to describe both feed-forward prediction (UNet outputting a fixed set of Gaussians, epipolar/cross attention on UNet features, training objective in Eq. 6) and per-object fitting (iterative optimization, adaptive density control with split/clone, 15-minute optimization time). These two processes are incompatible, and it is not explained how they interact. (R1, R2, R3) R2 detailed conflicting clues:
- Lines 150ff: “3D Gaussians G are then optimized with these multi-view images through an accelerated Gaussian Splatting.”
- Lines 267ff: epipolar and cross attention applied to “the intermediate UNet feature[s]”.
- Lines 375ff: “the network that maps the input images to the mixtures of Gaussians is architecturally identical to the UNet”.
- Use of adaptive density control (split/clone) suggests optimization, not feed-forward.
- Reconstruction takes 15 minutes (Table 4), too long for feed-forward.
R3 asks: how do point splitting/cloning and GDS work if the UNet outputs a fixed number of Gaussian points (like LGM)? R1 and R2 request a clear step-by-step description of the entire reconstruction process and how Sections 3.3 and 3.4 relate temporally and functionally.

**2. The “geometry-aware” multi-view generation lacks evidence of true 3D decomposition.** The proposed orthogonal-plane decomposition is only a variant of multi-head attention: QKV are split into three heads, with an additional summation in Eq. 3. There is no mechanism to enforce that the decoupled features (F_xy, F_yz, F_xz) actually correspond to orthogonal 3D planes. (R1, R2, R5) R2 notes that F_yz and F_xz may be equivalent and therefore redundant. R5 states: “it seems to process 2D features solely through 2D attention modules.” R1 asks for an ablation between multi-head attention and the “cat-sum” operation, and for visualizations showing the claimed decomposition. R2 adds that the geometric conditioning is missing from the training objective (Eq. 4), and it is unclear how the features are used by the diffusion model.

**3. Epipolar attention is not novel and lacks proper citations to prior work.** Multiple reviewers point out that epipolar attention has been extensively used in multi-view stereo (e.g., MVSNeRF, SparseNeuS), generalizable 3D reconstruction (pixelSplat, latentSplat), and multi-view diffusion (EpiDiff, MVDiff). The paper does not cite these methods and claims novelty without differentiating from them. (R1, R2, R3, R4) R1 suggests the work sounds like a simple extension of Zero123 and LGM. R4 explicitly states that the epipolar attention mechanism is too similar to EpiDiff (CVPR 2024), which is not cited nor compared as a baseline. R4 further notes that EpiDiff should have been used as a baseline.

**4. Gaussian Divergence Significance (GDS) is not novel.** GDS is simply the Wasserstein distance between two Gaussians, which is an existing distance measure. Renaming it does not constitute novelty. (R4) R3 and R5, however, praise GDS for improving speed and quality, creating a disagreement. R4 suggests that the authors should re-consider wording their strategy (using a Wasserstein-distance threshold to decide when to split/clone) rather than claiming a novel metric.

**5. Time complexity claims are incorrect or misleading.**
- R3: The complexity of KNN is O(N log N), not O(N) as stated in Line 320 (because each point queries a KD-tree).
- R4: The claim that GDS reduces time from O(N²) to O(N) is invalid because worst-case complexity (noisy, compact scene) still requires comparing many pairs.

**6. Insufficient baselines and missing comparisons.** The paper does not compare against state-of-the-art methods such as Era3D, Zero123++, TripoSR, SF3D, or EpiDiff. Current baselines do not reflect the state of the art. (R5, R4)

**7. Evaluation details are unclear or missing.**
- R2: It is unclear whether the 3D reconstruction evaluation in Table 2 is disentangled from the multi-view generation (i.e., whether all methods use the same generated views as input). If not, the comparison is confounded.
- R5: The qualitative comparisons in Figures 4 and 6 lack ground truth references.
- R4: No in-the-wild object reconstructions are shown, unlike prior work (e.g., Zero123).
- R5: The paper should specify how many views are generated by the diffusion model, how the 32 views are selected, and whether poses are fixed.

**8. Other technical and presentation issues.**
- R1: The paper claims epipolar attention as novelty but should cite prior work.
- R2: Self-attention on a learnable embedding (Line 221) is unnecessary.
- R2: How relative camera poses are encoded by the CLIP text encoder is unexplained.
- R2: The claim that epipolar attention reduces computation is not justified (it is a weighted dense attention).
- R3: The learnable embedding “u” is absent from Figure 2; the transformation from CrossAttn(u, h) to triplane representations is not explained.
- R4: Table captions are not informative (e.g., Table 3 does not specify what variants a, b, c, d are).
- R4: GDS formulation (a major claim) and epipolar attention details are placed in the appendix; they should be in the main text.
- R5: Eq. 4 is missing the geometric condition; Eq. 6 has a subscript error (s not appearing in summation).
- R5: The ablation on orthogonal-plane decomposition removes geometric guidance entirely instead of removing the decomposition module itself; previous works use input image features as condition, so removing condition entirely will certainly degrade performance.
- R5: Section 3.2 lacks explanation of how multi-view consistency is enforced — the training objective involves a single target view (Eq. 4); it is unclear whether the model generates one view per diffusion process or multiple views with cross-attention (like Wonder3D).
- R2: The introduction is repetitive regarding contributions (first in text, then as a list).
- R2: The extensive recap of 3DGS in preliminaries is unnecessary.
