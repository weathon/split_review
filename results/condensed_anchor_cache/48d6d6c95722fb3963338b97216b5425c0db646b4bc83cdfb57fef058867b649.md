## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a “metanetwork” approach to interpret ANNs by training an autoencoder on the weights of single-layer fully-connected networks. The resulting low-dimensional embedding is used to predict properties of the models’ training datasets (modality, dataset identity) via a k-NN classifier, without access to the original data. The idea is motivated by task-embedding methods (MeLA, Task2Vec, Model2Vec). The experiments are limited to simple single-layer ANNs trained on small datasets (audio/video features, 20-class subsets). Evaluation uses t-SNE visualization and confusion matrices.

### Strengths
- **Relevant and interesting idea**: The concept of representing entire networks as low-dimensional vectors to compare them is novel and addresses a relevant topic (interpretability) (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Well-written paper** (with minor formatting issues) (R2).
- **Data-free encoding**: The approach encodes model weights without needing the training dataset, which could be useful when data access is restricted (R2).
- **Adequate experiments for simple architecture**: For the very simple single-layer ANN case, the experiments support the claim that modality/dataset can be predicted from the embedding (R2).
- **Meta-learning perspective**: The use of an autoencoder over trained weights is a meta-learning approach to understanding networks (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear how this improves interpretability** (all reviewers). Multiple reviewers note that predicting modality or dataset ID does not provide insight into the model’s internal decision-making, learned features, or biases. The paper does not explain how the encoded representation reveals which inputs are influential or what patterns the model learned. The link between embedding space and actual interpretability is not established (R2: “unsubstantiated”; R3: “poor connection”; R4: “unclear what we can say about interpreting ANNs”).
- **Poorly explained methodology** (R1, R3). The model setup, role of each component (meta-encoder, meta-autoencoder, k-NN, etc.), and training procedure are confusing. A clear figure early in the paper would help (R3). Figure 1 lacks a proper caption and the model-to-be-interpreted appears to be an autoencoder, which is not stated (R4).
- **Very limited experimental scope** (R1, R3, R4). Only single-layer fully-connected ANNs are tested; the approach’s scalability to deep networks is not addressed. It is unclear which layers to encode for multi-layered networks, how to handle varying input sizes, or whether the method works with ReLU activations as claimed (R2). Basic autoencoders on toy datasets (audio/video features, 20-class subsets) limit practical implications (R4). No comparisons to baselines or alternative embedding methods (R3).
- **No novelty over existing task/model embedding methods** (R4). The paper does not clearly distinguish itself from Task2Vec, Model2Vec, or other weight-space approaches (e.g., Navon et al., ICML’23). The contribution is rated as fair (R1,R2,R3) or poor (R4).
- **Practical limitations for training the meta-encoder** (R2). In practice, users train few networks; it is unclear how to train the encoder when only a small number of models are available. The reliance on many pre-trained models is a significant limitation.
- **Unclear design choices** (R2, R4). Why are two separate meta-autoencoders (unit-wise and inter-unit) needed instead of directly encoding the full weight matrix? If the meta-encoder is frozen, what is “trained” in the k-NN classifier (R4)?.
- **Minor formatting issues**: “Indeed” capitalized (R2,R3), missing figure references (“Figure” instead of “Figure 1”) (R2,R3,R4), typos (“the all 20 classes”, “each dataset as v:” → “a:”) (R3), incomplete references (e.g., missing publication venues) (R4). R2 also notes use of “[1]” instead of “Fig. [1]”.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R1) rated the paper very poorly (score 1) due to poor explanation, no experimental data, and confusion between ability representation and feature modality classification. The other three gave score 3. R1’s specific criticisms: the meta-network does not directly assess the ability of the specific neural network but merely meta-classifies the modality of features, which is quite different from what the authors claim.
