## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper protects images from unauthorized diffusion-based mimicry (editing, inpainting, textual inversion) by adding adversarial perturbations. The key finding is that the encoder of a latent diffusion model (LDM) is more vulnerable than the denoiser. Based on this, the authors use Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to remove gradient propagation through the U-Net, doubling speed and halving memory. They also counterintuitively minimize semantic loss (rather than maximize it) to generate more natural perturbations. Experiments on multiple tasks and datasets show competitive or better results compared to existing methods (e.g., AdvDM, Photoguard).

### Strengths
+ The observation that the encoder is more vulnerable than the denoiser is novel and insightful (all reviewers).
+ The proposed SDS-based attack is more efficient (faster, lower resource), making protection more practical for individual users (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
+ The method is effective against several latest baselines and outperforms them in many settings (Reviewers 1, 2).
+ The paper attacks multiple tasks: image editing, inpainting, textual inversion (Reviewer 3).
+ Minimizing semantic loss yields more harmonious and imperceptible perturbations (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear mathematical justification for Eq. 5**: The approximation of the U-Net Jacobian as an identity matrix is not rigorously justified; it is unclear under what conditions this holds and what happens when it does not (Reviewer 1).  
- **Insufficient evidence for encoder vs. denoiser vulnerability**: The 10× perturbation budget in Figure 2 should have been enough for attacks on the denoiser, yet they fail – the conclusion needs stronger evidence and theoretical grounding (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing relative perturbation scale**: The paper compares absolute embedding changes; a normalized L-infinity norm (perturbation in input vs. embedding space, divided by their respective ranges) is needed to fairly assess attack impact (Reviewer 1).  
- **Lack of transferability analysis**: It is unclear whether perturbations generated on one LDM (e.g., Stable Diffusion) protect images from misuse by an unknown model; experiments on cross-model transfer are missing (Reviewer 2, Q1).  
- **No defense against image transformations**: The method is not evaluated against common image manipulations (JPEG compression, cropping, rotation, rescaling) that could remove the adversarial perturbation (Reviewer 2, Q2).  
- **Minimizing semantic loss is counterintuitive and poorly explained**: Why encouraging the LDM to make better predictions protects images is not adequately justified; the explanation of “trapping in a local optimum” is speculative and needs empirical support (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
- **Missing evaluation of the LDM’s own performance after perturbation**: The paper does not show that the added perturbation does not degrade the normal functioning of the diffusion model (e.g., on benign tasks) (Reviewer 4).  
- **Threat model not clearly defined**: The concept of “diffusion mimicry” is confusing; the paper should clarify how diffusion models are used for mimicry and why the proposed method is a good defense (Reviewer 4).  
- **Incomplete experimental details**: The main text lacks key setup information: number of iterations, learning rate, perturbation budget, the fixed δ_x used in Figure 2. Metrics and human study explanations should be introduced more clearly (Reviewer 2).  
- **Comparison with Photoguard’s diffusion attack missing**: Related work and experiments only mention the encoder attack of Photoguard, but Photoguard also attacks the diffusion model; this omission is unexplained (Reviewer 2).  
- **Pseudocode bug in Appendix A**: `x = x.detach().clone()` detaches `x` from the computational graph, preventing gradient computation (Reviewer 2).  
- **Lack of visualization for SDS approximation**: Showing how the loss changes and the gradient landscape of the original vs. SDS loss would help validate the approximation’s effectiveness (Reviewer 2).  
- **Unaddressed question about L_S vs. L_T performance**: If the encoder is less robust, why does attacking with the semantic loss L_S outperform the texture loss L_T? This needs a clearer explanation (Reviewer 2, Q5).  
- **Limited contribution in broader security context**: The encoder vulnerability observation is only empirically demonstrated for this specific protection task, not generalized to developing more secure LDMs. The use of SDS is an existing technique (Reviewer 3).  
- **How z and z_adv are visualized in Figure 2 is not explained** (Reviewer 2, Q4).
