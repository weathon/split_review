## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PLLaVA, a parameter-efficient adaptation of the image-language model LLaVA for dense video understanding. The authors identify performance saturation when directly fine-tuning image models on video data, attributed to bias from high-norm visual features. They introduce an adaptive pooling strategy (spatial and temporal) to smooth feature distributions and a post-training model merging technique to combine image- and video-tuned weights. Experiments on benchmarks like Video ChatGPT, MVBench, MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, and ActivityNet-QA report competitive or state-of-the-art results. The approach is tested at 7B, 13B, and 34B scales.

### Strengths
- **Feature downsampling study**: Provides a detailed investigation of pooling in spatial and temporal dimensions, plus model merging tuning. This is valuable for practitioners who need to adjust parameters for specific applications [R1].
- **Post-training optimization**: The technique of mixing weights from image and video domains retains image understanding while incorporating temporal dynamics. It balances generalization across both modalities [R1].
- **Performance on benchmarks**: Measurable improvements over prior state-of-the-art, especially on MVBench (58.1% accuracy, 14.5% higher than GPT4V) [R1]. The model performs reasonably well across a wide range of video QA and captioning tasks [R4].
- **Scalability**: Evidence that PLLaVA scales effectively across model sizes (7B, 13B, 34B) without significant degradation [R1].
- **Analysis of dominant token issue**: The demonstration that high-norm visual feature tokens bias fine-tuning is a novel insight, though the resulting pooling approach is not new [R4].
- **Simple and easy-to-follow overall method** [R3].
- **Extensive experiments and ablation studies** show the effectiveness of the design [R2, R3].

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:  
  - *AdaptStructPooling* is not properly defined—details of the pooling operation (average, max, or learned), kernel sizes, and strides are missing [R1, R4].  
  - Logical gaps: the connection between softmax instability and dominant token norms is not mathematically or empirically justified [R1].  
  - Conflicting statements: Section 4.2 says “pooling along the temporal dimension always downgrades model performance” yet later asserts “pooling over more video frames improves robustness.” The conditions under which temporal pooling helps or harms are not clarified [R1].  
  - Figure 2(a) lacks axis labels, and no direct correlation between norm distribution and model performance is shown [R2].  
  - Writing contains typos (e.g., “Adaptative” → “Adaptive”) and needs significant improvement [R4].
- **Technical novelty limited**: Pooling/downsampling is a widely used technique. The paper does not clearly differentiate its specific combination of spatial and temporal pooling plus post-training from existing adaptive pooling methods [R1, R4]. The main contribution appears to be applying spatial pooling instead of temporal, plus LoRA weight aggregation [R4].
- **Baseline comparison concerns**:  
  - The initial failure analysis compares PLLaVA (16 frames) with a 4-frame method; using more frames is known to improve results, making the comparison unfair [R2].  
  - Missing comparisons with recent baselines [1][2][3] [R2].  
  - LLaVA-Next-Video (34B) results in Table 4 should also appear in Tables 2 and 3 [R3].  
  - Within the same 7B parametric complexity, PLLaVA performs lower than IG-VLM [R4].  
  - The specific technical improvements over LLaVA-Next are unclear; an ablation isolating the effect of pooling on that model is needed [R4].
- **Data quality and reproducibility concerns**:  
  - The observation that adding 249K VideoChat2 data worsens Video-ChatGPT training may be due to data quality or distribution, not solely frame pooling. Showing performance from training on that data alone would strengthen the claim [R2].  
  - The phenomenon of performance saturation could be caused by low-quality video training data (redundant visual tokens from high frame rate/minimal motion). Using higher-quality datasets like LLaVA-Video [A] or dynamic scenes with lower frame rates should be investigated [R3].  
  - The post-training mix ratio is dataset-dependent, with no systematic guidance for selecting it, limiting reproducibility and practical adoption [R1].
- **Post-optimization and training ambiguity**:  
  - The post-optimization step seems counterintuitive—does fine-tuning worsen vision-text alignment? How does this compare to unfreezing the LLM during fine-tuning? [R2]  
  - It is unclear whether any training is involved in the post-optimization step, and what data is used [R2].  
  - The method’s relationship to LoRA or distillation is not explained, and its difference from VideoChatGPT’s average pooling needs clarification [R4].
- **Impact on complex temporal reasoning and image performance**:  
  - Unclear how PLLaVA handles long-term temporal dependencies, intricate motion understanding, or multi-event reasoning. Specific examples and comparison of long vs. short video tasks would help [R4].  
  - The pooling strategy may lose finer details in complex scenes with many objects or fast motion [R4].  
  - No evaluation on image-based tasks is provided, so it is unclear if pooling harms image performance or general applicability [R3].
- **Theoretical justification**: The explanation for why spatial pooling works is generic; the justification regarding softmax distributions and sharp/balanced tokens does not specifically justify spatial pooling over temporal [R4].  
- **Minor**: In Figure 5(a), the cause of a performance drop at spatial pooling shape 20 and increase at 24 is not explained [R2].
