## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Delay Neural Networks (DeNN), a feedforward spiking network trained with backpropagation that uses only synaptic delays (no weights). Each neuron spikes once; the spike time is a weighted sum of presynaptic spike times via a temporal kernel (later spikes contribute less). Delays are represented by a Gaussian function of a learned parameter; the sign of the parameter indicates excitatory/inhibitory. A standardization procedure can ignore late spikes. The authors convert event-based data into frames with equal event counts, preserving timing information. For audio, a learnable long-term timing memory input stage is added. DeNN achieves competitive performance on MNIST, CIFAR-10, N-MNIST, DVS-Gesture, and GSC with modest compute/memory cost compared to baselines.

### Strengths
- **Originality and significance**: First work to train an SNN with backpropagation using only delays (no weights). Explores delays as a powerful computational primitive, valuable for understanding the brain and building efficient neural networks on novel hardware.
- **Quality**: Tested on multiple benchmarks (vision, audio) against many baselines.
- **Efficient use of temporal information**: Uses exact continuous spike timing in both forward and backward passes, enabling accurate temporal dynamics without approximation.
- **Compact model size**: Achieves competitive performance with fewer parameters and less computation than many baselines.
- **Sparse learning paradigm**: By selectively ignoring a proportion of neuron firings (q-value), the method provides a general approach to sparse computation with accurate gradients.
- **Clarity**: Equations in the text aid understanding (Reviewer 1). The idea of training with purely synaptic delays is interesting (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor clarity and incomplete exposition**: Multiple specific points that hinder understanding and reproducibility.
  - Unclear definitions: “analog neuron” (line 107), “ReLU-like function” (line 182), “some value” (line 183), “std” function in Eq. 5, “rN” vs. “2rN” (line 301), units of “# comp” in Table 1.
  - Contradictory or confusing statements: delays vs. weights comparison (line 144) after saying no weights; “almost equivalent” without clarifying significance (line 185); “incompressible delay” term -κ(t_i+1) (line 170); discontinuity at d_ij=1 (line 170).
  - Figure issues: Figure 1 – must next layer wait for all previous neuron spikes in q=1 case? This is a major limitation that should be stated; explains why delay-only network may differ from weight/activation DNN. Figure 2 – unclear purpose of 2rN, δt, T, whether events are continuous or discrete. Figure 3 – too many similar colors, unclear if plotting one sample; recommend redrawing. Figure 5 – “Bottom line” not explained. Figure 6 – missing labels for panels. Table 1 too wide, grammar error (line 236).
  - Missing overview of symbols: too many symbols (Reviewer 3); recommend a table.
  - Event2time algorithm unclear: does it split by time or event count? Accumulate? Convert to single spike? (Reviewer 3). Also reviewers note line 231 exposition is insufficient.
  - Reviewer 4: Introduction and Related Works are loose; theoretical comparison of delay mechanism vs. weight/membrane potential is missing; different spike encoding methods (rate coding, latency coding, delta modulation) and their pros/cons vs. DeNN are not covered.
- **Technical incorrectness / overclaims**: Several statements are too strong or false.
  - “DNNs are not energy efficient” (line 30) ignores efficient DNNs; should say “can use a lot of energy”.
  - “Temporal plasticity is essential to treat temporal information” (line 50); RNNs without temporal plasticity can process time.
  - Eq. 8 is not the Adam update (Adam is used later).
  - Line 207: “event-based datasets consist of images from event-based cameras” – not all are images; line 2010: “number of images and events is huge” – event count doesn’t get huge.
- **Computational and memory costs**:
  - DeNN requires computing exponential functions, which is expensive on digital hardware and neuromorphic systems; this limits practical deployment (Reviewer 2). Energy consumption vs. other SNNs is not measured (Reviewer 4 question).
  - Long sequences (e.g., > a few seconds) incur high memory cost, restricting applications (Reviewer 2).
  - The “active parameters” advantage is unclear: can the remaining parameters be pruned after training? (Reviewer 2). On N-MNIST, ~75% of parameters are active, so computation is nearly dense; rationale for this is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Performance variability and missing analysis**:
  - DeNN underperforms SOTA on vision benchmarks (e.g., CIFAR-10) but outperforms on audio (GSC). The reason for this modality-dependent behavior is not analyzed theoretically (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
  - Acc/#Params is not a fair indicator because accuracy may scale logarithmically with parameter count; authors should compare under the same architecture (Reviewer 3).
- **Learning dynamics complexity**:
  - Introducing synaptic delays adds complexity to backpropagation; the formalism complicates mathematical analysis. The essence of Eq. 5 vs. MAC in weight-based networks is not clarified (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
  - Questions about negativity in Eq. 4 (t_q , spike response kernel κ(t) only meaningful for t≥0) and whether t_j implies each neuron spikes only once (Reviewer 3). Also Eq. 7 uses s for time step; should specify summation range (s=0 to T or s=1 to T) (Reviewer 3).
- **Scalability concerns**:
  - Will DeNN’s advantages in parameter count, computation, and active proportions degrade when scaling to larger tasks? (Reviewer 4). This is not investigated.
- **Minor issues**: Font appears wrong (Reviewer 3). Table 1 includes grammar mistake (“where #*Li* is the of size of the list”). Figure 3 colors too close.

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 3) is notably more negative, emphasizing poor presentation (font, symbol table, unfair metric, figure quality) and lack of clarity on algorithmic details. Their criticisms are included above.*
