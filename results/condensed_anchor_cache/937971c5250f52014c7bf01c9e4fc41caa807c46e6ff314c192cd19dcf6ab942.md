## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes RHGIB, a robust heterogeneous GNN explainer using Graph Information Bottleneck and denoising variational inference. It infers latent distributions of graph structure and features to mitigate noise, incorporates heterogeneous edge types into subgraph generation, and optimizes with GIB. A theoretical analysis of noise amplification in heterogeneous GNNs is provided. Experiments on several real-world heterogeneous graph datasets show improved performance over baselines.

### Strengths
1. **Novel and important problem** – Addressing explanation under noise for heterogeneous GNNs is new; there is no prior work on this specific task.
2. **Theoretical foundation** – A solid theoretical analysis of the noise amplification effect in heterogeneous graphs (especially for meta-path-based methods) is provided, grounding the approach.
3. **Effective design** – The denoising variational inference integrated with edge-type attention and the GIB framework enables both robustness and semantic capture in explanations.
4. **Strong experimental validation** – Extensive experiments (including ablation and fidelity-sparsity analysis) demonstrate superiority over several baselines; the results are convincing to most reviewers.
5. **Well-structured presentation** – The paper is logically organized and the method description is clear.

### Weaknesses
1. **Motivation and philosophical concern (reviewer disagreement)** – One reviewer strongly questions whether denoising in a post-hoc explainer is appropriate: the explainer should faithfully reflect the target GNN’s reliance, including on noisy edges; denoising may obscure important failure modes. Other reviewers find the motivation compelling and practical for real-world noisy graphs.
2. **Insufficient handling of heterogeneous graph specifics** – The methodology is described in a way that is nearly agnostic to heterogeneity. Key modelling choices are missing: how $p(G|\mathbf{Z})$ and $p(\tilde{G}|G)$ account for different edge types, node types, and meta-paths. The acquisition of edge-type embedding $r_{\phi(e)}$ is not explained. The theoretical analysis focuses on meta-path-based methods; it is unclear if it applies to non-meta-path architectures (e.g., simple GCN/GAT on target node domain). The paper would benefit from testing on more diverse HGNN architectures.
3. **Limited related work coverage** – The review omits several relevant works: “Counterfactual Learning on Heterogeneous Graphs with Greedy Perturbation,” “On the Robustness of Post-hoc GNN Explainers to Label Noise,” and other heterogeneous graph explainers. The paper does not clearly distinguish RHGIB from these existing works, weakening the claim of originality.
4. **Incremental technical novelty** – The core components (GIB for explanation + VAE for denoising) have been studied separately; the paper mostly combines them with modest adaptations for heterogeneous graphs. The framework is highly similar to the baseline V-InfoR (robust encoder + GIB explainer). The specific modifications for heterogeneous graphs are not sufficiently justified.
5. **Evaluation gaps**  
   - **Metrics**: Only MAE and RMSE are reported, while classification tasks (node classification) would benefit from precision, recall, F1, AUC to assess explanation quality. The reasonability of MAE/RMSE for this task is not clarified.  
   - **Baselines**: No heterogeneous-specific explainers are included as baselines, making it difficult to isolate the advantages of RHGIB over existing heterogeneous methods.  
   - **Noise types and generalizability**: Experiments are limited to artificial edge perturbations; node feature noise is not tested despite being mentioned in the introduction. The noise addition protocol (training vs. test set) is not clearly stated.  
   - **Synthetic graphs**: No evaluation on synthetic heterogeneous graphs with known ground-truth communities (e.g., SBM, Girvan-Newman) to quantitatively assess robustness and correctness of explanations under controlled noise conditions.  
   - **Other tasks**: Only node classification is evaluated; link prediction and graph classification are omitted.
6. **Case study not convincing** – The visualisation only shows RHGIB’s output without comparison to other explainers. The significance of the specific examples (e.g., <Paper-5558> → <Author-1034>) is opaque, and the analysis is superficial, lacking deeper reasoning about why the subgraph is critical for the prediction.
7. **Unaddressed questions from theory**  
   - In the proof of Theorem 1, why does the neighbor aggregation analysis ignore the influence of node $v_j$’s neighbors on node $v_i$? This assumption needs justification.  
   - The connection between the noise amplification theorem and the specific form of the denoising variational inference objective (Formula 4) is missing. The motivation for the distribution transformation in Formula 4 (vs. standard VI) is unclear.
8. **Hyperparameter insensitivity concern** – The Lagrange multiplier controlling GIB compression is reported to have little effect on performance, contradicting prior work (“Graph Structure Learning with Variational Information Bottleneck”) where insufficient/over-compression degrades results. This raises questions about whether the model is adequately capturing data patterns.
9. **Writing and presentation** – The methodology section could be more concise and easier to follow. Figure 1 is too rough and lacks insight; it should illustrate the proposed method and its advantages more clearly.
