- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 3, 3, 3, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers assess PACE, a 684K-parameter physics-informed climate emulator that combines a 2D advection-diffusion Neural ODE with convolutional deep learning to map greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions to surface air temperature and precipitation. It is trained on 15 CMIP6 climate models from ClimateSet and compared against baselines such as SFNO, ClimaX, ConvLSTM, UNet, ACE, and LUCIE. PACE achieves lower RMSE on many individual models and shows stable 86-year predictions. The response ranges from a highly positive reviewer (score 5) who praises the lightweight design and uncertainty quantification (UQ) promise, to a highly negative reviewer (score 1) who finds the physical motivation unsound and the presentation incomprehensible. Most reviewers rate the paper low on clarity (presentation 1-3) and have major concerns about the handling of physics, UQ, and evaluation methodology.

### Strengths
- **Lightweight architecture (684K parameters)** – orders of magnitude smaller than SOTA emulators (107M+ parameters). Highlighted by Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 as a key advantage.
- **Physics-informed design** – incorporating the advection-diffusion equation is original and well-motivated (Reviewers 3, 5, 7). Reviewer 7 calls the idea of adding diffusion, noise, and GHG forcings to advection ODEs “excellent.”
- **Strong RMSE performance** – PACE outperforms baselines on 13/15 models for temperature and 9/15 for precipitation. Reviewers 1, 3, and 5 note these results.
- **Ablation study** – clearly demonstrates the importance of advection, diffusion, Neural ODE, and attention modules (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Long-term stability** – stable rollouts for 86 years without blow-up (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 1 also notes stable emulation.
- **Uncertainty quantification (UQ) inclusion** – Reviewer 2 sees this as a novel and desired feature, contrasting with previous SOTA methods that lack UQ. Reviewer 7 also praises the addition of noise to the ODE.
- **Use of standard benchmark dataset (ClimateSet)** and comparison against appropriate baselines (Reviewer 4 acknowledges this despite reservations).
- **Green computing aspect** – low parameter count potentially reduces carbon footprint (Reviewer 1).
- **Outstanding claims by Reviewer 7** – “The paper boasts outstanding results” and the idea of adding diffusion and noise is excellent.

### Weaknesses
- **Fundamental misunderstanding of the problem** – The advection-diffusion equation is not appropriate for emulating GHG-to-temperature/precipitation mappings. Emissions are not advected; changes are socio-economic. The climate system evolves via Navier-Stokes, not advection-diffusion, and over monthly-to-decadal timescales there is minimal large-scale advection. This undermines the rationale for the Neural ODE and makes coefficient estimation (D, v) nonsensical (Reviewer 4; also echoed by Reviewers 5, 1, 7).
- **Stability claim is misleading** – Diagnostic-type emulation (regression-based) is stable by construction, so the stability claim is not a genuine challenge. The paper’s statement that deep learning weather models are “unstable” for climate emulation is false; counterexamples exist (ACE, LUCIE, Spherical DYffusion) (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4 calls the claim “nonsense”).
- **Periodic boundary condition implementation is incorrect** – Applying periodicity in latitude identifies the North and South poles, treating Earth as a torus, not a sphere (Reviewers 5, 4). Reviewer 1 also questions this. Implementation details are absent.
- **Uncertainty quantification is poorly justified and unevaluated** – The Gaussian noise term lacks physical or theoretical justification (e.g., Central Limit Theorem). Its covariance structure is undefined; it is not presented as a proper Wiener process, raising concerns about stability (Reviewers 1, 5, 6, 7). No experiment evaluates the quality of uncertainty estimates: only RMSE is used; metrics like log-likelihood, confidence intervals, CRPS are missing. UQ is shown only in one boxplot and an error map, not in predictions (Reviewers 2, 3, 5, 6). Reviewer 2 initially praises UQ but later notes the lack of evaluation.
- **Evaluation timeline is wrong** – ClimateBench protocol evaluates on the last 20 years of SSP245 (2096-2100), not the full 2015-2100 period. The first ~50 years are too similar to training scenarios. The evaluation shown (up to 2026) is incorrect (Reviewer 4). The choice of 86 years is also questioned (Reviewer 1).
- **CBAM misnamed and unclear** – The “Convolution Block Attention Module” contains no attention layers; it uses pooling and MLPs (Reviewers 2, 3). Motivation and design choices are not explained.
- **Missing comparisons to important baselines** – No comparison to FourCastNet, GraphCast, ClimODE, Neural GCM, WeatherGFT, Spherical DYffusion, or simple non-neural methods (GP, linear regression) (Reviewers 2, 3, 6). These are critical for assessing real contribution.
- **Results lack statistical rigor** – No error bars from re-training with different random seeds. Predictions are pixelated (poor resolution). RMSE may not be the right metric for climate emulation, where temporal means/statistics matter more than day-to-day accuracy (Reviewers 3, 5, 6). Reviewer 5 strongly argues RMSE is more akin to weather forecasting and not appropriate.
- **Advection-diffusion coefficients not dynamically updated** – D, v_x, v_y are estimated only from initial data; how they evolve over time (if at all) is unclear. The initialization D = var(C) lacks motivation and does not connect GHG emissions to diffusion: extra energy should amplify advection, not smooth (Reviewers 1, 5, 6, 7). Questions about f_theta and its role (Reviewers 1, 7).
- **Neural ODE implementation unclear** – The paper uses the dopri5 solver, a traditional numerical integrator, not a neural network. The description contradicts the term “Neural ODE.” No algorithm box or complete model description; Figure 2 is unhelpful and even counterproductive for understanding (Reviewers 3, 5, 7). The relationship between Eq. 1, Eq. 6, and Eq. 13 is confusing.
- **Model limited to two variables** – Only surface air temperature and precipitation; other important variables (wind, pressure) are not predicted, unlike ClimaX (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair baseline comparisons in Table 1** – ACE and LUCIE are designed for autoregressive dynamics emulation (different input/output spaces), so comparing them on a diagnostic task is unfair (Reviewer 3). Transfer fidelity of ACE and LUCIE to this task is doubted (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing or insufficient architecture details** – Shape of F is never given; Adaptive Pooling module at the end is not described; Harmonic Embeddings not shown in Figure 2 and their use is unclear; how PBC is implemented numerically is absent; how many time points are used for the ODE solver; batch size, gradient accumulation, time step not reported (Reviewers 3, 5, 6, 7). The ODE and the CBAM components are not clearly connected.
- **Poor presentation and many errors** – Grammar, typos, imprecise language, missing citations, broken sentences, incorrect notation (e.g., using $\in$ incorrectly, missing parentheses). Figures are upside-down, text illegible. Equations lack divergence and source terms; f_theta undefined; Eq. 19 missing a square. Hanging equations and confusing variable definitions (Reviewers 1, 3, 5, 6, 7).
- **Abstract and claims are inaccurate** – Abstract says “only being trained on emissions data,” but the model actually learns from temperature/precipitation targets as well (Reviewer 5). Claim of “data-efficiency” refers to computational efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- **Related work incomplete** – Fails to acknowledge prior diagnostic emulation work (ClimateBench, Bouabid et al., Castruccio et al., Holden & Edwards). Missing discussion of recent neural weather models (Kochkov et al., Cachay et al., Salva Rühling et al.) (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Green computing claims too vague** – No quantitative comparison of carbon footprint or energy consumption (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues** – SSP labels missing from figure captions; Table 2 not referenced in text; unclear why specific 15 models were chosen; bars in Figures 6/7 make precipitation results hard to read; no PACE results in Figure 5 ablation plot (full model absent). Notation inconsistencies (σ used for three different quantities). “Fourier” missing in “Spherical Fourier Neural Operator” (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
