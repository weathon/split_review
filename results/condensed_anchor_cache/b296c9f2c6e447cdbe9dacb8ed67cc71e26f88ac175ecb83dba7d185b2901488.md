## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the problem of identifying latent causal variables in temporal data when the generation process from latents to observations is non-invertible. The authors provide an identifiability theory assuming that a mapping m exists from a window of past observations (x_{t:t-\mu}) to the latent z_t, and propose CaRiNG, a VAE-based method with normalizing flows for the transition prior. Experiments on synthetic data validate the theory, and a real-world traffic QA dataset shows competitive performance.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and interesting variation of ICA with clear practical usage (R1, R4).
- Motivation and idea of using temporal context to overcome non-invertibility is intuitive and novel (R1, R3, R4).
- Theoretical results are impressive, solid, and well-supported by experiments on synthetic data (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper is among very few theoretical works that also evaluate on realistic data (R3).
- Thorough theoretical analysis and experimental comparisons to baselines (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Positioning and novelty:** The work takes a confrontational stance toward the ICA community; it should be framed as complementing existing work (R1). CaRiNG is not significantly different from Sequential VAE with normalizing flows; the core architecture remains a recurrent VAE, limiting novelty (R2). The use of normalizing flow for the prior is technically sound but not a substantial departure from existing techniques (R2).
- **Assumptions and justification:** The existence of a mapping m such that z_t = m(x_{t:t-\mu}) is a strong assumption; no concrete mathematical example (specifying f, g, m) is provided to show it is non-vacuous (R3). It is unclear how to determine μ in practice (R2, R4). The paper repeatedly claims "identifiability under mild conditions," but this condition is not trivial and lacks justification for real-world scenarios (R2).
- **Math mistakes and imprecision (R3):** Wrong definition of surjectivity at the beginning of 2.1. "Continuous manifold" is undefined. Theorems do not reuse Definition 1’s “observational equivalence” and leave the equality ambiguous (distributional or pointwise?). It is unclear whether x has a density (especially when dim(x)>dim(z)). The function m appears in the definition’s subscript but is not part of the generative model. Corollary 1 is not a simple consequence of Theorem 1. Equation (3) missing quantifier over x_{t:t-\mu}. Terminology: “continuous domain/set” should be “path-connected”. Jacobian notation with asterisks is unclear.
- **Lemma 1 issues (R3):** Section 3.3’s point about local vs. global disentanglement was already made in previous work [4]. Example 6 from [4] provides a counterexample to Lemma 1, implying Lemma 1 is false. The proof of Lemma 1 is unclear (e.g., “n-dimensional axis except 0”).
- **Presentation and writing quality (R1, R2, R3):** Notation is convoluted and non-standard (union for Cartesian product, Eq.(6) overly complex, inconsistent jargon like “secondary differentiable” vs. “second order differentiable” and “normalized flows” vs. “normalizing flows”) (R1). Section 4 is hard to read; connections between theory and model design are insufficiently explained (R1, R2). Sections 3.3 and 3.4 are distracting and could be moved to appendix (R2). Many sentences are weirdly formulated (e.g., “Non-invertibility by vision persistence”, “Thus, we assume that there exists a maximum time lag μ and an arbitrary nonlinear function m”, “We say latent causal processes are identifiable if…”). The paper lacks high-level intuition (e.g., a cartoon) explaining why temporal context helps and what the assumptions mean (R4). The paper should better connect to existing works: iVAE, the line by Hyvärinen et al., and [1] (R1, R3).
- **Experimental shortcomings:** No comparison with iVAE despite citing it (R1). Number of parameters and training times are missing (R1). Only one real-world dataset is used; QA accuracy is an indirect metric that does not directly assess disentanglement or causal properties of the latent space (R2). The claim that CaRiNG outperforms baselines is deceivingly positive because CMCIR beats CaRiNG on average and significantly on some question types (R1). The “All” column in Table 2 is ambiguous—numbers do not appear to average the other columns (R1). The proposed method should be compared to Sequential VAE (a degenerate case of CaRiNG) (R2). Hyperparameter search for transition lag τ is needed in real-world settings to avoid model misspecification (R2).
- **VAE misunderstanding (R3):** The paper states that “the distribution of p(z_t | x_{t:t-\mu}) is constrained by the prior” to enforce conditional independence. This shows misunderstanding: q should be used for the variational posterior, and KL divergence in the ELBO does not enforce conditional independence—that is hard-coded in the generative model. The same point is questioned by R4.
- **Model definition change (R3, post-discussion):** The authors updated the model definition to allow the mixing function g to take a window of past z_t (previously only z_t). This non-trivial change requires a full re-reading to verify proofs and consistency; it is not reasonable to evaluate during discussion.
- **Minor questions that reflect weaknesses (R4):** How to estimate the required temporal context μ? What happens as μ→∞? Is identifiability still possible when model and data distributions do not match but m is correctly recovered?
