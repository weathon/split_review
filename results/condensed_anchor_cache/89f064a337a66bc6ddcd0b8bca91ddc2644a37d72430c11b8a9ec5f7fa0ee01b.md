## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces UO-Explainer, a unified framework for explaining Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) at both model-level and instance-level. It leverages graphlets (small, connected, non-isomorphic induced subgraphs of 2–5 nodes) and their orbits as human-interpretable units to decompose GNN predictions. Model-level explanations are obtained by decomposing class weights into a linear combination of orbit bases; instance-level explanations decompose the prediction of a target node into orbit units. Experiments on 8 node classification datasets (synthetic and real-world) show UO-Explainer outperforms existing baselines. Two reviewers (scores 5,5) found the idea promising and the approach novel; two reviewers (scores 3,3) questioned the justification of graphlets/orbits as interpretable units and raised concerns about scope and experimental evaluation.

### Strengths

- Relevant and timely problem: GNN explainability is critical as GNNs are deployed across domains (Reviewer 1).
- Unified framework providing both instance-level and model-level explanations is interesting and novel (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Technical approach is novel, incorporating orbit basis learning and class-orbit score learning (Reviewer 3).
- Source code is publicly available (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments are comprehensive: 8 node classification datasets, multiple baselines, and varied settings (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Paper is well-organized with self-contained figures (Reviewer 3).
- Allows users to define their own interpretable units instead of orbits if desired (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental results show UO-Explainer provides more accurate explanations than baselines at both levels (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of justification for graphlets/orbits as human-interpretable units**: Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3, 4) note that it is not clearly explained why graphlets and orbits are human-interpretable, what unique properties they have, and how they improve interpretability over existing subgraph-based methods. Reviewer 1 suggests a user study would be needed; Reviewer 2 asks for real-world examples; Reviewer 4 questions the intuitive representation (what are orbits, why used, advantages). No human evaluation was conducted (Reviewers 1, 2). [Reviewer confidence: R1=5, R2=4, R3=5, R4=4]
- **Strong assumption that both explanation levels depend on these graphlets/orbits** (Reviewer 1). The method presumes that model-level explanations are linear combinations of orbit bases; Reviewer 1 questions what if model-explanation depends on completely different factors. Reviewers 2 and 3 also note the linear combination loses contextual information (e.g., interactions between distant nodes, overall graph structure) and that small, isolated graphlets may miss broader patterns. [R1 confidence=5, R2 confidence=4, R3 confidence=5]
- **Restricted to node classification**: The method only handles node classification tasks; reviewers (1, 3) note the title/abstract overstate scope and ask how it generalizes to other tasks (e.g., graph classification). [R1 confidence=5, R3 confidence=5]
- **Limited to 2–5 node graphlets**, which restricts the explanation patterns. Larger graphlets that may be meaningful are not considered. Reviewers (3, 4) note that important patterns may involve larger structures; Reviewer 4 asks if the base set can be dynamically extended. [R3 confidence=5, R4 confidence=4]
- **Model-level evaluation only on synthetic datasets**: Reviewers 1 and 2 question whether model-level explanations make sense on real-world datasets (e.g., do meaningful model-level graphlets exist for molecular graphs?). Reviewer 1 also asks why only sub-recall measure is used and why measures from GLGExplainer are not employed. [R1 confidence=5, R2 confidence=4]
- **Fidelity measurement concerns**: The paper uses Fidelity+ without clearly specifying which fidelity variant (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 notes the OOD issue: Eq. 10 computes f_prob on subgraphs that differ in distribution from training data, making predictions unreliable. Recommends robust fidelity measures (Robust Fidelity, OAR, SimOAR, GInX-Eval). [R3 confidence=5]
- **Method ignores node features/types**: In real-world datasets like Gene, C-C and CO substructures correspond to the same graphlet but have different meanings, limiting interpretability (Reviewer 3). [R3 confidence=5]
- **Instance-level explanation motivation unclear** (Reviewer 2). The method decomposes prediction values into orbit units but lacks clear intuition. [R2 confidence=4]
- **Efficiency concerns**: The instance-level explanation requires searching all graphlets containing all 0-72 orbits with time complexity O(|O||V| d^{k-1}) based on Algorithm 3 and 4, making it expensive for large-scale graphs. No running time comparison is provided (Reviewer 2). [R2 confidence=4]
- **Notations need improvement**: The notation for the downstream layer does not consider activation functions (Reviewer 3). [R3 confidence=5]
- **Need to predefine graphlets/orbits rather than learning from data** (Reviewer 4). [R4 confidence=4]
- Some experimental settings could be improved (Reviewer 1, no specifics). [R1 confidence=5]

**Reviewer disagreement**: The two higher-rated reviewers (scores 5) found the idea promising and novel, and the experiments comprehensive; the two lower-rated reviewers (scores 3) expressed stronger reservations about the interpretability justification and methodological assumptions. This contrast is particularly evident in the stricter questioning about interpretability and the limited evaluation scope. (Note: Reviewer 4 gave score 3 but still acknowledged the unified framework and leverage of orbits as strengths; Reviewer 1 gave score 3 but noted the unified framework is interesting.)
