- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 5, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses information asymmetry in text-video retrieval (TVR) by enriching textual representations. During training, videos are segmented into event-level clips and captioned using a VLM. During retrieval, an LLM generates diverse queries, and a Farthest Query Sampling (FQS) mechanism selects relevant and diverse queries to improve efficiency. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the paper is well written, but there is significant disagreement on the novelty, validity of generated content, fairness of comparison, and depth of analysis.

### Strengths
- The problem of information asymmetry in TVR is important and under-explored; a data-centric approach to improving performance by identifying pitfalls in current captions and retrieval is timely (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written, structured, and easy to follow (all reviewers).
- The method effectively utilizes VLMs and LLMs to enrich textual representations during both training and inference (Reviewer 2).
- The data-centric approach shows promising improvements in practice, with strong performance across several benchmarks (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- The oracle query experiments demonstrate a large gap in current methods, are novel and interesting, and open up new avenues for data-centric research in this domain (Reviewer 3; however, Reviewer 1 questions the validity—see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of substantial innovation and limited distinction from prior work**:
  - The performance gains appear largely attributable to the inherent capabilities of VLMs and LLMs rather than a novel framework; the paper does not isolate the contribution of the proposed data-centric architecture or training procedure (Reviewer 2, also implied by Reviewer 1).
  - There is insufficient comparison with existing data augmentation works (e.g., Cap4Video). The difference from prior methods is not clearly articulated (Reviewer 3).
  - One reviewer (score 5) argues the method essentially performs image-text retrieval, since it uses an image captioner and does not address temporal cues beyond simple segmentation (Reviewer 1).

- **Unreliability of generated captions and queries**:
  - Relying on VLM- and LLM-generated content introduces significant risk of hallucination and propagation of biases. The paper lacks analysis of how often generated text deviates from actual video content and the impact on retrieval performance (Reviewer 1).
  - Oracle query experiments: While Reviewer 3 finds them inspiring, Reviewer 1 (score 5) questions whether the improvement truly stems from query supplementation or simply from including the ground-truth query itself. The selection process rather than supplementation could drive the gains (Reviewer 1). Further explanation of the factors behind the oracle performance is needed (Reviewer 3).

- **Computational cost and unfair comparison**:
  - The method incurs much higher training and inference cost than compared works, yet no detailed breakdown of GPU hours, training time, inference latency, or memory usage is provided. Direct comparison without accounting for added computation is unfair (Reviewer 1). The added cost should be investigated (Reviewer 3).

- **Insufficient evaluation and ablation**:
  - Comparative experiments are outdated: most SOTAs cited are from 2022; Tables 2 and 3 omit recent methods such as Cap4Video (Reviewer 2). The paper should be compared with more recent approaches (Reviewer 2).
  - The query selection mechanism (FQS) is only evaluated against a random baseline and majority voting; alternative diversity-based or distance-based selection algorithms are not compared, making it hard to assess whether FQS offers a significant advantage (Reviewer 2).
  - Reviewer 2 suggests adding relevance constraints (e.g., minimum similarity threshold) to FQS and comparing with methods that minimize the minimum distance between selected queries.
  - Ablation shows performance gain mainly from Retrieval Phase Enrichment, while Training Phase Enrichment appears marginal (Reviewer 3). This needs explanation.
  - The implementation of majority voting for enriched queries is not described; details should be provided (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing details and clarification**:
  - Figure 3 is confusing and needs more illustration of design ideas and process (Reviewer 1).
  - No examples of the generated video captions or expanded queries are provided, which would help understand the method’s contribution (Reviewer 1).
  - The reasoning for requiring diversity in supplemented queries is not justified (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper does not explain how the method distinguishes itself from existing approaches beyond leveraging VLMs/LLMs; unique components should be emphasized (Reviewer 2).
  - It is unclear how the framework would perform in real-world, large-scale TVR systems (Reviewer 2).
  - The authors are asked whether they will open-source the enriched dataset and code (Reviewer 3).
