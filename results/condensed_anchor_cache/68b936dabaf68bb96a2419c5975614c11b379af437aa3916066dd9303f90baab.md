## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses Continual Federated Learning (CFL), where new tasks appear over time on decentralized data and the global model suffers from Global Catastrophic Forgetting. The authors propose Federated Orthogonal Training (FOT), which extracts the global input subspace for old tasks and projects new-task updates to be orthogonal to that subspace, thereby reducing interference. FOT is designed to preserve privacy, avoid client-side data storage, and incur minimal additional cost. Empirical evaluations show that FOT outperforms several existing CFL methods in average accuracy and forgetting reduction.

### Strengths
- Clearly describes the CFL problem and the FOT framework using well-structured illustrations, formulas, and step-by-step explanations.
- Modifies global updates for new tasks to reduce interference with old tasks, effectively addressing Global Catastrophic Forgetting.
- Introduces a novel aggregation method, FedProject, that guarantees orthogonality in a global manner without compromising privacy and without incurring additional communication overhead (according to Reviewer 3).
- Uses randomized SVD on the server to aggregate local principal subspace information from clients in a privacy-preserving way.
- Discusses important practical aspects, especially privacy, and shows that FOT avoids client-side storage of past data while still performing well.
- Empirically demonstrates significant improvement in average forgetting over extensive baselines in various benchmarks (Reviewer 2 particularly positive about the results).
- Outperforms existing methods in cross-device settings even when those methods require additional computation and storage.

### Weaknesses
- **Communication overhead and convergence speed**:  
  Reviewer 1 considers communication overhead a significant weakness; the paper’s analysis is superficial and lacks comparison with baseline experiments. The argument that overhead is minimized is not sufficiently elaborated.  
  Reviewer 2 adds that projecting aggregated updates onto the orthogonal subspace intuitively slows convergence, which directly implies extra communication costs; the authors did not discuss this trade-off.  
  These points collectively require a more rigorous analysis of communication cost and convergence behavior.
- **Missing baselines**:  
  Reviewer 2 notes that several relevant baselines are not compared, specifically:  
  – Learning without Forgetting (Li & Hoiem, 2017)  
  – Federated Continual Learning with Weighted Inter-Client Transfer (Yoon et al., 2021)  
  – TARGET (Zhang et al., 2023).  
  Including these would strengthen the evaluation.
- **Convergence analysis**:  
  Reviewer 1 remarks that the convergence analysis (Remark 1) is straightforward and lacks comprehensive theoretical underpinnings.
- **Limited technical novelty (minority negative view)**:  
  Reviewer 3 is critical of the paper’s differentiation from prior work. They point out that the core idea of projecting gradients onto a subspace orthogonal to previous tasks’ gradients is already used in the GPM method for centralized continual learning (Eq 8, 9 of GPM vs Eq 12 of this paper). The additional aggregation layer that produces \(A^l\) via secure aggregation is seen as a straightforward application of existing secure aggregation techniques. Consequently, Reviewer 3 questions the extent of technical innovation and requests a clearer articulation of novelty beyond applying secure aggregation to an established continual learning approach. (In contrast, Reviewers 1 and 2 view the method as clean and effective, and they highlight the privacy-preserving aggregation as a positive contribution.)
- **Applicability of randomized SVD when client data is limited**:  
  Reviewer 2 asks about Theorem 1’s assumption that \(n\) (number of data points per client) is sufficiently large. In experiments with 100 clients, each client may have very few data; the reviewer questions whether the condition holds and, if not, how the effectiveness of FOT is still explained.
- **Equal round number for all methods**:  
  Reviewer 2 asks how the round number was decided and whether other methods would converge earlier. If so, a fairer comparison would consider total rounds or compute.
- **Lack of ablation on layer selection**:  
  Reviewer 2 notes that FOT might work well even when applied only to a subset of layers, and asks whether the authors have experimented with such selective application.
- **Need for intuitive explanation of orthogonality correctness**:  
  Reviewer 1 requests a more intuitive explanation of why the orthogonal projection process is correct.
- **Clarity of novelty statement**:  
  Reviewer 3 asks for a clear statement of technical novelty and contribution relative to centralized continual learning methods like GPM.
