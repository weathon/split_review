## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes VDLM, which introduces a structured text-based intermediate representation called Primal Visual Description (PVD) to improve large multimodal models’ (LMMs) fine-grained perception and reasoning on vector graphics. Images are first converted to SVG via a rule-based encoder, then an LLM (fine-tuned on synthetic data) translates the SVG into PVD (9 geometric primitives with attributes). This PVD is fed into an off-the-shelf LMM (e.g., GPT-4o) for downstream tasks. Experiments on low-level perception benchmarks (e.g., angle classification, length comparison) and a high-level QA benchmark (VGBench-QA) show significant gains over directly using raw images or SVG as input. The paper also provides analyses of interpretability and the correlation between PVD quality and task performance.

### Strengths
- Addresses a significant problem: current LMMs struggle with precise, low-level visual details (e.g., shape size, layout), and VDLM offers a low-resource, task-agnostic solution that does not require human-annotated data.
- First attempt to enhance fine-grained visual understanding by converting images into a vector-based (SVG→PVD) format that aligns better with language models, as opposed to using raster images or raw SVG code.
- PVD is intuitive, compact, and effectively encodes geometric information about each object, improving interpretability via disentangled perception and reasoning.
- The introduced low-level perception benchmarking dataset is a unique contribution that can serve as a quantitative evaluation for future VLMs.
- The proposed method achieves notable performance gains over strong baselines (GPT-4V, GPT-4o, LLaMA, ViperGPT) on both low-level and high-level reasoning tasks.
- Writing is clear and well-structured; the experimental and analysis sections are thorough.

### Weaknesses
- **Narrow scope and limited generalizability:** The evaluation is restricted to simple, synthetic SVG images. Real-world applications (e.g., web, OS environments) involve far more complex vector graphics with textures, shading, occlusion, and non-geometric elements. The paper does not analyze what visual information is lost during SVG conversion or how PVD would scale to real-world scenes. *(R1, R2, R3)*
- **Insufficient justification for the two-stage pipeline:** The rationale for converting raster→SVG→PVD instead of directly producing PVD from the image is not rigorously justified. Reviewer 2 specifically questions: (a) What fundamental distinction besides syntax does PVD offer over SVG? The claim that PVD is a “cleaner, higher-level abstraction” is unsubstantiated. (b) Why is an LLM necessary for SVG→PVD – could a rule-based parser suffice? Examples of SVG complexities that make rule-based conversion “impractical” are missing. (c) Why not modify the rule-based SVG encoder to directly output PVD? The claim that it “lacks awareness of semantics of shapes” requires more detail. *(R2; also noted by R1, R4)*
- **PVD definition has overlapping primitives and lacks formal specification:** The 9 primitives (e.g., “composition-filled” vs. “polygon”, “path” vs. “line segment”) have ambiguous boundaries. No formal grammar or hierarchy is provided, making it hard to assess expressive power. Additionally, sequential PVD descriptions for complex scenes may exhaust context length. *(R1, R2)*
- **Reliance on synthetic data for training the PVD translator:** The LLM is fine-tuned only on synthesized SVGs; the paper does not analyze the diversity of shapes/complexities in that data, nor does it address potential overfitting or measure how well the model generalizes to real-world vector graphics. *(R2, R3)*
- **High-level reasoning evaluation is too limited:** Only one benchmark (VGBench-QA) is used, and only GPT-4V/GPT-4o are tested as the final reasoning model. Whether open-source, less capable LMMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL) could benefit from VDLM is not explored, limiting assessment of broader applicability and cost-effectiveness. *(R3)*
- **Approach sidesteps genuine visual perception:** The method essentially translates the image into text and performs all reasoning in the language domain. This limits applicability to complex imagery that requires true visual inspection. Figure 5 shows improvement only for top-tier LLMs (GPT-3.5/4), not for weaker text-only models, suggesting dependence on strong text reasoning rather than improved perception. *(R4)*
- **Unsubstantiated claim about fine-tuning being infeasible:** The paper claims (line 085) that directly fine-tuning a model on raw SVG codes is “inefficient and infeasible” – this claim is not supported by any experiment. A reviewer notes that fine-tuning a small LMM (e.g., LLaMA 3.2 11B, LLaVA) on the same synthetic data could improve visual perception without bypassing vision. *(R4)*
- **Missing comparisons with symbolic reasoning approaches:** No symbolic or programmatic baselines are included, making it unclear how VDLM compares to explicit symbolic reasoning (e.g., using geometric properties directly) in terms of accuracy and computational cost. *(R1)*
- **Terminology inaccuracies:** The term “vector graphics” is sometimes used to refer to raster images rendered from SVG (e.g., title, L91), and “SVG” is conflated with vector graphics in general (L21–22). SVG is only one of many vector formats. *(R2)*
- **Minor discrepancies in reported numbers:** Table 1 reports GPT-4V at 58% on Angle Classification and 64% on Length Comparison, while Table 3 (Appendix) shows 58% and 57% respectively. This inconsistency is not addressed. *(R2)*
- **Missing explanations:** The figure “VF” in Figure 1(b) is undefined; related work [A] (Prism: A Framework for Decoupling and Assessing Capabilities of VLMs) is not discussed, despite its relevance to disentangled perception and reasoning. *(R3)*

*Note: One reviewer (R2, score 3, confidence 4) is notably more negative, particularly questioning the necessity of the two-stage pipeline, the specification of PVD, and the reliance on synthetic data. The other three reviewers (scores 5, 5, 5) are generally positive but still raise the concerns above.*
