## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper presents MOBINS, a collection of mobility networked time-series forecasting benchmark datasets covering transportation (taxi, bus, subway) and epidemic domains across four cities in two countries. The dataset combines origin-destination movements, spatial networks, and multiple time series. Nine baseline methods (RNN, Transformer, GNN, CNN, Linear) are benchmarked, yielding four findings. Two reviewers (R1, R4, both score 5) find the dataset and benchmarks valuable, citing long temporal range (>=2 years), multiple transportation modes, and edge movement information. Two reviewers (R2, R3, both score 3) are more critical, raising concerns about coarse spatial granularity (especially NYC with only 5 nodes), narrative complexity, limited contribution beyond data organization, and missing analyses (e.g., impact of different modes, single vs multiple data sources). Reviewer confidence is high (3, 4, 4, 4).

### Strengths

- **Extensive benchmark comparisons across multiple model backbones** (RNN, Transformer, GNN, CNN, Linear) on the six datasets, with four summarized findings that provide guidance for future studies (R1[1][2]; R2[1]; R4[s3]).
- **Long temporal range (≥2 years)** enhances reliability of analysis (R2[2]; R4[s2]).
- **Multiple transportation modes** (taxi, bus, subway) and coverage of both transportation and epidemic domains offer comprehensive insight into mobility dynamics (R2[3]; R1[3] notes downstream applications; R3[S2] shows applicability to time series and OD prediction).
- **Unique data composition**: MOBINS provides a combination of origin-destination movements, a spatial network, and multiple time series, with movement information encoded on edges – a feature absent from prior mobility datasets (R3[S1]; R4[s1]).
- **Dataset is publicly available, diverse, and supports explainability** in mobility networked time-series forecasting (R3[S3]).

### Weaknesses

- **Coarse spatial granularity**: Administrative regions are used as nodes, resulting in only 5 nodes for NYC. Multiple reviewers note this limits fine-grained analysis and undermines the dataset’s utility compared to existing datasets with finer resolution (R2[2]; R4[w1]).  
  *R1 and R4 (score 5) did not raise this issue, but R2 and R3 (score 3) highlight it as a major limitation.*
- **Narrative complexity and lack of focus**: The paper introduces too many concepts simultaneously – the South Korea smart card data, NYC Taxi data, and an epidemic dataset – making the narrative unfocused and overwhelming. The integration of transportation and epidemic data is not well motivated (R2[1]; also R1[4] questions the choice of transportation+epidemic over other mobility sources like SafeGraph).  
  *R1 and R4, despite their positive scores, also noted lack of clarity (R1[2][3]) and insufficient justification for the data selection.*
- **Limited unique contribution beyond dataset organization**: R3[W2] argues the paper only organizes existing data and does not explain advantages over prior urban mobility datasets. R2[1] similarly states the integration complicates the narrative without clear added value.  
  *R1 and R4 consider the dataset a contribution (R1[3], R4[s1]), but they also call for stronger explanation of explainability (R1[6]) and unique research opportunities from the human mobility modal (R4[w4]).*
- **Missing analyses on the claimed primary contribution of different transportation modes**: The experimental section focuses only on baseline comparisons but does not articulate or quantify the impact of using multiple transportation modes (R2[3]).  
  *All reviewers implicitly support this as a missing analysis; no reviewer disagrees.*
- **Insufficient exploration of model suitability**: R3’s Q1 notes that Linear-based and CNN-based models perform well, raising the question of whether complex models are unsuitable for this dataset. This remains unanswered – an ablation or deeper analysis is missing (R3[Q1]).
- **Lack of comparison between single vs multiple data sources**: The paper does not run experiments using only a single data source (e.g., OD movements alone) versus multiple sources combined, which would justify the dataset's added value (R3[W1]).  
  *No reviewer contradicts this need.*
- **Omission of a conclusion section**: The paper ends without summarizing its findings and implications (R2[4]).
- **Missing SOTA GNN comparisons**: The benchmark should include more recent graph neural network models beyond the few tested (R4[w2]).
- **Unclear data composition and visualization**: The detailed composition of each city’s dataset (e.g., whether taxi, bus, subway, and epidemic data cover the same time period) is not clear from Table 1; Figure 1 is uninformative; Figure 2 only shows an increasing number from 10 to 120 without meaningful differences (R1[2][3]). A table of detailed data fields is needed (R4[w3]).
- **Vague claims about CNN**: Lines 431 and 472 state “CNN is good at spatio-temporal data” without justification (R1[5]).
- **Explainability claimed but not explained**: The paper repeatedly highlights explainability as a feature of the dataset but does not elaborate (R1[6]).
- **Minor formatting error**: In Appendix Table 5, the line above NYC should be placed below (R3[W3]).
