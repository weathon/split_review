## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a neuro-symbolic program synthesis framework called COOL that combines a “Chain of Logic” (CoL) DSL, which organizes synthesis into an activity-flow with heuristic control, and a “Neural Network Feedback Control” (NNFC) mechanism that uses multiple neural networks in series to guide search and prune infeasible paths. Experiments on CLUTTR (logical family relation problems) and a toy quadratic equation dataset show 100% accuracy, greatly reduced tree operations and time, and further improvements with NNFC. One reviewer (score 5) finds the ideas interesting and the experimental validation solid, with the CoL structure mirroring human problem-solving and the ablation studies clarifying contributions. Three reviewers (scores 1, 1, 3) find the paper extremely hard to understand, with insufficient detail on inputs/outputs, training, and core mechanisms; they cannot properly evaluate the contribution.

### Strengths
- **Interesting and novel ideas** (Reviewers 2, 3): The Chain of Logic structure mirrors human problem-solving steps, enhancing interpretability. The NNFC control system is novel, and the analysis of inner coupling as a key factor to improved synthesis quality seems interesting.
- **Thorough and complex technical content** (Reviewer 2): The paper contains a lot of detail and appears very complex.
- **Impressive experimental results** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4): The approach shows both improved accuracy (e.g., 70% improvement, plus 6% from NNFC) and large reductions in computational cost (e.g., 91% fewer tree operations, 95% less time, 64% reduction in tree operations under challenging conditions). Ablation studies demonstrate the contribution of each component.

### Weaknesses
- **Extremely poor clarity and presentation** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4): The method is never described end-to-end in a coherent way. After reading, one cannot answer:
  - What is the exact form of the input a user provides? What additional information beyond a standard DSL must be given to use COOL? (Reviewers 1, 4)
  - How are the components constructed? For example, what is a DSNN (feed-forward network? LLM?)? What are THD, SSPH, SGH? (Reviewer 1)
  - What are “tree operations” and “transformation pairs”? These are never defined. (Reviewer 2)
  - For the quadratic equation dataset, what do the problems look like exactly? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the relationship between the figures and the text? Do figures represent user-provided activity diagrams, execution traces, or schematic explanations? (Reviewer 4)
  - What are “heuristic vectors”? When should logic jumps/abort commands be used, and are they written by a programmer or output by a neural network? (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient explanation of NNFC and DSNN training** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4):
  - How are the neural networks trained? What training data or ground truth is used, especially for partial programs? Is it supervised learning, reinforcement learning, or a wake/sleep loop? What architectures, loss functions, and optimization algorithms are used? (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
  - How exactly does NNFC “suppress incorrect neural network predictions through filtering”? How do the series of DSNNs interact? What thresholds are used for cross-validation? (Reviewer 3)
  - How do DSNNs generate error signals from partial programs? What constitutes a “correct” path? Is there a universal ground truth? (Reviewer 3)
  - How much data does each DSNN require to perform effectively? (Reviewer 3)
- **Generality and user effort concerns** (Reviewers 3, 4):
  - The CoL framework is highly domain-specific; applying it to new domains (e.g., NLP) would likely require a complete overhaul of heuristic vectors and keywords, raising questions about reusability. (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper does not clarify how much additional effort is required from the user to annotate the DSL with CoL structures. Without this, it is unclear how much of the performance gain is due to increased scaffolding (making the task easier) rather than the algorithm itself. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing definitions and experimental details** (Reviewers 2, 3):
  - The evaluation is not convincing without proper definitions of metrics like “tree operation.” (Reviewer 2)
  - Did the dynamic experiments include any resource constraints (time or computational power limits)? (Reviewer 3)
  - What are “multidomain tasks” and what is their role in the experiments? (Reviewer 3)
- **Majority of reviewers cannot assess the contribution** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4): Due to the lack of clear presentation, these reviewers feel unable to properly evaluate the soundness, presentation, or contribution of the paper. One reviewer (score 1) states the only way forward is a complete rewrite and recommends getting feedback from colleagues not familiar with the project before resubmission.
