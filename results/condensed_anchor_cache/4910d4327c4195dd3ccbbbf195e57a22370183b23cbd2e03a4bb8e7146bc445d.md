## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AUGCAL, a training-time patch for unsupervised Sim2Real domain adaptation (UDA) that improves calibration and model reliability. It replaces original synthetic images with strongly augmented views and adds a calibration loss on those augmented predictions. The authors provide a theoretical justification showing that the target calibration loss can be bounded by the source calibration loss, motivating the design. Experiments on semantic segmentation (GTAV→Cityscapes) and object recognition (VisDA) demonstrate effectiveness across multiple UDA backbones, tasks, and domain shifts.

### Strengths
- Extensive experiments across different tasks, datasets (GTAV→Cityscapes, VisDA), UDA backbones, and domain shifts. Baselines with AUGCAL outperform those without in all metrics (mIoU, ECE).
- The paper provides a clear, understandable theoretical derivation (upper bound for target calibration loss via source calibration loss and distribution distance) that motivates the method and offers insights useful for related fields.
- The method is simple, straightforward, and easily re-implementable: it introduces only two small changes (strong data augmentation and a calibration loss) to existing UDA pipelines.
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow.
- Novelty: augmentation and calibration are shown to be complementary and are integrated naturally; miscalibration is an overlooked factor in UDA, and AUGCAL provides an easy way to address it.  
  **Contrast in reviewer opinions:** While one reviewer (Rating 8) praised the contribution as excellent and highlighted these novelty aspects, two reviewers (Ratings 6) found the technical contribution limited—combining data augmentation and calibration loss is not new, and the novelty of the specific combination was not clearly established.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty (Reviewer 1):** Data augmentation and DCA/calibration losses have been extensively explored; the paper does not clearly establish the novelty of their specific combination for UDA.
2. **Missing comparisons to dedicated post-hoc calibration methods (Reviewer 1):** The evaluation only compares AUGCAL against baseline UDA models. It should also compare with techniques like temperature scaling or other post-hoc calibration methods to isolate benefits.
3. **Paper exceeds the 9-page limit (Reviewer 1).**
4. **Intuition for improved mIoU (Reviewer 2):** Adding calibration loss typically lowers accuracy (mIoU) while reducing ECE; Table 3(a) shows better mIoU with AUGCAL—can the authors provide intuition for why calibration loss on source improves mIoU?
5. **Intuition for augmentation satisfying properties (Reviewers 2, 3):** The paper proposes two properties and empirically verifies PASTA and RandAugment satisfy them (Table 1 shows PASTA/R.Aug-SIM are closer to Real than SIM based on MMD). It is not intuitive:
   - Why does RandAugment bring synthetic data even closer to real data than PASTA, despite PASTA being designed to bridge the syn-to-real gap?
   - If large-scale unlabeled real data is available, would style-transfer-based augmentation (e.g., StyleNet [1]) perform better because it directly uses real data?
   - How exactly do these appearance augmentations affect the distribution distance (Property 1)?
   - Do the two augmentation choices meet Property 2 (is ε as small as before augmentation)?
   - It would be good to include this intuition in the main text.
6. **Unclear definition of overconfidence (OC) metric (Reviewer 3):** The paper introduces OC after Eq. 3 and in Sec. 4, but the exact definition is not provided.
7. **Need more insights on the upper bound (Eq. 9) (Reviewer 3):** Eq. 9 is a useful upper bound for target calibration loss. What specific issues (e.g., a large syn–real gap) cause the bound to become loose? How is this bound related to mIoU (Reviewer 2)?
8. **Missing analysis of StyleNet (Reviewer 2):** Reference [1] (Kim et al., ECCV 2022) uses StyleNet for style transfer; it would be interesting to analyze whether that inherently satisfies Property 1.
