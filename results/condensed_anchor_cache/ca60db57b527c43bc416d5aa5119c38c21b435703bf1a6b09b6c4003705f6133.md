## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes iTransformer, an inverted Transformer architecture for multivariate time series forecasting. Instead of forming temporal tokens across variates, it embeds each univariate series as a token (variate token) and applies self-attention across these tokens to capture multivariate correlations, while the feed-forward network is applied per variate token for non-linear representation learning. The model uses only the encoder. Experiments on six real-world datasets and six market-based datasets show that iTransformer establishes new state-of-the-art results. The method is praised for its simplicity and effectiveness, and the paper is generally well-written with extensive ablation studies and analyses.

### Strengths

- **Simple and effective idea**: The inverted view repurposes Transformer components without architectural modification, leading to a straightforward yet powerful model.
- **State-of-the-art results**: The model outperforms a range of Transformer-based and linear baselines across multiple datasets and prediction horizons (Tables 3 and 7). All three reviewers rating 8 consider the results convincing and the contribution important.
- **Better utilization of longer lookback windows**: Figure 6 demonstrates that longer histories generally improve performance for iTransformer, addressing a known limitation of prior Transformer forecasters. This finding is highlighted as interesting and plausible.
- **Generalization to unseen variates**: The model shows robust performance when only a subset of variates is used for training (Figure 5), indicating better generalization across variates.
- **Extensive experiments and ablation studies**: Comprehensive comparisons against numerous baselines, along with analysis of representation space and correlation maps, strengthen the evaluation.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of statistical rigor**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R4) note that the reported MSE values are often very small (e.g., below 0.5) and differences between models are frequently in the range of 0.001–0.005. No standard deviations or significance tests are provided, making it difficult to judge whether improvements are meaningful or due to random variation. One reviewer (R1, score 6) was particularly concerned that such marginal gains could be within experimental noise and strongly recommended reporting standard deviations and significance tests. R2 and R3 also suggest multiple runs for experiments (e.g., random variate selection in Figures 5 and 8, the jump in Figure 6) and thus these concerns are shared.
- **Missing key baselines and discussion**: The paper does not include comparisons to recent linear-based models (RLinear, RMLP, NLinear, DLinear) and does not compare against RevIN-enhanced Transformer variants (e.g., revInformer). Reviewer 3 (score 8) considers this a significant missed opportunity to position iTransformer in the broader context of channel-dependent vs. channel-independent modeling and distribution-shift handling. The abstract’s claim that Transformer superiority is “shaken” by linear models is not fully supported, as only a few linear baselines are included.
- **Discrepancy with published PatchTST results**: Reviewer 4 points out that the reported PatchTST results on Electricity (horizon 96) give MSE 0.195, while the original PatchTST paper reports 0.129. The source of this discrepancy is not explained, raising concerns about reproducibility.
- **Limited dataset coverage**: The reviewer notes missing standard datasets (Exchange, ILI, ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2) that are commonly used in TimesNet and other works. This makes it harder to confirm consistent superiority across the full benchmark suite.
- **Unsupported claims**: Reviewer 3 identifies several claims lacking evidence:
  - The assertion that variates have “potentially unaligned timestamps” (abstract) is not justified. In datasets like Weather, all variates come from the same station and are clearly aligned. The claim needs a reference or demonstration.
  - The explanation that “extremely fluctuating series” cause PatchTST’s patching mechanism to lose focus, while iTransformer aggregates whole-series variations, is given without proof (e.g., no weight visualizations or supporting analysis).
  - Figure 7(b) (right part) is difficult to interpret; the axes and the difference between Score Map and Correlations are unclear.
  - Figure 8 lacks x-axis labels.
  - Figure 1 places 1 at the outer edge for iTransformer but uses inverted scaling, which may bias visual perception.
- **Missing multi-run experiments for random variate selection**: Reviewer 3 requests that when randomly selecting P% of variates for training, results should be reported as box plots or error bars over different random subsets, and it should be clarified that the same set of variates is used in the CI transformer comparison.
- **Lack of explanation for design choices**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) notes that the paper is “short of explanation/justification about the effectiveness” of the inverted approach. For example, the assumption that heterogeneity of variates is more important than temporal dependency is not explicitly discussed.
- **Efficiency comparison missing**: Reviewer 1 (score 6) criticizes the omission of efficiency comparisons with linear models, which are known to be computationally cheaper and often competitive. Without this, the practical benefit of iTransformer in resource-constrained settings is unclear.
- **Questions about specific results**:
  - Reviewer 2 asks about the poor performance of the “both attention” variant in the second row of Table 3.
  - Reviewer 2 also questions a jump in the red line of Figure 6 from lookback 336 to 720 and whether multiple runs would smooth it.
  - Reviewer 1 asks why TiDE results differ from those in its original paper.
  - Reviewer 2 requests clarification on the train-validation-test split to avoid data leakage, especially when year-based splits are used.
- **Minor language and presentation issues**:
  - Abstract: “However, Transformer is challenged” missing article (“the Transformer”).
  - “the duties of the attention mechanism” is unclear; “duties” is vague.
  - “this goal can be hardy achieved” – typo: should be “hardly”.
  - “Soloar-Energy” – typo: should be “Solar Energy”.
  - Reviewer 3 suggests calling Electricity dataset “ECL” and specifying which ETT (ETTm1 or m2) and which PEMS dataset are used.
  - The term “irrationality” (p.2) is considered too strong.
- **One reviewer (R1, score 6) was significantly more critical** than the other three (R2–R4, all score 8). The minority reviewer emphasized concerns about statistical significance, the small magnitude of improvements, and the lack of linear model baselines, while the majority found the results convincing and the contribution solid. This contrast in confidence should be accounted for when assessing the paper’s claims.
