## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the connection between sharpness minimization (Hessian trace) and simplicity bias in two-layer neural networks with fixed-to-1 output weights. Building on Li et al.’s result that label noise SGD with infinitesimal step size follows gradient flow that minimizes the trace of Hessian on the zero-loss manifold, the authors prove: (1) under high-dimensional data (d > n) and activations with strictly positive first and third derivatives, all global minimizers of the Hessian trace on the zero-loss manifold have a rank-1 feature matrix (all neurons identical on training data); (2) label noise SGD (interpreted as this gradient flow) converges exponentially fast to such minimizers. A technical contribution is the identification of local geodesic convexity of the Hessian trace at points where its gradient is small, linking sharpness to manifold geometry.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important open problem: the relationship between flatness and simplicity in neural networks. It takes a good step by proving that, in a specific setting, all flattest global minimizers yield a rank-1 feature matrix, a strong form of simplicity.
- The theoretical framework is clean and justifies two popular conjectures: sharpness-reduction implicit bias implies simplicity bias, and label noise SGD converges to global minimizers of sharpness on the zero-loss manifold.
- The setting (two-layer network with arbitrary width and fixed output weights) is more general than existing work in some respects.
- The technical insight that a small gradient on the zero-loss manifold implies positive semidefinite Hessian of the Hessian trace (local geodesic convexity) is non-trivial and potentially of wider interest.
- The appendices contain helpful background on manifolds, tangent spaces, and covariant derivatives, and the proofs are carefully carried out (Reviewer 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic assumptions:**
  - The data dimension is assumed larger than the number of points (d > n), so a linear regressor fits perfectly. This ensures realizability regardless of width but deviates from practice where n ≫ d and realizability requires sufficient width. It sidesteps under-parameterization and the need for complex feature learning.
  - The output weights are fixed to 1. This avoids the non-convexity and symmetry of the output space, which is a key challenge in real training. The claim that results “generalize” to any fixed second layer is not proven, and the extension is likely non-trivial.
  - The activation assumptions (strictly positive first and third derivatives, Assumptions 1 and 3) rule out most common activations like ReLU, sigmoid, and tanh. The paper does not discuss how far one could go without these assumptions or what can be said for activations whose second derivative is only invertible up to sign.
- **Overclaiming and lack of clarity:** The paper does not clearly state that it takes Li et al.’s asymptotic flow (label noise SGD with infinitesimal step size) as given, which is itself not a general regime and only asymptotic. Theorem 1 lacks a precise definition of label noise SGD and quantitative dependencies on step size and noise. Quantitative non-asymptotic results in this direction are significantly difficult (cf. Pillaud-Vivien et al., COLT 2022).
- **Presentation issues:** Section 4.3 is hard to read – the proof sketch for Theorem 3 is split confusingly, functions \(g\) and \(\gamma\) are not defined in the main text, and it is unclear how local geodesic convexity yields the convergence rate. The paper contains numerous typos, LaTeX errors, and English issues (e.g., “Equation equation 1”, “non=degeneracy”, missing articles, inconsistent font, Assumption numbers mismatched).
- **Missing comparisons and related work:** The paper does not discuss how it relates to “Sharpness-Aware Minimization Leads to Low-Rank Features” (Andriushchenko et al., 2023) or to “SGD with large step sizes learns sparse features” (Andriushchenko et al., 2023), which are directly relevant. Future work suggestions are absent from the conclusion.
- **Reproducibility:** No code is submitted, making numerical experiments harder to reproduce.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 8) is more positive about the contribution and presentation, while others (scores 5, 5, 6) are more critical, finding the assumptions too restrictive and the overclaiming problematic.
- **Questions that indicate weaknesses:**
  - Why not simply assume \(n = d\)? That would make optima unique and avoid the orthogonal subspace that plays no role.
  - How does the result on the flattest minimizers being rank-1 rely on the high-dimensional assumption? Could any structure be proven without global convergence under weaker conditions?
  - The paper claims that by initializing neurons in the span of the data, all neurons converge to a single vector, but this is not discussed in the main text. Would weight decay make this true for any initialization?
  - The reliance on Lemma 15 with Assumption 1 to claim that weaker assumptions automatically imply boundedness is circular unless positivity of \(\varphi', \varphi'''\) is already assumed.
  - Why is the relation to \(\varphi''\) in the claim about generalizing fixed second layer?
