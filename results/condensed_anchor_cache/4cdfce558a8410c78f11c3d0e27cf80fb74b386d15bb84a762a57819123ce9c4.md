## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a prompt-based continual learning method that accumulates knowledge in a single prompt using contrastive learning without negative pairs (treating current and previous prompts as positive views). The goal is to approximate an upper-bound prompt trained on all tasks jointly, avoiding task‑id prediction. Reviewers note the idea is novel, the writing is clear, and the method reduces parameters and inference time. However, they raise major concerns about insufficient comparisons (missing CODA-Prompt, HiDe-Prompt), only marginal gains over DualPrompt, missing baseline comparisons to classic regularization (LwF), lack of upper‑bound performance, limited experiments on other pretrained models/datasets, missing training‑cost analysis, and incomplete ablation of loss hyperparameters. One reviewer (Rating 3) is more critical, especially regarding the lack of regularization comparisons; another (Rating 6) is more positive about the comprehensive analyses.

### Strengths

1. The paper is well‑written, well‑structured, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4).
2. The application of contrastive learning (without negative pairs) to prompt‑based continual learning is novel (R1, R2).
3. The approach uses only a single prompt, reducing parameters and inference time while maintaining competitive performance (R2, R3, R4).
4. Empirical analyses on multiple continual learning benchmarks and module ablation are comprehensive (R3).
5. The motivation (avoiding misaligned prompts by accumulating knowledge in one prompt) is clear and supported by empirical analysis (R4).
6. The capacity of a single prompt to achieve comparable performance to a prompt pool is demonstrated (R4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient comparison to recent SOTA prompt‑pool methods.**  
   The paper misses comparisons to CODA‑Prompt (CVPR’23) and HiDe‑Prompt (NeurIPS’23), which are critical for establishing novelty and effectiveness. R2 notes CODA‑Prompt appears to outperform the proposed method even without ensemble (cf. Table 3 of CODA‑Prompt). R4 questions whether there is enough room for further development of the single‑prompt idea given the small gains. (R1, R2, R4)

2. **Minor improvement over DualPrompt and weak justification for single prompt.**  
   The performance gain over DualPrompt is marginal on all benchmarks. R3 and R4 question why learning a single prompt is preferable over a prompt pool; the advantage is not well justified. R4 adds that the improvement is small and it is unclear whether the single‑prompt approach scales to more complex scenarios. (R3, R4)

3. **Lack of comparison to classic regularization (LwF).**  
   The proposed loss forces current and previous prompts to be similar, which is equivalent to regularizing output similarity between old and new models (as in LwF). The paper does not compare to knowledge‑distillation or other direct output‑space constraints. R1 asks which regularization is better and why. (R1)

4. **Missing upper‑bound prompt performance.**  
   The paper claims the prompt approximates an upper‑bound prompt (trained on merged data) but does not report its performance. R1 questions whether the upper‑bound prompt itself outperforms SOTA task‑specific prompt methods, making it difficult to assess practical benefit. (R1)

5. **Missing experiments with different pretrained models and datasets.**  
   The method may restrict learning when there is a large gap between pretrained and new‑task data. R1 recommends using other pretrained models (e.g., MoCo v3 on ImageNet‑1K) and datasets (citing [4]). R2 asks how the approach behaves without a pretrained model, and suggests comparison to expansion‑based methods [2,3] that do not use pretrained models. (R1, R2)

6. **Training‑cost analysis omitted.**  
   Contrastive learning typically requires extra computation. R4 requests quantification of forward/backward passes and comparison of training time per epoch and total training time against other methods. (R4)

7. **Incomplete ablation of loss hyperparameters.**  
   The two loss terms (L_ctr and L_prev) differ only by the weight matrix W, and it is unclear which part is more important. R3 asks for experiments showing how λ_ctr and λ_prev affect performance across benchmarks. (R3)

8. **Missing analysis of prompt parameter growth vs performance.**  
   While Table 4 shows parameter growth, R2 notes the paper does not explore how performance changes when prompt parameters are increased, especially for complex tasks that may require more capacity. (R2)

9. **No quantitative analysis of prompt‑selection misalignment in prior work.**  
   The paper claims that L2P and DualPrompt suffer from misaligned prompts but does not measure the extent of misalignment. R2 asks for such evidence. (R2)

10. **Conceptual connections to a recent work [1] not discussed.**  
    R4 notes that a recent work discusses similar issues (misaligned prompts, contrastive learning) and encourages at least conceptual comparison. R1 also asks for evidence supporting the use of a single prompt over task‑specific prompts. (R4, R1)
