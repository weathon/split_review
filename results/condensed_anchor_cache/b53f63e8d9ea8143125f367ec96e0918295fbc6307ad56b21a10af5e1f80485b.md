## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Cocoon, an object- and feature-level uncertainty-aware fusion framework for robust multi-modal 3D object detection in autonomous driving. It designs a valid uncertainty estimator for heterogeneous representations via a feature aligner and a learnable surrogate ground truth called Feature Impression, then dynamically fuses multi-modal sensor features. Experiments indicate effectiveness in various unrobust scenes, outperforming existing static and adaptive fusion methods under normal and challenging conditions. The framework is claimed to have good scalability for additional sensor types and modalities (e.g., camera+LiDAR+radar, visual+language).

### Strengths
- The motivation and design (feature aligner, Feature Impression, uncertainty quantification) are intuitive, valid, and well-grounded; the innovation is feasible from both principles and experiments.
- Cocoon uniquely combines object-level and feature-level uncertainty awareness in a single framework, addressing a significant gap in existing adaptive fusion approaches.
- The method is thoroughly evaluated under diverse corruption scenarios (both natural and artificial), with solid mathematical foundation and clear derivations validated across multiple datasets.
- The paper is clearly presented and easy to understand (Reviewer 3; note: Reviewer 1 finds the explanation too complicated – see Weaknesses for the contrasting view).
- The framework demonstrates good scalability and can potentially be extended to other modal fusions (e.g., combining camera, LiDAR, and radar, or visual-language models).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation clarity**: The explanation of the method is too complicated, which will inconvenience the reader (Reviewer 1). This contrasts with Reviewer 3’s positive view on clarity.
- **Computational overhead and real-time concerns**: The entire Cocoon process, particularly the structural comparisons in Figure 1 and the feature aligner/uncertainty quantification modules (Sec 4.2, 4.3), imposes substantial computational overhead. Integration with FUTR3D increases latency from 1.1s to 1.6s, making real-time deployment questionable.
- **Modest gains and performance relative to prior work**: Actual improvements over baselines are modest in many scenarios. Cocoon yields marginal gains and sometimes performs worse than existing methods such as CMT, BEVFusion, and DeepInteraction.
- **Limited sensor and dataset validation**: Only camera and LiDAR fusion are evaluated; other important sensors (radar, ultrasonic) are missing. Validation is lacking on additional datasets like Waymo and Argoverse 2, raising concerns about generalizability.
- **Dependence on feature alignment/calibration quality**: The feature aligner projects features from different modalities into a common space. If the quality or sampling frequency of each modality’s features differs significantly, accurate alignment becomes difficult, directly affecting the reliability of uncertainty quantification and the overall fusion effect.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The hyperparameter α (set to 0.1 in Table 4) is critical; the limitations mention that changes in α cause significant variation in algorithm performance, raising concern about excessive hyperparameter dependence.
- **Missing ablation studies**: The contributions of individual components (feature aligner, Feature Impression, uncertainty quantification) to overall performance are not ablated.
- **Accuracy-speed tradeoff not addressed**: The suggestion of using fewer decoder layers or queries to reduce latency is not evaluated; the effect on the accuracy-speed tradeoff remains unclear.
