## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviews are largely aligned that the paper identifies a key problem in Transformer training—spectral energy concentration in the product of the query and key matrices, leading to entropy collapse and model crash—and proposes a modified AdamW optimizer that bounds the learning rate using a threshold based on singular-value ratios to remove the need for learning rate warmup. The analysis and method are deemed interesting, but the reviews express significant concerns about experimental scale, theoretical clarity, novelty of the observations, computational overhead, and the possibility that the proposed scheme merely reparameterizes warmup.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical insight:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis identifying spectral energy concentration in \(W_q^\top W_k\) as the cause of malignant entropy collapse and model crashes. The use of singular values to quantify this collapse is interesting. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Application of Weyl’s inequality:** Applying Weyl’s inequality to the optimizer update to bound the step size and stabilize training is a nice development. (Reviewer 2)
- **Removal of warmup:** The biggest contribution is that the proposed algorithm frees practitioners from using learning rate warmup. (Reviewer 2; also noted by Reviewer 3 as an alternate method)
- **Clear experiments linking spectral norms to crashes:** The experiments convincingly show that in crashed models the spectrum of the query-key product matrix concentrates on top eigenvalues, providing novel insight. (Reviewer 4)
- **Well-structured with mathematical definitions:** The paper includes necessary mathematical definitions and proofs in the main text and supplementary material, and is clearly organized. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scale and scope:** Experiments are small; no results are provided with larger models (0.5B, 1B, 3B) where training instabilities are more apparent. It is unclear whether the method works at the scales (e.g., 8B for ViTs) where instabilities are known to occur (Deghani et al. 2023, Worstman et al.). (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 also notes the need for results with linear-complexity attention models like Linformer or Flatten Transformer, and encoder-decoder architectures.)
- **Theoretical contribution weakens the paper:** Theorem 1 is vague: key terms like “malignant collapse” and “spectral energy concentration” are not formally defined. The proof (Appendix D) lacks clarity, assumes low rank of \(W\) due to collapse (circular reasoning), and heavily relies on a Gaussian distribution assumption. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 also finds the argument in Section 3.2 unclear, particularly how low rank of \(X^\top\) implies low rank of \(X^\top \otimes X^\top\) given the square relationship.)
- **Novelty concerns:** The observation that parameter norms blow up when training fails is well known. The spectral collapse of attention logits has been observed in prior work (e.g., Deghani, Worstman). The proposed SEC measure introduces a hyperparameter ‘s’ that a smooth rank measure would not require. (Reviewers 2,3)
- **Proposed method may be a reparameterization of warmup:** The algorithm modifies the step size \(\alpha_t\) based on spectral norms. It likely produces a schedule that resembles warmup. The paper does not show how \(\alpha_t\) evolves over time or contrast it with standard warmup schedules, making it difficult to argue the method is fundamentally different. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational overhead:** The method adds a power iteration step per gradient update, which is computationally more expensive than standard warmup. No time complexity or runtime measurements are provided to justify the practicality. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing and clarity issues:**
    - Mathematical notations appear in the abstract and introduction without proper definition (e.g., \(\delta \text{vec}(P)\) in the contributions). (Reviewer 1)
    - References to internet sources (e.g., Wikipedia) instead of proper academic references. (Reviewer 1)
    - Figure 5: The implication from the concentrated low-rank structure of \(\partial \text{vec}(P)/\partial \text{vec}(W_q^\top W_k)\) to \(W_q^\top W_k\) becoming low rank is not straightforward. (Reviewer 4)
    - The claim that attention maps are “sparse yet low-rank” lacks a clear quantitative rank measure; the provided plots are difficult to interpret and would benefit from effective rank or better visualization. (Reviewer 3)
    - The definition of “non-symmetric positive quasi-definite square matrix” is used without a reference or appendix clarification. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing ablations and hyperparameter discussion:** No guidance on how to set the threshold parameter \(\tau\) for an unseen model or how \(\tau\) may vary with model scale. (Reviewer 4) Also, lacks discussion of whether benign collapse occurs in real training. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear whether the problem is specific to softmax:** If attention is computed without softmax (e.g., linear attention models), would the same crash occur? The paper does not address this. (Reviewer 1)
