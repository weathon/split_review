## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a reparameterized discrete diffusion model (RDM) for text generation. It formulates a route-and-denoise process that splits the diffusion into noise and unnoise tokens, simplifies training to a reweighted cross-entropy loss, and uses a discriminative routing mechanism for decoding. Experiments are conducted on machine translation, question generation, and paraphrasing. The authors claim significant improvements over existing discrete and continuous diffusion models.

### Strengths
- **Novel framework and fresh perspective**: The reparameterization provides a new viewpoint on discrete diffusion, dividing the generative process into routing and denoising steps, which reduces generative difficulty (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The paper is well written and the mathematics are clear (Reviewer 4).
- **Improved training and decoding**: The training objective simplifies to a reweighted cross-entropy, and the adaptive routing strategy (route tokens to denoising only when router scores are high) makes decoding more efficient and effective (Reviewers 1, 4). This enables fast non-autoregressive generation (Reviewer 1).
- **Solid performance on machine translation**: RDM achieves milestone-level results on MT, competitive or superior to prior non-autoregressive translation models (Reviewer 1). Extensive experiments across multiple conditional tasks show improvements over existing diffusion models (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**: The evaluation is restricted to conditional generation tasks (machine translation, question generation, paraphrasing). There are no experiments on unconditional text generation (e.g., enwik8, text8), which were tested in prior work by Austin et al. (2021) and Hoogeboom et al. (2021) (Reviewers 2, 3). This limits the generality of the claims; Reviewer 1 notes the gap between deterministic tasks like MT and open-domain diverse generation, suggesting claims should be restricted to MT.
- **Unclear motivation and theoretical foundation**: The paper lacks a rigorous proof that the reparameterization is mathematically equivalent to the original discrete diffusion process, and does not formally explain why separating routing and denoising should improve performance (Reviewer 3). The recursive computation for \(b_t\) is confusing (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient comparison with strong non-autoregressive baselines**: Levenshtein Transformer is cited but not compared in most experiments; results could be directly borrowed from its original paper (Reviewer 1). The comparison with CMLM does not follow the original experimental settings, making it hard to assess true performance (Reviewer 3). Figure 2(b) compares with Diffuseq, a continuous model, which is not a direct or suitable comparison (Reviewer 3). A running-time study comparing RDM with other non-autoregressive models (beyond diffusion and CausalLM) is requested (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty concerns and overlap with existing methods**: The approach borrows heavily from masked language modeling techniques (CMLM, DisCo) and existing discrete diffusion models (improved VQ-Diffusion). The reparameterization is seen as an incremental improvement rather than a fundamentally new contribution (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 4 considers the work novel and insightful, indicating disagreement among reviewers.
- **Inconsistent performance**: Improvements over the discrete diffusion baseline are not consistent across all tasks and metrics, raising concerns about robustness and generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of ablation study**: No comprehensive ablation study is provided to demonstrate the contribution of individual components (e.g., the routing mechanism, the reweighted loss) (Reviewer 3).
- **Training-inference discrepancy**: The decoding method is similar to existing mask-predict and easy-first policies, and the paper does not analyze the impact of the mismatch between training and inference procedures (Reviewer 3).
- **Length scalability**: Additional experiments on longer sequences are needed, as diffusion models typically struggle with longer generations compared to autoregressive models (Reviewer 4).
- **Effect of training objective**: Reviewer 2 asks how using the loss functions from Hoogeboom et al. (2021) or Austin et al. (2021) would affect generation quality.
