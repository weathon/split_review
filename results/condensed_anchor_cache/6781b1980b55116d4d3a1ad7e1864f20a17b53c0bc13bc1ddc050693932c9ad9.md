## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Don’t Trust, Verify (DTV), which uses LLMs to autoformalize informal math problems and solutions into Isabelle formal code, then verifies them with a theorem prover and selects the answer by majority voting over verified solutions. On GSM8K, MATH, and MultiArith, DTV outperforms vanilla majority voting by up to 12% on GSM8K (and 7% on the full test suite) across Minerva 8B/62B/540B. The method also includes symbolic and neural filters (e.g., self-critique) to improve translation reliability. The framework is related to Draft, Sketch, and Prove (Jiang et al., 2023) but adds comprehensive experiments and new mechanisms.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow; the background and related work sections effectively position the contribution. (R1, R2, R3)
- The problem is well-motivated: combining LLMs with external tools to improve soundness, and the proposed method is simple with low additional cost. (R2, R3)
- The approach is original (obvious in retrospect) and carefully designed for the reasoning task, with meaningful differences from DTP (Jiang et al.). (R2, R3)
- The empirical evaluation is comprehensive, with detailed analysis and a well-written limitations section that addresses many potential concerns. (R1)
- The inclusion of both symbolic and neural filters is a valuable new mechanism. (R1, R4)
- Significance: the work advances the autoformalization literature, which will continue to gain importance; it capitalizes on LLM strengths while mitigating soundness weaknesses. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- A minority reviewer (R4) considers the originality and theoretical contribution limited, describing the method as merely a clever combination of existing tools. (R4)
- The same reviewer (R4) notes that the experimental comparison is mainly against vanilla voting, with no comparison against the many alternative methods discussed in the related work (e.g., other reasoning-enhancement techniques), and the rationale for selecting baselines is not explained. (R4)
- Reviewer R2 raises concerns about the fairness of comparing DTV (using Minerva 62B) against baseline methods that use GPT-3.5 (Table 1); the improvement appears marginal when GPT-3.5 is not involved, and the claim would be stronger by showing results when DTV is combined with multiple LLMs (as in Wang et al., 2022). (R2)
- The benefits of DTV appear to decrease as model size scales (e.g., Minerva 8B → 540B in Table 2). (R1)
- The efficiency of DTV is a concern: because generating one sample solution requires at least three LLM queries (informal solution, formal statement, formal sketch) plus additional queries for the self-critique filter, the paper should compare DTV against majority voting with proportionally more samples (e.g., if DTV uses m queries per sample, compare with n*m samples) and show how performance changes with number of samples n. (R2)
- Missing ablation/experiment: the authors could explore selecting the “smallest” verified solution instead of majority voting (R3), and estimate performance if GPT-3.5 handled formalization and self-critique (including the extra time cost) (R3).
- Missing comparison: a direct head-to-head comparison with Jiang et al.’s DTP (Draft, Sketch, and Prove) would be meaningful. (R3)
- The paper does not describe how a solution is extracted from the informal LLM answer; a discussion of extraction based on the final answer versus the informal reasoning steps is requested. (R1)
- Minor typos: “treated unverified” → “treated as unverified”; “autoforamlization” (R3).
- Clarification needed: Figure 3 caption implies that the formalized statement is too high-level for Isabelle to solve in one step, but the formalized individual steps are granular enough for proof and verification—this should be clarified. (R3)
