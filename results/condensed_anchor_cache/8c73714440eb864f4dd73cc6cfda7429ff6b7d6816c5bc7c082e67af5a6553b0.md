## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Spatial 3D-LLM, a 3D MLLM designed to improve spatial perception and reasoning for 3D vision-language tasks. It introduces a progressive spatial awareness scheme to enrich 3D scene embeddings, and two novel tasks (3D object distance measurement and 3D layout editing) alongside a 263K-annotation instruction dataset called MODEL. The architecture aims to capture multi-level location information without relying on a pre-detected 3D object detector. Experiments report SOTA results on several 3D visual grounding and understanding benchmarks.

### Strengths
- The approach achieves SOTA results on multiple 3D vision-language benchmarks (R1).
- Writing is mostly clear and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- Most important baselines are comprehensively compared (R2).
- Ablations on proposed tasks and components are appreciated (R2).
- The newly introduced tasks (distance measurement, layout editing) have potential for valuable applications (R3).
- The architectural improvement (progressive spatial awareness) directly addresses the current limitation of 3D LLMs in capturing rich spatial information (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and contribution of new tasks questioned**: Reviewer 2 finds the two new tasks (distance measurement, layout editing) are generated by template filling from the ScanRefer dataset, and solving them largely relies on 3D visual grounding from ScanRefer plus simple integer addition/subtraction. Reviewer 2 questions their necessity and notes that Table-4 shows only marginal improvement from including these tasks. In contrast, Reviewer 3 sees potential but agrees baseline comparisons for these tasks are insufficient.
- **Missing key baseline comparisons**:
  - No comparison with LEO (An Embodied Generalist Agent in 3D World, ICML’24); reported performance on public benchmarks appears to underperform LEO’s results, and this is not explained (R3).
  - For the distance measurement and layout editing tasks, no simple baselines are provided (e.g., off-the-shelf 3D segmentation/detection to extract bounding boxes for direct measurement, or constructing scene graphs and using existing LLMs for both measurement and movement) (R3). Reviewer 3 also asks how other baseline methods would perform if fine-tuned with the proposed new data.
  - Missing discussions of object-level and point-based 3D LLMs (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, MiniGPT-3D) and token-based 3D localization models (SceneScript) (R2).
  - Reviewer 2 notes that Chat-3D-v2 has an updated version (Chat-Scene, NeurIPS 2024) with much better performance; although a full comparison may not be required, the authors should acknowledge that their method does not require an off-the-shelf 3D detector (unlike Chat-Scene and other baselines), making direct comparisons potentially unfair.
- **Missing ablations on existing benchmarks**: Only ablations on the new tasks (METHOD dataset) are provided. Reviewer 2 requests ablation studies on standard benchmarks (ScanRefer, ScanQA, SQA3D) to show the effect of training data and components (e.g., effect of distance/layout data on ScanRefer) (R2).
- **Insufficient details about METHOD dataset**: The annotation pipeline is not described (R1).
- **Insufficient qualitative results**: Reviewer 2 requests running the model on ScanNet validation scenes (scene0568_00, scene0169_00, scene0300_00) with specific prompts (“Describe the scene in detail”, “List the objects and their quantities”) and for scene0169_00 also with “Find the red backpack” with visualization of its location.
- **Confusing notation**: In the method section, the visual referent representation after the Intra-Referent module and after the Inter-Referent module are both named \(F_{vr}\); the authors should use different notations (R1).
- **Technical and experimental ambiguities**:
  - How are \(q_{gt}\) and \(k_{gt}\) determined for the losses \(L_{center}\) and \(L_{psc}\)? (R1)
  - The number of visual referents is fixed at 256, but the number of objects varies across scenes; how are \(L_{center}\) and \(L_{psc}\) applied when the number of objects differs from 256? (R1, R3) Also, how are object centers obtained for the refinement loss? (R3)
  - For the movement task, does the model consider whether there is space for the movement, or is it more like object localization followed by applying a movement vector? (R3)
- **Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 2 is notably more critical (score 3) than reviewers 1 and 3 (both score 5). Reviewer 2 is willing to raise the score if most concerns (especially regarding the necessity of new tasks and missing ablations/baselines) are addressed.
