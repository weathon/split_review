## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a conceptual "Copyright Plug-in Market" for text-to-image copyright protection, with three operations: addition (via LoRA), extraction (via an "inverse LoRA" approach), and combination (via data-ignorant layer-wise distillation). Experiments on style transfer and cartoon IP recreation (e.g., Picasso, Mickey) are presented. Reviewers rated the paper 6, 3, and 5. Two reviewers found the topic important and the idea interesting, while one reviewer was more critical, citing poor writing and insufficient novelty. All reviewers noted weaknesses in evaluation and clarity.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and timely problem in generative AI: copyright infringement and credit attribution (R1, R2, R3).
- The overall framework (addition, extraction, combination) is liked and could have high industrial impact (R1).
- Results, though limited, look great (R1).
- The idea and design are preliminary but make sense (R2).
- The use of LoRA for concept extraction is interesting (R3).
- Novelty of the topic is clearly stated (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and lack of clarity (R2):** The paper is poorly written; no system diagram or workflow to define interactions between users, content creators, and models. It is unclear how the addition operation is initiated and how financial transactions are handled. A concrete diagram is needed.
- **Novelty and technical depth (R2, R1):** Most components (LoRA for addition, distillation for combination) are widely adopted. The inverse LoRA extraction method (Section 2.2.1–2.2.2) is not sufficiently detailed—the process of learning a LoRA, flipping its sign, and fine-tuning with memorization is not intuitive. The optimization objectives and potential limitations of inverse LoRA are not discussed (R2). R1 also asks for clarification of this process.
- **Evaluation is weak and incomplete (R1, R3):** Only limited qualitative results are shown; more diverse examples for extraction (the key novelty) would strengthen the paper (R1). The current results do not fully support effectiveness (R3).
- **Missing quantitative results (R1, R3):** Human evaluation is mentioned on page 7 but no results are presented (R1). Impact on FID on larger datasets (e.g., Laion, COCO) is not reported (R3).
- **Ad-hoc design of operations (R2):** There is no theoretical justification why the three operations (addition, extraction, combination) form a complete or sufficient set. For example, what if a user wants to modify a copyrighted concept? The framework should be more flexible.
- **Limited scope of concept forgetting (R3):** The method assumes concepts are well-defined by text words; it is unclear how to forget concepts that are not well-defined by text. This missing experiment/ablation is not addressed.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** R1 (score 6) found no major weaknesses and praised the framework, while R2 (score 3) found the paper poorly written and the contributions incremental. R3 (score 5) was intermediate, highlighting weak evaluation and missing experiments.
