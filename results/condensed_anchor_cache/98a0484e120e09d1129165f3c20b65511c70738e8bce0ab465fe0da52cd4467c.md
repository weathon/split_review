## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Topic and Description Reasoning Generation (TDRG), using LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3.1-8B) to generate topic titles and descriptions from Chinese YouTube comments, evaluated via BLEU, ROUGE, and semantic textual similarity. Reviewer evaluations are split: two rate the paper 3 (weak accept), citing a novel framework and rigorous methodology; two rate it 1 (reject), citing lack of innovation, limited dataset, and insufficient evaluation.

### Strengths
- Introduces a structured framework that bridges topic modeling and text generation by generating both concise topic titles and detailed descriptions (Reviewer 4).
- Implements a prompt engineering approach and compares multiple LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3.1-8B) under zero-shot, with-captions, and fine-tuning conditions (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Uses a human-annotated YouTube comment dataset and includes experiments with additional context (video captions) to examine performance variation (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The paper is generally well-structured, with clear presentation of problem formulation, methodology, and results (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The paper primarily functions as a case study, applying fine-tuned LLMs to a specific task without introducing new algorithms or theoretical contributions. Reviewer 3 states it “lacks innovation” and is “more like a large course assignment.” Two reviewers (1, 2) note the same limitation; Reviewer 4 acknowledges the framework as novel but still criticizes the absence of comparisons with traditional approaches.
- **Limited dataset and language scope**: The dataset is restricted to Chinese-language comments from only 25 YouTubers and 112 clusters. No other languages or platforms are included, raising concerns about generalizability (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 3 adds that annotation details and consistency are not provided, making dataset quality questionable.
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: The paper relies solely on BLEU scores and semantic textual similarity; these do not fully capture the quality or interpretability of generated topics and descriptions. Several reviewers call for human judgment, user studies, or task-specific metrics (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 4 specifically notes the absence of comparisons with traditional topic modeling (e.g., LDA, BTM) and recent neural topic models.
- **Inadequate exploration of model limitations**: The paper observes that Llama-3.1-8B underperforms GPT-4o models but does not analyze the reasons (e.g., architectural constraints, training data biases). This missing analysis is flagged by Reviewers 1 and 2.
- **Lack of methodological clarity**: The fine-tuning process is not sufficiently detailed: hyperparameters, training procedures, and computational requirements are missing, hampering reproducibility. The influence of video captions as additional context is also not explained with quantitative or qualitative analysis (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Small workload**: Reviewer 3 describes the paper as having “relatively small workload, more like a large course assignment.” Reviewer 4 notes the small number of clusters (112).
- **Need for proofreading and editing**: Reviewer 1 recommends extensive proofreading to improve clarity before resubmission.
