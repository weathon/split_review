## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a fine-grained knowledge unit retrieval-augmented generation (KU-RAG) method for knowledge-based visual question answering (KB-VQA). It constructs multimodal knowledge units (text fragments and entity images) from external knowledge bases, retrieves them using a multimodal retriever, and integrates retrieved knowledge with an MLLM’s internal knowledge via a Knowledge Correction Chain (KCC) for reasoning. Experiments on GPT‑4o show improvements of up to 10% over previous KB-VQA solutions. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem and the potential of the approach, but raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental scope (only GPT‑4o), missing baselines, insufficient details of the knowledge base, and writing clarity. One reviewer found the framework useful and well-illustrated (score 5), but the other three rated the paper 3, citing limited technical contributions and weak empirical validation.

### Strengths
- The research problem is important and interesting: enhancing multimodal LLMs with retrieval-augmented generation for VQA.  
- The concept of fine-grained knowledge units (text + image) and the KU-RAG framework provide a structured way to incorporate external multimodal knowledge into MLLMs.  
- The Knowledge Correction Chain (KCC) introduces a multi-turn reasoning mechanism that integrates retrieved external knowledge with the MLLM’s internal knowledge, potentially reducing hallucination.  
- The paper is well-illustrated and easy to understand (figures are clear, the architecture is described step‑by‑step).  
- The proposed knowledge unit construction is adaptable to other multimodal tasks requiring external knowledge.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**: All experiments are conducted only with GPT‑4o. Results with open-source MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA1.5, LLaVA‑Next) are missing, raising concerns about generalizability and the method’s applicability to weaker models. At least 3–5 different MLLMs (both open‑source and proprietary) should be tested.  
- **Insufficient baselines**: No comparison with multimodal RAG baselines is provided. The paper should include CLIP‑based image‑to‑image retrieval (e.g., EchoSight) and CLIP‑based text‑to‑image retrieval (e.g., InfoSeek). The zero‑shot GPT‑4o baseline already outperforms the trained SOTA methods in Table 2, making it unclear whether the proposed retrieval adds value.  
- **Lack of novelty / limited technical contribution**:  
  - The knowledge units are a straightforward implementation of organizing existing KB entries (text + image); the technical contribution in this aspect is limited.  
  - The Knowledge Correction Chain resembles a simple prompt‑based CoT approach and is described as a naive experimental attempt; error propagation and knowledge conflicts are inevitable in real‑world scenarios.  
  - The overall framework is similar to traditional multimodal RAG (e.g., Lin et al. 2023, “Fine-grained late-interaction multi-modal retrieval”); the combination of extraction, retrieval, and correction is more an engineering integration than a novel method.  
- **Missing details about the knowledge base**: The paper does not specify the knowledge source, scale, length, or quality of the constructed knowledge units. How was the quality of knowledge units measured? Can the knowledge unit construction be shared across datasets, or must it be rebuilt per dataset?  
- **Unclear justification for knowledge units vs. direct multimodal querying**: The necessity of constructing explicit knowledge units (rather than querying the knowledge base with raw multimodal inputs) is not sufficiently justified.  
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Section 1 reads more like a related‑work survey than an introduction; Section 3 is confusing and the overall writing can be improved (e.g., phrasing such as “Traditional wisdom” suggests LLM involvement in drafting).  
- **Confidence in KCC’s effectiveness**: The KCC relies on the MLLM’s ability to distinguish incorrect retrieved results; if retrieval is poor, the method merely falls back on the MLLM’s inherent knowledge. Its effectiveness on weaker MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA1.5) has not been demonstrated.
