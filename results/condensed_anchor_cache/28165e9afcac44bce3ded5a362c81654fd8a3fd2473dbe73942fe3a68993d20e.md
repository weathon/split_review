- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a new task, Dataset Distillation for Domain Generalization (DD for DG), which aims to generate synthetic datasets that enable training domain-generalizable models. It evaluates state-of-the-art DD methods on the DomainBed benchmark (VLCS, PACS, Office-H, TerraInc) and introduces a method combining domain transfer learning and domain style mixing. Experiments show promise, especially under low IPC (images per class) settings, and Table 1 demonstrates that naively applying standard DD across multiple source domains fails for DG. Reviewers rated the paper 5, 5, 3, 3, with diverging opinions on clarity, novelty, and experimental scope.

### Strengths
- The problem of dataset distillation for domain generalization is novel, meaningful, compelling, and under-explored. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
- The general idea is reasonable. (Reviewer 1)
- Empirical results are good, with notable improvements under low IPC settings. (Reviewer 1, 4)
- The use of the DomainBed benchmark provides a rigorous evaluation framework. (Reviewer 2)
- Practical implications: reducing costs and complexities of large-scale data handling in real-world applications. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is well-organized and well-written, with a good introduction to dataset distillation. (Reviewer 3)
- Validation across various architectures and datasets is attempted. (Reviewer 3)
- Table 1 clearly shows that direct application of existing DD methods to multi-source DG is ineffective. (Reviewer 4)
- The method shows some promise, especially for low IPC. (Reviewer 4)
- The authors made an effort to focus on larger-scale dataset settings (though one reviewer disputes the term “large-scale”). (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Writing clarity and missing details**: The paper is hard to follow, with grammatical errors, typos (e.g., L271, L288, L379; Eq. (8) subscript should be \(S\) not \(\tilde{S}\)), and incoherent sentences (e.g., L205–207, L266: “we set the target style of the synthetic images as the sum of the MSE loss”). Key details are missing or inconsistent: the definition of \(\mathcal{L}_{ce}(S)\) differs between Eq. (1)/(3) and Eq. (10); how to obtain \(\phi(x)\) in Eq. (10) is not explained; what parameters are updated by Eq. (12); how to update \(S_{k,m}\) in Algorithm 1; the difference between Figure 3(a) and 3(b) (both called “normalized domain-specific style loss”); the purpose of the output \(\phi\) from Algorithm 1. Figure 1 is not useful—it is poorly referenced and the caption is too short to decipher. Algorithm 1 is unnecessary (four nested loops and two lines of text). Figures and images have low resolution, hindering interpretation. (Reviewer 1, 4, partially Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of large-scale validation**: Only small datasets with fewer than 1000 samples are tested (VLCS, PACS, Office-H, TerraInc). DomainNet (the largest dataset in DomainBed) is omitted. The paper claims “large-scale” but these datasets are not. The authors note that DD has been scaled to ImageNet-1K, but such experiments are not provided. Validation on DomainNet scale is needed. (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Missing comparisons with existing DG methods**: The paper does not compare with standard DG methods applied after distillation (e.g., distill first, then use DG techniques). It also lacks comparison with other style manipulation DG methods (e.g., StyleMix [R2]). (Reviewer 3)
- **Dependence on domain labels**: The method explicitly uses domain labels throughout, but obtaining such labels may be impossible or expensive in real-world environments. It is unclear if the method can work without domain labels. (Reviewer 3)
- **Novelty concerns**: Using style features for domain adaptation/generalization is not novel; minimizing style discrepancies and style mixing (e.g., MixStyle) have been widely used in DG. The contribution of applying these ideas to dataset distillation is incremental. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of reference baselines in tables**: Tables 2 and 3 should show performance on the original (non-distilled) dataset as a reference to gauge the effectiveness of distillation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited generalization evaluation**: The method is only tested on a few datasets from DomainBed; broader evaluation on diverse datasets or real-world scenarios (e.g., auto-driving) is missing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reliance on distilled dataset quality**: If the initial synthetic datasets are flawed, the results may not hold. (Reviewer 2)
- **Additional complexity**: The proposed method may introduce extra complexity, limiting adoption in less technical environments. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential failure under strong domain shifts**: The approach may underperform when unseen domains differ significantly from training conditions. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unresolved questions**: Why does the method show more improvement under low IPC than higher IPC? What specific challenges were encountered in implementation and how were they addressed? In the DSM process, are the weights of the style transfer network interpolated? (Reviewer 2, 3, 4)
