## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper attempts to bridge two perspectives on generalization in deep neural networks: loss landscape flatness and representation manifold compression (e.g., neural collapse). It derives an inequality showing that flatter minima (measured by Hessian eigenvalues) imply an upper bound on the volume of neural representations during the interpolation phase, and presents experiments on VGG10/CIFAR-10 that show a correlation between compression and sharpness reduction. Reviewers are sharply divided: two (scores 6,8; high confidence) find the connection original, the paper well-written and the theory–experiment pairing convincing; two others (scores 3,3; high confidence) consider the work poorly presented, the theoretical justification flawed, the experimental evaluation limited, and the bound imprecise.

### Strengths

- The paper draws an original connection between two independently studied generalization indicators—loss landscape flatness and representation compression—and argues for a dual perspective on parameter space and feature space.
- It provides a theoretical derivation (building on Ma and Ying) that links flatness (Hessian eigenvalues) to an upper bound on representation volume, with some mathematical guarantees.
- Empirical validation on a VGG10/CIFAR-10 setup shows that, during the final training phase, flatter minima correlate with compressed representations.
- The work is relevant to the important question of why flatness helps generalization and could contribute to a unified understanding.
- Two reviewers found the paper clearly structured, easy to follow, with intuitive explanations and rigorous proofs; they praised the combination of theory and experiments (though one noted experiments are not extensive).
- The paper acknowledges its own limitations.

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and clarity:** Multiple reviewers report poor presentation. The text contains unclear passages (e.g., Frobenius norm undefined at L73; ambiguous “input weights” at L94; assumption of equal input/output dimensionality in the volume measure at L112 is not justified). The term “interpolation phase” is used for zero *loss* rather than zero *error*, conflicting with the Neural Collapse literature. Figure 1 is unclear and its caption misleading (“From left to right” does not list the train loss). Figures are placed far from their first textual reference, making back-and-forth necessary. Equation 14 is introduced at the very end of the paper but used throughout the experiments. The short mention of entropy in Section 3 is confusing and should be elaborated or removed.
- **Theoretical foundation:** The central assumption—that after reaching zero loss the derivatives are also zero—is ill‑posed because SGD would then stop. Referenced works on the “second phase” rely on label noise to sustain dynamics. The paper does not clarify which SGD dynamics model it assumes for the “second phase” and “interpolation regime.” This disconnect makes the theoretical justification weak and leaves the experiments (where the network does not achieve zero loss/gradients) unconnected to the theory.
- **Bound imprecision:** Section 4.2 shows the derived bound to be extremely imprecise, which severely diminishes its practical value.
- **Limited measure of sharpness:** Only Hessian eigenvalue magnitudes are used. Recent work (Yang et al.) suggests the Hessian trace may relate more to landscape ruggedness than to basin sharpness, and local eigenvalues may not capture broader landscape characteristics. The paper does not explore alternative sharpness measures (trace, directional curvature).
- **Insufficient experimental validation:** Only a single CNN architecture (VGG10) on CIFAR-10 is tested; reviewers request experiments with standard MLPs and on more diverse learning problems. The experiments do not fully demonstrate the correctness or tightness of the bound.
- **Missing related literature:** Several relevant works are omitted, including Ben-Shaul & Dekel (2022), Ben-Shaul et al. (2023), Rangamani et al. (2023), Elsayed et al. (2018), and Galanti et al. (2023).
- **Flatness–generalization assumption:** The paper treats flatness as a proven indicator of generalization, but this remains a hypothesis; a recent paper (arXiv:2302.07011) challenges the relationship for modern architectures.
- **Novelty attribution:** It is not clearly signified which derivations are novel contributions versus reproductions of existing results.
- **Key concepts not clarified:** How the used volume metric connects to representation collapse (neural collapse), and what the additional metric introduced in the last section is, are not adequately explained.
- **Minor issues:** Inconsistent equation referencing (“equation N” vs “Eq. (N)”); figure parentheses missing in text.

*Note on reviewer split:* Two reviewers (R2, R3) assessed the paper positively on clarity, originality, and soundness (scores 6 and 8). Two others (R1, R4) found the presentation poor, the theoretical foundation flawed, and the contribution limited (scores 3 and 3). The above weaknesses reflect the criticisms raised by the latter group, as well as constructive suggestions from the former.
