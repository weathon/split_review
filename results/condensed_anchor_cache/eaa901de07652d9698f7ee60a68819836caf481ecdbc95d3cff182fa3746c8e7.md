## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies reinforcement learning in partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs). It introduces a structured POMDP with a low-rank property that enables a linear representation (multi-step latent variable representation, µLV-Rep), and proposes planning algorithms that implement both optimism and pessimism for online and offline settings. Theoretical sample-complexity and PAC guarantees are provided. Empirically, the method is evaluated on continuous and visual control benchmarks (e.g., DeepMind Control Suite, visual domains) and compared against baselines including DreamerV2 and DrQ-v2. The paper claims state-of-the-art performance and extension to offline POMDPs, but no offline results are shown.

### Strengths

- The work addresses a fundamental challenge (partial observability) in RL and is highly relevant to the community. One reviewer rated the paper more positively (score 6) and particularly emphasized the value of extending the linear MDP framework to the POMDP setting.
- The proposed method combines efficient linear representations with L-step decodable POMDPs, which is seen as a relatively novel approach.
- The planning algorithm incorporates both optimism (for online) and pessimism (for offline) in the face of uncertainty, a principled design.
- The paper provides theoretical analysis, including sample complexity bounds and PAC guarantees. (Reviewers note that the analysis heavily builds on Ren et al., 2023a; the extent of new technical contributions is questioned.)
- Empirical results on multiple continuous and visual benchmark tasks show that the proposed algorithm outperforms existing baselines (DreamerV2, DrQ-v2, etc.) in most domains. All three reviewers found the empirical performance persuasive and strong.

### Weaknesses

1. **Theoretical novelty and reliance on prior work** – Two reviewers note that the analysis and core algorithmic structure are largely borrowed from Ren et al. (2023a). The proof of Theorem 12 is described as a “direct extension” of Ren et al., and the paper does not clearly detail the specific modifications needed for the POMDP setting or why they are non-trivial. The technical contribution is therefore considered limited.
2. **Strong assumptions** – The analysis relies on several assumptions (Finite Candidate Class with Realizability, Normalization, Regularity, Eigendecay Conditions). The paper does not discuss how performance degrades when these assumptions are violated, especially the realizability assumption (that the true latent representation lies in the chosen function class), which is rarely satisfied in practice. One reviewer asks which assumptions are essential.
3. **Vague or missing algorithmic details** – The description of how pessimism is implemented for offline RL is vague; it is unclear how the algorithm ensures safe, conservative exploration in the offline setting where no new data can be collected. The definition of latent variable \(z\) is introduced late and not properly defined before Equation (12); the objective in Equation (17) appears equivalent to a belief-based ELBO, yet the paper does not clarify the difference from standard belief representation or when \(z\) would differ from the belief state.
4. **Insufficient empirical analysis** – The evaluation is limited to comparisons with baselines; no ablation study is provided (e.g., performance without optimistic exploration, impact of the representation learning component) and no hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (e.g., different values of \(L\)). One reviewer questions whether 200K environment steps are sufficient for convergence of all algorithms. Another reviewer notes that DreamerV2 and DrQ-v2 (2021) are no longer state-of-the-art, weakening the claim of “state-of-the-art” performance; benchmarking against more recent methods is requested.
5. **Unsubstantiated claims** – The abstract and title are described as vague and overly broad. The paper claims to address “real-world” problems and “offline POMDPs,” but the evaluation is limited to standard simulation benchmarks (no real-robot experiments, no offline results). The assumption of L-step decodability is not justified for real-world applications where history dependence may be more complex and non-linear.
6. **Presentation and clarity issues** – The paper suffers from poor presentation (rated “poor” by two reviewers). Specific problems: inconsistent notation and undefined symbols (e.g., \(\theta\) in Equation (5), \(\mu\) used for both initial distribution and feature map, missing delimiters in Equations (6) and (9), mixed finite-horizon and discounted formulations, wrong type-setting of the observation function, incorrect index in actions of Equation (2), \(R(s,a)\) vs. \(r(s,a)\) in Equation (8)). Important definitions are absent or unclear, including the policy \(\mu_\pi\) and the role of parameter \(l\) after Equation (16). The theoretical analysis is condensed, stating assumptions and an informal sample complexity result without further explanation or analysis.
7. **Missing definitions and connections** – “Low-rank POMDPs” is not defined, and its connection to L-step decodability is not made explicit. It is unclear whether L-step decodability is equivalent to or subsumed by L-order MDPs. One reviewer questions how partially observable the visual benchmarks actually are, since Markovian methods have also been applied to them.
