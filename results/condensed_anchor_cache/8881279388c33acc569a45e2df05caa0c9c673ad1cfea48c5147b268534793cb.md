## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Multimodal Iterative Adaptation (MIA), an optimization-based meta-learning framework for learning implicit neural representations from multimodal data. MIA uses State Fusion Transformers (SFTs) to aggregate the parameters and gradients of independent unimodal INR learners and guide their updates through iterative cross-modal interactions. Experiments on 1D synthetic functions, CelebA (RGB + sketch), climate data, and AVMNIST (audio-visual) show improvements over unimodal baselines, especially in low-data regimes. The reviews raise significant concerns about theoretical justification, clarity of the core mechanisms, novelty relative to prior multimodal meta-learning, experimental fairness, and missing analyses (statistical tests, hyperparameter sensitivity, overfitting, computational cost).

### Strengths
- The idea of applying meta-learning to multimodal INRs with iterative adaptation is interesting; the combination of USFTs, MSFTs, and Fusion MLPs is considered creative and novel by some reviewers.
- The State Fusion Transformers (SFTs) provide a new perspective by encoding both parameters and gradients.
- The experimental evaluation is extensive, covering diverse modalities (1D synthetic, vision, climate, audio-visual) and including ablation studies.
- MIA significantly improves the performance of unimodal learners by leveraging multimodal information, particularly in data-scarce settings.
- The paper is well-structured with consistent mathematical notation and effective figures/tables.
- Ablation studies isolate the contributions of different components and offer some theoretical discussion.
- The work has potential significance for real-world multimodal applications and data-efficient learning.

### Weaknesses
- Theoretical and intuitive justification for the iterative adaptation (MIA) is missing: why it is better, how it handles limited data, and why slowing convergence leads to overfitting (claimed without evidence).
- Novelty is disputed: one reviewer finds the USFT/MSFT/Fusion MLP combination novel, but another notes prior multimodal meta-learning work (Vuorio et al. 2018, 2019; Abdollahzadeh et al. 2021; Sun et al. 2022) already explores cross-modal relationships and Transformers; the motivation that existing methods focus on unimodal setups may be overstated, and it is unclear whether other modalities are treated as noise or out-of-distribution.
- The architecture and guidance mechanism of SFTs are not clearly explained: how SFTs aggregate states and "better guide" unimodal learners is unclear from Figure 2; the composition of USFT, MSFT, and Fusion MLPs lacks a detailed breakdown; potential gradient conflicts between modalities and the rationale for the sequential fusion-then-meta-learning process are not addressed.
- The choice of L2 loss in Eq. 1 is not justified; for noisy data, L1 or Huber loss might be more suitable.
- The meta-objective (Eqs. 3–4) introduces bi-level optimization without analysis of saddle points, local minima, or convergence properties.
- Figure 2 lacks quantitative metrics (e.g., convergence rates, error reduction) to illustrate performance.
- Quantitative comparisons (Tables 1, 2, and others) lack statistical significance tests (e.g., t-tests, ANOVA) to support claims of superiority.
- Datasets used (RGB + sketch for CelebA, climate variables, AVMNIST) differ from the paper’s motivation (e.g., images and text); they rely on different features from a uniform data format rather than truly distinct modalities.
- Choice of AVMNIST is not sufficiently justified; deeper analysis of why MTNPs fail on audio signals is missing.
- In Section 5.2, ALFA outperforms MIA when sufficient support sets are present; the paper does not discuss whether this is a limitation of MIA or an artifact of the dataset.
- The practical significance of MSE improvements on CelebA (e.g., visible reconstruction quality) is not discussed.
- Use of a pre-trained model to obtain surface normals (Section 5.2) is not ablated or analyzed for its effect on generalizability.
- Audio signal trimming (Section 5.4) is not explained regarding its impact on meta-learning or necessity.
- For climate data (Section 5.3), the claim that atmospheric variables are "relatively stable" lacks support, and no analysis of how prediction errors could propagate in real-world applications is provided.
- Ablation studies (Section 5.5) are shallow: they do not isolate the contribution of each module (USFT, MSFT, Fusion MLP) to overfitting or convergence speed; the metric "relative error reduction" is used without justification.
- Lack of interpretability discussion for the complex SFT architecture, important for real-world deployment.
- No hyperparameter sensitivity analysis across experiments, making it difficult to assess robustness.
- Reproducibility is hindered by the absence of a clear list of hyperparameters.
- Risk of overfitting given the high model complexity (USFTs, MSFTs, Fusion MLPs); no empirical or analytical evidence of robustness is provided.
- Missing evidence for the claim of "facilitating rapid convergence" — no convergence curves or related experiments.
- It is unclear whether the performance improvement over unimodal baselines is fair given that MIA uses additional multimodal information.
- Table 5 does not explain why MLPs are not used in certain settings, especially when they achieve better results on CelebA.
- The computational cost of SFTs relative to the performance gain is not addressed; it is unclear if the improvement justifies the extra complexity.
