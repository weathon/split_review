## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents TimeMixer++ (TM++), a general-purpose time series pattern machine (TSPM) for forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, and imputation. It processes multi-scale time series via (1) Multi-Resolution Time Imaging (MRTI), (2) Time Image Decomposition (TID) with dual-axis attention, (3) Multi-Scale Mixing (MCM), and (4) Multi-Resolution Mixing (MRM). Three reviewers note the model is thorough; one finds the performance gains negligible and the theoretical insight lacking.

### Strengths
- **Innovative architecture**: Multi-resolution time imaging, dual-axis attention, and both multi-scale and multi-resolution mixing are novel and interesting. The model captures short- and long-term dependencies and frequency-domain patterns, validated by ablation.
- **Strong empirical validation**: Outperforms SOTA on eight benchmark tasks (long/short forecasting, zero-shot forecasting, classification, anomaly detection). Hyperparameter ablation is thorough. Reviewers 1 and 2 affirm SOTA results; Reviewer 3 agrees the evaluation is comprehensive.
- **Flexibility across tasks**: The hierarchical mixing adapts to diverse tasks (forecasting, anomaly detection, imputation, classification), promoting robust performance.
- **Presentation**: Manuscript and appendix are well-prepared (Reviewer 2). Methods such as time imaging and image decomposition are interesting (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer 1 and 2 are positive**; Reviewer 3 is more critical of the performance gain magnitude and theoretical depth (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Negligible forecasting gains**: In Tables 3 and 4, the performance improvement is minor. One reviewer (R3) argues that such marginal gains could be due to parameter tuning (well-tuned for TM++, weak settings for competitors). Others do not comment on this.
- **Lack of theoretical/experimental insight**: The paper offers mostly intuition and model blending; there is no theoretical understanding of why TM++ works or how time imaging and multi-resolution mixing improve results (R3).
- **Missed comparisons and analysis**:
  - No comparison with recent models like **TimesNet** and **iTransformer** beyond what is in the tables; more detail on specific tasks is needed (R2).
  - No discussion of **computational efficiency** (FLOPs, memory usage, inference-time cost). Reviewer 1 asks whether inference cost has been studied; Reviewer 2 asks for such analysis.
  - No ablation comparing the **benefit of multi-resolution time imaging** over existing frequency-based methods (e.g., [1][2][3]) that capture periodic patterns; these papers should be cited (R3).
  - No exploration of **scaling model size**; zero-shot experiments are only in-domain, not cross-task (R1).
  - Reviewer 3 asks: What is the naming connection to **TimeMixer**? What is the specific benefit over TimeMixer? The paper does not clearly explain this.
- **Code and reproducibility**: The source code has not been released. Reviewer 2 states they would raise the score if code is released and results are reproducible. Reviewer 1 does not mention code.
- **Figure presentation**: In Figure 1 (right), the label “Benchmarking model performance across representation analysis in four tasks” is blurred; fonts are too small. Consider a single legend for all four tasks (R2).
- **Open questions treated as missing experiments** (overlap with criticisms):
  - Has the model’s inference-time cost been compared to other methods? Could a task-specific routing model lower cost? (R1)
  - Has the approach of using MLPs (as in MTS-Mixer) been explored for channel decomposition instead of attention? (R1)
