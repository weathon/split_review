## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies how activation functions affect the training dynamics of Neural ODEs, focusing on two regimes: "optimize-then-discretize" and "discretize-then-optimize." The authors prove that smooth (Lipschitz-continuous) activations with Lipschitz derivatives guarantee well-posedness and unique solutions for both forward and backward ODEs, and that sufficient nonlinearity (non-polynomial) preserves the spectral properties of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK). They further show that, under these conditions, gradient descent in the overparameterized regime achieves global convergence. The theoretical results cover well-posedness (Propositions 1–2), convergence of infinite-depth, infinite-width limits (Propositions 3–4, Theorems 1–2), positive definiteness of limiting kernels (Lemma 3, Proposition 5, Corollary 1), and explicit convergence rates (Theorem 3). Numerical experiments validate the theoretical claims.

### Strengths

- Precise, rigorous theoretical results: the paper proves well-posedness in both "optimize-then-discretize" and "discretize-then-optimize" settings under Lipschitz continuity of activations and their derivatives, with explicit convergence rates for the Euler discretization (Propositions 1–2).

- Extends fundamental NTK results to Neural ODEs using classical ODE analytical tools such as Picard’s theorem and the Groenewald theorem (Propositions 3–4, Theorems 1–2). The analysis and results are novel for Neural ODEs.

- Provides a global convergence proof for Neural ODEs under gradient descent in the overparameterized regime, with conditions on activation nonlinearity and non-polynomial nature (Theorem 3). This gives a strong foundation for understanding optimization behavior.

- Empirical experiments support the theoretical findings: convergence of Neural ODE outputs and gradients to finite-depth ResNet approximations, NTK behavior, and convergence under gradient descent.

- The paper is well-written, clear, and presents a novel mathematical framework for studying continuous models from an approximation theory perspective.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing dedicated experiment for "discretize-then-optimize" convergence issue**: The paper theoretically predicts convergence problems when using non-smooth activations (e.g., ReLU) with "discretize-then-optimize," but does not provide a numerical demonstration of this issue. Without empirical evidence, the practical implications remain unsubstantiated.

- **Title mismatch**: The title emphasizes "the impact of activation functions," but most results treat activation regularity as an assumption for theorem-proving rather than empirically comparing activation functions. Two reviewers independently note that the title should better reflect the paper’s main theoretical contributions on global convergence and NTK analysis—or present more activation-comparison experiments.

- **Missing discussion of related NTK literature**: The paper does not reference or compare with existing NTK extensions to CNNs (commonly used in image-based Neural ODEs), Transformers (relevant for time-series), or physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that also involve solving differential equations. This context would strengthen the paper’s positioning.

- **Lack of SGD analysis**: The convergence analysis only covers full-batch gradient descent, while SGD is more common in practice. The paper should discuss how results might extend to SGD, including the impact of mini-batch size and noise on convergence behavior, or identify challenges for such an extension.

- **Results specific to a special ODE form**: Theorem 2 and the analysis assume the ODE of the form in Equation (2). The paper does not discuss how the results would apply to more general ODEs or PDEs (e.g., non-autonomous dynamics, higher-order derivatives, nonlinear PDEs). The limitations of the current analysis for complex systems are not addressed.

- **Non-polynomial activation necessity may be overstated**: The paper claims nonlinear, non-polynomial activations are crucial for convergence (Theorem 3), but it may be possible for polynomial activations to also yield convergence if Condition (1) of Assumption 1 is strengthened to ensure linear independence. A more rigorous justification or acknowledgment of this possibility is needed.

- **Minor issues**: 
  - Typo in Equation (1): the index of the last h should likely be T instead of t.
  - Figure 1 panels (a,b,c) should be presented in log-log scale to better appreciate the slope comparison with 1/L.
  - Proofs are in the appendix, which sometimes interrupts the main narrative flow.
  - The paper does not explore how the choice of numerical ODE solver in "optimize-then-discretize" affects gradient computation accuracy and training dynamics.
  - No concrete guidance is given for practitioners on selecting activation functions based on the trade-off between theoretical guarantees and empirical performance.
