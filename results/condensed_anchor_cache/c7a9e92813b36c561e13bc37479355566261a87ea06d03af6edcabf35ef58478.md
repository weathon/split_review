## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MuseD, an automated method for generating synthetic multi-step deductive reasoning data (syllogisms) with controllable complexity. The data is used for post-training LLMs via RLHF (PPO) with step-level reward signals. Experiments on Llama3-8B show improvements on in-domain and out-of-domain reasoning tasks (PrOntoQA, ProofWriter, FOLIO, LogicalDeduction, AR-LSAT) compared to baselines, including RLHF on Ultrafeedback. The method also serves as an evaluation benchmark.

### Strengths
- Good premise: generating high-quality synthetic reasoning data at scale is increasingly important.
- Explores post-training (RLHF) for reasoning, which is less explored than fine-tuning approaches.
- Step-level feedback for reinforcement learning is a reasonable approach for tasks where sequential reasoning steps matter.
- Incorporating reward signals from domains outside of math is exciting. Dense positive rewards for steps have a positive impact on the aligned LM.
- Positive results with Llama3-8B, with comparisons to RLHF on Ultrafeedback. Improvements are shown on several logical reasoning tasks.
- Thorough ablation study on output formats (natural language, JSON) and different scoring compositions (step score, negative score, result-only score) — noted by the minority Reviewer 2 (score 5) as a strength; other reviewers found the ablation missing but this positive contrast is preserved.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation (majority view, strongly negative):** The paper is difficult to read/understand; grammar mistakes obscure discussions. No figure explaining tree construction or question/reward structure. Too many models in Table 1 with confusing subscript naming; they should be separated and introduced clearly. Section 3 is redundant (introduces A/E/I/O notation that is never referenced again). The explanation of MuseD generation (Section 4) is hard to follow and lacks a single concrete example (even in appendix). The automated labeling method tailored to syllogism limits applicability to other tasks. Handling of incorrect steps is crude; penalizing them may degrade performance, and a nuanced discussion (e.g., whether unfruitful steps are necessary exploration) is missing. No error analysis or discussion of why models get MuseD questions wrong (e.g., o1 only 89%, surprising for simple syllogisms). Open-source review questions: website referenced in footnote is unavailable; authors should list the 15 formats in appendix.
- **Limited benchmarks and baselines (multiple reviewers):** Only Llama3-8B is used for post-training experiments. No comparisons with other fine-tuned small models (e.g., ProofWriter) that already perform well on formal reasoning. MuseD’s results are not compared to GPT-model fine-tuning, raising doubt about effectiveness on stronger LLMs. OOD datasets (PrOntoQA, ProofWriter, LogicalDeduction) are similar to in-domain syllogisms; FOLIO also includes syllogisms. Testing on a broader range of reasoning tasks is needed. From Table 3, LLMs already perform well on MuseD, questioning whether multi-step reasoning is a particular challenge.
- **Missing baselines and justification for PPO (Reviewers 1, 3):** No SFT baseline to show that PPO is necessary; simpler alternatives like DPO or SFT should be tried. Reward model training variations appear ad hoc; using an explicit stepwise ground-truth score (since the dataset is synthetic) could avoid training a separate reward model altogether. Questions asking why RLHF was chosen overlap with this missing justification.
- **Novelty concerns (Reviewers 2, 3):** Step-level feedback is not novel; it has been introduced by prior work (Lightman et al. ’23, Wang et al. ’24, Ma et al. ’23). The automated labeling specifically for syllogistic reasoning is trivial because correct steps are easily identified in a constrained formal setting. Constructing formal logic datasets is not innovative; the generated problems look overly simple and similar to ProofWriter’s structure.
- **OOD degradation and robustness (Reviewer 2):** PPO fine-tuning degrades performance on AR-LSAT, which involves a different logical paradigm; this suggests sensitivity to data shift.
- **Missing details and unanswered questions:** Rules of inference used in MuseD are not specified. Compute cost of Table 1 experiments is not given. How natural language steps are verified for correctness (Section 4.2) is not explained. Does the dataset include conclusions that cannot be determined (i.e., neither the conclusion nor its reversal is derivable)? Average tree sizes should be reported. Performance across question types (A, E, I, O) is not shown.
