## Merged Review

### Summary
HeurAgenix is a multi-agent LLM-based framework for generating, evolving, evaluating, and selecting heuristics for combinatorial optimization problems. The framework comprises heuristic generation, evolution, benchmark evaluation, and heuristic selection agents, utilizing LLMs for knowledge synthesis and adaptive decision-making. It is evaluated on classical problems (TSP, CVRP, JSSP, MaxCut, MKP) and one novel problem (DPOSP) using GPT-4, compared against LLM-based baselines like EoH and ReEvo. All reviewers acknowledge the research direction as interesting and the approach novel, but they raise serious concerns about reproducibility, missing ablation studies, limited evaluation scope, overclaimed performance, insufficient experimental details, and presentation quality. Two reviewers (R2, R3) rate the paper 5, emphasizing its promising results and novel components, while three reviewers (R1, R4, R5) rate it 3, pointing to critical shortcomings.

### Strengths
- The research direction of using LLMs to create or adapt heuristics for combinatorial optimization is innovative and timely (R1, R3). The multi-agent framework is principled and preferred over prior manual LLM+EA designs (R3).
- The method is unique among concurrent work (R1) and is the first to combine the multi-agent concept with LLM-based heuristic generation (R5).
- The introduction of new components—benchmark evaluation and heuristic selection—enhances both performance and efficiency (R2). The efforts to extract knowledge from academic papers and related problems are interesting (R2).
- The framework dynamically chooses the most appropriate heuristics for different COP instances (R4).
- Results are demonstrated across a variety of combinatorial optimization problems and are promising when compared to existing LLM-based hyper-heuristics like EoH and ReEvo (R2, R3, R5). The inclusion of a novel problem (DPOSP) ensures meaningful evaluation (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Reproducibility and clarity**: The paper does not provide enough detail to make the method fully reproducible; descriptions are high-level and purely verbal, lacking essential mathematical definitions and formulas for key steps (e.g., conditions for single vs. multiple rounds of evolution) (R1, R4). The methodology involves substantial engineering and manual prompt design, which limits novelty (R5). Several components appear case-specific rather than principled: It is unclear how LLMs learn from reference papers or transfer knowledge from related problems, whether the involved knowledge sources and instructions are manually provided (R2, R3), and how the agent knows transferability between DPOSP and CVRP (R3). In single-round evolution, the perturbation heuristic may need to be manually designed in advance (R3).
- **Missing ablation studies**: Multiple reviewers strongly criticize the lack of ablation experiments (R1, R2, R3, R5). Without ablations, it is unclear which components (e.g., heuristic generation from papers, benchmark evaluation, selection) contribute most to performance, which hyperparameters are important, and how each component affects robustness and generalization. Ablations are necessary to clarify which components require problem-specific adjustments and to what extent they influence results (R2).
- **Limited evaluation and overclaimed performance**:
  - The evaluation covers only six problems and only one truly new problem, making the paper feel like an early case study. Testing on a wider variety (e.g., 10–20 problems) would better support the claim of easy adaptability (R1).
  - All experiments use exclusively GPT-4; the robustness of HeurAgenix when applied to other LLMs (e.g., Llama3-70b) is not evaluated (R4, R5).
  - The claim of “significantly outperforming state-of-the-art approaches” is too strong: comparisons are only against other LLM-based methods, not traditional operations research or deep learning hyper-heuristic approaches (R1). Moreover, the comparison to LLM methods is only shown on TSP, and the results for EoH and ReEvo are included in Figure 8 but are missing from Figure 6, causing inconsistency (R4). One reviewer notes that in Figure 9, `GLS+EoH` performs significantly better than `GLS+Ours`, undermining the claimed advantage (R5).
  - Important experimental details are omitted: the number of independent runs, whether the same initial heuristics are used for all methods, how fairness is ensured (e.g., same number of heuristic evaluations) (R2, R3), and the definition of the “cheapest insertion method” baseline (R4).
  - Comparisons to deep learning-based hyper-heuristics for heuristic selection (e.g., R4, citing [3,4]) are absent, which would demonstrate effectiveness beyond LLM baselines (R4).
  - It is unclear how many reference papers are utilized for generating initial heuristics in each task, how related problems are determined (whether domain knowledge is required), and how the method adapts when no related papers or existing heuristics are available (R2).
  - The performance on the online bin packing problem (tested by FunSearch and EoH) is not reported (R2).
- **Methodological and efficiency concerns**:
  - Generating features and selecting heuristics at every solution step leads to substantial latency; solution efficiency (runtime, API costs) and the number of LLM queries per component are left undiscussed (R1, R2, R3). These are clear limitations of the agentic framework (R3).
  - The characterization of LLM-generated incorrect heuristics as “hallucination” is imprecise and should be replaced with a more accurate description of the specific issues (R4).
  - The paper states that existing methods “rely heavily on existing approaches” but then acknowledges its own dependence on existing heuristics for generation; the key innovations over prior LLM-based methods need explicit articulation (R4).
- **Presentation and formatting**: The paper contains grammatical errors, incoherent sentences, and undefined terms (R4). Figures are visually unappealing, vector graphics are preferred (R3, R5), and there are formatting errors such as missing spaces (e.g., “Figure3”, “AppendixG.2”) (R3). Page 6 is entirely filled with figures (R5).
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: Two reviewers (R2, R3) rate the paper 5 and highlight its promising results and novel components, while three reviewers (R1, R4, R5) rate it 3 and emphasize significant shortcomings in reproducibility, evaluation, and novelty. The majority points to critical weaknesses that need to be addressed, particularly the lack of ablation studies, limited problem scope, and insufficient experimental rigor.
