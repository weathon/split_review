## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates whether training on mathematical problem-solving (MPS) enhances LLMs' general reasoning capabilities. It tests three training paradigms: (1) continual pretraining on mathematical text (RhO-Math, DeepSeekMath), (2) instruction pretraining on diverse QA pairs (MammoTH2), and (3) instruction tuning on MPS datasets (Math-COT SFT, Math-POT SFT). Both two-stage (math training then UltraChat) and mix-data training are considered. Evaluations cover seven reasoning domains. The key finding is that only continual pretraining on raw mathematical text yields meaningful generalization to some non-math tasks, while other methods primarily improve MPS-specific performance. The paper concludes that most current math data sources do not support the “math for AI” objective and that identifying better data sources remains an open question.

### Strengths

- Clear writing; easy to follow.
- Interesting and important question: whether math training can improve general reasoning (timely given the scarcity of complex chain-of-thought data).
- Thorough experimental framework comparing three distinct training strategies across multiple reasoning domains.
- Exhaustive benchmarking with many reasoning datasets; claims are well-supported.
- The conclusion that only continual pretraining on MPS data is useful for general reasoning tasks is valuable for practitioners.
- Experiments are well-designed and abundant.
- Novel perspective: distinguishes “AI for math” from “math for AI,” providing a new evaluation viewpoint.
- Aligns with AI development goals and can improve data utilization efficiency.
- Also examined training on other non-mathematical step-by-step datasets (a pilot study showing limited gains).

### Weaknesses

- Reviewer opinions diverge: one reviewer found the topic novel and the experimental framework thorough (rating 6), while two reviewers considered the work lacking in novelty and analysis depth (ratings 3), and a third gave a moderate score (rating 5) also noting correlation-focused analysis and limited models.
- **Limited model diversity:** Only two models tested (Mistral-7B and DeepSeek-Coder). Findings may not be model agnostic; models with varying reasoning capabilities could behave differently.
- **Narrow training approaches:** Only continual pretraining and fine-tuning are explored. Reinforcement learning methods (e.g., GPT-o1-preview) that have demonstrated improved reasoning are not considered.
- **Lacks causal analysis:** Paper focuses on correlations, not causations. No deep analysis explaining why only continual pretraining works, why some instruction pretraining variants work while others do not, and why all instruction tuning fails. No mechanistic interpretability perspective.
- **Insufficient analysis of results:** For example, for instruction tuning on MPS (Table 1), why does COT outperform POT? Why does the mixed method perform much better than the two-stage method? No specific examples demonstrating how training improves or degrades different reasoning abilities.
- **Problematic categorization of non-mathematical domains:** STEM reasoning should not be a distinct category; it interweaves mathematical reasoning, logical reasoning, and domain-specific STEM knowledge. In particular, MMLU-STEM is heavily intertwined with mathematical computation (physics/finance problems requiring equation solving), so performance improvements here may reflect mathematical ability rather than generalization.
- **Main claim possibly overstated:** The paper claims “continual pretraining generally improves non-math reasoning” based on improvements in 3 out of 5 domains. If STEM reasoning is excluded, the claim becomes questionable. Even including STEM, the significant degradation in commonsense and agent reasoning tasks weakens the claim of “significant” generalization.
- **Limited technical contribution:** No universal framework is proposed for evaluating general reasoning in the “math for AI” context. The section “WHAT OTHER DATA SOURCES CONTRIBUTE TO REASONING” does not provide effective methodologies or actionable insights for constructing better datasets.
- **Potential data leakage:** Task classification relies solely on input-output format; no deeper similarity detection or filtering is performed. This could lead to overlap between MPS and general reasoning datasets, causing misleading conclusions about reasoning skill transfer.
- **Missing analysis:** What data would truly improve general reasoning? How could such generalized reasoning capability be better assessed automatically? (Reviewer asks for ablation-style suggestions.)
