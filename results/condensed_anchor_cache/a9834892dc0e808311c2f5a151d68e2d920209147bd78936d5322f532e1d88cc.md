## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the detection of machine-generated texts (MGTs) using maximum mean discrepancy (MMD). The authors argue that directly applying vanilla MMD incurs increased variance when MGTs come from multiple LLMs (multiple populations), so they propose MMD-MP, a multi-population aware optimization that drops the comparison term between machine-generated samples from different sources. They develop paragraph-based and sentence-based detection methods. Experiments on a corpus of ~3,100 paragraphs, evaluated via hypothesis test power and sentence-level classification, show the proposed method outperforms baselines across several LLMs (GPT-2, GPT-3, ChatGPT, GPT-Neo). The paper provides mathematical justification for the modification and analyzes asymptotics and test power.

### Strengths
- Attacks a crucial and contemporary problem (MGT detection) with a well motivated approach (MMD for subtle distributional discrepancies), especially valuable for short sentences (R1, R3).
- Technically sophisticated; provides extensive theoretical justification, including asymptotics, test power analysis, and statistical properties (Propositions, Corollaries) (R1, R2, R4).
- Results on the tested settings are very clearly positive – MMD-MP outperforms baselines (R1, R2).
- Innovative introduction of MMD-MP to handle variance from multiple LLM populations, supported by a clear mathematical formulation and optimization reasoning (R2, R4).
- Comprehensive experimental design: evaluates detection of text from unseen LLMs, both short and long text, and investigates impact of training data size (R3).
- Method is supported by both theory and experiments (R3).
- Empirical evidence across diverse LLMs (GPT-2, GPT-3, ChatGPT, GPT-Neo) and settings (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited to variation from LLMs, ignoring genre/style/author variation.** The paper only varies the LLM source, but uses a single small corpus without considering diverse human authors, genres, or writing styles – a significant real-world factor (R1).
- **Small corpus and potential overfitting.** Only 3,100 paragraphs used, with cross-validation; baselines (e.g., ChatGPT-D) trained on much larger external data are at a disadvantage. Single-sentence detection is also less realistic than paragraph-level (R1). Furthermore, only the first 20 prompts were used for generating MGT, while evaluation uses 100 paragraphs/1,000 sentences for testing and 3,100 for training – the rationale is unclear and may introduce bias (R2).
- **Missing SOTA baseline.** A recent strong baseline for MGT detection (reference [1]) that shows impressive performance on short texts is not discussed or compared (R3).
- **Computational cost unaddressed.** The loss is quadratic in sample size; no approximation or complexity analysis provided (R1). Lacks detailed cost comparison with simpler baselines (R3).
- **Method complexity and accessibility.** The heavy mathematical formulations can be intimidating and limit accessibility to non-experts (R1, R4). The main contribution is a small, intuitive modification (dropping a term); the paper would be stronger with more upfront intuition (R1). The algorithms are tightly coupled to MGT detection and may not generalize to other domains (R4).
- **Missing experimental details.** Omitted: corpus language; architecture/scale of neural models and whether same for all baselines; which baselines train on provided corpus vs. external data; kernel hyperparameters; test power comparison derivation (R1). Decoding method parameters and sampling method not specified (R2). Sensitivity of MMD to kernel bandwidth not discussed (R3).
- **Test power metric not straightforward.** The definition of test power in Section 2 is harder to interpret; easier explanation needed. (R2)
- **Unexpected easier detection of ChatGPT.** Tables 1–4 show ChatGPT (a better model) yields the highest detection accuracy, but one would expect it to be harder to detect. No explanation is given (R1).
- **Variance calculation still tied to kernel distance; potential instability.** The variance \(\sigma^2_{s} = 4(E[H_{i3}^2] - E[H_{i2}^2])\) relies on expectations of squared kernel differences, making it sensitive to kernel choice and parameters, with no explicit handling of adversarial examples – could undermine robustness in diverse real-world samples (R4).
- **Contrast: More positive minority reviewer.** One reviewer (score 8) is more positive, finding the method clear and effective, but still flags prompt usage and complexity. Another reviewer (score 6) acknowledges innovation while raising robustness and generalizability concerns.
- **Unsolved questions about LLM diversity.** How does including a more diverse or less capable set of LLMs affect performance? Could treating an entire paragraph as a single sentence for detection be feasible? These should be discussed (R3).
