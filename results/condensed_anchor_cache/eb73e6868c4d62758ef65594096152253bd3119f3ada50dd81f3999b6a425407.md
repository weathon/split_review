## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MarineMaid, a dataset for marine visual understanding with 12,873 fine-grained instance-caption pairs and 42,217 bounding boxes covering 670 marine categories under a hierarchical taxonomy. It supports object detection, open-vocabulary detection, visual grounding, and instance captioning. The authors benchmark 14 state-of-the-art algorithms across these tasks. The dataset was constructed using automatic labeling (including SAM) and human curation. However, key details of the process and evaluation are contested.

### Strengths
1. Rich annotations: The dataset provides detailed instance-level captions describing biological traits alongside bounding boxes, offering an advantage over existing marine datasets. It includes 670 categories across 6 hierarchical granularities (R1, R2, R4).
2. Comprehensive benchmarking: The authors evaluate 14 models across multiple tasks (close-set and open-vocabulary detection, visual grounding, instance captioning), providing a broad assessment of current algorithms in the marine domain (R1, R2, R4).
3. Clear motivation and presentation: The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a sound motivation for a domain-specific dataset (R2, R4). Reviewer 2 additionally noted that the labeling process involves both auto-labeling and human curation to ensure quality (R2).
4. Potential value for the community: The dataset can be a useful resource for underwater computer vision research (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. Writing quality issues: The paper contains problematic statements that undermine credibility. For example, it claims “average word length is 42” (likely sentence length) and asserts it is “the first marine dataset” despite contradicting its own references. Several reviewers noted the presentation falls below the threshold of acceptability (R1, R3).
2. Unclear and insufficiently described labeling pipeline: The annotation process relies heavily on SAM and generative AI models, but the description is vague and confusing. Fig. 2 indicates that “prompts” were given to SAM for bounding box generation, yet this is not explained in the main text. The pipeline lacks specifics on prompt design, quality control, and how many and what kind of domain experts were involved, as well as per-expert effort (R1, R2, R3). This raises concerns about the quality and reliability of the annotations, especially for small or hard-to-detect fish groups (R2, R3).
3. Limited contribution beyond dataset creation: The paper does not propose any new algorithm or provide deep analysis. The experimental section is straightforward and mostly reports baseline performances without novel insights. Reviewers expected either improved algorithms or more insightful analysis of the failure cases (e.g., why image-level VLMs produce short captions, why grounding algorithms perform poorly) (R1, R3, R4). The contribution is therefore limited (R1, R4).
4. Evaluation metrics and comparisons: Only mAP50 is reported for detection, which is insufficient. More comprehensive metrics are needed (R4). Additionally, the comparison in Table 2 where DECOLA significantly outperforms others raises fairness concerns: it is unclear whether all methods use the same training sets and what pre-trained datasets were used. Without this transparency, the benchmark’s validity is questionable (R4). Also, the paper does not verify whether multi-task joint pre-training improves performance, given that the dataset supports multiple tasks (R1).
5. Dataset size and novelty trade-off: While MarineMaid offers richer captions and taxonomy, it contains fewer instances than some existing large-scale marine datasets (Table 1). A suggested alternative is to extend existing datasets with captions rather than creating a smaller new dataset (R4). The paper also lacks a complete literature review of existing marine datasets; Table 1 may be missing some, which is critical since no new algorithm is presented and the paper should serve a survey role (R1).
6. Minority negative view: One reviewer (R3) considered the paper’s English and presentation well below the acceptance threshold and deemed the experimental section not useful, questioning whether annotation quality translates to downstream performance benefits. This contrasts with other reviewers who found the paper clear and the dataset potentially valuable, but the severity of the criticism (score 1) should be noted.
