## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Chain-of-Jailbreak (CoJ) attack, which bypasses safeguards in text-based image generation models (GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Gemini 1.5 Pro) by decomposing malicious queries into harmless sub-queries and iteratively editing generated images. The attack achieves ~60% success on a new benchmark (CoJ-Bench, spanning nine safety scenarios, three edit operations, three edit elements). The authors also propose a defense, "Think Twice Prompting," which stops 95% of CoJ attacks. Reviewer 1 (score 6) finds the methodology innovative and the evaluation comprehensive; reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 5) are more critical, citing limited scope, missing baselines, and insufficient explanation of the defense and threat model.

### Strengths
- **Novel and effective attack method** – CoJ decomposes malicious prompts into multiple editing steps, bypassing safeguards that block single unsafe prompts. It outperforms existing jailbreaking methods (60% vs. 14% success) on four widely-used services (GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5, Gemini 1.5 Pro).  
- **Systematic decomposition** – The edit operations (Insertion-then-Delete, etc.) and edit elements (object, background, text) are structured, not just token splitting, giving diversity to the attack.  
- **Comprehensive benchmark (CoJ-Bench)** – Covers a wide range of safety scenarios with detailed sub-group divisions, enhancing reproducibility and evaluation standards.  
- **Proposed defense** – "Think Twice Prompting" achieves a 95% defense rate, demonstrating a practical countermeasure.  
- **Important topic** – Safety of text-to-image models is a significant and timely concern.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope limited to harmful words** – The method only generates images containing harmful text (words); this restriction is not clear from the title or abstract.  
- **Missing comparison with simpler alternatives** – More straightforward approaches (e.g., directly merging harmful words into an image) may achieve the same goal without needing a text-to-image model or the proposed attack. The paper does not compare CoJ against any baseline methods for generating such images, nor does it justify why an attacker must use a text-to-image model.  
- **Defense method under-specified and not robust** – "Think Twice Prompting" lacks implementation details: exact phrasing, whether it is prepended/appended/integrated, and how it interacts with model internals. It appears overly simplistic and may be trivially bypassed by slightly more sophisticated attacks; no robustness analysis against attack variations is provided.  
- **No clear threat model** – The paper does not specify realistic deployment scenarios or how such attacks could be used to cause real harm (e.g., propagation to others). Without this, the practical significance is unclear.  
- **Insufficient background on existing jailbreaking methods** – Traditional attacks for text-to-image models are not cited or explained in the background section, making it hard to gauge novelty and contribution.  
- **Method robustness concerns** – The decomposition relies on manually crafted seed queries (five) and an LLM (Mistral-Large-2) to generalize. It is unclear how reliably the LLM follows the defined Edit Operations and Edit Elements across queries, whether optimal decomposition (e.g., ideal number of sub-queries) can be consistently found, and whether smaller/cheaper models could achieve similar performance (relevant for resource-limited attackers).  
- **Questions on evaluation details** – In the automatic evaluation, it is unclear why the LLM (GPT-4) responding "No" indicates no refusal, and what the purpose of observing the LLM's response is. Also, the reason why "Insertion-then-Delete" is the most effective edit operation is not explained.

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (score 6) praises the innovative methodology and comprehensive evaluation, while reviewers 2 and 3 (score 5) consider the scope too limited, the baseline comparison absent, and the defense explanation insufficient, lowering overall soundness and contribution.
