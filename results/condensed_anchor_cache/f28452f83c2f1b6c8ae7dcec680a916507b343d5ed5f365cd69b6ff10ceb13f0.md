## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a modified natural gradient descent (NGD) method that clips singular values of the Fisher information matrix based on a criterion derived to reduce generalization error. The analysis assumes the NTK regime, Gaussian data likelihood, and infinite width, yielding explicit dynamics and a generalization bound. Experiments on synthetic data show improved out-of-distribution performance over basic NGD. Three reviewers rate the paper 3 (fair), citing limited scope, insufficient novelty, and weak theoretical foundations; one reviewer (score 5) finds the idea interesting and clearly presented but also notes restrictive assumptions and lack of strong experimental validation.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly defines its contributions (R1).
- The connection between natural gradient and NTK to improve generalization is novel and less well-known (R2).
- The result that modified NGD can improve generalization is appreciated (R2).
- Experiments demonstrate very good performance compared to basic NGD, especially under distribution shift (R3).
- The paper is written clearly, ideas are in proper order, and the proposed modification of Fisher eigenvalues is interesting and may inspire future optimization algorithms (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope and restrictive assumptions**: The analysis relies on the NTK regime (constant Jacobian), Gaussian likelihood, and infinite width. Under NTK the dynamics are linear and analytically easy; it is unclear whether the improvement holds for practical neural networks that do not behave like kernels (R1, R2, R3). The Gaussian assumption is restrictive and may not hold for classification tasks (R4). The overparameterization requirement is also restrictive (R4).
- **Role of NGD and novelty**: The final predictor simply corresponds to a complete fit of training residual projected on selected eigenspaces; other methods (e.g., projecting residual then training GD) would produce the same predictor, raising questions about the actual role of NGD (R3). The optimization result (Theorem 1) is based on existing work [32], limiting novelty (R4). The idea is essentially applying well-established kernel methods to NTK (R2). Modifying the gradient to change the functional gradient is not a unique contribution (R1).
- **Theoretical concerns**:
  - No proof outlines are provided (R1).
  - Equation (24) seems impossible because the left-hand side can have only N non-zero entries while the right-hand side presumably has all entries non-zero; this reflects a broader issue of learning population quantities from finite data (R3).
  - Theorem 2 minimizes an upper bound (eq. 54) on the generalization error, not the true error; the bound itself is not discussed in the main text, and its derivation (e.g., transition from first to second line) appears to miss a term from components orthogonal to non-zero singular values (R3).
  - Criterion (23) in Theorem 2 depends on the true data distribution; if one uses empirical Fisher and gradient, the condition reduces to no modification (by eq. 26), essentially returning to standard NGD (R4).
  - It is unclear whether criterion (23) is applied only at initialization or during training (R4).
  - The justification that eq. (55) minimizes the bound is not fully explained (R4).
  - Assumptions 2 and 3 are similar; the distinction is not clearly motivated (R3).
  - The proof of Theorem 2 assumes deterministic outputs given x (eq. 42), but this is not stated in the main text (R3).
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - Only comparison to NGD is provided; comparison to standard GD is missing (R2).
  - No baseline modification method for Fisher is included to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed criterion (R3).
  - Experiments use realistic finite networks with evolving Jacobian (feature learning), contradicting the NTK assumption; the impact of regularly updating Fisher is not clearly addressed (R3).
  - In Figure 5, modified and basic NGD start from different accuracy values; they should start from the same point (R3).
  - Figures 1 and 3a show very little dynamics; extending the plots would be informative (R3).
  - Only synthetic data experiments are conducted; image classification tasks (e.g., CIFAR-10/100) would strengthen the results (R4).
- **Applicability of natural gradient**: The natural gradient is derived under KL divergence, but the paper uses L2 loss; the reason for its applicability is not explained (R2).
- **Presentation issues**: Many typos (e.g., “wights”) and grammatical errors (R1). Equation (26) last step incorrectly writes “1/2 > 1” (should be “1 > 1/2”) (R1, R4). Left-hand side of eq. (41) should be $f_{\theta_\infty}(x)$ (R4). A factor $1/N$ is missing in eq. (35), leading to an unnatural $N$ factor in the exponent of eq. (20) (R3). Predictor $f$ depends on output $y$ in the definition of the Fisher matrix (just below eq. 5) (R3). Theorem 1 states the gradient flow solution but eq. (8) describes gradient descent; this should be clarified (R3). Figure captions could be more informative about the inferences (R1).
- **Missing intuition**: The paper lacks intuition for how the modifications improve out-of-distribution generalization (R1).
