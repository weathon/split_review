## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes QO-DETR, a DETR-based detector for small objects in remote sensing images, with three modules: Query Proposal Generation (QPG), Group Denoising (GD), and Query Cascade Refinement (QCR). Experiments are conducted on DIOR and AI-TOD. Reviewers unanimously raise major concerns about novelty, with two reviewers (scores 1,1) alleging plagiarism from prior works (DN-DETR, DINO, DAB-DETR) and three reviewers (scores 3,3,3) also finding the method’s novelty insufficient and experiments lacking.

### Strengths

- Small object detection in remote sensing images is a significant and challenging research topic (Reviewers 1,3,5).
- The paper explores and improves DETR-based detection, achieving higher accuracy than several well-known methods on DIOR and AI-TOD (Reviewer 3).
- The article structure is well-organized, with essential sections (methodology, experiments) and a reasonably comprehensive experimental setup (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of novelty / plagiarism concerns**:
   - The three main modules closely resemble prior works without citing them (all reviewers).
   - **QPG module** is similar to DINO’s *Mixed Query Selection*: both select top-K encoder features using multi-class classification scores, initialize only positional queries, and leave content queries static (Reviewers 1,4,5). Figure 2 in QO-DETR is similar to Figure 5(c) of DINO (Reviewer 5).
   - **GD module** is nearly identical to DINO’s *Contrastive DeNoising (CDN) Training*: both generate positive/negative queries with hyperparameters λ1, λ2; positive queries reconstruct ground truth, negative queries predict “no object” (Reviewers 1,4,5). Figure 3 is almost the same as Figure 3 of DINO (Reviewer 5). Equation 1 matches DN-DETR’s attention mask (Reviewer 4).
   - **QCR module** mirrors DINO’s *Look Forward Twice*: Equation 2 matches DINO’s formula (Reviewer 5). Section 3.3 text is plagiarised from DINO section 3.5 (Reviewer 2).
   - Section 3.2 is plagiarised from DINO section 3.3 and DN-DETR section 4.4 (Reviewer 2). L275-L286 from DINO, L287-L299 from DN-DETR.
   - Figure 1 is actually Figure 1 of DINO (Reviewer 2) / highly similar to Figure 2 of DINO (Reviewer 4). Figure 3 is Figure 3 of DINO (Reviewer 2).
   - None of these prior works ([a] Li et al. DN-DETR, [b] Zhang et al. DINO) are cited (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5).
   - The method appears to be a direct adaptation of existing techniques to remote sensing without new improvements; no effective innovation is introduced (Reviewer 4).
   - No supplementary material or code is provided to validate claimed differences from DINO (Reviewer 5).

2. **Insufficient experiments and comparisons**:
   - Comparative methods are outdated; missing comparisons with recent small-object detectors such as QueryDet, CFINet, CEASC, and DQ-DETR (Reviewer 4).
   - DINO itself is not compared or cited (Reviewers 3,4,5). Reviewer 4 asks: *How does QO-DETR compare with DINO on small objects?*
   - Ablation study is only a single table; no quantitative analysis of individual module effects (e.g., recall of proposal selection, impact of denoising on loss). No qualitative visualizations of detection results or attention maps (Reviewer 4).
   - The denoising module is only used during training, not inference, contradicting the claim that it improves robustness to background noise in remote sensing images (Reviewer 5).

3. **Writing quality issues**:
   - Errors, e.g., “TS TS-Deformable DETR” in Line 405 (Reviewer 3).
   - Figure 1 caption is lowercase; other figure captions lack sufficient information (Reviewer 3).
   - Long paragraphs and repeated content, e.g., the second paragraph of the Introduction (Reviewer 5).
   - Misleading improvement calculation: “AP is improved by 50.7%” (Line 491) should be 17.0% absolute improvement (Reviewer 3).

4. **Title relevance**:
   - The method is not specifically designed for remote sensing images or small objects; it appears to be a general object detection approach (Reviewer 5).

5. **Questions and missing clarifications** (overlap with criticisms):
   - Reviewer 3 asks: *What is the difference between the proposed denoising and DINO’s denoising module?*
   - Reviewer 5 asks: *Are there two types of loss functions? Clarify the exact formulation and purpose of each (especially focal loss vs. DETR’s original loss).*
   - Reviewer 5 asks: *The paper says “reconstruction loss” for noisy queries and “DETR’s same training loss” for anchor box queries, but DETR did not use focal loss; later it says reconstruction loss includes L1, GIoU, and focal loss. This is inconsistent.*
