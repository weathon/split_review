## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EDIT, a distillation method that uses dual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data—pairs with similar reasoning paths but divergent conclusions—generated by prompting a teacher LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo) to rectify wrong CoTs and corrupt correct CoTs. A minimum edit distance algorithm identifies the key reasoning steps that differ between the two CoTs, and a fine-grained loss optimizes the likelihood of only those tokens. This approach aims to help smaller language models (SLMs) learn critical reasoning steps rather than merely imitating the teacher’s reasoning form.

### Strengths
- Distilling strong reasoning from LLMs into SLMs is a valuable research topic.
- The design of dual CoTs (positive-negative pairs with similar paths but different conclusions) and the use of minimum edit distance to pinpoint key reasoning steps is a novel and unique approach.
- The fine-grained loss that focuses training exclusively on tokens differing in the dual CoTs is resource-efficient and reasonable, preventing simple imitation of the teacher’s reasoning form.
- The method outperforms baselines on both in-domain (BBH) and out-of-domain benchmarks; additional analysis shows EDIT generates higher-quality CoTs with more correct key reasoning steps, and that logical errors in dual CoTs are especially beneficial for student SLMs.
- The methodology is sound: generating rectified wrong CoTs and corrupted correct CoTs creates a rich training set, and EDIT improves over simple fine-tuning.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty**: Limited novelty—many existing works already use mistake retrieval to guide CoT inference.
- **Data generation issues**: Constructing dual CoT pairs with similar reasoning paths but divergent conclusions is costly and the pairs may be scarce; the generation prompt must be highly accurate without human oversight. Further, the quality of rectified CoTs (produced by providing the correct answer) is not evaluated—there is no guarantee the teacher generates completely correct output or retains similar reasoning steps, which could affect the minimum edit distance calculation. The ratio of correctly corrupted key steps is also not reported.
- **Performance**: Reviewer 1 notes minimal improvement over the teacher model, suggesting only marginal gains. In contrast, Reviewer 2 claims EDIT outperforms baselines, indicating disagreement on the magnitude of improvement relative to the teacher.
- **Overfitting and generalization**: Relying on teacher errors may introduce inconsistencies and overfitting to specific reasoning patterns, potentially harming generalization to varied reasoning styles.
- **Methodological questions**: (a) Greedy decoding is used for inference—if this is to ensure similarity of dual CoTs, how is formal similarity otherwise guaranteed, and could non-key steps be misidentified as key? (b) Ablations show rectified wrong CoTs (RWC) contribute more than the key step loss (KRSL) and \(D_{\text{dual}}^-\) outperforms \(D_{\text{dual}}^+\); what would happen if using only RWC and \(D_{\text{dual}}^+\)? (c) How are the weight values \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) set and how do they impact KRSL performance? (d) Are there metrics to assess whether EDIT’s generated reasoning steps are more accurate than SFT’s, beyond final answer accuracy?
- **Experimental weaknesses**: Compared baselines are from 2022; need comparison with more recent methods (2023–2024). In-domain experiments are conducted on only one dataset (BBH, 2022); at least one more in-domain benchmark is needed to demonstrate effectiveness. The large gap between teacher and student performance is not explained. Scalability when high-quality dual pairs are insufficient is unclear.
- **Presentation and structure**: Figure 2’s depiction of the mistake-driven key reasoning step distillation is unclear; a more explicit explanation at the start of the methodology is needed. Some results (e.g., Figure 5 middle and Table 9) should be moved to the main Table 1 for better support, and Figure 5 right should be placed separately in Section 5. Typo: Table 24 and Table 25 are reversed.
- **Lack of ground truth comparison**: The cases do not compare EDIT’s generated reasoning process against ground truth; it is unclear whether EDIT actually captures the true key reasoning steps.
