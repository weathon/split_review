## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a new data type and quantization method, Super Floating-Point (SuFP), that uses multi-region piecewise quantization with a tensor-wise scalable bias to better handle outlier values during DNN quantization. SuFP defines three modes for representing different data regions. A co-designed hardware accelerator uses only integer arithmetic units and shifters. The method is evaluated on vision, language, and generative (text-to-image) models, reporting improvements in memory, throughput, and energy efficiency compared to FP8, BF16, MSFP, and BSFP.

### Strengths
+ The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear illustrative figures (especially Figures 3 and 4 showing data modes and hardware outline).
+ It targets a critical and well-motivated problem: handling outliers in quantization.
+ Co-design of the MAC hardware architecture for the proposed data type enables better hardware efficiency.
+ Evaluation spans diverse tasks: vision, language, and generative models (including text-to-image).
+ Hardware efficiency is evaluated across multiple performance categories.

### Weaknesses
Reviewer enthusiasm varies: one reviewer (rating 3, confidence 4) finds the gains over MSFP marginal and the ablation study insufficient; another (rating 6, confidence 5) commends the problem focus but notes insufficient hardware details; a third (rating 5, confidence 4) raises several design and clarity concerns.

- **Missing ablation study**: The paper does not quantify the individual contributions of multi-region piecewise quantization versus tensor-wise scalable bias to accuracy or efficiency gains. (Reviewer 1)
- **Incomplete accuracy evaluation on larger models**: Results for MSFP and BSFP are missing on larger models (Table 3). Performance on large language models such as LLaMA2 is not shown, limiting demonstration of scalability. (Reviewer 1)
- **Marginal improvements over MSFP (MX9)**: SuFP achieves only 6% memory reduction, 1.05× throughput improvement, and 1.03× energy savings over MSFP (MX9). One reviewer (rating 3) questions the practical significance given the added complexity of a new data type. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear bit-width comparison with MSFP variants**: The paper does not explain how SuFP’s bit width relates to MSFP’s MX4, MX6, and MX9 configurations. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient hardware details**: The architecture of Processing Elements (PEs), memory hierarchy, and interconnection network are not described, making feasibility and scalability assessment difficult. (Reviewer 2)
- **Hardware evaluation uses only normalized results**: Area and power are reported as percentages of a baseline without absolute numbers; the baseline itself is unclear, preventing comparison with other implementations. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of comparison with quantization frameworks beyond data formats**: The paper compares only floating-point and custom data types but does not include other quantization frameworks (e.g., PACT, LSQ). (Reviewer 2)
- **Questionable design choice of mode 3**: By its bitwidth alone, mode 3 appears strictly worse than mode 1; it is unclear whether the different bias justifies its inclusion. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear advantage of multi-mode support**: Since PEs must process the largest exponent and mantissa, the benefit of having multiple modes is not obvious. The paper does not show per-mode coverage (e.g., a figure like Figure 2) and does not explain how modes are dynamically selected or what overhead that selection incurs. (Reviewer 3)
- **Ambiguous definition of “exponent baseline”**: For mode 2, the baseline includes the exponent bits, but how the baseline is used in the final calculation is not explained (Equations 2–5). (Reviewer 3)
- **Over-claiming**: The paper overstates its contributions (e.g., “superior performance”) without sufficiently rigorous justification. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor concerns**: Bitwidth information is missing from comparison tables, making cross-format comparisons difficult. Equation 1 is poorly explained. Equations 2–5 are not aligned for readability. In Figure 2, BSFP is shown with 7 bits while all others are 8 bits—why? (Reviewer 3)
- **Unanswered questions** (from Reviewer 3):
  - Are there additional floating-point quantization scale factors beyond the SuFP format? (FP8 often uses extra higher-precision scales.)
  - What is the typical distribution of SuFP modes? Does mode 3 appear least often? How does the encoding reflect mode distribution?
  - Which FP8 variant (E4M3?) is used for comparison?
  - Why not include BF16 and other formats in all evaluation categories (space is available in tables)?
  - At what granularity does the method operate? The bias is tensor-wise, but distributions cluster per channel—why not make it per-channel? The hardware diagram suggests bias per 16 elements; is this correct? Could the mode be shared over a block?
  - Does the ALU perform only standard floating-point multiplication, or does it have additional functionality?
