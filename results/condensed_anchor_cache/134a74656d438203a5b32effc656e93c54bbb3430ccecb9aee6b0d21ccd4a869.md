## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a detailed theoretical analysis of a flow-based generative model (with a two-layer autoencoder and skip connection) trained on a bimodal isotropic Gaussian mixture in a high-dimensional finite-sample regime. All three reviewers acknowledge that the theoretical results (characterizing the velocity field and the generative ODE) are accompanied by experiments that closely match predictions. Two reviewers (R2, R3) rate the paper highly (8), praising the exact characterization, clear presentation, and Bayes-optimal convergence rate. One reviewer (R1) is much more critical (rating 3), citing significant concerns about presentation and relevance of the research question. The following strengths and weaknesses reflect this divergence.

### Strengths
- Exact and complete characterization of the velocity field and generative flow for the considered setup, with tight closed-form formulae (e.g., Theorems 2.1, 3.1, 3.2) (R2, R3).
- The distance between the learned cluster means and the true means decays as Θ(1/n), matching the Bayes-optimal rate (R3; also noted by R1 in the summary).
- Solid experiments that closely match the theoretical predictions, increasing credibility of the results (R1, R2, R3).
- Clear and concise presentation of the results (R2).
- The paper addresses an interesting question about how generative models generalize as a function of the number of training samples (R1).
- The solution for the bimodal Gaussian mixture appears sound and plausible (R1), with a neat symmetric ansatz (R3).

### Weaknesses
- One reviewer (R1) finds the presentation of the main technical result misleading. Specifically, the paper could describe the relevant subspace via the empirical means $\hat{\mu}_+$ and $\hat{\mu}_-$ and the offset $\eta$, making it clearer that the model only has access to empirical means. This would also highlight that the convergence rate is driven by how fast empirical means converge to the true means, independent of the regularization $\lambda$ and without varying the architecture (R1).
- The same reviewer (R1) argues that the research question posed in the beginning—whether the network architecture determines whether a generative model memorizes training data—is not actually addressed; the model effectively memorizes the empirical means, and the rate is simply the standard rate of empirical mean estimation.
- The approach heavily relies on the Gaussianity of the target distribution (R2). One reviewer (R2) asks whether the method could be applied to more complex distributions. Another reviewer (R3) similarly notes the lack of correlation structure in the input data, which limits the practical relevance of the conclusions.
- The analysis is restricted to an isotropic Gaussian mixture, and the approach is still an ansatz (R3, minor).
- Minor presentation issues: formatting of contributions (need itemize), a sentence structure issue (“Note that (9) is a special case…”), a typo (“Le X_t” should be “Let X_t”), and a repeated word (“that that the DAE on p.9”) (R1).
- Additional questions raised by R1 that point to missing experiments or clarifications:
  * How many dimensions are needed to span $\hat{w}_t$ for all $t$? The reviewer suspects two dimensions suffice (related to the misleading presentation criticism).
  * Why is $\mu \propto d$ a reasonable scaling? The reviewer considers this unrealistic; for $\|\mu\|^2 = O(d)$ the other directions might become relevant, and the sample complexity behavior could change.
  * Provide an intuitive drawing of the vectors $0, \eta, \mu_+, \mu_-, \mu$ and a learnt trajectory; if the required dimension is only two, this should be easy.
  * What is the shape of the learnt distribution within each cluster (local density)?
  * In Fig. 2, why are the learnt means biased in one direction? Is more training data added sequentially?
