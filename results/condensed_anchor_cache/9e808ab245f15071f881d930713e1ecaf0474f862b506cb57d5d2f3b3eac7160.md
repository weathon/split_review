## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces BBMDP, a Markov decision process formulation for variable selection in Branch and Bound (B&B) that restricts node selection to depth‑first search (DFS) to obtain a canonical Bellman optimality operator. The authors apply Deep Q‑learning (DQN) to BBMDP and compare against prior RL‑based branching agents (including TreeMDP) on four standard MILP benchmarks. Results show that DQN‑BBMDP outperforms previous RL agents and narrows the gap with imitation learning (IL) methods, though IL still achieves better performance overall. Reviewer opinions vary: one finds the primary innovations unclear and the performance inferior on medium instances (score 3), while another finds the empirical results promising (score 6); others note the framework is principled but have concerns about experimental depth and trade‑offs (scores 5,5).

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- BBMDP provides a principled, vanilla MDP formulation for variable selection in B&B that, by restricting node selection to DFS, preserves optimality and convergence properties, potentially allowing broader use of RL algorithms (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- Experimental evaluation covers multiple standard MILP benchmarks (Reviewer 3) and the proposed agent outperforms prior state‑of‑the‑art RL agents (DQN‑TreeMDP) on easy instances, narrowing the gap with IL approaches (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The paper discusses related RL‑based methods clearly (Reviewer 4) and the research direction – learning optimal B&B branching strategies – is important (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and differentiation from prior work**: The primary innovations of BBMDP over existing formulations (e.g., TreeMDP) are unclear (Reviewer 1). Only DQN is tested, so the claimed advantage of broader RL applicability (e.g., multi‑step TD, model‑based methods) is not empirically demonstrated (Reviewer 3). The paper does not clearly summarize its contributions or distinguish itself from prior MDP‑based branching works (Reviewers 1,4). One reviewer questions whether the method applies only to binary integer programming, limiting novelty (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental scale and difficulty**: Experiments are conducted on a small scale; medium instances require only about 1 minute to solve (Reviewer 1). Only 20 instances are used for medium transfer testing, which may be insufficient to represent each problem class; at least 100 instances should be tested, and per‑instance variance should be reported (Reviewers 3,4). Hard difficulty levels are not tested (Reviewer 4). Larger datasets like those of Gasse et al. are missing (Reviewer 1). Performance on real‑world datasets is not examined (Reviewer 3).
- **DFS restriction trade‑offs**: The paper does not explore the impact of mandating depth‑first search for node selection, which is not generally efficient for B&B. There is no analysis comparing DFS against other node selection strategies or discussing whether the performance gains from the simplified MDP outweigh the potential loss of efficiency (Reviewer 3). Inclusion of IL‑DFS as a baseline on medium problems is missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and metrics**: Additional metrics such as wins and primal‑dual convergence plots are requested (Reviewer 1). No comparison is made on the facilities dataset (Reviewer 4). Primal‑dual gap or integral for instances that cannot be solved within runtime limits is not reported (Reviewer 2). No sample complexity analysis is provided (Reviewer 2). The paper does not compare against IL‑DFS on medium instances (Reviewer 3).
- **Inconsistencies between node count and runtime**: DQN‑BBMDP sometimes achieves lower node counts but higher solution times than IL, and vice versa. The paper should investigate the computational cost per node for each method (Reviewer 3).
- **Performance gap with imitation learning**: Despite improvements, IL methods still significantly outperform the proposed RL approach, raising questions about practical applicability (Reviewers 1,3). Motivation for using RL over IL is unclear, given IL’s superior training speed and testing effectiveness (Reviewer 4).
- **Reward and choice of parameters**: The reward is set to −2 for all transitions until termination; the rationale for −2 versus commonly used −1 is not explained and may result from tuning (Reviewers 3,4). The discount factor is set to 1, yet convergence guarantees for RL often assume γ<1; theoretical or experimental support for this choice is missing (Reviewer 4). The choice of HL‑Gauss cross‑entropy loss is not motivated (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and presentation**: Some symbols are not defined (e.g., (v,ε) on line 84), making the digesting process harder (Reviewer 4). The paper lacks a pipeline diagram explaining how the proposed method works (Reviewer 4). The related work section on imitation learning for variable selection is insufficient (Reviewer 4). The meaning of “ρ necessarily defines a total order on nodes” given that node and variable selection influence each other is unclear (Reviewer 1).
