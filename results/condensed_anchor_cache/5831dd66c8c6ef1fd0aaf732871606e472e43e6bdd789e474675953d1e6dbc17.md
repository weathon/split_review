## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes N-ForGOT, a framework for Open Temporal Graph Learning (OTGL) that addresses catastrophic forgetting and distribution shifts when new classes emerge over time. It introduces two plug-in modules: Temporal Inter-class Connectivity Regularization (TICR) to preserve prior inter-class connectivity and decision boundaries, and Localized Temporal Graph Discrepancy Optimization (LTDO) using temporal Weisfeiler-Lehman subtree patterns to handle structural and temporal shifts with reduced complexity (O(n²) to O(n)). A theoretical analysis of generalization error bounds is provided. Experiments on four public datasets show competitive performance on accuracy, forgetting, and generalizability against state-of-the-art baselines.

### Strengths

- **Novel perspective and problem formulation**: The joint treatment of forgetting and generalizability in temporal graphs is original and well-motivated, with a clear demonstration of two core challenges (distribution shifts and knowledge forgetting) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Innovative LTDO module**: The use of temporal WL subtree patterns for distribution discrepancy measurement is a novel and efficient design, significantly reducing computational complexity from O(n²) to O(n) (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical grounding**: The PAC-Bayesian analysis of generalization error bounds provides a strong justification for the method (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). However, the direct implications of Theorem 1 for the proposed modules are not explicitly stated (Reviewer 2). One reviewer found the theory thorough and clear (Reviewer 1), while another requested a clearer connection (Reviewer 2).
- **Thorough empirical evaluation**: The experiments cover diverse real-world datasets and robust metrics (forgetting, generalizability, accuracy). The results are promising (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Notably, one reviewer (score 8, Reviewer 1) considered the work highly significant and practical, while others (scores 5 and 6) found the results competitive but had concerns about clarity and completeness.
- **Good presentation and reproducibility**: The paper includes a clear diagram, pseudo-code, and detailed experimental settings (hyperparameters, computational resources) (Reviewer 2). However, several notation issues were noted (Weaknesses).
- **Practical relevance**: The problem is important for applications like social networks and e-commerce (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Notation and symbol clarity issues (Section 4.1)**: Several mathematical symbols are undefined or inconsistently used, making the method description hard to follow. Specifically:
  - \(\mathcal{S}_i\) appears without definition; its relationship to \(\mathbf{S}_i\) (line 259) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - \(\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_j\) is not defined (Reviewer 2).
  - The indicator function \(I(i,j)\) depends on \(n\) and \(j\) without proper justification (Reviewer 2).
  - \(\mathbf{p}_{k-1}\) likely means \(\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\), but this is inconsistent (Reviewer 2).
  - In line 243, is the category \(n\) supposed to be \(i\)? (Reviewer 4)
  - Some statements (line 420) and figure references (line 484, should be Figure 4) need correction (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient discussion on trade-offs and hyperparameters**:
  - The trade-off between preserving old decision boundaries (TICR) and adapting to new classes deserves more analysis (Reviewer 1).
  - The impact of batch size on LTDO performance is not analyzed, which could affect practical deployment (Reviewer 1).
  - Hyperparameters \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) (Eq. 9) should have their constraints and chosen values reported (Reviewer 2).
  - Are there accuracy trade-offs when using the efficient LTDO approach compared to traditional MMD? (Reviewer 1)
- **Clarification of the open-set setting**: The model incrementally learns tasks with new classes, but the test set only evaluates on classes seen during training (e.g., six tasks). This raises the question: does this truly constitute open-set learning, where predictions on completely unseen classes are possible? The paper should clarify how the setting goes beyond standard incremental learning (Reviewer 3).
- **Definition and modeling of distribution shifts**: The paper mentions dynamic graph distribution shifts, but it is unclear whether these refer only to label shifts or also to changes in features, interactions, or topology. How are these shifts modeled and integrated into the method? (Reviewer 3)
- **Role of the bounding risk (Theorem 1)**: The paper provides a theoretical bound but does not state how it inspires or guides the design of the N-ForGOT components (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons and related work**:
  - The related works section is not comprehensive; recent graph continual learning works (e.g., Niu et al. 2024, Zhang et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2023) are omitted (Reviewer 4).
  - The differences between the studied OTGL problem and standard graph continual learning should be discussed more clearly, as distribution shift and forgetting also exist in that area (Reviewer 4). More recent graph continual learning baselines should be included for comparison (Reviewer 4).
- **Data availability for previous tasks**: When learning a new task, is the data from previous tasks available and included for training? In graph continual learning, there is no access to previous data. This needs clarification (Reviewer 4).
- **Suggested additional analyses**:
  - Present a plot or illustration showing how distribution shifts (e.g., measured by MMD) change across tasks (Reviewer 2).
  - Explain how temporal WL subtree patterns specifically capture temporal information (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues**: Line 349 contains overlapping text; line 892 lists multiple learning rates without explanation; the figure caption and main text spacing in 484 need margins (Reviewer 4).
