## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a test-time adaptation method that fine-tunes a pre-trained Gaussian denoiser on individual noisy images by constructing pseudo noise-noise pairs via self-similarity (pixel bank from non-local patches). The method is evaluated on synthetic (Gaussian, Poisson) and real-world noise (SIDD, PolyU). The four reviewers are divided: two (scores 3, confidence 5) find the contribution limited and the experiments unfair/incomplete; the other two (scores 5, confidence 3 and 4) see novelty and strong performance but point out clarity and generalization issues.

### Strengths
- The idea of using self-similarity (patch-matching) to construct pseudo noise-noise pairs for test-time adaptation is interesting and appears valid (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The method shows strong performance on Gaussian (in-distribution) and Poisson (out-of-distribution) noise, outperforming several other self-supervised denoising methods (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Test-time adaptation is achieved with low computational overhead compared to other single-image denoising approaches (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The Self-Similarity Based Pseudo-Instance Construction (SSPC) is clearly presented and intuitive (Reviewer 3).
- A comprehensive ablation study is provided (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; code is released (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparisons with single-image methods**: The proposed method uses a pre-trained Gaussian denoiser (trained on a large dataset), while many baselines (e.g., DIP, S2S, ZS-N2N) operate on a single test image without any pre-training. Tables 4 and 5 should therefore be interpreted with caution, and comparisons with dataset-based supervised denoisers (e.g., SIDD results) still show a performance gap. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Novelty concerns**: A very similar core approach (generating pseudo-instances by non-local patch-matching for self-supervised denoising) was already introduced in [4] (Patch-Craft). The differences (e.g., using pixel‑level matching after patch matching, requiring only a single image instead of a burst) need to be explicitly clarified, along with an analysis of why they lead to better performance than [4]. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of theoretical justification for Gaussian pre‑training**: The paper claims Gaussian denoising provides a general prior, but provides no mathematical reasoning. Why does pre-training must be on Gaussian noise specifically? Is there a fundamental connection to the fine‑tuning procedure? (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Incomplete experimental validation**:
  - More recent methods and benchmarks (e.g., DND dataset, LAN [2024]) are missing. (Reviewers 1, 4)
  - For Poisson noise, the comparison set should include methods specialized for Poisson denoising, not only the same set used for Gaussian noise (Reviewer 1).
  - Results for SDAP are inconsistent with the original paper; comparisons with SDAP(S)(E) are required (Reviewer 4).
  - Report should include LPIPS as an additional perceptual metric (Reviewer 1).
  - Experiments with more challenging noise types (e.g., real camera noise, correlated noise) are needed to fully demonstrate the method’s generalization (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and limitations**:
  - Optimal hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, number of similar pixels) vary between synthetic and real‑world noise; ablation studies in the appendix show that using fewer similar pixels works better for real‑world cases (20 vs. lower numbers). This suggests the method may require careful tuning per noise type (Reviewer 3).
  - The non-local search may produce pseudo-instances that violate the Noise2Noise assumption (image residual bias), but this issue is not theoretically addressed (Reviewer 1).
- **Applicability concerns**: On real-world benchmarks (e.g., SIDD), performance still lags behind fully supervised methods. How can the framework be developed for practical use? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing explanations**: 
  - Why was the denoiser pre‑trained in a self‑supervised manner rather than supervised? Supervised pre‑training could yield better initial weights (Reviewer 2).
  - Criteria for selecting different learning rates in synthetic vs. real‑noise experiments are not provided; an ablation on the learning rate is asked for (Reviewer 3).
  - The paper does not discuss failure cases or weaknesses of the proposed method (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor typo**: “synthesis noise images” in Figure 2 should be “synthetic noise images” (Reviewer 3).
