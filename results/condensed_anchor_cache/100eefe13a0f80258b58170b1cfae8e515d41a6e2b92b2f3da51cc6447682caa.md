## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies that slow convergence in learned image compression (LIC) arises from energy compaction in nonlinear transforms, comprising feature decorrelation and uneven energy modulation. It proposes a linear auxiliary transform (AuxT) with wavelet-based linear shortcuts (WLSs) – including wavelet-based downsampling, orthogonal linear projection, and subband‑aware scaling – to achieve coarse energy compaction, allowing nonlinear transforms to focus on fine details. AuxT is lightweight and plug‑and‑play into diverse LIC models (mbt2018, ELIC, TCM, STF, Minnen’18). Experiments show that AuxT reduces training time by roughly 2× (or ~45%) while yielding ~1% BD‑rate reduction (or compatible performance). The model with AuxT requires only 1M iterations to reach the R‑D performance that the base model achieves at 2M iterations.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and direction** – The paper opens a new underexplored research area of accelerating convergence in LIC. The analysis from the energy‑compaction perspective is interesting and insightful. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Well‑written** – The paper is clear and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)
- **Extensive and complete experiments** – The proposed AuxT is validated on multiple modern LIC architectures (mbt2018, ELIC, TCM, STF) and shows strong generalization, achieving significant training time reduction with a slight performance gain. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)  
  - Specifically, using AuxT, the model reaches comparable R‑D performance at 1M iterations that the original model needs 2M iterations to achieve. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Training time is reduced by approximately 45% (Reviewer 4) / 2× (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Simple yet effective** – The method is straightforward and delivers substantial improvements. (Reviewer 4)
- **Universal plug‑in** – AuxT can be integrated into existing models with minor modifications. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Applicability with context models** – The paper only studies cases without a context model. It is unclear whether AuxT is beneficial when a context model is present, or whether the decorrelation from AuxT may interfere with the context model’s ability to exploit latent dependencies. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient architectural justification** – The need for a parallel bypass branch (AuxT) is not adequately motivated. It is unclear why the proposed operations (DWT/IDWT + subband‑aware scaling + OLP + orthogonality) cannot be merged directly into the main analysis/synthesis transforms. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unfair ablation in Appendix A.2** – The comparison with alternatives that only replace subsampling with DWT/IDWT (without OLP and orthogonality) is not controlled. The performance gains of the full AuxT may be due to the bypass structure or to the additional components; a proper ablation isolating each component is missing. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing baseline comparisons**  
  - No comparison with “Towards Efficient Image Compression Without Autoregressive Models” (NeurIPS 2023), which also addresses latent decorrelation in LIC. This is a significant oversight. (Reviewer 1)  
  - No comparison with two‑stage training (e.g., DCVC series: first training with only reconstruction loss, then adding entropy constraint). It is important to understand whether observed energy compaction is an artifact of the rate‑distortion trade‑off or the training procedure. (Reviewer 3)  
  - No comparison with curriculum training (train a small model, e.g., only hyperprior, then reload transforms and add a complex entropy model) in terms of total training time. (Reviewer 3)
- **Subband‑scaling coefficients** – The coefficients (1, 0.5, 0.5, 0) for LL, LH, HL, HH subbands are used without justification for being optimal. It is unclear whether they are fixed or learnable, and whether they should be shared across all WLS layers. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Loss function details missing** – The orthogonality loss \(L_{orth}\) is not explicitly formulated. The relationship between \(L_{orth}\) and the rate‑distortion loss, and how it affects training dynamics, is unclear. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Training time baseline ambiguous** – In Table 1, the reductions “-64%”, “-40%” are reported without specifying the anchor model used for comparison. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited improvement on TCM** – AuxT provides relatively small gains on the TCM architecture; the paper does not analyze why this is the case. (Reviewer 2)
- **Sensitivity of \(\lambda_{orth}\)** – The optimal value of \(\lambda_{orth}\) likely differs per model (Supplementary Table 2 shows large performance variations). No guidelines for selecting \(\lambda_{orth}\) are provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation issues and missing details**  
  - The claim that the method accelerates training by 2–3× is not fully supported: at ~1/3 training time, no BD‑rate reduction is observed; the actual acceleration is ~2×. (Reviewer 4)  
  - The phrase “this is the first successful attempt” is overstated; earlier contemporaneous work (e.g., “Accelerating Learned Image Compression with Sensitivity‑aware Embedding and Moving Average”) should be acknowledged. (Reviewer 4)  
  - A missing word in the sentence “realize energy (i.e., feature decorrelation…)” – should be “energy compaction”. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Figure 1 lacks an explanation of how “energy” is measured. (Reviewer 1)  
  - In the ablation on linearity of AuxT, it is unclear whether the orthogonality constraint is applied before or after the non‑linear layer. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Cross‑references to figures in Section 5.4 are wrong. (Reviewer 1)  
  - In Figure 8, “[A4] DWT → Conv” should be “[A3] DWT → Conv”. (Reviewer 1)  
  - No discussion of training acceleration methods from other tasks in RELATED WORK. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minority critical view** – One reviewer (score 6) was notably more critical, raising the concerns about hyperparameter sensitivity (\(\lambda_{orth}\)), the interaction of entropy constraints with energy aggregation, and missing comparisons with two‑stage training and curriculum strategies. These points are reflected above.
