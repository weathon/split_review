## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the FineCVR-1M dataset (1,010,071 video-text triplets) for fine-grained composed video retrieval, constructed by identifying concept changes between video pairs via LLMs. The authors also propose FDCA, a framework that disentangles text features at sentence and token levels and aligns them with video features. Two reviewers are highly positive (8) about the dataset contribution and method, one is moderately positive (5) but raises concerns about generalizability and comparisons, and one is negative (3) citing insufficient explanation and minor errors.

### Strengths
- The FineCVR-1M dataset is a high-quality, fine-grained large-scale resource (1M triplets) that addresses a critical gap in composed video retrieval. (R2, R3, R4)
- Dataset construction is ingenious: using LLMs to label retained, injected, and excluded components between video pairs to generate fine-grained textual descriptions, surpassing the simpler CoVR (WebVid-CoVR) descriptions. (R1, R2)
- The FDCA method follows a conventional attention-based principle and achieves superior performance compared to existing methods on the introduced dataset. (R1, R3, R4)
- The paper is well-structured and clearly communicates its objectives and methodology. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Method explanation gaps (multiple reviewers)**:
  - The derivation of \(\mathcal{L}^{T}\) and \(\mathcal{L}^{S}\) (and \(\mathcal{L}^{R}\)) is not provided in the Methods section. The specific similarity metric and mathematical formulation for these loss functions must be clearly defined. (R1, R3, R4)
  - The Transformer encoder structure (line 269) is underspecified: number of layers, attention heads, hidden dimensions; also unclear whether the same encoder is used for text and video. (R1, R3)
  - The four losses in Eq. (8) (\(\mathcal{L}^{T}, \mathcal{L}^{S}, \mathcal{L}^{R}\)) appear partially redundant; further explanation is needed. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons and experiments (mixed concerns)**:
  - Table 2 is presented in Section 5.2 but not discussed in the text, limiting the comparison's effectiveness. (R4)
  - All experiments are conducted solely on FineCVR-1M; this raises concerns about generalizability. The authors should also test on existing benchmarks (e.g., WebVid, EgoCVR) to validate robustness. (R4)
  - In Table A4, the proposed method performs worse than CoVR on certain metrics, which questions robustness. (R4)
  - Related works are incomplete; missing citations: Dicosa [1] (disentangled conceptualization / text-side [cls] token temporal compression), DPC-KNN usage [2], FreestyleRet [3] (composed image retrieval). (R2)
  - The reviewer suggests exploring the temporal encoder from Dicosa—using text [cls] token cosine similarity with each video frame to compute attention-weighted aggregation—as a potential improvement for composed video retrieval. (R2)
- **Other presentation and technical issues**:
  - Table headings are mislabeled (e.g., Table 4 labeled as Figure 4; also Tables 5, 7, 9). R1 and R3 request thorough proofreading.
  - Figures 2 and 3 have mixed vector and scalar fonts; Figures A10, A12, A14, A15 should be vector graphics to avoid distortion when magnified. (R4)
  - Inference efficiency and model parameters are not reported; e.g., Pic2word uses only one MLP as trainable parameters, highlighting efficiency concerns. (R4)
- **Minor missing references/clarifications**:
  - Provide references for CLIP (lines 253, 256, 405) and BLIP (line 405). (R3)
  - In the abstract, the challenge description is too simplistic; fine-grained video-text modeling is a long-standing problem and should be described more nuanced. (R2)
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Do the weights in Eq. (8) have different effects on the results? (R4)
  - During WebVid-CoVR construction, did mixing short videos (MSRVTT) with longer ones (ActivityNet) cause retrieval mismatch due to video length? (R2)
