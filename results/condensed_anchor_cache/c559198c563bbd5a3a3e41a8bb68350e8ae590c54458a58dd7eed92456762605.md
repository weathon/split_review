- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes ModalPrompt, a dual-modality guided prompt learning framework for rehearsal-free continual learning of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). It introduces task-specific prototype prompts with a selection and fusion mechanism guided by image-text supervision. Experiments on eight datasets report a +20% performance gain and 1.42× inference speedup over baselines. The majority of reviewers (three with score 3, one with score 5) find the idea interesting but have major concerns about novelty, baseline fairness, clarity, and missing comparisons with established prompt-based continual learning methods. One reviewer (score 5) considers the work the first prompt framework for LMM continual learning, well-written, and significantly improved. Others (score 3) find the paper poorly presented, lacking differentiation from prior work, and requiring substantial revision.

### Strengths

- The paper investigates a critical and practical problem: catastrophic forgetting in LMMs during sequential task learning.
- It proposes the first framework that applies prompt learning specifically to rehearsal-free continual learning of LMMs.
- The method is data-efficient and avoids task-specific expansion or data replay, which addresses privacy and resource concerns.
- Strong empirical performance is demonstrated with a reported +20% gain on continual learning benchmarks and a 1.42× inference speedup.
- The design is efficient and scalable, with inference complexity depending on the number of selected prompts rather than tasks.
- One reviewer (score 5) praises the writing and the novelty of applying prompt learning to LMM continual learning, noting strong results across diverse tasks and datasets.
- Another reviewer (score 3) appreciates that learned prompts offer new “viewpoints” and that exploring prompt-based continued learning is practically beneficial for large models.

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient novelty and weak differentiation from prior work**
   - Prompt-based continual learning (e.g., L2P, DualPrompt, S-Prompts, HiDe-Prompt, CODA-Prompt) is already widely used for vision-language models. The paper does not convincingly differentiate its approach and lacks comparison with these methods. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
   - The proposed method (modality-specific prompts per task) is considered too simple relative to existing advanced prompt strategies. (Reviewer 4)

2. **Weak baselines and unfair comparisons**
   - The baseline is too weak: e.g., zero-shot accuracy on the REC task is 0.00, making the claimed improvement unconvincing. (Reviewer 1)
   - Only the LoRA method is compared; other fine-tuning methods (e.g., full fine-tuning, adapter-based) are missing. A direct comparison between LoRA and prompt learning may be unfair. (Reviewer 3)
   - Speed, FPS, and storage comparisons are not provided. (Reviewer 3)

3. **Poor presentation, unclear writing, and missing definitions**
   - The paper is difficult to read; simple ideas are presented in an abstract and complex manner. It requires substantial revision before soundness can be evaluated. (Reviewer 2)
   - Multiple notation issues and missing definitions:
     - Eq. (3) lacks a dummy variable; unclear whether summing over prompts within a set or across sets. (Reviewer 2)
     - Eq. (7): What is \(X_p\)? The prompt selection process is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
     - Eq. (9) reuses \(\mathbf{x}^t_p\) for prototype features, conflicting with its earlier use for prompt features in Eq. (3). (Reviewer 2)
     - Eq. (10): definitions of \(\alpha^{\le t}\) and \(\beta^{\le t}\) and the shape of \(\tilde{X}^t_p\) missing. (Reviewer 2)
     - Line 265: parameters \(\theta_p^t\) of prototype prompts are not defined. (Reviewer 2)
     - Line 161: unclear whether “characteristic of LMM continual learning” refers to a general consensus or the authors’ own proposal. (Reviewer 2)
     - Line 242: “dual-modality features” and “multimodal distributions” are not self-explanatory; it is unclear why closeness to distributions enhances knowledge transfer. (Reviewer 2)
     - Line 201: projection definition error; should be \(\text{Proj}_v(\cdot): \mathbb{R}^{d_v} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_t}\). (Reviewer 2)
     - Line 203: Encoder dimensions given as \(E_I(\cdot): \mathbb{R}^{n_v \times d_v} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_v}\) and \(E_T(\cdot): \mathbb{R}^{n_t \times d_t} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_t}\) contain errors. (Reviewer 4)
     - Typos: “prpredominant” (line 100) and “set `prompt of prompts’” (line 128). (Reviewer 4)

4. **Flawed loss design (Eq. 12)**
   - The loss in Eq. (12) aims to increase similarity between \(x_P^t\) and \(x_{instruct}\). However, as they become more similar, the prompt provides no additional information, which is detrimental to prompt learning. One reviewer notes no difference between the two addends in Eq. (12). (Reviewers 1 and 4)

5. **Confusion about prompt selection and the role of \(k\)**
   - The paper uses \(k\) to denote both the number of selected prompts and the number of sets of prototype prompts; it is unclear which is intended (lines 448–455 vs. subsection 3.2). (Reviewer 2)
   - In subsection 3.2, “set of prompts per task = prototype prompts,” so with eight tasks there should be eight sets, yet line 456 mentions “choosing three sets.” (Reviewer 2)
   - Table 1: metric for the first two methods is missing. (Reviewer 2)
   - Table 2: meaning of \(B_i\) and \(M_i\) is unclear. (Reviewer 2)

6. **Missing experimental results and analyses**
   - No results showing the performance of a single task over time (time axes), which would directly reflect transfer capability as more tasks are learned. (Reviewer 4)
   - The claim that the method “refrains from computation expansion in proportion to the number of tasks” is unclear: how many tasks can be handled effectively in one model? (Reviewer 4)
   - The method for assessing forgetting is not described: does it require saving a lightweight projection layer per task, or re-tuning the projection layer after learning a new task? (Reviewer 4)
   - Figure 5 (similarity visualization) is not clearly labeled; it is unclear whether it plots average similarity between prototype features and task features. (Reviewer 2)
   - The visualization on page 10 is cherry-picked and does not represent overall model behavior; it does not support the claim that previous knowledge is retained. (Reviewer 2)

7. **Contradictions in figures**
   - Figure 5 shows GQA task features having highest similarity with ImageNet prototype features, yet Figure 6 shows selected prototype prompts are primarily from GQA. There is a discrepancy. (Reviewer 2)

8. **Questionable dual-modality ablation**
   - The ablation comparing full (image+text) guidance vs. single modality uses only \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) for selection. A fairer comparison would use two text encoding methods (e.g., \(\beta + \beta'\)) to show the necessity of dual-modality rather than simply multiple similarities. (Reviewer 2)

9. **Complexity and accessibility**
   - The need to configure the number of prompts and selection features may limit broader adoption without further simplification or automation. (Reviewer 3)
