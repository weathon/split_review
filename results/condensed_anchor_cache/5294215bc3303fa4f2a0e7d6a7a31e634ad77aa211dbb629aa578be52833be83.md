## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the online selection of multiple large language models (LLMs) under cost and reward constraints, formulating it as a cost-effective combinatorial multi-armed bandit problem with versatile reward models (AWC, SUC, AIC). The proposed framework, C2MAB-V, selects a subset of LLMs from a scheduling cloud to handle user queries, balancing exploration-exploitation trade-offs and budget constraints. The authors provide theoretical regret and violation bounds, and evaluate the algorithm on the SciQ dataset using nine LLMs across three application scenarios.

### Strengths
- The problem formulation (combinatorial bandit with budget constraints, versatile reward models, multiple LLMs) is timely, clear, and well-motivated; it is applicable to real-world cost-sensitive LLM deployment (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- The paper provides rigorous theoretical analysis, including regret bounds (Theorem 1) and violation bounds (Theorem 2), with case-by-case analysis for different reward models and a discretization rounding procedure (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Some reviewers note that the analysis matches state-of-the-art results in degenerate cases (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical evaluations use nine LLMs across three scenarios, demonstrating that C2MAB-V outperforms baselines (ε-greedy, Thompson sampling, combinatorial UCB) in balancing performance and cost-efficiency (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- The core explore-exploit algorithm is new and interesting to some reviewers (Reviewer 1; also noted as novel by Reviewer 3), though others view it as a natural extension (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and technical depth**: Several reviewers (2,3,4) consider the algorithm and analysis to be natural or straightforward extensions of existing combinatorial bandit results, with limited new technical insight. The reward models (AWC, SUC, AIC) are described as simple parametric equations that may not accurately reflect the real evaluation of combinatorial LLMs (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited empirical validation**: Experiments are conducted on only one dataset (SciQ). The choices of hyperparameters (budget threshold ρ, α, etc.) are not explained (Reviewer 2). Additionally, LLaMA 3 was not included; only LLaMA 2 was used (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical deployability concerns**: The paper does not compare with alternative practical approaches such as Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or Composition of Experts, making it unclear how C2MAB-V compares with learned routing (Reviewer 1). The method does not incorporate task context (e.g., required expertise), and the regret analysis would change substantially in a contextual setup (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: The paper is dense and difficult to follow, with squeezed formatting (Reviewer 4). The three reward models are not clearly differentiated; analysis and regret guarantees are provided only for the AWC model, raising the question of why SUC and AIC are presented in the main text (Reviewer 4). Lines 1240–1241 contain unrelated content that should be removed (Reviewer 3). The related work section omits a formal review of Combinatorial Multi-Armed Bandit with Knapsack constraints (Reviewer 3).
- **Specific technical concerns**:
  - Fig 4's y-axis "reward / budget violation" is puzzling: any feasible solution should have zero budget violation, making the denominator zero (Reviewer 1).
  - The violation bound in Theorem 2 shows a rate of O(√(K/T)) but the text later claims O(√(KT)) – a discrepancy (Reviewer 4).
  - The relaxation strategies in Section 4.1 may produce infeasible solutions; it is unclear how to map them back to feasible integer solutions (Reviewer 4).
  - The upper bound in Theorem 1 has potentially large coefficients (e.g., (K+1)r* on the order of (K+1)NL) – tightness is unclear (Reviewer 4).
  - The definition of violation V(T) uses a max operator; its necessity is not justified compared to standard definitions without max (Reviewer 4).
  - The relation between the cardinality constraints (K and N) is ambiguous (Reviewer 4).
  - For linear reward models, can instance-dependent bounds based on sub-optimality gaps be derived? (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority disagreement on novelty**: While Reviewers 1 and 3 consider the algorithm and analysis new/interesting, Reviewers 2 and 4 view them as incremental extensions of existing combinatorial bandit work. This contrast should be considered when assessing the overall contribution.
