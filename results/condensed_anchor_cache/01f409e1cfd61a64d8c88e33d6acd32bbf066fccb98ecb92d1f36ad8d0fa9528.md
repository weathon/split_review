## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the lack of rigorous mathematical understanding of training instability in Transformers by providing a theoretical analysis of initialization, normalization, and attention mechanisms. The authors propose three stabilization techniques: StableInit (initialization), StableNorm (normalization), and StableAtten (attention), each replacing the vanilla component (Xavier initialization, LayerNorm, standard attention) and supported by theoretical justification. The techniques are combined into a Stable-Transformer. Experiments on GPT2-S and ViT backbones demonstrate improved training stability; supplementary materials show that the methods enable training with larger learning rates.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical contribution:** The paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis of training instability in Transformers and proposes mathematically grounded improvements to initialization, normalization, and attention mechanisms.
- **Novel stabilization techniques:** StableInit, StableNorm, and StableAtten are introduced and individually validated; together they form a comprehensive stabilization package.
- **Experimental validation on canonical architectures:** Experiments on GPT and ViT confirm that each proposed module enhances training stability, and there is a clear mapping between each experiment/theoretical analysis and the stability claims.
- **Larger learning rate tolerance:** The methods demonstrably enable the network to tolerate larger learning rates without training collapse.
- **Clarity and organization:** The paper is well-written with a clear structure, logical progression, and effective figures/diagrams that illustrate key concepts and findings.
- **Robust theoretical foundation:** The mathematical formulations and proofs are clearly articulated and well-supported.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scalability validation (R1’s stronger criticism):** The experiments do not test the techniques on models with billions of parameters (e.g., massive language models), which is crucial for practical large-scale application. The absence of such validation leaves the scalability claims unsubstantiated.
- **Insufficient comparison with alternative stabilization methods (R2):** Gains from each proposed module appear incremental. The paper does not compare StableNorm against other recent normalization techniques like DeepNorm, nor StableAtten against methods such as L2 Self-Attention (especially when query and key projections are distinct, a common real-world setting). A comparative analysis should include computational overhead and convergence rate.
- **Missing analysis of downstream benefits (R2):** The paper does not show whether improved stability yields other practical benefits such as better generalization, faster convergence, or improved out-of-distribution robustness. Explicit experiments measuring the generalization gap, training time, and performance on distribution-shifted datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C) are needed.
- **Limited experimental scope and architectural generality:**
    - Only GPT and ViT are evaluated; other common Transformer variants (BERT, T5) are absent, questioning generalizability (R1, R2).
    - No testing with deeper models (e.g., 200–1000 layers) to assess if stability improvements enable scaling depth (R2).
    - No evaluation on downstream tasks beyond language modeling and image classification (e.g., fine-tuning ViT on CIFAR-10/100, or multi-modal models like CLIP or Flamingo) (R2, R3).
    - Impact on robustness to sample or label noise is not explored (R2).
- **Implementation complexity and hyperparameter guidance (R1):** StableNorm and StableAtten require additional hyperparameter tuning, but the paper provides insufficient guidance on how to choose these hyperparameters, making practical application difficult given the computational cost of hyperparameter search.
- **Minor reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 5, low confidence) finds the limitations on scalability and complexity more severe, while the other two reviewers (score 6, high confidence) are generally satisfied but still request the additional comparisons, evaluations, and analyses listed above.
