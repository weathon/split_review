## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LOB-Bench, a benchmark to evaluate the realism of generative models for limit order book (LOB) data. It provides a theoretical framework and Python package using LOBSTER data, and evaluates an autoregressive state-space model, a (C)GAN, and a parametric baseline. Empirical results show the autoregressive GenAI approach outperforms traditional models.

**Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 6) is much more positive, praising the framework’s comprehensiveness and open-source code. Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 1 and 3) find the benchmark insufficiently justified, lacking comparison with existing methods, and note that the evaluation metric (L1) and the overall novelty are questionable. Reviewer 3 appreciates the idea of binned aggregation but finds the execution lacking.

### Strengths
- **Quantitative evaluation framework:** Addresses the need for numerical metrics beyond stylized facts. Proposes unconditional and conditional evaluations, accounting for model drift and market impact metrics (cross-correlations, price response functions). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Empirical relevance:** Uses the widely adopted LOBSTER dataset, making results relevant to practitioners. (Reviewer 1)
- **Open-source code:** Enhances reproducibility and practical use. (Reviewer 1)
- **Visualizations:** Effectively present quantitative results in an intuitive way. (Reviewer 2)
- **Novel idea of binned aggregation:** Evaluating autoregressive models by binning generated orders into an aggregation function Φ to avoid accumulating errors, using L1 distance between p(x) and p̂(x). (Reviewer 3 – though clarity is needed)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison with existing alternatives:** The paper does not adequately compare LOB-Bench with existing benchmarks or demonstrate superiority over simpler evaluation methods (e.g., training a model on generated data and testing on real data). Specific experiments showing how LOB-Bench handles distribution shifts and autoregressive gaps are missing. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Missing advanced deep learning architectures:** The benchmark only tests autoregressive models, (C)GANs, and an agent-based baseline, missing models that capture temporal dependencies (transformers, diffusion models). (Reviewer 1)
- **Error accumulation over long sequences:** The benchmark does not evaluate or mitigate error accumulation, a critical issue for financial time series. (Reviewer 1)
- **Data sparsity for rare events:** The benchmark does not address performance on rare events (e.g., large market orders) or include methods for data augmentation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Evaluation metric concerns:**
    - L1 distance is presented as a quantitative metric but lacks substance; it overshadows the novelty. A simpler alternative (e.g., train a model on generated data for mid-price prediction and test MSE on real data) might suffice. (Reviewer 3)
    - Why L1 and Wasserstein-1 over KL-divergence? No experiments comparing metrics are provided. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
    - How do these metrics handle fat-tailed distributions, volatility clustering, or outliers/extreme events? (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of discussion and future guidance:** As a benchmark paper, it does not give insights or directions for improving generative LOB models, undermining its usefulness for future researchers. (Reviewer 3)
- **Hidden liquidity and model limitations:** The framework does not account for hidden liquidity. (Reviewer 1)
- **Scalability concerns:** Conditional evaluation for high-dimensional data is not addressed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Generalizability:** Results may not generalize beyond LOBSTER’s limited asset coverage. (Reviewer 1)
- **Non-anonymized code link:** The GitHub link (line 097) and repeated sentences reveal reviewer identity, violating double-blind protocol. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 – reported to ACs)
- **Incomplete editing:** Non-functional GitHub link (line 033), repeated sentences (lines 097-099), missing reference for aggregator functions (line 76), unclear justification for true data seeding causing derailment (lines 86-89), missing information about the “coletta” model and limited GOOG results (lines 390-394), unclear claim “GANs are worst-case aggregators”, improper referencing of equations/sections, and non-standard abbreviation “xent”. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 2)
- **Figure 6 issue:** Trend of MO₀ and MO₁ is not captured by *lobs5* – how justify? (Reviewer 3)
- **Figure 15 (AUCROC) concerns:** AUCROC of 82% means generated orders are easily identified as fake, contradicting original claims. What are the AUCROC values for *coletta* and the *baseline*? (Reviewer 3)
- **Ambiguity of baseline model:** The baseline model is not clearly defined. (Reviewer 3)
- **Question about visualizations:** Does Figure 2 measure L1 loss on all dimensions? (Reviewer 3)
