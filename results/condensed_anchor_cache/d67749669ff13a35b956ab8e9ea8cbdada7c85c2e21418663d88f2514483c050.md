## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Actra, a Transformer architecture for vision-language-action models in robot imitation learning. Contributions are: (1) trajectory attention with a custom self-attention mask, (2) learnable action queries per action dimension, and (3) a multi-modal contrastive loss. Experiments are conducted on CALVIN, VIMABench, Maniskill2, Relay Kitchen, and Push-T. Reviewers note that the direction of questioning the autoregressive mask is interesting and the paper is well-written with thorough ablations, but they also raise concerns about performance relative to SOTA, incremental novelty, missing comparisons, insufficient evaluation, and questionable claims.

### Strengths
- Novel dynamics self-supervised loss that improves policy learning without requiring pretraining. (R1)
- Architecture changes produce consistent improvements over standard Transformer baselines across multiple benchmarks. (R1, R2)
- Paper is well written, easy to follow, and has clear illustrative figures. (R1, R2)
- Explores an interesting and under-examined direction: whether the autoregressive mask pattern is optimal for VLA models. This is beneficial to the community even if it is less supported by efficient inference methods like flash-attention or ring-attention. (R3)
- Thorough ablation studies on attention patterns, which help the reader understand how modifications affect performance. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading terminology and incorrect claims:**
  - The term “VLA” is misleading because the model lacks vision-language pretraining; this is fundamentally a standard Transformer policy without language generation or image understanding capabilities. (R1)
  - Claim: “Additionally, the autoregressive generation approach falls short in generating multi-dimensional actions” (Line 15-16). Many existing VLAs such as Octo and OpenVLA are AR models and successfully generate multi-dimensional actions for real robot tasks. (R3)
  - Claim: “VLAs predominantly build upon the pioneering foundations laid by Decision Transformer and Trajectory Transformer… This paradigm has become a cornerstone across recent VLAs” (Line 46-49). Many recent approaches (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) are structured as VQA models (LLaVA, BLIP, Transfusion) and do not interleave vision, action, and proprioception in long sequences. (R3)

- **Performance concerns and insufficient baselines:**
  - CALVIN results (average rollout length ~1.x) are far below SOTA (3-4 from methods like GR-1 or 3DDA). Existing methods already implement similar trajectory attention and learnable action queries. (R1)
  - VIMA improvements over baselines are marginal, and the method underperforms compared to [4] (Li et al., 2023), which also uses per-dimension action tokens. (R1)
  - The low Diffusion baseline performance on Relay Kitchen and Push-T deviates from originally reported metrics – discrepancy is not explained. (R1)
  - Missing comparisons with current SOTA VLAs (Octo, OpenVLA) and other important baselines: Behavior Transformers (NeurIPS’22), CoTPC (ICML’24), Implicit Behavioral Cloning, Diffusion Policy, and multi-task diffusion-based VLA (Octo, scaling up and distilling down). Without these, it is not obvious that Actra is more sample-efficient than methods that work with <50 demonstrations. (R2, R3)

- **Limited novelty in contributions:**
  - Trajectory attention is an incremental modification already seen in prior works (Octo, GR-1). (R1)
  - Learnable action queries are also incremental, previously explored in GR-1, ACT, and [4]. (R1)
  - Sequence-level contrastive learning has been explored in past work (e.g., ConDT); the paper would benefit from additional ablations over different types of contrastive learning frameworks. (R3)
  - Aside from the self-supervised loss, there are no substantial architectural innovations. (R1)

- **Methodological and evaluation gaps:**
  - No scaling analysis across model sizes or dataset sizes. (R1, R3)
  - No large-scale experimental validation. (R1)
  - Training duration of only 5-10 epochs is insufficient to demonstrate convergence and the performance ceiling. (R1)
  - No real-robot experiments. (R3)
  - No confidence intervals (standard deviation or standard error) reported; without them it is hard to conclude that contrastive learning helps Actra. (R3)
  - The design changes need evaluation on large-scale datasets to see if they scale as intended. (R1, R3)
  - How does the proposed attention mechanism affect inference speed? (R2)
  - What are the failure modes of the method? (R2)
  - What is the number of action tokens per timestep, the sequence length, and the training slowdown caused by the contrastive loss? (R3)
