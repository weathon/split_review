## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper critically examines popular OOD detection procedures based on supervised learning models that use predictive uncertainty or feature-space distances to identify OOD points. It argues that these methods are fundamentally misspecified: uncertainty-based methods conflate high uncertainty with being OOD, feature-based methods conflate far feature-space distance with being OOD, and there is no principled reason to expect a classifier trained only on in-distribution classes to detect OOD inputs. The paper demonstrates these pathologies through mini-experiments across feature-based, logit-based, uncertainty-based, hybrid, scaling, Bayesian, outlier exposure, and generative methods, concluding that these failures are irreducible and not resolved by common interventions.

### Strengths

1. **Fresh perspective and critical examination**: The paper provides a well-argued, thought-provoking critique that challenges the foundational assumptions of widely used OOD detection methods (e.g., the unjustified conflations underlying both feature-based and logit-based approaches). This goes beyond incremental improvements and could reshape the field’s direction.  
2. **Clear presentation and illustrative examples**: The paper is well structured, easy to follow, and uses clear figures and concrete examples to make the argument accessible.  
3. **Broad coverage of methods**: The critique spans multiple families of OOD detection (feature, logit, uncertainty, hybrid, scaling, Bayesian, generative, outlier exposure), each illustrated with mini-experiments. Code is intended for release, supporting reproducibility.  
4. **Relevance and timeliness**: The topic addresses a growing and high-interest area in deep learning safety, raising necessary awareness about the limitations of current OOD detection framing.

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty and lack of technical depth** (noted by multiple reviewers; scratch Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
   - The observation that feature-based methods fail when OOD and ID features overlap is largely self-evident and already well-established in the literature. Instead of merely demonstrating this existence, a deeper analysis of root causes (e.g., the impact of training method, regularization, model architecture depth/width, pre-training) would provide more value.  
   - The claim in Section 4.2 that “OOD examples often have low uncertainty” is supported by a single experiment using LeNet-5 on automobiles and trucks from CIFAR-10. This limited scope is insufficient to support a generalized conclusion; testing across diverse architectures (ResNets, Transformers) and datasets with varying semantic similarity is needed, along with analysis of uncertainty calibration.  
   - The failure mode for generative models (Section 5.6) is demonstrated only via a simplified two-class Gaussian example with no accompanying experiments. Its relevance to modern generative models (diffusion models, normalizing flows) is unclear; more comprehensive analysis on complex datasets and with different training objectives is required.  
   - One reviewer (score 6) noted that OOD features indistinguishable from ID may stem from small model representation space—larger models with more classes could learn detailed representations that distinguish them. This possibility is not explored.  
   - The paper does not propose new approaches or concrete suggestions to address the identified limitations, leaving the practical contribution unclear. Even speculative guidance or future directions would make the work more actionable.

2. **Lack of scientific rigor in experiments** (scratch Reviewer 1).  
   - Each failure mode section follows a pattern of claiming a pathology and demonstrating it with a basic experiment (e.g., ResNet-18 on a subset of CIFAR-10 for feature overlap, LeNet-5 on two CIFAR-10 classes for uncertainty). Conclusions are drawn from a single model and dataset, raising questions about generalizability.  
   - Experimental variables are not systematically controlled (e.g., varying number of layers, width, activation functions) to isolate the factors contributing to observed failure modes.

3. **Conflation of OOD score and uncertainty** (scratch Reviewer 3).  
   - Recent OOD methods explicitly separate the definition of OOD score from label uncertainty. A sample can have low label uncertainty yet a high OOD score—this is precisely why SOTA OOD methods outperform MSP. The paper’s critique that uncertainty-based methods “conflate high uncertainty with being OOD” may therefore oversimplify current practice and needs more nuanced treatment.

4. **Practical utility questioned** (scratch Reviewer 4).  
   - Despite the paper’s critique, existing OOD detection methods perform well on established benchmarks. It remains unclear whether a method that directly answers “Is this unlabeled point from a different distribution?” would improve performance, and the paper provides no evidence or experimental scenario to support such an improvement.

5. **Unaddressed counterarguments and missing comparisons** (scratch Reviewers 3, 4).  
   - The paper does not test its claims on more advanced architectures (e.g., ResNet) or larger/real-world datasets beyond CIFAR-10, leaving generalizability open.  
   - The paper does not discuss or cite related critiques that are highly relevant, e.g., Tajwar et al. “No True State-of-the-Art? OOD Detection Methods are Inconsistent across Datasets” (2021) and Teney et al. “ID and OOD Performance Are Sometimes Inversely Correlated on Real-world Datasets” (NeurIPS 2023).

6. **Minor writing issues** (scratch Reviewer 2).  
   - Figure 1 is not referenced in the main text.  
   - Line 89: The argmax notation should specify the index (e.g., `argmax_{i in {1..K}}`).  
   - Line 99: “features or features” appears to be a typo.  
   - Line 344: “OO” should be “OOD”.

7. **Disagreement among reviewers on overall contribution**: One reviewer (score 3) found the work lacking in new insights and scientific rigor, while another (score 6) found the critique valuable despite the concerns above. The consensus (scores 5, 5, 6) leans toward moderate interest but insufficient novelty for a main-track venue; Reviewer 2 recommended submitting as a review article or position paper.
