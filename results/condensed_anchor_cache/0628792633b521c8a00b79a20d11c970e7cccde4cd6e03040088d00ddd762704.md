## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces NCP-ACL (Neighboring-Cluster Pursuit Adversarial Contrastive Learning), a method for self-supervised adversarial robustness. It builds on SimCLR pre-training and generates adversarial perturbations by moving an anchor toward the centroid of its second-nearest cluster in the representation space. The optimal number of clusters is selected automatically via the silhouette score. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 demonstrate improvements in robust accuracy (up to 2.25% against PGD, 5.05% against Auto-Attack) over prior adversarial contrastive learning methods, with slight improvements in standard accuracy in most cases.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a notable limitation:** Existing adversarial contrastive learning methods do not explicitly control minibatch composition, leading to perturbations that may not cross downstream decision boundaries. NCP-ACL tackles this by using cluster structure to generate more meaningful perturbations. (R1, R4)
- **Clear methodology and extensive analysis:** The paper provides a clear three-stage pipeline (SimCLR pre-training, cluster assignment, adversarial training) and includes detailed ablation studies on the number of clusters K, balance parameter λ, and perturbation strategies. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Empirical effectiveness:** NCP-ACL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 for both robust accuracy against strong attacks (PGD, Auto-Attack) and standard accuracy. The gains are up to 5.05% against Auto-Attack. (R1, R4)
- **Practical hyperparameter selection:** Using the silhouette score to automatically determine the number of clusters removes the need for manual tuning of K. (R4)
- **Rigorous experiments:** The evaluation covers three datasets, multiple attack strengths, and both standard and adversarial fine-tuning regimes. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Empirical justification of perturbation hardness:** The paper demonstrates that cluster-directed perturbations are harder for the model to process than random perturbations, supporting the core hypothesis. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification for second-nearest neighbor choice:** The selection of the second-nearest cluster (rather than nearest, third, or a combination) is motivated only by empirical top-5 comparisons (Appendix). The paper does not provide a theoretical or conceptual explanation for why this particular strategy is optimal, making the choice appear arbitrary. (R1, R2, R3) *One reviewer (R1) noted that the empirical analysis lacks depth; another (R2) stressed that even the comparison is only among second, third, fourth neighbors.*
- **Motivation and relationship to minibatch composition is unclear:** The paper claims existing methods are limited by uncontrolable positive/negative minibatch composition, but it does not clearly explain how this affects perturbations or how NCP-ACL addresses it. The discussion of false negatives (lines 176-181) is confusing, and the paper does not clarify why the proposed method handles false negatives better than prior approaches. (R1, R3)
- **Insufficient baselines in perturbation comparison:** The proof-of-concept experiment (Table 1) only compares cluster-directed perturbations to random noise. To convincingly attribute improvements to crossing decision boundaries, comparisons with perturbations generated under the same paradigm (e.g., adversarial directions from RoCL, DeACL, AdvCL) are needed. (R1, R3)
- **Dependence on clustering quality:** The method relies heavily on spherical K-means producing well-separated clusters. If clustering is poor (e.g., due to high dimensionality, non-convex data, or suboptimal number of clusters), perturbations may be ineffective. The paper does not analyze failure cases, convergence guarantees of K-means, or impact of suboptimal cluster assignments. (R1, R4) *Reviewer R4 additionally noted scalability concerns: K-means complexity grows with dimensionality and convergence is not guaranteed, yet no analysis or mitigation is provided.*
- **Limited dataset scale and generalizability:** Experiments are restricted to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10). No results on larger, more diverse datasets (e.g., ImageNet) are provided, raising questions about scalability and real-world applicability. (R1, R3)
- **Computational cost not discussed:** The added clustering step (including silhouette score calculation and spherical K-means) increases training time compared to baseline methods, but the paper provides no runtime analysis. (R4)
- **Attack evaluation diversity is narrow:** Only PGD and Auto-Attack are used. Additional recent attacks (PGD-100, CW, LGV, SPSA, DeepFool) would strengthen robustness claims. (R2)
- **Use of weak adversaries during training:** The method employs 5-step PGD during adversarial training, which is relatively weak. The paper should justify this choice and evaluate with stronger attacks. (R2)
- **Drop in standard accuracy on CIFAR-10/100:** Table 4 shows improved robustness but decreased standard accuracy on these datasets (compared to no adversarial training). The paper attributes this to a trade-off without providing supporting evidence or references. (R3, R1 on SFL/ALF presentation)
- **Theoretical guarantees lacking:** There is no formal analysis connecting cluster structure to downstream decision boundaries or ensuring that generated perturbations are adversarial. The assumption that moving toward the second-nearest cluster crosses the boundary is intuitive but unvalidated. (R1, R4) *Reviewer R4 asked for specific conditions under which the method is guaranteed to work.*
- **Novelty concerns:** The method primarily integrates clustering into the existing DeACL framework. Several reviewers questioned the originality, noting that neighbor-based perturbation strategies have been explored in other contexts (e.g., graph learning, targeted adversarial attacks like TARO). (R1, R3)
- **Missing comparisons to clustering-based ACL methods and targeted attacks:** The paper does not compare with other approaches that use clustering or targeted directions for adversarial contrastive learning, nor does it discuss how NCP-ACL differs from or improves upon them. (R1, R3)
- **Questions on silhouette score usage and manual interpretation:** The paper does not clarify whether selecting K via silhouette score involves manual inspection or an automatic rule. (R1)
- **Ablation missing n=1 (first-nearest cluster):** In Table 5, the ablation of perturbation targets does not include n=1 (first-nearest neighbor cluster), which would help justify the second-nearest choice. (R3)
- **Minor presentation issues:** Several typos ("vulnerablility", "roubstness", "Consitent"), concepts introduced before definition (adversarial linear fine-tuning), and inconsistencies in encoder notation (lines 262, 269). (R3)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Two reviewers (R1, R2) rated the paper 5 and were more critical, emphasizing lack of theoretical justification, weak novelty, and insufficient baselines. The other two reviewers (R3, R4) rated 6 and were more positive overall, but still raised substantial concerns about motivation, scalability, and theoretical grounding.
