## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes D2P2-SGD, a differentially private optimizer that combines dynamic DP (time-varying noise variance) with automatic gradient clipping and random projection (JL lemma). Theoretical analysis claims tighter error bounds than DPSGD for convex and non-convex objectives. Experiments are conducted on FashionMNIST and SVHN using a 4-layer CNN.

### Strengths
- **Unified framework**: D2P2-SGD consolidates several existing methods (dynamic DP, auto-clipping, random projection) into a single optimizer; the unified angle is appreciated (Reviewer 1).
- **General theoretical analysis**: Utility bounds and convergence rates are provided for both convex and non-convex functions (Reviewer 1). Privacy and utility theorems are supplied (Reviewer 2).
- **Detailed related work**: The paper gives a very thorough overview of prior methods and explains how D2P2-SGD differs from them (Reviewer 1).
- **Improved clipping bias analysis**: The authors improve the clipping bias analysis over prior work (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear motivation and solid proofs**: The ideas and motivations are clearly presented; the proofs appear solid (Reviewer 4). Some reviewers found the proofs solid (Reviewer 4), though others raised concerns about consistency (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical accuracy gains**: Experiments show that D2P2-SGD achieves higher accuracy than baselines on FashionMNIST and SVHN (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The combination is largely an incremental consolidation of existing techniques (auto-clipping, random projection, dynamic budget) and does not appear to be greater than the sum of its parts (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The motivation for combining auto-clipping with random projection is unclear (Reviewer 2). Recent work [De et al., 2022] shows that parameter reduction via projection may not improve DP image classification (Reviewer 2). Dimension reduction has already been proven unable to asymptotically improve the utility–privacy tradeoff due to projection bias, and D2P2-SGD in Table 1 does not improve convergence rate—it leads to a worse one (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical inconsistencies and lack of self-containment**: Theorem 3 requires \(\sigma_\epsilon^2 = O(\log K)\) for the stated rate \(O(1/\sqrt{K} + \log^2 K / K^{1.5})\), but the privacy analysis in Theorem 1 requires \(\sigma_\epsilon^2 = O(K^2)\) to achieve differential privacy—these conditions conflict and would cause the bound to diverge (Reviewer 3). Since dynamic DP-SGD already converges at \(O(1/\sqrt{K})\), it is unclear why D2P2-SGD achieves a better rate (Reviewer 3). The theory does not demonstrate an improvement over prior work and follows standard convergence analysis for DP optimizers (Reviewer 2).
- **Dynamic privacy budget not characterized**: The theorems provide no instruction on how to select the noise schedule across iterations; the experiments use a decaying noise schedule but without formal analysis to support that choice (Reviewer 4). The dynamic protocol would consume additional privacy budget if adapted based on gradient features (Reviewer 4). Moreover, the dynamic DP privacy loss increases much faster than static DP in the rightmost panels of Figures 1 and 2, which is not explained (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Insufficient and poorly justified empirical evaluation**: Experiments are limited to two small datasets (FashionMNIST, SVHN) with a 4-layer CNN (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The paper does not compare against SOTA methods such as “Unlocking High-Accuracy Differentially Private Image Classification through Scale” (De et al., 2022) (Reviewer 4) or other subspace projection baselines such as GEP (Yu et al., 2020) and PDP-SGD (Zhou et al., 2021) (Reviewer 2). No privacy-versus-utility plot at a fixed privacy level is included; Figures 1 and 2 show D2P2-SGD with higher accuracy but also higher privacy loss, making it impossible to conclude superiority at a given \(\epsilon\) (Reviewer 1). In Figures 3 and 4, for \(\sigma_\epsilon > 6\), D2P2-SGD has both larger privacy loss and lower test accuracy, so it is worse at stronger privacy levels (Reviewer 1). It is also unclear why the accuracy of DP-SGD and D2P-SGD remains almost unchanged when \(\sigma_\epsilon\) is increased in Figure 3 (Reviewer 2).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper contains numerous typos and sentences that need rewording for clarity (Reviewers 1, 3). The experimental setup and result presentation are confusing (Reviewer 1). Minor formatting issues: colors in Figure 4 are inconsistent with other figures (Reviewer 1). The use of constant \(\gamma\) in lines 172–174 for numerical stability of clipping is not significant (Reviewer 2).
