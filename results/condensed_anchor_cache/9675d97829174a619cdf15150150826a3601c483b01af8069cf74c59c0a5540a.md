## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Unified Alignment (UNA), a framework that integrates RLHF/PPO, DPO, and KTO through a generalized implicit reward function. UNA replaces RLHF’s separate reward model and policy training with supervised learning, handling pairwise, binary, and scalar feedback. Experiments compare UNA against DPO, KTO, and RLHF across several benchmarks.

### Strengths
- **Unification and versatility**: The framework unifies RLHF, DPO, and KTO and supports multiple feedback types (pairwise, binary, scalar), enhancing practical applicability (R1, R3, R4).
- **Simplification of RLHF**: Replaces the unstable, memory-heavy RL process with supervised learning, potentially making alignment more straightforward and efficient (R1).
- **Mathematical foundation**: The paper provides a mathematical proof linking the RLHF objective to a generalized implicit reward function (R1, R3).
- **Strong ideas and detailed experiments (minority positive view)**: One reviewer (R2) found the unifying advantages significant, the ideas strong, and the experimental results detailed and strong. This contrasts with other reviewers who questioned novelty and fairness.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty vs. DPO**: Multiple reviewers (R3, R4) argue the method is essentially equivalent to DPO. DPO already proved the equivalence between optimal policy and reward objective under the BT model, and the role of the introduced \(f(x)\) and constant \(c\) is unclear. The claim of a novel mapping is therefore not substantiated.
- **Unfair and insufficient experimental comparisons**:
    - Table 1 compares four UNA variants only to KTO and Mistral, without reporting which single UNA variant is best; the best scores are spread across variants (R3, R4).
    - No experimental details are given for the RLHF baseline; the paper claims UNA outperforms RLHF but provides no RLHF results, training setups, or efficiency comparisons (R1, R3, R4).
    - The online RLHF experiments use only a 1.5B model, raising doubts about scalability to larger (~8B) models, despite using 8×80G A100 GPUs (R1).
    - Improvements over baselines are not clearly significant; one reviewer (R1) calls them “not significant,” another (R2) asks for quantification of significance (e.g., confidence intervals, p-values).
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: The claimed simplifications in speed, stability, and memory are not supported by concrete measurements (training time, GPU memory, convergence speed) against all baselines (R1, R3, R4).
- **Presentation quality is poor**: Numerous typos, missing references (e.g., Line 215, Line 352), notation inconsistencies in proofs, and compilation errors (R1, R2, R3, R4). This severely impacts readability.
- **Proof errors and lack of intuition**: The main proof in Appendix B contains errors; e.g., the expectation over \(y\) is mis-specified (R2). No intuitive explanation is provided for why a unified approach should outperform individual methods (R2, also echoed by R3/R4 regarding the distinction from DPO).
- **Conceptual issues**: Using the DPO-derived implicit reward for supervised learning is confusing; it is unclear how UNA differs from simply using a well-trained DPO model, and noisy labels arise if the DPO model is not well-trained (R3, R4). The method is essentially a distillation process, not an RL algorithm, making speed/hardware comparisons with PPO meaningless (R3, R4).
- **Missing baseline results**: The original DPO results are not included in Table 3 (R1); other reference models beyond GPT-4 in AlpacaEval are suggested (R1). The very low win rates against GPT-4 make comparisons difficult (R1).
- **Convergence concerns**: When the implicit and explicit rewards are identical (\(f(x)=0\)), it is not guaranteed that optimizing with \(c=0\) leads to convergence (R1). The stability of UNA over DPO is not demonstrated (R1).
