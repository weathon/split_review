## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework to optimize 4D Gaussians from a single landscape image for dynamic scene video. Reviews are mixed (scores: 3, 6, 8, 6). Key strengths include novel application to landscape 4D scenes, reasonable pipeline using 3DGS/4DGS, and improved performance over baselines (Shen et al., Li et al.). Key weaknesses include limited novelty (much follows prior work like Luciddreamer, 4DGS), heavy reliance on pretrained models, narrow applicability (only fluid motions), missing implementation details (camera parameters, motion estimation, 4D training), lack of theoretical innovation, and insufficient evaluation (PSNR insufficient, need more baselines and user study). One reviewer (score 3) is significantly more critical, questioning contributions and consistency; another (score 8) is positive, praising novelty and SOTA quality.

### Strengths
- **Novel application to landscape 4D scenes:** The method targets generating dynamic 3D video from single landscape images, a new domain for 4DGS (R2, R3). A more positive reviewer (score 8) highlights that the flow-based animation of 3D scenes is novel and fits landscape generation well (R3).
- **Reasonable pipeline using 3DGS/4DGS:** The choice of Gaussian splatting provides better depth approximation than Layered Depth Images (LDI), leading to fewer artifacts in novel view synthesis (R4). Adaptation of 3DGS from point cloud initialization and 4DGS with motion priors is noted (R2).
- **3D-MOM for multiview motion consistency:** The proposed 3D Motion Optimization Module (3D-MOM) balances merging different 2D motions across multi-view images, a good attempt at a challenging problem (R1, R3). One reviewer (score 8) specifically praises 3D-MOM's role in ensuring smooth animations (R3).
- **Improved quantitative and qualitative results:** The method outperforms baselines (Animating Pictures with Eulerian Motion Fields, 3D Cinemagraphy, Make-It-4D) on public datasets, showing higher PSNR and sharper, more natural dynamics with fewer artifacts (R1, R2, R4). However, one reviewer (score 6) notes PSNR may not be ideal for motion evaluation (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty; heavy reliance on prior work:** One reviewer (score 3) states that Sec. 3.1 (3D Gaussians) and Sec. 3.3 (4D Gaussians) simply follow prior work (Luciddreamer, 4DGS) without additional novel effort (R1). Another reviewer (score 6) notes the approach relies heavily on pretrained models (depth, motion, video generation), making the core contribution merely lifting generated 4D content from 2D guidance (R4). A more positive reviewer (score 8) acknowledges missing citation for 4D Gaussian Splatting (Wu et al., CVPR 2024) in Sec. 3.1 (R3).
- **Lack of theoretical innovation:** The method assumes constant speed and direction for fluid motion; a reviewer (score 6) expects greater theoretical contribution, e.g., showing different physical-based models for fluids (R2). The method is also limited to fluid motions (water, smoke) and does not discuss handling other types (wind, rigid body, articulated objects) (R2, R3, R4). One reviewer (score 8) questions how Eulerian motion fields can be extended to more complex motions or human animation (R3).
- **Missing implementation details:**
    - *Camera parameters and monocular depth:* Reviewers (score 3 and 6) ask how camera intrinsics/extrinsics (focal length, principal point) are obtained from a single image, given monocular depth outputs only normalized depth lacking absolute scale (R1, R4). The impact of scale ambiguity on motion estimation and 4D training is unclear (R4).
    - *Multiview supervision and novel view generation:* Eq. 3 (multiview loss) is confusing: a single image cannot provide ground truth multiple views; if novel views are generated by manipulating camera, it would replicate input mono-depth, raising a chicken-and-egg problem (R4). The paper does not address holes, distortion, or missing content from reprojecting point clouds—common issue, and generative model inpainting is not discussed (R1, R3). A reviewer (score 8) recommends using viewcrafter and asks how rendering paths are chosen (R3).
    - *Motion estimation:* The use of 2D optical flow is ambiguous: how is it computed and what model is used? How are occlusions handled? (R4). Semantic masks (e.g., for water) are used but how they are obtained (SAM?) is not specified; if a novel view has black regions, how does the motion model produce reasonable results? (R4). The 3D-MOM method minimizes reprojection error for motions, but one reviewer (score 3) argues this is still based on 2D information and cannot guarantee 3D consistency, similar to SfM bundle adjustment that doesn't consider inter-3D-position relationships (R1). More implementation details of the 3D Motion Optimization Module are needed (R1).
    - *4D Gaussian training process:* Training 4D Gaussians requires video, but input is single image. Line 343 mentions using a video generation model (OpenSORA?) to produce a pseudo-video; which model is used and how its quality affects the final 4D result? How are initial 3D Gaussian parameters and motions transferred to the 4D representation? (R4). The pipeline already estimates 3D Gaussians and motions in steps 1 and 2, so it is unclear why further 4D training is needed (R4).
- **Narrow scope and limited evaluation:** The method only handles fluid motions and landscape images; reviewers (score 6 and 8) consider this too specific, and suggest that applicability to general 4D content would have much larger impact (R2, R3). The paper should include more datasets and baselines (e.g., animation-based methods the authors mention as insufficient) (R1). Only "zoom in" functionality is supported; capabilities like "turn left/right" or "zoom up/down" are missing, and comparison with VividDream is recommended (R3). PSNR may not be a meaningful metric for motion generation; a user study would be more insightful (R4). The maximum temporal length the method can achieve with good quality is not reported (R2).
- **Figure quality:** Figure 2 has motion content that is hard to see; too many small arrows and squares hurt readability (R2). Paper presentation should be more compact, shortening parts that follow prior work and emphasizing novel contributions (R1).
