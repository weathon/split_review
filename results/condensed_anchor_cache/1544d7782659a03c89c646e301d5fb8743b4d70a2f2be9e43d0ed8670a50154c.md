## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a predictor-free Quantum Architecture Search (QAS) framework that uses unsupervised representation learning (inspired by Arch2vec) to learn latent embeddings of quantum circuits, then explores this latent space with REINFORCE and Bayesian Optimization. An improved DAG-based encoding with explicit control/target qubit positions is introduced. Results are reported for quantum state preparation, Max-Cut, and ground-state energy estimation on circuits with up to 12 qubits and max depth 5.

### Strengths
- **Decoupling representation learning from search** is an important direction for generalizing variational quantum ansatz design. [R2, R3; R4 also finds the latent approach interesting and worth further research]
- **Improved circuit encoding** with a novel gate matrix and adjacency matrix that captures control/target qubit positions and detailed gate connectivity. [R2]
- **Predictor-free and label-free**: unsupervised representation learning eliminates the need for labeled datasets and pre-trained predictors. [R3]
- **Versatile search**: the framework demonstrates both REINFORCE (RL) and Bayesian Optimization (BO) as search strategies within the learned latent space. [R3]
- **Open-source code** and clear Figure 1 contribute to reproducibility and clarity. [R4]
- Paper is well-structured (minor strength). [R1]

### Weaknesses
- **Severe scalability concerns** (raised by all reviewers):
  - Only tested up to 12 qubits (mostly 4 and 8) with at most 5 layers; 12-qubit VQAs are unlikely to yield quantum advantage. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
  - Reconstruction accuracy for 12 qubits is 98.76% and degrades as qubit count rises because the design space grows exponentially. [R2]
  - The training data required for the encoder/decoder also scales exponentially. [R2]
  - For larger qubit counts, the size of the adjacency and gate matrices becomes exponential if arbitrary unitaries are targeted. [R2]
  - Figures 3 and 4 show that performance degrades markedly from 4 to 8 qubits, indicating negative correlation with scale. [R4]
  - No compelling argument is given for how the method would perform on practically interesting sizes (e.g., 50 qubits, hundreds of qubits with near-linear depth). [R2, R4]
  - The representation model is trained for a fixed qubit number and Hamiltonian, limiting generalizability and scalability. [R1]

- **Motivation and necessity of the approach are questioned**:
  - Recent QAS methods can directly generate circuit architectures with already-optimized internal parameters, avoiding the need for repeated evaluations—contradicting the paper’s characterization of prior work. [R1]
  - Given that quantum circuits can be evaluated straightforwardly once parameters are set, the need for a representation learning model to estimate circuit performance is unclear. [R1; R2 similarly asks whether arbitrary ansatz is required, noting that building blocks like EfficientSU2 already capture comprehensive unitaries, analogous to classical NAS with conv/attention/MLP.]

- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**:
  - Only one baseline method [Ref 2] is used, which is weak given numerous other QAS approaches exist. [R1]
  - The paper mentions evaluating PPO and A2C but does not show their results; these should be included, even in an appendix. [R4]
  - Expanding to broader baselines would improve the strength of comparisons. [R3]

- **Experimental evaluation limitations**:
  - For ground-state energy estimation, chemical accuracy (1.6 mHa) is the standard; max depth 5 is too shallow to produce meaningful results. [R1]
  - Deeper circuits and larger qubit counts should be explored. [R3]

- **Missing analysis and detail**:
  - No ablation studies of the key components (fusion layer, encoder/decoder adaptations for QAS). [R3]
  - The paper should specify how the decoder ensures that the generated adjacency matrix and gate matrix are consistent with each other. [R2]
  - The choice of GAE/VGAE as baselines is questionable given they are older models. [R2]
  - Subjective visual claims (e.g., “more loosely distributed red points, our new encoding results in a more concentrated and smoother latent representation”) should be quantified. [R4]
  - Table 1 pretraining results need uncertainty estimates (e.g., over random initializations). [R4]
  - Hyperparameter details should be consolidated in the experimental section rather than embedded in methodology. [R3]
- **Minor quality issues**:
  - Figure 3(c) left panel has incorrect coloring for random search. [R4]
  - Text too small to read in Figure 4. [R2]
  - Line 290: “ops”, “qubit”, “adj”, “mean” should be underlined (formatting). [R2]
  - Grammar: “We are considering using” → “We use”. [R4]
  - Background check: the claim that cited QAS methods require evaluating numerous circuits is incorrect; those methods can generate circuits with parameters already optimized. [R1]

- **Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 3 is notably more positive (score 5 vs. 3,3,3), praising the novelty and versatility, but still agrees on the need for scalability tests, more baselines, and an ablation study. The other three reviewers are more critical, especially on motivation, scalability, and empirical justification.
