## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes O2VIS, a framework for video instance segmentation that introduces Instance Occupancy Memory (IOM) and a Decoupled Object Association (DOA) strategy to improve long-term tracking consistency. The IOM tracks global instance features with an occupancy status, and DOA handles existing and new objects separately. Experiments on YouTube-VIS 2019/2021/2022 and OVIS show AP improvements over prior methods. Reviewer scores are split: two reviewers gave 6 (positive) and three gave 3 (negative), reflecting disagreement on novelty, significance, and execution.

### Strengths
1. **Well-motivated design**: The idea that objects should be treated differently based on whether they have been seen before (occupancy-aware memory) is well-grounded and provides an elegant solution to maintaining identity consistency when objects disappear and reappear. The decoupled association (DOA) avoids background misalignment by separating existing and new objects. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
2. **Strong empirical support**: The method achieves state-of-the-art AP on YouTube-VIS 2019/2021/2022. Ablation studies validate the individual contributions of IOM and DOA, and thorough comparisons with existing VIS methods are provided. (R1, R3, R4, R5)
3. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (R5) *[Note: one reviewer (R2) rated presentation 1 and found Figure 1 very hard to understand — see Weaknesses.]*

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty and prior art**: Multiple reviewers note that core ideas have appeared before. The IOM is similar to global track queries in TrackFormer (Meinhardt et al., CVPR 2022), and the Hungarian matching in DOA has been widely explored. Decoupling seen/unseen objects appears in DeepSORT and Detecting Invisible People (not segmentation, but not cited). The calculation of foreground probability is essentially a weighted adjustment, not a novel computational framework. Two reviewers consider the contributions applied enhancements rather than a fundamental breakthrough. (R1, R2, R5)
2. **Incremental improvements without statistical evidence**: On YouTube-VIS 2019 with ResNet-50, O2VIS improves only 0.2 AP over DVIS++. Similar small margins appear on other datasets/backbones. The paper does not report error bars, so improvements may be due to randomness. No ID-switch metric is reported to directly validate the claim of improved long-term consistency over the entire dataset. (R2, R5)
3. **Missing important baselines and comparisons**: SAM2 is not compared for video instance segmentation. CAROQ (Choudhuri et al., CVPR 2023) uses query-based propagation for global tracking but is not evaluated. The missing comparisons weaken the claim of state-of-the-art. (R2, R5)
4. **Insufficient implementation details**: Key aspects are unclear: how object occupancy \( O \) is computed (near L206), why a new feature should be added to memory when occupancy is 0, the update mechanism of IOM, and the interaction between TE and TA trackers. Figure 1 lacks labels and a clear takeaway. Pseudocode for occupancy-guided Hungarian and DOA would improve clarity. (R2, R3, R4)
5. **Computational cost not quantified**: The addition of IOM and DOA increases complexity (multi-frame memory updates, two trackers), but no comparison of model parameters, inference speed, or memory consumption is given. This makes it hard to assess practical trade-offs. Reviewers request runtime and parameter comparisons specifically for the tracking components (e.g., with CAROQ). (R3, R4, R5)
6. **Limited generalizability and failure-case analysis**: Experiments focus on standard YouTube-VIS datasets. Performance under high occlusion, rapid object movement, or on longer video sequences is not examined. No analysis of failure cases is provided, leaving robustness unclear. The method is only evaluated on VIS; applicability to MOT or other tasks is not demonstrated. (R1, R3, R4)
7. **Dependence on pre-trained classifier accuracy**: Foreground probability relies on classification outputs; errors in those outputs could cause incorrect memory updates, potentially destabilizing tracking, especially in long or complex videos. (R1)
8. **Minor typos**: e.g., a capitalized letter mid-sentence at line 47. (R5)
