## Merged Review

### Summary
MotionAura presents a complete text-to-video generation framework built on discrete diffusion. Key elements are the 3D-MBQ-VAE (a VQ-VAE trained with random masking and Masked Frame Index loss) that delivers state-of-the-art video compression/reconstruction, a spectral transformer that processes the discrete latent space in the frequency domain via the Fourier Transform, and a downstream sketch-guided video inpainting task that uses LoRA for efficient fine-tuning. The model achieves strong reconstruction quality, temporally coherent video outputs, and promising results on T2V and inpainting benchmarks.

### Strengths
- **3D-MBQ-VAE with novel training strategies**: The random masking and Masked Frame Index Loss (MFI) enable the VAE to learn spatial relationships and temporal consistency, leading to SOTA compression and reconstruction quality. Pretraining with both random and complete masking yields better results than prior compression models (R1, R3). Visualizations demonstrate strong feature extraction.
- **Spectral transformer**: A novel denoising network that operates in the frequency domain using the Fourier Transform, effectively capturing global context and long-range dependencies. Ablation experiments confirm it outperforms its non-spectral counterpart (R1, R2, R3).
- **Discrete diffusion framework for T2V**: The diffusion process in discrete space with the spectral transformer achieves satisfactory generation speed and quality, producing temporally consistent, high-quality videos that are well-aligned with text prompts (R1, R3).
- **Sketch-guided video inpainting as a downstream task**: The paper is the first to introduce sketch-guided generation for videos. The use of LoRA adaptors provides parameter-efficient fine-tuning and serves as a proof of concept for the framework’s scalability (R1, R2). *Note: one reviewer (R2) noted that the task is first of its kind, but also found the technical design straightforward—see Weaknesses.*
- **Potential dataset contribution**: The constructed dataset, if released open-source, would be a significant contribution to the community (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ablations for VAE losses**: The contribution of the random masking strategy and the MFI loss is not supported by ablation studies. Specifically, the paper does not explore:
  - The impact of varying masking ratios in random masking.
  - The effect of different temporal distances between masked frames in MFI.
  - An ablation that includes MFI in a continuous-space VAE to isolate its benefit.
  - The exact masking ratios used for random masking and full-frame masking in the VAE training (R1, R3).
- **Lack of clarity on codebook design**: The paper does not disclose the codebook size or embedding dimensions used in 3D-MBQ-VAE. An analysis of how varying codebook sizes affect reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) is expected (R2).
- **Joint image/video training not addressed**: W.A.L.T. has shown that a 3D causal VAE can support joint image and video training, which improves performance. The paper does not discuss whether the proposed 3D-MBQ-VAE can also support such joint training, and no experiment is provided to compare with W.A.L.T. (R2).
- **Temporal downsampling inconsistency**: The reported Frame Compression Rate of 4 appears inconsistent with the illustration in Figure S.8, and the paper does not explain how temporal downsampling is performed in the 3D-MBQ-VAE encoder (R3).
- **T2V evaluation fairness**: In Table 3, the proposed model is trained and tested on WebVID10M, while all comparison methods are evaluated in a zero-shot manner (i.e., not trained on WebVID10M). To ensure a fair comparison, the authors should evaluate all methods (including their own) in a zero-shot setting on a common test set such as UCF-101 (FVD). Additionally, the sampling schedule, number of sampling steps, and classifier-free guidance scale used for the proposed method and for the baselines are not reported, hampering reproducibility (R2). *One reviewer (R2, score 6) was notably more critical on this evaluation point than the other two.*
- **Justification for discrete space**: The choice of discrete latent space over the continuous space used in standard diffusion models (DDPM, LDM) is not sufficiently justified. A clear reason is needed, especially since existing diffusion models work well in continuous space (R3). *This concern is raised by a single reviewer (R3).*
- **Spectral transformer analysis gaps**:
  - The application of FFT to text embeddings in cross-attention (where Q comes from image tokens, K,V from text tokens) lacks justification—it is not obvious that text tokens share the frequency-domain structure of continuous images (R3).
  - The rationale for applying FFT in cross-attention at all (neither Q nor K,V form a continuous image domain) is not well explained. Additional analysis, such as visualizing attention activations in the spectral domain, would strengthen the contribution (R3).
- **Condition injection for sketch-guided inpainting is simplistic**: The method relies on a basic concatenation or addition of sketch embeddings with video latents and does not discuss how the model resolves potential conflicts between sketch guidance and video context. No innovative fusion technique is proposed (R1). *Minority note: R1 raises this; R2 only noted the novelty of the task, not the design.*
- **Watermarks in training data**: The WebVid10M dataset contains watermarked videos, yet the generated videos in the paper appear watermark-free. How the model avoids reproducing watermarks is not explained (R2).
