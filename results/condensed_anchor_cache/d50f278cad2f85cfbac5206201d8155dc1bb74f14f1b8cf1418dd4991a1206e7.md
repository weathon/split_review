## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ChartMoE, which replaces the traditional linear projector with a Mixture of Expert (MoE) architecture for chart understanding in MLLMs. It also introduces ChartMoE-Align, a dataset with nearly 1 million chart-table-JSON-code quadruples for alignment training. The method achieves 84.64% accuracy on ChartQA, improving over the previous SOTA of 80.48%.

### Strengths
- **Innovative MoE connector design for chart tasks**, with task-specific expert initialization from distinct alignment tasks (chart-table, chart-JSON, chart-code). Most reviewers found this novel for the chart domain; one noted it is the first MoE MLLM for chart tasks. Another reviewer praised the three-stage training paradigm that increases accuracy.
- **ChartMoE-Align dataset**: large-scale (nearly 1M quadruples) and diverse, valuable for alignment pre-training and as a community resource. Creation of this dataset is considered a significant contribution.
- **Extensive experiments and thorough ablation studies** demonstrating effectiveness on multiple benchmarks, with detailed quantitative and qualitative analysis. One reviewer highlighted the comprehensive ablation as a strength.
- **Clear, well-motivated writing** that explains concepts and methodology methodically.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient differentiation from prior MoE MLLM works**: A minority reviewer (score 5, high confidence) explicitly stated that MoE in MLLMs is not particularly novel; prior works have explored MoE structures, and the reliance on MoE does not introduce a distinctly new approach, making this a weakness in contribution depth. Another high-scoring reviewer also noted limited innovation, calling for a more rigorous analysis of the specific benefits of applying MoE to the connector and a discussion of the trade-off between performance gain and increased parameters/inference time.
- **Dataset construction details are insufficient**: The paper does not specify which LLMs were used for meta CSV generation, how code templates for different chart types were obtained (e.g., manually constructed?), or the criteria for chart selection/filtering. Steps taken to ensure data quality and validation are missing, limiting reproducibility.
- **Generalizability concerns**: Experiments use only a single vision encoder and LLM (e.g., CLIP+LLaMA). The method’s effectiveness on diverse MLLM architectures (different encoders like ViT/Swin, different LMs like Flan-T5) remains untested. Additionally, training data is mostly synthetic, potentially limiting robustness to real-world, noisy, or out-of-distribution charts.
- **Lack of analysis of the MoE routing mechanism**: It is unclear what types of inputs or chart features are routed to each expert and why. The paper provides no theoretical analysis of learned representations within experts beyond ablation. One reviewer suggested testing random expert initialization with balanced loss from scratch to better justify the proposed initialization strategy; this ablation is missing.
- **Underperformance in some settings not explained**: The paper mentions weaknesses in some settings (Tables 2, 3) but does not adequately analyze why ChartMoE underperforms there. Also, the error analysis notes limitations of string matching metrics, but it is unclear if this limitation equally affects comparison models.
- **Practical limitations unaddressed**: No discussion of computational cost of training/inference with MoE, potential overfitting to the alignment dataset, or robustness to noisy/incomplete chart data. The added complexity of multi-expert structure and diverse alignment tasks (explicitly flagged by a lower-scoring reviewer) is not justified against performance gains.
- **One reviewer (score 5) questioned the necessity and efficiency of the large-scale alignment dataset**, suggesting that similar performance might be achievable with a smaller, curated dataset, and that the heuristic generation approach raises resource-efficiency concerns.
