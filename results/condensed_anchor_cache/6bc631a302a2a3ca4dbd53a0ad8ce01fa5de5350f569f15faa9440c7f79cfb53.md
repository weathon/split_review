## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers gave scores of 6, 8, 8, and 6, indicating a split in overall assessment. Reviewers 2 and 3 were more positive, praising the mixed‑reality platform, the scale and diversity of the data, and the potential for modeling pedestrian intention. Reviewers 1 and 4 were more cautious, highlighting domain gaps between simulated and real data, concerns about motion diversity and subject representativeness, and the complexity of the setup. All reviewers acknowledged the dataset’s novelty, scale, and public availability, but several pointed to missing evaluations, outdated comparisons, and a need for richer pedestrian‑object interactions.

### Strengths
- **Mixed‑reality data collection platform:** The combination of real‑world MoCap with VR and the CARLA simulator is interesting and provides a safe, cost‑effective way to collect realistic pedestrian motion data, including rare/dangerous scenarios (e.g., collisions) that are difficult to capture in the real world. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Large‑scale and diverse data:** The dataset includes 54 subjects, 13 urban settings, 7 objects, 12,138 sequences, and multiple weather conditions and vehicle speeds, offering a rich foundation for multi‑view and multimodal pedestrian intention modeling. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Multi‑perspective and multimodal coverage:** Third‑person RGB videos from moving vehicles, LiDAR data, and egocentric VR perspectives are provided, making it the first dataset of its kind to incorporate such diversity for pedestrian intention. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Realistic motion distribution and object interactions:** The dataset captures real‑world pedestrian motion distributions (unlike datasets that simply replay existing MoCap) and includes real‑world object interactions and tracking. (Reviewer 2)
- **Good validation and labeling:** The dataset’s annotation quality and validation are well done. (Reviewer 1)
- **Public availability:** The dataset is openly available, promoting further research. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Domain gap between simulated and real data (egocentric and motion diversity):** The egocentric data is collected with VR headsets in a simulated setting, which differs from real head‑mounted cameras (e.g., Ego4D). The MoCap system may not capture the subtle variations of natural human gait and behavior. This gap could limit the generalizability of models trained on PMR to real‑world applications. (Reviewers 1, 4)  
  *Note:* Reviewer 1 considered this a minor flaw, while Reviewer 4 saw it as more significant.
- **Main benchmark tasks discard scene context:** The focus on human pose estimation ignores most of the scene context (vehicles, weather, pedestrian‑vehicle interactions). The paper should include pedestrian behavior generation/forecasting tasks to fully leverage the dataset’s strengths in rare/dangerous interactions. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing validation on real‑world human pose estimation benchmarks:** The domain gap is evaluated on 3D object detection, not on human pose estimation (e.g., 3DPW, EMDB, RICH). The paper should demonstrate that adding PMR as training data improves performance on established real‑world human pose benchmarks. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited pedestrian‑object interactions:** Interactions beyond simple rigid‑body contact are missing – no scenarios such as pedestrian conversations, phone calls, or using umbrellas on rainy days. This lack of everyday human‑object interactions (including deformable objects) limits applicability to complex real‑world settings. (Reviewer 3)
- **Outdated dataset comparisons:** The comparison with existing datasets does not include recent multimodal human body reconstruction datasets such as RELI11D (CVPR 2024) and HiSC4D (TPAMI 2024). This makes it difficult to assess the novelty and contribution relative to state‑of‑the‑art. (Reviewer 3)
- **Predetermined encounters lack randomness:** All pedestrian‑vehicle encounters are fixed by program design; incorporating procedural generation to introduce random, unpredictable events (e.g., sudden speed or direction changes) would better capture the diversity of real‑world interactions. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited subject diversity:** Data collected from only 54 subjects may not fully represent the global population, potentially introducing biases in gait, reaction times, and interaction styles that hinder generalization. (Reviewer 4)
- **Complex setup limits reproducibility:** The mixed‑reality platform involves multiple synchronized hardware and software components (VR headsets, MoCap, CARLA), making it challenging for other researchers to replicate the data collection process. (Reviewer 4)
- **No video examples of rare cases:** The paper does not provide videos showcasing the rare/dangerous scenarios, which would help demonstrate data quality. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor presentation issues:** Figure 6 has text blocked by the picture (Reviewer 3). There is a typo on line 363: “taccount” (Reviewer 2).
