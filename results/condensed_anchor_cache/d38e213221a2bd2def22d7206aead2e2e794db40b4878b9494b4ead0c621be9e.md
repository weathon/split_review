## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a benchmark and method for automatic essay critique generation using LLMs, aiming to reduce hallucinations (irrelevant or incorrect feedback). The main contributions are a Chinese essay critique dataset (EssayC) and the RedHat method, which incorporates document-level question-answering (Q-A) into the prompt to anchor critiques to the essay text. Experiments compare RedHat with baselines (post-pretraining, supervised fine-tuning) using both automatic and human evaluation. Results show some reduction in hallucination and preference by human experts in 20% of cases.

### Strengths
- The work is intriguing and makes clever use of prompt engineering (Reviewer 1).  
- The definition of hallucination types (Table 1) is clear and helpful for task specification (Reviewer 3).  
- The evaluation setting is solid, including both automatic and human evaluation (Reviewer 3).  
- RedHat is grounded in solid assessment practices, with critiques based on concrete question-answering (Reviewer 2).  
- The use of a non-English (Chinese) dataset and task contributes to diversity in the LLM landscape (Reviewer 2).  
- The paper provides a clear, position-based analysis highlighting where improvements occurred (Reviewer 2).  
- Results indicate some success (Reviewer 1), and automatic and human evaluation demonstrate effectiveness (Reviewer 3).  
- [Note: Reviewer 1 is less convinced about the magnitude and generalizability, and Reviewer 2 is moderately positive; Reviewer 3 is the most positive.]

### Weaknesses
- **Literature grounding**: The work is not firmly rooted in argumentation literature. Reviewer 1 requested a comparison between induced questions and argumentation theory; the provided references involve only LLMs/computational methods and miss the point. (Reviewer 1, high confidence)  
- **Impact of RedHat is limited**: Across multiple models, human preference for RedHat over baseline is weak. Reviewer 1 reframes the claim: "for most cases, humans have no preference, but if they do they slightly prefer RedHat over baseline generations." (Reviewer 1, high confidence)  
- **Unexplained model-specific success**: RedHat with GPT-4 significantly improves scores but not other models; no explanation is given for why this happens. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Trivial/incremental improvement**: RedHat simply adds document-level Q&A to the prompt, which may be an incremental improvement. (Reviewer 2, moderate confidence)  
- **Unclear definition of hallucination**: It is ambiguous whether the issue addressed is truly "hallucination" or general task performance improvement. A clear definition distinguishing critique generation errors that are not hallucinatory would be valuable. (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 3’s Q1: If LLM fails to focus on desired argument or is too focused on the conclusion, is that hallucination?)  
- **Vague presentation on language and generalizability**: The paper does not clearly state that the tasks/datasets are in Chinese. There are limited insights on whether findings apply to other datasets, languages, or base LLMs (e.g., English, ChatGPT). (Reviewer 2, moderate confidence)  
- **Dataset information insufficient**: Very little overview of EssayC (types, size, annotation process, quality assessment) is provided. It is unclear if the dataset will be open-sourced. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)  
- **Baseline method details missing**: Limited information about the SFT and PT baselines (e.g., training data) in the main body; navigation between paper and appendix is challenging. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Metric issues**: The metric "Detailedness" appears to be lower-is-better (ambiguity), but the objective function maximizes it; renaming to "ambiguity" might clarify. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Reliability of GPT evaluation**: Figure 4 suggests GPT evaluation results are not reliable; humans trust expert annotators more, but untrained human annotators may also be random. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Metrics choice**: Why use ROUGE and BLEU, which only consider word overlap? Consider BERTScore or BLEURT. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Writing structure confusion**: Chapters 2, 3, and 4 each contain a literature review plus own work, making the structure unclear. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Stability of open Q-A results**: It is uncertain whether the Q-A generation is stable across multiple runs of the model. (Reviewer 3, Q2)  
- **Missing few-shot baseline**: A few-shot approach (providing example critiques in the prompt) could be a useful comparison, especially for non-native speaker essay scoring. (Reviewer 2, question)  
- **Discrepancy between claims and evidence**: Reviewer 1 notes a discrepancy between the claimed efficacy and empirical results (e.g., "human prefer redhat" overstated). (Reviewer 1, high confidence)  
- **Need for reproduction with more LLMs**: Only a limited set of models was tested; more LLMs could strengthen the findings. (Reviewer 1)
