## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Relevance-Based Embeddings (RBE) to improve upon AnnCUR by replacing matrix factorization with neural embeddings and by better selecting support items/queries. Reviewers assigned scores 3, 5, 8, 5, 3, indicating strong disagreement. One reviewer (score 8) praised the theoretical and empirical contributions and found the work well-motivated; two others (scores 3) questioned novelty, literature coverage, and baseline strength. Common positive points include consistent improvement over AnnCUR, a universal approximation guarantee, and diverse datasets (ZeShEL, MS MARCO, production recommender). Common negative points include incomplete literature review (missing memory networks, multi-embedding work), lack of runtime/efficiency experiments, weak baselines, and presentation issues.

### Strengths
* **Empirical improvement over baseline AnnCUR:** The proposed method always outperforms AnnCUR across all tested variants and datasets (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
* **Well-motivated extension from matrix factorization to neural embeddings:** The generalization of AnnCUR into a neural framework is considered original and timely (Reviewer 3).
* **Theoretical contributions:** Authors prove a universal approximation guarantee for AnnCUR and a stronger result for RBE at an abstract level (compact topological spaces), confirming the framework’s generality (Reviewer 3). This theoretical analysis is seen as a strength by one reviewer, while another found it similar to prior multi-embedding proofs (see Weaknesses).
* **Diverse experimental settings:** Experiments span entity linkage (ZeShEL), question answering (MS MARCO/QA), and recommendation systems (RecSys, Military, QA), testing the method on both standard and production datasets (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5).
* **Heuristic exploration for support selection:** Several simple heuristics and a theoretically justified (l2-greedy) approach are tested; the effectiveness of these heuristics is validated experimentally (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
* **Important topic:** The problem of relevance function approximation for efficient retrieval is relevant to search, language modeling, and recommendations (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
* **Incomplete literature review:**
  * Missing comparison and citation of memory-based architectures such as End-To-End Memory Networks (NIPS 2015) and OAK (ICML 2024) for extreme classification (Reviewer 1).
  * The concept of “relevance-based embedding” is seen as identical to multi-embedding/multi-vector retrieval (e.g., MIND, ColBERT, Multi-CLS BERT, Ding et al. 2024, Dhulipala et al. MUVERA), which is neither cited nor compared (Reviewer 5).
  * The main theoretical result—that arbitrary relevance functions can be approximated by such embeddings—is claimed to be similar to proofs in Ding et al. (2024) and Dhulipala et al. (2024) (Reviewer 5).
  * Random choice of support items is considered a weak baseline given that prior multi-embedding work uses co-trained query/item vectors (Reviewer 5).
* **Weak baselines and missing comparisons:**
  * Evaluations are performed against non-standard or outdated baselines. ZESHEL is not common for relevance retrieval; QA (MS MARCO) uses a 2019 baseline, failing to compare with recent SOTA (e.g., Autoregressive Entity Retrieval, Sentence-T5, NCI) (Reviewer 5).
  * RecSys datasets are proprietary and use a gradient‑boosting teacher which is not SOTA (modern cross‑encoders, deep stacked networks, generative approaches are not considered) (Reviewer 5).
  * Recommendation baselines disregard widely‑used dual encoder improvements such as knowledge distillation, making the reported gains less convincing (Reviewer 3).
  * No comparison on standard retrieval benchmarks like BEIR and NQ (Reviewer 2).
  * No comparison with memory network literature and retrieval augmented retrieval approaches like OAK (Reviewer 1).
* **Lack of runtime/efficiency experiments:**
  * No wall‑clock time reported for training or inference (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). Only the number of cross‑encoder calls is reported, which is insufficient; the overhead of computing relevance vectors should be quantified in real time (Reviewer 4).
  * Universal approximation requires wide hidden layers; experiments do not study the trade‑off between quality and efficiency for practical retrieval (Reviewer 5). The l2‑greedy approach may be expensive, and its runtime is not given (Reviewer 2).
  * The method requires $S_I$ cross‑encoder calls per inference; this overhead needs to be compared to simpler dual encoders (Reviewer 4).
* **Scalability concerns:**
  * Datasets have relatively small item sets (~10K); as the number of items grows, more anchors are needed, potentially making the method less practical than dual encoders (Reviewer 3). A scaling study with respect to the number of items is recommended (Reviewer 3).
* **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  * The difference between Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.2, and how the theory informs the method, are not clearly explained (Reviewer 2). Terms like $n$ (line 272), $\hat{R}$, $X$, $K$ are left undefined; the notation for the metric HitRate(P,T) is very hard to read (Reviewer 2).
  * Table 1 descriptions are insufficient to fully understand differences between support selection heuristics, and separating the l2‑greedy approach from other heuristics may confuse readers (Reviewer 4).
  * The choice of the AXN model and the dimensions of $\theta_I$ are not justified (Reviewer 4). The authors should explain how these were chosen and whether hyperparameter tuning was performed (Reviewer 4).
  * The abstraction of compact topological spaces is not explained for practitioners; concrete instantiations (e.g., Euclidean embeddings) should be discussed (Reviewer 3).
  * Inconsistency: Table 1 uses QA.small while Table 2 uses QA dataset; reason not given (Reviewer 4).
* **Specific unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms:**
  * Will RecSys and RecSys2 datasets be publicly released? (Reviewer 2)
  * Why is RBE so much better on RecSys2 compared to other datasets? (Reviewer 2)
  * The claimed computational advantage over CUR is unclear: is $O(M \cdot |Q_{train}|)$ correct, or is it $O(S_I \cdot |Q_{train}| + S_Q \cdot M)$? (Reviewer 3) Does RBE avoid computing $R(I, Q_{train})$ and thus scale better? (Reviewer 2)
  * In what exact sense is Theorem 3.2 stronger than Theorem 3.1? (Reviewer 3)
  * How many CE calls does AXNDE use? Why is adding 100 items considered a fair comparison? (Reviewer 4)
