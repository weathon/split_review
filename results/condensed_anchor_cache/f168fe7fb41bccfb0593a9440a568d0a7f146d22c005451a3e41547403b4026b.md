## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper theoretically and empirically compares Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) with MLPs on expressiveness and spectral bias. It shows that MLPs can be embedded into KANs with moderate size increase, and KANs into MLPs with a parameter increase proportional to the grid size G. The spectral bias analysis (Theorem 4.1) examines the Hessian conditioning of single‑layer B‑splines, suggesting reduced low‑frequency preference compared with ReLU MLPs. Experiments on PDE solving and Gaussian random field fitting illustrate spectral bias. One reviewer (Reviewer 2, rating 8) finds the analysis “considerably rigorous” and the topic important; the other three (ratings 5,6,6) consider the theoretical scope too shallow and the experimental support insufficient.

### Strengths
*   **Theoretical characterization of representation capacity** – Establishes that any MLP with a \(C^k\) activation can be exactly represented as a KAN (B‑spline order \(k\), grid size 2) with depth doubled and parameters multiplied by about 4; conversely, a KAN can be represented by an MLP but with parameter count multiplied by the grid size \(G\). This suggests KANs with large \(G\) may be more parameter‑efficient for certain functions. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
*   **Spectral bias analysis (Theorem 4.1)** – Provides a rigourous characterization of the Hessian spectrum for a single layer of B‑splines, explicitly depending on embedding dimension and grid size. The result supports the claim that KANs are less biased towards low frequencies than ReLU MLPs. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
*   **Experimental visualisation of spectral bias** – Numerical comparisons (e.g., Figures 1‑4) illustrate the multi‑level learning feature (grid extension) and its effect on high‑frequency components. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
*   **Shallow analysis limits generality** – The theoretical results (Theorem 3.1‑3.4, Theorem 4.1) are derived for shallow (single‑layer) KANs. The extension to deep networks is not discussed, making the conclusions about practical deep KANs speculative. (Reviewers 1, 2)
*   **Unfair comparison with MLP activations** – The spectral bias comparison (Theorem 4.1 and experiments) uses ReLU MLPs, whereas the KANs use B‑splines of order \(k\). A fairer comparison would be with MLPs using \(\text{ReLU}^k\) activations, which share the same smoothness class. Without this baseline, the advantages attributed to KANs may simply reflect activation regularity. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Corollary 3.4 is not properly contextualized** – The approximation rate in Corollary 3.4 appears to rely on the mapping from KANs to MLPs, and the authors do not clearly state whether a standard \(\text{ReLU}^k\) MLP would achieve the same rate. The brief mention of a “non‑standard” MLP with \(\text{ReLU}^k\) is insufficiently defined. Hence the claimed expressiveness advantage of KANs is unclear. (Reviewers 3, 4)
*   **Connection between Theorem 4.1 and spectral bias is opaque** – The theorem characterizes the Hessian, but the link to actual learning dynamics (e.g., frequency bias) is not explicitly drawn. No corollary or discussion explains how the Hessian spectrum translates to high‑frequency performance. (Reviewers 1, 4)
*   **Experimental validation is insufficient** – No statistical tests are provided to support the claimed advantages. Test loss is omitted (e.g., for the PDE experiments); only training loss is shown, as in prior work [1]. Comparisons are limited to MLPs; state‑of‑the‑art neural architectures for scientific computing are not considered. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
*   **Boundedness of key theorems** – The bounds in Theorem 3.2 and Theorem 3.3 are not tight. (Reviewer 4)
*   **Instability during grid extension unexplained** – Figures 3 and 4 show non‑monotonic learning for KANs (attributed to grid extension in the “undersampled regime”), but no description is given of whether the grid size is dynamically increased during training or how this affects optimisation. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Practical shortcomings not addressed** – The paper lacks discussion of KAN computational cost, scalability, overfitting risks, and implementation challenges. (Reviewer 1)
*   **Presentation issues** – Figures 1 and 2 are nearly identical; Figures 3 and 4 could be synthesised. Legend font sizes are too small for printed readability. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Conceptual confusion in parameter/depth comparison** – The paper directly compares parameter counts and depths between KANs and MLPs without accounting for the different structural roles (e.g., KAN depth vs MLP depth), which may mislead interpretation of Theorems 3.1‑3.4 and the experiments in Sections 4.1‑4.2. (Reviewer 1)
*   **Limited problem contextualization** – The practical significance of overcoming spectral bias is not established, and real‑world tasks beyond simple PDEs and GRFs are not considered. (Reviewer 1)
