## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SCHEME, a channel mixer for vision transformers that replaces dense MLPs with a block diagonal MLP (BD-MLP) and adds a lightweight Channel Covariance Attention (CCA) module during training only (discarded at inference). The goal is to improve efficiency and feature learning, especially at lower complexity regimes. Reviewers generally found the topic important, the methodology clear, and the experimental results promising across classification, detection, and segmentation. However, they raised significant concerns about insufficient comparisons with state-of-the-art and related baselines, limited scaling experiments (only small models <60M), missing implementation details, lack of on-device efficiency validation, and presentation issues. One reviewer (score 3) was notably more critical, while others (scores 5–6) were moderately positive but still demanded corrections.

### Strengths
- The channel mixer design is an interesting and important topic for backbone architecture research.
- SCHEME introduces a clever way to reduce complexity using block diagonal MLPs, directly targeting the computational bottleneck of standard transformers.
- The addition of CCA as a regularizer during training, which is discarded at inference, is innovative; it improves feature clustering without adding inference cost.
- The approach is demonstrated across multiple transformer backbones (MetaFormer, T2T, Swin) and tasks (image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation), showing adaptability and consistent accuracy gains over prior designs.
- The paper is well-structured, with clear explanations of methodology and motivation; writing is easy to follow.
- Experimental results show strong accuracy improvements, especially at low complexity, establishing new Pareto frontiers for accuracy vs. FLOPs, model size, and throughput (e.g., Tables 4 and 8).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparisons and limited baselines:** The paper focuses heavily on MetaFormer and lacks thorough comparisons with state-of-the-art transformer models (e.g., PVT, UniFormer, TransNeXt), modern ConvNet architectures that also use FFN/MLP (e.g., ConvNeXt, FasterNet, InceptionNeXt), efficient models (FasterViT, FastViT, MobileOne), and previous specialized channel mixer designs (gMLP, MogaNet, S2-MLP). This limits the evaluation’s thoroughness and generalizability. (R1, R3, R4; R1 was especially critical.)
- **Limited scaling experiments:** Only small model scales (<60M, base-size at most) are tested. Large or X-Large models are not evaluated, which restricts conclusions about scaling capability and applicability in high-capacity scenarios. (R3, R4)
- **Missing implementation details and theoretical analysis:** The paper does not specify how feature vectors are split into disjoint groups for BD-MLP (criteria, motivation). The motivation and design details for CCA are insufficient; its resemblance to linear attention mechanisms in NLP and XCiT needs discussion and proper citations to clarify novelty. A deeper analysis of how SCHEME specifically influences learned representations is lacking. (R2, R3)
- **On-device efficiency underexplored:** Only limited throughput comparisons are provided (Tables 4 and 8). Detailed on-device benchmarks (including hardware, configurations, input shapes) are missing, especially on mobile devices, which is necessary to validate real-world efficiency claims as done in recent efficient model studies (e.g., FastViT, MobileOne). (R3)
- **Unexplored design choices:** The learnable mixing weight α enables CCA decay during training, but the paper does not discuss why this approach is chosen over established techniques like Structural Re-parameterization; potential optimization conflicts are not addressed. Additionally, it is unclear whether the smoothing factor τ is learnable or a constant. (R3)
- **Grammar, typos, and figure formatting:** The manuscript contains grammatical errors (e.g., double “of” in abstract, “In result”), typos (dimension notation N→d in Lines 196 and 221), and figures (1, 3, 4) with illegible labels and coordinates, falling short of publication standards. (R1, R3, R4; R1 rated presentation especially low, score 1.)
- **Generalization concern:** The paper does not test on diverse datasets beyond standard benchmarks, leaving its generalization to other domains unclear. (R2)

**Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 3) was markedly more negative about presentation and scope, while the others (scores 5–6) were moderately positive but still raised substantial criticisms.
