## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DSpodFL, a decentralized federated learning algorithm that introduces sporadicity in both local SGD computations and model aggregations. The analysis provides convergence rates under strong convexity. The three reviewers have mixed assessments. One reviewer (score 5) found the algorithm novel and the theoretical contribution solid, with easy presentation. The other two (scores 3,3) raised significant concerns: the novelty is limited and the method is incremental over existing asynchronous and sporadic communication methods; the strong convexity assumption is restrictive; empirical evaluation is weak (only one dataset, 10 nodes, no comparison to FedAvg or other key baselines); the theoretical analysis lacks discussion of parameter dependencies and transient rates; and important related works are omitted.

### Strengths
- The algorithm combining sporadic SGD computations and sporadic aggregations is seen as new by one reviewer (R1).
- The theoretical analysis provides linear convergence rates for both the average model error and consensus error, with a finite optimality gap diminishing in step size (R1, R2). The analysis of both errors is appreciated as often missing in FL papers (R2).
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1).
- The problem of heterogeneous data and resource constraints in decentralized FL is important and worth addressing (R2).
- The modelling and analysis of DSGD with random computation and communication skipping are acknowledged as a contribution (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and differentiation**: Two reviewers (R2, R3) consider the idea incremental and insufficiently differentiated from existing asynchronous and sporadic communication methods. R2 notes that allowing local gradient updates to either happen or not without specifying an optimal rate/schedule does not represent a significant advance. R3 states the framework is incremental and does not introduce significant analytical challenges due to the uncorrelated random variables assumption.
- **Literature review gaps**: R3 points out that several pivotal works are omitted, including Srivastava and Nedic (2011), Zhao and Sayed (2014), Lian et al. (2018), Wang et al. (2017), and Liu et al. (2022).
- **Theoretical assumptions and scope**:
  - The analysis relies on strong convexity, which limits applicability to modern ML (largely non‑convex). The non‑convex experiments are not supported by theoretical guarantees (R2, R3).
  - The convergence rate \(\rho(\Phi)\) is not expressed in terms of problem parameters such as step size, number of devices, computation/communication probabilities, or graph connectivity. A bound in those terms is needed to understand the method’s behavior (R1, R3).
  - Only asymptotic rates are given; no transient rates are provided. The error only goes to zero if the step size is diminished (R3).
  - No theoretical comparison to standard DSGD or baselines (e.g., [1]) is provided (R1, R2, R3).
- **Empirical evaluation limitations**:
  - Only one small dataset is tested, limiting generalizability (R2).
  - Only 10 nodes are used; scalability to larger networks is unclear (R1).
  - Key baselines are missing: FedAvg (or a decentralized variant) is not included (R2, R3); AD‑PSGD and OSGP are missing (R1). The method does not appear to outperform Distributed Gradient Descent (DGD) in raw iterations (R2).
  - The performance improvements over existing communication‑efficient algorithms are not significant (R2).
- **Missing guidance and ablations**: No guidance is given for selecting the expected connectivity or the sporadicity of local updates. Ablation studies are needed (R2).
- **Presentation issues**: Pseudo‑code should be in the main text. The notation around equation (3) is unclear, and equations/notations are somewhat disorganized (R3).
- **No demonstrated theoretical advantage**: The analysis does not highlight any theoretical benefit from using sporadic updates compared to standard DSGD (R3).
- **Comparison concerns**: Reviewers ask how the suboptimality gap (Eq. (9)) compares to that of FedAvg under convex assumptions, and whether convergence can be shown in the non‑convex FOSP sense (R2). The influence of parameters on the convergence bound and how it compares to standard DSGD is also an open question (R3).
