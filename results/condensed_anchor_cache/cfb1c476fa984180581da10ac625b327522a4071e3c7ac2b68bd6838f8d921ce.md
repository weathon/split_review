## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a zero-shot RL method (“imagination engine”) that uses LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5) to generate diverse synthetic goal-directed dialogues (suboptimal but human-like), then trains a smaller conversational agent via offline RL to optimize multi-turn conversational outcomes. Experiments on teaching and preference elicitation tasks claim state-of-the-art performance over directly prompted LLMs and behaviour cloning. Reviewers find the idea interesting but raise concerns about novelty, evaluation subjectivity, missing task-oriented benchmarks, generalization to out-of-domain topics, and insufficient comparison with existing methods.

### Strengths
- The idea of using LLMs to imitate human behavior and generate diverse synthetic conversations for training is interesting (R1, R2, R3).  
- Shifts LLMs from direct interaction to data generation; introduces a zero-shot RL algorithm with an “imagination engine” that creatively creates synthetic conversation datasets (R2).  
- Detailed and methodical experimental analysis; the case is clear and intuitive (R1).  
- Empirically, agents trained this way outperform state-of-the-art LLMs in interactive tasks (R2) and produce responses that are more helpful and less overwhelming (R3).  
- Training a smaller agent to improve over generated goal-directed conversations is a useful approach (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution:** The method is essentially a pipeline of LLM generation plus offline RL training; limited novelty (R1). (Note: one reviewer (R2, score 6, contribution 3) was more positive about the approach, but the other two (R1, R3, scores 5, contribution 2) were more critical.)  
- **RL for long-term planning:** It is not convincing that offline RL can take all long-term planning responsibility in goal-oriented conversation tasks (R1).  
- **Evaluation subjectivity:** All evaluations are human, which is highly subjective (R1). Need details about evaluators (education background) (R1). Also, how were responses scored on the four criteria, and what was the inter-annotator agreement? From examples, GPT responses did not seem significantly inferior (R3).  
- **Missing benchmarks and comparisons:** The paper should use widely-used task-oriented dialogue benchmarks like MultiWOZ and SGD (R1, R3). Specifically, compare with [1] that trains a small model with RL to generate dialogue actions guiding LLMs (R3). More relevant works should be compared (R1).  
- **Dependence on human prompts:** The method still relies on human-generated prompts; automating zero-shot training without task-specific human input would be a further improvement (R2).  
- **Generalization to out-of-domain topics:** The smaller agent may handle specific domains but lacks the broad knowledge of LLMs; can it handle out-of-domain tasks? If not, its practical usefulness for real-world applications is questionable (R3).  
- **Unnecessarily complex terminology:** The data generation section introduces many RL concepts when it appears to be a simple data generation process, making it harder to understand (R3).  
- **Missing implementation details:** The specific version and hyper-parameter configurations of GPT-3.5 used in the imagination engine, and how these parameters affect dialogue quality and diversity, are not provided (R2).
