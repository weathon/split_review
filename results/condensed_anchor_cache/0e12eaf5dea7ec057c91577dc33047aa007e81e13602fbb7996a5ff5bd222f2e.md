## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper provides a theoretical explanation of robust overfitting in adversarial training (AT) by extending neural tangent kernel (NTK) theory to the AT setting. It proves that adversarially trained wide DNNs can be approximated by linearized DNNs, and derives closed-form AT dynamics for squared loss, revealing an “AT degeneration” phenomenon where prolonged AT causes the network to revert to a state similar to standard training, explaining robust overfitting. Based on this, the authors propose Adv-NTK, an AT algorithm for infinite-width DNNs. Experiments on SVHN and CIFAR-10 show Adv-NTK can achieve robustness comparable to finite-width DNNs, supporting the theoretical findings.

### Strengths

- Provides a novel theoretical perspective on robust overfitting by extending NTK theory to adversarial training and uncovering the AT degeneration phenomenon.
- Derives closed-form dynamics for squared loss, giving clear intuition on why AT fails in the NTK regime.
- Introduces the Adv-NTK algorithm, the first AT method for infinite-width DNNs, with empirical validation on real-world datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10) showing robustness comparable to finite-width counterparts.
- The approach of analyzing robust overfitting via a time-dependent regularizer matrix derived from the adversarial NTK is appreciated.
- Addresses an important and timely problem (robust overfitting) from a theoretically rigorous standpoint.
- One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the theoretical soundness and detailed explanation.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited practical scope**: The proposed Adv-NTK algorithm only works under the NTK regime (infinite-width, squared loss), and may have limited practical applicability; real-world DNNs use cross-entropy loss and stochastic gradient descent.
- **Strong assumptions**: The analysis relies on the NTK regime where the network is close to initialization. It is unclear whether robust networks exist in this regime (as noted by [1]), questioning the practical relevance of the setting. A more thorough justification of why the NTK regime is appropriate for studying AT is needed.
- **Perturbation size and attack learning rate**: The claim that an adversarially trained DNN can be approximated by a linearized DNN assumes a small perturbation size, but there is no quantitative analysis of how small it must be. The attack learning rate (η_S) and total attack steps (S) are not specified in magnitude; if the attack learning rate is infinitesimally small, the perturbation may be too small for meaningful robustness. The paper also lacks a guarantee that the gradient-flow-based adversarial example search satisfies the constraint ||x' - x|| ≤ ρ; the scale of perturbation depends on ∂_x L rather than S, introducing a discrepancy with the practical setting.
- **Validity of linearization approximation**: The paper does not clearly explain why the linearization approximation holds. Theorem 1 shows kernel convergence at initialization, but the time-constant properties of the kernels are only presented in Appendix C.2. An informal presentation of those results between Theorems 1 and 2 would improve clarity. The conditions under which a small step size leads to linearization are also not clearly stated.
- **Proof details and dependencies**: In Theorem 2, the dependency of “polyt” is inadequately defined—it depends on the norm of W_t, which could go to infinity. Lemma C.5 also relies on “polyt”, making the argument circular unless clarified. The proof does not attempt to find an efficient attack direction; it is unclear whether this affects the derived bounds. One reviewer asked if the derived formula’s proof is still not too loose despite using an additional learning rate term (Equation 7) instead of constrained-space conditions.
- **Missing analysis of η_s and S**: Section 5.1 lacks discussion of the impact of η_s (attack learning rate) and the total attack steps S on the AT degeneration phenomenon. If the adversarial training intensity is too high, results in Section 4 may not be valid.
- **Insufficient experiments to validate theory**: The experimental validation needs strengthening:
  - Robust accuracy on SVHN and CIFAR-10 is significantly lower than standard adversarial training; it is unclear whether this is due to the architecture (e.g., why not use ResNet?).
  - No experiment directly confirms the AT degeneration phenomenon (e.g., tracking robustness/loss trends over iterations for vanilla AT vs. Adv-NTK).
  - The connection between experiments and the theoretical results (theorems) is not clearly established.
  - The distinction between “AT degeneration” and “robust overfitting” is ambiguous; if they are synonymous, using separate terms is confusing.
- **Writing and motivation**: The paper focuses heavily on formulation and lacks emphasis on motivation and importance of the problem. More intuitive explanations of theorems and contributions are needed to improve readability.
- **Missing references**: Several missing references on convergence of DNNs ([1], [2], [3]).
- **Generalization to classification**: The paper only studies squared loss; it is unclear whether the results extend to the classification setting with cross-entropy loss (question raised by multiple reviewers).
