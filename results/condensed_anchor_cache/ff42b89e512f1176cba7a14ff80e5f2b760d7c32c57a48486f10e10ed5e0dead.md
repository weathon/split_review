## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically analyzes initialization schemes for state-space model (SSM) layers from three perspectives: the discretization timescale Δ, the real part of the eigenvalues of the state matrix, and the imaginary part. It characterizes the dependency of Δ on sequence length via input autocorrelation, shows that setting the real part to zero mitigates the curse of memory, and demonstrates that the imaginary part determines optimization conditioning and reveals an approximation-estimation tradeoff. The analysis is rigorous and supported by experiments on synthetic/ simple benchmark data. The work provides novel insights into SSM initialization, though the practical scope and coherence of the three components could be strengthened.

### Strengths
- The paper presents novel and interesting insights into SSM initialization schemes, particularly the roles of timescale, real part, and imaginary part.
- The analyses are well grounded, thorough, and rigorous, with clear explanations connecting theoretical results to initialization strategies.
- Claims are clear and are supported both theoretically and empirically (on synthetic/simple data).
- The topic is important as SSM-based models are increasingly used; the implications of the dynamical systems perspective are new and useful.
- The paper is well-structured, with illustrative examples that help clarify the theoretical concepts.

### Weaknesses
- Empirical evaluation is limited to synthetic or simple benchmark datasets; it remains unclear how the results generalize to larger-scale, real-world settings involving noise, non-stationarity, and high dimensionality. (Raised by all reviewers; Reviewer 2 noted this does not diminish the paper’s value, while Reviewers 1 and 3 considered it a significant limitation.)
- The paper focuses on initialization, but the analysis could also be used to constrain parameter values (e.g., ℜ(W)) during training. A discussion on whether the insights are only relevant at initialization or should be enforced throughout training would strengthen the work. (Reviewer 2)
- The three theoretical components (Δ, real part, imaginary part) feel somewhat separated; a stronger connection and discussion on how they interact and collectively affect model behavior would give the paper a more cohesive narrative. (Reviewer 3)
- The theoretical results are presented densely; more emphasis on distilling key messages and providing practical guidelines (e.g., a step-by-step recipe for selecting Δ, real components, and imaginary components for a given task) would enhance accessibility. (Reviewer 3)
- How are the expected to hold when sequence length varies? Can the authors extrapolate to settings where length is not fixed? (Reviewer 1)
- The analysis is restricted to the single-input single-output case. How would the results extend to multiple-input multiple-output settings (e.g., S5)? (Reviewer 3)
- The paper assumes ZOH discretization; how would the results differ with bilinear discretization? (Reviewer 3)
- Several questions regarding Theorem 4.1: (a) The upper bound is on the expected output – is there a corresponding probabilistic bound? (b) Can the upper bound be shown to be tight? (c) How does the real part of w_j affect the bound (increasing/decreasing)? (d) The analysis focuses on the final output; how would the results change if the sum of outputs ∑ y_ℓ² (as used in many SSMs) were considered? (Reviewer 3)
- Given a specific task, how should one determine the appropriate Δ? Is there a principled method beyond hyperparameter tuning? (Reviewer 3)
- When the real part of w is set to zero at initialization, is it still trained in practice? If so, is non-positivity enforced? How is reparameterization handled (e.g., not using logarithm)? Could the behavior of the real part after training be shown? If it becomes negative, what is the benefit of zero initialization? (Reviewer 3)
- In line 376, the analysis assumes c is real-valued, but many SSM implementations use a complex c. What necessitates this assumption? (Reviewer 3)
- Given a specific task, how should one determine the appropriate balance between approximation and estimation tradeoff—does this rely on theoretical insights or hyperparameter tuning? (Reviewer 3)
