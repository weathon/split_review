## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a mask-guided video generation method that uses foreground mask sequences and first-frame sharing to improve motion control and temporal consistency while requiring limited training data and a single GPU. The approach leverages a diffusion model with mask-aware attention and incremental generation for longer videos.

### Strengths
- **Mask guidance**: Multiple reviewers found the introduction of mask sequences into video generation beneficial. Reviewer 2 noted it "improves the performance of generated videos" via masked attention. Reviewer 4 considered it "novel for me as a video generation condition."
- **First-frame sharing and incremental generation**: Reviewer 1 highlighted both strategies for enhancing temporal consistency and enabling long video generation.
- **High-quality outputs**: Reviewer 3 stated "the showcased videos have high quality, benefiting from the pre-trained text-to-image generation model."
- **Technical clarity**: Reviewers 2 and 3 agreed the paper is well written and provides clear descriptions of the method and implementation details.
- **Efficiency**: Reviewer 4 appreciated that "this method requires only a small dataset for training."

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution and novelty**: Multiple reviewers considered the contribution insufficient. Reviewer 1 noted that first-frame sharing cannot compete with SOTA open-source models like CogVideoX and Open Sora, mask guidance is similar to ControlNet for conditional image generation, and incremental generation is already proposed in StreamingT2V. Reviewer 3 stated "this paper’s approach differs by introducing masks as a strong constraint... [it] merely combines LAMP with mask-guided generation in a straightforward manner." Reviewer 4 added that "generating videos using small datasets from stable diffusion is out of fashion; current SOTA methods directly generate videos from large-scale training." While Reviewer 4 found mask guidance novel, Reviewer 1 highlighted its prior art.
- **Practicality concerns due to mask dependency**: The requirement to provide motion masks during both training and inference severely limits real-world applicability. Reviewer 2 asked "How to get the motion masks for arbitrary videos?" and noted no sensitivity analysis to mask quality. Reviewer 3 emphasized that "users must not only train a new model for each video set but also should supply a mask sequence, combining the drawbacks of few-shot training and controllable video generation." Reviewer 4 similarly questioned the motivation, asking "Why do we need mask-aware video generation?" and "how to get diverse results when the mask is provided?"
- **Lack of video results for temporal comparison**: Reviewer 2 explicitly requested "video results to directly compare the temporal consistency of generated videos" and found the absence of such comparisons a major limitation. Reviewer 4 also asked "Where is the video demo?" This undermines the ability to assess temporal coherence. Reviewer 3, in contrast, praised the quality of the showcased videos, but the lack of direct video comparisons remains a critical gap noted by the majority.
- **Insufficient quantitative and ablation studies**: Reviewer 2 noted "there is no quantitative results in the ablation study" and recommended reporting metrics like FID. Reviewer 3 added that "the experimental results and analysis are quite limited" and specifically pointed out the lack of motion-specific evaluation in Table 1. Reviewer 4 also asked for "numerical results in the larger scale." Furthermore, Reviewer 3 questioned whether the motion quality stems from learned motion concepts or the constrained search space from the mask, and recommended ablations with perturbed masks.
- **Limited experimental scope and outdated comparisons**: Experiments mainly show single-subject videos. Reviewer 2 highlighted challenges for complex effects like "changing illuminations" or "variable number of subjects." Reviewer 3 noted no comparison with other mask-guided methods like FateZero. Reviewer 4 stated "the comparison methods are too old to evaluate the performance of this method." Reviewer 1 asked for comparisons with CogVideoX and Open Sora.
- **Separate training per motion pattern**: Reviewer 2 asked "Does the model need to be trained separately for each motion pattern?" – a concern echoed by Reviewer 3's criticism that the approach "requires retraining a new model on each set of videos of new motion concepts."
- **Weird task setting**: Reviewer 3 argued that few-shot training on a small set with shared motion concepts has already been addressed by Tune-A-Video and LAMP, and that this paper’s addition of masks "significantly limits the diversity of generated videos." The same reviewer suggested reorganizing the related work section to better contextualize the paper.
