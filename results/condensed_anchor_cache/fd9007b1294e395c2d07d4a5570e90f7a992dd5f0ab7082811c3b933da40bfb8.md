- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces AdaLip, an optimization-based framework for adversarial defense of GNNs via adaptive Lipschitz regularization, combining graph denoising and network regularization. Three reviewers give low scores (3 each), citing serious weaknesses in presentation, mathematics, and experimental evaluation; one reviewer gives a higher score (5), praising the unified framework and interpretability but also noting mathematical sloppiness and limited experiments. Overall, the method is seen as potentially interesting but poorly executed.

### Strengths
- Attempts to introduce an optimization-based framework for GNN robustness (Reviewer 1).
- Combines graph denoising and network regularization, offering flexibility through different loss functions (Reviewer 4).
- Derives the framework from an optimization perspective, providing interpretability (Reviewer 4).
- Considers both graph modification and injection attacks (Reviewer 3).
- Evaluates on both homophily and heterophily datasets (Reviewer 3).
- Results appear promising and the paper is easy to follow (Reviewer 2; though others note poor presentation).

### Weaknesses
1. **Notation and clarity problems** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):
   - Many undefined or inconsistently used symbols (e.g., $f$ in eq(2), $\lambda$ in eq(4), $\mathcal{X}$ in eq(6), $\theta(x,\Delta)$ in eq(6), $d$ in eq(7), $\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}$ in Lemma 2; $\theta$ used for both a function in eq(6) and parameters in eq(10); $\phi$, $\omega$, $E$, $L_q$ undefined; $\mathcal{N}$ in eq(1) undefined).
   - Terms introduced without explanation: “smoothness of vertices”, “data adaptivity”, “high-frequency components”, “adaLip w/o GD” in Figure 1, Remark 2 unclear.
   - Grammatical errors and missing punctuation after all equations (Reviewer 1).

2. **Imprecise or unjustified claims** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):
   - “Without loss of generality, the adaptive Lipschitz regularization in (9) can be equivalently replaced by a logarithmic counterpart” – no proof or justification; likely changes the optimization landscape (Reviewers 1, 4).
   - From eq(3) to eq(4), claimed as “rewritten” but eq(4) is a relaxed version; equivalence not shown, hyperparameter introduced without discussion (Reviewer 4).
   - “One effective method for enforcing Lipschitz constraints … using Projected Gradient Descent” – no citation given (Reviewer 1).
   - Unsupported claims: unclear why prior adversarial training (PGD) cannot be applied to different GNN architectures; claim of adaptability to various GNNs unconvincing as only GCN is tested; assertion that PGD is not suitable for solving eq(4) lacks empirical evidence (Reviewer 3).

3. **Theoretical issues** (All reviewers):
   - Theorem 1 derivation ambiguous, missing assumptions; $|X^{(0)}|_F=\sqrt{d}$ unjustified; proof assumes specific GCN structure but theorem claims generality (Reviewers 1, 4).
   - Lemma 1 proof lacks clarity, contains undefined terms (Reviewer 1).
   - Proof of Theorem 1: the first inequality drops $\sigma$ without justification (requires $\sigma$ to be 1‑Lipschitz) (Reviewer 4).
   - Upper bound in Theorem 1: tightness not discussed (Reviewer 3).
   - Optimization method requires a potential function but GCN is not guaranteed to be one; convergence not proven for the two-stage approach used in experiments (Reviewers 2, 4).

4. **Motivation and presentation** (Reviewers 1, 3):
   - Paper lacks clear structure and coherent motivation; lemmas (e.g., Lemma 1) appear disconnected from the narrative (Reviewer 1).
   - “Stable hypothesis” and “robustness of training across diverse architectures” need rigorous definitions (Reviewer 1).
   - “Adaptive attack” missing – only transfer attacks evaluated; true robustness requires attacks that exploit AdaLip’s specific mechanisms (Reviewer 3; reference: likely “Are Defenses for Graph Neural Networks Robust?”).

5. **Inadequate experimental evaluation** (All reviewers):
   - **Limited attack scenarios**: no poison or evasion attacks, no white‑box/black‑box differentiation; only transfer attacks (Reviewers 1, 3).
   - **Missing baselines**: 
     - Established Lipschitz regularization works for GNNs not compared (Reviewer 1).
     - “Robust Mid-Pass Filtering Graph Convolutional Networks” not included (Reviewer 2).
     - Defense methods designed for both homophily and heterophily (e.g., [2] in Reviewer 3) omitted.
   - **No adaptive attacks** (Reviewer 3).
   - **No computational cost or runtime analysis** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4; only Cora runtimes reported, insufficient).
   - **Scalability unclear** – experiments only on small graphs; no complexity analysis (Reviewer 4).
   - **No sensitivity analysis** for hyperparameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (Reviewer 3).
   - Claim about heterophily datasets unsupported by specific analysis or results in the experimental section (Reviewer 1).

6. **Minority reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 4 (score 5) is more positive, emphasizing the unified framework and interpretability, but still notes mathematical sloppiness, limited scalability, and theoretical flaws. The other three reviewers (scores 3) find the presentation, theory, and experiments seriously insufficient.
