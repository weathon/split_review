## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SENSEI, a model-based RL framework that uses VLM annotations to define semantic “interestingness” as an intrinsic reward, combined with epistemic uncertainty (Plan2Explore) via an adaptive weighting scheme. The approach follows two phases: pre-collecting a dataset of observations via self-supervised exploration, then training a reward model from VLM preference pairs (MOTIF-style), and finally distilling it into the world model (DreamerV3). Experiments on MiniHack and Robodesk show improvements over Plan2Explore and VLM-MOTIF.

### Strengths
- Valuable research direction: integrating human priors into RL agents via VLMs (R1, R3).
- The combination of intrinsic rewards from VLMs (semantic) and epistemic uncertainty is well-motivated and empirically effective; the adaptive solution is a nice contribution (R1, R4).
- The extension of MOTIF from LLMs to VLMs is natural and improves applicability beyond text-based environments (R4).
- The method is straightforward and reasonable; the paper is generally well-written, especially Sections 1 and 2 (R2, R4). Illustrations are clear and helpful (R1).
- Experiments are diverse and convincing, with solid empirical validation and sufficient ablation study (R3, R4). Good coverage of related work (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on domain-specific prompt engineering limits generality.** Reviewers note that the VLM is given manually crafted prompts that explicitly define what is “interesting” (e.g., “the robot arm is near the blue box”, “grasping the red ball” in Robodesk, Appendix C.3). This means the VLM is not inferring interestingness from visual input alone; the method may require re-engineering for each new environment, contradicting the claim of generality (R1, R4). R1 further argues the prompt is essentially a more specified version of the one used in MOTIF (asking for progress toward a goal), reducing distinction from prior work. R4 suggests leaning more on VLM inference to avoid such specific prompts, even at the cost of performance.
- **Dependence on pre-collected dataset and static reward model.** The reward model is trained only on data from an initial self-supervised exploration phase, so it is biased toward that distribution and cannot adapt to novel observations in more complex environments. This could lead to failure in large-scale settings (R4). Reviewer 4 suggests adding iterative phases of data collection and retraining to reduce bias.
- **High computational cost.** For Robodesk, the VLM was prompted 139,409 times to obtain preference annotations. Querying VLMs this often raises scalability and cost concerns for real-world applications (R4). Reviewer 3 also asks about training time and whether a smaller dataset (200K) could be used, especially for real-world embodied agents.
- **Method is largely a straightforward combination of existing techniques (Plan2Explore and MOTIF).** While this alone is not a rejection reason, several reviewers found the results unsurprising and lacking deeper insight (R2, R3). More extensive ablations are needed to justify design choices:
  * Is MOTIF the only way to define a semantic reward model, and why is it better than alternatives? (R2)
  * Is the two-phase design (separate pre-training stage) necessary? (R2)
  * Is the adaptive coefficient strategy (Eq. (7)) necessary and how sensitive is performance to it? (R2)
  * How sensitive is SENSEI to hyperparameters (quantile, batch size, learning rate, weight decay) – especially the individually tuned quantile values (0.75, 0.75, 0.80, 0.85 per Robodesk task)? Were baselines tuned with the same effort, and were tuning and evaluation runs decoupled? (R2)
  * Ablation on different prompt choices is missing (R1).
- **Limited baselines and experimental comparisons.** The main experiments compare only to ablations (Plan2Explore, VLM-MOTIF). Missing comparisons to other exploration methods (e.g., LEXA, PEG) and other VLM-based methods (e.g., ELLM, OMNI, LAMP) (R2, R1). R1 notes there is only one baseline in the main experiment.
- **Only 3 seeds** are used, making it difficult to evaluate the empirical contributions statistically (R2).
- **Possible performance gain from stronger VLMs not controlled.** Since MOTIF uses weaker VLMs, the improvement may be driven by the model choice rather than the proposed framework. An experiment with a less powerful VLM is needed to isolate the effect (R3).
- **Minor issues:** Line 146 contains a small error (“In reward training phase”) (R3, R4). The section title “UNLEASH YOUR SENSEI” is unprofessional (R3, R4).
- **Unaddressed or contradictory results:** In Figure 4, SENSEI performs worse than VLM-MOTIF on “at chest with key” in MiniHack. Reviewers question why the VLM preferences didn’t guide the agent to explore opening the chest (which should be more interesting), and whether annotations rated “key+chest” higher than an open chest (R4). Figure 5 lacks a VLM-MOTIF comparison that was present in Figure 4 (R4). It is unclear how the agent switches back to the semantic coefficient when it becomes interesting again after exploring uncertainty (R4).
