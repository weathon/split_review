## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MTGRN, a transformer-based model that infers cell-lineage gene regulatory networks (GRNs) from scRNA-seq data. It frames GRN inference as a multivariate time series forecasting problem: cells are ordered via pseudotime, and a model with causal temporal attention (capturing cell–cell dynamics) and spatial attention (using prior knowledge from NicheNet) learns an attention matrix representing weighted gene–gene edges. The method is evaluated on five datasets (including mouse embryonic stem cells and hematopoietic stem cells) against six baselines (GENIE3, GRNBoost2, NetREX, CEFCON, CellOracle, and one multimodal approach) and achieves superior AUPRC on most cell types. A perturbation experiment on Gata1 in mouse hematopoietic stem cells yields gene expression changes consistent with known differentiation effects.

### Strengths
- **Novel framing of GRN inference as multivariate time series forecasting**, enabling continuous modeling of differentiation dynamics instead of discrete cell-type clustering (Reviewers 1–4).
- **Well-written, clear, and easy to understand**; the algorithm is described sufficiently for reproduction (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Causal temporal attention and spatial attention using prior interaction networks** are smart and interesting architectural choices (Reviewer 3).
- **Interpretability methods to identify key transcription factors** (e.g., highlighting three genes in mouse ESC development) are valuable (Reviewer 1).
- **Outperforms six methods on five datasets**, including multimodal approaches, with consistent improvements (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Multiple datasets and baselines strengthen the evaluation (Reviewer 4).
- **Perturbation experiment on Gata1** accurately models erythrocyte differentiation changes, demonstrating practical utility and correspondence with known biology (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Choice of embryonic stem cells** appropriately showcases application to cell differentiation (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Likely circularity from prior knowledge** – The model uses the NicheNet network as prior during training and is evaluated on the ground-truth network from Pratapa et al. (2020). No analysis shows whether performance gains are due to overlap between NicheNet and the evaluation network. Edges present in the ground truth but absent from NicheNet can never be predicted. Baselines that do not use such prior (e.g., GENIE3, GRNBoost2) are not restricted to NicheNet edges, making the comparison unfair (Reviewers 1, 4; high severity – Reviewer 4 soundness 1).
- **No experiment ruling out that top-K edges simply reflect highly expressed genes/TFs** – A quantile plot of selected TFs/targets ranked by expression is needed to show the method adds value beyond picking top expressed genes (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental data does not match the claimed dynamic inference** – The method is evaluated on static datasets from different cell lines rather than truly dynamic or developmental time-series data, undermining the claim of capturing continuous dynamics (Reviewer 2). The authors could use time-series scRNA-seq datasets with known time points instead of learned pseudotime (Reviewer 4). Similarly, no metrics are reported for the gene expression forecasting task itself (e.g., Spearman/Pearson correlation) – only GRN accuracy is measured (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparison with latest state-of-the-art methods** (Reviewer 2).
- **Perturbation analysis only on Gata1** – Demonstrating robustness would require perturbing other known TFs and showing GRN changes. The analysis confirms known factors (Gata1) but does not validate predicted GRNs with previously unknown gene–gene interactions, even at lower confidence (Reviewer 3). The knockout experiment is biologically interesting but could be a separate paper; a more thorough technical analysis (e.g., ablation studies) would better support the main method (Reviewer 1).
- **Architectural details are incomplete** – Variables Q, K, V are not defined; the transformation from input shape \(G \times W \times d\) to a \(W \times W\) attention matrix in TemporalAttention is unclear; Q, K, V used in SpatialAttention appear to have different meanings (Reviewer 4). The model’s complexity may hinder replication; hyperparameter settings require more explanation (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited novelty** – The use of attention for GRN inference from scRNA-seq has been explored previously (Reference [1] cited by Reviewer 4), reducing the methodological novelty.
- **Learned pseudotime introduces extra hyperparameters and variance** (Reviewer 4).
