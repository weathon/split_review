## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a training-free masking method for style transfer in diffusion models to address content leakage from style-reference images. It builds on IP-Adapter by clustering the element-wise product of content and style features and masking out high-mean tokens. The paper includes theoretical justifications (divergence analysis) and experiments on CIFAR-10 and additional styles.

### Strengths
- The motivation is well elaborated; limitations of prior methods (IP-Adapter, InstantStyle) are clearly described, making the core problem easy to understand. [R2]
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow. [R1,R2,R3] One reviewer finds the writing good and the story complete, though some details are missing. [R4]
- The masking-based technique is intuitive, novel, and effective. [R1,R3,R4] Fig.3 clearly illustrates the difference from previous approaches. [R2] Removing tokens with high content–style correlation improves stylized image fidelity. [R1]
- Theoretical proofs (divergence comparison with InstantStyle) support the experiments. [R3,R4] One reviewer specifically notes the proofs are valid and well-support the claims. [R4]
- Experiment design is comprehensive overall, with good qualitative and quantitative performance. [R4] One reviewer (score 8) finds results superior to alternatives; others note limitations but still see promise.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** Compared to IP-Adapter and InstantStyle, the contribution of sampling masked features is not significant. Masking mechanisms have been shown effective in prior work for synthesizing high-quality images. [R2]
- **Token selection method is questionable and not clearly justified.**
    - Clustering is performed on the element-wise product \( e_1 \cdot e_2 \). Why this product? What is its relationship to the energy function? [R3] This approach risks disproportionately removing foreground style elements; a more nuanced analysis (e.g., spatial frequency or semantic content) is needed. [R1]
    - The selection criteria (how clustering works, how high-mean tokens are identified, and whether hyperparameters like cluster number \( K \) are consistent across all images) are not clearly described. [R1,R2,R4] Specifically, how does \( K \) affect performance? [R4]
- **The problem is not formally formulated.** It is unclear why masking achieves decoupling and what the formal definition of content leakage is. [R2] Theorem 1 shows smaller divergence, but smaller divergence does not necessarily imply better style alignment (factors like same background or content leakage could also reduce divergence). What is the definition of "style" in an image? [R4]
- **Inference speed is slower** due to the clustering algorithm. What is the actual inference time in practice? Could the clustering be replaced to improve efficiency? [R3] The paper claims efficiency but does not provide timing comparisons vs. baselines. [R2]
- **Evaluation is insufficient and comparisons are unfair.**
    - The method is only evaluated on CIFAR-10 with subjective metrics that are not clearly defined. Objective metrics (FID, LPIPS, CLIP score) are missing. The fidelity score depends on the chosen classifier; how does performance change with a different classifier? [R2, R4] More experiments on different datasets, especially high-resolution images, are needed. [R2]
    - Visual comparisons appear cherry-picked. StyleDrop and StyleShot focus on traditional painting style transfer, but the paper often uses photographic references – this mismatch makes the comparison unfair. Figure 8 compares with InstantStyle but not StyleShot, even though the proposed method builds on StyleShot’s architecture. [R1] One reviewer used the official StyleShot demo on the provided example and obtained better results than those shown in the paper. [R1] The paper should evaluate on existing StyleDrop/StyleShot benchmarks and include results there. [R1]
    - Figure 8 shows cases where InstantStyle gives better results (preserves content, fewer artifacts). Figure 1 shows that the proposed method also fails to produce visually plausible results preserving the style. [R1] Figure 5(a) shows image alignment dropping – why? [R4]
- **Missing comparisons with recent baselines** for content leakage: RB-Modulation (training-free, attention feature aggregation) and CSGO (trained style projection layer). Both are recent and relevant. [R4]
- **Technical details are missing or ambiguous.**
    - L229: should \( m^i \) be 1 or 0? [R4]
    - Figure 3(b) is not referenced but appears to be used in experiments. How is the linear layer in that figure used and how does it learn the content feature to be subtracted? [R4]
    - Binary classification evaluation (L369): what model and threshold were used? [R4]
    - Are style descriptions used in the prompt? [R4]
    - User study details (instructions, presentation, number of images per rater) are needed. With 10 content × 21 styles × 8 variations × 50 generations, the total is 84000 images; the paper’s description is unclear. [R4]
- **Minority reviewer (score 8) is more positive** about the overall performance and theoretical grounding, but still raises the above concerns about missing baselines, unclear ablation of K, and incomplete reporting of evaluation details. [R4] In contrast, reviewer 2 (score 6) is notably skeptical about novelty and experimental sufficiency. [R2]
