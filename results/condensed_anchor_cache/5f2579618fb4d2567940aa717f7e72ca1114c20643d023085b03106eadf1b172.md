## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the computational complexity of deciding injectivity, surjectivity, and verification for ReLU neural networks. It proves coNP-completeness for injectivity, coNP-hardness for verification over general “reasonable” input domains, and NP-completeness for surjectivity. It also provides a fixed-parameter tractable algorithm for injectivity of a single ReLU layer parameterized by input dimension, and links surjectivity to a zonotope containment problem. The reviewers agree that the topic is important and the results are novel, but they raise significant concerns about proof clarity, missing related work, insufficient empirical validation, and some question the fundamental significance of the injectivity problem itself.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses the previously open problem of the complexity of injectivity for ReLU networks, establishing coNP-completeness (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
- The connection between ReLU network surjectivity and zonotope containment is a novel insight linking neural network analysis to computational geometry (Reviewer 1).  
- The coNP-hardness result for verification is shown to be intrinsic to ReLU networks, not dependent on specific input domain assumptions (Reviewers 1,3).  
- The parameterized algorithm for injectivity in small input dimension provides a practical handle on an otherwise hard problem (Reviewer 1).  
- The paper poses interesting open problems for the community (Reviewer 2).  
- Many results appear novel, and intuition is provided throughout (Reviewer 3).  
- Some reviewers found the paper well-structured and the theoretical rigor apparent (Reviewer 1), though others disagreed (see Weaknesses).  

### Weaknesses
- **Proof clarity and rigor** (Reviewers 2,4):  
  - The proof sketches in the main paper are imprecise; for example, the sketch for Proposition 4 appears to show that non-injectivity is polynomial-time (by reducing to LP feasibility) rather than demonstrating coNP membership (Reviewer 2).  
  - Key terminology (e.g., “extreme ray”, “polyhedral fan”, “digraph”) is not defined before its first use, making arguments hard to follow (Reviewer 2).  
  - Notation such as $w(E(S,V\setminus S))$ is ambiguous (Reviewer 2).  
  - Reviewer 4 states that the writing makes it impossible to check the proofs end-to-end, even for a researcher experienced in theory.  
  - In contrast, Reviewer 1 describes the theoretical rigor as apparent and the proofs thorough, but still notes the paper is dense.  

- **Significance and validity of the injectivity problem** (Reviewer 4):  
  - Reviewer 4 argues that injectivity from $\mathbb{R}^m$ to $\mathbb{R}^d$ is either impossible when $m>d$, or easily checkable when at least one neuron can be dead (dead neurons imply non-injectivity). Moreover, deciding whether all neurons are active can be done precisely via IBP on a single linear layer. This suggests the claimed hardness may not hold in useful settings. Other reviewers did not raise this concern, but it is a fundamental challenge to the paper’s contribution.

- **Missing related work** (Reviewers 3,4):  
  - The paper does not connect to results on universal approximation with certified networks and interval relaxations (Baader et al. ICLR 2020, Zi et al. POPL 2022, Mirman et al. TMLR 2022, Baader et al. ICLR 2024). Reviewer 3 requests clarification of how the coNP-completeness result relates to existing NP-hardness for certifying neural network ranges (e.g., Zi et al.).  
  - Reviewer 4 criticizes that the paper cites “random papers” instead of established verification methods such as IBP, DeepPoly, DeepZ, cutting planes, multi-neuron certification, LiRPA, and CTBench.  
  - Reviewer 4 also disputes the claim that Katz et al.’s hardness result only applies to polyhedra; Katz et al. already showed hardness for polyhedra, so the generalization claim is not novel.  

- **Insufficient empirical validation** (Reviewer 1):  
  - The parameterized algorithm for injectivity is not implemented or tested on real or synthetic networks; experiments could validate runtime and the claim of fixed-parameter tractability.  

- **Lack of discussion on practical implications** (Reviewer 1):  
  - The paper does not suggest how practitioners should design networks to avoid hardness (e.g., architecture choices, dimensionality, depth).  
  - No guidance is given on how heuristic or approximate verification methods could be used in light of the results.  

- **Over-strong claims** (Reviewer 3):  
  - The conclusion’s statement that the results exclude polynomial-time algorithms may be too strong, as worst-case complexity is analyzed; the reviewer requests comparison with the universal approximation / certification literature.  

- **Accessibility and presentation** (Reviewers 2,3,4):  
  - The paper uses technical jargon without definitions (e.g., “polyhedral fan”, “extreme ray”, “digraph”) and proof sketches are insufficient for a general ICLR audience (Reviewer 2).  
  - Reviewer 4 finds the writing poor and suggests making the proofs more intuitive.  
  - Reviewer 3 encourages adding illustrations and intuition for graph-theoretic reductions.  

- **Minor issues** (Reviewers 2,3):  
  - Typos: “completenss” (line 242), “v^+, v^+” (line 440 should be v^-, v^+), missing definition of wlog, missed indexing in projection mappings.  
  - Definition 2 notation could be simplified; Observation 3 needs definition of extreme ray.  
  - Set $\mathcal{R}$ in line 168 is not well-defined because $\mathbf{r}_\rho$ is not unique.  
  - The recap of complexity classes (P, NP, coNP, XP, FPT) should appear earlier, before the terms XP and FPT are used in the introduction.
