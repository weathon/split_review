## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ExpoComm, a scalable communication protocol for multi-agent reinforcement learning that uses exponential graph topologies to enable rapid information dissemination among agents. It employs memory-based message processors and auxiliary tasks (global state grounding or contrastive learning) to align messages with global information. Experiments on MAgent gridworld (Battle, AdversarialPursuit) and Infrastructure Management Planning benchmarks show improved performance and zero-shot transferability. Reviewers consider the problem important and the graph topology innovation novel, with extensive baselines and ablations, but raise concerns about definition of communication overhead, incomplete evaluation (e.g., missing comparisons to broadcast/random graphs), limited analysis of scalability trends, potential learning instabilities, and unaddressed limitations (task-specific or adversarial settings).

### Strengths
- The problem of scalable communication in large-scale MARL is important and well-motivated.
- Using exponential graphs as a communication topology is a novel and innovative approach that balances dissemination speed and redundancy.
- The combination of memory-based message processing and auxiliary tasks (global state alignment, contrastive learning) to enhance message relevance is a strong contribution.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; the toy example and figures in Section 3.1 build clear intuition for exponential graph design.
- Extensive experiments with multiple baselines (MAGIC, DGN, CommFormer, ER, etc.) on MAgent and Infrastructure Management Planning environments.
- Zero-shot transferability experiments (for both K=log2N and K=1) demonstrate generalization.
- The one-peer version (K=1) of the exponential graph performs best despite linear scaling in communication cost.
- Ablation studies effectively support the necessity of the proposed components.

### Weaknesses
- **Communication overhead definition is ambiguous.** The paper should formally define "communication overhead" (e.g., number of messages, unique messages, or bandwidth). It is unclear whether ExpoComm reduces overhead compared to methods that communicate with a subset of peers (e.g., DGN). Table 1 lacks a direct comparison of message counts against these baselines. If ExpoComm does not result in fewer messages, the wording should be clarified. (Reviewer 4)
- **Scalability claim is not fully supported.** The performance gap between ExpoComm and baselines does not consistently increase with the number of agents in Figure 4, weakening the claim that the method excels specifically in large-scale settings. (Reviewer 1) Reviewer 3 echoes that the benefit is more obvious in AdversarialPursuit than Battle, and learning appears delayed in Battle.
- **Incomplete evaluation compared to standard broadcast/random baselines.** The paper should compare ExpoComm to traditional broadcast methods (e.g., CommNet) that propagate global information, and to random selection of communication peers (keeping the same number of peers as the exponential graph). (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing theoretical or motivational analysis.** It is unclear why the exponential graph is theoretically helpful beyond the toy example; random global communication was not tested in that toy example. A more formal argument or analysis is needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Auxiliary tasks may not be universally applicable.** The paper does not analyze how sensitive the auxiliary tasks are to different task characteristics (e.g., whether the global state is available). This limitation is not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **No discussion of potential message saturation or interference** as the number of agents or communication hops increases, which could impact performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of explicit limitations discussion.** Scenarios where exponential topology may not be ideal—such as tasks requiring task-specific pairwise communication links or settings with adversarial/non-cooperative agents—are not addressed. (Reviewer 2; echoed by Reviewer 3’s question on adapting to pairwise connectivity)
- **Section 3.3 presentation is unclear.** The prediction function f: what are the input and output spaces? Why a two-layer MLP? The time variable t' (line 354-355) is ambiguous—is it fixed or variable, and how is it sampled during training? (Reviewer 1)
- **Potential training instability**: In Figure 4(f) (test win rate for ExpoComm, presumably Battle environment), the win rate drops consistently after 4×10⁶ steps, which is concerning and requires investigation/hyperparameter analysis. (Reviewer 1)
- **CommFormer performs poorly in AdversarialPursuit 25-agent case**; no explanation is provided for why it does not generalize well there. (Reviewer 4)
- **ER and ExpoComm have very similar performance in the transferability case K=log₂N**; the reason for this similarity is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Only two K values explored (log₂N and 1)**; it is unclear whether intermediate or higher K values would behave differently. (Reviewer 4)
- **Possible labeling error in Figures 4 and 6**: The x-axis is labeled "test return" but appears to show training return; authors should clarify. (Reviewer 2)
- **Evaluation could be strengthened with more diverse environments**, particularly those with complex spatial structures or agent dynamics beyond MAgent and Infrastructure Management Planning. (Reviewer 2)
- **Contributions appear limited beyond the exponential graph**; the paper would benefit from clearer articulation of what is novel versus incremental. (Reviewer 3)
