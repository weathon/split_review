## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Isometric Diffusion, a method to regularize the latent space of diffusion models by enforcing an approximate isometry between the noise space (via stereographic projection of the hypersphere) and a feature space (h-space from the U-Net bottleneck). The isometry loss, derived using Riemannian metrics and Jacobian-based regularization, encourages a more disentangled and semantically linear latent space. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ, LSUN-Church, and LSUN-Bedrooms, evaluating interpolation, inversion, and linear editing. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and mathematically grounded, but raise significant concerns about motivation, evaluation quality, terminology, and performance trade-offs.

### Strengths
- This paper is a pioneer in addressing disentanglement in diffusion models, motivated by the observation that noise points lie on a hypersphere and naive interpolation fails. The method uses stereographic projection and Riemannian metrics to derive an isometry loss. (R2)
- The approach is pragmatic and easy to use with only two hyperparameters (regularization parameter and skip ratio), which are tunable according to the ablation study. (R2)
- Experiments demonstrate feasibility: the method unfolds a sphere perfectly in a 2D toy example (where an autoencoder fails) and produces better interpolation results quantitatively and qualitatively on four datasets while maintaining comparable FID scores (though one reviewer questions the "comparable" claim). (R2, partially R4)
- The mathematical explanation is simple, meaningful, and systematically explained; no significant errors were found. (R1, R4)
- An intuitive illustration of the effect of the proposed method in a simple setting is included (Fig. 4 and its description). (R3)
- The paper addresses an important problem: finding a way to leverage diffusion models for representation learning. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Ambiguous benefits of semantic linearity:** The advantage of having a semantically linear feature space via Slerp is unclear. The interpolation in Fig. 1 (young man → middle-aged man → young woman) shows some semantic progression, but it is not convincing that it goes beyond a smooth transition between different modes in the data distribution; it may not be truly linear in semantic space. (R1)
- **Limited applicability due to Gaussian noise assumption:** The method relies on the assumption that the deepest feature map has semantically locally linear properties, which holds only when the noise distribution is close to Gaussian (i.e., later stages of diffusion). This limitation restricts the method's applicability and leaves its performance in earlier stages unclear. (R1)
- **Connection between isometry and disentanglement not adequately explained:** The paper asserts that an isometric X‑space leads to disentanglement, but the reasoning is not rigorous given the varied definitions of disentanglement in generative modeling. More precise definitions and justification are needed. (R3)
- **Performance trade-offs are insufficiently addressed:** The proposal to impose isometry leads to degraded FID scores (Table 1 numbers are significantly worse than competing methods according to Reviewer 4, while Reviewer 2 calls them "comparable"). The paper should transparently acknowledge this gap and discuss the trade-off between isometry, disentanglement, and generation quality. (R3, R4)
- **Overclaiming perceptual relevance:** The paper claims that isometry yields a representation that reflects human perception, but there is no rigorous connection or empirical evidence linking isometry to human perceptual similarity. The paper should tone down these claims or support them with more rigorous arguments. (R4)
- **Method only regularized toward isometry, not perfect:** The communication implies the representation is an isometry, but it is only regularized toward being one. This distinction is crucial for understanding limitations. (R4)
- **Lack of comparison with related work:** The paper does not compare with [2] (which also addresses meaningful representations in diffusion models) and mentions Kwon et al. [1] but does not provide direct comparisons in interpolation or editing. Since the proposed method requires training from scratch, such comparisons are critical. (R3)
- **Missing discussion of Riemannian geometry literature:** The paper does not discuss existing work on equipping latent representations with Riemannian geometries (e.g., Arvanitidis et al., "Latent Space Oddity"), which shares similar tooling. (R4)
- **Evaluation relies heavily on subjective visual inspection:** Key claims (interpolation quality, disentanglement) are supported by visual examples (Fig. 1, Fig. 8) where it is often unclear which method is better (e.g., DDPM looks better to Reviewer 4 in Fig. 8). A more systematic evaluation with perceptual metrics or smoothness quantification is needed. Multiple reviewers call for quantitative metrics. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Confusing notations and missing definitions:**
  - The roles of $z$ and $z'$ are not clearly defined. (R3)
  - $f$ in Fig. 2 is defined as a mapping between Riemannian manifolds but its implementation is unclear; using a different letter from the encoder $s_\theta$ is recommended. (R3)
  - $\phi$ in Fig. 2 is not explained. (R3)
  - The input of $\mathcal{L}_{iso}$ is not explicitly stated. (R3)
  - The term 'isometry' is used extensively before being defined late in the paper; an early definition is needed. (R4)
- **Questions about the necessity of isometric X‑space:** Given that the H‑space in Kwon et al. is already a reliable semantic space, it is unclear why achieving isometry in X‑space is preferable, especially if it compromises generation quality. The paper should justify why direct manipulation in X‑space is advantageous over existing indirect manipulations via H‑space (which require two score model evaluations and extra network training). (R3)
- **Assumption that H‑space properties are preserved after retraining:** The method trains diffusion models from scratch with an additional objective, yet assumes the H‑space retains the same semantic properties as in Kwon et al. This assumption needs justification; the isometric objective might alter those properties. (R3)
- **Computational cost and approximation quality:**
  - The computational overhead of the isometry loss is not reported, and the accuracy of the stochastic trace estimator (used to approximate the trace of the Jacobian) is questioned. (R2, R4)
  - Reviewer 4 asks for experiments showing how much is lost through the efficient approximation in Eq. 7. (R4)
- **Specific figure and experiment concerns:**
  - In Fig. 1, the start and end points appear different across rows, making direct comparison between methods invalid. (R4)
  - In Fig. 4, panel (d) appears to show a highly distorted representation by tearing the sphere to fit Euclidean topology; the illustration is not clearly explained. (R4)
  - In Sec. 4.4, the meaning of "$p < 0.5$" is unclear. (R4)
  - The baseline interpolation is likely naive Slerp; comparison with Kwon et al. [1] as a baseline is mandatory. (R3)
  - In Fig. 8 (semantic editing), the starting/ending points should be the same across methods for fair comparison, and the method for finding the editing direction is not described. (R3)
- **Lack of evidence for disentanglement:** The paper claims better disentanglement but no quantitative measure is provided to support this claim; only interpolation results are shown. (R3)
- **Relation to existing isometric regularizers:** The proposed isometry loss in Section "Isometry Loss" is presented as entirely novel, but similar regularizers exist for autoencoder-like models (e.g., Lee et al.). How is this different? (R4)
- **Dimensionality reduction aspect:** Representation learning typically involves dimensionality reduction, but here the representation space has the same dimension as the input. Are there fewer 'interesting' directions? If so, most directions correspond to weird image changes? This is not addressed. (R4)
