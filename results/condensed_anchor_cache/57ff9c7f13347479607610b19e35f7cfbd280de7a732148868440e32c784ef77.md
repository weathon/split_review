## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Stein Mixture Inference (SMI), which extends Nonlinear Stein Variational Gradient Descent (NSVGD) to variational Bayes by letting each particle parameterize a component distribution in a mixture model. SMI optimizes an ELBO-like objective and aims to mitigate the variance collapse problem of SVGD. The method is validated on synthetic and UCI regression benchmarks, showing improved uncertainty quantification over standard SVGD and mean-field VI. Reviewer 1 considers the extension straightforward and effective, while others (R2, R3, R4) are more reserved, raising concerns about experimental scope, missing comparisons, and insufficient analysis of why the method works.

### Strengths
- The method is a novel and sound extension of SVGD/NSVGD, combining them with VI concepts (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear background and thorough summary of related work (R1, R2).
- The problem of variance collapse is important and compelling (R2).
- SMI is validated across synthetic and real datasets; on UCI regression benchmarks it achieves the best NLL in most cases compared to other Stein-based methods and OVI (R2, R3, R4).
- A user-friendly codebase is provided (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**: Experiments are limited to small-scale regression tasks with homoscedastic known observation noise; no classification or heteroscedastic regression (R1). Models are low-dimensional; no evaluation on high-dimensional posteriors (R2), deeper BNNs, or modern large benchmarks like posteriorDB (R4). No ablation on how performance degrades with increasing model depth/width (R1). R1 notes that results primarily show relative performance to prior SVGD methods, not absolute performance.
- **Missing important baselines and comparisons**:
  - No comparison with HMC (R1, R3) – e.g., on UCI datasets to see how close SMI comes to exact inference.
  - No comparison with MMD descent (Arbel et al., 2019; Ba et al., 2021) (R3).
  - No comparison with the resampling method from Ba et al. (2021) (R3). The authors exclude it citing computational infeasibility, but R3 questions this given available GPU resources (NVIDIA Quadro RTX 6000) and requests empirical justification or a theoretical discussion.
  - No comparison with ADVI with mixtures (R4). R4 notes that only mean-field VI is used as a baseline, which is weak.
  - The claim that “Annealing SVGD (ASVGD) is the only alternative directly addressing variance collapse” is disputed by R3, who notes that Ba et al. (2021) also targets this problem.
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis of why SMI mitigates variance collapse**:
  - It is not clear how repulsive forces in the kernelized SVGD framework interact with the variational distributions to prevent variance collapse (R3). The role of entropy regularization is ambiguous: Figure 2 shows variance collapse is mitigated even for small α, suggesting regularization may not be critical – this raises the question of whether the NSVGD framework is necessary at all (R3).
  - The paper lacks theoretical guidance on how performance depends on the number of particles m, making it difficult to choose m or understand trade-offs with computational cost (R2).
  - R4 argues that the claim “SMI is more particle-efficient than SVGD” essentially reduces to “VI with mixtures is more particle-efficient than SVGD”, undermining the need for Stein methods. Furthermore, using a parametric mixture is a strong assumption that contrasts with SVGD’s nonparametric appeal, especially when using fewer particles (R4).
- **Missing evaluation of posterior shape accuracy**: The paper demonstrates that SMI overcomes variance collapse, but does not assess whether the approximate distribution captures the true posterior shape (e.g., comparing estimated covariance matrix to target) (R2).
- **Missing runtime comparison**: There is no report of increased runtime costs compared to baselines (R1).
- **Minor issues**: 
  - Figure 2 is hard to read; lines for different methods (e.g., SMI₁, SMI₂₀) are difficult to distinguish (R2).
  - A citation for ReLU (Agarap, 2018) in line 478 is odd and unrelated (R1).
  - R4 notes the title “ELBOing Stein” is a misnomer; a discussion of [1] (a related mixture-based posterior approximation, reference unclear) is missing.
