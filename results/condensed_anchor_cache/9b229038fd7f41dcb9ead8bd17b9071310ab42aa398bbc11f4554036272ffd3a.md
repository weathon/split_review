## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper improves L2 deterministic certifiably robust training via three components: (1) adding additional dense layers to increase capacity, (2) using Cholesky-based orthogonal layers in the neck, and (3) data augmentation with a newer diffusion model (DDPM). The combination yields state-of-the-art VRA improvements of up to 8.5 percentage points across several benchmark datasets and perturbation sizes (e.g., +8% on CIFAR-10). The work also argues that prior Lipschitz-based certification evaluations were overly constrained by standardized benchmark design spaces, and that exploring architecture and data pipeline choices can further unlock performance.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a more comprehensive evaluation of the design space for Lipschitz-based certification methods, showing that prior work’s framing around single novel techniques limited performance discovery. (Reviewer 3)
- It discovers that adding large “Cholesky-orthogonalized residual dense” layers to the end of existing Lipschitz-controlled ResNet architectures is especially effective for increasing network capacity and performance. (Reviewer 3)
- The empirical improvement is significant and consistent across all tested datasets (e.g., +8% on CIFAR-10). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The work combines multiple techniques (architecture, orthogonal layer construction via Cholesky decomposition, filtered generative data augmentation) and demonstrates that this synthesis furthers state-of-the-art deterministic VRA by up to 8.5 percentage points. (Reviewer 3)
- It provides an overview of existing methods for controlling the Lipschitz constant and proposes a method that can be combined with other approaches. (Reviewer 3)
- It discusses the role of data augmentation (DDPM) in training high-capacity models and proposes an alteration to the augmentation strategy that further boosts performance. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper offers practical suggestions for robust training settings, including model architecture, orthogonalization method, and data augmentation. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- The paper largely appears to manually search for settings (model architecture, orthogonal layer type, diffusion model) and has engineering merits but lacks novelty: adding more dense layers is straightforward, and replacing the diffusion model already used in Hu et al., 2023 is not new. (Reviewer 1)
- The benefits of the best choices are not well explained. For example, the paper states that Cholesky-based orthogonalization is more numerically stable and faster but does not explain why it improves VRA. (Reviewer 1)
- Adding orthogonalized dense layers is not new; existing orthogonal layer methods are available. The comparison in Table 3 shows that the proposed Cholesky-based Orthogonal Layer is not clearly better than existing methods, suggesting the empirical improvement mainly comes from relatively trivial parts (especially adding additional layers) rather than from novel orthogonal layer design. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper lacks intuitions when designing the layers. (Reviewer 2)
- In Section 4.3, the paper mainly discusses comparison with RS-based methods, but Table 5 includes several other works that achieve better performance, and these works are not discussed in detail. (Reviewer 3)
- Missing training time and inference time for the new architecture. (Reviewer 2)
- A question remains: how does Cholesky-based orthogonalization help on VRA? (Reviewer 1, overlaps with lack of explanation)
