## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **FedQLoRA**, a framework for federated fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) where clients have different quantization levels. It identifies **quantization bias** when aggregating LoRA adapters in mixed quantization settings, and proposes a **quantization-aware adapter** to estimate and separate quantization error from the LoRA update. An **iterative version** alternates refinement of the quantization-aware and LoRA adapters to also address data heterogeneity (non-IID). The method is evaluated on **DistilBERT-base-multilingual-cased** (134 M parameters) using 2‑bit and 4‑bit quantization, compared against **LoRA**, **FFA‑LoRA**, and **H‑LoRA**. Code/data are released.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: First work to identify and address quantization bias in federated LLM fine-tuning with mixed quantization levels across clients. The quantization-aware adapter is a novel solution to separate quantization error from the LoRA adapter.
- **Quality**: Thorough comparison with multiple baselines (LoRA, FFA‑LoRA, H‑LoRA) on three datasets; results show better converged accuracy.
- **Clarity**: Well‑written, methodology is clearly articulated with mathematical formulations; easy to follow.
- **Significance**: Addresses a practical problem for resource‑constrained, heterogeneous federated settings; could enable more accurate yet privacy‑preserving distributed LLM training.
- **Reproducibility**: Code and data provided.

**Minority contrast**: Two reviewers (ratings 5) consider the work promising and well‑motivated, while two reviewers (ratings 3) find the scenario contrived and the evaluation insufficient.

### Weaknesses
1. **Contrived scenario**: The quantization bias scenario is uncommon in practice; quantization methods can be pre‑defined across clients. If resources differ, why not simply use the lowest quantization (e.g., 2‑bit for all) given that the “mix” setting performs worse than “all 2‑bit” (Table 1)? The practical motivation is weak.
2. **Limited model scale**: Experiments use **DistilBERT-base-multilingual-cased (134M)** , far from modern LLMs (billions). This conflicts with claims of “LLMs” and reduces the need for quantization. Evaluation should be extended to **LLaMA‑2‑7B** or **LLaMA3‑8B** on **natural language generation** tasks (e.g., question answering, summarization) as in [OpenFedLLM, FedLLM‑Bench].
3. **Small‑scale federated setup**: Only up to 10 clients; real FL deployments involve hundreds/thousands. Scalability in terms of number of clients and partial participation is not demonstrated.
4. **Lack of comparison with personalization approaches**: Quantization bias can be seen as a type of data/model heterogeneity. Existing **personalized FL methods + LoRA** should be compared.
5. **Unclear quantization error approximation**:
   - Why can a learned adapter approximate quantization error? No empirical correlation shown (e.g., compare with true quantization error computed from original float model).
   - The approach is conceptually similar to **LoftQ** (a QLoRA variant) which also compensates quantization error via SVD. How does FedQLoRA differ? Does initialization use LoftQ? If the unquantized model is inaccessible on the client, how is the SVD performed (on the server)?
6. **Insufficient experiments**:
   - Only one dataset domain (NLU classification); need **multiple datasets** and tasks.
   - Only DistilBERT tested; need **encoder‑only, encoder‑decoder, decoder‑only** architectures.
   - No experiments with **8‑bit quantization** or **varying bit‑widths**.
   - No **partial participation** scenarios.
7. **Missing analyses**:
   - **Memory footprint** and **training time** per client (critical for resource‑efficiency claims).
   - **Communication and computational overhead** compared to baselines.
   - **Theoretical convergence analysis** – no formal guarantees.
8. **Ambiguity in focus**: The paper addresses both quantization bias and heterogeneity bias, but they are fundamentally different issues (model vs. data heterogeneity). It is unclear which is the primary contribution and how the method separates them.
9. **Implementation details missing**:
   - Aggregation method used in experiments (FedAvg? Weighted?).
   - Initialization of LoRA and quantization-aware adapter – Fig. 1 suggests LoftQ‑style initialization, but text does not clarify.
10. **Writing / minor**: Line 178 “OPT1 in (19) and OPT2 in (20)” should reference equations; formatting needs fix.
