## Merged Review

### Summary

This theoretical paper studies multi-task reinforcement learning (RL) under non-Markovian decision processes, specifically low-rank and γ-well-conditioned predictive state representations (PSRs). It introduces the η-bracketing number to quantify joint model complexity and task similarity. An algorithm, UMT-PSR, is proposed for upstream multi-task learning, and its sample complexity is shown to benefit when the joint model class has a smaller η-bracketing number than the sum of single-task classes. For downstream learning, the paper applies OMLE to leverage upstream learned representations for faster learning of a new target task. Concrete examples (e.g., multi-task POMDPs sharing state space, action space, and transition kernel; low-rank PSRs with similar core-set matrices) illustrate the gains. The paper is entirely theoretical, providing upper bounds.

### Strengths

- Tackles a challenging and relatively unexplored setting: multi-task RL with PSRs/POMDPs (R2, R3, R4).
- Paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).
- Introduces the η-bracketing number as a key complexity measure that captures task similarity and quantifies when multi-task learning is beneficial (R1, R4).
- Provides a generic theoretical framework together with several concrete examples that demonstrate applicability (R2, R3).
- The theoretical results (Theorems 1 and 2) show clear sample complexity advantages of multi-task over single-task learning (R1, R3).
- Downstream analysis shows how upstream information can reduce sample complexity for a new task (R1, R2).
- The framework recovers the batch setting when all tasks are identical, which is a nice sanity check (R1).
- The combination of non-Markovian processes and multi-task RL is novel relative to prior work on MDPs and bandits (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- Reviewer 1 saw no weaknesses, but other reviewers raised several significant issues.
- **Downstream learning lacks novelty**: It simply applies OMLE on a reduced downstream model class, without introducing new ideas (R2).
- **Presentation and clarity are rushed**: Important assumptions (rank-r, γ-well-conditioned PSRs) and notation (φ_h, M_h, the norm ∥·∥_∞^p) are never adequately explained or given intuitive discussion; examples of PSRs that do or do not satisfy assumptions are missing, and what fails in proofs if each assumption is relaxed is not discussed (R3). The paper crams too much content into the main text without using the appendix to provide necessary detail (R3).
- **Literature review is incomplete**: The claim that “none of the existing studies considered multi-task POMDPs/PSRs” overlooks prior work (e.g., Li, Liao & Carin, JMLR 2009; Omidshafiei et al., ICML 2017) (R3).
- **Choice of upstream objective is not justified**: The paper only analyzes average performance over tasks (max_π (1/N) Σ_n V_n^π - V_n^π̂), rather than per-task worst-case guarantees; the rationale for this choice is missing (R3).
- **Definition of η-bracketing number is problematic**: For the model class {ℙ_θ : θ∈Θ} of probability measures over trajectories, the standard definition of a bracket (with pointwise inequalities on sets) would force the bracket size to be trivial, making the bracketing number equal to |Θ|. The paper’s calculation assumes finite observation and action spaces (Appendix E), but the general definition remains unclear (R3).
- **The norm ∥·∥_∞^p is used without justification**; it is not clear whether it satisfies the norm axioms (R3).
- **Discussion of pairwise additive distance in multi-task planning is insufficient**: A distance between product distributions appears sufficient, but the paper uses a different form; the relationship is not explained (R3).
- **Novelty of the η-bracketing number is limited**: It essentially reduces to a covering number of the joint model class, a standard tool in prior multi-task RL (e.g., linear setting reduces log covering number from nm to nk+km). The authors should clarify the precise relationship between bracketing numbers and standard covering numbers (R4).
- **Scope is limited to finite action and observation spaces**; generalization to infinite spaces or to structures beyond low-rank is not addressed (R2).
- **Algorithm is computationally inefficient**: The generic upstream algorithm relies on building a confidence set and performing optimistic planning; no optimization details are given for specific settings (e.g., multi-task POMDPs), making its practical feasibility unclear (R4).
- **Relationship between r (from Definition 1) and N is unclear**; the parameter r is used as a proxy for multiple tasks but could be formalized differently (R1).
