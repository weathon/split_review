## Merged Review

### Summary
ZipVL proposes an efficient inference framework for LVLMs that combines dynamic token sparsification (prefill phase) with mixed‑precision KV‑cache quantization (decoding phase). It uses a layer‑wise adaptive ratio to select important tokens based on normalised attention scores, reducing both compute and memory. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem and the promising results, but raise major concerns about limited novelty, missing evaluations (decoding latency/throughput, overhead analysis, hyperparameter sensitivity), weak baselines, and insufficient comparison with prior work. After merging, two reviewers (scores 5) were more positive while two (scores 3) were significantly more critical.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important and urgent problem: computational and memory bottlenecks in large vision‑language models.
- The motivation for using an adaptive, layer‑wise token selection ratio is solid and well‑presented.
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow.
- Evaluations show the approach works: prefill latency improved by 2.6×, GPU memory reduced by 50%, with minimal accuracy loss (0.2% on Video‑MME over LongVA‑7B).
- A good mix of models (LLaVA‑v1.5‑7B, LLaVA‑NeXT‑7B, LongVA‑7B) and benchmarks (Video‑MME, MMBench, SEED, GQA, etc.) is used.
- Results demonstrate superiority over fixed‑ratio methods (e.g., FastV) in terms of model accuracy.
- Related works section discusses recent works and highlights their drawbacks.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** – Sparsity and mixed‑precision KV‑cache quantization are well‑known techniques. The KV‑cache compression method is nearly identical to that in ZipCache (2024); the only difference is the token selection ratio. Prior works such as PyramidInfer and PyramidKV also emphasise layer‑wise sparsity (though in LLMs).
- **Missing evaluations on decoding** – No results for decoding latency or throughput; it is unclear whether the overhead of KV‑cache compression (e.g., Equations 6–8) is included in the reported times.
- **Overhead of token selection is not quantified** – The paper claims the overhead is “negligible” for large contexts, but no breakdown of execution time is given. It is unclear whether sorting (required by Equations 6 and 8) runs on CPU or GPU, and how much it contributes. For shorter context lengths fixed‑ratio schemes might be preferable, but this is not explored.
- **Insufficient hyperparameter analysis** – Two key hyperparameters are introduced: τ (threshold for important token selection) and the decoding compression interval (set to 100 in Algorithm 1). Only τ is partially evaluated, and only on a single model (LLaVA‑v1.5‑7B) on GQA. The impact of varying the compression interval on accuracy, latency, and throughput is not reported. The choice of τ is not shown for other models or tasks.
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons** – Baselines in Section 5 do not include PyramidInfer or PyramidKV, which also use layer‑wise ratio assignments. A comparison with a fixed‑ratio scheme (e.g., FastV) in latency benchmarks (Figure 6) is missing. Latency information for the accuracy tables (Tables 1–3) is not provided, so it is impossible to judge whether the reported accuracy gains come at the cost of slower inference.
- **Insufficient explanation of the probe‑token approximation** – It is unclear how 64 recent + 64 random tokens can adequately approximate the full attention matrix for very long contexts, and how the attention scores of all tokens are derived from these probes. The reliability of the selected attention scores for guiding token importance is not justified.
- **LVLM‑specific properties not well addressed** – The paper does not detail how attention differs between vision tokens and text tokens, or how the concatenation of vision tokens during prefill influences decoding. The claim that the method works for both LLMs and LVLMs (Table 3) raises the question of why the target system is limited to LVLMs.
- **Risk of dropping important tokens in decoding** – Using only the attention score of the last token to evaluate existing tokens may unexpectedly discard key tokens.
- **KV‑cache compression not separately evaluated on LVLMs** – The compression method is tested only on LLMs; its effectiveness on LVLM models (which are the main proposal) is not shown.
- **Clarity issues** – Figure 1: the differences between subfigures are not visually apparent because the dark‑blue areas dominate. The order of operations (how attention can be performed “solely” on important tokens before the attention scores needed to determine importance are computed) is confusing.
- **Supplemental material is very limited** beyond code.

*Reviewer disagreement*: Two reviewers rated the paper 5, considering the adaptive ratio scheme and positive results sufficient contribution. The other two gave a rating of 3, pointing to the lack of novelty, missing ablation studies, and insufficient comparison with existing methods. These conflicting assessments reflect a divergence on whether the incremental improvements over prior work (ZipCache, PyramidInfer, etc.) justify acceptance.
