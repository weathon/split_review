## Merged Review

### Summary
MME-RealWorld is a large-scale, fully manually annotated benchmark designed to evaluate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in high-resolution real-world scenarios. It contains 13,366 high-resolution images and 29,429 QA pairs across 43 subtasks and 5 real-world scenarios (e.g., OCR in the Wild, Video Monitoring, Autonomous Driving), making it the largest manually annotated benchmark to date. Annotations are rigorously cross-checked by a team of 25 annotators and 7 experts. The dataset also includes a Chinese subset of 5,917 QA pairs to address cultural and translation gaps. The authors evaluate 29 MLLMs (4 closed-source, 25 open-source) and report that even the most advanced models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) fail to exceed 60% accuracy, revealing significant limitations in fine-grained perception and dynamic information understanding.

### Strengths
- **Largest fully manually annotated benchmark for MLLMs**: 13,366 high-resolution images, 29,429 QA pairs, 43 subtasks across 5 real-world scenarios (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5).
- **Highest resolution among manually annotated benchmarks** – critical for domains like autonomous driving and monitoring (Reviewers 1,4,5).
- **Comprehensive evaluation of 29 MLLMs** including closed-source (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) and open-source models (Reviewers 2,3,5).
- **Reveals key insights**: no model surpasses 60% accuracy; models lack high-resolution detail perception and dynamic information understanding; biases exist across model sources (Reviewers 1,5).
- **Novel real-world scenario focus** (OCR in the Wild, Video Monitoring, Autonomous Driving) is valuable for practical deployment (Reviewer 3).
- **Detailed comparisons with existing benchmarks** (Table 2) and solid experimental analysis provide clear differentiation and statistical indicators (Reviewers 3,5).
- **High-quality annotations** due to professional manual annotation and double-checking by a dedicated team (Reviewer 4).
- **Dedicated Chinese subset** (5,917 QA pairs) avoids translation and cultural issues common in other datasets (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited diversity and domain bias**: The benchmark overemphasizes autonomous driving and monitoring; it is unclear why these areas were chosen over others (e.g., robotics for embodied understanding). This may introduce bias toward specific domains (Reviewer 4). A comparative analysis of image/QA/answer-type distributions with other benchmarks is requested to verify diversity (Reviewer 1).
- **Restricted evaluation format**: Reliance on multiple-choice questions (with a fifth “E” option) limits assessment depth; open-ended evaluation would better capture model understanding (Reviewer 4). Detailed analysis of when and why models choose option “E” is needed (Reviewer 1). Furthermore, the claim that higher frequency of “E” outputs is due to limited image perception is not sufficiently justified (Reviewer 2).
- **Shallow analysis**: The paper largely summarizes results without providing in-depth analysis of model failure modes. The discussion of “limitations in understanding dynamic information” lacks qualitative or quantitative evidence (Reviewer 2). More insights into future directions are expected.
- **Limited task difficulty**: Questions focus primarily on image content recognition and simple single-step reasoning, which reduces the requirement for deeper understanding capabilities (Reviewer 5).
- **Unaddressed resolution-induced errors**: For models with fixed input resolution, directly resizing images may lose information needed to answer questions. It is unclear whether errors stem from missing information at input or inability to locate correct information in complex content (Reviewer 5). Adding the maximum supported resolution of each baseline model to tables would improve readability (Reviewer 5).
- **Scalability concerns**: Heavy reliance on full human annotation restricts both dataset size and language coverage (currently only English and Chinese). The pipeline appears manual and not easily scalable to more languages or data sources (Reviewer 4). A smaller, curated mini-set balancing task types and difficulty is recommended for community convenience (Reviewer 1).
- **Knowledge leakage not discussed**: The benchmark does not consider potential knowledge leakage from LVLMs that may have been exposed to similar data (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues**: 
  - The claim of collecting from “more than 300K sources” is questionable; the collection timeline is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 2a would benefit from explicit domain/task distribution plots; Section 3.3 needs a paragraph title for the last paragraph (Reviewer 2).
  - Possible typo in line 315: “InternVL-2 demonstrates the strongest perception abilities” versus Table 3 showing Qwen2-VL as top performer (Reviewer 5).
- **High computational resource requirement** for evaluation may limit accessibility for researchers with fewer resources (Reviewer 3).
