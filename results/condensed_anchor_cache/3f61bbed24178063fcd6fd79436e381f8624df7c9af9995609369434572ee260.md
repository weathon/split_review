## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OPTQ-RD, a post-training compression method that extends OPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) by introducing a rate-distortion trade-off via a bit-cost penalty derived from the entropy model of NNCodec (Becking et al., 2023). The Lagrange multiplier balances quantization fidelity and compression rate, guided by layer-wise Hessian information. Evaluations on ResNet and VGG16 models using CIFAR-10 and ImageNet show that OPTQ-RD achieves higher compression (up to 2.7× smaller file sizes than NNCodec) while maintaining accuracy, supporting both accelerated inference and reduced storage. Reviewer ratings were 6, 5, 6, 3, indicating substantial disagreement about the experimental scope and novelty.

### Strengths
- The combination of quantization (for faster inference) and compression (for storage) is a straightforward and interesting idea, bridging two research lines (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- The method works post-training with minimal calibration data and does not require architecture modifications, making it practical for deployment (Reviewer 1).
- The integration of the entropy model (DeepCABAC) into the quantization process to guide the rate-distortion trade-off is a meaningful technical contribution (Reviewer 2).
- The penalty works as hypothesized: adjusting the Lagrange multiplier effectively controls the trade-off between compression rate and model quality (Reviewer 4).
- In the evaluated settings (ResNet, VGG16 on CIFAR-10/ImageNet), OPTQ-RD consistently outperforms the baselines (NNCodec, OPTQ+DeepCABAC) in weight compression while preserving accuracy on par with them (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well written and structured (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental scope and generalizability** – Experiments are limited to VGG16 and ResNet on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet (reviewers 1, 2, 4). No tests on modern architectures (e.g., Transformers, MobileNets, EfficientNet) or other domains (text, audio) (reviewers 2, 4). VGG16 is known to have sparse weights, making generalization to denser architectures questionable (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 specifically demands tests on MobileNets (challenging benchmarks) and suggests EfficientNet. Reviewer 4 also notes that the evaluation is only on image classification, not representative of the breadth of applications where model compression is critical.
- **Missing comparisons with relevant baselines** – Comparison is limited to a few methods (Table 1) and does not include previous works that address the same problem (reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 1 cites missing references [1,2]; Reviewer 2 points out no comparison with methods combining quantization, pruning, or knowledge distillation; Reviewer 4 notes the absence of Deep Compression [1] (a seminal work reporting significant compression on VGG16), making the uplift unclear. Reviewer 2 also criticizes that the related work section lists techniques without comparing against them.
- **Novelty is incremental** – The main components (OPTQ and DeepCABAC/NNCodec) are published and well-known; the paper merely combines them with a Lagrange formulation (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 also states that it is unclear what the unique contribution is, and the paper does not clearly articulate how it differs from existing approaches.
- **No validation on large language models (LLMs)** – OPTQ was designed for LLMs, and the abstract and introduction discuss LLM quantization, yet the paper only evaluates on CNNs (reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 explicitly asks for tests on small language models (e.g., Pythia-70M, comparable to ResNet50 in size) and notes that the authors’ acknowledgment of LLM challenges implies a degradation that should be characterized. Reviewer 1 questions compatibility with various quantization schemes for LLMs.
- **Inference speed claims are unsupported for CNNs** – Only weights are quantized; activations remain floating point. For CNNs, this typically yields minimal inference time reduction (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 also questions whether the method benefits inference speed. The paper does not provide latency or energy metrics (Reviewer 1).
- **Weaknesses in reporting and analysis** – The design space for the Pareto frontier is unclear (Reviewer 4). The baseline quantization levels (bit widths) for OPTQ are not disclosed; Reviewer 4 asks whether weights+activations were quantized to 4 bits, not just 8 bits, and notes that a compression penalty would be unsurprising at 8-bit. Bits-per-weight is not linked to practical storage or latency (Reviewer 1) nor to actual file size (Reviewer 3). The improvement over vanilla OPTQ+DeepCABAC is only a narrow range (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing analysis of key aspects** – No exploration of how the entropy model choice impacts results (Reviewer 1), how the method handles outliers in weight distributions (Reviewer 1), or the trade-offs in calibration set size (Reviewer 2). The abstract’s mention of LLMs is misleading given the limited experiments (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 requests an explanation of how the design space for the Pareto frontier is constructed.
