## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dataset Quantization V2 (DQ V2), which replaces the pre-trained MAE model used in original DQ with a random CNN-based data augmentation strategy (Tobias). This augmentation mimics pixel compression and reconstruction by preserving semantic regions and replacing backgrounds, aiming to reduce computational overhead while maintaining or improving performance. Evaluated on ImageNette, CUB-200, Food-101, and ImageNet-30, DQ V2 achieves comparable or better accuracy than original DQ (average +1.57%). However, all four reviewers raise critical concerns about missing comparisons, incomplete computational analysis, unclear motivation, and lack of evaluation on large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1k. All reviewers gave the same score (5), but differ in the severity of specific criticisms: Reviewer 2 is most skeptical about the method’s core motivation and logic, while Reviewers 1 and 3 emphasize missing baselines and analysis; Reviewer 4 focuses on scalability and outdated comparisons.

### Strengths
1. **Removal of pre-trained MAE reduces reliance on large models and achieves comparable/better performance without them.** This is confirmed across multiple datasets (ImageNette, CUB-200, Food-101, ImageNet-30) and is viewed as an interesting and practical insight. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
2. **Good insight that the pre-trained MAE acts like a data augmentation method** by introducing prior knowledge and implicit regularization; the paper replaces this with a simpler CNN-based augmentation that simulates pixel compression and reconstruction. (R1, R3)
3. **Extensive ablation studies** on method-specific parameters, including patch size and data selection methods, providing empirical tuning insight. (R2)
4. **Clear and well-organized writing** that is easy to follow. (R1, R3, R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing computational cost analysis.** The paper claims efficiency gains but provides no quantitative comparison of GPU hours, memory usage, training time, parameter count, or FLOPs between original DQ (using MAE) and DQ V2 (using random ResNet). Both the coreset selection overhead and final model training costs should be reported. (R1, R3, R4)
2. **Unclear motivation.** The original DQ’s key value is reducing dataset size and storage, but DQ V2 appears to increase storage (by including augmented images in the coreset) without clearly identifying any negative effects of MAE that motivate the change. The authors do not demonstrate any significant drawback to using MAE in practice. (R2)
3. **Confusing method logic.** The augmentation step (Tobias) is applied before coreset selection, leading to a final coreset that contains both original and augmented images. This is an unfair comparison with standard coreset methods that select only original images. It is also unclear whether adding Tobias augmentation directly to the original DQ pipeline (after selection) would be equally effective. (R2, R3)
   - Question: Does the augmentation significantly increase the dataset size, potentially resulting in similar training costs as regular (full-dataset) training? (R4)
4. **Missing comparison with recent strong baselines.** The paper compares only with methods from 2021 (GraNd, Grad-Match, GC, etc.) and omits newer state-of-the-art approaches such as D2 Pruning, CCS (Continuous Coreset Selection), Moderate Coreset, Tan et al., Yang et al., Maharana et al., etc. This limits the assessment of the method’s current competitiveness. (R1, R3, R4)
5. **No evaluation on ImageNet-1k.** The paper tests only on ImageNet subsets (ImageNette, ImageNet-30) while existing coreset selection methods (including DQ) are typically evaluated on full ImageNet-1k. The lack of large-scale evaluation makes it difficult to assess scalability and real-world applicability. (R1, R3, R4)
6. **Potential semantic ambiguity in background replacement.** The Tobias augmentation replaces image backgrounds, but the paper does not detail how patches are shuffled or scaled, nor whether new backgrounds could inadvertently introduce other objects that alter semantics. (R3)
7. **Line 210 conclusion may be biased.** The authors attribute MAE’s better reconstruction on ImageNette (vs. smaller datasets) purely to dataset size, but MAE was pretrained on ImageNet, so the comparison is confounded by the effectiveness of MAE itself, not just dataset size. (R2)
8. **Missing ablation study on the augmentation method itself.** The paper does not compare against no augmentation or alternative augmentation techniques, nor does it isolate the effect of masking strategies and background replacement on final performance. (R1)
9. **Incomplete literature review.** Several closely related recent works on data pruning and coreset selection are not discussed (Tan et al., Xia et al., Yang et al., Maharana et al., Yang et al.), which affects the context and clarity of the contribution. (R3)
