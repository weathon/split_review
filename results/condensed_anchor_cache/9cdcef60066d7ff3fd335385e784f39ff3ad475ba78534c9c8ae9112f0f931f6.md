## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CAB-KGC, a text-based KGC method that finetunes BERT using the head entity, relation, and their neighboring contexts to predict tail entities, thereby eliminating reliance on entity descriptions and negative triplet sampling. It also introduces the EDAS evaluation metric to provide a more comprehensive assessment across multiple metrics. On FB15k-237, WN18RR, CoDEx-S, and ConceptNet100K, CAB-KGC reports improvements in Hit@1 of 6.88%, 14.32%, and 17.13% on the latter three datasets, and is ranked top by EDAS. The authors claim the method reduces computational complexity and outperforms state-of-the-art baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach using contextual information without negative sampling or entity descriptions**: CAB-KGC leverages neighboring entities and relations to provide contextual signals, sidestepping the common reliance on external entity descriptions and the high overhead of negative triplet sampling. This design makes the method applicable to KGs lacking descriptions and leads to more efficient training (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).
- **Introduction of the EDAS metric for comprehensive evaluation**: EDAS (Evaluation based on Distance from Average Solution) is a novel criterion that considers both positive and negative deviations from average performance across multiple metrics, offering a potential improvement over simpler ranking metrics for KGC (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Strong experimental results on standard benchmarks**: CAB-KGC achieves notable improvements in Hit@1 on WN18RR (6.88%), CoDEx-S (14.32%), and ConceptNet100K (17.13%) over several baselines, with competitive MRR and other metrics on FB15k-237 and WN18RR (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Clear problem definition and well-identified limitations**: The paper clearly outlines the limitations of existing text-based methods (e.g., negative sampling overhead, dependence on entity descriptions) and positions CAB-KGC to address them (Reviewers 5).
- **Elimination of negative sampling improves training speed and robustness**: By using maximum likelihood estimation (cross-entropy) instead of contrastive loss, CAB-KGC avoids the computational burden and sensitivity to negative sample selection (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Thorough experimentation on standard benchmarks**: The paper conducts extensive experiments on FB15k-237 and WN18RR, including comparisons with multiple state-of-the-art methods and ablation studies in the appendix (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: Several reviewers find the method to be a straightforward application of BERT – concatenating head entity, relation, and their contexts, then computing probabilities over all tail entities. Compared to prior work like SimKGC, the only major differences are the removal of head entity descriptions and the use of a classification loss instead of contrastive loss. The approach is considered incremental and not sufficiently innovative for a top venue (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Insufficient methodological clarity**:
    - The core methodology is vague; it is not clearly explained how contextual information is extracted from neighboring nodes and relations, nor how it is encoded and integrated with BERT (Reviewers 1, 2).
    - The paper does not explicitly state how the output of the language model (likely the CLS token embedding) is used for multi-class classification to compute tail entity probabilities. This lack of detail hinders reproducibility (Reviewers 2, 4).
    - There is inconsistent symbolic notation: the introduction uses $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{R}$, while the methodology uses $E$ and $R$; the knowledge graph $G$ is defined with three different symbol expressions in the problem formulation (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Weak motivation and insufficient validation of EDAS**:
    - The paper fails to clearly justify why EDAS is needed over traditional metrics like MRR and Hit@k; it does not provide a detailed analysis of specific biases or shortcomings of existing metrics that EDAS addresses, nor a rigorous comparison demonstrating its advantages (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Inadequate experimental validation**:
    - **Limited datasets**: Experiments are conducted only on FB15k-237 and WN18RR (plus small ablation datasets). Larger and more complex datasets (e.g., Wikidata5M) and datasets with entity descriptions are missing, limiting generalizability claims (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5).
    - **Missing commonly reported metric**: Hits@10 is not reported; the paper should either provide these results or discuss why they are omitted (Reviewer 5).
    - **Unfair training epoch setting**: The authors set 30 epochs for CAB-KGC and all baselines, which may disadvantage models that require more epochs to converge (Reviewer 4).
    - **Missing comparisons to stronger SOTA methods**:
        - NBF-Net (NeurIPS 2021), a GNN-based method, achieves Hits@1 of 0.321 on FB15k-237 and 0.497 on WN18RR – higher than CAB-KGC’s reported numbers. KICGPT (EMNLP 2023 Findings) achieves Hits@1 0.327 and MRR 0.412 on FB15k-237, also outperforming CAB-KGC. The paper does not discuss these non-concurrent SOTAs and thus has an incomplete SOTA comparison, making the claim of “SOTA” inappropriate (Reviewer 5).
    - **Incomplete baseline reporting**: The full performance of KICGPT is not presented (Reviewer 5).
    - **No computational cost comparison**: Despite repeatedly emphasizing efficiency, the paper does not include any runtime or time complexity comparisons between CAB-KGC and baselines (Reviewer 3).
    - **Ablation study shortcomings**: Ablation results are reported only in the appendix, not in the main text; there is inadequate explanation of the ablation results, and ablation experiments are performed on small datasets (NATION, LOCATION, COUNTRY, SPORT, UML) rather than main benchmarks, raising questions about that choice (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Significant presentation and formatting issues**:
    - Figures are poorly labeled and unclear; Figure 1 lacks annotations, text alignment is off, and there are misaligned graphics (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
    - Table line thickness is inconsistent; the acknowledgment template text is left at the end of the main PDF (Reviewer 1).
    - Numerous errors in details: the Hadamard product symbol in RotatE’s score function is incorrect; there is an undefined symbol $g$ in Equation (11); citations for ChatGPT zero-shot and one-shot in Table 2 are incorrect; duplicated references in the list (e.g., Conv2E, KICGPT appear two-three times) (Reviewers 2, 5).
    - LaTeX errors: “Equations 1-??” on page 4; misuse of $\epsilon$ instead of $\in$ for set membership (Reviewer 5).
    - Grammar and typographical errors throughout (Reviewer 4).
    - The main text is slightly shorter than the venue’s suggested page limit, possibly indicating missing details (Reviewer 1).
- **Imbalanced content focus**: The paper devotes disproportionate space to the EDAS metric, detracting from a more thorough explanation and analysis of the CAB-KGC model itself (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor classification issue**: References to BERT as an LLM are inaccurate; “text-based” is the correct term given BERT’s parameter count (Reviewer 5).
