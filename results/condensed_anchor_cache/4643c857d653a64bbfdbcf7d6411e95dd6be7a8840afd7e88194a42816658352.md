## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CRL-Net, a deep network for accelerated multi-coil MRI reconstruction that learns coil representations via a coil-wise encoder and a coil-attention module, combined with coil sensitivity map estimation. Experiments on the fastMRI dataset (knee and brain, 4× and 8× acceleration) show improved quantitative metrics. Reviewers find the topic well-motivated but raise substantial concerns about novelty, evaluation, clarity, and experimental rigor.

### Strengths
- **Investigation of acceleration factors:** The paper explicitly evaluates reconstruction quality at 4× and 8× acceleration, providing evidence of performance under different undersampling levels.  
- **Weighted SSIM+L1 loss:** Using a weighted fusion of SSIM and L1 loss leads to improvements, a point noted by one reviewer.  
- **Accessibility and motivation:** One reviewer (confidence 4) found the paper well motivated, clearly written, and accessible to non-MRI experts, contrasting with other reviewers’ criticism of writing quality.  
- **Inspiring architecture element:** Another reviewer (confidence 5) highlighted that the coil-attention module is an inspiring way to incorporate multi-coil information into DL-based reconstruction.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing quality and presentation:** Multiple reviewers (ratings 1 and 3, confidences 4 and 5) note that the paper is poorly written, contains numerous grammatical errors, is redundant, and uses unprofessional tables (e.g., PSNR reported without decimals). The presentation hinders reading and information retrieval.
- **Insufficient qualitative analysis:** There are no visual comparisons of reconstructed images with other methods. For medical imaging, visualization is critical to assess hallucinations and diagnostic quality; relying solely on quantitative metrics (SSIM, NMSE, PSNR) is considered insufficient. One reviewer specifically requests more qualitative results for Figure 1.
- **Limited novelty:** Most components are well-established:
  - Joint coil sensitivity map estimation and image reconstruction was already proposed in E2E-VarNet.
  - The attention mechanism is widely used in MRI and other medical problems (e.g., HUMUS‑Net, NeurIPS 2022).  
  One reviewer (confidence 5) still found the coil-attention module inspiring, but overall contribution was rated low.
- **Ablation studies are weak and incomplete:** The main ablation only varies the loss weighting (SSIM vs. L1), which is trivial. The paper lacks ablation on the core proposed components: the coil-attention module and the coil representation learning structure. Questions such as “How does the coil-attention module help, why it works?” and “Why use SSIM loss instead of MSE?” are left unanswered.
- **Missing analysis of generalizability:** The method does not appear to be based on unrolled networks, raising concerns about generalization to different sampling patterns and undersampling trajectories. No experiments or analysis are provided to address this.
- **Over-reliance on standard metrics without clinical validation:** The evaluation uses only SSIM, NMSE, and PSNR, which may not capture diagnostic utility. Inclusion of radiologist assessments or a more robust qualitative comparison would strengthen validation, as noted by two reviewers.  
- **Inefficient presentation of dataset and metrics:** The paper devotes nearly a full page to describing the fastMRI dataset and metrics while providing very few visual or ablation studies in the main text.
