## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses generating a uniformly distributed Pareto front in multiobjective optimization. It proposes a neural surrogate model to map preference angles to aggregated objective values, with an adaptive weight adjustment method integrated into MOEA/D, a new uniformity measure, and theoretical analysis of asymptotic uniformity and generalization error. Experiments are conducted on synthetic and real-world problems.

### Strengths
- The idea of directly modeling the preference-to-Pareto-front mapping is interesting and novel (Reviewer 1, 3).
- The paper highlights and formalizes limitations of existing methods (Reviewer 2).
- The work is technically sound with solid theoretical analysis, including a generalization error bound (Reviewer 1, 4).
- A new indicator is proposed for measuring the uniformity of Pareto objectives (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer found the paper easy to follow (Reviewer 3), although others raised serious presentation concerns (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- The related work is insufficiently covered: the most recent MOEA/D adaptive preference paper cited is from 2014, ignoring a large body of recent MOEA/D variants (Reviewer 4). Baselines are weak and old; many state-of-the-art evolutionary multiobjective optimization methods with adaptive reference vectors are missing (Reviewer 3). Comparisons to gradient-based methods (MOO-SVGD, EPO) are insufficient; the criticism of gradient methods only addresses aggregation methods, not those with theoretical guarantees (Reviewer 1, 2). Pareto Set Learning methods are dismissed too quickly despite claims of convergence to the Pareto front; formal and experimental comparisons are needed (Reviewer 2).
- The presentation is poor and the paper is not self-contained: Figures 1 and 3 are unclear and not properly explained; details are deferred to the appendix without a high-level description (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). Concepts like Pareto Front Learning and preference angles are introduced without clear preliminaries, forcing readers to jump back and forth (Reviewer 2). English is not entirely satisfactory (Reviewer 2). The paper lacks an ablation study (Reviewer 3).
- Theoretical contributions need clarification: Lemma 1 does not explain why the function should not have weakly Pareto solutions (Reviewer 2). Theorem 1 is badly presented; the constraints on `h` that guarantee uniformity are unclear (Reviewer 2). The technical novelty relative to Borodachov et al. (2019) is not clarified (Reviewer 1).
- The motivation is improper for neural network optimization: assuming the problem is a black box is unnecessary when gradients are available; equation (5) can be optimized without a neural surrogate. The paper should show efficiency gains on large-scale neural network tasks using a small surrogate (Reviewer 3).
- The observation that uniform sampling of preference vectors does not guarantee uniform Pareto front generation has already been made in Liu et al., 2021 (Reviewer 2).
- The claim that MOEA/D fails to achieve uniform objectives (Section 4) is not rigorous; specific references or variants must be cited (Reviewer 4).
- The claim in Section 3.3 that the proposed method aims for "global optimal MOO solutions" is improper (Reviewer 3).
- The method requires generating Pareto solutions for a given preference from the learned front, but it does not explicitly involve the decision space; how this is done is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- The scope of the adaptive weight method is questionable: is it only applicable to decomposition-based MOEAs? If not, how could it be used in dominance-relation or indicator-based frameworks? (Reviewer 4) The experiments are too limited: only DTLZ1-2 from the DTLZ suite, and more complex problems (WFG, UF) and state-of-the-art algorithms from the last eight years are needed (Reviewer 4). More details are required on when the method uses real objective evaluation versus model-based estimation (Reviewer 4).
