## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies unsupervised pretraining for tabular Learning-to-Rank (LTR) problems, an underexplored area. It formulates existing contrastive methods (SimCLR, SimSiam) for LTR and proposes SimCLR-Rank, a variant that samples negatives only from the same query group. Experiments on three public datasets and one private industry dataset show that pretrained deep models consistently outperform GBDTs—by up to 38% on some ranking metrics—under label scarcity (more unlabeled than labeled data). The paper identifies two beneficial scenarios: query sparsity and label sparsity. It also analyzes performance on outlier queries.

### Strengths

1. **Novelty of direction**: Representation learning in tabular LTR has not been much studied. This is the first work to show promises for pre-trained DNNs in this setting. (R1, R3)
2. **Task‑specific pretraining**: SimCLR-Rank provides a strategy that leverages the structure of LTR tasks by using hard negatives from the same query group. The motivation is clear and easy to understand. (R1, R2, R3)
3. **Practical performance**: The paper demonstrates that pretrained deep models can achieve performance close to or surpassing GBDT in ranking tasks, which has practical value. (R2)
4. **Identified beneficial scenarios**: The paper identifies two concrete scenarios where pretrained deep models help: query sparsity and label sparsity. (R3)
5. **Clarity and simplicity**: The paper is clearly written; methods are straightforward and easily implementable. (R2)
6. **Effectiveness relative to SimCLR**: SimCLR-Rank shows reasonable effectiveness and in certain scenarios outperforms SimCLR. (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: The technical novelty is limited. The change from SimCLR to SimCLR-Rank (narrowing contrastive computation from the whole batch to a single query group) is incremental. SimCLR with small batches might already address the hard‑negative issue, making the need for a new loss unconvincing. SimSiam is not a competitive baseline. The paper does not comprehensively compare SimCLR-Rank against SimCLR itself. (R1, R2, R3)
2. **Insufficient experimental comparisons and related work**:
   - The paper only compares against GBDT, non‑pretrained MLP, and fine‑tuning/probing. It lacks comparisons with recent deep learning methods for tabular data and LTR, including other self‑supervised methods such as STab, RegCLR, and SubTab.
   - GBDT could use semi‑supervised learning (e.g., pseudo‑labeling, consistency regularization) to exploit unlabeled data; it is unclear whether pretrained deep models still outperform GBDT in that setting.
   - The related work discussion is inadequate. (R1, R2)
3. **Conceptual concerns about SimCLR‑Rank**:
   - The method essentially distills semantic information from the retrieval model that produced query groups (QG). If retrieval model features were input to GBDT, it might achieve better performance, which would limit the applicability of the proposed approach.
   - Because SimCLR already emphasizes samples from the same QG via the softmax over the batch, the potential improvement from SimCLR‑Rank may be limited. The paper does not provide sufficient explanation. (R2)
4. **Extendability and feature‑space dependence**: Unlike text, tabular datasets have a fixed feature space. Adding new features can invalidate pretraining, and pretraining must be performed separately for each dataset. This limitation makes pre‑trained tabular models much less generalizable than in the text domain. (R3)
5. **Limited datasets and unclear real‑world significance**: Only three public datasets and one private dataset are used. No online results are provided for the industry dataset. The significance of the industry results is unclear due to unclear baselines, the private nature of the data, and unknown real‑world implications. (R1, R3)
6. **Transferability unclear**: The paper uses unlabeled data from the same downstream task for pretraining. It does not investigate whether pretraining can transfer across different datasets. (R1)
7. **Lack of explanation for key findings**: The paper does not explain the intuition behind why SimCLR‑Rank works better or why pretraining improves outlier NDCG. (R1)
8. **Minor presentation issue**: There is a potential typo in the caption of Figure 2 (“non‑pretrained counterparts”?). (R3)
