## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a policy gradient method for confounded partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) with continuous state and observation spaces in the offline setting. The core contribution is a novel identification result for non-parametrically estimating any history-dependent policy gradient under POMDPs using offline data, achieved via bridge functions from proximal causal inference and solved with a min-max learning procedure. The authors provide a finite-sample non-asymptotic bound for gradient estimation and prove global convergence of the resulting gradient ascent algorithm to the history-dependent optimal policy under certain conditions. Numerical experiments demonstrate effectiveness.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and problem motivation**: The confounded offline POMDP setting is well-motivated, and the paper is the first to study policy gradient methods for POMDPs in the offline setting. Most existing policy gradient work focuses on fully observable environments.
- **Novel identification and estimation**: The idea of using bridge functions to identify not only the policy value \(V(\pi_\theta)\) but also the policy gradient \(\nabla_\theta V(\pi_\theta)\) is new and is of independent interest for future research in confounded offline RL.
- **Strong theoretical guarantees**: The paper provides comprehensive theoretical analysis including a finite-sample non-asymptotic bound for uniform gradient estimation over a pre-specified policy class, as well as global convergence of the proposed algorithm. The statistical error of gradient estimation and the suboptimality of the obtained policy are both discussed.
- **Self-contained and sound**: The theoretical derivations are self-contained and sound. The results are solid, and establishing any form of convergence for policy gradient algorithms in POMDPs is immensely valuable.
- **Numerical experiments**: The theoretical results are complemented by experimental validation.

### Weaknesses
- **Readability and notation overload**: The 9-page format significantly hinders readability; contents in Appendix A are critical and warrant inclusion in the main text. The paper introduces a heavy number of symbols (e.g., \(\mathcal{Z}_t\) and \(\mathcal{W}_t\) on page 5) that may overwhelm readers. A symbol table or simplification of notation is needed.
- **Assumption strength and practical applicability**:
  - **Assumption 4(a) (full coverage of policy class by offline distribution)**: This assumption requires the offline distribution \(P_{\pi_b}\) to calibrate \(P_{\pi_\theta}\) for all \(\theta\). In real-world domains like healthcare or finance, such coverage is often impractical due to ethical, logistical, or financial constraints. The paper does not discuss how to assess this assumption or suggest alternatives when it is violated.
  - **Assumption 5(a) (optimal policy coverage)**: Requires that the optimal policy \(\pi^*\) is covered by all policies in the class, which may be too restrictive.
  - **Assumption 2**: Identified by one reviewer as the most critical assumption, yet no specific rationale is given for its reasonableness.
- **Technical contribution relative to prior work**: The main theory (Sections 6.1 & 6.2) is largely based on prior work on minimax estimation for bridge functions in confounded POMDPs and global convergence analysis of policy gradient in MDPs. This weakens the novelty of the core theoretical results. The paper does not sufficiently highlight its own technical contributions compared to these previous lines.
- **Assumptions for policy gradient analysis not justified for POMDPs**: The smoothness and positive definiteness assumptions on the policy gradient and Fisher information matrix are adapted directly from MDP setups. Their validity in POMDPs with history-dependent policies and the impact of history length on these assumptions and convergence rates are not addressed.
- **Lack of concrete policy class examples and discussion of history dependence**: The paper does not provide any concrete example of a history-dependent policy class. How the dependence on history changes the difficulty of policy gradient (e.g., computational complexity, NP-hardness concerns from Vlassis et al. 2012) is not discussed. The paper would benefit from lower bound examples and a discussion of computational complexity for specific classes of POMDPs.
- **Limited practical demonstration**: While numerical experiments are included, the paper does not show how to implement the estimation and algorithm on real practical RL problems, which one reviewer expects even for a theory paper.
- **Potential venue mismatch** (minority view: one reviewer): The highly technical nature may be better suited for a journal rather than a conference, as the 9-page limit compromises readability and validation.
- **Unaddressed questions on partial coverage**: How the proposed method could be adapted for partial coverage assumptions and what technical challenges would arise is not discussed.
