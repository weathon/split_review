## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers critique a paper that proposes SimVQ, a method that reparameterizes VQ code vectors through a linear transformation (C = ĈW) to address representation collapse caused by disjoint codebook optimization. All reviewers agree on the core idea and its effectiveness in reconstruction. Two reviewers (R1, R4, scores 3) question novelty and missing generation results; two (R2, R3, scores 5) find the simple solution promising and important.

### Strengths
- **Simple and effective solution** – All reviewers note that the proposed linear-layer reparameterization is straightforward to implement (R1: “only requires adding one linear layer”; R3: “I really like it … solve a problem that’s annoying, important but also relatively ignored”). (R2: “straightforward yet effectively addresses codebook collapse”)
- **Identifies a key cause** – R1: “identify disjoint optimization of the codebook as the key reason for representation collapse in VQ”.
- **Competitive reconstruction** – R2: “experiments on reconstruction tasks are convincing”; R4: “better reconstruction results” on two modalities (image, audio).
- **Maintains effectiveness with larger codebooks** – R1: “Maintains effectiveness with increasing codebook size”.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited literature review and missing related-work** – All reviewers flag insufficient coverage of prior methods that address codebook collapse or use similar linear-combination ideas. Specific missing references:
  - R1: Residual Quantization with Implicit Neural Codebooks (ICML 2024) and other recent VQ papers.
  - R2: SQ-VAE [1], VQ-WAE [2], HVQ-VAE [3], CVQ-VAE [4]; also notes that simple reset techniques (e.g., Jukebox [5]) can mitigate collapse.
  - R3: No connection to classic dictionary learning, sparse coding, vector quantization; does not compare to M. Huh et al. “Straightening Out the Straight-Through Estimator”.
  - R4: “Auto-regressive Image Generation Using Residual Quantization” and “SC-VAE: Sparse Coding-based Variational Autoencoder with Learned ISTA” – no discussion or comparison.

  As a result, missing baseline comparisons with these methods (R2, R4) weaken the experimental validation.

- **Missing generation results** – R2 and R4 explicitly note that VQ-VAE is designed for high-quality generation (image/audio), but the paper provides only reconstruction results. R2: “It is unclear how these representations will impact codeword effectiveness. Generation results could clarify this.” R4: “How will you train a transformer model based on these coefficients?” Both reviewers recommend including generation experiments.

- **Novelty questioned by minority** – R1: “the main contribution can be summed up by the fact that … one can use a weighted combination of the whole codebook … I don’t think a whole new paper is needed on this.” R4: “the idea … has been explored on existing works.” In contrast, R2 and R3 find the solution novel and important. This disagreement is explicitly documented.

- **Theoretical analysis claimed but not rigorous** – R3: “I personally think claiming ‘theoretically analyze’ the dead unit phenomenon is too much. The analysis … is not rigorous. To do so, I think you want to prove or cite some result for single layer dictionary learning.” R2 also questions the analysis: “Remark 4.1: The simultaneous optimization … may lead to collapse – why is this the case?” and “It is unclear whether the analysis of codebook utilization is based on C or CW, as the codebook initialization ablation appears to be applied to C.”

- **Missing toy example details** – R1: “toy example section … needs to be properly fleshed out where the reader can work out the example by hand.”

- **Minor technical issues** – R3: “index for equation 9 should be cK not cN?”

- **Unclear generality claim** – R3 questions the paper’s claim that other methods require dictionary element dimension to be small: “I happen to read [1]; correct me if I’m wrong but I don’t think they have this assumption.”

- **Open questions that overlap with criticisms** (treated as weaknesses):
  - R2: How does Remark 4.1 about collapse follow from the formulation? Is the analysis of codebook utilization based on C or CW?
  - R3: How does the method work for simple vector quantization (sparse coding or clustering)? Would the same logic apply?
