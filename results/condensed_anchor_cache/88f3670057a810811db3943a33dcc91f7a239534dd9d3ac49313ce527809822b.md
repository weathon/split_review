## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Ref-Gaussian, a Gaussian splatting framework for reflective object reconstruction that uses physically-based deferred rendering (split-sum approximation) and a Gaussian-grounded inter-reflection model computed on a TSDF-extracted mesh. The method achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality with real-time performance. Reviewers generally acknowledge the strong rendering quality and sensible design, but also raise concerns about novelty, the specular-only inter-reflection approximation, dependence on mesh quality, missing baselines, and several unclear or missing experimental details. One reviewer (score 8) is more positive about the technical contributions, while the other three (score 6) find novelty limited.

### Strengths
1. **SOTA reflective rendering quality.** The method achieves state-of-the-art quantitative and qualitative results on reflective objects, with excellent rendering and accurate surface normals (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
2. **Deferred PBR outperforms per-Gaussian PBR.** The physically-based deferred rendering (split-sum) significantly improves PBR over per-Gaussian methods like Relightable 3DGS, as confirmed by ablation (Reviewer 1).
3. **Material-aware normal propagation.** This technique enhances normal estimation by periodically increasing the scale of 2D Gaussians with high metallic and low roughness, demonstrating an interesting material-normal interaction (Reviewers 1, 3).
4. **Real-time efficiency.** Training time and rendering speed are satisfactory, offering a good performance–efficiency trade-off (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
5. **Comprehensive ablation study.** The paper includes extensive ablations covering many design choices (Reviewer 3).
6. **Well-written and reproducible.** The paper is clear, easy to follow, and provides sufficient detail for reproduction (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty of core components.** Deferred shading with split-sum approximation and per-Gaussian indirect lighting attributes have been adopted by prior 3DGS-based inverse rendering methods; the main novelty is the visibility modeling through mesh-based ray-tracing and associated optimization techniques (Reviewer 2).
2. **Inter-reflection only handles fully specular surfaces.** The visibility and indirect light computation trace a single reflected direction using \(2(\omega_i \cdot N)N - \omega_i\), ignoring the BRDF roughness term. The approximation from Eq. (8) to Eq. (9) effectively assumes perfect specular reflection, making the model inaccurate for glossy or diffuse surfaces (e.g., the Potion bottle lid in Fig. 5) and producing overestimated indirect light (Fig. 12) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
3. **Missing evidence for inter-reflection contribution.** Qualitative indirect-component visualizations (e.g., for Fig. 5 or the Ref-Real dataset) are lacking, and the ablation (Table 3) shows only a slight PSNR drop when removing inter-reflection. Geometry metrics (e.g., normal MAE) are not reported for ablation components (Reviewer 1).
4. **Dependence on extracted TSDF mesh.** The method relies on a TSDF-fused mesh for visibility, which is non-differentiable, sensitive to noise, and may poorly represent thin or intricate reflective geometries, potentially limiting end-to-end optimization and final quality (Reviewer 4).
5. **LPIPS worse than 3DGS-DR on real datasets.** On real-world data, the proposed method underperforms 3DGS-DR in LPIPS, indicating it may capture fine details less effectively and raising questions about generalizability (Reviewer 4).
6. **Missing baselines.** Comparisons to NeRO (SIGGRAPH 2023) and Neural Directional Encoding (CVPR 2024) are absent (Reviewer 3).
7. **Missing implementation details and unclear explanations.**  
   - The period (number of steps) for TSDF mesh extraction is not specified (Reviewer 3).  
   - The meaning of \(N\) in Eq. (10) (\(i \in N\)) and whether explicit ray-tracing or splatting is used for indirect lighting is unclear (Reviewer 3).  
   - The phrase “rendering equation at the Gaussian level” (L254) and its difference from Eq. (8) are not explained (Reviewer 3).  
   - It is unclear how the integral in Eq. (9) is evaluated without a full hemisphere integration (Reviewer 2).  
   - Training hyperparameters may vary across scenes, but this is not stated (Reviewer 3).
8. **Questions that overlap with criticisms (addressed above as missing experiments or limitations):**  
   - Does the performance gain come largely from the 2DGS geometry rather than the proposed inter-reflection? A controlled ablation using 3DGS as representation with the rest of the pipeline unchanged is requested (Reviewer 2).  
   - Is indirect lighting transferable under relighting? The per-Gaussian SH attributes cannot be reconstructed for relighting, limiting application (Reviewers 1, 2).  
   - How does material-aware normal propagation differ from reflection-strength-aware normal propagation in 3DGS-DR? The improvement appears incremental (Reviewer 1).  
   - Does PBR modeling also benefit geometry reconstruction? No geometry metrics are provided to demonstrate this (Reviewer 4).  
   - Extracted mesh results are not shown qualitatively (Reviewer 3).
