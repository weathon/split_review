- Scores: 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a recurrent deformation learning framework for cortical surface reconstruction (CSR) from infant brain MRI, using a sphere template and iteratively warping it to white and pial surfaces. Two reviewers rate the paper 3, citing insufficient novelty and missing details; one rates it 5, noting performance improvements. All agree the method is not clearly described, and key experiments are absent.

### Strengths
- **Performance improvement**: The method shows improvement over competing methods (Reviewer 1).
- **General sphere template**: Using a sphere as the starting shape for reconstruction is highlighted as an advantage (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 also notes the presentation is clear and experiments/visualizations are sound.
- **Adaptive edge length loss**: This loss is an interesting and intuitive approach to handle dramatic volume changes in infants’ first year of growth (Reviewer 3).
- **Multi-modal input**: Using T1w & T2w MRI is beneficial for infant surface extraction due to lower contrast (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient methodological details**: The paper is unreproducible – no network architecture, code, dataset preprocessing details, data augmentation, or baseline tuning are provided. The implementation subsection is sparse, listing only learning rates, iterations, and loss weights, with the rest describing instance normalization (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Lack of novelty / incremental contribution**: The ODE-based deformation formulation in Section 3.2 is the same as in prior CSR work (CortexODE, CortexFlow, etc.) with only the change from an unrolled UNet to a recurrent UNet. The multi-step sequence (template → white → pial) closely resembles CortexODE. The recurrent framework itself is a well-known technique from medical image registration, not a conceptual leap (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Unjustified use of sphere template**: The sphere template is topologically and geometrically distant from cortical surfaces, making the deformation task harder. The paper does not justify why a sphere is superior for *reconstruction* itself, only for spherical mapping. This makes the core reconstruction more complex, potentially negating benefits (Reviewer 2).
- **Unreliable baseline comparison**: The reported differences (≥1 mm) between the proposed method and baselines (Vox2Cortex, CorticalFlow++) contradict previous infant-data results showing differences of ~0.01 mm (see CoTAN, MICCAI’23). It is unclear how baselines were implemented and tuned, whether their proper initial templates (not a sphere) were used, and whether hyperparameters were re-tuned for the sphere initialization (Reviewer 3).
- **Only one dataset**: Only the Baby Connectome Project dataset (infants 0–12 months) is used. The method is not specific to infants; experiments on additional datasets (e.g., HCP, ADNI, dHCP) are needed to evaluate generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- **Topology correction not validated**: The paper does not explicitly validate topology correction (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing runtime and segmentation performance**: Runtime is not reported, and segmentation performance metrics are absent (Reviewer 1).
- **Overstretched claims**:
  - The introduction claims that graph-convolution-based methods (Hoopes et al., Bongratz et al.) do not generate topologically valid surfaces and require correction. This is false; both methods deform a template similarly and do not need separate topology correction (Reviewer 3).
  - The paper asserts that infant brain myelination poses a significant challenge due to poor segmentation, but this only holds in the unsupervised setting. In the fully supervised training used here, tissue segmentation is no more challenging than in adults (as shown on iSeg) (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority positive reviewer**: Reviewer 1 rated the paper 5, finding the performance improvement sufficient, but other reviewers (scores 3) strongly contest novelty, reproducibility, and experimental rigor. This disagreement is unresolved.
