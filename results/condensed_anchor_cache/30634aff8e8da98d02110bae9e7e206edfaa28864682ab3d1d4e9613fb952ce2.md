## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers show clear disagreement. Reviewer 1 (score 3, confidence 5) finds the paper significantly lacking: missing comparisons with related efficient architectures, limited evaluation (zero-shot only, no length generalization tests, no LRA, no memory benchmarks), and a direct comparison with H3 that appears to favor the baseline. In contrast, Reviewers 3 and 4 (both score 8, confidence 5/4) consider the work very strong, praising the simple yet effective selection mechanism, thorough empirical results, and clear exposition. Reviewer 2 (score 6, confidence 2) acknowledges impressive results but points out quadratic memory during training and requests scaling and hyperparameter sensitivity analyses.

### Strengths
- **Simple, effective modification to SSMs:** Making SSM parameters input-dependent (selective SSM) addresses the key limitation of content-based reasoning, combined with a hardware-aware parallel algorithm for efficiency. (All reviewers)
- **Strong empirical results:** Pre-trained models from 130M to 1.4B parameters show improvements over Pythia and Transformers of the same size; Mamba-3B outperforms Transformers twice its size. (R1, R2, R4; R4: “thorough and strong results”)
- **Clear exposition and well-defined approach:** The paper identifies the core weakness of prior SSMs (lack of selection) and the connection to gating in RNNs is appreciated. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Use of simple toy tasks:** Selective copying and associative recall guide design choices that yield state-of-the-art impact on real-world data. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with related efficient architectures:** No direct comparison with S4-diagonal, SGConv, MEGA, SPADE, efficient Transformer models, Linear Attention methods, or LongConv on a standard language modeling task like Wikitext-103. Both performance and efficiency need to be demonstrated. (R1, R3)
- **Length generalization not tested:** Mamba’s ability to generalize to longer sequences than trained on is not evaluated; this is a key advantage of many Transformers (e.g., relative positional encoding, Alibi). Extrapolation experiments on language modeling are requested. (R1, R3)
- **H3 comparison missing:** As a direct predecessor SSM, H3 reports perplexities of 8.8 (125M), 7.1 (355M), 6.0 (1.3B) on the Pile, which are better than Mamba’s results. A fair comparison with the same tokenizer is necessary. (R1)
- **Evaluation limited to zero-shot inference:** No fine-tuning experiments; long-sequence tasks such as document summarization (e.g., arXiv, average >8k tokens) are needed to validate Mamba’s claimed benefits. (R1)
- **No evaluation on LRA:** The Long Range Arena is a standard benchmark for long sequence understanding and should be included. (R1)
- **Memory benchmarking missing:** Section 4.5 only reports speed; memory usage is not compared. Experimental setup for Figure 8 left (model layers, sizes, convolution details) is insufficiently described, and intuition for FlashAttention being slowest at very large sequence length is needed. (R1)
- **Quadratic memory during training:** Like Transformers, Mamba still requires quadratic memory when training due to materializing the expanded state. (R2)
- **Scaling beyond 1.4B not evaluated:** No experiments at larger scales (e.g., 10B parameters) to compare with Transformers. (R2)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity unclear:** The input selection mechanism introduces additional hyperparameters (e.g., projection rank); no analysis of their impact is provided. (R2)
- **Incomplete ablation in Table 6:** Only 6 of 8 possible combinations (2³) are shown; the two missing combinations should be included. (R3)
- **Scaling law setting not justified:** The ratio of token count to model size follows Chinchilla, but FLOPs for Transformers and SSMs differ given the same parameter count. The effect on final accuracy is not discussed. (R3)
- **Underspecified implementation details:** The parameterization of the first convolutional layer in the Mamba-Block is not described. (R3)
- **Core code not provided.** (R3)
