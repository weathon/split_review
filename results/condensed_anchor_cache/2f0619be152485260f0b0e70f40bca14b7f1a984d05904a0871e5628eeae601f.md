## Merged Review

### Summary
From the five reviews (scores 1, 3, 6, 3, 6), the paper investigates generalization vs memorization in DNNs trained with label noise, proposing MASC – a classifier using PCA on hidden representations and angular distance. The claim is that intermediate representations retain generalization ability even when the model overfits to corrupted labels. Reviewers generally acknowledge the problem’s importance and the breadth of experiments (5 datasets, 3 architectures). However, many raise concerns about novelty (similar ideas exist in prior work), insufficient scale (AlexNet is the largest model), lack of baselines (random initialization, other classifiers), inconsistent results (especially on ImageNet), and presentation issues. Two minority reviewers (R3, R5) find the investigation novel and well-executed, though they note limited novelty and scale.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Analysis across 5 standard datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, etc.) and three architectures (MLP, CNN, AlexNet). [R1: very comprehensive; R5: multiple datasets and architectures support conclusions]
- **Important problem**: Understanding generalization-memorization relationships in overparameterized models is a worthy area. [R2]
- **Novel investigation** (minority positive): The paper investigates important questions in a novel way with claims mostly backed by evidence. [R3]
- **Concrete technique**: Presents a concrete method (MASC) to extract generalization from intermediate activations; the idea that overfitting happens in later layers is interesting. [R4]
- **Clear formulation** (minority positive): Experiments studying whether networks learn useful visual representations when memorizing random labels are clearly formulated and conclusions supported. [R5]
- **Well-versed in literature**: Authors seem reasonably versed in the memorization/generalization literature. [R2]

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of literature and novelty** (multiple reviewers, high severity):
  - The paper does not adequately discuss related works. Prior theoretical works [1][2] already indicate that models first learn primary eigenspace with correct label information and later memorize noise, making the finding that top PCA components retain class information unsurprising. [R1]
  - Sec.3’s idea is not new; MASC is essentially clustering using PCA and cosine similarity, akin to nearest subspace classifiers. [R1]
  - No situating with prior work that probes intermediate layers (e.g., [2,3]) to understand memorization-vs-generalization; many conclusions seem obvious in light of that literature. [R3]
  - Clustering/dimensionality reduction on intermediate activations has been explored for various tasks; the paper does not sufficiently differentiate its approach. [R4]
  - Minority positive reviewers (R3, R5) acknowledge some novelty but note conclusions are not surprising from existing work [R3] or the phenomenon is specialized to MASC [R5].
- **Insufficient scale and model diversity** (multiple reviewers, high severity):
  - Largest model is AlexNet (40M parameters), achieving ~18% test accuracy on TinyImageNet, far below modern models (e.g., ResNet-152 ~80% on full ImageNet, DenseNet 60% on TinyImageNet). [R3] This raises questions about generality. [R4]
  - Effect is minimal on AlexNet+TinyImageNet, the most “large-scale” representative; without full ImageNet with a modern model, conclusions are incomplete. [R3]
  - Request experiments with ResNet (from 2015), MobileNet, ViT, or scaling parameters (2x/0.5x). [R3, R4] How does the phenomenon generalize to ResNet50 with careful training on CIFAR100/ImageNet? [R4]
- **Methodological concerns about MASC** (multiple reviewers, moderate to high severity):
  - **Overfitting and regularization**: Models are trained to 99-100% training accuracy, likely overfit. The MASC classifier, regularized by dimensionality reduction, may naturally outperform the unregularized output layer. [R2]
  - **Missing baseline**: MASC should be applied to a randomly initialized model. A random projection with a regularized classifier might achieve similar results, especially as corruption increases. [R2]
  - **Lack of analysis of MASC properties**: The degree of regularization (number of principal components, etc.) is only sparingly discussed (mainly in the appendix) and is crucial for interpretation. [R2]
  - **MASC label requirement**: The algorithm requires labels; the paper should clearly clarify which labels (corrupted or true) are used in each experiment, particularly in Sec.4 where true labels are used for subspaces from corrupted models. [R5]
- **Inconsistency across results** (R2, R4, high severity):
  - Across Figs. 1-3, authors mainly focus on MLP and CNN results, largely neglecting AlexNet results which are often inconsistent with the pattern, leaving generality in question. [R2]
  - MASC accuracy is not always higher than test accuracy, especially on ImageNet; a thorough analysis of these failures and potential reasons is missing. [R4]
- **Interpretation issues and questionable claims** (R3, moderate severity):
  - Sec.5: The experiment constructs subspaces from noisy labels for models trained without noisy labels and shows MASC can retrieve noised labels. The claim that this “supports the idea that memorization can not only coexist with generalization, but that in some cases memorization can be accompanied by superior generalization” is questionable. It may only show that the subspace is expressive enough to separate arbitrary points, especially with many principal components, and says nothing about generalization. [R3]
  - The narrative that “memorization is generally considered antithetical to generalization” is demonstrably false; since 2016/17 works (Zhang et al., Arpit et al.) it has been clear that generalization and memorization are not at odds. [R3]
- **Presentation and clarity** (multiple reviewers, moderate severity):
  - Paper is hard to read due to dense language, complex sentences, and grammar errors (e.g., ln 76-96, ln 304-318; “we wanted to” -> “want”). [R1]
  - Figs. 1-3 each contain 30 sub-panels and are hard to digest; alternative visualizations or highlighting key panels are needed. [R5] Table elements are not well separated; figures exceed page margins. [R1]
  - Benefits from more formal, mathematical definitions of empirical probes; e.g., unclear what the training set was for “MASC Accuracy on Corrupted Training”. [R2]
  - Fig. 2 should include the “MASC Accuracy on Corrupted Training” lines from Fig.1 for proper comparison. [R3] Adam method should be cited. [R3] Label sizes and zoom level need improvement. [R3]
- **Specific missing experiments or controls** (various):
  - Apply MASC to randomly initialized model for each architecture. [R2]
  - Include other simple classifiers besides MASC to test generality. [R5]
  - Repeat with 2x/0.5x parameters to study capacity effect. [R3]
  - Show results on ResNet (at least) for CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. [R4]
  - Analyze cases where MASC fails (e.g., ImageNet). [R4]
  - Measure representation similarity (e.g., CKA) to confirm that models learn similar representations under memorization and generalization, not just that a probe can decode. [R5]
