## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FruitBin, a large-scale synthetic dataset for 6D pose estimation in fruit bin picking, built on PickSim with ROS+Gazebo. It contains over 1M images, 40M instance poses, 8 fruit types, varied lighting and camera views, and benchmarks for scene/camera generalization and occlusion. Two baseline models (PVNet, DenseFusion) are evaluated. Reviewer assessments are strongly mixed: one reviewer (score 8) endorsed the dataset’s value and presentation, while three others (scores 3,3,5) raised significant concerns about novelty, real-world applicability, evaluation breadth, and platform choices.

### Strengths
- Well-written and clearly presented (R1, R2).
- Large-scale dataset with extensive images, configurations, and annotations (R1, R2).
- Detailed insights into dataset composition aiding effective utilization (R1).
- Synthetic nature enables highly detailed annotations not easily obtained in real world (R1).
- First 6D object pose estimation dataset tailored for fruit bin picking (R4).
- Generalizes scenes and camera positions for wider acceptability (R3).
- Good reference to prior work on datasets (R3).
- Targets an industry need for a good dataset in this domain (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Sim-to-real gap unaddressed**: No real-world validation, test set, or discussion of sim-to-real transfer. Synthetic data lacks photorealistic textures and lighting phenomena (shadows, reflections). Gazebo cannot do PBR rendering, limiting transferability compared to BlenderProc/Kubric (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer asks whether the dataset will include real-world samples (R2).

2. **Limited object diversity**: Only 8 fruit types, 75% spherical. Shape/texture variation is low (smooth, spherical). Diversity is in image count, not object variety. Comparison to other 6DOF datasets in object diversity is missing (R1). Real-world fruits have size/shape variations within categories, but FruitBin does not model this (R4).

3. **Necessity of 6DOF pose for fruit picking**: For spherical fruits and suction grippers, a simpler bounding box may suffice. The paper does not justify why 6DOF pose estimation is necessary for the targeted use cases (R1).

4. **Real-world relevance of scene configurations**: Random mixing of fruits in bins may not represent common harvesting (monocultures, separate packing). Alignment with practical bin-picking scenarios is unclear (R1).

5. **Synthetic lighting too uniform**: Lighting variations do not capture complex real-world conditions (strong shadows, specular reflections, ambient variations). Representative images look similar (R1).

6. **Overstated language and limited evaluation**: Describing evaluation with only two models as “comprehensive” is grandiose. Evaluation is limited (R1). More robust baselines are needed (R3). Benchmarking methods (PVNet, DenseFusion from 2018-2019) are outdated; newer SOTA methods should be included (R4).

7. **No robotic grasping demonstration**: The paper targets bin picking but provides no physical robot or high-fidelity simulation of a gripper to validate the dataset’s utility for grasping (R1, R4).

8. **Limited novelty**: Core simulation environment and methodology are inherited from PickSim; the only change is substituting object models. This contribution may be insufficient for a top-tier conference (R2). One reviewer (score 8) disagreed, finding the dataset valuable and well-referenced.

9. **Platform choice (ROS+Gazebo)**: Gazebo is less popular in the robot learning community than mujoco, PyBullet, Isaac Gym. This may hinder adoption (R2).

10. **Format and presentation issues**: Table 1 is too wide and has formatting issues; references have formatting issues; supplementary materials attached to main paper; Table 2 should mark symmetric objects with “*”; minor writing issues and need for proofreading (R2, R4).

11. **Missing experimental details**:
    - In the experiments, does PVNet use ground-truth bounding boxes for cropping to handle multiple instances? (R4)
    - How do diffusion-generated backgrounds contribute to performance? (R4)
    - The paper mentions delicate fruits (bananas, apricots) requiring haptic feedback; how is this modeled? Is it only relevant for pose estimation? (R1)
