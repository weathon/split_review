## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Principled fRamewOrk for Deep Subspace Clustering (PRO‑DSC) that jointly learns structured representations and self‑expressive coefficients. It adds a log‑determinant regularization (Eq. 3) to the self‑expressive loss to prevent feature collapse and provides theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–3) showing that the regularized model can yield representations lying on a union of orthogonal subspaces. An efficient implementation based on reparameterization and differential programming is given. Experiments on synthetic and real‑world image datasets demonstrate improved clustering performance. Reviewers agree the motivation is clear and the method is technically sound, but point to significant concerns about novelty, theoretical rigor, experimental fairness, missing comparisons, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- **Clear and important motivation:** The paper identifies the feature‑collapse problem in deep subspace clustering and provides a principled solution (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Strong theoretical support:** The method is backed by theoretical proofs showing that the learned representations can form a union‑of‑subspaces structure under certain conditions (Reviewers 3, 4; Reviewer 1 questions this – see Weaknesses).
- **Comprehensive experiments:** Both synthetic and real‑world datasets are used, with quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations. Reviewer 1 especially appreciates synthetic tests that check the behavior when adding an extra subspace (0‑dimensional or intersecting curves). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Well‑written and sound:** The paper is well‑structured and technically clear (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 2 notes it is “technically sound”.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** 
  The combination of the log‑determinant regularization (Eq. 3) with a subspace‑clustering loss was already explored in Ding et al. (2023), yet the current paper does not discuss this prior work. One reviewer (R1) finds the contribution incremental; another (R2) states that the subspace‑description coefficients and manifold parts rely on existing results and lack innovation.
- **Theoretical concerns (raised by R1):**
  * Lemma 1 is taken from Haeffele et al. (2021) and is not a contribution.
  * Theorem 1 is difficult to connect to the main objective (Eq. 5): the condition on the Lagrangian multiplier ν seems detached from the actual optimization, and it is unclear how to verify it in practice or relate it to algorithm convergence.
  * Theorem 3 is unclear: (i) “the optimal solution” – are there multiple solutions? Is it a sufficient condition for *one* optimal solution or *all*? (ii) Z, C vs. Z*, C* confusion; Γ is defined to permute columns of Z but then used with Z*; (iii) the sufficient condition 〈(I‑C)(I‑C)^⊤, G‑G*〉 – should C be C*? How is C defined? (iv) The condition is difficult to interpret; a simple case (e.g., orthogonal subspaces) where it holds would strengthen the result.
- **Insufficient implementation details and experimental fairness (R2, R4):**
  - Only the proposed method is evaluated over multiple runs (3 trials, with mean±std). It is not reported whether baselines (e.g., k‑means, SC, which are initialization‑sensitive) use the best or mean results over runs. A consistent evaluation setting (e.g., 10 runs for all methods) is needed.
  - No discussion of limitations or failed cases (R2).
- **Missing comparisons:**
  - Several recent deep clustering methods are omitted: *Learning Representation for Clustering Via Prototype Scattering and Positive Sampling* (TPAMI 2023) and *Towards Calibrated Deep Clustering Network* (2024). (R3)
  - State‑of‑the‑art subspace clustering methods are not included: AGCSC (CVPR 2023) and SAGSC (Pattern Recognition 2023). (R4)
  - In synthetic experiments, DSCNet is used as the representative SEDSC method instead of the more recent SENET. (R4)
- **Dataset scope:** Evaluation is limited to image datasets. Generalizability to other data types (e.g., Reuters for text, UCI HAR for time‑series) is not demonstrated. (R4)
- **Log‑determinant term (Eq. 4) not fully motivated:** Why is this particular term chosen? Is it solely for preventing collapse? What is its physical meaning? Are there alternative ways to solve collapse? (R3)
- **Ablation studies missing:** The contribution of each component (log‑determinant regularization, self‑expressive learning, reparameterization) is not isolated. (R3)
- **Scalability/extensibility not analyzed:** Computational complexity w.r.t. data size and number of subspaces, and adaptability to different network architectures, are not discussed. (R4)
- **Significance of UoS challenge underdeveloped:** The paper should empirically illustrate the consequences of non‑UoS representations (e.g., increased within‑cluster variance, poor separation) and why solving this is critical. (R4)
- **Baseline inconsistency across tables:** The set of comparison methods differs between Tables 1 and 2 without justification. (R4)
