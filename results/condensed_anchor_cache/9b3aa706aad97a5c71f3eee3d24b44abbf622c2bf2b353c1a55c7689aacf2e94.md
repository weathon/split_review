## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes scaling speech-text pre-training for SpeechLMs by generating large-scale synthetic interleaved data from text corpora, bypassing the need for parallel speech-text data. It introduces an ASR-based (Whisper) supervised speech tokenizer with a vector-quantized bottleneck that achieves strong semantic preservation at 12.5 Hz while retaining audio reconstruction quality. A text-to-token language model (trained on TTS data) converts text spans directly into speech tokens without generating audio. Starting from a pre-trained LLM and scaling pre-training to 1 trillion tokens (600B synthetic interleaved speech-text), the method achieves SOTA on spoken language modeling and spoken question answering (improving from 13% to 31% on spoken questions over Moshi). Fine-tuning with a speech dialogue dataset yields an end-to-end spoken chatbot competitive with baselines in conversational ability and speech quality.

### Strengths
- **Novel scaling approach**: Leverages abundant text corpora to produce synthetic interleaved speech-text data, enabling large-scale pre-training without collecting real speech data. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5)
- **Supervised speech tokenizer**: The ASR-based tokenizer with a supervised bottleneck preserves semantic content well and produces decent speech quality, even at low frame rates (12.5 Hz). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Text-to-token model**: Synthesizing speech tokens directly from text without generating audio is efficient and avoids high-bitrate audio generation and OOD issues. (Reviewers 3, 5)
- **Effectiveness of interleaved data**: Randomly replacing text spans with synthetic speech tokens improves cross-modal alignment and is shown to be beneficial in ablations. (Reviewers 3, 5)
- **Extensive experiments and ablations**: Comprehensive evaluations on spoken LM, spoken QA, and fine-tuned chatbot, with well-done ablation studies. (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
- **Strong results**: Achieves SOTA on sTopic-StoryCloze, sStoryCloze, Web Questions, Llama Questions, TriviaQA, and the spoken question answering tasks (31% vs. 13% for Moshi). (Reviewers 2, 4, 5)
- **Easy to implement**: The method’s components are straightforward, facilitating reproducibility and extension. (Reviewer 4)
- **Text-guided response as chain-of-thought**: The ability to use text for reasoning before speech output is interesting. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and clarity issues**: Several reviewers noted that the paper’s contribution (especially “synthetic interleaved data”) is not clearly explained in the abstract and introduction, and the overall presentation needs improvement. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Missing methodological details**:
  - How speech and text tokens are interleaved (e.g., how spans are selected, ratio η) is not described with sufficient detail. (Reviewer 1)
  - The text-to-token model’s architecture, training scheme, and training data are not provided. (Reviewer 1, 4)
  - The tokenizer evaluation methodology (Section 2.1, Table 1) lacks explicit definitions: e.g., how “Content Preservation-LS” is computed (assumed WER? Which ground truth? Is the speech decoder part of the evaluation?). (Reviewer 3)
  - The supervised speech-text data used for tokenizer training, the data for the text-to-token model, and the datasets for fine-tuning encoder/decoder are not clearly listed. (Reviewer 4)
  - Training hyperparameters for the final fine-tuning step (learning rate, batch size) are omitted. (Reviewer 3)
- **Evaluation shortcomings**:
  - No human evaluation of speech quality (e.g., MOS or MUSHRA). (Reviewer 4)
  - No demos or sample pages for speech continuation, spoken QA, or interleaved outputs. (Reviewer 3)
  - Lack of evaluation on real speech input; all evaluation datasets are TTS-synthesized (concern about generalization to natural speech). (Reviewer 5)
  - No evaluation of the text-to-token model’s output quality (e.g., ASR-WER after decoding synthetic tokens). (Reviewer 5)
  - Baseline numbers in tables are not clearly sourced (e.g., whether taken from papers or from independent re-evaluation). (Reviewer 4)
  - In Table 3, SpeechGPT and Spectron are described as speech-to-speech methods but results are reported as speech-to-text. (Reviewer 4)
  - Inconsistent speech quality metrics: Table 1 uses MOSNet, Table 4 uses UTMOS without explanation. (Reviewer 4)
  - Metrics like ASR-WER in Table 4 are more content quality than speech quality; suggestions to use ASR-ROUGE/BLUE instead. (Reviewer 3)
  - Poor output speech quality (high ASR-WER) even after large-scale training (700k hours) compared to Llama-Omni trained on 100 hours; no explanation provided. (Reviewer 5)
- **Novelty concerns**: While the assembly is impressive, individual components (Whisper-based tokenizer, text-to-token model similar to prior work, interleaving strategy from vision) are not entirely novel. (Reviewer 2)
- **Claims about novelty**: The paper claims “first to use supervised semantic tokens for SpeechLMs,” but Mini-Omni also uses a Whisper-based speech tokenizer. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing ablation/parameter justifications**:
  - No rationale for architectural/training parameter choices. (Reviewer 4)
  - Why ablation is done only on a 1.5B model, and why Chinese data are used for pre-training. (Reviewers 4, 5)
  - No explanation of why causal encoder/decoder are required for training the speech tokenizer when streaming is not needed. (Reviewer 5)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Figure 1a is hard to follow (color legend missing). (Reviewer 3)
  - Few typos (e.g., “AudioLLM” → “AudioLM”, “Citet” → “citep”). (Reviewer 3)
  - Difference between “∅” and “–” in Table 3 is unclear. (Reviewer 3)
- **Open issues for future work**: Multilingual extension, open-sourcing of models, and computation costs are not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Contrast in reviewer opinions**: Three reviewers (scores 8) were largely positive, highlighting the solid engineering and impressive results. One reviewer (score 5) was significantly more critical, citing unclear writing and insufficient evaluation, and recommended borderline rejection. Another reviewer (score 6) also noted missing presentation details and questions about novelty.
