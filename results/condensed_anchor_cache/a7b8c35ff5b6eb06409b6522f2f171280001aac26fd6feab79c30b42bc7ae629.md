## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a post-training method (TeminAL) to infuse temporal reasoning into contrastive Audio-Language Models (ALMs) like CLAP. It uses a two-stage training scheme: TeminAL A (distinguishing single vs. dual events) and TeminAL B (learning temporal order with prompts like before/after/while). The paper also introduces a zero-shot evaluation benchmark (ZSTE) to assess temporal understanding. Experiments on ESC-50 and AudioCap/Clotho show a 5.28% average gain on temporal tasks but mixed results on standard classification and retrieval. Reviewers acknowledge the underexplored temporal gap but raise concerns about limited novelty relative to prior work (especially CompA), insufficient ablation, missing comparisons, and presentation issues.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a critical gap**: The paper identifies that current ALMs lack temporal reasoning and act as “bag-of-words” models, motivating a post-hoc alignment method for temporal ordering.
- **Two-stage post-training approach**: The progressive training stages (TeminAL A then B) are based on a reasonable assumption about the difficulty gap between learning the number of events and learning their temporal order. The adaptation of TNCE loss from prior CV work is noted. (R1, R2, R4)
- **ZSTE benchmark**: The proposed zero-shot temporal evaluation scheme provides a multitask benchmark for assessing temporal understanding in ALMs, which is a novel and under-explored area. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Temporal modeling focus**: The research area of temporal understanding in audio-language models is still novel and underexplored; the benchmark can be a useful contribution to the community. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and differentiation from prior work**:  
  - The core idea of using augmented captions (concatenation, overlay) to improve temporal understanding has already been explored in prior works such as CompA [1] and T-CLAP [2]. (R1, R4)  
  - The curriculum learning approach remains very similar to CompA’s modular contrastive pre-training; the paper does not clearly articulate how it differs or improves upon CompA. (R2, R4)  
  - The first listed contribution (improving temporal reasoning) is already demonstrated in prior works [1-3]; the second and third contributions need more evidence and direct comparison. (R4)  
  - CompA uses a larger dataset (110k samples) and different temporal prompts (“succeeded”, “preceded”, “amidst” vs. “before”, “after”, “while”). The paper does not discuss the impact of dataset size or prompt design, nor does it evaluate on CompA’s public benchmark to enable a fair head-to-head comparison. (R4)  
  - T-CLAP also uses ESC-50 to generate data; the paper should at least discuss its methodology even if the model is not public. (R4)

- **Insufficient ablation and experimental setup**:  
  - Table 2 is a partial ablation; it omits the results for TeminAL A-only, making it impossible to assess each stage’s individual contribution or whether the gains are simply from adding more data in the second stage. (R1, R2)  
  - The advantage of two-stage training over one-stage training is not demonstrated; a comparative study is needed to determine which stage contributes more to temporal understanding. (R2)  
  - The paper does not discuss why both objectives (A and B) cannot be trained jointly; ablation results are needed to justify the sequential choice. (R4)  
  - The method was not applied to existing CLAP models (LAION-CLAP, Microsoft-CLAP, CompA-CLAP) to showcase flexibility; the authors appear to have trained from scratch rather than post-training a public checkpoint. (R1)  
  - It is unclear whether the baseline results in Table 1 come from models retrained by the authors or from publicly available checkpoints; this affects comparability. (R3)

- **Limited generalization and scalability**:  
  - The method focuses only on two-event audio scenarios and restricts training prompts to a narrow set {before, after, while}. Real-world scenarios involve more events and natural language descriptors. Scaling to more events/captions would make the contrastive matrices prohibitively large. (R1)  
  - Only ESC-50 is used to synthesize temporal audio segments; more diverse sources like AudioSet Strong (500+ time-aligned labels) are not explored, and the rationale for choosing ESC-50 is not given. (R2)

- **Missing evaluations and performance trade-offs**:  
  - Important zero-shot experiments on standard audio classification datasets (AudioSet, FSD50K, USD8K, TUTUrban) are absent. (R2)  
  - Performance on existing temporal reasoning benchmarks (COMPA-Order, COMPA-Attribute) is missing. (R2)  
  - According to Table 3, T-CLAP does not consistently outperform prior models. It sacrifices substantial accuracy on the most basic classification task 1A (drops from 82% to 75%), falling below ML-ACT (the pre-CLAP baseline). CompA-CLAP and T-CLAP each achieve top-1 on about half the tasks, suggesting a trade-off between temporal enhancement and core audio classification. (R1)  
  - The paper lacks direct comparison with CompA and T-CLAP on common benchmarks; without this, it is difficult to claim superiority of the proposed objective. (R4)

- **Poor presentation and clarity**:  
  - The equations in Section 3 are not easy to follow and do not connect well with Figure 3; variables like l_text, x_c, x_a are referred to as raw inputs but the loss takes embeddings (should be z instead of x). (R3)  
  - TeminAL A training details are insufficiently explained: how are audio-text pairs curated? Is it trained with contrastive loss or classification loss? A figure similar to Figure 4 for TeminAL B is needed. (R3)  
  - The detailed explanation of ZSTE is relegated to Appendix B.4; as a main contribution, it should be in the main paper. (R3)  
  - The definitions of categories A, B, C, D used in Table 2 are only explained in the caption of Table 3; this information should be in the main text. (R3)  
  - Fonts in Figures 3 and 4 are too small; overlapping annotations reduce readability. (R3)  
  - Line 334 has a repeated “equation” and an incorrect equation number (Eq. 3 does not contain C^{cr} and C^{co}). (R4)  
  - The title is vague; it could be more specific (e.g., “Temporal Relation Enhancement in Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining”). (R1)

- **Unaddressed methodological questions**:  
  - In TeminAL A, it is unclear whether overlay cases (e.g., “Dog while Cat” alongside “Dog” and “Cat”) are included in the same batch and how being trained alongside single-event samples produces a positive bias for dual-event retrieval tasks (2A, 2C). (R1)  
  - The paper does not justify the absence of alternative temporal prompts beyond [before, after, while] (e.g., then, followed by, and then) during training. (R1)  
  - The fairness of comparing against CompA-CLAP is questionable because CompA may not have been trained on the [before/after] keywords used in TeminAL. (R1)
