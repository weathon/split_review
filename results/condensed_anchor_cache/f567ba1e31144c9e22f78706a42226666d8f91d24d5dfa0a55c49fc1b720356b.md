## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ArticulateRules, a dataset of few-shot text classification tasks generated by simple rules with natural-language explanations, to test whether LLMs can provide faithful high-level explanations of their own decision-making processes. The authors evaluate GPT-3 (various sizes) and GPT-4 in-context and through finetuning, finding that articulation accuracy increases with model size (especially from GPT-3 to GPT-4), but that even finetuned GPT-3 fails to articulate explanations matching its classifications for many tasks. GPT-4 shows some emerging ability. The dataset is released as a benchmark.

### Strengths
- Timely and important topic: evaluating faithful self-explanation in LLMs (also noted as related to Turpin et al.).
- Novel dataset ArticulateRules provides a concrete methodology and testbed for evaluating faithfulness of explanations in classification tasks.
- Scope clearly described; paper well-written with clear descriptions of dataset and tasks.
- Comprehensive evaluation of a range of model sizes in-context, demonstrating a clear correlation between model capability (especially the GPT-3 to GPT-4 jump) and articulation accuracy.
- The finding that even a large model like GPT-3 fails completely on a significant portion of simple rules highlights major limitations.
- The dataset and testbed constitute a valuable foundation for future investigations.

### Weaknesses
- **Core assumption strongly questioned by one reviewer (R3):** The claim that high accuracy + adversarial robustness implies the model is using the specified rule is too strong; a model could exploit a different rule invariant to the chosen perturbations. This reviewer considers it the most significant limitation—if the assumption does not hold, the methodology is invalidated. The behavioral verification beyond a handful of perturbations is insufficient.
- **Brittle freeform articulation test:** An articulation that does not match the ground-truth rule may still describe a different but equally valid strategy for the examples, yet it is labeled inaccurate. The paper does not test whether “incorrect” articulations could also explain the in-context examples (one reviewer suggests evaluating this by hand or LLM-in-loop).
- **Limited dataset scope:** The dataset is small and simple (syntactic string-matching rules), with <256 examples. This limits generalizability to complex natural language understanding tasks involving semantics, context, and world knowledge.
- **Narrow model coverage:** Only GPT-3/4 models are tested; other architectures (BERT, Llama, Pythia) are not explored. White-box or open models would allow input attribution analysis (e.g., contrastive input erasure, perturbation methods) and improve reproducibility.
- **Faithfulness of explanations largely ignored:** The evaluation does not address whether generated explanations correspond to the model’s actual internal processes (see Jacovi & Goldberg, 2020). Even if a model can self-explain, faithfulness remains unclear; conversely, failure to self-explain does not prove absence of the right reasoning.
- **Unsubstantiated claim about GPT-4 size:** The paper speculates GPT-4 has >175B parameters without evidence. Moreover, differences between models include training data, architecture, and objectives, so performance gains cannot be attributed solely to parameter count.
- **Low sample size for finetuning:** Finetuning multi-billion-parameter models with only tens of examples (<256 total) likely contributes to the lack of improvement in articulation accuracy.
- **Missing diagnostic analysis:** The paper emphasizes finetuning GPT-3 but does not provide deeper insights into why models fail (e.g., failure patterns, attribution correlations).
- **Presentation issues:** Tables and figures exceed paper margins; Section 2 jumps into task definitions without clarifying their purpose (should have an introductory paragraph); Figure 2 shows 3 words while text says “5 space-separated lower case words”; figure references in introduction disrupt flow; “Curie showed the first signs of life” is informal phrasing; caption alignment should be left-aligned.
- **Citation inaccuracy:** GPT-4 should cite the OpenAI technical report, not Bubeck et al. (2023). “AGI Alignment” in the introduction is undefined and should be clarified.
- One reviewer (R2) questions the practical benefit of the defined self-explaining capability, noting that even consistent articulation does not guarantee faithfulness.
