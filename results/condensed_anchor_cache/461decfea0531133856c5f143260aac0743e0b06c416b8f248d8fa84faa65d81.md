## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes the Geometric Spatiotemporal Transformer (GST) for long-term physical dynamics simulation. The task is framed as autoregressive next-graph prediction from spatiotemporal graph inputs. The model integrates E(3)-equivariant spatiotemporal blocks (alternating spatial and temporal modules) and a Temporal Difference Graph (TDG) derived from the difference between the last two input frames to capture global dynamic patterns and mitigate cumulative errors. GST handles sequences of arbitrary length. Evaluations are conducted across molecular, protein, and macro-level physical systems (e.g., N-body, MD17, motion capture, protein dynamics). Reviewers note that the method shows improvements over GNN-based baselines, but raise substantial concerns about technical novelty, experimental rigor, scalability, and the true effectiveness for long-horizon prediction.

### Strengths

- Exploring the efficacy of Transformers over GNNs for physical dynamics is an important and interesting research direction; the adaptation is well-motivated (R3, R4).
- GST handles spatiotemporal sequences of variable length (R2).
- The Temporal Difference Graph (TDG) is a compelling strategy to mitigate error accumulation by predicting deltas instead of absolute positions, similar to techniques used in diffusion models (R1, R4).
- The method shows strong and consistent improvements over state-of-the-art competitors across multiple datasets (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper provides a clear mathematical and visual description of the method, including theoretical proof of equivariance (R2, R4).
- Using Transformers instead of GNNs leverages well-established scalability and flexibility (e.g., incorporating arbitrary history via TDG) (R4).
- Experiments offer insights into the contributions of individual components (equivariance, TDG) (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited technical novelty** (R3): The architecture is a straightforward extension of prior work. The equivariant component builds on previous geometric deep learning; the alternating spatio-temporal operations are well-explored in Video Transformers (e.g., TimeSformer). Essentially, the method replaces spatial self-attention with an equivariant MPNN.
- **Effectiveness for long-horizon prediction is not convincingly demonstrated**:
  - The considered datasets have at most 20 rollout timesteps; calling them "long-horizon" seems a stretch (R4). Similar concern: the long-term evaluation horizon is short (R3).
  - In Table 2, GST consistently outperforms prior methods but does not show a marked improvement at longer horizons; to substantiate the claim of efficacy on long sequences, the paper should provide evidence of stable performance over extended horizons while prior methods show significant degradation (R3).
  - The TDG partially mitigates error accumulation but does not fundamentally address the long-sequence issue, as the accumulation of temporal differences also grows over time (R3).
  - It is unclear why $\mathbf{X}_\delta$ can reduce cumulative error in long-term predictions; it appears to be only a short-term bias term. A theoretical basis is missing (R2).
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**:
  - No comparison with newer equivariant graph neural network architectures beyond EGNN. EGNN has been proven to not be truly equivariant (R2).
  - Predicting the difference instead of directly predicting the next frame has been used in methods like GNS and MGN; why are these not compared? (R2)
  - At least one additional dataset should be included to demonstrate generalizability, especially on popular datasets where multi-frame prediction methods may underperform (R1).
- **Architecture and training details insufficiently explained or justified**:
  - In the temporal module, equivariant self-attention is used for each node. Are nodes shared parameters? How does the model handle non-constant numbers of nodes across dataset samples? (R2)
  - The causal attention strategy is not clearly reflected; how does it differ from the traditional attention mechanism? (R2)
  - The detailed architectural differences between GST-V and GST-F should be explained. If GST can be extended to variable lengths based on attention, why can't ESTAG? (R2)
  - What is the temporal position embedding? Is it the standard sine/cosine positional embedding? (R4)
  - For GST-F, how many timesteps are provided? (R4)
  - In Figure 2, why is $X'_T$ summed with the delta instead of $X_T$? Is $X'_T$ just copied from $X_T$ for visualization? (R4)
- **Scalability, efficiency, and reproducibility concerns**:
  - The paper lacks runtime/flops and parameter count comparisons. Performance gains could be due to investing more compute/parameters; without such comparisons, the results are not conclusive (R4). Also, high time complexity of Transformers should be addressed with an efficiency comparison (R2).
  - Processing the history, especially in GST-V, could become quite costly; the runtime necessary for a rollout should be reported (R4).
  - The models are extremely small (hidden dim 16 with 2 layers per the Appendix). This limits the interaction capabilities of GNNs and plays into the hands of Transformers. A comparison with deeper models or a justification why small models are not limiting for GNNs should be provided (R4). Why are larger models not used? Are the datasets so small/simple that larger ones would overfit? Scaling properties should be discussed (R4).
  - Autoregressive models are commonly trained with next-step prediction. Due to the time aggregation and history components, multi-step prediction during training is necessary, which greatly limits scalability to larger models, longer rollouts, and larger problem sizes (node counts) (R4).
  - The problem sizes (number of nodes per sample) are not stated in the paper or appendix, only the number of samples per dataset (R4).
  - The architecture's complexity (equivariant modules, TDG) presents a reproducibility challenge. Providing an easy-to-use module would improve accessibility (R1). A clear, modular implementation is requested (R1, R2).
- **Minor issues** (R4): Typos in lines 72 ("inFig" space missing), 123 ("xpatiotemporal"), 264 ("the decoder process" should be "the decoder processes").
- **Additional questions indicating weaknesses** (from multiple reviewers):
  - Can the TDG framework be adapted for other symmetry types beyond E(3)? (R1)
  - Given the similar performance of GST-F and GST-V, it would be interesting to visualize attention maps to see if GST-V actually uses all history or only recent ones (R4).
  - "We collect all the long-term predictions" – does the MSE loss weight all predictions equally? (R4)
