## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Narrowing Information Bottleneck Theory (NIBT) to improve interpretability of multimodal models like CLIP, replacing the previous M2IB method. NIBT aims to remove randomness and hyperparameter dependency by using a single scalar λ to control information flow and a multivariate Gaussian noise distribution. The method also identifies negative features that hurt predictions. Experiments on Conceptual Captions, ImageNet, and Flickr8k report a 9% improvement in image interpretability, 58.83% in text interpretability, and 63.95% speed increase over state-of-the-art methods. Code is provided.

### Strengths
- Addresses multimodal interpretability, which existing unimodal methods handle poorly.
- Reports 9% image and 58.83% text interpretability gains, and 63.95% faster processing speed.
- Eliminates randomness and hyperparameter dependency from previous bottleneck methods (M2IB).
- Avoids gradient-based issues common in other attribution methods.
- Novel concept of identifying and separating negative features (adversely impacting predictions), offering a new perspective on model understanding.
- Provides thorough theoretical derivations and mathematical proofs for main claims.
- Method is simple and grounded in information theory (replacing univariate with multivariate Gaussian).
- Code is publicly available, aiding reproducibility.
- Paper is well-organized and clearly written; includes a comprehensive literature review covering M2IB, COCOA, FALCON.
- Mathematical formulae are meticulously derived and presented.

### Weaknesses
- Theorems 1 and 2 are trivial or merely remarks; they do not constitute substantial theorems.
- Qualitative example (dog/cat image) shows strong response at a corner, which is not interpretable; more examples needed for cluttered images or when relevant information is a small proportion. The only visual comparison is with M2IB, and NIBT appears less specific than M2IB in that example.
- Choice of layer 9 for attribution lacks theoretical justification.
- Using a single scalar λ to control information flow across all dimensions may oversimplify heterogeneous feature contributions, potentially over-constraining important features or under-constraining irrelevant ones.
- Gaussian noise assumption (ϵ∼N(0,σ²)) is not justified for non-Gaussian multimodal feature distributions; no mechanism to adapt noise distribution to data characteristics.
- Equation 1 eliminates β, raising questions about managing the trade-off between task-relevant and redundant information; fixed λ may limit feature discrimination.
- Equation 2 normalizes across spatial dimensions assuming uniform importance, which may not hold in multimodal contexts and risks losing fine-grained spatial information.
- Theorem 1 monotonicity proof relies on Gaussian assumption and independence of noise/features; deviations may break monotonicity.
- Theorem 2: reducing σ to zero reduces robustness in low SNR scenarios; discussion of trade-offs between interpretability and stability is missing.
- Theorem 3: negative features need empirical validation across model types; gradual reduction of influence as λ decreases may fail for nonlinear multimodal interactions.
- Computational efficiency is emphasized but no breakdown of costs relative to model complexity and dataset size is provided.
- Statistical significance of results (e.g., t-tests or bootstrap) is not reported.
- Negative feature attributions are not empirically validated as truly detrimental to predictions.
- Ablation studies on num steps and target layer are limited; interplay with other hyperparameters not explored.
- Paper exceeds ICLR page limit (main text >10 pages).
- Despite claiming relevance to high-risk domains (healthcare, content moderation), no experiments on such datasets are included; M2IB included radiology and remote sensing.
- Dataset variety is limited to standard image datasets; not sufficiently diverse to reflect real-world multimodal scenarios.
- Tables 2 and 3 have inconsistent significant figures, reducing readability.
- Minor typos: “blackbox” formatting (line 44-45), missing reference link for Appendix (line 322).
- The method is essentially changing univariate noise to multivariate Gaussian; the connection to limitations of the Information Bottleneck Principle is unclear, and theoretical analysis is loosely tied to algorithm implementation. The story is hard to follow.
- The method claims no randomness, but computation of z_{ic} still involves sampling noise.
- Why is text interpretability improvement (58.83%) much higher than image (9%)? Is NIBT better suited to text features?
- How do negative features practically improve debugging or bias detection? Are there failure scenarios?
