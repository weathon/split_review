## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LD3, a lightweight framework to learn optimal time discretization for sampling from diffusion models (DPMs) by minimizing the global truncation error between a high-NFE teacher and a low-NFE student. Two objectives are presented, with a “soft” variant achieving best results and theoretically bounding KL divergence. LD3 requires only ~100 samples and <1 hour on a single GPU (batch size 2) to train, and can be combined with various ODE solvers. Experiments on 7 pre-trained models (pixel-space, latent-space, unconditional, conditional) yield FIDs of 2.38 (10 NFE) on unconditional CIFAR10 and 2.27 (10 NFE) on AFHQv2; results also include ImageNet and text-to-image. Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of decoupling timestep and step size, and of using LPIPS over L2.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to follow.
- Simple solution with limited training time; soft teacher loss is effective and easy to implement.
- Thorough evaluation across multiple models, datasets, and sampling strategies.
- LD3 is extremely lightweight: only 100 samples and <1 hour on a single GPU needed to learn optimized schedules.
- Proper ablation study on hyperparameters/choices.
- Proofs of soundness of the minimization objectives.
- Substantial experiments include difficult datasets (ImageNet) and text-to-image model.
- Cheap training: a very low batch size of 2 is permissible.
- Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of each proposed component separately.
- Qualitative samples use fixed random seed and show clear improvement over hand-crafted schedules.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation protocol unclear (Reviewer 1).** Metrics in Tables 2–5: is FID computed against a fixed set of real images or against a set of generated images from the teacher model at high NFE? This distinction is crucial for interpreting absolute performance.
- **Teacher steps in Table 6 unexplained (Reviewer 1).** The teacher model is limited to 8 steps in that table. Why not use a higher number of steps to obtain a more accurate ODE solution? The rationale for choosing 8 steps needs clarification.
- **Model simplicity may limit performance (Reviewer 1).** The model is only a single vector (or two in the decoupled version). Table 7 shows that increasing the number of parameters yields better results; the paper should explore more complex architectures (e.g., a small neural network) to understand if the gains are due to increased capacity.
- **Limitation: retraining needed for each target NFE (Reviewer 1).** A model trained for 2-step sampling cannot be reused for 3 steps; retraining is required. This is a significant limitation for applications needing varying sampling schedules.
- **Typos (Reviewer 2).** Algorithm 1 Line 6: `x'_T ← x'_T + ...` should be `x'_T ← x_T + ...`; Line 251: `x'T` → `x'_T`; Line 251: `Ψ*` → `Ψ_*`.
- **Theorem 1 invertibility assumption inadequately justified (Reviewer 2).** The teacher solver’s invertibility is not guaranteed for all ODE solvers (e.g., explicit Euler unless step sizes are sufficiently small and vector field properties hold). The student solver’s invertibility is even more questionable at low NFE. A rigorous justification is missing. Can this assumption be relaxed and still obtain an upper bound on KL divergence?
- **Reliance on LPIPS raises generalization concerns (Reviewer 2).** Table 7 shows a significant quality drop when switching from LPIPS to Euclidean loss. For point clouds the performance gain is marginal, and for protein docking the improvement over a uniform schedule is small (and the uniform schedule may not be the optimal baseline). This dependence on a perceptual metric may limit applicability to domains without a well-defined perceptual distance.
- **Comparison to distillation methods is not apples-to-apples (Reviewer 3).** Table 9 compares LD3 to progressive distillation and consistency distillation, but details are missing (which models were compared, where baseline scores come from). The NFEs across methods do not match, making it impossible to draw firm conclusions. Progressive distillation still achieves better FID at NFE=8, albeit with much higher compute cost.
- **Lack of careful qualitative comparisons to other step reduction methods (Reviewer 3).** The vast majority of qualitative samples are compared to hand-crafted schedules (the weakest baselines). Side-by-side comparisons using the same pre-trained model, same initial noise, and matching NFE would be more convincing to show that LD3 improves over prior step-reduction methods (e.g., GGDM-style adversarial samples).
- **Claim of complementarity to distillation not justified (Reviewer 3).** The paper suggests LD3 is complementary to distillation, but it is not demonstrated that distillation methods are compatible with later optimization of timesteps (e.g., progressive distillation trains to match two-step outputs, and changing timesteps afterward may break the distilled model).
