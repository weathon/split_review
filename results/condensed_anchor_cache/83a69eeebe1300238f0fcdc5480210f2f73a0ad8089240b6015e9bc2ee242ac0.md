## Merged Review

### Summary
ShareLoRA reduces LoRA parameters by sharing adapters across transformer layers deemed redundant via a similarity-based grouping (cosine similarity of last-token representations, greedy algorithm). Performance is competitive on several tasks (classification on LLaMA 7B/8B), with up to 23% fewer trainable parameters. Reviewers found the idea interesting and the paper well-written, but raised major concerns about the practical value of the parameter reduction, missing scalability/generalization experiments, sensitivity to the threshold, technical sloppiness, and limited novelty. One reviewer (score 3) states the paper is below the acceptance threshold even at face value, while another (score 3) gives a similar critical opinion; the three score-5 reviewers are more positive but also point to significant missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The direction of reducing LoRA memory by exploiting pre-trained weight redundancy is interesting and the proposed method is sound, simple, and elegant. (R1, R2, R4)
- Experiments are conducted on multiple tasks with comparison to several baselines; the method reduces parameters while maintaining competitive performance. (R2, R4, R5)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R4)
- The method can potentially apply to a wide range of cases. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited parameter reduction vs. complexity**: A ~20% reduction in LoRA parameters (already very few) is not impressive given prior work on layer duplication and parameter sharing; the performance gain in Tables 2 and 3 is not convincing enough to justify the added complexity. (R2, R5)  
- **Missing scalability experiments**: Only evaluated on LLaMA 7B/8B; experiments on LLaMA-13B or larger (70B, 405B) are needed to demonstrate scalability. The similarity computation and sharing mechanism may face challenges with increased dimensionality. (R1, R4)  
- **Missing NLG and instruction-tuning tasks**: Current experiments focus on classification; NLG tasks (e.g., general instruction fine-tuning across a combined SFT set) are needed to show generalizability. (R1, R4)  
- **Requires a calibration dataset D\***: The method needs an external sample dataset, making it less applicable unless a common calibration set can be used across tasks – not shown in the draft. (R2)  
- **Sensitive hyperparameter (similarity threshold)**: Performance is very sensitive to the threshold (Table 4). There is no principle or rule of thumb for setting it across different models/tasks, and different thresholds are used in different experiments without justification. (R2, R3, R5)  
- **Dynamic parameter count and unclear resource impact**: The number of training parameters is dynamic and must be estimated case by case, hampering resource allocation. Reducing 20% of LoRA parameters may not change minimum GPU requirements; no memory analysis is given for training or serving. (R4)  
- **Ignores intra-layer redundancy**: Focuses solely on inter-layer redundancy, ignoring potential sharing within attention heads or submodules, which could yield further savings. (R3)  
- **Lacks analysis of where redundancy is most prominent**: No quantification of layer similarity via entropy or similarity scores to guide sharing decisions. (R3)  
- **Missing ablation studies**: No granular ablation on varying similarity thresholds, rank configurations, or sharing strategies. (R3)  
- **Technical sloppiness in writing and equations**:  
  - Typos: "LoRA LoRA" (Line 061), "AutoLoRA" instead of ShareLoRA (Line 182). (R1, R2)  
  - Eq.2 counts layers but not tied to actual parameter saving (e.g., L/2 pairs vs. one large set). (R5)  
  - Eq.3 is sloppy (“for each set s in S”, typo of x_i twice) and should be defined more carefully. (R5)  
  - Eq.4 defines cosine similarity but does not specify that the last token representation is used; this is also missing from Figure 1. (R5)  
  - Algorithm 1 is sloppy: “i not in S” is undefined (means i not in the union of s). (R5)  
- **Greedy heuristic not optimal**: The grouping algorithm is a greedy heuristic with no guarantee of optimality; no justification is given for why this particular heuristic was chosen over others. (R5)  
- **Unanswered questions about implementation details**:  
  - Are Q, K, V LoRAs shared across layers or separate? (R4)  
  - Are added ranks (according to the ratio on p.5) used in experiments, and if so what are the results? (R5)  
  - Why not distribute added ranks proportionally to group size? (R5)  
  - Why not use a criterion that directly maximizes parameter reduction instead of Eq.2? (R5)  
- **Minor**: The word "surprisingly" (p.2, para.3) is subjective given existing literature. “Improves finetuning efficiency” – no evidence given for runtime improvement, only parameter/memory reduction. (R5)
