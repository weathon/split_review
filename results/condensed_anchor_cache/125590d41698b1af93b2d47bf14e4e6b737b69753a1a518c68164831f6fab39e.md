## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a parameter-efficient codebook module that acts as a task-conditioned filter over visual representations (e.g., CLIP). The module learns a bottleneck to select task-relevant information and is trained jointly to optimize reward. The approach achieves significant performance gains on object goal navigation and object displacement across five benchmarks (ProcTHOR, ArchitecTHOR, RoboTHOR, AI2-iTHOR, ManipulaTHOR) and transfers to Habitat. Qualitative analysis shows more effective exploration and retention of task-relevant cues. The motivation is inspired by human selective attention.

### Strengths
- Strong motivation from human selective attention; paper is well written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Simple, parameter-efficient method that yields large performance improvements over EmbCLIP baseline across diverse tasks and benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Thorough ablation studies: Table 3 shows information selection effect; Grad-CAM visualizations confirm attention to relevant objects; linear probes and codebook latent size ablations (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Clever finetuning strategy: frozen codebook with a lightweight adaptation module enables transfer to new visual domains with minimal finetuning (Reviewers 2, 3).
- End-to-end learning of codebook and policy (Reviewer 3).
- Comprehensive evaluation including a new metric (Success Weighted by Episode Length) that accounts for time/effort of actions like changing viewing angles (Reviewer 3).
- Saliency visualizations are informative and highlight relevant task objects (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Agent exhibits more efficient exploration and navigation by ignoring irrelevant visual details (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Explicit incorporation of human priors (Reviewer 4, score 6):** The motivating example (keys lying only on flat surfaces) suggests incorporating priors like “the chair is usually on the floor” via text prompts, but the current approach only relies on the codebook to learn such priors implicitly. This is a missed opportunity to fully realize the potential of the motivation.
- **Codebook collapse analysis missing (Reviewer 4):** An ablation study should examine codebook usage distribution with and without dropout, and visualize whether only a small subset of codes is used. This is crucial to confirm that the codebook effectively filters redundant information and does not collapse.
- **Limited to CLIP encoder (Reviewers 2, 3):** The method is only demonstrated on EmbCLIP. It is unclear whether it transfers to other pretrained visual encoders (e.g., ViT-based models).
- **No comparison to alternative bottleneck methods (Reviewer 1, also raised by Reviewer 2):** No baselines such as a self-attention module or autoencoder bottleneck (also conditioned on goal and previous action) are compared. Reviewer 2 further suggests testing the bottleneck architecture without the codebook vectors to isolate the importance of the codebook itself.
- **No comparison to full fine-tuning of visual encoder (Reviewer 2):** While computationally expensive, a baseline with full fine-tuning of the visual encoder would show whether the codebook approach is competitive or advantageous.
- **Missing zero-shot / few-shot analysis (Reviewer 1):** The paper does not evaluate performance on novel tasks. Reviewer 1 hypothesizes that EmbCLIP might adapt faster to new tasks because the codebook discards information that could be useful for transfer.
- **Missing before/after finetuning results (Reviewer 1):** Table 2 reports performance on Habitat and HM3D Semantics after finetuning the adaptation module, but does not show the before-finetuning results of EmbCLIP+codebook, nor what the performance is when fine-tuning the entire model on Habitat. This makes the comparison apple-to-apple unclear.
- **Unclear whether codebook learns to localize goals vs bridge modalities (Reviewer 3):** Because the input includes both visual observation and language goal, the codebook might be acting as a simple alignment mechanism rather than filtering task-irrelevant information.
- **Lack of failure case analysis (Reviewer 3):** No discussion of instances where the codebook fails to identify task objects, or scenarios with two objects of the same class where the task requires selecting one.
- **Questions about details and presentation (Reviewer 2):** The term “Samplers” in experimental details is undefined. Some metrics are missing for RoboTHOR in Table 1. Typos: “HMD semantics” should be “HM3D Semantics” (also missing citation); “temperture” should be “temperature”; “codebook better encodes” should be “codebook encodes better”; “gent’s” should be “agent’s”.
- **Skip connection suggestion (Reviewer 4):** The reviewer asks whether adding a skip connection between the original embedding E and the codebook output hat{E} (i.e., hat{E} = theta(E) + Code Module(E)) would improve results, given common practice in residual networks.
