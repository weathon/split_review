## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies SGD dynamics via an SDE approximation, focusing on rescaling symmetries that yield a “law of balance.” It then analyzes a diagonal linear network with one-dimensional input and derives the stationary distribution of stochastic gradient flow, revealing phase transitions, ergodicity loss, fluctuation inversion, and other phenomena.

### Strengths
- Interesting topic and approach; the paper revisits symmetry phenomena in a novel way. (R1)
- The paper dives directly into the subject; some empirical evidence complements the claims. (R1)
- Studies a hard subject and successfully reveals interesting aspects of SGD in the presence of rescaling symmetry. (R2)
- Provides an analytical stationary distribution for diagonal linear networks, allowing clear analysis of the effects of width and depth. (R2)
- The width/depth ratio is particularly interesting for making training stable. (R2)
- Detailed analysis of the stationary distribution yields several novel insights into SGD dynamics. (R3)
- Represents an improvement over prior work (Du 2018, Wang 2022) and advances understanding of SGD in deep networks. (R3)
- Offers valuable implications connecting results to edge of stability and Bayesian inference, presenting a well-rounded theoretical contribution. (R3)
- If applicable to minibatch SGD, provides great insights. (R4)
- Focus on SGD dynamics is essential; the law of balance under the SDE approximation is potentially novel. (R5)
- The closed‑form stationary distribution for the diagonal linear net with one‑dimensional input is technical and highly non‑trivial. (R5)
- The toy model exhibits rich phenomena (phase transitions, fluctuation inversion); efforts to connect theory to practical phenomena are appreciated. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **SDE approximation vs. minibatch SGD** – A major concern: the paper claims to analyze minibatch SGD but uses an SDE formulation. There is a long line of work (Yaida 2018, Smith et al. 2021, Li et al. 2022, and 40+ papers) showing that SGD and its SDE approximations have qualitatively different dynamics, regardless of the randomness used. The paper does not discuss this literature. (R4)
  - Related reviewer questions: Does the result apply to minibatch SGD with a precise finite step size? If not, the paper is actively misleading; if yes, under what assumptions on the data? (R4)
  - R1 also asks: In Fig. 1, what is the difference between “SGD” and “Gaussian”? If an additive Gaussian model is used, they should be the same; otherwise a section explaining the noise model is needed. (R1)
- **Clarity and theorem presentation** – Writing, claims, and flow are confusing. Theorems are opaque: setting, preciseness, and assumptions are often unclear; the appendix does not help. (R1)
  - Thm 3.1 (and Fig. 1) is very confusing. The equation appears to be stated as a differential equation driven by population quantities – does it hold only in expectation? It is unclear how \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) become expectation quantities. Interpretation of the equation’s convergence and whether \(C_1,S_2\) are positive definite is ambiguous. (R1)
  - Thm 4.1: the proof is very confusing; does the theorem assume balance? (R1)
  - Section 4.3 is difficult to follow; Eq. (15) leaves many open points. (R1)
  - Many temporary notations (\(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\ldots\)) make it hard to understand how parameters change when problem parameters are varied. (R3)
- **Inconsistency in arguments** – Eq. (1) uses SGD with \(T=\eta/S\) and Section 4.4’s “infinite \(D\) limit” gives \(d/D \cdot S/\eta = \text{const}\) implying \(D\propto 1/\eta\); however, Fig. 7 uses Adam, whose scaling behavior differs substantially from SGD. (R3)
- **Edge of stability argument is invalid** – The EoS phenomenon is observed in full‑batch GD without gradient noise. The paper’s claim that “the fluctuation of model parameters decreases as the gradient noise increases” is opposite to the EoS setting and does not apply. (R5)
- **Limited scope of analysis** – The analysis is confined to a simplified linear network (diagonal, one‑dimensional input). It may not translate to practical architectures with skip connections, layer normalization, or high‑dimensional inputs. (R3, also noted by R5)
- **Law of balance – insufficient evidence** – Thm 1 involves \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) that depend on \(u,v\) and may be ill‑conditioned; it does not directly imply balancedness of \(\|u\|\) and \(\|w\|\). The argument is limited to toy cases (both theory and experiments) and does not generalize to practical settings. More evidence (e.g., deep ReLU networks with \(L\ge 3\)) is needed to verify universality. Otherwise the paper should refocus on the diagonal linear net results. (R5)
- **Missing connections to prior work** – R4 asks whether the result is a specific case of label noise (Damian et al. 2021) or random reshuffling (Beneventano 2023); the paper does not address this. (R4)
- **Convergence speed and learning rate** – No bounds on convergence speed or learning rate are provided for the discrete minibatch SGD dynamics. (R4)
- **Lack of practical verification** – The analysis is purely theoretical; it would be much stronger if the insights (stationary distribution, depth/width rule, sign coherence) were tested on more complex tasks and architectures, either theoretically or empirically. (R2)
- **Minority positive vs. majority negative** – Two reviewers (R2, R3) rated the paper 6, praising the analytical results and contribution, while the other three (R1, R4, R5) rated it 3, raising fundamental concerns about the SDE approximation, clarity, limited scope, and validity of key arguments.
