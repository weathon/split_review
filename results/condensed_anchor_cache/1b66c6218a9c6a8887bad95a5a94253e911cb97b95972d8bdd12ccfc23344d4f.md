## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a "calibrate-and-distill" framework for training binary neural networks on long-tailed datasets. It uses a pre-trained full-precision teacher (trained on a balanced dataset like ImageNet) to distill knowledge into a binary student trained on long-tailed data. Additional components include adversarial balancing of loss terms and multi-resolution learning. Experiments are conducted on 15 datasets (including newly derived long-tailed versions) and 6 baselines. All four reviewers find the problem relevant and the paper clearly written, but there is significant disagreement on novelty, fairness of baselines, and practical motivation (scores 3,3,6,6).

### Strengths

- The problem of long-tailed recognition in binary networks is new to the community and directly relates to real-world resource-constrained applications (R2, R3, R4).
- The calibrate-and-distill framework is logically coherent: aligning a balanced pre-trained teacher with long-tailed data is a novel approach in long-tailed recognition (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Extensive empirical evaluation: 15 datasets, 6 baselines, and the proposed CANDLE method shows large performance improvements (R1, R3, R4). R3 and R4 (scores 6) find the effectiveness impressive; R1 and R2 (scores 3) acknowledge the breadth but question fairness and novelty.
- The design of adversarial balancing and multi-resolution learning is clearly detailed and appears convincing (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty / generic components**: The method builds on existing techniques (knowledge distillation, multi-resolution, adversarial training, binary networks) without clear tailoring to long-tailed data. The loss functions are not long-tail‑specific (R1, R2). R2 asks: why is this a long-tailed method? Multi‑resolution and adversarial balancing could also help balanced datasets (R2).
- **Unfair or outdated baselines**: The compared baselines are long-tailed methods not designed for binary networks, so they perform poorly. Fair comparisons require combining a reasonable binary‑network training method with a long‑tail method (R2). Suggested reference: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08562.pdf (R2). Also, the set of compared methods is outdated; more contemporary baselines are needed (R1).
- **Practical motivation / teacher mismatch**: Using a pre‑trained network from a different (balanced) dataset is not practical for real‑world long‑tail problems, where natural imbalance is key (R1). The teacher does not share the target long‑tail distribution, so it is unclear whether the classes truly constitute a long tail (R2). How can a large teacher help if there is a large domain gap (e.g., ImageNet → MRI dataset)? (R1)
- **Scalability concerns**: The teacher model is large; Table 2 shows that only single‑resolution learning is used due to VRAM limits, which may restrict practical deployment (R1).
- **Missing analysis of LT challenges for binary networks**: No experiments, illustrations, or theoretical analysis on why long‑tail distributions are specifically difficult for binary NNs (R3).
- **Unclear hyperparameter / component roles**:
  - The adversarial balancing parameter λφ converges to 1 in Figure 6 (focusing on classifier over encoder); the mechanism for generalization across distributions is not explained (R1).
  - The architecture and hyperparameters of the adversarial MLP are not analyzed; it is unclear how they affect performance (R4). With three models to train (teacher, binary student, adversarial net), each may need different hyperparameters, making tuning cumbersome (R4).
  - The connection of multi‑resolution learning to the long‑tail problem is not well defined. Is it mainly for efficient training, or integral to the framework? (R3)
- **Missing mathematical details**: The LT‑aware cross‑entropy and feature similarity loss are not mathematically defined in the Approach section (R3).
- **Missing references**: Recent works on flatness in long‑tail learning that could be applied to quantized networks are missing (R2: references R1, R2, R3).
- **Writing / presentation issues**:
  - Introduction mentions large generative models, but core task is discriminative; better examples would be DINO/MAE (R3).
  - Related Work should include domain adaptation and model distillation (R3).
  - Figures 8 and 9 are low‑resolution (R4).
- **Algorithm inconsistency**: Algorithm 1 uses the same learning rate for all three components; it is unclear whether this matches the experimental setting (R4).
