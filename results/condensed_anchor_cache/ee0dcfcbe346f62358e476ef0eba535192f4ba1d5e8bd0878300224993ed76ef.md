## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Poly-Mamba, a multivariate time series forecasting method that extends standard state space models (SSMs) to a multivariate orthogonal basis projection. Key components include Multivariate Orthogonal Polynomial Approximation (MOPA), Linear Channel Mixing (LCM), and Order Combining to model channel dependencies that vary with time (CDT). Experiments on six real-world datasets claim state-of-the-art performance, especially for datasets with many channels and complex correlations.

### Strengths
- The problem of modeling high-dimensional multivariate time series with dynamic correlations is important, interesting, and long-standing with many prior works (R2).
- The motivation to extend standard SSMs—which are limited to univariate modeling and projection/reconstruction of univariate functions—to explicitly capture inter-feature dependencies is meaningful and offers an elegant solution (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper presents a well-motivated approach for capturing inter-feature dependencies, addressing a gap where most current models (including standard SSMs) rely on channel independence and fail to explicitly model these relationships (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Performance gains are marginal and likely not significant.** On the Weather dataset (highlighted by authors), improvements are only 0.2%–2%; many model fits in Figure 4 are visually indistinguishable and make similar errors (R2). Ablation studies also show ~2% effects (R2). Poly-Mamba lags behind several recent Mamba variants for time series [6,7,8,9] and is even inferior to simply using the SSM core with an additional channel attention mechanism [10] (R3).  
- **Method lacks theoretical foundations and relies on ad hoc heuristics.** MOPA’s simplification via dot product lacks theoretical analysis of approximation errors and may reduce modeling expressiveness (R1, R3). The method does not effectively address the stated issue: the weight matrix in MOPA is element-wise multiplied, so it cannot explicitly capture inter-feature dependencies (R4). Heuristics used to constrain the interaction matrix (to avoid combinatorial explosion) have no mathematical support, and failure modes or scaling to 1000s of variables are not discussed (R2). The theoretical analysis resembles storytelling without rigorous proofs (R3).  
- **Limited novelty and awareness of prior work.** The method appears to be a straightforward improvement of Gu et al. (2020, 2021, 2023) using Legendre polynomials (R2). The paper misattributes the origin of state-space models to Gu et al. (2020) and contains a factual error about the A matrix form in S4/S4D (R2, R3). Several relevant methods are not compared or discussed (e.g., orthogonal stochastic linear mixing model) (R2).  
- **Missing experimental analysis and insufficient evaluation.** The paper only conducts standard forecasting experiments; it lacks (a) other time series analysis tasks to assess Poly-Mamba as a foundational model, and (b) experiments with varying input lengths to verify long-range dependency modeling—especially since the 96-length input may not leverage SSM advantages (R1, R3).  
- **Efficiency concerns and implementation disparity.** Code reveals Poly-Mamba does not use parallel scan but recursive computation, raising doubts about the efficiency analysis in Table 3 (R3). Compared to MambaT5, Poly-Mamba is modestly better but at ~3× memory cost and 50% speed reduction (R2).  
- **Writing and presentation are crude.** Many sentences consist of one or two words, substantial white space, and poor figure/table quality (R1, R2, R3). Large tables (Table 1 & 2) should be in the appendix (R3). Odd capitalization, confusing word choice, omitted words, numerous typos, and the variable “L” used in multiple inconsistent ways (R2).  
- **Reviewer-voiced questions that overlap with criticisms.** (i) How does MOPA’s dot product operation guarantee learning complete multivariate dependencies? (ii) With the standard SSM A matrix (Laplacian decay), how can MOPA’s dot product model relationships between multivariate polynomial coefficients? (iii) How does MOPA approximate Equation 8—the formulations appear very different? (iv) What is the theoretical approximation error of MOPA in time series modeling? (v) Can algorithmic complexity be provided to prove advantage? (vi) Recent work shows Mamba components are detrimental to time series; why not improve from the SSM core? (R1, R3, R4)  
- **Contrast in reviewer opinion.** While two reviewers gave a rating of 5 (positive), the other two gave 3 (negative). One reviewer (R3) found the paper’s shortcomings severe, stating the method does not effectively address the problem and lags behind existing variants; another (R2) called the results practically meaningless and the novelty lacking. The positive reviewers (R2, R4) still noted major weaknesses including marginal performance and limited novelty.
