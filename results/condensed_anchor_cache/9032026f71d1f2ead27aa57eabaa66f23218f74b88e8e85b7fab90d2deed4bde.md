- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) to trajectory planning by adding conditional guidance and gradient guidance, producing “Guided-BFNs.” It compares Guided-BFNs with the Diffuser baseline on D4RL MuJoCo, Maze2D, and Kuka environments, reporting competitive or superior quantitative results. Ablation studies examine the roles of parameters like conditional scaling and gradient scaling, and a training‑free sampling method is proposed. Two reviewers assign scores of 3 (weak reject) and two assign scores of 5 (weak accept), reflecting disagreement on novelty, experimental rigor, and clarity.

### Strengths
- First application of BFNs to trajectory planning – an interesting and meaningfully different generative model for decision‑making tasks. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The paper builds on relevant prior work (BFNs, Diffuser) and extends it with conditioning and guidance functionality, enabling conditional trajectory sampling for RL. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Guided-BFNs surpass the Diffuser baseline on many D4RL environments (Table 1). (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The background on BFNs is clearly presented, and the figures/diagrams effectively illustrate the approach. (Reviewers 1, 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Core novelty is unclear.** The conditional and gradient guidance are very close to classifier‑free and classifier‑based diffusion guidance from Janner et al. (Diffuser). The paper largely combines BFNs with these existing techniques, and several reviewers question the novelty beyond this combination. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)  
  - Reviewer 2 notes that the training‑free guidance in Section 3.1 (definition of \(h(\tau_t)\)) and Section 3.2 (gradient \(g\)) are “almost identical” to the concepts in Janner et al. (ICML 2022).
  - Reviewer 1 asks: “Is BFN a direct replacement for diffusion models? What significant advantages does it have?”
- **Missing experimental comparisons.** The paper compares only to Diffuser, omitting other generative models and offline RL baselines such as Decision Diffuser, Decision Transformer, GAN, MAMBA, and other standard offline RL algorithms. (Reviewer 1)
- **No statistical significance reported.** Results in Table 1 lack mean, variance, and number of seeds, making it impossible to assess whether performance differences are reliable. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Mathematical presentation is flawed or unclear.**  
  - Equation (7) seems to have a typo: perhaps \(p_O \leftarrow p_O h(\tau)\) is meant; the current use of proportionality is ambiguous. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The unlabelled equation between (8) and (9) incorrectly specifies a Dirac delta function, and the derivation from Equation (4) (already a Dirac delta) leads to an inconsistency. (Reviewer 3)  
  - In Section 4.2, an unlabelled equation appears to set \(\alpha = h(\tau)\), but the text describes interpolation between sampled and constrained values—the notation does not match the description. (Reviewer 3)
- **Advantages of BFNs over diffusion models are not convincingly demonstrated.**  
  - The paper claims BFNs allow variable sampling steps while DMs are fixed to training steps, but DMs can also sample at varying numbers of steps (noise levels are randomly sampled during training). This claim appears incorrect. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
  - The qualitative results (Figure 3) do not clearly favor BFNs: Reviewer 2 finds the DM trajectory more natural (not getting too close to obstacles), while the BFN trajectory in the same figure shows the yellow star inside an obstacle when \(\alpha \to 0\) (possibly a default state). (Reviewer 2)  
  - Figure 3 (left) suggests BFNs learn faster and produce smoother trajectories, but no explanation is given for why this might be the case. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Figure 2(a) shows performance that is not monotonic with sampling steps, and it is unclear how performance scales beyond 256 steps. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing related work and background.**  
  - The paper lacks discussion of existing training‑free guidance approaches. (Reviewer 2)  
  - If BFNs are a fundamental architecture, more foundational works should be introduced; if an improvement over diffusion, more diffusion‑based works should be discussed. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The appendix closely paraphrases the original BFN paper (Graves et al., 2023) without sufficient citation. (Reviewer 3)
- **Too many hyperparameters** (e.g., conditional scaling, gradient scaling) pose a challenge for usability. (Reviewer 1)
- **Figures and presentation problems.**  
  - Figure 3 (left) has no axis label. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
  - Figure 2(c) legend: what does “\(i\)” refer to? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Figure 4 left axis label should be “\(\alpha'\)” not “\(\alpha\)”. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Fig. 2 does not specify which optimization step the checkpoints come from, and for planning steps 150, 200, 250 it is visually hard to distinguish performance. (Reviewer 4)  
  - The caption of Fig. 2 claims “finding enlightens similar results in original testbed” but does not cite supporting evidence. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Typos: line 72 capital “We”, line 94 “a” interpolation, and a missing citation for the network architecture (same as Diffuser). (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Computational complexity questions.**  
  - Why is Guided-BFN only a little slower than Diffuser when it has >7× the FLOPs? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Table 2 shows training time ~17 seconds for BFN vs ~16 seconds for Diffuser – is this correct? More detailed compute metrics (wall‑clock, flops) are needed. (Reviewer 3)
