## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates why torchvision v2 models, despite higher in-distribution accuracy, show degraded OOD detection performance compared to v1 models. The authors identify mixup and label smoothing as the root cause through both empirical and theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.2). They propose two fixes: AugDelete (fine-tunes only the linear layer on clean data) and AugRevise (adds a regularization term to preserve max-logit separability during from-scratch training). Experiments are conducted on OpenOODv1.5 with ResNets on CIFAR and ImageNet. Reviewers differ in enthusiasm: one (rating 6) found the motivation strong and novel; others (rating 3,3,5) raised concerns about scope, proof strength, modest improvements, practical limitations, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The core observation—that label smoothing and mixup, though improving ID accuracy, harm OOD detection—is clearly motivated and interesting. (R1, R2, R3)
- Paper is straightforward: problem identification, explanation, and fix are presented in a coherent story. (R1)
- Experiments isolating label smoothing and mixup as the cause are convincing and thorough. (R1)
- AugDelete is simple, lightweight, and shows consistent and believable improvements across settings. (R1, R4)
- Extensive experiments across multiple architectures and benchmark datasets support the claims. (R2)
- Theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.2) is provided to explain the mechanism. (R2)
- AugRevise and AugDelete outperform baselines in the tested settings. (R4)
- The subject of how training techniques affect OOD detection is relevant and often overlooked. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Scope limited to torchvision v2 models trained from scratch.** How findings translate to other codebases (timm), large-pretraining schemes (ImageNet21k, CLIP, Laion, distillation), zero-shot models, or recent SOTA feature-based methods is unclear. (R1, also raised by R4 on transfer learning significance and applicability to self-supervised models)
- **Unproven claim about feature-based methods.** The statement “feature-based methods are likely similarly compromised” is not backed by experiments: KNN AUROC differences between v1/v2 are marginal (Tables 7–9), and for CIFAR10 the best model is actually a v2 model with KNN. AugRevise experiments omit Mahalanobis, Relative Mahalanobis, Vim, etc. (R1)
- **Weak proof in Proposition 4.2.** The proof relies on strong, unsubstantiated assumptions (e.g., cosine similarity between ID samples is smaller than between ID and OOD). Without justification, the logic appears circular—it assumes the conclusion. A clearer justification or empirical verification is needed. (R1, R3)
- **Modest gains from AugRevise.** For v1+mixup-AugRevise and v1+LS-AugRevise, Table 1 shows improvements of only 0.72 and 0.17 respectively, which is unexciting given additional computational cost and hyperparameters. (R3)
- **Computational cost not clarified.** AugRevise (and RegMixup) forward both mixed and clean samples through the network, effectively doubling batchsize if batch size is fixed to “clean” data—an unfair advantage over baselines that forward only mixed or only clean. The paper does not explain or cost-account for this. (R1)
- **AugRevise still uses AugDelete, sometimes hurting ID accuracy.** E.g., ResNet-50 on ImageNet-1k accuracy drops significantly below the v2 baseline. It is unclear whether other augmentation strategies (autoaugment, 3-Augment [4], RSB [5]) would achieve similar OOD results with AugDelete. Only ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 are tested. (R1)
- **Retraining requirement is a limitation.** The proposed fixing methods require retraining or fine-tuning; a post-hoc correction method would be more favorable. (R3)
- **Lack of broader augmentation exploration.** Only label smoothing, mixup, RandAugment, and Style Augment are examined. The rationale for these four (vs. CutMix, etc.) is not explained, and generalizability to other augmentations is unclear. (R2)
- **Prior similar observations.** The claim that these results “challenge conventional understanding” is overstated; previous work (Müller & Hein, ICLR 2024 R2FM workshop) already noted such effects. (R1)
- **Self-supervised learning alternative.** Many studies show self-supervised models are effective for OOD detection. The paper does not address why a single classifier must handle both classification and OOD detection, nor whether the proposed methods apply to self-supervised representations or transfer learning settings. (R4)
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** Figures 1 and 2 are hard to read (small markers, unclear text). Tables lack self-contained labeling (dataset, score, model). Main paper reports only average OOD scores instead of separate near/far OOD splits (given in appendix). Methods are inconsistently omitted across tables (e.g., MDS in Table 19, ASH in Table 5). Typo: “Proposition 4.1” in line 255–256 should be “Proposition 4.2”. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Missing negative sign in loss equations.** Equations (3) and (7) omit the standard negative sign in cross-entropy. (R2)
- **Figure 2 anomaly.** “All augs (v2)” reduces both OOD detection and ID accuracy, which requires explanation. Grouping RE+TA and mixup+LS might clarify. (R2)
