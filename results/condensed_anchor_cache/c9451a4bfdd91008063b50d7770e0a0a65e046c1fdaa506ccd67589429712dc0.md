## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces geometry-informed neural networks (GINNs), a framework for training shape-generative neural fields *without data* by formulating design requirements as differentiable objectives and constraints. A modulated conditional neural field represents shapes, with latent variables ensuring diversity via an explicit diversity constraint to avoid mode collapse. The method is validated on several toy problems and a single realistic 3D engineering design problem (a jet engine bracket). Reviewers broadly describe the approach as a constrained optimization problem written as differentiable losses (e.g., zero-level set alignment with the surface, first-order derivatives matching normals, minimizing genus, smoothness, surface sampling).

### Strengths

*   Well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2).
*   The overarching framework is meaningful, promising, and has high potential for generative design and shape optimization, especially where data is scarce or expensive (R1, R2).
*   The diversity constraint is a potentially critical contribution that demonstrably works to avoid mode collapse in implicit field learning (R1, R3).
*   The paper provides a comprehensive summary and discussion of common constraints used in the validation experiments, which can guide future work (R3).
*   (Minority view: R4 acknowledges the idea is interesting and worthy of exploration, but stresses that it is only at the beginning stage and lacks a working instantiation. R4 sees the concept as promising but notes significant, predictable obstacles.)

### Weaknesses

*   **Lack of meaningful designs and insufficient evaluation.** None of the generated shapes are recognizable as mechanical, biological, architectural, or intentional engineering objects; they are abstract and appear to prioritize diversity for its own sake over functional requirements (R4, R2, R3). The quality of generated results is not discussed and appears unsatisfactory compared to prior work [1,2] (R3).
*   **Very limited experimental evaluation.** Only a single engineering design example (the jet engine bracket) is tested, making it impossible to assess generalizability, robustness to hyperparameters, or how much of the result is due to tuning (R1, R2, R3, R4). This single example does not demonstrate universality across different industrial parts with varying geometric and functional requirements (R2).
*   **No comparison to existing methods.** The paper claims no established baselines exist, but this is disputed by reviewers, who point to relevant work (e.g., “Fit and Diverse: Set Evolution for Inspiring 3D Shape Galleries”) that should have been compared or at least discussed (R3, R4). The evaluation would be strengthened by demonstrating that GINN beats such baselines (R4).
*   **Practical implementation challenges and lack of working instantiation.** The method is fundamentally optimization-based (iteratively adjusting neural network weights) and is seen by R2 as not novel, simply a repackaging of existing techniques with a specific loss formulation. Defining feasible, differentiable, non-conflicting objective functions for complex real-world mating conditions (e.g., a nut matching a screw, a joint bearing) is extremely difficult and the paper provides no universal paradigm for constraint design (R2). R4 states the paper introduces a conceptual novelty but does not provide a working realization; the concept alone should not be sufficient for publication, and the paper does not demonstrate that optimization alone can produce interesting, meaningful designs (R4).
*   **Missing important practical requirement.** The implicit representation lacks direct compatibility with standard CAD/CAE workflows (meshes, NURBS surfaces), which is a major barrier for adoption in engineering design where explicit boundary representations are needed for simulation (R1).
*   **Lack of integration with available data.** The approach focuses solely on data-free learning and does not explore whether small amounts of data could be combined with the constraint-based framework to improve robustness and accuracy (R1).
*   **Presentation issues.** The paper is at times difficult to parse; key information (e.g., definitions of “topology”, “smoothness”, “surface sampling” metrics) is not clearly grouped or highlighted, and the structure in Section 4.3 has too many headings (R3).
*   **Missing ablations or experiments to address reviewer concerns.** The paper does not show how robust the method is to hyperparameter choices (R1), does not test on more than one engineering design example (R1, R2, R3, R4), and does not offer a universal or automated paradigm for generating constraints (R2). R4 suggests a three-step research program (single meaningful design → diverse designs from optimization → combine optimization with generative modeling) that the paper fails to demonstrate even the first step of.
