## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies a partial prototype collapse (PPC) problem in clustering-based self-supervised learning (SSL) where prototypes become redundant even when the marginal latent class distribution matches a prescribed prior. The authors propose a KoLeo-proto regularization that maximizes the differential entropy of prototype vectors to encourage diversity and mitigate PPC. Experiments focus on long-tailed fine-grained datasets (e.g., iNaturalist-2018) and show improvements over the iBoT-vMF baseline, but gains on standard SSL benchmarks (ImageNet, transfer learning) are small or negative.

### Strengths

1. The paper is the first to propose and formally define the partial prototype collapse problem as an inherent challenge in SSL, offering a clear and reasonable definition and quantitative metric for prototype uniqueness.
2. The analysis of regularizations in the DINO family of SSL methods is helpful to grasp the principle of the proposed method, and the writing is clear with good organization and comprehensive literature review.
3. The proposed KoLeo-proto regularization is simple, technically sound, and achieves noticeable improvement on long-tailed fine-grained datasets with minor design changes, potentially serving as a new baseline.
4. The experiments are relatively comprehensive, covering ImageNet classification, transfer learning, long-tail datasets, and sensitivity analysis, and the claims are substantiated by the results.
5. The motivation of solving prototype collapse (which seriously affects feature quality) is sound.

### Weaknesses

1. **Scope and claims mismatch.** The title suggests the work covers all clustering-based SSL methods, but all explorations and discussions are focused on the DINO family without clarification. The last sentence of the Conclusion claims superior learning efficiency, but no experiment supports this. *(One reviewer [score 3] was significantly less positive than the others [score 6] and highlighted these gaps.)*
2. **Limited theoretical grounding and alternative designs.** It is not theoretically guaranteed that minimizing \(L_{KP}\) makes prototypes uniformly distributed in latent space. Alternative objectives like \(-\sum_i\sum_j\|\mu_i-\mu_j\|^2\) are not considered or compared.
3. **Weak motivation for long-tailed efficacy.** Although the paper targets long-tailed datasets, the connection between preventing prototype collapse and handling minority classes is not clearly explained. Why does maximizing prototype diversity inherently help with imbalanced distributions? How does KoLeo-proto move away from the uniform prior used in prior works?
4. **Incremental improvements and limited general applicability.** Gains over iBoT-vMF are small (e.g., ImageNet Table 2 results are very close). Transfer performance on ViT-base drops by 0.7% (only +0.1% on ViT-small). Finetuning improvements are negligible (Tables 2, 4). No convincing explanation is given for this trade-off, raising concerns about applicability beyond long-tailed fine-grained settings.
5. **Missing comparisons and ablations.** No direct comparison with existing regularization techniques (e.g., neural-collapse-inspired methods from [R1] that scatter prototypes into an ETF geometric structure). The impact of the regularization parameter \(\lambda\) on robustness is not thoroughly explored. It is unclear whether the method benefits other baselines qualitatively or quantitatively.
6. **Lack of visualizations and qualitative results.** The paper relies heavily on textual descriptions; visual illustrations of PPC (e.g., prototype embeddings or collapse patterns) would improve comprehension.
7. **Experimental details and design choices not clarified.** The optimizer used is not stated. The method for determining the number of prototypes for different datasets is not discussed, nor whether the initial number must be set before execution. Robustness under different hyperparameters is not evaluated.
8. **Generalizability to other architectures.** Only ViT-based architectures are tested; it is unknown whether PPC occurs in CNN-based architectures or whether KoLeo-proto helps there.
9. **Limited novelty of objective.** The employed KoLeo regularization is rather conventional, and the method does not provide convincing explanations for why it works on long-tailed and few-shot settings beyond assumption that improving uniformity of the representation space yields better distributions. Standard SSL evaluations show only limited improvements.
