## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper evaluates the impact of embedding fMRI-derived functional sub‑circuits (e.g., the Default Mode Network) into echo‑state networks (ESNs) whose reservoir is based on structural connectomes. It reports that different sub‑circuit topologies yield performance differences across cognitive tasks, with the DMN showing superior performance attributed to its functional dichotomy property. The experimental paradigm modifies network structure, observes performance variations, and provides graph‑theoretical analysis.

### Strengths
- The study innovatively embeds human brain functional topologies (especially the DMN) into ESNs, bridging neuroscience and machine learning and offering a valuable experimental perspective in computational neuroscience. (R1, R3)
- The paper is clearly written and easy to understand, with clear descriptions of the ESN model and results accessible to both neuroscience and AI readers. (R2, R3)
- Comparisons on different setups (e.g., various connectivity generation variants) are provided. (R2)
- The approach of understanding brain topology with ESN is interesting and potentially inspiring for future work. (R2)
- Thorough citation of similar works is provided, with clear distinction from the present work. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and mechanistic insight**: The paper introduces no novel methodological advancements and provides limited depth of insight into the observed results. Theoretical explanations or a mathematical basis for the phenomena are missing. (R1) The conclusion that “different topological properties are conducive to processing different tasks” highlights a lack of substantial findings. (R4)
- **Missing related works**: Several relevant studies are not discussed, such as Ref[1] (similar analysis with RNN), Ref[2] (modularity with HebbFF), and Ref[3] (paradigms for integrating connectomes into ANNs). Relating to existing work would help position the contribution. (R2)
- **Robustness of sub‑circuit partition**: The paper evaluates performance only the 7‑readout Yeo network; results may depend on this partition. The authors should verify robustness with other potential partitions. (R2)
- **Confound due to differing numbers of neurons across networks**: The sizes of the sub‑circuits differ; covariates need to be controlled. (R2)
- **Lack of statistical significance**: Many results (e.g., Figures 3–5 and Table 1) are reported without statistical testing. Perturbation‑based or bootstrap statistics are needed to assess reliability. (R2)
- **Isolation of results**: Table 1 appears disconnected from the rest. The authors should focus on cognitive tasks and provide mechanistic insight into which specific topological properties improve model performance. (R2) – *Also related: expand on how DMN modularity/centrality contribute to ESN performance beyond cognitive tasks. (R3)*
- **Missing across‑subject analysis**: The study could use subject‑specific FC/SC from HCP or other datasets with task data to investigate how network measures and performance vary per subject. (R2)
- **Questionable rationale for using DMN**: The DMN is a resting‑state network; its use for task‑specific performance enhancements needs justification. Comparisons with task‑relevant networks (e.g., Executive, Attention networks) are needed to clarify whether gains are unique to DMN topology or simply due to modularity. (R3) – *Minority view: Reviewers 1 and 2 did not raise this concern.*
- **Lack of comparison with other biologically plausible topologies**: Only random networks are compared; small‑world, scale‑free, or other brain‑inspired networks should be included. (R3)
- **No empirical neurobiological validation**: The paper lacks comparisons with empirical fMRI/EEG data or comparison of modeled task performance with human data. (R3)
- **Parameter sensitivity unexplored**: The effects of connection density, node centrality, and other key parameters on ESN performance are not ablated. (R3)
- **Unclear real‑world applicability**: The tasks are experimental; testing on more complex, real‑world‑inspired tasks would improve practical relevance. (R3)
- **Conceptual errors on echo‑state property and memory capacity**:
    - The paper claims that the echo‑state property is determined by the spectral radius being below 1, but this is a misunderstanding: networks with spectral radius above 1 can still satisfy the property. This undermines a key claim. (R4)
    - Memory capacity (MC) is incorrectly attributed to Suarez et al. (2020) instead of Jaeger (2002). (R4)
- **Confusing structure and terminology**:
    - “Structural and functional connectomes” is placed under dataset description rather than in the model formation section. (R4)
    - Terms like “control” model are not standard in machine learning. (R4)
- **Temporal scale mismatch**: How does the temporal scale of the biological networks compare to the temporal scale of the tasks? Is there any motivation for relevance? (R4)
- **Evaluation metric choice**: Why is Pearson correlation used for the memory capacity task? Why is F1‑score used instead of accuracy? (R4)
- **Results too weak to be compelling** compared to traditional ESN models; the paper reads more like a biology paper in structure and vocabulary. (R4) – *Minority contrast: Reviewers 1 and 2 found the work interesting despite limitations.*
