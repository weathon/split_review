## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies linear regression under constraints on the bias operator measured by Schatten p‑norms (nuclear, Frobenius, spectral). It derives closed‑form optimal estimators for p=1 and p=∞ and provides an extended Gauss–Markov‑type result. For the Frobenius case (p=2), ridge regression is recovered. Analytic expressions for the average test error are derived in the thermodynamic limit (N,d→∞, d/N→λ) for two data ensembles: spherical Gaussian and diagonal Gram matrix. These theoretical formulas closely match simulations on synthetic data. The main empirical claim is that the nuclear‑norm estimator often has a flatter validation‑error landscape than ridge regression, leading to better cross‑validated performance in several synthetic experiments, including with random Fourier feature mappings.

### Strengths

- The observation that allowing a small amount of bias can yield wider (flatter) minima in the test error as a function of the regularization parameter is interesting and practically relevant (R1, R2, R3).
- The theoretical derivations of optimal estimators for nuclear and spectral norms and the exact expression for the average test error for the spherical Gaussian ensemble are non‑trivial and well matched by experiments (R1, R3). Figure 2 shows close agreement between theory and simulation (R3).
- The paper provides a thorough presentation of the problem setup, main theorem (Theorem 2), and several case studies (R2). The constrained formulation (bias operator bounded in Schatten norm) is intuitive and conceptually appealing (R2).
- Experiments demonstrate that ridge regression (Frobenius norm) is not always optimal; the nuclear norm achieves comparable or better MSE in several settings, including when the data is mapped via random Fourier features (R3, R1).
- The work is of sufficient interest to the ICLR community given the centrality of linear models (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear relation to the Gauss–Markov theorem**: Several reviewers noted that the results are not a true extension of the Gauss–Markov theorem because they hold only under expectation over \(X\) for specific ensembles (Gaussian, diagonal) and in the thermodynamic limit, whereas the classical theorem holds for any fixed \(X\) (R1, R3). The paper’s claim of an “extended Gauss–Markov theorem” may be overstated (R1: “rather than extending Gauss–Markov, the paper generalizes ridge regression”; R3: “result looks not an extension”).
- **Theoretical limitations**: All theoretical results are asymptotic (\(N,d \to \infty\) with \(d/N \to \lambda\)) and assume either a spherical Gaussian or a diagonal Gram matrix (R3). This severely limits applicability to common high‑dimensional settings where the number of observations is sub‑linear in the dimension, and to real data that deviate from these distributions (R3). One reviewer (R3) stressed that the practical relevance of the findings is reduced because real datasets rarely satisfy these assumptions.
- **Insufficient theoretical support for the “flat minima” claim**: While the empirical evidence shows flatter minima for the nuclear norm, the paper lacks a rigorous theoretical framework to explain why this occurs and how it relates to the constraints on the bias operator (R2). One reviewer (R2) considered this a major weakness.
- **Weaknesses in experimental methodology and scope**:
    - Experiments are conducted only on synthetic data; no real‑world datasets are included (R1, R3). This limits the practical relevance of the conclusions (R1).
    - The grid search for the regularization parameter \(\alpha\) uses only 9 log‑spaced values. This may bias the comparison in favor of methods with flatter minima, because a deeper but narrow minimum (ridge) might be missed (R1). The paper does not investigate the sensitivity to the number of grid points (R1).
    - Figure 5 reports only winners, not the actual MSE values; a method could win by a tiny margin (R1). Including full MSE would be more informative.
    - The paper does not compare with regularization outside the Schatten‑norm family, such as Lasso (\(\ell_1\) norm), which is widely used in high‑dimensional settings (R3).
    - One reviewer (R1) noted that the improvement in flatness (and thus the benefit in cross‑validation) is sometimes minor (Figure 2).
- **Missing or unclear links between \(C\) and \(\alpha\)**: In Definition 1 the constraint parameter \(C\) appears, but later results (Theorem 2, Figure 1) refer only to \(\alpha\). The relationship between \(C\) and \(\alpha\) is not explained, nor is the range or interpretation of \(\alpha\) discussed (R2, R3).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
    - The paper contains numerous typographical errors (e.g., “somewht”, missing periods, reversed quotation marks in the introduction) and inconsistencies in notation (e.g., \(L(X)\) used once; asterisk undefined; variance formula \(\operatorname{var}_\epsilon = L^T L\) should be \(\sigma^2 L L^T\); \(L \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times N}\) should be \(\mathbb{R}^{d \times N}\)) (R1, R2, R3).
    - Figures and equations are often unnumbered, making cross‑references difficult (R1). References to non‑existent figures (e.g., “Figure 2.2.4”) appear in the text (R1).
    - The MSE expression in Section 2.2.1 differs significantly from that in Section 2.2.2 without explanation; the integrals are almost identical but with different measures, raising concerns about possible typos (R1).
    - In Theorem 2, the notation \(\max(\Sigma,\alpha)\) is unconventional (element‑wise maximum of a matrix and a scalar?) and requires clarification (R2).
    - Figure 1 plots only the special diagonal case of \(X\); the paper would benefit from similar plots for other ensembles (R3).
- **Minor correctable issues**:
    - The expectation in Theorem 2.1 should be over \(\epsilon\) only, since the average over \(X\) is already taken in the MSE definition (R3).
    - Figure references: “Figure 3.1” should be “Figure 3”; “Figure 3.2.1” should be “Figure 5” (R3).

**Note**: One reviewer (R2) rated the paper lower (5 vs. 6) and expressed lower confidence (3 vs. 4) and lower soundness (2 vs. 3). The main points of disagreement are the theoretical depth supporting the flat‑minima claim and the overall presentation quality. The other two reviewers also noted presentation issues but found the contribution stronger. The experimental methodology concerns (9‑point grid, only synthetic data) were raised by R1 and echoed by R3; the lack of comparison with Lasso was raised by R3; the unclear \(C\)–\(\alpha\) relation was raised by R2 and R3. All reviewers agreed that typos and clarity issues should be fixed.
