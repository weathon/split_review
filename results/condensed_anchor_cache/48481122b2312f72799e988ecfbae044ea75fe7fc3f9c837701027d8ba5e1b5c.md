## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes InvICL, a permutation-invariant ICL method that simultaneously achieves information non-leakage and context interdependence. Reviewers find the idea novel and clearly motivated, the theoretical connection to gradient descent straightforward, and the experimental results strong across synthetic, OOD, and real-world tasks. Concerns center on practical applicability (computational overhead, reliance on MetaICL finetuning, outdated primary model), experimental completeness (missing standard deviations, sparse training checkpoints, questionable Prefix ICL baseline results), and limited connection to real LLM ICL (existence proof vs. learned weights, unrealistic training assumptions). Several implementation and theoretical questions remain.

### Strengths
- Novel and clear idea: formalizes three key properties for invariant ICL (permutation invariance, information non‑leakage, context interdependence) that were not simultaneously satisfied by prior work.
- Simple but effective theoretical analysis: Theorem 4.1 shows InvICL approximates standard gradient descent.
- Strong experimental results: outperforms both invariant and non‑invariant baselines on synthetic tasks (Figure 3), OOD scenarios (Figure 7), and real‑world datasets (Table 2); demonstrates superior length generalization.
- Smart parallel implementation: achieves the same order of computation as standard ICL using duplicated input sequences and a leave‑one‑out attention pattern; memory overhead is discussed (14% increase from 4.2 GB to 4.8 GB for GPT‑2 Large when input length grows from 512 to 1024).
- Well‑written and well‑presented: clearly distinguishes contributions from prior invariant ICL methods (e.g., Prefix ICL, PCW, BoE).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of mechanistic explanation for length generalization.** The observed improvement is not analyzed; the paper does not provide ablation studies or attention visualizations to isolate the contribution of the permutation‑invariant attention mechanism.
- **Missing statistical significance and training dynamics.** Most results lack standard deviations. Only checkpoints at 50k and 200k training steps are reported; training curves (e.g., squared error vs. epoch) are needed to assess convergence and compare learning rates.
- **Prefix ICL linear‑regression results are questionable.** Reported squared errors around 0.5 even with 40 examples, whereas prior work (von Oswald et al., ICML 2023) reports errors <0.1 for 5‑layer transformers with 10 examples and dimension 10. Questions arise about the experimental setup:
   - Is the loss taken over only the test token or all tokens (autoregressive loss) in Prefix ICL during training? If the latter, it misuses the architecture because context examples can attend to their own labels.
   - Can the test token attend to itself in the Prefix ICL implementation? Other papers (von Oswald et al.; Ahn et al., NeurIPS 2023) avoid such self‑attention.
   - Does the “positive encoding” in the footnote correspond to positional encoding?
- **Computational efficiency concerns.** Doubling the input sequence length (Figure 2d) increases memory usage. The proposed attention mask is incompatible with FlashAttention optimizations; for long contexts (e.g., 64k tokens) the overhead becomes super large. Additionally, training inefficiency: in InvICL, \(\hat g(x_i)\) and \(\hat g(x_{i+1})\) cannot be computed in a single forward pass.
- **Practical applicability is limited by MetaICL finetuning.** The method relies on expensive ICL‑specific finetuning; it is unclear how InvICL would perform in few‑shot settings without such finetuning, especially for scenarios where finetuning is not practical.
- **Unfair comparison to standard AR ICL in real‑world experiments.** If AR ICL is not also fine‑tuned on the ICL tasks, comparing a MetaICL‑fine‑tuned model to a general LLM is not a level playing field.
- **Primary model (GPT‑2 Large) is dated.** While appendix results with GPT‑Neo 2.7B and Pythia‑2.8B are provided, evaluating on more recent models like Phi‑3.5 or Llama 3.2 would better demonstrate the method’s scalability and relevance to current architectures.
- **Theoretical analysis is limited.** Theorem 4.1 is an existence proof: it shows that *if* weight matrices take a particular form, InvICL approximates gradient descent, but it does not discuss whether trained transformers (especially those not explicitly trained with an ICL objective) converge to such weights. Results in Section 5.1 assume training with an ICL objective, which is unrealistic for large pre‑trained LLMs (see e.g., the ICML 2024 paper [arxiv:2310.08540] that questions the compatibility of ICL‑objective training with real LLM ICL). The paper should discuss these limitations.
- **Presentation inconsistency.** Figure 1 uses “PCW” while Table 1 uses “Inv ICL” for the same method, making comparisons confusing.
- **Novelty is partially incremental.** Some reviewers view InvICL as a concatenation of prior works (PrefixICL and a variant of PCW).
- **Theoretical and design questions remain.**
   - Definition 3.3: the condition \(j \neq i\) for context interdependence may conflict with information non‑leakage. Should the condition be \(j < i\) instead? Moreover, why is context interdependence necessary for auto‑regressive LLMs that cannot attend to future demonstrations?
   - External modification of attention masks (through BoE aggregation) deviates from how the model was trained to read activations. How does this affect internal representations and potentially increase hallucination?
   - Does InvICL exhibit a learning plateau during training?
   - If trained with InvICL, do the weight matrices align with the form required by Theorem 4.1?
   - Why does adding only symmetric positional embedding not improve performance?
