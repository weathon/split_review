## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces FreCaS, a training-free cascaded sampling framework for higher-resolution image generation. It decomposes sampling into stages with progressively increased resolution, uses frequency-aware classifier-free guidance (FA-CFG) to assign different guidance strengths to different frequency components, and fuses cross-attention maps across stages to maintain layout consistency. Experiments show FreCaS is about 2.86× and 6.07× faster than ScaleCrafter and DemoFusion for 2048×2048 generation with SDXL, with FID_b improvements of 11.6 and 3.7, and can be extended to SD3.

### Strengths

- **Efficiency**: FreCaS is significantly faster than existing training-free methods (ScaleCrafter, DemoFusion) for 2048×2048 generation.
- **Fine-grained frequency control**: FA-CFG assigns different guidance strengths to different frequency components, achieving more fine-grained control than standard CFG.
- **Adaptability**: FreCaS can be extended to more complex models (e.g., SD3), demonstrating broad applicability.
- **Simple and effective design**: Leveraging latent space of lower resolutions reduces computational cost in early stages; efficient reuse of low-frequency information streamlines generation.
- **Thorough ablation study**: Ablations cover critical factors (inference steps per resolution stage, adjustments of CFG weights, reuse of cross-attention maps).
- **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper is well-written with a straightforward motivation.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing standard FID metric**: The paper reports only FID_b and FID_p, not the standard FID between generated and real image sets, which is the most widely used metric for this task. This makes it difficult to comprehensively judge effectiveness. (Reviewers 2 and 3)
- **Lack of comparisons with training-based and super-resolution methods**: The evaluation focuses solely on training-free baselines. Missing comparisons with training-based high-resolution generators (e.g., PixArt-Sigma, UltraPixel) or super-resolution methods (e.g., ESRGAN, SUPIR) would provide a more complete picture. (Reviewer 1)
- **Marginal novelty and similarity to prior work**: The coarse-to-fine cascade and frequency-domain enhancement are common in training-free high-resolution generation (e.g., HiPrompt, ResMaster, DemoFusion, FouriScale). The idea of frequency-dependent guidance is somewhat similar to FreeU and FreeEnhance. Overall, the contribution is considered marginal without a comprehensive evaluation that isolates the improvement from FA-CFG and the frequency-step calculation. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Limited realism in high-resolution results**: Most 4K comparison results are in non-realistic styles (painting); more comparisons in realistic styles are needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: FreCaS relies on several hyperparameters (e.g., w_l, w_h, w_c) that may require careful adjustment for optimal results, increasing experimental complexity. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential viability concerns**: Training-free methods like FreCaS typically have low success rates, often requiring many attempts for a single satisfactory result. Reviewer 1 expresses skepticism that such approaches offer a viable solution compared to training-based methods, which are generally faster, more reliable, and stable even without large computational resources. (Reviewer 1)
- **Question about cross-attention map role**: In Figure 6, varying the weight of the cross-attention map (0–1) appears to affect fine details/textures (e.g., blueberry surface) rather than layout, which remains stable. This seems to contradict Section 3.4’s claim that cross-attention maps capture semantic layout. The reviewer requests clarification on whether cross-attention maps primarily control details/textures or layout. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing GPU memory comparison**: GPU memory requirements for FreCaS and compared methods are not reported. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited real-world evaluation**: The experiments do not explore performance across different image contents, styles, and complexities. Comprehensive metrics beyond FID/IS (e.g., human subjective evaluation) are suggested. (Reviewer 4)
- **High computational demand despite efficiency**: Although FreCaS improves efficiency, the computational resource demand remains high when generating high-resolution images. (Reviewer 4)
- **Marginal improvement on SDXL**: Some results on SDXL show only marginal improvement. (Reviewer 2)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) is considerably more negative, emphasizing the limited novelty and questioning the viability of training-free methods, while the other three (score 6) note solid efficiency and fine-grained control but agree on missing FID and marginal contributions relative to prior cascade frameworks.
