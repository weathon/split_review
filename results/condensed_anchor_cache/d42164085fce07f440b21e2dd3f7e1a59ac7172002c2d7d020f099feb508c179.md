## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces WebRL, an online reinforcement learning framework for training open LLMs as web agents. WebRL uses a self-evolving curriculum that generates new tasks from failure cases, an outcome-supervised reward model (ORM), adaptive RL strategies, and a replay buffer with KL regularization. On WebArena-Lite, WebRL improves Llama-3.1-8B from 4.8% to 42.4% success rate and GLM-4-9B from 6.1% to 43%, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo (17.6%) and GPT-4o (13.9%) and prior open-LLM agents (AutoWebGLM 18.2%). Reviewers agree the topic is important and results are strong, but raise concerns about the RL algorithm’s novelty and derivation, reliance on manual filtering for curriculum generation, unfair experimental comparisons, insufficient ablations and training details, clarity of writing, and evaluation on a single benchmark.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles the important problem of training open LLMs as web agents without expensive proprietary APIs.
- WebRL achieves strong and consistent performance gains: Llama-3.1-8B improves from 4.8% to 42.4% on WebArena-Lite, outperforming GPT-4-Turbo (17.6%) and GPT-4o (13.9%).
- The experimental evaluation includes a good variety of baselines (prompt-based, imitation learning, DigiRL) and an in-depth analysis (noted by one reviewer, R2).
- The online RL framework avoids the need for large-scale pre-collected training data, in contrast to SFT approaches.
- The framework demonstrates scalability to larger models (70B version reported).
- One reviewer (R2, score 8) found the paper well-written and enjoyable to read.

### Weaknesses
- **RL algorithm novelty and justification**: The authors derive a new policy gradient algorithm without clear motivation over standard approaches (e.g., PPO with KL-regularized reward). The algorithm appears nearly equivalent to REINFORCE with a value baseline but adds a β scaling factor that makes the gradient sensitive to β; convergence properties are not analyzed. The derivation from Eq. (4) to Eq. (5) is mathematically unsound because it relies on a potential shaping result that does not hold under KL-regularized RL (R1). R3 similarly questions why Eq. (6) is preferred over standard KL loss.
- **On-policy / off-policy mismatch**: The algorithm is on-policy (policy gradient) but uses an off-policy replay buffer. No off-policy correction term (e.g., importance sampling) is discussed, risking instability and divergence (R1).
- **Missing core ablation on curriculum learning**: The paper attributes DigiRL’s weaker performance to lack of curriculum learning but does not ablate the curriculum component in isolation (e.g., WebRL without curriculum, DigiRL with curriculum). This makes it impossible to credit the improvement to curriculum learning versus other components (new RL algorithm, KL, replay buffer) (R2, R3). R1 also questions the “self-evolving” label given manual filtering.
- **Unfair experimental comparison**: All other RL-based baselines (including DigiRL) are trained only on the initial 500 instructions, while WebRL trains iteratively over multiple phases. Training curves (steps vs success rate) and error bars over multiple seeds are not provided (R3).
- **Self-evolving claim undermined by manual steps**: The curriculum’s “self-evolving” nature is weakened because new tasks are ultimately curated by human review (L261). The extent of this manual filtering’s impact on training is unknown (R1, R2, R3).
- **Writing clarity and presentation**: The paper is difficult to follow; the introduction of ORM and curriculum learning is confusing and lacks clarifying examples. The flow should introduce curriculum learning earlier. Appendix A.3 is empty of explanatory text (only figures). Citations should list oldest to newest and refer to published venues rather than arXiv (R1, R2, R3).
- **Reproducibility**: Limited training details are reported: hyperparameters, observation/action space specification, ORM training procedure, replay buffer usage, per-step perplexity computation, discount factor choice, and phase definitions are missing or unclear (R3).
- **Single benchmark and task complexity concerns**: Only WebArena-Lite is evaluated; generalizability to other environments (e.g., Android as in DigiRL) and real-world tasks is not demonstrated (R1). Additionally, the claim that WebRL “excels in more complex instructions” is not supported by Figure 5, where both WebRL and DigiRL show significantly lower success rates at higher complexity levels (R1).
- **Missing related works**: Several RL-for-LLMs works (GLAM, TWOSOME, RL4VLM, ArCHer) are not cited (R3).
- **KL ablation mislabel**: The “WebRL without KL” variant performs SFT rather than RL without KL; it should be called “WebRL-SFT” to avoid confusion (R1).
