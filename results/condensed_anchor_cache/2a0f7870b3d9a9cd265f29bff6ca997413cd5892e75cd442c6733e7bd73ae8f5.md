## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the statistical foundations of conditional deep generative models for distribution regression. It proposes a likelihood-based sieve maximum likelihood estimator and establishes convergence rates in Hellinger and Wasserstein metrics depending on intrinsic dimension and smoothness of the true conditional distribution. The analysis uses neural network approximation theory, covers both sparse and fully connected architectures, and introduces a noise perturbation strategy for manifold-supported data. Numerical experiments on synthetic data and MNIST support the theoretical claims.

### Strengths
- Very well written; the method is clearly explained and proofs are followable (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical results appear correct and sound; there are detailed analyses for different setups and a matching lower bound (Reviewer 2).
- The error analysis is useful for anticipating the number of data points needed to achieve a targeted error with respect to Wasserstein or Hellinger distances (Reviewer 3).
- The paper tackles a very interesting topic with little existing theory for conditional generative models; Theorem 1 presents a nice approach splitting the problem into covering number and approximation accuracy (Reviewer 4).
- The noise injection strategy (Corollary 2) is neat, particularly for data distributions close to singular (Reviewer 4).
- Writing and mathematics are rigorous, with clearly defined objects (Reviewer 4).
- Numerical results qualitatively support the main claims (Reviewers 2, 3, 4) and qualitative behavior of theorems is verified on understandable toy examples (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 4 (rating 8) is substantially more positive than Reviewers 2 and 3 (ratings 5) and Reviewer 1 (rating 6), praising the topic and approach after rebuttal.

### Weaknesses
- The method appears to extend techniques from previous work (e.g., Schmidt-Hieber 2020, Tang and Yang 2023) with some details filled in; novelty is not strongly differentiated (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Numerical examples are very simple and loosely connected to the theoretical results (Reviewers 1, 2). For validation, experiments should consider manifolds with known intrinsic dimension (maybe conditional) and use a pretrained generator as ground truth to directly verify the impact of smoothness and approximation error (Reviewer 4).
- Paper is not in standard ICLR format: left/right margins are altered significantly; under the standard style template the submission would exceed the page limit (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The paper is not easy to follow in places due to technical presentation and mixing background with main results; a preliminary section before Section 2 would help (Reviewer 2).
- Many technical assumptions limit practical impact; Assumption 3 excludes interior points — does this hold (approximately) in practical applications? (Reviewer 2).
- Theoretical results are hard to verify in practice: asymptotic rates are given but it is unclear how they manifest with finite datasets; constants in the theorems depend on model parameters and domain characteristics but have no clear way of being estimated, and their sensitivity to perturbations and scaling with dimensionality is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- The modelling assumption in Section 2.1 (independent Gaussian on latent Z to account for mismatch) seems unrealistic; a running concrete example (e.g., pushforward of Gaussian under a linear operator) would help explain rates and corollaries (Reviewer 4).
- Scaling in Corollary 1 for σ*: as noise level tends to zero, approximation accuracy must increase substantially; controlling Wasserstein distance directly (since W₁(p, p*N(0,σ)) is close for small σ) might alleviate this (Reviewer 4).
- Section 2.4 is not rigorous regarding whether it treats joint measures of (X,Y) or conditional measures Y|X=x; proofs appear to consider conditionals for arbitrary X=x but this should be clarified (Reviewer 4).
- Questions that overlap with criticisms: (a) Partition of unity in multiple-chart manifold case (Section 2.4.3 and Appendix C): when using partition of unity to define local transport maps, weights vanish on boundaries so induced local distribution density is not lower bounded — how can optimal transport regularity theory be applied? (Reviewer 1). (b) Do numerical results empirically support the proven rates? Perhaps take log of sample size and inspect slope of error (Reviewer 2). (c) How easy are the conditions for convergence theorems to verify? (Reviewer 3). (d) Can in-probability bounds be strengthened to expectation bounds over x under stronger assumptions? (Reviewer 4). (e) What is a practical example where smoothness disparity is obvious? (Reviewer 4). (f) Missing citations on generative models and manifolds, the relation between conditional and joint measures, and how different conditional generative models (NFs, VAEs, diffusion) optimize the MLE estimator (Reviewer 4). (g) How does the composition approach relate to flow matching/diffusion with ODEs, which are more SOTA than VAEs? (Reviewer 4).
