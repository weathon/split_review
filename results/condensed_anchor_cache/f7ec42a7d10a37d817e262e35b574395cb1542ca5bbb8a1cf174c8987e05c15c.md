## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReViWo, an autoencoder-based framework that learns a view-invariant representation (VIR) and a view-dependent representation (VDE) from multi-view data, then uses the VIR to train a world model and policy for robotic manipulation. The approach is evaluated on Meta-world (3 tasks) and PandaGym (1 task) under camera installation position (CIP) disturbances and camera shaking (CSH), and on a real-world ALOHA robot. Results show improved robustness over baselines, though performance degrades under severe viewpoint changes.

### Strengths
- The training setup to enforce separation of view-invariant and view-dependent encoders is clever and appears novel (R1, R2, R3).
- View generation results indicate that the encoders and decoder learn the intended representation (R1, R3).
- The problem of viewpoint robustness is extremely important and challenging; the solution is appealingly elegant and original (R3).
- ReViWo achieves strong improvements over prior world model approaches in generalization to novel viewpoints on Meta-world and PandaGym (R1, R3).
- Comparative experiments and ablation studies are thorough (R2, R4).
- The work leverages the Open X-Embodiment dataset, which is a promising direction in robotics (R4).
- The approach of using a learned low-dimensional state representation for offline RL is interesting (R5).
- The paper is easy to follow (R5), and the supplementary video demonstrates real-world manipulation (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited evaluation scope** – Experiments are conducted on only 3 of 50 Meta-World tasks and 1 Panda-Gym task, raising concerns about cherry-picking and insufficient diversity (R3, R4, R5). One reviewer (R5) considers this evaluation insufficient for the claimed generality. Tasks are simple; it is unclear how ReViWo would perform on more complex scenarios such as deformable object manipulation (R4) or tasks requiring depth information (R5).
2. **No real-world validation of the core method** – The paper includes only one real-world experiment on ALOHA, while the main evaluation is in simulation. It is not shown how the approach works in real-world settings that lack abundant multi-view data (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer (R5) argues the setting is impractical: only single-view RGB without depth is used, whereas robotics typically uses multi-view or RGBD for point-cloud reprojection.
3. **Figure 4 is misleading** – The y-axis skips the 40%–80% range, exaggerating the differences between methods. This must be corrected (R2, R3). Also, success rates for ReViWo still decline significantly under CIP and CSH (e.g., Drawer-Open CSH drops below 40%), indicating limited robustness (R2, R3).
4. **Overclaiming in text** – The paper states ReViWo “consistently surpasses the baselines,” but on Window-Close (CIP), MVWM achieves ~85% success rate, higher than ReViWo; on CSH, MVWM is also slightly better on that task. Authors should comment on these exceptions (R3).
5. **No comparison with important related works** – Baseline comparisons do not include RT-X series works, RoboUniView, Perceiver Actor, or 3D Diffuser Actor, which address viewpoint robustness via calibrated camera poses or 3D keyframes (R1, R3).
6. **Action space not specified** – The choice of action space (joint-space delta, end-effector camera-frame delta, or manipulator-frame delta) is critical for viewpoint transfer. It is not stated which was used, nor whether the approach works for all types (R1).
7. **Lack of justification for hyperparameters** – The λ₁ and λ₂ coefficients (VQ and contrastive terms in Eq. 3) are given in Table 3 but with no explanation of how they were chosen or how sensitive performance is to their values (R3). Similarly, the weighting factor for unlabeled Open X-Embodiment data is not reported or analyzed (R3).
8. **Inconsistencies in ablation studies** – Inclusion of Open X-Embodiment data degrades performance on Door-Open (CSH) compared to not using it (Table 1). The world model (WM) component degrades performance on Drawer-Open (CSH) compared to the policy without WM (Table 2). Yet the text claims “consistent performance enhancement” – authors should comment on these drops (R2, R3).
9. **Drop in behavior cloning (BC) results** – The BC results in Appendix E.2 show a significant performance drop; the paper does not explain why. The action space or reference frame may be a cause (R1).
10. **No failure case analysis** – Figure 7 shows successful view reconstructions, but it is not specified whether they come from training or test data. Given the large performance gap between training and test, authors should show and discuss failure cases to help readers understand when view-invariance fails (R3).
11. **VQ-VAE and world model blurring** – Using a VAE for image reconstruction may produce blurry outputs, and compounding predictions in the multi-step world model could amplify errors. The paper does not address this (R4). Also, no guarantee is given that the two encoders actually disentangle view-invariant and view-dependent information (R5).
12. **Fundamental limitation (minority reviewer)** – One reviewer (R5) argues that focusing on view-invariant representation is inherently limiting: robotic tasks often benefit from multi-view information (e.g., merging static top camera and gripper camera). View-invariant representations lose object-layout sensitivity (e.g., a moving camera is equivalent to a moving desk). The paper provides no mathematical or empirical justification that the intended disentanglement holds; the two encoders may simply work in parallel without achieving the claimed property (R5).
13. **Technical contribution questioned (minority reviewer)** – The use of COMBO for world model and reward model training is not novel; the paper’s method section is considered hand-wavy (R5). The claim that ReViWo learns a “world model” is debatable because COMBO only predicts the next low-dimensional state, not a full generative model (R5).
