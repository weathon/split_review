## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces intervention extrapolation: predicting the effect of an unseen intervention \(A=a^*\) on outcome \(Y\) when \(A\) is exogenous and affects \(Y\) through a latent variable \(Z\), where observed \(X\) is an injective (noiseless) function of \(Z\) (\(X=g_0(Z)\)) and the effect of \(A\) on \(Z\) is linear. Under these assumptions, the authors prove that \(E[Y\mid do(A=a^*)]\) can be identified up to an affine transformation of \(Z\), propose an algorithm combining an autoencoder with a maximum moment restriction (MMR) regularizer to recover the encoder, and then apply a control function (CF) approach to estimate the causal effect. Theoretical identifiability results (building on Newey et al. (1999) and D’Haultfoeuille (2011)) and synthetic experiments support the approach. All four reviewers rated the paper 8, praised its clarity and novelty, but raised concerns about the strength of the assumptions and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Important problem**: OOD estimation of intervention effects / intervention extrapolation under a well-chosen set of assumptions (R1, R3, R4). The paper fills a need for theoretical arguments showing concrete benefits of identifiable representation learning (R4).
- **Excellent writing and presentation**: Extremely well‑written, clear, well‑structured, and easy to follow despite technicality (R1, R2, R3, R4). Propositions are placed exactly when the reader thinks about the question (R1). Notation is crisp and transparent (R4).
- **Serious, detailed, and novel theoretical contributions**: The proof of extrapolation is non‑trivial and brings together causal tools (invariance principle, mixing‑unmixing, instrumental variable approaches) (R1). The theoretical analysis is rigorous and detailed (R2). The main proof ideas are given in the main text, which is rare in this literature (R4). Results are well modularized to facilitate reuse (R4). The method to learn the encoder up to affine transformation is novel and interesting (R4). The identification strategy is novel (R3); using a control function to achieve intervention extrapolation is a nice idea (R2).
- **Practical algorithm**: Straightforward and practical (R3).
- **Overall impression**: “I’m left with a good feeling of having learned something new.” (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Strong and hard‑to‑verify assumptions**: The linear relationship \(A\to Z\), the injective noiseless mapping \(Z\to X\) (so \(X=g_0(Z)\)), and the additive noise model \(Z\to Y\) are all strong and involve the hidden variable \(Z\), making them difficult to assess in practice (R1, R2, R3, R4). The linearity assumption is “reasonably strong” and would benefit from future extension to GLMs or non‑linear representation learning (R1). The injectivity of \(g_0\) (and the noiseless proxy) is particularly strong – can \(X\) be a noisy observation of \(g_0(Z)\)? (R2, R3, R4). The authors should provide concrete examples (e.g., latent health status with observed symptoms) and guidance on how practitioners could evaluate the plausibility of these assumptions (R3).
- **Missing sensitivity and limitation discussion**: The paper ends abruptly without a conclusion or discussion of limitations (R4). The method’s performance under assumption violations (e.g., non‑linear \(A\to Z\), non‑injective/noisy \(Z\to X\), non‑additive \(Z\to Y\)) is not explored. Additional experiments (e.g., varying the strength of unobserved confounders, comparing with other methods, replacing the MMR part with iVAE (Khemakhem et al., 2020)) are needed (R2, R4). The accumulation of errors in the multi‑step estimation procedure is not addressed (R3).
- **Insufficient comparison with related work**: The paper should more thoroughly contrast its assumptions and results with iVAE (Khemakhem et al., 2020), which uses an identical causal graph \(A\to Z\to X\) and also assumes injectivity of \(g\) but allows additive noise on \(X\) and non‑linear \(p(Z\mid A)\) in the exponential family – the relative strength of assumptions is not clearly compared (R2). The connections to Wu & Fukumizu (2021, 2022) on treatment effect estimation with identifiable models (which mention CMR and CF ideas) should be discussed (R2). The relationship to the negative control literature would also be informative (R3). Affine identifiability results should be contrasted with linear identifiability in disentanglement/sparsity works (R4).
- **Technical novelty concerns**: Theorem 4 appears to be a direct adaptation of the control function approach of Newey et al. (1999), and Theorem 6 an adaptation of the instrumental variable approach of D’Haultfoeuille (2011). The paper should explicitly discuss what is new compared to these earlier works, or be more explicit about this limitation (R2).
- **Clarifications needed on specific points**:
  - The role and use of **Wiener’s Tauberian theorem** (mentioned in the abstract but not delineated in the main text) (R1, R2).
  - **Proposition 1**: Clarification is needed on how the “set with positive Lebesgue measure outside supp(A) is defined given that the supports of the two settings agree on supp(A)” (R1).
  - **Proposition 3** appears to be a trivial restatement of Definition 2 and adds confusion (R2).
  - **Theorem 4**: The sudden change of notation is confusing (R3).
  - **Equation (16)** (after Eq. (3) in the main text?): The reasoning that the inside of the conditional expectation equals \(V\) without assuming \(E[V]=0\) should be explained further, as it is crucial to the algorithm (R4).
  - **Lemma 7** in Appendix A: The proof technique and the definition of conditional independence used should be cited or explained (R4).
- **Minor issues**:
  - \(M_0\) having full row rank implies \(\dim(A)\ge\dim(Z)\); this might be worth making explicit (R4).
  - The noise variables \(U\) and \(V\) are easy to confuse; renaming to \(V_z\) and \(V_y\) would help (R4).
  - Footnote 1: what is \(\mathcal{B}\)? (R4).
  - The paper could consider whether the linear assumption can be relaxed to \(M_0 t(A)+V\) for a known transformation \(t\) (e.g., \(A^2\)) or to a parameterized class \(m_\theta(A)+V\) (R3).
  - Can the linear assumption be viewed as a Taylor approximation around \(\text{supp}(A)\), and how would that compare with extrapolation based on Lipschitz assumptions (Ben‑Michael et al., 2021)? (R3)
  - The title could better stress the control function approach, since that arguably contributes more to extrapolation than “identifying representation” (R2).
  - “We allow for potentially unobserved confounders between \(Y\) and \(Z\)” – how can there be confounding when the action is exogenous? (R3)
