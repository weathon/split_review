## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper argues for fair comparison in online continual learning by standardizing computational (FLOPs) and memory (bytes) budgets, replacing iteration-based or replay-size-only metrics. It proposes aL-SAR, combining adaptive layer freezing (using batch Fisher Information to freeze less informative layers) and similarity-aware retrieval (prioritizing under-used and gradient-similar samples from episodic memory). Experiments on CIFAR-10/100, CLEAR-10/100, and ImageNet-1K show aL-SAR outperforms ER, DER++, MIR, MEMO, REMIND, EWC, OCS, LiDER, X-DER, CCL-DC, CAMA by 5‑10% average AAUC and last accuracy under tight budgets. An extension to LLaVA-1.5-7B is also reported. One reviewer is notably more critical (score 6 vs. 8,8,8), questioning the method’s ability to handle distribution shifts and its neglect of catastrophic forgetting.

### Strengths
- Standardizing computational (FLOPs) and memory (bytes) budgets is important and needed for fair comparison among online CL algorithms.
- Adaptive layer freezing based on batch Fisher Information is innovative; the batch-wise, dynamic freezing is well-motivated for online settings.
- The similarity-aware retrieval strategy (frequency-based and gradient-similarity) is computationally efficient and novel.
- Comprehensive empirical validation across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10/100, CLEAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K) with thorough ablation studies and comparisons to many state-of-the-art methods (ER, DER++, MIR, MEMO, REMIND, EWC, OCS, LiDER, X-DER, CCL-DC, CAMA).
- Successful application to a large model (LLaVA-1.5-7B) demonstrates scalability.
- The paper does an excellent job explaining the motivation and algorithm; all components are adequately justified.
- Shows significant performance improvements while reducing computational costs under constrained resource settings.

### Weaknesses
- **FLOPs metric limitation**: FLOPs ignore real-world runtime, memory access patterns, parallelization, and hardware/software optimizations. Two architectures with similar FLOPs can have different inference times on the same hardware. (R1)
- **Overhead of layer-freezing decisions**: Calculating the full Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) is computationally intensive (second-order derivatives), especially for large networks. The paper must explain why this does not introduce additional cost. (R1)  
- **Generalizability of Fisher Information**: Fisher Information is computed on the current mini-batch; results may not generalize across diverse batches in online CL (a layer may appear uninformative for one batch but essential for another due to shifting distributions). This needs justification. (R1)
- **Retrieval strategy may not ensure sufficient learning**: Prioritizing less frequently used samples could overemphasize rare or uninformative examples, potentially introducing noise and harming generalization. The paper needs to justify how this maintains acquired knowledge. (R1)
- **Minority reviewer (R3) notes that freezing shallow layers blocks their representations, causing them to become outdated under frequent distribution shifts. This is a fundamental concern not addressed by the method.**
- **Minority reviewer (R3) points out that the adaptive freezing treats layers independently, ignoring complex inter-layer dependencies that neural networks typically exhibit.**
- **Minority reviewer (R3) states the method does not directly address catastrophic forgetting, focusing only on resource efficiency. The trade-off between resource efficiency and forgetting prevention is unclear.**
- **Computational overhead of class-wise gradient similarity**: Maintaining and computing gradient similarities for each class can become prohibitive for problems with a large number of classes or frequent distribution shifts. (R3) Also noted by R4 as overhead in large-scale applications.
- **Missing comparison with iCaRL** (model-based approach). (R4)
- **Missing explanation of memory selection mechanism**: How are samples selected for memory and how is the memory updated? (R2)  
- **No difference between in-memory and new samples besides similarity-aware retrieval**: Are there any other differences in training? (R2)  
- **Lack of background in introduction**: The introduction jumps to solutions without providing proper background on online CL and current challenges; readers may not appreciate the contributions without context. (R2)
- **Suggested metric**: Reporting forgetting as an additional common CL metric would provide further insight. (R2)
- **Writing issues**: (R1) Line 071: 'ImageNet)' should be 'ImageNet'. Line 087: 'ER' has no citation or abbreviation explanation. Fig. 1: y-axis is not explained.
- **Minority reviewer (R3) questions how cosine similarity of gradients between classes is calculated in Appendix A.17, specifically whether using 0.05% of model parameters means randomly sampling parameters among unfrozen layers.**  
- **Missing experiments**: (R4) Compare aL-SAR under an epoch-based setup with larger memory constraints. (R4) Apply aL-SAR to more diverse datasets (e.g., text, multimodal) to demonstrate generalizability.
- **Minority reviewer (R3) asks whether the adaptive layer freezing could be extended to handle cross-layer dependencies for more optimal freezing decisions.**

**Note**: Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more critical than the other three (scores 8,8,8); their specific concerns about outdated representations, independent layer treatment, and lack of forgetting focus are flagged above.
