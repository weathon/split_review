## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies reinforcement learning (RL) under state-adversarial attacks and proposes a test-time adaptation framework. It defines a regret minimization problem over a policy class and argues for the necessity of refining the policy class prior to test time. During training, a set of “non-dominated policies” is discovered via a novel algorithm, aiming to form a minimal finite set that is near-optimal for any attack strength. At test time, policies are adaptively selected from this set using an adversarial bandit subroutine. Theoretical results include hardness results, optimality gaps, regret bounds, and a worst-case size bound for the refined policy class. Experiments on four MuJoCo environments (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant) compare against several baselines across no-attack, weak-attack, and worst-case attack scenarios, showing improved performance.

### Strengths

- The problem formulation—improving RL performance under non-worst-case attacks while maintaining worst-case robustness—is important and interesting.
- The definition of “non-dominated policy” and the algorithm to discover the refined set \(\tilde{\Pi}\) are novel and non-trivial.
- Theoretical results are provided, including bounds on optimality gaps and regret.
- Empirical results are strong: on four MuJoCo environments, the proposed method shows strict improvements over baselines (e.g., PA-AD, SA-PPO, RADIAL, WOC) in no-attack, non-worst-case, and worst-case scenarios. Several attack strategies are considered.
- The security model is carefully considered, including how to attack the test-time adaptation framework.
- The approach is considered novel and natural; some reviewers found the paper a nice read.

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and writing clarity are weak.** Multiple undefined notations and typos appear: \(H\) is not defined in Section 3; “discounted factor” is a typo; in the definition of \(J(\pi,\nu)\) the sampling distribution of \(z\) is missing; \(\pi^tt\) appears to be a typo for \(\{\pi_t: t\in[T]\}\); the update rule for \(\omega\) is not given in the main text; “finite and compact” is redundant because every finite set is compact; \(k\) in Algorithm 2 line 6 is not defined. These issues make it difficult to follow the setting and to evaluate the theoretical contribution accurately. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Regret definition (Definition 4.2) raises concerns.** The regret is defined in terms of value functions rather than rewards, and its relationship to standard regret is not discussed. It depends on an arbitrary attacker policy \(\nu^t\) without confinement, leaving its well‑definedness unclear. More importantly, the regret compares against the best *fixed* policy in hindsight, which can be too weak when the policy set is restricted to a finite \(\tilde{\Pi}\); the optimal adaptive policy may no longer be representable, creating a potentially significant gap. The authors should clarify why this notion of regret is not overly weak for the problem. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Experimental results appear too good without explanation.** The proposed method outperforms methods explicitly designed for worst‑case attacks (e.g., on Walker2d against PA‑AD) even in worst‑case scenarios, which seems to contradict “no free lunch” intuitions. The authors should provide further analysis (e.g., ablation studies) to explain this behavior and to discuss whether any tradeoffs exist; otherwise the results raise doubts about hidden limitations. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Scalability and practical bounds are unclear.** Theorem 4.11 indicates the worst‑case size of \(\tilde{\Pi}\) can be exponential in the time horizon \(H\), raising questions about applicability to infinite‑horizon MDPs. Moreover, Theorem 4.10 lacks a concrete upper bound on \(K\) (the size of the refined policy class); a discussion of how \(K\) scales with the state/action space size and environment complexity is needed to assess practical implications. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **The adaptive‑attack part is weakly formulated.** It is not clearly defined what adaptivity means for the adversary, and what would happen if the adversary has more knowledge about the defender; this is noted as a future direction. (Reviewer 3)
- **Other missing details.** A discount factor \(\gamma\) is introduced in Section 3 but never used; the paper shifts to a finite‑horizon \(H\) MDP without discussing extension to infinite horizon. Proposition 4.3 does not explain how the choice of \(\alpha\) relates to the rest of the problem setup. (Reviewers 1, 2)

*Reviewer disagreement:* Two reviewers (ratings 8) emphasized the novelty and strong empirical results, while two other reviewers (ratings 6) found the presentation deficits, regret definition, and unexplained experimental superiority to be significant limitations that hinder full assessment of the contribution.
