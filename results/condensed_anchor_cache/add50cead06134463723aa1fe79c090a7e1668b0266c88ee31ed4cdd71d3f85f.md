## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers evaluate a paper that introduces *manifold mismatch* in Mixup—the phenomenon where mixed samples fall outside the original class manifolds, harming calibration. The paper proposes SK‑Mixup, which dynamically adjusts the distribution of interpolation coefficients based on the distance between samples to reduce this mismatch. All reviewers acknowledge the research problem as well‑motivated and the method as clear and efficient. There is strong agreement that empirical results on classification and regression tasks, including computational cost, demonstrate effectiveness. However, three of four reviewers (ratings 6,6,6) raise substantial concerns about theoretical justification, novelty relative to prior work (k‑mixup, AdaMixup), missing baselines, and presentation quality. The fourth reviewer (rating 8) considers the paper free of rejection‑worthy weaknesses and especially highlights the strong motivation, proof, and comprehensive experiments. Key disagreements center on whether the core claim (“distance impacts manifold mismatch”) is sufficiently novel, whether the connection to calibration is adequately justified, and whether the empirical comparison is complete.

### Strengths
- **Well‑motivated problem** – Manifold mismatch is a novel, unified terminology that directly targets calibration‑driven Mixup; theoretical definition and proof are provided (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Innovative yet efficient method** – SK‑Mixup reduces manifold mismatch by tailoring interpolation to sample distance while maintaining diversity; computational overhead is low (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Comprehensive empirical validation** – Extensive experiments on classification and regression tasks (including CIFAR, ImageNet, tabular data) with a focus on both performance and computational cost show the method’s effectiveness (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Clear and well‑organized exposition** – The method and its derivation are presented in a clear, easy‑to‑follow manner (Reviewers 1,2,4).  
  *Note: One reviewer (R3) strongly disagrees on presentation quality (soundness 2, presentation 1); see Weaknesses.*
- **Correlation between sample distance and manifold mismatch** is derived and empirically validated (Reviewer 3).
- **Algorithm balances diversity and uncertainty** in generating virtual examples (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer 1 explicitly states** that the paper has no weakness justifying rejection and gives a contribution score of 3 (the highest among reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical justification** – Theorem 1 shows existence of manifold mismatch but does not validate the central assumption that larger distances increase mismatch likelihood; a stronger theoretical link is needed (Reviewers 2,3).  
  The connection between manifold mismatch and calibration is not clearly established (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty concerns** – The idea of forcing mixtures between closer points resembles k‑mixup, and dynamic adjustment of mixing coefficients appears in AdaMixup; these prior works are not sufficiently discussed or compared (Reviewer 3).  
  Missing baseline comparisons:  
  * A simple baseline dividing sample pairs by distance and using two Beta distributions (Reviewer 4).  
  * Non‑linear interpolation methods (e.g., manifold mixup, input mixup variants) – empirical results for image datasets (Tables 2‑4) and computational cost comparison (Table 6) are missing (Reviewer 4).  
  * Comparisons on complex datasets like ImageNet, ImageNet‑C, ImageNet‑R, ImageNet‑P between the proposed method and mixup‑related works [1,2,3] (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental gaps and inconsistencies**  
  * SK RegMixup is not applied to ViT in Table 4; potential challenges or limitations are not discussed (Reviewer 1).  
  * Accuracy on ImageNet‑A (Table 4) is below 3%, which is worse than reported in prior work (e.g., CVPR 2021); setup discrepancies need clarification (Reviewer 4).  
  * ECE and AECE are exactly identical in OOD settings – explanation or verification is required (Reviewer 1).  
  * Computational cost for SK RegMixup is omitted from Table 6, unlike other methods (Reviewer 1).  
  * Baseline selection in Table 6 appears arbitrary; rationale needed (Reviewer 4).  
  * How the method performs under label noise / data corruption is not addressed (Reviewer 2).  
  * Effect of parameter choices (τ_std, τ_max) on calibration and generalization (e.g., possible U‑curve behavior) is not explored (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation quality** – Poor presentation reported by one reviewer (R3):  
  * Notation is inconsistent (e.g., input/output dimensions of encoder, label space definition).  
  * Terms like “performance” and “calibration of confidence” are used without specification.  
  * Manifold definition (§2.2) and class‑disjointness assumption are unclear.  
  * Theorem 3.1 uses interval notation `]λ₁,λ₂[` instead of `[λ₁,λ₂]`; the proof’s handling of λ=0 requires correction (Reviewer 1 also notes this issue).  
  Minor issues (Reviewer 1): Figure 4 caption erroneously mentions “Circles” toy dataset; Table 4 bold formatting for ECE/AECE on ImageNet‑R is inconsistent; typo “cccross” in line 879.  
  Hyperlinks appear as strange text on pages 7‑8 (Reviewer 3).
- **Unaddressed reviewer questions overlapping with criticisms**  
  * Why is there a `-1` inside the exponential in Eq. (3) (Reviewer 2)?  
  * How to choose τ_std and τ_max (Reviewer 3)?  
  * How does SK Mixup actually improve calibration – is manifold mismatch the true cause (Reviewer 3)?  
  * Can the proposed method be combined with non‑linear interpolation (Reviewer 4)?
- **Reviewer disagreement**: While Reviewer 1 finds no weakness justifying rejection, three other reviewers have multiple substantial concerns (ratings 6,6,6). The paper’s contribution was rated 3 by R1 and R2, but only 2 by R3 and R4.
