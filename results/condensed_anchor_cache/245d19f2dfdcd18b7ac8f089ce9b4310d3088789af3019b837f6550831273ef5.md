## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the optimal strategy for integrating image data into the pre-training of vision-language models (VLMs). Rather than the common two-stage pipeline (fully pre-training a text-only LLM, then adapting it with image-text data), the authors explore a one-stage approach where image tokens are introduced during the LLM’s pre-training. They run over 300 experiments on 79M and 1B parameter models (with some ablations on 1.4B) using 28B tokens (following the 20× parameters scaling rule) to study: the timing of introducing visual tokens (e.g., 80% through pre-training), the ratio of image to text tokens (e.g., 10–20% visual), the effect of mixing instruction-tuning data during the image-text stage, the learning rate schedule (e.g., single cosine cooldown vs. separate warmup), and freezing vs. updating the vision encoder. Models are fine-tuned on image instruction data (e.g., LLaVA-style) and evaluated on VQA and text-only tasks. Key findings are: (1) adding image-text data during the cooldown phase of LLM training (e.g., at the 80% checkpoint) outperforms starting from a fully pre-trained model (a ~2% average improvement on VQA benchmarks for a 1B model); (2) a 90–10 image-to-text token ratio works best; (3) mixing instruction-tuning data during the image-text pretraining stage hurts performance; and (4) the text-only baseline shows that image instruction fine-tuning degrades text-only task accuracy.

### Strengths
- The paper is experiment-heavy and systematic: over 300 runs, spanning two model scales (79M and 1B), and ablating many factors (timing of visual introduction, data ratios, learning rate schedules, instruction tuning mixing, vision encoder freezing, etc.). The experiments are well thought out and the figures (e.g., Fig. 4) highlight potential difficulties such as different learning rate schedules.
- The research question is interesting and valuable: most VLM pretraining details are hidden in proprietary setups or not ablated due to cost; this work provides transparent empirical guidelines.
- Several insights are impactful and likely to advance VLM pretraining research: (a) continued training at 80% of text-only pre-training is preferable to starting from the 100% checkpoint, using a 90–10 image-text ratio; (b) training from scratch (Section 3.3) highlights the importance of a strong text-only model before introducing images (contrasting with older “from-scratch” vision-language models like OSCAR, UNITER); (c) image instruction fine-tuning degrades text-only task performance (Section 3.5), suggesting that multimodal LLMs finetuned with instruction data are suboptimal when no image is present.
- The paper is very well presented visually and well-written; the text is fluent, understandable, and the key takeaways are clearly communicated. Potential weaknesses (e.g., points with different LR schedules in Fig. 4) are explicitly noted.
- The finding that image-text data should be injected earlier (e.g., during the cooldown) is directly applicable to many existing VLM training pipelines.
- The tangible improvement numbers are provided: for a 1B model, introducing visual tokens 80% of the way through pre-training yields a 2% average improvement over introducing them to a fully pre-trained model (averaged over 6 diverse VQA tasks).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison to existing VLMs**: The paper introduces “vision stable-score” as its main metric but evaluates only its own trained models. Without reporting benchmark numbers for models in Table 1 (e.g., LLaVA-Next, LLaVA-One-Vision, Qwen2-VL, etc.) or other SOTA VLMs, it is difficult to assess how close this work’s models are to the state of the art. If the models are too far from SOTA, the findings might not transfer. At minimum, a comparison to the DCLM-1B text-only model (the authors’ base LLM) before any image training is needed to contextualize the text-only performance drops. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Evaluation limited to VQA accuracy on short answer benchmarks**: Only VQA datasets (e.g., GQA, OK-VQA, maybe others) are used, which reward exactly matching short answers and are known to suffer from language bias (models answering without looking at the image). The paper lacks evaluation on a broader set of vision-language tasks: captioning (e.g., COCO Captions, NoCaps), visual entailment (e.g., SNLI-VE), VQA with textual explanations (VQA-X), OCR-based benchmarks (e.g., OCR-Bench), reasoning benchmarks (e.g., A-OK-VQA, MMMU), and text-only benchmarks beyond ARC Easy. Good VQA performance does not necessarily reflect general VLM ability, and insights drawn solely from VQA may not generalize. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Potential hyperparameter confound in the main finding (80% vs. 100%)**: In Section 3.1, the peak learning rate for the 100% checkpoint model is set to 3e‑3, while the best peak LR for the 80% model (green cosine curve in Figure 3) appears to be around 1e‑4. The authors do not ablate this choice; the main result that 80% introduction is better could be partly due to suboptimal LR for the 100% model rather than the timing itself. (Reviewer 2, high priority)
- **Text stable score is very low and not properly compared**: The text stable score (e.g., ~15% in Fig. 4) is near zero after subtracting random baseline. The authors should compare these numbers to the DCLM-1B model without any image training (reported to achieve ~75% on ARC Easy). The abstract claims “retains performance within 2% of text-only baseline” but it is unclear which baseline is used (the DCLM-1B model, or a text-only model trained on 28B tokens?). Without this clarification, the claim is ambiguous. (Reviewer 2)
- **Model scale limitation**: Experiments are mainly on a 1B LLM (with one 1.4B experiment); results may not generalize to 7B, 13B, or larger models. The paper lacks discussion of how the optimal timing or data ratios might change with model capacity and amount of pre-training data. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Practical limitations**: The proposed guidelines require access to intermediate checkpoints from the original LLM training, including optimizer states and learning rate schedules. Many strong LLMs do not publicly release 80% checkpoints, making the approach difficult to apply post-hoc. The paper does not discuss alternatives or approximations for practitioners who only have a fully trained model. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing qualitative analysis**: The paper does not analyze what kinds of questions the 2% drop or improvement corresponds to, leaving it unclear whether the gains are due to particular categories (e.g., OCR, counting, reasoning). (Reviewer 1)
- **Decision to freeze vision encoder based on 79M model only**: The ablation for freezing vs. unfreezing the vision encoder is run only on a 79M model; there is no argument or experiment showing this holds for larger models. (Reviewer 2)
- **Concern about VQA bias without images**: In Figure 5, when the image ratio is 0% (no image data added, only text training), the VQA performance is ~75% even though the model has never seen images. This suggests the model may be answering VQA questions from language priors or biases. The paper should report results with images masked or on datasets like A-OK-VQA that require visual reasoning. (Reviewer 4)
- **Instruction tuning data masking issue**: In Appendix L739, the authors mask out system prompts and human questions during image instruction tuning, keeping only model responses. This means no question is present, which does not constitute instruction finetuning; the rationale is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
- **Omission of interleaved image-text data**: The paper acknowledges this limitation, but only evaluates image-text pairs; real-world VLM training often uses interleaved data, which may affect the findings. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reproducibility details**: The paper does not report approximate GPU hours for the image-text training stages (only text-only in Appendix A). Also, the choice of OpenLM over other architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen) is not justified. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Clarification needed on early incorporation of images**: When images are introduced earlier, does the model see more total image data, or simply earlier in the schedule? This should be clarified. (Reviewer 3)
- **Data repetition**: In Section 3.4, when x% of image instruction tuning data is added during image-caption training, it is unclear whether that same data is repeated during the later instruction finetuning stage. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor typo**: “LLaVa” should be “LLaVA”. (Reviewer 3)
