## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents PASSAT, a hybrid deep learning model combining the advection and Navier–Stokes equations with a spherical graph neural network (GNN) for weather forecasting. PASSAT solves the PDEs on the sphere to account for Earth’s topology and uses the GNN to model unresolved Earth–atmosphere interactions. On the ERA5 dataset at 5.625° resolution, PASSAT outperforms several deep learning baselines (FourCastNet, Pangu, etc.) and the operational IFS T42, but shows only marginal (~1%) improvement over a reimplemented GraphCast*.

### Strengths
- **Novel hybrid framework**: The combination of advection and Navier–Stokes equations with a spherical GNN is a novel step beyond purely data-driven models (R1, R2, R3, R4). The model explicitly incorporates physical processes and spherical geometry, aiming for better interpretability and reduced error accumulation (R3, R4).
- **Spherical topology handling**: The model correctly identifies and remedies the planar projection errors common in other weather models by solving PDEs on the sphere and using a spherical GNN (R2, R3, R4). This is a satisfying treatment of Earth’s geometry (R4).
- **Experimental performance**: PASSAT outperforms several state-of-the-art deep learning baselines and IFS T42 on the ERA5 5.625° dataset, showing competitive results in medium-term forecasting (R1, R3). Reviewer 3 was significantly more positive (rating 5), emphasizing the model’s strong results and practical potential, while Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 (rating 3) note that the improvement over GraphCast* is marginal (~1%) and that the physics component contributes little in ablation studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Role of physics is unclear and ablation impact is minimal**: In Table 2, the version without Navier–Stokes achieves similar or even better RMSE, and the pure GNN variant (no physics) is only slightly worse yet still outperforms most baselines except GraphCast*. This suggests the main contribution is the GNN design, not the physics (R2, R4). The marginal gains from adding the Navier–Stokes and advection equations do not justify the increased complexity (R2, R4). Similarly, the hyperparameter λ = 1/60 in Algorithm 1 heavily dampens the NS update, which may explain the weak effect (R4).
- **Insufficient architectural detail**: The paper lacks a detailed description of the spherical GNN, the interaction and velocity branches, tensor dimensions, and the specific message-passing/aggregation operations (R1, R3). The claim to “no longer trust the velocity field v” after the initial time is unclear and needs explanation (R3). The relationship of the PASSAT GNN to the GraphCast multi‑scale mesh graph is not discussed (R4).
- **No computational efficiency benchmarks**: Training time, inference time, memory usage, and number of parameters are not reported. A breakdown of time spent in GNN computations versus PDE solving is missing, hindering assessment of practical viability (R1, R3).
- **Missing comparisons and unfair reimplementation**: 
  - The paper claims to compare with operational NWP models but only includes IFS T42, which may not represent the state-of-the-art operational IFS (R1). 
  - The GraphCast* reimplementation is questionable: the number of nodes (4096) appears inconsistent with a multi‑scale icosahedral mesh, raising concerns about fair comparison (R4). 
  - Baseline compression (Appendix E) lacks detailed justification, especially for GraphCast and Pangu with large original parameter counts (R3).
- **Dataset and variable selection limitations**: 
  - Only 13 years of ERA5 at 5.625° (coarse resolution) are used, and only a few variables (T2m, 10u, 10v, Z500, T850) are predicted (R2, R3). The variables are not all at the same altitude, which impedes accurate application of the Navier–Stokes equations (R2). 
  - Temperature is used only as an input and not modeled through thermodynamic equations, unlike more comprehensive physics‑informed models such as NeuralGCM (R2). 
  - Reviewer 2 recommends using 1.40625° resolution and a longer time series to capture extreme events; Reviewer 3 asks about the impact of predicting fewer variables than FourCastNet (20 variables).
- **Stability claim unsupported**: The paper claims “remarkably improved stability” but provides no experiments on rollouts beyond 5 days. Most modern deep learning models are stable to 10+ days, so the claim is not substantiated (R4). The motivation that the physics reduces error accumulation is already addressed by rollout training (R4).
- **Missing related work**: The paper does not discuss important related models that handle spherical geometry (Bonev et al., 2023; Esteves et al., 2023) or graph‑based models (Keisler, 2022; Oskarsson et al., 2024; Lang et al., 2024) (R4). The terminology “earth–atmosphere interaction” should be clarified—it appears to actually refer to sub‑grid parameterizations (R4).
- **Unjustified physical assumptions**: 
  - No theoretical justification is given for selecting the Navier–Stokes equations over other fluid dynamics models, and the incompressibility assumption is not validated for the Earth’s atmosphere (R1, R4). 
  - The advection equation is applied independently to each variable (including u and v wind components), which may not be physically realistic given their coupling (R4). 
  - Boundary conditions (land‑sea, orography, different pressure levels) are not addressed (R1, R2).
- **Presentation issues**: Sections 1.1 and 1.2 read more like related work than introduction (R4). The claim that pure deep learning models are “unreliable” is inaccurate for modern models like Pangu and GraphCast (R4). The statement that GraphCast, Pangu, and NeuralGCM have not provided open code is incorrect (R4).
