## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PORRL (Partially-Observed Reward States in RLHF), a framework that models RLHF with internal states and intermediate feedback, subsuming traditional RL, RLHF, and reward machines. For cardinal feedback, it proposes model-based methods POR-UCRL and POR-UCBVI with regret and sample complexity guarantees improving over naive history-summarization. For model-free settings, it analyzes GOLF with a new history-aware Bellman-eluder (HABE) dimension that can be exponentially smaller. For dueling feedback, it shows that naive reduction fails and provides the first explicit reduction converting cardinal regret guarantees to dueling regret.

### Strengths
- The PORRL framework is novel and highly general, subsuming a wide range of RL problems including traditional RL, RLHF, and reward machines by incorporating partially-observed internal states and intermediate feedback.  
- The paper provides technically solid theoretical contributions, analyzing both model-based (POR-UCRL, POR-UCBVI) and model-free (GOLF) algorithms with regret guarantees under cardinal and dueling feedback.  
- The proposed model-based algorithms achieve improved regret compared with existing results, and their guarantees subsume and extend past work.  
- For dueling feedback, the paper delivers the first explicit reduction that converts guarantees for cardinal regret to dueling regret, after demonstrating that a naive blackbox reduction fails.  
- A new complexity measure, the history-aware Bellman-eluder (HABE) dimension, is introduced. It can be exponentially smaller than the standard Bellman-eluder dimension (e.g., in the combination lock example), and yields guarantees for GOLF that can be exponentially sharper in settings with recursive internal states and dense intermediate feedback.  
- Some reviewers considered the framework highly novel and the theoretical contributions significant for the RLHF theory landscape.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experimental validation:** No experiments are provided to validate the effectiveness, implementability, or practical behavior of the proposed algorithms and theoretical results.  
- **No concrete algorithm within PORRL framework:** The practical takeaways in the conclusion are mainly conjectures; the paper does not present a concrete, implemented algorithm for the PORRL setting.  
- **Computational inefficiency:** The algorithms rely on standard techniques like optimism in the face of uncertainty and require exploring the entire state-action space even with function approximation, leading to potentially impractical sample complexities. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of computational cost, making scalability for real-world RLHF unclear.  
- **Deterministic internal state assumption:** The internal state generation function \(g\) is assumed deterministic, which overlooks inherent stochasticity in human feedback (e.g., mood, context). This limits the framework's applicability to more nuanced human feedback scenarios.  
- **Writing density and clarity:** The paper is too dense and hard to follow.  
- **Insufficient motivation:** The motivation for combining POMDPs and RLHF is not sufficiently elaborated; the paper should provide 1–2 concrete application scenarios to illustrate the relevance.  
- **Questionable technical novelty:** One reviewer questioned the technical novelty, viewing the work as a straightforward combination of existing POMDP works and preference feedback, rather than a fundamentally new theoretical contribution. (This contrasts with the positive novelty appraisal in Strengths.)  
- **Missing query complexity comparison:** The framework requires intermediate feedback at each time step, which demands substantially more human queries than single preferential feedback at the end of an episode. The paper does not compare its query complexity to prior work (e.g., [1,2]) to assess the additional human effort.  
- **Insufficient explanation of technical challenges:** The paper mentions three main technical challenges for model-based algorithms on Page 7 but does not clearly explain how the new design addresses them.  
- **Missing detail on Bellman completeness:** The adaptation of GOLF to this setting likely requires Bellman completeness (BC), but this is not explicitly stated in the main text.  
- **Unjustified Bernoulli assumption for cardinal feedback:** The paper assumes cardinal feedback follows a Bernoulli distribution without discussing whether this is standard in prior cardinal feedback works (e.g., KTO uses a more complex model). The restrictiveness of this assumption should be justified.
