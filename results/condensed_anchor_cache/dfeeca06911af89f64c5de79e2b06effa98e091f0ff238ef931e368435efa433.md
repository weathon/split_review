## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Normalized Space Alignment (NSA), a pseudometric for comparing representation spaces by measuring pairwise Euclidean distances between point clouds. The authors prove it is a pseudometric and differentiable, enabling use as a loss function. They apply NSA to analyze GNN representations, adversarial robustness, and as a regularizer in autoencoders. Reviewers note that NSA combines efficiency of CKA with differentiability of RTD, but raise concerns about missing invariance properties, limited experimental validation, unclear structure preservation, and superficial analyses. One reviewer is positive about the soundness and comprehensiveness, while three others find major weaknesses.

### Strengths

- NSA is a novel combination of Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) and Representation Tree Distance (RTD), offering computational efficiency and differentiability (Reviewer 1, 4).  
- The paper proves NSA satisfies pseudometric properties and provides a differentiation scheme for use as a loss function (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).  
- Reviewer 4 praises the soundness, building on Representational Similarity Matrix-Based Measures, and finds comprehensive experiments.  
- Reviewer 3 notes that NSA is well-described with accessible proofs, and the focus on fundamental measures is a crucial endeavor.  
- Reviewer 2 acknowledges the significance of the problem and novelty of the metric.  
- The paper attempts a broad range of applications (Reviewer 3).  

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of invariance to permutation**: NSA is not invariant to permutation of vectors in the point cloud, which is a critical issue since vectors are not assumed aligned. No sufficient motivation for the metric is given (Reviewer 2, high confidence).  
- **Missing fundamental analysis and superficiality**: The measure may be too coarse, essentially comparing averaged distance representations. The paper does not demonstrate that NSA captures "interesting" properties; experiments are superficial and speculative. Correlation with performance metrics alone is insufficient without deeper analysis (e.g., early stopping criteria) (Reviewer 3, absolutely certain).  
- **Structure preservation concerns**:  
  - NSA ignores graph edges, so its ability to preserve graph structure is questionable (Reviewer 1).  
  - NSA may measure small differences even when point structures differ entirely, as it ignores inter-point structure (Reviewer 4).  
- **GNN focus mismatch**: Despite the paper's GNN emphasis, there is no actual exploration of GNN-specific capabilities. The autoencoder experiment is on non-graph data, creating inconsistency (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 also finds the GNN connection confusing.  
- **Autoencoder experiment unclear**: If NSA compares reconstructed and original representations, this does not prove usefulness because the representations are already aligned. It is unclear what exactly is compared (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 suggests replacing it with toy examples (e.g., Swiss Roll) to build confidence.  
- **Lack of downstream task evaluation**: No evaluation on how NSA-shaped latent embeddings improve downstream performance (Reviewer 1).  
- **Limited datasets**: Only Amazon Computers is used for node classification; broader evaluation is needed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Adversarial analysis shortcomings**: NSA values vary significantly across architectures (e.g., GCN has highest misclassification but GCN-SVD has highest NSA), complicating interpretation (Reviewer 1).  
- **Readability issues**: Fonts in figures, especially Figure 2, are too small (Reviewer 1, 4).  
- **Unclear motivation and RTD explanation**: Motivation behind NSA is not sufficiently explained. The RTD description uses jargon (e.g., barcode, Vietoris–Rips) without adequate explanation (Reviewer 2, 3).  
- **Additional questions with critical implications**:  
  - Why focus on Euclidean distance? How robust is NSA to misleading distances (e.g., Swiss Roll)? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Is NSA sensitive to removal of low-variance PCA components? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Why only slightly outperforms or performs worse than prior methods in Table 1? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Can NSA be used to detect or predict poisoning levels? (Reviewer 3)  

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6, Reviewer 4) is substantially more positive, finding the technique sound, well-organized, and comprehensively validated. The other three reviewers (scores 3) are critical, with confidence levels ranging from moderate to absolute, and find major deficiencies in invariance, experimental validation, and clarity. The positive reviewer still raises weaknesses about point structure and performance comparisons.
