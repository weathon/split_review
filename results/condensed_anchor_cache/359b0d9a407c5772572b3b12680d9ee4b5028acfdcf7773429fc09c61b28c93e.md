## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Context-Aware Meta-Learning (CAML), a meta-learning algorithm that learns new visual concepts during inference without meta-training or fine-tuning. Inspired by in-context learning in LLMs, CAML recasts few-shot image classification as sequence modeling over labeled support examples and an unlabeled query, using a frozen CLIP feature extractor, a Transformer encoder, and a novel Equal Length and Maximally Equiangular Set (ELMES) label embedding. CAML is pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, Fungi, MSCOCO, and WikiArt, and evaluated on 11 few-shot benchmarks. Without meta-training, it exceeds or matches the state-of-the-art P>M>F on 8 of 11 benchmarks, and outperforms other universal meta-learning baselines on 15 of 22 evaluation settings.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated by LLM in-context learning:** The paper draws a clear analogy between visual meta-learning and in-context learning in LLMs, addressing the gap of learning new concepts during inference without fine-tuning. The analysis in Fig. 2 on how dynamic in-context examples impact representation learning is insightful. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Clean and straightforward framework:** The approach recasts meta-learning as sequence modeling in a simple, principled way, with theoretical justification for the ELMES label embedding (permutation invariance and label symmetry). (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Competitive empirical results:** CAML shows impressive and robust performance across 11 few-shot benchmarks, outperforming or matching P>M>F on 8 of 11 and surpassing other universal meta-learning methods on 15 of 22 settings. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Novel ELMES class encoding:** The ELMES encoding is an innovative contribution not seen in prior in-context learning work in NLP or VLMs, aligning with studies suggesting class embeddings may be replaceable. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 finds the theoretical analysis interesting)
- **Sufficient theoretical work:** The theoretical analysis of ELMES and label symmetry strengthens the paper. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work:** The core idea of formulating meta-learning as a sequence (or set) modeling problem and using an ICL objective has been explored before (e.g., [1] General-Purpose In-Context Learning by Meta-Learning Transformers, NeurIPS 2022; [2]). The adaptation to CLIP embeddings, while practical, does not represent a substantial theoretical advancement—the conceptual approach remains similar. (Reviewers 2,4; Reviewer 2 notes high similarity in problem formulation, only architectural differences)
- **Unclear dichotomy between “meta-training” and “universal meta-learning”:** The paper claims CAML avoids meta-training, yet pre-training on tasks from ImageNet-1k etc. can be viewed as meta-training. This weakens comparisons, which are mainly against other universal meta-learning methods (ProtoNet, MetaOpt, MetaQDA). (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of ablation and hyperparameter analysis:** No ablation experiments for key modules (Transformer architecture, ELMES choice, number of layers) and no quantitative hyperparameter sensitivity analysis. This makes it hard to assess robustness and support design choices. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient experimental details:** Missing details on training: transformer implementation and parameter scale, pre-training dataset size, ProtoNet baseline implementation (trained on same data with ProtoNet loss?), and unknown class embedding initialization for ELMES. (Reviewers 2,3) Also, since miniImageNet is a subset of ImageNet-1k, potential task leakage from pre-training needs clarification. (Reviewer 2)
- **Weak out-of-domain performance not adequately addressed:** CAML’s performance on unseen categories is weak, likely due to the uniform “Unknown [class] Embedding” encoding. The paper does not adequately explain how this bottleneck affects generalization or propose solutions (e.g., leveraging semantic relationships between seen and unseen classes). (Reviewer 1)
- **Claims of label symmetry and permutation invariance lack empirical support:** The paper asserts these properties for ELMES but provides no experiments varying demonstration order or permuting class labels to validate them. (Reviewer 4)
- **Justification for separate label encoding is missing:** The paper uses CLIP to encode images and labels separately; it does not discuss why joint embedding (e.g., using MSCOCO) was not attempted, raising questions about optimality. (Reviewer 1)
- **Mathematical derivations in Section 4 are hard to follow:** The presentation is unclear, making verification difficult. (Reviewer 4)
- **Known number of classes as a limitation:** The need to know the number of classes in advance is inherent to few-shot learning, but the paper could have discussed fine-tuning more modules to mitigate this. (Reviewer 3)
