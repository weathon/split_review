## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes D3G for out-of-domain generalization using domain relations from domain metadata to learn domain-specific models and reweight their predictions at test time. Reviewers describe the idea as straightforward and well-written, with theoretical analysis showing superiority over equal weighting and strong empirical results on toy and real-world datasets (TPT-48, FMoW, ChEMBL-STRING), including many baselines and ablations.

### Strengths
- Clear writing and organization; idea straightforward and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3, R5, R6)
- Theoretical analysis showing that the relation-aware consistency loss yields better out-of-domain generalization than equal domain weighting. (R1, R2, R4)
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation with an illustrative toy task, many tables/figures, a large number of baselines, and thorough ablations that answer fundamental questions about the method. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- Approach is distinct from most existing methods by learning domain-specific models instead of a single domain-invariant model. (R3)
- Applicable to real-world applications; superior performance makes a significant practical difference. (R3)
- Originality: ensembling domain-specific classifiers using weights learned from domain metadata is novel, even though learning domain-specific heads has been proposed before. (R5) – note: R1 and R4 consider novelty limited (see Weaknesses).
- Domain relations are learned rather than fixed. (R6)
- Strong motivation and relevance. (R2)
- Experiment setups are detailed. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty limited / similarity to ensemble methods**: R1 and R4 consider novelty limited, noting similarities to ensemble methods and mixture-of-experts (MoE). R1 asks for explicit distinction from boosting/bagging and clarification that a shared feature extractor does not differentiate. R4 points out that MoE [1] constructed domain-specific models and used ensemble for out-of-distribution robustness. R3 and R5, however, find the approach distinct and original (see Strengths).
- **Missing comparisons with ensemble baselines**: R1 requests baseline models incorporating ensemble methods (boosting, bagging) and clarification of how the method differs from simply training multiple models on different domains and combining via weighted average. R3 requests comparison with ensemble models of existing approaches. R6 specifically asks for a baseline where each head is trained on all training domains by supervised loss and predictions are averaged.
- **Theoretical assumptions and limitations**: R1 states assumptions (e.g., domain relations accurately capture similarity, determined solely by distance between domain representations) may not hold in real-world datasets; theorems only prove superiority in minimax sense, not global superiority; they show uneven weights > equal weights but not optimality. R2 notes strong assumptions unlikely true; suggests showing effect of noisy domain relations. R5 points out that the generality of assumptions is not discussed. R1 recommends expanding analysis to sensitivity and empirical validation.
- **Domain metadata definition and usage**: R4 notes no clear definition of meta-data, how to obtain it, or why it works. R5 states designing similarity definition for different tasks requires specific expertise; learned relations can be less helpful on some tasks (Table 10). R6 asks for clarification of the relationship between m_i and a_ij^g, and how metadata is processed to obtain relations.
- **Dependence on user-defined similarity function**: R2 points out the method requires a user-defined domain similarity function from metadata, which may not always be available or credibly defined, introducing bias and limiting applicability; sensitivity to this function is not addressed. R5 similarly questions robustness and generality.
- **Potential triviality of learned relations**: R6 raises that if both supervised loss and consistency loss reach their minimum, learning a_ij would be useless; it is unclear what prevents the model from converging to equal a_ij, negating the benefit of learning relations. R6 also asks whether the consistency loss implies each head is trained on all domains with a weighted combination, making the method similar to training with a weighted loss across domains.
- **Evaluation metrics**: R1 notes metrics (e.g., MSE) differ from previous works on TPT-48, FMoW, ChEMBL-STRING, and it is unclear if they are suitable for classification tasks. The paper should justify metrics and compare with commonly used ones (accuracy, F1-score) to ensure comparability.
- **Training details of neural network g and consistency loss**: R6 asks whether the two-layer neural network g is trained, how weight vectors w_r are learned, and whether the consistency loss is backpropagated through g. The paper needs to clarify optimization.
- **Limited training data in certain domains**: R4 asks how the consistency loss addresses the challenge of limited training data in some domains.
- **Organization and presentation**: R4 suggests the method section needs adjustment, recomputing domain relations a in advance to make it clearer. R3 and R6 note typos/language issues (“Eqn. equation x…”; “We using weighted…”). R4 recommends adding venue and year of comparison methods to tables.
- **Applicability without metadata**: R2, R3, R5 raise questions about whether the method works when domain metadata is unavailable, and whether domain relations can be learned from data itself. R2 asks if a domain discovery method (e.g., clustering) could be used to learn domains and relations from unlabeled data, and if the method can be adapted to learn end-to-end.
- **Negative domain relations**: R2 asks if the proposed method can utilize negative relationships between domains.
