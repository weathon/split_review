## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper examines how transformer-based language models retrieve information from long contexts. Using the Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) task across model families LLaMA, Yi, Qwen, and Mistral (scales 6B to 8x7B, base/chat, dense/MoE), the authors identify “retrieval heads”—a small subset of attention heads responsible for retrieval. They propose a novel retrieval score that measures copy-paste behavior. Five properties are documented: (1) universal—present in all explored models; (2) sparse—less than 5% of heads; (3) intrinsic—already exist in short-context pretrained models and persist after continual pretraining; (4) dynamically activated—some heads always attend to required info, others are context-dependent; (5) causal—pruning retrieval heads degrades retrieval and causes hallucination, while pruning random non-retrieval heads does not. Retrieval heads also influence paraphrasing, QA, and chain-of-thought reasoning.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, with a clear overarching research question and a well-organized structure. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Extensive experiments cover LLaMA, Yi, Qwen, and Mistral families at scales from 6B to 8x7B, including base and chat variants, dense and MoE architectures. (R1)
- A novel retrieval score is introduced to identify and analyze copy-paste behavior, and it is used to guide pruning/masking experiments. (R1)
- Results robustly support claims; methodology is clear with few exceptions. (R2)
- In-depth analysis with clear graphics, easy-to-follow explanations, and numerous examples and case studies. (R3)
- Impactful: proposes prospective research directions involving retrieval heads. (R4)
- Demonstrates that retrieval heads are activated not only in copy-paste tasks but also in paraphrasing, QA, and chain-of-thought reasoning. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- Lack of dataset and experimental details: how NIAH pairs are created (manually or by prompting? potential bias?), diversity of topics and token variety, validation methods, nature of haystack documents (single source or diverse?), length and complexity. Also missing details for datasets used in Sec 2–4, additional evaluation tests in Sec 4.1–4.3, prompts used, and links to existing assets. (R1, R2)
- Notation issue (L156): $\mathcal{R}$ should be $\mathbb{R}$ and $a$ should be explicitly defined as unnormalized attention scores. (R1)
- Figure 3 caption states 5% of heads are retrieval heads, but the figure shows less than 1% of heads activated more than 50% of the time; this discrepancy needs resolution. (R1) Caption is also hard to interpret without detailed reference—needs revision. (R2)
- L194: unclear how to handle cases where the model generates a word $w$ that is not the next token in the needle $k$—is the example skipped, or is it interpreted as no head performing copy-paste even if attention was paid to the correct token? (R1)
- L203: define “stabilize” and “converge” or provide plots. (R1)
- Figure 7: include dataset name in caption. (R1)
- Acronym “KV” not defined (should be Key-Value). (R2)
- Figure 5 is hard to interpret; consider overlaying heatmaps for comparison or omitting if redundant with Figure 6. (R2)
- Section 4 would benefit from qualitative examples (perhaps in an appendix) to illustrate downstream task claims. (R2)
- The work is limited to the NIAH task; it is unknown whether findings transfer to paraphrasing or other tasks requiring more complex use of context (not just copy-paste). Provide evidence on paraphrasing tasks. (R4)
- Missing investigation on whether retrieval heads are consistent across different architectures (e.g., decoder-only vs. encoder-decoder) and whether properties are universally applicable. (R3)
- Dynamic activation mechanism is unclear: how does the model determine when retrieval heads become active? Is there a threshold or mechanism? (R3)
- Minor: lines 247–250 contain a repetition; Figure 9 caption has a typo (“needels”). (R4)
- Recommend more appendix figures for evaluation results of the Retrieval Head Detection Algorithm on various settings (line 199). (R4)
