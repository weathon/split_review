## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a view-consistent point sampling method for NeRF training, using both low-level RGB features and high-level distilled features (from DINOv2) to compute a multi-view consistency metric per 3D point. This metric guides importance sampling during the fine stage. A depth‑pushing loss is also introduced to penalize background collapse. The method is evaluated on two datasets with varying numbers of input images, consistently showing improvements over NeRF baselines. One reviewer (rating 6) found the approach interesting, methodologically sound, and yielding superior results, while three reviewers (ratings 3,3,5) raised concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, and scope.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written, well‑organized, and easy to follow; the motivation is clear and the idea is reasonable.
- Visualizations are clear and effectively illustrate the improvements in novel view synthesis (e.g., Figure 4).
- The two components – view‑consistent sampling and depth‑pushing loss – are sound, well‑motivated, and can be integrated into existing NeRF‑based models.
- Quantitative results (e.g., Table 4) and qualitative results (Figure 4) demonstrate clear improvements over several depth‑regularization methods and NeRF variants.
- Results on two datasets with different numbers of input images consistently show that the proposed method outperforms previous NeRF baselines.
- One reviewer emphasized that the approach is interesting and methodologically sound, with superior experimental performance (rating 6).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** Multiple reviewers point out that 3DGS is now the prevailing representation for novel view synthesis, being much faster and often achieving better quality. The paper does not compare with 3DGS, nor does it discuss how the proposed sampling technique could be adapted to 3DGS. The authors should either provide a comparison or explain why they focus solely on NeRF.
- **Novelty concerns regarding the view‑consistency metric:** Using both low‑level RGB features and high‑level features to compute multi‑view consistency is not new; prior works on learning‑based feature matching (e.g., SuperGlue, GIM) have explored similar ideas. The paper does not clearly articulate how the proposed method differs from existing feature‑matching approaches, and the overall contribution is considered insufficient for ICLR by some reviewers.
- **Depth‑pushing loss vs. distortion loss in MipNeRF360:** The depth‑pushing loss is conceptually similar to the distortion loss used in MipNeRF360 (CVPR 2022). A detailed comparison and discussion of the differences and benefits is missing.
- **Missing comparisons to other efficient sampling techniques:** The paper should compare to existing efficient sampling methods such as the coarse‑to‑fine online distillation in MipNeRF360 and probabilistic ray sampling in SceneRF (ICCV 2023).
- **Poor or incomplete ablation study:** 
  - The ablation table (Tab. 2) lacks explanation for each row and the effect of each component is not clearly isolated.
  - The impact of the distilled feature dimension on both training speed and rendering quality should be shown in a separate table.
  - A plot of ray distributions with and without the depth‑pushing loss would better illustrate its effectiveness.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Many hyperparameters (e.g., depth‑pushing loss weight, threshold δ) are set without discussion. The sensitivity of these parameters to performance, and whether δ varies across datasets or across different ray locations, is not explored. Excessive depth‑pushing weight might lead to suboptimal ray distributions.
- **Dependence on DINOv2 features:** The method relies on DINOv2 features; it is unclear whether other foundation models (or weaker models) would achieve similar performance. The indispensability of DINOv2 should be examined.
- **Limited evaluation scope:** 
  - The method is only evaluated on the Nerfacto model. It is unclear if it generalizes to other NeRF‑based methods, though it seems applicable.
  - The evaluation uses non‑standard dataset splits (varying numbers of input images), making it difficult to compare with many previous baselines. Results on the standard split with all available images should be reported, and comparisons with more existing approaches (including 3DGS) are needed.
- **Training time increase:** According to Table 1, the proposed method significantly increases training time.
- **Missing experiment with very few views:** Figure 5 only reports performance when the number of training views is above 10; it is unclear if the method still works with very few views (e.g., 3 or 5).
- **Unclear impact of feature matching quality:** The paper does not quantitatively analyze the performance of the learned point matching and its influence on downstream rendering.
- **Clarification needed on sampling usage:** It is unclear whether the proposed sampling method is used only during training or also during inference.
- **Minor typo:** Duplicated “the” in Line 270 of the main paper.
