## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a generative model that integrates energy-based models (EBMs) with diffusion-based processes to learn intermediate latent distributions implicitly, without a predefined noise degradation schedule. The algorithm unrolls the sampling process from noise to clean input using Langevin dynamics and a time-dependent energy function, imposing a reconstruction loss. The authors claim improvements in image generation quality and sampling efficiency on datasets including CIFAR-10, CelebA, and LSUN.

### Strengths

- **Innovative approach** (all reviewers): The integration of EBMs with diffusion-like transitions to adaptively learn intermediate latents, sidestepping traditional fixed noise schedules, is original and theoretically interesting.
- **Addresses a known EBM limitation** (Reviewer 1): The method removes the need for a separate generator by unrolling the sampling process and imposing a reconstruction loss, which is a practical improvement over standard EBM training.
- **Appropriate technical choices** (Reviewer 2): Use of Langevin dynamics and WGAN-LP as stabilizing techniques aligns with best practices in EBM training.
- **Potential for better latents** (Reviewer 3): Removing the predefined noise schedule offers the possibility of finding better intermediate distributions than in diffusion models.
- **Baseline comparisons** (Reviewer 3): The evaluation includes comparisons to other methods (though see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Underwhelming empirical performance** (Reviewers 1,2,3 – consensus): FID on CIFAR-10 is ~17, far below older methods like Karras et al. (2020) and DDPM (FID ~3) and recent work like CTM (Dongjun et al., 2023) achieving FID <2 in one step. Gains on CelebA/LSUN are marginal. The practical value is questionable.
- **No improvement in sampling efficiency** (Reviewers 1,2): The reduction in inference steps is modest; DDIM and other optimized samplers achieve comparable or better efficiency with higher quality. The claim of faster sampling is not convincingly demonstrated.
- **Limited theoretical contribution** (Reviewer 1): The connection to diffusion models is tenuous (no corruption in the forward process). The learning objective appears similar to prior work (Xie et al., 2016b) without a diffusion connection.
- **Insufficient baselines and metrics** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not compare to recent efficient sampling techniques (e.g., DDIM) and only reports FID/Inception scores; additional metrics (e.g., perceptual similarity) would strengthen the analysis.
- **Potential longer training time** (Reviewer 1): The algorithm requires backpropagation through time because the sampling process is unrolled from noise to clean. No comparison of model size or training iterations with EBM baselines (e.g., Gao et al., 2020a, 2020b) is provided.
- **Methodological clarity and justification** (Reviewers 2,3): Several technical choices are poorly justified:
  - The decision to omit a generator and rely solely on energy functions does not lead to significant performance gains, raising questions about its practical benefit (Reviewer 2).
  - The inference procedure is not clearly stated (Reviewer 3): Is one (or more) LD step run per iteration using the time-dependent score \(f_\theta(x_t, t)\)? The intuition for why fewer steps are needed is unclear (removing Gaussian transitions?).
  - The advantage of energy parametrization over score parametrization is not explained (Reviewer 3). The idea of implicit latents might also work with score models, but EBMs typically lag behind (e.g., Table 1: FID ~17 for this method vs ~3 for DDPM).
  - The motivation of the algorithm and the role of \(t\) is confusing (Reviewer 3): It is unclear whether \(t\) represents a time index or the Langevin step number. Equation (9) suggests a standard LD iteration with time-dependent \(f_\theta\), but how/why this helps is not explained.
  - In Eq. (10), the target for \(x_t\) appears to always be the data distribution; how “useful” latents (that do annealing) are obtained is unclear (Reviewer 3). Possibly via limited ability of \(f(x_t,t)\) to predict \(x_0\) (as in \(x_0\)-prediction in diffusion), but this needs clarification.
- **Minor issues** (Reviewers 1,3):
  - Eq. (5) should be \(\exp(f_\theta(x))\) (Reviewer 1).
  - L148-9: energy should be *low* when \(x\) belongs to the distribution (Reviewer 3).
  - Section 4.2 is a fairly standard calculation (e.g., Luo 2022) and could be moved to an appendix (Reviewer 3).
  - L267: “second term” (Reviewer 3).

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 5) is more positive than Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 3), but still raises significant concerns about clarity and performance relative to diffusion models. The overall assessment across reviewers is that the method is interesting but empirically weak and insufficiently justified.*
