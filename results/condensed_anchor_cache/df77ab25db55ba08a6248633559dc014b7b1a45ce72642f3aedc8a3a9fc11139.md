## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ConceptPrune, a training-free method for concept editing in diffusion models (tested only on Stable Diffusion) that identifies and prunes “skilled neurons” in the UNet’s feed‑forward networks (specifically FFN‑2). By computing activation‑based importance scores for target vs. reference prompts, it prunes ~0.12% of weights to erase concepts (artistic styles, nudity, objects, gender biases) while maintaining generation quality and showing resistance to adversarial attacks.

### Strengths
1. The paper investigates how specific weights influence abilities in text‑to‑image models, offering insights into model internals. (R3)
2. Experiments cover multiple concept‑unlearning tasks (artistic styles, nudity, object erasure, gender debiasing) and evaluate ASR against both white‑box and black‑box attacks alongside utility metrics (FID, CLIP score). (R2, R3)
3. The method is training‑free and requires only minor weight modifications (~0.12% of total weights). (R2)
4. The paper is well‑written, well‑structured, and clearly explains the methodology with mathematical formulations and visual examples. (R1, R3, R4)
5. The work addresses significant ethical concerns (unsafe content, copyright, biases). (R1)

### Weaknesses
1. **Weak baselines and lack of comparison with recent SOTA methods.** The baselines are early machine‑unlearning methods for diffusion models and do not include stronger recent approaches (e.g., AdvUnlearn [R2/R1]). Several reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) emphasize that comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art methods are missing, making it hard to assess true superiority. The paper should discuss and compare against these SOTA baselines and also include related concept‑pruning works ( [2,3,4] in R3).  
   *Minority reviewer (R4, score=3) considers this lack of baselines critical; R2 (score=8) also notes weak baselines but still finds the method effective.*  
2. **Evaluation limited to a single model (Stable Diffusion).** Generalizability to other diffusion models (e.g., [4,5] in R1) is not demonstrated. (R1)  
3. **Insufficient robustness evaluation.** The robustness against adversarial prompts is claimed but not thoroughly tested with dedicated adversarial attacks. Recent research shows machine unlearning can be vulnerable; stronger robustness experiments are needed (R4).  
4. **Missing visualizations for several tasks.** Only style unlearning is visualized; nudity and object unlearning lack qualitative examples, limiting qualitative assessment (R2). The CLIP‑based similarity metric used for style erasure may confound style with content; a dedicated style classifier (e.g., [8] in R1) is recommended for more precise evaluation (R1).  
5. **Choice of pruning target (FFN‑2) and timestep aggregation (last 10 steps) appear empirically motivated.** No theoretical justification is provided for pruning FFN‑2 specifically, nor for aggregating over the last 10 timesteps. Insight from related literature on denoising timesteps (e.g., [6,7] in R1) could support the choice; ablation studies on these design decisions are needed (R1).  
6. **Determination of optimal pruning ratio.** The criteria for selecting the pruning ratio are not explained; the authors should describe how the ratio is chosen (R3).  
7. **Table 5 missing FID score and base model (SD) performance.** For clarity, the FID metric and the base model’s performance should be included (R2).
