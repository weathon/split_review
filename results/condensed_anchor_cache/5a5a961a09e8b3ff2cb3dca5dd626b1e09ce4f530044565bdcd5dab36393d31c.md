## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReVaR, a method that learns an instance-dependent weighting function via bilevel optimization to capture predictive uncertainty for both training (reweighting via the auxiliary network) and test-time applications (e.g., selective classification). The auxiliary network is trained with a meta-objective that minimizes dropout variance, an approximation of Bayesian predictive uncertainty. Experiments on multiple datasets (ImageNet, Cifar100, diabetic retinopathy, Camelyon, WILDs, ImageNet-C/-A/-R, Clothing-1.6M) and tasks (selective classification, label noise, domain adaptation, calibration) show improvements over baselines, including PLEX. One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive overall, highlighting strong empirical evidence and applicability; the other two reviewers (score 6) found the method simple and intuitive but raised significant concerns about theoretical justification, presentation clarity, and missing proof.

### Strengths
- Strong empirical evidence at impressive scale, with a rich set of recent baselines and fair comparisons (R1). The method can augment prior robust approaches such as PLEX, boosting applicability (R1).
- Simplicity and intuitiveness: the method is easy to implement (R2).
- Comprehensive evaluation across many datasets and tasks: selective classification, label noise, calibration, domain adaptation (R2, R3).
- Original and significant contribution: the premise of an all-purpose uncertainty evaluation tool, usable at both train and test time and adaptive to various sources of uncertainty, is considered novel (R3).
- Section 4’s classification of uncertainty into types 1–3 and the discussion of how instance weights should respond to these types is a useful step toward a theoretical framework (R3).
- Computational efficiency concerns are thoroughly addressed in the appendix (R1).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty and distinction from prior bilevel optimization reweighting methods are unclear in Section 3; the contributions of instance-conditioning and the meta-regularizer for variance minimization need explicit contrast with existing work (R1).
- Section 4 is hard to read. Design choices (e.g., $X_c$, $X_e$, $W_c$, $W_e$) appear arbitrary, with unclear motivation for partitioning the input space and no justification of dimensionalities (R1). The derivation of the “theoretical ideal” instance-dependent weights in Table 1 is unclear; more information on how these targets were determined is needed (R3).
- Missing reference/citation for a theory in Section 4, Scenario 1 (“?” broken reference) (R1, R2, R3).
- Many undefined terms and functions: $g(x)$, $R^2$, $G$ not properly introduced when first used (R2). Missing citation for MMCE (R2). Notation inconsistencies: Equation 3’s definition of $\theta^*$ should likely be $g_{\Theta^*}$; text after Equation 6 introduces variables not used in that equation (R3). Inconsistent notation elsewhere (e.g., “p=w=g(x)” in Section 1.1) (R1).
- Lack of error bars in Tables 4–7 (R2).
- Only evaluated on ResNet-50; other architectures could be trained with similar compute (R2).
- No code provided, hindering reproducibility (R1).
- Grammatical and orthographic issues (e.g., missing articles, “captures captures”) (R1).
- The architecture of the U-score model is described only in the appendix; it should be hinted at in the main paper (R1).
- Theoretical concern: minimizing dropout variance may simply collapse the dropout distribution to all identical parameters (Dirac distribution), rather than truly capturing uncertainty. A proof or investigation why this yields improved uncertainty is missing (R2). The synthetic setup in Section 4 should include a formal proof (R2).
- Missing baseline comparison: in distribution shift settings where a validation set from the test distribution is available, fine-tuning on that validation set (or part of it) could be a relevant baseline (R3 question).
- Learning dynamics of $g$ in real-world experiments are not shown (patterns of learned weights, evolution over training, effect on training speed vs. caution). This analysis would be useful (R1 question).
- It is not explored whether the U-SCORE and predictor weights could be shared and what effect that would have (R1 question).
