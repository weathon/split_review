## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Continual Momentum Filtering (CMF), a Kalman filter (KF) approach to online test-time adaptation that averages/filters model parameters (specifically normalization parameters) to balance adaptability and information retention, reducing catastrophic forgetting. Reviewers generally find the problem practical, the methodology soundly derived from KF with probabilistic interpretation, and the experimental evaluation thorough across image classification (e.g., ImageNet‑C) and speech tasks, showing competitive or superior performance. However, one reviewer (score 3) is notably more negative, questioning the KF’s linear/Gaussian assumptions and the novelty relative to existing Bayesian filtering works. Other concerns include limited analysis of approximation errors, marginal gains over ROID, missing comparisons with replay‑based methods, and insufficient investigation of hyperparameter sensitivity and failure modes.

### Strengths
- The investigated problem (adaptability vs. anti‑forgetting trade‑off in OTTA) is practical and important for real‑world deployment. [R3, R4]
- The proposed CMF framework is simple yet effective, properly derived from a Kalman filter with probabilistic interpretations, and can be adapted to deep networks where only normalization parameters are updated. [R1]
- Casting OTTA as a Bayesian filtering problem and leveraging the closed‑form KF inference is an interesting approach, though one reviewer notes the linearity assumption may not align with real‑world scenarios. [R2]
- Experimental evaluations are thorough across various backbone networks, datasets, and scenarios (covariate and label shifts, speech recognition), demonstrating strong performance against state‑of‑the‑art methods. [R1, R3, R4]
- The paper is well‑structured and communicates the method details effectively. [R4]

### Weaknesses
- **KF assumptions and novelty concerns (minority reviewer more negative):** One reviewer argues the Kalman filter assumes linear system dynamics and Gaussian posteriors, which may not fit real‑world multimodal distributions, and that applying KF to model adaptation is not entirely novel. They recommend rigorous theoretical and empirical comparison with established works like EKF [1] and PFDE [2] to highlight unique contributions. [R2] (Other reviewers did not raise this as a severe weakness.)
- **Missing comparison with related Kalman filter adaptation methods:** The paper should compare (theoretically and empirically) with prior KF‑based online adaptation methods, e.g., [1,2] as cited by Reviewers 2 and 4. [R2, R4]
- **Missing comparison with replay‑based catastrophic forgetting methods:** The paper does not analyze advantages/disadvantages of CMF versus replay‑based approaches like ER [3] and A‑GEM [4] for overcoming forgetting. [R4]
- **Performance gains marginal compared to ROID:** Reviewer 3 notes that improvements over ROID are small. [R3]
- **Analysis of approximations in Section 3.4:** The simplifications (e.g., using scalar parameters instead of matrices) lack an evaluation of their accuracy. The paper should compare the simplified scalar version with the full matrix KF on a smaller model to quantify cost vs. performance. [R2, R4]
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters:** Reviewer 1 requests a study on the sensitivity of each hyperparameter; Reviewer 3 specifically asks about parameter \(I\) in Algorithm 1. [R1, R3] Reviewer 4 asks whether hyperparameters \(\alpha\) and \(\gamma\) in the source‑conjugated transition model ablation remain the same, and suggests exploring their effects individually. [R4]
- **Limitations and failure modes:** It is unclear whether CMF relies on a well‑pretrained network (cannot be trained from scratch), how many adaptation parameters it can handle, and how many tasks it can continually learn without forgetting. Reviewer 1 asks for toy experiments on parameter averaging to understand when the method breaks. [R1]
- **Missing ablations / further experiments:**
  - What is the performance of “DW‑SLR + SCE” without CMF? [R3]
  - Can CMF replace the weight ensemble in ROID (i.e., ROID with CMF)? [R3]
  - Can CMF help MEMO work stably in the online setting? [R3]
  - What is the in‑distribution performance of compared methods after adapting to out‑of‑distribution data (following the comparison manner in EATA)? [R3]
  - What is the computational complexity (wall‑clock GPU time) of CMF? [R3]
- **Clarity and motivation:** More high‑level explanation of why CMF achieves a better adaptability/information‑retention trade‑off is needed. [R3] Implementation details (hyperparameters) of baselines on different models and datasets should be provided. [R3]
