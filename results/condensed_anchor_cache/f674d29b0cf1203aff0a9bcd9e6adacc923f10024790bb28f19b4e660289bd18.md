## Merged Review

### Summary
This work extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to multi-objective fine-tuning (MOFT) by replacing the Bradley-Terry-Luce model with the Plackett-Luce (PL) model, enabling listwise ranking data. The HyperDPO framework uses a hypernetwork (input-conditioned network) to profile the Pareto front of multiple objectives via one-shot training, with post-training control over trade-offs via weight conditioning and temperature. Applied to learning-to-rank (LTR) and LLM alignment (GPT-2, Alpaca-7B) tasks, HyperDPO shows improved hypervolume (HV) and reduced training time compared to baselines DPO-LS, DPO Soup, and MO-DPO. While some reviewers found the integration potentially novel (R3, score 8; positive), others judged the contribution incremental and the system overclaimed (R1 score 5, R2 score 6, R4 score 3; more critical). The main concerns revolve around novelty, mislabeling of “hypernetwork”, experimental validity, and proof issues in Proposition 3.1.

### Strengths
- **Timely and important problem**: Multi-objective fine-tuning of LLMs for alignment (e.g., helpfulness, safety, fairness) and for learning-to-rank; matches real-world deployment needs (R1, R2, R4).
- **Clear theoretical derivations**: Provides mathematical proofs (e.g., linear transformation property in Proposition 3.1) and connects learning-to-rank with LLM alignment, which may guide future methods (R2, R3, R4).
- **Potential novelty of integration** (R3): Incorporating an input-conditioned network (hypernetwork) with DPO via Hyper Prompt Tuning (HPT), using objective weights as inputs, is seen as a novel contribution by one reviewer; the approach is clearly written and theoretically grounded.
- **Empirical improvements**: Experiments show HyperDPO achieves higher hypervolume (HV) and faster training than baselines on both LTR and LLM tasks (R3, R4). Pareto curves (Figure 4) demonstrate dominance over baselines in some settings.
- **Efficiency**: One-shot training for Pareto front profiling, offering post-training control over trade-offs without retraining (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental and overclaimed contributions** (R1, R2, R4):  
  - The generalization via Plackett-Luce was already proposed in Liu 2024.  
  - Using a hypernetwork to profile the Pareto front originates from Navon 2020 and Ruchte & Grabocka 2021.  
  - The core scientific novelty is unclear: HyperDPO is a combination of existing techniques (DPO + input-conditioned networks + ListNet loss) (R1, R2, R4).  
  - The term “hypernetwork” is misused; the system implements an input-conditioned network, not a hypernetwork (which outputs network parameters) as defined by Ha et al 2017 and Chauhan et al 2024. This distinction affects theoretical properties (R4).  
  - Hyper Prompt Tuning and temperature-conditioned networks (core contributions listed in Section 1.1) are described only in experiments or the supplemental material with insufficient detail (R4).

- **Proposition 3.1 issues** (R2, R4):  
  - The linear transformation property is proven for \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{ListNet}}\) (Eq. 12) rather than \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{Hypernet}}\) (Eq. 13), thus ignoring Pareto fronts.  
  - The property is not validated experimentally, and its practical applicability is not discussed (R2).  
  - The proof logic is unclear: how does comparing \(s_{\theta,c\beta}(y,w|x)\) with \(s_{\theta,\beta}(y,w|x)\) imply that numerators are equal? (R4)

- **Experimental shortcomings** (all reviewers):  
  - Only hypervolume (HV) is reported for Pareto front quality; sparsity and other metrics are missing (R3).  
  - For LLM alignment (GPT-2, Alpaca-7B), no error bars or statistical significance are provided; HV and training times lack standard deviation (R3, R4).  
  - Results on Alpaca-7B show DPO-LS performing close to HyperDPO, suggesting the superiority depends on the base model (R3).  
  - MO-DPO is missing from Table 2 without explanation (R3).  
  - Training time comparisons are unfair: differences depend on number of w-vector samples and epochs; justification and analysis needed (R4).  
  - Missing comparisons to other multi-objective hypernetwork methods (e.g., Navon et al 2021, Hoang et al 2023) (R4).  
  - Significance of improvements over baselines not quantified (R1).

- **Reproducibility and presentation gaps** (R3, R4):  
  - Source code not available; number of random seeds not reported (R3).  
  - Description of dataset, objectives, and optimization for LLM tasks is too brief to allow replication (R4).  
  - Inline citations improperly formatted; reference section has capitalization errors and arXiv-versions where published versions exist; floating-point notation (e.g., “1.473e-01”) should be mathematical (“0.1473e”) (R4).

- **Unaddressed questions** (overlap with weaknesses):  
  - Can HyperDPO be extended to non-linear utility functions (R3)?  
  - How does Proposition 3.1 apply in practice (R2)?  
  - What exactly is the scientific claim supported by experiments (R4)?

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R3, score 8) found the integration novel and the paper well-written, while the other three (R1, R2, R4) scored 3–6 and emphasized incremental novelty, overclaiming, and experimental validity concerns.
