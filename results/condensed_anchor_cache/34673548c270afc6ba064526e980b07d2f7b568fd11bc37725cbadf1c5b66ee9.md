## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) by embedding it into a probabilistic graphical model and deriving a maximum entropy (MaxEnt) objective. It proposes the Heterogeneous-Agent Soft Actor-Critic (HASAC) algorithm, proving monotonic improvement and convergence to quantal response equilibrium (QRE). It also generalizes a template, Maximum Entropy Heterogeneous-Agent Mirror Learning (MEHAML), which provides the same guarantees. Empirical evaluation across six benchmarks (Bi-DexHands, Multi-Agent MuJoCo, StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge, Google Research Football, Multi-Agent Particle Environment, Light Aircraft Game) shows that HASAC achieves the best performance in 31 out of 35 tasks, with better sample efficiency, robustness, and exploration. Reviewers broadly praise the clarity, theoretical grounding, and empirical results, but raise concerns about unclear assumptions, sensitivity to hyperparameters, limited novelty (minority view), and the need for more comprehensive experimental reporting.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and presentation**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) note the paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and well-motivated (e.g., with an illustrative matrix game example). R4 rates presentation as “excellent.”
- **Theoretical contribution**: The paper provides rigorous proofs of monotonic improvement and convergence to QRE, grounded in a probabilistic graphical model formulation (R2, R3, R4). R3 considers the theoretical combination “remarkable” and rigorous. R4 emphasizes that the method is not a simple combination of MaxEnt RL and MARL, but built on control-as-inference.
- **Empirical performance**: All reviewers acknowledge strong results across many benchmarks. R4 highlights that HASAC achieves the best performance in 31 out of 35 tasks. R1 states results support conclusions. R3 notes consistent superior performance and high sample efficiency. R2 acknowledges evaluation on a variety of benchmarks.
- **Algorithmic simplicity**: R3 observes that the practical HASAC algorithm is straightforward and does not require recomputing Q-value estimates for sequential policy updates.
- **Novelty (majority view)**: R3 and R4 find the technical contribution clear and novel. R4 calls it a “clear technical contribution.” **Minority disagreement**: R2 rates contribution as “fair” (score 6), stating that the main contribution is simply applying SAC to the multi-agent setting. This contrast is noted below.

### Weaknesses
- **IGO assumption not explained**: Both R1 and R4 note that the IGO assumption from prior work is introduced without definition or explanation of its limitations. R1 asks for a clear definition and why it is restrictive. R4 says the term will be unfamiliar to readers outside MARL.
- **Sensitivity to entropy temperature and hyperparameter tuning**: Multiple reviewers note concerns. R3 finds the ablation study shows the algorithm is not robust to the entropy temperature; each domain uses a different temperature (fixed or automatically adjusted with a fixed target entropy), and suggests a more effective adaptive method (e.g., ADER). R1 asks for elaboration on the automatically adjusted α schedule and its coupling to learning dynamics. R2 asks about sensitivity of performance to the choice of α. The reliance on manual tuning or task-specific target entropies raises questions about generalizability and robustness (R3).
- **Sequential policy update unclear**: R3 points out that the necessity of sequential updates for the MaxEnt MARL objective is not clearly justified. The multi-agent soft Q-function (Eq. 7) includes the entropy of complementary agents, so the practical policy update (Eq. 10) reduces to optimizing over a term that appears similar to a simultaneous update. R3 requests an example or technical explanation showing why sequential updates are needed, rather than just being additional sampling.
- **Experimental validity concerns (minority view)**: R2 (high confidence, score 6) questions the validity of reported results. Training curves show significant fluctuations; only a selection of results is presented in the main paper, which may give a biased view. R2 recommends results from a larger number of seeds and testing in scenarios where sample efficiency is crucial (e.g., real robots, stock exchange) given the off-policy nature. Other reviewers (R1, R3, R4) found the empirical evaluation strong. R2’s critical perspective is noted.
- **Limited novelty (minority view)**: R2 states that the novelty is limited, as the core idea is applying SAC to the multi-agent setting, and rates contribution as “fair.” The other three reviewers see significant technical novelty. This disagreement is preserved.
- **Convergence to QRE, not optimal NE**: R4 asks if it is possible to guarantee convergence to the optimal Nash equilibrium instead of QRE. R1 similarly asks for an intuition of why QRE is preferred over NE. This is a recognized limitation of the method.
- **Missing definitions**: R1 asks for a definition of the ω-limit (point 4 in Theorem 3). R1 also notes that acronyms are abundant and suggests adding a table of definitions in the appendix.
- **Need for design guidance on drift functional and neighborhood operator**: R1 asks what factors should be considered when constructing these terms and how they depend on the task. The paper provides limited discussion on their design in the experiments.
- **Connection to PPO**: R1 asks whether the constraint on policy drift shares high-level similarities with PPO, which could be explored. (This is a minor comment, not a strong criticism.)
- **Minor**: R1 notes “2 hundred” should be written as “200”.
