## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes applying statistical methods from physics (f-divergences, linear dissimilarity) to automatically detect phase transitions in the output distributions of large language models. It studies three control parameters: a varying numeric in the prompt, the softmax temperature, and training epoch (Pythia suite). Changes are quantified via statistical distances, with peaks interpreted as signatures of phase transitions.

### Strengths
- Innovative and interesting connection between physics concepts and LLM behavior (R1,R2,R3).
- The proposed metric (linear dissimilarity) is theoretically grounded via a link to Fisher information (R1).
- Study covers multiple open LLMs (R1) and a large number of models/checkpoints; obtaining the numbers and graphs required substantial work (R3).
- The method is significant for LLMs because order parameters are entirely unknown, unlike in physics; the black-box nature of the approach is a potential advantage (R4).
- The paper reviews potentially useful measures from statistical physics, placing widely used measures like KL divergence in a broader context (R3).
- Findings such as phase transitions identified at specific training epochs may be of interest (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of external validation and unclear usefulness**: There is no independent way to verify that the detected transitions are meaningful or interesting. The findings might depend heavily on the choice of divergence (e.g., linear dissimilarity vs. TV, JSD) (R1,R2,R3). The paper does not show that its favorite tools are more useful than better-known alternatives (R3). No use case or clear interpretation of the phases from a linguistic perspective is provided (R1,R4). For example, three phases are claimed when temperature is varied, but what each phase represents linguistically is unexplained (R4).
- **Case studies stop short**: In each case study the analysis ends where it becomes interesting, teaching nothing new about LLM behavior or underlying mechanisms.
  - Case study 1 (numerical prompts): The fact that output distributions change when the input necessitates a different response is a trivial consequence of training for categorical prediction; this has nothing to do with phase transitions (R3). The results (e.g., that LLMs build representations of integer magnitudes, that tokenization affects performance) are not news (R3).
  - Case study 2 (temperature): The graphs only show that high temperatures yield random distributions and low temperatures are sensitive – this is the starting point, not a discovery (R3).
  - Case study 3 (training dynamics): Figure 5 shows peaks (e.g., near epochs 20K, 40K, 80K in 5b) but the paper admits some may be noise, and no effort is made to confirm that they represent macroscopic phase changes via another method or to characterize representations before and after (R3). It is unclear which Pythia model is used and why only layers 0–5 are plotted (R3).
- **Insufficient experimental validation**: The paper uses only a limited number of prompts and test cases; more extensive experiments with diverse prompts are needed (R2).
- **Ambiguous definition of phase transition vs. crossover**: The detected changes may be crossovers rather than true phase transitions (R4). For instance, in Figure 2 the linear dissimilarity for the Llama3 base model reaches only 0.4 – is this considered a phase transition? (R2). The paper lacks a clear, formal definition (R2).
- **Prompt-dependence weakens the physical analogy**: If the “phase” of an LLM depends on details of the prompt (e.g., the temperature at which a transition occurs changes with the prompt), it is unclear whether this is analogous to physical phase transitions, which depend on macroscopic parameters (R4).
- **Lack of engagement with existing LLM literature**: The paper cites relevant works (Wei et al., Schaeffer et al., Chen et al.) but does not engage with them. For impact, the results need to be linked to known phenomena, e.g., the emergence of numerical circuits (Hanna et al., 2024) or progress measures for grokking (Nanda et al., 2023) (R3).
- **Unclear main contribution**: If the contribution is the automated detection method, the validation is insufficient; if it is theoretical connections to physics, the explanations are not accessible (e.g., Ising model comparison lacks mathematical formalization) (R2). The paper provides no parallel visualizations comparing physical phase transitions (e.g., water heat capacity) to LLM findings (R2).
- **Methodology questions**: 
  - What advantages does linear dissimilarity offer over total variation distance and Jensen-Shannon divergence? Figure 1 shows all divergences capture similar behavior (R2).
  - How was heat capacity calculated? Is there evidence for negative heat capacity, and can it be linked to long-range interactions like attention? (R4)
  - Can the authors specify the order of the phase transition (first-order vs. second-order)? (R4)
  - Figure 4(b): Is it truly a zoom of (a)? The black line and y-axis values appear different; linear dissimilarity does not connect as specific heat does – explanation required (R2,R4).
- **Minor issues**: “-1” in line 166 should be in math mode (R4). Figure 1 caption references the Appendix incorrectly (R4).
