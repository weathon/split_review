- Scores: 3, 1, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dynamic Smoothing (DSMOOTH), an extension of randomized smoothing using anisotropic Gaussian noise and Mahalanobis-distance-based certification to handle complex adversarial attacks (e.g., multi-norm, localized). The smoothing covariance matrix is derived from the empirical covariance of adversarial attack perturbations. Experiments on CIFAR-10 (subset of 500 images) and ImageNet compare certified accuracy against isotropic Gaussian smoothing (Cohen et al. 2019, L2 certificate) and Laplace smoothing (Teng et al. 2020, L1 certificate), under a multi-attack scenario combining Square Attack + FGSM. The authors claim significant improvements in certified robustness.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea** (Reviewers 1, 3): Using a covariance matrix derived specifically from the empirical distribution of adversarial attack directions is novel and promising. The theoretical extension to Mahalanobis distance for anisotropic noise is a valuable direction in randomized smoothing.
- **Theoretical foundation** (Reviewer 1): The paper provides a strong theoretical grounding for generalizing randomized smoothing beyond isotropic Gaussian noise.
- **Clear presentation of theoretical results** (Reviewer 3): The more specific Lemma 4.5 (anisotropic Gaussian certificate) is presented clearly, and the overall structure of the claims is understandable.
- **Experimental support** (Reviewer 1, though limited): The reported results on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet support the theoretical claims, showing improved certified accuracy in the tested multi-attack setting.

### Weaknesses
1. **Incorrect Theorem 4.3** (Reviewer 3): The general claim (Theorem 4.3) is false. Reviewer 3 provides a concrete 1D counterexample with an invertible function \(p\) and piecewise-constant classifier \(f\), showing that the certification condition fails even when \(|p^{-1}(\delta)|_2\) is within the bound. The proof provided in the paper is therefore invalid. Lemma 4.5 (anisotropic Gaussian) happens to be true but can be proven directly as a corollary of Cohen et al. 2019 using linearity of the Cholesky transformation, without relying on Theorem 4.3.
2. **Missing related work and diminished novelty** (Reviewers 2, 3): Anisotropic Gaussian randomized smoothing has been addressed by prior works, which are not cited:
   - **ANCER** (Reviewer 2, [1]): Generalized Gaussian smoothing to anisotropic distributions and provided a robustness certification, significantly diminishing novelty.
   - **Eiras et al. 2022** (Reviewer 3): Corollary 1 is logically identical to Lemma 4.5; the method for computing \(\Sigma\) differs but the certificate itself is not new.
   - **Tecot 2021, Pfrommer et al. 2023, Rumezhak et al. 2023** (Reviewer 3): Provide related certificates or methods for determining covariance. The authors do not discuss similarities/differences with these works.
   - **Hong and Hong (2022)** and **Eiras et al. (2021)** (Reviewer 1) also discuss anisotropic approaches; these are not referenced.
3. **Insufficient experimental rigor** (Reviewers 1, 3):
   - Evaluation uses only **500 images** from CIFAR-10 test set, not the full set (standard practice). This choice is not justified and limits generalizability (Reviewer 1).
   - Only **one multi-attack combination** (Square Attack + FGSM) is tested. Effectiveness under diverse real-world multi-attack scenarios remains unverified (Reviewers 1, 3).
   - No **empirical accuracies under actual attack** are reported; claims about effectiveness against complex attacks (line 477-478) are too strong based on certification results alone (Reviewer 3).
   - The paper does not compare to baselines designed for \(l_p\)-norm defenses with \(p>2\). The justification (“impossibility results”) is vague; these defenses are common benchmarks (Reviewer 1).
4. **Unfair or unclear comparisons** (Reviewers 2, 3):
   - The x-axis in Figures 1 and 2 is labeled “certified radius,” but it is unclear whether this is L1, L2, or Mahalanobis distance radius for each method. Comparing certificates in different metrics is not apples-to-apples; prior work (Pfrommer et al. 2023, Tecot 2021) compares certified volumes (Reviewer 3).
   - It is not stated whether baseline methods (Cohen et al., Teng et al.) were also adversarially trained. If not, the comparison is unfair (Reviewer 3).
   - Reviewer 2 asks: Why is Mahalanobis-norm certified accuracy compared to L1- and L2-norm certified accuracy? The rationale for using Mahalanobis distance and its practical applicability should be clarified.
5. **Clarity and writing issues** (Reviewers 2, 3):
   - The abstract includes “Code: [removed for review]” – should be omitted (Reviewer 2).
   - The paper contains typos: “isotopic” instead of “isotropic” (consistently, Reviewer 1); “then” vs “than” (line 17); “highly-dimensional” → “high-dimensional” (line 125); duplicated reference for Cohen et al. (2019a and 2019b are the same) (Reviewer 3).
   - The definition of the threat model (Eq. 1) only covers additive attacks, contradicting the text’s claim to encompass recoloring/physical attacks (Reviewer 3).
   - The experimental setup is insufficiently explained: How is \(\Sigma\) constructed? If it depends on attacks on the base classifier \(f\), that should be stated. If \(\Sigma\) is not full-rank, the inverse in the Gaussian and Mahalanobis definitions is undefined; the footnote and low-rank approximation need clarification (Reviewer 3).
   - The description of GPUs (lines 391-392) states clock speed 33 MHz – extremely slow; likely incorrect (Reviewer 3).
   - The paragraph lines 112-117 is not correct as written (Reviewer 3).
   - Use of \(\mathbb{P}\) for both event probability and distribution is confusing (Reviewer 3).
6. **Outdated L1 baseline** (Reviewer 3): The comparison to Laplace smoothing (Teng et al. 2020) is outdated; Yang et al. 2020 (uniform noise), Levine & Feizi 2021 (splitting noise), and Voracek & Hein 2023 provide better L1 certificates.
7. **Circularity in constructing \(\Sigma\)** (Reviewer 3): The smoothing covariance \(\Sigma\) depends on the distribution of optimal attacks (which depend on the classifier), yet it is part of the definition of the final smoothed classifier \(g\). This circularity needs explicit resolution.
8. **Limited benefit justification** (Reviewer 2): The benefits introduced by the modified noise are not well-presented.

Note: Reviewer 2 is more negative overall (score 1), emphasizing the prior work ANCER and lack of novelty, and criticizes clarity more strongly. Reviewer 3 also notes that the primary theoretical novelty is flawed (incorrect Theorem 4.3) and that the remaining contribution (method for computing \(\Sigma\)) is not compared to prior methods. Reviewer 1, while positive on the idea, points out experimental limitations. These positions are preserved above.
