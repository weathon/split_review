## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the unbalanced training in end-to-end Transformer-based MOT caused by label assignment that binds most objects to track queries and leaves few positives for detection queries. The authors propose Coopetition Label Assignment (COLA), which allows tracked objects to be reassigned to detection queries in intermediate decoders, and Shadow Sets, which expand each query with slight disturbances for one-to-set matching. On DanceTrack, CO-MOT achieves 69.4% HOTA; on BDD100K 52.8% TETA. It requires only 38% FLOPs of MOTRv2 for similar performance with 1.4× faster inference. Experiments are performed on DanceTrack, BDD100K, and MOT17.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written with a clear, logical structure (R1). However, some reviewers note presentation issues (R4: axis label overlap in Fig.3/4; R2/R3: presentation scores 2).
- The observation that tracking queries disproportionately dominate label assignment, starving detection queries, is insightful and often overlooked in transformer-based MOT (R1, R2, R4). This motivates the method well (R4).
- COLA is a simple and effective strategy that boosts performance across multiple e2e-MOT frameworks including TrackFormer and MOTR (R1, R2).
- The introduction of shadow sets for one-to-set matching is novel and enhances robustness without requiring additional detectors (R3). The method shows strong performance with higher efficiency (R3).
- Experiments are thorough across several benchmarks (DanceTrack, BDD100K, MOT17), demonstrating robust and consistent improvements over baselines (R1, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Simpler alternative not ablated**: Joint training of detection queries on image detection datasets (treating all objects as newborns) is a straightforward baseline to compare against COLA; an ablation study is needed (R1).
- **Decoder arrangement**: The paper uses the first 5 decoders with all queries and the last separately; an ablation on different arrangements (e.g., first decoder for track queries, last five for all) would clarify optimal configuration (R1).
- **Generalizability to image-rich scenarios**: In many practical settings (large-scale MOT, open-vocabulary MOT), image detection data far exceeds video tracking data, so joint training is common and detection queries are already well-supervised. The paper should discuss these scenarios and whether COLA remains beneficial (R1). Minority viewpoint: This concern is raised only by R1; other reviewers did not mention this.
- **Missing comparisons with recent SOTA**: Many 2024 methods (DiffMOT, MambaTrack, TrackSSM) are not compared; authors should update the literature and compare (R2). R2 also notes that the paper was previously submitted to ICLR2024 and the reviewers’ weaknesses were not addressed in this version (R2).
- **Technical detail on COLA vs. TALA**: The paper lacks a detailed discussion of the differences between COLA and TALA, including exact loss formulation, matching cost computation, and how coopetition affects gradient flow (R3).
- **Detection performance analysis**: Since the motivation is that tracking queries hurt detection, the authors should show whether CO-MOT improves mAP (detection accuracy) compared to the baseline, especially under occlusion or clutter (R3).
- **Sensitivity of hyperparameters λ and Φ** (introduced in Section 2.5): A sensitivity analysis is needed to understand their impact on performance and the trade-off between detection and tracking (R3). Similarly, the number of shadow sets and other COLA parameters require sensitivity analysis (R4).
- **Computational complexity of shadow sets**: Fig.4 only reports FLOPs, not actual training or inference time. Adding more queries intuitively increases cost; the authors should explain why shadow sets do not increase computational overhead and provide real-time analysis (R4).
- **Evaluation on MOT20 and expanded comparisons on BDD100K**: MOT20’s dense scenes would better test detection recall improvements, and comparisons on both MOT20 and BDD100K should include more end-to-end and traditional tracking methods (R3, R4). R1 also asks for evaluation on the TAO dataset for generalizability to large-scale tracking (R1).
