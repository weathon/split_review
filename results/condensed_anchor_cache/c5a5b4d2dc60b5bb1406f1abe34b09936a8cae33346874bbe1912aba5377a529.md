## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PITS, a self-supervised representation learning method for time series that challenges the conventional masked modeling strategy of capturing patch dependencies. Instead, it introduces patch-independent (PI) learning: a simple patch reconstruction task that autoencodes each patch without looking at others, and a patch-wise MLP architecture for independent embedding. Complementary contrastive learning is added to hierarchically capture adjacent time series information. Experiments on forecasting (7 datasets) and classification (5 datasets) show performance improvements over state-of-the-art Transformer-based models while being more parameter- and time-efficient.

### Strengths
1. **Novel perspective**: The paper introduces the concept of patch independence for time series representation learning, challenging the dominant patch-dependency paradigm. Reviewer 3 considers this original and highlights that it is the first model to integrate masked time series modeling (MTM) and contrastive learning (CL).  
   *Contrast*: Reviewer 4 acknowledges the novelty of the concept but questions whether the empirical evidence sufficiently supports the claim that PI is superior to PD.

2. **Thorough experiments**: Extensive evaluation on two tasks (low-level forecasting and high-level classification) with a total of 12 datasets, including transfer learning settings. Comparisons with multiple state-of-the-art methods and detailed ablation studies (e.g., PI vs. PD analysis, component ablations). An interesting analysis of distribution shift is also provided.

3. **Method clarity and simplicity**: The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow. The method is straightforward to implement, using simple patch reconstruction and an MLP architecture.

4. **Efficiency**: The PI design reduces parameter count and training/inference time while maintaining or improving performance, which is highlighted as practical for real-world applications.

5. **Strong performance**: The proposed method achieves superior results compared to existing Transformer-based models in both forecasting and classification tasks, including in transfer learning scenarios.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty concerns**:  
   - The simple autoencoding approach used for patch reconstruction is well-established in representation learning (pre-dating masked modeling). The main contribution is exploring this within time series.  
   - Mixing contrastive learning and masked autoencoders has been explored in other domains (e.g., audio-visual), and a specific related work is missing: Gong et al. "Contrastive audio-visual masked autoencoder" (2022).  
   - Reviewer 4 notes that the complementary masking strategy for CL, claimed as a main contribution, has already been proposed earlier in other domains (e.g., SdAE, Complementary Mask SSL, Deep Feature Selection using Complementary Feature Mask).  
   - Reviewer 4 also argues that the evidence for “PI is superior to PD” is insufficient; comparisons among representative methods do not support the broad claim. Reviewer 3, by contrast, finds the originality strong. This disagreement should be noted.

2. **Clarity and exposition issues**:  
   - Figure 1(a) has inconsistencies (e.g., “MLPmixer” vs. “MLP-Mixer”), lacks detail on how input time series are converted into patches, and does not clearly explain whether the MLP-Mixer operates on patches independently or allows interactions.  
   - Figure 1(b) does not explicitly label the two layers corresponding to the CL task and the PI task, making the implementation hard to follow.  
   - Notation “No(SL)” in Table 1 is unexplained.  
   - Some sentences are ambiguous, e.g., “which is not only more efficient but also performs better” – the referent of “which” is unclear.  
   - Figure 5 (left) is unclear in conveying what the colors represent; Figure 5 (right) is confusing regarding what exactly is changed to create distribution shift in the time series data.

3. **Missing experimental details**:  
   - For the forecasting task (Table 3), the input horizon (look-back window length) is not reported.  
   - It is unclear whether results in Table 3 are averaged over multiple runs (different seeds) or are single runs.  
   - Reviewer 4 asks whether “46” in Table 2 means 46 hours of training time.  
   - Hyperparameter configurations and their ablations are not sufficiently detailed (Reviewer 3).

4. **Insufficient comparative baselines**:  
   - For the forecasting task, only two baselines using self-supervised mask reconstruction are included. More comparisons with other self-supervised time series approaches (e.g., autoencoders, alternative SSL methods) are needed to establish the superiority of the proposed method.  
   - Training and inference efficiency comparisons with supervised learning methods are missing; the contrastive learning and reconstruction losses may increase training time, and such comparisons would be useful.

5. **Qualitative analysis and limitations**:  
   - The explanation for why PI is superior to PD is mostly quantitative; a qualitative analysis would make the argument more convincing.  
   - The paper does not discuss limitations of the proposed method or specific scenarios where it might perform suboptimally (e.g., highly non-stationary or very long-horizon time series).
