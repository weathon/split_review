## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to improve the adversarial robustness of Vision Transformers (ViTs) by analyzing the nullspace of the model, focusing on the patch embedding layer. It identifies an approximate nullspace through optimization and uses noise sampled from this nullspace as data augmentation during fine-tuning. The method is validated on various datasets under white-box and black-box attacks and distribution shifts. The concept is novel and interesting, but the theoretical grounding and experimental evaluation have significant weaknesses. Reviewer 3 (score 8) is notably more positive, while Reviewer 4 (score 3) is sharply critical; Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 5) are moderately positive but with important concerns. Confidence varies: Reviewers 1,2 are fairly confident (4), Reviewer 3 less confident (2), Reviewer 4 fairly confident (3).

### Strengths
- The idea of using nullspace for ViT robustness is novel and interesting (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is clearly organized and well written (R1, R2); Reviewer 4 disagrees on overall clarity (see Weaknesses).
- The derivation of the existence of an encoder-level nullspace for the patch embedding layer is sound and provides a theoretical foundation (R1, R2). R3 calls the methodology comprehensive.
- The paper highlights practical applications such as model patenting and image watermarking (R3). *Note: other reviewers did not comment on these applications.*
- The proposed method achieves good performance under distribution shifts (R2). R1 and R3 also note improvements in robustness.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear mechanism**: It is not explained why nullspace noise augmentation (i.e., adding vectors that do not change the output) improves adversarial robustness, which is opposite to adversarial training that uses worst-case perturbations (R1, R4). R4 further notes that maximizing the norm of the perturbation without constraint (Equation 6) is not lower bounded, likely requiring early stopping; the meaning of “close to a non-zero vector” is vague.
- **Insufficient adversarial evaluation**: Only FGSM and DamageNet are used; stronger white‑box attacks like PGD, Auto‑Attack, and CW are missing (R1, R2). For black‑box attacks, transferability from other models should be tested using methods like MIM series (R1). Patch‑level attacks specific to ViTs should also be considered (R2).
- **Insufficient baselines**: The paper lacks comparisons with important related works on ViT robustness, e.g., Li & Xu (CVPR 2023), Zhou et al. (ICML 2022), Paul & Chen (AAAI 2022), Wu et al. (ECCV 2022), Chefer et al. (NeurIPS 2022) (R2).
- **Adversarial improvement not promising**: Although distribution‑shift performance improves, the gain under adversarial attacks is not strong (R2). R1 also calls the experimental evaluation weak overall.
- **Generality to CNNs**: The method should be tested on CNNs since it is derived for ViTs but seems architecture‑agnostic (R1, R2).
- **Unfinished / confusing writing**: Section 3 is chaotic and unfinished, with unrelated sentences and equations (Equations 7–10 are unclear) (R4). Section 4.4 on the “$\epsilon$ region” is hard to follow; the claim that “noise always entered the $\epsilon$ region” is not supported by Figure 3 (R4). The overall narrative is difficult to follow despite locally well‑written sentences (R4). Some notations (e.g., $f(u)[0]$) mix mathematical and programming conventions (R4).
- **Inappropriate tone**: Phrases like “we offer a fresh narrative”, “provocative question”, “our work marks an important stride” sound overconfident or unscientific (R4). Reviewer 4 suggests a more neutral tone.
- **Mathematical contributions are minor** and the paper contains approximations (e.g., “closeness” instead of “closedness”) (R4).
- **Nullspace vs. invariance**: The paper focuses on invariance properties of the full ViT, rather than the nullspace of the patch embedding layer. The linear‑algebra nullspace concept does not directly apply to a nonlinear model; the actual object of study is approximate invariance (R4). The limitations due to non‑linearity should be more explicitly addressed (R3).
- **Practical applicability unclear**: Can the authors provide concrete real‑world examples and use cases for nullspace noise? Also, the resulting perturbed images ($u+\tilde{v}$) are not natural images—how does this translate to practical robustness? (R4, also implied by R3).
- **Minor** (from R4): “0 is always a solution” but the paper claims nullspace may not exist, which is incorrect; the remark after Proposition 1 needs better justification; the NeurIPS watermark should be changed to ICLR watermark (if applicable).
