## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a coarse-grained (CG) modeling approach that combines a graph neural network (GNN) potential with a high-frequency potential energy surface (PES) landscape function. The GNN captures structure via differentiable training to reproduce the target radial distribution function (RDF); the high-frequency term emulates the ruggedness of the all-atom (AA) energy landscape to correct dynamics (e.g., spurious fast diffusion). The method is demonstrated on simple bulk systems: SPC/E water, argon, and methane. The authors report accurate reproduction of both RDF and mean-square displacement (MSD) compared to AA simulations.

### Strengths
- **Originality (disagreement: reviewer 4 only):** The PES modulation approach and its theoretical orthogonality with RDF are considered novel. Other reviewers found the overall approach (combining GNN with a high-frequency term) insufficiently novel and the GNN architecture conventional.
- **Quality (reviewer 4):** The paper is technically sound with detailed derivations; results clearly demonstrate accurate structure and dynamics; well-conceived and implemented.
- **Clarity (reviewer 4):** The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a logical flow, supporting diagrams, and equations. (Other reviewers 2 and 3 noted poor writing quality, unclear contributions, and convoluted explanations.)
- **Significance (reviewer 4):** Potential impact for simulating complex biomolecular and material systems, and for addressing key challenges in structure and dynamics accuracy.
- **Inductive bias (reviewer 1):** The approach combines strong inductive biases about interatomic interactions, particularly the high-frequency term.
- **Empirical performance (reviewer 1; disagreement with reviewers 2 and 3):** Good performance on the selected systems (water, argon, methane).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and architecture:** Multiple reviewers (2, 3) find the overall approach insufficiently novel; the GNN components (graph construction, node embedding, message passing) are conventional. Reviewer 4 considers the PES modulation novel, creating a contrast in assessment.
- **Limited experiments and validation:**
  - Only three simple systems tested (SPC/E water, argon, methane), which are molecular liquids with a single heavy atom and CG hydrogens. More complex systems are needed to demonstrate utility and scalability.
  - Generalization not tested: the potential is trained and tested under the same conditions (e.g., same temperature, system size). No results for different temperatures or system sizes.
  - Only RDF and MSD are validated; other important metrics like angular distribution functions (ADF) are not considered.
  - The baseline “AAMD” is not defined or referenced; no other baseline methods (e.g., DeePCG, see missing reference) or related works addressing the same problem are compared.
  - Reviewer 2 considers the experimental results insufficient to support the claims; reviewer 1 and 3 also note the lack of quantitative/qualitative analysis beyond Figure 5.
- **Writing and presentation:**
  - Major flaws (reviewer 2): unclear, convoluted sentences; introduction needs restructuring to separate background, novelty, and contributions; contributions are not clearly listed.
  - Missing references: DeePCG not cited; related recent work in CG modeling and ML potentials is insufficiently discussed, making it hard to contextualize novelty.
  - Reviewer 1 notes a missing reference on page 2 for DeePCG; reviewer 4 points out mixed use of w and B in appendix and latex formatting issues (italic vs. roman in equations).
- **Mathematical rigor and justification:**
  - The orthogonality derivation in the appendix is flawed: the authors incorrectly discard a non-zero term (2nd term in the 3rd equation). The correct asymptotic argument (expansion to g′′, then ∫g′′(r) sin(wr)/w² dr ~ O(1/w²)) gives the same conclusion but the current reasoning is not rigorous. The last equation of the appendix also contains a small error.
  - The choice of U_HF form (sin(r)/r²) is not justified; why not other functions or powers? And since the term diverges at r→0, why is an LJ term still needed?
  - Equation 6 is confusing: subnet2 should take edge input, and ρ_G is not explained.
- **Methodological questions that overlap with criticisms:**
  - Why is adding a high-frequency potential term advantageous compared to simply tuning the Langevin thermostat friction coefficient to modulate diffusivity?
  - In Figure 1, the training of GNN and HF term appears parallel but there is an order; the workflow should be clarified.
  - Figure 2 contains redundant graphs (circles and arrows) given Figure 3A.
  - U_AA in Eq. 11 actually means the CG potential + HF potential, not the true AA potential; notation should be changed or clarified.
  - The orthogonality should be noted as approximate/asymptotic, not exact.
- **Transferability:** It remains unclear how the PES amplitude tuned for one system or state point translates to other molecular species or conditions.
