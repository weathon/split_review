## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes **DAB (Discrete Auto-regressive Biasing)**, a controlled decoding algorithm for large language models that operates entirely in discrete token space. It addresses the deficiency of prior energy-based methods (e.g., BOLT, LM-Steer) that sample in a continuous relaxation and struggle to balance fluency with constraint satisfaction. DAB defines a joint distribution over the generated sequence **Y** and an auxiliary bias sequence **B**, and alternates between biased auto-regressive generation and gradient-based discrete MCMC (using the Discrete Langevin Proposal technique from Zhang et al. 2022) to sample **B**. The bias then steers the output via a penalty based on embedding distance. Experiments on sentiment control, language detoxification, and keyword-guided generation show DAB outperforms baselines on constraint satisfaction while maintaining or improving fluency, with lower computational cost than other energy-based methods.

### Strengths
1. **Identifies a key limitation of previous work** – the continuous-space sampling trade-off between fluency and control – and presents a method that balances both effectively, supported by benchmark results (Reviewers 1,2).
2. **Innovative application and adaptation** of the DLP technique (Zhang et al. 2022) to the general problem of controlled text generation, and creative use of an auxiliary bias sequence with Gibbs-like alternation (Reviewer 2).
3. **Empirical advantages**: DAB demonstrates better constraint satisfaction and comparable or better fluency than baselines (BOLT, LM-Steer) on multiple tasks, and is computationally more efficient than other energy-based decoding methods (Reviewers 1,2,4).
4. **Discrete-space operation** leads to improved stability, robustness, and lower cost (Reviewer 1,4). The joint distribution formulation and Langevin-within-Gibbs sampler are principled (Reviewer 4).
5. **Good summary of prior work** on controlled text generation (Reviewer 3).
6. **Ablations** on both hard and soft constraints are performed (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Omission of significant related work** on *discrete* sampling from energy-based models for controlled generation, e.g., Khalifa et al. (ICLR 2021), Eikema et al. (TMLR 2022), Kruszewski et al. (ACL 2023). This line is directly relevant to the discrete-sampling focus of the paper (Reviewer 2, Weakness A).
2. **Lack of clarity and missing discussion around key modeling decisions**:
   - Equation (5): unclear whether conditioning **X** on the bias sequence **B** means concatenation; the rationale for not directly defining the EBM \(P(Y|X)\propto P^{LM}(Y|X)\exp(f(Y))\) and applying DLP to it is not addressed (Reviewer 2, Weakness B.1).
   - The method section is poorly written: Equation (6) appears circular; the unnumbered equation after it probably should be \(P(B|Y,X)\) (Reviewer 3).
   - The term \((1-b_{ij})\) in Equation (7) and the use of argmax in Equation (10) (which is supposed to be a sampling distribution) are unexplained (Reviewer 3).
   - Equation (7) (the DLP update) is not described in a self-contained way; the gradient computation is obscure (Reviewer 2, Weakness B.2).
   - Initializing **B** = **Y** seems counterintuitive; initializing **B** = 0 would be more natural (Reviewer 3).
3. **Implicit limitations not discussed**:
   - The response length **n** must be specified in advance, a practical constraint (Reviewer 2, Weakness B.2).
   - Step 6 of Algorithm 1 (which corresponds to the bias update) is **deterministic**, detracting from standard Gibbs-sampling practice (Reviewer 2, Weakness B.2).
4. **Benchmark saturation and generalizability concerns**: The tested benchmarks (sentiment, toxicity, lexical control) are mostly saturated and simple prompting can solve them; the paper does not evaluate harder problems such as model jailbreaking, where MCMC-style control would be more relevant (Reviewer 3).
5. **Mixed results on sentiment control**: DAB shows *decreased fluency* compared to BOLT in some sentiment experiments (Reviewer 4; also mentioned by Reviewer 2 as a general claim, but Reviewer 2 reports advantages overall).
6. **Computational cost still high**: The method is faster than other energy-based approaches but remains slow due to the autoregressive loop inside the step iteration (Algorithm 1, lines 3‑7) (Reviewer 4).
7. **Unclear evaluation choices and open questions**:
   - For keyword-guided generation, the paper suggests meaning similarity is a better metric than actual keyword presence; Reviewer 2 disagrees, arguing that presence is more important and that the value of the control function \(f(Y)\) should be reported directly.
   - Reviewer 2 also questions the notation \(P(Y|B)\) in lines 238‑243, which appears to omit conditioning on input **X**.
   - Reviewer 4 asks why L2 distance is used in Equation (8) for the bias penalty instead of the inner product (which is typical in LLM logit computation), and whether other distance metrics were tried.
