## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Multi-Label Cluster Discrimination (MLCD) to enhance visual representation learning in CLIP-style models. The method first clusters the LAION-400M dataset into one million centers using off-the-shelf CLIP features, then assigns the \(l\) closest centers as auxiliary pseudo-labels per image. A multi-label classification loss (Eq. 6) that decouples positive and negative class terms is introduced to train the model. Experiments across multiple scales and downstream tasks (linear probe, zero-shot classification, retrieval) show improvements on several benchmarks. The approach uses 80 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

### Strengths
- Extends CLIP’s instance discrimination with a multi-label loss to better capture the semantic structure in images; the motivation that natural images often contain multiple objects/attributes is well‑taken.
- The loss design (Eq. 6) elegantly separates positive and negative class losses, which is claimed to improve parallelism and scalability during distributed training.
- The analysis and decomposition of the triplet/margin loss is intuitive and may be broadly applicable.
- The experiments are extensive with thorough ablations (e.g., number of clusters, positive centers \(l\), sample ratio), showing notable gains on certain datasets (e.g., ImageNet linear probe 84.6 in Table 5).
- The work’s emphasis on multi‑label discrimination is a meaningful direction for contrastive clustering‑based pre‑training.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty / technical depth**: The method largely combines existing techniques (offline clustering, multi‑label classification) with moderate overall improvement (Tables 1, 2). The novelty does not justify the huge compute cost (80 NVIDIA A100 GPUs), especially given the already high performance of current vision‑language models.
2. **Unclear effectiveness of the key loss modification**: Eq. 5/ 6 introduces new terms into the multi‑label loss, but the ablation study does not clearly verify their contribution. How would performance change if \(L'_{\text{MLCD}}\) (Eq. 6) were replaced with a standard multi‑label classification loss \(L_{\text{MLC}}\)? The effect of the proposed changes remains unclear.
3. **Doubt about what multi‑label assignment actually teaches**: The top‑\(l\) closest clusters may not reveal the true objects/attributes in an image. Samples of the same cluster are likely to share nearest clusters (cluster‑to‑cluster co‑occurrence) rather than reflecting sample‑to‑cluster assignments. A verification on data with known non‑overlapping class structure (e.g., ImageNet) is missing.
4. **Missing experimental details / reproducibility**:
   - Number of classes \(k\) and number of positive centers \(l\) used for LAION‑400M pre‑training are not explicitly stated.
   - Distributed training techniques (e.g., how Eq. 6 enables minimal communication overhead) are claimed but not explained or quantitatively supported.
   - The clustering step’s impact on training time is not evaluated.
   - In Table 3, the best result includes both the proposed method and FLIP (89.1%) but only the proposed method is highlighted.
   - The reported CLIP baseline in Tables 1‑2 differs from the original CLIP paper, potentially affecting the validation of improvements.
5. **Clarity issues in text and figures**:
   - Fig. 1 is referenced but lacks discussion/explanation; Fig. 2 is the first reference but Fig. 1 appears earlier.
   - In the figure pointing to “contrastive loss”, the meaning of blue and green cells in the grids is not defined.
   - Eq. 1 uses inner product without normalization (presumably cosine similarity); this may be a mistake.
   - Eq. 1 index starts from 0, Eq. 2 from 1 – is this deliberate?
   - In Eq. 3, the exponential is denoted both as \(\exp\) and \(e\), causing confusion when feature embeddings are also \(e_i\).
   - Section 4.6 charts lack y‑axis labels.
   - General mathematical notation requires more explanation to reduce reading barriers.
6. **Ablation inconsistency**: The sample‑ratio ablation reports best linear probe 75.2 at 0.1, while the final model (Table 5) achieves 84.6 – the source of this mismatch is not clarified.
7. **Efficiency claims unsupported**: The paper asserts efficient parallel computation and scalability (Section 3.2) without quantitative runtime data. Does the clustering step significantly increase training time?

*Note*: While all reviewers rated 5, Reviewer 2 expressed stronger doubt about the fundamental multi‑label assumption; this concern is preserved above in point 3.
