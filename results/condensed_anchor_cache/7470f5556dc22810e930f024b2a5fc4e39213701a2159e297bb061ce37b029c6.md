- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 1, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes to replace the standard inner-product decoder in graph autoencoders (GAEs/GVAEs) with a Generalized Random Dot Product Graph (GRDPG) decoder in order to handle adjacency matrices with negative eigenvalues. The method multiplies node embedding vectors by a diagonal matrix with entries ±1, thereby allowing the reconstructed adjacency to have negative eigenvalues. Experiments on link prediction and node clustering are reported.

### Strengths
- The identification of the limitation of standard dot-product decoders (they require the reconstructed adjacency to be positive semi-definite) is interesting and practically relevant. [Reviewer 4] Some reviewers found the intuition behind negative eigenvalues well explained. [Reviewer 4]
- The proposal to use the GRDPG as a decoder is a natural extension of prior work on random dot product graphs. [Reviewer 1] The method is flexible and can be applied to any dot-product-based reconstruction function. [Reviewer 2]
- The introduction is well-written. [Reviewer 1]
- The illustration of the method is clear and straightforward. [Reviewer 3]
- Experiments show some improvement over the vanilla GAE/GVAE baselines. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4]

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical depth.** The core idea (Equation 3) is already present in the GRDPG literature and in hyperbolic GNNs that use a diagonal matrix with ±1 (e.g., the Lorentz model). The application to graph decoding, while potentially useful, does not represent a significant theoretical advancement. [Reviewer 3] The paper lacks an in-depth analysis of how the proposed matrix multiplication affects the spectral properties of the generated adjacency matrix beyond simply allowing negative eigenvalues; there is no discussion of the distribution, magnitude, or impact of these eigenvalues on downstream tasks. [Reviewer 4] The paper also does not provide any theoretical justification or empirical evidence linking negative eigenvalues to specific graph substructures or statistics. [Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4] The reliance on a manually chosen negation matrix (the number of negative signs) is a significant limitation; the paper offers no guidance on how to select it for different graph structures or datasets, and only mentions this as future work in the conclusion. [Reviewer 4] Two reviewers noted that the paper insists on the term “non-bipartite graphs” unnecessarily (bipartite graphs have spectra symmetric around zero, making the negative-eigenvalue discussion trivial). [Reviewer 1]
- **Missing experimental rigor and reproducibility.** Standard deviations, confidence intervals, and random seeds are absent from all reported results. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4] The experiments focus only on reconstruction tasks (link prediction, node clustering) and do not include true generation tasks, which would be needed to validate the decoder’s generative capabilities. [Reviewer 3] The results are not convincing, especially on the “structure perception” link prediction task. [Reviewer 2] The paper compares only against the backbone GAE/GVAE and does not compare with recent advanced GNN methods that significantly outperform GAE on various tasks. [Reviewer 4] It is unclear whether the generated adjacency matrix actually contains negative eigenvalues, and whether the original training matrix also had negative eigenvalues. [Reviewer 4] There is no empirical or theoretical quantification of how often negative eigenvalues occur in real graphs (only toy examples in the appendix). [Reviewer 4]
- **Critical methodological details missing.** The GRDPG requires knowing the number of positive and negative eigenvalues (i.e., the dimensions of the two parts of the diagonal matrix). The paper does not explain how this is set: is it fixed per dataset, tuned per dataset, or something else? [Reviewer 1] The description of the GAE loss function in the preliminary section uses an expectation “with respect to some \(q\)” that is never defined. [Reviewer 1] Several questions from reviewers overlap with these missing details: 
  - Which \(I_{p,q}\) works best for different graph structures? [Reviewer 2]
  - How does the proposed method compare with hyperbolic GNNs (Lorentz model), which also use a diagonal matrix with ±1? [Reviewer 3]
  - Could one use the graph Laplacian \(L = D - A\) instead of the adjacency matrix to avoid the positive semi-definite issue? [Reviewer 3]
  - Does the manually set negation matrix produce a generated matrix that indeed has negative eigenvalues, and does the original graph also have such eigenvalues? [Reviewer 4]
- **Presentation quality.** The paper is poorly structured: it spends four pages describing negative eigenvalues without explaining the proposed architecture, then jumps from Section 3 (preliminaries) to Section 4 (experiments). [Reviewer 1] Repetition of the same meaning occurs in multiple places (e.g., first two paragraphs of page 2, fourth paragraph of page 3). [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2] Typos are frequent: for example, the third equation has a missing closing bracket (\(p(Z|X,A \$\)), variable names are inconsistent (e.g., \(Z=z_i^Tz_j\)), and equations lack proper punctuation and indexing. [Reviewer 2] The use of bold/unbold for vectors is inconsistent, and some subscripts are missing. [Reviewer 4] Overall, the quality is far below the requirements of a top conference. [Reviewer 2]
- **Task appropriateness.** It is unclear why node clustering is an appropriate experiment to verify a decoder whose main contribution is about negative eigenvalues; one reviewer suggested that graph structure perception tasks would be more relevant. [Reviewer 2]
