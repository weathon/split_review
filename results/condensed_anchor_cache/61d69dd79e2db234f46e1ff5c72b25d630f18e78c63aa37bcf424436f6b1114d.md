## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Hierarchy-Aided Sparse Attention (HASA) to accelerate the pre-filling phase of LLMs with long-context inputs. HASA applies diagonal block sparse attention to each chunk for local attention, and adds a specialized transformer branch that extracts global embeddings via average pooling of each chunk. These global embeddings attend to previous global embeddings using a shift mask, enabling cross-chunk interaction. HASA fine-tunes LLMs with this architecture. Experiments on language modeling, few-shot, and long-context benchmarks (including LongBench) show that HASA reduces latency while maintaining or improving performance compared to baselines.

### Strengths
1. The key idea of using a special embedding (average pooling per chunk) and a dedicated transformer branch to capture inter-chunk dependency is interesting and novel in the context of efficient pre-filling. [R1, R2, R3]
2. HASA reduces attention-related FLOPs by over 90% and achieves lower end-to-end latency compared to full attention and baseline methods, addressing the time-consuming pre-filling phase. [R1, R2, R3]
3. Experiments are thoroughly conducted across multiple benchmarks (LM, few-shot, long-context tasks). Table 4 shows HASA achieves superior performance on specific LongBench tasks such as SQA and MQA. [R2, R3]
4. The paper is well-written with clear analysis and a step-by-step presentation of the improvements from diagonal block sparse attention to HASA. [R3]

### Weaknesses
1. The additional specialized transformer branch introduces significant computational overhead and increases GPU memory consumption. One reviewer notes that the branch roughly doubles the time to pass transformer layers, and another notes that ablation with the original diagonal block sparse attention and HASA without this branch is missing to quantify its efficiency impact. [R1, R2, R3]
2. Novelty is limited; the method is a combination of existing techniques (block sparse attention, hierarchical/global attention) without a fundamentally new component. [R1]
3. The performance comparisons with prior methods are not fully convincing. Specifically:
   - YaRN-7B was trained on PG19, while HASA was trained on SlimPajama (stage 1) and a mixed instruction tuning dataset. A fairer comparison would require reimplementing YaRN with the same training data. [R2]
   - A strong baseline is sliding window attention plus some global attention at the end (to allow information flow between overlapped blocks). The paper does not compare efficiency and performance against this baseline. [R3]
4. The approach does not incorporate sink attention (first token), which prior work shows captures a large portion of attention scores in LLMs. Dropping the sink token may require additional training cost and network design to compensate. [R3]
5. Compared to training-free methods for efficient long-context inference (e.g., Streaming LLM, MInference), HASA requires fine-tuning, limiting its adaptability to various LLMs and scenarios. [R2]
6. Figure 2 lacks color descriptions, hindering presentation clarity. [R1]

Note: All three reviewers gave a score of 5, but Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 expressed significant concerns about novelty (R1) and comparison fairness (R2), while Reviewer 3 rated the paper less critically on those aspects and praised its clarity and technical soundness.
