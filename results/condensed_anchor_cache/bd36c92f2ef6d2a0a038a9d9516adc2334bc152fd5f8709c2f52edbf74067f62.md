## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LeBD and CA-LeBD, run-time backdoor defenses for YOLOv5 object detection in both digital and physical worlds. LeBD uses LayerCAM to locate backdoor triggers and issue risk warnings; CA-LeBD enhances this with counterfactual attribution. The defense works by identifying crucial regions via attribution, occluding them, and comparing model outputs to detect triggers. Evaluations are performed on COCO (digital) and real-world video streams (physical), focusing on patch-based triggers.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses an important and under-explored problem: run-time backdoor defense for object detection, with a focus on physical-world applicability (R1, R3, R4).
- Writing is clear and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- The idea of using LayerCAM (replacing GradCAM) for trigger localization is inspiring and shows practical improvements over NEO in efficiency and relaxing blocker size constraints (R1, R4).
- Incorporating counterfactual attribution to enhance LayerCAM is a novel and intriguing approach (R4; note: R2 argues the core concept of counterfactual attribution for backdoor detection is not new, having been exploited by Februus (Doan et al., 2020) and others, but the specific application to object detection and combination with LayerCAM is considered novel by R4).
- Consideration of real-world scenarios and physical attacks is commendable (R3).
- Extends an existing technique to a new domain (object detection) (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited scope and generalizability:** The method is specialized for YOLOv5 and patch-based triggers (e.g., BadNets-like patterns). No experiments on other object detection architectures (e.g., YOLOv7, Faster R-CNN, vision transformers) or other trigger types (rotational, semantic, augmentation-based triggers). Reviewer 4 notes that rotation-based backdoors have demonstrated real-world applicability in OD (R3, R4). Effectiveness on scattered triggers or triggers not at the center of the bounding box is not evaluated (R3). Whether the approach can extend to vision transformer architectures is unclear (R4 question → missing experiment).
- **Limited technical novelty and comparison with prior work:** The core concept of using counterfactual attribution to identify crucial regions and occlude them for backdoor detection is already present in Februus (Doan et al., 2020) and other unmentioned works. The application to object detection is a straightforward extension that lacks in-depth analysis of why existing methods (e.g., GradCAM) specifically fail for YOLO. The paper does not compare with other input-level backdoor detection methods, making it hard to assess effectiveness (R2). The replacement of GradCAM with LayerCAM is treated as a simple swap without rigorous justification of why LayerCAM is superior to other CAM variants (R3).
- **False positive concerns and hyperparameter issues:** Occluding ground-truth features (e.g., a face) could cause label flips, leading to high false positive rates. The paper does not provide a convincing analysis of false positives under various object types and occlusion scenarios. Hyperparameter tuning for the occlusion ratio lacks justification and analysis of its effect on performance (R2).
- **Performance gaps and missing digital-world experiments:** The performance gap between digital and physical worlds for LeBD/CA-LeBD is much larger than that of baselines (e.g., Table 1), suggesting sensitivity to trigger strength with weak triggers yielding larger improvements. Digital-world experiments only use the same attack as in the physical world, ignoring many other attack types applicable in digital settings (R1).
- **Ambiguous definition of digital vs. physical world:** The distinction is unclear; the assumption that other attacks are hard to apply in the physical world (e.g., that pixel-level triggers can be printed and placed) needs a more explicit definition and justification (R1).
- **Runtime overhead of CA-LeBD:** While claimed to be faster than NEO, runtime could be several times higher if applied to all 80 COCO classes. No discussion on how to select the number of classes in practice or its influence on defense performance (R1).
- **Lack of adaptive attack analysis:** No discussion of resistance if attackers know the defense—could they design triggers that bypass it easily? (R3)
- **Typo:** "LayrCAM" on page 5 (R4).

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Reviewer 1 is highly positive (rating 6) and finds the idea inspiring, while Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 rate the paper low (1, 3, 3) and raise substantial concerns about novelty, limited scope, and missing evaluations.
