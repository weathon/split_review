## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents LeFusion, a lesion-focused diffusion model for generating lesion-containing image-segmentation pairs from lesion-free medical images. It uses forward-diffused backgrounds combined with reverse-diffused foregrounds, a lesion-focused diffusion loss, and two modality-specific strategies—histogram-based texture control (LeFusion-H) for multi-peak lesions in CT and multi-channel decomposition (LeFusion-J) for multi-class lesions in MRI. It also introduces DiffMask, a diffusion model for generating lesion masks from basic spatial constraints. Evaluated on 3D cardiac MRI and lung nodule CT datasets, LeFusion improves downstream segmentation performance (nnUNet, SwinUNETR) over baseline methods including Cond-Diffusion and RePaint.

### Strengths
- The problem is well-motivated: real-world clinical data suffers from scarcity and long-tail imbalances, and lesion synthesis is a promising mitigation tool (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Technical novelty is decent overall: the combination of forward-diffused backgrounds, lesion-focused loss, histogram conditioning, and multi-channel decomposition yields a strongly-performing method (Reviewers 2,3,4). Reviewer 1 judges novelty as limited, considering the changes as only input variations (minority view).
- Results are strong: clear improvements over baseline methods (Cond-Diffusion, RePaint) on both datasets for image quality and downstream segmentation metrics (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Improvements are consistent across two segmentation models and multiple training settings (Reviewer 2,3).
- Evaluation is thorough and fair, including extensive ablation studies, comparison to multiple recent baselines (within last two years, Reviewer 4), and use of challenging 3D datasets (cardiac MRI, lung CT) (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- The choice of a 3D method is appropriate for CT/MRI to avoid slice-wise discontinuities (Reviewer 3).
- The method addresses modality-specific challenges: histogram conditioning solves texture mode collapse for multi-peak lesions (evident in Figure 3), and multi-channel decomposition targets multi-class lesions in MRI (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- The paper is considered well-written and well-presented by Reviewers 2,3,4; Reviewer 1 finds the writing lacking organization and difficult to follow (minority view).
- Figures and tables are insightful and adequately formatted (Reviewers 2,3).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty concerns: Reviewer 1 argues the method does not significantly modify the underlying conditional diffusion process, only introducing variations in the input. Reviewer 3 notes that the lesion-focused loss may not be entirely novel, citing prior works [2,3] that employ similar approaches; acknowledgment of these would strengthen contextualization.
- Presentation clarity issues (multiple reviewers):
  - Figure 2 is unclear, especially the lesion-focused loss component; should be refined to highlight unique mechanisms (Reviewer 1).
  - The RePaint method description is ambiguous—whether it is Cond-Diffusion using the RePaint inference scheme should be explicitly stated (Reviewer 3).
  - DiffMask’s boundary mask definition is unclear: is it the bounding box volume crop? The statement “removes areas outside the boundary at each diffusion step” is not well explained. Also, it is only implicit that DiffMask takes the CT/MRI volume crop as input alongside the control sphere; Section 3.3 needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
  - The reverse diffusion sampling process is not clearly defined; it appears to rely solely on the transformation in Equation (1) without detailing the sampling process or providing theoretical justification for omitting it (Reviewer 4).
  - For the segmentation experiments using healthy scans (N’), details are missing on how conditioning histograms are selected when using LeFusion-H variants with generated masks (Reviewer 3).
  - Training/checkpoint selection details for RePaint, Cond-Diffusion, and Cond-Diffusion (L) are not provided (Reviewer 3).
  - The meaning of red/blue shading in Tables 1, 2 (significantly adverse/positive effects) is not explained in the table captions or text (Reviewer 2).
  - Terminology inconsistency: “LeFusion” in Table 2 vs. “LeFusion-S” in Figure 5 and Appendix for the baseline modeling of two tissue classes separately (Reviewer 3).
  - Dataset preprocessing details (e.g., volume resolution) and the choice of crop size are insufficiently motivated; effect of crop size on realism is not studied (Reviewer 3).
- Missing analysis and evaluations:
  - Analysis of lesion-background boundary continuity and smoothness, and its impact on downstream tasks, is missing (Reviewer 4).
  - No standard deviations or cross-validation are reported for segmentation results in Tables 1, 2; “significance” is vague—results appear based on a single fold (Reviewer 3).
  - Unpaired perceptual metrics like FID, KID, SWD are not evaluated; reviewers suggest explaining why they were omitted and considering them for future work (Reviewer 2). Image quality quantitative results (Table A2) are in the appendix and could be moved to the main text if space permits (Reviewer 2).
  - No radiologist validation of the generated lesions to safeguard against potential biases (Reviewer 3).
  - No comparison to GAN-based methods (e.g., [4,5]) for the lesion synthesis task, which would be informative (Reviewer 3).
- Limited impact/scope: the task is clinically important but niche within medical image analysis, and requires lesion annotations which are expensive to acquire; this limits the work’s impact at a general ML venue like ICLR compared to a specialized venue (Reviewer 2).
- Multi-channel decomposition benefits are small: comparing “-J” vs. no “-J” in Table 2 shows only minor improvements. Reviewers request analysis or discussion on why this is the case (Reviewers 2,3). Additionally, it is unclear whether multi-class synthesis was already a challenge for prior methods—quantitative evidence supporting this claim would be helpful (Reviewer 2).
- For the multiclass lesion case, scalability of performance with adding more classes was not studied (Reviewer 2, minor question).
