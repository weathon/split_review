## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FLAIR, a federated learning method that uses Class Variational Autoencoders (CVAE) for feature augmentation and Reptile meta-training to address non-IID data, communication overhead, and privacy. The server and clients collaboratively train a CVAE, and only CVAE parameters (not model gradients) are shared. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet show accuracy improvements over baselines, especially in extreme non-IID settings. Reviewers find the approach promising but raise concerns about unsubstantiated claims (communication and privacy), missing comparisons with prior VAE-based FL methods, insufficient ablations, unclear experimental details, theoretical gaps, and presentation issues. One reviewer considers the privacy experiments a strength; others find the privacy claims unsubstantiated.

### Strengths
- Shows considerable improvements over prior work in extreme non-IID scenarios (Reviewer 1).
- Combines Reptile meta-learning and CVAE to address heterogeneity (Reviewer 1).
- Identifies communication overhead in standard FL and proposes an alternative to sharing model gradients (Reviewer 2).
- Provides experiments against five baselines across multiple datasets and releases code for reproducibility (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of data-free black-box knowledge transfer across heterogeneous clients is interesting and promising (Reviewer 3).
- Well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 3).
- Proposes an alternative to transmitting local model parameters directly, aiming to enhance communication efficiency and privacy (Reviewer 4).
- Conducts experiments to quantify privacy, providing numerical evidence of privacy guarantees (Reviewer 4). *[Note: Reviewer 2 considers these privacy claims unsubstantiated – see Weaknesses.]*

### Weaknesses
- **Unsubstantiated central claims (communication & privacy):**
  - No evidence that CVAE communication complexity O(E) is lower than baselines’ O(St) (i.e., E << St not shown) (Reviewer 2).
  - No comparison with baselines that also avoid sharing model gradients; if FLAIR is first, this should be highlighted; if not, other such baselines should be included (Reviewer 2).
  - Privacy claims are not substantiated: no protection demonstrated against Membership Inference Attacks (MIA); no details on how privacy metrics are evaluated; terminology like “privacy attacks” used loosely (Reviewer 2). *[Disagreement: Reviewer 4 views the privacy quantification as a strength, but Reviewer 2 finds it unsubstantiated.]*
  - Multiple performance claims lack evidence: “performance gap widening as dataset complexity increases” (line 429) and “noticeable jumps” (line 445) (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations:**
  - No comparison with prior work using VAE in FL, especially Heinbaugh et al. 2023 (Data-free one-shot FL with VAE) (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - No ablation on hyperparameters in the combined loss function (λ_f, λ_~f, λ_c, λ) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
  - No ablation isolating the role of CVAE (e.g., replacing with vanilla VAE, adding individual losses one by one) (Reviewer 4).
  - No ablation on the effect of Reptile meta-learning (is there a significant drop without it?) (Reviewers 1, 4).
  - No ablation on number of clients, client participation rate, or local epochs (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental details insufficient or unclear:**
  - Number of clients K not reported (Reviewer 3).
  - Heterogeneity defined by β values is not clearly explained; need metrics showing label distribution across clients (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - Feature skew condition not well explained and lacks citations (Reviewer 2).
  - “Increasing noise levels” experiment unclear – is it DP-SGD? (Reviewer 2).
  - Only final accuracy reported; learning curves and communication rounds needed to demonstrate training dynamics (Reviewer 3).
  - It is unclear if experiments are repeated over multiple trials; means and standard deviations should be reported (Reviewer 1).
  - In Tables 2 and 6, FLAIR is highlighted even when baselines outperform it, making the tables hard to read (Reviewer 1).
  - Table 3 reports “No” for FLAIR in MIA, Gradient Leak, and Info Theoretic metrics, yet the significance of this is unclear – why report if FLAIR cannot obtain these estimates? (Reviewer 3).
  - Datasets: line 356 incorrectly says “three widely-used datasets” instead of four (Reviewer 4).
  - Hardware environment (OS, CPU, GPU) not specified (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Figure 1 is very hard to read; needs a legend explaining chronology, arrow styles, and colors (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - Vague and confusing expressions reduce readability (Reviewer 3).
  - Detail errors: parameter θ in Eq. (2) not specified; inconsistency between θ_{k,t} and θ_{t,k} (Reviewer 3). Also, “Class VAE” vs “Conditional VAE” abbreviation ambiguity (Reviewer 4).
  - Organization: CVAE and Reptile should be presented as preliminaries before Section 2.1/2.2 (Reviewer 1).
  - Theorems section lacks equations, making theoretical contributions hard to evaluate (Reviewer 1).
  - Missing references and grammatical errors (e.g., line 169 wording error) (Reviewer 2).
  - Appendix includes proofs 4,5,6 but their relation to main theorems is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical concerns:**
  - Theorem 1 convergence guarantee: how is convergence achieved despite unbounded client drift? This must be highlighted if true (Reviewer 2).
  - Theorem 2 generalization bound: surprising that it has no constraints on hyperparameters in the combined loss function (Reviewer 2).
  - Theorems 3 and 4 rely on unspecified “suitable conditions” (Reviewer 2).
  - Equation (3): the opposing signs of ℒ_vf and ~ℒ_vf suggest a min-max optimization; this needs clarification. Also, the impact of removing inter-class/intra-class consistency losses is not analyzed (Reviewer 1).
- **Methodological concerns:**
  - Why was CVAE (2015) chosen over more recent VAE variants? No justification (Reviewer 4).
  - Unclear how representations from different clients’ CVAEs do not clash (e.g., class 1 on client 1 overlapping with class 2 on client 2) (Reviewer 1).
  - The Reptile-based component is not clearly distinguished from standard CVAE training in the algorithm description (Reviewers 1, 4).
  - In Algorithm 2 initial phase, what representations does the CVAE train on before the feature extractor is well-trained? (Reviewer 1).
  - Computational complexity (FLOPS or wall-clock time vs accuracy) not compared with baselines (Reviewer 1).
  - Number of model parameters: FLAIR includes CVAE + feature extractor + classifier, while baselines may not – this makes comparison unfair (Reviewer 1).
  - Purpose of generating and including y_tild in training is not explained (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 1: what is the difference between total clients and number of local models? For FLAIR, shouldn’t communication complexity be O(E×S_t) instead of O(E)? (Reviewer 4).
  - Section 2.5 line 307 – where are lines 34 and 35? (Reviewer 4).
  - Only vision tasks are evaluated; a brief explanation for this choice would be beneficial (Reviewer 2).
