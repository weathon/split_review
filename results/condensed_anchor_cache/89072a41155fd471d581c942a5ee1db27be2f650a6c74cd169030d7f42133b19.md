## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces port-Hamiltonian Deep Graph Networks (PH-DGN), a framework that embeds message passing within a port-Hamiltonian dynamical system by splitting node states into position (q) and momentum (p) components coupled through skew-symmetric matrices. Two variants are proposed: a conservative PH-DGN (PH-DGN_C) that preserves energy and a full port-Hamiltonian version (PH-DGN) with learnable dissipative terms (internal dampening and external forces). Theoretical analysis provides conservation properties, lower bounds on sensitivity, and gradient behavior for the conservative case. Experiments on synthetic tasks, graph transfer, molecular property prediction, and long-range graph benchmarks show that PH-DGN variants achieve strong performance, often outperforming existing models, while PH-DGN_C is more efficient than prior Hamiltonian GNNs. Reviewers overall found the paper solid and original, with one reviewer (rating 5) being more critical about theoretical generality and practical performance of the conservative variant, while the other two (rating 8) were more positive about the contribution and experimental rigor.

### Strengths
- Very clear motivation building on Hamiltonian dynamics; background on port-Hamiltonian systems explained carefully.
- Original and clever approach to allow non-conservative dynamics while maintaining long-range message passing.
- Strong theoretical results for the conservative case: lower bounds on sensitivity (Theorem 2.4) proving long-range propagation capability, and energy conservation guarantees.
- Improved efficiency over previous Hamiltonian GNN approaches.
- Generality: can incorporate different message-passing schemes.
- Excellent experimental setup: model details, choices explained clearly, strong suite of competitors (MPNNs, Transformers, rewiring methods), comprehensive appendices for proofs and experimental details.
- Thorough validation on synthetic datasets (energy conservation, gradient behavior) and favorable results on real-world long-range benchmarks.
- Good balance of theory and empirics; clear comparisons to prior work.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical guarantees limited to conservative case.** The majority of theoretical results (energy conservation, sensitivity bounds) are derived only for PH-DGN_C. No rigorous analysis of how properties change when dissipative forces are added – e.g., bounds on the sensitivity matrix or energy dissipation rates for the full port-Hamiltonian case. One reviewer (rating 5) particularly emphasized this gap, contrasting with the stronger conservative-case theory.
- **Novelty is incremental.** The core idea builds on existing Hamiltonian GNNs and message passing; the main contribution combines these effectively rather than introducing fundamentally new concepts (Reviewer 1).
- **Conservative variant underperforms on real LRGB datasets.** PH-DGN_C does not outperform existing methods on real long-range graph benchmarks, limiting its practical usefulness despite theoretical guarantees. (Reviewer 2)
- **Theoretical bound comparison not rigorous.** The argument that the upper bound in Theorem 2.4 (proposed) is larger than Theorem 2.3 (existing) does not guarantee better sensitivity; a looser bound may not reflect the actual sensitivity of PH-DGN. This should be shown theoretically or experimentally (Reviewer 2).
- **Statement about "retains its complete past" is too strong.** Theorem 2.3 shows the backward sensitivity matrix is bounded, but does not imply perfect preservation or equal weighting of all past information; a more nuanced discussion of information flow is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Dissipative forces lack hard data/visualization support.** Explanations of why dissipative terms improve performance (e.g., acting as adaptive filters) are intuitive only. Visualizations of learned force fields or analysis of frequency content in node embeddings would strengthen claims. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 1 noted stronger ablation on dissipative terms would be beneficial.)
- **Missing experiments on physically meaningful Hamiltonian dynamics.** Given the physical inspiration, experiments on problems with actual Hamiltonian dynamics (e.g., molecular dynamics) would validate whether conservation properties provide advantages beyond improving information flow (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing analysis on when port-Hamiltonian approach is most beneficial.** No analysis of which problem properties favor PH-DGN over standard message passing, or how to balance conservative vs. dissipative components for a given task. (Reviewer 1 questions)
- **Missing extension for classical GCN aggregation.** It is unclear how Theorem 2.4 would extend to standard GCN aggregation (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing empirical verification on controlling growing behavior.** The paper mentions that growing behavior can be controlled by regularizing weight matrices or using normalized aggregation; it is unclear if this was tested empirically (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing interpretability analysis.** Whether the learned non-conservative forces are interpretable for the associated problem is not examined (Reviewer 3).
- **Tables and experimental reporting unclear.** Tables 2 and 5 need clarification: correspondence between columns (e.g., "MPNNs" in Table 2 vs. "re-evaluated" in Table 5; GCNII not listed as re-evaluated), and whether all methods in Table 5 were re-evaluated by the authors (Reviewer 2). Use of positional encoding only mentioned in the caption of Table 2, not in the main text (Reviewer 2).
- **Questionable setup description for Graph Transfer Task.** If all node features are 1D random uniform and the target is 0 for source, 1 for target, it is unclear how the model distinguishes source/target from other nodes (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions about discretization and assumptions.** The derivation of the discretization scheme and assumptions on the structure of W and V matrices for explicit updates feel restrictive and need more justification (Reviewer 1). Why port-Hamiltonian is more appropriate than simpler alternatives could be better explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues:** Misleading phrasing "non-dissipative (i.e., long-range)" – non-dissipative implies energy-preserving, not equivalent to long-range (Reviewer 2). Claim that framework shows benefit without requiring extra strategies (position encoding, attention, rewiring) should be clarified by indicating which specific comparisons justify each claim (Reviewer 2). Table 6 misleadingly suggests PH-DGN simultaneously achieves Hamiltonian conservation and learnable driving forces, but these are two separate variants (Reviewer 2). Missing citation for Adam (Reviewer 2). "n. layers" typo should be "the number of layers" (Reviewer 2).
