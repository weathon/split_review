## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GeoLoRA, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method based on dynamical low-rank approximation theory. GeoLoRA addresses issues in existing LoRA variants (e.g., stiffness/convergence problems, need for rank adaptivity, multiple backward passes) by reparameterizing the gradient flow and updating only the singular values in a controlled manner, requiring a single backward pass. The method includes adaptive rank allocation with theoretical guarantees (convergence, descent, error bounds). Experiments on language (GLUE, E2E, etc.) and vision (VTAB-1k) tasks show GeoLoRA matches or outperforms LoRA, AdaLoRA, and other baselines while being more computationally efficient and robust to hyperparameters.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach to stiffness/convergence**: GeoLoRA provides a new way to solve the stiffness and convergence issues in existing low-rank adaptation solvers, finding LoRA factors at roughly the same rate as full fine-tuning (Reviewer 2).
- **Efficiency and adaptive rank finding**: GeoLoRA adaptively determines rank without specifying it a priori, at significantly lower computational cost than AdaLoRA. Timing experiments in Table 2 directly demonstrate efficiency improvements (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong theoretical support**: The method is backed by rigorous analysis: convergence guarantees, error bounds, and optimality conditions. Proposition 1 extends guarantees to the general multilayer case, implying optimal rank configuration for arbitrary architectures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Robust empirical performance**: GeoLoRA robustly outperforms or matches existing PEFT methods (LoRA, AdaLoRA, etc.) across diverse language and vision benchmarks. It also shows robustness to hyperparameter changes (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Clear analysis of low-rank optimization limitations**: Section 3 clearly identifies that optimality requires the gradient projection to zero and that parallel optimization (as in standard LoRA) cannot achieve this, motivating GeoLoRA (Reviewer 4).
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The paper is well-motivated, and the methodology is described clearly (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). GeoLoRA’s implementation is straightforward (Reviewer 4).
- **Demonstrated rank adaptivity**: Experiments showing GeoLoRA converging to similar parameter counts whether starting with more or fewer parameters confirm its rank-reduction capability (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal improvements**: The gains over baselines are sometimes minor—especially in Table 2 (language tasks) where differences are small, and in some GLUE subsets GeoLoRA performs worse than vanilla LoRA (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Incomplete or unfair experimental comparisons**:
  - Table 2: GeoLoRA’s results are reported individually (only once), while for other methods it is unclear if the same configuration is used across datasets. AdaLoRA and other adaptive baselines show a single parameter count for all datasets, raising questions about whether the same budget/configuration is used, while GeoLoRA’s parameter count varies per dataset (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - Table 3: LoRA results are missing. Table 4: AdaLoRA results are missing. More discussion and justification for these omissions are needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baselines**: The paper should include additional LoRA variants for comparison, such as DoRA, LoRA+, and PiSSA (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Need for larger-scale validation**: GeoLoRA should be tested on larger models (e.g., LLAMA) and datasets (e.g., MetaMathQA) to validate its claim of converging to an optimal low-rank solution, especially in settings where LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning (Reviewer 4).
- **Intuitive explanation lacking**: While the formal derivation in Sections 3–4 is presented, the paper lacks an intuitive explanation for why GeoLoRA is designed the way it is. The theoretical contribution’s clarity is diminished as a result (Reviewer 4).
- **Claim of low-rank pre-training not validated**: Section 4.1 claims GeoLoRA can be used for low-rank pre-training, but no empirical evidence is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity on vision tasks**: Questions about choosing τ and initial rank remain; Table 7 shows different values being used across datasets, suggesting potential sensitivity (Reviewer 4).
- **Motivation section length and clarity**: Section 3 is lengthy and the description of the algorithm is delayed, making it unclear where the motivation ends and the method begins. A more structured presentation would help (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions about further acceleration**: Could approximate methods (e.g., power iteration) be used to accelerate the orthonormalization and SVD steps in GeoLoRA (Reviewer 2)?
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: While Reviewer 2 gave a high score (8), emphasizing novelty and efficiency, other reviewers (1, 3, 4) rated 5–6, noting marginal improvements and insufficient experimental justification. This contrast highlights that the contributions are seen as more significant by Reviewer 2 than by others.
