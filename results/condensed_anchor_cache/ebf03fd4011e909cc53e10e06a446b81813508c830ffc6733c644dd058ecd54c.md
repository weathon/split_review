## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes REFLEXSE, an iterative reflexion framework for clinical note summarization from doctor-patient dialogues. It uses a large language model (e.g., GPT-4) with in-context learning to generate initial notes, then iteratively refines them using a small domain-specific scorer model (fine-tuned to evaluate content quality) and Error2Correct demonstrations (error examples, analysis, and corrections) to guide error identification and rectification. Experiments are conducted on one Chinese and one English dataset (ACI-Bench). The method reportedly achieves state-of-the-art performance over several fine-tuned and zero-shot ICL baselines. Two reviewers found the approach novel and effective (one scored 8), while three reviewers rated it 3, citing major weaknesses in evaluation, presentation, and practical significance.

### Strengths
- **Novel combination:** The unique integration of an iterative refinement loop, a small fine-tuned scoring model for feedback (instead of self-evaluation by the LLM), and Error2Correct demonstrations is original and relevant to medical NLP and summarization (Reviewer 3).
- **Application to two languages:** Experiments on both Chinese and English datasets demonstrate the method’s cross-lingual applicability (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Comprehensive ablation analysis:** The paper separately evaluates different modules (small model, iterative scheduler, error demonstrations) and quantitatively justifies their inclusion (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Solid research quality:** Clear problem statement, data description, and method outline; evidence for the effectiveness of iterative refinement and the necessity of each component (Reviewer 3).
- **Societal relevance:** Applications to clinical note generation are important for healthcare AI and align with commercial tools (e.g., AWS HealthScribe, Nuance DAX); the general idea may transfer to other domains with strict target-text rules (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Trending topics:** The use of GPT, BaRT (Bidirectional and Auto-Regressive Transformers), and in-context learning is timely (Reviewer 2).
- **Well-organized writing (one reviewer):** Paper is easy to follow and well organized (Reviewer 3); one other reviewer found it “carefully written” (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of human evaluation:** All evaluations rely solely on automatic metrics (ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore), which are known to correlate poorly with human judgments. Without human assessment, it is unclear whether improvements in lexical scores translate to better adherence to clinical note guidelines (Reviewers 3, 4). This is a critical missing piece (Reviewer 4).
- **Marginal improvement with substantially higher cost:** On the English dataset, the proposed method achieves only 48.82 vs. 48.24 (GPT-4+ICL) – a tiny gain despite requiring multiple LLM calls and fine-tuning a separate scorer. The paper does not analyze latency, inference cost, or trade-offs between iterations and improvement (Reviewer 4). One reviewer requested a plot of performance vs. number of iterations or inference time to show diminishing returns (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete evaluation scope:** On ACI-Bench, the framework is applied only to the HPI and A/P sections, yet evaluation is reported across all note sections. The authors should separately evaluate sections affected by the framework (Reviewer 3).
- **Poor presentation and readability (three reviewers):** The paper is difficult to follow, particularly in describing the iterative process, the Iterative Scheduler, and the scorer’s role. Figure 1 is cluttered and the step order is unclear (Reviewers 1, 4). The ITERATIVE SCHEDULER subsection is unclear (Reviewer 1). More details on how the content scorer is trained are needed, e.g., how negative samples are generated – are they from the dataset or produced by the LLM? (Reviewer 1). The paper should be restructured with a high-level system description before diving into modules (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparison with LLM-based scorer:** The authors claim LLMs lack domain knowledge, yet they do not compare their small model scorer against an LLM scorer (e.g., GPT-4 with few-shot) as a baseline, which would directly test this claim (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient related work coverage:** The paper neglects prior reviews on clinical/biomedical text summarization (e.g., Mishra et al. 2014, Pivovarov & Elhadad 2015, Wang et al. 2021). The focus should be contextualized among broader summarization work (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing research ethics statement:** No information about approvals or permissions for using electronic health data, nor discussion of potential risks and benefits (Reviewers 2, 3). An “Ethical Consideration” section should be added (Reviewer 3).
- **No statistical significance analysis:** The paper reports means and standard deviations but does not perform significance tests or confidence intervals; the reader cannot assess whether differences are meaningful (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of dataset statistics:** No average lengths of dialogues, summaries, or notes are provided (Reviewer 1). The paper should also report typical generated summary length or length ratio relative to references to investigate length bias in ROUGE (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear construction of Error2Correct examples:** It is not explained how the error examples, analysis, and corrections are selected or created (e.g., randomly, similarity-based, or manually annotated) (Reviewer 3).
- **Rule prompts not specified:** No table or examples of the rule prompts used to encode clinical note guidelines are given (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor presentation issues:** Acronyms not always introduced; citation style inconsistent; figure text too small; some in-text references need correction (e.g., repeated citation of the same paper) (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Lack of open code/resources:** No code or dataset links are provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Over-optimized for a narrow task:** The method is highly specialized for doctor-patient conversation summarization, which may limit its appeal to the general audience of the venue (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential metric misinterpretation:** Some statements about metrics (e.g., ROUGE-L capturing conciseness, METEOR emphasizing recall) are not well justified. The authors should clarify whether precision/recall/F1 is used and analyze why different metrics show different improvement patterns (Reviewer 3).
