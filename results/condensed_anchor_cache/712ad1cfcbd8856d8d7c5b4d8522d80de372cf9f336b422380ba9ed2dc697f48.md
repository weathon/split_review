## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Scalable Graph U-Net (SGUNET) with a depth‑first‑search (DFS) pooling strategy to handle varying mesh sizes and introduces a transfer‑learning pipeline with mapping functions and a parameter‑restriction loss. A dataset of 20,000 physical simulations from the ABC dataset is created. Experiments on the 2D Deformable Plate benchmark show that a model pre‑trained on ABC and fine‑tuned on 1/16 of the data improves position RMSE by 11.05% over training from scratch. The problem of scalability and the transfer‑learning approach are considered important by most reviewers. However, the reviews diverge: R3 (score 3, confidence 5) finds the technical contribution weak, comparisons insufficient, and ablations missing; R4 (score 6, confidence 2) views the architecture and transfer learning as creative. R1 and R2 (both score 5, confidence 4) note limited novelty and experiment gaps.

### Strengths
- Decent presentation of the core idea (R1).  
- Importance of improving scalability for GNN‑based physical dynamics simulation (R1).  
- Well‑organized structure (R2).  
- The newly defined transfer‑learning tasks for physical simulation are interesting and novel (R2).  
- A new multigrid method based on spatial pooling, enabling processing at different scales and faster information propagation (R3).  
- First real analysis of transfer‑learning capacities for mesh‑based GNNs, to the best of the reviewer’s knowledge (R3).  
- The novel dataset offers a powerful pre‑training strategy for solid deformation (R3).  
- SGUNET with DFS pooling is a creative and original contribution; it efficiently handles varying mesh sizes (R4).  
- Application of transfer learning to GNNs for physical simulations is relatively unexplored; the mapping functions for parameter alignment are innovative (R4).  
- Clear presentation of the architecture and experiment settings (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Technical contribution is limited; the graph pooling and weight‑alignment strategies have been proposed before and applying them does not pose novel challenges (R1).  
- Motivation is unclear: why is current graph U‑Net not scalable enough, how does DFS help adapt to different mesh sizes, and why cannot existing solutions achieve this? (R1).  
  * Questions that overlap with this criticism: Why is pre‑training/fine‑tuning needed for physical simulation? (R1). How does DFS help adapt to different resolutions? (R1).  
- The two strategies (pooling and weight alignment) appear separate and not closely connected; the presentation lacks a systematic, unified approach (R1).  
- Experiments are insufficient and lack a systematic benchmark to robustly support superiority (R1).  
- Literature review is not comprehensive; it only discusses mesh‑based simulation and misses recent GNN‑based simulation works and related mesh‑based developments. Reviewers suggest splitting the related‑work section and citing, e.g., “Equivariant graph neural operator for modeling 3d dynamics” (2024), “DEL: Discrete Element Learner” (2024), “A Neural Material Point Method” (2024) for GNN‑based simulators; and “MAGNet: A graph u‑net architecture for mesh‑based simulations” (2024), which uses a similar architecture (R2, R3).  
- Line 145: Reference “Appendix ??” is an error (R2, R3).  
- Parameter restriction: When Wp and Wt have unequal shapes, how is the Frobenius norm of their difference computed? (R2).  
- Equations should be labelled with numbers (R2).  
- “Number of stages” and “message‑passing steps” – are these manually specified or adaptive? Adaptive adjustment could enhance generalizability (R2).  
- As a fine‑tuning method, the paper should compare time required for fine‑tuning and model size, not only data amount. MGN is an old baseline; more recent studies should be included for comparison (R2).  
- The method is only compared to MGN; multiple recent multi‑grid architectures are missing, e.g., “Learning Flexible Body Collision Dynamics with Hierarchical Contact Mesh Transformer”, “Simulating Continuum Mechanics with Multi‑Scale Graph Neural Networks”, “Efficient Learning of Mesh‑Based Physical Simulation with BSMS‑GNN” (R3).  
- No ablation on the new methodology: pooling ratio, number of layers, etc. (R3).  
- Table 3 indicates that, given enough data, SGUNet performs worse than MGN. Combined with the lack of comparison to other multi‑grid methods, this does not prove the method should become a new standard (R3).  
- When comparing fine‑tuned models with models trained from scratch, the total number of training steps is not accounted for, leading to a false comparison (R3).  
- Several questions that overlap with missing experiments/ablations (R3):  
  – l51: Previous multi‑grid methods already offer dynamic scaling – was that tried?  
  – l53: Could one keep the same architecture and simply apply a trained model to another dataset?  
  – l67: Has pretraining on 2D and fine‑tuning on 3D been attempted (e.g., by padding z=0 coordinates)?  
  – l67: Has pretraining on the Deforming Plate dataset before fine‑tuning on ABC been tried?  
  – Figure 2: Why use concatenation for skip connections instead of the usual sum?  
  – l242/245: If a larger model does not perform worse, why not simply pretrain a large model and fine‑tune it directly?  
  – l254: Was keeping the same encoder and decoder attempted?  
  – Table 1: The hidden size appears much smaller than the usual 128. Since 128 is a known plateau for the Deforming Plate dataset, the results may not be optimized – why choose a smaller size?  
  – Table 6: What performance is obtained when training a model from scratch for the same number of steps as the fine‑tuned model (1M+ steps)?  
  – Figures 6 and 9: Given the large standard deviations, can t‑tests be performed to compare results more precisely?  
- Additional minor issues (R3):  
  – l154: Acronym MLP is never explained.  
  – l371: Reference error in appendix.  
  – l263–271: Formulations are hard to read.  
  – l70: No comparison with any recent architecture.  
  – l79–85: What about unsupervised methods such as Physics Informed models?  
  – l46/47: Transfer learning is older than the papers cited.  
- The experiments focus only on 2D and 3D quasi‑static mechanical simulations; more diverse and dynamic tasks (fluid dynamics, thermal simulations, multi‑physics interactions) would better demonstrate generalizability (R4).  
- No detailed information on resource consumption (GPU memory, training time); a comparative analysis of training efficiency under different data scales would show practical advantages (R4).
