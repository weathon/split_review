## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GTBO (Group Testing Bayesian Optimization), a high-dimensional BO method that operates in two phases: first, an adaptive group testing procedure identifies active input dimensions using mutual information from a Gaussian process; second, standard BO (Matern 5/2, qLogNEI) is run with different length-scale priors for active vs. inactive dimensions. The approach extends binary group testing to continuous functions, is probabilistic and handles noise, and improves interpretability by revealing which variables are relevant. Experiments on synthetic and two real-world benchmarks show competitive performance against several baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and organized in an easy-to-follow manner (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The group testing methodology is clearly explained, including its mathematical foundation and implementation details (R2, R3, R4).
- Good coverage of baselines and relevant literature (R1, R4).
- Experiments demonstrate strong performance compared to baselines on both synthetic and real-world tasks (R1, R2, R4).
- The method provides interpretability advantages over projection-based dimensionality reduction, helping practitioners understand active parameters (R1, R4).
- Sensitivity analysis and ablation studies are included (R1).
- The group testing efficiently identifies active variables (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparison due to initial evaluations.** The group testing phase consumes 39–112 function evaluations (depending on dimension) that are not counted in the total budget for GTBO in Figs. 3–4, while baselines start from 0. This gives GTBO an unfair advantage (R2). Additionally, the best initial point of GTBO is always better than those of baselines, further biasing results (R3). The authors did not show results with the same initial points for all methods (R3).
- **Method is effectively a decoupled feature selection + BO, not a fully integrated BO algorithm.** The group testing phase is independent of the subsequent BO phase; any standard BO could follow group testing, and any other feature selection could precede the BO step. This limits adaptive refinement of the active subspace (R2). The group testing phase does not consider maximizing the objective—designs evaluated may yield low function values, making variable relevance learned in a region that may be irrelevant for optimization, yet the method works; the reason is unclear (R4).
- **Strong independence assumptions.** The model assumes features are relatively independent and that probabilities of dimensions being active are independent. This is a critical weakness for real-world datasets with correlated or synergistic/antagonistic features; performance may degrade significantly (R1).
- **Weak motivation for key hyperparameters and priors.** The choice of \(\sqrt{D}\) for the number of active dimensions, and the log-normal prior (vs. Gamma, a common hyperprior for lengthscales) are not well justified. No detailed sensitivity analysis on these choices is given, raising robustness concerns (R1). Also, the determination of log-normal length-scales for inactive dimensions needs clarification (R2).
- **Missing recent baselines.** Important recent high-dimensional BO methods are omitted: MCTS-VS (a variable-selection method) (R3), Sparse Bayesian Optimization (AISTATS 2023), and Random Decompositions (ICML 2023) (R4).
- **Benchmark selection issues.** Synthetic benchmarks have 2–8 active dimensions out of ≈300 total without justification. Real-world benchmarks contain no noise, which is unrealistic for many high-dimensional problems. Noisy real-world benchmarks are needed to assess robustness (R2). Only two real-world datasets are tested; more would strengthen the evaluation (R1).
- **Many hyperparameters** for determining relevance and encoding it in the GP prior, without thorough analysis (R4).
- **Incomplete details.** The values of \(C_{lower}\) and \(C_{upper}\) are not given (R1). The choice of acquisition function is not motivated (R1). The batch evaluation description (e.g., “several” and “close”) needs significant clarification (R2). The batch size used in experiments is not reported, and the claim that GTBO integrates well with batch BO is unsupported by experiment (R3). The search bound is not specified; it is unclear if GTBO leverages symmetric search space like BAxUS (R3). The choice of the default configuration as the search center is not justified; performance of GTBO with other centers (e.g., best random point) is not shown (R3).
- **Paper’s own limitations are not properly listed** (R1).
- **Experiment section writing** has poor flow, with figures introduced in a confusing order (R1). Minor: plot lines could be thicker (R1). A typo in Eq. (4) (R4).
- **Minority contrast:** One reviewer (R4) found almost no weaknesses beyond hyperparameters, while others raised substantial concerns about fairness, assumptions, and missing baselines.
