## Merged Review

### Summary
Two reviewers (R1, R3) rate the paper 5, while two (R2, R4) rate it 3, reflecting a split on novelty and experimental rigor. All agree the paper is the first to apply Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) to Temporal Action Detection (TAD) and is well-motivated. The proposed dynamic switching teacher strategy integrates a dynamic teacher (EMA of student) and a static teacher (periodically weight-exchanged with student) to reduce label noise. Experiments are conducted on three benchmarks (ActivityNet1.3→THUMOS14, THUMOS14→FineAction, FineAction→ActivityNet1.3) using I3D features and a TAD head (e.g., GFocal). State-of-the-art results are reported against Mean-Teacher (MT) baselines. However, substantial concerns were raised about limited novelty (method not TAD-specific), insufficient ablation, small dataset scales (10–11 classes), missing comparisons with video DA methods, lack of evidence that weight switching mitigates noise, and conceptual mismatches in action semantics across domains.

### Strengths
- **First SFDA work for TAD**: The paper pioneers source-free domain adaptation in temporal action detection (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Clear motivation and problem framing**: The need for generalization without source data is well-justified and the limitations of Mean-Teacher under domain shift are clearly explained (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **High-level concept is reasonable**: The overall idea of combining two teachers (dynamic EMA and static periodic-exchange) is easy to follow and intuitively targets error cascades (R1, R2, R3).
- **Experimental results show component effectiveness**: Ablation studies (e.g., Table 3) demonstrate that each part of the method contributes to final performance (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and lack of TAD-specific design**: The dynamic switching teacher strategy is generic; no component explicitly addresses temporal modeling, variable-length sequences, or temporal dependencies. Replacing I3D with an image encoder would allow the same pipeline on image DA (R1, R2, R3). The method’s only borrowed TAD-specific element is weighted box fusion (Solovyev et al., 2021) – no unique temporal contribution (R1). Dynamic weight switching itself was already proposed by Liu et al. (ICCV 2023) (R2). To justify TAD specificity, the authors should either add temporal-tailored components or apply the method to image-based SFDA as evidence of generality (R1, R3).
- **Benchmark limitations and insufficient evaluation**:
  - Datasets are small with only 10–11 action classes, oversimplifying the problem and reducing reliability of conclusions (R1, R2).
  - Missing common action class mapping for each domain pair; aside from “Diving” vs. “springboard diving” in ActivityNet1.3→THUMOS14, no obvious semantic shift is shown (R1). The choice of transfer directions (A→T, T→F, F→A) is not justified; why not T→A, F→T, A→F? (R2)
  - The closed-vocabulary setting (only videos with shared categories) is not sufficiently challenging for generalization, especially compared to recent open-vocabulary VLM-based methods (R4). A more rigorous benchmark should be proposed (R4).
- **Lack of evidence that weight switching reduces label noise**: The paper repeatedly claims noise reduction but provides no direct ablation measuring pseudo-label quality before/after switching (R1). The static teacher’s periodic weight exchange with the student model is not clearly motivated: if the static model exchanges weights, why is it called “static,” and how does it differ from the dynamic teacher (which is an EMA of the student)? At some point they will be identical, undermining the claimed noise mitigation (R2). In Table 3, Mean-Teacher with simple pseudo-label filtering sometimes outperforms the dynamic teacher with filtering, raising doubt about the dynamic teacher’s usefulness (R2).
- **Insufficient ablation, missing experiments, and unclear design choices**:
  - No ablation on hyperparameters of pseudo-label filtering heuristics (R2). No analysis of “weight exchange” variations or the lambda weight for teacher fusion (R2). No comparison with a standard dual-teacher setup (R2).
  - Missing comparisons with prior video domain adaptation methods: STHC, ATCoN (source-free video DA), TA3N, MM-SADA, SAVA, CoMix, TranSVAE, and other action recognition DA works (R3). These should be adapted to TAD or at least discussed why they are insufficient (R3). Missing action segmentation citations that could be related (MS-TCN, SSTDA, etc.) (R3).
  - Compute cost (GFLOPs) and parameter count comparison missing (Table 3); the method is computationally expensive and its worth cannot be assessed without it (R2).
  - No details on weak/strong augmentations (R2). Unclear if loss functions match baseline Mean-Teacher (e.g., focal loss) (R2).
  - Missing ablation on choice of pretrained source model (R3). The paper uses I3D only; why not transformer backbones (MViT, VideoSwin)? Justification for I3D is missing (R3).
  - Numbers in text (lines 522–529, e.g., 25.7 and 30.8) are not aligned with Table 3 (R3).
- **Conceptual mismatch in action annotations**: Identical class labels across datasets (e.g., “cooking”) may denote different granularities and temporal spans (short clip vs. full process). Without even one labeled target instance, SFDA cannot correct this mismatch, challenging the approach’s validity (R4).
- **Performance inconsistency at lower tIoU thresholds**: The method shows noticeable performance drops at thresholds 0.3 and 0.5, raising concerns about robustness (R2).
- **Technical and notation issues**:
  - Line 267: “select predictions with an tIoU greater than 0.5 and belonging to the same class as part of the same cluster” – IoU of what? Both teacher predictions? (R2)
  - Line 078: Pretrained teacher model description insufficient – no details on TAD model architecture or initialization (R3). Kinetics-pretrained I3D may still introduce biases since Kinetics overlaps with some action categories (e.g., diving) and is also YouTube-sourced (R2).
  - Section 3.2: Conflicting statements about which teacher updates which (dynamic vs. static) not aligned with Figure 3 (R3). Notation confusing: pseudo-labels (t̃, c̃, s̃) are per-video but t̃ and s̃ are per-cluster in Eqs. (1)–(2) (R3).
  - Line 295: “In each epoch, the static teacher model learns solely through weight exchange” is misplaced; Section 3.2.2 should only describe the dynamic teacher (R3).
  - Figure 2: unclear why multiple ground-truths appear in one video and what pl-i ordering means (R3). Term “teacher bounding box fusion” is inappropriate for temporal boundaries (R2).
- **Missing details and clarifications requested**:
  - Provide class mapping for each dataset protocol (R1).
  - Explain why method struggles at lower thresholds (R2).
  - Clarify how exactly MT updates the static teacher and how weight exchange updates the dynamic teacher; reconcile with Figure 3 (R3).
  - Provide architecture details and justify choice of I3D; consider backbone generality experiments (R3).
