## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Parameter-Redundant Fine-Tuning (PREFT), a framework to reduce noise in LoRA fine-tuning by eliminating redundant parameters before merging. It introduces Noise Reduction with Reserved Majority (NORM), which uses random singular value decomposition to decompose LoRA updates and a Sim-Search method to retain components with highest subspace similarity to the base model weights. Experiments on instruction tuning, math reasoning, and code generation show competitive results, sometimes surpassing full fine-tuning.

### Strengths

- Clear writing and logical story; the paper communicates insights without overclaiming contributions.
- Central goal (reducing redundant PEFT parameters that deviate from the network distribution) is well motivated and validated through meaningful preliminary experiments.
- Highly competitive results across diverse tasks; in some cases outperforms full-rank fine-tuning where LoRA underperforms.
- Extensive experiments across multiple models and datasets demonstrate generalization; ablation study supports the subspace-similarity-based rank selection.
- Novel methodology combining random SVD with Sim-Search offers a fresh perspective and potential impact on future LLM optimization strategies.
- Ample illustrations and well-structured sections make the method accessible and reproducible.

### Weaknesses

- **Notation issues (multiple reviewers):** Several equations deviate from standard notation. In Lines 188–190, “such that” is followed by an argmax, making the selection condition unclear. In Line 297, the set-builder notation `$\tau \cdot s, (\tau+1) \cdot s, \dots, r$` does not form a clear predicate over `c`. In Line 155 and Line 297, it is unclear whether the starting value `s` relates to earlier `s_1, s_2`. In Equation 14, `$\mathbf{U}_{cr}$` is undefined. These hinder clarity and precision.
- **Missing comparisons with latest PEFT methods (Reviewer 3):** The paper lacks comprehensive comparisons with recent parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning advances, making it hard to precisely position NORM in the research landscape. Reviewer 3 asks whether specific recent methods were considered and why they were excluded.
- **Insufficient analysis of computational demands (Reviewer 3):** No detailed comparison of resource usage (e.g., training time, memory) against other fine‑tuning methods. Reviewer 3 requests such analysis to assess practicality in resource‑constrained environments.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not explored (Reviewer 3):** The influence of SVD rank and other hyperparameters on performance is not analyzed. Reviewer 3 asks for a sensitivity analysis or guidelines for selecting optimal hyperparameters.
- **No discussion of long‑term effects in continuous learning (Reviewer 3):** The paper does not address how NORM performs when models undergo multiple fine‑tuning cycles. Reviewer 3 requests preliminary results or insights on stability over iterations.
- **Evaluation methods questioned (Reviewer 4):** The evaluation benchmarks are not fully standardized, and the results are not entirely convincing. Reviewer 4 strongly recommends experiments on established vision‑language benchmarks like GLUE and VTAB, noting that the method did not excel on GLUE (presumably from the authors’ response) and that the authors only provided intuitive reasons for not testing on VTAB. This raises doubts about the method’s actual effectiveness. (Reviewer 4 is the most negative, score 6; this criticism is not shared by other reviewers.)
- **Lack of deeper mathematical analysis on redundancy source (Reviewer 1):** More mathematical explanation of why redundancy arises in PEFT would be beneficial. Reviewer 1 also questions the value of increasing rank to improve accuracy, given its emphasis in Figure 1.
- **Why not design an end‑to‑end training with uneven rank? (Reviewer 1):** The post‑hoc search is not end‑to‑end; reviewer asks whether a smaller or uneven rank during training could avoid the search step.
