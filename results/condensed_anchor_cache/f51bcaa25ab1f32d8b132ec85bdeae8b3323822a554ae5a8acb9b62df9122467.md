## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedBPT, a federated black-box prompt tuning framework using CMA-ES for gradient-free optimization and random input perturbation to mitigate overfitting. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and the problem relevant, but raise concerns about limited novelty, insufficient evaluation of computational and communication trade-offs, privacy and feasibility of the black-box setting, missing state-of-the-art baselines (e.g., LPT, IDPG, FedProx, LoRA), and weak experimental results, particularly on large models and non-IID data. One reviewer (R4) is strongly critical (score 3, confidence 5), while others are moderately positive (scores 5–6, confidence 3–4).

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R5, R6).
- It addresses important challenges of applying FL to LLM fine-tuning: limited parameter access, high computation, and communication overhead (R2, R5, R6).
- Provides theoretical analysis of the CMA-ES aggregation step size and the derivation of the optimal step length (R2, R3, R4).
- The use of random perturbation to address overfitting in prompt tuning is intriguing and has proven effective in other domains (R1).
- Experiments are comprehensive, covering RoBERTa and Llama 2 with comparisons on model performance, communication cost, memory, etc. (R2, R6). The use of state-of-the-art pretrained models like LLaMA shows broad coverage (R6).
- Clear figures and illustrations help understanding (R6).
- The approach reduces trainable parameters and communication costs compared to other prompt tuning methods (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: FedBPT appears to be a direct extension of BBT/CMA-ES with aggregation; the core components (gradient-free optimization, input masking) are not novel. The paper should clarify its contributions and distinguish from existing work (R3 Q1, R4). One reviewer (R4, score 3, confidence 5) is particularly negative on novelty.
- **Computation cost analysis lacking**: Gradient-free CMA-ES may need many more iterations for convergence, yet no wall-clock time comparison is provided. The paper should analyze the number of function evaluations required and how it scales with prompt size and number of clients. Evolutionary algorithms are typically slower than SGD, raising time-efficiency concerns (R2, R4).
- **Privacy and feasibility issues in the black-box setting**:
    - To compute the loss in Eq. (1), clients either must upload input/output to the server (violating FL privacy) or load the full model locally (contradicting the limited-access assumption) (R6). The paper does not resolve how to enable inference without data transfer (R5 Q1).
    - Sending vector z (or F^k(z) for step-length optimization) may leak information about training data; the learned prompts should be analyzed for data reconstruction risk (R3 Q3, R5 Q2, Q3).
- **Missing important baselines**: Comparisons are needed with more recent state-of-the-art prompt tuning methods (e.g., LPT, IDPG [R4]), FL-specific non-IID algorithms (e.g., FedProx [R2,R5,R6]), and communication-efficient approaches like LoRA (R6).
- **Experimental weaknesses**:
    - Accuracy decline on large-scale Llama 2, especially under non-IID settings, is obvious (R1).
    - FedAvg (full fine-tuning) performs noticeably worse than prompt tuning in Table 1 – explanation needed (R4 Q2).
    - Tasks are too simple (only classification, binary/4-category); more complex tasks like QA should be considered (R6).
    - Manual prompt yields comparable performance to FedAvg; could the method improve on manual prompts? (R1 Q3).
- **Overfitting mitigation via random masking**:
    - Despite the dominant-class issue shown in Fig. 3, the improvement from random masking seems quite limited; if the issue is severe, a larger improvement would be expected (R3 Q5).
    - The result that 80% masking/replacement outperforms vanilla BBT is surprising; more analysis and comparison with other aggregation algorithms (e.g., FedProx) are needed (R2).
- **Insufficient ablation on population sensitivity**: Only three population trials are used to claim parameter insensitivity; more trials are required (R1).
- **Justification for modifying standard aggregation**: The claim that standard aggregation is not effective (Sec 4.3) is backed only by Fig. 2; more evidence or justification is needed (R3 Q2).
- **Dominant class issue**: What is the loss for local clients that are not well fitting across different classes? Are dominant classes "easy samples"? (R3 Q4).
- **Prompt length dimensions d and D**: The effect of these two values on performance should be discussed (R6 Q3).
- **Figure clarity**: Rectangle colors in Figure 3 are not explained in the caption (R1 Q1).
- **Perturbation details**: Is the random perturbation purely random or does it carry semantic information? What effect would changing "zero" to "some" (as in Fig. 4) have? (R1 Q2).
