## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a generative approach (DDPM with Dirichlet boundary conditions, 3D U‑Net + transformer) for one‑shot generation of 3D turbulent flow states without relying on an initial flow condition. A new dataset of 45 incompressible flow simulations on 192×48×48 grids with various objects is introduced, along with physics‑based evaluation metrics (turbulent kinetic energy, marginal velocity, vorticity, pressure distribution, vortex location). Comparisons against autoregressive baselines (Turbulent Flow Net, DilResNet) show competitive accuracy and runtime. Reviewers agree the idea is novel and promising but raise concerns about reproducibility, ablation, novelty, and the small dataset.

### Strengths
- **Originality and simplicity**: The generative model learns the manifold of turbulent states without requiring an initial condition, offering a simple, scalable alternative to autoregressive models. [Multiple reviewers]
- **Targets 3D turbulence**: Unlike much prior work on 2D flows, the method tackles substantially harder 3D problems and handles complex boundary effects and objects. [Reviewer 4]
- **Impressive one‑shot generation**: The ability to generate realistic turbulent flows from a one‑shot diffusion process is remarkable, especially given the small data regime (27 train, 9 val, 9 test airfoils), suggesting the model captures intrinsic physics. [Reviewer 2 – the most critical reviewer still found this noteworthy]
- **New dataset and metrics**: The introduced 192×48×48 3D turbulence dataset and the novel evaluation metrics (TKE, marginal velocity, vorticity, pressure distribution, location of strongest vortices) are valuable contributions to the field. [Reviewers 1, 3]
- **Competitive performance**: The model outperforms autoregressive baselines (Turbulent Flow Net, DilResNet) in both accuracy and runtime. [Reviewer 3]
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well‑written, clearly explains the challenges of autoregressive models for 3D turbulence, and is easy to follow. [Reviewers 1, 4]

### Weaknesses
- **Ablation studies missing**: The contribution of individual architectural blocks (DDPM, U‑Net, transformer) is not isolated; without ablation the understanding of their interplay is incomplete. Details of the architecture and figures are insufficient. [Reviewer 1]
- **Reproducibility concerns**: No mention of releasing dataset or source code, which is a major hindrance to reproducibility. [Reviewer 2]
- **Small dataset**: The dataset contains only 45 simulations. While generalization is surprising, the small size limits conclusions; scaling to larger sample sizes (e.g., 10⁵) would strengthen the work. [Reviewer 2]
- **Incremental novelty**: The method is related to prior generative flow approaches; the claim of circumventing the “roll‑out dilemma” by modeling the marginal distribution \(E_t[p((u,p)_t | u_0,p_0)]\) needs clarification. The removal of the initial‑state dependency and the expectation operation are not well justified, and the innovation over existing stochastic‑process perspectives (e.g., Yang and Sommer) is unclear. [Reviewer 4]
- **Speedup concerns**: The nominal 30× speedup is less impressive when considering that an A100 GPU is already ~50× faster than a Xeon‑2630; the hardware comparison may inflate the real speed gain. [Reviewer 2 – minority negative opinion]
- **Limitations of A#F**: The work inherits noticeable limitations from A#F (likely a prior method), including lack of equivariance and geometry constraints, suggesting this is an initial work not yet mature for a top venue. [Reviewer 2 – minority negative opinion]
- **Missing baselines and evaluations**:
  - No experiments on unstructured data (graph meshes, point clouds). [Reviewer 1 question]
  - Performance at boundary layers and metrics on geometry surfaces (the hardest part of CFD) are not reported. [Reviewer 1 question]
  - Invariance to meshing/discretization scheme not discussed. [Reviewer 1]
  - Complex fluid phenomena (e.g., fluid–air interaction, Kármán vortex street) are not explored. [Reviewer 3]
- **Visualization and dataset details**:
  - Figs. 5 and 6 do not state what quantity is visualized (velocity norm? pressure?). [Reviewer 3]
  - Data augmentation techniques (rotations, flips) are not tested. [Reviewer 3]
  - The choice of grid size 192×48×48 may be due to CFD solver limitations; larger grids are common for higher accuracy. [Reviewer 3 question]
- **Proofreading**: The text contains a duplicated sentence (“We conclude that many use cases can be solved…”). [Reviewer 4]
