## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FedDistr, a one-shot federated learning algorithm that uses stable diffusion models (Latent Diffusion Models) to disentangle and communicate data distributions rather than model parameters. The approach assumes the global distribution is a mixture of base distributions, with local distributions varying in mixture coefficients. FedDistr extracts base distributions from clients via diffusion models, aligns them on the server (identifying orthogonal versus parallel components), and broadcasts consolidated distribution parameters back to clients. Empirical results on CIFAR100 and DomainNet show improved utility and communication efficiency compared to traditional FL methods. However, the writing lacks clarity, the algorithm description is incomplete, the theoretical justifications are questionable, and privacy risks are not adequately addressed.

### Strengths

- Novel application of diffusion models to disentangle and aggregate data distributions in federated learning (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Achieves high accuracy with only a single communication round in heterogeneous federated learning scenarios (Reviewers 1, 4); “saves communication cost compared to baseline federated learning algorithms with little utility loss” (Reviewer 1).
- The idea of disentangling local distributions is original and significant (Reviewer 4).
- Theoretical support provided in the appendix (Reviewer 4); “The experiment results look good” (Reviewer 2).
- Both communication cost and data heterogeneity are important challenges in FL (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Incomplete and unclear algorithm description** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). “Algorithm 1 and Section 4 only covers the parts until ‘Activate Distribution Alignment’… the description of the algorithm is incomplete, lacking crucial details about how the synthetic data generation and local training are performed” (Reviewer 1). “The description of the approach omits important details, in particular, how the local models are trained and if local models can differ” (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 asks whether a classifier is trained and fused, or only the LDM learns to generate images and labels; “it must be assumed by the reader that they do not, but one who reads the related work [Liang et al. 2024] will wonder if that is truly the case.”

- **Poor motivation and insufficient distinction between “entanglement” and “heterogeneity.”** (Reviewer 1). “In federated learning, heterogeneity is a more common term compared to the so-call disentanglement.” The claim “There is a consensus that this inefficiency stems from the entanglement of data distribution across clients” is not backed by references (Reviewer 1). The paragraph between Eq. (2) and (3) makes an unsubstantiated claim: “data heterogeneity is a blessing rather than a curse, as long as data distributions among different clients can be completely disentangled” – no citations support this bold statement (Reviewer 4).

- **Privacy concerns inadequately addressed** (Reviewers 1, 2, and 3). “Transferring cluster centers, even in a latent space, could potentially reveal information about the client’s data distribution. A rigorous privacy analysis, possibly using differential privacy or similar frameworks, is needed” (Reviewer 1). “If clients and the server have information about the base distributions, can they infer the distribution information of other clients? Would this potentially lead to privacy leakage?” (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 asks “How do you plot figure 5? do you add noise somewhere to guarantee DP?” Reviewer 2 asks similarly.

- **Theoretical results are flawed or rely on unrealistic assumptions** (Reviewer 3 specifically, with strong concern). “Lemma 2 appears to be incorrect, as the bound does not hold without further assumptions on the distributions S and Ŝ. … The counterexample provided demonstrates that the expected loss cannot be bounded as stated.” “Theorem 1 relies heavily on the flawed Lemma 2, and its conclusion seems to contradict both the impossibility result in Marfoq et al. [1] and classical transfer learning results. If local tasks are independent, it is unclear how learning from each other is possible.” “The paper does not explicitly state that the theoretical results require finding the global risk minimizer, which is infeasible for non-convex problems like deep learning, thus limiting the practical applicability of the theory.” (Reviewer 3).

- **Fails to discuss or compare with closely related prior work** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 3 states the approach is similar to [1] Marfoq et al. (2021) and [2] Wu et al. (2023) but does not discuss them. Reviewer 2: “The idea of transferring information contained in data rather than the data itself has been reflected in some previous works, such as methods based on distillation and those using data Mixup. It is recommended to include a review and discussion of similar methods in the paper, and ideally, to add these baselines in the experiments.” Reviewer 4: The paper does not break down how its core is similar to Liang et al. (2024), “Diffusion-Driven Data Replay…”, which should be introduced in the Related Works section with a proper comparison.

- **Unclear experimental setup and missing details** (Reviewer 4). “The authors did not explain precisely enough what the federated architecture looks like. … How many classes of each dataset were used in total? How were these classes distributed among the clients? Was heterogeneity artificially induced, and if so, how?” “What does [subsets of classes] mean and how is the data distributed to the agents? If 20 classes are used and 5 clients exist, are you forcing each agent to have a different subset of the 20 classes to force heterogeneity?” (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 asks: “It seems the final training is performed over the so-called base distributions, in case of classification, is the learned model useful for the original client dataset?”

- **Contradictory statements in the algorithm description** (Reviewers 2, 4). “In Section 4.3, each client k uploads the distribution parameters to the server, but the paper states that ‘the server does not have access to the sequence of the distribution parameters.’ This seems contradictory” (Reviewer 2). “What does ‘orthogonal and parallel data base distributions’ mean?” (Reviewer 4).

- **Unexpected SCAFFOLD underperformance** (Reviewer 3). “SCAFFOLD generally performs well on heterogeneous FL tasks; however, your results show it underperforms. Could you elaborate on potential reasons for this discrepancy and clarify whether experimental settings or parameters might have affected SCAFFOLD’s performance?”

- **Writing quality and formatting issues** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Multiple grammatical errors (Reviewer 2 lists specific corrections). Missing punctuation at end of equations (Eqs. 8,9,10) (Reviewer 2). Inconsistent reference formatting (Reviewer 2). Proof of Theorem 1 contains stray braces (Reviewer 4). “The paper is not well-motivated. … The authors need to clarify the distinction between heterogeneity and their notion of ‘entanglement’” (Reviewer 1). Overall “lack of clarity in the writing and explanation provided by the authors” (Reviewer 4).
