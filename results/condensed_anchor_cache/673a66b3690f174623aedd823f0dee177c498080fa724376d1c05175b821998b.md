## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Swiss Army Knife (SAK), a multi-task learning framework that adaptively distills knowledge from multiple Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) using a Teacher-Agnostic Stem, Teacher-Specific Adapter Path modules, and Mixture-of-Representations (MoR) Routers. The goal is to preserve and synergize the unique representation biases of different VFMs. Experiments on NYUD-v2 and PASCAL-Context show significant improvements over prior state-of-the-art. Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty and experimental rigor, but raise concerns about complexity, missing comparisons, inference/training costs, and hyperparameter sensitivity. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive, while others (score 6) request additional validation.

### Strengths
- Novel approach that preserves individual VFM representation biases via Teacher-Specific Adapter Paths and synergizes them through MoR Routers, offering an alternative to traditional knowledge distillation (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- Systematic study of representation biases in VFMs, including quantitative and qualitative analysis of their unique contributions to downstream tasks (Reviewers 3, 5).
- Lightweight and parameter-efficient design: the stem and adapter paths reduce inference costs compared to running multiple VFMs independently, and the MoR routers are easy to implement (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- Extensive experiments on two public benchmarks (PASCAL-Context, NYUD-v2) across multiple tasks, with thorough comparisons to baselines, ablations, and visualizations of expert choices that provide insight into the method (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Paper is well-organized and clearly written (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Complexity vs. necessity:** The framework introduces high algorithmic complexity with multiple VFMs, adapter paths, and MoR routers. There is no rigorous theoretical justification for the architecture, and it is unclear whether gains stem from design choices or simply increased model capacity. Ablation studies on the number of adapters and router complexity relative to performance/computation are missing. Additionally, the training difficulty of the router’s gating functions (common in MoE) is not discussed (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Inference cost disagreement:** While some reviewers emphasize that SAK reduces inference cost by distilling multiple VFMs into a single model (Reviewers 2, 4), another notes that the MoR router requires forward passes of all teacher models, increasing inference cost (Reviewer 3). Training and memory costs compared to baselines are also not reported (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons and upper bounds:** 
  - No results on image-level reasoning or large vision-language models such as RADIO (Reviewer 2) or multimodal LLMs (LLaVA, Sphinx) (Reviewer 5).
  - Upper-bound results using the encoders of the three VFMs directly (without distillation and routers) are not provided (Reviewer 2).
  - No comparison to individual foundation models on tasks they specialize in (e.g., SAK vs. SAM on semantic segmentation, DINOv2 on depth estimation) to assess knowledge embedding (Reviewer 4). Relatedly, Fig. 1 shows SAK does not always outperform the best teacher per task, yet sometimes surpasses them—this needs explanation (Reviewer 4).
  - More vision tasks (e.g., instance-level segmentation/detection) could be evaluated (Reviewer 3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Extensive parameter tuning may contribute to results, but the paper lacks details on the hyperparameter search space and optimization strategy. Experiments with fixed hyperparameters or cross-validation are needed to isolate architectural benefits from tuning effects (Reviewer 1).
- **Teacher selection principles:** The choice of base VFMs is not justified. For instance, RADIO selected SigLIP and Theia used ViT-H; the paper should explain the rationale for teacher selection and include sensitivity experiments on teacher choice (Reviewer 2).
- **Extensibility:** It is unclear whether SAK can be easily adjusted to add new VFM teachers or adapt to specific downstream tasks (Reviewer 2).
