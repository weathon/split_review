## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes EraseDiff, an unlearning algorithm for diffusion models that formulates the problem as a bi-level optimization. The outer objective preserves utility on the remaining data (D_r), while the inner objective scrubs information from the forgetting data (D_f) by deviating the generative process from the ground-truth denoising procedure. A first-order method solves the bi-level problem. Experiments cover class, attribute, and race removal on UTKFace, CelebA, CelebA-HQ, and CIFAR10 for both conditional and unconditional models.

### Strengths
- First to propose a bi-level optimization approach specifically for unlearning in diffusion models (R1, R4).
- More efficient than full retraining (R1, R4).
- Extensive empirical evaluation across multiple datasets: UTKFace, CelebA, CelebA-HQ, CIFAR10 (R1, R2, R4).
- Uses a diverse set of metrics: FID, accuracy, Membership Inference Attack (MIA), KL distance, weight distance (R2).
- Applied to both conditional and unconditional image generation (R1, R4).
- Grounded in a theoretical framework with a clear problem, motivation, method, and pipeline (R1, R4).
- Idea is simple and effective, achieving an arguably better tradeoff between forgetting and remaining performance (R5).
- Addresses a crucial privacy concern in generative models; contribution could be significant if privacy scrutiny increases (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption of access to D_r may be unrealistic** for large-scale models (e.g., Stable Diffusion). The paper assumes both forgetting data D_f and remaining data D_r are available, but often only a pretrained model and D_f are accessible. The algorithm's behavior without D_r is not considered (R1). [Reviewer 1, score 6]
- **Claim of being first** to study unlearning in diffusion models is incorrect. Related prior works: [1] Gandikota et al. (2023) – Erasing Concepts, [2] Heng & Soh (2023) – Selective Amnesia, [3] Gandikota et al. (2023) – Unified Concept Editing, all fine-tune diffusion models for concept/class removal. Comparisons to [2] and [3] are missing (R1). [Reviewer 1, score 6]
- **Performance drop in generated samples** is significant. Table 2 shows FID on remaining classes is on average above 20 for CIFAR10; Selective Amnesia [2] reports FID 9.08, indicating EraseDiff underperforms. Table 3 shows the proposed model is worse than simple fine-tuning on some metrics (R1, R5). [Reviewer 1, score 6; Reviewer 5, score 5]
- **Evaluation is over-reliant on class labels** – a small subset of potential unlearning cases. The method targets class-wise unlearning, which may not handle arbitrary unstructured forgetting subsets (e.g., nudity removal, artistic style removal) (R2, R4). For unconditional models, erasing all data from a specific class does not guarantee the model truly “forgets” those samples, especially for similar classes or face datasets (R4). Demonstrations on random or poorly labeled subsets are lacking (R4). [Reviewer 2, score 6; Reviewer 4, score 8]
- **Formulation as bi-level optimization is unconventional.** The inner and outer objectives both optimize model parameters, making it naturally a multitask problem (preserve remaining data, eliminate forgetting data) rather than a bi-level one. The inner objective’s rationale is unclear; a more intuitive approach would ensure that after unlearning, model parameters match those obtained from training only on D_r (R3). [Reviewer 3, score 3]
- **No theoretical guarantee** for the alternating optimization procedure. Could maximization (scrubbing) be done first followed by fine-tuning? The relative merits are unexplored (R5). [Reviewer 5, score 5]
- **Hyperparameter λ** is not defined in the paper; it presumably balances retention and forgetting but needs clarification (R1). [Reviewer 1, score 6]
- **Figure 3 is not readable** – axis labels, y-axis meaning (frequency?), and interpretation are unclear (R1). [Reviewer 1, score 6]
- **Lack of robustness analysis** – how effective is the unlearning against adversarial attacks designed to recover forgotten data (R2)? [Reviewer 2, score 6]
- **Connections to [1],[2],[3]** are insufficiently discussed. For instance, [1] also fine-tunes the score network to minimize generation probability of specific classes (R1). [Reviewer 1, score 6]
- **Minor typo:** page 3 says “the variational bond” instead of “bound” above equation (1) (R5). [Reviewer 5, score 5]

**Contrasting reviewer opinions:**  
One reviewer (score 3) rates soundness and contribution as “poor,” finding the formulation misguided and contribution limited. Another reviewer (score 8) rates contribution as “excellent,” praising the significance and clarity. A third reviewer (score 5) notes that empirical performance fails to outperform baselines, undermining the claimed tradeoff.
