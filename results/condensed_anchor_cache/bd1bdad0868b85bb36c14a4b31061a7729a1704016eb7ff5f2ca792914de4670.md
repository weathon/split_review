## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes conjugate and splitting integrators to accelerate diffusion sampling, including a hybrid method. Reviewers agree that the formulation is novel and promising, the writing is clear, and the hybrid method shows strong results on small datasets. However, opinions diverge: one reviewer found no weaknesses (score 8), while three reviewers raised substantial concerns about incomplete comparisons, theoretical gaps, arbitrary design choices, missing large-scale and conditional experiments, and questionable baseline implementations. Overall, the paper presents a potentially impactful framework but requires major revisions to address these issues.

### Strengths
- The formulation of conjugate and splitting integrators is fundamental and sound, providing a unified perspective that generalizes existing samplers like DDIM. [R1, R4]
- The theoretical analysis of stability and truncation error is solid. [R1]
- The hybrid method achieves excellent FID scores for low-to-medium NFE (e.g., 2.11 deterministic, 2.36 stochastic at 100 NFE on CIFAR-10), outperforming several SOTA baselines. [R2, R3]
- The paper is clearly written, well-structured, and demonstrates a clever application of numerical integration from physics. [R2, R3, R4]
- The work has high potential impact for improving sample quality under fixed computational budgets. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Redundant NFE regime**: 50–100 NFE may be excessive; typical state-of-the-art (e.g., DPM-Solver) achieves good results in 15–25 steps. The experimental focus on higher NFE reduces practical relevance. [R1]
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: No comparison with DPM-Solver, despite similarity and influence. It is unclear how the proposed method relates to DPM-Solver and whether it can benefit from prediction-correction techniques. [R1] Additionally, the EDM + preconditioning baseline (which achieves 1.97 FID at 35 NFE) is omitted from Figure 5; this would have outperformed the paper’s best result (2.11 at 100 NFE). A direct comparison is needed. [R4]
- **DDIM baseline inconsistency**: The DDIM baseline in Figure 2(a) reports FID >50 at NFE=10, while the original DDIM paper reports 13.36. Such a large discrepancy raises concerns about the experimental setup or implementation. [R4]
- **Incomplete evaluation on large-scale/conditional tasks**: Results are limited to small datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA-64, AFHQ-64). No experiments on higher-resolution images (≥512), class-conditional generation, or text-conditional generation (e.g., ImageNet, Stable Diffusion). [R1, R2, R4]
- **Theoretical gaps in stability and error analysis**: 
  - The stability analysis (Theorem 2) is considered naive; it assumes stable invertibility of \(A_t\) without explaining when this condition holds. \(A_t\) is an integral of multiple components, so it is not obvious when it is stably invertible. A two-phase analysis (when \(A_t\) is invertible, then when the ODE solver is stable) is desired, along with clarification of real-world scenarios where conditions are satisfied. [R1]
  - Theorem 3 analyzes truncation error in \(h\) rather than \(t\); an analysis in the original time variable is preferred (as in DPM-Solver). [R1]
  - No theoretical comparison of truncation errors across all proposed integrators; the paper relies mostly on numerics. [R4]
- **Arbitrary design choices and unclear contributions**: 
  - There are many proposed integrators without clear guidance on when to prefer which (e.g., DDIM-I vs DDIM-II, choice of \(B_t = \lambda I\) or \(\lambda 1\)). The rationale behind \(B_t\) choices is not theoretically justified. [R4]
  - No theory is provided for DDIM-II, which performed best. [R4]
  - The hybrid conjugate+splitting integrators do not consistently outperform the non-conjugate versions (e.g., Reduced OBA beats Conjugate OBA in Table 2). The paper attributes this to suboptimal \(B_t\) without analysis, which confuses the reader. [R4]
- **Missing implementation details for augmented space**:
  - The initial momentum \(m_0\) and training procedures (Algorithms 2, 3) are not specified. [R2]
  - It is not discussed whether the position transformation \(x_t\) remains a diffeomorphism in the augmented setting; if not, density estimation and mode coverage could be harmed. [R2]
- **Hyperparameter selection unclear**: Corollary 1 relates stability to \(\lambda\), but it is not explained how \(\lambda\) is chosen in practice, nor does it explain why \(\lambda\)-DDIM-II performs best. [R2]
- **Inconsistent baseline coverage**: In Figure 5, EDM is not tested on CelebA-64; experiments should be extended to ImageNet to demonstrate scalability. [R2]
- **Unintuitive sampler behavior**: The relative performance of deterministic vs. stochastic samplers reverses at low vs. high NFE in Figure 5(bottom), contradicting common knowledge. An explanation is needed. [R4]
- **Speed claims not verified**: Total integration time is not reported; generation speed should be measured to support claims of acceleration. [R2]
- **Minority positive review**: One reviewer found no weaknesses and gave the highest score (8), while the other three reviewers raised significant concerns, indicating a clear disagreement about the paper's readiness.
