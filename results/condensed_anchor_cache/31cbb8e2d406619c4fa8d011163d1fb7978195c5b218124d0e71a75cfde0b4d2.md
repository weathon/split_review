## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles the storage overhead of soft labels in dataset distillation by proposing HeLlO (Heavy Labels Out). Instead of storing large soft-label sets, the method generates labels online from synthetic images using image-to-label projectors built from CLIP and fine-tuned with a LoRA-like strategy. Experiments on large-scale datasets (ImageNet-1K, Places365) show that HeLlO achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art distillation methods while using only ~0.003% of the original soft-label storage.

### Strengths
- Clear, well-motivated problem: reducing the storage cost of soft labels in dataset distillation, which is an under-researched aspect of the field (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The proposed method (image-to-label projector via CLIP + LoRA) is simple, effective, and novel in blending recent advances from adjacent domains (Reviewers 1,3).
- Significant storage reduction is demonstrated (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- Results are convincing, with ablations and downstream applications (e.g., continual learning) (Reviewer 3).
- Comparable performance to state-of-the-art methods on large-scale datasets (Reviewer 4).
- Writing is clear and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,4).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and scope of storage problem**: Reviewer 3 doubts whether soft-label storage is a significant bottleneck in practice, especially at low IPC (e.g., 1–50 images/class, soft labels are only a few GB on ImageNet-1K at 10 IPC) and suggests comparing with simple compression methods (e.g., logit quantization). Reviewer 1 notes that the storage of classifier weights (0.7 MB) is negligible compared to labels.
- **Teacher model storage and fair comparison**: The comparison in Table 1 is considered unfair because the proposed method requires storing a teacher model (or its low-rank version), while baseline methods do not. A comprehensive comparison including both teacher model and label storage for all methods is requested (Reviewer 2). Missing entries in Table 1 also hinder comparison; at minimum, IPC 50 results for SRe2L and G-VBSM on Places365-Standard should be included, as HeLlO underperforms those baselines (Reviewer 2).
- **Technical choices**: Why use CLIP instead of stronger supervised models (e.g., supervised ViT) or self-supervised models (e.g., DINOv2)? If we can generate labels with CLIP, why not use it directly for the target task? And why not fine-tune the foundation model (CLIP or DINOv2) on the distilled dataset with LoRA? (Reviewer 1). The definition of “weak teacher” (line 237) and whether it differs from the teacher in line 162 is unclear (Reviewer 2). The reviewer also asks about using a model pre-trained on the original real dataset as the label projector (Reviewer 2).
- **Effectiveness of components**: The improvements from Text-Embedding-Based Init and Image Update are marginal (≈0.1 accuracy). Most gains come from LoRA-Like Knowledge Transfer (Reviewer 1). Units: ‘M’ should be ‘MB’ (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing evaluations and comparisons**:
    - Missing comparisons with DREAM, DataDAM, SeqMatch in Table 2 (Reviewer 4).
    - No evaluation of cross-architecture generalization on ViT models (Reviewer 4).
    - No tests on high-resolution datasets (256×256 ImageNet-1k) or practical datasets (clinical, aerial) (Reviewer 4).
    - Performance at higher IPC (100, 200) not reported (Reviewer 4).
    - No analysis of distillation costs (memory, training time) comparing to state-of-the-art (Reviewer 4).
    - Downstream tasks (object detection, segmentation) not explored (Reviewers 3,4).
    - No comparison with logit compression techniques (e.g., quantization) (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Poor writing with typos and ambiguous notations in the proof of Proposition 1 (e.g., both \(r_i\) and \(r^{(i)}\) used for class text description; \(v_T\) vs \(v_T^{(i)}\); \(W\) defined as both a matrix and a set) (Reviewer 2). Figure 1 has words covered by symbols, reducing clarity (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing details in tables**: Table 1 lacks “Size of Labels” for L Yin et al. (2024) and Sun et al. (2024) (Reviewer 1).
