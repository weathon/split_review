## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores how vision-and-language models (VLMs) encode tasks across modalities via task vectors derived from few-shot exemplars or instructions. The authors demonstrate that task vectors are cross-modal—transferable between text and image—and can be extracted from language-only models for use in VLMs. They also show that ensembling exemplar- and instruction-based vectors yields better task representations, and find a consistent three-stage token evolution (input, task, answer) across modalities. Experiments support these findings, though methodological clarity and evaluation breadth are concerns.

### Strengths
- Cross-modal task vectors are an innovative contribution, advancing multi-modal interpretability by showing that VLMs generalize tasks across input modalities (e.g., text to image) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The paper reveals a consistent three-stage token evolution (input, task, answer) across modalities, providing deeper insight into VLM internal mechanisms (Reviewer 3).
- Demonstrates that task vectors from language-only models can be applied in VLMs, underscoring versatility of cross-modal representations (Reviewer 3).
- The experiment is thoughtfully designed and well-executed, fully exploring the task space for LLaVA-like auto-regressive models (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The paper is the first to explore multi-modal few-shot task learning via patch/steering learning, and is significant for mechanistic interpretability and model steering (Reviewer 2).
- Figures are intuitive and results are clearly presented overall (Reviewer 2).
- Well-written and easy to understand (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and description** (major for Reviewer 2, also noted by Reviewers 1 and 3):
  - The method is extremely difficult to follow; the paper does not clearly state until page 5 that it focuses on auto-regressive VLMs like LLaVA. Notation is unusual and appears borrowed from prior work on generative image models, causing confusion (Reviewer 2).
  - The patching process at test time is never described: how the task vector is extracted and injected into the model (e.g., aligned across different embedding spaces of text and image). No hyper-parameters (e.g., normalization, scaling factor) are given (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - The logit lens is introduced and used (Figure 3) without explanation of how it works (Reviewer 2).
  - Key elements like xPatch, xBase, and the combination of Instruction + Exemplar xPatch are not described clearly or in sufficient detail (Reviewers 1, 3). The mapping \(G\) that generates the task vector is not explained—which layers/operations are used and how it differs between text and image (Reviewer 1).
  - Reviewer 2 states: “While I believe the proposed method would be of interest to the community, I cannot recommend the paper in its current form.”

- **Limited methodological novelty**: The method appears to be a straightforward extension of prior work on function vectors / visual task vectors. The applicability is limited to MLLMs with a feature encoder (e.g., LLaVA); models like QWen without such an encoder are incompatible. This limitation should be acknowledged more clearly (Reviewer 1).

- **Insufficient validation and reliance on qualitative evidence**:
  - The paper is validated only on a narrow range of task types; expanding evaluation to more diverse tasks would strengthen claims (Reviewer 3).
  - For experiments such as task conflict and image ICL transfer, the paper primarily presents qualitative examples without quantitative metrics, making it hard to assess robustness and generalizability (Reviewer 1).

- **Contradictory or unsupported claims** (Reviewer 1):
  - Lines 241–244 initially state text and image ICL top-1 decoding are similar, then claim alignment with language is “not immediately obvious” for image ICL. The table shows no significant difference. The speculation about mapping to “unused nonsense tokens” lacks supporting evidence; analysis of the embedding spaces is needed.
  
- **Missing details and errors**:
  - The paper references an appendix that does not exist (Reviewer 1).
  - Line 351 should refer to Table 4 instead of Figure 4 (Reviewer 1).
  - Table 1 has a hard time rendering (high-resolution image); lowering resolution is recommended (Reviewer 2).

- **Minor reviewer note**: Reviewer 2 felt misdirected because mechanistic interpretability/model steering is not foregrounded, despite being the underlying technique.
