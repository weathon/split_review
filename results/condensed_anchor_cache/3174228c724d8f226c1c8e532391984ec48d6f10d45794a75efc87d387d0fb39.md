## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies transfer-based attacks on skeleton-based human action recognition (S-HAR), identifies loss surface sharpness as a key cause of poor adversarial transferability, and proposes the first transfer-based attack, TASAR. TASAR uses a post-train Dual Bayesian optimization strategy with an appended MLP to smooth the loss surface and incorporates motion dynamics (first-order velocity and second-order acceleration) to disrupt spatial-temporal coherence. The authors build a large-scale robust S-HAR benchmark comprising 7 S-HAR models, 10 attack methods, 3 datasets (NTU-60, NTU-120, HDM05), and 2 defense models, and evaluate extensively. Reviewers’ opinions vary: one (score 8) found the method reasonable and experiments extensive, while another (score 5) raised major concerns about clarity and motivation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written with clear motivations for each step.
- The proposed approach is easy to apply on existing methods since only a few layers are needed at the end of the network.
- Thorough experiments and ablation studies; extensive experiments overall.
- The post-train Dual Bayesian Motion attack method is reasonable in general.
- The paper identifies a crucial limitation of existing S-HAR attacks and proposes a practical solution; TASAR is innovative and demonstrates potential improvements in adversarial transferability.
- Large-scale evaluation on the benchmark provides valuable insights into the effectiveness of existing and proposed methods.
- The authors have submitted the code, making the work reproducible.
- Some figures are quite nice and provide insights.
- Overall, the research problem is interesting.

### Weaknesses
- Modeling p(θ'|D, θ) with frozen pretrained models is not as good as modeling p(θ, θ'|D); if the model were trainable, it would be better to justify the performance difference on a small subset of data.
- Besides visualization of loss surfaces, quantitative evaluation of surface smoothness should be added.
- In Table 4, why does K=3 yield better results than K=5? One would expect more samples to lead to better approximation (unexplained ablation pattern).
- Although it is reasonable that a smoother loss landscape benefits transfer attacks, directly concluding that smoothing the loss landscape is the sole solution may not be entirely logical; comparing differences among surrogate models could reveal other factors.
- The consideration of first-order velocity and second-order acceleration has already been addressed by SMART (Wang et al., 2021), which limits novelty.
- The Bayesian optimization approach is similar to BEAT (Wang et al., 2023); the differences between the two should be discussed further.
- Some concepts are not properly explained: e.g., “white-box scenarios”, “its sharpness contributes to weak transferability”.
- Fig. 1 is not clearly introduced: what are spatial attack, spatial-temporal attack, frozen/trainable, lightweight/demanding, appended models, dual Bayesian? How to tell which is better?
- The introduction is not well-motivated; Table 1 concepts (e.g., “white-box”, “low adversarial transferability”) are not explained before being referenced.
- In related work, the differences between existing works and the proposed method (insights, how challenges are addressed) are not highlighted.
- Fig. 2 font sizes are invisible; x and y axes and “random direction vectors” are not explained.
- Math notation is confusing: Eq. (2) boldface x and tilde x; “clean motion” and “adversarial sample” in Lines 158 and 183; Eq. (3) is not explained step by step. A notation section is suggested.
- Minor presentation issues: remove full stop in Sec. 3.4.1 and 3.4.2 headings; table font sizes are inconsistent (main paper tables too small, appendix table 6 too large).
- The paper relies heavily on tables; more plots and visualizations would improve evaluation and interest for a wider community.
- The authors should cite more recent top conference papers from the last two years.
- More related literature could be included, e.g., a topic on noise-robust human action recognition.
