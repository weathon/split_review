## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes COCOA (COmpositional COnservatism with Anchor-seeking) for offline RL, which applies transductive reparameterization by decomposing states into an anchor and a difference. Using a reverse dynamics model, the approach seeks in-distribution anchors and differences to achieve conservatism in the compositional input space. COCOA is model-agnostic and can be combined with any actor-critic offline RL algorithm. Experiments on D4RL Gym MuJoCo tasks show that adding COCOA improves performance for four base algorithms (CQL, IQL, MOPO, MOBILE), particularly in medium-expert domains where excess conservatism may be a problem. However, not all algorithms benefit equally; average gains are modest (~0.8 points overall decrease per Reviewer 3’s calculation) and improvements occur in only about 58 % of tasks. The approach is novel (extending bilinear transduction from supervised learning to RL) and ablation studies confirm the anchor-seeking component is crucial.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and provides code for reproducibility.
- Introduces a novel perspective on conservatism: compositional input space conservatism via anchors and differences, distinct from prior behavioral conservatism.
- COCOA is agnostic to the base offline RL algorithm and is shown to improve four different state-of-the-art methods (CQL, IQL, MOPO, MOBILE).
- Experimental results are solid and thorough across many D4RL benchmark tasks, with particularly strong gains on medium-expert domains (reviewer 2 highlights this as “very interesting”).
- The ablation study comparing the proposed anchor-seeking procedure to a baseline anchor selection demonstrates that the anchor-seeking method is essential for good performance.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation domain:** All experiments are performed on Gym MuJoCo tasks only. Evaluation on additional D4RL domains (AntMaze, Adroit) or an offline-to-online setup is missing (reviewers 1 and 3 request this; reviewer 3 considers it “essential” at least for IQL and MOBILE).
- **Inconsistent improvement across algorithms and tasks:** COCOA improves performance in only ~58 % of cases and can decrease performance dramatically (reviewer 3 computes an average drop of 0.8 points across the whole table). Specifically, IQL shows little improvement or even suffers from COCOA; the authors are asked to provide insight into why IQL does not benefit (reviewers 1 and 3). Reviewer 3 also notes that for MOPO and MOBILE, hyperparameters were heavily tuned for each dataset, which may explain why they benefit more than other algorithms.
- **Missing comparisons with related model-based approaches:** The method is conceptually similar to forward dynamics–based offline RL (e.g., COMBO), and a direct comparison between COCOA + CQL and COMBO would be informative (reviewer 2). Likewise, recent work showing that synthetic experience replay from generative models improves RL should be cited or compared (reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 considers these “not huge weaknesses” but worth exploring.
- **Training world models is time-consuming:** Reviewer 3 raises a concern about computational cost and asks for the training time of algorithms with and without COCOA.
- **Lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis:** Reviewer 3 asks whether adding COCOA makes the hyperparameters of MOPO/MOBILE more sensitive, and suggests using EOP (arXiv:2110.04156) for this purpose.
- **Missing details and ablations:**
    - Why does the reverse policy use random divergent rollouts instead of training a reverse policy? An ablation on this design choice is requested (reviewer 1).
    - What are IQL scores on random datasets? (reviewer 3)
    - How does COCOA behave with offline RL algorithms that regularize the policy, such as ReBRAC (arXiv:2305.09836, a state‑of‑the‑art algorithm from that family)? (reviewer 3)
    - Average scores for each algorithm (with and without COCOA) should be added to Table 1 so the overall impact is clear (reviewer 3).
- **Minor text issues:** The second paragraph of Section 2.1 duplicates information from the first paragraph; the Results section contains a typo: “FFor IQL” should be “For IQL” (reviewer 3).

**Reviewer disagreement note:** Reviewer 2 (score 8, high confidence) found the paper very solid and had “no real big questions”, while Reviewer 3 (score 5, high confidence) was more critical, citing limited scope, inconsistent gains, and missing comparisons. Reviewer 1 (score 6, moderate confidence) raised several specific technical questions.
