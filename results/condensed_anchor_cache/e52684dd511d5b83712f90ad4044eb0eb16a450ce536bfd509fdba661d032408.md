## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the concept of “hidden waves” – the successful reconstruction of images using a set of one-way wave equations with hidden, learnable speeds. Each image corresponds to a solution with a unique initial condition computed from the image via a visual encoder (e.g., CNN). The solution decomposes into a collection of first-order autoregressive special solutions with shared coefficient matrices, and the product of these matrices forms a diagonal matrix with the wave speeds as diagonal entries. The paper extends the prior FINOLA framework in two ways: (a) generalizing it to a set of one-way wave equations and (b) relaxing local constraints. Numerical results show improved PSNR (e.g., from 24.8 to 27.8) when using a superposition of multiple FINOLA solutions.

### Strengths
- The theoretical intuition behind using multiple-path FINOLA is discussed thoroughly, and the paper shows that FINOLA is a special case of the proposed method (Reviewer 1).
- The diagonalization idea is interesting, as it simplifies the way to solve the wave equations, and the model defines a powerful way to represent natural images with a limited number of parameters (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well written, and the continuous interpretation of FINOLA as a solution to a wave equation is very interesting. The fundamental question of whether certain wave equations are a common mathematical principle for image reconstruction has large potential if answered positively (Reviewer 3).
- The section comparing “hidden wave representation” with image autoregression or transformations is well written and provides clear insights into the encoding (Reviewer 4).
- The authors conducted numerous experiments to validate the improvements they implemented, developing a deep understanding of FINOLA through mathematical enhancements and experiments (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Mathematical clarity and rigor**: The central idea of diagonalization lacks clear justification. It is not guaranteed that the real-valued, learnable matrices \(A\) and \(B\) (Eq. 2) make \(AB^{-1}\) diagonalizable with real eigenvalues; in general, \(V\) and \(\Lambda\) could be complex-valued (Reviewer 2). The motivation for studying the real-valued eigenvalues \(\lambda_k\) in Table 2 is unclear, and the assumption that \(AB^{-1}\) is diagonalizable (even over \(\mathbb{C}\)) is not mathematically guaranteed – it may only occur because the set of non‑diagonalizable matrices is measure-zero in learning (Reviewer 2). Notation is not rigorous; e.g., it is unclear why \(x_i(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^C\) (top of page 4) (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: To support the claim that wave equations (or FINOLA) are a fundamental mathematical property shared by all natural images, the paper lacks comparisons to other encoding-decoding techniques, such as truncated PCA, DCT/wavelet coefficient retention, and standard trained convolutional autoencoders. The proposed approach would need to perform significantly better than these competing methods (Reviewer 3). After the rebuttal, to claim that a superposition of FINOLAs is one of the strongest latent compression techniques, more detailed comparisons with learned image compression works are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental validation concerns**: 
    - In Figure 4, it is unclear whether reconstruction quality plateaus with respect to the hyperparameters \(M\) and \(C\); the curves appear to increase linearly without signs of saturation (Reviewer 1).
    - The experimental design overall yields uncertain conclusions due to a limited number of data points and a lack of significant differences between conditions. For example, the interpretation of Table 3 remains unclear – does it suggest that initial condition positions should be chosen differently from the center? (Reviewer 4). Many experiments are conducted from various perspectives but do not produce meaningful insights (Reviewer 4).
    - The decoder is linear, which may cause loss of image details (e.g., sharp edges); this is not apparent from the PSNR metrics alone, and visual examples should be provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Model and training details missing**: 
    - It is not clearly shown which parts of Figure 1 are trainable. Is the encoder also trained, and if not, how is it defined? What is the training loss? (Reviewer 2).
    - Technical details (e.g., the architecture of the convolutional autoencoder) are missing, making it difficult to assess the contribution (Reviewer 3). 
    - Hyperparameter sensitivity and training time are not reported (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of interpretation and future direction**: 
    - There is no interpretation of the latent space encodings \(q_i\) or the corresponding autoregressive solutions \(z_i\), nor any motivation for why the wave equation could be of particular importance to images (Reviewer 3).
    - It remains unclear how this method could be applied in future research or practical applications; the paper would be improved by discussing potential future perspectives (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Quotation marks are missing in some places (should use `...'), and some citation brackets are omitted (e.g., “known as FINOLA Chen et al. (2023)”). The citation for Chen et al. (FINOLA) lacks a journal, conference, or arXiv ID and should be completed (Reviewers 3, 4).
