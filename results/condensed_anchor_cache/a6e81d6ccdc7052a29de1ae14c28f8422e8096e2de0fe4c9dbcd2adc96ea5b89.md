## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes INSIDE, a method for hallucination detection in LLMs by leveraging their internal states. The key contribution is the EigenScore metric, computed as the log determinant of the covariance matrix of internal states from multiple sampled responses, measuring semantic consistency (differential entropy). A test-time feature clipping technique truncates extreme activations to reduce overconfident hallucinations. Experiments on QA benchmarks with LLaMA and OPT models demonstrate state-of-the-art performance over baselines including perplexity, lexical similarity, length-normalized entropy, and energy. Ablation studies verify both components.

### Strengths
- Studies a timely and important problem: reducing hallucinations at inference time (R1).
- Well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).
- Novel angle: using internal states and EigenScore for hallucination detection (R2, R3). The method is simple, intuitive, and easy to implement without model tuning (R3, R4).
- Significant performance improvement over established uncertainty-based baselines (R1, R3, R4).
- Solid experimental setup: diverse datasets, multiple LLMs, and thorough ablation studies (R2, R4). R4 particularly noted complete ablations and clear explanation of the method’s advantage on short-answer benchmarks.
- One reviewer (R4, score 8) found presentation and soundness excellent with only minor suggestions; the other three reviewers (score 6) noted several limitations and areas for improvement.

### Weaknesses
- Missing comparison with internal-state-based hallucination detection methods, e.g., Li et al. (2023) (ITI) and Representation Engineering (R1, R2, R3). R3 noted that novelty may be diminished if concurrent works are considered.
- No ablation on which transformer block’s internal state is used; the choice of layer is unspecified (R1).
- Method is not applicable to blackbox LLMs (e.g., GPT, PaLM, Claude) since it requires internal states; this limitation is not discussed (R2).
- Computational cost is a concern: sampling-based approach may be expensive for real-world deployment; no cost estimation or reduction strategy provided (R2). Reviewer 2 requested resource estimation.
- The connection between uncertainty estimation and hallucination detection is not clearly established despite promising results (R2).
- Beyond detection, it is unclear if the framework can also mitigate hallucinations (R2).
- Missing baseline comparisons, including selfcheckGPT and semantic uncertainty (ICLR 2023) (R2). Also missing comparison with ITI on TruthfulQA (R2, R3).
- Experiment setting is limited to QA benchmarks; broader hallucination types and datasets (e.g., TruthfulQA) and newer LLMs (LLaMA2, Falcon) are missing (R2, R3).
- Covariance of internal states may capture distracting information (expression, style, terminology), raising concerns about generalization (R3).
- Need exact match score to enable comparison with other works (R3).
- Feature clipping requires a dynamic memory bank; it is unclear why this is necessary and whether activation distributions vary significantly across samples (R3).
- For the clipping technique: no correlation analysis between outlier activations and confidence/hallucination; no examples of outliers; distribution across layers not analyzed (R4).
- Table 2: it is unclear whether clipping improves correct answers or only reduces confidence of incorrect ones (R4).
- No qualitative examples of hallucinations uniquely detected by EigenScore compared to baselines (R4).
- Typos: "centralized matrix" should be "centering matrix"; "prior works that rectifying" is ungrammatical; "nli-roterta-large" is misspelled (R4).
- R3 rated soundness only 2 (fair), indicating methodological concerns.
- Post-rebuttal, Reviewer 2 noted that the authors’ response and experiments addressed their concerns, leading them to maintain their positive evaluation.
