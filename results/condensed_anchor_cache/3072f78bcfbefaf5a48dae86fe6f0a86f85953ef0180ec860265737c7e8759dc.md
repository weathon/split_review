## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OTP-DAG, a framework for learning parameters of directed graphical models by minimizing the Wasserstein distance between the empirical and model marginal distributions of observed variables, using reversed kernels for hidden variables. Reviewers find the idea of combining optimal transport with parameter learning interesting and the derivations clear, but express significant concerns about the novelty and contribution, the experimental support, and the computational cost. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive and willing to increase score if experiments are strengthened; another (score 3) is more negative, finding the contribution unclear and the paper repackaging existing ideas. The third reviewer (score 5) is moderate.

### Strengths
- The paper is clear and well-structured.
- The proposed OTP-DAG is derived step-by-step with solid mathematical proofs, making the method intuitive.
- The idea of combining optimal transport with parameter learning of probabilistic models is interesting.
- The method is presented as versatile and scalable to different models and applications (topic models, HMMs, discrete representation learning), suggesting broad practicality.
- Optimal transport is a popular approach with broad interest; parameter estimation in DAGs is a classic topic.

### Weaknesses
- **Contribution unclear / novelty insufficient:** Multiple reviewers struggle to identify the main contribution. The claim that minimizing transport cost equals minimizing reconstruction error is noted to already appear in VAEs and WAEs, and is not clearly distinguished as new. The introduction and text contain many “empty claims” (e.g., “laying a foundation stone,” “entirely different view”) without concrete insight into how this approach is related to or differs from existing methods (VI, autoencoders, etc.). The paper is seen as repackaging known results rather than presenting a compelling novel framework [Reviewer 3, score 3].
- **Experimental support weak / missing comparisons:** 
  - For LDA, Gibbs sampling is not included as a baseline.
  - For §4.1 (LDA), authors should provide a table of mean parameter estimation error w.r.t. true parameters (similar to Appendix Table 4) and identify a problem where OTP-DAG clearly outperforms all baselines.
  - For §4.2 (HMM), the synthetic data generation is not credible: why not sample hidden \(Z_t\) from the Markov process instead of specifying state-changing points? The experiments should test multiple true parameter sets (e.g., randomly sampled from a hyperprior) and report metrics with means and error bars. Also, why not learn the transition probabilities \(p\)? Traditional EM can learn the transition matrix, so if OTP-DAG is not even comparable to EM, this example is only acceptable, not supportive.
  - Experimental setup is introduced before the baselines, which inverts the logical order for informative contrasts.
- **Latent variable inference:** Less discussed and compared; the paper focuses on parameter estimation but does not adequately evaluate or benchmark latent variable inference.
- **Computational cost / complexity:** Gradient descent in Equation (2) appears to sum over all parent nodes \(PA_{X_O}\), which may be very costly. A comparison to belief propagation or message-passing algorithms (in terms of complexity) is requested but absent.
- **Clarification needed on statement about VI:** Page 2, line 8: “where the data distribution is the source and the true model distribution is the target” – this is confusing because VI minimizes KL between two posteriors, not between data and model distributions.
- **Clarity of certain claims and terminology:** 
  - “While the formulation in Eq. (1) is not trainable” – what does “not trainable” mean?
  - “for solving it efficiently” – what is “it”?
  - “Instead of achieving state-of-the-art performance on specific applications” – please explain why not.
  - The “more challenging setting” of Discrete RepL (VQ-VAE) is not adequately motivated: what makes it challenging, and why should the reader care?
  - “except the special setting of Discrete RepL” – what makes it special?
  - Figure 1’s purpose is unclear.
  - The future research section is not informative.
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** While one reviewer (score 6) finds the method promising and would increase score with stronger experiments, the other (score 3) finds the contribution unclear and not compelling, indicating a gap between potential and current presentation.
