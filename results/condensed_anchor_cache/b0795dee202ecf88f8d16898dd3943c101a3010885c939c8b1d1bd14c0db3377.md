## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies Vision Transformers (ViTs) on the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC), using a supervised, large-data (1M examples per task), single-task training setting—a departure from the original few-shot challenge. It first shows that a standard ViT fails dramatically on most ARC tasks. The paper then proposes a series of architectural modifications (pixel-level 1×1 tokens, 2D positional encodings, row-padding with `<arc_pad>` and `<arc_endxgrid>` tokens, a Positional Encoding Mixer (PEmixer), and an Object-based Positional Encoding (OPE) via OpenCV contour detection) that together form the ViTARC model. ViTARC achieves near-100% test solve rates on more than half of the 400 public ARC tasks. The work highlights the importance of spatial and object-level inductive biases for visual reasoning in transformers.

### Strengths

- The paper studies an interesting and important problem: visual abstract reasoning in the ARC benchmark, which demands strong reasoning capabilities from vision models.
- The findings on the importance of positional encodings and 2D representations are valuable for the community. (Reviewer 1)
- The detailed, incremental presentation of model improvements (ViT‑Vanilla → ViTARC‑VT → ViTARC) aids reproducibility and understanding of what works. (R1, R2)
- The analysis in Figure 6 (attention maps) helps explain what the model does and does not attend to. (R2)
- The quantitative improvement over a naive ViT baseline is clear and significant, demonstrating that the proposed modifications imbue ViTs with new capabilities. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The paper explores a popular architecture (ViT), so the findings are of broad interest. (R4)
- The demonstration that ViTs fail on ARC despite abundant data points at intrinsic representational deficiencies of the architecture. (R4)

### Weaknesses

*Note: Reviewer 4 rated the paper 8 and found no significant flaws, while Reviewers 1–3 rated 5 and raised the concerns below.*

- **Unclear generalization and evaluation protocol.** The training/evaluation setup is not clearly defined. All tasks used for evaluation have some training examples seen during training; the paper does not test generalization to unseen tasks (zero-shot transfer). (R1, R3)  
  One reviewer (R3) asks whether the model works on other complex reasoning tasks outside ARC (e.g., Raven’s progressive matrices).

- **Marginal novelty and over-engineering for ARC.** Several key techniques (2D relative positional encoding, padding tokens) are not new and appear straightforward. The overall contributions are incremental. (R1, R3, R4)  
  The architectural changes (2D padding with `<arc_pad>` and `<arc_endxgrid>` tokens, border tokens, object PE) are highly specific to ARC and do not clearly transfer to general visual reasoning. (R3)  
  The model is endowed with strong a priori knowledge about grid structure (e.g., that one token corresponds to one grid cell), which sidesteps a core ARC challenge—inferring output dimensions. (R3)  
  Reviewer 4 notes that the task itself (supervised, single-task, large-data) is uninteresting and makes no progress toward the original ARC challenge; the contributions will only have value if shown useful on a meaningful task.

- **Disconnect and lack of ablation across contributions.** The paper reads as a sequence of attempts without a clear final justification. Several later additions (PEmixer, OPE) actually *decrease* performance when added on top of ViTARC‑VT, but this is not properly ablated or explained. (R2)  
  For example, learnable embeddings are removed in favor of positional embeddings, then a PEmixer is needed to learn position-specific weights, and later RPE is added because APE encodes spatial changes too slowly. This suggests the final encoding should be presented directly rather than as a patchwork. (R2)

- **Unclear necessity of padding and EOS tokens.** The original sequential padding (ignored in attention) should be equivalent to per‑row padding, and the per‑row `<arc_endxgrid>` tokens alone might suffice. The final model still uses attended padding tokens that require correction, raising questions about efficiency and necessity. (R2)  
  The paper does not ablate the padding scheme (e.g., comparing with no attended padding, or with a single end‑of‑grid token). (R2)

- **Model departs from vision modeling.** By encoding pixels as a 1×1 grid (effectively a sequence), adding per‑row end‑of‑sequence tokens, and applying object‑based positional encodings that abstract away low-level vision, the model is more a structured prediction system than a vision model. The lessons learned may be about structured predictions rather than visual reasoning. (R2)  
  In typical vision tasks (classification, detection), token content matters more than spatial relationships. (R2)

- **No baselines or comparisons.** The only baseline is the authors’ own vanilla ViT. There are no comparisons to other approaches (e.g., LLM‑based sequence models, prior ARC methods) nor performance on the private ARC test set. (R2, R3)

- **OPE based on OpenCV contours will struggle on complex shapes.** The objectness that OPE provides should ideally be learned implicitly by visual tokens. (R3)

- **Missing ablations and clarifications.** Several design choices are not ablated: hyperparameters α and β in Equation (10) (R1); whether 100k samples would be sufficient or if 1M are needed (R2); the effect of joint training with task IDs (R2); the influence of architectural changes on other vision tasks (R2).  
  The derivation of Equation (12) for computing the relative encodings \(r_{left}, r_{right}\) is unclear. (R1)  
  Figure 8 (Appendix A) appears to motivate a key technical contribution and should be in the main paper. (R2)

- **Minor presentation issues.** The large variance in Figure 7 for ViTARC‑VT is not explained. (R1)  
  The task in Figure 2 (6e02f1e3) would benefit from an explanation; a vanilla ViT may appear superhuman on it. (R4)
