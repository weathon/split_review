## Merged Review

### Summary

All four reviewers rate the paper 3, acknowledging a theoretical contribution to affine invariance in continuous-domain CNNs but pointing to major shortcomings that prevent publication. Reviewers agree that the problem is important and the mathematical analysis is rigorous, but they uniformly criticize the absence of any empirical validation, poor presentation quality, unclear motivation, sloppy notation, and several technical errors. The paper lacks a convincing case for practical relevance and reads as a work in progress. Disagreement exists on novelty and contribution: Reviewer 1 assesses contribution as 2 (fair), while Reviewers 2 and 4 assess it as 3 (good); Reviewer 3 finds the novelty not strong, whereas Reviewer 4 sees the extension to GL₂ as a good theoretical improvement.

### Strengths

- The paper tackles the important and challenging problem of affine invariance/equivariance in neural networks for visual data, which extends beyond isometric or similarity groups and is of significant interest to the geometric deep learning community. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- The mathematical analysis is rigorous, with detailed proofs provided. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- The introduction of a new criterion (ε-affine invariance) and the analysis of lifted signals and integration over G₂ are contributions of some significance. (Reviewer 3)
- The approach, in theory, should be of practical value for achieving affine-equivariant representations. (Reviewer 2)
- Relaxing previous results from subgroups like SO₂ to the full GL₂ group is a good theoretical improvement. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- **No empirical validation**: The paper lacks any experimental component, even a toy example verifying Theorem 4, which is a significant omission given the computational motivation. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Poor presentation and organization**: The paper reads as a work in progress and requires substantial polishing. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
  - Motivation and problem statement are not clearly introduced upfront; the goal of the paper is hard to follow. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
  - Background material is cluttered and presented in a confusing order (e.g., cosets introduced after normal groups, notations scattered throughout Section 1). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
  - The first three theorems (1–3) are nearly identical and could be merged; an intuitive explanation of their purpose is missing. (Reviewer 2)
  - Section 2.2 (the main computational result) feels rushed and is difficult to grasp. (Reviewer 2)
  - Equations should be numbered for clarity. (Reviewer 4)
  - General writing contains typos, grammatical errors, and imprecise language (e.g., “Our focus are affine spaced…”, “First of all, the input f is usually a picture…”, a broken LaTeX reference on page 6). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- **Notation and definition errors**:
  - Definition 2 (Lie groups) is incorrect: “symmetries of Riemannian manifolds” is not a definition. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
  - Definition 6 (Coset) is incomplete: notations are undefined, only left cosets are given. (Reviewer 4)
  - Definition 4 introduces α but never uses it; instead ⊙ is used but not defined. (Reviewer 2)
  - In Definition 4 (planar correlation), the factor 1/|det h| appears redundant with Equation (2). (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - Equation (4) omits the determinant of A or B, which played a role in earlier theory (Definition 10, Theorem 1). (Reviewer 1)
  - The symbol G₂ is used throughout but never properly defined (it should be ℝ² ⋊ GL₂(ℝ) but the group product is not given). (Reviewer 2)
  - Theorem 1 overloads g (both as group element and coordinate). (Reviewer 2)
  - Measures (dλ, dμ_𝒳, dμ_{G₂}) are not clearly identified as left-/bi-invariant Haar or general; the reader must infer from context. (Reviewer 1)
  - The equation above (2) uses the symbol h before h is defined, and the expression should not depend on h. (Reviewer 2)
- **Technical errors**:
  - Example 3 has a typo: missing parentheses in f(R⁻¹_θ(y−x)). This error propagates to Example 4, making Example 4 incorrect—the kernel 𝒦 should evaluate to f(x)/|det h|. (Reviewer 2)
  - The short discussion on Clebsch-Gordan theory is odd, lacks references, and is not clearly connected to the paper. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - The claim that existing methods rely on “solving complex optimization problems over G₂” is made repeatedly without any references or concrete explanation. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
- **Missing references and related work**:
  - The paper does not adequately cite related works on steerable networks that handle similarity or scale transformations (e.g., Weiler, Cohen, and others) and would benefit from a more precise discussion. (Reviewer 1)
  - References to separable group convolutions are missing (e.g., Knigge et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2021). (Reviewer 2)
  - The reference to perspective distortions is missing. (Reviewer 4)
  - The statement “from (Bekkers, 2019) we know that…” would be better supported by a standard group theory textbook. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unconvincing motivation and lack of clarity**:
  - The motivation for avoiding “complex optimization problems” is unclear; the paper does not explain how its method replaces such optimization. (Reviewer 2)
  - The definition of ε-affine invariance does not explain how to determine when two functions are ε-invariant; the practical use of the approach remains vague. (Reviewer 2)
  - The paper loosely speaks about “matching functions f₁ and f₂” without concretely stating how the learned representations are to be compared. (Reviewer 2)
  - The claim that “parallel lines remain parallel but angles can be distorted” on page 6 sounds contradictory and needs clarification. (Reviewer 4)
- **Efficiency unproven**: The proposed computational scheme (splitting the convolution over four integrals with Fourier transforms and change of variables) may not be more efficient than out-of-the-box group convolutions; no complexity analysis or proof of principle is given. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing derivations**: The authors refrain from detailing the integration in the projection layer (“stabilizer is specifically GL₂(ℝ)”) but do not include the derivations in an appendix. (Reviewer 4)
- **Questions left unanswered**: Can the method be generalized to affine equivariance? Have the authors implemented it numerically? (Reviewer 4)
