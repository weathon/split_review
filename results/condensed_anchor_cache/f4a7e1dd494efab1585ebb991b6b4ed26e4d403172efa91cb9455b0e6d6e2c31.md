## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SpikeLLM, the first spiking large language model (SNN) scaled to 7–70 billion parameters, using Generalized Integrate-and-Fire (GIF) neurons and an Optimal Brain Spiking (OBS) framework to reduce energy cost and accelerate inference via saliency-based spike length compression and outlier handling. Experimental results on LLAMA-2-7B/70B show improvements over quantization baselines (e.g., OmniQuant, GPTQ) in perplexity (25.51% reduction on WikiText2) and downstream accuracy (3.08% average gain). Reviewers recognize the novelty of applying SNNs to LLMs at this scale and the technical soundness of combining ANN-SNN conversion with quantization, but raise concerns about missing comparisons with recent works (e.g., QuaRot, SliM-LLM), incomplete experimental validation for 1-bit neuromorphic inference, insufficient analysis of computational overhead and scalability, and the practicality of the method given the performance gap with the full-precision model. Scores: 6, 8, 8, 6 — two reviewers (2,3) highly positive, two (1,4) more reserved.

### Strengths
- The paper is the first to apply spiking neural networks to LLMs with up to 70B parameters, filling a gap in the field (R2, R3). It demonstrates that ANN-SNN conversion can be evaluated at this unprecedented scale (R3).
- It addresses a genuine problem—the increasing energy cost of LLMs—with a well-motivated approach (R1, R4). The research problem is important and attracts broad interest (R4).
- The proposed GIF neurons leverage the temporal steps of SNNs to mitigate outliers that arise in quantized LLMs (R2, R3). Compressing spike length via grouping (GLIF) and per-channel dynamic simulation steps (SASS) are clear logical contributions (R4).
- The OBS framework provides a saliency-based calibration (via Hessian information) to allocate higher precision to important channels, combining known concepts from quantization and SNN literature in a novel way (R3).
- Extensive evaluation on standard LLM benchmarks (Tables 3, 4) covering 7B to 70B models, using both language modeling and downstream tasks, meets community standards (R2, R3). Experimental results show improved performance and energy efficiency over baselines like OmniQuant and GPTQ (R1, R4).
- The method is compatible with both standard GPU GEMM and potential neuromorphic hardware, enhancing feasibility (R4).
- Reviewer 2 and 3 consider the contribution significant and well-presented, with clear figures (Fig.1,2) aiding understanding.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with recent SOTA quantization methods:** The paper does not compare against SliM-LLM (saliency-driven mixed-precision), QuaRot (outlier-free rotation), or 1.58-bit LLM / IR-QLoRA (R1, R3). QuaRot challenges key claims (e.g., line 370 on the “necessity of SpikeLLM”) by showing outliers can be eliminated via weight rotation, potentially allowing basic ANN-SNN conversion to work at low precision (R3). These comparisons are essential for demonstrating the advantages of the spiking approach.
- **Insufficient validation of spiking behavior on neuromorphic hardware:** No experimental results show 1-bit inference on neuromorphic chips or even a simulation demonstrating spiking activity converging to merged spikes (R1). It is unclear how to run the network in a 1-bit setting; without such evidence, calling the method “spiking” is questionable (R1). Tables 3 and 4 use activation quantization levels ≥4 bits, raising the question whether the models are genuinely spiking (R1, Q3).
- **Undocumented temporal dynamics and computational overhead:**
  - The temporal dynamics of the model (especially during on-chip 1-bit inference) are not explained with a schematic diagram (R1, Q1). The GLIF model division of binary sequences is not illustrated (R2, Q3).
  - The increased computational cost per neuron from merging IF steps in GLIF is not analyzed; reducing auto-regressive steps does not automatically imply energy savings, and implementation on neuromorphic hardware with multiple quantization levels is unclear (R4, W4).
  - Whether saliency calculation (SASS/OBP) is required during inference or only during calibration is not clarified. Equations 7 and 9 may need to be executed during inference, adding overhead, while eq.10 may be one-time (R4, W5). How to combine with KV-cache for further reduction is not discussed (R4, W5).
- **Scalability and stability issues:** Table 4 lacks many results for the 70B model, attributed to GQA causing training instability. No detailed analysis of the key reasons or potential fixes is provided, leaving scalability to 70B+ models uncertain (R4, W6).
- **Performance gap with full-precision model:** In Table 3, the gap to the original model is still >10%, limiting practicality. The paper mentions continuous pretraining as a boost but does not discuss limits or performance with sufficient training (R4, W7). The paper could also be compared with classical quantization methods that achieve smaller gaps (R1, Q4).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Equation 3 appears confusing (R4, W1).
  - Equation 5 has an incorrect summation over t (R4, W2).
  - Figure 4(a) lacks a baseline label (R4, W3).
  - The related work section merely lists studies without systematic analysis of SNN and quantization development (R2, Q1).
  - The description of Equation 5 (lines 239-244) is not intuitive (R2, Q2).
  - The term “auto-regressive” is used as a synonym for recurrent/recursive, which is inaccurate (R3, Q).
  - Typographical issue: “7~70B” appears out of place (R3, Q).
- **Feasibility of Hessian-based saliency:** It is not specified whether approximations are used to compute the Hessian for such large models (R3, Q1).
- **Lack of code release:** No code provided hinders reproducibility and community extension (R1, R2).
- **Limited energy-efficiency comparison:** Table 5 only compares with LLAMA-1-7B; broader comparison with other efficient LLMs is missing (R1).
