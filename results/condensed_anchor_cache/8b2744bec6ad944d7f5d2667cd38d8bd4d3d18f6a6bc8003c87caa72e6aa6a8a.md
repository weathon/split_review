## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SparseFormer, a vision backbone that processes images using a small number of latent tokens (down to 49) via sparse feature sampling, bypassing dense processing of pixels/patches. It consists of the Focusing Transformer (which extracts features, decodes into latent tokens, and dynamically adjusts token regions of interest) and the Cortex Transformer. The complexity is O(N·P·C) independent of image size. On ImageNet, SparseFormer achieves 1.7× faster inference than Swin‑T with small accuracy degradation and outperforms ResNet50 with higher speed, while also offering lower memory footprint. Experiments on video classification are included.

### Strengths
- The proposed sparse paradigm is novel and solid, providing an alternative to dense vision modeling by operating on a limited number of latent tokens, which reduces computation and memory (R1, R2, R3).
- The Focusing Transformer with dynamic token ROI adjustment is a functional improvement over Perceiver; it effectively focuses on foreground regions, as shown by visualizations and ImageNet results (R2, R3).
- The method achieves a favorable accuracy‑throughput tradeoff: 1.7× faster than Swin‑T, outperforms ResNet50 with faster speed, and has lower memory footprint (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper is clearly written and well‑motivated, with thorough explanations of the design and comparisons (R2, R4).
- Experiments on ImageNet and video classification are solid and demonstrate the efficacy and computational economy (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Reviewer 1 reported no weaknesses**, but Reviewers 2–4 identified several concerns.
- **Novelty constrained**: The architecture is difficult to distinguish from Perceiver; the primary addition (FocusTransformer with ROI) is similar to DeformableDETR, making the truly innovative contribution unclear (R2, R4).
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Only image classification is reported in the main paper. No results are shown on dense prediction tasks (object detection, semantic segmentation, image generation) where sparse token efficiency is even more critical. Appendix A.1 notes inferior performance compared to recent transformer networks. Fixed sparse attention patterns may limit capturing long‑range dependencies and adapting to complex scenes with occlusions or cluttered backgrounds (R2, R3).
- **Missing comparisons**: The paper lacks comparison with other efficiency‑oriented techniques, such as token pruning (R4).
- **Token ROI spatial supervision**: The ROI mechanism lacks strong spatial supervision, which may cause performance degradation on dense prediction tasks requiring precise localization. In complex images, ROIs could drift from object boundaries, and the reliance on learned attention weights may not be robust to noisy/cluttered backgrounds (R3).
- **Experimental validation not fully solid**: Detection results are not as favorable despite the ROI mechanism being well‑suited for such tasks. No ablation study is provided to clarify the necessity of the ROI mechanism (R4).
- **Speed evaluation concerns**: The reported speedups are on V100 with FP32 precision. The efficiency of the DeformableDETR‑like FocusTransformer under lower precision (INT8/FP16) with TensorRT backend is unclear, and V100 is somewhat outdated for modern DNN inference (R2).
