## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a budget-constrained active learning approach for survival data with right-censoring. It extends BatchBALD (Kirsch et al., 2019) by adapting an algorithm from Khuller et al. (1999) to handle a budget and modifies BatchBALD’s mutual information criterion to work with censored survival times (e.g., by zeroing probabilities and renormalizing). The method is evaluated on three real-world medical datasets. Reviewer opinions are sharply divided: two reviewers (R1, R2, both score 1, confidence 4) gave strong rejections citing unrealistic assumptions, poor writing, and insufficient support; two others (R3, score 3, confidence 2; R4, score 3, confidence 4) found the problem relevant and the approach somewhat original but raised concerns about novelty, soundness, and empirical rigor.

### Strengths

- **Relevance of the problem**: All reviewers agree that active learning for survival data is a relevant and interesting topic, and existing work in this area is limited (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Originality of the approach**: R3 found the method “very new and original.” R4 called it an “original combination of existing ideas” (budgeted learning and BatchBALD). R1 noted the inclusion of three alternative methods for comparison.
- **Clarity of problem framing and introduction**: R3 stated that the paper is clear and introduces both domains well. R1 said the problem is well stated.
- **Comparison with baselines**: The authors compare against “naive” methods and provide sanity checks (R3). R3 found the results convincing.

### Weaknesses

- **Poor writing and presentation**: All but R3 (who only had minor comments) noted major presentation issues. R1, R2, and R4 report numerous typos (e.g., line 210, line 139), unclear sentences (e.g., lines 269, 282), informal language, and missing equation numbers. Notation is confusing: “I” is used for the oracle, a unit of time, and mutual information (R2); $a\_BB\_surv$ is undefined (R1). The transition from the mutual information equation to its right-most term is unclear (R4). Example of unclear phrasing: “the model sees only when the data points are uncensored” (line 140, R2).
- **Unclear contributions and low novelty**: R4 explicitly requests a clear statement of claimed contributions and finds the novelty low. The budget-constrained extension does not clearly differ from existing budgeted learning; the survival adaptation of BatchBALD is incremental and lacks adequate grounding. R4 also notes that the authors claim no prior work, but arXiv:2402.11973 applies BALD to right-censored data. R3 notes the algorithm is “merely an adaptation of the algorithm by Khuller et al. (1999).” R1 asks for clarification on how the bounds are “asymptomatically equivalent to standard active learning methods.”
- **Unrealistic or restrictive assumptions**: R2 strongly criticizes that the formulation (Section 3) requires the training dataset to contain complete event times and censoring times for all subjects, which is not typical in survival analysis; this biases the initial model and makes results problematic. The scenario of extending the study to “de-censor” instances is not standard in clinical trials with random right censoring (R2). R3 and R4 both note that assumptions (e.g., censoring independent of features) are insufficiently discussed and should be made formal. R4 questions how the approach handles instances that remain censored after querying and whether censored times are known a priori—a critical limitation in practice. The method relies on discretized time bins (Multi-Task Logistic Regression), and no discussion of generalization to continuous time is given (R4).
- **Soundness concerns**: The adaptation of BatchBALD via setting probabilities to zero and renormalizing is presented without theoretical justification (R4). “Omitting the first two lines” of the Khuller et al. algorithm is not grounded (R4; R1 asks what this means). The distributional properties of the BALD model (prior and likelihood) are not discussed (R1). The probability handling for censored instances is confusing—e.g., why is $p(y|x,w,D_{train})=0$ for all $y$ greater than $ctime\_i+I$ (R2, line 301). R2 also notes multiple notation issues (e.g., “I” having three meanings).
- **Insufficient empirical support**: Despite R3 finding results convincing, R4 points out that improvements over standard BatchBALD in Table 1 are very marginal, and the text’s claim that “BBsurv outperforms other algorithms when budget is equal to 20 across all 3 real world datasets” does not consistently hold (Entropy performs comparably). No statistical significance tests are reported (R4). The Table 1 caption states “Budget = 10” while the text refers to budget 20, causing inconsistency; also Table 1 appears inconsistent with Figures 1 and 2 (R4). Only budget values 0, 1, 5, 20 are used, which seems cherry-picked—more points should be evaluated (R1). A controlled synthetic study is missing (R1). The MAE-PO measure is not described in the main text (R3).
- **Limited scope and missing discussion**: The application scope is mostly medical; other domains (finance, engineering) mentioned in the abstract are not discussed (R4). The realistic aspects of how the selected datasets are censored are insufficiently addressed (R3). Previous literature is not sufficiently discussed (R3, R4). The learner model is not described clearly (R1). The authors’ response to reviewers was brief and referred to an unavailable revised version (R3).
