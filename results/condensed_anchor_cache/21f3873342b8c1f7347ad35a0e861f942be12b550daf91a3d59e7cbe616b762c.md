## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RefKG, a framework that enhances LLMs’ reasoning on knowledge-intensive tasks via reflective interaction with knowledge graphs. It decomposes complex queries, retrieves and prunes evidence subgraphs, transforms structured knowledge into textual evidence, and performs evidence-enhanced reasoning. A multi-task tuning strategy using GPT-3.5-turbo-generated data is employed to infuse knowledge and teach LLMs how to use it. Experiments on FactKG (fact verification) and MetaQA and WebQuestionsSP (KGQA) show improvements over previous state-of-the-art models. Reviewers rated the paper 6,6,5,6; the reviewer giving 5 found the technical novelty lower and the benchmarks insufficient, while others found the approach sound but noted various limitations.

### Strengths
- The approach is straightforward and intuitive; using an LLM to iteratively explore and retrieve reasoning paths from a KG is novel and interesting (R1, R2). The reflective reasoning integration effectively addresses limitations of prior KG-augmented methods (R3). The idea of query decoupling, retrieval, and reconstruction is sound (R4).
- Strong empirical performance: RefKG outperforms baseline methods on FactKG, MetaQA, and WebQuestionsSP (R1, R2, R3). The multi-task tuning strategy shows substantial improvements across tasks (R2, R3). The framework is evaluated across various open-source LLMs, demonstrating adaptability (R3). Experiments are clean and figures easy to follow (R4).
- A generated training dataset is provided, which could be valuable for future model training and evaluation (R1).
- The paper is well organized and clear (R3, R4), though some typos exist.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution**: Multiple reviewers highlighted insufficient technical novelty. Using LLMs to decompose questions and iteratively retrieve information has been explored in prior “self-ask” and subsequent works (R1). Each step (query decoupling, retrieval, reconstruction) appears in existing papers, undermining the contribution of the unified framework (R4). One reviewer (R3, minority) stated the pipeline is simple and not exciting. Using GPT-3.5-turbo to generate training data is common practice (R1). The claim that previous methods do not filter extracted triplets (lines 144-145) underestimates works like Retrieve-Rewrite-Answer and KAPING; filtering is already discussed in ToG and FiDeLIS, and these references are missing (R4). The training process is quite similar to existing method RoG, yet no comparison or analysis is provided (R4). 
- **Unfair baseline comparisons**: Table 1 comparison is problematic: KG-GPT, KB-BINDER, and ToG are training-free, while Retrieve-rewriter requires training with a different target. Comparing properties like multi-task tuning and knowledge refinement under these conditions is not sound (R4). The authors should include more training-required baselines (e.g., RoG) (R4). Additionally, a simple baseline that trains/prompts an LLM to directly generate reasoning paths from the KG and use them as context would better isolate the value of RefKG’s additional steps (R1). 
- **High computational cost**: Answering a multi-hop question requires multiple LLM calls, often at least four, raising scalability concerns (R1). The iterative retrieval and reflection process is computationally intensive for large-scale applications (R3). The average number of LLM calls per question or fact should be reported (R1). 
- **Error accumulation and noise handling**: The error accumulation issue in the multi-step pipeline is not adequately addressed (R2). Specific concerns: (1) Query decomposition introduces errors – the paper’s ablation shows 62% of total errors come from incorrect entity identification; how is reliability ensured? (2) No analysis of how the refinement (expert model) handles conflicting or complementary evidence (R2). (3) Lack of quantitative analysis showing how the refinement process reduces noise propagation in multi-hop reasoning chains (R2). (4) The knowledge reconstruction step (transforming KG triplets into textual statements via LLM) may introduce noise/hallucinations; controlled curation or error analysis is needed (R4). 
- **Limited domain generalization and benchmarks**: Effectiveness beyond fact verification and KGQA is not explored, and the benchmarks are insufficient (R3, minority). The approach may not generalize to domains with sparse or highly specialized KGs and may rely heavily on KG completeness and accuracy (R3). Generalization across different knowledge domains is not thoroughly explored (R2). 
- **Interpretability concerns**: The decision-making process and reasoning paths during multi-task learning could be better explained (R2). 
- **Training necessity and ablation gaps**: It is unclear whether the training process is necessary; the method might work with inference only. An ablation study using advanced models like GPT-4o or o1 is suggested (R4). The model used in Table 5’s ablation study is not specified (R4). 
- **Missing details and technical clarifications**: Several questions from reviewers remain unaddressed: (a) The term “reflection” of LLMs (lines 72-73) needs explicit definition (R4). (b) How is the entity subset in Section 3.1 collected and grounded to the KG? For datasets like WebQSP where such subsets are not provided, how is the method adapted? (R4). (c) How is the ending point of the chain Pt in evidence subgraph retrieval defined? Is the entire process controlled by the LLM? (R4). (d) Which LLMs are used across the method – are they fine-tuned or naive? Which LLM underpins the expert model? (R4). (e) What steps were taken to identify and mitigate potential biases in the LLM-generated corpus used for multi-task tuning? (R3). 
- **Missing references and typos**: Several relevant references are omitted (e.g., KnowledgeNavigator, Chain-of-Knowledge, Paths-over-Graph, LightRAG) (R3). There are typos: missing blank in line 040, inconsistent citation format for ToG in Table 1, capitalization errors in section headings (lines 207, 506) (R3).
