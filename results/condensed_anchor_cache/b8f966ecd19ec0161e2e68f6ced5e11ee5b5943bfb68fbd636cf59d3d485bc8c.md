## Merged Review

### Summary
All six reviewers acknowledge the paper’s clear writing and the usefulness of the region-constrained formulation for visual counterfactual explanations. The proposed RCSB method, built on I²SB with classifier guidance and gradient stabilization, is shown to outperform several baselines on three ImageNet class pairs. However, reviewers converge on major concerns: limited technical novelty, insufficient comparison with closely related prior work, overclaims about causality and trustworthiness, narrow evaluation scope, lack of user studies, unfair metric comparisons, and missing ablations. The minority most positive reviewer (R2, score 8) is particularly impressed by the technical execution but still raises substantial criticisms about overclaims and missing baselines. The most critical reviewers (R3 and R5, scores 5) emphasize limited novelty and evaluation bias.

### Strengths
- **Clear presentation and problem formulation.** All reviewers praise the writing quality and clarity (R1, R2, R4, R5, R6). The paper is well-structured, with well-motivated technical choices and extensive qualitative examples. R2 calls it “a pleasure to read” and notes the explicit problem formulation can help move the field forward.
- **Region-constrained VCEs are reasonable and align with human perception.** The formulation of RVCEs is well-motivated and makes intuitive sense for interpretability (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). R5 highlights the potential for interactive user-specified regions.
- **Effective method with high-quality results.** The I²SB-based conditional inpainting approach produces realistic counterfactuals that successfully flip classifier decisions. The ADAM-derived gradient stabilization and normalization is an interesting technical contribution (R2, R3). R1 notes numerous qualitative results demonstrating effectiveness. R6 finds the evaluation structure of high quality.
- **Outperforms baselines on chosen metrics.** On the three evaluated class pairs, RCSB achieves better FID, flip rate, similarity, and sparsity compared to methods like ACE and LDCE (R5, R6).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing detailed comparison to prior work and undefined baselines.** The paper does not properly discuss or cite closely related region-constrained VCE methods (Jeanneret et al. 2023, Weng et al. 2024) that also use automatic masking and conditional inpainting (R2). Gradient stabilization techniques (DiME, FastDiME, He et al.) and the related framework of Karunratanakul et al. (2023) are not adequately compared; the specific advantages of the proposed approach over these are unclear (R2). The baseline models in Table 1 (ACE, DVCE, etc.) are not introduced, cited, or defined in the text or table (R5, R6). This undermines reproducibility and the significance of the results.
- **Limited technical novelty.** The method is a straightforward application of I²SB to conditional inpainting without significant algorithmic innovation (R4). The paper does not address specific challenges like convergence or mode collapse in the region-constrained setting (R4). R3 notes the approach resembles standard inpainting with classifier guidance, making the explanation quality entangled with the generative model’s capacity.
- **Overclaims about causality, trustworthiness, and confirmation bias.** The paper asserts that RVCEs allow “causal inference” and “increasing trust,” but constrained explanations may miss model reliance on shortcut features *outside* the mask (R2). This could actually increase confirmation bias, contrary to the paper’s argument. The claim that standard VCEs suffer from confirmation bias is not rigorously substantiated; the Figure 1 example does not convincingly illustrate it (R4). Region selection methods (attribution maps, LangSAM) may introduce their own biases, skewing the explanation (R3). The method does not reveal which features are important, only that a region can be changed to flip the decision, limiting causal conclusions (R2, R3). Claims should be tempered to describing plausible counterfactuals rather than causal inference (R2).
- **Narrow evaluation scope and dataset bias.** Evaluation is limited to three ImageNet class pairs where classes share similar shape but differ in texture/color, favoring the region-constrained approach (R5). No experiments on tasks where shape changes are critical (e.g., American chameleon vs. common iguana) or on standard benchmarks like CelebA-smile (R2, R5). The method’s performance on more diverse datasets and domains (including medical imaging) is not shown (R6). Generalizability to arbitrary counterfactual pairings is unproven (R5).
- **Lack of user studies and interpretability validation.** It is unclear whether the generated counterfactuals actually help human understanding (R5). Maximizing quantitative metrics like FID and S³ may produce nearly invisible changes that are useless for users (R5). The paper claims user interaction benefits but provides no user study to support this (R5, R6).
- **Unfair metric comparisons.** FID comparisons to methods without region constraint are misleading because the region constraint inherently limits changes and thus improves FID; a region-based FID would be more appropriate (R6). LangSAM-based regions yield considerably higher FID than other region types, suggesting unique challenges that are not explained (R1).
- **Missing ablations and additional experiments.** Key ablations are absent: (a) using a random non-semantic region of similar shape/size to isolate the importance of semantic relevance (R1); (b) using the *inverse* mask (allow only background changes) to assess robustness to shortcut features (R2); (c) forcing the target class to a very different category (e.g., animal to dining table) to see what is generated (R3); (d) testing on adversarial examples to understand model vulnerabilities (R5). The three configurations A, B, C in Table 1 lack explanation of their differences and rationale (R1). Numeric results for different generative models and classifiers are only in the appendix and should be in the main paper (R6). The method’s limitations (e.g., inability to capture shape changes) are not explicitly stated (R6).
