## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses robustness in knowledge editing for LLMs using locate-and-edit methods. It proposes a theoretical framework and a Robust Edit Pathway (REP) that separates editing keys from LLM representations via a project-and-gate mechanism. Experiments on LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B with the CounterFact dataset claim improvements. However, reviewers raised substantial concerns about experimental scope (only two models, one dataset), missing baselines (MEMIT not in tables), clarity of theoretical sections, insufficient quantitative evidence, and multiple typos. One reviewer (Rating 3) was markedly more negative, finding the paper immature and poorly presented; others (Ratings 6, 5) saw potential but noted significant limitations.

### Strengths
1. The REP framework uses a project-and-gate mechanism to separate editing pathways, offering a novel approach to knowledge editing. (R1)
2. The paper combines theoretical derivation with empirical evidence. (R1)
3. REP shows improvement on the representative locate-and-edit method ROME. (R1)
4. Knowledge editing is an important problem for improving LLM quality. (R2)
5. Related work is well covered and reviewed. (R2)
6. The solution sheds light on key-value approaches in knowledge editing. (R2)
7. Code for the approach is provided (though noted: no README, hardcoded paths with programmer name). (R3)
8. The subject is well-motivated at the beginning. (R3)
9. Attempts are made to link practical and theoretical sides. (R3)
10. One reviewer noted the paper appears largely written or heavily edited by an LLM (noted as a stylistic observation). (R2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited model and dataset scope.** Experiments only use LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B on CounterFact. Generalizability to more LLMs and real-world knowledge editing (more diverse/complex) is unclear. (R1, R3) The KnowEdit benchmark (a more comprehensive benchmark) was not used. (R3) One reviewer asked for evaluation on real-world facts (citing arXiv:2402.18909). (R1) Train set size is not specified. (R3)
2. **Missing baselines and incomplete experiments.** Only ROME results appear in tables; MEMIT is mentioned but results are omitted. (R1, R3) Reviewer 1 asked “Where are the results of MEMIT?” Table 1 only includes ROME. (R1) Not all metrics from the original ROME paper are used; more baselines should be added. (R3) No statistical significance or standard deviation is reported. (R3)
3. **Theoretical rigor and presentation issues.** Sections 3 and 4 are hard to follow; Section 3 copy-pastes ROME content without context; Section 4 is a disconnected series of lemmas and corollaries lacking intuition. (R3) Specific errors: Definition 3.1: k should be f. (R3) Remark 3.3 sum over wrong index. (R3) Lemma 4.1 proof is confusing (simply say W=VK⁺); sum starts at 0 not i. (R3) Lemma 4.4 epsilon not used and assumption questionable. (R3) Equation 11 sign error (1 – 1/p ≤ 0). (R3) Lemma 4.6 “robustly retrieved” undefined. (R3) Appendix proofs incomplete (sentence ends in the middle). (R3) The key-value data model’s conceptual adequacy is questioned: a lot of knowledge is stored in subgraphs, not individual triples. (R2) The “group discussion” model’s dynamics are unclear (R2 asked what it would cover). (R2)
4. **Empirical analysis lacks quantitative evidence.** Conclusions in Section 5.2 are drawn from a single example (e.g., Delphine de Girardin vs. Slovenia mismatch in Figure 3). (R3) No average distances or metrics are provided. (R3) PCA is used on a single subject, which is not meaningful without multiple subjects. (R3) Figures 3 and 4 are hard to read (black-and-white, dots invisible); left panels need normalization. (R3) Baseline is not shown in the plots. (R3) Whitening similarity is undefined; K_s definition is unclear. (R3)
5. **Clarity, typos, and presentation flaws.** Typos: “prefixs” → “prefixes”, “increasin” → “increasing”, “unrelevant” → “irrelevant” (R1); “utilze”, “adaoptor”, “theorectical”, “raito”, “resutls”, missing ‘g’, random tildes over numbers, “are” to remove (line 367). (R3) English issues and unstable terminology/definitions. (R2) Poor examples: triple (USA, president-of, Biden) is counterintuitive unless interpreted as “has-president”. (R2) Missing summary of contributions at end of introduction. (R3) Figure 2 never mentioned in text. (R3) Line 368 incomplete: “…prefix has the same subject token” as what? (R3) Line 370 “high whitening noise” unquantified. (R3) Line 419 bsz, L, D undefined. (R3) Line 435 “whether to activate this branch” ambiguous – projection only? (R3) Table 1 first column not bold. (R3) Line 453: “We use LLaMa2” and Mistral (inconsistent style). (R3) References are Arxiv links, not real citations. (R3)
6. **Failure cases and error analysis missing.** Reviewer 1 asked for failure cases of REP in editing and robustness tests. (R1) Reviewer 3 also requested an error analysis (e.g., does rephrasing/shuffling make sense; what would a human say?). (R3)
7. **Missing definitions and insufficient introduction of concepts.** “Whitening similarity” is used without definition. (R3) “Robustly retrieved” in Lemma 4.6 undefined. (R3) K_s definition unclear. (R3) The project-and-gate mechanism is not fully explained (only projection?).
8. **Reviewer disagreement on writing quality:** One reviewer noted the paper appears heavily LLM-written/edited (R2), while another (R3) considered the clarity poor with many typos. This contrast is preserved.
