## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Auto-Bench, an automated pipeline using LLMs to generate question-answer-reasoning triplets from image verbalizations (captions, object locations, etc.) and to evaluate Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The benchmark covers 4 primary abilities and 16 sub-abilities, including perception, reasoning, planning, and human value alignment, with a total of 28.5K human-verified and 3,504K unfiltered examples. The authors validate the pipeline by benchmarking several state-of-the-art VLMs. Reviewers are mixed: one (score 8) finds the work substantial, addressing important limitations of existing benchmarks, and well-executed. Two others (score 5) appreciate the motivation but raise major concerns about fairness, novelty, and experimental setup.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important challenge: existing VLM benchmarks cover narrow capabilities and are limited in size due to manual curation. (R1, R3)
- The benchmark is substantial: >3 million examples, including a train split and a human-curated validation set. (R1)
- Covers a broad range of capabilities, including planning and human value alignment, which are underexplored in prior work. (R1)
- Tasks are appropriately framed as open-ended or close-ended depending on the nature of the task. (R1)
- Good comparison with human-curated VQA datasets in Section 4.2. (R1)
- Experiments are well done, including qualitative model comparisons using an ELO system. (R1)
- Analysis of alignment between LLM and human judges in Section 4.4. (R1)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. (R1)
- The diversification into skills and sub-skills for understanding model usefulness is appreciated. (R2)
- The pipeline is innovative, comprehensive, and scalable for VLM evaluation. (R3)
- The curated dataset enables both evaluation and supervised fine-tuning of VLMs. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- The benchmark is built only on MS-COCO images, which is a narrow domain; VLM performance on Auto-Bench may not reflect performance on other domains (e.g., VizWiz, medical images). Furthermore, many LMMs are trained on COCO captions (e.g., InstructBLIP, LLaVA), making evaluation potentially unfair. (R1, R2)
- Using LLMs (GPT-4) as data curators raises fairness concerns: models trained on GPT-4 generated data (e.g., LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl) may have an advantage over those that are not. (R2)
- The method for curating the 28.5K high-quality validation samples is not described, leaving the process unclear. (R2)
- It is unclear how an image is assigned to a particular sub-skill (e.g., how does one determine that an image can support physics-related questions?). (R2)
- The choice to format reasoning-based questions as multiple-choice is not sufficiently justified; examples and rationale are needed to explain why open-ended answers are not feasible. (R2)
- Figure 3b shows overlapping distributions in question length and cosine similarity, failing to demonstrate semantic richness compared to other datasets. Additionally, Figure 3 should include human-collected datasets like Visit-Bench or Touchstone for comparison, as human questions are likely the most diverse. (R2)
- The cost of adding a new model to the benchmark (28.5K instances) is not provided. (R2)
- The validation of LLM as a judge lacks correlation with human judgments and does not compare to simpler lexical-similarity metrics, which would be necessary to establish its reliability. (R2)
- The claim of novelty is weak given the similarity to Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA); the paper does not compare against that work in benchmark statistics or clearly differentiate its advantages. (R3)
- The use of GPT-4/3.5 for data generation and evaluation has been explored in prior work (citations [2],[3]). (R3)
- Assessments of different visual capabilities have already been addressed in other evaluations (citations [2],[3],[4],[5]). (R3)
- (Minor) The term "rationality" in Section 4.2 is not defined, leaving its meaning unclear. (R1 question)
