## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a unified theoretical analysis of three mainstream self-supervised learning (SSL) paradigms—contrastive learning (spectral contrastive learning), feature decorrelation (Barlow Twins), and masked auto-encoding (MAE)—through the lens of matrix information theory. It proves that the loss functions of Barlow Twins and spectral contrastive learning implicitly optimize matrix mutual information and matrix joint entropy. Building on this insight, the paper proposes the Matrix Variational Masked Auto-Encoder (M-MAE), which adds a total coding rate (TCR) regularizer to the standard MAE loss. Empirical results on ImageNet show a 3.9% improvement in linear probing with ViT-Base and a 1% improvement in fine-tuning with ViT-Large compared to MAE/U-MAE. Reviewers agree the work attempts a meaningful unification, but strongly disagree on the soundness of the theoretical foundations and the validity of the experimental design; two reviewers (R3, R4) rate the paper lower (5) while another rates it higher (8).

### Strengths
1. **Theoretical innovation and unified view**: Introducing matrix information theory to analyze and connect diverse SSL methods (BarlowTwins, spectral contrastive learning, MAE) is a novel and potentially valuable contribution. Reviewers note it could provide new insights into feature dependencies and information propagation within neural networks (R1, R2). The unified lens for both contrastive and feature decorrelation paradigms bridges a gap in the literature (R2). R3 acknowledges the attempt as somewhat valuable, though with significant reservations (see Weaknesses).

2. **Potential for enhanced robustness and efficiency**: The exploration of joint entropy in SSL models addresses the critical aspect of input data uncertainty, laying groundwork for more robust and efficient algorithms (R2).

3. **Promising empirical results on ImageNet**: The paper reports strong initial results: 3.9% improvement in linear probing (ViT-Base) and 1% improvement in fine-tuning (ViT-Large) over MAE/U-MAE. R1 considers these results great and indicates the paper's initial findings are promising. R3 and R4 are less convinced due to experimental limitations (see Weaknesses).

4. **Writing clarity**: The paper is fairly well written and accessible, at least in its high-level narrative (R1). R4 rates presentation as good (3).

5. **Relevance**: The paper tackles important questions in self-supervised learning (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Mathematical foundations and misuse of matrix entropy** (R3, R4 strongly critical; R1 assumes math is sound):
   - No detailed discussion linking the introduced matrix entropy (Definition 1) to Shannon's information entropy. The reviewer notes that Rényi entropy is defined for a specific matrix family (density matrix / Gram matrix), not for arbitrary matrices. The paper is argued to misuse the concept throughout; for example, on page 14 the statement "Take K1 = Z1 and K2 = Z2" is incorrect because K1 and K2 must be Gram matrices, while Z1 and Z2 are feature matrices, not Gram matrices (R3).
   - Proofs of Theorems 1 and 2 are not clearly or completely presented. The proof of Theorem 1 in the appendix (including Lemma 1) does not follow a clear mathematical reasoning; it is unclear how the last sentences constitute a valid proof (R4). The connection of Lemma A.1 to the proof of Theorem 3.2 is not clear (R4). The proof for Theorem 2 similarly appears incomplete (R4).
   - The results in Section 3 are not generalizable to generic SSL methods; they are only applicable to Barlow Twins and spectral contrastive learning. Moreover, the proof for spectral contrastive learning is argued to be wrong because it requires K1 and K2 to be Gram matrices, a condition not satisfied (R3).
   - It is not fully clear how Theorems 1 and 2 are not contradictory given Definition 2 (R4).
   - No discussion of why maximizing mutual information (or joint entropy) between Z1 and Z2 is a good measure for SSL; the motivation for M-MAE is therefore weak (R3).
   - There is no connection between Section 3 and Section 4. In Section 3, TCR is used as a measure of joint information, but in Section 4 it is applied to a single variable. The regularizer term for MAE is thus irrelevant to the preceding theoretical analysis (R3). (R1 also questions how the TCR loss on latent codes contributes to entropy, and whether a plot analogous to Fig. 1/2 can be provided.)

2. **Experimental concerns** (all reviewers; R1, R3, R4):
   - The implementation uses U-MAE's settings rather than the original MAE hyperparameters (e.g., batch size), which may cause discrepancies. The final converged performance of MAE ViT-L on ImageNet is ~85.5%; it is unclear whether the reported improvement trends hold after full training. The reviewer suggests running to convergence on ImageNet or providing CIFAR-100 results as a lighter alternative (R1).
   - The proposed M-MAE is highly sensitive to the choice of hyperparameter μ (Table 2). The performance gap between different μ values varies significantly, which implies that the original labels would be needed to tune μ, violating the spirit of self-supervised learning (R3).
   - The experimental setup lacks many details: training objectives, hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate schedule, etc.) are not fully specified. The appendix contains no experimental details; only limited information appears in the main paper (R4).
   - The paper only compares MAE, U-MAE, and M-MAE. Much broader comparisons with other SSL methods are needed, including BarlowTwins, MoCo, SimCLR, BYOL, DeepClustering, SwAV, Data2Vec, DINO, iBot, SimMIM, etc., both in terms of information-theoretic quantities and performance. Comparisons with other MAE variants (e.g., SimMIM) would also strengthen the paper (R3).

3. **Scalability of information-theoretic measures** (R2):
   - The paper does not discuss the computational complexity of calculating matrix mutual information and joint entropy. These measures can be computationally intensive for large-scale datasets and high-dimensional embeddings. For example, computing the covariance matrix and its determinant/logarithm for high-dimensional features is expensive. No approximations or efficient algorithms are proposed to mitigate this burden.

4. **Generalization and robustness** (R2):
   - It is unclear how generalizable the matrix information-theoretic approach is to other SSL architectures, such as transformer-based or recurrent neural networks. Empirical evidence or theoretical justification is needed (R2).
   - The robustness of the proposed metrics to hyperparameter variations (e.g., temperature in contrastive learning, weight decay) is not examined. A sensitivity analysis under different hyperparameter settings would be beneficial (R2).

5. **Presentation and organization** (R1, R4):
   - Some definitions (e.g., TCR) are used before they are formally defined; the paper should at least point to the appendix where they appear (R1).
   - The paper contains both propositions and theorems, but the appendix only provides proofs for "theorems"; the reader must manually match the correct proof to the correct proposition/theorem (R4).

**Contrast among reviewers**: Reviewer 1 (score 8) finds the theoretical framework and initial results largely convincing, while Reviewer 3 (score 5) and Reviewer 4 (score 5) raise fundamental concerns about mathematical correctness and experimental validity. Reviewer 1 suggests the writing is clear and the mathematics sound (though not checked in detail), whereas Reviewer 3 argues the paper misuses information-theoretic concepts and the proofs are flawed. Reviewer 2 (score 6) is positive about the theoretical innovation but points to missing scalability analysis and lack of generalization discussion. All reviewers agree on the need for better experimental details and broader comparisons; Reviewer 3 is the most critical about hyperparameter sensitivity and the connection between sections.
