## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the first method for computing Lipschitz constants of convolutional classifiers with respect to the Levenshtein distance, enabling certified robustness in text classification. The approach uses a real-valued relaxation of Levenshtein distance to train 1-Lipschitz models, and computes certified radii in a single forward pass. Results are reported on AG-News, SST-2, IMDB, and FakeNews, with verified accuracy at distances 1 and 2. Reviewers agree the problem is significant and the approach is novel and efficient, but raise concerns about limited model complexity, loose bounds at distance 1, scalability, and insufficient comparison to existing methods.

### Strengths
- Addresses an interesting, challenging, and practically relevant problem in NLP robustness – certifying robustness under Levenshtein distance.
- First work to provide closed-form deterministic certificates for Levenshtein perturbations without resorting to randomized approaches.
- Proposed solution is novel, efficient, and simple: runtime is orders of magnitude faster than IBP and brute-force baselines (by construction of Lipschitz constant).
- Mathematical derivations appear correct; theory is carefully presented and proof strategy in Appendix B is sensible.
- Paper writing is clean, clear, and well-structured; figures and tables are thoughtful (e.g., coordinated highlighting between Table 2 and 3).
- Strong motivation for practical verification; potential practical relevance recognized.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited model complexity and scalability concerns:** The certified edit distances are very small (1 or 2 characters), models are tiny (only one convolutional layer), and the potential for future improvement is minimal. Layer-wise Lipschitz bounding has been largely discarded in image robustness due to exponential signal attenuation; this problem is arguably harder and not scalable to deeper networks. The clean accuracy drops significantly when more than two layers are used (Figure S4), and this drop is not adequately analyzed (e.g., optimization challenges, vanishing gradients). [R1, R3]
2. **Loose bounds at Levenshtein distance 1:** In most \(k=1\) settings (SST-2, IMDB), IBP and BruteF achieve higher verified accuracy than LipsLev. The results displayed in Table 2 are not convincing – the method appears loose. The claim that LipsLev “provides the best performance in AG-News” is inaccurate because BruteF has higher verified accuracy there. [R2, R4]
3. **Lipschitz constraint harms clean accuracy.** The Lipschitzness constraint is strong and likely reduces model expressiveness, as also theoretically studied in Zhang et al. 2022. The method does not show a clean accuracy advantage over simpler IBP at small perturbations. The performance on SST-2 and IMDB is not comparable with the simplest IBP. It is unclear why LipsLev has exactly the same performance as IBP on FakeNews at distance 1. [R2, R4]
4. **Speculative extension to transformers (Section 6).** The discussion of extending to transformers is far removed from the one-layer convolutional classifier presented; challenges are not concretely addressed, making the section feel speculative. [R3]
5. **Missing comparisons and literature review gaps.** No evaluation against randomized methods (which form a significant part of existing robustness verification work). The literature review is not comprehensive; it misses relevant works (e.g., Zou et al. on adversarial attacks). Need a more thorough analysis of existing certifiable robustness methods for text classifiers. [R3]
6. **Incomplete definitions and notation inconsistencies.** Key terms are not explicitly defined: Lipschitz continuity (local vs global), clean accuracy, verified accuracy, \(d_\text{edit}\), the embedding matrix \(\mathbf{E}\). Notation is inconsistent (commas in subscripts vs no commas, mismatch between Sections 4.1 and 4.2 on local vs global Lipschitz). Typo: “tenths” should be “tens of thousands”. [R1, R4]
7. **Questions about theoretical aspects.** 
   - Discuss completeness of the approach, not just soundness. [R1]
   - Relevance of methods like alpha-beta-CROWN in this setting? [R1]
   - Confirm that Theorem 4.3 holds for any \(p, r\) satisfying \(1/p + 1/r = 1\). [R3]
   - Explain how Remark 4.5 gives a local Lipschitz constant (it appears global if it holds for all P and Q). [R4]
8. **Figure 1 presentation could be improved.** A line graph showing fraction of sentences verified vs. sentence length would be clearer than the current bar chart; a stacked bar chart based on proportions is another alternative. [R3]
9. **Lack of empirical verification of certificates.** Authors did not empirically verify certificates by running inference on random string modifications within the certified Levenshtein distance to check for proof errors. Showing the gap between empirical and theoretical certificates (e.g., via a greedy attacking strategy) would strengthen the paper. [R3]
