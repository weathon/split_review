## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies spurious correlations in Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) for visual representation learning. It first shows empirically and theoretically (in a toy setting) that common augmentations can induce undesired invariances and that dataset re-sampling does not consistently lead to invariant representations. The paper proposes a method, LATETVG (or LATE TVG), which removes spurious information during pretraining by regularizing later encoder layers via pruning. The method improves worst-group accuracy on several benchmarks without requiring group or label information during SSL.

### Strengths
1. **Originality and importance**: The problem of spurious correlations has not been studied in SSL before, and the proposed later‑layer pruning approach is novel (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 1). The paper presents a creative combination of existing concepts to address a critical challenge (Reviewer 1).
2. **Theoretical insight**: The analysis in Section 3.3 provides theoretical understanding of how common SSL augmentations affect the model’s reliance on spurious features for downstream linear classifiers (Reviewer 2).
3. **Empirical demonstration of spurious feature learning**: The paper empirically shows that traditional techniques like dataset re‑sampling with group information do **not** consistently improve core feature representations, exposing their limitations (Reviewer 2). The proposed method improves worst‑group accuracy on four datasets (Waterbirds, CelebA, etc.) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
4. **Practical advantage**: The method does not require group or label information during SSL, which is a significant advantage over supervised debiasing approaches (Reviewer 1).
5. **Interesting finding**: The conclusion that balancing the data may not be effective for remedying spurious correlations is highlighted as extremely interesting (Reviewer 3).
6. **Clear depiction**: The paper presents a clear depiction of the prevalent issues with SSL arising from spurious features (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited scale and dataset scope**: Most results are on relatively small datasets (e.g., Waterbirds, CelebA). The analysis of Section 4 could have used publicly available SSL models trained on large datasets. The paper lacks evaluation on more challenging, real‑world distribution‑shift benchmarks such as WILDS, FOCUS, Hard ImageNet, or SpuCo (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
2. **Scalability and efficiency not addressed**: The computational overhead of pruning (FLOPs, memory, training time) is not discussed, and it is unclear whether the method scales to very deep networks or extremely large datasets (Reviewer 1).
3. **Missing references and literature connection**: The paper omits important related works on spurious feature learning (e.g., Salient ImageNet, WILDS) (Reviewer 2). It also does not discuss the equivalence of the spurious‑correlation problem in SSL to *feature suppression* studied in prior work (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/628f16b29939d1b060af49f66ae0f7f8-Abstract.html) (Reviewer 3).
4. **Lack of comparison with state‑of‑the‑art SSL debiasing methods**: The paper does not provide extensive comparison with recent SSL methods that explicitly target spurious correlations, making it difficult to gauge relative progress (Reviewer 1).
5. **Poor clarity and notation overload**: The paper is hard to follow in places. Symbols α, β, γ appear overloaded; the relationships between connectivity and the P matrix are unclear; definitions are missing, creating ambiguity (Reviewer 4). An algorithmic representation of the Late TVG method is missing (Reviewer 4).
6. **Weak connection between theory and practice**: The theoretical foundation leans heavily on Spectral Contrastive Learning, but that framework is not used in experiments. The paper does not explain how theoretical insights translate into the practical implementation (Reviewer 4). The intuition for why pruning later layers helps forget the spurious feature is insufficiently explained (Reviewer 3).
7. **Unclear definition and measurement of spurious features**: For real data, it is unclear how spurious attributes and core features are identified, and how the correlation in Table 1 is computed. The methodology for quantifying connectivity error rates is insufficiently detailed, making results hard to reproduce (Reviewer 4). This overlaps with the question of how the authors identify spurious features.
8. **Missing experimental details**: The paper does not report which training parameters were used for Late TVG (Reviewer 4). It is also unclear whether models on Waterbirds etc. are initialized from ImageNet pretrained weights (Reviewer 3).
9. **Unexplored long‑term effects and robustness**: The impact of pruning on the network’s ability to learn new tasks or adapt over time is not investigated (Reviewer 1). Robustness to adversarial attacks is not examined (Reviewer 1).

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (Reviewer 4, rating 5, confidence 4) is substantially more critical than the others, particularly concerning clarity, the theory‑practice disconnect, and unclear definitions (soundness 2, presentation 2). The other three reviewers rate soundness and presentation as good or excellent (confidence 3–4). This contrast is preserved: while most found the paper clear and well‑structured, Reviewer 4 found it hard to follow and lacking reproducibility.
