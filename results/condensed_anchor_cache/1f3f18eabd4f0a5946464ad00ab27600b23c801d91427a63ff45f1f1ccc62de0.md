## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a semi-supervised learning framework that uses unlabeled samples from a slightly distribution-shifted population to improve generalization, combining Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) with self-training / pseudo-labeling. Theoretical guarantees are provided for linear classification under a mixture of two Gaussians in \(\mathbb{R}^d\), showing improved error bounds over ERM. Experiments on synthetic and real datasets (e.g., NCT-CRC-HE-100K) are reported. Reviewer opinions vary: two reviewers (scores 8,8) find the theoretical guarantees for a DRO setting novel and the method well-motivated; two others (scores 6,6) question the significance of the Gaussian analysis, clarity of contributions relative to existing DRO-based semi-supervised works, and the restrictiveness of the distribution shift model.

### Strengths
- The paper analyzes the generalization error of a new algorithm that combines DRO and self-supervised (self-training) learning, demonstrating improvement over traditional ERM (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- Provides a novel non-asymptotic learning bound for Gaussian mixture models in a distributionally robust framework, with controls for the bias–variance trade-off (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- The method pushes the classifier to avoid crowded areas of the input space, similar in spirit to large-margin methods, and uses unlabeled data to improve this ability (Reviewer 3).
- The research topic is highly relevant: leveraging unsupervised samples from a different (but similar) distribution is of practical interest, and the theoretical guarantees are valuable (Reviewer 4).
- The algorithm is natural, building on prior robust loss formulations, and the paper is well-written and clear (Reviewer 2, though Reviewer 4 found presentation only fair).

### Weaknesses
- The theoretical analysis is limited to Gaussian mixture models, which are very restrictive and may not represent real-world data (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 notes this limits practical applicability.
- Contributions relative to existing DRO-based semi-supervised learning methods (e.g., Najafi et al. 2019, [R1], [R2]) are unclear. The paper does not clearly articulate how its approach differs from those that use self-training or constrain the ambiguity set with unlabeled data (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 also notes the comparison with related works is not thorough; a detailed comparison contrasting assumptions, methodologies, and guarantees is needed, and why adversarial robustness settings (Carlini et al., Carmon et al.) are not directly applicable should be explained.
- The distribution shift assumed (small Wasserstein distance between marginal distributions of labeled and unlabeled data) appears too simplistic and straightforward for DRO methods; the cluster assumption and slight covariate shift may not justify the complexity of the approach (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental results are insufficient: they lack systematic hyperparameter choices, architecture details, sensitivity analysis, variance across trials, and ablation studies to justify design choices (Reviewer 2). Moreover, comparisons with existing semi-supervised methods or large-margin methods are missing, and more common benchmark datasets should be used (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). The current experiments do not adequately demonstrate effectiveness over baselines (Reviewer 4).
- When \(n=0\) (no out-of-domain samples), Theorem 4.2 yields dimension dependence \(d^{3/8}\), meaning the reduction in the exponent is not due to unlabeled data. It is unclear why non-robust error outperforms ERM in that case (Reviewer 1).
- The bounds appear to be based on an upper bound of the Rademacher complexity; comparing Rademacher complexity with VC-dimension-based bounds is not an advancement, and the nature of the bounds should be clarified in the main text (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 similarly questions whether bounds based on parameter norm (Rademacher complexity) would be tighter than those in (8).
- The paper dedicates substantial space to established definitions and findings, while the main contributions are only in the last three pages (Reviewer 1).
- Several unexplained aspects in experiments: why “different distribution” case sometimes achieves higher accuracy than “same distribution” for few labeled samples (Reviewer 2); empirical verification of dimension dependence is missing (Reviewer 2).
- Terminology issues: “self-supervision” is misleading; the method uses pseudo-labels better termed “self-training” (Reviewer 4). There is a typo “given” in the abstract (Reviewer 3) and a typo “Hilbert space of constrained probability measures” after equation (6) (Reviewer 4).
- The condition \(n > \Omega(m^2/d)\) does not incorporate the amount of distribution shift; data from totally different domains would not help (Reviewer 3).
- In Theorem 4.1, the expectation is only w.r.t. \(P_0\), but unlabeled samples come from \(P_1\); if this is folded into a high-probability statement, it seems the randomness of the unlabeled dataset is not separately considered (Reviewer 2).
- Intuition for why the error in the robust loss increases with \(\gamma\) is missing (Reviewer 2).
- Notation in (1) \(\hat{R}(\theta,S)\) is confusing and not used later (Reviewer 2).
