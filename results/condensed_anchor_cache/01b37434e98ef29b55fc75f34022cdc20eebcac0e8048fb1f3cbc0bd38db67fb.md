## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an Individual-Global-identically-Distributed (IGD) principle for offline cooperative multi-agent decision making (MADM) using diffusion models. IGD generalizes the IGM principle by requiring that the centralized diffusion model’s generated outcome be identically distributed as the collective outcomes of multiple individual agent diffusion models. The authors introduce DoF, a diffusion factorization framework that uses a noise factorization function to decompose a centralized diffusion model into multiple decentralized ones (theoretically satisfying IGD) and a data factorization function to model inter-agent data relationships. Experiments on SMAC and MPE benchmarks show DoF outperforms existing MADM methods, improving scalability and cooperation.

### Strengths
- **Novel and promising idea**: IGD is an interesting and promising generalization of IGM to diffusion models, with potential to address the CTDE paradigm gap in diffusion-based offline MARL (cited in Chen et al. TMLR 2024). It recovers IGM or risk-sensitive IGM principles.
- **Theoretical grounding**: The paper theoretically shows that the noise factorization functions in DoF satisfy the IGD principle.
- **Simple yet effective algorithm**: The algorithm design is simple and demonstrated effective across a series of benchmarks with various baselines.
- **Strong empirical performance**: Extensive experiments on SMAC and MPE show DoF outperforms competing methods, with good scalability as the number of agents increases.
- **Novel factorization methods**: DoF introduces noise and data factorization functions that align agent-generated data with a centralized diffusion model while enabling decentralized execution, addressing coordination complexity and resource efficiency.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical concerns about IGD generalization**: The claim that IGD generalizes IGM is not well-defined. IGM uses a value function, not a distribution; no explicit mapping is provided (e.g., given a Q_tot satisfying IGM, what distribution p_θ_tot yields Eq.(1) for any history?). Formal statements and proofs are requested. Reviewer 3 finds the theory under Gaussian assumption (component-wise independence) uninteresting and hardly holds for non-diagonal covariance. Theorem 1 essentially restates that each component of isotropic Gaussian is independent.
- **Assumption of agent independence**: IGD’s definition appears to assume independence among agents’ behaviors/rollout trajectories, reflected in the simple concatenation noise factorization. This is a potential limitation.
- **Limited modeling power of factorization functions**: The primary options used (Concat, WConcat) may not capture complex inter-agent dependencies, given diffusion models’ sensitivity to noise estimation. Data factorization also primarily uses WConcat, with attention showing minimal improvement (Table 14). Centralized vs. decentralized implementation details for attention missing: how is centralized attention executed? Does it have access to information unavailable in real deployments, serving as an upper bound? Evidence that more complex h can compensate for simple f is requested.
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Algorithms missing from main manuscript; unclear how to formulate a diffusion algorithm based on Theorem 1.
  - Notations not well defined: ε in Theorem 1, role of θ in Eq (2), ϵ_1^{θ_1} in Eq (5). In Eq (3), “θ_i ⊂ θ_tot” should be “(θ_1,…,θ_K)=θ_tot”.
  - Section 4.3 overview confusing; a refined overview needed.
  - DoF-Policy vs. DoF-Trajectory: suitable scenarios not specified, results section fails to indicate which algorithm used, Figure 6 only shows DoF-Policy; missing implementation details for inverse dynamics model in DoF-Trajectory.
  - Figure 2(a) mislabels reverse diffusion output (should be x^{k-1} not x^k); caption lacks necessary descriptions.
  - POMDP setting needs more description in main text (transition depends on product space of all agents’ actions).
  - Typos: Def 1 “there exist … functions”, sentence before Section 3: “to for x^k”.
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - Scalability experiment only compares GPU memory and time with MADIFF, not with other baselines. Training time not reported (only inference time). Do diffusion-based methods have significantly higher computational cost compared to traditional methods?
  - Benchmarks use state vectors; unclear if the method applies to high-dimensional observations.
  - Codebase incomplete and lacks detailed instructions to reproduce all numerical results; fixing this would improve rating (Reviewer 1).
- **Baseline choice questioned**: QMIX is an online algorithm not designed for offline RL; rationale for including it and insights from comparison are unclear.
- **Potential issues**:
  - Can diffusion methods handle out-of-distribution situations? Can they be applied to competitive or mixed-motivation scenarios?
  - Reviewer 1 questions (overlapping with criticisms): (a) Why do agents trained with MADiff still suffer collisions and perform worse than DoF in Figure 1 if the difference is mainly scalability? (b) Why are both f and h necessary if they are functionally similar? (c) Suggestion: policy distillation methods (e.g., CDG) could be important baselines for factorization.
  - Reviewer 2: how long is training time compared to other methods? Are agents doing global training then individual sampling? Can agents train simultaneously?
- **Minority negative view**: Reviewer 3 (rating 3, confidence 2) has strong concerns about the theoretical contribution and assessment of IGD generalization, suggesting rejection. The empirical results show only slightly better performance. Reviewer 1 (rating 6) raises code reproducibility concerns. In contrast, Reviewers 2 and 5 (both rating 8) are more positive, praising the idea and empirical strength.
