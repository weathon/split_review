## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CatVTON, a lightweight virtual try-on diffusion model that reduces complexity by concatenating garment and person images along spatial dimensions, eliminating the need for ReferenceNet, image encoders (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2), text conditioning, and preprocessing steps like pose estimation or human parsing. The architecture comprises only a VAE and a denoising UNet (899.06M total parameters), with the text encoder removed (saving 167.02M) and only 49.57M trainable parameters (~5.51% of backbone) identified through ablation of self-attention modules. Inference memory usage is reduced by over 49% compared to diffusion-based baselines. Experiments on VITON-HD and DressCode datasets, plus in-the-wild scenarios, report superior FID, KID, SSIM, and LPIPS versus baselines (OOTDiffusion, etc.). Training uses a limited 73K-sample open-source dataset. A mask-free variant is derived from the trained mask-based model.

### Strengths
- **Lightweight architecture**: Removes the redundant ReferenceNet and all extra image encoders used in prior work, relying solely on concatenation in spatial dimensions. (All reviewers)
- **Parameter-efficient training**: Only 49.57M parameters trained by focusing on self-attention modules; retains priors from pre-trained diffusion models. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Simplified inference**: Eliminates pose estimation, human parsing, and text input; requires only person image, garment reference, and mask. Memory usage reduced by >49% vs. diffusion baselines. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Strong quantitative and qualitative results**: Outperforms OOTDiffusion and other recent methods on VITON-HD and DressCode across FID, KID, SSIM, LPIPS. Generates realistic details (logos, patterns). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4 – Reviewer 4 agrees on good performance)
- **Good generalization in the wild**: Performs well even with limited training data (73K samples) and in real-world scenarios. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Originality and clarity**: Novel approach of concatenation without ReferenceNet/encoders; well-structured writing with clear figures and tables. (Reviewer 3)
- **Significance**: Low training cost and inference memory make deployment more practical; insights may transfer to other diffusion-based tasks. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Mask dependence**: Effectiveness relies on accurate input masks; inaccurate masks significantly degrade quality. The mask-free model is derived from the trained mask-based model, but details on error accumulation, mechanisms to guide attention without masks, and quantitative comparison between mask-based and mask-free are lacking. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Numerical discrepancies with baselines**: Table 1 and Table 2 results for baseline methods (e.g., OOTDiffusion) differ from the original papers; it is unclear whether these numbers were reproduced by the authors or quoted, raising concerns about reliability and reproducibility. (Reviewers 1, 4) Reviewer 4 (score 5) considers this a critical unresolved issue and notes the authors disregarded a suggestion to use original evaluation scripts. Reviewer 3 (score 8) did not flag this, so there is clear disagreement.
- **Missing inference speed and computational cost metrics**: No FPS, latency, or deployability on embedded devices (mobile, edge) reported, despite the model’s claimed efficiency. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Lack of theoretical justification for removing all image encoders**: Many prior works (AnyDoor, MimicBrush, Uni-ControlNet) show that strong pre-trained encoders (CLIP, DINOv2) improve detail generation. The paper claims simple VAE concatenation yields better results without a valid explanation or analysis of why encoder features are not needed. (Reviewer 4) Reviewer 1 also notes the approach is overly engineered without theoretical validation.
- **Transferability not discussed**: No experiments or discussion on applying the efficient training strategy to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT) or to non-diffusion pipelines (GAN, flow-based). (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Insufficient detail on mask-free training**: The dataset used for pseudo-data construction, exact process, visual comparisons with mask-based model, and whether this dataset will be open-sourced are not provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inconsistent description of input concatenation**: The overall architecture figure and main text are unclear whether concatenation is performed in pixel-space or latent-space. (Reviewer 4)
- **Undefined terms**: “DREAM λ” is introduced without explanation. (Reviewer 4) “IPS metric” is mentioned but not defined. (Reviewer 4)
- **Formatting and presentation issues**: Inconsistent font sizes in Table 1 and Table 4, excessive whitespace in Table 4, tightly arranged images in Figures 5 and 6. (Reviewer 3) Misuse of \cite{} instead of \citep{} for citations. (Reviewer 4)
- **Need for training code**: Code not provided, hindering reproducibility verification. (Reviewer 1)
- **Trade-offs in training modules not analyzed**: Table 3 shows training Unet or Transformers individually yields better results but with higher memory/slower training; no analysis of when to prefer one over the other. (Reviewer 1)
