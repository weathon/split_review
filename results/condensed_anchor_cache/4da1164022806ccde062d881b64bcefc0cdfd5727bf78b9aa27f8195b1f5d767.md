## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores the scaling law in downstream vision tasks, focusing on visual object tracking. It investigates three scaling factors (training data volume, model size, input resolution) and observes that conventional training yields diminishing returns as models scale. To address this, the authors propose DT-Training, which uses small teacher transfer and dual-branch alignment, and a closed-loop scaling strategy that incrementally scales the model. The resulting tracker outperforms existing methods on multiple benchmarks, and the approach is validated on other tasks (e.g., object detection).

### Strengths
- Thorough empirical analysis of scaling law dimensions (data, model size, resolution) on visual tracking.
- DT-Training and closed-loop scaling are novel contributions that tackle optimization challenges and enable iterative refinement.
- Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks demonstrate effectiveness and robust transfer ability.
- Ablation studies are well-designed and confirm the benefits of each component.
- Validation on other downstream vision tasks (e.g., detection) shows generalizability.
- The problem is interesting and the proposed solution is sensible.
- The framework effectively identifies and mitigates the bottleneck in training large models for tracking.
- Competitive performance is achieved on most datasets.
- One reviewer (score 6, minority more positive) particularly noted the well-designed ablations and experimental evidence supporting effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and clarity are questioned.** The paper’s motivation for applying scaling law to downstream tasks is unclear; the specific problems it solves are not well articulated. The relationship between the three factors and scaling law is vague. One reviewer found the paper well-organized, but another reported difficulty understanding key elements (e.g., what the closed-loop scaling strategy entails, Figure 2 structure).
- **Computational cost and efficiency trade-offs are insufficiently addressed.** The closed-loop training and dual-branch alignment introduce significant cost; a detailed cost analysis including inference speed, training FLOPs, and comparison with simpler training strategies is missing. The practical value for real-time tracking is questionable. (All reviewers raise this.)
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.** The influence of core hyperparameters (those in DT-Training and closed-loop scaling) is not studied.
- **Limited real-world validation.** No case studies or experiments in realistic scenarios are provided to demonstrate practical utility.
- **Little discussion of limitations or future directions.** The paper does not explicitly state what the method cannot handle or how it might be improved.
- **Choice of visual tracking as the primary task requires stronger justification.** Object detection, being more fundamental and semantically richer, might be a better testbed for scaling law. The class-agnostic nature of tracking may obscure scaling effects on semantic understanding.
- **Comparison with prior work is incomplete.** The proposed method underperforms LoRAT-L384 on LaSOT (73.1% vs. 75.1% success rate) despite being more complex. Direct comparisons on computational cost and performance across a wider set of benchmarks are lacking. Additionally, key recent trackers (e.g., OSTrack, ODTrack) are not compared in depth.
- **Scaling factors analysis is superficial.** The impact of model size, data volume, and resolution is already known from prior work (e.g., OSTrack, ODTrack). The paper only provides a simple scale comparison (Base, Large) without detailed insight into how specific parameter increases affect tracking behavior.
- **Teacher–student component lacks novelty.** Knowledge distillation with a smaller teacher is common in tracking (e.g., Mixformer-V2, AttTrack). Using a smaller teacher to guide a larger model is counterintuitive and not justified; why not use a larger teacher?
- **Contradictory evidence in Table/Line 287.** Expanding training data from the baseline (OSTrack) caused a drop in AUC from 69.1 to 68.4, contradicting the claim that scaling data improves performance.
- **Benchmark evaluation alone is not a major contribution.** No new dataset or evaluation protocol is introduced. The influential VAST dataset (larger, more challenging) is not used.
- **Convergence behavior is unexplored.** It is unclear whether the proposed method speeds up convergence compared to standard training.
- **Upper limit of scaling law in downstream tasks is not discussed.** Whether performance saturates and at what point is not analyzed.
- **The specific benefits of each sub-method (small teacher transfer, dual-branch alignment, closed-loop steps) are vague; how exactly each unlocks model potential is not explained.**
