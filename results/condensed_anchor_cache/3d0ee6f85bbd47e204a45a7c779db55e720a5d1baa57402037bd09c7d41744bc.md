## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BreZOSBA, a query-efficient algorithm for nonsmooth stochastic black-box bilevel optimization. It combines zeroth-order (ZO) gradient estimation via Gaussian smoothing with a Bregman distance-based proximal term to handle nonsmoothness and lack of gradients. The lower-level objective is assumed to be smooth and strongly convex. The method achieves a query complexity of \(\mathcal{O}(d_1(d_1+d_2)^2 \epsilon^{-2})\) to reach an \(\epsilon\)-stationary point. Experiments on data hyper-cleaning and hyper-representation learning (on MNIST, FashionMNIST, Cifar10, SVHN) show competitive performance against two baseline methods.

### Strengths
- The combination of zeroth-order gradient estimation and Bregman distance is novel and inspiring for black-box bilevel problems (Reviewer 2).
- The theoretical convergence guarantee provides non-asymptotic analysis with query complexity \(\mathcal{O}(d_1(d_1+d_2)^2 \epsilon^{-2})\), which is an improvement over existing methods and considered nearly optimal (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The single-loop structure yields computational savings compared to double-loop alternatives (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results on data hyper-cleaning and hyper-representation learning tasks demonstrate that BreZOSBA outperforms the baseline methods (Reviewers 1, 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of motivation for fully black-box setting and scalability concerns** [R1, R3]: The practical applicability of bilevel optimization with both inner and outer levels as black-boxes is not adequately motivated. In many ML applications (e.g., hyperparameter optimization, meta-learning), the inner-level variables represent deep neural network parameters, where using zeroth-order methods for the inner loop would be extremely slow and may not converge. The choice of ZO for both levels restricts scalability; experiments are limited to small-scale toy tasks (data hyper-cleaning, hyper-representation learning on MNIST, FashionMNIST, Cifar10, SVHN), raising doubts about real-world applicability. There is no discussion of computational cost in high-dimensional settings compared to gradient-based alternatives.

2. **Limited experimental evaluation and baseline selection** [R1, R3]: The experiments include only two baselines, which are not particularly strong. In particular, HOZOG [1] is designed for hyperparameter optimization but used in settings where it is not adequate, making the comparison unfair. Missing comparison with PZOBO [2], another hessian-free ZO method of direct relevance. Need more diverse and challenging benchmarks; baselines should reflect state-of-the-art in bilevel optimization. The authors should clarify why the chosen baselines are appropriate.

3. **Sparse literature review** [R1, R3]: The paper does not adequately cover recent relevant works, such as PZOBO [2]. It also lacks discussion on extensions and limitations in the broader BO context, e.g., bilevel problems without lower-level singleton constraint. A more comprehensive review and clear positioning of the proposed work among existing BO approaches is needed.

4. **Restrictive strong convexity assumption** [R2, R4]: The convergence analysis relies on strong convexity of the lower-level objective, which may not hold in many practical applications, potentially affecting algorithm performance (R2). Furthermore, such assumptions have been extensively studied in bilevel optimization; the authors should highlight the novel techniques that enable the complexity bound for black-box BLO (R4).

5. **Unjustified stationarity measure** [R4]: The stationarity measure \(\|G^t\|\) used in the theory lacks explanation; the authors should justify why this measure is equivalent to those used in compared methods.

6. **Subproblem difficulty with Bregman distance** [R2]: The subproblem for updating \(x\) may be difficult for some Bregman distance functions, especially when the structure of \(h(x)\) is unknown or complex.

7. **Error accumulation from Gaussian smoothing** [R1, R2]: Additional errors introduced at each iteration from Gaussian smoothing may accumulate. Applying smoothing twice (to convert second-order gradient to first and then to zeroth order) may introduce significant errors; the authors should clarify how these errors are mitigated (R1 Q3). The variance may be high, and although convergence is guaranteed in expectation, the actual iterates may be far from the solution (R2).

8. **Questionable convergence condition on smoothing parameters** [R1 Q2]: The theoretical convergence relies on sufficiently large values of \(B\) and \(B'\). The effect when these are of \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) is not explored, which could affect fairness of comparison with existing methods.

9. **Loose connection between Gaussian smoothing and Bregman distance** [R1 Q1]: The paper feels like two separate parts (Gaussian smoothing and Bregman distance for the nonsmooth outer problem) that are loosely connected. The authors should clarify why the nonsmooth problem necessitates zeroth-order gradient descent.

10. **Unclear benefit and selection of Bregman distance** [R2 Q1, R4 Q]: The impact of the Bregman distance function selection on performance is not studied; there is no systematic approach to choose the optimal function. The particular Bregman distance used in experiments is not specified (R2 Q1). Moreover, the overall benefit of using Bregman distance in the algorithm is not clearly explained (R4 Q).

11. **Missing clarity on feasibility of Gaussian smoothing** [R2 Q2]: Does the algorithm have a special structure that supports the feasibility of Gaussian smoothing? Can this technique be adopted by other gradient-based algorithms?

12. **Presentation issues** [R1, R3, R2]:
    - (a) The inner-level objective \(g\) is smooth but this is not explicitly stated after the problem definition.
    - (b) The text mentions that using ZO for the inner level is not efficient, yet the algorithm uses it (Equation 14).
    - (c) There is a typo: to derive Equation (64), the coefficient of \(\mathcal{B}_{\Psi_t}(x, x_t)\) in Equation (63) should be \(1/\alpha\) (R2).
