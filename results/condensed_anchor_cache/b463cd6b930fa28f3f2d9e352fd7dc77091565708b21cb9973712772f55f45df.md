## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CBF-LLM, a control-based framework that uses a Control Barrier Function (CBF) safety filter during decoding to enforce user-specified constraints (e.g., sentiment, topic) in LLM generation. The filter, implemented with a RoBERTa classifier, checks token candidates from a top-k set (e.g., k=30) and zeros out probabilities of tokens that violate a constraint (hard filtering). Experiments are performed on Llama3 with a single prefix per constraint (positive sentiment, topic). The work draws an analogy between collision avoidance and safe text generation and introduces a hyperparameter α to adjust constraint tightness.

### Strengths
1. The method is learning-free and broadly applicable, requiring only a pretrained classifier for the constraint, and can be retrofitted to any LLM, including Llama3, in a general way. (Reviewers 1, 4, 5)
2. The connection between control engineering (CBF, safety filter) and text generation is novel and interesting; the paper provides a detailed theoretical transfer and explanation, including a hyperparameter α to adjust tightness. (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
3. The paper is well-written, with clear figures and intuition behind the equations. (Reviewers 3, 5)
4. The framework offers inspiration for future non-parametric optimization methods for LLMs and demonstrates a weak-to-strong alignment (RoBERTa as constraint to LLaMA). (Reviewer 5)
5. One reviewer (Reviewer 4, rating 8) finds the approach has a useful theoretical component and has been applied under different constraints (positive/negative sentiment, topic). (Reviewer 4 – minority more positive view)

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of comparison to existing baselines.** The paper does not compare against published decoding-time alignment methods, including:
   - Mudgal et al., "Controlled Decoding from Language Models", ICML 2024.
   - SafeDecoding (Xu et al., ACL 2024).
   - FUDGE (Yang et al., 2021).
   - DeAL (Huang et al., 2024).
   Even a naïve blocklist baseline is used, but no standard constrained decoding techniques. The proposed method is very similar to SafeDecoding and related inference-time constrained decoding. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
2. **Insufficient and unconvincing experiments.** All experiments use only a single sentence per constraint (positive sentiment, topic). No evaluation on standard LLM benchmarks or safety datasets (e.g., AdvBench, HarmBench, Anthropic HH-RLHF) is provided. The paper lacks quantitative metrics for toxicity, bias, fluency, naturalness, helpfulness, or general LLM capability impact. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
3. **No human evaluation of generated outputs.** The paper provides only example sentences but no human rating of generation quality, making it unclear whether the method adds value over simpler approaches. (Reviewers 1, 2)
4. **Reliance on a questionable classifier (L-CF).** The paper assumes RoBERTa is a golden classifier for the constraint, without evaluating its accuracy, reliability, or potential biases. Using a sentiment analysis model for toxicity is unnatural; existing toxicity classifiers or guardrails (e.g., Llama-Guard) are standard. The robustness of the approach under varying classifier reliability is unexplored. (Reviewers 2, 5)
5. **Hard-filtering vs. soft scoring.** The method uses hard filtering (zero out violating tokens), which may unnecessarily prune promising sequences. For example, generating "Despite a slow start, the movie blossomed..." would be cut off at "slow start" when controlling for positive sentiment, whereas soft-scoring methods (like FUDGE or DeAL) would allow it. The paper provides no ablation comparing hard vs. soft filtering. (Reviewer 3)
6. **Single-token decisions are unreliable.** A single token change does not necessarily shift a sequence from safe to unsafe; safety often depends on the entire generated text. (Reviewer 2)
7. **Computational cost not discussed.** The method applies the filter at each decoding step on a top-k set (k=30). No average token generation time or cost comparison to baseline is provided. (Reviewers 1, 2)
8. **Theoretical framework is cumbersome.** The control engineering analogy (car avoiding obstacles) is not persuasive—in a car, internal processes change, but here the LLM generates normally and the filter only prunes later. The presentation of standard greedy decoding as a control process is confusing and roundabout. (Reviewers 2, 4)
9. **Missing details and ablations.** 
   - Is the RoBERTa classifier trained on whole sequences or prefixes? (Reviewer 3 question – missing ablation)
   - Does the method handle multiple constraints simultaneously? (Reviewer 1)
   - What is the role of data size used to train the classifier? (Reviewer 1)
   - Why not evaluate on a small-scale widely-used dataset like a subset of Anthropic HH-RLHF? (Reviewer 5 question – missing evaluation)
   - The paper should relate more to existing NLP decoding algorithms to clarify contributions and differences. (Reviewer 2 question – missing discussion)
