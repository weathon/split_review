- Scores: 8, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers evaluate a paper providing the first theoretical analysis of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for continual learning (CL) in an overparameterized linear regression setting. The work proposes a locality loss and early termination of router updates to stabilize expert specialization, derives bounds on forgetting and generalization error showing MoE outperforms a single expert, and validates on synthetic data, MNIST, and CIFAR-10. Reviewers find the theoretical contribution original and rigorous, but note limitations in scope (linear only, small-scale experiments), lack of intuitive explanations, missing comparisons to state-of-the-art CL methods and benchmarks (e.g., CIFAR-100, ImageNet), and overclaimed novelty.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical foundations**: Provides the first rigorous theoretical bounds on expected forgetting and generalization error for MoE in CL, establishing clear benefits over a single-expert model (R1, R2, R3).
- **Novel algorithmic components**: The locality loss and early termination mechanism are original contributions that theoretically motivate task clustering and stable expert specialization (R1, R2).
- **Load balancing**: The analysis shows balanced expert utilization, which improves generalization and avoids overloading any single expert (R2).
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments on synthetic linear regression and real datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) support theoretical findings, including the counterintuitive result that more experts may not improve performance (R1, R2, R3).
- **Clarity and structure**: Paper is well written and organized, making the theoretical derivations accessible (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of theoretical analysis**: The analysis is restricted to overparameterized linear regression tasks and a one-layer MoE with per-task routing. Extensions to non-linear deep networks, per-token routing, and modern large-scale MoE architectures are not addressed, raising questions about practical applicability (R1, R3). One reviewer notes that "nonlinear deep neural network cases can be more practical" (R3).
- **Small-scale experiments**: Empirical evaluation is limited to synthetic data, MNIST, and CIFAR-10 with only three tasks. Lacks experiments on standard CL benchmarks (CIFAR-100, ImageNet) and comparisons with state-of-the-art CL methods (e.g., SEED [1], other MoE-based approaches). This makes it difficult to assess practical impact and how the method compares to existing approaches (R2). Reviewer 2 raised their score to 6 only after seeing MNIST/CIFAR-10, still considering it insufficient.
- **Technical weakness in Proposition 4**: The model gap term uses Euclidean distance between weights, which treats all weight differences equally and does not account for varying importance of features. This may not accurately capture task divergence or its impact on forgetting (R2).
- **Overclaimed novelty and missing related work**: The claim of being "the first theoretical analysis of MoE" (Line 532) is overstated given existing theoretical work on MoE (e.g., [1] referenced by reviewer); also, the idea that forgetting in MoE can be mitigated solely through specialization is not entirely new (R1 cites [1], [2]). The paper should discuss these works and contextualize its contribution (R1, R3).
- **Lack of intuitive explanations**: The presentation would benefit from more intuitive descriptions of key ideas (e.g., that correct routing alone can prevent forgetting, why early termination helps, and how the locality loss induces specialization) (R1).
- **Computational overhead**: The expert parameter update relies on closed-form matrix inversion (Line 221-224), which may be more costly than gradient-based methods, especially when scaling (R3).
- **Unclear definition of continual learning**: The setting has more experts than tasks (M > N), which may reduce to training task-specific experts rather than true continual learning. Clarification is needed on how this setup differs from standard CL and why it is studied (R2).
- **Early termination and load balancing**: The motivation for early termination is partly to prevent imbalanced loads, but whether it actually improves balanced expert usage is not explicitly shown (R3).
- **Missing analysis on cross-task/cross-expert transfer**: It is unclear if the proposed system allows any transfer between tasks or experts, or if it lies at the extreme of no transfer (R1).
- **Questions unresolved** (overlap with weaknesses or missing experiments):
  - Is expert specialization necessary for CL with MoEs, or could other routing strategies work? (R1)
  - For load balancing, why use the proposed loss rather than entropy maximization? What are its benefits? (R1)
  - For MNIST, which DNN model is used and how are 10 classes split into 3 tasks? (R2)
  - Does the same early termination strategy work in more complex experiments? (R3)
- **Minority contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 6) is more critical of the experimental validation and technical soundness of Proposition 4; they explicitly state the evaluation is insufficient without standard benchmarks and SOTA comparisons. Reviewer 1 and 3 (score 8) are more positive overall but still note scope limitations. This disagreement reflects the gap between theoretical contribution and empirical demonstration.
