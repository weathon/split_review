## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses robust MDPs under a static model of transition uncertainty (transition function fixed per episode) and assumes a rectangular uncertainty set. It leverages the known non-duality-gap property when optimizing over stationary policies, showing equivalence between static and dynamic stationary formulations. The authors propose a meta-algorithm IWOCS that incrementally builds a discrete set of transition functions by identifying worst-case kernels and solving the corresponding non-robust MDPs, then deriving a robust policy from this sequence. A deep RL version (IWOCS+SAC) is introduced for continuous state-action spaces. Experiments on Windy Gridworld and MuJoCo control tasks compare against robust RL baselines. Reviewers are split: two rate the paper as 3, one as 1 (very negative), one as 3, and one as 6 (very positive). Common appreciation for the problem relevance and empirical evaluation; major concerns about originality, theoretical depth, scalability, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- Robust MDPs and improving robustness of RL against transition uncertainty are important and timely problems, with relevance to robotics and real-world RL applications.
- The paper is well-written, with clear notation, concise arguments, and a demonstrated deep understanding of prior robust RL literature.
- The proposed methodology (IWOCS and its deep variant) is clearly and comprehensively explained, and appears simple to implement.
- Strong empirical results are provided: extensive experiments on classic (tabular) and deep RL benchmarks (MuJoCo continuous control tasks) validate effectiveness, especially in continuous state-action spaces. The amount of empirical results is appropriate.
- The Deep IWOCS variant represents a meaningful improvement over existing robust MDP methods that mainly focus on discrete state-action spaces (Reviewer 1), though this is contested by Reviewer 3 due to prior work.
- The algorithm demonstrates effectiveness across a wide range of deep RL control benchmarks (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Originality and prior work**: The paper misses a key reference (Gadot et al. 2024, ICML) that proposes a similar approach—estimating the worst-case kernel to solve robust MDPs using a single transition function without requiring specialized SAC implementation. This limits originality and puts the empirical contribution in check. A clear discussion of differences, and preferably an empirical comparison, is needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Theoretical contribution**: Most theoretical results presented (no duality gap, equivalence of static and dynamic stationary models) are well-known; their application to IWOCS is trivial. No in-depth analysis of advantages such as scalability analysis, sample complexity, or convergence guarantees is provided. (Reviewer 4) The sampling-based approach (for the worst-case search) raises concerns: solving max/min via sampling generally requires an exponential number of points for accuracy; there is no discussion on how many transition samples are needed to obtain a robust solution. (Reviewer 1)
- **Precise problem statement and motivation**: The paper lacks a precise problem statement, making it impossible to prove meaningful results—critical when dealing with uncertainties and performance/safety. (Reviewer 2) The motivation appears contradictory: the static model is claimed to be harder than the dynamic model, yet later equivalence is shown under mild assumptions, making the approach seem similar to robust value iteration but with added complexity. (Reviewer 4) The contrast with dynamic uncertainties is an odd choice; the dynamic model is a generalization, so why claim the static setting is more challenging without a precise description? (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparisons and experiments**: No comparison with model-free algorithms (Q-learning, SAC, PPO, TRPO) or on larger benchmarks (Atari, DM Control). (Reviewer 1) Training time of IWOCS is about twice that of SAC (Table 6); a detailed breakdown of computational cost (worst-case search vs. policy optimization), potential optimization methods, and training times of other robust RL baselines should be provided. (Reviewer 5) The method does not seem scalable; tests on larger benchmarking tasks are needed. (Reviewer 1) The paper should relate to Meta-RL methods for uncertain transitions (task inference) and clarify similarities/differences. (Reviewer 5)
- **Methodological concerns and missing analysis**: The method is described as ad hoc, leaving many choices to the user. (Reviewer 2) The presentation of the worst-case identification in Deep IWOCS is difficult to parse; the supplemental material lacks instructions for using the code, and a pseudo-code is needed for reproducibility. (Reviewer 3) Algorithm 1 has a missing close bracket in the if statement. (Reviewer 4) How does the sampling-based approach converge as the number of samples increases? Theoretical and experimental analyses are missing. (Reviewer 1) Why is IWOCS necessary if the discrete set of MDPs is small? The complexity of finding the worst-case transition should be compared to direct computation of \(\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\max_\pi V_T^\pi(s_0)\). (Reviewer 4) The claim that \(T_{i+1}\) can be chosen with another criterion without losing properties may not hold for non-average/extreme criteria, potentially limiting generalizability. (Reviewer 4) In the deep setting, it is unclear why IWOCS converges to a better optimizer than RVI; in the tabular setting, would IWOCS and RVI yield the same optimizer? (Reviewer 4) Would the approach work with non-rectangular uncertainty sets? (Reviewer 1) Could a simple dual-gradient method solve the minimax problem? (Reviewer 1) How to compute \(V_{T_{i+1}}^{\pi_i}(s_0)\) and \(Q_i(s_0,\pi_i(s_0))\) for a distribution of starting states (continuous control tasks)? (Reviewer 5) Provide a detailed comparison of computation times for IWOCS vs. IWOCS* (grid-search vs. CMA-ES) and guidelines for when to use each. (Reviewer 5) Can the idea be used for robust RL under observation perturbations? (Reviewer 5)
- **Literature gap**: The paper misses a large body of relevant work from probabilistic formal methods on RL and IRL for robust/uncertain MDPs (e.g., NeurIPS22, arXiv 2312.06344, ICAPS). (Reviewer 2) Also missing reference to Gadot et al. (2024) as noted above.
- **Minor issues**: Typo in line 085: "denote" should be "denotes". (Reviewer 5)
