## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree the paper addresses an interesting and well-motivated problem—how overparameterization affects Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)—and provides extensive experiments across vision, language, graph, and reinforcement learning domains. However, multiple reviewers find the theoretical analysis unable to support the main empirical claims, and some empirical conclusions are either anticipated or contradicted by the paper’s own results. The significance of the contribution is questioned: two reviewers (R2 and R3) consider the novelty marginal, while R1 and R4 are more positive about the work’s interest and potential usefulness.

### Strengths
- Interesting and well-motivated problem; the perspective of investigating SAM under overparameterization is significant (R1, R3).
- Very well written; clear structure and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- Extensive experiments across diverse settings (vision, language, graph, reinforcement learning) and architectures (MLP, ResNet, LSTM, CNN, ViT) (R1, R3, R4).
- The experiments are insightful: they show that overparameterization increases the performance gap between SAM and SGD, and highlight the roles of label noise, sparsity, weight decay, and early stopping (R2).
- Nice empirical and theoretical work on overparameterization and SAM; useful for the ICLR community (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical analysis does not support the main empirical findings.**  
   - The linear stability analysis (Section 6.1) does not demonstrate *how* overparameterization affects SAM (i.e., why greater overparameterization leads to flatter minima). It only shows that a larger perturbation radius ρ gives a tighter bound on the maximum Hessian eigenvalue, without explaining why overparameterization permits or necessitates a larger ρ (R2).  
   - The convergence analysis (Section 6.2) lacks relevance to generalization (the paper’s core focus), does not clarify how the degree of overparameterization influences convergence speed, and the exponential convergence result also holds for SGD (Bassily et al., 2018). The proof appears to be a minor extension from SGD, treating SAM as SGD + small perturbation, and does not address whether overparameterization accelerates convergence (R2).  
   - The theoretical analysis in Sections 6.1 and 6.2 uses a variant of SAM (without normalization) rather than the original SAM; this should be clearly presented to avoid confusion (R2).  
   - The authors themselves note in Section 7 (limitations) that the theoretical analysis cannot support the findings in Sections 3 and 4, making the theoretical contribution limited and the main discovery unsupported (R3).  
   - No clear theoretical support is given for the central claim that overparameterization critically influences SAM (R2, R3). A reviewer specifically asks for analysis on diagonal linear networks to provide such support (R2).

2. **Empirical findings are partially anticipated, incomplete, or contradictory.**  
   - It is already well known that overparameterization improves generalization for SGD (e.g., ResNet-152 outperforms ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10). That overparameterization also improves SAM is unsurprising; the non-trivial aspect is the *increase in gap* between SAM and SGD (Fig. 1). The title and abstract should be reconsidered to avoid trivializing the contribution (R2).  
   - Overparameterization does not *consistently* help SAM find more generalizable solutions as model size scales. In Fig. 1 and Fig. 7, results for MLP on MNIST, ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, LSTM on SST2, and CNN on Atari show that generalization improvement sometimes plateaus or declines. Furthermore, without sufficient regularization, SAM may not benefit (Fig. 5). Also, ViT on CIFAR-10 is tested in Fig. 5 but not included in Fig. 1; the authors should include it in Fig. 1 (R3).  
   - Increasing width (neurons per layer, Fig. 1) versus increasing depth (number of layers, Fig. 17) yields different conclusions. In Fig. 17, for ResNet on CIFAR-10, deeper models do not show a benefit from overparameterization for SAM, contradicting the main claim (R3).  
   - The claim that overparameterization secures robustness to label noise is overstated. In Fig. 12, the increased margin improvement of SAM over SGD with larger model size is primarily due to SGD’s performance dropping dramatically under high label noise, not because SAM itself becomes more robust. SAM’s robustness to label noise is already well known (R3).  
   - The effect of sparsity (Fig. 5) contradicts the main argument that SAM benefits from overparameterization. As sparsity increases (making the model more under-parameterized), the generalization improvement of SAM over SGD *increases*. The authors should explain this contradiction (R3).  
   - In Section 4.2, the statement “implicit bias of SAM increases with overparameterization” is over-claimed. The authors only showed that a larger perturbation radius can be used when scaling up parameters; this does not imply that larger scale induces a stronger implicit bias toward flatter minima (R3).

3. **Missing comparisons and analyses.**  
   - A discussion of higher-order moments of the Hessian is missing. The authors should compare their results on convergence of higher-order Hessian moments with existing literature (e.g., Tahmasebi et al., “A Universal Class of Sharpness-Aware Minimization Algorithms”, ICML 2024) and provide a literature review on using higher-order moments to define flatness (R1).  
   - In Fig. 2 and 3, the comparison is between GD and SAM; a comparison between SGD and SAM would be more informative (R3).  
   - Clarify the shaded regions in Fig. 1 (and other figures): are they confidence intervals, standard deviations, or other measures of uncertainty? (R4).  
   - Provide plots (e.g., in the appendix) showing the relative improvement over a baseline for both SAM and SGD across different model sizes, to properly compare how each optimizer’s performance changes with overparameterization (R4).  
   - Min-max optimization (used in SAM) has known limitations: high complexity, oscillatory behavior, sensitivity to initialization. How are these addressed in the current work? Could properties of equation (3) help mitigate them? Did the authors observe these specific issues in experiments? (R4).  
   - Comment on whether Elliptic Loss Regularization could be a viable alternative to the min-max formulation in SAM, and what potential advantages or disadvantages it would have (R4).  
   - Is there an “optimal” number of parameters where computational cost is acceptable? Did the authors observe diminishing returns in performance improvement as model size increased? Provide guidance on balancing computational cost with performance gains when using SAM in practice (R4).
