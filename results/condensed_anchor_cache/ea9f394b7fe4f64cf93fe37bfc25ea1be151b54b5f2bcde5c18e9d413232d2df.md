## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper presents PSHead, a coarse-to-fine framework for reconstructing high-fidelity 360° 3D avatars from a single face image using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS). The coarse stage leverages a mixture of diffusion priors (T2I, I2I, T2V) via SDS losses, and the fine stage refines the result using face-specific priors (DreamBooth, landmark-guided ControlNet, CodeFormer). Experiments on PointAvatar, CelebA, and a private dataset show improved quantitative/qualitative results over baselines (Magic123, DreamGaussian, Era3D, PanoHead). Two reviewers (R1, R4) rate the paper 5 (positive), while two (R2, R3) rate 3 (more critical), highlighting concerns about novelty, side-view quality, and computational cost.

### Strengths

- **Effective integration of multiple diffusion priors:** PSHead successfully combines T2I, I2I, and T2V diffusion models via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) for view-consistent coarse geometry – the first such attempt (R1, R4). R4 specifically notes that components like DreamBooth, T2V‑SDS, and Landmark ControlNet improve head generation quality over the DreamGaussian baseline.
- **Addresses a significant problem:** The task of photorealistic 3D head reconstruction from a single image is well-motivated (R2).
- **Comprehensive validation of design choices:** The paper includes thorough ablations (e.g., Fig. 4, Tab. 3) evaluating each component and its contribution (R4). R2 agrees that the work effectively validates critical design elements.
- **Good writing and literature review:** The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1), and includes a comprehensive review of related works (R2).
- **Impressive photorealistic results:** Despite side-view weaknesses (see below), the method generates high-fidelity 360° 3D-GS avatars with detailed facial features, outperforming several baselines in novel-view metrics (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited technical novelty / incremental contributions:** The work mainly refines existing modules (DreamBooth, ControlNet, CodeFormer, SDS) into a pipeline, with predictable improvements (R2). R1 and R2 call for more design insight rather than a purely empirical engineering combination. R1 specifically notes the authors should explain why I2V was not used and provide an ablation for I2V. R4 similarly questions whether a single image-to-video model might be sufficient instead of the three separate priors – an ablation is missing.
- **Severe side/back view quality degradation:** The claimed 360° rendering exhibits excessive noise and blur in side and back views, not clearly better than PanoHead (R3, R4). The 360° videos in the supplementary show severe blur, especially in back views (R4). This undermines the claim of high-fidelity full-head reconstruction.
- **Multi-view inconsistency not addressed:** It is unclear how the method handles multi-view inconsistency in the refined novel views (R3). The refinement stage uses 2D face priors but does not explicitly enforce 3D consistency.
- **Identity preservation weak in novel views:** In Fig. 3 (column 4), there is a noticeable change in identity between the input and novel views (R3).
- **Geometry may not capture intrinsic details:** The geometry is primarily driven by SDS loss; it is not demonstrated that the personalized diffusion models capture geometric details better than generic SDS methods (R3).
- **High computational cost:** Optimization takes ~1.5 hours on a single NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPU, making the method impractical for many applications (R1, R2, R4). A runtime vs. quality trade-off analysis is missing (R4).
- **Lack of expression-drivable capability:** PSHead cannot drive expressions or animate the avatar, unlike prior works such as HeadGAP and Morphable Diffusion (R2). R1 also asks if the head model can be driven and requests novel pose synthesis results.
- **Missing comparisons and details:** The paper omits crucial information about model parameters and reconstruction times compared to other 3D generation works (e.g., in Tab. 2 and Tab. 3) (R2). R4 requests more geometry comparisons with Panohead (e.g., geometry quality).
- **Motivation on varying face scales is weakly supported:** The introduction claims existing methods struggle with varying face scales, but PanoHead’s results (e.g., Fig. 3 with shoulders) do not appear severely affected; more comparisons are needed in the main paper, not only in the appendix (R4).
- **Metrics in feature space may inflate results:** Novel-view metrics are computed in feature space (e.g., LPIPS, not PSNR/pixel space), which could explain why blurry views still score highly (R3). R3 recommends pixel-space evaluation for novel views.
- **Ablation shows T2V SDS gives only marginal gains:** Fig. 4 suggests T2V SDS offers marginal improvement over I2I SDS alone; it is not shown whether T2I+I2I alone, followed by the refinement stage, achieves similar performance (R3, R1). R1 similarly asks if a single diffusion prior (e.g., T2I) combined with self-enhancement is sufficient; additional ablations (T2I + self, I2I + self, T2V + self) are needed.
- **Gaze direction observation not corroborated:** In the ablations, the gaze direction observation is intuitive, but the same issue is not visible in Fig. 3 (R4).
- **Open questions about scope and bias:** R2 asks whether PSHead can handle reconstruction of head, upper body, and full body, and whether it exhibits racial inductive biases. R1 notes missing references (AvatarCLIP, DreamHuman, TADA!, HeadStudio).
