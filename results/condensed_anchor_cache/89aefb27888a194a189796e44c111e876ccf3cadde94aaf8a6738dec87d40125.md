## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SUMIT (Self sUpervised MIL dIsTance), an instance-embedding-based distance metric for Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) that maximizes bag distinction. SUMIT is optimized with five criteria: self-similarity within a bag (contrastive loss), instance reconstruction quality, robustness to sampling depth (invariance loss), preservation of triangle inequality, and instance-to-cluster separation. It uses energy distance for bag-level metric learning. Experiments are conducted on MUSK (molecule), Corel (image), and a novel Wikipedia-based multi-class text dataset. The authors claim within-bag similarity loss is the most important criterion. The approach bridges instance-level and bag-level embeddings by keeping all instance embeddings while enforcing within-bag proximity.

### Strengths
- Novel research direction: first method to optimize instance distance at the bag level, combining self-supervised learning with metric learning (Reviewers 1,2,3). The method is relevant for MIL applications beyond classification, such as clustering, statistical tests, and dimension reduction.
- The core idea of explicitly considering the distribution of instances within a bag for bag-to-bag metric learning is sound and reasonable (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is coherent and fluid, and the method is generally clear to read (Reviewers 1,3).
- Some experimental evidence is provided, including an ablation study on MUSK and KDE visualizations on a toy dataset (Reviewers 1,4).
- The proposed technique has potential applicability to other fields like metric learning (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority positive opinion (Reviewer 3, score 5):** This reviewer found the novelty and language strong, and considered the contribution level 3, higher than the other reviewers (each rated contribution 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparisons with state-of-the-art MIL methods and existing metric learning approaches.** No baselines are compared against SUMIT (Reviewers 1,2,4). Several relevant existing works are missing from the related work and experiments: e.g., Multi-instance Metric Learning (ICDM 2011), multi-task MIL distance metric learning, and multiple instance metric learning from faces (Reviewer 4). This makes it impossible to assess relative performance.
- **Unclear motivation and problem framing.** The paper does not clearly articulate what specific weaknesses exist in prior bag-level distance methods and how SUMIT addresses them (Reviewers 1,4). The importance of the problem itself is not well established (Reviewer 1).
- **Technical details are insufficient and unclear.**
  - The five losses are listed but their weighting, relative importance, and the necessity of each are not systematically studied across all datasets (Reviewers 1,2,3). Ablation is shown only on MUSK; no ablation on Corel or Wiki (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes weights are not specified.
  - How the five losses are combined and optimized together is unclear (Reviewer 1, Q1). What is the role of each loss in handling instance sparsity or overlap? (Reviewer 3, Q3).
  - The method description in Section 4 is confusing and insufficient to understand how SUMIT works (Reviewer 4).
  - The definition of X' and Y' in Equation 10 is not explained (Reviewer 1, Q2).
  - It is unclear why batch normalization is used in the encoder-decoder but layer normalization is used for the latent layer; no justification or reference is given (Reviewer 1, Q3).
- **Insufficient and narrow experimental validation.**
  - Experiments are limited in scope: only three datasets (MUSK, Corel, Wiki) with one type of evaluation (Reviewer 3). No experiments demonstrate labeling at the instance or bag level, nor is the method's predictive performance reported (Reviewer 3, Q4). The paper claims to bridge instance-level and bag-level approaches but does not quantify this.
  - The Wikipedia dataset is multi-class; it is unclear whether the method was applied as binary one-vs-rest or directly in multi-class setting, and how it would extend to multi-label data (Reviewer 3, Q5).
  - The experiment does not show how the method addresses the claimed challenges of sparsity or overlap between bag instances (Reviewer 3, Q3).
  - The claim that within-bag similarity loss is the most important is only supported by one ablation on MUSK; more systematic analysis is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and writing quality need significant improvement.** Several reviewers mention poor organization, unclear innovation description, and writing issues (Reviewers 2,3,4). Reviewer 4 suggests rewriting the entire paper. Minor: Line 146 states two types of datasets but three are used (Reviewer 1, Q5).
- **Self-supervised label is questionable.** The connection to self-supervised learning is unclear; the method appears to be a simple combination of energy distance and multiple losses without clear self-supervised justification (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing discussion of applications.** The paper does not explain how the learned distance metric would benefit downstream MIL tasks (e.g., clustering, classification, statistical tests) (Reviewer 3, Q1).
