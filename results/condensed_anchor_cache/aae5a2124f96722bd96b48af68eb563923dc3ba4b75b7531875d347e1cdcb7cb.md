## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LAST, a 3D human reconstruction framework from monocular video that integrates a pre-trained Image-to-Point (I2P) model into 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) optimization. The core idea is to disentangle latent factors from input video frames and model their dependencies, enabling dynamic adjustment of Gaussian point density and attributes. Experiments are performed on ZJU-MoCap, MoCap, and DNA-Rendering datasets. The method receives a wide score range (3, 6, 3, 3): one reviewer is notably more positive (score 6) while the other three are critical (score 3). Points of contention include novelty, clarity of motivation, qualitative results, and robustness.

### Strengths

- The I2P network produces a point cloud from 2D images and enhances the optimization of 3DGS (Reviewer 1). Extracting a high-dimensional latent vector rather than just a point cloud is interesting compared with existing baselines that use SfM (Reviewer 1).
- The method is composed of a pre-trained I2P model and an enhanced 3DGS module; the second module is informed by prior knowledge from the first. A point cloud is generated from latent factors disentangled from observed data using an encoder-decoder with intervention in VAE training (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well-written, presents a clear methodology, and is easy to follow (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The problem is challenging (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation studies and experiments are conducted on multiple datasets to verify each component (Reviewer 1). The quantitative results in Table 1 are strong (Reviewer 3). The benchmarks used for comparison are recent and up-to-date (Reviewer 3).
- The method appears fairly original due to its use of disentangling latent factors (Reviewer 3). Experimental results validate the method to some extent (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Method clarity and motivation**: The method section needs clarification: how are parent and child latent defined? Is this related to the kinematics chain? What are the explicit semantics produced by I2P? How was the I2P network trained? (Reviewer 1). Despite explanations, it is hard to comprehend the motivation (e.g., the claim about wrinkles correlating with body occlusion); a diagram is needed (Reviewer 3). The reviewer remains unconvinced why disentangling latent factors is necessary; concrete examples are required (Reviewer 3). The claim "existing methods lack research in 3D visual information" is questionable; relevant works (e.g., ConceptGraphs, LangSplat, LERF) should be discussed (Reviewer 4).

- **Quantitative and qualitative evaluation issues**:
  - Rendering results on ZJU-MoCap are not high-quality. The test poses/viewpoints across methods appear inconsistent: for the first human subject (e.g., 377), poses differ between Ours and GT; for the second subject, camera viewpoints differ between Ours and Neural Body (Reviewer 1). Experimental details are missing: training/test split, rendering resolutions on the three datasets; results in Fig. 4 appear very low resolution (Reviewer 1).
  - Qualitative results are not significant. In Fig. 2, the improvement is not significant (Reviewer 3). In Fig. 3, the improvement is also not obvious (Reviewer 3). Why is HuGS missing in Fig. 3 when it outperformed GauHuman in Fig. 2? (Reviewer 3).
  - Quantitative results for the DNA-Rendering dataset are not shown (Reviewer 3).
  - The quantitative evaluation seems somewhat limited (Reviewer 2). Although quantitative results are better than reported in the literature, the differences are not very large; the claim that the method extracts latent information that other methods do not is not fully substantiated (Reviewer 2).
  - Comparisons are mostly against NeRF-based methods; the authors should consider more 3DGS approaches (e.g., HuGS, GauHuman) and discuss theoretical differences and how they reflect in experiments (Reviewer 2).
  - Fig. 2 is partial, showing only a 2D view. Full 3D estimation should be displayed from multiple views (Reviewer 2).

- **Lack of dynamic and temporal evaluation**: The paper claims to handle dynamic postures but does not provide dynamic rendering results to verify temporal consistency or generalisation to new poses. A video demo is needed (Reviewer 1). Baseline AnimateNeRF is animatable; is LAST animatable? (Reviewer 1). The supplementary video could not be found, making it difficult to evaluate rendering quality (Reviewer 4).

- **Dependence on strong priors and robustness concerns**: The method exploits ground-truth 3D human representation as a strong prior; the concept of "reconstruction" is not completely true (Reviewer 2). The pipeline depends heavily on the causal learning module (dependency graph); it remains unclear how robust and easy it is to train. How resilient is the pipeline to unreliable causal inference results (noisy latent structure)? (Reviewer 4). The paper lacks a discussion of limitations and future work (Reviewer 4).

- **Minor**: The sentence “Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting, it is possible to achieve high expressivity with significantly faster training and rendering speeds compared to NeRF-based methods” is awkwardly phrased (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing information**: Training and inference time compared with existing approaches is not reported (Reviewer 1). The paper had more space for analysis and explanations but did not use it (Reviewer 2). Extra qualitative evaluations (multiple people, deformations) would help (Reviewer 2).

- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, acknowledging the method's clarity and the challenge of the problem. However, even that reviewer notes that all ingredients are well known, comparisons are limited, quantitative differences are small, and the exploitation of 3D ground truth is a limitation. The three other reviewers (score 3) consider the novelties insufficient, the qualitative results weak, and the motivation/explanation unconvincing.
