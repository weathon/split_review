## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies a shared underlying mechanism linking three phase transitions in the training of sinusoidal INRs for image regression: emergence of wave patterns in residuals, transition from fast to slow learning, and NTK alignment. It relates order parameters for each phenomenon to common variables from a local NTK approximation and presents experimental evidence that these transitions coincide. The work also introduces a new order parameter (MAG-Ma) that tracks violations in the translational symmetry of the NTK.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to follow and accessible to practitioners familiar with NTK and SIREN literature (R1, R3, R4).
- It provides a novel identification and unification of phase transitions in SIREN training, linking measures such as the spatial distribution of residuals, principal eigenvectors of the NTK, and training curves (R2, R5).
- The introduced order parameter MAG-Ma is a valuable measure of spatial alignment and explicitly tracks translational symmetry breaking of the NTK, which is a novel and interesting empirical finding (R2, R4, R5).
- The proposed metrics (e.g., MAG-Ma, AUC(v₀, ∇I)) are practical and fairly easy to calculate, suggesting potential utility for practitioners (R3).
- Claims are reasonable and partially justified by a mixture of approximate calculations and empirical insights; the work is modest and not overly bold (R3).
- Numerical experiments impressively show the alignment of critical points across order parameters and the effect of depth on critical behavior (R5).
- The paper demonstrates that the NTK provides a rich theoretical tool for deriving and relating order parameters to understand training dynamics (R1, R4).
- The analysis is sound and establishes unifying links between different measures of phase transitions (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental scale:** Experiments are limited to only a few images (e.g., cameraman, cat; five images total) and a single problem (2D image fitting). This is insufficient to draw general conclusions about training dynamics of SIRENs. Reviewers requested evaluation on a larger, more diverse set (e.g., ImageNette, 1000 images) to ensure robustness and generalizability (R2, R3, R4, R5). The paper does not address other common INR use cases (MRI/CT reconstruction, waveform inversion, SDF fitting) (R4).
- **Lack of generalizability to other architectures:** It is unclear to what extent the results are specific to sinusoidal networks versus applying to other INRs (e.g., ReLU MLPs) or CNNs. There is no comparative analysis with CNNs or ReLU-based networks, which is important given their prevalence in image tasks (R2, R3, R4). The paper does not disambiguate which aspects of the analysis are specific to SIREN versus general to any INR parameterization (R4).
- **Theoretical contributions are unclear/speculative:** Several key theoretical arguments are insufficiently justified. The analysis in Section 3.1 (ripple emergence) is based on fitting isotropic Gaussians to parameters, which is later contradicted in Section 3.2, making its contribution unclear (R4). The approximation that leads to the diffusion equation (Eq. 15) is handwavy and lacks validation—no experiment shows that training dynamics actually resemble that PDE (R3). The approximations in equations (13), (14), (28), and (29) are central to major findings but lack rigorous justification or error analysis; additional numerical verification or theoretical justification is needed (R5). The paper does not explain _why_ translational symmetry breaking or other phenomena occur—they are entirely empirical (R4). The argument that “NTK changes when training works” risks being tautological; the metric AUC(v₀, ∇I) simply tracks when image edges appear, which correlates one‑to‑one with loss/PSNR/SSIM (R4).
- **Missing theoretical analysis of ω₀:** The frequency parameter ω₀ is known to be the most important hyperparameter for SIREN training, yet it is excluded from the theoretical analysis. The experiments sweep ω₀ and show different behaviors, which only underscores the need for a theoretical account (R4, R5).
- **MAG-Ma may be a time-shifted order parameter:** In Figure 4, the confidence regions of MAG-Ma and other order parameters hardly overlap, suggesting MAG-Ma is delayed. The paper lacks an explanation for this temporal shift and whether it is consistent across settings (R5).
- **Presentation issues:** The writing is dense and not always sequential; e.g., the derivation from Eq. 2 to Eq. 3 is unclear—it should explicitly state that gradient descent is used with updates proportional to the negative gradient of the loss (R2). There are no forward references to the supplementary material where derivations for MAG-Ma etc. are provided (R5). Typos: "equation equation 5" on page 3 (R3, R4, R5). Figures repeatedly lack useful features such as colorbars and clear axis labels (R3). The preamble of Section 3 should be moved to the Introduction (R4). The term “field theory” is confusing when referring to spatial gradients/residuals (R4). The title and abstract imply multiple phase transitions, but there is effectively only one (R4).
- **No code provided:** Reproducibility and building on the work is hindered by the lack of shared code (R2).
- **Limited practical suggestions:** The paper offers no concrete guidance for improving ML performance, aside from noting ω₀ as important (Section 4.2). It would be beneficial to show how the proposed metrics could be used as training termination criteria or to adjust hyperparameters (R1, R2, R3).
- **Minority critical perspective:** One reviewer (R4, score 5) was significantly more critical, questioning whether the theoretical contributions are novel or merely speculative/tautological, and argued that the paper's empirical findings, while interesting, do not explain the underlying mechanisms. This contrasts with the overall positive but still conditional assessments from R2, R3, and R5 (score 6 each).
- **Specific reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms:** Are the data in Fig. 2, 5, 6 mean/variance over multiple runs of the same image or different images? (R2). Can order parameters be computed efficiently during training to serve as stopping criteria? (R2). Do the theoretical arguments for 2D image regression generalize to wider INR tasks (MRI, CT, etc.)? (R4). Why is ω₀ excluded from theoretical analysis when it is central to SIREN behavior? (R4). Are the results in Figs. 4–6 only valid for this specific configuration, or do they hold more generally? (R5). Is the temporal shift of MAG-Ma consistent across experiments? (R5). What is the train‑test gap in the theory and experiments (are test pixels used)? (R4).
