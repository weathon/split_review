- Scores: 8, 6, 10, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
NeuralPlane is a multi-stage neural reconstruction framework that outputs 3D scene plane primitives from multi-view posed images without ground-truth plane labels. It first generates 2D planar segments using a monocular module (combining a pre-trained normal predictor and SAM), then optimizes two neural fields: a density field guided by geometric losses and a self-supervised Neural Coplanarity Field that encodes semantic relationships. A final extraction stage converts the learned field into explicit 3D planes via feature clustering and RANSAC. Evaluated on ScanNetv2 and ScanNet++, the method achieves state-of-the-art geometry and semantic segmentation, producing crisp, semantically aligned planar reconstructions. Reviewers agree the paper is well-written and the system is technically novel, but raise concerns about complexity, hyperparameter sensitivity, limited scene count, dependence on 2D priors, and incomplete ablation on certain aspects.

### Strengths
- **Novelty in combining geometry and semantics**: The paper innovatively fuses geometric cues (surface normals, depth) with semantic consistency via the Neural Coplanarity Field and a contrastive loss, enabling unsupervised grouping of coplanar regions with distinct semantics. (R1, R3)
- **Unsupervised learning paradigm**: No ground-truth plane annotations are required; the method effectively learns from noisy monocular outputs (surface normal predictor, SAM) and multi-view consistency. (R1, R2)
- **High-quality reconstruction**: Outperforms existing methods (both learning-based and Geometry+RANSAC) on most geometric and semantic metrics on ScanNetv2 and ScanNet++, achieving clean, coherent plane segmentation. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Efficiency advantage**: The volume density representation allows faster training compared to purely implicit methods. (R3)
- **Comprehensive ablation studies**: Multiple ablation experiments support the effectiveness of each proposed module (e.g., geometric losses, coplanarity field). (R3)
- **Leveraged 2D plane observations to guide neural field learning**: Unlike some works that only detect planes, this framework uses the detected planes to direct the neural field optimization. (R4)
- **Clear writing and presentation**: Diagrams and technical descriptions are well structured, making the complex pipeline accessible. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Thorough experimental comparison**: Quantitative and qualitative comparisons are conducted against competitive baselines on multiple indoor datasets. (R1, R4)  *(Note: R2 questions the limited number of test scenes; see Weaknesses.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Complex system with many hyperparameters**: The pipeline contains numerous submodules and hyperparameters (e.g., push loss thresholds *to, tn, m*, RANSAC parameters, number of semantic prototypes, SAM mask size, loss balancing weights, thresholds for negative pairs in coplanar field). Most reviewers note this complexity as a limitation. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: The paper does not provide systematic ablation or investigation of how key hyperparameters affect results across different indoor scene types. Whether a universal set of parameters works for all scenes remains unclear. (R1, R3)
- **Limited evaluation scope**:
  - Only 12 scenes are used for quantitative evaluation (possibly from ScanNet and ScanNet++), raising concerns about generalizability to larger and more diverse environments. (R2)
  - The method is only tested on indoor datasets (ScanNet, 7-scenes). The unsupervised advantage is not demonstrated on outdoor or street-view scenes (e.g., autonomous driving) to verify cross-domain adaptability. (R1)
- **Dependence on initial 2D plane segmentation quality**: The method relies on pre-trained monocular normal prediction and SAM, which may produce inaccurate normals or over-segmentation in challenging environments. The paper does not explicitly address how to mitigate such errors or propagate corrections through the pipeline. (R3)
- **Over-segmentation from SAM**: SAM tends to over-segment large planar surfaces (e.g., walls, floors) into many fragments. The method lacks a clear strategy to merge these fragments into coherent global planes, potentially leading to fragmented reconstructions. (R3) *See also: visualization on GitHub shows small segments that often produce inaccuracy.*
- **Computational cost not thoroughly addressed**: Combining geometric, semantic, and coplanar features within the neural field introduces significant overhead in GPU memory and training time. The paper does not report these costs in detail nor compare them with related methods (e.g., PlaneRecon, which offers real-time online reconstruction). (R1, R4)
- **Unclear ground-truth definition in quantitative evaluation**: It is ambiguous whether the ground truth refers only to planar parts of the scene or the entire mesh. For methods that reconstruct the full mesh (e.g., first baselines in Table 1), comparison on planar metrics may be misleading without separate evaluation on planar regions. The evaluation section should clarify this. (R1)
- **Missing failure case discussion**: As a complex system, the paper does not present or analyze failure modes, which would help readers understand limitations. (R2)
- **Missing related works**: 
  - "Recovering 3D planes from a single image via convolutional neural networks" (R1)
  - "PlaneMVS: 3D Plane Reconstruction From Multi-View Stereo" (R1)
  - "Single-image piece-wise planar 3D reconstruction via associative embedding" (R1)
  - "PlanarNeRF: Online Learning of Planar Primitives with Neural Radiance Fields" (Chen et al., 2023) – a concurrent work highly relevant to the proposed method. (R4)
- **Unclear mechanism of Neural Parser**: The illustration and description of how the Neural Parser groups coplanar segments and learns feature centroids is insufficiently clear; more mathematical and implementation details are needed. (R4)
- **Missing definition in Equation 4**: *n_i* is not explicitly defined (should be the surface normal of the selected local planar primitive). (R3)
- **Reproducibility concern**: The complexity makes reproduction difficult; code availability is not stated. (R2)
- **Generalizability vs. output quality**: PlaneRecon trains over multiple scenes, supports test-time inference without optimization, and offers real-time speed. The paper does not discuss whether NeuralPlane could achieve such generalizability or efficiency, nor compare its computational footprint with such online methods. (R1)
- **Lack of analysis on hyperparameter robustness**: The sensitivity of push loss parameters (*to, tn, m*) and RANSAC parameters to varying scene scales and semantic distributions is not studied. (R1)
- **Minor reviewer disagreement**: While most reviewers commend the thoroughness, one reviewer (R2) considers the evaluation limited to only 12 scenes, whereas another (R1) calls the experiments extensive on two datasets. The method’s generalizability remains debated. (R1, R2)
