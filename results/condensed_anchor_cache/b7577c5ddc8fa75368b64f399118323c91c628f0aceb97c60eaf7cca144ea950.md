- Scores: 6, 8, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Double Sparse Factorization (DSF), which replaces each dense weight matrix with the product of two sparse matrices, solved via alternating minimization with ADMM. The method is applied to pruning pretrained LLMs (e.g., LLaMA2-13B) and CNNs, aiming to achieve higher compression with better performance than standard pruning or low-rank decomposition. Reviews agree the idea is neat and novel in the model compression context, with strong empirical results that outperform SOTA baselines (OBC, etc.) in many settings. However, concerns about practical efficiency (inference speed, memory overhead, hardware support), missing ablations, omitted baselines (SparseGPT, Wanda at some densities), reliance on perplexity alone for LLM evaluation, and insufficient detail on key algorithmic steps temper enthusiasm. Reviewer scores: 6, 8, 5.

### Strengths
- Novel application of factor-matrix pruning to pretrained model compression, especially for LLMs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Method distinguishes itself from prior art (Le Magoarou & Gribonval 2016) by focusing on compression at scale.
- Problem formulation is neat and ADMM optimization is elegant (Reviewer 1).
- Empirical results are convincing and outperform strong SOTA baselines on LLMs (LLaMA2-13B) and CNNs (ResNet) across multiple sparsity levels (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). On LLaMA2-13B, DSF enables 50% reduction while maintaining better performance than the dense LLaMA2-7B model.
- Paper is well written and easy to follow overall (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 notes some clarity issues but still finds the paper largely accessible.
- Authors consider practical concerns such as mask overhead and the fixed-mask variant with shared A factor; the fixed-mask variant is particularly compelling for its low (~1%) mask overhead (Reviewer 2).
- Results before and after retraining/fine-tuning are reported, which is appreciated and often missing in pruning papers (Reviewer 3).
- The topic is timely and important given growing model sizes (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Inference speed / latency concerns**: Using the product of two sparse matrices instead of one sparse matrix is unlikely to be faster on existing hardware, despite equal total multiplications. The additional matmul operations increase memory bandwidth and overhead. Reviewers request a discussion on runtime latency, possibly with preliminary benchmarks using Neural Magic’s DeepSparse Engine, and a breakdown of time spent per operation (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The paper’s FLOPs analysis may be misleading for real inference (Reviewer 2).
- **Memory overhead during fine-tuning**: DSF requires ~37% more memory to store intermediate activations (X@A@B vs X@W) for an intermediate layer in LLaMA2-7B at 50% sparsity. This could limit batch size and impact convergence. The paper should acknowledge and analyze this (Reviewer 2).
- **Mask storage overhead**: Using bit‑mask compression with no shared A masks incurs ~37% overhead compared to single‑layer sparsity. With shared masks this drops to ~1%. The trade‑offs need a more detailed analysis (Reviewer 2). The suggestion to use index‑based storage (uint16 indices) is poorly motivated because it is only practical at sparsities ≥15/16, which is currently unobtainable for LLMs and near the limit for CNNs (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of hardware support for structured sparsity**: DSF cannot support 2:4 sparsity because the best sparsity for factors is ~33%, not 50%. This limits applicability to hardware optimized for N:M formats. The authors should discuss potential extensions (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and missing ablations**: 
  - The paper fixes sparsity distribution between factors (e.g., 16%/25%) without ablation. How were these values chosen? A sensitivity analysis is needed (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
  - No ablations provided for initialization of A and B (Reviewer 3).
  - No ablation of the cubic schedule for sparsity mask finding (how many iterations, interval between increases, preconditioning purpose) (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing SparseGPT baseline in Table 1**: Reviewer 3 notes that SparseGPT is a strong baseline and should be included. Why is it omitted? Also, Wanda results at 30% density are missing. Is Wanda’s finalization step being used? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing downstream evaluation for LLMs**: The claim that “the larger pruned model is better than the dense smaller model” relies on perplexity alone, which is known to be misleading for compressed models. The authors should validate on OpenLLM Leaderboard v1 or similar real‑world tasks (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair SVD comparison**: Comparing DSF with SVD using sparsity ratio is misleading because SVD is designed for low‑rank compression, not sparsity. The authors should include a FLOPs‑based comparison (Reviewer 1).
- **Compute cost of ADMM**: No asymptotic time complexity or empirical runtime analysis is provided. The trade‑off between computational cost and compression quality should be discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Fine‑tuning details**: The fine‑tuning hyperparameters for CNNs (e.g., 70 total epochs, stepped schedule) appear arbitrary. Best practices (linear/cosine schedule, adaptive initial learning rate after pruning) should be used to give more realistic results (Reviewer 3). Also, fine‑tuning results for compressed LLMs are missing, and the memory overhead during naive fine‑tuning (with masked parameters instead of compressed representations) should be reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity issues**: 
  - The ADMM method description lacks details on initialization of Z and U; an appendix with more remarks would help (Reviewer 3).
  - The cubic schedule for sparsity mask finding and preconditioning are insufficiently explained (Reviewer 3).
  - The Wanda saliency map formula and “scaling one factor back” should be given mathematically (Reviewer 3).
  - Minor: typo “the layer-wise pruning problem” (line 131). Inconsistent abbreviations: DSF vs DFS (e.g., line 315, line 686 caption) (Reviewer 3).
- **Questions addressed as missing experiments / ablations**:
  - Which LLaMA model is used in Table 1 (LLaMA1 or LLaMA2)? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why are Wanda results at 70% sparsity missing from Table 1? (Reviewer 2)
  - Can DSF be extended to 2:4 sparsity? What is the trade‑off with generalization? (Reviewer 2)
  - Does DSF provide latency/throughput benefits over dense or typical sparse networks when using DeepSparse Engine? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the memory overhead when fine‑tuning DSF LLMs in a naive way (masked parameters)? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why does the method not support gradual pruning with fine‑tuning between pruning steps? (Reviewer 3, line 465)
  - How is the “projection problem” (line 196) not the entire problem? Clarification needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Claim strength concerns**: The assertion that DSF is “the first layer‑wise pruning method in which the larger pruned model is better than the dense smaller model” may be too strong—SparseGPT already gets fairly close, and non‑uniform layer‑wise sparsity improvements exist (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 2 partly echoes via need for downstream eval).
- **Overclaiming without ablation**: Reviewers note the paper fixes many hyperparameters (sparsity distribution, initialization) but provides no ablations to justify those choices (Reviewer 3).
