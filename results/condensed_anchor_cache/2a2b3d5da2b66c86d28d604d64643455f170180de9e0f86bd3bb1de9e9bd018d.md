## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the expressive power of ReLU networks under various convex relaxations used in certification, measuring ability to represent (subclasses of) CPWL functions precisely. For univariate functions: IBP can precisely express monotone CPWL; Δ and DeepPoly can also express convex CPWL; Multi-Neuron relaxations can express all univariate CPWL. For multivariate functions, even the most precise single-neuron relaxation (Δ) cannot precisely represent simple functions like the max function in ℝ². Results suggest that more precise relaxations increase expressivity for univariate functions but single-neuron relaxations are fundamentally limited for multivariate, motivating multi-neuron approaches and further study.

### Strengths
- First in-depth systematic study on expressive power of ReLU networks under a wide range of convex relaxations (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Covers both univariate and multivariate functions, and function classes like (monotone) convex CPWL (Reviewer 2).
- Clearly differentiates capabilities of different relaxations through precise mathematical results and constructive proofs (Reviewer 2).
- Relates theoretical results back to certified training, drawing interesting hypotheses about potential benefits of more advanced relaxations (Reviewer 2).
- Paper is clearly structured and provides detailed mathematical proofs (Reviewer 2).
- Novel direction: first to consider expressive power in the precise representation setting (Reviewer 3).
- Wide coverage: discusses a broad range of convex relaxation methods, covering most popular ones in practice (Reviewer 3).

Note: One reviewer (Reviewer 1, rating 8, confidence 2) did not state specific strengths, citing difficulty understanding the paper, which may reflect clarity issues rather than absence of strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Restricted univariate assumption (Reviewer 3):** All positive results for expressive power hold for univariate functions, which is restricted both practically (most functions are multivariate) and theoretically (univariate avoids high-dimensional difficulties and may not generalize).
- **Insufficient negative multivariate result (Reviewer 3):** The negative multivariate result only holds for Δ, and the precision gap can be arbitrarily small. It is not clear whether multivariate methods can express multivariate CPWL functions precisely.
- **Motivation for precise analysis (Reviewer 3):** Given that for any ε>0, IBP can express general multivariate continuous functions up to ε error (Baader et al. 2020), the significance of considering precise (ε=0) analysis beyond pure theoretical interest is not fully justified.
- **Narrow scope of activation functions (Reviewer 2):** Focus is exclusively on ReLU networks, not covering sigmoid, tanh, or other activations commonly used; different activations may behave differently under convex relaxations.
- **Limited architecture scope (Reviewer 2):** Only fully-connected feedforward networks are considered, not convolutional or residual architectures widely used in practice, which could affect expressivity and robustness.
- **Only deterministic networks (Reviewer 2):** Stochastic networks (e.g., Bayesian neural networks) are not touched, though their behavior under convex relaxations is not well understood and is relevant.
- **Only standardized perturbation sets (Reviewer 2):** The theoretical analysis is limited to ℓₚ-norm bounded perturbations; results may not generalize to other domains or perturbation types.
- **Lack of empirical validation (Reviewer 2):** While hypotheses for certified training are provided, no experiments are conducted to validate the conjectured benefits; the theoretical results may not translate into practical improvements.
- **Dense, mathematical writing (Reviewers 1, 2):** The paper could benefit from more intuitive explanations and examples to make results more accessible to a broader audience. Reviewer 1 (rating 8, confidence 2) reported difficulty understanding the high-level ideas, definitions, and motivation.
- **Clarity of definitions and motivation (Reviewer 1):** Specific questions indicate weakness in exposition: elaboration on how convex relaxations help formal robustness guarantees, what exactly is being relaxed in Section 2.1, whether vector inequalities are entrywise, simpler explanation of D-analysis and why it matters for expressivity, and why we need to analyze expressivity for these function classes given universal approximation results.
- **Missing comparison to convex optimization of ReLU networks (Reviewer 2):** The paper does not discuss recent work showing that ReLU networks can be trained using convex optimization (Pilanci & Ergen, ICML 2020) and how this relates to the need for convex relaxations. This question also ties to the motivation of the paper.
- **Limited discussion of other applications (Reviewer 2):** Aside from certified training, implications for other applications (e.g., compression, pruning, interpretability) are not discussed.
