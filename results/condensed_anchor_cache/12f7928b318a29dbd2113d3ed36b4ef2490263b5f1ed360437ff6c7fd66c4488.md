## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a proximal point method (PPM) based procedure to efficiently approximate many Pareto efficient robust solutions, reducing the computational requirement. For robust LPs with uncertain objective under simplex domain and ellipsoidal uncertainty, PPM trajectories are exactly Pareto efficient robust solutions. For robust LPs with a random polyhedron domain, high-probability bounds are provided. Numerical experiments on portfolio optimization and adversarially robust deep learning are presented.

### Strengths
- The paper raises the interesting and novel question of efficiently computing the Pareto frontier balancing robustness and efficiency in robust optimization. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The proposed use of the proximal point method (PPM) to generate many Pareto efficient robust solutions in one pass is a novel combination of well-known techniques. (R1, R2, R3)
- The paper provides an original theoretical result: for robust LPs with uncertain objective under simplex decision domain and ellipsoidal uncertainty, the PPM trajectories are exactly Pareto efficient robust solutions. For robust LPs with a random polyhedron domain, it proves with high probability that the performances of the Pareto efficient robust solutions are bounded by the performances of two PPM trajectories. (R4; also noted by R1 and R2)
- The paper is generally well written, well organized, and clearly presents core ideas and theorems. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Technical claims are generally well explained, and the authors are honest about strengths and weaknesses. (R2, R3)
- Empirical results on portfolio optimization and adversarially robust deep learning demonstrate effectiveness of the proposed procedure. (R1, R3)
- The visualizations (Figures 1 and 2) are informative. (R4)
- The results are likely to be significant from a computational efficiency perspective and may be used by practitioners. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- W1: The paper claims a computational cost reduction from N×T to 2×T (Line 054), but this claim is insufficiently justified and potentially misleading. For linear programs, each iteration of PPM requires solving a quadratic optimization problem of comparable complexity to the original robust problem; thus the total cost may not be reduced compared to the brute-force method of solving for multiple α values. (R1, R2, R4) A running-time comparison against baselines is missing. (R4)
- W2: The theoretical analysis is limited to a narrow setting: linear objective functions with ellipsoidal uncertainty sets and a simplex decision domain (Equation (3)). The objective function has a very particular form, and the analysis appears difficult to generalize to non-linear objectives or non-ellipsoidal uncertainty sets. The paper also does not discuss these limitations sufficiently. (R1, R3, R4)
- W3: The literature review is inadequate; comparisons to previous work on computing robust solutions or Pareto frontiers are lacking, making it unclear how the method differs from prior contributions and whether it advances the state of the art in a demonstrable way. (R2, R3)
- W4: Several key implementation details are missing or unclear:
    - Theorem 1 and Proposition 3: The existence of α(ω_k) such that the equality holds (Line 239, Line 299) is not explained. It is also unclear how α(ω_k) depends on x, and how to compute it in practice. (R1, R2)
    - The algorithm generates a sequence x_k that are Pareto efficient only for a series of special α(ω_k). It is unclear how these α(ω_k) vary with ω_k and whether the sequence covers enough α values to approximate the full Pareto frontier. (R1, R3)
    - The computation of x_R when ξ∈Ξ(∞) (Line 234) is not specified; what is the uncertainty set U for the linear problem with ellipsoidal uncertainty (Line 253)? (R1)
    - The "two passes" referenced in Line 247 are not explained. (R2)
- W5: The approximation quality of the proposed PPM trajectory is not rigorously analyzed. The paper does not provide theoretical guarantees on how well the approximate PPM solutions approach the true Pareto frontier, especially when using approximate PPM updates. (R4)
- W6: The numerical experiments, particularly for portfolio optimization, do not provide a compelling case for practical benefits.
    - The substantial discrepancy between the out-of-sample performance of robust Markowitz++ portfolios, despite their similar in-sample performance, requires further explanation; it is not clear why the distribution shift impacts the two out-of-sample performances differently. (R4)
    - The portfolio optimization section feels somewhat disconnected from the core theoretical contributions, and the results do not clearly demonstrate advantages over existing techniques. (R4)
    - Figures 1 and 2: it is not explained why the trajectories are not one-to-one; are these the gradient step trajectories? (R3)
- W7: The claim in Corollary 1 (Line 329) about a high-probability bound is not precise: m cannot be arbitrarily large, which makes the probability upper-bounded, so this is not exactly a high-probability bound. (R2)
- W8: Minor issues:
    - Some parts of the paper lack necessary citations (e.g., first and second paragraphs of introduction). (R2, R4)
    - Line 91: E is not empty but only includes the zero vector.
    - Line 173: the connection of the central path to the robust optimization problem should be explained earlier.
    - Line 231: define e from the simplex domain.
    - Typos: Line 157 bracket misplaced; Line 180 "by" should be "be"; Figure 1 typo "Porfolio". (R4)
- W9: The method's generation of robust solutions is limited when the radius of uncertainty sets is not freely selectable. (R3) One reviewer (R4, confidence 4) expressed particularly strong concerns about the computational efficiency claim and the lack of rigorous guarantees, and rated the contribution lower (1) than the other reviewers (2).
