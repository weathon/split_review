## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree the paper identifies an important problem (information split in diffusion-based representation learning with auxiliary latent variables) and proposes DBAE as a principled solution that turns the latent variable into an information bottleneck by making the diffusion endpoint \(x_T\) dependent on \(z\). The method is supported by theoretical justification and comprehensive experiments showing improvements in downstream inference, reconstruction, disentanglement, unconditional generation, and inference speed. Three reviewers find the motivation sound, the presentation well-organized, and the empirical evidence convincing. One reviewer (Reviewer 4) is significantly more critical, raising major concerns about presentation clarity and insufficient motivation for the problem, while still acknowledging the approach’s potential. That contrast is preserved throughout.

### Strengths
- The paper identifies and addresses an important problem in diffusion-based representation learning: the “information split problem,” where the auxiliary latent \(z\) and the diffusion endpoint \(x_T\) divide information from \(x_0\), making \(z\) less useful as a representation. All reviewers recognize the significance of this issue.
- The proposed solution (turning \(z\) into an information bottleneck by making \(x_T\) conditionally independent of \(x_0\) given \(z\)) is theoretically sound and principled. The framework integrates autoencoders with diffusion models using Doob’s \(h\)-transform, combining dimensionality reduction and efficient encoding/decoding with rich generative capabilities.
- Theorems support that the proposed loss increases mutual information between input data and the latent variable, and that the generated data distribution is guaranteed to be close to the input data distribution.
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate substantial improvement on multiple benchmarks, downstream tasks, reconstruction quality, disentanglement, unconditional generation, and attribute manipulation.
- Inference speed is notably superior.
- The paper addresses the challenge in a self-contained, didactic manner, introducing the problem gradually and motivating the solution clearly. (Reviewer 4 does not share this positive view of the presentation.)

### Weaknesses
**Methodological and Theoretical Concerns**
- Reviewer 3 raises a substantive concern: the diffusion bridge derivation (Eq. 5) assumes a deterministic target, but in the proposed model \(x_T\) is stochastic due to the randomness of the VAE’s latent variable. The paper lacks rigorous justification for applying the diffusion bridge framework with a stochastic target.
- Reviewer 2 notes that it is unclear how the proposed loss actually makes the endpoint dependent on the latent variable—the theorems only show the relationship between input data and the latent variable, not between the latent variable and the endpoint, which is not aligned with the claim.
- Reviewer 1 finds Eq. 9 suspicious: both \(p_\theta^\text{ODE}(x_0 | z, x_T)\) and \(p_\theta^\text{ODE}(x_T | z_0, z)\) are delta-distributions, so the log-density does not exist and the KL divergence should be infinite. The “cancellation of infinities” needs rewriting to avoid relying on such cancellation.
- Reviewer 2 notes that intuition is missing in Section 4.1, particularly why the forward SDE is defined as in Eq. 10. More intuition would help readers appreciate the forward process.

**Presentation and Clarity (Minority reviewer disagrees with others)**
- Reviewer 4 finds that presentation significantly diminishes the paper’s impact, a view not shared by the other reviewers. Specific concerns: the Introduction lacks a clear articulation of why the information split problem is critical (especially for reconstruction), and these models should be positioned as state-of-the-art to contextualize the importance of addressing their limitations.
- Terminology is imprecise in several places: “inference of \(x_T\)” (Line 49) is ambiguous and should be “estimating \(x_T\) conditioned on \(x_0\)”; “\(D_{KL}(q_\theta^\text{ODE}||p_\text{prior})\) explains the information split problem” (Lines 196–197) is unclear about whether the term illustrates or quantifies the problem; Section 4.1 title “latent variable inference” does not clarify whether it refers to \(z\), \(x_T\), or both; the nature of the encoder (deterministic or stochastic) is not clearly defined.
- Reviewer 1 strongly recommends running the entire paper through a grammar checker due to numerous grammatical errors (singular/plural, articles, missing verbs) that sometimes render sentences unintelligible (e.g., lines 132–133, 160–162, 287–288). This diminishes otherwise excellent presentation.
- Reviewer 4 requests a concise pseudocode or boxed algorithm in Section 4 summarizing DBAE’s key steps (inferring \(z\) from \(x_0\), inferring \(x_T\) from \(z\), bridging \(x_0\) and \(x_T\) using Doob’s \(h\)-transform) for both training and inference.

**Experimental and Task Clarity**
- Reviewer 3: it is unclear whether different objectives and models were used for different tasks or whether one model is trained end-to-end for both reconstruction and generation. The paper should clarify the training procedure and architecture used for each task.
- Reviewer 3: the paper lacks discussion of training difficulty compared to the model without the autoencoder part—specifically, whether there is a trade-off between the expressive power of the AE and the diffusion model, increased training complexity, sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, or potential for mode collapse in the latent space.
- Reviewer 4 requests that Section 5 provide precise mathematical descriptions for each of the six experimental tasks (as is done for the interpolation problem in Lines 474–476) to clearly communicate goals, challenges, and DBAE’s benefits.
- Reviewer 4 notes that essential experimental details (e.g., encoder and model architecture choices) are relegated to the Appendix and should appear in the main text.

**Significance Boundary**
- Reviewer 1 questions the significance of the information split problem in unconditional generation: if only generation quality matters, it is unclear why the split matters, yet DBAE+AE empirically outperforms baselines even there. This point is echoed by Reviewer 2, who asks why and when the information split is problematic given that generation can combine information from both \(z\) and \(x_T\).
- Reviewer 1 defers on novelty and baseline choice, stating they are not an expert on the relevant literature regarding other solutions to the information split issue or on state-of-the-art empirical performance.
