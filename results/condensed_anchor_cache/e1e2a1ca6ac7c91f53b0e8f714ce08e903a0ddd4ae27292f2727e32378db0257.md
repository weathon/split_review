## Merged Review

### Summary
LogQuant is a 2-bit KV cache quantization method for LLM inference. It is based on the observation that the positions of high-attention spikes follow a log distribution – becoming sparser farther from the current token. LogQuant keeps a set of tokens at log-distributed positions in full precision and quantizes all others to INT2. The paper also proposes a throughput optimization by reordering the Softmax(Q·K^T) and V computations. Reported gains include 25% throughput improvement, 60% batch size increase, and 40–200% accuracy improvements on math/code tasks at the same compression ratio. The method is designed to integrate with frameworks like HuggingFace transformers.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an important problem (LLM inference memory efficiency) and the target area of KV cache quantization is highly relevant for deployment.
- Several reviewers found the observation that high-attention spikes follow a log-like distribution to be interesting and valuable for guiding future approaches.
- The log-distributed token selection is seen as innovative, addressing a core limitation of prior methods that assume later tokens are always most important.
- The reordering observation for throughput optimization is also noted as interesting and potentially useful.
- The empirical results are considered to set a new state-of-the-art for 2-bit KV cache quantization (reviewer 3, who gave rating 5). Another reviewer (rating 6) called the framework simple but effective.
- The paper is generally well-written with good figures and tables; presentation is praised by some.
- The method’s compatibility with popular inference frameworks is a practical strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** Several reviewers noted that LogQuant is highly similar to KIVI – both use integer quantization with a fixed-size full-precision cache, differing only in token selection. It is also similar to mixed-precision KV cache approaches that preserve outlier tokens at higher precision. One reviewer called the approach “heuristic” (reviewer 4).
- **Token selection is fixed and non-adaptive:** The selection of which tokens to keep in full precision depends only on token position and log distribution, not on attention scores or learned importance. As authors illustrate in Figure 2, attention outliers do not follow a fixed pattern, raising questions about universality across tasks and layers. The method does not account for varying importance across layers – some layers might benefit from recent tokens, others from different positions.
- **Limited evaluation compared to baselines:**
  - Only KIVI is compared for task accuracy. Missing baselines: KVQuant, SKVQ, eviction-based methods, and other compression strategies at similar rates.
  - No 4-bit quantization comparison, which would offer better quality and is standard in the field.
  - Missing latency comparison – the most important efficiency metric.
  - The efficiency comparison (Figure 7) does not include KIVI for memory and throughput and uses only a naive BF16 baseline, not real-world inference traces.
  - Prompt and generation lengths appear very small; no evaluation with large context windows (e.g., 128K).
  - No eviction-based accuracy baseline.
- **Accuracy degradation is significant:** For many tasks, LogQuant shows large accuracy drops (10+ points) compared to full precision. Multiple reviewers questioned where such a trade-off is acceptable. The drops are noted to be larger than those reported for other KV cache compression methods at similar rates.
- **Technical details are unclear or incomplete:**
  - The choice of INT2 is not justified; no analysis or comparison with INT3, INT4, or ternary quantization.
  - It is unclear which specific quantization/compression technique is used (e.g., group size, per-channel, etc.). Group size is not specified, nor whether its overhead is accounted for in comparisons.
  - The paper uses “quantization” and “compression” interchangeably, which have distinct meanings.
  - Figure 5 and its caption are considered unclear (reviewer 4).
  - The presentation of LogQuant (Section 3) needs revision to better clarify the solution.
  - There is no discussion or profiling of the overhead from operations like slicing and concatenating.
  - No evaluation of the quantization error of the tokens that are quantized.
- **Missing discussion and analyses:**
  - The paper does not compare accuracy against a smaller baseline model (e.g., using same memory budget).
  - No guidance is given on when to use LogQuant versus the proposed PartialLogQuant variant.
  - The paper does not test on models LLaMA3-8B, Mistral, or LongChat; one reviewer requested results following the longctx_bench settings.
- **Minority positive contrast:** While reviewers 3 and 5 were more positive overall (ratings 5 and 6), they still raised significant weaknesses – notably the arbitrary design choices, lack of broader evaluation, and significant accuracy drops. Reviewer 3, despite calling it new SOTA, found the evaluation insufficient (small context, no quantization error analysis, no justification for INT2). Reviewer 5 noted the lack of latency comparison and real-world traces.
