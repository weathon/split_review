## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the paper proposes a dynamic hierarchical message passing network (DHMP) for mesh-based physics simulation, with anisotropic message passing and differentiable node selection via Gumbel-Softmax. The method is well motivated, presented with clear visualizations, and evaluated on standard benchmarks including out-of-distribution tests. However, significant concerns were raised about novelty (similarity to existing graph attention and prior Gumbel-Softmax node sampling work), large discrepancies between reproduced baselines and original published results (suggesting possible unfair comparison), missing ablations and hyperparameter analysis (hierarchy count, temperature), inadequate complexity analysis, and missing training details. While one reviewer (R3, score 6) was more positive about the dynamic adaptation and generalization, the other three reviewers (scores 5,5,5) found these issues substantial.

### Strengths
- New implementation of attention-based and hierarchical message passing in GNNs (R1)
- Learnable hierarchy (R1)
- Generalization and ablation studies (R1)
- Method well motivated, presented clearly with nice visualizations and comprehensive analysis (R2)
- Extensive evaluation with out-of-distribution evaluations (R2)
- Dynamic adaptation of graph structure; effectively captures long-range dependencies; handles unseen mesh structures (R3)
- Novel multigrid method based on Gumbel-Softmax sampling with very few modifications to the graph net block; use of K-hop edges (R4)
- New anisotropic message passing (AMP) layer replacing sum aggregation with a weighted sum (R4)
- Good results improvements on meaningful and standard benchmarks (R4)
- Clever usage of previously defined importance to improve fine mesh interpolation (R4)

### Weaknesses
- Anisotropic message passing differs only in small implementation details from graph attention; cannot be considered really novel (R1)
- Similar idea of differentiable node selection using Gumbel-Softmax already proposed in “Multiresolution equivariant graph variational autoencoder” by Hy and Kondor (R3)
- Missing comparison with Multi-Grid Graph Neural Networks with Self-Attention for Computational Mechanics (R4)
- Incorrect statement that GAT networks do not use edge features during attention computation (R4, l202-3-4)
- In Table 2, results from cited works (especially MGN) are far from those reported in the original papers. For example, MGN paper reports 0.314 RMSE-1 on Airfoil, but the submitted paper reports 0.7738 for MGN (R1, R2, R4). This raises suspicion that MGN (and possibly other baselines) were undertrained, making the comparison potentially unfair (R4)
- Missing important training details such as number of training steps and precise learning rate schedule (R4)
- Figure 3 suggests the model assigns many nodes to uninformative (laminar) regions, contradicting the claim that nodes are allocated to challenging regions (R2)
- Number of hierarchies is an important hyperparameter; no ablation or discussion of how it is selected (e.g., based on problem size) (R2)
- Stability of DiffSELECT (Gumbel-Softmax) is sensitive to temperature τ; no discussion of how τ is selected or whether it is the same across scenarios (R3)
- Dynamic adaptation and anisotropic message passing introduce additional computational overhead; authors should provide time and space complexity analysis and compare with baselines (R3, R4)
- Missing analysis of training/inference time and memory usage for different K-hop values (R4)
- Generalization to other domains or non-mesh-based applications may require modification; authors should consider testing on turbulence datasets (e.g., PDEBench) to demonstrate stability (R3)
- Paper not well structured; main contributions are not written separately (R1)
- Architecture details missing for other models besides MGN (R4)
- Typos and errors: Table 7 noise typo, l807 typo, issues with sentences at l108, l115, l152 (R4)
- Figure 1 gradient arrows are of unclear usefulness (R4)
