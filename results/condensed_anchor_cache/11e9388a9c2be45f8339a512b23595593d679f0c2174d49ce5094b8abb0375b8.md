## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes AttnGCG, an enhancement to the GCG jailbreak attack that manipulates attention scores to direct model focus away from safety prompts toward adversarial suffixes. The authors observe a correlation between attention to system prompts and attack effectiveness, and modify GCG’s loss function to incorporate attention terms. They evaluate on open-source and closed-source models (Llama-2, Gemma, GPT-3.5, GPT-4) and report average ASR improvements of ~7% on Llama-2 and ~10% on Gemma.

### Strengths

- Addresses a critical and highly relevant topic in LLM safety and robustness (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Novel approach that uses the attention mechanism to guide adversarial optimization, offering a new perspective on bypassing safety measures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Evaluations include both white-box (direct attack) and black-box (transfer) settings, demonstrating cross-model transferability and broader applicability (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Attention heatmap visualizations improve interpretability of the attack process and provide insights into why certain jailbreak attempts succeed (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Paper is well-structured and clearly written, with helpful illustrations and tables (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Extensive experiments provide good insight into the components of the proposed method (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited comparison with modern baselines**: The paper compares only against GCG, ignoring more recent methods such as AutoDAN [1], COLD-Attack [2], AdvPrompter [3], and AmpleGCG [4] (Reviewers 1, 2). This does not position AttnGCG within the current state of the art and weakens claims of effectiveness.
2. **Marginal empirical gains**: Attack success rate improvements are small—under 10% in most cases, e.g., from 74.1% to 78.0% on one task (Reviewers 2, 4). Some reviewers consider these gains insufficient to justify the added computational complexity (Reviewer 4). In adversarial attack research, improvements generally need to exceed a 10% increase to be practically significant (Reviewer 4).
3. **Limited novelty and prior art**: The core idea of incorporating attention scores into the GCG loss is incremental; similar attention-based attack strategies already exist (e.g., papers cited by Reviewer 4: [arxiv/2001.06325], [EMNLP 2023 Findings]) and the paper does not sufficiently differentiate from them or provide theoretical insights into attention vulnerabilities (Reviewer 4). The paper also lacks thorough analysis of existing attention-based attack literature (Reviewer 1).
4. **Unrealistic experimental settings (temperature=0)**: Experiments use temperature=0, leading to deterministic outputs. Real-world applications often use higher temperatures (e.g., 0.7–1.0) for diverse responses, raising concerns about generalizability (Reviewer 4). The paper does not include experiments with non-zero temperature.
5. **Lack of evaluation under safety system prompts**: The attacks are tested without active safety prompts. Real-world applicability requires testing against common safety mechanisms (e.g., self-reminders [5], goal prioritization [6], in-context adversarial game [7]) (Reviewer 2).
6. **Concerns about dataset and evaluation**: Only 100 harmful questions from AdvBench are used instead of the full 520, potentially limiting representativeness (Reviewer 1). The use of GPT-4 for output evaluation is common and not novel; Table 1 shows that both GCG and AttnGCG produce significant false positives in jailbreak classification (Reviewer 1).
7. **Susceptibility to simple defenses**: Optimization-based attacks like GCG (and by extension AttnGCG) are vulnerable to perplexity-based defenses; the paper does not discuss or test against such defenses (Reviewers 1, 3, citing [1] from Reviewer 3).
8. **Poor performance on latest models**: AttnGCG fails on recent strong models like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro-latest, with limited transferability. The paper provides no in-depth analysis or strategies to address this (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 asks for more research and data on potential improvements.
9. **Resource requirements and white-box dependence**: The method relies on white-box access and extensive optimization; this resource demand could itself act as a defense (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 suggests that directly manipulating the attention matrix might be a more attractive approach.
10. **Presentation issues**: Figures referenced in the introduction appear on later pages, disrupting reading flow (Reviewer 1).

**Reviewer disagreement note**: Opinions are divided. Reviewers 2 and 4 (scores 5) found the approach a logical extension with clear presentation and relevant topic, and rated contributions/presentation slightly higher. Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 3) were more critical, emphasizing the limited novelty, marginal improvements, lack of comparative baselines, and missing defense analysis. Despite these differences, all reviewers noted the need for stronger baselines and broader evaluation.
