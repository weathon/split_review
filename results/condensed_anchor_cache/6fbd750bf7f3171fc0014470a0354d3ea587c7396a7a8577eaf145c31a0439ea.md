## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper develops a computational model of reciprocally coupled continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs) to study how place cells in hippocampus (HPC) and grid cells in medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) integrate environmental and self-motion cues. The model uses a single CANN with a location coordinate for place cells and multiple CANNs with phase coordinates for grid cell modules of different spacings. The coupling conveys correlation priors and the dynamics implement gradient-based optimization of the posterior, achieving Bayesian optimal cue integration and reducing non-local errors in grid cell phase coding. The work is primarily theoretical, with 20 equations in the main text and 79 in the supplement, and is validated through simulations.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses an important neuroscience topic: the complementary roles of place and grid cells in spatial representation.
- The theoretical analysis is rigorous and detailed, with clear mathematical foundations for the proposed integration mechanism (gradient-based optimization of the posterior, GOP). No errors were found in the equations (reviewer 3).
- The basic idea—that the place cell attractor network selects the most likely position via winner-take-all dynamics—is intuitive and well-formalized.
- Simulation results are well-designed to validate the model's effectiveness, showing Bayesian optimal integration and reduction of non-local errors under multiple noise levels.
- The paper is generally well-organized, with key concepts (LSC vs. PSC) clearly explained and effective use of figures.
- The model has significance for understanding how the brain forms stable spatial maps and could inform neural-inspired AI systems.
- The raw code is uploaded, supporting reproducibility.

*Disagreement on novelty:* One reviewer found the approach novel and creative as a combination of probabilistic inference with neural dynamics. Another reviewer considered the main point (recurrent network denoising via MAP inference) not particularly novel, given prior Bayesian interpretations of Hopfield networks and continuous attractor networks (e.g., Hopfield networks, Anderson & Abrahams 1993; Wu et al. 2023). This disagreement is noted.

### Weaknesses

1. **Assumptions and biological plausibility:**  
   - The model assumes independent neural response noise, well-known to be violated in biology (Abbott & Dayan, 1999). This limitation is not discussed.  
   - Parametric assumptions (Eq. 1: unimodal Gaussian tuning curves for place cells; Eq. 5: Gaussian noise model) are biologically unrealistic. Place cells can be multimodal in large environments (Fenton et al. 2008, Rich et al. 2014). The Gaussian noise model neglects non-negativity and variance-mean dependence of spiking, which could affect behavior at low firing rates.  
   - The different grid cell modules lack reciprocal connections, which may limit the model’s ability to capture error correction mechanisms.  
   - The model gives grid cells direct position-based input (Eq. 13) rather than path-integrated input from velocity/head direction, as experimental evidence suggests. This neglects error accumulation from path integration and the correction mechanisms often attributed to border cells (Hardcastle et al.) or place cell inputs (Bonnevie et al.).  
   - The model does not fully discuss anatomical evidence for reciprocal HPC-MEC interactions (e.g., CA1→MEC layer V, subiculum→MEC II/III), limiting biological grounding.  
   - The use of prime spacing ratios for grid modules is a theoretical tool without direct experimental evidence; observed ratios are approximately constant (1.4–1.7) between modules (Stensola et al.).

   *Minority view:* One reviewer questioned whether these parametric assumptions are critical to the main story and suggested demonstrating their non-criticality or relegating details to the appendix.

2. **Theoretical and validation gaps:**  
   - The claim that "optimal decoding is achieved by MAP" (line 220) is incomplete/incorrect: for squared-error loss, the posterior mean (not mode) is optimal. The paper does not formalize a loss function for the decoder.  
   - The critical theoretical prediction (Eq. 19) that network dynamics perform gradient ascent on the posterior is not numerically validated.  
   - The model’s claim of reducing non-local errors is only partially supported. Specific tests that induce non-local errors (controlled phase shifts, systematic noise increase) and statistical validation against baselines are missing.  
   - The approximation $\prod_i \lambda_i \approx \bar{\lambda}^M$ (primes) is not justified; using $\overline{\ln \lambda}$ would give the same conclusion.  
   - The paper relies on several parameters (coupling strengths, noise levels, time constants) but provides no systematic sensitivity analysis to assess generalizability or stability.

3. **Missing comparisons and literature integration:**  
   - No comparison with alternative models such as classic attractor networks (ring/torus), the constrained range model (Sreenivasan & Fiete 2011), or models addressing catastrophic errors (Lenninger et al.) and landmark integration in MEC (Ocko et al., Campbell et al. 2018, 2021). Such comparisons would clarify whether the model truly overcomes non-local errors or trades limitations.  
   - Recent experimental results showing Bayesian cue integration in grid cells (Campbell et al. 2021) are not discussed; aligning model predictions with these data would strengthen the paper.  
   - Missing citations for co-prime factors (Fiete et al. 2008), Fisher information / Cramér-Rao bound, and Bayesian interpretations of attractor networks. Citation formatting errors noted (MacKay cited inconsistently).

4. **Experimental validation:**  
   - There is no validation of the model against experimental data. Many open-source datasets on place and grid cells are available (Buzsáki Lab, Moser Lab, CRCNS, Dombeck Lab 2024 Nature Neuroscience). The paper remains purely theoretical.

5. **Presentation and fit to ICLR:**  
   - The main text includes excessive theoretical detail; sections 3 and 4 could be streamlined (e.g., Eqs. 12–13 in appendix) to allow expansion of section 5.  
   - Two of three reviewers questioned the fit of this purely computational neuroscience work to ICLR, noting the lack of machine learning or deep learning components and suggesting alternative venues (Neural Computation, PLOS Computational Biology, Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience, NeurIPS). One reviewer did not raise this concern.

*Minority view on color and typo:* One reviewer requested changing colors in Figs. 3c/3d for accessibility (yellow not identifiable; suggest Matplotlib default colors) and noted an apparent stray character “c” under “$\pi$” in Fig. 1d.
