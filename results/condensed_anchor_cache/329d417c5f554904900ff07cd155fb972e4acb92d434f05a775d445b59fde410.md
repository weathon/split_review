## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the important but often ignored issue that effective representations differ across tasks, even for the same multi-view entity. It proposes Task-Oriented Multi-View Representation Learning (TOMRL), which uses a meta-learning paradigm to generate task-specific biases that modulate view-specific encoding and fusion modules. The method defines unsupervised multi-view tasks in an episode fashion and formalizes meta-learning and multi-view learning as a nested bi-level optimization. Experiments on four datasets show performance improvements on classification and clustering for both few-shot and routine tasks. However, the reviewers have major concerns about the novelty, clarity of motivation, experimental rigor, and theoretical grounding. One reviewer (R3, score 6) is more positive, highlighting consistent gains across tasks, while the other four reviewers (scores 3,3,3,5) are substantially more critical, citing limited novelty, insufficient evaluation, and unclear explanations.

### Strengths
1. Highlights the critical observation that effective multi-view representations are task-dependent (R1, R5).
2. Integrates meta-learning with multi-view learning through a bi-level optimization framework (R1, R2).
3. Defines unsupervised multi-view tasks in an episodic manner and designs a meta-learner to modulate view-specific and view-shared features (R2).
4. Reports performance improvements on classification and clustering tasks across four datasets, including few-shot scenarios (R2, R3).
5. The motivation to focus on both unified representations and task-specific view information is reasonable (R5).
6. The direction of learning task-oriented multi-view representations is recognized as important (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Incremental novelty.** The core idea of leveraging task relationships to modulate feature extraction and fusion does not fundamentally depart from existing multi-task learning paradigms; the contribution is incremental rather than transformative (R1). R3 adds that the claim “how representations from multiple views can better serve the task” is a natural requirement, not a novel insight.
2. **Unclear motivation and explanation.**
   - The motivation is not convincingly established; many existing methods could already learn task-oriented or multi-view representations, and the paper only provides examples that are inapplicable to out-of-sample data or that learn only uniform representations (R3).
   - It is not clearly explained how the meta-learning paradigm generates task-specific biases, nor why that improves performance (R4, R5).
   - The writing and organization are difficult to follow, making it hard to understand the motivation and why the proposed model is good (R3).
3. **Strong, unsupported assumption.** The claim that a typical multi-view representation learning framework consists of exactly four components (View-specific encoding, SVL, MVL, Fusion) is a strong assumption without comprehensive study or a unifying formulation (R3, R4).
4. **Fusion inconsistency.** The paper does not adequately explain how features of similar instances are aligned across tasks when the fusion process is modulated by task embeddings, leaving it unclear how potentially disparate fused representations are handled for consistent learning (R1).
5. **Missing discussion of lifelong/multi-task multi-view learning.** The paper does not discuss connections to lifelong multi-view learning or multi-view multi-task learning, nor how new tasks are handled without catastrophic forgetting (R1).
6. **Lack of task-specific loss weighting.** The loss function treats all tasks equally; there is no mechanism to account for different data distributions, importance, or dataset sizes across tasks, which can lead to suboptimal performance (R1).
7. **Presentation and notation issues.** The manuscript needs careful polishing: notation errors (e.g., `R^(d_H)` not `R_(d_H)`), inconsistent font thickness in Table 2, unclear notations in Equation (1) (R1). The pseudocode contains a likely error: line 20 should be placed after line 21 (R5). Bold formatting in experimental tables is inconsistent: sometimes bold indicates the best result, sometimes it denotes TOMRL; this should be unified (R2).
8. **Unclear utility of two modulation processes.** The paper does not discuss why modulating both the view-specific encoding and the fusion modules is beneficial, nor does it analyze the effect on different dataset domains (e.g., NoisyFashion → Caltech101-7). A gradient analysis would be helpful (R2).
9. **Cross-task performance drop.** In Table 3, TOMRL causes a decrease in NMI when transferring from NoisyFashion to EdgeFashion; the paper does not explain this decline (R3).
10. **Framing as representation learning vs. task-specific supervision.** The paper artificially defines downstream tasks during representation learning, whereas representation learning typically aims for a general representation that is later fine-tuned. It is questionable whether the learned representations will generalize to new tasks not considered during training, and whether the comparison with methods that do not use task information is fair (R4, R5).
11. **Insufficient experimental evaluation.**
    - The datasets used are not diverse enough; commonly used benchmarks in multi-view learning (e.g., NoisyMNIST, EdgeMNIST, Caltech20, PatchedMNIST) are missing (R4, R5). R1 also suggests validation on a dataset with a significantly larger domain difference.
    - The method is only integrated into three base approaches, all from the same source paper; validation on a wider range of multi-view methods is needed to enhance credibility (R4, R5).
12. **Few-shot overfitting not addressed.** Despite using few-shot scenarios, the paper lacks an in-depth discussion of overfitting and does not introduce method-level strategies to mitigate it (R1).
13. **Multi-view appears optional.** There is no information exchange between the multi-view sub-networks, making it unclear whether the multi-view aspect is essential or merely superficial (R1).
14. **No theoretical results.** The paper provides no theoretical analysis or guarantees (R4).
