## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the novel problem of self-supervised dataset distillation for transfer learning. It proves that naive bilevel optimization with an SSL objective yields biased gradients due to randomness from data augmentations or masking. To address this, the authors propose an MSE-based inner objective that eliminates randomness and aligns representations with those of a pre-trained target model, enabling a closed-form kernel ridge regression solution. Experiments on CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet with various architectures demonstrate effectiveness for transfer learning.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem setting**: Self-supervised dataset distillation for transfer learning is under-explored and this is the first work in this direction (R1, R2, R4, R5). The setting may produce task-agnostic condensed data and boost transferability (R1).
- **Theoretical contribution**: Proves that the gradient of SSL objectives with data augmentation or masking is a biased estimator of the true gradient, providing detailed proof (R1, R4, R5). This motivates the use of an MSE-based inner objective that removes randomness (R1, R4).
- **Proposed method**: Minimizes MSE between model representations of synthetic examples and learnable target feature representations for the inner objective, and uses MSE between inner model and target model representations on the full dataset for outer optimization (R1). This eliminates bias and stabilizes training (R4).
- **Computational efficiency**: Assumes a fixed feature extractor and optimizes only a linear head, leading to a closed-form solution via kernel ridge regression, substantially reducing overhead (R4).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluated on three datasets (CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, ImageNet) with a broad range of source/target datasets and network architectures; demonstrates advantages over baseline methods (R3, R5). Outperforms supervised distillation methods in the SSL setting (R1).
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written, coherent, and easy to follow (R2, R3, R4).
- **Useful application**: Validated on transfer learning, architecture generalization, and data-free knowledge distillation (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for MSE alignment**: The rationale for minimizing MSE between representations of the inner model and the pre-trained target model on the original data is unclear. The paper needs a more rigorous justification linking this to transferability or generalization theories (R1).
- **Why SSL outperforms supervised**: Only empirical results are provided; a deeper analysis (theoretical or systematic) is needed to explain why self-supervised distillation works better than supervised in this setting (R1).
- **Missing state-of-the-art baselines**: The paper does not compare with recent SOTA dataset distillation methods such as IDC and IDM (R2). This limits the strength of the experimental validation.
- **Missing test accuracy on distilled dataset alone**: The paper only reports transfer learning accuracy, but does not show test accuracy of a model trained solely on the distilled dataset. Without this, it is unclear whether the distilled images retain enough information relative to other baselines, or if the method only performs well in transfer learning (R2).
- **Missing oracle comparison**: The method is not compared against a model pre-trained on the full dataset (oracle). This is needed to indicate how far current distillation methods are from practical use (R5).
- **Small-scale experimental setup**: Experiments are limited to low-resolution images (e.g., 32×32, 64×64) and small models (MobileNet, ResNet10). No experiment on 224×224 ImageNet or larger downstream models (e.g., ResNet18) is provided, raising concerns about scalability and generalization. One such experiment is necessary (R3). A related concern: MobileNet and ResNet10 from scratch get <4% accuracy on Cars, indicating weak baselines (R3).
- **Data augmentation not used when training on distilled data**: The paper does not use data augmentation when training models on the distilled dataset for evaluation. However, augmentation is central to many SSL methods (e.g., Barlow Twins). This omission may limit the method’s applicability and effectiveness for larger models and more complex datasets (R3).
- **Clarity of “target representation”**: The concept of target feature representation is not sufficiently explained; more elucidation is needed (R4).
- **Theoretical analysis of instability in supervised setting**: The paper analyzes instability in bilevel optimization with SSL objectives, but it is not discussed whether this instability also applies to supervised dataset condensation formulations. This would be a useful extension of the analysis (R4).
- **Rationale for kernel ridge regression**: The choice of kernel ridge regression (KRR) for the inner optimization is not well-justified. Could alternative matching strategies (e.g., distribution matching, gradient matching) be integrated? (R4).
- **Explanation of SSL augmentation instability**: The paper states that back-propagating through data augmentations introduces instability, but does not fully explain why SSL augmentations cause this issue (R4).
- **Analysis of learnable targets Yₛ**: In Equation (2), both Xₛ and Yₛ are trainable, but how Yₛ is initialized and its impact on performance is not analyzed. Does initialization matter? (R5).
- **Visual similarity of distilled images**: The distilled images appear nearly identical to original ones. The paper should report other metrics beyond visualization to quantify differences between synthetic and real examples (R5).
- **Unclear adaptation of baselines**: It is not clear how dataset distillation baselines (e.g., DSA, DM) were adapted to the self-supervised setting, which is non-trivial (R5).
- **Minor: Abstract readability**: The abstract may be difficult to follow for readers unfamiliar with prior dataset distillation work (R3).
