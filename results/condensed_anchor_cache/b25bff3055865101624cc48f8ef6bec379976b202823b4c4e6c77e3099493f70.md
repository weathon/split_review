## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes L3RS, a learned optimizer specialized for finetuning pretrained models. L3RS dynamically combines update directions from base optimizers (e.g., SGD, Adam) via layer-wise coefficients predicted by a small MLP and per-layer embeddings, trained with natural evolution strategies. On ImageNet and PLACES finetuning of ResNet-34, L3RS outperforms both hand-designed (Adam, SGD) and general learned optimizers (VeLO) in early training steps, with 50% speedup over VeLO and 100% over the best hand-designed optimizer, while using 2–3 orders fewer parameters. Reviewers unanimously find the approach novel and the experiments on the chosen tasks comprehensive, but raise concerns about limited architectural/dataset diversity (only ResNet-34 on image classification), the narrow finetuning horizon (1000 steps), the computational overhead of meta-learning, and missing comparisons to other optimizers (e.g., Shampoo, Adabelief, ATMO). One reviewer (score 3) is significantly more critical, arguing that L3RS only surpasses Adam in the very early steps and that total convergence time (including meta-learning) may be slower than simply using Adam.

### Strengths
- Novel and useful approach: L3RS eliminates manual learning-rate schedule tuning and is a unique adaptation of learned optimizers to the finetuning scenario, distinct from general meta-learned optimizers like VeLO.
- Clear presentation and well‑designed experiments (Reviewers 1,3): ablation studies verify each design choice; comprehensive experiments on task distribution (ImageNet, PLACES).
- Significant early‑training acceleration: L3RS shows 50% speedup over VeLO and 100% over the best hand‑designed optimizer (Reviewer 4), with enhanced performance in the initial finetuning steps (all reviewers).
- Parameter efficiency: uses a small shared MLP and layer embeddings, resulting in 2–3 orders fewer parameters than VeLO (Reviewer 4).
- Method demonstrated on multiple datasets (ImageNet, PLACES) with robust generalization to unseen data distributions (Reviewer 1, though limited).
- Preliminary studies (ablation) confirm usefulness of each component (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited architecture and task scope.** Experiments only use ResNet-34 on image classification (finetuning). Missing comparisons on other architectures (transformers) and tasks (language understanding/generation) are a major gap (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). One reviewer (3) notes that the absence of attention‑based models makes the evaluation unsuitable for many current problems.
- **Short finetuning horizon.** Only 1000 finetuning steps, which may not represent practical finetuning scenarios (Reviewer 1). **Critical minority view (Reviewer 2):** Figure 3 shows L3RS only outperforms Adam in the early small‑step regime; for larger steps Adam achieves comparable performance. Hence the extra meta‑learning cost may not be justified, and total convergence time (including meta‑training) could be slower than simply using Adam.
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** The main learned‑optimizer baseline is VeLO; no comparison to other notable optimizers such as Shampoo (Reviewer 4), Adabelief (Reviewer 2), or ATMO (Reviewer 3). Ablation at L476 is unclear if it represents ATMO (Reviewer 3). Baseline schedulers (cosine, constant) lack reasoning for their selection (Reviewer 3).
- **Generalization and practicality concerns.** L3RS must be trained independently per architecture, which limits broad adoption. Authors show generalization across datasets (ImageNet ↔ PLACES) but more benchmarks are needed (Reviewer 1). Practical use demands either a generalized training recipe or pretrained checkpoints for popular architectures (Reviewer 1). The extra time and memory of meta‑learning make the approach unattractive for practitioners unless substantial benefits are demonstrated (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Lack of deeper analysis.** No analysis of how the learned coefficients favor SGD vs. Adam per layer or per training stage (Reviewer 1). No theoretical or experimental convergence analysis—does the finetuned model converge, or only reach a certain accuracy? (Reviewer 4). Problem setup: the intro raises the short‑horizon issue but finetuning a pretrained model may make that issue moot, which is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Abstract is vague (“transforms the updates,” “various statistics,” “hand‑designed”); overall paper seems rushed with inconsistent quality (Reviewer 3). Specific issues: L154 (“a lot of parameters” should be quantified), L153 (“2-hidden layer, 4-hidden MLP” unclear), L155 (“lean” vague), L189 casual style (“is probably the closest”), single‑sentence paragraphs in preliminaries (Reviewer 3). Figure 3 caption is hard to read; tables should use booktabs format if allowed (Reviewer 3).
