## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Soft-TransFormers (Soft-TF), a continual learning method that combines prompt techniques with learnable soft masks to select task-adaptive subnetworks. The method is inspired by the Well-initialized Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and aims to minimize catastrophic forgetting. Experiments on ViT and CLIP across CIL and TIL scenarios show competitive or state-of-the-art performance against baselines like DualPrompt, L2P, and L2P-PGP. The paper also provides convergence theory.

### Strengths
- The presentation is clear, well-organized, and logically flows, effectively communicating complex ideas.
- Extensive experiments across various datasets and tasks provide comprehensive evidence of the method’s effectiveness, achieving competitive or state-of-the-art results.
- The idea of dividing the prompt pool into groups so that different groups acquire different knowledge is reasonable, and combining soft masks or binary masks on pre-trained weights further boosts training.

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear Motivation and Novelty**  
   - The claimed motivation (addressing reliance on well-pretraining) is contradicted because the method also requires a well-initialized network (lines 96-99).  
   - The core ideas (soft-subnetworks, learnable masks, prompt pool construction) have been widely studied in model compression, model purification, and PEFT; the method appears to be a combination of prompt tuning with LoRA/Adapter-like components. The novelty is unclear.

2. **Missing Parameter Efficiency and Comparison**  
   - The superior performance over baselines is suspected to be due to more learnable parameters. The paper does not report the number of tunable parameters for the proposed method versus baselines.  
   - The experimental analysis is incomplete:  
     * Why does the method achieve higher performance than the “Upper bound” when the method modifies the model architecture (so the upper bound is not directly comparable)?  
     * Why is the performance improvement larger over DualPrompt than over L2P?  
     * In Table 1, why is 20-Split-CIFAR100 accuracy higher than 10-Split-CIFAR100? The reviewers suspect this is due to more parameters used.

3. **Insufficient Technical Explanation of Soft Masks**  
   - **Initialization, selection, optimization of mask `m`**: The paper does not specify initialization strategies, criteria for mask selection per task, or the optimization process beyond general backpropagation.  
   - **Storage cost**: Each task requires an independent soft mask, so storage grows linearly (not exponentially) with tasks, which is prohibitive for many tasks. Mitigation strategies (e.g., compression or sharing) are not discussed.  
   - **Parameter `α_t`**: It is unclear whether `α_t` is a fixed hyperparameter or learnable, how the sum across tasks is enforced to equal 1, and what role it plays in mask composition.  
   - **Redundant calculation in Algorithm 2**: `t_x` is computed twice; the justification is missing.  
   - **Inference cost**: Subnetwork selection (Algorithm 2) requires backpropagation, increasing inference cost significantly; no analysis of computational overhead is provided.

4. **Unclear Core Mechanism of Task-Specific Differentiation**  
   - The combination mechanism of soft masks for different tasks is ambiguous. How is each task’s mask selected (by task ID or combined)?  
   - There is no explanation of how masks are ensured to be unique across tasks to avoid interference and catastrophic forgetting. Overlap or similarity between masks would increase forgetting risk.

5. **Training Efficiency and Latency**  
   - The use of a large subnetwork/softnetwork raises concerns about training efficiency and inference latency compared to baselines. No runtime or memory comparisons are provided.

6. **Minor Issue**  
   - The statement that task-specific prompts can be regarded as explicit task-specific fine-tuning (lines 224-225) is unclear and should be clarified.
