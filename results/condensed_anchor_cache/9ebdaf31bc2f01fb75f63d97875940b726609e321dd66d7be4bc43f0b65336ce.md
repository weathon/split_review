## Merged Review

### Summary
MetaAdapter addresses Few-Shot Class Incremental Learning (FSCIL) by using meta-learning (Reptile) to initialize adapters, a Feature Compactness Loss (FCL) to reserve feature space for future classes, and a three-phase training strategy: (1) meta-training adapters on base-class episodic tasks, (2) backbone pretraining with FCL and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), and (3) few-shot adaptation where adapters are fine-tuned while the backbone is frozen. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on mini‑ImageNet, CIFAR­‑100, and CUB‑200. The framework is presented clearly with figures and ablation studies, and the idea of integrating adapters with meta-learning is considered novel by some reviewers. However, other reviewers raise fundamental issues about unclear method descriptions, insufficient analysis of the core FCL, missing comparisons, and fairness of baselines.

### Strengths
1. **State-of-the-art performance** on multiple FSCIL benchmarks (mini‑ImageNet, CIFAR‑100, CUB‑200), demonstrating effective adaptation to new classes with few examples. (R1, R3, R5)
2. **Comprehensive framework** integrating meta-learning, feature compactness loss, and knowledge distillation provides a holistic approach to FSCIL challenges. (R1)
3. **Novel architectural design** combining meta-learning with adapter modules enhances both plasticity and stability. (R3)
4. **Systematic three-phase training strategy** (meta-initialization of adapters, feature space management during pretraining, few-shot adaptation) directly addresses key FSCIL challenges. (R3)
5. **Thorough empirical validation** across datasets with extensive ablation studies (Table 2, Figure 4) that provide insight into component contributions. (R2, R3)
6. **Well-structured presentation** with clear figures that is easy to follow. (R1, R2)
7. **The feature compactness loss and adapter integration are impressive** and the ablation is substantial. (R2)
8. **Enhancing plasticity via adapters while freezing the backbone** is a sound technical approach for balancing stability and plasticity in FSCIL. (R5)
9. **Preserving feature space for incremental classes** is an effective performance-improving strategy. (R5)

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient novelty articulation and related work gaps.**  
   - The meta-learning initialization of adapters resembles existing few-shot meta-learning methods; the paper does not clearly explain what makes its approach fundamentally different from learning initialization parameters or adaptation strategies in standard few-shot learning. (R1)  
   - Two highly related works with the same FSCIL setting are not compared [1,2]. (R2)  
   - MetaFSCIL, a meta-learning method that also updates backbone representations, is conceptually close yet misinterpreted in L60‑63 and L113‑115, and not discussed in the context of balancing stability/plasticity. (R5)  
   - The feature compactness loss shares motivation with FACT’s forward compatibility strategy but lacks discussion or comparison with FACT. (R5)  
   - Only one method from 2024 is included in comparisons; a broader set of recent methods is needed. (R1)  
   - The paper does not discuss a large body of continual learning research that updates the backbone while balancing stability and plasticity. (R5)

2. **Unclear and incomplete description of the proposed method.**  
   - The meaning of “c_pseudo” (L223‑224) is not formally defined; it is unclear how prototypes for unseen classes are derived and incorporated into the loss. (R4)  
   - Equation (4) is ambiguous: the dimension of “p_concat” is not specified – if “c_batch” has shape (B, C, d) and “p” has shape (B, d), concatenation fails; further, a softmax applied to a scalar from cosine similarity is not meaningful. (R4, R5)  
   - Section 3.5 and Figure 2 are hard to interpret: the adapter’s structure is vague (possible sharing of convolutional layers, but colors are ambiguous; channel numbers appear different, yet Eq. (13) adds them without explanation). The expansion of the weight matrix W_n^{t-1} to accommodate new classes is not shown (only W_n^t appears in Fig. 2(b)). (R4, R2)  
   - The claim that adapters encode task-agnostic knowledge (L67) is not elaborated; it is unclear how this property is ensured. (R5)  
   - The generation of pseudo-targets is not described. (R5)  
   - The statement “randomly initialize the adapter parameters for the j‑th task” (L197‑198) is inaccurate; adapters should be updated iteratively via Eqs. (1)–(2), not randomly reinitialized. (R5)

3. **Insufficient analysis of Feature Compactness Loss (FCL).**  
   - The trade-off between feature compactness and inter-class separability is not discussed; it is unclear why more similar inter-class features do not degrade classification. (R2)  
   - There is no proof that compacting the feature space actually reserves embedding space rather than causing overall space shrinkage (similar to collapse in contrastive learning). (R2)  
   - The claim that reserved space helps future tasks is not isolated from other factors (e.g., simply reducing the number of novel categories might also “reserve space”). (R2)  
   - Table 2 does not include an ablation with only FCL (without SAM and other components). (R2)  
   - The motivation for FCL is not justified theoretically: existing works maximize inter-class margins, whereas FCL reduces the overall feature space; the paper should explain why compacting is more effective than margin-maximization for preserving space for new classes. (R4)  
   - FCL and SAM reduce distances among base classes, but the potential degradation in base-task performance is not analyzed or mitigated. (R5)  
   - The analysis should reference [3], which similarly proposes reducing inter-class distance in FSCIL. (R4)

4. **Training complexity and computational overhead.**  
   - The three-phase training (meta‑training, backbone pretraining with FCL and SAM, few‑shot adaptation) adds significant complexity. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of computational cost per phase, especially the added cost of SAM, nor a comparison with other methods. (R1)  
   - The use of a rotation technique in the implementation is not mentioned in the manuscript, and other implementation details are missing. (R3)

5. **Base-task performance concerns.**  
   - The framework underperforms on base classification tasks compared to other methods using the same backbone. A detailed ablation study is needed to understand the impact of each component (SAM, FCL, prototype classifier) on base-task accuracy and to explore the trade-off between incremental learning optimization and base-task retention. (R1)  
   - The same concern that FCL might degrade base performance is raised independently. (R5)

6. **Fairness of benchmark comparisons.**  
   - The paper uses ResNet‑12 for mini‑ImageNet and CIFAR‑100, but several existing methods (e.g., FACT, ALICE) use ResNet‑18 for mini‑ImageNet. This makes comparisons unfair and undermines the claimed effectiveness. (R4)  
   - Network architecture specifications and model parameter sizes are not listed for compared methods, especially since MetaAdapter introduces additional adapter parameters. (R3)

7. **Minor issues.**  
   - Typographical errors: “rataining” (L012), “minImageNet” (L448), “leanring” (L95). (R1, R2)  
   - It is unclear whether the approach could be migrated to ViT backbones. (R2)
