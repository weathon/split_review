## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses domain generalization and unsupervised domain adaptation for multi-camera 3D object detection (MC3D-Det). It identifies perspective bias—overfitting to camera intrinsics/extrinsics of training views—and proposes perspective debiasing: rendering perturbed view maps from BEV features and enforcing consistency with 2D detections from an off-the-shelf single-view detector. The method is model-agnostic with no extra inference cost. Evaluated on nuScenes, Lyft, and DeepAccident, it shows gains over BEVDepth and DG-BEV in both DG and UDA settings.

### Strengths

- The main idea is clear, intuitive, and technically novel; the framework can be plugged into existing BEV-based detectors (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- The perspective bias is derived theoretically (Eq. 2) from first principles, motivating the approach (R1).
- Extensive evaluations across multiple datasets (nuScenes, Lyft, DeepAccident) and settings (DG, UDA, Oracle, pseudo-labeling) with relevant baselines (BEVDepth, DG-BEV) (R1, R3).
- Ablative studies (Tables 2, 3) clearly show the contributions of source/target debiasing and the plug-and-play capability (R1, R4, R5).
- The method achieves significant performance improvements, e.g., 2–4% mAP gain over baseline, and outperforms DG-BEV on nuScenes (R1, R4, R5).
- No inference latency cost; the auxiliary components are only used during training (R5).
- The paper is well-organized and well-written (R4, R5).
- The task is highly important for practical deployment of multi-camera 3D detection across different camera setups and environments (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Baseline reproduction concerns**: The reported BEVDepth mAP on nuScenes (32.6) is much lower than published results (41.8 with R101-DCN). It is unclear if the gains hold when BEVDepth is used at full capacity. Similarly, reproduced DG-BEV results are worse than published (e.g., Lyft→nuScenes DG: DG-BEV published ≈ MC3D-Det > DG-BEV reproduced), though the code for DG-BEV is not available (R1).
- **Computational overhead not quantified**: The added re-rendering and auxiliary networks incur extra parameters and GFLOPs; these should be reported for the methods compared in Tables 1 and 3 (R1, R2).
- **Limited target categories**: Evaluations appear to focus on the car/vehicle category only. It is unclear if the method works for pedestrians, cyclists, or other rigid classes (truck, bus, trailer). More diverse categories (e.g., from Waymo→nuScenes protocol) would strengthen the claims (R1).
- **Camera perturbation details missing**: The magnitude of translation/rotation perturbations for perspective debiasing is not specified. It is also unclear how to balance avoiding overfitting (by not deviating too far from target extrinsics distribution) and achieving sufficient diversity. An ablation on perturbation magnitude from anchor positions is needed (R1).
- **Clarity of figures and mechanism**: Figure 3 (the key figure explaining perspective debiasing) is uninformative; it does not show how re-rendering improves heatmaps or how the implicit foreground volume (IFV) is constructed and connected to BEV features. The refinement process using the 2D detector is not clearly explained. Figure 1 is also ambiguous in conveying robustness to domain shifts (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Novelty concerns and insufficient ablation**: The idea of using 2D detection consistency to supervise 3D detection is common in monocular 3D detection; the necessity of rendering heatmaps (vs. directly projecting 3D boxes to 2D) is questionable. An ablation studying the effect of the 2D prediction branch on the source domain only is missing; the reported gains may partly come from simply adding a 2D detection task. Related works on consistency learning and 2D supervision (e.g., [a] Probabilistic and Geometric Depth: Detecting Objects in Perspective, [b] Towards 3D Object Detection with 2D Supervision) should be discussed (R4, R5).
- **No validation on sparse-query methods**: The method is only evaluated on BEV-based detectors (e.g., BEVDepth). It has not been tested on sparse-query approaches like DETR3D, Sparse4Dv2, SparseBEV, which do not use explicit BEV and may have different domain shift characteristics (R3, R4).
- **Lack of justification for 2D feature generality**: The paper assumes 2D detectors generalize better across domains but provides no statistical analysis or specific documentary evidence to support this claim (R5).
- **Operational details unclear**: How are features warped between images? Which components need retraining vs. lifted off-the-shelf from existing BEV methods? How does the network learn depth and height in the target domain without 3D data? (R2)
- **Minor issues**: Typos (“D_vitural” → “D_virtual”, “Detetctor” → “Detector”, “there is not 3D labeled” → “there are no 3D labels”, “or 2D pre-trained 2D detectors” → “or pre-trained 2D detectors”); figures too small (Fig. 2 font, Fig. 3 bounding boxes and images); some variables undefined (C, X, Y, Z in Sec 4.1); need to specify whether settings of BEVDepth and PC-BEV are aligned in Table 1 (R1, R5).
