## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Robo-Centric World Models (RCWM) that decouple robot dynamics from environmental dynamics for visual model-based reinforcement learning. Robot and environment are modeled independently with separate branches, while a cross-attention interaction module captures the effect of robot actions on the environment (but not vice versa). A mask-guided warmup phase using SAM2 segmentation masks (for the robot) initializes the model, followed by mask-free pre-training and fine-tuning on downstream tasks. Experiments on 8 MetaWorld tasks show improved sample efficiency and robustness to background disturbances compared to the vanilla DreamerV3 baseline. The approach is presented with clear visualizations, but several reviewers question the attribution of improvements and the generality of the method.

### Strengths
- Well-written and clearly organized; the logic is easy to follow.
- The idea of decoupling robot and environment dynamics is novel and valuable; it enables transfer of shared knowledge across tasks.
- The distillation of SAM segmentation into world models for distinguishing robot and background is an interesting and practical implementation.
- Promising quantitative and qualitative experimental results on MetaWorld tasks, showing improved sample efficiency over DreamerV3.
- The method demonstrates resilience against visual disturbances (e.g., background texture changes) and produces informative visualizations of learned dynamics.

### Weaknesses
- **Attribution of gains is unclear.** The additional branch, transformer interaction model, mask-guided encoder, and predictor head increase model capacity and architectural complexity. Without ablations, it is impossible to determine whether the improvements stem from robot-centric modeling itself or from these added components. Specifically, expanding DreamerV3 to a similar capacity could isolate the effect (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Missing ablation studies.** No ablations are provided for the warm-up stage, choice of warm-up data (e.g., using a single-task replay buffer vs. multi-task data), removal of the interaction module, the mask-guided decoder, or the predictor head design. Such analyses are needed to understand each component’s contribution (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Limited baseline comparisons.** The paper only compares to vanilla DreamerV3. Missing comparisons to state-of-the-art model-based RL (e.g., TD-MPC2) and leading model-free methods (e.g., DrM) make it difficult to assess relative performance. The method should also be tested with other MBRL frameworks like TD-MPC2 to show extensibility (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Generalization to disturbances is limited.** Only background texture changes are tested. Under environmental disturbances, significant performance drops occur (e.g., Sweep-Into in Figure 8(b)). Robustness to variations in robot arm appearance, base position, and camera viewpoint is not evaluated, which is crucial for real-world deployment (Reviewer 2).
- **Tasks are simple; complex embodiments not tested.** The MetaWorld tasks are relatively basic. The method should be evaluated on more complex embodiments such as dexterous hands (Adroit, MyoSuite) or humanoids (HumanoidBench) to validate effectiveness for high degrees of freedom and intricate dynamics (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Reconstruction error is not a good metric for robot dynamics quality.** The overall reconstruction error could be dominated by environmental dynamics error. A more direct metric, such as predicting the robot’s future state given current state and action, would better isolate robot dynamics performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Asymmetrical information flow is questionable.** The robot branch receives no input from the environment branch, yet claims to learn the effect of environment on the robot. This suggests the true mechanism may simply be two separate models. Validation by disabling the interaction part and comparing prediction quality is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **No real-world validation.** While simulation results are promising, real-world experiments (e.g., Box Close task) are necessary to demonstrate robustness to sensor noise, model inaccuracies, and physical variations (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing discussion and comparison to related work on action-free pre-training.** The paper claims action-free methods are limited for manipulation tasks, yet other action-free pre-training methods also perform well on MetaWorld. A direct comparison and discussion is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Limitations insufficiently addressed.** The one-way interaction (robot→environment) is acceptable for MetaWorld but may fail in real-world scenarios where the environment alters robot dynamics (e.g., different friction, table tilt). The paper does not discuss how RCWM handles such externally-modified dynamics (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear experimental details.** In Figure 7(a), it is unclear whether RCWM and vanilla WM use the same evaluation trajectories or independently sampled ones, and if shared, how they are sampled (Reviewer 4). In Figure 7(b), the sharp roll-out prediction error turn in stick-push between steps 10-20 is unexplained (Reviewer 4). The attention maps in the figures appear meaningless; the highlighted regions are not clearly justified (Reviewer 2). The gripper reconstruction in Figure 5 appears inadequate (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis for important scenarios.** The paper does not discuss cases where prior robot segmentation masks are unavailable or difficult to obtain, or how the method would handle environments with unpredictable dynamic elements like deformable objects (Reviewer 3). Performance in a multi-task setup with shared policies (as in TD-MPC2) is not tested (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor issues:** Inconsistent notation (“vanilla WM” vs. “DreamerV3”) and a typo: “dynamic model” should be “dynamics model” (Reviewer 4).
