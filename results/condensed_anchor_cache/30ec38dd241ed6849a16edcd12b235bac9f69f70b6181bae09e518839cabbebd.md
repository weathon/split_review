## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper applies two existing CNN architectures (ResNet-50 and EfficientNetV2) to classify renal diseases from CT scans into four binary tasks: Cyst_vs_Normal, Cyst_vs_Stone, Cyst_vs_Tumor, Stone_vs_Tumor. The models are trained using transfer learning and achieve up to 100% testing accuracy. While some reviewers found the paper well-organized and the dataset relatively large-scale (R1, R2), the majority (R3, R4) consider the work to have negligible novelty, insufficient evaluation, and strong evidence of LLM-generated content.

### Strengths
- **Organization and clarity**: The paper is well-organized and clearly written (R1).
- **Technical soundness**: The proposed method is technically sound (R1).
- **Dataset scale**: The collected dataset is relatively large-scale (R1).
- **Use of multiple datasets**: The study utilized multiple datasets (R2). *(Note: the nature of “multiple datasets” is not further elaborated.)*
- **Problem presentation**: The paper presents the applied problem well (R4).
- *Contrast*: Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 3,3) found these strengths; reviewers 3 and 4 (scores 1,1) stated no strength or that the paper “has virtually no novelty and doesn’t really seem like a research paper” (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: No methodological contribution. Transfer learning on pre-trained CNNs is well-established; the paper offers no novel method or architecture (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Lack of interpretability**: No model explainability results, which is crucial for clinical practice (R1).
- **Insufficient evaluation**:
  - Missing metrics: ROC AUC and determination of cut-off points should be reported; only accuracy, precision, recall are given (R1).
  - No hyperparameter search, no comparisons on multiple methods/datasets, no confidence intervals, standard errors, or statistical tests (R4).
  - Figures quality needs improvement (R2).
  - Binary classification design is unexplained: why transform a multi-class problem into four binary tasks (e.g., Cyst vs. Normal, etc.) instead of a single multi-class classifier? This appears suboptimal; no benefit for the multi-class objective is shown (R1, R3).
- **Execution and evaluation deficiencies**:
  - 100% testing accuracy is suspicious; even state-of-the-art models on CIFAR-10 cannot achieve such perfect results, indicating a possible data leakage or trivial dataset split (R3).
  - A similar 2023 work (IEEE, same Kaggle dataset) already compared ResNet50, EANet, and a proposed approach, making this paper’s contributions negligible (R3).
  - All steps of the ML pipeline would need improvement: more methods, more datasets, proper hyper-parameter search, and rigorous statistical validation (R4).
- **Strong indications of LLM generation / hallucinations** (R3):
  - Inconsistent citations: e.g., “Kidney cancer affects over 10% of the global population” – the cited source does not mention this; “nephrolithiasis affects approximately 17% of the global population” – the cited source says 12%.
  - CT scan citation (Schmidt, 2012) does not provide the described information.
  - Attribute “CNN” to Tan & Le (2020) (EfficientNet) instead of foundational CNN works.
  - TensorFlow citation uses Tan & Le (2021) and Khan et al. (2020) instead of the correct Abadi et al.
  - Batch normalization cited with Ioffe & Szegedy (2015) and Chen et al. (2020) – the latter is SimCLR, irrelevant.
  - Transfer learning attributed to Zhuang et al. (2020), an unusually recent reference for a long-established technique.
  - The phrase “I ….. I ….. I …..” appears in page 2, resembling Claude Sonnet’s writing style (R3).
- **Reviewer question** (overlap with criticism of authenticity): “Do you really come up with the idea, experiment design and writing by yourself?” (R3).
- **No scientific contribution**: Even without methodological novelty, no new insight from the application is provided (R4). The paper simply applies two existing models to a public dataset; the highest mean accuracy is dubbed the winner (R4).
- **Not suitable for ICLR**: The work would be more appropriate for a clinical journal after rigorous evaluation (R4).
