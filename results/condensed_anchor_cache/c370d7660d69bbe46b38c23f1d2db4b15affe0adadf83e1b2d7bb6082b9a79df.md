## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies how mechanistic interpretability can improve knowledge unlearning and editing in LLMs by localizing edits to components associated with the "lookup-table mechanism" (FLU). Reviewers agree the topic is important and the experimental design is generally comprehensive. Reviewer 4 (score 8) was highly positive about the strength and thoroughness of the results, particularly the relearning and parameter-sweep evaluations, while Reviewer 3 (score 3) raised critical concerns about the lack of theoretical guarantees, adaptive attacks, and sequential unlearning. Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 5, confidence 2–3) had moderate concerns about limited generality, missing ablations, and presentation issues. There is clear disagreement: Reviewer 4 finds the results very strong and a breath of fresh air, whereas Reviewer 3 considers the method vulnerable without theoretical proof or adaptive attack testing.

### Strengths
- Studying unlearning from the perspective of mechanistic interpretability and knowledge storage is an important and promising direction (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper provides an in-depth analysis and comparison between mechanistic unlearning (FLU) and previous output-tracing (OT) methods, confirming that causal-tracing-based localization is not suitable for editing/unlearning (R1, R2).
- The motivation of breaking factual recall into more granular steps to improve editing accuracy and generalization is interesting (R3).
- Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Fine-tuning FLU-localized components effectively eliminates specific knowledge and slows relearning. The use of multiple-choice questions helps eliminate input-pattern influence (R3).
- **Reviewer 4 (score 8) highlights:** Results are very strong; finetuning the "manual interp" subset yields qualitatively different dynamics and quantitatively stronger outcomes. This is a breath of fresh air for interpretability’s utility for unlearning/editing.
- The suite of evaluations is impressively thorough, especially:
  - Relearning evaluations (Figure 2) showing mechanistically unlearned models relearn facts much more slowly (R4).
  - Parameter-sweep results (Figure 5 right) revealing that mechanistic unlearning generalizes to MCQ rephrasing substantially better than any other technique (R4).
- The paper is well presented with a thorough literature review (R1); the experimental design is generally comprehensive (R1).
- Directly comparing different localization methods (OT, EAP, FLU) and analyzing their limitations under distribution shift and adversarial relearning is valuable (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset and model scope:** Experiments are limited to two datasets (Sports Facts, CounterFact) and two models (Gemma-7B, Gemma-2-9B). The paper should test more diverse models (e.g., LLaMA) and tasks to demonstrate generalizability (R1, R2). The knowledge format is constrained to triplets; applicability to more flexibly expressed knowledge (continuous text) is unclear (R1).
- **Missing theoretical justification:** The claim that FLU components are the primary locus of factual recall requires rigorous justification, especially given distributed representations. Why FLU leads to more robust unlearning is not theoretically explained; the mechanism by which edits propagate through the network and differ from other component edits is not analyzed (R2). Reviewer 3 deems **[Critical]** the lack of theoretical proof that knowledge is fully forgotten—approximate unlearning is vulnerable and a guarantee is essential for security.
- **[Critical] No adaptive attack experiments:** Only standard unlearning/editing experiments are conducted; no membership inference or adaptive attacks are tested. Approximate unlearning methods are particularly susceptible to adaptive attacks, so their absence leaves a major gap in robustness assessment (R3).
- **[Critical] No sequential/adaptive unlearning experiments:** The paper does not test sequentially unlearning specific types of knowledge (e.g., basketball, then football), which is a realistic scenario. Impact on efficiency is unexplored (R3).
- **Editing task evaluation incomplete:** For editing, only the decline in original answer accuracy is reported; correctness of the *new* answers (e.g., accuracy on edited facts) is missing. This is crucial to show the model is learning the new information, not just forgetting the old (R3).
- **Methodology originality and completeness:** The proposed unlearning method lacks sufficient originality; works attempting unlearning from mechanistic interpretability already exist (e.g., [2]) (R1). The probing analysis of intermediate representations is derived from existing work and not novel (R3). The method only considers knowledge stored in MLP, but knowledge can reside in attention mechanisms as well (R1). Lack of discussion on Representation Engineering methods [3,4] (R1).
- **Manual localization lacks scalability:** Manually analyzing and selecting layers for operations is not convenient or flexible for large-scale editing/unlearning (R1).
- **Missing ablation studies:** No ablation on loss weights (e.g., the forget and inject losses in Section 2.3). The relative weighting likely impacts effectiveness, and sensitivity to these hyperparameters is not assessed (R2).
- **Presentation quality is poor:** Important details are missing from the main text: the FLU (or "manual interpretability") method is explained entirely in the appendix; the unlearning loss is only referenced as “log(1-p) measure from Mazeika et al.” without definition of p (R4). Two different sports tasks (unlearning basketball facts vs editing athletes to golf) are not given distinct names, making discussions vague and hard to follow (R4).
- **Ineffective result presentation:** Tables of small numerical differences obscure large qualitative differences between methods; bar charts would be better. The large number of figures across three tasks leaves synthesis to the reader without clear summarization of key takeaways (R4).
- **CounterFact retraining experiment uninformative:** The main takeaway that relearning on some facts does not generalize to other facts for any technique should be moved to an appendix, as it does not provide useful insight into unlearning effectiveness (R4).
