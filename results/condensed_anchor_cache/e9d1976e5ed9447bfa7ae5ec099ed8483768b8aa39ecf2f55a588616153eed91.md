## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TF-HNN, a training-free hypergraph neural network that decouples hypergraph structural processing from model learning by precomputing a message passing module (TF-MP-Module). The authors derive a unified theoretical framework covering popular HNNs, remove learnable parameters and nonlinearities, and collapse multi-layer aggregation into a single step. Experiments on seven datasets for node classification and hyperlink prediction show that TF-HNN achieves competitive or superior accuracy with drastically reduced training time (e.g., 10% higher accuracy on Trivago with 1% of baseline training time). Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the theoretical motivation systematic, but raise significant concerns about missing citations, insufficient ablation, hyperparameter selection methodology, theoretical clarity, and computational cost reporting. One reviewer (score 10) sees no weaknesses, while others (scores 3, 5, 8) identify critical issues.

### Strengths
- **Novel and practical contribution**: Addresses high computational cost of HNN training by shifting structure processing to preprocessing stage. (R2, R4)
- **Clear, well-organized presentation**: Paper is well-structured and easy to follow; comprehensive summary of prior HNNs in Table 1. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Systematic theoretical foundation**: Unified framework linking popular HNN approaches; provides theoretical support for efficiency, information usage, and oversmoothing robustness. (R2, R4)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluation on diverse real-world datasets for node classification and hyperedge prediction; competitive performance against state-of-the-art baselines. (R2, R4)
- **High efficiency and effectiveness**: TF-HNN outperforms baselines on large-scale Trivago (+10% accuracy) with only 1% training time, demonstrating both strong performance and speed. (R2, R3)
- _Minority view_: One reviewer (R2, score 10) considers the paper to have no weaknesses, praising its novelty, elegance, and thorough evaluation.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing APPNP citation and insufficient discussion of similarity** (R1 major): Proposition 4.2 shows strong similarity between TF-HNN and APPNP; Eq. (6) and Eq. (14) appear to apply APPNP after clique expansion. The paper does not cite APPNP, nor discuss this connection in depth, limiting originality claims.
- **Insufficient ablation study** (R1 major): The proposed framework removes learnable parameters from several baselines (Eqs. 4a–4d). The authors compare weighted S variants for node classification, but do not report performance of the modified parameter-free baselines themselves. This obscures the source of performance gains.
- **Hyperparameter selection methodology** (R3 major):
  - Hyperparameters appear to be chosen based on test set performance (from attached anonymous GitHub code), not validation set – violates standard fair evaluation practice.
  - Search space is extremely large (e.g., α ∈ {0.05, 0.15, 0.3, 0.6, 0.65, 0.7}; learning rates {0.0006, 0.0001, 0.005, 0.001, 0.0002}) on small datasets, but computational cost of tuning on large datasets (Trivago: 172,738 nodes) is not discussed.
  - Trivago hyperparameter settings are omitted from Table 13; the paper states a smaller search space was used but gives no details.
  - It is unclear whether the same search strategy was applied to all baselines; a hyperparameter sensitivity analysis is needed.
- **Theoretical claim vs. practical implementation** (R4 W1): Proposition 3.2 depends on a specific clique expansion that satisfies a certain property. However, in practice (Eq. 5) a fixed clique expansion is used. The paper does not discuss how well the chosen expansion aligns with the theoretical one, weakening the connection between theory and method.
- **Unclear definition in Proposition 4.1** (R4 W2): The phrase “entropy of information” is ambiguous – does it refer to mutual information between features and node labels? Further clarification is needed.
- **Missing reporting of initial computational cost** (R4 W3): The one-time cost of computing the TF-MP-Module should be explicitly reported (time and memory). If this precomputation exceeds typical training time of existing HNNs, it could limit practical efficiency.
- **Missing YELP dataset** (R1 minor): The YELP dataset from Chien et al. is challenging for many baselines. Reporting results on it would provide a more comprehensive evaluation.
- **Unclear assumptions about hypergraph structure** (R2 Q1): The theoretical results assume no isolated nodes or empty hyperedges. The paper does not discuss what happens when these assumptions are violated, nor why they are needed.
- **Lack of explanation for performance gap on Trivago** (R2 Q2): The 10% accuracy improvement over SOTA baselines is notable, but the paper does not explain why other HNNs learn worse representations despite similar theoretical capacity.
- **Minor issues**:
  - Line 209: “shonw” → “shown”. (R3)
  - Line 144: citation format “Ortega et al. (2018)” → “(Ortega et al. (2018))”. (R3)
  - Lines 52-53: The claim of \(n^k\) memory for dense incidence tensors is misleading; sparse tensor formats are standard in practice. (R4 minor)
  - Lines 79-80: Missing period. (R4 minor)
  - Source of datasets should be clarified. (R4 minor)
- _Disagreement note_: One reviewer (R2, score 10) stated they saw no weaknesses. The above points represent the majority and are drawn from other reviewers’ assessments.
