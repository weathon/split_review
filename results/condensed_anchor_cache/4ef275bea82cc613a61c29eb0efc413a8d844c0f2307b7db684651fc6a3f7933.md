## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes VColRL, a reinforcement learning framework for the vertex coloring problem (VCP). It formulates VCP as an episodic Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a GraphSAGE-based function approximator trained using PPO. Two reward functions (max-color and color-count) and rollback mechanisms are introduced. Experiments show competitive performance against advanced mathematical solvers and metaheuristics, particularly on large and dense graphs, with good generalization across graph types. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, clearly highlighting gaps in prior work.

### Strengths
- Integrates GraphSAGE architecture into an RL framework specifically designed for VCP, building on prior MDP formulations with new modifications (roll-back strategies, two reward functions).
- Introduces a novel reward strategy that prioritizes minimizing the highest-numbered color, which efficiently manages color allocation and reduces convergence time.
- Extensive experiments demonstrate effectiveness over state-of-the-art baselines (mathematical solvers and metaheuristics), especially on larger graph sizes and dense graphs.
- The paper is well-structured, with clear presentation of the main contribution.

### Weaknesses
- The contribution shares similarities with prior work by Ahn et al. (using Deep RL and GraphSAGE for Maximum Independent Set); more discussion is needed on how VColRL is unique and where the overlap lies.
- Lack of insight into why the color-count reward underperforms the max-color reward; no analysis or discussion of the underlying reasons.
- Baselines do not include any machine learning-based approach for VCP, which limits the comparison.
- The paper states VCP is NP-complete, but the optimization version (minimizing number of colors) is NP-hard, not the decision version.
- Computational demands are high; the paper lacks a detailed breakdown of time and memory complexity per component (GNN, RL agent, reward calculation), making it difficult to assess practical feasibility for real-time or resource-limited applications.
- VColRL is less effective on the mug family of graphs, but the paper provides no analysis of why it underperforms there (e.g., structural properties like connectivity patterns or symmetries).
- Insufficient rationale for key design choices: when to activate hard-rollback vs soft-rollback, how the initial number of colors is set (from zero or a given value), and hyperparameter selection (e.g., number of GNN layers, learning rate). Systematic ablation studies or sensitivity analysis are missing.
- The paper does not include a comparison with iterative search heuristics (e.g., tabuCol), which is relevant because VColRL employs rollback mechanisms similar to iterative search.
- Gurobi execution time should be set to a consistent time across all benchmarks, and a longer execution time result should be provided as a reference for the optimal solution.
- The COLOR02 table’s benchmark set could be more comprehensive; e.g., include DSJC125.5, DSJC125.9, DSJC250.1, and other standard instances.
- In the comparison of MDP configurations, it is unclear whether the color-count reward was tested with settings other than hard-rollback and deferred actions; if not, a different configuration might outperform the HDM setup.
