## Merged Review

### Summary
All five reviewers rate the paper 3 (weak reject/borderline) and agree the problem of remote reinforcement learning (RRL) under communication constraints is interesting and practically relevant. However, they raise fundamental concerns about the clarity and coherence of the problem setup, the novelty of the GRASP algorithm (which combines behavioral cloning and importance sampling but lacks theoretical advances), missing or unfair comparisons, incomplete experimental reporting, and insufficient presentation detail. A minority reviewer (Reviewer 5) is more positive on the contribution (score 3) and judges the experiments as solid, while others find the evaluation incomplete or the baselines inadequate.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel problem setting – remote reinforcement learning with communication constraints – that is not extensively explored in the literature and has real-world relevance (e.g., remote control over wireless channels). [R2, R3, R4, R5]
- The paper is well-organized and clearly explains the problem, methodology, and experimental design. [R2]
- The related work section appears comprehensive. [R3]
- The proposed GRASP algorithm provides a concrete method to reduce communication load while maintaining performance comparable to conventional RL algorithms. [R4]
- The use of information theory (remote sampling) is a promising tool for the problem. [R5]
- One reviewer (R5) specifically notes that the experiments are solid, although several other reviewers raise significant concerns about missing comparisons and incomplete results.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear/poorly motivated problem setup**: The scenario assumes the controller has full access to both state and reward without communication, but it is not explained how this is possible (e.g., how can the controller observe the state if communication is limited?). Also, if the controller can observe state and reward, it is unclear why it cannot also observe actions. A clear real-world justification is needed. [R3] Similarly, the practicality of the scenario is questioned: rewards can be quantized (as in DQN with clipped rewards), and delayed/quantized rewards may be more tolerable than delayed/noisy actions; moreover, rewards are only needed during training while actions are needed also at deployment. The paper does not sufficiently argue why sending actions is feasible but sending rewards is not when both can be continuous. [R4]
- **Limited novelty/originality**: The proposed GRASP primarily combines existing techniques – behavioral cloning (imitation learning) and importance sampling – without introducing significant new methodological advances. The novelty lies mainly in the application to the remote RL setting, not in the techniques themselves. [R1, R2] The approach closely resembles an actor-critic framework where the controller acts as a critic and the actor learns to mimic it, which is not a fundamental departure from standard methods. [R1]
- **Missing theoretical guarantees**: The paper does not provide any convergence analysis, performance bounds, or theoretical justification for the heuristic design. [R1]
- **Unfair or incomplete comparisons with baselines**:
    - The Action Source Coding (ASC) benchmark transmits a single action, while GRASP uses importance sampling to explore multiple action choices, giving it an inherent advantage. The action selection method should be consistent across benchmarks to isolate the effect of communication constraints. [R2]
    - A benchmark that transmits rewards directly to the actor is mentioned in Section 4 but its results are missing from tables and figures, making the comparison incomplete. [R2]
    - No comparison with other imitation learning algorithms such as VIPER (NeurIPS 2018) or DAGGER (AISTATS 2011). [R1]
    - No comparison with a model where rewards are quantized under equivalent communication load, which would strengthen the motivation for the proposed setup. [R4]
    - The experimental tables and figures do not include any classic RL algorithm that can access the reward; the paper should demonstrate that remote RL provides comparable performance to state-of-the-art RL with lower communication overhead. [R5]
- **Claims about multi-agent RL (MARL) not substantiated**: The paper claims applicability to MARL, but no MARL settings are presented, and it is not explained how GRASP is adapted for multi-agent scenarios. [R1, R3]
- **Insufficient presentation and missing details**:
    - The formal problem statement (minimizing KL divergence) is not introduced until Page 6; the paper lacks a clear, early formulation of the optimization objective. [R5]
    - Key algorithmic details are missing from pseudocode, including definitions of channel simulation encoding, channel simulation decoding, online RL, and supervised learning components. [R3, R5]
    - The connection between remote sampling from information theory and the algorithm for minimizing KL divergence is not explained, making it difficult to understand how the method works. [R5]
- **Importance sampling concerns**: It is unclear whether importance sampling introduces high variance during training or whether it enhances training by providing diverse behaviors. An ablation study quantifying this impact is missing. [R2]
- **Actor computation contradiction**: The paper assumes the actor has limited computation and cannot perform RL, yet Algorithm 2 requires the actor to perform supervised learning, which also imposes non‑trivial computational demands. This contradiction needs clarification. [R3]
- **Minor note on introduction**: The first paragraph mixes motivation from reward acquisition challenges (solving optimization problems, costly reward) with communication constraints, but the actual scenario focuses on communication, not reward acquisition (the controller still needs the reward). [R4]
