## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LaMo (Language Models for Motion Control), a framework for offline reinforcement learning (RL) that improves upon prior attempts to leverage pre-trained language models (LMs) in decision transformers. LaMo adds four components: (1) initializing Decision Transformers with sequentially pre-trained LMs, (2) using LoRA fine-tuning instead of full-weight fine-tuning, (3) replacing linear projections with non-linear MLPs for input/output embeddings, and (4) integrating an auxiliary language prediction loss during fine-tuning. Empirical results report state-of-the-art performance on sparse-reward tasks and competitive performance on dense-reward tasks, particularly in low-data regimes. The paper is clearly written and the experimental design includes ablation studies. However, several reviewers raise concerns about experimental validity (performance diverging from original papers), limited generality (only GPT‑2, only Decision Transformers, missing state-of-the-art baselines such as diffusion methods), insufficient ablation depth (changing benchmarks across ablations, no full appendix), and the modest technical novelty of the individual components.

### Strengths
- **Originality and motivation**: The paper identifies and addresses important shortcomings in prior work (Wiki-RL) by (a) including sparse-reward experiments that better motivate pre-trained LMs, (b) using LoRA to fine‑tune LMs in RL, and (c) performing an ablation with a random corpus to demonstrate the importance of sequential pre‑training. (R1)
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well structured, easy to follow, and clearly explains the motivation and contributions. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Significance of results**: LaMo performs competitively in the low-data regime, showing it can better leverage pre-trained LMs than Wiki-RL. The auxiliary language loss (and possibly LoRA) helps mitigate overfitting, a major problem in offline RL. (R1) The results span a diverse range of scenarios (sparse/dense rewards, varying data scale) and include a comprehensive ablation study. (R4)
- **Quality and reproducibility**: Technical details for reproducing results are provided, and the related work is well discussed. (R2) Experiments are meticulously designed to empirically validate individual components. (R4)
- **Simplicity and potential impact**: The proposed changes are simple and intuitive, showing strong performance improvements over DT and DT+Wiki across three benchmarks. (R2, R3) The framework could potentially be adapted to other LLMs and offline RL models/tasks. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Validity of experimental results**: Several reported baseline numbers diverge significantly from original papers:
  - BC and CQL on Franka-complete (D4RL) achieve 43.8 and 33.8 in the original D4RL paper but are reported as 0 in this paper. (R1)
  - On Kitchen, BC and CQL often perform better with 1% of the data than with the full dataset; similarly for Wiki-RL and DT on some splits. (R1)
  - Wiki-RL underperforms the same model (ChibiT) on Atari in the original Wiki-RL paper (e.g., Breakout: this paper reports Wiki-RL ≈130, original Wiki-RL paper reports ≈280). DT in this paper outperforms the value reported in the Wiki-RL paper (≈350 vs ≈267). (R1) These discrepancies question whether fair comparisons were made.
- **Incomplete ablation analysis**: Ablation studies are presented on different task/dataset-ratio combinations, leading to potential biased reporting. The full ablation tables are not provided in the appendix (only the pre-training ablation is given). (R1) For example, Section 5.5 benchmarks change between components, and the rationale for selecting those benchmarks is not given. (R4)
- **Limited generality and outdated baselines**: Only GPT‑2 and Decision Transformers are studied; it is unclear if conclusions hold for newer, stronger LLMs or other offline RL models. (R3) Baselines are missing recent strong offline RL methods, especially diffusion-based approaches such as Diffusion-QL and DD. (R3) Experiments only include medium datasets from D4RL, excluding medium-replay and medium-expert datasets that would better highlight language pre‑training advantages. (R3)
- **Modest technical novelty**: The main additions compared to Wiki-RL are a different fine‑tuning method (LoRA) and larger projection/output layers—neither is a new technique. (R2) Several elements (language loss, ImageGPT ablation, baseline selection) are already present in Wiki-RL and are not clearly distinguished as original contributions. (R1)
- **Weak motivation for LoRA**: The paper claims LoRA helps with early overfitting, but Figure 5 also shows drops for LaMo, and the argument is not well supported. (R1) The rationale for only applying LoRA to Q, K, V attention weights, while freezing large MLPs inside the transformer, is not explained. (R4)
- **Insufficient analysis of MLP choice**: The switch from linear projections to MLPs in attention and embeddings lacks theoretical motivation, especially given that LoRA already limits model capacity. The risk of overfitting with higher‑capacity MLPs and the computational/parameter efficiency compared to linear projections are not discussed. (R4)
- **Domain gap question**: It remains unclear why language pre‑training helps RL control tasks with a large domain gap. (R2) Would replacing the language loss with a regularization loss that keeps the fine‑tuned model close to the pre‑trained model work as well? (R3)
- **Missing details and notation**: 
  - The distributions in Equation 1, \(a'\) in Equation 3, and \(T\) in Equation 4 are underspecified. (R1)
  - Citations lack journal/proceeding details, making it hard to identify the exact version of referenced papers. (R1)
  - The first paragraph of Section 4.1 is confusing: it initially suggests the authors pre‑trained GPT‑2 themselves, but they only used a pre‑trained model from Hugging Face and pre‑trained special variants. (R3)
- **Missing experiments and clarifications** (also expressed as reviewer questions):
  - What are the specifications of the Reacher2d environment (not a standard sparse‑reward environment)? Provide the expert score and the score of the policy that generated the medium dataset. (R1)
  - Which Atari offline datasets were used (D4RL does not provide Atari by default)? (R1)
  - How much hyperparameter tuning was spent on the language loss weight and the fraction of parameters trained with LoRA? Are the hyperparameters in Appendix E used for all transformer models (LaMo, Wiki‑RL, DT)? (R1)
  - Do the transformer and non‑transformer baselines have comparable parameter counts to ensure fair comparison? (R1)
  - In Figure 6(a), why do the curves not start from the same point at step 0? Why does the cross‑entropy loss decrease significantly for an already pre‑trained model (red curve) and eventually also decrease for the ablation (blue curve)? (R1)
  - How do different scales of GPT‑2 (e.g., small, medium, large) affect the performance? (R4)
- **Statistical reporting**: Performance curves use shaded areas of \([μ - 0.5σ, μ + 0.5σ]\), which makes improvements harder to assess. (R1)

*Reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R1, score 8) is notably more positive, praising the originality and significance, while the other three (scores 5, 6, 6) are more critical, particularly on experimental validity, novelty, and breadth of comparisons.
