## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces LoCo-LMs, a fine-tuning method that uses a neuro-symbolic semantic loss (based on weighted model counting and sentential decision diagrams) to improve the logical consistency and factuality of LLMs. Experiments on BeliefBank and EntailmentBank show gains over vanilla models and the ConCord baseline, with better generalization to unseen similar facts and efficiency on small datasets.

### Strengths
- **Novel neuro-symbolic integration**: The semantic loss grounded in weighted model counting is a novel and interesting framework that is generalizable to complex logical constraints (negation, implication, reverse implication) and does not rely on an external solver at inference time.
- **Strong empirical results**: Detailed experiments demonstrate clear improvements in logical consistency, factuality, and transferability over baselines (XENT, ConCord, zero-/few-shot) even when using only 5–10% of training data.
- **Generalization and transfer**: The method shows informative generalization to unseen but semantically similar facts and constraints, an important property for real-world sparse data scenarios.
- **Efficiency**: The approach is simple, requires no additional inference-time compute, and reduces reasoning costs compared to external-tool methods.
- **Alignment with commonsense**: The method effectively enforces complex logical relationships (positive implication, reverse implication, negation), improving response quality.
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 2) was more positive** (score 8, soundness 4) and highlighted the novelty, generalizability, and strong experimental support even on small datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation scope and artificiality**:  
  * Tests only basic constraints (negation, implication, reverse implication); does not cover multi-hop reasoning or more complex logical scenarios needed in real applications.  
  * Datasets have outputs of fewer than 4 tokens; unclear how the method supports generating longer, factually consistent responses.  
  * Tasks are highly artificial (e.g., unnatural phrasing like “Is an albatross not an organism?”), which may negatively affect LM performance.  
  * No evaluation on downstream tasks (QA, reading comprehension, math reasoning) to verify that language capabilities are maintained beyond perplexity.

- **Baselines and comparisons**:  
  * The only external baseline not implemented by the authors is ConCord (2019, using RoBERTa-ANLI), and only two numbers are reported.  
  * The comparison may be unfair: LoCo uses hard constraints guaranteed true, while ConCord uses a weaker inference model. More details on the comparison setup are needed.  
  * No comparisons with modern methods for model updating (e.g., targeted fine-tuning, knowledge editing, RAG).  
  * Few-shot baselines are poorly described (what examples are given? how are they helpful?) and often underperform zero-shot.

- **Method clarity and details**:  
  * Section 2 is overly elaborate (e.g., defining XOR, expressing implication via negation/OR) while critical details of the method are insufficient: the conversion of logical constraints into differentiable graphs (paragraph starting line 232) and the roles of circuits and sentential decision diagrams are not explained clearly.  
  * Figure 1 is unhelpful; it only shows three correct/incorrect examples without conveying the method or aggregate results.  
  * Notation is confusing: \(z \sim p_\theta(z)\) appears to sample unconditionally but is meant to sample truth assignments conditioned on \(\alpha_i\).  
  * The structure of \(\alpha_i\) in \(\mathcal{D}_c\) is not clearly defined upfront.  
  * It is unclear whether the semantic loss accounts for facts that require multi-hop inference from the KB and constraints (e.g., transitive relations). This limitation is not addressed explicitly.

- **Assumptions and limitations**:  
  * Assumes facts are conditionally independent given the model state, which may miss dependencies (e.g., transitivity: learning “A implies B” and “B implies C” does not guarantee “A implies C” without explicit training).  
  * Generalization only works within the same constraint type; performance degrades when constraints differ between fine-tuning and testing (Table 4).  
  * Sensitivity to prompt format: gains may be partly due to prompt selection; broader testing across diverse templates and alternative prompting (e.g., sequential conditioned questions) is missing.  
  * The method may be vulnerable to adversarial attacks (e.g., just-in-time injection) that bypass learned constraints.  
  * Scalability concerns: fine-tuning with complex logical constraints may become a bottleneck for larger models or diverse datasets compared to RAG/knowledge editing.

- **Model generation and size**:  
  * Main experiments use Macaw-Large (pre-LLM); later experiments use Llama-2, which is much less capable than current Llama-3.2, raising doubts about applicability to modern LLMs.  
  * Only one small model size is tested; no results on larger models (e.g., Llama-3 vs Llama-2 with/without LoCo).  
  * Reviewer 2 notes that experiments on outputs <4 tokens leave the method’s performance on longer responses unclear.

- **Missing analyses**:  
  * No evaluation of whether pre-trained baseline scores improve with greedy decoding (Tables 1–2).  
  * Possible label bias in probability estimates for yes/no tokens is not addressed (cites OpenReview and arXiv papers).  
  * The vague notion of “similarity” for transfer (line 192) is not defined.  
  * Line 469 refers to results but does not specify the table (likely Table 3).  
  * Practical impact on real applications is not demonstrated.
