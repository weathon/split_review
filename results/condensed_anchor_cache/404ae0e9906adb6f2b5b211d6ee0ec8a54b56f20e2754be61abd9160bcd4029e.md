## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TA-STVG, a Target-Aware Transformer for spatio-temporal video grounding that replaces zero-initialized object queries with adaptively generated queries from the video-text pair. Two modules are introduced: Text-guided Temporal Sampling (TTS) for selecting target-relevant frames, and Attribute-aware Spatial Activation (ASA) for extracting fine-grained visual attributes. TA-STVG achieves state-of-the-art results on HCSTVG-v1, HCSTVG-v2, and VidSTG. Reviewers generally commend the clear motivation, effectiveness, and thorough ablations, but concerns include limited innovation (one reviewer), missing computational cost reporting, unanswered technical questions about module design, and insufficient robustness evaluation.

### Strengths
- Intuitive and clear motivation: generating target-specific object queries improves discriminative feature learning over zero-initialized queries (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The method is concise and effective, achieving state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks (HCSTVG-v1/v2, VidSTG) (R1, R2, R4).
- Extensive ablation studies validate the contributions of TTS and ASA modules, and the generality of the modules is demonstrated by improving existing methods (TubeDETR, STCAT) (R3).
- Qualitative results and analysis adequately illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed components (R1).
- Writing is good and easy to follow (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited innovation (minority view)**: One reviewer (R3, rating 6) considers the contribution incremental, as it focuses primarily on attention-based keyframe filtering and attribute extraction for query initialization, noting that the two core ideas are not fundamentally novel. This contrasts with the other reviewers (R1, R2, R4, all rating 8) who view the approach as novel and significant.
- **Lack of related work discussion**: The paper does not sufficiently discuss connections to related techniques such as spatial activation learning [1] and concept classification [2] used in weakly-supervised video grounding, which would clarify the novelty of ASA (R1).
- **Global pooling of object queries**: Object queries are generated via global pooling of multimodal features and reused for every frame, discarding frame-specific information. This design may be suboptimal for handling dynamic appearance changes, occlusions, or motion across frames. The authors should justify this choice or consider frame-specific queries (R1).
- **Loss weight selection**: The weights for TTS loss and ASA loss are reported without any ablation study or rationale, making it difficult to assess sensitivity and reproducibility (R1).
- **Missing computational complexity analysis**: The paper does not report parameters or FLOPs compared to other methods, and the method may require significant computational resources, limiting practical accessibility (R2).
- **Insufficient robustness evaluation**: The model’s performance under occlusion, similar-appearing distractors, noisy text descriptions, or vague queries (where the target lacks clear attributes) is not adequately explored or discussed (R2, R3).
- **Unclear ASA multi-label classification**: It is unclear whether extracted subject features for appearance and motion are identical, how weak attribute labels relate to subject features, and whether the weak labels update dynamically during training (R3).
- **Unanswered technical questions**:
    - Is a single subject representation extracted from the text, or multiple? If multiple, how are they processed through decoding? (R4)
    - Do the sampled target-relevant temporal appearance and motion features need to have the same quantity? How is this controlled? (R4)
    - What happens if the frames selected by TTS do not contain the target object? (R4)  
    (These questions overlap with concerns about temporal sampling robustness.)
- **Minor typos**: e.g., "such" should be "such as" in line 29 (R1).
