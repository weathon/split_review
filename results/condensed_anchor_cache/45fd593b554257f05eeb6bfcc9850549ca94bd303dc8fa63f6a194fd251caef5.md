## Merged Review

### Summary  
The paper proposes to combine a global noise transition matrix with implicit regularization to handle diverse label noise, replacing per-instance matrix estimation. The method adds a sparse residual term \(\gamma(X)\) between the noisy posterior and the transition-corrected posterior. Theoretical consistency and generalization bounds are provided. Experiments on synthetic and real-world noisy-label benchmarks show strong performance, often outperforming prior transition matrix methods and some semi-supervised approaches.

### Strengths  
- **Clear motivation**: Estimating instance-dependent transition matrices is challenging; the paper tackles this by learning a global matrix plus instance-level residuals. [R1, R2, R4]  
- **Novel combination**: Integrating transition matrix methods with implicit regularization is a seldom-explored direction that leverages strengths of both. [R1, R2]  
- **Theoretical support**: Provides consistency and generalization results (e.g., Theorem 1–2), preserving the statistical consistency advantage of transition matrix methods. [R1, R2]  
- **Strong empirical results**: Outperforms prior transition matrix methods (e.g., VolMinNet, SOP) and some robust sample-selection and semi-supervised baselines across multiple synthetic and real-world noise types, without requiring auxiliary techniques. [R1, R2, R3, R4]  
- **Well-written and clear** (though with some terminology issues). [R3, R4]

### Weaknesses  
- **Limited novelty / similarity to existing works**:  
  – The method is essentially a combination of SOP [1] and VolMinNet [2]; the theorems resemble those in SOP. [R1]  
  – The residual term \(\gamma(X)\) is conceptually equivalent to the \(\delta T\) in T-revision (NIPS 2019), since \(\gamma(X) = \delta T \cdot P(Y|X)\). The paper must clarify essential differences. [R3]  
  – The idea of learning a global matrix plus instance-level residual is similar to [R1] (Shikun Li et al., 2023), which is not discussed. [R4]  
  *One reviewer (score 3) was particularly critical of the novelty, emphasizing the strong similarity to T-revision.*

- **Unclear sparsity assumption**:  
  – No formal definition of sparsity (element-wise vs. structured, e.g., low-rank) for the residual \(\gamma(X)\). [R2]  
  – Lack of empirical or theoretical justification for why \(\gamma(X)\) should be sparse across diverse noise types (symmetric, asymmetric, instance-dependent). Why would this residual be sparse? [R2, R3]  
  – The relationship between sparsity and handling instance-dependent noise is not explained. [R3]

- **Theoretical analysis shortcomings**:  
  – The notion of a “valid” transition matrix and residual term is not formalized; no conditions under which clean posterior is provably recovered. [R2]  
  – Justification for ignoring \(\log\det(T)\) in the generalization bound is insufficient; the term may affect the bound. [R2]  
  – Theorem 2’s error does not go to zero as \(n \to \infty\); it depends on \(\epsilon\). [R4]  
  – Condition 1 (network parameters zero at initialization) is impractical. [R4]  
  – The analysis relies on neural tangent kernels (NTK), which is less general. [R4]

- **Identifiability and optimality concerns**:  
  – The decomposition \(\gamma(X) = \sum_{i=1}^N u_i v_i\) (Eq. 5) has no regularization on \(\{u_i, v_i\}\). No guarantee of identifiability, uniqueness, or optimality, which is critical for transition matrix learning. [R3]

- **Experimental issues and missing comparisons**:  
  – Backbone inconsistency: TMR uses ResNet-18 while SOP uses ResNet-34; this may bias comparisons. [R1]  
  – Missing comparison with CCR (Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2022), which also improves VolMinNet. [R1]  
  – Missing comparison with other recent SOTA methods [3,4]. [R2]  
  – When accuracy is close to baselines, statistical significance tests are needed. [R3]  
  – Would semi-supervised boosting (e.g., DivideMix) further improve results? [R4]

- **Writing and terminology issues**:  
  – Inconsistent use of “sample” vs. “example”. [R3]  
  – Incorrect use of “diagonally dominant” (should be \(T_{ii} > \sum_{j\neq i} |T_{ij}|\), not just \(T_{ii} > T_{ij}\)). [R3]  
  – Notation “\(-\top\)” is ambiguous (inverse + transpose?). [R4]  
  – Introduction lacks definitions of \(Y\) and \(X\). [R4]  
  – The reason for using \(u_i, v_i\) is confusing. [R4]  
  – Claims about time efficiency vs. traditional methods are not supported by evidence. [R4]  
  – The claim of handling “diverse” noise types is vague; the paper should specify which noise types and provide rationale for each. [R2]  
  – The method actually handles a weak version of instance-dependent noise (requires similar noise patterns across instances); this overclaiming is noted. [R4]
