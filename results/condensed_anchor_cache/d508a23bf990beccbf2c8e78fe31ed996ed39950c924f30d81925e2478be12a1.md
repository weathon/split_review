## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Cloud-Edge Model Adaptation (CEMA) paradigm for dynamic model adaptation under distribution shifts. Edge devices only perform forward propagation; all adaptation workload is delegated to the cloud. To reduce communication, two criteria exclude high-entropy (unreliable) and low-entropy (low-informative) samples from uploading. On the uploaded data, a replay-based entropy distillation from a stronger foundation model (teacher) updates the affine parameters of normalization layers of the edge model. Experiments on ImageNet‑C and ImageNet‑R achieve SOTA performance with 60 % lower communication cost. One reviewer found the framework highly novel and the improvements large, while others raised more reservations about the simplicity of the selection scheme, overconfidence of the logit-based uncertainty, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The CEMA paradigm addresses a highly practical problem: cloud-edge model deployment under distribution shifts with limited edge resources. It is a novel, pioneering way to divide adaptation tasks between cloud and edge.
- The paradigm only requires edge devices to perform forward computation, avoiding backpropagation on the edge (which is often infeasible).
- The dynamic unreliable and low-informative sample exclusion criteria are simple yet effective; CEMA reduces communication costs by 60 % compared to SOTAs on ImageNet‑C and ImageNet‑R while delivering the highest out-of-distribution performance.
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies are provided, showing large performance improvements over previous methods.
- The paper is well-written, clearly structured, with informative figures, and the related‑work review in the appendix is thorough (including a valuable comparative analysis with various methods).
- The approach has high practical value for the community, especially in real-world latency-sensitive scenarios.

### Weaknesses
- **Synchronization issue**: The paper states that inference and uploading can be executed simultaneously. It is unclear how the edge device decides which version of the updated model to use for subsequent samples, given network latency and asynchronous updates (R1).
- **Dual pseudo-label interaction**: Equation (6) uses pseudo-labels from the foundation model, while entropy minimization introduces another pseudo-label (the maximum logit). The interaction and possible conflicting gradients are not explained; the justification for using both losses and the effect of removing the entropy loss are missing (R1).
- **Minor writing/typo**: Algorithm 1 line 3 refers to “Eqn. (x)” instead of a specific equation (R1).
- **Overconfidence concern**: The selection scheme relies on entropy of logits as an uncertainty measure, but neural networks are known to be overconfident, which can lead to misidentification of informative samples (R2).
- **Lack of threshold analysis**: The impact of different entropy thresholds on performance is not explored, which could be critical in real-world applications (R2).
- **Missing computational overhead analysis**: Neither the computational overhead of the cloud-based adaptation nor the cost of calculating entropy on the edge device is evaluated or discussed (R2, R4).
- **Missing connection to self-paced learning**: The dynamic threshold adjustment resembles self-paced learning; the relationship is not discussed (R2).
- **Limited task generality**: The method is only evaluated on classification tasks; it is unclear whether it can be extended to other tasks such as object detection (R3).
- **Replay buffer design**: Table 15 shows that performance improves as the replay buffer size increases, yet the paper does not justify why not all uploaded samples are used for adaptation (R3).
- **No teacher ablation**: The performance without the teacher model is not investigated (R3).
- **Limited novelty in data filtering**: Low-informative data identification via entropy is relatively simple; existing methods for filtering such data are not compared or discussed. More sophisticated approaches (e.g., based on gradient norms, uncertainty sampling) could be considered (R4).
- **Clarification needed**: In Equation (6), it is unclear whether \( f_\theta(x) \) and the pseudo-labels \( \hat{y} \) refer to the same thing or different quantities (R4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The behavior of hyperparameters \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) under different dynamic scenarios is not analyzed (R4).
