## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a watermarking method that intervenes in token sampling by multinomially sampling multiple candidate tokens and selecting the one maximizing a secret number derived from SHA256 hash of the candidate token plus previous k tokens. Detection uses a hypothesis test on the average secret number across tokens. The method is compared to the Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a) baseline on OPT-1.3B, BTLM-3B, and Llama2-7B, with detectability and quality evaluated under token-level substitution attacks.

### Strengths
1. The method is easy to understand and simple to implement. [R2]
2. It provides a way to compute statistical confidence intervals for detection. [R1]
3. The watermark is robust to token-level paraphrasing attacks, maintaining detectability. [R1,R3]
4. The approach is model-agnostic, applicable across different LLMs. [R3]
5. The method achieves high detection rates while only slightly decreasing textual quality according to the metrics used (P-SP, MAUVE, etc.). [R1,R3]  
   *Reviewer 2 contests the appropriateness of the quality metrics (see Weaknesses).*
6. One reviewer (R3, rating 5) views the evaluation as comprehensive and the method as novel; this contrasts with the other two reviewers (R1, R2 both rating 3) who see limited novelty and insufficient evaluation. [R3 positive vs. R1,R2 negative]

### Weaknesses
1. **Significant similarity to prior work (Kirchenbauer et al. 2023a):** The algorithm closely mirrors Kirchenbauer's green-red list partitioning: both compute next-token probabilities, hash previous tokens to influence selection, and modify sampling. Here, candidate tokens (effectively a green list) are resampled to maximize a secret number. The contribution is limited due to this overlap, and the paper lacks formal analysis or improved experimental breadth compared to the baseline. [R1,R2]  
   *Reviewer 1’s questions “What is the main difference? Why does it work better?” highlight the unresolved distinction.*
2. **Overclaiming about not changing the distribution:** The paper claims the method does not alter LLM’s probability distribution, but uniform resampling over candidate tokens (given well-behaved SHA256) clearly changes the distribution; this claim is false. [R2]
3. **Narrow robustness evaluation:** Only token-level substitution (paraphrasing) attacks are tested. Missing are deletions, insertions, human paraphrasing, unicode attacks, and other diverse perturbations. This narrow scope weakens robustness claims. [R1,R2,R3]
4. **No control over watermark strength:** The method always selects the candidate maximizing the secret number; no parameter exists to balance watermark detectability against text quality. [R1]
5. **Questionable quality evaluation metrics:** The use of a paraphrasing similarity model (P-SP) for open-ended generation is inappropriate; perplexity under a larger LM (as in Kirchenbauer) is a better proxy. Quality degradation is likely underestimated, especially for low-entropy predictions where sampling without replacement (SWOR) forces uniform selection among unlikely tokens. [R2]
6. **Detectability performance not convincingly superior:** Gains over Kirchenbauer are modest (e.g., z-scores ~17 vs ~10 in Table 1). The paraphrasing-attack advantage is limited to SWOR, but SWOR significantly degrades quality (Table 3). Overall performance is not significantly better and is influenced by model, dataset, and prompt. [R2,R3]
7. **Missing formal analysis:** No formal analysis of the watermark; missing studies of watermark strength as a function of sequence length and other important ablations. [R2]
8. **Dependence on datasets and prompts:** Performance varies with given prompts and datasets; factual questions with limited flexibility challenge the method. Generalizability across tasks and domains is not demonstrated. [R3]  
   *Reviewer 3’s question about performance on unseen models/datasets is embedded here.*
9. **Focus only on text completion tasks:** Evaluation is limited to text completion; applicability to question-answering, summarization, and other downstream tasks is not shown. [R3]
10. **Unclear distinction between SWR and SWOR detectors:** The paper distinguishes detectors for sampling with and without replacement without clarifying why they are different algorithms. [R2]
11. **Extension to multiple models unexplored:** The question of whether the method can watermark multiple models simultaneously is raised but not addressed; potential challenges for detection accuracy are unknown. [R3]
