## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Visual Haystacks (VHs), a vision-centric needle-in-a-haystack benchmark with 1k yes/no questions designed to evaluate large multimodal models on multi-image question answering. Evaluations reveal vulnerabilities to visual distractors, difficulty in cross-image reasoning, and positional bias. A new open-source framework, MIRAGE (Multi-Image Retrieval Augmented Generation), processes up to 10k images on a single 40GB A100 GPU, achieving up to 13% improvement over open-source LMMs on VHs and setting state-of-the-art on RetVQA.

### Strengths
- **Novel benchmark direction**: The VHs benchmark provides a fresh, challenging vision-centric evaluation for long-context LMMs. All three reviewers agree on its importance and relevance to the community.
- **Comprehensive evaluation with insightful findings**: Systematic testing of open- and closed-source models uncovers three critical insights (vulnerability to visual distractors, multi-image reasoning failures, positional visual bias). Reviewer 2 notes that random guess achieves 50% accuracy yet some open-source VLMs drop significantly even at small haystack sizes despite high scores on other benchmarks.
- **Detailed experiments**: Ablations on needle position and running time are performed, providing transparency.
- **Open-source release**: Benchmark, model, and code are publicly available under MIT license, benefiting community reproducibility.
- **Novel MIRAGE framework**: Combines a compressor and retriever to efficiently handle up to 10,000 images on one 40GB A100 GPU, demonstrating scalability and performance gains on VHs and RetVQA.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited object diversity**: Only COCO’s 80 categories are used, lacking fine‑grained distinctions (e.g., sedan vs. truck, husky vs. golden retriever), which restricts evaluation of nuanced visual understanding.
- **Restricted question diversity**: Only yes/no questions are generated; templates appear very limited (“seems only three”). This can let models exploit superficial keyword patterns instead of genuinely processing visual content. Missing question types such as comparative reasoning or attribute‑based queries.
- **Focus on recognition over reasoning**: Many questions (e.g., “For the image with a truck, is there a dog?”) primarily assess object presence/absence rather than complex reasoning like spatial relationships, object interactions, or contextual understanding. The benchmark is thus more about strong multi‑image object detection than true visual QA reasoning. Reviewer 3 (confidence 4) explicitly states the task does not simulate real‑world long‑context visual reasoning.
- **Design concern with multi‑needle vs. single‑needle difficulty**: In Figures 2 and 3, models like Gemini, GPT, and MIRAGE perform better on the intended‑to‑be‑harder multi‑needle tasks than on single‑needle ones. This suggests a potential calibration flaw or evaluation bias in the benchmark design (Reviewer 2, confidence 2 – minority more critical).
- **MIRAGE circumvents the core VHs challenge**: By deselecting unrelated images, MIRAGE largely avoids long‑context reasoning, which is the very challenge the benchmark is meant to test. The approach appears tailored to recognition‑focused tasks and may not benefit or generalize to holistic visual understanding (Reviewers 2 and 3).
- **General‑VQA performance drop**: MIRAGE shows a significant performance decrease on 4 out of 7 general VQA tasks (e.g., SEED, MME, CHAIR are among the many untested datasets). This raises questions about its generalizability to broader multimodal settings.
- **Missing related work**: The paper does not cite recent multi‑image QA benchmarks such as CompBench, MANTIS, MUIRBENCH, nor the similar retrieval approach ColPali.
- **Questions from reviewers that overlap with criticisms**:
  - How many templates were used to generate questions? (Reviewer 1)
  - What advantages does VHs offer over recent multi‑image QA benchmarks? (Reviewer 1)
  - Could the authors add randomly sampled failure cases from GPT or Gemini to show more insights? (Reviewer 2)
  - Could the address of ethics concerns around the code license be clarified? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is there confusion about the difference between MIRAGE and the Q‑former model, given MIRAGE uses Q‑former? (Reviewer 3)
