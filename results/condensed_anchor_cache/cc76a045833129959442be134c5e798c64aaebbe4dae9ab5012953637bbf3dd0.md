## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a new task, Interactive Video Corpus Retrieval (IVCR), and contributes a bilingual multi-turn dataset IVCR-200K built from five existing video datasets using GPT‑4 to rewrite captions, synthesize dialogues, and generate responses, along with a benchmark and an InterLLaVA framework (MLLM + frozen LLM + fine-tuned video encoder). Reviewers agree the problem is relevant and understudied, and the scale of the dataset is a notable contribution. However, all three reviewers raise serious concerns about the dataset’s genuineness (overwhelmingly GPT‑4 generated), insufficient survey evidence for claimed user preferences, lack of clarity in task definition and evaluation metrics, and methodological flaws in the proposed model (especially the cross‑entropy re‑ranking loss and poor moment‑retrieval performance). One reviewer is markedly more positive (score 6), while the other two are more critical (scores 5 and 3), with the lowest scoring reviewer pointing out critical technical flaws and missing citations.

### Strengths
- **Problem significance & originality**: The focus on interactive video retrieval is understudied and has clear commercial potential. The mixed query types within a single dialogue (Fig. 1c) is a natural and interesting direction (R2).
- **Dataset scale & analysis**: The large-scale benchmark IVCR‑200K is a substantial resource; its quality analysis is thorough (R1), and the hierarchical visualisations (Fig. 9 supp.) are effective (R2).
- **Framework as a baseline**: InterLLaVA provides a concrete example for future work in this area (R1).
- **Effort on comparison**: The paper implements existing methods to create a reasonable benchmark for video and moment retrieval on the new dataset (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Survey evidence for user preferences is insufficient** – R1 considers it a strength, but R2 and R3 note that the 80.8% claim is too strong given the lack of survey questions, methodology, population demographics, and response statistics. The ShareGPT analogy (7.27 rounds) is weak because ShareGPT is primarily text‑based, not video‑based (R2). The paper should clarify or weaken these claims (R3).
- **Dataset not “real interaction”** – The dataset is overwhelmingly generated by GPT‑4 (pipeline in Fig. 3). The “Real interaction” checkmark in Table 1 is misleading; human refinement is mentioned but not described (R2, R3). The multi‑turn interaction scenarios are not defined: what types of interactions (e.g., search→refine, video→moment) are covered? How are they benchmarked? Without this context, Table 4 results are hard to interpret (R3).
- **Missing important citations** – The original ActivityNet dataset is by Caba Heilbron et al. (2015), not Krishna; and the paper should cite Escorcia et al. (2019) for moment retrieval in video corpus (R3).
- **Clarity issues in writing and figures** – (a) Definition of “Real interaction” (L099‑L123) is ambiguous; “truly understanding users” is vague. (b) Figure 1 caption is missing; the green/red faces are unexplained. (c) Table 2 caption says “bold represents optimal performance,” yet the InterLLaVA row is entirely bolded even when it is not the best (e.g., R@1 IoU=0.5 moment retrieval lags behind 2D‑TAN). (d) BLEU‑4 and GPT‑4 score evaluation: what is the ground‑truth answer? Only captions? Needs clarification (R3).
- **InterLLaVA moment‑retrieval performance is poor** – It is not state‑of‑the‑art; 2D‑TAN outperforms it by over 40 absolute points on R@1 IoU=0.5. The MLLM can only process a limited number of frames, which makes accurate timestamp regression difficult (R1, R3). While R1 attributes this partly to stage‑wise training, R3 argues the approach itself is inappropriate for fine‑grained grounding.
- **Technical flaw in MLLM re‑ranking training** – Using cross‑entropy loss (Eq. 2) on dynamically changing sets of top‑k video indices treats each instance as a classification with unstable class labels; this is not a principled re‑ranking objective. Pairwise or list‑wise ranking losses would be more appropriate. No ablation study isolates the impact of this loss (R3).
- **Stage‑wise training may limit complex interactions** – Dividing questions stage by stage could hinder handling of “jumping” questions or topic shifts (R1).
- **Long‑tail distribution not discussed** – Figures 4 and 5 show a long‑tail distribution; the paper does not discuss its potential effect on model learning for rare queries (R1).
- **Missing evaluation metrics** – R@10 is not reported for the method in Table 2 (only R@1). Why? (R2, also R3 asks same).
- **Multi‑turn setup harms video retrieval unexpectedly** – In Table 4, multi‑turn context significantly hurts video‑retrieval performance. The paper offers no explanation for this surprising result (R2).
- **Conclusions on fine‑grained localization are not generalizable** – The paper only evaluates its proposed system on the multi‑turn setting; a natural baseline would pair a state‑of‑the‑art retrieval system with a state‑of‑the‑art moment retrieval system to isolate the effect of multi‑turn interaction (R3).
