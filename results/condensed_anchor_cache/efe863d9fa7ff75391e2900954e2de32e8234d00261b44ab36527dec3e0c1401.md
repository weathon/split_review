## Merged Review

### Summary
RODIN extends ODIN with language capabilities for 3D vision-language tasks by adding a text encoder/decoder based on Mask2Former and a 3D mask-language decoder. It directly operates on posed RGB-D frames, bypassing mesh-based point clouds. The model is end-to-end trainable and achieves state-of-the-art performance on SR3D, NR3D, ScanRefer, ScanNet200, Matterport3D, ScanQA, and SQA3D. The authors argue that sensor point clouds cause a 5–10% drop in grounding performance for existing methods, motivating direct RGB-D processing.

### Strengths
1. **Significant performance improvements**: RODIN outperforms prior SOTA by >10% on SR3D, NR3D, and ScanRefer (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4).
2. **Comprehensive ablation studies**: Tables 4 and 5 effectively demonstrate the contribution of each design component, especially the visual token updating in the query refinement module (Reviewer 1, 2).
3. **Direct sensor input**: Works on raw posed RGB-D without requiring mesh reconstruction, increasing scalability for real-world settings (Reviewer 1, 3).
4. **End-to-end trainable for multiple tasks**: Unified architecture for referential grounding, object detection, and question answering (Reviewer 1, 4).
5. **Leverages pre-trained 2D features**: Effectively uses internet-scale 2D foundation model weights, adapted via ODIN (Reviewer 1, 4).
6. **Unique combination of ODIN encoder and 3D mask-language decoder** (Reviewer 3).
7. **Well-written and clearly presented**: Easy to follow, related work comprehensive (Reviewer 2, 3).
8. **Sound and valid design** (Reviewer 2).
9. **First end-to-end method that leverages 2D features** for 3D vision-language tasks (Reviewer 4 – though novelty is debated, this claim is presented as a strength by this reviewer).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited architectural novelty**: The model is primarily an extension of ODIN with added text encoding/decoding and cross-modal attention borrowed from Mask2Former. The main new component is the visual feature updating in query refinement. Several reviewers characterize this as an engineering combination rather than a fundamentally new contribution (Reviewer 1: “limited contribution”; Reviewer 2: “model designs are not new”; Reviewer 3: “key idea is simply extending ODIN … engineering combination”; Reviewer 4: “technical contribution seems focused on engineering”).
2. **Dependence on pre-trained 2D models**: The reliance on ODIN’s backbone and CLIP language encoder may limit generalization. It is unclear whether fine-tuning the pre-trained 2D weights would further improve performance (Reviewer 1). Moreover, the benefit of processing raw RGB-D comes from ODIN, not from this paper (Reviewer 3, 4).
3. **Unclear camera pose encoding and robustness to noise**: The paper does not specify how camera pose is encoded for each RGB-D frame. It also does not explain why direct RGB-D processing outperforms sensor point clouds, given that both must handle sensor noise. ODIN itself notes that inaccurate depth or camera poses cause sharp performance drops, yet RODIN’s sensitivity to these factors is not analyzed (Reviewer 1, 3). Generalization to different camera configurations is unaddressed (Reviewer 1).
4. **Fairness of comparisons in Table 1**: It is unclear whether all compared models are trained on the same dataset combination and have similar model sizes (Reviewer 2). The authors mention scale similar to prior SOTA but do not provide explicit training data or parameter counts for baselines.
5. **Open-vocabulary claim lacks validation**: No zero-shot experiments are conducted to demonstrate generalization to unseen categories or descriptions. ODIN already uses open-vocabulary prompts (e.g., GLIP), undermining the novelty of this aspect (Reviewer 4).
6. **Underperformance on ScanNet-200 and Matterport3D**: Despite incorporating more training data, RODIN underperforms ODIN on these detection/segmentation benchmarks. The paper does not investigate why (Reviewer 4).
7. **No discussion of computational efficiency**: Hardware requirements, inference time, and real-time applicability are not reported, which is critical for robotic or embodied AI use cases (Reviewer 3).
8. **Dependence on segmentation annotations**: The model requires datasets with segmentation labels for training, limiting scalability. The paper acknowledges this in the Discussion but does not propose a solution (Reviewer 3).
9. **Missing evaluation metrics for ScanQA**: CIDEr and BLEU scores are not reported; only exact-match metrics are provided (Reviewer 4).
10. **Insufficient architecture details in Figure 2**: The visual encoder design (cross-view mechanism from ODIN) is not shown, making the figure hard to understand (Reviewer 4). Also, details on how “posed” RGB-D is input (e.g., camera pose per view) are missing (Reviewer 1).
11. **Missing citations**: Relevant 2D methods (MDETR, GLIP) and recent 3D VL works (EmbodiedScan, LLaVA-3D) are not cited or discussed (Reviewer 3, 4).
12. **Limited consideration of MLLM trends**: The paper does not explore how the idea could be applied to multimodal LLMs, which may bring more significant impact (Reviewer 3).
13. **Minor presentation issues**: Typos (e.g., L263: “do not update the visual features through during query refinement”; L272: “naswers” → “answers”; missing parentheses in Eq. 5 & 6; \(f\theta\) → \(f_\theta\)) (Reviewer 2). The meaning of \(V^T\) in equations is not defined (Reviewer 2). Loss terms in Eq. 7 are not all explained (Reviewer 2). Handling of multi-word “none” phrases in Eq. 6 is unclear (Reviewer 2).

**Reviewer disagreement preserved**: Reviewer 3 (score 8) is more positive about performance and clarity, while Reviewer 4 (score 5) is more critical of novelty, fairness, and missing experiments. Reviewer 1 and 2 (scores 6) note limited novelty but acknowledge strong results.
