## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies whether distributed (non-slot-based) representation models can develop linearly separable object representations through unsupervised training on videos of object interactions, without object-centric inductive biases. Using multiple datasets and comparing slot-based models (CWM, CSWM with Slot Attention) to distributed autoencoders and contrastive models, the authors find that distributed models often match or exceed slot-based models in dynamics prediction, that linearly separable object representations can emerge via next-state prediction, and that these representations remain partially entangled (shared neural codes). The paper proposes a metric for object separability based on linear decoding of which object moved between frames.

### Strengths
- Tackles an interesting and relatively unexplored question: whether object-centric inductive biases are necessary for learning compositional representations of objects. Revisits this important assumption.
- Well written and clearly presented. The methodology to systematically control which object moves between consecutive frames is elegant and informative.
- Uses a variety of datasets (cubes, Multi-dSprites, MOVi-A) and multiple metrics, which solidifies the conclusions.
- Provides evidence that partially overlapping neural codes in distributed models can still enable effective transformation generalization, offering new insights into representation learning.
- The paper focuses on understanding representations rather than merely improving performance on a benchmark.

### Weaknesses
- **Conceptual clarity and novelty**:
  - The paper conflates linear separability with compositionality. In ML, compositionality typically means the ability to represent novel combinations of concepts; linear separability is a different notion that should be distinguished more clearly (Reviewer 1).
  - The claim that next-state prediction leads to disentangled representations is not new; this phenomenon has been documented in prior work (Reviewer 4, minority negative). The novelty is limited to the specific object-centric application.
  - Missing argument for why the emergence of disentanglement is surprising: objects are not entangled in the training data, so there is no obvious reason prevention from separating them (Reviewer 1).
  - In high dimensions, linear separability is easy depending on the number of classes; this fact weakens the significance of the separability metric (Reviewer 1).

- **Baseline selection and experimental design**:
  - The choice of baselines is problematic. CWM/CSWM use convolutional channels as “slots,” which is fundamentally different from true slot-based models and not designed for generalization to diverse datasets; their underperformance is predictable and weakens comparisons (Reviewer 4, minority negative).
  - Slot Attention is designed for static images, not dynamic scenes. For a fair dynamic evaluation, parallelized spatiotemporal binding (e.g., Parallelized Spatiotemporal Binding) should have been considered; Slot Attention’s underperformance is unsurprising (Reviewer 4, minority negative).
  - The related work section focuses only on slot-based models, omitting patch-based (e.g., SPACE, MarioNette) and keypoint-based (e.g., Jakab et al., DLP) object-centric approaches (Reviewer 2).
  - The prediction accuracy metric is unclear: how is distance computed between set-based latent representations (CWM/CSWM) that lack ordering? (Reviewer 2)
  - The result that CWM outperforms CSWM in dynamics prediction contradicts the original CSWM paper (Table 1, 3D Blocks, 10 steps). An explanation is needed for this discrepancy (Reviewer 2).
  - Why compare prediction abilities in latent space? Latent spaces have different structures, making them less comparable than image reconstructions. The authors should train a stop-gradient decoder to obtain image reconstructions and use perceptual metrics (LPIPS) (Reviewer 2).
  - Autoencoder models are not studied in the dynamics prediction experiments (Section 4.1); they should be included (Reviewer 2).
  - Reconstruction metrics should be supplemented with pixelwise MSE, SSIM, and FID (Reviewer 2). The comparison between static and sequential autoencoder is questionable because the former is trained on reconstruction and the latter on next-frame prediction, causing a distribution shift (Reviewer 2).
  - Each experiment compares only two models, missing a unified big picture. The authors should present a single figure for one dataset containing all models and metrics, and provide complete architecture details and parameter counts in the appendix (Reviewer 3).

- **Compositional generalization and downstream implications**:
  - It is unclear whether test data actually contains novel compositions of objects or transformations. The authors should clarify the train-test split regarding relevant factors of variation (e.g., number of objects, object properties) and demonstrate that high prediction accuracy requires compositional generalization (Reviewer 2).
  - Downstream task performance—a major motivation for unsupervised representation learning—is not assessed. Experiments should test sequential decision-making or inference of scene properties (e.g., number of objects remaining) (Reviewer 2).
  - The two main claims (object separability and transformation invariance) need their implications for downstream tasks more thoroughly discussed and experimentally validated (Reviewer 2).
  - Compositional generalization is not systematically assessed. The paper would benefit from defining factors of variation (e.g., number of objects, color/shape/mass/friction combinations, dynamic transformations) and creating train-test splits that test generalization to unseen combinations (Reviewer 2).

- **Additional missing experiments and questions**:
  - With more training data, does the gap between CSWM and CWM close? (Reviewer 3)
  - Is it possible to linearly decode object identity only for the first or last frame? Slot models should decode all objects, but distributed models may only decode moving objects (Reviewer 3).
  - Why is CSWM used as the baseline for RSA analysis instead of CWM, which performs best on most metrics? (Reviewer 3)
  - Would varying the latent dimension size affect the results? Augmenting capacity could make representations more distributed and alter separability (Reviewer 3).
  - Have the terms of the dynamic loss for AE-dynamic been tried separately? (Reviewer 3)
  - What are the actions available in the cubes and Multi-dSprites environments? (Reviewer 2)
  - Figure 6A: add labels for objects/actions to the similarity matrices for easier interpretation (Reviewer 2).
  - Eq. 6: t should be replaced by t+1 (Reviewer 4).
  - How general are the results to other object-centric approaches beyond CWM/CSWM? (Reviewer 1)
  - What specific properties of architecture or training procedure lead to linear separability? (Reviewer 1)
