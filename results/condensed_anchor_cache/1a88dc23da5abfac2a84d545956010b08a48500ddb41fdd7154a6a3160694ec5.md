## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CelluFormer, a Transformer-based model with a weighted diversified sampling algorithm for gene-gene interaction discovery from single-cell data. Reviewers are divided: Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 4) finds the approach effective and well-validated, while Reviewers 2 (score 3, confidence 2) and 3 (score 1, confidence 4) raise fundamental concerns about clarity, missing comparisons, and evaluation methodology.

### Strengths
- The method significantly reduces computational requirements for large-scale single-cell data by sampling only 1% while maintaining accuracy (Reviewer 1).
- Adapts Transformer models (CelluFormer) to bioinformatics for gene-gene interaction discovery, applying attention mechanisms to a new domain (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive validation across multiple datasets with baselines including Pearson Correlation and Spearman’s Correlation (Reviewer 1).
- The problem of detecting gene-gene interactions from scRNA-seq data is important (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Model description is insufficient: what is "f"? The Transformer architecture is not clearly defined; the paper merely refers to it without specifying differences from existing models. Condition 2.1 on permutation invariance is misleading for vector data (Reviewer 2).
- How does CelluFormer differ from scGPT, GeneFormer, and scFoundation? (Reviewer 3)
- Problem setting unclear: given dataset X, what is disease D? What are dimensions |V| and m? The data are vectors but the model expects a set structure (Reviewer 2).
- The core idea of using Transformer attention maps to infer gene-gene interactions is considered obvious (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed weighted diversified sampling algorithm is not compared to any existing cell subset selection methods despite a well-studied literature (e.g., Hie et al., Cell Systems 2019; Yang et al., ACM-BCB 2020; Yi & Stanley, bioRxiv 2023; Hao et al., Nat Biotech 2024) (Reviewer 3).
- Inadequate comparison with existing gene-gene interaction discovery methods: no comparison to SCENIC (Aibar 2017), GRNBoost2 (Moerman 2019), PIDC (Chan 2017), SCODE (Matsumoto 2017), SCRIBE (Moerman 2019) or other non-Transformer approaches (Reviewer 3); Reviewer 1 also requested more comparison with non-Transformer methods.
- Parameter sensitivity (e.g., choice of sample size) not discussed; scalability to larger datasets or different cell types not addressed (Reviewer 1).
- Explanation for scGPT’s lower performance on some datasets—attributed to overfitting to pretrained knowledge or mismatch—is insufficient; alternative interpretations exist (Reviewer 1).
- Alzheimer’s disease labeling is problematic: Alzheimer’s is not an infection, and individual cells likely cannot be labeled as “infected”; clarification needed on whether cells are labeled based on donor status (Reviewer 3).
- Evaluation of gene-gene interactions is flawed: the approach filters known interactions to those implicated in Alzheimer’s, ignoring non-Alzheimer’s gene functions and treating unconsidered pairs as non-interacting (Reviewer 3).
- The finding that 1% of data achieves performance comparable to 100% could indicate either excellent use of 1% or poor use of 100%; the paper does not clarify (Reviewer 3).
- Data sparsity claim is unclear: cells have up to ~12,000 expressed genes, most with 2,000+, so sparsity is not evident (Reviewer 2).
- Definition of Min-Max density is clumsy and closely related to kernel density estimation, which should be acknowledged (Reviewer 2).
- The “scatter addition operations” (line 231) description is unclear and would benefit from a formal treatment (Reviewer 3).
- Derivation of cell type labels in Table 1 is not described, making experiments non-reproducible (Reviewer 3).
- Potential limitations and biases of using Transformer attention maps for biological interpretability and generalizability are not discussed (Reviewer 1).
