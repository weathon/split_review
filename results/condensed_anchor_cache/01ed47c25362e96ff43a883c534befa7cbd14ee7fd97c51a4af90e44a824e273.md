- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 1, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a hybrid visual tokenizer that combines a low-resolution VQ-VAE branch (to produce a downsampled guide image) with a high-level semantic branch (based on SEED’s Causal Q-Former and a reverse Q-Former) and a diffusion branch (Stable Diffusion) for image reconstruction. The goal is to reduce the token count per image (to 372 tokens) while preserving structural accuracy. The method is evaluated with SSIM, PSNR, and a user study, comparing against VQGAN, MoVQ, SEED, and LaViT.  
Reviewers’ opinions diverge: most rate the paper low (scores 3,3,1,3), with Reviewer 3 being far more critical (score 1). Several strengths are noted, but the weaknesses are extensive and severe.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to follow and attempts to study an interesting topic: mapping images to fewer tokens (not bounded by patches) for downstream vision-language tasks. (Reviewer 1)
- The discussion about the relative benefits of SEED (semantic tokens) and VQ-VAE (pixel-level details) is insightful. (Reviewer 2)
- The low-resolution guide branch improves reconstruction quality compared to SEED and LaViT alone. (Reviewer 4; but see weaknesses regarding fairness of this comparison)
- The paper identifies a real challenge in visual tokenization (balancing token efficiency and structural accuracy) and attempts to combine multiple frameworks. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Fundamental misunderstandings and misclassifications**  
   - The paper misclassifies diffusion models as “tokenizers” (“two main types: VQ-VAE-based and diffusion-based”). This reflects confusion between tokenization, reconstruction, and generation. (Reviewer 3; echoed by Reviewer 4)  
   - The classification of visual tokenizers is inaccurate: many tokenizers use continuous features, and “diffusion-based” is ambiguous. (Reviewer 4)  
   - The claim that “the number of tokens increases exponentially with image resolution” is false (it increases quadratically). (Reviewer 3)  
   - The statement that “diffusion-based tokenizers use tokens that encode high-level semantic information, disregarding pixel-level details” is generally false. (Reviewer 3)

2. **Irrelevant “LLM” focus and lack of connection to actual image generation**  
   - The term “LLM” is used prominently in the title and method, yet the paper is not about LLMs. The only language part is using text embeddings to train the tokenizer. (Reviewer 1)  
   - The paper does not demonstrate how the proposed token representation would be used in an actual text-to-image generation setting. Previous works (SEED, LaVIT) are designed for generation from text; reconstruction of the original image is an intermediate test, not the goal. (Reviewer 2)  
   - Any advantage in reconstruction over SEED would be moot for generation: (a) when generating from pure text, there is no original image; (b) when generating from text + image, one could use the image directly instead of compressing and reconstructing it. (Reviewer 2)  
   - It is unclear how both low-level pixel tokens and high-level semantic tokens would need to be generated simultaneously and coordinated during actual generation. (Reviewer 4)

3. **Overcomplicated architecture with unjustified design choices**  
   - The pipeline uses three branches (Low-Res Guide, High Semantic, Diffusion) but the justification for each is unconvincing. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)  
   - For generating the low-resolution guide, why use a VQGAN encoder/decoder pipeline instead of simple interpolation or a small learned downsampler? (Reviewer 1)  
   - The semantic branch quantizes embeddings via Causal Q-Former and then applies a reverse Q-Former to de-quantize them. Why not learn continuous embeddings directly? What is the benefit of quantization followed by de-quantization, and how does it compare to simpler CLIP-based text-image alignment? (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4 also notes the purpose of the reverse Q-Former is unclear and no ablation is provided)  
   - The final generation uses Stable Diffusion; how does adding VQ-VAE actually help if reconstruction is still performed by diffusion? (Reviewer 3)  
   - The architecture is not novel; each branch is derived from prior work (SEED, VQGAN). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)  
   - The method is claimed to need “minimal cost” despite having three complex branches. (Reviewer 3)

4. **Poor quantitative and qualitative results**  
   - From Table 2: Ours SSIM 0.33, PSNR 23.23; MoVQ SSIM 0.52, PSNR 27.57; VQGAN SSIM 0.58, PSNR 28.29. The proposed method significantly underperforms simpler approaches. (Reviewer 3)  
   - The claim “our method solves the problem of structural differences” is directly contradicted by the SSIM score (0.33 vs. VQGAN’s 0.58). (Reviewer 3)  
   - Qualitative samples in Fig. 2 show artifacts and significant deviations from the original. (Reviewer 3)  
   - Only 4 different images are shown; far more visual examples are needed to judge model success. (Reviewer 1)

5. **Misleading and unfair comparisons**  
   - The paper compares its method with SEED and LaVIT while acknowledging that “SSIM and PSNR are not particularly meaningful for those models” (because they are not trained for reconstruction). Yet the paper still uses these comparisons to claim improvement. (Reviewer 3)  
   - Comparing against models designed for generation (SEED, LaVIT) on a reconstruction task is methodologically unsound. (Reviewer 3)  
   - Similarly, the token representations from SEED/LaVIT are not intended for image file compression; there is a separate body of work on neural image compression. (Reviewer 2)

6. **Lack of abaltion studies and missing experiments**  
   - No ablation studies are provided, e.g., to justify the reverse Q‑Former or the need for three branches. (Reviewer 4)  
   - The paper does not discuss or ablate how the two-phase token representation would be used in an actual image generation pipeline. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The claim of “clearer detail reconstruction” is not supported by evidence given the low SSIM/PSNR. (Reviewer 3)

7. **Writing quality and presentation issues**  
   - Numerous typos (e.g., “we proposes” in the abstract) detract from quality. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)  
   - The title is not very comprehensible. (Reviewer 3)  
   - The paper gives the impression of being submitted in a rush. (Reviewer 3)

8. **Minority reviewer disagreement** (Reviewer 3 is far more negative)  
   - While other reviewers (1, 2, 4) see some potential in the idea (e.g., improved reconstruction over SEED/LaViT, insightful discussion), Reviewer 3 finds the work fundamentally flawed: confuses tokenization and generation, results are much worse than baselines, comparisons are misleading, and the architecture is overcomplicated without novel contributions. This reviewer recommends rejection with a score of 1 and a request for major rewriting.
