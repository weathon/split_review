## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes k-sparse (TopK) autoencoders for LM interpretability, directly controlling sparsity via TopK activation instead of an L1 penalty. This simplifies tuning, avoids activation shrinkage, reduces dead latents (via initialization and auxiliary loss), and achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction-sparsity frontiers. The authors train SAEs on GPT-2 and GPT-4 (up to 16M latents, 40B tokens), demonstrate clean scaling laws w.r.t. autoencoder size/sparsity/model size, and introduce evaluation metrics (downstream loss, probe loss, explainability via Neuron2Graph, sparsity of downstream effects). These metrics improve with autoencoder size. The work is highly significant for scaling SAEs to frontier models, though some weaknesses remain.

### Strengths
1. **Novel TopK SAE training recipe for LMs**: Replacing L1 penalty with TopK activation directly sets sparsity, avoids activation shrinkage (Figure 8), simplifies hyperparameter tuning (no L1 coefficient), and is competitive with/better than prior methods (Gated, ReLU, ProLU, etc.) on the reconstruction-sparsity frontier. The recipe is simple, with few knobs.

2. **Scaling laws demonstrated**: Clean scaling laws for SAE reconstruction accuracy w.r.t. number of latents, sparsity (k), language model size, and training compute (FLOPs). The authors find an irreducible loss (no need for non-zero term), which they contextualize by comparison to compressing synthetic noise. Scaling held for large GPT-4 SAE runs (16M latents, 40B tokens).

3. **Effective dead latent mitigation**: Auxiliary loss (supervising next-aux latents) and decoder-unit-norm initialization reduce dead latents from ~90% to ~7% even at largest scales, enabling training of very wide autoencoders.

4. **Comprehensive evaluation metrics beyond MSE/sparsity**: Introduce conceptually appealing downstream metrics: (a) downstream loss when SAE reconstructions are patched in, contextualized as a fraction of pre-training compute (lines 311-314); (b) probe loss using individual SAE latents as 1D logistic probes on 61 binary classification tasks, showing 25% better loss than residual stream probes; (c) automated interpretability via Neuron2Graph (simplicity and explainability scores); (d) sparsity of downstream effects (logit ablation). These metrics generally improve with autoencoder size.

5. **Transparency and clarity**: Paper is well written, unusually detailed about training recipe (e.g., decoder initialization to encoder transpose, auxiliary loss citation). Discussion of limitations of autointerpretability methods (e.g., recall-precision tradeoff) is illuminating.

6. **Contribution significance**: Scaling SAEs to GPT-4 (contemporaneous with Templeton et al.’s Claude 3 Sonnet work) is a major step for mechanistic interpretability. Multiple contributions (training, scaling, evaluation) are valuable.

### Weaknesses
1. **Fixed-k sparsity is overly restrictive**: Each token must use exactly k latents, which may be suboptimal; adaptive sparsity could better handle varying input complexity. This may force many unimportant features to activate (concurrent BatchTopK work). Reviewer questions: Could adaptive sparsity improve performance? Limitations for real-world applications? The method loses the ability to compute a single latent’s activation independently of others (must compute all latents then select top-k).

2. **Limited context length (64 tokens)**: Experiments primarily use 64-token contexts, which may miss long-range dependencies and higher-level abstract features. The authors do not test whether SAEs generalize to activations from longer prompts. This may affect scaling law results and interpretability conclusions.

3. **Evaluation metrics need stronger baselines and validation**: (a) Probe loss improvement is small (~0.52 to ~0.49); the paper lacks baselines like a linear probe on all residual stream neurons, on random noise, or on similarly-sized noise. The only baseline (residual stream loss = 0.600) is unclear whether it is initial or final. (b) Downstream loss and explainability metrics are computationally expensive, limiting reproducibility. (c) The explainability metric (N2G) has high recall but lower precision, creating an “illusion of interpretability.” (d) The “sparsity of downstream effects” metric is high-level and hard to interpret. (e) No qualitative error analysis or examples of latents clearly tracking known features. (f) No attempt to compare explanations to LM-generated explanations (the norm in SAE literature). (g) Using individual latents as probes for high-level binary tasks (like sentiment) may be too restrictive given feature splitting.

4. **Missing comparisons with other methods on new metrics**: The paper compares architectures only on the sparsity-reconstruction frontier. It does not check whether TopK SAEs find the same latents as other methods, nor does it compare them on the new downstream metrics (probe loss, explainability, etc.). Gated/TopK SAEs might differ in quality on these evaluations.

5. **Lack of toy model validation**: The authors do not test TopK SAEs on toy models of superposition (as done in prior work to justify architectures). This would help understand why TopK might be better.

6. **Irreducible loss explanation is vague**: The experiment with Gaussian noise does not clearly explain why an irreducible error exists for LLM activations. The paper should explore whether this term varies across layers, feature complexity, latent dimensionality, and whether it is an SAE limitation or data property.

7. **Reproducibility concerns with GPT-4**: GPT-4 is closed-source, limiting verification. The paper should provide more training details (hyperparameters, compute budget) and include experiments on open-source models to demonstrate generalizability.

8. **Probing dataset justification inadequate**: The 61 binary classification tasks are not well justified; the set should be larger and more diverse, including complex reasoning and NLU tasks. Distribution of probe losses across datasets should be shown.

9. **Scaling laws not compared to concurrent work (Anthropic scaling monotonicity)**.

10. **Minor reviewer conflicts**: Reviewer 1 (score 3) found the approach not clearly scalable to much larger models, and noted limited empirical testing in real-world applications (anomaly detection, mechanistic interpretability). The reliance on auxiliary loss adds complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity.

11. **Typos and unclear phrasing**: Missing words (line 132: “instead of” assumed L1; line 367-368: “similar” missing word; line 384: “trie” should be “tree”; line 327: negation missing for cross-entropy). Notation inconsistent (N vs n). Figure 7 caption lacks explanation of N2G and bigram baseline.

12. **Initialization technique not cited**: The encoder-to-decoder transpose initialization is described in Conerly (2024) but cited only as independent replication.
