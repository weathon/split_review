## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes RDANAS, a method that combines Neural Architecture Search (NAS) with cross-layer knowledge distillation (KD) to enhance the adversarial robustness of point cloud DNNs. RDANAS uses a robust teacher model to guide a compact student architecture by automatically selecting the optimal teacher layer for each student layer via a Gumbel-Softmax search and attention loss minimization. Evaluations on ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN, and ScanNet against FGSM, PGD, and JGBA attacks are reported, claiming improved adversarial accuracy and efficiency over existing models.

### Strengths

- **Novelty (mixed views).** Reviewers 1 and 2 see novelty in combining NAS with cross-layer KD to produce compact, resource-efficient architectures that inherit teacher robustness without dedicated adversarial training. The automatic teacher–student layer matching is highlighted. However, Reviewer 3 strongly contests this novelty, stating that all method subsections follow RNAS-CL [1] and the only change is testing on point cloud data; Reviewer 4 similarly notes that NAS+KD has been studied on 2D images and no design considerations specific to 3D point cloud are provided.
- **Thorough experimental evaluation.** Multiple reviewers praise the evaluation across three datasets (ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN, ScanNet) and three attack methods (FGSM, PGD, JGBA). The paper examines defense strategies, ablates training paradigms, and reports both clean and adversarial accuracy, demonstrating RDANAS outperforming baselines in compactness and robustness (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Clear background and figures.** Reviewer 4 appreciates the detailed background introduction and the quality of Figure 1.
- **Potential for resource-constrained deployment.** Reviewer 1 emphasizes that RDANAS generates compact models suitable for environments where efficiency and robustness are both needed.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty / insufficient difference from RNAS-CL [1].** Reviewer 3 (confidence 5, lowest score 1) asserts that RDANAS is essentially RNAS-CL applied to point cloud data without architectural or algorithmic innovation. No justification is given for the method’s uniqueness beyond prior work. Reviewers 1 and 4 also question the rationale and intuition, noting that NAS+KD has been extensively studied for 2D images and that no 3D-specific design choices are motivated.
- **Missing comparison with other adversarial defense methods for point clouds.** Reviewers 2 and 4 note that the paper does not compare RDANAS to established point cloud defense techniques. Reviewer 2 asks why such a comparison is not needed; Reviewer 4 criticizes the related work for omitting baseline defenses against 3D point cloud adversarial attacks, which is the paper’s claimed focus.
- **Outdated attack baselines.** Reviewer 4 points out that the evaluated 3D adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD, JGBA) are old and recommends adding more recent attacks (e.g., [4] AAAI 2023, [5] AAAI 2024) to support the robustness claim.
- **Lack of computational cost details.** Reviewer 2 notes that while Table 4 gives training time and FLOPs, it is unclear how NAS scalability behaves in practice; the pre‑trained robust teacher model is assumed without explaining how to obtain one in a realistic setting.
- **Unclear rationale for robustness transfer.** Reviewer 1 argues that Section 3.2 lacks a detailed explanation and theoretical basis for how aligning teacher–student layers on critical input parts directly improves adversarial robustness.
- **Methods section focus.** Reviewer 1 states that the paper’s methods contain topics like low latency which are unrelated to robustness, making the narrative confusing.
- **Missing PGD results in Table 2.** Reviewer 4 questions why Table 2 does not include PGD attack results.
- **Lack of evaluation against real-world noise/physical attacks.** Reviewer 1 notes that the paper does not test under realistic corruptions or physical adversarial attacks, which is essential for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
- **Clarity issue.** Reviewer 1 finds the phrase “Table 3, upper right quadrant of the graph” (line 375) confusing.
