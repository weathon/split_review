## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces EEG-ImageNet, an EEG dataset from 16 subjects viewing 4000 ImageNet images via Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP), with multi-granularity labels (40 coarse-grained, 40 fine-grained). Benchmarks for object classification and image reconstruction are established, achieving ~60% accuracy and ~64% two-way identification. The dataset is 5× larger than existing similar EEG benchmarks. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the dataset could advance the field, but they raise major concerns about experimental design (block design temporal confounds), missing comparisons to key datasets (especially THINGS-EEG), insufficient deep learning validation, and contradictory benchmark goals. One reviewer (rating 6) is more positive, emphasizing the contribution; two reviewers (ratings 3) are strongly critical, claiming the block design invalidates the data; the fourth reviewer (rating 5) notes strengths but points out methodological shortcomings.

### Strengths
- Tackles a fundamental problem: the lack of comprehensive EEG-visual datasets. The paper is well-written and precise about methods, within-subject evaluation, and the two-step reconstruction pipeline. (Reviewer 1)
- Provides a larger dataset than prior work, addressing size limitations. (Reviewer 2)
- The problem is important; constructing such a dataset helps the area if correctly done. Benchmarks are set properly with several models, and presentation is good. (Reviewer 3)
- Leverages EEG advantages (ease of use, low cost, high temporal resolution) and includes detailed description of acquisition and preprocessing. The multi-granularity label design is a novel aspect. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing critical dataset comparison:** The paper fails to discuss the widely used THINGS-EEG dataset (Grootswagers et al., 2022; also version 2). A direct comparison is necessary. Additionally, other recent datasets are missing from Table 1: EIT-1M (Zheng et al., 2024), Alljoined (Xu et al., 2024), Gifford et al. (2022), and Kaneshiro et al. (2015). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Block design temporal confounds:** The RSVP paradigm uses a block design where images of the same category are presented consecutively, as in Spampinato et al. (2017). Li et al. (2020) showed that such a design lets models classify temporal artifacts (e.g., expectation, fatigue) rather than stimulus-related brain activity. The authors’ claim of mitigation via train-test split and narrower segmentation is insufficient; Fig. 6 itself shows higher accuracy on the first few test samples, confirming temporal leakage. The artifact is unlikely to vanish—the confusion matrix will likely show a bias toward the category that follows. This issue invalidates the dataset’s applicability for real-world EEG decoding. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Benchmarks encourage wrong optimization:** The paper’s final sentence states the dataset can help *mitigate* temporal effects, yet the classification and reconstruction benchmarks reward methods that exploit temporal artifacts (e.g., high accuracy from block-order features). This creates a contradictory objective. (Reviewer 3)
- **Poor EEGNet performance:** EEGNet achieves very low accuracy (Table 2), casting doubt on the dataset’s suitability for deep learning. More networks should be tested, e.g., ShallowConvNet, DeepConv, EEGConformer, ATCNet, EEGNex, EEGChannelNet. (Reviewer 1)
- **Hyperparameter tuning favors traditional ML:** The parameter search appears to favor machine learning models over neural networks; better tuning (e.g., following Borra et al., 2024) is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing discussion of known confounders:** Studies (Kilgallen et al., 2024; Bharadwaj et al., 2023; Li et al., 2020; Ahmed et al., 2021) show that even within-subject, single-trial EEG decoding can be driven by non-neural confounds (e.g., eye movements, attention shifts). The paper does not address these. (Reviewer 1)
- **No comparison with SOTA models or other EEG benchmarks:** The classification validation uses only accuracy, and the reconstruction uses only four metrics. Multi-granularity labels are introduced but never explicitly compared across model performance at different granularities. (Reviewer 4)
- **Data imbalance in fine-grained task:** The 40 fine-grained categories are grouped into 5 sets of 8; this may cause imbalance and hinder comparability across granularity levels. (Reviewer 4, question)
- **Missing ablation/explanation for poor EEGNet results:** Why does EEGNet perform so poorly? The asterisk on time-domain features in Table 2 is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor:** The tone of paragraphs 212–215 overstates the dataset’s novelty compared to THINGS-EEG. (Reviewer 1)
- **Alternative datasets with better randomization:** THINGS-EEG uses a separate validation stage after training (subject unaware), and SEED-DV separates training and testing stimuli by stage. These avoid the block-design confound and should be cited. (Reviewer 3)
