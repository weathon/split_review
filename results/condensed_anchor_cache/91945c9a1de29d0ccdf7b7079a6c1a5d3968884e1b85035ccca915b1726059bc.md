## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a training-based method to refine uncertainty estimation (UE) in LLMs. It formalizes over- and under-confidence mathematically, then trains a RoBERTa-based regression corrector on QA data (question, response, correctness) to predict a corrective term. This term is combined via a convex weighted sum (grid-searched weight) with existing UE scores. Experiments on TriviaQA and SciQA with OPT-2.7B and LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct claim improvements in AUROC and ECE over several logits-based and verbalized baselines.

### Strengths
- The idea of using an external model (not internal representations) for UE is novel and has the potential to be more unbiased than internal methods (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5).
- The paper provides a nice mathematical formalization of over-confidence and under-confidence (Reviewer 2) and a detailed theoretical analysis of preliminaries (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed calibration method (adding probability of incorrect answer) can be applied to different existing UE methods (Reviewer 5).
- Experimental results on TriviaQA and SciQA show improvement over baseline methods that do not incorporate training, demonstrating effectiveness of the corrector (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
- The paper is well-organized with a logical flow, and the training process is clearly explained (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Major novelty concerns (all reviewers; Reviewer 2 most critical – soundness 1, contribution 1):** The formalization of over/under-confidence is not novel (known issue) and is discarded after definition, not used subsequently (Reviewer 2). Training an accuracy classifier is well-established (p(IK); Accuracy Probes; BERT-like classifiers). Liu et al. (LitCab) use similar training set construction, and Ulmer et al. train an external model for confidence prediction, closely resembling the proposal, reducing novelty (Reviewer 3). The theoretical analysis (Section 2.3) only addresses logits-based methods, not others (Reviewer 3).

- **Evaluation is weak and unfair (all reviewers):**
    - **Missing baselines:** no study includes trivial single‑sample logits baselines: Perplexity, Maximum Sequence Probability, Mean Token Entropy (Reviewer 2). Missing advanced trained methods: p(IK), Accuracy Probes, internal state probes (Azaria & Mitchell; Kapoor et al.) (Reviewers 2, 3). Missing post‑processing calibration baselines: isotonic regression, Platt scaling, Bayesian recalibration, linguistic calibration (Mielke et al.) – current comparison is only against confidence elicitation (pre‑processing) methods, which is unfair (Reviewer 4).
    - **Suspiciously low baseline results:** RBS baseline AUROC on TriviaQA is close to 0.5, contradicting known results (e.g., EMNLP 2023 demo, Nature 2024, arXiv 2406.15627). The evaluation protocol should be double-checked (Reviewer 2).
    - **Incomplete reporting:** raw Corrector score without convex combination is not reported (Reviewers 2, 3). No confidence intervals given, so it is unclear whether the minimal improvement on Llama-3 (CBS) is meaningful (Reviewer 2).
    - **Different resource requirements ignored:** The method requires training FLOPs, data construction, and grid search for weight *w*, yet it is compared to methods that require no training. This analysis is completely overlooked (Reviewer 2, also Reviewer 4).
    - **Results on OPT‑2.7B are misleading** because the base model is poor; UE methods rely on its logits (Reviewer 2).
    - **Only two datasets (TriviaQA, SciQA) and two models (OPT‑2.7B, LLaMA‑3‑8B‑Instruct)** – insufficient to draw conclusions about generalizability or scaling with better models (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).

- **Generalizability claims are unsupported (Reviewers 1, 4, 5):**
    - The corrector learns biases on a specific domain/task; it is unclear why it should generalize across domains or models without similar biases (Reviewer 4). Current “generalization” likely stems from both tasks being easy factoid QA where biases are similar (Reviewer 4). Performance decreases in Table 4 (across domains) confirm the issue and contradict the claim of general-purpose utility (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5).
    - The method requires constructing a dataset of the same domain as the evaluation set, but details of the construction process are lacking, and the influence of different data selection strategies is not examined (Reviewer 5).
    - It is unclear how the method applies to non-QA tasks (Reviewer 5).

- **Lack of methodological analysis (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4):**
    - Why use an external model rather than training the original model’s hidden states? (Reviewer 4)
    - How does the corrector complement the original UE score? If given full weight, the corrector determines the score – does improvement come from simply relying more on the corrector, or from genuine complementarity? (Reviewer 4)
    - Which specific biases (over‑/under‑confidence) are addressed? No analysis of the corrector’s impact on these biases is provided (Reviewer 4).
    - Could a strong LLM evaluator (e.g., GPT‑4o) substituted as corrector yield similar or better results? (Reviewer 4)
    - What is the advantage of weighted correction over using the corrector score directly? (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
    - The validation set used for grid search over *w* is not specified (Reviewer 1). The selected *w* values are not reported (Reviewer 3).

- **Inaccuracies and unclear statements (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 1, Reviewer 5):**
    - Equations 8 and 9 appear incorrect (entropy formula should be –1/C Σ P ln P) (Reviewer 2).
    - Equation 2 (uncertainty as complement of probability) does not hold for all UE methods (e.g., INSIDE) (Reviewer 2).
    - The claim that “internal state‑based methods often lack stable theoretical frameworks” is false; INSIDE, for example, has a theoretical framework (Reviewer 2).
    - The statement that internal state/verbalized methods “generally fall short of advanced logits‑based methods” contradicts published results (e.g., INSIDE) (Reviewer 2).
    - Equation 14 uses strict inequality (>), but Jensen’s inequality gives ≥; justification is needed (Reviewer 2).
    - The conclusion that “U(x) is overestimated due to the considerable contribution of 1−p*” is not well elaborated (Reviewer 2).
    - The usage of p(TRUE) with few‑shot examples is not clarified (Reviewer 2).
    - Terminology “representative” vs “challenging” for baseline categories is confusing; the claim that lower uncertainty scores are due to “ease of implementation” is not justified (Reviewer 1).
    - The paper devotes ~2.5 pages to preliminaries (Section 2) that are widely understood and do not directly contribute to the method or provide theoretical bounds, reducing space for critical analysis (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
    - Code is missing parts, contains not‑anonymized paths, and is not usable (Reviewer 2). Template is broken (no line numbers) (Reviewer 2).
