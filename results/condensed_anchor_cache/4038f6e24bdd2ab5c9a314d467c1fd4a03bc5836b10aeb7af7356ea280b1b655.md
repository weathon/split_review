## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DriveTransformer, a unified Transformer framework for end-to-end autonomous driving with three key features: task parallelism, sparse representation, and streaming processing. It uses three unified operations (task self-attention, sensor cross-attention, temporal cross-attention) to reduce system complexity and improve training stability. Experiments on Bench2Drive (closed-loop) and nuScenes (open-loop) show competitive or state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths
- **Novel architecture design:** Proposes a unified framework combining sparse representation with parallelized task processing, distinct from existing methods. The design where all modules interact with each other should capture complicated spatio-temporal dependencies among modules. (R1, R2)
- **Simple and effective:** A vanilla transformer unifies sensor tokens and task queries; the simple design enables scaling up to achieve better performance. (R3)
- **Potential robustness:** Sensor cross-attention and temporal cross-attention may, in theory, improve generalization with sensor noise or failures. (R1)
- **Quality and clarity:** Each module is reasonably designed and effectively addresses existing problems. The paper is clearly structured and figures are easy to follow. (R2, R3)
- **Significance:** Provides new directions and ideas for autonomous driving research. (R2)
- **Closed‑loop performance:** One reviewer found the closed-loop results remarkable, surpassing previous methods by a large margin. (Note: another reviewer described closed-loop metrics as mixed; see Weaknesses.) (R3 vs. R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation and limited benefit over modular architectures:** Making all tasks interact ignores the benefits of modular end‑to‑end architectures (debugging, maintainability). The performance gains over modular baselines are not substantial enough to justify this design. Open‑loop results are not considerably better, closed‑loop metrics are mixed, and latency is comparable. (R1)
- **Insufficient originality:** The task‑parallel concept is not entirely new (ParaDrive), the sparse representation follows PETR & PETRv2, and the streaming process lacks strong originality. (R2)
- **Missing comparisons with similar methods:** There are no comparisons with sparse‑based methods (e.g., Sparsedrive, SparseAD) or other task‑parallel methods (e.g., ParaDrive in closed‑loop). Only open‑loop comparisons with ParaDrive are shown. (R1, R2)
- **No results for detection, prediction, or mapping:** While planning is primary, showing related task results would make the method more convincing. (R1)
- **No evidence for the claimed training‑stability improvement:** The paper states that BEV Sequential Paradigm causes bad training stability, but provides no supporting comparison between parallel and sequential methods on cumulative errors or training stability. (R2, R3)
- **Potential shortcut in open-loop experiments:** The open‑loop experiment on nuScenes should exclude ego‑status information to prevent short‑cut learning. (R3)
- **Selection bias concern for Dev10 subset:** The criterion for selecting 10 scenes out of 220 for Dev10 is not justified, making results prone to selection bias. (R1)
- **Naming and terminology issue:** “Task parallelism” is incorrectly used—what is parallel? (R1)
- **Streaming process vs. BEV temporal fusion:** The streaming process maintains a FIFO queue, but traditional BEV temporal fusion also maintains fixed‑length BEV features; the paper does not clarify what is special about its streaming process. (R2)
- **Scaling ability not fully explored:** The largest model used is 0.6B; performance seems not saturated—could larger models or the full Bench2Drive dataset yield further gains? (R2, R3)
- **Typographical errors:** The manuscript contains many typos and needs a grammar/spell check. (R1)
- **Missing clarifications:** Figure 1 does not explain why the training stability of design (b) is poor, nor why the task relation in (d) is acceptable compared to (c). (R2)
- **Table reference error:** Table 6c and Table 8 show robustness gains; it should be clarified whether Table 6c should be Table 7. (R2)
