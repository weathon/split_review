## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a two-stage lung nodule segmentation pipeline for CT scans: Stage 1 uses a custom Deformable Detection Transformer (Deformable-DETR) with Focal Loss for region proposal; Stage 2 refines bounding boxes into segmentation masks via a fine-tuned variant of Meta’s Segment Anything Model (SAM, with MedSAM adaptation). A 7.5 mm Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) preprocessing step enhances spatial context. The model reports a 92.4% Dice coefficient, 95.2% sensitivity, and 93.2% precision on the LUNA16 dataset (nodule sparsity 5%). The four reviewers rated the paper 3, 3, 3, and 1 (minority reviewer much more negative), with confidence scores all 5. They agree the MIP usage is notable and the two-stage framework addresses class imbalance, but raise major concerns about lack of novelty (assemblage of established components), missing ablation studies, insufficient evaluation (single dataset, no subclass analysis, no qualitative results), and presentation issues. The minority reviewer (rating 1) specifically cites a critical lack of technical insight and missing comparisons to prior work.

### Strengths
- Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP, 7.5 mm, five slices) is a thoughtful preprocessing step that improves differentiation between nodules, bronchioles, and vessels; it mirrors clinical practice used by radiologists (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Framing lung tumor segmentation as anomaly detection and adopting a two-stage pipeline (Deformable-DETR → SAM) offers a logical, straightforward structure for handling sparse nodules and class imbalance (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with a methodology that is accessible and well-explained (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Quantitative results (92.4% Dice, 95.2% sensitivity, 93.2% precision; 94.2% F1 for bounding boxes) demonstrate strong performance on a modified LUNA16 dataset (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and technical contribution:** The deep learning components (Deformable-DETR, SAM, MedSAM, Focal Loss) are well-established; the framework resembles an assemblage of existing methods without clear innovation or adaptation. The ROI-based segmentation approach is conventional (Reviewers 1, 2, 4; minority reviewer 4 states the work “lacks novel insights” and questions “what is the technical insight of this work?”).
- **Missing ablation studies:** No ablation is provided to isolate the contribution of each component (e.g., Deformable-DETR, Focal Loss, SAM, MIP). It is unclear which stage drives the most improvement or how performance compares to the original SAM/MedSAM baseline. This limits insight into the design’s effectiveness (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Insufficient evaluation and validation:**
  - Only one dataset (LUNA16) is used; external validation on additional lung datasets is absent, raising concerns about generalizability and overfitting (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - No subclass analysis (e.g., subsolid/GGO nodules, nodules near thoracic wall, nodules <10 mm). All preprocessing and modeling are performed in 2D, which may be inadequate for nodules adjacent to the thoracic wall; thicker MIP slices may reduce visibility for GGO/sub-solid nodules (Reviewer 2).
  - No qualitative results (e.g., visual segmentation comparisons) are provided to complement the quantitative evaluation (Reviewer 3).
  - The accuracy of the Otsu lung segmentation step in Figure 1-b is not reported (e.g., DSC), leaving its quality unverified (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper lacks discussion of the learning paradigm (end-to-end vs. not) and its merits/limitations (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons to prior work:** The paper does not compare or discuss relevant prior methods, e.g., the IEEE TMI paper “Closing the Gap between Deep Neural Network Modeling and Biomedical Decision-Making Metrics in Segmentation via Adaptive Loss Functions” (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of mathematical explanation and proof of concept:** The encoding/decoding process for feature maps across the two stages is described without detailed mathematical formulations, making it unclear how effectively characteristics are captured and retained (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and formatting issues:** Table 2 extends beyond the page margin, citations contain errors/inconsistencies, and there are grammatical errors that hinder readability (Reviewer 3).
- **Methodological clarification needed:** The generation of 7.5 mm MIP from 1 mm isocubic resolution using five slices (Figure 1-c) requires clearer explanation. A histogram analysis of nodules under 10 mm from the LIDC dataset is recommended (Reviewer 2).
