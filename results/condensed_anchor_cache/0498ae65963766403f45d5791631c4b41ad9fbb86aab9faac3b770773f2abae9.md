## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Warm Diffusion, a unified Blur-Noise Mixture Diffusion Model (BNMD) that bridges hot diffusion (noise-only) and cold diffusion (blur-only). The authors argue that noise prediction provides good diversity and data manifold coverage, while blur prediction offers better spectral dependency and convergence. The Blur-to-Noise Ratio (BNR) is introduced to analyze the trade-off. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, FFHQ, and LSUN-church. While the idea is interesting, weaknesses include unclear motivation, missing large-scale datasets, and potential optimization difficulties. One reviewer (score 5) was notably less positive, stating that the 1-2 point FID improvement does not yield noticeable visual quality and that results lag far behind FLUX, questioning the paper’s impact.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written (Reviewer 1).
- The concept of combining blur and noise in a unified diffusion framework is impressive/interesting (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). It exploits spectral dependencies while preserving the data manifold (Reviewer 2).
- The analysis of the limitations of hot and cold diffusion and the derivation of a unified architecture are insightful (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 notes that supporting evidence or visualization for these claims is missing (this point is also listed as a weakness below).
- The introduction of the Blur-to-Noise Ratio (BNR) enables better analysis of diffusion models (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 states that the BNR analysis is clearly discussed.
- Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments on CIFAR-10, FFHQ, and LSUN-church (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Comparisons with state-of-the-art methods are provided (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in image generation (Reviewer 3). Results on the conducted small-scale datasets are promising (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1.  **Insufficient Visual Quality Improvement (Minority Negative View)** – Reviewer 1 (score 5) states that an improvement of 1-2 points in FID does not result in any noticeable change in visual effects. The actual visual quality is still much worse than current generative models (e.g., FLUX), leaving no strong motivation to accept the paper. This reviewer expects results that are sufficiently stunning or impactful.
2.  **Unclear Motivation and Contradictory Evidence** – The motivation for introducing blur is not clearly presented in the introduction; the exact necessity is not given (Reviewer 2). Figure 5 shows that lower BNR yields better FID, which seems to suggest that “blur” does not help improve generated image quality (Reviewer 2).
3.  **Unclear Definition of “Spectral Dependency”** – The term “spectral dependency” is not clearly defined. It is unclear what the “strong correlation” between high-frequency and low-frequency structures refers to (Reviewer 2, Q1). Figure 3 does not adequately explain why “shifting more responsibility to the deblurring task” means “effectively utilizing the spectral dependency of images” (Reviewer 2, Q1).
4.  **Insufficient Figure Captions** – Figure 1 lacks sufficient explanation: the meaning of different-sized circles in the left chart, the significance of “Data manifold (indexed by noise level),” and the depicted images are unclear (Reviewer 3, Q1). A more detailed caption is requested (Reviewer 3).
5.  **Sampling Initialization from Eq. (4)** – Algorithm 2 starts sampling from a zero-mean Gaussian distribution. It is unclear how this can be revealed from Eq. (4) at time T (Reviewer 2, Q2).
6.  **Parameterization Deviations** – Table 5 shows large deviations among results produced by different parameterizations. More explanation is needed (Reviewer 2).
7.  **Confusing Training Target and Optimization Difficulty** – The training target decomposes into deblurring and denoising parts. The deblurring part predicts the residual (difference between clean image and blurred image), which is not traditional deblurring; the network must simultaneously model the distribution of blur and residual, and the blur level also varies per timestep. This may make distribution modeling significantly harder than simply predicting noise or clean data, especially at large data scales (Reviewer 4, also listed as a question). The network has to learn a complex mapping from noisy inputs to both blurry versions and high-frequency residuals, which could lead to optimization challenges (Reviewer 4).
8.  **Missing Large-Scale Dataset Experiments** – Experimental results on large-scale datasets such as ImageNet are absent. Since recent state-of-the-art diffusion models are typically evaluated on ImageNet and larger datasets, the actual performance and superiority of this method are ambiguous (Reviewer 4). Generalizability to complex and diverse datasets is unclear (Reviewer 4).
9.  **Need for Additional Baseline Architecture** – The paper uses improved DDPM++/NCSN++. Experimenting with other baselines, such as the original DDPM [1], would help demonstrate the generalizability of the method beyond specific model improvements (Reviewer 3, also listed as a question). Reviewer 3 explicitly requests experimental results using DDPM as a baseline, showing performance improvements compared to the original model and alternative approaches.
10. **Missing Supporting Evidence for Claims about Hot/Cold Diffusion** – The claims about the limitations of hot and cold diffusion are insightful, but supporting evidence or visualization to verify these claims is missing (Reviewer 4, noted as a suggestion in the strength).
11. **Question on BNR Timestep Dependence** – Are the noise level and blur level constant for all timesteps, or does the model actually have a sequence (a_1,…,a_T) and (b_1,…,b_T) for each timestep? (Reviewer 2, Q2).
