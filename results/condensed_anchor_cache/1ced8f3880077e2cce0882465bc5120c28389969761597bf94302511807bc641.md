## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents PUMA, a 3PC (semi-honest, honest majority) protocol for secure inference of Transformer models, focusing on LLaMA-7B. It introduces approximations for GeLU and softmax, and implements embedding and layer normalization in MPC. The authors claim a 2× speedup over MPCformer and that LLaMA-7B can be evaluated in about five minutes for generating one token from four input tokens. Reviewers highlight strengths in end-to-end secure inference, simple yet effective GeLU approximations, extensive evaluations, and timely focus. Weaknesses center on limited novelty, missing ablated speedup analysis, insufficient comparison of the GeLU approximation against existing MPC-friendly approximations, unclear contributions for softmax/embedding/layer normalization, overclaimed experimental performance (five minutes only for tiny inputs), and lack of evaluation for realistic input lengths or multi-token generation. Reviewers diverged: one (score 3) considered the contributions minimal and the claims overstated, whereas two others (score 6) appreciated the practical speedups and approximations.

### Strengths
- End-to-end secure inference framework for large language models (LLaMA-7B) using 3PC, demonstrating feasibility of private inference at this scale.
- New polynomial approximation for GeLU that is simple yet effective, and may serve as a useful component for future MPC work.
- Achieves about 2× speedup over MPCformer (ICLR 2023) while preserving accuracy similar to plaintext models without fine-tuning.
- Effectively brings together recent SOTA MPC techniques and supplements them with custom gadgets. The paper is well laid out and easy to follow.
- Extensive evaluations showing the protocol runs on a model with 7B parameters, a timely step towards practical private LLM inference.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The protocols are largely straightforward adaptations of prior MPC work. The contributions are incremental, with no novel design or construction. One reviewer (score 3) considered this the primary limitation; others also noted the incremental nature.
- **Missing ablated speedup analysis**: The claimed 2× speedup over MPCformer is not dissected by component (GeLU, softmax, embedding, layer normalization). Without such a breakdown, it is unclear whether the advantage stems from the proposed approximations or simply from using Replicated Secret Sharing (RSS) as the underlying protocol.
- **Insufficient comparison for GeLU approximation**: The piecewise polynomial approximation is not compared against existing MPC-friendly alternatives (e.g., minimax polynomial approximations, rational function approximations) in terms of computational/communication cost and accuracy. The potential impact of its approximation error on large models like LLaMA-7B is not adequately justified, especially given that the error appears relatively large.
- **Unclear contributions for softmax, embedding, and layer normalization**: The paper does not discuss the specific challenges or novel optimizations introduced for these components in the MPC setting. It is unclear whether they go beyond a straightforward combination of existing protocols, making it hard to discern their novelty.
- **Overclaimed experimental performance**: The title claim “Secure Inference of LLaMA-7B in Five Minutes” is misleading because it only applies to very short input (4 tokens) and generating a single token. For common input lengths (e.g., 128 tokens) or multiple token generation, the cost is likely much higher, and no such results are reported. The practical relevance of generating one token in five minutes under a semi-honest, honest majority 3PC model is questionable for near-term deployment.
- **Missing evaluation for realistic settings**: No experiments are reported for longer input sequences or multiple output tokens, leaving open whether the framework can scale to practical use cases. One reviewer explicitly asked about the cost for a 128-token input, and another questioned any plausible near-term application.
