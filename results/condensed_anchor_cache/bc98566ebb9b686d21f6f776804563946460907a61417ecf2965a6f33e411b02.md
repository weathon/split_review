## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes low-coherence subspace projection (LcSP) as a relaxation of orthogonal projection methods (e.g., GPM) for continual learning. To escape the capacity degradation of orthogonal subspaces when task sequences grow long, the method optimizes projection matrices on the oblique manifold to minimize inter-task and intra-task coherence. Experiments are conducted on Permuted MNIST, Rotated MNIST, Split CIFAR-100, and Split miniImageNet. The paper includes theoretical analysis (Lemmas 1–2) of stability and plasticity.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear and well-founded: orthogonal projection methods limit the learning capacity on long task sequences, and low-coherence projection offers a reasonable relaxation (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Experimental results show that LcSP outperforms GPM and most baselines; compared to TRGP its accuracy is slightly lower but it is more efficient (Reviewer 2). Figures 1 and 2 clearly illustrate the advantage on long task sequences (Reviewer 2).
- The paper attempts to provide theoretical guarantees for stability and plasticity, which adds credibility to the approach (Reviewer 2). (Reviewer 1 however finds the limits and assumptions behind low-coherence forgetting prevention insufficiently analyzed – see Weaknesses.)
- The method is plug-and-play and could be combined with other continual learning methods (Reviewer 1).  
- **Minority positive note:** Two reviewers (2 and 3) rated the contribution 3 (good soundness) and 3 (good presentation for Reviewer 3) and found the idea novel enough; one reviewer (1) rated the paper lower (3) with stronger concerns about incremental novelty, clarity, and experiment scope.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and prior work:** The idea of relaxing the orthogonal constraint on the gradient subspace via an oblique manifold or weighted bases has been explored before. For example, Restricted Orthogonal Gradient Projection (2023) and Scaled Gradient Projection (AAAI 2023) propose similar relaxations (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also considers the contribution incremental, as the switch from Riemannian to Oblique manifold does not bring deep new insight.
- **Theoretical weakness:** It remains unclear in principle why low-coherence subspaces prevent catastrophic forgetting as well as orthogonal subspaces. The limits and assumptions of the method are not analyzed or discussed (Reviewer 1). The theoretical results are not written clearly: Lemma 1 appears to state a condition under which forgetting is overcome, but the proof then shows that LcSP satisfies that condition; the second proof lacks a clear claim, and intuitive explanations are missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Restricted to task-incremental learning:** The method and experiments require task identifiers at both training and test time (task-incremental setting). Although the paper claims the method can be applied to class-incremental learning, no experiments validate this claim (Reviewers 1,3). The experimental settings (backbones, datasets, etc.) are not clearly described (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and presentation errors:** The paper contains many typographical errors (e.g., “Single Value Decomposition” for “Singular Value Decomposition”) and inconsistent table references (Table 3 is referenced in the main text but appears only in the appendix). Several references lack the publication venue (journal/conference). These errors undermine the perceived reliability of the experiments (Reviewer 2). One reviewer (1) found the paper well-written aside from a few areas, while another (2) judged the presentation poor (rating 1).
- **Comparison inconsistency:** Reviewer 1 questions why the reported results for some compared methods (e.g., ORTHOG-SUBSPACE, Chaudhry et al. 2020) differ from their original papers, given that the network backbone, datasets, and settings are stated to be the same.
- **Technical contribution:** Optimization on the oblique manifold is a well-studied technique, so the technical novelty is limited (Reviewer 3).
