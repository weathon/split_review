## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces RL2Grid, a benchmark for RL in power grid operations built on the Grid2Op simulator. It defines standardized state/action spaces and reward functions, and provides tasks of increasing difficulty for topology optimization and re-dispatching. The authors evaluate out-of-the-box implementations of DQN, PPO, SAC, and hybrid heuristics. Reviewer 1 (score 5) found the benchmark valuable for advancing real-world RL, while the other three reviewers (score 3 each) saw limited novelty, insufficient analysis, and missing comparisons, leading to a largely critical assessment.

### Strengths

- **Real-world problem and practical motivation for RL:** The benchmark addresses a complex, realistic setting that is essential for driving practical RL research (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Well-structured framework:** Integration of Grid2Op with Gym and CleanRL provides a standardized interface that facilitates comparison and reproducibility (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Clear introduction of a complex environment:** The paper presents the power grid domain accessibly for non-experts (Reviewer 1).
- **Curriculum-learning‑like difficulty levels:** The creation of increasingly challenging subsets of the topological action space is a relevant contribution that can foster more meaningful domain research (Reviewer 4).
- **Inclusion of heuristic baselines and carbon impact consideration:** Using heuristics as fall‑back policies is realistic, and the authors explicitly considered the carbon footprint of their experiments (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution:** The work largely reuses existing components from Grid2Op, Gym, and CleanRL. The main claimed contribution is standardization of state/action spaces and rewards, but the paper does not explain what was changed from the original Grid2Op environment, why the specific definitions were chosen, or how the framework improves upon prior scenarios (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4). “The evaluation of state-of-the-art RL algorithms is not sufficient to build a benchmarking framework” (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete baselines and missing comparisons:**
    - No algorithms from the L2RPN competitions (e.g., “WINNING THE L2RPN CHALLENGE: POWER GRID MANAGEMENT VIA SEMI-MARKOV AFTERSTATE ACTOR-CRITIC”) are included for comparison (Reviewer 1, 3).
    - Non‑RL baselines such as random or simple rule‑based strategies are absent, making it impossible to assess whether the environment actually requires RL or if simpler methods already perform well (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- **Insufficient analysis of results:** The paper only reports survival rates and performance tables without explaining why specific algorithms (e.g., SAC, DQN) underperform relative to PPO, or providing insight into agent behaviour and failure modes (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).
- **Incomplete and unclear MDP formulation:**
    - The state space is presented as a list of variables without explaining their meaning or ranges (appendices are insufficient) (Reviewer 2).
    - The reward function (Appendix C.2) is not fully defined – e.g., how \(R_{cost}\) and \(R_{topology}\) are computed is missing (Reviewer 2).
    - Formalization of the MDP is lacking; line 89 incorrectly assumes finite state/action spaces, while re‑dispatching uses a continuous action space (Reviewer 4).
    - The initial state distribution is described as uniform (line 90), which is unrealistic for real grid operations (Reviewer 4).
- **Key methodological details are missing or unclear:**
    - No mathematical definition of the primary metric “survival rate” is given; how is it exactly computed? (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 1 Q1).
    - The process of fractioning the action space to create difficulty levels is not explained; it is not enough to say “sampled uniformly” because sequential actions and the Markov property affect the resulting tasks (Reviewer 4).
    - Termination conditions are not specified in Section 3.1 (Reviewer 1 Q3).
    - The time step length and the distribution governing changes in generators/loads are not described (Reviewer 1 Q4, Reviewer 2 Q).
    - The discount factor \(\gamma\) is treated as a tunable hyperparameter (Appendix F), but it is part of the problem definition, not something to tune (Reviewer 2).
    - No seeds are reported for reproducibility (line 322) (Reviewer 4).
- **Inadequate scope and presentation:**
    - The paper claims 39 tasks but only presents a few; an analysis of all tasks is expected (Reviewer 2).
    - Basic concepts (Section 2.1) and future work (Section 7) occupy space that should be used for a proper MDP description, plots, and performance insights (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
    - Only 48‑hour episodes are used; longer scenarios (e.g., seasonal, weekday/weekend) are needed to assess realism (Reviewer 2).
- **The conclusion seems to pre‑expect RL failure:** “Model-free methods are expected to struggle” contradicts the purpose of a benchmark; stronger hyperparameter tuning or algorithm adaptation could have yielded better results (Reviewer 4).

**Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 4) gave a more positive overall assessment, stating that the paper “introduces a real-world problem for RL, which is essential for advancing practical RL applications” and praised its accessibility. The other three reviewers (score 3, confidence 4) were uniformly critical, emphasizing the lack of novelty, missing comparisons, and insufficient analysis.
