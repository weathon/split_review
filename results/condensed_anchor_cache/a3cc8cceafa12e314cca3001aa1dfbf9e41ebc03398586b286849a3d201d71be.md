## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces GoLLIE, a large language model fine-tuned to follow annotation guidelines for zero-shot information extraction (IE), unifying tasks like NER, EE, and EAE via a Python code-based representation. It uses regularization and is evaluated on 19 datasets. Reviewers generally find the idea compelling and the results impressive, but raise significant concerns about the validity of the zero-shot claim due to label space overlap, insufficient validation of the code-style prompt, missing ablation studies, and experimental gaps (e.g., outdated baselines, lack of prompt sensitivity analysis). One reviewer (Reviewer 4, rating 8) is more positive, emphasizing the thoroughness and contribution to the IE-LLM gap, while another (Reviewer 3, rating 5) is more critical of novelty and experimental completeness.

### Strengths

- **Impressive performance and valuable pre-trained model**: The results are strong and the GoLLIE model is a useful resource (Reviewer 1).
- **Intuitive and compelling idea**: Using annotation guidelines to boost zero-shot IE is a well-motivated approach (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 highlights that LLMs have underperformed in IE and GoLLIE addresses this gap.
- **Experimental results properly demonstrate effectiveness**: The method outperforms baselines, especially in zero-shot settings (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 notes thorough experiments across diverse IE tasks.
- **Clear, well-written presentation**: The paper is easy to follow with few typos (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Reviewer 3 specifically praises the error analysis.
- **Detailed error analysis provides valuable insights**: The study methodically identifies issues such as label ambiguity, conflicts between fine‑grained and coarse entities, and strong label preconceptions, offering a roadmap for future work (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also commends the extensive error analysis.
- **Significant prompt engineering contribution**: Crafting prompts for intricate IE tasks and demonstrating that the model follows complex instructions represents substantial domain expertise and effort (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 notes this as a novel solution to a long‑standing challenge.

### Weaknesses

- **Label space overlap undermines zero‑shot claim**: Training and testing datasets (e.g., RAMS vs. WikiEvents) share role labels (e.g., *transporter*, *vehicle*, *place*) and entity types. The model may leverage learned concepts rather than generalizing to truly unseen labels. A more rigorous evaluation with entirely disjoint label spaces is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Outdated experimental comparisons**: The reported state‑of‑the‑art numbers in Table 2 are no longer current (Reviewer 1). More recent baselines (USM, InstructUIE, GPT‑4) should be included; methods like UIE are outdated (Reviewer 3).
- **Unjustified code‑style prompting**: The motivation for using Python code over natural language (NL) is not empirically validated. LLMs pretrained on code may lack NL understanding crucial for IE; the advantage may only be easier output parsing. No ablation compares code‑style vs. NL prompts with guidelines (Reviewers 2,3). Prompt sensitivity to varying definitions, code structures, and comment styles is unexplored (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation studies**:  
  - Effect of class order shuffling and guideline paraphrasing (Reviewer 2).  
  - Impact of training dataset diversity on the ability to follow unseen guidelines (Reviewer 3).  
  - Sampling strategy for datasets of different scales (Reviewer 3).  
  - Empirical comparison of QLoRA vs. full‑model fine‑tuning (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited novelty**: Similar zero‑shot entity typing approaches using type descriptions from Wikipedia have been proposed (Reviewer 3). Pre‑LLM work on label descriptions and verbalization (e.g., zero‑shot RE via reading comprehension, fine‑grained entity typing, label verbalization for relation extraction) is omitted (Reviewer 4).
- **Code‑based prompting may hinder usability**: Users unfamiliar with coding may find the Python‑style prompt format inaccessible, narrowing real‑world applicability (Reviewer 3).
- **Overstatement of “annotation guideline” usage**: The paper only uses label name, description, and few‑shot examples, not the full, complex guidelines curated by linguists (e.g., TACRED slot filling rules like “GPEs below the city level are not valid fillers”). This may not capture true guideline understanding (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 asks whether more fine‑grained guidelines beyond basic definitions were considered.
- **Missing methodological details**:  
  - Number of samples used to fine‑tune GoLLIE (Reviewer 2).  
  - Human/domain‑expert effort required to craft the prompts (Reviewer 4).  
  - How prompt length varies with many labels (e.g., over 1000 entity types) and associated efficiency concerns (Reviewers 3,4).  
  - Handling of dataset imbalance during training (Reviewer 3).
- **Small formatting issues**: Use of quotes (should be `` instead of ''); Table 6 would be clearer with absolute numbers rather than percentages (Reviewer 4).
