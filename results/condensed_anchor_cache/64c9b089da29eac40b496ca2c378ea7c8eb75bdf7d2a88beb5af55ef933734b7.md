## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper derives refined generalization bounds for the Deep Ritz Method (DRM) and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) applied to linear elliptic PDEs. For DRM, the authors consider Poisson and static Schrödinger equations on the d‑dimensional unit hypercube with Neumann boundary conditions. For PINNs, they consider general linear second‑order elliptic PDEs with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The analysis uses localization techniques and local Rademacher complexity in a multi‑task learning framework, assuming solutions lie in either Barron or Sobolev spaces. The paper claims sharper bounds compared to prior work (Lu et al., 2021c [1]; Lu et al., 2021 [2]) and provides theoretical results showing that functions in certain Barron spaces can be well approximated in the H¹ norm by two‑hidden‑layer ReLU MLPs.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a rigorous mathematical analysis of two popular deep learning methods for PDEs (DRM and PINNs), which is valuable for understanding their theoretical properties.
- For DRM, sharper generalization bounds are obtained for the Poisson and Schrödinger equations with Neumann boundary conditions under the assumption that solutions lie in Barron or Sobolev spaces.
- For PINNs, the generalization error is bounded in the H^{1/2} norm for linear second‑order elliptic equations, using a multi‑task learning perspective.
- The paper demonstrates that the function space B² can be approximated in H¹ norm by two‑hidden‑layer ReLU networks with controlled weights, leading to better bounds than in prior work [1] when the solution is in a Barron space.
- The presentation is clear, includes a thorough review of related work on Rademacher complexity variants, and provides proofs of essential lemmas.
- The analysis fits within the ongoing effort to build mathematical foundations for PINNs and related methods.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of PDEs**: The PDEs considered (Poisson and Schrödinger on the unit hypercube with Neumann/Dirichlet conditions) are very simple. Classical methods (FEM, FDM, etc.) solve them faster and more accurately. The paper does not discuss extending the theory to more complex or practically relevant PDEs.
- **No numerical experiments**: The paper lacks empirical validation of the theoretical bounds.
- **Lack of connection between DRM and PINN analyses**: The two methods are studied with different mathematical tools for different PDEs, and no clear link is drawn between them, leading to confusion about the paper’s main message. (A minority reviewer – Reviewer 2 – emphasized this point.)
- **Overly strong assumptions on solution regularity**: The assumption that PDE solutions lie in Barron space is too restrictive. The Barron space is designed for two‑layer MLP approximation and is too narrow to represent general PDE solutions. The paper does not justify why solutions to the specific PDEs considered would belong to such a constrained space. (This concern was most strongly raised by Reviewer 2, who rated the paper lower.)
- **Focus on loss minimization rather than solution accuracy**: The goal of DRM and PINNs is to find the true PDE solution, not just minimize empirical loss. Because the loss functions contain unbounded derivative operators, a small loss difference does not guarantee that the functions are close to the true solution. The paper addresses this only briefly in equations (16) and (30) and under very restrictive assumptions. A more rigorous analysis of the relationship between loss minimization and convergence to the true solution is needed, especially with unbounded operators. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limitations of activation function choice**: The analysis uses ReLU^k (RePU) for convenience, but practical networks are deeper and use different activation functions. RePU’s power increases with depth, causing floating‑point precision issues. The paper does not discuss how results change with other activation functions. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing or unclear comparison to prior work [2] and Weinan E et al.**: The paper does not clearly delineate its contributions relative to [2] (Lu et al., 2021). Specific novelties are unclear; e.g., Proposition 1 requires an extra assumption (∫f dx=0) compared to [2]. Remark 2’s claimed rate improvement from n^{-(2k-2)/(d+2k-4)} to n^{-(2k-2)/(d+2k-2)} is suspect (the second rate is larger), and the same rate (up to log factors) was already achieved in [2] via the peeling method. For PINN, the paper claims to avoid strong convexity (unlike [2]), but Lemma 17 serves a similar role as Theorem B.2 in [2], and the convergence rate is the same. The norm used (H^{1/2} vs H¹ in [2]) changes the interpretation; the paper does not provide intuition. Also, [2] provides lower bounds certifying statistical optimality; the paper does not discuss whether bounds (13) and (27) are optimal for Barron spaces. The paper does not compare with other mathematical frameworks (Weinan E et al., Deep Double Ritz Method (Uriiarte et al., CMAME 2023)).
- **Inconsistency in claimed results**: The abstract and introduction claim sharper generalization bounds for both Poisson and Schrödinger equations via DRM, but Theorem 3 only covers the Poisson equation; results for Schrödinger appear to be missing from the main theorems. (Reviewer 3)
- **Omission of dimension-dependent constant**: Equation (16) combines Proposition 1 and Theorem 3 but appears to omit the Poincaré constant from Proposition 1, which is dimension‑dependent. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of discussion of limitations**: The paper does not address its own limitations, such as the restrictiveness of assumptions or practical applicability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation issues**: The “Related Works” section feels disjointed, making it hard to grasp core contributions and their relation to the literature. There are grammatical issues (e.g., line 40‑41). The paper could benefit from a broader literature review including works such as Sirignano & Spiliopoulos (2018), Han et al. (2018), Khoo et al. (2021), Zang et al. (2020), Chen et al. (2021), Li et al. (2020), Lu et al. (2021) (DeepONet), and the L‑HYDRA multi‑head PINN framework. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Missing comparison to other multi-task learning frameworks**: The paper does not compare its PINN analysis under the multi‑task learning perspective with other multi‑head PINN approaches (e.g., L‑HYDRA by Zou & Karniadakis). (Reviewer 4)
