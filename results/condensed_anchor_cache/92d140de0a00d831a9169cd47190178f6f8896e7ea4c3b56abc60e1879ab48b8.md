## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper presents MATRIX, a multi‑agent simulator for generating post‑training data (SFT and DPO). It uses 1000 agents based on real‑world human profiles and a structured communication mechanism inspired by social homophily to create realistic, diverse text‑based scenarios. The generated scenarios drive a scenario‑based instruction generator. Experiments fine‑tune LLaMA‑3‑8B with only 20,000 MATRIX‑generated instruction‑response pairs and show improvements over existing real and synthetic datasets on AlpacaEval2, Alpaca‑Hard, and domain‑specific tasks (coding, safety, multi‑turn dialogue). The paper also includes analyses of model scaling, scenario diversity, and data quality.

### Strengths

- **Interesting and promising idea**: grounding data synthesis in real‑world human profiles via multi‑agent simulation, going beyond PersonaHub by creating complex scenarios through agent interactions (Reviewer 1).
- **Comprehensive experiments** with many baselines (MagPie, WildChat, UltraFeedback, Orca) across both general benchmarks (AlpacaEval2, Alpaca‑Hard) and domain‑specific tasks (coding, safety, multi‑turn dialogue). Demonstrates robust data efficiency (Reviewer 2).
- **In‑depth analysis** including model scaling, scenario diversity, and data quality/complexity (Reviewer 2). The experimental section is detailed (Reviewer 3; note Reviewer 1 finds methods lacking specifics – see Weaknesses).
- **Clear motivation** (Reviewer 3).
- **Remarkable results**: only 20k synthetic pairs outperform Llama‑3‑8B‑Instruct (trained on >10M pairs) on AlpacaEval and Alpaca‑Hard, and outperform existing SFT/DPO datasets (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2; however, Reviewer 2 notes the comparison may be unfair – see Weaknesses).
- Methods section is thoroughly explained (Reviewer 3; contrast with Reviewer 1’s lack‑of‑specifics criticism).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of critical implementation details** (Reviewer 1): user profile sources and distribution, web data processing, LLM prompts for profiles and goals, meaning of “past actions” (Line 200), number of goals per agent, and the difference between “simple” (SFT) and “complex/specialized” (DPO) instructions (Lines 154‑155). Hurts reproducibility.
- **Scenario generation mechanism** (agents and modulators) is not clearly described; no concrete examples or specific communication protocols are provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Table 9 contradictions**: using specialized instructions for SFT and simple instructions for DPO yields best results (last row of Table 9), but the authors instead use simple SFT and specialized DPO without explanation (Reviewer 1).
- **Inconsistent domain evaluation setups**: unclear why different base models were chosen for coding, safety, and multi‑turn dialogue evaluations (Reviewer 1).
- **Knowledge distillation effect not discussed**: target responses are generated by Llama‑3‑8B‑Instruct, yet the MATRIX‑tuned model outperforms the teacher (Llama‑3‑8B‑Instruct) (Reviewer 1).
- **Unfair comparison with over 10M data points**: Llama‑3‑8B‑Instruct’s training covers capabilities (multilingual, self‑recognition, safety) not tested by AlpacaEval, making the 20k vs. 10M comparison misleading (Reviewer 2).
- **Potential GPT‑4 bias in evaluation**: AlpacaEval uses GPT‑4‑0314 as reference, and OpenAI models (model unspecified) appear to be used as instruction generators; improvements on AlpacaEval/Hard may be partially due to this bias. Domain‑specific gains are clearer; human evaluation and case studies are needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Data leakage check missing**: need to verify that generated instructions do not have high similarity with test‑set problems (e.g., HumanEval, MBPP) (Reviewer 2).
- **Need analysis of instruction importance**: which instructions drive improvement most? Are all data points equally influential? (Reviewer 2).
- **Structured communication mechanism**: Table 7 indicates it is the primary factor; further analysis (quality/difficulty of data from random vs. no communication) and a case study explaining its impact are required (Reviewer 2).
- **Code‑domain comparison unclear**: MATRIX‑Code vs. OpenCodeInterpreter – why are OpenCodeInterpreter results so low despite reported HumanEval 75.6 / MBPP 69.9 for CodeLlama‑7B? Fine‑tuning details need to be provided to assess fairness (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient novelty discussion**: role‑playing based multi‑agent data generation is widely used; more explicit differentiation from related work (e.g., AutoGen) is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited model evaluation**: only LLaMA‑3‑8B is tested; experiments on other model sizes would demonstrate robustness (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational cost of MATRIX framework not reported** (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure 4(a) anomaly**: increasing agents from 10 to 100 shows no improvement on Arena‑Hard; this phenomenon requires investigation (Reviewer 3).
