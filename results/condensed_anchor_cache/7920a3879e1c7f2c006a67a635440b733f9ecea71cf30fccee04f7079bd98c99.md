## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MMAD, claimed as the first benchmark for evaluating Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in industrial anomaly detection (IAD). It defines seven key subtasks (anomaly discrimination, defect classification, object classification, defect localization, object analysis, defect description, defect analysis) and generates a dataset of 39,672 questions from 8,366 industrial images sourced from existing seed datasets using a novel synthetic pipeline involving LLMs. Evaluations of multiple state-of-the-art MLLMs show that the best commercial model (GPT-4o) achieves 74.9% accuracy, which the authors note falls short of industrial requirements. The paper also explores two training-free performance enhancement strategies: Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) for domain knowledge and visual “Expert Agents” using highlights, contours, and bounding boxes. Additional analysis examines the usefulness of template images, model scale effects, and the impact of image count.

### Strengths
- **First benchmark targeting MLLMs in IAD:** Fills an important real-world evaluation gap with clear practical relevance for industrial quality inspection.
- **Comprehensive subtask coverage:** Seven subtasks cover diverse aspects of anomaly detection, contributing to task diversity and novelty.
- **Large-scale dataset:** 39,672 questions from 8,366 images enables fine-grained evaluation.
- **Thorough evaluation of multiple models:** Includes both commercial (e.g., GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini) and open-source MLLMs; specific model versions are listed (e.g., “gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18”).
- **Informative analysis:** Detailed investigation of template image usefulness (Table 3), model scale trends, number of images, and the surprising effectiveness of RAG.
- **Useful exploration of enhancement strategies:** RAG and expert model collaboration are practical for industrial practitioners deploying MLLMs.
- **Clarity and presentation:** The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and figures are well-constructed.
- **Minority positive reviewer:** One reviewer (Reviewer 3, rating 8, confidence 4) was notably more positive, emphasizing the originality, significance, and thoroughness of the evaluations. Other reviewers gave ratings of 6.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of human baseline and annotator details:** No human expert baseline is reported; it would be informative to compare MLLM performance to industrial experts. Details on annotator expertise, guidelines, and inter-annotator agreement are absent, reducing transparency and reproducibility.
- **Insufficient diversity analysis:** There is no systematic analysis of semantic diversity across questions and answers (e.g., cosine similarity, topic modeling). It is unclear whether LLM-generated questions follow only a few fixed templates or represent a genuinely wide range of scenarios, potentially limiting benchmark effectiveness.
- **Lack of in-depth error and qualitative analysis:** The paper does not classify types of model errors (e.g., misclassification vs. localization failures) or provide detailed qualitative failure examples. Ablation studies on dataset construction or enhancement strategies are missing.
- **Limited evaluation metrics:** Only multiple-choice accuracy is reported; recall, F1-score, and other metrics important for anomaly detection (where class imbalance is common) are omitted.
- **Multiple-choice format limitations:** The benchmark uses only multiple-choice questions. This format can be sensitive to option construction and number of choices, and does not capture open-ended or location-based outputs that are directly useful in production.
- **Missing frontier models:** Several recent strong MLLMs are not evaluated, such as Claude Sonnet 3.6, limiting the comprehensiveness of the comparison.
- **Contribution and novelty concerns:** The benchmark is generated primarily from existing seed datasets using LLM-based synthetic question generation, without introducing new methodologies or significant domain-specific optimizations. Two reviewers (R2 and R4) question the depth of the contribution to the general ML community, noting that industrial anomaly detection may not be fundamentally distinct from other visual QA tasks, and that the enhancement strategies are standard instantiations from broader fields. One reviewer (R4) argues that the claim “first-ever comprehensive” is misleading given the reliance on existing datasets.
- **Missing discussion on domain-specific challenges:** It is unclear how industrial anomaly detection tasks differ fundamentally from general visual QA, which would clarify the benchmark’s impact.  
- **Unanswered questions from reviewers that overlap with criticisms/requests**:
    - How does RAG with domain knowledge affect performance, especially as context windows grow? (R2)
    - Beyond visual highlights/contours/BBox, can expert information be provided via direct text or other modalities? (R2)
    - What specific mechanism links this dataset to real manufacturing improvements? (R3)  
    - Why is this called the “first-ever comprehensive” benchmark when it reuses existing datasets? (R4)
