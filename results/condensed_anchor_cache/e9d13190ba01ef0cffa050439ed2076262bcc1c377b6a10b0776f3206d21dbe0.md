## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a flexible residual binarization (FRB) method for image super-resolution (SR) to address the loss of high-frequency information and representation content distortion caused by binarization. FRB consists of two components: Second-order Residual Binarization (SRB), which binarizes both the primary weights and the reconstruction error to counter information loss, and Distillation-guided Binarization Training (DBT), which aligns feature content between binarized and full-precision networks using normalized attention. The method is applied to binarize both convolution-based (FRBC) and Transformer-based (FRBT) SR networks. Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate superior performance compared to existing binarized SR methods.

### Strengths
- The method is well-motivated: binarization compresses and speeds up SR models, but the loss of high-frequency features is critical for SR; the paper addresses this by residual binarization and distillation.
- SRB is efficient and effective: residual binarization adds negligible inference burden (since heads and tails are unquantized and the residual operates on weights, not activations) while significantly improving accuracy, and it maintains compatibility with XNOR-POPCNT instructions.
- FRB is structurally universal: it acts at the operator level without affecting overall architecture, making it applicable to both CNN and Transformer variants (FRBC and FRBT).
- DBT uses the full-precision counterpart with the same architecture, making the distillation flexible and general across architectures.
- Accuracy and efficiency results are outstanding: FRB achieves state-of-the-art performance among binarized SR methods by a convincing margin, and produces good visual effects.
- The paper is well-written, understandable, with well-presented figures and formulas (noted by multiple reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Fairness of comparisons and missing details**: The quantitative comparison is not fair because the adopted pipelines (SRResNet and SwinIR_S) were binarized without the proposed SRB and DBT components, but no counterpart under the same binarization setting is provided. Parameters, FLOPs, and FPS metrics are absent in Table 1. Dataset introductions (size, annotations) are missing.
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:
  - Ablation studies are insufficient: e.g., to prove SRB’s effectiveness, the residual binarization should be removed and compared with the complete model; SRB should also be tested as a plug-and-play module in other methods.
  - No quantitative evidence that residual binarization reduces binarization error.
  - The dense middle feature distillation in DBT is a widely used technique; the difference from general knowledge distillation is not reported.
- **Technical novelty and contribution**: Some reviewers note that residual binarization and knowledge distillation from full-precision to binarized models have been studied in other tasks, so the technical contribution is limited. The distillation method is not compared with previous knowledge distillation techniques to demonstrate superiority.
- **Missing comparisons with other compression methods**: No comparison with multi-bit quantization methods or other compression methods (e.g., distillation without binarization) to justify the advantages of 1-bit binarization despite larger performance degradation.
- **Overhead and extension of SRB**: Residual binarization inevitably introduces additional parameters; this overhead should be discussed. Whether higher-order binarization (order >2) could further improve performance, and what the optimal order is for accuracy-efficiency trade-off, is unexplored.
- **Deployment feasibility**: No discussion of whether FRB can be efficiently deployed on real hardware (e.g., ARM CPU using open-source libraries like Larq or daBNN); the paper only discusses FLOPs reduction, not actual acceleration on edge devices.
- **Motivation and analysis gaps**:
  - The two issues described in the introduction (insufficient high-frequency information and representation content gap) seem to describe the same problem (loss of high-frequency information), differing from the two motivations in the abstract.
  - The key challenges of binarization for low-level vision tasks vs. high-level tasks are not analyzed.
  - The motivation for using blockwise distillation granularity (rather than the more flexible layerwise distillation) is unclear; more analysis is needed.
- **Missing recent references**: References lack literature published in 2022 and 2023.
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with criticism** (included as weaknesses):
  - In SRB, values of the reconstruction error surpass the binarization; how to explain the effect when weight binarization is supposed to be primary?
  - What GPU was used for training?
  - What is the actual acceleration on real hardware?
  - Why not use layerwise distillation?
  - Can FRB be deployed on edge devices like ARM CPUs, and what is the performance?
  - What are the advantages of binarization over multi-bit quantization and other compression methods?
