## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes adversarial robustness from a data perspective, proposing a notion of “perceived curvature” of the data manifold as a measure of per-sample robustness. It shows that neural networks gain robustness more slowly on high-curvature (least robust) examples and suggests emphasizing these examples during training via oversampling and diffusion-based augmentation. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 report modest robustness gains (e.g., 0.2% on CIFAR-10 against AutoAttack) along with visualizations and ablation studies.

### Strengths
- Novel perspective on adversarial robustness through the lens of data manifold curvature (Reviewers 1, 3). One reviewer (score 5) particularly highlights the originality and significance of this direction.
- Elaborated theoretical developments supporting the proposed connection (Reviewer 1).
- Interesting application of diffusion models (Karras et al., Wang et al.) to investigate per-sample robustness (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive visualizations and plots demonstrating the relationship between per-sample robustness, sensitivity, and margin (Reviewer 2).
- Claims are backed by empirical results with multiple experiments (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer finds the writing generally good, though others disagree (Reviewer 4 vs. Reviewers 1, 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is very difficult to follow (Reviewer 1). The notion of “perceived curvature” is unclear and not sufficiently differentiated from the local Lipschitz constant (Reviewers 1, 2). The connection between curvature and robustness is not well explained, especially for Figures 2 and 3 (e.g., why pseudo-labeled data show different sensitivity patterns, why MDS and RTE exhibit inverted patterns) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 provides examples of confusing sentences (e.g., “the labels evoke the impression of disconnectedness…”). Reviewer 3 requests subcaptions for clarity.
- **Experimental details and reproducibility**: Insufficient details for reproducibility and interpretation (Reviewer 1). Specific omissions: definitions of “most/least robust elements” and “relative sensitivity” (Reviewer 3, Figures 1 and 2); what “validation set” refers to (Reviewer 3 notes odd usage); whether sensitivity is computed on a pre-trained model (Reviewer 3). Figure 1 is confusing regarding tracking of the same data points and epoch comparisons (Reviewers 2, 3). The diffusion model augmentation (Karras et al., Wang et al.) and the claim that it “connects regions of non-robust data to more prominent semantic changes” are not made precise (Reviewer 2). The explanation of Table 2 setup (e.g., “-v” meaning removal, validation accuracy based on v) is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Marginal improvement and lack of statistical significance**: The reported adversarial robustness improvement is very small (e.g., 0.2% on CIFAR-10 in Table 2), not convincing of practical impact (Reviewers 1, 3). The claim that this is equivalent to 800 epochs of training does not offset the small absolute gain. No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multiple-run statistics are provided, making reliability impossible to assess (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparison to prior work**: The paper does not discuss or compare with existing methods that emphasize vulnerable examples, such as instance-reweighting (Zhang et al., Geometry-aware Instance-reweighted Adversarial Training, ICLR 2021; Wang et al., Probabilistic Margins, NeurIPS 2021), subsampling (Zhang et al., Attacks Which Do Not Kill Training Make Adversarial Learning Stronger, ICML 2020), curriculum learning in adversarial training, or methods based on input space margins and decision boundary dynamics (Reviewers 2, 4). The relevance of the off-manifold hypothesis for adversarial examples (Stutz et al.) is not addressed (Reviewer 4). Without this context, novelty and significance are difficult to assess.
- **Contribution and significance**: While one reviewer (score 5) finds the perspective novel and significant, others argue that the basic observation that some examples are more vulnerable is well-known, and the perceived curvature measure does not offer a fundamentally new geometric insight beyond the local Lipschitz constant (Reviewer 2). The theoretical developments are not directly translated into practice (Reviewer 1). The paper does not demonstrate a clear advantage over existing techniques for improving robustness (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Reviewer questions indicating missing clarity or experiments**:
  - Reviewer 1 asks whether Eq. (1) reduces to looking at 1/distance to the nearest sample from a different class (given Euclidean distance and one-hot labels), and whether this matches the experimental practice—pointing to insufficient explanation of the curvature measure.
  - Reviewer 3 asks about “differently labelled duplicate images” in CIFAR-100 and the effect of excluding the 40 least robust elements.
  - Reviewer 3 asks about the difference between MDS and RTE mechanisms leading to inverted patterns in Figure 3.
  - Reviewer 4 asks how the off-manifold assumption relates to the findings.
  None of these are clarified in the paper.
