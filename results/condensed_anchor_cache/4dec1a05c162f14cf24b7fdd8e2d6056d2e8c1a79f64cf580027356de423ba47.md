## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Distillist-Generalist-Specialist (DGS), a three‑agent LLM‑based pipeline that iteratively compresses in‑context demonstrations to reduce token count without sacrificing predictive accuracy. Evaluated on GSM8K, BoolQ, and MultiRC using ChatGPT, ChatGLM, and AutoCompressor. Most reviewers (score 3) find the idea intuitive and well‑presented but raise significant concerns about cost trade‑offs, baselines, innovation, experimental rigor, and applicability. One reviewer (score 5) is considerably more positive, calling the pipeline interesting and the results promising.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow, with clear explanations and helpful case studies. (R1, R2, R3)
- The motivation (reducing prompt length to cut computational/financial burdens) is timely and relevant. (R2, R5)
- DGS is a simple, intuitive, and reasonable approach that considers essential aspects such as quality, relevance, and coherence. (R1, R2)
- The agent pipeline is novel and “LLM‑first”; the final results demonstrate practical value in compression ratio while maintaining performance. (R4, R5)
- The analysis of why distilled demonstrations sometimes perform better (exploiting natural language redundancy) is insightful. (R4)
- In contrast to the majority, one reviewer (R4) rates the work highly, praising the interesting pipeline and promising outcomes even under simple heuristics.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines & comparisons**: Only AutoCompressor is compared, but the related work mentions at least two other compression methods that should be included (R1). Additional baselines are missing: a zero‑shot (infinite compression) baseline (R5‑A), a simple “rewrite each QA pair concisely” baseline (R5‑F), and ablations of DGS without Generalist and without Specialist (R5‑F). Results for AutoCompressor on GSM8K are omitted (R5‑E). Comparison to demonstration selection approaches (e.g., aclanthology/2023.findings‑acl.273, arxiv/2211.04486, arxiv/2310.10707, arxiv/2302.05698, arxiv/2104.08786) is also needed (R1).
- **Cost‑effectiveness unclear**: DGS itself requires many iterative calls to ChatGPT, potentially negating the cost savings it claims. No detailed cost analysis (API calls, tokens, monetary cost) is provided (R1, R2, R4). It remains unclear whether the method reduces overall expenditure or merely shifts costs to the distillation phase (R2). The argument against scalable positional embeddings (fine‑tuning is once, DGS per task) is unfair (R5‑I).
- **Zero‑shot baseline shows no ICL benefit**: On BoolQ, 3‑shot accuracy (~60%) is similar to reported zero‑shot performance for GPT‑3. If in‑context examples provide no gain, then maintaining accuracy after compression is trivial (R5‑A). Table 1 also shows no improvement and even regression as the number of ICL examples increases on GSM8K, questioning whether DGS applies to tasks where ICL typically helps (R5‑B). The authors should test on standard tasks (e.g., SST‑2, AG News, RTE, MNLI) where more examples improve accuracy (R5‑B, R5‑C).
- **Limited model generality**: DGS is only tested with ChatGPT (closed‑source). Experiments with 1–3 open‑source LLMs are needed to assess sensitivity (R1). The method’s generalization to smaller models (e.g., GPT‑2) is untested (R4).
- **Lack of robustness assessment**: Only one randomly sampled ICL prompt is used per task; average results over multiple prompts (with variance) are required (R4). Confidence intervals or bootstrap estimates are missing from Tables 1–3; claims of “uniform” improvement (sometimes 0.1% gap) may be insignificant (R5‑D).
- **Punishment Score undefined and unreliable**: The “Punishment Score” lacks a clear mathematical definition—it is simply evaluated by ChatGPT, raising concerns about reliability (R3). The scoring system for the Generalist is misleading: the text says “more than 10 points deducted,” but a single violation already deducts 10 points; it should be “any violation triggers regeneration” (R5‑G). The Specialist prompt does not include the ground truth answer or the LLM prediction on the original long input, making its judgment harder (R5‑K). There is no separate verification of distilled answers (only answers from the distilled question are checked) (R5‑L).
- **Limited innovation**: Using LLMs to compress text is a known technique (e.g., langchain summarization). The main contribution may amount to proposing specific prompts for demonstration compression (R3). The claim that DGS exhibits an “emergent” feature is unsupported (R5‑P).
- **Applicability concerns**: For tasks involving causal reasoning, sarcasm, or emotional inference, compressing with LLMs likely loses critical information (R3). The method appears to rewrite demonstrations in a chain‑of‑thought style; if test questions are also compressed, any improvement could be due to question rephrasing rather than demonstration distillation (R3, R5‑Q). The iterative loop is unclear: when a score exceeds 90, demonstrations are sent back to the Distillist – how does the process terminate? (R5‑M). A random question from the training set is used instead of the demonstration’s own question, which is unexplained (R5‑N). The claim that DGS works “regardless of how demonstration and question are selected” is not demonstrated (R5‑O). Distilling QA pairs individually (instead of in batches) was not tried (R5‑J).
- **Randomness not controlled**: The experiments do not account for LLM randomness; temperature should be set to 0 for reproducibility (R2).
