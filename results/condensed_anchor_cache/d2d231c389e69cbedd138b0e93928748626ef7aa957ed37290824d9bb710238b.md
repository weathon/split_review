## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MvL-GNN, a multi-view graph neural network framework that integrates user-item interactions, social trust networks, and user-generated textual reviews for recommender systems. It uses LightGCN for user-item interaction embedding, a pre-trained language model (BERT) for encoding reviews, an attention mechanism to fuse trust and review information for users, and a meta-path-based item graph. Contrastive learning aligns representations across user-user, item-item, and user-item views. Experiments on Epinions, Yelp, and Ciao datasets compare against baselines without additional information, reporting improvements in Hit Ratio and NDCG.

### Strengths
- The majority of the paper is easy to follow and clearly structured; the proposed method is well described.
- Dataset and code are provided, ensuring reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed multi-view framework effectively integrates diverse information sources, including user-item interactions, trust networks, and review data (Reviewers 1, 5).
- The integration of pre-trained language models (BERT) for encoding textual reviews and contrastive learning to align heterogeneous views is noted as effective; experimental results show performance improvements on multiple real-world datasets (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 2 also finds the experiments demonstrate effectiveness of the proposed method.
- An ablation study is performed to evaluate the effect of each component (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The core components—LightGCN, attention mechanisms, meta-path-based item graphs, and contrastive learning—are well-explored in recent literature (references [1–4] cited by Reviewer 5). The paper does not sufficiently highlight fundamental differences or unique innovations (Reviewers 3, 4, 5). Reviewer 3 states that these techniques are several years old and should not be the main selling points; the work is considered too basic for ICLR.
- **Unfair baseline comparisons:** Most baselines do not incorporate review data or social trust information. Without providing these additional features to the baselines, it is impossible to tell whether improvements come from the added information or the multi-view design itself (Reviewers 1, 4). Comparison with backbone models that only use the user-item graph (e.g., LightGCN, NGCF) is missing (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Marginal performance gains and lack of statistical significance:** The improvements in Table 2 are small given the added complexity of multi-view data; no significance tests (e.g., p-value) are provided to validate that gains are not due to random variation (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- **Insufficient analysis of model behavior under varying sparsity levels:** There is no experiment on how the model performs when the user-item interaction graph is sparse, which is a main motivation for including additional data sources (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also notes that the motivation of using social network and reviews to ease sparsity is well studied in prior work (cold-start, transfer learning).
- **Missing efficiency and scalability analysis:** The paper lacks runtime comparisons or time complexity analysis versus baselines, and does not evaluate efficiency for large-scale datasets (Reviewer 3, 5). Reviewer 3 notes that a basic work like this should have advantages in time complexity but appears to lack them.
- **Data processing discrepancy for Epinions:** The number of nodes and edges reported in Table 1 is notably lower than in prior studies; the authors should detail the data processing approach (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of cost-benefit justification for including textual data:** The addition of raw textual reviews adds computational cost, yet no cost-benefit analysis is provided to justify it (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient analytical experiments:** Beyond the ablation study, there is a lack of experiments on the effectiveness of individual views, on the impact of different sparsity levels, and on comparative advantages over existing heterogeneous graph contrastive learning methods (e.g., HGCL) (Reviewers 4, 3). Reviewer 3 specifically asks for advantages over HGCL and whether the pre-trained language model was fine-tuned or used off-the-shelf (Transformers API).
- **Minor presentation issues:** There are several typos in the conclusion (Reviewer 1).

*Note: Some reviewers (2, 3) were more positive overall (score 5) than others (scores 3), but all raised significant weaknesses regarding novelty, baselines, and statistical rigor.*
