## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Hough Voting-based Spatial Prompt Learning (HoughSpaPL) and Hough Voting-based Semantic Prompt Learning (HoughSemPL) for Segment Anything Models (SAMs). It designs three types of voting mechanisms (inner, middle, outer region) to learn distinct spatial prompts for different subregions, which vote together to generate a segmentation mask. A similar voting mechanism is applied for semantic prompts. Experiments are conducted on Cityscapes, Mapillary, ADE20K, and ACDC datasets, using the SEEM model. Reviewers are split: two rate the paper 5, two rate it 3, with disagreement over novelty, experimental validation, and fairness.

### Strengths

1. **Creative application of Hough Voting to prompt learning** – The idea of voting-based prompt aggregation for spatial and semantic prompts is novel and interesting (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
2. **Clear ablation experiments** – Table 4 validates each proposed component’s contribution, and ablation studies are well-designed (Reviewers 1, 2).  
3. **Simple yet effective method** – The proposed approach achieves superior performance over existing prompt learning methods on multiple segmentation datasets (Reviewers 3, 4).  
4. **Good illustration of prompt roles** – Figure 3 clearly explains how different spatial prompts (inner, middle, outer) contribute (Reviewer 1).  
5. **Multi-focus spatial prompts align with segmentation tasks** – The design of complementary prompts for different subregions is conceptually reasonable (Reviewer 2).  
6. **Thorough empirical evaluation across diverse conditions** – Experiments cover multiple datasets, including adverse conditions (Reviewer 4).  

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited validation beyond SEEM** – All experiments use only SEEM, despite frequent references to “SAMs”. Generalization to original SAM, Semantic-SAM, SAM2, MobileSAM, EfficientSAM, etc. is unverified (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
2. **Unfair comparison with fewer prompts** – Figure 1 compares one prompt (original SAM) vs. three prompts (proposed). If original SAM is given three points, the effect may not be worse; this missing baseline undermines the claimed advantage (Reviewer 4).  
3. **Core design choice not sufficiently validated** – The subdivision into inner, middle, outer regions is presented as optimal, but no ablation explores alternative partitions (e.g., four quadrants). Thus the contribution of the specific voting scheme is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
4. **Missing comparisons with other efficient SAM tuning strategies** – No comparisons to LoRA, BOFT, hq-sam, partial finetuning, or token tuning (Reviewer 1). Also missing comparisons to default SAM prompt strategies like tuning a prompt encoder to predict bounding boxes/points (Reviewer 1).  
5. **Unfair prompt count comparison with SSprompt** – The method uses 3N spatial prompts (three times SSprompt). The paper does not control for total prompt number; additional experiments on influence of prompt count are needed (Reviewer 3).  
6. **Claimed improvements not supported by tables** – Line 407 claims 5.5 mIoU, 5.5 AP, 5.5 PQ average improvement over SOTA, but Table 1 does not directly show these averages (Reviewer 3).  
7. **Limited visual evidence** – Only four traffic-scene images are shown. More visualizations of learnable prompt outputs and mask results are needed, including for diverse domains and for the case of dense same-category objects or disconnected semantic regions (Reviewers 1, 2).  
8. **Computational efficiency and memory not discussed** – Table 5 should include training memory and model parameter sizes (Reviewer 1). The method may be more complex than necessary, and no efficiency comparison is provided (Reviewer 4).  
9. **Implementation details unclear** – Where is the segmentation loss applied (before or after voting)? How are semantic prompts (C much smaller than N) assigned to different spatial regions for multi-granularity? The method inherits from SSprompt but is an incremental change (Reviewer 3).  
10. **HoughSemPL’s role overshadows spatial prompt contribution** – Ablations (Table 4) show HoughSemPL drives most improvements. This weakens the paper’s stated focus on learning *spatial* prompts; how HoughSemPL works without region voting is not explained (Reviewers 2, 3).  
11. **Editorial issues** – Incorrect figure reference (Line 180 should be Figure 2), multiple grammar errors (e.g., “a accurate”, “to learns”, “Segmentation Anything Models”, “fed into the to The mask deocder”, “object It”) (Reviewers 2, 3).  
12. **Handling of dense objects and disconnected semantic regions** – It is unclear how the method automatically generates prompts per object in crowded scenes, or handles cases where same-category regions are disconnected (Reviewer 1).  
13. **Scope of datasets limited** – Experiments are restricted to Cityscapes, Mapillary, ADE20K, ACDC; performance on other types of datasets or real-world scenarios is not discussed (Reviewer 4).  

**Reviewer disagreement note**: Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 5) find the method novel, effective, and well-presented, while Reviewers 1 and 4 (score 3) raise concerns about fairness, missing comparisons, lack of validation of core design, and weak support for claimed contributions.
