## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BandVAE, a method that disentangles interest factors from ratings and textual modalities, aligns them via optimal transport (treating factors as discrete measures), and transfers knowledge using a regularisation term and barycentric mapping. The approach is evaluated on three real-world datasets against seven VAE-like baselines, with ablation and interpretability studies.

### Strengths
- Addresses a challenging problem: aligning factors from sparse user signals with side information to improve recommendation (R1).
- Use of optimal transport for mutual alignment of modalities is relatively new in the field (R2).
- Sound formalization with a clear mathematical foundation (R2, R3).
- Extensive experimental setting: three datasets, seven VAE-like baselines, ablation studies, hyper-parameter analysis, and interpretability assessment (R2, R3).
- Code released at review time (R2).
- Interpretability analysis provides insights into factor alignment (R2, R3; though its significance was contested – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption of modality relatedness not justified.** The work implicitly assumes that behavioral data (ratings) and side information (text) are mutually related. No empirical evidence supports this in general; side features may not be alignable and can lead to overspecialization (see Pilászy & Tikk 2009). Cold-start testing, where hybrid models are most relevant, is missing (R3).  
- **Positioning as hybrid recommender.** Using ratings as a “modality” is unusual; the work should be explicitly connected to the hybrid recommender literature (e.g., Factorization Machines, HybridSVD, EASEr). No comparisons are made with such simpler hybrid models, which would be natural baselines (R2, R3).  
- **Marginal empirical gains.** In Table 1, improvement over VAE-like baselines is not large; the authors do not provide statistical significance tests or confidence intervals. Gains may not justify the added complexity. One reviewer (R2) found the improvement acceptable after rebuttal, but another (R3) noted the absence of stronger baselines and called for further evidence (R2, R3).  
- **Interpretability claims lack statistical support.** The claim that “turning off the effect of $\mathcal{L}^t_u$ hurts recommendation accuracy” is not backed by significance testing; visible improvement may stem from overfitting to specific factors or data artifacts (R3). The observed trade-off (smaller $\epsilon$ favors interpretability but hurts accuracy) undermines the claimed utility of the text decoder (R3).  
- **Limited to textual side information.** Only textual descriptions are considered; the title and text overgeneralize to arbitrary modalities. No user features or other modalities (e.g., images) are tested (R3).  
- **Missing critical testing scenarios.** No cold-start evaluation is provided, which is the standard scenario where hybrid models are expected to excel (R3).  
- **Theoretical and methodological concerns persisted despite rebuttal.** One reviewer (R2) was satisfied after the authors addressed all raised questions, but another reviewer (R3) maintained that the problem conceptualization (obscuring a hybrid recommender behind the “modalities” framing) remains unresolved, and that experimental evidence is insufficient to prove superiority over both hybrid methods and strong non-hybrid collaborative filtering (e.g., EASEr) (R3).  
- **Presentation issues.**  
  - Figure 1 lacks notation (e.g., weight matrices) and is hard to follow (R3).  
  - Notation $(r^{uy}_k, o^{uy}_k)$ is unclear (is it a concatenation?) (R3).  
  - The Movielens dataset description (15,000 users, 7,892 items, 1,005,820 interactions, 8,000 words) does not match any standard Movielens variant; a dedicated dataset statistics table is needed (R3).  
- **Insufficient computational efficiency analysis.** Only one training epoch is examined; the total number of epochs required for optimal performance is not reported. A comparison with simpler models that do not use side features is missing (R3).  
- **Lack of real-world application context.** The paper does not discuss practical deployment or application scenarios (R1).
