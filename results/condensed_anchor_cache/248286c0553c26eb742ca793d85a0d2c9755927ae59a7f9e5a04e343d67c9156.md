## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents DiTTo-TTS, a latent diffusion model (LDM) for zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) that uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone and removes reliance on domain-specific factors such as phoneme alignments and durations. The model encodes speech into latent representations via a fine-tuned Mel-VAE, conditions on text via a pre-trained encoder (ByT5 / SpeechT5) with cross-attention, and predicts total speech length using an auto-regressive speech length predictor. Extensive experiments scale training data to 82K hours and model size to 790M parameters, achieving state-of-the-art or comparable performance in naturalness, intelligibility, and speaker similarity across multiple languages. Reviewers note the comprehensive ablation studies, comparisons with many baselines, and the clear exposition of design choices, but raise concerns about novelty given prior LDM-based TTS works (e.g., SeedTTS, Simple-TTS, NaturalSpeech 2/3) and the reliance on pre-trained components.

### Strengths
- **Extensive ablations and comparisons**: The paper systematically investigates model scaling (small to XLarge), data scaling (up to 82K hours), architectural choices (DiT vs. U-Net, skip connections, text encoders), fixed vs. variable-length modeling, and compares against a wide range of SOTA TTS systems (AR: VALL-E, CLaM-TTS; NAR: YourTTS, SimpleTTS, etc.) across multiple metrics (CMOS, SMOS, CER/WER, SIM).  
- **Novel contributions (per Reviewer 2)**: The fine-tuning of MelVAE to align with pre-trained text encoders and the introduction of an auto-regressive speech length predictor are considered novel and provide valuable insights for the field. Reviewer 2 specifically highlights these as novel contributions.  
- **Clear writing and detailed experiments**: The paper is well-written, with high-detail experimental sections that make the study easy to follow.  
- **Achieves competitive performance without domain-specific factors**: DiTTo-TTS demonstrates that LDMs can achieve SOTA quality without phonemes or durations, and with significantly reduced inference time compared to prior approaches.  
- **Practical insights**: The ablation on long-skip connections and variable-length modeling offers concrete guidance for future LDM-based TTS design.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty (majority concern)**: Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 find the overall approach not entirely novel. Reviewer 1 notes that SeedTTS (arXiv:2406.02430) has already demonstrated a fully diffusion-based TTS without phoneme/duration dependency. Reviewer 3 points out that Simple-TTS and NaturalSpeech 2 also use pre-trained text encoders and neural audio codecs with cross-attention, and training only the LDM weights. Reviewer 4 states that LDM-based zero-shot TTS is not new (e.g., NaturalSpeech 2, 3).  
  - *Minority contrast*: Reviewer 2 considers the MelVAE fine-tuning and AR speech length predictor as novel contributions, and finds no major weaknesses.  
- **Dependency on domain-specific pre-trained components**: Reviewer 3 questions the claim of avoiding domain-specific factors, noting reliance on a pre-trained Text Encoder (ByT5, SpeechT5), a pre-trained neural audio codec (Mel-VAE), and a Speech Length Predictor. The paper does not clearly define “domain-specific factors” and how this differs from traditional TTS systems that also use pre-trained components.  
- **Missing details in evaluations**:  
  - Table 3 (subjective metrics) does not report the number of speakers or test utterances, making it hard to assess diversity.  
  - Confidence intervals or p-values are not provided for comparisons in result tables, so statistical significance of claimed improvements cannot be determined.  
  - The CMOS evaluation instructions (Appendix A3) only ask about quality comparison; the main text claims assessment of naturalness and intelligibility via CMOS, but it is unclear how these dimensions were evaluated separately.  
  - Reporting sim-o for all ablations (especially when comparing neural codecs) would benefit community comparisons (Reviewer 2).  
- **Design and analysis gaps**:  
  - Why model a latent representation instead of directly modeling mel-spectrograms? (Reviewer 1).  
  - Inference time comparison is incomplete: for models lacking a speech length predictor, inference time cannot be measured; but even with ground-truth lengths, their longer latents slow generation. What is the feature rate of MelVAE? (Reviewer 2 mentions original MelVAE from CLaM-TTS has 100 Hz rate; EnCodec is 75 Hz; provide this info for self-containment).  
  - The speech editing/infilling process is not clearly described (e.g., how durations are handled when prefix is null). An alignment model is likely required, which may contradict the claimed simplicity.  
  - Ablation on the number of training steps is suggested (Reviewer 2).  
  - Performance in small-data scenarios (e.g., single-speaker, less than LibriTTS) is of interest; the scaling trend appears less steep than in other works (e.g., Voicebox), and the authors’ perspective on robustness with limited data is requested (Reviewer 4).  
- **Questions on methodology and comparison**:  
  - In Table 7, are DiTTo-EnCodec and DiTTo-DAC also fine-tuned with an LM objective? If not, a direct comparison with DiTTo-MelVAE is needed.  
  - Would the diffusion model’s superiority over AR models (e.g., CLaM) hold with larger datasets? (Reviewer 1 notes training data is 82K hours; AR language models may scale better).  
  - Code and checkpoints are not planned for release, limiting reproducibility (Reviewer 4).
