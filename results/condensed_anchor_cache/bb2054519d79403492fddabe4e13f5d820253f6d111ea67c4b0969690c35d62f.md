## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a linear log-normal attention mechanism (LLN Attention) that reduces quadratic complexity to linear while aiming to preserve key properties of standard softmax attention. The authors characterize three properties of softmax attention: its attention matrix distribution is log-normal, entropy increases monotonically with temperature, and variance decreases with temperature. Based on these, they design LLN Attention using exponential feature embeddings and moment matching (up to 2nd order) to emulate that distribution and concentration behavior. Experiments on several NLP tasks (Table 1, Table 2) show LLN Attention outperforms other linearized alternatives but yields only modest gains relative to Nystromformer. One reviewer (score 8) found the paper strong with no major weaknesses; three other reviewers (scores 5, 5, 6) raised concerns about limited improvements, narrow evaluation, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The theoretical analysis of softmax attention is novel and detailed: it characterizes the attention matrix as log-normally distributed and proves monotonicity of entropy and variance with temperature. The proofs appear correct and are accompanied by useful intuitive explanations. (R1, R2)
- Based on those properties, the authors carefully select exponential feature embeddings and introduce a moment‑matching technique (up to 2nd order) to align the concentration behavior of LLN Attention with that of softmax attention. This design is creative and provides insights for future efficient attention mechanisms. (R1, R2)
- Empirical results on NLP benchmarks (e.g., Table 1, Table 2) confirm that LLN Attention outperforms other linear attention methods. The authors also experimentally validate that the entropy and spectral gap of the attention matrix behave monotonically with temperature as theoretically predicted. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The paper is well‑written, with clear visualizations and intuitive explanations that make the analysis and design easy to follow. (R1, R3)
- Reviewer 1 (score 8, confidence 3) considered the paper strong overall and found no major weaknesses, only minor notation and explanation issues. This contrasts with the more critical assessments of the other three reviewers (scores 5, 5, 6 confidence 4,4,3 respectively).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited improvement over existing methods**: The performance gains compared to Nystromformer (published two years ago) are not substantial across NLP tasks (Tables 1, 2). Reviewers note that the improvements are not significant. (R3, R4)
- **Narrow evaluation scope**: Experiments focus exclusively on NLP tasks; computer‑vision tasks (e.g., ViTs) are not tested. This limits evidence of general applicability. Additionally, a relevant related work (KVT, ECCV2022) is missing. (R2, R3)
- **Lack of practical efficiency metrics**: Only theoretical complexity and accuracy are reported. Missing comparisons on latency, training time, convergence rate, and stability make it hard to assess the practical advantage over softmax attention. (R2, R4)
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:
  - The theoretical approximations (e.g., Fenton theorem for log‑normal sums) are not empirically validated for accuracy. (R2)
  - Moment matching is limited to 2nd‑order statistics; higher‑order matching is not considered or discussed. (R2)
  - Only exponential feature embeddings are studied; the impact of other embedding functions on distribution and concentration is unexplored. (R2)
  - No combination with sliding window or dilated patterns is tested, which could help handle local contexts. (R2)
  - Ablation on the temperature parameter, block size in diagonal attention, and layer depth is missing. (R2)
- **Task diversity**: The benchmarks do not include tasks with longer sequences, which would better highlight the benefits of linear complexity. (R2)
- **Minor issues**:
  - The temperature parameter τₛₘ in Eq. 5 lacks an intuitive explanation (e.g., connection to the temperature in softmax used in reinforcement learning for exploration/exploitation). (R1)
  - Notation inconsistency: Eq. (6) uses P_ij^(SM) while Eq. (7) uses **P**_ij^(SM). (R1)
  - The location of the proof for Theorem 3.4 is unclear; it may be Theorem A.3 but is not explicitly referenced. (R1)
