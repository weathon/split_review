## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a VAE-based Bayesian optimisation (BO) method that uses a Gaussian process (GP) prior VAE to condition the generative and inference model on auxiliary covariates. This leverages additional low-dimensional data (e.g., covariates) available for points in the high-dimensional search space, and can handle missing covariates via variational inference. BO is performed in the latent space, using a GP surrogate on the latent representations. The method is evaluated on synthetic data, a mathematical expression tuning benchmark, and a molecule optimisation (penalized logP) benchmark, showing improvements over baselines. The paper is well-written and technically rigorous, but the core model (GP prior VAE with partial observations) was previously published, raising novelty concerns. The empirical evaluation includes missing baselines and potential unfair comparisons due to the use of extra covariates.

### Strengths
- The work addresses an impactful and underexplored problem: high-dimensional BO with additional meta-data (auxiliary covariates) for points in the search space. The combination of GP prior VAE and VAE-BO is an intuitive and general architecture.
- The framework is principled and general, enabling conditioning on auxiliary covariates within a probabilistic VAE-BO setting. It can be used with many VAE-BO architectures.
- The paper is exceptionally well-written and clear, with strong presentation and scholarship (e.g., tracing label-guidance ideas to Urtasun et al. 2007).
- Technical rigor: the probabilistic model and variational inference for missing covariates are carefully described. Accounting for missing data demonstrates broader practicality.
- Experimental thoroughness: experiments span multiple domains (synthetic, expression, molecule). The molecular optimisation example highlights the benefit of incorporating covariates. Results are impressive pending clarifications.
- The method is evaluated against several baselines, including VAE-BO, LOL-BO, and deep metric learning approaches.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The core contribution is the application of GP prior VAE to BO, but the underlying GP prior VAE model with partial observations was published previously (Ramchandran et al. 2024). This limits the novelty of the work, as noted by Reviewer 3 (score 5), while Reviewers 1 and 2 are more positive.
- **Missing baseline (LOL-BO) in synthetic experiment:** Reviewer 3 notes that LOL-BO is not included in the first synthetic experiment. Since LOL-BO shows competitive performance on other tasks, its absence weakens the comparison. The authors should include it or justify its exclusion.
- **Unfair use of additional covariates:** In the molecular optimisation experiment, the ZINC-250K dataset was augmented with five extra covariates (molecular weight, H-donors, H-acceptors, rotatable bonds, polar surface area) that are highly correlated with the logP objective. It is unclear whether baseline methods were given access to these covariates. A fair comparison would require either giving baselines the same information (e.g., by including the covariates in the molecular representation) or acknowledging that the improved performance likely stems from these correlated features. The method’s advantage is that it can incorporate such covariates without requiring a VAE for heterogeneous data.
- **Discrepancy with LOL-BO paper results:** The optimisation trace for LOL-BO in Figure 5b differs substantially from the trace reported in the original LOL-BO paper (Maus et al. 2022). The authors should explain this discrepancy.
- **Need for diagnostic experiments:** Reviewer 2 requests experiments (e.g., log-likelihood on holdout data) to verify that the GP surrogate fits the latent space better when using GP prior VAE, as in Grosnit et al. 2021. This would provide evidence for the mechanism.
- **Lack of bias towards high-valued y:** Reviewer 1 notes that Tripp et al. (2020) uses weighted retraining to bias the VAE toward high-value regions. The proposed ELBO does not include such a bias, and it is not clear why learning a GP from fitness to latent (inverse mapping) would help BO. The inverse GP must map a scalar to level sets, modeled by a unimodal Gaussian, which seems counterintuitive. The paper lacks intuition or justification for this design.
- **Scalability dependency on sparse GP inducing points:** Reviewer 3 highlights that the method relies on sparse GP with inducing points, but when the dataset changes over BO iterations, updating inducing points in a principled and efficient manner is non-trivial. The paper does not discuss this issue.
- **Lack of limitations discussion:** The paper does not discuss failure modes, e.g., when auxiliary covariates are noise or when optimal points have very different covariate values. The method allows more ways to break, but no warnings are given.
- **Minor issues:** (1) The related work could better note that VAE-BO is most beneficial for structured inputs (molecules, images, sequences) rather than for high-dimensional continuous spaces. (2) Some references need formatting corrections (capitalizations). (3) The variable naming (y for high-dimensional observation) may cause confusion. (4) Section 4 describes prior works that could be moved to related work; Section 4.3 is dense and might be better placed in an appendix with more space for intuition. (5) The code has not been released, raising reproducibility concerns.
