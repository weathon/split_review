## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies and addresses threshold inconsistency in deep metric learning (DML) by proposing the Operating-Point-Inconsistency-Score (OPIS) metric and the Threshold-Consistent Margin (TCM) loss, validated on DML benchmarks. Reviewers agree the problem definition, metric, and loss are valuable and clearly presented. However, one reviewer (score 5) raises significant concerns about missing experiments in face verification, outdated comparisons, and insufficient discussion of related generalization work, while another reviewer (score 8, high confidence) finds the method rigorous and effective. A third reviewer (score 6) praises the comprehensiveness but notes minor questions about broader applicability.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly identifies and defines the threshold inconsistency problem in DML.
- The proposed OPIS and ϵ-OPIS metrics provide valuable new insights for measuring inconsistency.
- Experiments objectively compare high accuracy with high threshold consistency.
- The TCM loss is simple, intuitive, and easy to understand.
- Visualization of the TCM effect (e.g., on representation structures) is interesting.
- Experiments are comprehensive and include detailed implementation, coverage of mainstream metric learning settings, and extensive ablations (margin, DML losses, different architectures, time complexity).
- The method is validated against state-of-the-art approaches such as RS (Ring loss).
- The paper is well-written, making it easy to follow while offering comprehensive comparisons with current methods.
- The research demonstrates improved threshold consistency without sacrificing accuracy in several instances.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of face verification experiments (most significant concern for one reviewer):** The proposed method’s focus on threshold consistency appears particularly suited for face verification (threshold-based metrics like TAR@FAR), yet no experiments are conducted on face recognition datasets. This omission raises questions about practical applicability in a domain where threshold determination is paramount. A reviewer also asks how open-set metric learning differs from face recognition/re-ID and whether the method can be applied there.
- **Missing comparisons with related work on generalization and threshold consistency:** 
  - The threshold inconsistency problem overlaps with the generalization problem discussed in DML literature (references [1,2] cited by a reviewer). Specifically, [1] (MVC) proposed a variance-based constraint; [2] discussed how feature compression affects generalization. The paper should incorporate discussions and direct comparisons with these works, especially since TCM shares similarities with MVC.
  - Related work like OneFace and Liu et al. (2022) also address threshold consistency, but no experimental comparisons on face recognition datasets or metrics are provided. Such comparisons are crucial to validate the method’s advantages.
- **Misleading use of "large-scale":** A reviewer notes that the datasets used (e.g., iNaturalist-2018, CUB, Cars-196) are not truly large-scale (typically >10M samples), contrasting with LAION-400M/5B. The authors should clarify the scale and consider removing "large-scale" to avoid overstatement.
- **Outdated state-of-the-art results on CUB and Cars-196:** The paper should update its comparisons to include recent work such as HIER [1], which has shown superior performance on these datasets. Without this, the evaluation is not fair or current.
- **Potential disconnect between threshold consistency and accuracy in face verification:** The paper states high accuracy does not imply high threshold consistency, but in face verification consistent thresholds often directly improve accuracy (e.g., TAR@FAR). A more detailed discussion of this relationship is needed.
- **Question about cross-dataset threshold consistency:** A reviewer asks whether the method can use a single model to maintain the same threshold across multiple test sets, referencing Univ_Emb. This would be a meaningful extension but is not explored.
- **Question on ProxyAnchor (ResNet50) low threshold consistency (Figure 3):** A reviewer requests a comparison of improvement in R@K and OPIS using TCM, ideally showing significant OPIS improvement. The paper should include such an analysis.
- **Minor note from a reviewer:** The idea of pulling positive pairs toward a fixed score and negative pairs toward another fixed score is meaningful but sounds simple—this is not a weakness but indicates the approach is straightforward. (Included for completeness; no action required.)
