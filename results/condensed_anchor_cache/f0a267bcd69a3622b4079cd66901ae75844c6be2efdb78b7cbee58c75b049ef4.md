## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ACCO (ACcumulate while COmmunicate), a memory-efficient distributed optimization algorithm for LLM training that overlaps gradient computation and communication. ACCO introduces a one-step delay by splitting each mini-batch into two halves: the first half computes an approximate gradient to update a shadow weight prediction (θ̃), compensating for the delay; the full mini-batch then computes the actual gradient update. This allows communication to be hidden behind computation and enables sharding of optimizer states. Experiments include pre-training GPT-neo 36M on TinyStories, GPT-neo 125M on OpenWebText, and fine-tuning GPT-neo 2.7B on Alpaca. Results claim up to 87% reduction in training time (though contested) with convergence comparable to DDP.

### Strengths

- Addresses a significant problem: reducing communication overhead in synchronous distributed LLM training while keeping memory low and supporting optimizer state sharding.
- Algorithm design is well-illustrated (Figure 2) and compatible with ZeRO and data-parallel sharding; includes a strategy for heterogeneous hardware.
- Experimental results show notable training time reductions in realistic scenarios (pre-training and fine-tuning) while maintaining convergence similar to DDP, achieving higher throughput. Reviewer 4 highlights similar convergence curves and higher throughput.
- Memory efficiency is quantified in Table 1 (though noted as less efficient than ZeRO1 by some reviewers). Reviewer 4 found the idea novel; reviewer 2 found the results compelling.
- The method can be adopted without obvious memory overheads (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and prior art**: Several reviewers note that core components are previously proposed. One-step delay (DPU) corresponds to PipeSGD [1], and delay compensation via weight prediction to SAPipe [2]; neither is cited. Elastic scheduling (Nadiradze et al., AAAI 2021) also introduces delayed approximate updates. This weakens the claim of novelty and makes the contribution incremental. Reviewer 1 rates contribution 1; reviewer 5 rates contribution 2. (Note: Reviewer 4 found the idea “very novel”, creating a contrast.)
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: Convergence analysis is missing beyond a high-level sketch. Reviewers 1, 4, and 5 request a formal analysis, including bounds on the error introduced by using θ̃ instead of θ, and the impact of delayed/stale gradients. The analysis from SAPipe or elastic consistency could be adapted.
- **Experimental rigor and scale**:
  - Performance deteriorates when scaling workers from 8 to 32 (Table 2), suggesting a scaling issue (reviewer 4). No controlled experiment isolating worker count on identical hardware and steps is provided.
  - ACCO does not consistently outperform DDP; reviewer 1 questions why DDP is better in Figure 6.
  - Experiments cover limited model sizes (36M, 125M, 2.7B) and datasets (TinyStories, OpenWebText, Alpaca). Lack of diverse architectures and larger models to demonstrate generalizability (reviewer 3).
  - Insufficient detail on hyperparameter tuning for ACCO and baselines, making fairness of comparison unclear (reviewer 3).
  - Ablation studies are missing to understand impact of different components (e.g., half-accumulation fraction, communication frequency).
- **Memory optimization contribution is weak**: Primary contribution is communication overlap; memory usage is actually more than ZeRO-1 (reviewer 3). No detailed memory footprint breakdown compared to activation checkpointing or gradient accumulation. Reviewer 3 questions direct contributions to memory optimization.
- **Numerical instability**: Using half a mini-batch for the approximate gradient step may cause mismatch in gradient landscape, leading to divergence, exploding gradients, or oscillations over long training, especially with slow devices (reviewer 2).
- **Misleading claim**: The statement that ACCO is “plain SGD with no delay” is inappropriate because θ̃ introduces gradient staleness; it is more akin to delayed SGD (reviewer 4). The advertised “up to 87% reduction” in training time is considered unfair given that performance gains are not consistent (reviewer 1).
- **Unclear sharded optimizer details**: The paper does not clearly explain how optimizer states (momentum, variance in Adam) are partitioned across workers, what information is needed for the local update step, and whether optimizer states are updated in both Eq.(1) and Eq.(2) or only Eq.(2) (reviewers 2, 4).
- **Design choices not justified**: Why use half-accumulated gradients (first stage) for weight prediction? Could local accumulated gradients (without global synchronization) be used to allow a larger fraction? The trade-off between accumulation error (half vs full) and synchronization error (local vs global) is not explored (reviewer 5). Also, the paper lacks insights into why splitting the mini-batch in this specific way is particularly beneficial for LLM training (reviewer 3).
- **Motivation for LLM specificity**: The paper does not adequately explain characteristics of LLM training that make ACCO superior; rationale for design choices in the context of LLMs is missing (reviewer 3).
