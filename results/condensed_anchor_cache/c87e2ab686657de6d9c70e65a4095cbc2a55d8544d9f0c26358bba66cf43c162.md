## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces VQGLA, a quantum-driven graph learning architecture designed to overcome the expressive limitations of classical Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) graph representations. VQGLA encodes node and edge features using quantum circuits while maintaining permutation equivariance. Experimental results on both GNN-tractable and GNN-intractable MILP datasets show that VQGLA surpasses classical GNN baselines, demonstrating superior discriminative capabilities and generalization. The method is also applied to general graph tasks (classification, regression, node property prediction).

### Strengths
- Clearly articulates the limitations of classical (message-passing) GNNs in distinguishing certain MILP graphs and provides a well-motivated quantum-driven solution; successfully demonstrates that VQGLA can solve GNN-intractable MILPs with superior discriminative power, as supported by numerical results.
- The design of VQGLA (node feature layer, graph message interaction layer, auxiliary layer) is well-thought-out and comprehensive; the incorporation of quantum entanglement to enhance discriminative power is innovative. Theoretical proof of permutation equivariance strengthens the rigor.
- Experimental results are compelling: VQGLA outperforms classical GNNs and a quantum baseline (HEA) across multiple benchmarks (both GNN-tractable and GNN-intractable MILP datasets). The evaluation includes comparisons to several classical and quantum methods.
- Demonstrates versatility by applying VQGLA to graph classification, graph regression, and node property prediction, and by handling edge features. The model adapts to different levels of graph tasks.
- Represents an interesting application of quantum computing to real-world optimization problems (MILPs), combining power of quantum computing and GNNs.

### Weaknesses
- **Scalability and resource analysis lacking.** There is no detailed discussion of how the number of qubits and quantum gates scales with the number of nodes and edges, nor the circuit depth required. Without such analysis it is unclear whether VQGLA remains practical for larger, more complex graphs or is limited to small-scale problems. This concern is raised by both Reviewer 1 (score 6) and Reviewer 2 (score 8).  
- **Quantum operation details are insufficiently explained.** The mapping of inequality/equality constraints (inherently classical) to quantum gate operations is not described. The paper lacks a clear, step-by-step explanation of how gates (CNOT, Hadamard, parameterized rotations) implement message passing and feature transformation. A visual circuit diagram and an account of how gate parameters are learned during training would improve understanding. Both Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 note this lack of clarity.  
- **No consideration of quantum noise or near-term hardware feasibility.** The impact of quantum noise (gate errors, measurement errors, decoherence) on VQGLA’s performance is not explored. The paper does not propose error mitigation strategies or discuss hardware requirements for near-term devices, which weakens practical relevance (Reviewer 1).  
- **Motivation based on weak GNN baselines and incomplete comparison to advanced classical methods.** The paper’s motivation rests on the limitations of message-passing GNNs, but higher-order GNNs (e.g., subgraph GNNs) can already overcome the cited intractability (e.g., distinguishing the graphs in Figure 1). The comparison to graph transformers is a step forward but insufficient to demonstrate superiority over the full range of higher-order classical architectures. Reviewer 3 (score 5) is notably more critical on this point, stating the motivation “lacks persuasiveness”; Reviewer 1 also requests direct comparisons with models using attention/hierarchical representations.  
- **Comparison with HEA baseline is unclear.** It is not specified whether the HEA baseline encodes graph information in the same way as VQGLA; this affects the fairness of the comparison (Reviewer 2).  
- **Parameter transferability analysis is incomplete.** The paper shows parameter transferability but does not clarify whether the graphs used for transfer have the same structure. Without this clarification, the degree of generalization remains uncertain (Reviewer 2).  
- **Lack of a clear methodology for translating real-world MILP problems into the quantum domain.** The process of mapping variables, constraints, and the objective function to quantum states and operations is not explained in sufficient detail, making it difficult to assess the method’s applicability beyond the presented examples (Reviewer 2).
