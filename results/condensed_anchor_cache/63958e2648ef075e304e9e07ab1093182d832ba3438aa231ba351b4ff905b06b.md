## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a class unlearning algorithm that estimates a Retain Space and Forget Space from layer-wise network activations (via SVD), removes shared information from the Forget Space to isolate class‑discriminatory features, and then projects the model weights orthogonal to that space. The method requires only a few forward passes and a small subset of samples. Experiments on CIFAR‑10, CIFAR‑100, and ImageNet with VGG11, ResNet18, and ViT show that it achieves under 1% accuracy on forget classes with only ~1.5% drop in retain accuracy (on ImageNet/ViT), and is up to 6.5× faster than the proposed Stable Ascent baseline. The paper also provides saliency‑based analysis. One reviewer (score 8) is highly positive; the other three (scores 3,5,5) raise substantial concerns about definition, evaluation, missing comparisons, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The problem of machine unlearning is important and timely, especially for large‑scale models.
- The proposed method is simple, elegant, and easy to use – it applies layer‑wise SVD and requires only forward passes.
- The paper introduces a strong baseline (Stable Ascent / NegGrad+) and shows the proposed method outperforms it.
- Results surpass previous state‑of‑the‑art on several benchmarks for class unlearning.
- The SVD‑based separation of feature spaces for unlearning is novel.
- Empirical validation covers multiple datasets (CIFAR‑10, CIFAR‑100, ImageNet), architectures (VGG11, ResNet18, ViT), and scenarios (single‑class, multi‑class, sequential forgetting).
- The application to Vision Transformers (ImageNet) is the first demonstration of an unlearning method on such large‑scale models.
- Qualitative saliency analysis (Grad‑CAM) provides interesting insight into feature forgetting.
- The paper is generally well‑written and the approach is well‑motivated.

### Weaknesses
- **Definition and theoretical guarantees**
  - The paper lacks a precise, formal definition of unlearning. The condition \( y_i \neq f(x_i, \theta_f) \) is problematic because a retrained model can have non‑zero accuracy on the forget set; a probabilistic or distributional definition that matches the retrained model is needed.
  - The definition only considers test examples, not training examples, which is atypical for unlearning.
  - No theoretical unlearning guarantees are provided, especially against membership inference attacks (e.g., Izzo et al.’s approach using influence functions).
  - The method is purely empirical; a linear‑perturbation version would allow theoretical guarantees.

- **Related work and baselines**
  - The approach is very similar to Izzo et al. (2021) (proceedings.mlr.press/v130/izzo21a) applied per layer, which is not cited.
  - The baseline “Stable Ascent” has already been proposed as “NegGrad+” in prior work (e.g., [A] from the references); that prior work is not cited.
  - Several recent unlearning methods (references [A],[B],[C],[D] in Reviewer 3’s review) are missing from the Related Work and are not empirically compared.

- **Methodology details and missing ablations**
  - The paper only describes how to collect activations for linear and convolutional layers. For Transformers, it is unclear how attention‑layer projections (Key, Query, Value, Output) are handled, and how BatchNorm fits into the SVD space estimation.
  - A basic ablation is missing: compare applying the unlearning method only to the last layer vs. to all layers.
  - The contribution of the proposed “importance‑based scaling” is not ablated; a version without scaling should be evaluated.
  - It is not shown how the results change when using activations from only the top layer(s) instead of all layers.
  - The choice of \(\alpha\) in Eq. (1) is dataset‑dependent and manually tuned; a learnable or principled selection scheme would be more robust.
  - The scaling may deform the feature spaces; its justification is insufficient and the link to continual‑learning literature is not made.
  - No sensitivity analysis is provided on how the size or sampling strategy of the small retain/forget sample sets affects the estimated spaces and final performance.
  - It is not specified whether the classification head is modified during unlearning; the evaluation on datasets with many classes (e.g., CIFAR‑100) is unclear without this detail.

- **Evaluation and metrics**
  - Evaluation relies almost exclusively on accuracy metrics (retain accuracy, forget accuracy). Crucially missing are membership inference attacks (e.g., Golatkar et al.) to verify that information about the forget set is truly removed.
  - The stated goal is to match a model retrained without the forget class, yet weight‑space proximity or related metrics are not reported; accuracy alone can be misleading.
  - The retrain‑from‑scratch oracle is not included in most tables, making it impossible to gauge how close the method comes to the ideal target.
  - “Original” model accuracy is reported ambiguously: is it overall accuracy or separate retain/forget accuracy?
  - In the multi‑class forgetting experiment (CIFAR‑10 only), no baselines are compared, and the analysis is insufficient (e.g., why the unlearned model outperforms the original in Fig. 5b but not Fig. 5a).
  - The class selection for CIFAR‑100 (every 10th class) may not be representative of all classes; the paper should justify this or evaluate on a random subset.

- **Efficiency and applicability**
  - The method requires a small set of training samples for unlearning; zero‑shot unlearning methods exist (and are not compared).
  - In Figure 4, the proposed method is not more efficient than Tarun et al. (2023); a more detailed runtime analysis is needed.
  - The motivation for class unlearning (privacy, data deletion) seems at odds with the typical use case of unlearning individual data points; application scenarios for removing entire classes should be better justified.
  - The large confidence intervals in Figure 5b raise questions about the significance of the results.

- **Clarity and presentation**
  - The method section is imprecise: “for both linear and convolutional layers, where \(l\) is a layer and \(i\) is retain sample” – the variable \(i\) is not introduced.
  - The handling of Transformers and attention layers is not explained; the algorithm description only covers linear/convolutional layers.
  - “Generalization on retain samples” is a misnomer (these are training samples).
  - The paper contains numerous typos and grammatical errors (e.g., “we asks”, “this section focus”, “sorted in by the amount”, missing articles).
