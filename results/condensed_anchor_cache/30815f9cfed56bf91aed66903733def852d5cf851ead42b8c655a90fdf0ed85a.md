## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes InstaTrain, a novel learning paradigm that transforms traditional model training into an ultra-fast natural annealing process implemented on an electronic dynamical system. It extends existing hardware from inference-only to support both training and inference via the Iterative Natural Annealing based Training (INAT) algorithm. Experiments on high-frequency datasets (Carbon-Oxide, Methane, Stock) claim ~4000× training speedup, ~10^5× reduction in training energy costs, and lower test MAE compared to SOTA methods on GPUs. The approach is well-motivated and novel, but reviewers raised several concerns about dataset scope, experimental validation, clarity of comparisons, and missing theoretical justification.

### Strengths
- Novel transformation of traditional training into a natural annealing process on a dynamical system, combining statistical physics with machine learning (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Hardware-algorithm co-design that extends existing electronic dynamical systems from inference to training, enabling simultaneous rapid training and inference (R1, R2, R4).
- Clear theoretical foundation and well-structured presentation, particularly Section 3 (R1, R2, R3).
- Significant empirical gains: ~4,000× training speedup, ~10^5× reduction in training energy costs, and lower test MAE across three high-frequency datasets, with ablation studies supporting claims (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Evaluation against multiple baselines (NP-GL, GNNs, online learning methods) using FEA software simulation (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited dataset scope and missing standard benchmarks.** The three datasets (Carbon-Oxide, Methane, Stock) are low-dimensional with few features and relatively short time series. There is no discussion of scalability to higher-dimensional data, longer sequences, or more complex temporal dependencies. Standard time-series benchmarks (e.g., ETTm1, Electricity, Traffic, Exchange, Weather) are omitted, making it difficult to benchmark against existing SOTA and assess generalizability. Full dataset statistics (number of samples, nodes, sampling rate) are not provided. (R1, R2, R4; R2 particularly concerned about scalability; R4 notes missing standard datasets.)
2. **Confusing comparison with "not achievable" baselines.** In Figure 5 and the caption, some baseline results are labeled "not achievable" due to slow training. The methodology behind these comparisons is unclear—specifically, how "not achievable" results were obtained and what assumptions were made. This undermines the fairness and interpretability of the comparison. (R1, R2)
3. **Simulation-based results without real hardware validation.** Experiments rely on a CUDA-based software simulator, not actual hardware implementation. While the authors mention manufacturing of the real hardware in the reproducibility section, the simulation may not fully capture real-world non-idealities such as noise, component variations, and other physical constraints, which could impact performance. (R2)
4. **Energy reduction claim not fully justified.** In Table 1, the power consumption of InstaTrain (950mW) is higher than baselines BRIM (250mW) and NP-GL (260mW). A detailed calculation of energy savings—including a breakdown of training vs. inference time—is needed to support the claimed ~10^5× energy reduction. (R2)
5. **Lack of demonstration of convergence to equilibrium.** The algorithm's intuition relies on converging to the equilibrium state of the dynamical system. No theoretical proof or empirical evidence (e.g., energy landscape evolution over time) is provided to support this convergence behavior. (R1)
6. **Unclear handling of continuous data.** The model appears to operate on binary values (+1, -1). The paper does not explain how the natural annealing process adapts to continuous real-valued inputs common in time series, nor how the Hamiltonian formulation prevents polarization to extreme values. This raises questions about the model's ability to capture complex temporal dependencies. (R4)
7. **Incomplete baseline comparisons and presentation format.** The paper should compare to more regular time series prediction models beyond those tested. Results are presented as bar plots; tables of exact numbers would be preferable for clarity and reproducibility. (R3; this reviewer gave a lower overall rating (5) and assessed soundness as 2, suggesting weaker experimental validation compared to other reviewers who rated 6 or 8.)

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R3) was notably more critical, rating the paper 5 and assessing soundness as 2, citing incomplete baselines and presentation issues. Another reviewer (R2) raised specific concerns about energy calculations and hardware validation that were not echoed by others. Conversely, one reviewer (R1) was very positive (score 8, soundness 3) but noted dataset explanation and convergence justification as missing. The majority of reviewers (R2, R3, R4) expressed reservations about the experimental scope and clarity, while all acknowledged the novelty and motivation.
