## Merged Review

### Summary
This merged review reflects three reviewers with scores of 3, 3, and 5 (confidence 3–4). The paper addresses conformal prediction (CP) under distribution shifts without test labels, proposing ECP (entropy-scaled CP) and EACP (entropy base-adapted CP) that adjust scoring functions based on the model's uncertainty on unlabeled test data. All reviewers agree the problem is important but disagree on the solution's soundness and contribution. One reviewer (score 5) finds the method effective and the preliminaries well formulated; two reviewers (score 3) raise fundamental concerns about theoretical guarantees, heuristic design, missing baselines, and potential overconfidence issues.

### Strengths
- The problem of conformal prediction under distribution shifts without labels is important and has received limited attention.
- The core observation (using model entropy/confidence to adjust scoring) is simple and intuitively motivated.
- The method is evaluated on at least 6 shifted datasets (variations of ImageNet) with multiple architectures.
- The preliminaries are well formulated.

### Weaknesses
1. **Trivial correlation** (Reviewer 1): The observed monotonic relationship between confidence and entropy (Figure 1) is unsurprising, as both are standard uncertainty indicators. The claimed insight adds little over existing work [1].
2. **Overconfidence under OOD** (Reviewer 1): Models are known to be more overconfident and miscalibrated under distribution shift than in-distribution. The proposed method enlarges model outputs under OOD, which may exacerbate this overconfidence, harming CP reliability. The paper does not justify why a more overconfident model would yield better prediction sets.
3. **Lack of motivation for quantile mechanism** (Reviewer 1): Equation (3) uses a quantile-based rescaling, but the rationale is unclear. If the goal is to align average confidence on OOD and ID data, why not use standard temperature scaling instead? This design choice needs justification.
4. **No theoretical coverage guarantee** (Reviewer 2): The method is heuristic and does not provide any coverage probability bound (e.g., under covariate shift). This is a critical omission for conformal prediction, which traditionally relies on distribution-free guarantees. Can the authors provide any theoretical guarantee?
5. **Tuning knobs without labels** (Reviewer 2): The method introduces two knobs (β set to 1−α, and a function f). Choosing f is especially tricky under distribution shift without labels, limiting practicality. Could f be learned solely from unlabeled test examples?
6. **Missing baselines** (Reviewer 2):
   - No oracle comparison: SplitCP with access to labeled shifted examples should be included to show the desired coverage/efficiency upper bound.
   - No no-shift evaluation: The paper should demonstrate that the method works (or degrades gracefully) when no distribution shift occurs, as users may not know a priori whether a shift exists.
   - Missing comparison with Tibshirani et al. (2019) covariate-shift CP. The authors should apply their method to the setting in Figure 2 of that paper and report empirical coverage and set sizes alongside oracle-weighted results.
7. **Underperformance of EACP vs. ECP** (Reviewer 3): In some cases, entropy minimization (EACP) yields worse results than the simpler ECP. The paper should explain why and under what conditions this occurs.
8. **Presentation issues** (Reviewer 3): The abbreviation "resp." is used without definition in the first paragraph of the introduction.
9. **Foundation model alternative** (Reviewer 3): Could the problem be solved by foundation models like CLIP or LLMs? The paper does not discuss how the proposed method relates to or compares with such models.
10. **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) assessed the paper positively, noting well-formulated preliminaries and good performance, while two reviewers (score 3) considered the methodology heuristic, lacking theory, missing key baselines, and failing to convincingly address overconfidence concerns. This contrast highlights significant disagreement on the paper’s contribution and soundness.
