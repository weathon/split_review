## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using graph neural networks (GNNs) to jointly learn interaction rules and heterogeneous structure in dynamical systems from data alone, enabling virtual decomposition and inference of underlying governing equations. Reviewers generally acknowledge the problem motivation and extensive simulation experiments (particle interactions, wave propagation, reaction-diffusion, signaling networks), but express concerns about unclear definition of heterogeneity, lack of baselines and quantitative comparisons, missing related work and code, poor presentation and interpretation of results, and missing validation on real-world data. While some reviewers find the approach novel and well-presented (scores 5), others criticize it as a standard GNN application with insufficient rigor and clarity (scores 3).

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to follow, with clear writing flow and a well-motivated problem.
- The figures are well-presented and the experiment details are clear.
- The model can be applied to various complex realistic systems to reveal underlying structure and dynamics.
- The approach jointly learns interaction rules and heterogeneous structures directly from data, enabling decomposition of complex dynamic systems and inference of underlying governing equations; this is considered novel.
- Simulation experiments on moving particles, wave propagation, reaction-diffusion, and signaling networks demonstrate the model’s effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- **No related work section** and insufficient discussion of existing neural simulators (e.g., Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2020). Comparison to such baselines in experiments is needed.
- **No quantitative baselines** or comparisons. Reviewers request comparisons against conventional GNNs (e.g., with L2 loss), ODE/PDE-based GNNs (e.g., Continuous Graph Neural Networks), and runtime comparisons.
- **Definition of heterogeneity is unclear.** What does “heterogeneity” mean (e.g., different particle types)? Why is the latent embedding two-dimensional? The only definition given is “a two-dimensional learnable embedding \(a_i\) that is part of the node features.”
- **Method lacks rotational equivariance** for 3‑dimensional space; this issue is not addressed.
- **No code provided.**
- **Poor presentation:** readers must simultaneously check figures, tables, videos, and supplementary material without sufficient explanation; the flow is hard to track.
- **Lack of interpretation:** showing latent representations alone is insufficient to understand or validate the model’s strengths.
- **Choice of derivative order (first vs. second) is not justified.** Why not use both first- and second-order derivatives (or higher-order) at a single time point? The training is done with only one derivative type.
- **Novelty concerns:** ODEs/PDEs are well-established for dynamical systems; the paper uses simple MLPs instead of differentiable ODE solvers. The advantages of MLPs over ODE-based methods (e.g., computational efficiency, expressiveness, ease of implementation) are not discussed. A comparative analysis with methods like CGNNs is missing.
- **No experiments on real data.** The approach is tested only on simulated data; this limits assessment of generalizability to real-world dynamics (e.g., physical experiments, biological systems, social networks).
- **Claim of inferring governing equations** (line 17) is not supported by any experiment. How to extract a formula from the learned GNN is not shown.
