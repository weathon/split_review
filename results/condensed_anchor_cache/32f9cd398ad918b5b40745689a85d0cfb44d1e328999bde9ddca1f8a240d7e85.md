- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 1, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a unified framework (UUDS) for unsupervised cross-domain medical image segmentation, integrating domain adaptation and segmentation in an end-to-end manner. It uses CLIP with dual prompts (domain and segmentation prompts) to learn domain-invariant representations and uncertainty estimation to generate pseudo-labels for target domain supervision. Experiments are conducted on two public cross-modality MRI datasets. Two reviewers (ratings 3,3) find the design reasonable with good performance, while one reviewer (rating 1) strongly criticizes selective reporting, insufficient novelty, and poor presentation.

### Strengths
- The task is important: cross-modality medical image domain adaptive segmentation.
- End-to-end framework that integrates domain adaptation and segmentation, facilitating interaction between the two tasks.
- Employment of CLIP to leverage vision-language models for bridging the domain gap in medical images.
- Dual-prompts (domain and segmentation) to learn domain-invariant feature representations.
- Reasonable designs with modular ablation experiments supporting most components.
- Good segmentation performance reported on two public datasets (though reviewer 2 strongly disputes this).

### Weaknesses
1. **Selective / Manipulative Reporting of Baselines (Reviewer 2, strong concern; echoed by Reviewer 1):**  
   - Tables 1 and 2 copy results from FPL+ [Wu et al., TMI 2024] for many methods (w/o DA, Labeled target, ADVENT, SIFA, CUT, AccSeg, HRDA, CDAC, MIC) but **omit** the results of methods that outperform the proposed UUDS, specifically CycleGAN, DAR-NET, and FPL.  
   - For instance, on FLAIR→T2, FPL+ achieves 75.76% Dice vs. UUDS 69.83%; on T2→FLAIR, FPL+ achieves 84.81% vs. UUDS 75.22%. This is a significant drop.  
   - This omission constitutes a serious flaw that undermines the claimed superiority.

2. **Inadequate Comparison with Recent Medical UDA Methods (Reviewer 1, 3):**  
   - Comparisons are limited to UDA methods originally designed for natural images. Missing comparisons with recent medical-domain UDA methods such as FPL-UDA and FPL+.  
   - Reviewer 3 notes that the baseline (lower bound) performance in glioma segmentation already surpasses many comparable methods, raising questions about the benchmark fairness.

3. **Overclaimed Novelty / Lack of Uniqueness (Reviewer 2, 3):**  
   - The claim of a “unified” framework is not new; many pseudo-labeling-based UDA methods (e.g., FPL+) already combine adaptation and segmentation in one stage.  
   - Using two text prompts or sharing features does not sufficiently integrate the two tasks in a novel way.  
   - Uncertainty-regularized pseudo-labeling is a mature technique in semi-supervised learning and not a contribution.  
   - The idea of using CLIP and dual prompts overlaps with a CVPR 2024 paper (Du et al., “Domain-agnostic mutual prompting” for UDA classification) and should be discussed.

4. **Performance Not State-of-the-Art (Reviewer 2):**  
   - Even ignoring the selective reporting, the results of UUDS are close to methods from 2020 (CUT, SIFA), indicating no considerable improvement.  
   - Reviewers 1 and 3 note that the absolute Dice scores are lower than FPL+ baselines (see point 1).

5. **Poor Clarity and Presentation (Reviewer 2, 3):**  
   - Section 3 (Method) is hard to follow and lacks clear architectural details.  
   - Figure 1 is unclear: it does not specify whether CLIP encoders are frozen, the SAM architecture used, or how the generation and segmentation decoders interact.  
   - The related work on UDA (Section 2) is biased, focusing on GAN-based methods while ignoring pseudo-labeling approaches despite using pseudo-labels (Eq. 8).  
   - Many minor errors/typos (e.g., “the largely vision-language mode”, “novel using uncertainty estimation to”, “Dual-prompts leaning” vs. learning, “are typically segmentation target”).  
   - Table 1 and Figure 2 are inconsistent: MIC results appear in Figure 2 but not in Table 1.

6. **Lack of Generalization & Clinical Justification (Reviewer 1, 3):**  
   - Only evaluated on two MRI datasets. Additional datasets (e.g., MMWHS) would support generalization.  
   - The use of text prompts (CLIP) for medical images is not novel; it is unclear whether CLIP accurately captures domain differences for medical data. Alternatives like medical-specific prompts or one-hot codes should be explored.  
   - The concept of “domain-invariant feature” is not clinically justified; without unique clinical design, more diverse datasets (e.g., natural images) should be included.

7. **Insufficient Discussion of Foundation Models (Reviewer 3):**  
   - No comparison or discussion with other foundation models for cross-modal segmentation (e.g., SynthSeg, SAM in medical imaging).  
   - The performance of image synthesis (if any) and its usefulness for MRI sequence transformation is not evaluated.

8. **Minor Questions / Requests (Reviewer 2):**  
   - Domain prompts use only “A photo of a source domain” (page 7). Why not also input a target domain prompt and maximize cosine similarity with target image features while minimizing with source (an alternative form of Eq. 1, L<sub>DD</sub>)?  
   - Reviewer 3 requests improving contribution statements, clarifying baseline performance, improving Figure 1, and discussing prompt usage more extensively.
