## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends contrastive learning (CL) to the SPD manifold for visual classification, introducing a SymCL framework that applies CL on SPD matrices using a Riemannian distance. To improve discrimination, a Riemannian triplet loss (RML) is added. Experiments are conducted on four datasets (AFEW, MDSD, YTC, Virus). Two reviewers (ratings 5,5) find the paper well-written and the idea clear, with some positive performance. Two reviewers (ratings 3,3) see very limited novelty and weak experiments, and question the motivation and practical impact. The majority reviewer opinion is that the paper combines existing ideas (SPD features + CL) without significant technical advancement.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow, with a clear background on SPD manifolds (Reviewers 1, 3).
- It attempts to apply contrastive learning on the SPD manifold and proposes four data augmentation techniques adapted for SPD networks (Reviewers 2, 4).
- A Riemannian triplet loss (RML) is introduced to enhance discrimination (Reviewer 4).
- Experiments cover multiple tasks (Reviewer 1), and some results show good performance (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The method is a straightforward combination of existing SPD networks and CL. The original CL loss is applied directly on SPD manifolds by replacing Euclidean similarity with Riemannian distances, without clear structural or algorithmic changes (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The RML loss shares a similar concept with InfoNCE (both pull positives together and push negatives apart), yet the paper does not explain why they are complementary (Reviewer 2). One reviewer notes Figure 1 shows the method is simply RML + CL (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear motivation:** Why specifically contrastive learning for SPD manifolds? The paper mentions label noise as a motivation, but label noise is not a special issue for SPD manifolds, and no analysis is given of how the SPD geometry helps in that regard (Reviewer 3). The motivation for introducing SPD into CL is not provided convincingly (Reviewer 4). The practical application and how SPD contrastive learning would help the community remain vague (Reviewer 3).
- **Weak experimental validation:** The experiments are limited to only four datasets, some of which are considered out of date (Reviewer 3). Performance improvements are not significant (Reviewer 2). On MDSD and AFEW datasets, results are inferior to those reported in work [1] (Wang et al., 2022), and this discrepancy is not clearly discussed (Reviewer 2). On the Virus dataset, the paper explains poor performance by saying a lightweight SymNet is more suitable, but this suggests the backbone heavily affects results. Thus, a fair comparison using the same basic backbone is needed, but not provided (Reviewer 2). No comparison to state-of-the-art methods is included (Reviewers 1, 2) – only some ablation studies are given (Reviewer 1). The paper claims traditional data augmentation is unsuitable for SPD networks but provides no baseline comparison (Reviewer 2). Many experimental details are missing: which dataset was used for pre-training, the detailed setup of the pre-training phase, whether the entire network or only a classifier is trained on downstream tasks, parameter numbers of different methods, experimental environment configuration (Reviewers 2, 4). Sensitivity analysis and convergence analysis are absent (Reviewer 4). Visualization effects (e.g., embedding quality) are not provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of clear experimental description:** The algorithm flow (inputs, outputs) is not given, and definitions of some letter variables are missing (Reviewer 4). The four data augmentation methods are not considered a major contribution and their description is insufficient (Reviewer 4).
- **Disagreement between reviewers:** While two reviewers (ratings 5) see the paper as decent and well-conducted, two others (ratings 3) consider the experiments weak, the novelty very low, and the motivation unconvincing. The more negative reviewers stress that no SOTA comparisons exist and the method is essentially a trivial replacement of the similarity metric.
