## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes TNT, a table-language model that treats tabular data as a distinct modality with a BERT-size encoder, a table-language adaptor, and an LLM decoder. The training pipeline includes encoder pre-training, multi-task feature alignment (with novel contrastive objectives), and instruction tuning. Experiments on three text-to-SQL datasets (Spider, Spider-DK, Spider-Realistic) show improvements over standard supervised fine-tuning, with up to 14.4% higher execution accuracy compared to text-based serialization. However, the reviewers strongly disagree on the paper’s scope, significance, and generalizability.

### Strengths

- **Motivation and problem framing**: The paper addresses the important challenge of enabling LLMs to understand table structure and semantics, which is not solved by larger context windows. The idea of encoding tables as a separate modality is interesting (R1, R4). The method can potentially handle huge tables regardless of context size (R1).
- **Training pipeline and objectives**: The three-stage pipeline (pre-training, alignment, instruction tuning) is well explained and inspiring (R1). The multi-task feature alignment covers potential usage for multiple common table-related tasks (R2). The self-supervised objectives (e.g., column-wise contrastive learning) are novel (R2). Significant training effort is demonstrated (R3). Ablation studies help understand the contribution of each component (R3, R4).
- **Performance on NL2SQL**: Improvements over the SFT baseline are reported across three Spider variants. For example, TNT achieves 79.0% on Spider test vs. 78.2% for SFT (Table 2). The largest gain is on non-semantic column names (Spider-DK and Spider-Realistic), where TNT outperforms SFT by up to 2.1% (R1, R3). One reviewer (R4, rating 8) finds the paper well-written and the motivation for non-semantic columns sound.

### Weaknesses

- **Very limited task and dataset scope**: All experiments are confined to three text-to-SQL datasets (Spider, Spider-DK, Spider-Realistic). No evaluations on table QA, table-to-text, table fact verification, or table-based analysis generation (R1, R2, R3). The BIRD benchmark is omitted (R1, R3). Despite claiming “bridging the semantic gap between text and table,” the paper does not test on benchmarks with hierarchical tables like HiTab or AIT-QA, which would be necessary to validate bi-directional table claims (R2, R4 Q3). Reviewers R1, R2, R3, and R4 all question the scope.
- **Small performance improvements over SFT**: Gains are often marginal (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct: 73.0 → 73.2 on Spider dev; CodeLlama-7B: 66.5 → 67.6 dev, 73.9 → 74.6 test; Llama-2-7B: 67.5 → 68.0 dev, 72.8 → 73.5 test). This makes the statistical significance of the improvement unclear (R1, R3). Reviewer R4 notes that GPT-3.5 with chain-of-thought or self-consistency achieves similar or better performance on the Spider leaderboard without special training.
- **Low overall performance compared to existing methods**: On the Spider test set, TNT (79.0%) is below the 3B-parameter RESDSQL+NatSQL (79.9%, AAAI 2023) and far below the leaderboard best (91.2%). Even when using an 8B model, the method is not competitive with smaller, specialized models (R1, R3, R4). The major claimed benefit (non-semantic column names) may be addressable by simpler techniques like adding schemas or retrieving relevant documents at inference time, especially with more advanced models such as GPT-4 (R4).
- **Missing important baselines**: The baselines are limited to out-of-the-box checkpoints and two training variants (SFT and TNT). No comparison with other general table-oriented LMs such as TableGPT or TableLLaMa is provided (R2). Without these, the effectiveness of the TNT training recipe is not convincingly demonstrated (R2).
- **Methodological questions and missing ablations**:
    - The metric “Ex(%)” in Figure 1 and what “set1” and “set2” refer to are not defined (R4 Q1).
    - The contrastive learning positive/negative pair construction is unclear (R4 Q2).
    - The datasets used for instruction tuning (Section 4.3) and the distribution of pre-training data (especially anonymized column prediction samples) are not specified (R4 Q4, Q5).
    - In Table 2, what “original” means (few-shot inference or prompting?) is ambiguous; this is critical for comparing to Spider leaderboard results (R4 Q6).
    - Table 3 shows puzzling mismatches between dev and test performance when ablating components (e.g., IT-only: 31.7 dev vs 50.5 test; FA+IT: 31.9 dev vs 53.4 test). No explanation is provided (R4 Q7).
    - An ablation study on the hybrid table representation (dtype) is missing; it is unclear if “dtype” is essential for NL2SQL (R4 Q8).
- **Insufficient referencing**: Relevant papers on table reasoning (Chain-of-Table, TAP4LLM, StructGPT, Versatile Decomposers) are not cited (R4). The Spider leaderboard top methods are not discussed in related work (R3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R4) rated the paper higher (8) and found the idea interesting and the paper well-written, while the other three (ratings 5, 5, 6) stress that the scope is too narrow, improvements are marginal, and performance is below existing methods even from 2023. The more positive reviewer still raises significant concerns about the method’s advantages over simpler alternatives and the missing ablations.
