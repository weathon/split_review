## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers agree the paper studies induction and transduction for ARC, using a synthetic dataset generated by LLMs to train neural models. They note the finding that induction and transduction solve different problems and that ensembling them achieves strong performance. Reviewers 1 and 4 are highly positive (8/8), citing conceptual interest, novel combination, and compelling results. Reviewer 3 is moderately positive (6/8) but wants deeper analysis. Reviewer 2 is strongly negative (3/8), calling the work a technical report lacking insight and validation.

### Strengths
1. **Conceptual study of induction vs. transduction** – Reviewers 1 and 4 find the investigation of which problems each method solves to be novel and of great conceptual interest, and likely to spur further research in abstract reasoning. (R1, R4)
2. **Synthetic dataset generation pipeline** – Valuable and independently interesting resource produced by LLM + manual efforts. (R1, R3)
3. **Strong performance on ARC** – Achieves 40% accuracy with LLaMA 3.1, matching or beating stronger models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.5. (R1) – noted as a “noteworthy demonstration” (R4).
4. **Clear, well-written presentation** – Illustrations are informative; formulas are clearly defined. (R1, R3, R4)
5. **Complementarity finding** – The observation that induction and transduction solve largely disjoint problems is intriguing and valuable. (R4)
6. **Diverse experimental design** – Experiments thoroughly evaluate both approaches. (R2, R3)
7. **Simplicity and broad applicability** – The approach is straightforward and can be applied to other tasks. (R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing principled understanding / categorization** – What distinguishes induction-suitable from transduction-suitable problems is not analyzed in depth; Figure 6 provides insufficient insight. Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 all call for more detailed analysis of why the separation occurs. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
2. **Narrow validation on ARC only** – All tasks are grid-world rather than real-world; conclusions may not generalize. Reviewer 2 calls this “technical report”-level work; Reviewer 3 questions whether findings would hold on other real-world datasets; Reviewer 4 notes this as a limitation but agrees ARC is challenging. (R2, R3, R4)
3. **Ensemble method is insufficiently explained** – The sequential switching (use induction first, then transduction if no fit) lacks justification; it is unclear why induction is given precedence. Reviewer 2 calls for elaboration; Reviewer 4 asks whether a learned meta-function could blend them instead. (R2, R4)
4. **Definitional blurring of induction vs. transduction** – The paper conditions the hypothesis distribution on the test input x_test, which classical Vapnikian transduction would not. This undermines the claimed distinction. (R1)
5. **Data quality concerns** – LLMs hallucinate; no explicit quality control on the generated synthetic dataset is described. (R3)
6. **Limited theoretical analysis** – No formal theory or analysis beyond experimental results. Reviewer 2 finds this a severe weakness, rating soundness 2. (R2)
7. **Weak presentation of contributions** – Abstract does not summarize how induction and transduction are combined nor gives a specific statement of differences; paper reads more like a technical report than an insightful contribution (Reviewer 2, rating presentation 1). (R2)
8. **Missing detail on input representation** – How ARC grids (visual examples) are encoded as text for LLaMA 3.1 (a text-only model) is not referenced or explained. (R1)
9. **Lack of guidelines for practitioners** – No guidelines to determine when to use induction vs. transduction, which is what the community would find useful. (R2)
10. **Overclaim in title** – “Combining” is not sufficiently elaborated; the ensemble is only briefly described in Section 5. (R2)

*Minority contrast:* Reviewer 2 rates the paper 3/8 and considers it a “technical report around the ARC challenge” that “doesn’t bring significant insights,” while Reviewers 1 and 4 rate it 8/8 and find it conceptually interesting and valuable. Reviewer 3 sits in between (6/8). This disagreement primarily centers on depth of analysis and generalizability.
