## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes X-CLR, a contrastive loss that replaces binary positive/negative labels with soft targets in [0,1], derived from external similarity sources such as metadata or text embeddings of image captions. The authors experiment with vision models trained on ImageNet-1k, CC3M, and CC12M, using frozen sentence transformers to build the similarity graph. X-CLR is shown to outperform standard contrastive methods (SimCLR, SupCon, CLIP) on classification and background decomposition tasks, with notable gains in low-data regimes (e.g., +17.2% on ImageNet over CLIP when training on CC3M). The work is positioned as a general framework unifying self-supervised, multi-modal, and distillation-style objectives by varying the similarity source.

### Strengths
- Intuitive and clean generalization: the paper identifies the limitation of binary positive/negative similarity in contrastive learning and offers a straightforward soft-target relaxation (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Comprehensive experimental suite covering multiple datasets (ImageNet, CC3M, CC12M), ablations on similarity sources (e.g., Table 2, random vs. metadata vs. augmentation vs. true class graphs), and practical experiments such as finetuning and noisy similarity data (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Unifying view: X-CLR brings together SSL, multi-modal learning, and distillation methods under a single formulation, which is helpful for defining generalized training methods (Reviewer 2).
- Effective even with simple/small text encoders: the ablation shows that a strong text encoder is not necessary for good performance; simpler sentence transformers suffice (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well written, with a clear explanation of the problem and proposed solution; the experiment descriptions are concise and the paper is enjoyable to read (Reviewers 1 partly, 3 strongly, 4). (Note: Reviewer 1 found some presentation issues—see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and theoretical depth**:  
  The core idea of using soft targets in a contrastive loss is not new; the method is a simple replacement of binary labels with soft labels derived from a pretrained model (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). There is no theoretical analysis of convergence properties, impact on the learned representation space, or generalizability beyond the tested datasets (Reviewer 4). The “Theorem 1” is merely a matrix re-expression and not a true theorem (Reviewer 1).

- **Unfair comparisons and missing baselines**:  
  Tables 1, 3, and 4 compare X-CLR against SSL methods like SimCLR that do not have access to labels or pretrained text encoders. This is misleading because X-CLR uses both labels and a frozen text encoder (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The reported SimCLR accuracy (63.4% on ImageNet) is far below its published result (69.3%), and SupCon accuracy (74.3%) is also below the published 78.7% (Reviewer 1). These discrepancies question the fairness of the comparisons.  
  In line 354, the paper claims X-CLR is more general than distillation methods, but no comparison is made to state-of-the-art CLIP distillation methods such as TinyCLIP or MobileCLIP (Reviewer 2).

- **Dependency on external resources and computational overhead**:  
  The method requires both additional metadata (e.g., class labels, captions) and a pretrained text encoder to generate pairwise similarities. This limits applicability when such resources are unavailable, and the computational overhead of the text encoder is not factored into the overhead analysis in Section 4.5 (Reviewers 2, 4). A suggestion is made that deriving the soft graph without external models would improve applicability (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing analysis and clarifications**:  
  - **CLIP setup**: It is unclear whether the CLIP baseline is trained jointly or uses a pretrained model, and whether X-CLR with a frozen text encoder is equivalent to standard CLIP training (Reviewer 1).  
  - **Similarity matrix for ImageNet**: Using class labels to generate captions and then text embeddings yields a matrix where same-class similarity is 1 and cross-class similarities are non-zero. The paper does not analyze how this relates to SupCon, which also uses class labels (Reviewer 1).  
  - **Imbalanced data and batch size sensitivity**: No experiments are conducted on imbalanced datasets (e.g., ImageNet-LT) where rare instances would have lower similarities (Reviewer 3). The effect of batch size on X-CLR is also unexplored—standard contrastive learning is sensitive to batch size, but X-CLR might mitigate this; the paper does not investigate (Reviewer 3).  
  - **Image-text vs. image-only SSL**: The pseudocode in Figure 1b is only valid for paired image-text data and relies on detaching the text encoder to prevent collapse. The paper does not clearly separate the two settings or define a proper two-way loss (image2text and text2image terms) for multi-modal training (Reviewer 2).

- **Presentation inconsistencies**:  
  Some wording issues and a need for more detailed table captions (e.g., specifying that numbers are accuracy, explaining column meanings) (Reviewer 1). In Figure 3, the data scarcity plot (3a) shows SupCon outperforming X-CLR even though the text claims the opposite; also the coloring for SupCon and SimCLR is flipped between 3a and 3b (Reviewer 3). The paragraph for Table 4 does not mention that training is done on CC3M (Reviewer 3). Only one network architecture is tested, although a justification is given (Reviewer 1).

- **Reviewer disagreement**:  
  While most reviewers raise multiple weaknesses, Reviewer 3 finds no significant weaknesses, praising the paper’s writing and experiments highly. This contrast is reflected throughout the review.
