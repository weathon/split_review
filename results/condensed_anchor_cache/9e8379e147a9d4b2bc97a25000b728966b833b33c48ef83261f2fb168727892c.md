## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the NUPA benchmark for evaluating numerical understanding and processing ability in LLMs. The benchmark covers four number representations, 17 tasks (e.g., addition, subtraction, modulo, max, min, digit retrieval), and 41 combinations, with difficulty levels based on digit length. The authors evaluate nine off-the-shelf LLMs, showing frequent failures especially on larger numbers. They train small models from scratch to study the effects of tokenization, positional encodings (RoPE, Alibi), and number formats (e.g., reverse order). They also fine-tune LLaMA-3 8B with and without chain-of-thought (CoT). Findings: naive finetuning improves many but not all tasks; techniques designed for numeracy are ineffective during finetuning; CoT helps only modestly.

### Strengths
- The benchmark is comprehensive, covering a wide range of numerical tasks and difficulty levels, with good metrics for evaluating performance. (R1, R3, R4)  
- The evaluation procedure is well-structured, and some tasks provide useful insights into model failure modes. (R2)  
- The paper goes beyond description by investigating methods to improve numerical reasoning, such as tokenization, positional encodings, number formats, and CoT. (R1)  
- The tokenization analysis provides interesting empirical evidence that 1-digit tokenization yields stronger generalization. (R1)  
- The experiments are extensive, testing generalization, architectural changes (tokenizers, PEs), scale, existing models, and prompting. (R3)  
- Reviewer 3 (score 8, clearly more positive) views this as a potentially important contribution, provided the weaknesses are addressed. (R3)  
- Reviewer 4 appreciates the thoughtful curation and integrity of the benchmark, and values the CoT investigation, noting it aligns with broader findings about numeracy not scaling. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related work and poor attribution:** The paper lacks a related work section and has very few references. It fails to cite foundational works for methods it directly adopts: Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al.), LoRA (Hu et al.), and prior studies on tokenization’s impact on numerical reasoning (e.g., [9] in R2). Claims of novelty (e.g., “initial step towards understanding NUPA”) are overly strong. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient justification and normative claims:** Several rationales are unsupported or flawed. The claim that multiplication is O(L²) in digit length ignores that human mental multiplication relies on O(1) lookup and additive decomposition. The statement that NUPAs “are necessary and expected to learn by LLMs” and that tasks should be accomplished by any primary/secondary graduate are unsupported. The assertion that only the right answer matters for practical tasks is not backed. (R4) The discussion of innateness (lines 116–127) is out of scope. (R3)
- **Presentation and writing quality:** The paper has frequent grammatical errors. Sections 2.1 and 2.2 contain unnecessary discussion (e.g., how fractions arise) that could be cut to improve result presentation. Figure 2 (and similar appendix figures) is hard to read: colors are too similar, text is too small (requires 200% zoom), and it prematurely includes finetuning results discussed later. Excessive negative `\vspace` causes cramped spacing (pages 7, 9). Table 2 with finetuning results is introduced before the relevant discussion. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Missing training details for key experiments:** The experiments in Sections 3.1 and 3.2 (training small models) lack information on dataset size, number of training examples, hyperparameters, optimizer, learning rate schedule, number of repeats, and how best checkpoint is selected. The definition of in-domain vs. out-of-domain is unclear. Without these details, it is impossible to assess overfitting, whether models genuinely learn numerical concepts, or to reproduce the results. (R3)  
  *Contrast: Reviewer 3 is most critical here; other reviewers noted vagueness but not as central.*
- **Lack of statistical reporting:** No confidence intervals, p-values, or error bars are provided for any results. (R1)
- **Over-interpretation of results:** The attribution of digit retrieval difficulty to lack of digit concept is not evidenced; it could be a sequence-length issue unrelated to understanding. The claim that performance depends on training approach over size is not justified; architecture could also play a role. The statement that models failing on digit tasks is “surprising” ignores well-known tokenization limitations. (R3, R4)
- **Insufficient argument that the problem is worth solving:** The paper does not convincingly show that NUPA is a problem requiring deep investment, given that calculators, Python, or LLM code-generation can trivially solve these tasks. (R4)  
  *Reviewer 4 is most skeptical on this point; other reviewers assume the problem is important.*
- **Missing potential approach:** Few-shot prompting, which might significantly boost performance and prime output format, is not explored. (R3)
- **Open questions / missing details from reviews:**
  - How is ground truth reasoning obtained for rule-following fine-tuning (RFFT)? (R1)  
  - Provide details of the RFFT fine-tuning process (potentially in appendix). (R2)  
  - Why is NUPA important when LLMs can delegate to Python? (R4)  
  - To what extent do findings suggest the issues are architectural/paradigmatic rather than technical? (R4)  
  - How much compute (estimate) was used throughout? (R4)
