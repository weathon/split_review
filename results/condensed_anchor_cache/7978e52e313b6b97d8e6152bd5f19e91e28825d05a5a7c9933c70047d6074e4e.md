## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis of self-supervised learning (SSL) for vision transformers (ViTs), comparing masked autoencoders (MAE) with contrastive learning (CL). The authors model visual data as having dominant global features and minuscule local features, then analyze training dynamics of a one-layer softmax-based ViT under gradient descent. They show that MAE learns both global and local features, while CL focuses predominantly on global features, even under mild imbalance. The work aims to give rigorous theoretical explanation for empirically observed behavioral differences between MAE and CL in ViTs. The analysis also identifies two phases for learning feature-position correlations when an information gap is positive.

### Strengths
- Novel theoretical framework for analyzing SSL in ViTs, filling a gap in a primarily empirical field. (R2, R3, R4)
- Extensive, well-formulated, and thorough proofs; solid step toward understanding detailed mechanism differences. (R1, R4)
- Effectively illustrates and explains why MAE captures diverse local patterns while CL focuses on dominant global patterns, supporting empirical observations. (R2, R3)
- Detailed gradient descent analysis of a single-layer transformer yields concrete findings on how MAE and CL converge to distinct attention patterns, ensuring reliability and reproducibility. (R3)
- Framework simplifies analysis by avoiding patch disentanglement and positional encoding, providing an easy-to-understand problem setup. (R2, R4)
- Findings can help future researchers develop new approaches based on these theoretical insights. (R4)

### Weaknesses
**Note:** Reviewer 4 (score 8) was considerably more positive than the other reviewers (scores 5–6), but still raised important concerns. Conversely, Reviewer 2 was particularly critical of simplifications and practical relevance.

- **Simplified model limits realism:** The analysis considers only a single self-attention layer with a single weight matrix, and uses SGD rather than the Adam optimizer commonly used for ViTs. This cannot fully explain the complex behavior of deeper or multi-layer transformers, and may overlook dynamics of SSL in more practical networks. (R2, R3) Reviewer 3 specifically asked how the dynamics might differ in deeper architectures and whether current proof techniques generalize. (R3)

- **Strong assumptions with unclear practical relevance:** Many assumptions are made about input data distribution, positional encoding, and that the dominant concept occupies most of the input area. The orthonormality assumption (line 193) is not justified. For real datasets like ImageNet, average object-to-image-area ratio is 0.35, meaning background (dominant area) would dominate attention under this theory – contradictory to high kNN performance of DINO and MoCo. The theory’s validity for datasets where global area is small or local areas are larger than global is unclear. (R1, R2)

- **Insufficient experimental validation:** The paper lacks serious experiments to support theoretical claims. Experiments alluded to (e.g., “we observe that the selected area does not necessarily depend on the location of p”, line 377) are not shown. There is no measurement or visualization of the information gap (e.g., bounds, range, histograms for different token/patch sizes or datasets) despite central assumptions about its range. The abstract mentions “analysis” but not “convergence guarantees,” misleading expectations of extensive experimentation. (R1)

- **Focus on separate MAE and CL, not combined methods:** State-of-the-art methods like SiT, iBoT, DINOv2 combine both MIM and CL, a strategy that repeatedly proves superior. Building a theoretical framework for the combined setting would be more fruitful and practically relevant than analyzing MAE and CL in isolation. (R2)

- **Missing quantitative guidance for hyperparameters:** The analysis does not provide a clear, precise ratio of positive to negative samples required for optimal convergence and to prevent collapse (e.g., as in MoCo). This lack makes it difficult to translate theoretical findings into practical guidelines. (R4)

- **Assumption about cluster structure:** The assumption that the same cluster exists across different \(\mathcal{D}_k\) (Sec. 2.2, Fig. 2) may not capture nuances of how MAE and CL operate – for CL, positive pairs come from the same image; for MAE, local features are key. While not invalidating the proof, this limits generalizability to more complex scenarios where cluster structures vary across data subsets. (R4)

- **Presentation issues:** Key findings from Sec. B (e.g., Fig. 4 and explanations) should appear in the main paper instead of the appendix. Sec. 2.3 and 2.4 contain overlapping concepts and could be shortened to make room. (R4) Notation is also unclear: what are \(k_s\) and \(\Delta\)? For \(\Delta=-1,0,1\), what does each value mean? (R2) Minor: In Sec. 2.1 first paragraph, should \(\mathcal{M}_i \subset \mathcal{P}\) instead of \(\mathcal{M}_i \subset [P]\)? (R4)

- **No insight for preventing attention collapse in CL:** The theory does not offer clear guidance on how to avoid attention collapse in contrastive learning or develop new methods. (R2) 

- **Unaddressed questions:** How does the required ratio of positive to negative samples emerge from the proof? (R4) What happens when local areas are larger than global? (R2)
