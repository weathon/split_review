## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies optimization and generalization of infinitely deep and wide ResNets in the mean-field limit. The results – convergence via eigenvalue lower bound on a Gram matrix and a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound using KL divergence – are considered technically challenging and interesting by two reviewers (ratings 8,8), who note the novel approach and clear presentation. The third reviewer (rating 5) finds the practical relevance limited due to the restrictive r-regime scaling and absence of experiments. Key points of disagreement center on whether the dynamics truly exhibit feature learning (Reviewers 1,2 question reliance on the last-layer Gram matrix; Reviewer 2 notes the generalization bound’s requirement that weights stay near initialization), overstatement of novelty in the literature, and the need for empirical validation.

### Strengths
- Studies the challenging mean-field regime (feature learning) for deep ResNets, avoiding entropy regularization; combines mean-field analysis of infinitely deep/wide ResNet with estimation of the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK matrix to derive convergence rates (Reviewer 1). Technically challenging and skill-demanding work (Reviewer 3).
- Strong population risk bound for trained deep networks under reasonably mild assumptions (Reviewer 2). Technically solid (Reviewer 2). Unified presentation with three well-separated contributions (Reviewer 2). The idea of bounding Rademacher complexity via a bound on the KL divergence from initialization is novel (Reviewer 2).
- Well-organized, clear, and easy-to-follow presentation (Reviewers 1, 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Feature learning concerns (Reviewers 1,2).**  
  Reviewer 1: convergence relies on positivity of \(G_2\) (Gram matrix for the last layer), not \(G_1\); the property may reflect only last-layer optimization. The theory does not appear to work when the last layer is frozen, unlike many two-layer mean-field analyses. Reviewer 1 requests proof that feature learning occurs.  
  Reviewer 2: the generalization bound requires the KL-ball radius \(r\) to decrease as \(O(1/n)\); the weight distribution must thus stay increasingly close to initialization as \(n\) grows. This regime may not correspond to feature learning, contradicting the paper’s claim of “first generalization bound in the feature learning regime” – more caution or explanation is needed.
- **Role of the ResNet part (Reviewer 2).**  
  The convergence proof lower-bounds the smallest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix only for the final two-layer network; there is no guarantee on the Gram matrix of the infinite-depth ResNet part. If the proof would still hold with the ResNet part fixed or removed, this should be stated clearly, as it questions the prominence given to the ResNet architecture.
- **Literature overstatement and missing references.**  
  Reviewer 2: Section 4 overstates novelty by claiming “first convergence rate with mild assumptions”; other papers prove global convergence for deep residual networks (arXiv:1910.02934, arXiv:2204.07261, arXiv:2309.01213). Also, Barboni et al. do not “assume” parameters stay near initialization; they prove it. Please rephrase.  
  Reviewer 1: missing reference – Z. Chen, E. Vanden-Eijnden, J. Bruna, “On feature learning in neural networks with global convergence guarantees.”
- **Practical meaning and scaling of the r-regime (Reviewer 3).**  
  The r-region limits training time; taking large \(n\) while not giving the network enough time to learn many points is questionable. Additionally, the claimed bound \(R_n(F) = \beta \sqrt{r/n}\) becomes \(O(1)\) when \(\beta\) scales larger than \(\sqrt{n}\), not \(1/\sqrt{n}\). Reviewer 3 requests clarification on how \(r\) should scale with \(t_{\max}\) and \(n\), and how training error decreases with \(r\).
- **Lack of experiments / bounds’ tightness.**  
  Reviewer 3: results do not reflect task complexity; it is hard to believe the bounds give order-of-magnitude estimates for real networks that generalize well. Numerical experiments (even on toy data) are needed to show accuracy of the bounds. Reviewer 2 also raises related questions about practical scaling.
- **Assumption about deterministic labels (Reviewer 2).**  
  The output \(y\) is assumed a deterministic function of \(x\); can this be relaxed to include noisy labels?
- **Minor technical issues (Reviewer 2).**  
  Typos: “nerual” (p.3). Notation errors: \(\tau\) and \(\nu\) swapped (p.2); \(D\) should be \(D_n\) in equations (11),(14),(15); \(\Lambda\) undefined (should be \(\Lambda(d)\)?); Lemma B.2: \(N\) should be \(n\); Lemma B.7: infinite norm undefined. Duplicated references (e.g., Chizat and Bach, Ding et al.). The scaling \(1/L\) in the ResNet departs from practice and should be mentioned with citations. First paragraph of Section 2.1 is unclear. Sentence “We introduce a trainable MLP … zero initialization of the network’s prediction” (p.4) is unclear. In Theorem 4.10/4.11, \(r_0\) is defined twice.
