## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PB-LLM, a partial binarization method for LLMs that identifies a small fraction of salient weights and preserves them at higher bit-width (8-bit) while binarizing the rest (1-bit). It extends the approach to Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) via Hessian-guided weight reconstruction (GPTQ-PB) and to Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) via optimal scaling factors and salient weight freezing. Experiments on LLaMA-7B and OPT-1.3B show competitive results in perplexity and reasoning accuracy compared to full-precision and other quantization baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-motivated, and easy to follow, with a clear structure from preliminaries to proposed methods.
- It introduces binarization into LLM compression, pushing quantization to ultra-low bits, which is an important problem for memory efficiency.
- The approach analytically identifies non-salient weights using Hessian information (PTQ) or magnitude (QAT) and applies binarization selectively, demonstrating feasibility and performance gains.
- The PTQ ablation study (Table 1) effectively shows the efficacy of partial binarization and Hessian-based identification.
- The QAT method achieves higher accuracy with fewer training steps than LLM-QAT on CSQA tasks.
- Source code is provided, enabling reproducibility; reported results are impressive.
- Exploring quantization (and algorithmic approximation) of LLMs for memory/storage efficiency is a promising research direction.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation tasks and models**: Only LLaMA-7B and OPT-1.3B are studied; larger scales (e.g., 13B–65B) are not tested. The impact of the percentage of salient weights across model sizes is missing, questioning generalizability. Only pre-trained base models are evaluated; fine-tuned models (e.g., Alpaca, Vicuna) are not considered, limiting practical relevance. Evaluation is confined to CSQA tasks; for OPT-1.3B some CSQA subtasks (OBQA, ARC) have FP performance below random baseline, making fine-tuning effectiveness unclear. Multi-task evaluations like MMLU are needed. *[Reviewers 2, 3, 4]*
- **Novelty of optimal scaling factor**: One reviewer argues that the proposed optimal scaling factor (Eqs. 8–9) is essentially the same as the ternary function from Li et al. (Ternary Weight Networks, Eq. 3) with threshold set to zero, reducing to a binary function. This raises concerns about contribution. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Inconsistent salient weight identification across PTQ and QAT**: PTQ uses Hessian-based saliency, while QAT uses magnitude-based saliency. The rationale for this discrepancy is not explained. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Missing GPU memory/compression data**: The paper motivates PB-LLM for GPU memory efficiency, but no actual memory usage or model size after partial binarization is reported for PB-LLM or baselines. *[Reviewers 3, 4]*
- **Insufficient comparison and experimental details for QAT efficiency**: The claim that PB-LLM is more efficient (fewer training iterations) than LLM-QAT needs verification: it is unclear whether LLM-QAT was fine-tuned on the same dataset. The FP fine-tuning upper bound is missing from Figure 7, and it is not specified whether FP LLaMA-7B in Table 2 is pre-trained or fine-tuned. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Missing comparisons**: Performance of AWQ-PB under PTQ is not reported. Comparison with other low-bit QAT methods like QuantGPT (2-bit) and TSLD is not provided. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Clarity and presentation issues**: The algorithm section is comprehensive but complex, making it difficult to navigate; a flowchart or block diagram would help. *[Reviewer 4]*. There are minor typos (“bianrize” → “binarize” in Sec 3.3), inconsistent notation (LLaMA vs LLaMa in Sec 4.1), an incorrect reference link (“refer to 3.2” should be “refer to 3.4”), and spacing errors in Figure 7 caption. *[Reviewer 2]*
- **Practicality of QAT for LLMs**: The substantial computational resources (GPUs, training time, energy) required for QAT comparably to standard training or other quantization methods are not discussed. *[Reviewer 1]*
- **Generalization of optimal scaling to other bit configurations**: The paper only applies optimal scaling to the specific case; it is unclear how it extends to other bit-widths for salient weights and what the trade-off would be between compression and accuracy. *[Reviewer 1]*
- **Clarification of GPTQ-PB vs PB-LLM**: It is unclear whether GPTQ-PB is a separate proposed method or the same as PB-LLM; algorithmic differences and implementation choices need elaboration. *[Reviewer 1]*
