## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a self-supervised anomaly detection method, CON₂, that uses context-aware augmentations (transformations satisfying distinctiveness and alignment) to learn normal data representations. It trains an AutoEncoder with contrastive losses to cluster augmented samples by context while aligning them, and detects anomalies via kNN and Mahalanobis distance. Evaluations are on CIFAR-10 and medical datasets (e.g., CheXpert, OCT2017). Scores are 5, 5, 3, 5, indicating generally positive reception but one reviewer (R3, score 3, confidence 4) is substantially more critical about novelty and practical advantages.

### Strengths
- Mitigates bias from synthetic anomalies by using only normal data for training; no anomalous data required (R1).
- Leverages AutoEncoder and contrastive learning to extract multi-perspective information (R1).
- The desiderata for augmentations (distinctiveness and alignment) is novel (R2).
- Figure 3 is a clear illustration of the main technical point (R2).
- Strong results compared to other SSL methods (R2).
- Simple method that may be easy for practitioners (R4).
- Experiments on medical images show improvement over existing contrastive anomaly detection methods (R4).
- Proposes a new approach with context augmentations (R3); positive mention of medical experiments (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and unclear advantage over existing augmentations:** One reviewer (R3) finds novelty limited and advantage over standard augmentation-based AD (flip, invert, equalize) unclear. Another (R4) says differences between context and ordinary transformations are not clearly explained.
- **Marginal performance gains in many cases:** In Figure 4, UniCon-HA outperforms CON₂ on 4 out of 11 classes (Bird, Deer, Horse, Ship); overall gains are marginal (R1). No statistical tests or uncertainty estimates (e.g., multiple seeds) are provided (R1).
- **Missing comparisons to important baselines:** Only one non-SSL baseline is compared, and not the most standard one (R2). Need comparison with density ratio estimation, autoencoder-based, and SVDD-based methods (R4). Also need more pre-trained-based outlier detection methods (e.g., Cohen & Avidan 2022; Reiss & Hoshen 2023; Li et al. 2023) (R2).
- **Insufficient datasets in main results:** Main table only shows two datasets (R2). Request to extend to more medical datasets (ChestX-ray14, HAM10000) (R2).
- **Sensitivity to context augmentation choice:** No systematic method for selecting augmentations; wrong choice can degrade performance below baselines (Table 1) (R1). How to choose appropriate transformations for a given application? (R3). What happens when all transformations are included? (R3). Hyperparameter tuning (alpha, choice between LH/NND) is not guided (R3). Practitioners cannot determine hyperparameters with only normal data (R4).
- **Second anomaly score design is questionable:** The average over A/2 intermediate values may dilute the signal; a max-based score could be more appropriate (R1). The symbol “∘” in the score equation is unexplained (R1).
- **Ablation study is incomplete:** Missing: (1) using L_context or L_content alone, (2) each anomaly score’s individual contribution (R1). Also missing: effect of fixing alpha at 0 or 1 (R3). The linear annealing of alpha from 0 to 1 is not justified (R3).
- **Misleading claim about no prior knowledge:** The paper states “without assuming prior knowledge about anomalies” but priors are always needed (inductive biases from augmentations) (R2).
- **Writing and presentation issues:** Figure 1 is confusing (R2) and not referred to early; loss function mentioned in caption without reference (R1). Figures 2 and 3 misplaced (R1). Equations lack numbers (R1). Reviewers also ask to clarify the “∘” symbol, explain the second anomaly score in text, and add breakdown of precision/recall/F1 (R1).
- **Application domain limited:** No evidence that method applies to non-image domains (R4).
