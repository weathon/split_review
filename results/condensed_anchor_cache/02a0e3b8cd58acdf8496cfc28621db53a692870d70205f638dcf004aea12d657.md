## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Dual-level Bias Mitigation (DBM) to address fairness in fine-tuning pre-trained models. DBM combines Representation Neutralization for Fairness (RNF) with R-Divergence: RNF neutralizes sensitive information at the representation level via representation mixing (adopted from Du et al. 2021), and R-Divergence is used as a task-level regularization term to reduce distributional discrepancies between demographic groups. The paper also provides a theoretical generalization error bound for the fairness-guided distribution discrepancy. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets evaluate DBM against baselines including f-FERM (2024) using fairness metrics such as δ_DP (Demographic Parity) and δ_EO (Equalized Odds). Reviewer 2 (rating 5) found the paper well-structured and the method practical, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (ratings 3) raised concerns about novelty, missing experimental details, and insufficient comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Important problem**: The paper tackles the significant challenge of ensuring fairness when fine-tuning pre-trained models for downstream tasks, which is critical for real-world deployment (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical foundation**: The method is supported by well-grounded theory, including RNF and R-divergence, with a generalization error bound provided (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Clear structure**: The paper is well-organized, separately addressing representation and task-level fairness, and the experimental results demonstrate effectiveness across multiple fairness metrics (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Practical approach**: Reviewer 2 highlighted that the method is straightforward and practical: mixing pre-trained representations of sensitive attributes and training with fairness-guided distribution discrepancy. Reviewer 3 noted that experimental results on multiple benchmark datasets show effectiveness compared to representation-level and task-level baselines.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The main contribution is the application of existing techniques. R-Divergence is naively applied as a regularization term to measure group discrepancies (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 noted that Representation Mixing is adopted from Du et al. (2021) and Equation 6 resembles methods from prior work (e.g., [1] Technical Challenges for Training Fair Neural Networks). While Reviewer 2 found the approach straightforward, Reviewers 1 and 3 considered the novelty limited.
- **Missing experimental details and unclear settings**:
  - It is unclear how hyperparameters for baselines (e.g., the regularization weight in f-FERM) are tuned and chosen for reported results (Reviewer 3).
  - Whether pre-trained models are kept fixed or updated during fine-tuning on downstream tasks is not specified (Reviewer 3). This also makes Figure 1 difficult to interpret.
  - For tabular datasets, how pre-trained models (e.g., Multi-Layer Perceptron) are trained is not explained (Reviewer 3).
  - The definitions of RNF and R-Divergence need expansion in Section 3, including the role of neutralized labels and hyperparameter λ for controlling neutralization. Also, absolute value symbols appear missing on the right-hand side of equations (4) and (5) (Reviewer 1).
  - The evaluation metrics δ_DP and δ_EO need more detail to clarify their relevance (Reviewer 2).
- **Need for more baseline comparisons**:
  - Only f-FERM (2024) is included as a recent baseline. Additional up-to-date fairness methods are needed to strengthen the experimental results (Reviewer 1).
  - Does the method work without annotations for sensitive attributes? Many recent approaches, including RNF, address fairness without sensitive attribute labels. A comparison or discussion of this limitation is missing (Reviewer 1).
  - Reviewer 3 noted that the problem statement (line 27) focuses on ensuring fairness across various downstream tasks, but the method is designed for specific tasks. Any existing in-processing method could be applied during fine-tuning; what makes this problem special or challenging is unclear.
- **Incomplete evidence for representation-level fairness**: Although the paper claims to tackle bias at both representation and task levels, there is no result to support fairness mitigation at the representation level (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear or insufficient experimental findings**:
  - In Tables 1 and 2, the proposed method’s δ_EO is not the lowest across three of the four datasets. Does this align with the claim that differences in true positive and false positive rates between groups are low? (Reviewer 2 question – criticism of claim vs. results).
  - Why were label flip probabilities of 20% and 40% chosen? Only one test doubling the flip rate was conducted; additional percentages are needed to validate the claim that DBM outperforms baselines under increased label bias (Reviewer 2).
  - In Table 3, different pre-trained models are used, but no comparative conclusions are drawn from this experiment (Reviewer 2).
- **Unnatural or poorly motivated experimental settings**:
  - In line 223, taking HeavyMakeup as the sensitive variable and predicting gender is an unnatural setting. It would make more sense to predict HeavyMakeup and take gender as sensitive (Reviewer 3).
  - The purpose of the second experimental setting (line 234) is not explained; the motivation behind that setting and how it demonstrates the method’s effectiveness or robustness is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Unaddressed questions about the approach**:
  - Reviewer 2 asked for clarification on how different representations are mixed and how bias level is varied. Is bias increased only by flipping labels, or are other methods used? How are different types of bias defined and categorized?
  - Reviewer 3 questioned the claim in line 92 that “the task-specific constraint is more challenging to handle,” since most in-processing methods address task-specific constraints. What specific challenges are referred to, and how does DBM address them differently from existing methods?
