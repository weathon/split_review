## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes B$^3$CT, a three-branch coordinated training framework for unsupervised domain adaptive semantic segmentation. In addition to source and target branches, a third hybrid-attention branch uses an Adaptive Alignment Controller (AAC) to decide *where* to align features at each stage, and a coordinate weight derived from target pseudo-label accuracy controls *when* to strengthen alignment during training. Experiments on GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes show competitive results, but four reviewers (scores: 5,5,5,3) raise concerns about novelty, performance gains, missing baselines, computational overhead, and limited evaluation scenarios. Reviewer 4 is notably more negative (score 3, confidence 5) than the majority (scores 5, confidence 3–5).

### Strengths
- The problem of unsupervised domain adaptation is important and pertinent to the community (Reviewers 3,4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 2). The exposition on related techniques is comprehensive (Reviewer 3), and sufficient background is provided (Reviewer 4).
- The concept of using cross-attention (hybrid-attention) to align source and target features is elegant and logically sound (Reviewer 2).
- The design of the coordinate weight, which dynamically adjusts the loss based on pseudo-accuracy, is well-founded (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed three ideas – hybrid branch, Adaptive Alignment Controller (AAC), and coordinate weight – are generally applicable and work for feature alignment and fusion (Reviewer 4).
- The evaluation is thorough on two standard benchmarks, and the ablation studies are detailed with clear experimental configurations (Reviewers 1,3,4). The method shows superior performance when combined with the HRDA baseline (Reviewer 4).
- The paper addresses an important and challenging problem with potential applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and scene understanding (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Performance gains are limited.** Despite three carefully designed components, the improvement over HRDA on GTA5→Cityscapes is only ~1% mIoU (74.8 vs 73.8, Table 3), and on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes (Table 5) the performance is even lower than the HRDA baseline (46.1 vs 47.3) (Reviewers 1,3,4). Reviewer 1 notes that gains in ablation studies (Tables 1–3) are not significant and asks whether multiple random runs or seeds were used to assess randomness. Reviewer 4 points out that the result of ablating only the coordinate weight is missing from Table 1.
- **Missing state-of-the-art comparison.** The claimed SOTA result of 74.8 on GTA5→Cityscapes is incorrect; MIC (CVPR2023) achieves 75.9 and is neither cited nor compared (Reviewer 2). Several key related works on co-training for UDA, e.g., multi-feature-space and multi-classifier co-regularization methods [A,B,C,D], are missing, and the differences/pros/cons of the proposed coordinate weights versus class-balance weights or historical consistency weights are not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited applicability – transformer-only and computational overhead.** B$^3$CT is only applicable to transformer-based architectures (e.g., HRDA) and cannot be used with CNN-based models (e.g., DeepLab). The paper does not discuss potential adaptations for CNNs, which limits broader adoption (Reviewer 2). The three-branch hybrid-attention and AAC modules add extra parameters and computation; the paper lacks analysis of FLOPs, parameter count, inference time, and GPU memory consumption for both training and testing (Reviewers 2,3,4). Reviewer 3 raises the concern that performance gains could stem simply from increased model capacity (Oracle effect).
- **Insufficient justification of novelty and design choices.** The hybrid-attention mechanism, AAC, and coordinate weight appear to be natural/basic extensions without strong theoretical or empirical basis (Reviewer 4). The paper does not adequately explain why this specific combination is particularly effective or novel compared to existing domain adaptation methods. The AAC is described as a straightforward application of attention, and the coordinate weight does not demonstrate a significant advantage over simpler approaches (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited evaluation scenarios – only synthetic-to-real, missing adverse conditions.** The experiments are limited to simple synthetic-to-real transfer (GTA5, SYNTHIA → Cityscapes). More convincing and challenging scenarios, such as Cityscapes → ACDC (daytime-to-nighttime) and other clear-to-adverse or online adaptation settings (e.g., SePiCo, MIC, CoTTA), are not evaluated (Reviewers 3,4).
- **Missing experimental details and justifications.** It is unclear where exactly the hybrid branch is inserted into each stage of the feature encoder (Reviewer 4). The paper does not provide experimental results to support the claim that the third branch facilitates learning domain-invariant features for alignment (Reviewer 4). The advantages and limitations of using pseudo-accuracy (target predictions vs. teacher pseudo-labels) are not discussed; the potential for error propagation from inaccurate pseudo-labels and how the method mitigates it is not addressed (Reviewer 4). The results in Table 5 should be differentiated by network architecture (e.g., DeepLab vs. SegFormer) to assess generalizability (Reviewer 4). Performance on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes is less effective; insights and analysis are needed (Reviewer 1). Applicability to other baselines beyond HRDA should be tested to investigate generalization (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues.** In Eq. 2 and Eq. 5, target predictions $p_t^{i,j,c}$ should likely be the pseudo-label $\hat{y}_t^{i,j}$ (from teacher), and similar inconsistencies in Eq. 7 and Eq. 8 with subscript indices (Reviewer 4). Table 5 uses a different decimal precision than other tables; consistency is needed (Reviewer 4). Typo: "hybrid-attention different stages." should be capitalized (Reviewer 4). The conclusion should discuss limitations and potential negative societal impact (Reviewer 4).
