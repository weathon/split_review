## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies generalization bounds for adversarial training (AT) via uniform stability, analyzing how the expansiveness of the adversarial attack operator (used during training (\(J\)) and for evaluation (\(\pi\))) affects the generalization gap. For \(\ell_\infty\) perturbations, it derives bounds that depend on this expansiveness. By replacing the \(\text{sign}\) function in PGD with a smooth \(\tanh(\gamma \cdot x)\) approximation, the bound can vanish with sample size \(n\) for finite \(\gamma\). Experiments show a trade-off between generalization and robustness controlled by \(\gamma\). The work also connects AT to steepest descent methods.

### Strengths
- **Novel stability analysis of AT from an attack perspective.** The expansiveness of the PGD attack operator and its impact on generalization is a new angle that has not been considered before (Reviewers 1, 3). The separation of the perturbation used in training (\(J\)) and evaluation (\(\pi\)) provides a more abstract and generally applicable framework (Reviewer 4).
- **Simple and clear exposition.** Several reviewers found the paper easy to follow and the theory straightforward (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 2, however, found the paper difficult to follow due to presentation issues (see Weaknesses).
- **Proposal of a smooth relaxation of PGD.** Using \(\tanh(\gamma \cdot x)\) instead of \(\text{sign}\) is a sensible modification motivated by the theory, and experiments show that this substitution can improve generalization performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Vanishing generalization bound for a finite \(\gamma\).** When a \(\tanh\)-based attack is used, the bound converges to zero as the number of training samples \(n\) increases, distinguishing it from prior bounds that converge to a constant (Reviewer 1).
- **Highlights the understudied role of the inner maximization algorithm.** The choice of attack algorithm (e.g., sign-based PGD vs. smooth approximations) is shown to affect both optimization and generalization – an aspect that has received inadequate attention (Reviewers 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading claim that the bound vanishes for standard PGD.** The paper states that its generalization bound converges to zero with \(n\), but this holds only when using the \(\tanh\) approximation, *not* for the standard sign-based PGD attack. The bound in the same setup as prior work [1,2] does not vanish (see lines 369–371). The paper should clearly state that the vanishing result is contingent on the \(\tanh\) modification. (Reviewer 1)
- **Inadequate comparison with prior bounds.** The proof technique and assumptions are similar to Xiao et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2024), yet those bounds are not presented in detail. The role of the expansiveness parameter relative to the approximate smoothness parameter \(\eta\) from [2] is not discussed. A table comparing the bounds and assumptions is needed to clarify the novelty. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Presentation issues.** The paper contains many grammatical errors (e.g., "pernutation", "descend", "independent with") and uses obscure or ad-hoc terminology (e.g., "mis-matched generalization gap", "RG-PGD", "expansiveness property") for well-established concepts. The introduction should explicitly state that all results are for \(\ell_\infty\) perturbations. (Reviewer 2; also noted by Reviewer 4: typo in line 149 and unnecessary equation numbers like eq. (2))
- **Unclear how the analysis bypasses prior work to achieve an \(O(1/n)\) decay.** The specific part of the proof that enables the improved decay rate is not clearly identified. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of direct robustness benefits from the \(\tanh\) substitution.** Experiments show reduced overfitting for small \(\gamma\), but this corresponds to a nearly standard training (weak attacks). For \(\gamma\) close to the sign function, robustness is not improved. The method is not presented as a practical way to improve robustness against \(\ell_\infty\) attacks. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing analysis of the convergence trade-off of the PGD attack.** More iterations strengthen the attack but worsen stability. At test time with a strong attack (e.g., AutoAttack), balancing attack effectiveness and stability is critical. This trade-off is not discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- **The bound cannot explain robust overfitting.** The paper claims that an upper bound converging to a constant explains robust overfitting, but an upper bound is not sufficient for such explanation; a lower bound would be needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of practical examples for computing \(Q(c^*)\) and choosing \(c^*\).** The framework requires calculating a quantity \(Q(c^*)\) for specific \(J, \pi\), but the paper provides only overly simple assumptions (e.g., bounded second moment, bound with probability 1). Without concrete examples, the bound may be as meaningless as a VC dimension that cannot be computed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Experiments are insufficiently motivated or novel.** The connection between AT and steepest descent is well-known, and the experiments (Section 6) merely show that optimizing an \(\ell_\infty\) objective gives better coverage against \(\ell_\infty\) attacks – not a new insight. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor methodological requests.** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):
  - Try larger \(\gamma\) values (e.g., \(\gamma=10^5\) is far from sign-PGD in Fig. 2(b)).
  - Construct an experiment showing the dependence of the generalization gap on \(n\) (e.g., varying sample size on a synthetic dataset).
  - Clearly highlight the technical challenges of the theoretical contributions.
  - Compare with SOTA methods from RobustBench (e.g., synthetic data approaches) to demonstrate that the sign function is a key issue in AT.
  - Provide comments on fast adversarial training algorithms (e.g., free adversarial training, Cheng et al. 2024) in relation to this work.
- **Reviewer disagreement on clarity:** Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 found the paper clear, while Reviewer 2 found it difficult to follow due to poor presentation and grammatical errors. This contrast should be noted. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
