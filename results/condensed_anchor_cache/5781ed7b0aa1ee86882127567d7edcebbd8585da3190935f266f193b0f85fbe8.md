## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReKT, a knowledge tracing model that models student knowledge from three perspectives (question, concept, domain) and introduces the Forget-Response-Update (FRU) framework, composed of two linear regression units. FRU is inspired by human cognitive development models and is claimed to be extremely lightweight. The model is evaluated against 22 state-of-the-art KT models on 7 public datasets, achieving superior performance in question-based KT tasks and best/near-best performance in concept-based tasks, while using only 38% of the computing resources of Transformer/LSTM-based architectures. Reviewers broadly agree that the model is effective and efficient, but raise concerns about the novelty of FRU (similarity to GRU or simple MLPs), missing comparisons to classic KT methods, result discrepancies on specific datasets, and the lack of interpretability and knowledge-state visualization.

### Strengths
- **Lightweight and efficient architecture**: FRU uses only two linear regression units and requires approximately 38% of the computing resources of Transformers or LSTMs (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Multi-perspective knowledge state modeling**: The model considers question-level, concept-level, and domain-level representations, inspired by human teacher decision-making. This multi-view approach is logically self-consistent (Reviewer 1 acknowledges the method; Reviewer 4 calls it logically coherent).
- **Strong predictive performance**: ReKT outperforms 22 state-of-the-art KT models on 7 datasets in question-based tasks and achieves top or near-top performance in concept-based tasks (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation**: The paper includes extensive comparisons across many baselines and public datasets, with detailed descriptions of methodology, equations, and training procedures (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Well-structured presentation**: The paper is clearly organized, with helpful figures, tables, and illustrative examples (Reviewer 3).
- **Integration of cognitive learning principles**: The FRU framework is motivated by human cognitive development models like constructivism, offering a fresh blend of psychology and machine learning (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Overstated novelty of FRU; strong similarity to GRU or MLPs**:
  - FRU resembles a GRU without a reset gate, using a similar forget gate and hyperbolic tangent; GRU itself has only three linear regression units, so calling FRU “very lightweight” is not persuasive without citing GRU as a baseline (Reviewer 1).
  - The core of FRU could be easily mirrored by two multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs). The paper lacks an ablation study comparing FRU against a simple MLP-based design and does not explore the impact of different activation functions (Reviewer 3).
  - Similar approaches (e.g., exponential time decay as part of attention in transformer-based KT models like RAKT, AKT, [3]) already exist; the authors use MLP units for FRU but do not adequately differentiate from these works (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing comparisons to classic knowledge tracing methods and related literature**:
  - Methods such as Learning Factor Analysis (LFA), Performance Factor Analysis (PFA), Additive Factor Models (AFM), and Knowledge Factoring Machines (KFM) also model knowledge at concept, question, or history levels. In the spirit of “revisiting,” these should be cited and compared (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper does not clarify how ReKT improves upon or differs from these established approaches (Reviewers 1, 2).

- **Discrepancies with published results on important datasets**:
  - On ASSIST09, the AKT-R model (Ghosh et al., 2020) achieves an AUC of 0.8346 (per the AKT paper), which is significantly higher than the 0.7917 reported for ReKT. Several other baselines in the AKT paper also reach AUC > 0.8. The paper must explain why its results differ from these published numbers (Reviewer 1).

- **Lack of interpretability and knowledge-state analysis**:
  - The paper provides no interpretation of the model’s performance, which is critical for both students (recommending learning materials) and teachers (identifying difficult concepts or questions). Case studies or visualizations of student knowledge states are absent (Reviewer 2).
  - The naming of modules (“Forget”, “Response”, “Update”) suggests targeted functionalities, but without analysis (e.g., probing tasks, visualization of internal representations) it is unclear what each module actually learns. These names may be misleading in complex learning schedules (Reviewer 3).
  - No experiments demonstrate the tracking of dynamic knowledge states over time; such experiments would help explain why the proposed knowledge modeling is better (Reviewer 4).

- **No theoretical analysis of computational complexity**:
  - The paper claims efficiency but does not provide spatial‑temporal complexity analysis of FRU to make the resource‑saving argument more rigorous (Reviewer 4).

- **Limited discussion of FRU integration into larger architectures and broader applicability**:
  - There is little exploration of how FRU would perform when used within deeper or more complex architectures (e.g., as an attention mechanism or recurrent layer inside a Transformer). The paper does not discuss potential benefits or challenges of such integration (Reviewer 3).
  - The potential of FRU for other sequential prediction tasks (e.g., recommendation) is not addressed (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing clarifications and minor errors**:
  - How is FRU fundamentally different from GRU? (Reviewer 1)
  - How is the Rasch difficulty computed from three different interaction matrices, and how are multiple student submissions handled? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the role of different activation functions within the linear units of FRU? (Reviewer 3)
  - What does the symbol \(I_\alpha\) mean in Section 3.4? (Reviewer 4)
  - The loss function equation contains a syntax error: the index “I” should likely be “t” (Reviewer 4).
  - A more explicit explanation of the connection between FRU’s design and the cited cognitive development models is needed (Reviewer 4).
