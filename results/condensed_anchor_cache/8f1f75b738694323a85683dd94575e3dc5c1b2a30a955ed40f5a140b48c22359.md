## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies spuriously correlated attributes in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that cause poor generalization, and proposes Spurious Attribute Probing (SAP) to filter out these attributes, and Spurious Attribute Shielding (SAS), a plug-and-play module for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning methods. Experiments across 11 datasets and 3 generalization tasks show accuracy improvements on distribution shifts without sacrificing downstream performance.

### Strengths
- The direction of researching spuriously correlated attributes in VLMs is novel, less explored, and intriguing.
- The core ideas—quantitatively identifying spurious attributes and learning pseudo categories to mitigate their impact—are reasonable, effective, and novel.
- SAP and SAS enhance generalization of existing attribute-based methods and complement various PEFT methods.
- The methods achieve state-of-the-art results across 11 datasets and 3 generalization tasks, with consistent improvement over 10 baselines (as shown in Figure 3, etc.).
- The modules are efficient, intuitive, and easy to integrate.
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow.
- Reviewer confidence varies (2 high-confidence reviewers at level 5, two at level 3 and 2), but all agree on the novelty and effectiveness of the approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Fair comparison with existing attribute-based methods**: Most attribute-based methods are zero-shot, whereas SAP/SAS require a few labeled training images for attribute filtering. It is unclear whether the performance gains come from the additional training data (as in Figure 2a,b) or the proposed method; controls using the same extra data for baselines are missing. An ablation study separating data augmentation effects from the method is needed (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Missing related works and baselines**:  
  - Several key VLM adaptation works [1,2] and attribute-based methods introducing multi-granularity object class information [A,B,C] are not discussed; complementarity with these new approaches should be verified.  
  - The paper does not add a dedicated “Vision-Language Model” paragraph in Related Work or cite a relevant survey [E].  
  - The pseudo-category construction via generation/retrieval shares ideas with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG); similarity/differences with RAG for object recognition [D] should be clarified.
- **Generalization beyond CLIP and vision recognition**: The method is only tested on CLIP; experiments on multiple VLMs are missing. Generalization to other modalities (e.g., video) or tasks (e.g., language reasoning) is not explored (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Computational cost and efficiency**:  
  - Differentiating each category from synthetic pseudo categories during fine-tuning is time-consuming per category (Reviewer 4).  
  - Pseudo category construction using generative/diffusion methods may add computational overhead; no experiments validate efficiency or provide inference time analysis (Reviewer 2).  
  - The selective optimization does not fundamentally address prolonged optimization time (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient visualization**: Only three visualizations are provided in Figure 5, which do not effectively demonstrate removal of attention to spurious attributes (e.g., Figure 5b). More examples (in the paper or supplementary) are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential confounding from data augmentation**: Performance improvement may be primarily due to generating negative samples (data augmentation). The authors do not compare performance when generating an equivalent number of positive samples for each target category; an ablation study is required (Reviewer 4).
