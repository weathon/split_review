## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LeMoLE, an LLM-enhanced mixture of linear experts for time series forecasting, leveraging multiple lookback windows and a multimodal fusion mechanism that uses GPT-2 to condition expert weights. One reviewer (score 5) found the approach promising in terms of efficiency and predictive performance on standard and few-shot tasks, and praised its new perspective. Three reviewers (score 3) raised major concerns: limited novelty (the method is seen as a simple assembly of existing ideas – linear projections with varying lengths, MoE, static prompts from Time-LLM, dynamic timestamp-based prompts replacing standard embeddings), questionable experimental validity (results for several datasets differ substantially from prior works), insufficient ablation on expert count and selection, and missing details (e.g., CNN architecture, kernel sizes, visualizations). The consensus is that although the paper is clearly written and the ablation study on experts and frequency domain offers some insights, the technical contribution and experimental evaluation are not strong enough to support the claimed advances.

### Strengths
- Offers a new perspective on integrating LLMs with time-series models: instead of aligning time series to LLMs, the paper uses LLMs to condition the ensemble of linear experts via multimodal information (global static descriptions and dynamic timestamp texts). (R1)
- The method is clearly described and the paper is well organized. (R3, R4)
- First work to apply mixture-of-expert learning and multimodal learning to improve linear time series models; achieves state-of-the-art results compared to existing LLM-based forecasting models. (R2)
- Ablation study on the frequency domain and the number of experts provides valuable insights. (R3)
- Demonstrates computational efficiency improvements over LLM-based models while maintaining competitive accuracy on standard and few-shot forecasting scenarios. (R1)
- The proposed LeMoLE model effectively integrates multiple linear experts with different lookback lengths and adaptively combines them using LLM-derived features. (R1, R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The core model is a series of linear projections with varying input lengths – essentially an expanded linear layer. The conditioning module combines existing components: static prompts from Time-LLM (ICLR 2024) and dynamic timestamp prompts that replace standard time embeddings used in MoLE. No theoretical justification is provided for why this architecture is superior to alternatives. (R2, R3, R4)  
  - *Minority contrast*: R1 considered this a new perspective, while R2, R3, R4 judged the novelty as very low.
- **Experimental result discrepancies**: Reported values in Table 2 differ significantly from prior studies. For example, Electricity test results are roughly twice the self-test results of PatchTST; Traffic results are about half; ETTh1 and ETTm1 results are not in the same order of magnitude as several existing works. The paper does not explain these differences or clarify experimental settings. (R1, R4)
- **Missing datasets**: Exchange rate and Weather datasets are excluded from evaluation despite exhibiting non-stationarity, which is relevant for assessing the method’s robustness. (R1)
- **No automated selection of number of experts**: It is unclear how to choose the optimal number of experts for a new dataset. The paper tests experts from 1 to 5, but does not specify which column in Table 9 corresponds to which setting in Table 1, nor whether manual testing is always required. For ETTh1, accuracy improves as the number of experts increases; why not continue beyond 5 to explore performance limits? (R1, R4)
- **Unclear advantage of LLM-based timestamp embedding over standard methods**: The dynamic prompt uses only timestamps, which seems similar to the time encoding used in Autoformer/Fedformer (one-hot). The paper does not demonstrate what extra information the timestamp text provides or why it would convey non-linearity. (R1, R3)
- **Static prompts provide no additional information**: Channel descriptions are constant across training/validation/test sets within a dataset, essentially acting as a channel identifier. This may limit generalization and does not add meaningful multimodal information. (R3)
- **Parameter count inconsistency**: LeMoLE has similar parameter count to MoLE at H=96 but significantly exceeds it at H=720. Since the GPT-2 encoder size remains constant and the MoE architecture is largely unchanged, the source of additional parameters at long horizons is not explained. (R3)
- **Frequency-domain MoE considered meaningless**: The paper introduces mixture experts based on the frequency domain but only explains why they perform worse. No analysis of adaptation scenarios (time vs. frequency) is provided, making this addition seem pointless. (R4)
- **Missing architectural details for CNN**: The paper mentions a lightweight CNN for fusing linear features but does not specify kernel sizes (e.g., 3×3 or 7×7) or architecture for different lookback lengths \(T\) (e.g., T=96 with 5 experts). (R2)
- **Lack of visualizations**: No comparisons of time-domain versus frequency-domain outputs, nor visualizations of how different window lengths affect predictions. (R2)
- **Inconsistent experiment settings**: The lookback length \(T\) in the framework does not match the horizon \(H\) in Tables 2 and 3. Additionally, the caption of Table 4 states prediction length 336, but ETTh results in that table align with horizon 96 from Table 2. (R1, R2)
- **Text usage is basic**: Only timestamps are used for dynamic prompts; static prompts are limited to channel descriptions. The paper does not show concrete examples of how well LeMoLE integrates prompt text with time series data. (R2, R3)
- **Data volume requirement unclear**: LeMoLE performs better on larger datasets (Traffic, Electricity) than on ETT datasets, raising the question of whether it requires more data for effective training. (R3)
- **From earlier work, no clear distinction without prompts**: The ablation “w/o both prompts” in Table 4 still produces results that differ considerably from baseline MoLE, suggesting the model is not simply MoLE with prompts removed. The paper does not explain this gap. (R1)
- **Several typos and formatting errors**:  
  - Line 199: “Based on Equation (??)” – reference missing. (R1, R2, R3)  
  - Equation (3): \(b_i\) should be \(b_m\). (R2)  
  - Table 5 caption indicates units in seconds (s) but the table lists milliseconds (ms). (R3)  
- **No discussion of limitations**: The paper does not include a limitations section or acknowledge weaknesses of the approach. (R2)
