## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes an end-to-end decision-focused learning framework for multi-objective optimization (MOP) with unknown coefficients and conflicting objectives. It introduces three loss functions: landscape loss (using sRMMD), Pareto set loss, and decision loss, designed to align prediction training with downstream optimization. Experiments are conducted on synthetic benchmarks and a web advertisement allocation dataset from an Anonymous App, comparing against six baselines. The paper exceeds the ICLR page limit (main text to page 11). Reviewer 2 gave a rating of 1 (strong rejection) primarily citing the page limit violation; Reviewers 1 and 3 gave ratings of 3, acknowledging the problem setting but raising significant concerns about novelty, clarity, and experimental rigor.

### Strengths

- The paper investigates decision-focused learning in the multi-objective optimization setting, which is a novel problem to solve (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed method is evaluated on two types of datasets, including a real-world case of web advertisement allocation within an Anonymous App, and reportedly achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks and metrics compared to six baselines (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The design of loss functions (landscape, Pareto set, decision) attempts to capture discrepancies between predicted and true decision problems from solution space, objective space, and decision quality (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

1. **Page limit violation**: The main text extends to page 11; per ICLR policy the limit is 10 pages and this would normally result in desk rejection. Reviewer 3 also notes it exceeds the limit. Reviewer 2 emphasizes this as the primary defect, whereas Reviewers 1 and 3 mention it as a separate weakness. (Reviewer 2: "Your paper's main text exceeds the conference requirements... This limit will be strictly enforced. Papers with main text on the 11th page will be desk rejected." Reviewer 1: "The manuscript exceeds ICLR's 10-page limit for the main content." Reviewer 3: "The last paragraph of the paper exceeds the page limit. According to the policy, it should be desk rejected.")
2. **Lack of novelty and theoretical guarantees**: The empirical loss functions are proposed without theoretical guarantees; the overall novelty is questionable. The technical contribution is not sufficiently defined – sRMMD derivations occupy a full page but are largely taken from the source, while the second and third loss functions are straightforward. Ablation results show that most of the performance improvement derives from the sRMMD loss, undermining the distinct contribution of the other two losses. (Reviewer 1 weakness 1, Reviewer 3 weaknesses 2 and 3)
3. **Outdated comparisons and insufficient experimental validation**: The comparison methods are outdated and do not represent current state-of-the-art solutions. The experiments use few datasets and are basic, making it difficult to substantiate the method's effectiveness. Additional experiments on more datasets and comparisons with recent state-of-the-art methods are needed. (Reviewer 1 weaknesses 2 and 3, questions 1 and 2)
4. **Incomplete literature review**: The related work section covers literature only prior to 2022, lacking analysis of current research developments. (Reviewer 1 weakness 4, question 1)
5. **Poor clarity and motivation**: The problem description lacks clarity, and the technical challenges in MOP are not well articulated, making the motivation for the proposed methods unclear. This issue is present in all three loss components. (Reviewer 3 weakness 2)
6. **Unclear details**: Reviewer 3 asks what \(d\) is in the Pareto loss and what \(r_1, r_2,\) and \(r\) represent in the experiments. (Reviewer 3 questions) These remain unanswered in the paper.
