## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes NoisyTraj, a plug-and-play trajectory denoising module that uses mutual information maximization, a masking-based reconstruction loss, and a ranking loss to clean noisy observations before feeding them into any trajectory prediction backbone. It is validated on ETH/UCY and SDD datasets with additive synthetic noise. Reviewers agree the idea is novel and well-presented, but raise major concerns about unrealistic assumptions (clean future trajectories, additive Gaussian noise only), missing comparisons with prior works on tracking-aware robust prediction, weak baselines, and lack of real-world noise validation.

### Strengths

- **Well-written and easy to follow.** All reviewers note clarity and organization (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Innovative concept.** Denoising historical trajectory data with mutual information is novel (R5, R3). The mutual information and reconstruction losses are intuitive and well-motivated (R3, R4).
- **Strong experimental results under synthetic noise.** NoisyTraj beats all baselines (no denoising, Wavelet, EMA) on ETH/UCY and SDD across multiple noise settings (R2, R4, R5). It does not degrade performance on clean data (R2).
- **Comprehensive ablation studies.** All losses contribute (R2, R3, R4). The mathematical modeling of mutual information upper bound is sound (R4).
- **Plug-and-play generalizability.** Can be integrated into different backbones (R3, R5). Demonstrates cross-noise generalization (R2, R4).
- **One reviewer (score 8)** finds the task new and challenging, considers the experiments and ablations very convincing (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Unrealistic assumption of clean future trajectories.** The method assumes future ground truth trajectories are noise-free while only observations are noisy. Multiple reviewers (R2, R4, R1) argue this is unrealistic for autonomous driving, robotics, or surveillance—any sensor (camera, LiDAR) introduces noise. R4 questions why one would not simply use L2 loss to train a denoiser if clean future were available. R1 notes the paper only briefly mentions this assumption (Section 3). Requested: evaluation where both input and future have different noise levels (e.g., camera vs. LiDAR) (R2, R4).
- **Validation only on synthetic additive Gaussian noise, not real-world noise.** All reviewers criticize this. R1 details how real tracking errors include false positives, false negatives, ID switches, and association errors—completely different from additive position noise. Figure 3 shows unrealistic offset magnitudes (R1). R2 asks for evaluation at higher noise (e.g., sigma=2) to test robustness. R3 warns of potential overfitting to noise type. R5 notes insufficient baselines (only Wavelet and EMA) and requests comparison with prediction results before noise addition.
- **Missing comparison with prior works on robust trajectory prediction under tracking errors.** R1 lists four essential prior works [a-d] that handle tracking-related issues (noisy tracks, ID switches, missed detections) and use metrics like ADE-over-recall instead of pure ADE/FDE. R4 adds another recent paper (arXiv:2312.15906) on robustness to real noisy trajectories. These works are not cited, and NoisyTraj addresses a different (simplified) problem (R1).
- **Weak baselines limit conclusions.** R2 and R5 note the baselines (no denoising, Wavelet, EMA) are simplistic. R5 explicitly requests inclusion of clean historical trajectory results in all experiments.
- **Intrinsic connection of ranking loss is unclear.** R5 asks why ranking loss outperforms a simple prediction loss on denoised outputs, and requests ablation on loss hyperparameters. R4 also questions necessity of mutual information vs. direct L2 denoising.
- **Lack of detailed training description.** R5 asks whether training data is also noisy, and notes the limitation that the prediction backbone may need to be fine-tuned (freezing degrades performance)—this should be stated in the main text. R1 notes experimental unit inconsistency (meters vs. pixels on SDD).
- **Expression issues.** R1 points out line 360 “blue” arrow should be “green” arrow in Figure 2, and line 451 “Kalman” should be “Wavelet”. R4 notes typo in line 178.
- **Minority highly positive reviewer (score 8)** still agrees synthetic noise is a limitation but appreciates the efforts given the lack of real data (R3). The most negative reviewer (score 3) strongly emphasizes the mismatch with real tracking pipelines and missing prior art (R1).
