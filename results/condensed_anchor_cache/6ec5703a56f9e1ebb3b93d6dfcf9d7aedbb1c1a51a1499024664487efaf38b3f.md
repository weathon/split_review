## Merged Review

### Summary
LightSeq introduces a sequence-level parallel approach for distributed training of long-context LLMs, addressing limitations of head-based parallelism (e.g., Megatron-LM). It partitions the sequence dimension, enabling architecture-agnostic scaling and reducing communication volume by up to 4.7×. Key techniques include a load-balanced distributed attention (DistAttn) for causal models, overlapping communication with computation via two CUDA streams, and a re-materialization-aware checkpointing scheme. Evaluated on Llama-7B and variants with sequences up to 512K on 16 A100 GPUs, LightSeq achieves up to 2.01× end-to-end speedup over Megatron-LM and supports 2–8× longer sequences for models with fewer heads. Open-source code is provided.

### Strengths
- The research topic is important and timely; efficient long-context training is a key challenge.
- Demonstrates significant runtime speedup over Megatron-LM (up to 2.01×) and supports longer sequences for models with few heads.
- The general ideas (load balancing, overlapping communication/computation) are clearly presented.
- Reducing communication volume should enable model parallel training on cheaper hardware (e.g., clusters without InfiniBand).
- Proposes a new load-balancing schedule for distributed attention by shifting q/k/v compute from busy to idle workers.
- Implements system optimization overlapping remote q/k/v with local compute using two CUDA streams.
- Improves gradient checkpointing by leveraging FlashAttention’s recompute in the backward pass (highlighted as useful by Reviewer 3).
- Open-source code supports reproducibility.
- Evaluation on real clusters demonstrates practical applicability.
- The paper is well written, easy to follow, and clearly communicates motivation and contributions.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty in core ideas**: Sequence-level parallelism itself is not new (e.g., [4D sequence parallelism, arXiv:2105.13120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13120)), and overlapping communication/computation is a standard parallel systems technique. A minority reviewer (rating 3) further stated that the paper overclaims by saying it “proposes partitioning solely the input tokens” and that the approach is “agnostic to model architecture” — sequence parallelism by default is architecture-agnostic and similar schemes exist.
- **Missing comparisons to other sequence-parallel methods**: The evaluation compares only to Megatron-LM. Comparisons to Li et al. (sequence parallelism), TeraPipe (token-level pipelining), DeepSpeed Ulysses, and FlashAttention-2 (the base framework) are needed. For sequence lengths < 8K, LightSeq is slower than DeepSpeed Ulysses by up to 0.5×.
- **Limited model and task generality**: Only evaluated on Llama-7B variants. The 33-head case (33H) appears constructed and unlikely in practice; realistic speedup is ~1.5×. The approach assumes causal (uni-directional) attention and does not translate to bi-directional models (e.g., BERT). How DistAttn and load balancing work for sliding local windows, global windows, and MQA/GQA models is unclear.
- **No accuracy/quality results**: While longer sequences are supported, no experiments demonstrate that longer context improves model quality or task performance.
- **Unclear experimental setup and configuration choices**:
  - Batch size used is unclear; Table 3 uses batch size 1, which is not a typical training configuration. High batch sizes might allow tensor/data/pipeline parallelism without sequence parallelism.
  - Results are taken from different clusters (one with InfiniBand, one without) for different experiments, making it hard to compare; it is unclear which system corresponds to Table 1. Missing results on a cheaper cluster without NVLink/NVLINK for direct comparison.
  - Figure 3 does not specify if measurements are in a distributed setting or on a single GPU.
  - In Figure 5, why does sequence length 128K show 1.44× overlapped vs. 1× no communication? (Reviewer 3 question).
- **Insufficient explanation of key algorithmic details**:
  - The load-balanced scheduling in Figure 1 (right) is unclear: Are all circles independent computations? Why is bubble size only 4 after balancing? Could we shift worker 6,7,8’s compute on (kv6, kv7, kv8) as well? Is there an optimal way to find the best compute workload to shift? The relationship between Figure 1 and Figure 2 (communication pattern) is unclear — e.g., worker 7 computes attn(q7,k1v1) in Figure 1 but attn(q7,k7v7) in Figure 2. No simple formula is given for what query/key/value combinations are computed per worker per timestep and what is communicated.
  - Section 4 (discussion/analysis) is unpolished, hard to understand, and lacks cross-references; some figures are never referred to. Example analysis in Section 4.1 (MHA vs GQA) would benefit from actual runtime numbers and a table/figure. Section 4.3 needs a diagram illustrating work per time step and should remind readers that the balanced schedule completes 36 work units in 5 time steps (speedup 7.2).
  - Section 4.4 (Discussion) mixes future work, a brief comparison to DeepSpeed Ulysses (never mentioned elsewhere), and reiteration of pipeline parallelism drawbacks without new information.
- **Interplay with other parallelism strategies**: It is unclear how LightSeq interacts with FSDP — does it use FSDP for cross-node and LightSeq for intra-node? Can LightSeq scale to more nodes without FSDP? FSDP relies on large batch/sequence size to amortize AllGather overhead; how does enlarged sequence length affect LightSeq’s performance? (Reviewer 3 questions)
- **Potential limited impact on modern hardware**: Overlapping communication/computation may be less effective on newer GPUs like H100 with higher compute capability and Transformer Engine/Tensor Memory Accelerator optimizations.
- **Suggestion of adding dummy heads to Megatron-LM** is deemed unnecessary and inefficient by Reviewer 4.
- **Missing comparison to sequential training**: Without showing speedup over a single-GPU baseline, it is hard to judge the absolute scalability of both Megatron-LM and LightSeq.
- **Pipeline parallelism criticism**: The paper attributes low performance to “high memory pressure in the first stage,” but Reviewer 1 notes this could be due to poor partitioning between stages.
