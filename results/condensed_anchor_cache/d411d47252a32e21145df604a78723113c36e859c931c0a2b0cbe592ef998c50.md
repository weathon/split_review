- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 1, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes KnobGen, a dual-pathway framework for sketch+text-based image generation that adapts to varying sketch complexity (novice to professional) via a Coarse-Grained Controller (CGC) for high-level semantics and a Fine-Grained Controller (FGC) for detailed refinement. A “knob” inference mechanism and a training-time modulator control the relative influence of the two pathways. Experiments use MultiGen-20M and a newly collected 100-image novice sketch dataset, comparing against ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, etc. Reviewers find the inclusiveness and controllability meaningful but identify weaknesses in ablation, evaluation, comparison baselines, writing clarity (one reviewer strongly negative), and the knob’s novelty and effectiveness.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a practical gap**: The method allows users from novice to professional to control sketch influence on the generated image, democratizing sketch-based generation (R1, R2, R4).
- **Inclusive handling of varying abstraction**: Works for input sketches of differing complexity, from amateur to professional-grade, expanding end-user applicability (R2).
- **Interesting dual-pathway design**: The dynamic modulator that lets coarse-grained cues dominate early and gradually increases fine-grained impact is a sound intuition and prevents premature overfitting to fine details (R2, R4).
- **Controllable alignment during inference**: The knob mechanism provides flexibility to adjust sketch alignment, which is a desirable feature for practical use (R2).
- **Visually appealing figures**: The paper’s figures make the content engaging and easy to understand (R3).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The experiments demonstrate effectiveness and potential for real-world applications (R3). *(Note: R3 gave an overall score 1 due to writing issues, but acknowledged these strengths.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Ablation study is insufficient**:
  - Lacks a thorough quantitative ablation examining the impact of each component; the existing ablation is superficial and does not justify all design choices (R1).
  - The ablation shows only human portrait results; more categories and quantitative ablation (with the same metrics as the main experiments) are needed to demonstrate effectiveness (R4).
- **Missing quantitative information on the knob**: The reported quantitative results do not state which γ value was used, making it hard to gauge improvement; the effect of γ on metrics should be shown (R1).
- **Concerns about the training modulator**:
  - The knob during training increases the contribution of the micro (FGC) pathway each epoch without decreasing the macro (CGC) pathway – this may cause issues in the overall feature map magnitude if the contributions should sum to roughly 1 (R1).
  - The choice of a tanh function in the modulator is not justified; an ablation over other functions is needed (R1).
  - The modulator design is heuristic and may have scalability issues, requiring tedious hyperparameter tuning across diverse training data (R2).
- **Knob mechanism lacks novelty and clear effectiveness**:
  - Setting a hyperparameter γ to control conditional signal over denoising steps is common in T2I tasks; the paper should clarify the differences from prior art and show the relationship between the tanh-based training modulator and the inference γ (R4).
  - From Figure 6, the effect of γ is not very pronounced: the volcano example looks better matched at intermediate γ, and Keith’s facial details change little (R2).
  - The user must try different γ values until satisfaction, with no apparent relation to the input sketch’s complexity level (R2).
  - Fine-grained cues injected early are hard to manage precisely because their effect persists through later denoising steps (R2).
- **Incomplete comparison with proper baselines**:
  - Other SOTAs like ControlNet also provide a balancing factor; they should be compared on granularity of control (R1).
  - The compared methods are not designed for sketches; the paper should compare with sketch-guided generation works (e.g., “Sketch-Guided Text-to-Image Diffusion Models”, SIGGRAPH 2023) (R2).
- **Missing experimental details**:
  - Show “sketch-only” generation results (null prompt or text weight zero) to demonstrate the method’s behavior without text guidance (R1).
  - The 100 hand-drawn novice sketches used for evaluation should be displayed for completeness (R2).
  - Evaluation is subjective; a user study with >50 sketches evenly distributed across complexity levels, where users rank results, would strengthen the paper (R4).
  - Only aesthetic score is reported; use additional metrics like HPS-v2 and Pick score, which are trained on human preference datasets, for comprehensive quantitative assessment (R4).
- **Assumptions about professional sketches are not rigorous**: The “professional” sketches in MultiGen-20M are binarized HED edges, which are very different from what a real artist would draw, deviating from the paper’s ambitious objectives (R2).
- **Writing quality is poor (minority strong criticism)**: 
  - The paper contains many incomprehensible sentences, including awkward phrasing, repeated words (e.g., two “Additionally” in one sentence), inconsistent use of “Figure” vs “Fig.”, and excessive “and” in a single sentence (R3).
  - Figure captions lack clarity (e.g., “weights” in Figure 2 needs explanation) (R3).
  - The overall writing style may resemble that produced by a language model, lacking a personal touch (R3). *(Note: R3 scored 1, while other reviewers scored 5; this is a distinct minority view.)*
- **May fail on highly abstract sketches**: As shown in the last column of Figure 4 (second and third rows), the generated image reproduces distortions in the input sketch. While γ can adjust this, methods like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter have a similar “controllable scale”; authors should demonstrate that KnobGen provides a unique advantage without requiring γ tweaking (R4).
