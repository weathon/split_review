## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces AtmosArena, a multi-task benchmark for evaluating foundation models in atmospheric sciences, covering tasks in atmospheric physics and chemistry (forecasting, S2S, infilling, climate model emulation, downscaling, extreme event detection) using datasets including ERA5, ClimateBench, ClimateNet, and Berkeley Earth. It evaluates ClimaX and Stormer alongside traditional baselines and shows that pre-trained models generally outperform task-specific baselines. Reviewers acknowledge the benchmark’s standardization and broader task coverage, but raise concerns about its novelty as a collection of existing benchmarks, missing metrics, lack of deeper analysis, unclear definition of “foundation model” for the atmosphere, and reproducibility issues. One reviewer was notably more critical (score 5) than the other three (scores 6), emphasizing the need for additional metrics and stronger analysis, while the others found the contribution useful despite shortcomings.

### Strengths

- **New Perspective & Useful Combination**: The benchmark evaluates atmosphere modeling from a multitasking perspective, verifies the effectiveness of pre-trained models (ClimaX multi-source, Stormer single-source), and tests interesting aspects of generalizability across complementary tasks.
- **Open-Source Standardization**: Provides a standardized, open-source framework with data, metrics, code, and baselines, setting a new standard for reproducibility and transparency in multi-task atmospheric learning.
- **Finetuning Exploration**: Compares frozen versus fully finetuned backbones, exploring finetuning protocols for foundation models.
- **Diverse Task Coverage**: Includes both atmospheric physics and chemistry tasks, using well-regarded datasets (ERA5, ClimateBench, ClimateNet) – a larger set of tasks than any individual existing framework.
- **Clear Writing and Structure**: The paper is well structured and clearly written; subtasks are appropriate and baselines are well chosen.

### Weaknesses

- **Overlap with Existing Benchmarks and Unclear Necessity**: The benchmark largely collects existing frameworks (Weatherbench, ClimateBench, ClimateNet) and has great overlap with ClimateLearn, ClimaX, and Aurora. Almost all tasks and datasets except ClimateNet and Berkeley Earth are already present. The paper does not clearly compare with these benchmarks or justify why AtmosArena is needed (e.g., new annotations, easier use). One reviewer noted the paper feels like a collection of benchmarks without significant improvement; another questioned why not test on the other datasets directly.
- **Missing Evaluation Metrics**:
  - Inference FLOPs and model parameters are not reported.
  - Spectral metrics (e.g., Power Spectral Density, PSD) are missing for downscaling, forecasting, and inpainting tasks, despite being standard in atmospheric science.
  - Probabilistic metrics (proper scoring rules) are absent; simple point estimates are insufficient for the complexities of these tasks.
  - The newly used “Spectral Divergence” metric lacks a reference; it is unclear if it is proposed in this work.
- **Lack of Deeper Analysis**: The paper reports performance across tasks without further analysis of why models differ, how tasks complement each other, or what underlying atmospheric knowledge is being tested. One reviewer specifically called for explaining the complementarity of tasks in terms of covariance over spatial/temporal scales.
- **Questionable Claim of “First” Multi-Task Benchmark**: The paper states it is the “first multi-task benchmark”, but Table 1 lists existing works that consider multiple atmospheric tasks, so this claim does not hold.
- **Definition of Foundation Model**: It is unclear whether the presented models (ClimaX, Stormer) qualify as foundation models for the atmosphere – the system may be too specific. The paper should define “foundation model for the atmosphere” and justify why the chosen tasks (within narrow spatial/temporal scales) test foundational knowledge.
- **Reproducibility Concerns**: The paper requests evidence (frameworks, code) to demonstrate ease of use and reproducibility. One reviewer noted that code quality is difficult to assess in review but crucial for such a work.
- **Expected Public Leaderboard**: A public leaderboard (e.g., like Google Weatherbench) is only mentioned for the future, but was expected as part of the framework.
- **Suspicious Identical Numbers in Table 4**: In section 4.5, the reported values for ClimaX, ClimaX frozen, and ClimateBench-NN are exactly the same as in the ClimaX publication Table 2, which is surprising given the non-deterministic nature of deep learning training and the small data size.
- **Missing Pretraining Dataset**: AtmosArena only provides evaluation, not a pretraining dataset, which limits its utility for developing new foundation models.
