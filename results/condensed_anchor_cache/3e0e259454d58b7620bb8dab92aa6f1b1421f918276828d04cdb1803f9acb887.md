## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper is a pilot study on unlearning for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs. It identifies that naïve application of existing unlearning methods disrupts the router’s expert selection, causing selection shifts and excessive forgetting. The authors propose UOE (Unlearning One Expert): (1) identify the most relevant expert for the target knowledge via affinity scores from router outputs, (2) apply a router anchor loss to keep that expert activated, and (3) concentrate unlearning on that single expert. Experiments across architectures (including Qwen1.5 MoE and DeepSeek MoE) and benchmarks show improved forget quality and model utility compared to baseline methods.

### Strengths
- Novel and well‑motivated solution that specifically addresses the unique challenge of unlearning in MoE architectures, filling an important gap in the literature.
- Thoroughly conducted experiments and ablation studies that convincingly justify design choices: effectiveness of single‑expert vs. multi‑expert unlearning, impact of single/multi‑layer selection, sensitivity to expert ranking.
- Strong empirical validation across multiple unlearning algorithms, model architectures (Qwen1.5 MoE, DeepSeek MoE), and benchmarks.
- Resource‑efficient: only 0.06% of model parameters are modified.
- Problem is well formalized with clear visual demonstration of the challenge (Fig. 1(a), Fig. 2).
- Detailed introduction to recent studies on MoE and machine unlearning.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear notation and missing definitions** (R1, R2):
  - In the router anchor loss formulation, the term \( g(l) \) is not defined (Eq. (3)).
  - The character \( N \) is used both for number of experts and for size of the calibration dataset (Eq. (2) vs. preliminaries).
- **Insufficient implementation details** (R1, R2, R3):
  - Training hyperparameters are not fully documented (e.g., the \( \lambda \) hyperparameter for the retain loss is not discussed; what happens if \( \lambda = 0 \)?).
  - The \( \text{Unlearn}() \) subroutine in Algorithm 1 lacks specific implementation details; what \( \text{Ll} \) represents and what line 4 means (only router parameters in the selected layer or all router parameters?) are unclear.
- **Uncertainty about the router anchor loss** (R2, R3):
  - It encourages the previously identified target expert to remain consistently activated, but there is no guarantee that this expert is the true target expert. The process might stabilize routing to less relevant experts, undermining unlearning effectiveness.
  - The overall unlearning loss objective is unclear – is it a combination of equation (1) and equation (3)? What hyperparameter controls the anchor loss, and how sensitive is performance to it?
- **Shared experts in Qwen1.5 MoE and DeepSeek MoE are not addressed** (R3):
  - These models have always‑activated shared experts that are not subject to router choices. The paper does not discuss whether shared experts can retain knowledge of the forget set, nor does it provide evidence otherwise.
  - The affinity score is only taken from router outputs, thus ignores shared experts. The reason for this omission is not explained.
- **Justification for unlearning only the top‑1 expert is insufficient** (R2):
  - Figure 2 shows a long‑tail distribution where multiple experts handle a substantial number of tokens. Why restrict unlearning to a single expert? Further reasoning is needed.
- **Vague comparison of “UT at consistent forget efficacy”** (R2):
  - Tables 5 and 7 state “Model utility (UT) comparison, at the same level of forget efficacy” without explaining how hyperparameters are adjusted to keep forget efficacy constant while varying utility. The phrasing needs clarification.
- **Lack of compelling impact and unclear advantages over other MoE frameworks** (R2):
  - The novelty, while present, is not argued to have a compelling impact.
  - Other MoE frameworks also compute affinity scores between inputs and experts. The paper does not sufficiently clarify why UOE’s use of expert attribution is more advantageous. Statements like “it overlooks finer details important for precise comparisons” are vague.
- **Motivation and technical challenge are not fully clarified** (R4):
  - It remains unclear why directly applying decision from initial MoE training (expert selection / weight scores) together with unlearning methods is problematic. The challenge of leveraging pre‑existing routing decisions during unlearning is not crisply explained.
  - It is ambiguous whether performance gains come from reducing routing uncertainty or from routing tokens to the “correct” experts. The paper does not disentangle these two effects.
- **Lack of versatility with other routing mechanisms** (R4):
  - UOE is only demonstrated with TopK routing. Its applicability to dense routing or other forms of sparse routing is not discussed, limiting generalizability.
- **Robustness concerns** (R1):
  - The method selects the expert with the highest affinity score. What happens when multiple experts have closely competing scores? This ablation is missing.
  - If the model is frozen and soft prompt learning is used, can the soft prompt uncover the forget‑set knowledge from other experts? If so, knowledge may still reside elsewhere and be accessible via crafted inputs.
- **Methodology questions left unanswered** (R4):
  - Is the affinity score kept fixed during unlearning (like a golden label)? If so, why not use it as a roadmap for optimization instead of an optimization target? Is it appropriate to refine the routing strategy during unlearning?
  - Case studies on generated unlearning data would help illustrate what happens inside the MoE system.
- **Minor points** (R4):
  - In Fig. 3(a‑b), the difference between settings “Routers+Experts”, “Routers Only”, and “Experts Only” is not explained. The effect of deeper layers on the routing overlap ratio is also unclear.
