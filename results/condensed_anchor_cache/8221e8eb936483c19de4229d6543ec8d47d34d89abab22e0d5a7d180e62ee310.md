## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers evaluated this head-level KV cache compression method (HeadKV / HeadKV-R2). Scores: one reviewer gave 8, three gave 6. The high-scoring reviewer found the head-level allocation and importance estimation novel and praised consistent performance comparable to full KV. The other three reviewers acknowledged contributions (better granularity, dataset for retrieval-reasoning heads, improvements at extreme compression) but raised significant concerns: marginal methodological novelty over Wu et al. (2024), limited practical efficiency, missing implementation details, lack of comparison with concurrent Razorattention, insufficient NIAH length (max 8k), vague estimation equation for reasoning heads, and limited application to retrieval-only tasks. The paper claims 97% of full-KV performance with only 1.5% KV cache retained.

### Strengths

- **Head-level KV cache allocation** with dynamic budget across heads and layers, finer-grained than prior layer-level methods.
- **Performance better or comparable to full KV** across benchmarks (LongBench, LooGLE) under the same total KV cache budget, especially at extreme compression (KV size 64 & 128).
- **Refinement of head importance scoring** from Wu et al. (2024): using top‑k attention weights to refine the retrieval evaluation, justified by ablation studies.
- **Proposed retrieval-reasoning (R2) dataset** may benefit future work on identifying heads for both retrieval and reasoning.
- **Maintains constant total KV cache size and unchanged inference time** relative to other head-level methods.
- **Outperforms baselines by a considerable margin** when retained KV size is very small (e.g., 1.5% of total).

### Weaknesses

- **Marginal methodological contribution.** The core design (score each head, allocate budget) is identical to Wu et al. (2024); the modifications (shift from retrieval to “reasoning”, relaxation of weighting process) are incremental.
- **Retrieval-reasoning estimation equation is vague and unconvincing.**  
  - The first sigma (t-sigma) sum in the equation is not explained.  
  - The needle test includes reasoning logic and an incorrect answer, but the estimation only uses the correct answer (c2), so the reasoning component is not actually evaluated. The claim that the method identifies “reasoning” heads is not justified.
- **Missing details and formatting issues.**  
  - Normalization of \(S_h\) (Equation 4) is not described – it appears \(S_h\) should sum to 1 across all heads and layers, but this is not stated.  
  - Generation of the retrieval-reasoning dataset is not explained.  
  - Left subfigure of Figure 6 is labeled “decoding times” but the text says it includes prefilling time, making the figure confusing; the prefill time for length 0 is squeezed to one point.  
  - Equations (1) and (2): \(a_h\) likely needs a superscript \(t\).  
  - Typo in Figure 1: “Prefilling Phrase” should be “Prefilling Phase”.  
  - Typos in Figures 5 and 8: “Neele-in-a-Haystack” should be “Needle”.  
  - No pseudo code provided; including one would improve clarity.
- **Limited scope of evaluation: retrieval-only tasks.** The method compresses KV caches after reading the instruction, constraining generation to selected tokens. It may fail on summarization, creative generation, or other non‑retrieval tasks; the paper does not break this general limitation of cache‑selection methods.
- **Practical efficiency concerns.**  
  - Analysis is on a 7B model; for larger models, KV cache overhead becomes negligible.  
  - Decoding speed improvement only becomes observable when decoding sequence length reaches ~1000 tokens.  
  - Memory usage differences are only noticeable at context lengths of 32k or more, which is not typical for the tested datasets.  
  - One reviewer noted no clear improvement in latency or memory over Ada‑KV (the work this paper builds upon).
- **Missing comparisons.**  
  - No performance comparison with Razorattention (a concurrent, same‑trajectory head‑level compression method).  
  - No discussion or comparison with RAG methods.  
  - No comparison with other general KV‑cache compression works (Ge et al. 2024, Qin et al. 2024, Zeng et al. 2023).
- **Ablation missing:** Effects of hyperparameters \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) on performance are not studied.
- **NIAH test length insufficient.** The longest prompt in the NIAH evaluation is 8k; the paper should demonstrate reasoning‑head identification at longer contexts (64k or 128k) to support claims about long‑context capabilities.
- **Fragmentation and parallel computation.** Finer‑grained allocation across token/layer/head dimensions may cause memory fragmentation and affect parallel computation efficiency – this is not addressed.
- **Figure 6 conditions unclear.** The x‑axis is “context length” – what is the generation length? Can the method improve memory usage during pre‑filling (context encoding)?
- **Gaps in Figure 6 right subfigure:** It is unclear whether the performance gap between FullKV and HeadKV is solely due to KV cache size or other factors.
