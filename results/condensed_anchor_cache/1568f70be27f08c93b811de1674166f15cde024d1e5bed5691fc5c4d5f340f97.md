## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes Bayesian-OPRO (BOPRO), a search method that combines Bayesian optimization with an LLM-based iterative sampling strategy. BOPRO operates over latent-space embeddings of prompt representations, using a surrogate function to guide exploration and exploitation. The method is evaluated on two language-based search tasks: word search (Sematle) and molecule optimization, plus a joint hypothesis+program search on 1D-ARC. Results show BOPRO outperforms baselines on word search (≥10 points) and molecule optimization (higher quality, 17% fewer invalid molecules), but trails a greedy best-\(k\) prompting baseline on program search. Analysis reveals strong exploration capabilities and adaptability to task difficulty, but a failure to effectively exploit promising regions of the search space. The paper provides transparent discussion of these negative results and potential reasons (e.g., inability of code representation models to distinguish low-edit-distance sequences). One reviewer (rating 8) found the investigation valuable despite the essentially negative results; the other three (ratings 5,6,6) were more critical of the method’s performance and experimental scope.

### Strengths
- **Clear presentation and thorough analysis.** The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and clearly explains the method’s components (latent-space encoding, surrogate modeling, acquisition functions). All relevant works are cited. The authors provide in-depth analysis of BOPRO’s exploration/exploitation trade-off, including separation of solved vs. unsolved cases and search-path visualizations (e.g., Figures 1 and 5).  
- **Novel integration of BO and LLMs.** While other works combine BO and LLMs, BOPRO’s design—applying BO in latent space with a custom encoding-decoding procedure for LLM prompts—is distinct. The method addresses a real gap: dynamic adaptation of search strategy based on evolving uncertainty.  
- **Honest acknowledgment of limitations.** The paper openly discusses BOPRO’s failure to match the greedy baseline, its inability to balance exploration and exploitation, and the specific failure case on program search. This candidness is appreciated by all reviewers.  
- **Valuable insights despite negative results.** One reviewer (rating 8) emphasizes that the empirical investigation—even with negative outcomes—provides a useful starting point for future research on LLM-guided search. The detailed analysis of why BOPRO fails (latent-space discriminability) is informative.  
- **Well-designed illustrations.** Large, intuitive figures (Figure 1 for search path, Figure 5 for exploration/exploration trajectory) effectively communicate the trade-off and make the method more accessible.

### Weaknesses
- **Performance trails greedy baseline.** On the program-search (1D-ARC) task, all BOPRO variants are consistently outperformed by a simple greedy best-\(k\) prompting strategy. The method also underperforms OPRO in both tasks overall (Reviewer 2). This makes the method not yet ready for practical use.  
- **Insufficient experimental scope to support generality claims.** The abstract emphasizes the need for a “one-size-fits-all approach,” but experiments are limited to only two tasks (Sematle word search and 1D-ARC program search, with molecule optimization as a third sub-task). These tasks are relatively simple and not representative of the diversity needed to demonstrate generality. Reviewers (especially Reviewer 4) request additional tasks such as combinatorial optimization (TSP, knapsack), path planning, game problems (Go, RL environments), and program synthesis.  
- **Weak baselines.** Comparisons are limited to three basic strategies (greedy, repeated sampling, random). No state-of-the-art methods from the many works cited in the introduction (e.g., language-based BO variants, LLM-guided search) are included. The paper criticizes prior work for being “too expensive, not performant enough, or impractical,” but does not evaluate whether BOPRO actually overcomes those shortcomings.  
- **Reproducibility concerns.** No code or supplementary materials are provided. Key algorithmic details are missing: the exact expressions for the acquisition functions (e.g., EI, UCB) and their parameters are not given (appendix A.2 lacks formulas). The choice of base models is arbitrary (T5-base for Sematle, Qwen1.5 for 1D-ARC) with no justification or ablation; other models (Llama, GPT-4 series) are not tested despite the original 1D-ARC paper using GPT-4. An overall algorithm description in pseudocode is absent, making the relationship between BO and LLM steps unclear.  
- **Insufficient innovation.** Simply applying LLMs within a standard BO framework to two specific tasks may not qualify as a significant contribution. Numerous prior works already combine BO and LLMs; the paper does not clearly differentiate from them beyond the latent-space encoding.  
- **Exploration/exploitation balance may stem from BO, not LLM.** The method’s strong exploration is likely driven by the acquisition functions (which inherently balance exploration/exploitation), not by the LLM’s reasoning. No ablation study isolates the LLM’s contribution from the BO acquisition choices.  
- **Prompt design may cause underperformance.** The core failure (program search) might be due to task-specific prompts rather than the BOPRO framework itself. Whether modifying prompts would improve results is not explored.  
- **Dependence on an external verifier.** BOPRO requires a reliable reward/score function to evaluate solutions, limiting applicability where such feedback is unavailable or expensive.  
- **Missing details on heuristics.** The method uses PCA for dimensionality reduction and normalizes scores between 0.1 and 0.8 with claimed performance improvement. No analysis is given on how sensitive results are to these heuristics or whether they generalize across tasks. The warm-start prompt count \(W\) is a free parameter; its impact on exploration/exploitation balance is not systematically studied.  
- **Presentation and formatting issues.** Figure 2 does not clearly distinguish BOPRO variants from baselines (line styles or legend labels missing). The term “warm-start” is inconsistently hyphenated. Informal language (“For e.g.,”) and a missing word in a figure caption (“random sampling with low due to …”) should be corrected. Related work is placed late; moving it earlier would better contextualize the method.
