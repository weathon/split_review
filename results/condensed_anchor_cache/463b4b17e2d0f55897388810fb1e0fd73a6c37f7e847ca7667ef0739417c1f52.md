## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q*Agent, a method for training language agents using a learned Q-value function to provide step-wise feedback (process rewards) instead of only outcome-based rewards. The approach involves: (1) initializing the agent via behavioral cloning on expert trajectories, (2) constructing a reasoning tree via MCTS-like exploration with tree pruning (stopping branches that yield zero reward), (3) training a QNet using Bellman’s equation to estimate Q-values for each state-action pair, and (4) using the QNet at inference for action selection (Q*Agent-ST) and for filtering data in self-training. Evaluated on the WebShop benchmark, the method achieves state-of-the-art performance and demonstrates efficiency with limited annotated data. Reviewers generally find the motivation strong but raise significant concerns about limited experimental validation, missing comparisons, writing clarity, and assumptions.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated**: Addresses the important problem of missing process rewards in agent tasks. Reducing annotation costs via automatic step-wise reward generation is valuable (Reviewer 3).
- **Step-wise feedback**: Learning a Q-function provides per-step guidance instead of trajectory-level rewards, which is a clear benefit (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- **Two use cases of Q function**: The paper proposes both inference-time action selection (Q*Agent-ST) and training data filtering via Q-guided self-training, showing utility in both phases (Reviewer 2).
- **State-of-the-art performance**: Q*Agent achieves strong results on WebShop compared to baselines (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Effective MCTS-like framework**: Constructing step-wise rewards using a reasoning tree and tree pruning to reduce search space is a practical adaptation for large action spaces (Reviewer 4).
- **Efficiency with limited data**: Retains strong performance even with half the annotated data (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental domain**: The method is only evaluated on WebShop. Its effectiveness on other agent tasks (e.g., different websites, complex multi-step tasks) remains unclear (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 notes the improvement is small and no variance or number of test episodes is reported, making it hard to assess statistical significance.
- **Missing comparisons**: No results against related Q-learning methods (Q* Wang et al. 2024a, Zhai et al. 2024) or inference-time self-improvement approaches (Reflexion, LATS, “Autonomous Evaluation and Refinement of Digital Agents”, “Tree Search for Language Model Agents”) are provided (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). The cost of finetuning an additional QNet is not justified against methods that do not require it (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear contributions over prior work**: The paper is closely related to Q* and Q-value model enhanced (Zhai et al., 2024). It is unclear whether adding a behavioral cloning stage is a significant contribution; a detailed explanation is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Terminology issues**: Steps 2 and 4 in Figure 1 are called “exploration” but use the best action based on current knowledge (reward=1 or max Q), which aligns with exploitation in RL. This terminology may mislead RL researchers (Reviewer 1).
- **Tree pruning limitations**: The algorithm stops exploring a branch if it yields a zero reward, potentially discarding partially correct trajectories (only final parts incorrect) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). For tasks requiring many steps to get a positive final reward, the probability of finding a successful trajectory is very low; the authors even restrict expansion to the first 3–5 steps, limiting applicability (Reviewer 4).
- **Q-guided self-training omission**: The algorithm does not use the dataset collected during step 2 (reasoning tree trajectories) in Q-guided self-training – why? (Reviewer 1).
- **Confusion about Q*Agent-ST vs Q*Agent-I**: In step 4, Q*Agent-ST uses QNet to select the best action among m candidates, while Q*Agent-I samples an action without QNet. If that is correct, what is the purpose of QNet? (Reviewer 1).
- **MDP design and environment assumptions**: The state includes the entire interaction history, leading to many tokens on long episodes; alternative summarization is not explored (Reviewer 4). The paper assumes the environment is needed for state transitions (like MCTS) but does not discuss whether it is deterministic/stochastic or a black box, and how that affects the tree search (Reviewer 4).
- **Task-specific Q-network**: The method requires training a separate Q-value network per task, limiting generalization – a core advantage of LLMs. The trade-off between task-specific training and RL-based LLM fine-tuning is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Poor writing and missing details**: The paper lacks many essential explanations (Reviewer 3). Examples: implementation of “augmenting action diversity with perturbation” (Section 4.4), termination condition for tree construction, definition of \(C_t\) in Equation (4). Hyper-parameters (e.g., discount factor \(\gamma\)) are not listed. Metrics in Table 1 are not explained. The sampling number per step for Q*Agent-I is not provided. Equation (3) is not cross-entropy loss. Line 415: Table 2 is not organized into three sections. Notations in Appendix A.3 (\(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Q},q_m\)) are undefined, and \(\arg\max\) is used incorrectly (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Visualization issues**: Fig. 3(a) horizontal axis should indicate number of sampled actions; Fig. 3(b) lacks ordinate label (Reviewer 3).
- **Ablation experiments missing**: No ablation studies on tree pruning strategy, “early stage” length, or depth of exploration (Reviewer 4). The paper also does not compare against standard MCTS directly (Reviewer 4).
- **SFT already strong**: In experiments, SFT alone achieves satisfactory results; the paper should show Q*Agent’s performance when the SFT dataset is poor to demonstrate the impact of RL (Reviewer 1).
