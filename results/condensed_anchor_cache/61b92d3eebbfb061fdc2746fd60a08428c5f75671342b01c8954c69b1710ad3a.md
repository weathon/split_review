## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper develops dynamic data structures for two kernel-based problems: (i) kernel density estimation (KDE), maintaining estimates for a query set Q as the data set X undergoes insertions/deletions, and (ii) sparse approximation of the fully connected similarity graph on X with kernel-weighted edges. The KDE structure dynamizes the static algorithm of Charikar et al. (2020); the similarity graph structure builds on the method of Macgregor & Sun (2023). Theoretical guarantees include sublinear update time. Experiments compare against exact recomputation, DYNAMICRS (resampling 10% of points), and the static CKNS algorithm, using several real-world datasets.

### Strengths
- Kernel density estimation is a fundamental problem with many applications; the dynamic setting is less studied but practically important (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The application to dynamic similarity graph maintenance is interesting and relevant (Reviewer 1).
- The paper provides mathematically rigorous guarantees for the dynamic data structures (Reviewer 4).
- Empirical results show that the approach is implementable and performs favorably on multiple datasets (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Minority positive view:** One reviewer (R1, rating 8) judged the paper to have sufficient technical novelty and found no significant weaknesses, contrasting with the other reviewers’ assessments of limited novelty and experimental shortcomings.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to relevant prior work:** The paper does not experimentally compare its similarity graph construction to the method of Macgregor & Sun (2023) that it builds on (R1). It also fails to compare its KDE guarantees to those of Liang et al. (2024), which achieve qualitatively similar initialization and update times (R4). These omissions make it difficult to evaluate novelty and practical advantage.
- **Experimental design limitations:** The comparison to DYNAMICRS uses a fixed 10% sampling rate that may not be optimal; a trade-off plot between runtime and error (varying the sampling rate) is needed for a fair assessment across operating points (R2, R3). The error metric is not clearly defined as absolute or relative (R2). DYNAMICRS often achieves lower error than the proposed method, raising questions about the proposed method’s practical advantage without further parameter adjustment (R2, R3).
- **Limited technical novelty:** The central ideas for dynamizing hashing-based KDE estimators are naturally suited to dynamic settings (points can be added/removed from hash buckets), so the adaptation does not require significantly new ideas (R2, R3, R4). The paper does not clearly articulate the main algorithmic innovations needed (R2). *Contrast:* Reviewer 1 disagrees, deeming the novelty sufficient.
- **Dependence on the smallest query value:** The update time guarantee depends on the smallest query value; for kernels like the Gaussian, this value can be extremely small, potentially negating sublinear claims in practice. The paper does not adequately address this dependence or discuss how kernel choice affects practical performance (R4).
- **Lack of hyperparameter guidance:** The paper does not discuss how parameters of the proposed method should be selected in practice, a significant concern for real-world deployment (R3).
- **No lower bound analysis:** The paper does not address whether the $n^{1/4}$ time for Gaussian kernels can be improved; a lower bound or discussion of barriers would strengthen the contribution (R1).
