## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MambaQuant, a post-training quantization (PTQ) framework for Mamba family models. It identifies key challenges: outliers in gate projections, output projections, and matrix multiplications; the parallel scan operation amplifying these outliers; and Hadamard transform's failure to equalize channel variance. MambaQuant consists of: (1) Karhunen-Loève Transformation (KLT) enhanced rotation, replacing Hadamard with an adaptive rotation from eigen-decomposition of weight/activation covariance, for offline weight quantization; (2) Smooth-Fused rotation, applying SmoothQuant-style smoothing to Mamba's SiLU and parallel scan for online activation quantization. Experiments on vision and language tasks show less than 1% accuracy loss under W8A8, outperforming QuaRot, SmoothQuant, and GPTQ.

### Strengths
- The KLT-enhanced rotation is an interesting and well-motivated method for controlling variance in Mamba quantization (R1, R4). The motivation follows from both theoretical and empirical perspectives; the weaknesses of the Hadamard transform for Mamba are clearly demonstrated (R4).  
- MambaQuant demonstrates solid performance on both vision and language tasks, across different model sizes, with minimal degradation compared to full-precision Mamba (R2, R3, R4). It achieves <1% accuracy loss on W8A8, significantly improving over existing Transformer-focused PTQ methods like QuaRot and SmoothQuant (R1, R2).  
- The paper provides ablation studies and visualizations showing how the proposed methods improve activation distributions and quantizability (R3, R4).  
- Smooth-Fused rotation adapts SmoothQuant's smoothing vector to Mamba's non-linear SiLU and PScan operations (R2).  
- Authors intend to release the code (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **KLT fixed-input issue:** The KLT-enhanced rotation is computed offline, but the eigenvectors are fixed for all inputs. This may not uniformly transform channel variance across different input batches or datasets. The paper does not investigate the stability of the computed eigenvectors across diverse data, so the method’s claimed whitening effect is not empirically confirmed (R2, R4). Reviewer 4 (score 8) further notes that the computational overhead of the KLT (time and memory for both offline and online application) is not thoroughly analyzed beyond appendix estimates, and that runtime/memory changes in practice (e.g., prefill time) are not reported (R4). Reviewer 2 (score 5) questions whether a fixed K can address all input’s channel variance.  
- **Missing comparison to simpler baselines:** Reviewer 1 suggests a simpler alternative: after Hadamard transform, apply a diagonal scaling matrix to equalize variance across rows (similar to per-channel quantization). This direct comparison is missing and should be included to justify the added complexity of KLT (R1).  
- **Limited insight beyond straightforward linear algebra:** The KLT is essentially SVD on weights/activations and fusing eigenvectors into the Hadamard matrix. The paper does not articulate additional insights beyond this straightforward operation (R3). The method for deriving K from different input X is also not detailed (R2).  
- **Lack of runtime and memory benchmarking:** No actual prefill time, average latency, or memory footprint measurements are provided; only theoretical complexity estimates are given (R4).  
- **Scope limited to Mamba:** The methods are studied and presented as applicable only to the Mamba family. Reviewer 4 suggests exploring transfer to other architectures (e.g., Transformers) to broaden impact, even if modifications are needed (R4).  
- **Prior-art acknowledgment gap:** The paper claims to be the first comprehensive analysis of Mamba quantization, but prior work "Mamba-PTQ" already discussed outliers in gate and output projections. The specific differences between MambaQuant and Mamba-PTQ are not elaborated (R3).  
- **Insufficient explanation of why Transformer methods fail on Mamba:** The visualized value distribution in Figure 1 is reported to look similar to Transformers. More discussion is needed on why methods like QuaRot fail for Mamba while working for Transformers (R1). Additionally, 8-bit quantization is easy for Transformers without significant loss, but appears hard for Mamba—this discrepancy should be discussed (R1).  
- **Missing detailed per-task results:** For the LM-eval benchmark, detailed per-task results should be included in the appendix to improve reproducibility and allow easier comparison (R3).
