## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes DMM, a score distillation-based model merging paradigm to compress multiple specialized text-to-image (T2I) teacher models into a single versatile student model (same UNet architecture). A learnable style codebook (embedding) distinguishes each teacher and controls style at inference. The student is trained via MSE losses for score distillation and feature imitation, plus a multi-class adversarial loss. Experiments use 8 teacher styles (4 realistic, 4 anime/illustration) on SDv1.5. The method is also evaluated in a continual learning setup (4 realistic then 4 cartoon models). The paper introduces a per-task/per-style FID evaluation metric.

### Strengths

- The paper presents a compelling, well-written narrative that clearly introduces the problem of merging multiple specialized T2I models to reduce storage and parameter redundancy. The literature review and introduction are thorough and well-structured.
- The proposed pipeline is reasonable: combining score distillation, feature imitation, and adversarial learning yields the best results in ablations.
- The use of a learnable embedding as a style prompt (codebook) is interesting and enables controllable arbitrary-style generation.
- The evaluation metric (FID computed separately per task/style) is appropriate for the merging task.
- The method has promising application potential and practical value for consolidating diverse community models.
- Numerous visual results support the capabilities of the trained model, and the figures are well-presented (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited stylistic diversity and setup:** The evaluation uses only 8 teacher models, with 4 categorized as realistic and 4 as anime/illustration (essentially two broad categories). This makes it difficult to assess handling of a wide spectrum of artistic variations or more complex style mixtures. Reviewers recommend exploring more distinctive styles (e.g., MoXin, M_Pixel, Miniature World) to test robustness, and investigating scalability to 20 teachers, especially when styles overlap or are similar (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Lack of strong evidence for the claimed problem and improvement over simple baselines:**
  - The paper claims issues with naive merging (incompatibility, confusion) but does not provide concrete demonstrations of artifacts, conflict modes, or style blending failures (Reviewer 1).
  - Baseline results without any proposed additions (e.g., the initialized student model before training) are missing from Table 1. Without this, the magnitude of improvement is unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - Table 2 lacks upper-bound teacher performance for comparison, so the gap to ideal performance is unknown (Reviewer 1).
  - Visual results for baseline and ablation configurations are missing, making it hard to assess the practical impact of reducing FID from 80 to 77 (Reviewer 1).
- **Marginal gain over simpler alternatives:** As shown in Table 3, the proposed method achieves only minor FID improvements over weighted averaging (a much simpler approach). The benefits beyond claimed flexibility are not clearly demonstrated, and the method for manually determining weights in weighted merging is not explained (Reviewers 1, 2). The practicality of the method is further questioned given the prevalence of LoRA-based fine-tuning (which is already lightweight) and the computational cost of DMM training vs. using prompt engineering or LoRA combos with SDXL (Reviewer 2).
- **Scope limited to style; missing content/control merging:** The experiments only cover style transfer across different aesthetics. The paper does not explore mixing content domains (e.g., human faces with objects, indoor and outdoor scenes) or merging multiple control conditions (e.g., animation + control nets) – capabilities the community may need (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Weak incremental learning evaluation:**
  - The continual learning setup initializes with 4 realistic-style teachers then adds 4 cartoon-style teachers. Due to the clear visual distinction between these categories, the setup does not effectively test resistance to catastrophic forgetting. Reviewers recommend a more challenging sequence with interleaved styles and multiple new tasks, and reporting per-style FIDs instead of averages (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Potential style homogenization from generalized dataset:** The method uses a generic training dataset (no access to teacher original data). For highly specialized or artistic styles, this may cause loss of subtle nuances. The paper lacks analysis of how the generalized dataset affects fidelity, especially for less common styles (Reviewer 3).
- **Learnable embeddings and style prompts not clearly specified:**
  - The exact nature of the style prompts is unclear: are they special text tokens appended to the prompt, or trainable parameters in the model? How large are the embeddings? How does the method ensure it learns distinct style representations (MoE-like ability)? What happens if no style prompt is selected – does the output resemble base SD1.5? (Reviewers 1, 4, 3).
  - Model initialization (SD1.5 weights vs. random) is not explicitly stated (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient analysis of loss weights and sensitivity:** The method for determining weights for the total loss (score distillation, feature imitation, adversarial) and their sensitivity is not explained. Similarly, the weight tuning process for the weighted merging baseline is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparisons to style transfer/control methods:** If the goal is purely style migration, the paper should compare to methods like IP-Adapter, InstantStyle, StyleShot, CSGO (Reviewer 4).
- **Practicality limitation:** The proposed approach requires access to all fine-tuned teacher models during training, limiting applicability when such access is restricted or when models are stored only as LoRAs (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Exaggerated introductory claims:** The introduction presents a broad vision of merging diverse model capabilities, but the paper only demonstrates style learning. More exploration of broader capabilities is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor inaccuracies:** The paper incorrectly expands DDPM as “Denoising Diffusion Probability Model” (should be Probabilistic) and “Generative Adversary Networks” (should be Adversarial, attributed to Goodfellow et al. rather than Song & Ermon) (Reviewer 1).
