## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the paper’s effort in creating a large-scale dense caption dataset (nuDESIGN) and a baseline model (DESIGN-former) for 3D dense captioning in traffic scenes, and note the timeliness of combining LLMs with autonomous driving. However, they raise major concerns about the unclear effectiveness of the proposed middleware for downstream driving tasks, insufficient comparisons with existing 3D dense captioning methods, unconvincing end-to-end driving experiments, and over-claiming of comprehensiveness and scalability. Reviewer 3 is more positive about the dataset’s potential contribution (score 6, borderline accept) but still doubts its practical usefulness, while Reviewers 1 and 2 are more critical (score 3 each, reject) and question the actual value of the language-based representation for driving.

### Strengths
1. **Timely and important direction**: Exploring intermediate representations (DESIGN) that connect perception and planning, especially in the context of leveraging language foundation models for autonomous driving.
2. **New representation DESIGN**: Defines five components (Appearance, Direction, Distance, Motion, Road Map) aiming for a comprehensive and standard format that covers a wide range of information.
3. **Large-scale dataset nuDESIGN**: Contains 2,300k descriptions for 1,165k objects in 850 scenes, described as the largest traffic-scene captioning dataset. The dataset is open-sourced, with 2000 hours of expert human annotation effort, which could be useful for the community.
4. **Baseline method DESIGN-former**: Builds on existing detectors (BEVFusion-MIT, BEVFormer) with adapters on LLaMa; easy to reproduce. Achieves favorable results on the proposed dataset.
5. **Comprehensive dataset analysis**: Includes multiple graphs illustrating distributions of words and sentences.

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear downstream effectiveness**: The actual usefulness of DESIGN for autonomous driving tasks (e.g., planning) is not convincingly demonstrated. The end-to-end experiment with UniAD shows only marginal improvement and no significant difference compared to random initialization (Table 6). The smoother trajectory in Fig. 15 could be due to post-processing; no clear analysis shows that improvements are directly from DESIGN rather than other factors. The paper should use challenging interactive scenarios (e.g., ReasonNet, CVPR 2023) that require explicit modeling of socially semantic spatial-temporal relationships.
2. **Comprehensiveness is questionable**: The annotation starts from pre-labeled bounding boxes and primarily adds color descriptions (Fig. 2a,b), which are not critical for driving, while dropping accurate heading angle and velocity that are present in original nuScenes boxes. The language representation may not take more helpful information for driving compared to attaching metadata directly to boxes. Missing critical aspects: uncertain intents of objects and ability to facilitate forecasting. The paper does not address how the model handles agents with multiple possible intentions or how errors/collisions occur when intentions are misinterpreted.
3. **Missing comparisons with existing 3D dense captioning methods**: The paper lacks direct comparisons with Vote2Cap-DETR (CVPR 2023), 3DJCG (CVPR 2022), and D3Net (ECCV 2022). Without these, it is unclear whether the reported results are state-of-the-art or whether gains come simply from better detectors. The corresponding captions in 2D dense captioning are also not clarified.
4. **Over-claim of “scalable automatic labeling pipeline”**: Human evaluation is in the loop, so the pipeline is not fully automatic. The influence of human checks should be explained; it is unclear if a fine-tuned expert captioner could achieve full automation when scaling to more diverse data.
5. **Lack of ablations**: The paper would benefit from ablations on adapters or training strategies to provide more insight into the method. Comparisons to previous methods do not bring insights without such analysis.
6. **Experimental detail questions**: Are object proposals boxes (N×9) or features (N×C)? How can one tell from Fig. 15 that UniAD fails to avoid the bus stop? (Reviewer 2)
7. **Writing issues**: Section 2.3 (Learning-based / End-to-end Autonomous Driving) seems not directly relevant. Input modality in Table 1 may be incorrect (e.g., Talk2Car/Refer-KITTI can take LiDAR). Equation 1 uses \(P_O\) instead of \(P_{ego}\), and Equations 1–3 are too naive to show in a single line.
