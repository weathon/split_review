## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a VIPER framework for time series forecasting that decomposes the signal into trend and seasonal components. The trend is modeled linearly; the seasonal component uses FFT to select dominant frequencies, then passes through a Temporal Aggregation Block that applies global self-attention followed by frequency weighting. A backbone (CNN, RNN, etc.) produces the final seasonal representation, which is summed with the trend prediction. Experiments are conducted on standard forecasting datasets and baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to read, with a substantial and well-organized literature review (Reviewer 1).
- The conducted experiments are appropriate for the forecasting problem and include an ablation study (Reviewer 1).
- The appendices contain interesting attention visualization experiments (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed period representation block is reasonably designed for processing periodic time series and is versatile, capable of integrating into various forecasting models as a plug-in (Reviewer 2).
- The framework achieves good performance compared to other methods on some real datasets (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Overall novelty is limited. The decomposition into trend and seasonal components is standard, FFT is standard, and the Temporal Aggregation Block is essentially a simple seasonal filtering algorithm with a Transformer shell. This represents a minor innovation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Major omission of a key baseline**: PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023) is mentioned in the paper but its results are not included in the comparison table. PatchTST results are almost always better than VIPER, and this deliberate omission does not adhere to good scientific practices (Reviewer 1). This criticism is reinforced by Reviewer 2’s concern about flawed experiments and Reviewer 3’s lack of clear distinction from prior work.
- **Experimental flaws**:
  - The reported TimesNet results in Table 2 are inconsistent with the original paper. In the original work TimesNet outperformed DLinear, but the paper shows the opposite (Reviewer 2).
  - The reported average results in Table 3 are incorrect. According to Reviewer 2, the proposed block yields only a slight 2.9% improvement in MSE.
- **Presentation clarity issues**: While Reviewer 1 praised the writing, Reviewer 2 noted several confusions. The purpose of “Avg” in Equation (1) is unclear; the representation of Equation (3) needs clarification regarding which axis the attention operation is performed on; and it is not explained how the block captures intra-period and inter-period variations separately (Reviewer 2). Additionally, the technical details of the trend-seasonal decomposition are insufficient (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of distinction from prior work**: Many previous methods also decompose time series into trend and seasonality. The paper does not clearly articulate what sets VIPER apart, and the claimed efficiency advantage (mentioned in the introduction) is not emphasized in the experimental evaluation (Reviewer 3).
- **Assumption about periodicity and distribution shift**: The method implicitly assumes periodic patterns, yet the paper also discusses distribution shift, which contradicts periodicity. No analysis is provided to verify whether the normalized data indeed exhibits periodic behavior (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experiment on incomplete data**: The authors did not include experiments with masked data (incomplete time series), which is common in forecasting articles (Reviewer 1, question). This overlaps with the criticism that the experimental evaluation is incomplete.
