## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes DefNTaxS, a method that uses an LLM to automatically generate a taxonomy (subcategories grouping similar classes) and appends this hierarchical information to CLIP text prompts (“{c} which is/has {d}, {g(C,T_c)}”) for zero-shot classification. The method is fully automated without additional training. Experiments on ImageNet, CUB, Oxford Pets, DTD, Food101, Places365, EuroSAT show accuracy gains over simple “a photo of {c}” prompts and some comparisons to prior work. Ablations investigate prompt length, format, and taxonomic refinement.

### Strengths

- Method is simple and straightforward; changes prompts to a more carefully designed form using taxonomies [R1, R4].
- Clear and well-structured paper, easy to follow with logical flow [R3, R4].
- Fine-grained analysis and investigations on factors of building good semantic prompts offer practical inspirations [R2].
- Extensive experimental evaluations provide valuable insights; method shows consistent improvements across a majority of evaluated datasets [R4].
- Performance gains are good on some datasets, e.g., Oxford Pets and EuroSAT across all CLIP sizes [R2, R3].
- Method is both simple and well-motivated, easy to implement [R4].

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty.** Employing class hierarchy is not groundbreaking; closely related works include CHiLS, D-CLIP, Ren et al. (NeurIPS 2024) on ChatGPT-powered hierarchical comparisons [R1, R2]. Technical novelty is limited given many existing CLIP prompt augmentation works [R2]. WaffleCLIP already introduced adding one high-level concept to prompts; the baseline used for comparison may only include random characters/words without accounting for high-level concepts [R4]. The paper’s technical similarity to CHiLS is high if only a single layer of subcategories is generated (needs clarification and ablation) [R2].
- **Missing baseline comparisons.** Important baselines CHiLS and MPVR are mentioned in related work but not compared [R2]. CuPL is mentioned in Section 4.2 but never compared [R4]. Missing related works on prompt augmentation with semantic discriminativeness: S3A, Meta-Prompting, LLM Explainer [R2].
- **Methodological details unclear.** The paper fails to explain how clustering is done, what g(C, T_c) exactly is, and how the superset is refined (line 181) [R1]. The method uses many hyperparameters (|c|/10, |C|<20) that appear handcrafted and lack generalization; how are they decided? [R1].
- **Potential overclaim and unclear motivation.** The motivation starts from game theory, but competition/players in categorization contexts are not clearly defined; no direct relationship [R2]. Claimed “semantic interpretability” advantage is unsupported—no interpretability results/comparisons or qualitative examples [R2]. The paper states high intra-class similarity contributes to model confusion, but high intra-class similarity (images within same class visually alike) should benefit classification; this discrepancy needs clarification [R4].
- **Weakness in main experimental results.** Comparison to CLIP on ImageNet (68.03 with ViT B/16) is not aligned with best reported results (e.g., CLIP ICML 2021 gets 68.6 with certain prompting templates), suggesting implementation or baseline choice issues [R1]. Gains in Table 1 are generally minor and may be due to hyperparameter optimization rather than the method [R3]. Table 2 shows the method often does not outperform other approaches; for B/16 and L-14, D-CLIP achieves slightly better with negligible differences [R3]. Largest improvements are on Oxford Pets and EuroSAT (few classes, high inter-class similarity), contradicting claim that largest gains occur on high-class-count or high-intra-class-similarity datasets [R4]. The method underperforms on a substantial portion of datasets [R3].
- **Technical issues with taxonomy.** The LLM-generated subcategories are not guaranteed to satisfy completeness and disjoint constraints stated in Section 3.1; the extent to which constraints hold and implications need investigation [R2]. If only one layer of taxonomy, high similarity to CHiLS; otherwise more ablations needed [R2]. Appending taxonomy may not reduce inter-class competition because similarity often occurs within the same taxonomy; clarification needed, and a proposed experiment is to measure if false predictions occur inside vs. outside the defined taxonomy [R4].
- **Presentation issues.** Figure 1 occupies too much space; all equations lack equation numbers for reference [R1, R2, R4]. Paragraphs in Section 6.3 could use subtitles [R2]. Typos: Line 40 “also” split; Figure 1 “visualisation” should be “visualization”; Line 47 “labelsWhile” [R3].
- **Lack of qualitative examples.** No detailed qualitative examples of the proposed prompt contexts are provided [R2, R4].
