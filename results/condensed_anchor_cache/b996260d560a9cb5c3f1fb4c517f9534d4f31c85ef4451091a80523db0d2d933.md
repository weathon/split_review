## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends conformal prediction to language models (LMs) to produce prediction sets of generated responses with statistical guarantees. Instead of enumerating the entire output space, the method samples responses from an LM and applies a calibrated stopping rule (when the set-level confidence exceeds a threshold or a max sample count is reached) and a rejection rule (removing low-quality or too-similar responses). The configuration parameters are selected via the Learn-Then-Test (LTT) framework. An extension identifies individual components (phrases, sentences) that are each independently correct. Experiments on radiology report generation, news summarization, and open-domain QA using multiple LMs (GPT2-small, T5-XL, LLaMA-13B) validate the coverage guarantees.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important problem of providing statistical (distribution-free) guarantees on LM outputs.
- Extends conformal prediction, originally for classification/regression, to the generative setting of LMs.
- The algorithm is interpretable: sample, reject low-quality/duplicate responses, stop when the set is confident.
- Theoretical results (Theorem 4.2) are corroborated experimentally on three diverse tasks.
- The paper is well-written, with sufficient detail and easy to follow.
- Provides an interesting application of the LTT framework, formulating risk control over tuning parameters.
- Breaking down long generations into components (e.g., sentences) is a nice way to handle uncertainty quantification for longer texts.
- The framework is general, allowing independent future improvements to individual scoring functions.

### Weaknesses
- Several design choices are not adequately explained: why Eq. 7 for \(\hat{\lambda}\), why the definition of \(C^{\text{inner}}_{\gamma}\) differs during calibration, and how the Pareto Testing procedure (Section B.1) improves efficiency. (Reviewer 1)
- Proposition 4.4 (individual component guarantee) is not experimentally validated; no set-loss results for the component case. (Reviewer 1)
- Theorem 4.2 only guarantees at least one “acceptable” response per set, but says little about individual responses; the component analysis does not address the whole-response level. (Reviewer 1)
- The use of ROUGE‑L as a proxy for generation quality is a significant weakness: it captures only lexical similarity, may produce null hyperparameters if a stricter admission function is used, and since ROUGE also serves as the diversity detector in the pipeline, it effectively “judges itself.” (Reviewer 4)
- The benefit of the approach is unclear; one reviewer questions whether the stopping rule realistically requires access to test-set answers and whether simpler baselines (e.g., sampling N times and checking the ground truth) would suffice. (Reviewer 2, minority more negative)
- Lack of comparison with dedicated diversity-control decoding methods such as Stochastic Beam Search or NeuroLogic sampling. (Reviewer 2)
- The procedure in Section 4 may never terminate if the LM always returns similar outputs; using ROUGE/BLEU to score diversity is questionable. (Reviewer 2)
- Need for experiments across different model scales (e.g., GPT2‑small vs. GPT2‑XL) to assess whether the method becomes less important at larger scales. (Reviewer 2)
- The baseline “First‑\(k\)” does not use rejection; a fairer comparison would include First‑\(k\) with the rejection scheme. (Reviewer 1)
- Computational time relative to the choice of \(k_{\max}\) is not discussed; could \(k_{\max}\) be treated as an additional tuning parameter \(\lambda_4\) to avoid uninformative results? (Reviewer 3)
- For individual component pruning, it would be useful to tune \(\gamma\) jointly with the \(\lambda\)’s to avoid a union bound. (Reviewer 3)
- The achievable risk range for MIMIC‑CXR is very high; the paper should discuss how to reduce it for practical utility. (Reviewer 1)
- AUC values for set-loss curves are reported >0.5, but since all curves lie below the diagonal, AUC should be <0.5 – the experimental reporting or interpretation may be incorrect. (Reviewer 1)
- The data splits (train/calibration/test) are not clearly explained; e.g., how the dev set is used for conformal prediction. (Reviewer 1)
- Thresholds for ROUGE‑L (0.4, 0.35) are only said to be “picked through manual validation” without further detail. (Reviewer 4)
- Presentation issues: missing definitions (\(\mathcal{Y}\), \(L_{\text{test}}\), \(F_X, F_Y\)), inconsistent notation (\(\mathcal{Y}\) vs \(\mathcal{V}^*\)), missing superscripts (\(A^c_{\text{test}}\), \(\bar{L}^c\)), and need for more descriptive table captions in Appendix H. (Reviewer 1)
