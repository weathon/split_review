## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Frequency-based Decentralized Adaptation (FreDA) framework for Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) under heterogeneous data streams. The core idea is data-centric: decompose samples via Fourier transform, use high-frequency features to cluster similar distribution shifts, and adapt separate local models per cluster. These local models periodically aggregate parameters into a global model, and a Fourier-based augmentation (adding Gaussian noise in frequency domain) is used to improve robustness. Experiments on corrupted, natural, and medical image classification show performance improvements over prior TTA methods.

### Strengths
- The problem of heterogeneous data streams in TTA is important, practical, and one of the hardest in the TTA setting (R2, R3, R4).
- Strong empirical results across multiple datasets and settings (corrupted, natural, medical), with significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods (R1, R4).
- Novel application of Fourier analysis in TTA: the discriminative power of high-frequency features (Figure 1) provides strong motivation for clustering different distribution shifts (R1, R2).
- The overall idea is clear, interesting, and the data-centric approach is considered innovative by one reviewer (R4), while others note limited novelty in individual components (see Weaknesses).
- The approach offers a reasonable solution to the heterogeneous-data problem (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty in individual components**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2) note that the weight aggregation scheme closely resembles existing federated learning approaches, Fourier transformation and filtering are conventional, clustering with multiple local models is not new, and Fourier-domain augmentation was previously proposed (e.g., [R2] Fourier-basis Functions to Bridge Augmentation Gap, CVPR 2024). One reviewer (R4) finds the data-centric framing innovative, but this contrasts with the majority view on component-level novelty.
- **Missing ablation studies**: The contributions of the two main components—clustering-based decentralized adaptation and Fourier augmentation—need to be separated to understand their individual effects (R2).
- **No variance/repetition reported**: Only mean performance is given; standard deviation or number of experimental repeats are missing (R3).
- **Limited scenario and tasks**: Only the simplest mixed distribution (mix of covariate shifts) is considered; real-world scenarios often combine covariate and label shifts (R3). Experiments are restricted to image classification; it is unclear whether the method works with vision-language models like CLIP or can be combined with them (R3).
- **Missing related work**: A relevant TTA paper (ODS: Test-Time Adaptation in the Presence of Open-World Data Shift, ICML 2023) is not discussed (R3).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper does not provide generalization bounds or an explanation of the method’s limitations (R4).
- **Clarification needed on “data-centric”**: The paper contrasts with model-centric TTA, but most TTA methods also involve data adaptation; the distinction is unclear (R4).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Notation is inconsistent (parameters denoted as θ_k in Equation 4 but as W_k in Equation 5 without explanation) (R1). Visualizations are missing: examples of images after low-frequency filtering, cluster assignment characteristics, how different shifts are captured by Fourier features, and examples of augmented images (R1, R2).
- **Potential loss of local information in weight update**: The mechanism in Equation 6 (assigning global aggregated weights to local models) may shift local weights too far from their previous specialized state, reducing adaptation performance on each local domain (R2, question).
- **High computational overhead**: Using multiple local models, Fourier transforms, and an additional augmentation step likely increases running time; a runtime comparison against baselines is requested (R2).
