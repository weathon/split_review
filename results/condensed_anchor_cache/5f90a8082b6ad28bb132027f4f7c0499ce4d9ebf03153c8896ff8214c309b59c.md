## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses test-time adaptation (TTA) in multi-label settings (ML–TTA) by proposing Bound Entropy Minimization (BEM). Unlike standard entropy minimization that only boosts the most confident class, BEM increases confidence for top‑k predicted labels simultaneously. For each augmented view, a paired caption is retrieved to determine the number of labels (k); these labels form a weak label set (view) and a strong label set (caption) of identical size k. The framework learns both view and caption prompts using BEM. Experiments on MSCOCO, VOC, and NUSWIDE across architectures (RN50, ViT‑B/16) and prompt initializations show superior performance over existing methods and the original CLIP model.

### Strengths

- Focuses on the underexplored and important problem of test‑time adaptation for multi‑label classification.  
- Offers a good theoretical analysis that explains why vanilla entropy minimization fails in multi‑label settings.  
- The proposed Bound Entropy Minimization (BEM) is a significant and innovative theoretical and practical advancement for ML–TTA.  
- The use of paired captions as pseudo‑labels to determine the number of positive labels per instance is a clever strategy.  
- Leverages both visual and textual modalities (view prompts and caption prompts) for robust adaptation to distribution shifts.  
- Robust experimentation across diverse datasets (MSCOCO, VOC, NUSWIDE) and architectures (RN50, ViT‑B/16), demonstrating generalizability and efficacy.  
- Achieves best performance compared to the latest state‑of‑the‑art methods.  
- Paper is well‑structured and figures are well presented.  
- (Minority note: One reviewer, who gave an 8, finds the paper notably well‑structured, the problem clearly stated, and the solution particularly innovative; the other reviewers—scores 5,6,6—acknowledge the merits but raise more concerns about clarity and fairness.)

### Weaknesses

- Equation (6) is difficult to understand; more explanation is needed, particularly on how weak labels and strong labels are identified and the meaning of \(\hat{s}_{ij}^{x^{test}}\) (Reviewers 1,2).  
- It is unclear which parameters are learnable in the framework – all learnable parameters should be explicitly listed (Reviewer 1).  
- The motivation for view prompts and caption prompts, and why they are useful in this setting, needs more explanation (Reviewers 1,3).  
- The method involves multiple steps (view augmentation, caption retrieval, label binding), which introduces complexity that could hinder practical usability; simplifying the process would help (Reviewer 3).  
- The effectiveness heavily relies on the quality and relevance of the paired captions; in real‑world scenarios, captions may not accurately represent image content, affecting performance (Reviewer 3).  
- The choice of top‑k is important but not adequately justified; how is k determined? How is the selected weak label set guaranteed to be reliable? (Reviewer 4).  
- The term “augmented view” is not defined or explained – what constitutes an augmented view and what role does it play in the framework? (Reviewer 4).  
- The selected comparison methods may not be specifically designed for multi‑label datasets; the fairness of the comparison is questionable. The authors should include more ML–TTA‑specific frameworks (Reviewer 4). A related concern is the lack of detailed discussion about the choice of baselines and why they underperform (Reviewer 2).  
- Table 1 lacks a description of the evaluation metric; also, marking the second‑best result in experimental tables would benefit the reader (Reviewer 4).
