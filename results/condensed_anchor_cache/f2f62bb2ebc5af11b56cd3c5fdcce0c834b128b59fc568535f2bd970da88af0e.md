## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Flexible Meta Pruning (FMP) for lightweight image super-resolution (SR), combining structured (channel) and unstructured (weight) pruning via a hypernetwork that controls sparsity through channel vectors and weight indicators. These are optimized with proximal gradient and SGD, respectively. Experiments on CNN-based backbones (LSRB, EDSR) show competitive PSNR/SSIM and parameter reduction compared to several baselines and compression methods. A new baseline LSRB is also introduced. The paper promises code release.

### Strengths
- The core idea of simultaneously performing structured and unstructured pruning is straightforward, intuitive, and promising. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
- The method uses different optimization methods (proximal gradient for channel vectors, SGD for weight indicators) enabling differentiable and flexible pruning. (R3)
- Extensive ablation studies investigate key components: the LSRB baseline, pruning strategy, convergence criteria, and comparisons with other compression methods (NAS, KD, channel pruning). (R1, R3, R4)
- FMP achieves competitive or better quantitative (PSNR/SSIM) and visual results against several existing lightweight SR methods, with lower model complexity. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- The paper is well-written, clearly illustrated, and organized. (R2, R3, R4)
- A minority reviewer (R3, score 8) was notably more positive, praising the contribution as excellent, the presentation as excellent, and emphasizing the effectiveness of FMP and the extensive evaluations. Other reviewers (scores 5-6) were more critical of novelty, fairness, and generalizability. (Scores: 6,6,8,5,6)
- Code release is promised, aiding reproducibility. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Practical speedup and latency**: Multiple reviewers note that unstructured weight pruning does not guarantee actual inference acceleration, yet no runtime/latency comparisons are provided for the full model or in Table 1. The paper lacks a rigorous analysis of hardware-level implications (e.g., irregular memory access, sparse matrix overhead on GPUs). (R1, R2, R3 Q2, R5)
- **Missing comparisons and fairness**:  
  - Leading lightweight SR methods are omitted: MegSR, VapSR, Omni-SR (R2); RLFN and ELAN are mentioned in the paper but not compared (R4).  
  - Comparisons over varied model sizes (especially ~400K parameters) are missing, making it hard to judge generalizability of pruning across scales. (R2)  
  - Table 4 compares FMP only with DHP; more pruning methods using pretrained backbones should be included (pretraining does not increase inference cost). (R2)  
  - Table 7 comparisons may be unfair because the LSRB backbone itself brings improvements; applying pruning methods like ASSLN to LSRB is needed to isolate the contribution of FMP. (R2)  
  - In Figure 1, the baseline models differ between FMP and other methods, making it hard to assess the precise impact of FMP. (R4)  
  - The paper would benefit from comparison to more recent methods such as ICLR-2022 SRPN. (R3)
- **Incremental contribution and limited benefit of kernel pruning**: The main novelty over DHP is adding kernel weight pruning, but Table 6 shows that adding kernel pruning provides marginal gains over channel pruning alone. The paper does not analyze why kernel pruning does not yield substantial practical benefit, raising questions about the added complexity. (R4)  
  - Related: Table 6 is difficult to understand. (R5)
- **Generalizability limited**: FMP is only applied to CNN-based backbones (LSRB, EDSR); experiments on Transformer-based SR methods are missing. The modifications needed to apply FMP to other architectures or tasks are not discussed. (R3, R4, R5)
- **Clarity of method**: The implementation of combining structured and unstructured pruning is confusing—it is unclear whether channels are pruned and weights set to zero, or sparse regularization is used for structural pruning. The integration of the two pruning types needs clearer explanation. (R5)  
  - The advantages of FMP over standalone structured or unstructured pruning in terms of flexibility are not sufficiently emphasized. (R5)
- **Convergence criteria and training iterations**: The performance gains in Table 4 appear marginal, and it is unclear if FMP and DHP were trained with the same number of iterations. The paper mentions a convergence criteria, but this makes direct comparison difficult; if FMP converges faster, that should be clarified. (R3)
- **Missing analysis / clarification questions** (overlap with criticisms):  
  - What is the benefit of combining channel pruning with unstructured weight pruning? (R1)  
  - Why was proximal gradient chosen for channel vectors and weight indicators? (R1)  
  - Did the authors use self-ensemble during testing? A PSNR gain from self-ensemble would help contextualize results. (R1, R3)  
  - Is there an inconsistency: the introduction mentions SGD and proximal gradient, but the experimental setup says Adam is used? Clarify. (R4 Q1)  
  - Why does LSRB+FMP have higher parameters and FLOPs than RLFN, yet faster inference speed? (R4 Q2)  
  - Table 4 shows higher prune ratios sometimes improve performance for both DHP and FMP—this is counterintuitive; need explanation. (R4 Q3)  
  - Formula (6) and (7) both compute norms; why not determine the suitable norm through experiments as done for formula (7)? (R4 Q4)  
  - Is the ESA module (spatial attention) included in pruning? (R4 Q5)  
  - Does FMP require specific hardware to realize resource reductions (GPU memory, runtime)? (R3 Q2)
