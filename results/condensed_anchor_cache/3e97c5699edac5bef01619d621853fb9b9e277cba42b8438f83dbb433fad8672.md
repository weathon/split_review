## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper presents SageAttention, a post-training quantization method that uses INT8 for attention computation, leveraging the observation that the Keys (K) matrix exhibits per-token correlation to apply smoothing, combined with FlashAttention-style tiling and a low-precision FP16 accumulator for intermediate matrices. The method achieves approximately 2.1× and 2.7× speedup over FlashAttention2 and xformers, respectively, on RTX4090/3090 GPUs, while reporting negligible end-to-end metric loss across LLMs, text-to-image, and text-to-video models. Evaluation is extensive, covering multiple domains, GPUs, and baselines. However, concerns are raised about hardware generality (only consumer GPUs tested, not A100/H100), the depth of comparison with FlashAttention3 and task-specific quantizers (AWQ, Q-diffusion, ViDiT-Q), the heavy reliance on FlashAttention as a basis, marginal superiority over FlashAttention3, and clarity issues in writing and variant selection.

### Strengths

- **Well-motivated and novel approach**: The contributions are clearly motivated (R1, R2), and the method (INT8 attention with K smoothing and FP16 accumulator) is considered novel by multiple reviewers (R1, R4). The discovery of channel-wise consistency (Figure 4) and the valuable observation of keys correlation per-token are highlighted (R3, R4).
- **Impressive speedups with minimal accuracy loss**: SageAttention achieves 2.1× over FlashAttention2 and 2.7× over xformers in operations per second without measurable end-to-end metric degradation across diverse models (LLMs, T2I, T2V) (R1, R3).
- **Thorough evaluation**: Experiments cover two different GPUs (RTX4090, RTX3090), representative architectures for language, image, and video generation, and multiple datasets (R1, R2, R4). The inclusion of second-tier GPUs (3090) is appreciated (R4).
- **Problem is challenging**: Quantizing attention for plug-and-play inference is recognized as a difficult problem (R2).
- **Source code provided**: The work includes a code release, which adds to reproducibility (R4).
- **Well-written and logically structured**: The paper is generally clear and well-organized (R2, R3) – though reviewer 4 notes writing issues (see weaknesses for details).

### Weaknesses

- **Hardware generality unverified**: Design choices (INT8 quantization, low-precision FP16 accumulator) are evaluated only on RTX4090 and RTX3090 GPUs. It is unclear whether the same performance gains would transfer to A100, H100, or other GPU architectures with different tensor cores or memory hierarchies. Testing on stronger server-level GPUs is recommended (R1, R2).
- **Incomplete comparison with FlashAttention3**: FlashAttention3 is only briefly mentioned in the introduction and presented in Table 14, without deep analysis. The reported superiority appears marginal, raising questions about the significance of the improvement (R2, R3).
- **Dependence on FlashAttention**: The method heavily builds on FlashAttention, which weakens its technical contribution. The performance without FlashAttention as a basis is not examined (R3).
- **Missing comparisons with task-specific quantizers**: The paper does not compare SageAttention against dedicated quantization methods for LLMs (AWQ), text-to-image (Q-diffusion), and text-to-video (ViDiT-Q), limiting the comprehensiveness of the evaluation (R3).
- **Unexplained speedup variation**: Table 7 shows differing speedups across models/tasks, but no explanation is provided regarding how factors like transformer architecture, model size, or task complexity influence the acceleration. A more detailed analysis is needed (R1).
- **Writing and clarity issues**: The paper contains numerous typos and grammatical errors (e.g., “matrice” → “matrices”, “IO” → “I/O”, missing line numbers, etc.). It is unclear which attention variant is the final recommended one (many variants appear without clear justification). Additionally, the end-to-end model speed improvement and metric degradation after integration are not clearly separated from attention-level results. No qualitative examples are provided for image/video generation (R4).
- **Missing explanation for stability of Llama metrics in Table 1**: The table shows that Llama metrics remain stable even with quantization, and why that occurs is not clarified (R3).
- **Online softmax and initialization details not defined**: The term “online softmax” is used without explicit definition. The expression diag(l_i^j)^{-1} would produce infinities if l_i^j is initialized to zero; the initialization of O_i^j is not described (R4).
