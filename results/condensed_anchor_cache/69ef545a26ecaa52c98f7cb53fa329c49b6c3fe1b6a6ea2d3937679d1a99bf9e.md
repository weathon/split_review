## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Diffusion-based Adaptation (DiffAD) to address domain shift in single-image dehazing. A DDPM is trained on source hazy images to learn the source distribution; at test time, the reverse process adapts target hazy inputs to the source domain, where a pre-trained SOTA dehazing model predicts the clean output. Pseudo-labels are further used to fine-tune the base model. The method is model-agnostic and plug-and-play. While the motivation is meaningful, reviewers raise concerns about novelty relative to existing diffusion-bridge works (DDBM ICLR2024; Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge NeurIPS2023), heavy handcrafting of loss functions, insufficient experimental validation (limited datasets, sparse-fog-only qualitative results, missing ablations, non-reference metrics only), and complexity stemming from training an additional DDPM.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and direction**: Addressing domain gaps between synthetic and real hazy images is meaningful and a future research trend [Rev3, Rev5].
- **DiffAD concept**: Using diffusion-based domain adaptation to enhance dehazing is considered novel and intuitive [Rev4]; the idea of projecting OOD hazy images to the source domain via a diffusion bridge is a valid approach [Rev2].
- **Effectiveness**: The method improves performance of existing dehazing techniques in both synthetic and real-world scenarios and achieves SOTA on multiple datasets [Rev1, Rev2, Rev3, Rev4].
- **Plug-and-play nature**: DiffAD adopts an input adaptive approach without retraining the base model; it introduces an adjustable quality loss enabling user-controlled dehazing/color-balancing [Rev1].
- **Pseudo-labeling**: The framework provides a method to generate high-quality pseudo-labels to improve generalization; experiments verify effectiveness for real-world image dehazing [Rev1].
- **Loss functions**: Various loss functions are crafted for fidelity and quality [Rev2].
- **Paper structure**: Clear and well-organized [Rev3, Rev5].

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of core idea**: The approach of addressing domain shift with a diffusion bridge has been widely explored in prior works (DDBM, ICLR2024; Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge, NeurIPS2023), making the contribution somewhat less [Rev2]. Another reviewer notes contributions may be minor, as the work builds on existing approaches (controllable diffusion, DCP loss, spatial consistency loss), though the idea remains intuitive [Rev4].
- **Handcrafted losses with limited novelty**: The loss functions are heavily handcrafted with many hyperparameters, built upon existing losses (grey-world-assumption loss, DCP loss, etc.) and are thus less informative; the color consistency loss appears to be a workaround for MSE failures in diffusion rather than a fundamental contribution; the white balance loss is a revision of color constancy, and the region-aware DCP loss is a modification of standard DCP [Rev2].
- **Complexity and computational overhead**: The pipeline requires training a DDPM in addition to a SOTA dehazing model, adding significant computational overhead and creating a dependency on existing models, which limits flexibility and increases overall complexity [Rev1].
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: 
  - **Datasets**: Training and evaluation use synthetic or limited datasets (I-Haze, O-Haze) which are generated by professional haze generators, not truly real-world hazy images; concerns about generalization to real-world scenarios remain [Rev1, Rev5].
  - **Qualitative results**: Only sparse fog examples are shown; denser fog cases (both synthetic and real-world) are missing [Rev3]; the paper lacks sufficient visual comparisons—more results should be provided on Page 10 or in an appendix [Rev4]; the Tab. 2 experiment shows low PSNR even after DiffAD improvement, yet no qualitative results for that experiment are given [Rev5].
  - **Metrics**: Only non-reference metrics are reported; MOS-based subjective evaluation is necessary to assess perceptual quality and alignment with human visual preference [Rev1].
  - **Missing ablations**: Ablation studies for the fidelity loss and quality loss are absent but needed [Rev3].
  - **Depth estimation and pseudo-labels**: The framework assumes depth estimation and pseudo-labels are accurate, but in real-world dense fog these may be unreliable; the method does not address robustness in such cases [Rev3]. Also, if the dehazing model is diffusion-based, J* may contain artifacts—how is the artifact-free pseudo-label guaranteed? [Rev3]
- **Implementation details unclear**: It is unclear when the proposed loss functions are added—at test time (as in zero-shot inverse problem solvers) or during training [Rev2]. Whether the same parameters are used for synthetic and real-world evaluations is not specified [Rev3].
- **Inferences from fusion mask**: The direct masked addition between two dehazed images in Figure 5 may lead to inconsistencies because the sky and depth masks operate in different value ranges, making their direct addition insufficient in detail [Rev4].
- **Title overstatement**: The title “ADDRESSING DOMAIN SHIFT” may be an overstatement, as many unresolved problems remain and not all cases have been evaluated [Rev4].
- **Applicability to other tasks**: It is unclear if DiffAD can be extended to other low-level vision tasks like real image super-resolution or denoising [Rev1].
- **Verification of learned source distribution**: The paper does not show the captured source distribution in the trained diffusion model; training for only 50k iterations from scratch may be insufficient—generated source-domain hazy images should be presented and compared with original source images [Rev2].
- **Minor presentation issues**: Citation format should be revised (e.g., “ill-posed problem Wu et al. (2021); Chen et al. (2024a)” should use parentheses) [Rev4].
