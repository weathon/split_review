## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Continual Memory Neurons (CMNs), a key-value attention-based memory module that replaces standard neurons. It uses learnable keys and memory units, winner-take-all retrieval, and a forgetting mechanism to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in online continual learning. Experiments on Modes, Moons, MNIST-CI, CIFAR10, and NS-ImageNet are conducted, showing performance improvements over baseline continual learning methods that use replay buffers.

### Strengths
- **Novel and insightful neuron model (R1, R3, R4).** R1 specifically appreciates the discussion relating CMN to classic neurons, transformer networks, and kernel machines. R4 notes the creative utilization of existing ideas. R3 calls the neuron “novel and insightful” and the learning algorithm “sensible.”
- **Tackles an important problem with a general, distinct mechanism (R2 S1, R1).** R2 emphasizes that the approach is “quite distinct from current efforts in continual learning.” R1 states that CMN is “significantly better at online continual learning compared to baselines that use example buffers and replay.”
- **Well-engineered design (R2 S2).** The mechanisms to reduce computational overhead while improving forgetting are highlighted as a strength.
- **Valuable research direction for the lifelong learning community (R4).**

- **Contrast:** R1 (score 6) and R3 (score 5) are more positive, while R2 and R4 (both score 3) are critical. R1 and R3 find the experimental results convincing, but R2 and R4 raise serious concerns about evaluation and theoretical grounding (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing references and comparisons.** The paper omits discussion of retrieval-augmented continual learning works, which are the closest to this approach (R2 W1). Also missing comparison to Ren et al. (2021), which uses a slot-based memory with similar recycling and creation mechanisms (R1 W3). The claimed novelty (“we are the first to follow a significantly novel route”) is not adequately supported (R4 W3). R3 questions whether the approach is related to mixture of experts. The connections to kernel methods, Transformers, and ReLUs are considered non-central (R2 W1).
- **Lack of theoretical justification.** The learning algorithm is ad hoc, not derived from a well-defined loss function. The update rule for keys (Eq. 6) lacks theoretical motivation; its connection to online k-means is unclear (R3 main, R4 W2). The winner-takes-all mechanism and its impact on optimization are not fully explained (R3). Setting excluded components to zero after softmax (Eq. 4) is non-standard and its effect on gradients is unjustified (R4 W4). The combination of sparse selection and updates needs comparison to alternative proposals (R2 W1).
- **Experimental concerns: evaluation quality and fairness.** Absolute performance on benchmarks is too low to be meaningful. On MNIST-CI, a simple Nearest Class Mean classifier on raw pixels achieves >85%, while reported SOTA is 78%; CIFAR10 accuracy of 27% is “astonishingly low” (R2 W3). The memory comparison is unfair: CMNs store weight matrices which are much smaller than stored images, allowing effective storage of more samples. Equalizing MB storage is too low and deep CMN networks require more GPU VRAM than claimed HDD space (R2 W2). The experimental setup is ambiguous: whether a single sample is presented per time step is unclear (R4 W5, Figure 4 suggests many samples per distribution). Behavior of CMN differs on 2D vs high-dimensional data without sufficient explanation (R4 W6). Results on MNIST-CI show suspicious variation (e.g., ER-Reservoir-Imbalanced 70.3 vs ER-Random 21.0, despite approximating random sampling) (R2 Q2). Performance of some baselines (GDumb 14.3, ER-Random 21.0, MoE 23.2) on MNIST-CI with memory size 100 is called “suspiciously low” (R2).
- **Applicability and hyperparameters.** Only a single layer of CMN is tested, while the naming suggests general multi-layer use. A single CMN could be achieved with a standard online clustering algorithm (R1 W1). Multi-layer application may cause early layer commitment to winner slots, hindering learning (R1 W2). Hyperparameter selection (δ ∈ {2,5}, temperature γ, sparsity threshold) appears burdensome; optimal ranges and sensitivity need analysis (R1 W4). The added complexity over simpler alternatives (e.g., attention-based models) is not justified (R3 second weakness).
- **Writing quality.** The manuscript is hard to follow (R2). Writing needs significant improvement (R4 W1). R4 rates presentation “poor.”
