## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two correction methods (LoGF in logit space and LaGF in latent space) to address recurrent false positive (FP) classifications in object detection. The methods require a corrective dataset with additional FP annotations. LoGF modifies classification logits of FP predictions; LaGF uses an autoencoder with a learnable Gaussian mixture model to map FP embeddings to true negative (TN) embeddings. The paper evaluates on a subset of PASCAL VOC with synthetic label noise and in a few-shot setting. Reviewers find the idea novel but raise concerns about experimental fairness, writing clarity, limited evaluation scope, and practical assumptions.

### Strengths
- Proposes two distinct correction frameworks (LoGF, LaGF) that guide FPs toward TNs in either the logit or latent space, representing a novel use of FP annotations to correct detection models.
- The idea of leveraging spaces beyond logits to address classification errors is valuable and opens a new direction.
- Experiments demonstrate performance improvements in both general object detection and few-shot detection scenarios.
- Training details are provided to support reproducibility.
- The paper provides a rich theoretical basis, including a learnable GMM optimized via Wasserstein distance.

### Weaknesses
**Writing & Presentation**
- The overall writing quality is poor; the paper requires major revision of logical structure, grammar, and clarity. Colloquial language is overused (e.g., “we” appears 107 times).
- Inconsistent dataset usage: Section 4.1 mentions PASCAL VOC 2007 while Section 5.1 refers to training on MS-COCO 2017, and table captions do not specify which dataset is used for each experiment, harming reproducibility.
- Figure 1 (DETR network diagram) adds no new information and should not appear in the main text. The Motivation section lacks a needed figure showing: (a) visualization of false positive classification errors, (b) comparison with prior methods, (c) how the noisy and clean datasets are used.
- Section numbering and definitions are incomplete: Sec 3.3.1 contains “Definition 1” but no Definition 2 follows; the relationship between sections in the Method is unclear.
- The abstract and contributions claim correction in two spaces but lack a description of what logit-space correction entails.

**Experiments & Fairness**
- **Unfair comparison**: Both proposed methods benefit from additional annotations in the corrective dataset D_c, but the baseline (f_True) is fine-tuned only on D_True, not on the combination of D_c and D_True. This does not isolate the effect of the guidance frameworks from simply having more training data.
- Experiments only consider a single pair of misannotated classes; real-world scenarios with multiple misannotated pairs are not studied. The interaction and potential interference among multiple FP categories is unexplored, limiting evidence for general applicability.
- Only a small subset of PASCAL VOC classes is used; full-class mAP should be reported. The paper claims generalizability across datasets and detectors (Line 062) but provides no experiments on larger datasets (e.g., COCO, OpenImages) or with different detectors. The representativeness of VOC07 is insufficient, and computational requirements for larger-scale testing are not discussed.
- The improvements over simply using f_True are modest (e.g., Table 2), and in some cases performance drops. The paper does not compare the additional computational cost (time) of the two-stage process versus direct retraining.

**Motivation & Assumptions**
- The motivation example (people on billboards being detected as real people) may be reasonable in some contexts; forcing such detections to background risks model oscillation or overfitting. The paper should explain why this correction is necessary and not harmful.
- The method assumes access to a corrective dataset D_c that contains clear FP examples from the same domain as the original data. Such data is often unavailable after a model is deployed; the paper does not address how to obtain these annotations in practice.
- The connection between the motivation paragraph (Line 68+) and the actual use of D_True is unclear: D_True appears to be used in all cases, so why not directly use f_True? A clearer justification is needed.

**Missing Details & Inconsistencies**
- Weakness 5 from R4: Before Definition 1, the paper states that the TN position “only its position remains to be defined,” but after Definition 1 the TN is said to “share the same bounding box as the FP.” These statements appear contradictory and need alignment.
- Multiple loss functions are introduced, but the training stages and parameters for each loss are not marked in the corresponding figure. For the final L_decoder stage, it is unclear which of the two MLP_bbox modules the target y_bbox refers to.

**Additional Reviewer Questions (folded into weaknesses)**
- R1 asks whether “recurrent false positive classification” and “recurrent errors” have the same meaning; this reflects a lack of terminological clarity.
- R2 asks why contrastive learning is listed as a related work subsection, suggesting a mismatch with the paper’s focus.
- R3 requests qualitative experiments showing before/after FP proportion reduction and latent space visualization, which are currently absent.
- R4 questions how to solve the issue that FP data from the original training domain is often inaccessible – a practical limitation not addressed.
