## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Adaptive Contrastive Replay (ACR) to address the overlooked Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization problem in rehearsal-based class-incremental learning. ACR uses a proxy-based contrastive loss and an adaptive replay buffer management strategy that populates the buffer with task- and class-balanced high-variance (challenging) boundary samples. The method aims to achieve better stability-plasticity trade-offs and OOD generalization. Experiments on Split CIFAR-100, Split Mini-ImageNet, and Split Tiny-ImageNet show improved performance over rehearsal-based baselines. Reviewers are divided: two find the method insufficiently validated (score 3), one finds it solid with minor concerns (5), and one finds it relevant but notes several weaknesses (6).

### Strengths
- **Motivation and relevance**: The paper identifies and addresses the important but often overlooked OOD generalization issue in continual learning, enhancing practical applicability. (all reviewers)
- **Well-motivated design**: The dual optimization with proxy-based contrastive loss and adaptive buffer management is well-structured to tackle bad memorization and maintain clear class boundaries. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Enhanced memory management**: The strategy mitigates the long-tail effect by maintaining balanced class and task representation, improving stability and plasticity. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Efficiency gains**: ACR reduces computational complexity, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments. (Reviewer 4 only)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive experiments across multiple datasets (CIFAR-100, Mini-ImageNet, Tiny-ImageNet), algorithms, and memory buffer sizes, with insightful analysis and ablation studies. (Reviewers 1, 4) *Note: Reviewer 1 simultaneously questions the quantitative results and experimental scope.*

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of components**:
  - The proxy-based contrastive loss (PCL, Eq. 4) appears nearly identical to a standard cross-entropy loss with temperature-scaled softmax; its distinction must be clarified. (Reviewer 2)
  - Proxy-based contrastive learning is already widely used in continual learning (citations [1, 2]); the paper does not clearly delineate unique aspects. (Reviewer 3)
  - The adaptive buffer sampling (high-variance samples) resembles prior work that stores “most interfered” samples; differences from that baseline (e.g., [1]) must be explained, and [1] should be included as a comparison. (Reviewer 1)

- **Insufficient experimental validation**:
  - *Results lower than literature*: On Split CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, reported average accuracy (e.g., ER 24%, ACR 42%) is much lower than published results using ResNet-32 (ER ~40%, top methods ~60% in [4, 5]). This discrepancy may stem from using ResNet-18 instead of the standard ResNet-32; comparison on ResNet-32 is essential to confirm superiority. (Reviewer 1)
  - *Narrow setting*: All experiments use 32×32 images; results may not generalize to larger images (e.g., full-resolution ImageNet/ImageNet-100) or to different task scenarios (e.g., large first task, long task sequences). Additional evaluations are needed. (Reviewers 1, 4)
  - *OOD evaluation limited*: Only synthetic corruptions (Gaussian noise, blur) are used; real-world OOD scenarios (domain shifts, adversarial attacks, real-world variability) are missing. (Reviewer 4)
  - *Baseline scope*: Only rehearsal-based methods are compared; including non-rehearsal approaches (regularization- or architecture-based) would provide a broader perspective. (Reviewer 4)

- **Doubts about the effectiveness of challenging-sample selection**:
  - In Table 4, the proposed “Challenging” selection performs no better than “Random” in backward transfer (BWT), suggesting the selection mechanism does not actually help maintain decision boundaries for old tasks; the observed improvement may be solely due to balanced class/task sampling, which is already known. (Reviewer 2)
  - Additional experiments with larger buffer sizes (e.g., 2000) are needed to verify whether “Challenging” still outperforms “Random”. (Reviewer 1)
  - The assumption that high-variance boundary samples always improve performance is not validated under noisy labels or unbalanced datasets; using confidence variance as a proxy for informativeness should be tested more thoroughly. (Reviewer 4)

- **Critical methodological and hyperparameter concerns**:
  - The confidence variance used for sample selection is recorded only for the first 5 epochs (E=5) while training lasts 50 epochs. In early epochs the model may not be stable, making predictions nearly random and the variance unreliable. It is unclear whether the fixed buffer (static within a task) can adapt as decision boundaries refine later. A comparison of confidence variance at task start vs. end is needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - Hyperparameter sensitivity (temperature τ, buffer size) is not thoroughly explored; a detailed analysis to demonstrate robustness is missing. (Reviewer 4)

- **Missing ablation studies**:
  - The standalone effect of adaptive buffer management is not isolated: experiments with ER *without* proxy-based contrastive learning (PCL) are needed to evaluate the buffer policy alone. (Reviewer 1)
  - The performance gain from only the memory management component should be separated from gains due to contrastive learning and data augmentation; similarly, results combining other CL methods with ACR’s contrastive loss would be informative. (Reviewer 3)

- **Clarity issues**:
  - Figure 2 is unclear; the buffer update mechanism and the role of proxy-based contrastive loss require better visualization and explanation. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - The implementation of the comparison in lines 89–90 is not described, making Figure 1 hard to understand. (Reviewer 2)
  - The claim that no task label needs to be stored (lines 266–269) contradicts the statement that samples are arranged separately in the buffer by task; this needs clarification. (Reviewer 2)
  - The concept of “proxy” is not clearly defined (lines 182–184). (Reviewer 2)
