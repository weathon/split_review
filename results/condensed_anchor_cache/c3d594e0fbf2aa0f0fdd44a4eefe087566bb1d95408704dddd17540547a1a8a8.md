## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a differentially private framework for aligning large language models with reinforcement learning, combining DP-SGD with PPO to achieve privacy-preserving RLHF. It is evaluated on tasks like positive review generation and summarization using the GPT-2 family, with experiments on ROUGE and TweetEval metrics, showing competitive utility for single-digit privacy budgets.

### Strengths
- Original approach for privately aligning LLMs via RL, offering mathematical privacy guarantees.
- Paper is clearly written and well-organized, with comprehensive analysis of implementation challenges.
- Experiments are clear and reasonable, demonstrating utility benefits on NLP benchmarks (ROUGE, TweetEval) while ensuring strong privacy protections.
- Combines two well-understood algorithms (DP-SGD and PPO) in a well-motivated task, with adaptations clearly explained.

### Weaknesses
- The DP component is too condensed; lacks a clear explanation of how DP-SGD is implemented, including gradient clipping and noise addition mechanisms, sensitivity calculation, and specified privacy parameters (epsilon, delta) for experiments. The distinction between private and non-private reward models and the exact DP mechanisms used (beyond adding noise) are not clarified.
- While combining DP-SGD and PPO is a straightforward engineering application, the core RLHF algorithm remains largely unchanged; innovation appears limited, without significant theoretical or algorithmic novelty.
- Motivation is weak: the threat model and what is being protected by applying DP to human feedback are not clearly articulated; no examples of memorization from human feedback are provided.
- Experiments lack error bars, confidence intervals, and number of trials. Table 1 shows counterintuitive results where models with less privacy sometimes perform worse (GPT-2 Medium eps 4→8, GPT-2 Large eps 8→Inf), which cannot be interpreted without further ablations or confidence intervals.
- Use of corporate imagery (Reddit, OpenAI) weakens generality of results.
- Prior work in differentially private RL (e.g., Balle, Gomrockchi, Precup on DP policy evaluation) is not discussed.
- The paper touches on privacy accounting implications when T_PPO ≠ 1 but does not evaluate the impact of fixing T_PPO = 4; the utility trade-off of this setting is unclear.
- The privacy-utility trade-off is not presented intuitively; a Pareto frontier or visualization of utility degradation across different ε levels would be more informative.
- It is unclear whether applying DP to the reward model (step 2) makes DP-PPO in step 3 redundant; this interdependence is not analyzed.
