- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 1, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes VideoUntier for text-video retrieval, which disentangles video content into global-, object-, and event-level features guided by text queries. It uses a Part-of-Speech-based Token Generator (PTG) to extract object/event tokens from the query and a Language-guided Progressive Vision Merging (LPVM) module to merge visual tokens. The method reports state-of-the-art performance on MSRVTT, MSVD, and DiDeMo. Reviews were mixed: two reviewers rated the paper highly (5,5), one gave a borderline score (3), and one strongly rejected (1).

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly written and well-structured, with effective diagrams (Figures 1 and 2) that illustrate the methodology.
- The concept of decomposing text queries into object and event tokens to guide video feature extraction is interesting and intuitive.
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over existing methods on multiple benchmarks (MSRVTT, MSVD, DiDeMo), with better efficiency than some recent works (e.g., ProST).
- Ablation studies effectively highlight the benefits of separating objects and events and the proposed modules.
- Even the low-scoring reviewer (score 1) acknowledged the novelty of global/object/motion alignment and found the PTG and LGPM modules interesting, while also noting good performance.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty.** The idea of decomposing video features into global, object, and event levels, and aligning nouns/verbs with visual features via attention, has been explored in prior works. Reviewers 1 and 3 raised this concern, pointing to specific prior works that share similar ideas: *Fine-grained Video-Text Retrieval with Hierarchical Graph Reasoning* (CVPR 2020), *T2VLAD* (CVPR 2021), *Cross-Modal and Hierarchical Modeling of Video and Text* (ECCV 2018), *Text-Video Retrieval with Disentangled Conceptualization and Set-to-Set Alignment* (IJCAI 2023), *Beyond Coarse-Grained Matching* (ACCV 2024), and *Coarse-to-fine dual-level attention* (KBS 2022). Reviewer 3 noted that these works even go further by using optical flow for event cues. Several reviewers found the contribution marginal for a top venue. Reviewer 3 also suggested that the term "decomposition" is more appropriate than "disentanglement" since object and event information are inherently interconnected.
- **Missing baseline comparisons.** Several recent and important baselines are omitted, hindering a fair assessment. Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 flagged missing comparisons including: *Cap4Video* (CVPR 2023), *T2VLAD* (CVPR 2021), *Disentangled Representation Learning for Text-Video Retrieval* (ECCV 2022), *CLIP-VIP* (2022), and *Text Is MASS* (CVPR 2024). Reviewer 2 emphasized the lack of 2024 papers.
- **Efficiency and scalability concerns.** The Part-of-Speech-based Token Generator (PTG) adds computational overhead (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 pointed out that the model is a single-tower architecture requiring O(mn) complexity for retrieval (m = number of videos, n = number of texts), which limits real-world scalability.
- **Modest gains relative to complexity.** Reviewer 1 noted that the performance improvements over existing methods are relatively modest given the complexity of the framework, and questioned whether a parsing-based approach offers advantages over simpler direct query-based matching methods such as DRL or X-Pool.
- **Missing ablations and requested experiments.** Multiple reviewers identified gaps:
  - Reviewer 2 requests ablations on the LGPM and PTG modules individually.
  - Reviewer 2 asks for experiments without using text features at all for visual features, and experiments where visual motion features are derived from text motion cues (i.e., symmetry with object-level).
  - Reviewer 4 recommends an experiment that uses the entire sequence of tokens (without separating into nouns/verbs) in the PTG module, then applies LPVM to model temporal relations, to validate the effectiveness of the decomposition.
- **Potential loss of query coherence.** Decomposing the text into separate object/event tokens may compromise the query’s overall coherence and completeness (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 also asked how the method handles complex or ambiguous queries where nouns and verbs are not clearly separable.
- **Exceeding page limit.** Reviewer 2 noted that the main paper exceeds 9 pages, which normally leads to desk rejection (other reviewers did not comment on this).
