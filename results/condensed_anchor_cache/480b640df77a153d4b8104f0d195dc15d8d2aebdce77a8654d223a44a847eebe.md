## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Xformer, a hybrid X-shaped vision Transformer for image denoising that uses dual encoder-decoder branches for spatial-wise (STB) and channel-wise (CTB) self-attention, coupled via a Bidirectional Connection Unit (BCU) to model global information in both dimensions. The idea is motivated by combining strengths of SwinIR and Restormer. Reviewers had mixed assessments: some found the architecture elegant and effective, with extensive ablations and state-of-the-art results on both synthetic and real denoising (scores 8), while others considered the technical contribution incremental and noted limited improvement over Restormer, especially on real-world denoising (scores 5, 6). Disagreement exists on real-world denoising performance: one reviewer stated the method does not achieve SOTA on real-world datasets, another claimed SOTA with better average results.

### Strengths

- **Well-motivated and reasonable** combination of spatial-wise and channel-wise self-attention to capture both local fine-grained and global features (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Paper is well-organized, clearly written, and easy to follow** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4), despite some typos noted (Reviewer 1).
- **BCU is simple but effective**; ablation supports its contribution (Reviewer 2).
- **Extensive ablation studies** on STB, CTB, BCU, and shift operation (Reviewer 2).
- **Extensive comparisons on both Gaussian and real-world denoising**, with better average quantitative results and better visual results (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 3 notes good synthetic results but not SOTA on real (see Weaknesses).
- **Supplementary material includes sample code**, improving reproducibility (Reviewer 2).
- **X-shaped architecture is elegant and novel**, leveraging hybrid Transformer blocks effectively (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Potential impact on the broader image processing community** (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Technical novelty is considered incremental** by some reviewers: dual-branch architecture and bilateral interactions are typical designs, especially compared to SwinIR and Restormer (Reviewer 1). In contrast, other reviewers found the approach novel (Reviewers 2, 4). This disagreement should be addressed.
- **Performance on real-world denoising is disputed**: Reviewer 1 states improvement over Restormer is limited on real scenarios; Reviewer 3 notes the method does not achieve SOTA on real-world datasets; Reviewer 2 claims better average results; Reviewer 4 claims SOTA. The discrepancy needs clarification.
- **Model selection criteria unclear**: it is not specified whether the final model is chosen based on best validation performance or the final iteration (Reviewer 2).
- **Ablation Table 1(b) requires more analysis**: performance without BCU and with BCU-1 are comparable, as are BCU-2 and complete BCU; the differences are not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Applicability to other tasks**: it is unclear whether the method can be extended to other image restoration tasks or is specifically designed for denoising (Reviewer 2).
- **No failure cases** are discussed; it is unknown what types of details the method struggles to recover (Reviewer 2).
- **Parameter sharing in the last encoder of the two branches** is not justified; the performance influence is not quantified (Reviewer 3).
- **The claim that STB uses “shorter token sequences”** to obtain fine-grained local patches interactions is ambiguous – compared to what? (Reviewer 3)
- **QKV generation uses 1×1 Conv followed by 3×3 depth-wise Conv**: the rationale for not using a direct vanilla 3×3 Conv is not given (Reviewer 3).
- **Refinement only on the spatial branch** with a 3×3 depth-wise Conv: it is not explained why the channel branch does not use a similar refinement or how vanilla convolution would impact performance (Reviewer 3).
- **BCU description lacks detail** on the specific convolutional operations (standard, depth-wise separable, etc.) and the rationale for choosing convolution over other fusion methods (e.g., attention) is not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **The hybrid nature may be challenging** for readers; additional visual aids (e.g., architecture flow diagrams) could help comprehension (Reviewer 4).
- **Investigation of STB/CTB suitability** for different denoising scenarios (noise types) is missing, both theoretically and experimentally (Reviewer 1).
