## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TRAM (Trust Region Aware Minimization), a SAM-style optimizer that combines sharpness-aware minimization (low curvature in parameter space) with trust-region regularization (smooth, informative representations in function space) for fine-tuning. The key idea is to use a trust-region distance measure to adaptively set the SAM perturbation radius, aiming to preserve pre-trained structure while improving out-of-domain generalization. Building on ASAM and FSAM, TRAM is evaluated on language modeling (GPT-2 on M2D2 Corpus), cross-dataset vision adaptation, OOD language modeling, and zero-shot cross-lingual transfer. The method achieves best performance across all tasks and outperforms competing methods, especially for hard transfer between anticorrelated domains. The paper is well-written, intuitive, and provides detailed experimental settings.

### Strengths
- Intuitive and well-motivated combination of SAM and trust-region methods. (R1, R2)
- Paper is clearly written and easy to follow. (R3)
- Comprehensive experiments across multiple NLP tasks (language modeling, cross-lingual transfer) show TRAM outperforms SAM- and TR-based baselines. (R1, R2)
- The proposed method reduces extra forward-propagation count when combined with Fisher-SAM (same count as vanilla SAM). (R3)
- Experimental settings are detailed and reasonable; the paper summarizes existing approaches well. (R1, R2)
- Empirical validation on foundation models and large-scale NLP datasets is done. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing sanity-check experiments on original SAM benchmarks**: The method is not evaluated on standard vision benchmarks (CIFAR, ImageNet) that made SAM popular. Such experiments are needed to verify that TRAM does not harm performance on the tasks SAM was originally validated on. (R1) [Note: R1 considers this a required sanity check.]
- **Theoretical motivation lacking**: No rigorous mathematical analysis is provided for why unifying SAM and trust-region improves transferability (as opposed to IID generalization). The paper does not clearly explain why the proposed distance-as-radius choice gives “trust” or how it avoids harming pre-trained models. (R2, R4) [R4: “I could not understand why such transfer could encourage better transfer. When it fails?” R2: “Theoretical motivation not provided.”]
- **Clarity of core idea**: The method adaptively sets the SAM perturbation radius using a distance measure, but this does not follow the typical trust-region regularization (which adds a constraint on loss). The meaning of “trust” is unclear—why is the region outside the radius untrustworthy in SAM but not TRAM? Figures illustrating this are recommended. (R3) [R3: “I could not find a clear meaning why using such a distance as the neighbourhood radius could give the ‘Trust’.”]
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison with Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) or model soups, which can have a similar effect as trust-region methods. Relevant references: Kaddour et al. 2022, Wortsman et al. 2022. (R3)
- **Limited modality validation**: The method is only validated on NLP tasks; results on image or other modalities are missing. Title/contributions should be revised if the method is not general. (R1, R4) [R4: “Unless the author clearly revised the title and contributions, I would like to see results in other modalities such as image.”]
- **High variance in results**: Some experimental results exhibit high variance across runs; more runs are needed to better characterize performance. (R2)
- **Need for pre-training from scratch experiment**: To test whether TRAM’s benefits require a good pre-trained model, an experiment starting from random initialization is needed. (R1)
- **Unclear definitions and choices**: Equation (3) is not clearly defined (\(d_{\theta, x}\)). The choice of forward KL divergence (Eq. 4,5) is not justified; why not reverse KL or Renyi divergence? The role of noise variance in Eq. (5) is unclear. Equation (12) may not be a sample-efficient estimator of similarity in high dimensions. (R4)
- **Table 5 incomplete**: Statistics for the other two TRAM variants are not presented. (R1)
- **Figure 1 unclear**: Interpretation of negative/positive slopes is not explained. (R1)
- **Need for code release** to aid reproducibility. (R3)
- **Missing results on recent large-scale LLMs** (e.g., Llama) would increase impact. (R3)
- **Computational/memory complexity analysis** compared to other methods is missing. (R4)
- **Table 4 only reports accuracy** without discussing dataset balance or other metrics. (R4)
- **Paper positioning unclear**: If the paper is primarily empirical, it should be revised to highlight practical contributions rather than claiming novel optimization theory; the current analysis is not rigorous as a theoretical optimization contribution. (R4) [Minority view: R4 strongly questions the framing and theoretical depth.]
