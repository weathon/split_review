## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies a limitation of standard consistency models: training to map all intermediate PF ODE points to the endpoint is harder than needed for one-step generation. To address this, the authors propose truncating the training time range and using a two-stage procedure. Stage 1 trains a standard consistency model. Stage 2 trains a model on a truncated interval \([t', T]\) with a separate boundary loss in \([t', t'+\Delta t]\) and a modified timestep proposal distribution. The method achieves better one-step and two-step FID than iCT-deep on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64 using more than 2× smaller networks.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow; math derivations are correct and the method description is simple and efficient (R1, R3, R5).
- The truncation idea is novel and addresses training instability in consistency models (R1, R5).
- Strong empirical results: outperforms previous consistency models (e.g., iCT-deep) in one-step and two-step FID on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64 with significantly smaller networks (R1, R2, R3).
- The motivation (denoising–generation trade-off) is sound and well supported by experiments, especially Figure 2 illustrating the gradual weakening of denoising capability (R2, R3, R5).
- Comprehensive ablation studies on dividing time \(t'\) and the number of stages provide useful insights (R2, R5).
- The approach is orthogonal to network capacity and can potentially be combined with consistency distillation methods (R4).
- The boundary condition enforcement between the two models, while empirically challenging, is shown to be feasible – informative for future work using different models across time ranges (R4).
- The paper has good significance potential; the truncation principle may become a widespread trick for improving consistency models (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Causal attribution and necessary ablation**: It is unclear whether the gains come from truncation or from the changed proposal distribution (focusing more on boundary parts). An ablation is missing: training with the original loss (i.e., Stage-1 loss) but with the improved proposal distribution focusing on \([0,t']\). If that (tuned) baseline matches the two-stage method, truncation may be unnecessary. (R1)
- **Over-engineering and hyperparameter sensitivity**: The approach introduces many handcrafted designs and hyperparameters (weighting function \(\psi_t(t)\), \(\lambda_b\), \(N_B\), \(\Delta_t\), \(\Delta_{t'}\), splitting time \(t'\)). This complexity makes tuning expensive and may limit applicability. (R3)
- **Stage 1 termination**: The paper does not specify how to determine when Stage 1 training should stop; the number of iterations in Stage 1 is a crucial hyperparameter that may need additional ablation. (R3)
- **Missing ablations**: No ablation studies are provided for \(N_B\) (or \(\rho\)), \(\Delta_t\), or \(\Delta_{t'}\). (R3)
- **Novelty and relation to prior work**: One reviewer (R2) noted that ECT already identifies the denoising–consistency trade-off and proposes a dedicated iteration-dependent training schedule; the two-stage method does not fundamentally address the core issue but merely shifts the problem to a different paradigm. Other reviewers considered the truncation idea novel (R1, R5). The paper also lacks discussion of related work on truncation in diffusion models (Kim et al. 2021, Yang et al. 2023) and consistency models (PCM, Heek et al. 2024). (R2, R3)
- **Marginal improvement for 2-step generation**: The 2-step FID improvement over ECT is relatively small, and the benefit is limited given the added complexity and hyperparameter tuning. For high-quality 2-step generation (EDM2-XL), the improvement is negligible, questioning scalability to large models. (R2, R4)
- **Lack of theoretical justification**: The choice of dividing time \(t'\) and boundary width \(\Delta t\) appears arbitrary; there is no clear theoretical analysis of how these parameters affect learning the consistency function. (R2)
- **Unnecessary loss decomposition**: The decomposition in Eq. (6) may be redundant because the weighting function \(\psi\) can already give higher weight to timesteps near \(t'\), and the parameterization in Eq. (5) handles the corner case. (R5)
- **Reproducibility concerns**: Code is not included. Baseline results in Tables 1 and 2 lack specification of where they are reported from. The number of optimization steps is not listed in the hyperparameters (Section D). (R5)
- **Phenomenon at Stage 2 start**: In Figure 3, denoising FID increases at the beginning of Stage 2, and denoising FIDs do not start from the same value between Stage 1 and Stage 2 (even starting from the same checkpoint). These observations are unexplained. (R5)
- **“Forgetting” characterization**: Section 4.2 describes the truncated model’s loss of denoising ability as an advantage, but this is merely a byproduct of the training loss, not a direct confirmation of improved generation. (R5)
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparisons to other consistency distillation methods (e.g., CTM, TCD) are provided. SiD (score distillation) achieves better FID with fewer parameters, but the paper does not directly compare against it. (R4)
- **Only consistency training tested**: The paper only reports results for consistency training (CT); consistency distillation (CD) with truncation is not evaluated. (R1)
- **Continuity at the splitting time**: It is unclear how the model using the parameterization in Eq. (5) ensures continuity at \(t'\). Multi-step sampling with timesteps near \(t'\) could cause issues. (R3)
- **Applicability to guided sampling**: The paper does not apply the method to guided sampling or text-to-image generation, nor does it discuss the impact of guidance scale. (R4)
- **Stage 1 training details**: Total training iterations/time for Stage 1 and Stage 2 are not clearly stated. Is Stage 1 exactly the same as previous consistency models? Why is Stage 2 only initialized with ECT for EDM2-XL? (R2)
- **Limit case of single point truncation**: What would happen if the truncated region is reduced to a single point \(T\) (i.e., training directly on noise–data pairs)? Could that achieve better 1-step FID? (R2)
