## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CACL, a contrastive learning approach for fully independent multi-agent communication. The core idea is to treat agents’ messages as incomplete views of the environment state and maximize mutual information between messages from the same trajectory via a contrastive loss. Reviewers generally find the motivation clear and the framework clean, but express substantial concerns about experimental validation, benchmark selection, statistical reliability, scalability, and some methodological details. There is notable disagreement: Reviewer 3 (score 8) considers it a strong, well-scoped paper with good results and minor weaknesses only, while Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 (scores 5,6,5) are more critical, citing limited improvements, weak comparisons, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The idea is clear and well-grounded; enforcing message similarity under similar states is a straightforward motivation.
- The perspective of viewing messages as incomplete views of the state is innovative and motivates contrastive learning for communication.
- The paper is well-written and well-organised; the proposed framework is clean and easy to implement.
- The ablation studies and visualizations (e.g., message embeddings) provide useful insight into the learned communication.
- Tackles the important and under-explored problem of communication for fully independent learners (no parameter sharing).
- Mixing contrastive learning with MARL is an interesting and potentially influential direction.
- Reviewer 3 finds the originality, quality, and significance high, stating the work would be an important baseline for future emergent communication research.

### Weaknesses
- **Negative sampling principle is not technically sound**: States from different trajectories or outside a time window are treated as negative samples, but the same state can appear in different trajectories; the paper does not discuss this issue or justify the choice (Rev1).
- **Limited benchmark suite**: Only three relatively simple tasks are used (≤5 agents). Missing standard challenging environments such as SMAC, MATE, or other partial-observability tasks where communication is critical (Rev1, Rev2, Rev4).
- **Baselines are dated**: Comparison lacks recent works (2022–2023) and does not include CTDE methods using transformers or more advanced centralized training, making it unclear whether fully decentralized training (as in CACL) is competitive or necessary (Rev1).
- **Marginal improvement and high variance in Traffic Junction**: Performance gains are small, confidence intervals overlap, and convergence is questionable. More trials and additional checkpoints (e.g., in Figure 4) are needed (Rev3, Rev4).
- **Scalability concerns**: Only tested with up to 5 agents; communication overhead and cost of contrastive learning for larger numbers are unaddressed. Does CACL require all-to-all communication? (Rev1, Rev4). Also not demonstrated in continuous state/action spaces with many agents (Rev1).
- **Premise not novel**: The view that observations are local perceptions of the global state is already the standard Dec-POMDP formulation; the paper does not clearly differentiate its premise from existing communication methods (Rev2).
- **Missing details in loss and architecture**: The RL loss is not defined; the architecture diagram (Figure 8) is too simple and does not show gradient flow, which is important given fully independent learning (Rev2).
- **Confusing example**: The predator-prey example in Figure 1 (right) does not clearly illustrate similar vs. dissimilar views (Rev2).
- **Policy definition incomplete**: In Section 3, the policy π is conditioned only on local observation τⁱ, but agents should also condition on received messages (Rev4).
- **Risk of meaningless messages**: If local observations miss important information, the encoded “global state” approximation may be poor, undermining the contrastive objective (Rev4).
- **Redundant information**: Receiving multiple messages from all other agents may include redundancy; how CACL handles this is not discussed (Rev4).
- **Lack of before/after analysis**: The paper does not compare message similarity of positive/negative pairs before and after contrastive learning to demonstrate its effect (Rev1).
- **No integration with reward**: The reward signal currently hurts performance (Section 5.5); the authors do not propose how to improve this (Rev2).
- **Minor typos**: In Section 3, $m_{t-1}^{-1}$ should be $m_{t-1}^{-i}$; $a$ should be $a^i$ (Rev2).
- **Requested comparisons**: Should compare with more recent communication methods such as ATOC, MF-MARL, TarMAC, I2C, ToM2C, especially in complex settings (Rev1).
