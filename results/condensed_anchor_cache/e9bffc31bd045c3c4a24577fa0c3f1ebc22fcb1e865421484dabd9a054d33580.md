## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Spike Accumulation Forwarding (SAF), a method to reduce memory and training time for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) by accumulating spikes over multiple time steps, avoiding the need to track membrane potential at each step. The authors theoretically prove SAF’s consistency with Online Training Through Time (OTTT) and Spike Representation, and experimentally evaluate it on CIFAR-10, showing reduced memory and time with maintained accuracy.

### Strengths
- Novel and useful approach that addresses the major issue of memory footprint in SNN training, not only in the backward but also in the forward paths (Reviewer 1). The method simplifies the forward propagation equations of OTTT and demonstrates gradient consistency (Reviewer 2). The theoretical analysis is rigorous and proves the claims (Reviewer 1); SAF’s effectiveness is supported by both theory and experiments (Reviewer 4).
- SAF achieves measurable reductions in memory and training time while preserving accuracy (Reviewers 1, 3). Experimental evidence shows SAF can outperform OTTT in some scenarios (Reviewer 2).
- Two reviewers (1 & 4, scores 5) are strongly positive about the contribution and novelty; one finds the contribution excellent (Reviewer 1). The other two reviewers (2 & 3, scores 3) acknowledge the attempt to solve an important challenge and some improvement, but see it as limited.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental scope and detail**: Only evaluated on CIFAR-10, which is a relatively simple dataset (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Missing comparisons with BPTT and other successful training methods (Reviewer 3). No experiments on more complex datasets like CIFAR-100 or ImageNet, which are needed to demonstrate SAF’s superiority over OTTT and general applicability (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The paper lacks specific details on model architecture, training setup, and hyperparameters; source code is not provided, making the results non-reproducible (Reviewers 1, 4). Only one set of hyperparameters is used; no exploration of the effects of λ or T on robustness (Reviewer 3). Memory compression in Table 1 is somewhat limited; are better results available? (Reviewer 4)
- **Theoretical concerns about small time steps**: Spike Representation assumes large latency (T → ∞), but experiments use small T (e.g., T=6). Why does SAF work for small T? (Reviewer 1) Figure 5 shows firing rates for OTTT and SAF are NOT identical – why is equivalence claimed? (Reviewer 1) Equation 2 holds strictly only as T → ∞; using small T introduces errors due to uneven spike distribution. The authors should provide insights on differences between training (using Spike Representation) and actual LIF forward results (Reviewer 2).
- **Poor writing and incomplete explanations**: The paper is poorly written and very hard to follow; it would benefit from substantial language editing (Reviewer 1). Numerous equations omit intermediate reasoning (e.g., Spike Representation section), and variables like **x** in the weighted average input are not defined (Reviewers 1, 2). The purpose of Section 2.4.3 is unclear because the network structures in the paper and OTTT do not have feedback connections (Reviewer 2). The paper builds heavily on prior work without explaining it, making it not self-contained (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty beyond efficiency**: The theoretical results only prove equivalence to OTTT and Spike Representation, not new training guarantees. The contribution is thus reduced to finding an efficient forward method that fits existing training models (Reviewer 3). SAF’s training uses float-point representations; detailed explanation of computational overhead (float-point vs. spike computation) is missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation quality issues**: Experimental Figure 2 is blurry (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority critical view**: Two reviewers (2 & 3) find the experimental validation insufficient and the theoretical contribution modest, leading to a lower overall assessment (scores 3). They stress the need for more comprehensive evaluation and analysis to support the claims.
