## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviewers collectively acknowledge the paper's contribution to species distribution modeling by incorporating language (taxonomic hierarchy via LLM) and proposing a proximity-aware evaluation metric. The method enables zero-shot prediction and achieves strong results on a new dataset. However, there are significant concerns about the central assumption that taxonomic similarity implies range similarity, the necessity of using an LLM versus simpler embeddings, the clarity of the problem formulation and experimental evaluation, and the justification and robustness of the new metric. There is disagreement: two reviewers (ratings 5, 5) found the contributions valuable and the results compelling, while two reviewers (ratings 3, 3) identified major methodological and conceptual issues that undermine the paper's claims.

### Strengths

- The problem of large-scale species distribution modeling is important and underexplored in machine learning (R1, R2, R4). The proposed solution is straightforward to implement (R1).
- LD-SDM is the first SDM approach to incorporate language, using a pre-trained LLM (Llama 2) to encode the taxonomic hierarchy of species, enabling range mapping for any taxonomic rank and unseen species without retraining (R1, R2, R4). This zero-shot prediction is a novel and valuable capability (R1, R2).
- The model makes a prediction for all locations simultaneously for one species at a time, unlike prior methods that predict for a single location (R4).
- The new proximity-aware evaluation metric accounts for spatial proximity and enables more fine-grained analysis than existing metrics; the adaptation of chamfer distance is interesting (R1, R2, R3). The metric is intuitively reasonable (R2).
- The proposed method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines on the new metric (Table 2) (R2, R4). The improvement is non-trivial (R4).
- The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of different combinations of prior methods and losses, including multi-label learning losses ASL and RAL that had not been previously studied for SDM (R2, R4).
- The evaluation is separated into standard, unseen species, and rare species settings, which is interesting and valuable (R2).
- The paper is generally clearly written with well-designed figures (R2, R3). Hyperparameters are clearly specified and the results are reproducible (R2).
- The results show the method is competitive against two competing methods (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Assumption of taxonomic-range similarity**: The central claim that species belonging to the same genera tend to be found in similar locations lacks justification and runs counter to the ecological principle that speciation is often promoted by geographic isolation (R3 W1, R4 W1). No evidence from the literature or training data is provided to support this assumption (R3 Q1, R4 Q1). A reviewer notes that taxonomically similar species may share habitat affinity but not necessarily the same location (R4 Q1).

- **Need for LLM encoding**: It is unclear whether the LLM adds value over simpler approaches. Alternatives such as training per-species embeddings (as in SINR) and averaging embeddings of same-genus species for unseen species should be ablated (R4 W2). The paper does not compare full taxonomic names vs. binary names vs. one-hot encoding of species (R3 W3) and does not explore prompt engineering or using just the species name without additional taxonomic information (R2 W2). The use of "natural language" is also a mischaracterization because the input is a constrained taxonomic string (R2 W2). A reviewer suggests trying Llama 3 or CLIP for embeddings (R1 question).

- **New evaluation metric is insufficiently justified**: The metric is introduced without theoretical guarantees, bounds, or specification of when it is appropriate (R1 W1). It is not demonstrated that the problem the metric is meant to solve (e.g., spatial proximity sensitivity) exists in practice; examples would help (R2 W2). It is also not shown that the new metric ranks models differently than existing metrics like mAP; presenting mAP alongside would address this (R2 W2). The metric is sensitive to missing presences in the evaluation data, and the advantage of Cole et al.'s spatially dense evaluation is lost (R4 W4). The metric's handling of vagrant species (e.g., birds spotted far from their normal ranges) is not discussed (R2 W2).

- **Experimental methodology concerns**:
  - The paper curates its own training and evaluation datasets instead of using existing datasets from Cole et al. (ICML 2023) or SINR, making direct comparison difficult (R1 W4, R4 W3). The reason given (hand-crafted range maps are “not widely available”) contradicts the fact that Cole et al. use exactly such range maps (R4 W3).
  - The only direct comparison using pre-existing metrics is the Geo Feature regression in Table 2, where prior models (SINR) outperform the proposed model, raising concerns that LD-SDM may underperform on other standard tasks (R2 W2, R1 W4).
  - Computational resources are a potential confound: model sizes and training steps are not compared across methods (R2 W2).
  - The conclusion that spherical harmonics are important for SDM is not supported by the results in Table 5 because it is unclear whether the compared models differ only in the use of spherical harmonics (R3 W3).
- **Presentation and formulation issues**:
  - The problem formulation discretizes the region variable r as a uniform grid, but a continuous formulation may be more natural; discretization may assign zero probability to many locations and reduce granularity in high-probability regions (R1 W2).
  - The loss section (3.4) is not clearly tied to the problem definition, and the task description for “species range prediction” is not explicitly defined (R1 W3).
  - The claim that prior frameworks (e.g., Mac Aodha et al.) cannot use species-specific rich information is not justified; they could use fixed species embeddings or regularization (R2 W2).
  - The exclusion of species with fewer than 100 observations is claimed to avoid bias but the type of bias and how exclusion helps is not explained (R2 W2).
- **Missing details**: 
  - How is zero-shot evaluation performed? Is the full taxonomic name provided at inference time? (R2, R3, R4 Q2)
  - No statistics are provided on how many test species belong to genera present in the training set and the average number of species per genus (R4 Q3).
  - Several minor inaccuracies: SDM is not a remote-sensing task (R4 minor); “presence-only” data is not defined when first introduced (R4 minor); reference for iNaturalist should be www.inaturalist.org not Van Horn et al. (R2 minor, R4 minor); spatial binning will not give an even distribution around the globe (H3 cells would be better) (R4 minor); the different spatial distribution of unseen species in Fig 9 should be commented on (R4 minor).
  - Common names were not considered, though they might be closer to the language model's training distribution (R2 minor).
  - What exactly is r̂ in lines 223-224 and 234-235? (R1 question)
  - The paper should note the limitation of the assumption that taxonomy is a good proxy for range similarities (R4 minor).

Note: The reviewers' scores are split: two reviewers rated the paper 5 (strong accept) and two rated it 3 (weak reject), reflecting disagreement on the overall quality and significance of the contributions.
