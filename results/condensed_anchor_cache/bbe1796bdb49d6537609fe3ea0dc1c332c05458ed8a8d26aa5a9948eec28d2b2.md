## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper uses galvanized iron sheets (roughness-modulated emissivity) to physically realize QR‑like adversarial patches for infrared pedestrian detectors. Optimization employs differential evolution, EOT, and TV smoothing loss. Experiments are conducted on a small dataset (378 images, 479 person labels) with fine‑tuned YOLOv5 and tested on multiple detectors. Reviewers find the material choice interesting but note limited novelty, missing ablation studies, insufficient literature coverage, methodological flaws, and questionable experimental choices.

### Strengths
1. **Creative material‑based approach** – Exploiting emissivity variation via surface roughness of galvanized iron sheets adds a new angle to physical infrared attacks (R1, R2, R4).
2. **Thorough experimental design** – Includes ablation studies on patch pixel depth/resolution, testing over varied distances and angles, and evaluation on multiple black‑box detectors (Faster RCNN, Mask RCNN, YOLOv3) (R2).
3. **Controllable infrared‑map color** – The material selection makes adversarial mapping more physically feasible (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and prior art**  
   - Several reviewers note that exploiting emissivity differences for infrared attacks is not new: prior works (Multispectral Invisible Coating using low‑e films, Infrared Adversarial Car Stickers) already used emissivity for adversarial purposes (R1, R4).  
   - The paper omits a relevant reference (Niu et al. 2023) that uses reflective/insulation tapes for near‑infrared attacks; a comparison on pose/direction invariance and longer‑distance robustness is needed (R2).  
   - The optimization algorithm and pattern generation are directly adopted from prior work with no innovation (R4).  
   - The claim that all prior physical IR attacks rely on temperature changes is inaccurate because Infrared Invisible Clothing uses aerogel (which does not change temperature) (R1).

2. **Methodology and implementation gaps**  
   - Missing fine‑tuning details for YOLOv5, which could affect detector robustness and bias attack evaluation (R1).  
   - No ablation study on EOT Transfer or hyperparameters of the differential evolution algorithm (R1).  
   - The patch is affixed to a single side, limiting effective angle to ±30°; the paper does not discuss solutions for direction invariance or stealth (R2).  
   - The labor‑intensive process of controlling surface roughness for precise patches needs discussion on efficiency improvements (R2).  
   - TV loss is used, but since the patch is a grid with few pixels and clear boundaries, its relevance is questionable (R4).  
   - Besides EOT, no other methods (e.g., color mapping) are used to bridge the digital‑physical gap, which could be more effective given limited patch colors (R4).

3. **Experimental concerns**  
   - Detection threshold is set to 0.7, inflating attack success rate; the standard threshold of 0.5 should be used (R3).  
   - The dataset contains only 378 images (479 person labels); the train/validation split is not explained, raising overfitting concerns when fine‑tuning YOLOv5 (R1).  
   - Confidence and IoU thresholds for digital and physical attacks are not specified (R1).

4. **Literature and factual errors**  
   - Irregular reference in line 38 (Szegedy et al.) is inappropriate in this context (R3).  
   - Related work lacks important references: Zhu et al. (CVPR 2024), Zhu et al. (Applied Intelligence 2023), Hu et al. (Neural Networks 2024) (R3).  
   - Line 372 incorrectly states that Hotcold Block uses a genetic algorithm; it actually uses PSO (R3).  
   - Missing ethics discussion addressing both positive implications (improving detector robustness) and risks (misuse to evade detection) (R2).

5. **Physical implementation issues**  
   - The weight of galvanized iron sheet patches may cause clothing sagging, affecting wearability (R3).  
   - The pattern is optimized to a grid form; reviewers ask why not more complex patterns with more colors/pixels (R4).  
   - Could the material color be controlled to simultaneously achieve visible‑light adversarial attacks? (R4).
