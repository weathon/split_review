## Merged Review

### Summary
Ratings range from 3 to 8 (reviewers: 8, 3, 6, 6). The paper is praised for scaling context length to 1M tokens and open-sourcing models, but criticized for limited technical novelty, weak multimodal performance relative to recent VLMs, insufficient ablation studies, and a misleading title. One reviewer (score 3) strongly questions the paper’s framing and lack of analysis, while the others (scores 6 and 8) recognize its empirical contributions despite notable shortcomings.

### Strengths
1. **First to scale sequence models to 1M tokens** in both language and multimodal (text-video) tasks, pushing boundaries of long-context processing (Reviewers 1,2,4).
2. **State-of-the-art or strong results on long-context retrieval tasks** – multi-needle retrieval at 128K context achieves results comparable to or better than GPT-4 (Reviewer 1,2,4).
3. **Open-source release** of a family of 7B models (LWM-Text, LWM-Text-Chat, LWM, LWM-Chat) benefits community progress (Reviewer 1).
4. **VQGAN tokenization enables both understanding and generation** in a single model (Reviewers 3,4).
5. **Comprehensive evaluations** covering linguistic and multimodal tasks, with meticulous presentation of settings and hyperparameters (Reviewer 3).
6. **Well-structured and clearly written** paper with detailed explanations of architecture, training process, and results (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and insufficient ablation studies** – The technical improvements beyond Blockwise RingAttention are marginal; progressive training is effective but not novel; paper lacks in-depth ablation on key components (masked sequence packing, loss weighting) and relies heavily on previously proposed techniques (Reviewers 2,3).
2. **Misleading title “Large World Model”** – The paper does not adequately define or discuss the concept of a large world model or its relationship to multimodal LLMs with long context windows (Reviewer 2).
3. **Weak image and short-video understanding performance** – The model underperforms recent VLMs (LLaVA-NeXT, VILA, Qwen-VL, CogVLM) on standard benchmarks (Reviewers 1,3). The VQGAN choice degrades image understanding ability (Table 4) (Reviewer 4).
4. **Missing long-video benchmark evaluations** – No results on hour-long video benchmarks like VideoMME or MLVU; the current video benchmarks are not long (Reviewers 3,4). Reviewer 3 additionally asks about evaluations with increased resolution and lengthy vision tokens.
5. **Insufficient comparisons with existing models** – No comparisons with recent open-source LLMs supporting 128K context (e.g., Llama 3.1) and no baseline at 1M context (e.g., linear scaling from a 128K model) (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewer 2 calls for comprehensive comparisons with recent multimodal LLM works.
6. **No analysis of text-video interaction** – The paper lacks study of whether joint training benefits or harms each modality (Reviewer 2).
7. **Missing crucial training details** – The specific evaluation datasets and trainable parameters per stage (especially stage 2) are not clearly stated (Reviewer 3). The rationale for the 16% text data mix and loss balancing strategy is not explained (Reviewer 1).
8. **Simple concatenation of VQGAN without adaptation** – The VQ model is used off-the-shelf without novel architectural modifications for long sequences, limiting generation quality; no quantitative image generation results are reported (Reviewers 3,4).
9. **Unexplained training dynamics** – Large fluctuations in the purple curve of Fig. 9 are not explained (Reviewer 2).
10. **No quantitative evaluation for image/video generation** despite claims in Section 4.3.3 (Reviewer 2).
