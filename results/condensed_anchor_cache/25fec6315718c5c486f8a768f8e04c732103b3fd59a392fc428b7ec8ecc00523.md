## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a deep reinforcement learning framework for the dynamic capacitated vehicle routing problem (DCVRP), using a designed dynamic attention network with encoder-decoder architecture and dummy depots as placeholders for yet-to-be-revealed customer positions. The method is compared against two baselines (LKH and OR-Tools) on randomly generated instances and shows better computational speed and solution quality. The authors release code with GIFs.

### Strengths
- Addresses the dynamic CVRP, a subject of great significance for real-world applications; dynamic problems with learned methods represent an exciting research opportunity (R1, R2, R3).
- The algorithm adaptively addresses dynamic customer addition and demonstrates better performance than the LKH- and OR-Tools-based baselines (R1, R4).
- The paper offers innovative insights into the integration of DRL within the dynamic CVRP (R4).
- The authors have released several GIFs in the code repository (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and literature review are insufficient.** The paper claims that “DRL for dynamic CVRP has not been well addressed yet” but omits numerous prior works on DRL for dynamic VRP (e.g., Joe & Lau 2020; Oren et al. 2021; Zhang et al. TNNLS 2023; Ma et al. NeurIPS 2021; Li et al. ICDE 2021; Pan & Liu 2023; Basso et al. 2021; Peng et al. 2019) (R3). The concept of “dummy depots” is not novel, and the adaptation of the attention model for dynamic VRP appears direct and incremental (R3, R4, R5). The only apparent innovation is setting the input as dynamically disclosed customer locations in a mask fashion; otherwise the pipeline is standard and well-studied (R4). Similar ideas can be found in previous literature (e.g., Solving Dynamic TSP with DRL, TNNLS 2023) (R5).
- **Baseline comparisons are not state-of-the-art.** The methods based on LKH and OR-Tools are not competitive; the State-of-the-Art for dynamic VRP includes methods such as Vidal’s Hybrid Genetic Search (HGS) from the NeurIPS meets EURO competition, and Ulmer’s approach (which the authors cite but do not use) (R2). The offered baselines are “a decade behind the current state of the art” (R2).
- **Experiments are weak and incomplete.** The experimental evaluation is limited to uniformly randomly generated instances with only 50 nodes, which does not demonstrate robustness (R2, R3). Run times appear arbitrarily chosen (R2). There is no investigation of parameter sensitivity, ablation studies, or other systematic analysis (R2). The batch training issue for sequences of different lengths is common and easily tackled by padding, not a contribution (R5). The experiments are not sufficient or convincing (R5).
- **Mathematical formulation of the DCVRP is unclear and not rigorous.** The optimization problem (1)–(5) is poorly explained: notations are not introduced before the problem, making it difficult to parse (R1, R4). It appears to be a bi-level optimization problem (constraint (2) contains another optimization problem), but it is not addressed how to solve it (R1). The model is not structured as a mixed-integer linear program, even though the problem is linear; the unconventional format is not standard in OR (R2). The rationale behind equations (1)–(5) is not clearly explained (R4). Constraint (3) defines \(t_i^{cur}\) under various scenarios but requires further clarification (R4).
- **DRL formulation and implementation details are missing.** The paper does not specify the reward, states, state transitions, or transition probability models for the RL problem (R1, R4). How do parameters change dynamically? What is the logic behind using the attention model? (R1). The design of the action space needs reconsideration: vehicles may need to wait at certain locations, anticipating future order releases, which could enable more strategic planning (R3). No parameter settings or scenario specifications for training and testing are provided (R4).
- **Presentation and clarity are poor.** The paper is not well-written; many notations (e.g., mha, sha, \(D'\), \(X'\), \(\pi_j\) – unclear whether set or sequence) are ill-defined (R1, R4). There are multiple grammar issues (e.g., “The point with the highest probability il+1 is selected…”) (R4). The manuscript style seems more suited for a journal than ICLR; some sections appear incomplete with repetitive paragraphs and missing formulas (R3). The paper is difficult to parse and understand the main contributions (R1, R4). Reproducibility is questionable due to the significant modifications made to transform static VRP into dynamic VRP without clear documentation (R4).
- **Contrast in reviewer opinion:** While R1, R2, and R5 gave borderline scores (3), R3 and R4 were strongly negative (score 1), emphasizing minimal novelty, poor presentation, and insufficient contributions, especially relative to the existing literature and OR standards.
