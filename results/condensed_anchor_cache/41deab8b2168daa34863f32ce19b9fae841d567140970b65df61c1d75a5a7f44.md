## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FTS-Diffusion, a three-module generative framework for financial time series with irregular and scale-invariant patterns. Scores are 6, 8, 8. Reviewer 1 (score 6) is more critical, citing experimental clarity issues and missing details, while Reviewers 2 and 3 (each score 8) are more positive, commending the novelty and promising results. All reviewers note the need for stronger evaluation, ablation, reproducibility, and broader validation.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and originality.** The three-module framework (pattern recognition, pattern generation, pattern evolution) and the scale-invariant pattern recognition algorithm SISC are novel. The pattern‑centric approach combining clustering, variational inference, and classification+regression is original (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Clear problem motivation and exposition.** The irregular and scale‑invariant characteristics of stock data are clearly explained and motivated with examples and definitions. The method description and Figure 3 provide a concise overview. The paper is well‑positioned against existing work and has no grammatical flaws (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Promising empirical results.** Experiments on three real‑world financial assets show that FTS‑Diffusion outperforms state‑of‑the‑art alternatives in distribution matching and downstream prediction (e.g., up to 17.9% error reduction). The generated data closely matches real data properties (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Practical significance.** Addresses data shortage in finance, which is important for improving deep‑learning reliability. The framework may generalize to other irregular and scale‑invariant time series (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental details and risk of label leakage.** The data/training details for each module and the downstream LSTM are not clearly specified: how data are partitioned among the three modules, whether training is sequential or joint, the lookback window for the LSTM, the specific loss functions and optimization procedures, and the train/test split details. This lack of clarity makes it difficult to assess result validity and raises concern about label leakage (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited scenario coverage.** Experiments are conducted on only a few types of stock data (e.g., S&P 500, individual stocks). Broader financial scenarios (e.g., other asset classes, higher‑frequency data, multiple‑day forecasts) are not included (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing ablation studies.** The effectiveness of each of the three modules and of specific designs within them (e.g., the pattern recognition clustering, the pattern evolution network φ, the Info() function in Algorithm 2) is not ablated. This weakens the evidence that the pattern‑centric approach adds complementary information (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 3 questions the learning of φ).
- **No rolling‑window or multiple‑split validation.** Results are reported on a single train/test split, which is not reliable for financial time series. A rolling‑window evaluation is needed (Reviewer 2). Similarly, a naïve baseline (e.g., previous‑day prediction) is missing for the one‑day‑ahead forecast, and a multi‑step‑ahead forecast should be tested (Reviewer 3).
- **Weak justification of the pattern conjecture.** The claim that univariate financial time series embody recurring complex patterns is supported only by a fine‑grained analysis of one dataset (S&P 500). No details on clustering results (number of classes, items per class, multiple centers) are provided. The arguments in Appendix A/B remain insufficiently rigorous (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited feature scope.** Experiments only use price returns. Industry practice includes many derived features from price and order‑book data; it is unclear how easily the framework can incorporate additional features (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Reproducibility concerns.** No code is provided, and dataset descriptions are missing key details. This limits reproducibility (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 also asks about computational complexity and runtime compared to baselines (Reviewer 3, question).
- **Missing standard evaluation metrics.** In addition to the metrics used, standard finance metrics such as Sharpe Ratio should be reported (Reviewer 1).
- **Generalization to distribution shift not assessed.** Whether the pattern combinations found generalize to severe distribution shifts in financial scenarios is unclear (Reviewer 2, question).
- **Direct prediction use case not evaluated.** Whether FTS‑Diffusion can be used directly for forecasting (instead of augmentation) is not explored (Reviewer 2, question).
