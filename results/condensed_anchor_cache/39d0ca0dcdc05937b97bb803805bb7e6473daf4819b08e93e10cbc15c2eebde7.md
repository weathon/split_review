## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers rated the paper 6, 8, 6 (R2: 8, confidence 4; R1: 6, confidence 2; R3: 6, confidence 3). R2 is distinctly more positive, especially after the rebuttal, while R1 and R3 are more critical, with R3 questioning the soundness of the theory. The paper introduces a reconstruction attack based on the neural tangent kernel that requires initial and trained weights but no data distribution information. It provably recovers the entire training set in the infinite-width regime and ties reconstruction to dataset distillation. Reviewers agree the modelling via NTK is novel and the empirical results are strong, but raise concerns about theoretical rigor, presentation clarity, missing ablations, and the definition of outliers.

### Strengths
- **Novel modelling of memorization via NTK.** (R2) The way of modelling data reconstruction by NTK captures the main properties of memorization dynamics. Theoretical discussion coincides well with numerical experiments.
- **Algorithm for dataset distillation.** (R1) Developing a nice algorithm based on inducing point methods; Figure 8 in particular shows the value of the approach. Underlying theoretical connections to dataset inversion provide confidence.
- **Thorough experimental evidence.** (R1) Validates theoretical claims on restricted MNIST, CIFAR, and impressive reconstruction attacks on ImageNet.
- **Clear discussion of weaknesses inspiring future work.** (R2)
- **Main paper is clear and easy to understand.** (R3)
- **Interesting results on complex architectures and kernel choice.** (R1) Results on more complex architectures (Appendix J) and the insight about combining initial and final weight states (Appendix I) should be moved to the main text.

### Weaknesses
- **“Outlier” definition not well justified.** (R1) The paper defines outliers by high \(\alpha\) values, but does not clearly link this to an intuitive geometric or Euclidean notion of an outlier. A more rigorous justification connecting \(\alpha\) to the data manifold and training dynamics is needed.
- **Figures hard to read.** (R1) Text on most figures is too small, even on screen.
- **Reconstruction plots unexplained until page 6, but appear as early as page 4.** (R1) This hurts readability.
- **Model variants not centrally defined.** (R1) Definitions of RKIP, RKIP-finite, etc. are spread across the paper; a single table or section summarizing all variants, parameters, and training procedures would greatly improve clarity.
- **Theoretical presentation lacks rigor.** (R3 – a minority critical view; R2 was positive about theory) Theorem 1’s statement is hard to interpret as a formal mathematical statement; the proof is vague with few equations. Theorem 2’s derivation in the appendix misses error terms when approximating \(k_{\theta_0}\) by \(k_{NTK}\) – it must be shown that a negligible \(|k_{\theta_0}-k_{NTK}|\) leads to a negligible error in the first “->” on Page 19. Overall, the theoretical contribution is not as sound as the empirical side.
- **Missing investigation of self-supervised learning and structured outputs.** (R1, question) Does the attack extend to self-supervised representations and structured (non-scalar) outputs?
- **Missing analysis of different training losses.** (R1, question) The paper assumes MSE loss; how would the attack change for other losses (e.g., cross-entropy)? Is MSE a simplifying assumption or a fundamental requirement?
- **Missing clarification on weight decay prior.** (R1, question) Is there an underlying assumption in (1) that a prior (weight decay) is placed on the network parameters?
- **Missing analysis of data dimensionality.** (R2, question) Since reconstruction is cast as sparse coding and training as encoding (Haim et al., 2021), can conclusions about the effect of data dimension be drawn?
- **Missing study of reconstruction initialization sensitivity.** (R2, question) Algorithm 2 uses “randomly initialized reconstruction images”; how does the result diverge with different initializations (e.g., an image from one of two classes, a different class, a natural image of a different class, or a random matrix)?
- **Missing link to gradient inversion methods.** (R2, question) The paper should relate to GradViT (Gradient Inversion of Vision Transformers) and Deep Leakage from Gradients, which use local gradient information (even on poorly trained models) to reconstruct samples.
- **Minor issues.** (R1) Abstract wording: “its effective regime **which** datapoints are susceptible to reconstruction.” Introduction: calling non-privacy-preserving networks “useless” is hyperbolic. Table 1: specify whether the values are accuracy.
