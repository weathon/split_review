## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes why VAEs overestimate likelihoods for OOD data, identifying two factors: (1) the improper Gaussian prior design, and (2) the gap in dataset entropy‑mutual integration between ID and OOD distributions. To mitigate these, the authors propose AVOID, which combines a Post‑Hoc Prior (PHP) that replaces the standard normal prior with the learned aggregate posterior, and a Dataset Entropy‑Mutual Calibration (DEC) that adjusts the score using a complexity measure inspired by Serra et al. (2020). Experiments on FMNIST vs. MNIST, CIFAR‑10 vs. SVHN, and Celeb‑A vs. CIFAR‑10/100 show that AVOID improves OOD detection over the plain ELBO and several VAE‑based baselines. The paper includes theoretical analysis, toy examples, and ablation studies. All four reviewers rated the paper 6 and recommend rejection, citing several important unresolved issues.

### Strengths
- The analysis decomposing the ELBO into a KL‑divergence term and an entropy‑mutual integration term is interesting and provides a clear theoretical lens on the overestimation problem. [R2, R4]
- The identification of two concrete factors (improper prior and entropy‑mutual gap) is a useful contribution. [R1, R4]
- The proposed PHP method—substituting the aggregate posterior for the prior—is a clever and novel fix that yields a substantial performance gain on some benchmarks (e.g., FMNIST vs. MNIST). [R3]
- The paper is well‑written and well‑organized, with a logical flow from analysis to method to experiments. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- A comprehensive set of experiments and ablations is provided, covering many baselines and datasets, and including an ablation study to assess the individual contributions of PHP and DEC. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The toy examples in Section 3.2 are informative and help illustrate the prior mismatch. [R2, R4]
- The problem tackled—unsupervised OOD detection with VAEs—is significant, especially for safety‑critical applications. [R1]
- The observation that replacing the prior with the aggregate posterior improves OOD detection is a notable empirical result that highlights the role of estimation error. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Missing reverse‑direction experiments (high severity).** All experiments test the pathological direction (complex ID → simple OOD). To avoid harming performance in the reverse direction, results for e.g. MNIST (ID) vs. FMNIST (OOD) and SVHN (ID) vs. CIFAR‑10 (OOD) must be reported. [R2, R3]
- **DEC motivation and formulation are unclear.** The non‑scaled calibration function \(C_{non}(\mathbf{x}) = (n_{id} - (n_i - n_{id}))/n_{id}\) is presented without adequate justification, especially when \(n_i \ge n_{id}\). The scaling factor (Ent‑Mut of ID plus a KL term) also lacks a clear rationale; a more direct scaling would be expected. [R3] The reliance on SVD (a linear method) may not capture complex non‑linear structures in the latent space. [R2] While the authors note that many definitions could satisfy Property 1, the specific choice remains unmotivated. [R3]
- **Contribution of PHP and DEC is not clearly disentangled.** The ablation study shows that each component alone only minimally improves performance in some cases; it is unclear why combining them works. [R1, R3] Moreover, the experiments do not provide a figure analogous to Figure 3 for the CIFAR‑10 vs. SVHN task to explain why PHP fails there. [R2]
- **Computational overhead is high.** PHP requires training an additional LSTM-based model to estimate \(\hat{q}_{id}(z)\) beyond the standard VAE training, adding significant cost and risk of overfitting to the ID data. The runtime of computing \(C(x)\) (especially iterating over singular values) is not analyzed, and no efficiency comparison with other methods is provided. [R1, R2, R4] The reliance on SVD and additional training makes the method less self‑contained. [R2]
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity.** The method introduces a task‑dependent hyperparameter \(n_{id}\) whose sensitivity is not investigated. There is no principled way to choose \(n_{id}\), and small changes could cause large performance variations. [R2]
- **Definition 1 of overestimation is weak.** Defining overestimation by expected ELBO does not guarantee per‑sample separation. Even if the mean ELBO on OOD is lower, a threshold may still misclassify many samples due to overlapping distributions. [R4]
- **Novelty of the ELBO breakdown is disputed.** Although presented as new, a similar decomposition appears in Caterini et al. (2022) (not cited). This omission should be corrected. [R2] Conversely, other reviewers found the decomposition novel. The paper should clearly distinguish its contribution from prior work. [R2, R4]
- **Notation and presentation issues.** The notation \(x = \mathrm{ID}\) or \(x = \mathrm{OOD}\) is confusing; a proper classifier notation would be clearer. [R4] The term \(H_{q,p}(z|x)\) in Eq. (5) is not standard conditional entropy because it uses the model distribution \(p_{\theta}(z|x)\) under the true data distribution—this needs explanation and clarification of any assumptions. [R4] The statement that \(p(z)=\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) is an improper choice for complex data is an overclaim without a formal measure of complexity; it should be softened to “may be an improper choice”. [R4]
- **Experiments are limited in scope.** The main experiments focus on variations of FashionMNIST and CIFAR‑10; results on CIFAR‑100 as ID are missing. [R1, R4] No comparison with ensemble methods is provided, and the supervised/auxiliary baselines differ across tables without explanation. [R1, R4] Tables 1 and 2 lack error bars in the main paper (though error bars are given in Appendix Tables 10 and 11); the main text should reference them. [R4] Also, DEC achieves zero error in those appendix tables—this should be discussed or explained. [R4]
- **Robustness not evaluated.** There is no discussion or experiment on adversarial examples or noisy inputs, nor on how the method would handle real‑world decision‑making contexts. [R1]
- **Missing related work.** The paper should cite Schirrmeister et al. (2020) on OOD detection anomalies in invertible networks, and Loaiza‑Ganem et al. (2022) on flow‑based adjustments to the latent space of VAEs for OOD detection. The connection to the latter should be discussed to clarify novelty. [R2]
- **Method is tailored to VAEs only.** The paper does not discuss applicability to other latent‑variable models (e.g., latent diffusion models), which should be noted as a limitation. [R2]
- **Limitations are relegated to the appendix.** The limitations section in Appendix K should be moved to the main paper for self‑containedness. [R4]
- **Additional minor issues.** The word “counterfactual” is used incorrectly in the introduction. [R3] Eq. (24) in the appendix contains a typo. [R3]
