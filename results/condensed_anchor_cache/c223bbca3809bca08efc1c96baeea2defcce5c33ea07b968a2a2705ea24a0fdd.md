## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces IndianRoad, a video dataset of Indian traffic scenes from an ego-vehicle view. It contains 1,231 one-minute videos with 16 diverse actor categories (including animals, humans, vehicles) and 16 action types (e.g., cut-ins, zigzag, U-turns). The dataset provides over 13 million densely annotated bounding boxes with actor identification, of which more than 1.6 million include detailed action/behavior annotations. It is designed to benchmark five tasks: tracking, detection, spatiotemporal action localization, video moment retrieval, and multi-label video action recognition. Vulnerable Road Users constitute 41.13% of instances (vs. 23.71% in Waymo). Baseline models perform poorly on IndianRoad, demonstrating its challenging nature. Reviewers acknowledge the large annotation effort but raise significant concerns about novelty, writing quality, technical accuracy, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The dataset addresses the challenging scenario of vulnerable road users (VRUs) in complex, unstructured environments, with high VRU representation (41.13%) compared to Waymo (23.71%).
- Annotations cover 16 diverse actor categories (including animals, humans, various vehicles) and 16 complex action types (cut-ins, zigzag movements, U-turns) that require advanced reasoning abilities.
- The dataset provides over 13 million densely annotated bounding boxes, with more than 1.6 million including both actor identification and action/behavior details.
- Annotations are provided for five different tasks, allowing flexibility for various applications.
- The dataset includes videos across diverse weather conditions, times of day, road scenarios, and traffic densities, enabling evaluation of real-world generalization.
- Labeling a large-scale dataset is a substantial and nontrivial effort; reviewers commended the authors for sharing the resource and conducting extensive benchmarking experiments across multiple tasks (Tracking, Detection, Spatiotemporal Action Localization, Video Moment Retrieval, Multi-label Video Action Recognition).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The dataset’s novelty is questioned. Previous datasets targeting unstructured Indian traffic scenes (IDD [1], DATS_2022 [2]) are not referred to, and the manuscript does not provide new insights regarding data collection methods. Reviewer 4 notes that “unstructured scenes are difficult is not surprising” given prior work.
- **Missing comprehensive comparison with existing traffic scene datasets:** The introduction mentions only Waymo briefly, but overlooks many relevant video-based traffic scene datasets. Reviewer 3 lists nine prior works (Ramanishka et al. CVPR 2018, Malla et al. CVPR 2020, Fang et al. TPAMI 2022, Chandra et al. IROS 2022, Singh et al. TPAMI 2023, Yao et al. TPAMI 2023, Malla et al. WACV 2023, Agarwal and Chen ICCV 2023, Sachdeva et al. WACV 2024) that should be compared. A detailed comparison table or discussion of annotation types, scenario diversity, and VRU focus relative to these datasets is needed to clarify unique contributions.
- **Missing experiments with joint training:** Reviewer 1 points out that the paper does not demonstrate the value of integrating IndianRoad with other datasets as training data, i.e., whether this combination yields performance boosts compared to using existing datasets alone.
- **Problematic claim of dataset challenge:** The claim that IndianRoad is more challenging is based on evaluating pre-trained models from existing datasets, which introduces data distribution shift. Reviewer 1 recommends training models on a portion of IndianRoad and testing on its test split to more fairly assess difficulty.
- **Basic concept errors in tracking tasks:** Reviewer 2 identifies a serious error: the paper states that GOT-10k is a “zero-shot evaluation” (L651), but GOT-10k uses “zero-overlapped evaluation” (no category overlap between training and test) and SOT is a one-shot task (first-frame bounding box). This reflects a misunderstanding of the task definition.
- **Confusion between SOT and MOT:** The dataset examples appear more inclined to Multi-Object Tracking (MOT), yet comparisons and algorithms used are for Single Object Tracking (SOT). Reviewer 2 finds this mixing confusing and indicates a lack of clear task focus.
- **Writing and presentation issues:** The paper reads like a technical report rather than an academic paper. Many irregularities: numbers inconsistently formatted with commas (Table 4 shows both forms), tables are not standard three-line tables. Reviewer 2 notes that the related work contains a mistyped author name for the LaSOT dataset. The introduction and related work should be separated (Reviewer 4).
- **Outdated SOTA references:** The SOTA methods cited for each task are mostly from 2018 and 2019; the definition of “SOTA” is unclear. Reviewer 1 recommends including more recent methods.
- **Missing discussion of recent tracking datasets:** Reviewer 2 notes that high-quality SOT datasets like VideoCube (TPAMI 2023) and VastTrack (NeurIPS 2024) with greater scale and complexity are not introduced or discussed, undermining the diversity/complexity claim.
- **Limited consideration of existing traffic scene algorithms in benchmarks:** Reviewer 3 lists specific algorithms that should be benchmarked on IndianRoad: e.g., Action-slot [4] for multi-label action recognition and Khan et al. for spatiotemporal action localization. Their absence weakens the benchmark evaluation. The authors should either include these comparisons or justify why they are not applicable.
- **Annotation protocol not explained:** Reviewer 4 points out that there is no explanation of quality assurance measures, such as how consistency of text annotations across different annotators was ensured.
- **Domain limitation:** The dataset is limited to India; Reviewer 4 notes that collecting data across multiple countries/regions would lead to a more generalizable dataset.
- **Redundant main argument:** Reviewer 2 summarizes that the paper tries to cover all tasks but ends up being a general and unfocused introduction. The authors should identify the single biggest difference from prior work and make it the core of the paper.

*Contrast in reviewer opinions:* Reviewers 1 and 4 gave higher scores (5) and emphasized the dataset’s value for challenging VRU scenarios and multiple tasks. Reviewers 2 and 3 gave lower scores (3) and focused on lack of novelty, technical errors, missing comparisons, and poor writing. This disagreement highlights that the dataset’s contribution is not uniformly convincing.
