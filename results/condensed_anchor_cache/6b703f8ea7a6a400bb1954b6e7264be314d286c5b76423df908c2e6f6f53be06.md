- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 8, 6, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Efficient Cross-Episodic Transformers (ECET), an online meta-RL algorithm that combines intra-episode and cross-episodic information using a two-level transformer hierarchy: one transformer encodes each episode into a latent, and a second transformer processes these latents across episodes to form a context representation. The authors claim state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks including Meta-World ML45 and ManiSkill, outperforming previous methods such as TrMRL. The method is presented with clear explanations and visualizations, and an open-source implementation is provided.

### Strengths
- The paper reports state-of-the-art results on challenging tasks, particularly the Meta-World ML45 environment (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The method and algorithm are clearly explained and visually illustrated; the Method and Experiments sections are especially well written (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Experiments are well designed with explicit hypothesis statements (Reviewer 2 appreciates Hypothesis 1-3).
- The idea is simple yet promising, integrating insights from previous meta-RL research (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Related work is well positioned (Reviewer 2).
- Open-sourced code is provided to reproduce results (Reviewer 4).
- Results include few-shot adaptation to parametric variations and out-of-distribution tasks (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: While some reviewers found the method novel (scores 6, 8), reviewers 3 and 4 argued that the contribution is incremental. The main difference from TrMRL (Melo, 2022) is the decomposition of the encoder into two transformer stages. Reviewer 4 noted that the claim that previous methods used only within-episode transitions is false (VariBAD and TrMRL already incorporate cross-episodic information). No rigorous theoretical motivation is provided for why the two-stage architecture should outperform recurrent architectures (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 2 also asked for a clearer definition and measurement of feature generality beyond clustering (Fig. 13).
- **Missing baseline**: AMAGO (Grigsby et al., ICLR 2024) should be included since the paper claims SOTA results (Reviewer 1).
- **Architecture mismatch with TrMRL**: TrMRL uses an RNN in the outer loop while ECET uses a transformer; performance differences may be due to capacity, not the proposed method. The authors should match architectures (e.g., use a transformer in TrMRL's outer loop) to isolate effects (Reviewer 1).
- **Baseline hyperparameter tuning**: It is unclear whether baselines (especially TrMRL) were tuned to their best performance; no hyperparameter search details are given (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 noted that TrMRL results in Figure 5 do not match the original TrMRL paper, questioning fair comparison.
- **Insufficient empirical study**:  
  - Missing ablation study, especially over the two-stage transformer design (Reviewer 4).  
  - Missing benchmarks from the original works (e.g., VariBAD, RL^2) (Reviewer 4).  
  - For out-of-distribution generalization (Hypothesis 3), comparisons to theoretical/empirical works on generalization in meta-RL are missing (Reviewer 4).  
  - The method’s generalization to tasks completely different from the meta-training set remains limited; the approach is tailored to exploiting similarities, and this limitation should be discussed or empirically shown (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and experimental details missing**:  
  - How are transitions sampled from each episode (uniform? with replacement?)? What is the chosen sequence length \( T \)? How does \( T \) affect runtime/performance? (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - How are intra-episode and cross-episode information applied simultaneously? (Reviewer 3).  
  - Number of episodes, episode length, and \( T \) per benchmark not specified (Reviewer 4).  
  - Were the same hyperparameters used for all benchmarks? How sensitive is the method? (Reviewer 4).  
  - Number of seeds used for experiments in Section 5 not stated (Reviewer 4).  
  - Number of learnable parameters compared to baselines (especially TrMRL) not given (Reviewer 4).  
  - Why is positional encoding added to the intra-episode transformer (IET)? (Reviewer 4).  
  - Is Algorithm 2 an extension of prior work? Is Theorem 3 completely new? (Reviewer 3).  
- **Minor issues**:  
  - Typo "ihas" should be "has" (line 273) (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - Figure 2 bleeds into margins (Reviewer 2).  
  - Use "inter" instead of "cross" when contrasting with "intra" (Reviewer 2).  
  - Consider citing Bhatia et al. (RL^3: Boosting Meta Reinforcement Learning via RL inside RL^2) which also uses transformers for meta-RL feature learning (Reviewer 2).  
- **Reviewer disagreement**: Three reviewers gave scores 6, 8, 6, indicating overall positive reception, while one reviewer (score 3) found the novelty insufficient and the empirical study insufficient to support the claims. The first reviewer initially recommended reject but raised score to 6 after rebuttal, acknowledging that questions were mostly addressed.
