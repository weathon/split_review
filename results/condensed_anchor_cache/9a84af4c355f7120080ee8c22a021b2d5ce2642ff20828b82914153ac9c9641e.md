## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Neuron-level Balance between Stability and Plasticity (NBSP), a continual RL framework that identifies “RL skill neurons” via a goal-oriented scoring function, then uses gradient masking and experience replay to protect knowledge from the first task while learning a second task. Evaluations are on two-task sequences from Meta-World and Atari. All four reviewers rated the paper 3 (weak accept/reject), but with differing confidence and critical depth. Reviewers unanimously acknowledge that the paper is well-written and addresses an important problem (stability‑plasticity dilemma in DRL) and that the neuron‑level approach is interesting. However, all raise substantial concerns about the experimental scope (only two tasks, few environments), insufficient baseline comparisons (missing continual RL and plasticity‑loss methods), incomplete ablation studies, unclear definitions and formulation of the skill‑neuron scoring mechanism, and lack of scalability analysis. Reviewer 2 rated soundness 1 and contribution 1, highlighting severe methodological and validation flaws; Reviewer 4 rated contribution 2, also questioning the sufficiency of the validation. Reviewers 1 and 3 were less harsh but still identified major missing experiments and comparisons.

### Strengths

- The paper is well‑written, clearly structured, and easy to follow.
- It introduces a novel perspective on balancing stability and plasticity at the neuron level in deep reinforcement learning.
- The approach has low overhead: no extra parameters, no pseudo‑rehearsal.
- The idea of identifying “RL skill neurons” using both activation and goal‑oriented reward criteria is interesting and a potentially promising direction for future work.
- The method is theoretically reasonable and shows practical performance gains on the tested two‑task setups.

### Weaknesses

1. **Experimental scope and scalability**  
   - The evaluations are limited to only two sequential tasks. It is unclear how the method scales to longer sequences (e.g., 10+ tasks). Scalability concerns regarding memory and computational complexity for tracking skill neurons across many tasks are not addressed.  
   - The set of Meta‑World tasks is very narrow (mostly opening/closing the same object). Reviewers recommend testing on a wider variety of Meta‑World pairs (Push, Reach, Pick Place, Basketball, Sweep Into) and on more challenging benchmarks: Continual World (CW10/CW20), DeepMind Control Suite, Gym‑MuJoCo, BabyAI, and LIBERO.  
   - Atari experiments are only in the supplementary material and should be moved to the main paper.  
   - The paper often shows only the best results; a broader range of outcomes should be presented.

2. **Baseline comparisons**  
   - Missing comparisons with established continual RL baselines, including replay‑based methods (e.g., A‑GEM, ClonEx‑SAC), regularization‑based methods, and structure‑based methods that also operate at the neuron level (PackNet, CoTASP, Progressive Neural Networks).  
   - No comparison with state‑of‑the‑art methods specifically designed to address plasticity loss (e.g., [4][5][9] cited by Reviewer 4) or with lifelong learning approaches (e.g., [8] cited by Reviewer 4).  
   - The simple fine‑tuning baseline used for motivation in Section 3.1 is outdated and does not provide a strong foundation.  
   - The dormant neuron scoring function is closely related, yet it is not compared or even cited in the method section.

3. **Ablation and component isolation**  
   - The ablation study is incomplete. The main novelty (gradient masking) is not evaluated without experience replay, and the replay component is not evaluated alone. It is also unclear whether the other baselines use or could benefit from a similar replay mechanism.  
   - Suggested additional ablations: (i) alternative activation functions, (ii) using only \(Acc(N)\) in Eq. 4, (iii) using the dormant neuron score function, (iv) randomly selecting skill neurons, (v) gradient‑masking without replay, (vi) blocking gradients on both sides of a parameter (\(\Delta W_{i,j}\)).

4. **Definition and formulation of skill‑neuron identification**  
   - The continual learning problem is not clearly defined: Is it task‑incremental or class‑incremental? What access to previous task data is allowed? Are both policy and critic networks subject to the constraints?  
   - The term “RL skill neuron” lacks a precise, formal definition.  
   - **Score function issues**:  
     * Raw activation values are used instead of absolute values.  
     * The activation baseline \(\bar{a}(N)\) is a mean over time for a single neuron, not across neurons, making the distinction between neurons based on activation patterns unclear.  
     * The indicator function in Eq. 3 produces binary counts, losing information about how much a neuron’s activation exceeds the threshold.  
     * The reward‑based criterion \(q(t)\) is the same for all neurons, so it does not act as a neuron‑level measure.  
     * Eq. 4 is confusing: it assumes that neurons not meeting Eq. 3 still contribute positively, which undermines the intuition of skill‑neuron selection. A statistical analysis is needed to clarify what neurons are actually selected.  
   - **Gradient masking**: The strategy blocks only gradients connecting *from* the skill neuron (i.e., \(\Delta W_{:,j}\) but not \(\Delta W_{j,:}\) or only when both sides are skill neurons). The assumptions behind this choice are not stated.  
   - **Implementation**: It appears skill neurons are identified only once after the first task. For more than two tasks, it is unclear how masks accumulate across tasks. The algorithm pseudocode (Algorithm 1) does not include the skill‑neuron computation steps.  
   - **Hyperparameter and its impact**: The number of RL‑skill neurons is not clearly defined; it seems to be a hyperparameter. Only two values (300, 400) are briefly studied in the appendix, and the analysis needs to be expanded and moved to the main paper, showing how the number affects the stability‑plasticity trade‑off.

5. **Plasticity evaluation**  
   - The paper claims that NBSP alleviates plasticity loss but does not measure plasticity directly using established metrics such as the covariance metric or FAU. Such quantitative measurement is needed to validate the core claim.

6. **Literature review**  
   - The related‑works section lacks a comprehensive survey of continual RL methods, especially structure‑based (e.g., PackNet, CoTASP) and rehearsal‑based approaches. Methods for catastrophic forgetting (e.g., Section 5 of Khetarpal et al., Progressive Neural Networks) are overlooked or not discussed in the context of DRL.

7. **Minor issues and questions**  
   - The evaluation criterion \(q_\theta(t)\) is binary for Meta‑World (despite dense rewards that could be more informative) and return‑based for Atari; the rationale is unclear.  
   - How are the time‑steps \(T\) in Eq. (1) and (2) chosen?  
   - Is the method specific to ReLU activations? Would it perform differently with tanh?  
   - Experience replay frequency is high; what is the storage requirement from previous tasks?  
   - Learning curves do not indicate whether they correspond to training the first or second task; full curves should be shown.  
   - How is \(q(t)\) computed from the replay buffer when sampling?  
   - Computational complexity of measuring skill neurons should be reported.  
   - Section 3.2 is partially redundant with the related works.  
   - Figure 7: why not show average return on the x‑axis for easier comparison?  
   - There is a duplicate reference in the bibliography.  
   - Figure 3 could be moved to the appendix to free space for more experiments.
