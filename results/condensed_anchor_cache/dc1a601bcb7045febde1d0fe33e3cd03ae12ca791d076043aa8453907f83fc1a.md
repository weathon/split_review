## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Latent Diffusion Planning (LDP), an imitation learning framework combining a VAE-based latent encoder, a diffusion-based latent planner that forecasts future latent states, and a diffusion-based inverse dynamics model to extract actions from the planned latent sequence. LDP is designed to leverage heterogeneous data sources, including suboptimal demonstrations and action-free data. The method is evaluated on simulated visual robotic manipulation tasks from the Robomimic benchmark (Lift, Can, Square, and a custom PushT task). LDP uses a variational autoencoder to learn a compact latent space from images, then trains the planner and IDM as diffusion models. By separating planning from action prediction, the approach aims to improve performance in low-demonstration regimes by using additional action-free or suboptimal data.

### Strengths

- The paper presents a novel approach that breaks down imitation learning into a latent-space planning module and an inverse-dynamics prediction module, clearly delineating contributions in the introduction. (Reviewer 1)
- The specific design of using diffusion models for both the latent planner and the inverse dynamics model is novel. (Reviewer 1)
- Planning in a latent space (via VAE) is faster than methods requiring full video frame generation. (Reviewer 1)
- The idea of training from heterogeneous action-free and suboptimal data is important for robot learning, where collecting high-quality action-annotated demonstrations is difficult. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 3)
- Using a diffusion model for the latent planner is well-suited for capturing multimodal action distributions. (Reviewer 2)
- LDP achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to other imitation learning methods, notably on the Lift task where it attains 0.83 success rate with only 3 demonstrations. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is well-written: the method is explained clearly, and the related work and background sections are comprehensive. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5)
- **Minority positive view:** One reviewer rated the paper 5, citing strong novelty and clear presentation, while the other four rated 3, indicating disagreement on overall quality and contribution.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns relative to UniPi:** The method closely resembles UniPi (learning a video planner + inverse dynamics model). The main difference is that LDP uses a latent video diffusion model instead of a pixel-level video diffusion model, which a reviewer considered insufficiently novel. (Reviewer 5)
- **Experimental claims not fully supported:** The central claim about leveraging heterogeneous datasets is not well-justified by the experiments. The only “heterogeneity” tested is suboptimal teleoperation demonstrations from the same simulated domain (MuJoCo). (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of real-world validation:** All experiments are in simulation (4 tasks: Lift, Can, Square, PushT). No real-robot experiments, leaving open whether the method scales to real robotic setups. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Mixed task performance:** LDP outperforms baselines clearly only on Lift; on Can and Square it performs on par with DP+Repr; on PushT it performs significantly worse (poor absolute performance). This inconsistency weakens the results. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient experiments on action-free and suboptimal data:**
  - The quantity of action-free data used is not reported. (Reviewer 3)
  - No ablation varying the amount of action-free data to understand its impact. (Reviewer 3)
  - No experiment combining both suboptimal and action-free data. (Reviewer 3)
  - No baseline that uses IDM-labeled action-free data to train a standard diffusion policy. (Reviewer 3)
  - No evaluation of pretraining the planner on external action-free datasets (e.g., large-scale robot videos) to show scalability. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
  - The claim that LDP can learn from action-free data is unclear because the inverse dynamics model still requires action targets for training. (Reviewer 4)
- **Simplistic experimental setup:** The tasks have simple pushing and pick-place actions with uniform backgrounds. More complex scenarios (multi-step, cluttered scenes) could challenge the VAE latent representations. (Reviewer 2)
- **Single-view input only:** Experiments use only a single camera view. Many real and simulated tasks use multiple cameras (including in-hand), and it is unclear how LDP would scale to multiple views (e.g., requiring multiple planners, raising training and inference complexity). (Reviewer 2)
- **Suboptimal data generation is unrealistic:** The suboptimal data is generated by an under-trained behavior cloning agent. The paper does not specify what dataset that BC agent was trained on, and this method of generating suboptimal data does not resemble real-world suboptimal demonstrations. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing baseline comparisons and details:**
  - No BC-RNN baseline on the curated datasets. (Reviewer 1)
  - No comparison with other methods for imitation learning from suboptimal data [1-5] (Sasaki 2020, Kim 2022, Yu 2023, Xu 2022, Zhang 2023). (Reviewer 3)
  - Missing related works: “Imitating Human Behaviour with Diffusion Models” (Pearce et al., ICLR 2023) and “Goal Conditioned Imitation Learning using Score-based Diffusion Policies” (Reuss et al., RSS 2023) are not cited. (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  - Figure 3 is oversized and low-resolution, providing little intuition. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figures 1 and 2 are very similar; could be merged to free space for method details. (Reviewer 4)
  - Results tables lack clarity: the main text does not state the performance metric used nor report the number of seeds / rollouts; it is hard to judge statistical significance. (Reviewer 4)
  - The number of rollouts used to compute success rates is not stated. (Reviewer 1)
- **Architecture choices not justified:**
  - Why β-VAE is chosen; β can affect reconstructions and the method is typically used for disentangled representations. (Reviewer 2)
  - The VAE pretraining could be replaced by more powerful representation learning models. (Reviewer 2)
  - Unclear what latent horizon length and action horizon length are; when comparing to DiffusionPolicy, it is uncertain if the same action horizon is used. (Reviewer 2)
- **Counterintuitive results that need explanation:**
  - Why does LDP perform so poorly on PushT? (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
  - In Table 3, performance saturates or drops when suboptimal data increases from 1k to 2k; not clear if 0.5k data shows a trend. Why does training IDM on suboptimal data hurt performance on Can? (Reviewer 3)
  - On Table 1, UniPi+OL outperforms UniPi+CL in 2 of 4 tasks despite closed-loop control being typically better. (Reviewer 5)
- **Inference speed not reported:** The paper mentions speed drawbacks but does not provide inference times. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing experimental details:**
  - For the Lift task, the paper states “filtered 3 demonstrations out of 200 total”; unclear how many trajectories are used for each component (planner, IDM) and for other tasks (Can, Square). (Reviewer 5)
  - Why only 3 tasks from the Robomimic suite (coffee, tool hang, mug cleanup omitted). (Reviewer 1)
  - Is performance improved by having the IDM take the full predicted state latent rather than just the current and next latent? (Reviewer 2)
