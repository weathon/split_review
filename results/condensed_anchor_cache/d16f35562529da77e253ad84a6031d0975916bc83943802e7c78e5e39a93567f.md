## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TAGA, the first template-free, Gaussian-based method for reconstructing animatable avatars from monocular videos. It initializes Gaussians around each bone and stores skinning in a low-resolution voxel grid. To resolve ambiguities from forward mapping, it introduces an anomaly detection and backward mapping strategy (ambiguous Gaussian correction) using a GMM prior and EM optimization. Experiments on ZJU-MoCap and PeopleSnapshot show improved visual quality and drastically faster training (0.5 h vs. 30 h) and rendering (140 FPS vs. 0.25 FPS) compared to other template-free methods (NPC, HumanNeRF, TAVA). All five reviewers gave a score of 5 (confidence 3–5), sharing the view that while the method is novel as a template-free Gaussian avatar, the claimed advantages are largely inherited from 3DGS, the benefit of being template-free is not convincingly demonstrated, and the lack of experiments on loose clothing, animals, and in-the-wild scenarios limits the contribution.

### Strengths
- First template-free Gaussian-based animatable avatar (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- Self-supervised approach for simultaneously learning geometry and skinning using the canonical–observation correspondence (R2, R4, R5).
- Ambiguous Gaussian detection and backward-mapping correction effectively reduce geometric artifacts compared to other template-free methods (R1, R4, R5).
- Voxel-based skinning field yields smooth deformations and generalizes to unseen poses (R4).
- Clear and well-motivated writing; technical details are reasonable and easy to follow (R2, R4, R5).
- Significant speedups in training (60×) and rendering (560×) over existing template-free NeRF methods (R1, R3, R4, R5). *Note: multiple reviewers (R1, R3) attribute this speed primarily to the Gaussian representation itself, not the paper’s technical contributions.*

### Weaknesses
- **Missing video results**: No video demos provided; essential for evaluating temporal consistency, animation quality, and jitter (R1, R2). This is a standard expectation in the field.
- **Limited scope of experiments**: Results shown only on tight-clothed humans (ZJU-MoCap, PeopleSnapshot). The method is not validated on loose clothing, animals (e.g., pigs, dogs), or complex creatures, which would demonstrate the claimed template-free advantage (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). Both TAVA and GART showed such generalization.
- **Unclear benefit of template-free**: The paper does not clearly explain why template-free is critical (R3). Current template-based methods (e.g., GART, GoMAvatar) use off-the-shelf SMPL predictors (e.g., ReFit) that are efficient and accurate, so the manual annotation argument is weak (R5). The method still requires heavy handcrafted priors (GMM, bone initialization), which are not obviously superior to human templates (R5). Template-based Gaussian methods (e.g., GART) achieve better visual quality and comparable efficiency (R4).
- **Overclaimed speed advantage**: The reported speedup is attributed almost entirely to switching to Gaussian splatting, not to novel technical contributions (R1, R3, R4). Without comparisons to template-based Gaussian methods (GART, Animatable Gaussians, ExAvatar), it is unclear how much of the speed/quality gain (if any) is due to the template-free design (R2, R3, R4).
- **Missing comparisons with recent template-based Gaussian methods**: Should compare with Animatable Gaussians (CVPR 2024), ExAvatar (ECCV 2024), and GART (SMPL-based Gaussian method) (R3, R4). A discussion of trade-offs between template-free and template-based approaches when combined with Gaussian splatting is needed (R2).
- **Quality concerns**: 
  - In Fig. 5, HumanNeRF often appears sharper on faces and clothes, contradicting the quantitative claims. The paper needs a detailed analysis of where TAGA outperforms despite qualitative differences (R2).
  - Black pixels appear around avatars in TAGA and TAVA results (Fig. 5). This may be due to mask handling and harms visual quality; the cause and potential impact need addressing (R4).
  - Overall rendering quality is limited; comparisons with ground truth are missing, making it hard to judge identity preservation (R3).
- **Dataset limitations**: ZJU-MoCap images are low-quality and subjects rotate 360°, weakening the “monocular” claim (R3). The method should be tested on high-quality multiview datasets like ActorsHQ or THuman4.0 (R2). The paper does not validate on in-the-wild videos with predicted (and noisier) masks/poses (R5).
- **Method-specific concerns**:
  - The GMM+EM correction for ambiguous Gaussians appears ad-hoc and is not jointly optimized with model parameters (R3). An ablation study showing how the EM process improves detection and refinement over iterations is missing (R5).
  - The method is person-specific and still requires 30 minutes of training per instance (R3).
  - It is unclear whether the priors remain effective when body parts are close (e.g., arms stuck to torso in UBC-Fashion) or for challenging clothing like dresses (R5).
  - The paper does not support the claim that the EM-based refinement generalizes to in-the-wild scenes with less accurate inputs (R5).
- **Minor**: The advantage of being template-free is not clearly motivated; the paper would benefit from a more coherent explanation (R3).
