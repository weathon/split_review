## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes fine-tuning ControlNet models with Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) for human pose-conditioned image generation. It designs a reward function combining a VLM-based text alignment score and a pose similarity score (either feature similarity or keypoint cosine similarity). A 21K (human image, pose, caption) dataset is also collected. Experiments compare against ControlNet and HumanSD, showing OKS improvement but lower CLIP scores and inconsistent LPIPS.

### Strengths
- The topic (improving pose-conditioned human generation) is interesting and significant for downstream tasks (R1).
- The proposed method can generate images better aligned with input pose conditions, leading to improved OKS over the baseline (R2).
- The method is simple and easy to implement (R3); the paper is clearly described and easy to follow (R3, R4). (Note: R2 disagrees – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and ad-hoc construction**: Combining ControlNet and DDPO is not a contribution, as DDPO is standard and readily available (R1, R2, R3, R4). The only difference from the original DDPO is the optimized parameter group (R3). Most components are known (ControlNet architecture, DDPO training); the method simply adds a pose-alignment reward (R4). The motivation for why this combination mitigates pose misalignment is unclear (R4). The “novel” reward function is unexplained in the introduction (R4).
- **Poor presentation and incomplete writing**: The paper lacks proper structure, contains numerous typographical and logical errors, and shows signs of LLM involvement (R1). It is written in a rushed manner and is hard to follow in parts (R2). There are no rigorous mathematical definitions for the reward functions (R2). The use of “Model 1, Model 2, Model 3, Model 4” forces constant referral to Table 2, harming readability (R2). Important details like reward function weighting are missing (R2). Figures 1 and 2 are nearly identical and should be reorganized (R3).
- **Weak or incomplete experimental evaluation**:
    - OKS is a weak metric for pose estimation; AP and AR would be more appropriate (R1). Using LPIPS to assess controlled generation is unusual because generation is highly diverse (R1).
    - The proposed method yields lower CLIP scores and cannot consistently improve LPIPS (R2). In Table 3, the method is only better than ControlNet on half of the six metrics, which is not convincing (R3).
    - Comparisons in Table 4 are meaningless because different models use different condition types (R2). The authors should ensure apple-to-apple comparisons (R2).
    - Since the model is fine-tuned on extra data, a simple baseline of fine-tuning the pretrained ControlNet directly on the same training data is missing (R3).
    - No ablation study is performed to isolate the effect of the pose-based reward function, e.g., removing it to see if ControlNet alone would improve (R4). Also, the authors should combine all three reward terms (A+B+C) with proper weighting and ablate the weights (R2).
    - User studies are recommended because objective metrics do not always align with human perception (R4). Visualizations of failure cases should be provided (R4); Model 2 in Figure 9 still shows unsatisfactory results (R3).
- **Unreasonable keypoint similarity metric**: Using cosine similarity for keypoint vectors is inferior to existing metrics like OKS (R2).
- **Missing related work and baselines**: The paper should compare with Text2Human (SIGGRAPH 2022) and HyperHuman (ICLR 2024) (R3, R4). The authors should also provide results on the known DeepFashion-MultiModal benchmark (~40K triplets) (R4). The dataset release question is also open (R4).
