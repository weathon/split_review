## Merged Review

### Summary
This work introduces novel SDEs (weak approximation order 1) for SignSGD, AdamW, and RMSpropW. For SignSGD, the SDE reveals three distinct dynamic phases and an inverse relationship between noise and convergence rate, contrasting with SGD’s quadratic effect. The AdamW/RMSpropW SDEs are derived under much less restrictive assumptions than prior work, yielding a batch size scaling rule and analysis of stationary distribution and loss in convex quadratic settings. Decoupled weight decay is shown to stabilize dynamics at high noise. The SDEs are validated via Euler–Maruyama numerical integration across MLPs, CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers on CIFAR, MNIST, and Shakespeare.

### Strengths
- Intriguing insights: three distinct phases in SignSGD dynamics and the inverse noise–convergence relationship offer valuable perspectives on optimizer behavior.
- Practical implications: the batch size scaling rule and stationary distribution analysis have direct relevance for training practices.
- A rigorous mathematical framework for SignSGD and AdamW, with noise–convergence characterization and decoupled weight decay analysis.
- Technical assumptions are much less restrictive than in other works.
- Theoretical findings are supported by extensive numerical verification using Euler–Maruyama integrators on a diverse set of architectures.
- Results are presented very clearly (Reviewer 4) and the background section in the appendix enhances readability.
- Rich set of results that may be insightful for practitioners; theoretical results are backed by experiments on real networks.
- Strong validation across multiple architectures (MLPs, CNNs, ResNets, Transformers).

### Weaknesses
- **Toy model is overly simplistic:** The analysis relies on a quadratic function \(f(x)=x^THx\) with additive Gaussian noise, which lacks the complexities of real-world loss landscapes and noise that depends on parameters and time. This limits generalizability. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
- **Noise design is artificial and unrealistic:** The additive noise \(\gamma\) is time-independent and identically distributed, not capturing practical mini-batch noise that varies with the loss and parameters. The experiments use full-batch gradients plus synthetic Gaussian (or heavy-tailed) noise, which does not reflect typical usage of adaptive optimizers. (Reviewers 2, 4)  
- **Unclear terminology and notation:**  
  - What does \(\mathbb{P}\) represent in Theorem 3.2? How is the error function defined in Corollary 3.3? Does Lemma 3.7 explicitly establish the stationary distribution of SignSGD? (Reviewer 1)  
  - The notation \(\nabla f_\gamma\) is ambiguous (could be misinterpreted as \(\nabla(f_\gamma)\)). (Reviewer 2)  
  - The condition \(|Y_t| > 3/2\) in Lemma 3.4 (line 199) is unclear: is it element-wise (absolute value of each coordinate) or the norm? (Reviewer 4)  
  - In lines 245–246, “signal” and “noise” are not properly defined; the claim “SNR is large, meaning SignSGD behaves like SignGD” is unsupported and needs explicit reasoning/equations. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Lack of intuition and poor structure:**  
  - The paper is overly dense, with little intuition provided for theorems and proofs, and heavy reliance on in-line equations (Reviewer 2).  
  - Section 3.1.1 introduces notation without adequate definitions before lemmas; more introductory material on SDEs and their relevance to optimizers is needed. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Figure 2 lacks a description of the loss function/landscape, dimensionality, initialization, and parameter ranges. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The paper is hard to follow; intuitive explanations should precede each lemma to highlight significance and takeaways. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Decoupled weight decay analysis requires clarification:** Why is decoupled weight decay considered key in this context? The authors’ analysis does not explicitly show how it stabilizes dynamics at high noise; specific equations or results demonstrating this effect are missing. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Practical implications remain underexplored:** The findings do not clearly indicate how the studied optimizers can be improved or tuned. (Reviewer 4)  
- **Missing key experimental results in the main paper:** Important figures/tables should be in the main text rather than the appendix to allow assessment of empirical validity. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Generalization to mini-batch linear regression is needed:** The analysis should be extended to a linear regression model with random Gaussian design (cf. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15074) to increase realism. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Validity restricted to convex quadratics?** It is unclear whether the analysis holds beyond convex quadratics. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Contrast in presentation quality:** Reviewer 4 found the presentation clear (Presentation 4), while Reviewer 3 rated it very poor (Presentation 1, Soundness 2), citing lack of structure and intuition. Reviewer 2 noted the paper is generally well-written but too dense.
