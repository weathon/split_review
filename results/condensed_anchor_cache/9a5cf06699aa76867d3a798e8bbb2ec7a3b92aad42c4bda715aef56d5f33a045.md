- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes an early fusion architecture (FAVEN) for audio-visual embodied navigation using learnable fusion tokens with cross-attention and a Mamba-based fusion block. Experiments on Matterport3D and Replica datasets report significant improvements over prior methods (e.g., 88.8% search time reduction, +10.4 SPL on heard sounds). Real-world testing in a single apartment is also presented. Reviewers are split: two find the idea well-motivated with strong results and good ablation studies (ratings 5), while two criticize poor presentation, shallow experiments, weak novelty, and overclaimed real-world evidence (ratings 3 and 1).

### Strengths

- **Idea and motivation**: Early fusion of audio and visual features is well-motivated and, to our knowledge, the first such approach for this task. The use of learnable fusion tokens (LFT) with cross-attention is a simple but effective design. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Empirical results on benchmarks**: FAVEN achieves new state-of-the-art metrics on Matterport3D and Replica datasets, with substantial margins over prior SoTA (e.g., 93.6% search time reduction, +10.4 SPL on heard sounds, +6.5 on unheard sounds in Table 1). (Reviewers 1, 3, 2)
- **Ablation studies**: Ablations in Tables 3–4 justify design choices (e.g., number of fusion tokens, Mamba vs. softmax). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Real-world experiment**: Conducting a real-world test is a bonus; it demonstrates the system can run on actual hardware (though limited in scope). (Reviewer 3)
- **Potential impact**: The method is conceptually simple and likely to influence future work on multimodal fusion for embodied AI. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and clarity**:
  - The information flow through BLKs (unimodal transformers) and MM-BLKs (multimodal fusion blocks) is very confusing. Equations 1–5 and Figures 2–3 are not clearly connected; the ordering of operations and inputs/outputs are ambiguous. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - Symbols are inconsistent and poorly defined (e.g., $x_i^{av}$ vs. $f_i$, $n_{av}$, $n_a$, $n_v$, $n_f$ in Section 3 and Table 4). (Reviewer 3)
  - Figure 2 does not show the Mamba block; it is unclear where and how Mamba is applied to multimodal tokens. (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - The paper is hasty and repetitive (e.g., similar descriptions at L137-140, L172-175, L178-190, L263-269). Several reviewers struggled to follow the architecture without extensive guessing. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
  - The structure of the paper is unclear; real-world experiments (Section 3.5) appear before the main simulation experiments (Section 4). (Reviewer 4)

- **Insufficient experiments and analysis**:
  - No error bars or standard deviations are reported in any table (Tables 1–4). Training via RL is noisy; multiple seeds are required to assess significance. (Reviewer 1)
  - The paper focuses on early fusion, but lacks a strict comparison to mid-level or late fusion under the same pipeline. An ablation comparing LFT with conventional early, late, and cross-attention fusion is missing. (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - No comparison to other multimodal fusion methods from the VLM literature (BLIP, Unified IO, Flamingo, Chameleon, etc.) is provided, even qualitatively. (Reviewer 1)
  - The novelty contribution is weak: essentially only replacing the audio/RGB encoders (CNN → Transformer) and adding a Mamba-based fusion block, with no innovation in map construction, waypoint prediction, or navigation decision-making. (Reviewer 4)
  - The learnable fusion tokens closely resemble [REG] tokens from “Vision Transformers Need Registers”; the difference is not clarified. (Reviewer 1)
  - No comparison of model parameters is given for Table 1. (Reviewer 2)
  - Ablations are only on Replica; experiments on Matterport3D are missing for ablation tables. (Reviewer 4)
  - The paper claims a 88.8% search time reduction, but it is unclear whether this comes from fewer navigation steps or faster inference per step. The architectural/inference differences from prior works and the hardware used for speed comparisons are not provided. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)

- **Mamba-specific issues**:
  - The Mamba block is a key claimed contribution, but no details of its SSM configuration, how it processes multimodal non-causal visual/audio tokens, or why it is beneficial are given. (Reviewer 3)
  - Input sequence length is only 392 tokens, far below the typical range where Mamba shows advantages (e.g., 100k+ tokens). (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The speed comparison in Table 3 likely uses a raw PyTorch softmax attention without CUDA optimizations (e.g., FlashAttention-2), making the comparison unfair, as Mamba uses hardware-optimized kernels. (Reviewer 3)
  - It is surprising that Mamba achieves better results than standard cross-attention; no explanation is offered. (Reviewer 3)

- **Real-world experiments**:
  - The real-world test is very limited: one sound source, one environment, one episode. No metrics (Success, SPL, SNA) are reported for any method, and there is no comparison to baselines. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
  - Claims derived from this experiment (e.g., “reduce search time”) are overclaimed. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - No details are provided about the robot platform, sensors (RGB-D camera model, microphone array configuration, sampling rate, image resolution), or how the episode was set up (initial/target positions, distance threshold). The supplementary video appears to be captured by a person with a phone, not a robot camera. (Reviewer 4)

- **Missing related work comparisons**:
  - Section 3.1 (“Revisit Audio Visual Navigation”) lacks supporting references for the claims in lines 178–182. (Reviewer 2)
  - The LFT approach is conceptually similar to cross-modal prompts; comparison with recent works (Duan et al., NeurIPS 2024; Zhai et al., AAAI 2024) is needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - No qualitative visualization of token activations is provided to show what the learnable fusion tokens aggregate. (Reviewer 2)

- **Model training and loss unclear**:
  - The loss function and training procedure (e.g., RL details) are not specified. (Reviewer 1)
  - The role of visual encoders, GRU, linear layer, Action and Critic modules is not explained. (Reviewer 3)
  - Depth features are used as input but are not included in the feature fusion; no justification is given. (Reviewer 3)

- **Specific reviewer questions that serve as weaknesses**:
  - (Reviewer 3, Q1) What is the difference between $x_i^{av}$ and $f_i$?
  - (Reviewer 3, Q2) Are $x_j^{a,f}$ and $x_j^{v,f}$ simply concatenations?
  - (Reviewer 3, Q5) Where are the “unimodal self-attention transformers” in Figure 1/2?
  - (Reviewer 3, Q6) The fusion token path in Figures 1 and 2 is misleading; no arrows indicate that audio/visual tokens are passed to MM-BLKs.
  - (Reviewer 4, Q2) Why only one real-world episode? Multiple environments should be tested.
  - (Reviewer 4, Q4) Define “search time” and explain the 88.8% calculation.
  - (Reviewer 4, Q5) Ablation experiments are missing on Matterport3D and real-world settings; Table 3 does not specify heard/unheard condition for search time.

- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (1 and 2) found the paper well-written and the method clearly introduced, while the other two (3 and 4) found the presentation very unclear and the experiments insufficient. The positive reviewers still noted limitations in clarity and missing comparisons but rated the contribution higher. The negative reviewers considered the novelty weak and the real-world claims misleading. This split should be preserved when interpreting the overall assessment.
