## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Heavy Ball (HB) momentum and a higher-order variant Generalized Heavy Ball (GHVB) to expand the stability regions of existing diffusion ODE solvers (e.g., DPM++, PLMS), thereby mitigating divergence artifacts at low sampling steps. Experiments on pixel-based and latent diffusion models show reduced artifacts and improved image quality. The paper is well-organized and provides theoretical analysis linking artifacts to stability regions.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical insight**: The paper identifies the small stability regions of high-order ODE solvers as a principal cause of divergence artifacts and provides visualization and theoretical analysis to support this claim (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- **Training-free extensions**: HB and GHVB are training-free modifications applicable to existing solvers like DPM++ and PLMS. Code is provided (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- **Solid and extensive experiments**: Experiments cover both pixel-based and latent-based diffusion models. The methods are effective in reducing artifacts and improving image quality at low sampling steps (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- **Presentation**: The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, reviews basics of numerical ODE stability, and references up-to-date literature on high-order solvers (Reviewer 1, 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: Reviewer 3 found that the main techniques are directly borrowed from Polyak’s heavy ball momentum and built upon DPM++ and PLMS, making the novelty not significant. Other reviewers (1, 2, 4) considered the contribution solid or good, indicating disagreement.
- **Unclear guidance between HB and GHVB**: The paper lacks a clear comparison and guidance on when to use HB versus GHVB, including trade-offs in computational cost, memory usage, and convergence speed. The distinction in their algorithmic formulations (moving average after vs. before summing high-order coefficients) is insufficiently explained (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Insufficient demonstration of acceleration**: The stated motivation is diffusion sampling acceleration, but experiments are limited to very low step counts (e.g., 10 steps). It is unclear whether the methods provide benefits at higher step counts or overall acceleration beyond few-step sampling. Reviewer 2 questioned the practical improvement on sampling steps for decent image quality.
- **Lack of extreme step evaluation**: The paper does not evaluate performance for extremely small generation steps (e.g., 1 or 2 steps) or compare with methods like the consistency model that are designed for such scenarios (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing relevant metrics**: The paper does not report metrics such as HFEN and SSIM (suggested by Reviewer 1) or L2, LPIPS, and FID comparisons to DDIM at 1000-step benchmark (Reviewer 3), which could further substantiate artifact reduction claims.
- **Inconsistency in GHVB**: The behavior of GHVB 2.5 and 3.5 in Figure 12 is attributed to estimated error without further justification; more discussion is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Guidance scale interaction**: The paper does not systematically explore how the guidance scale 's' interacts with HB/GHVB in conditional generation, which is important for practical applications (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient motivation**: The paper should better clarify why PLMS and DPM-Solver++ perform worse than originally claimed and what specific challenges are addressed beyond prior work (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity of presentation**: The paper should clarify what "sampling" means in the diffusion context (e.g., in the appendix) and include a table listing all numerical formats used. The training paradigm (loss function) should be detailed. The conceptual difference between classifier-guidance and text-prompt generation should be explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Alternative explanations**: The paper does not discuss whether divergence artifacts could be mitigated by improving the dynamic range of pixel values (Reviewer 2).
- **Complexity analysis**: The complexity and cost of the proposed methods, especially with potential additional training, are not addressed (Reviewer 2).
- **Disagreement on acceleration**: Reviewer 2 expressed concern that the proposed methods show superior performance only in extremely low sampling steps and may not provide clear improvement on sampling step count for decent image quality, while other reviewers did not raise this issue.
