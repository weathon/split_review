## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CFG++, which reformulates classifier-free guidance as a manifold-constrained problem using score distillation sampling (SDS) loss to address off-manifold issues, with the goal of improving sample quality, invertibility, mode collapse, and guidance scale interpretability. The method is evaluated on text-to-image generation, DDIM inversion, editing, and text-conditioned inverse problems. Reviewers are split: two rated 6 (confidence 3–4), one rated 8 (confidence 5), one rated 1 (confidence 5). The highest-rated reviewer (R3) finds no obvious weaknesses and praises the derivation, analyses, and broad applicability. The lowest-rated reviewer (R4) acknowledges originality but raises fundamental concerns about experimental scope, theoretical rigor, scalability, and missing comparisons. All reviewers note the paper is well-written and the method is simple, but concerns exist about marginal gains on some benchmarks, formulation discrepancies, lack of evaluation of key claims, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- Well-written with coherent logic, clear notation, and a helpful high-level preliminary section (R1, R2, R3, R4). Figures and diagrams effectively illustrate key points (R4).
- Simple yet well-justified method: modifies the renoising process of CFG, with derivation from an inverse problem and SDS loss considered intriguing (R1, R3). The derivation for DDIM (and extensions to EDM and DPM-Solver) establishes CFG++ as a general method (R3).
- Extensive experiments covering multiple tasks: text-to-image with SDv1.5, SDXL, and distilled versions; DDIM inversion and editing; PF-ODE trajectories; and text-conditioned inverse problems (R2, R3). Quantitative and qualitative results are provided (R3).
- Analysis from multiple perspectives: manifold geometry, score matching loss throughout denoising, and posterior mean evolution (R3).
- High originality in reinterpreting CFG limitations as off-manifold behavior rather than inherent diffusion model issues (R1, R4). R4 highlights the creative synthesis of ideas and clear articulation of arguments.
- All reviewers agree on clarity of presentation, though R4 notes the paper is well-organized and technical jargon is well-defined.

### Weaknesses
- **Minor presentation issues**: Typos (line 228: $x_c$ not introduced; line 238: “renoising” typo) (R1). Incorrect arrow direction for CLIP score in Table 1 (R1, R2). Missing ImageReward metric for SD v1.5 in Table 2 (R2).
- **Formulation discrepancy**: The CFG formulation used differs from the original (Ho and Salimans, 2021) eq (6) (R1). The $w=0$ setting in experiments should be equivalent to no guidance per CFG++, yet Fig. 6 still shows edited images at $w=0$, contradicting expectations (R1).
- **Gains are marginal on key metrics**: FID and CLIP scores very similar between CFG and CFG++, especially with SD v1.5; gains small even in low NFE setting (R1). In Table 3 (inverse problems), CFG++ improves FID but not LPIPS; no average performance across corruptions is given, and R1 questions justification for the LPIPS discrepancy.
- **Invertibility and diversity claims not evaluated**: The statements that CFG++ is invertible and increases diversity are not empirically validated (R1). No comparison with methods that explicitly address DDIM inversion (e.g., [1]) to benchmark reconstruction quality and editability (R2).
- **Missing comparisons to broader SOTA**: Lacks direct comparison with recent advancements in guided diffusion (Stable Diffusion, etc.) using standardized metrics (R4). Only compared to traditional CFG; no benchmarking against other inversion-focused methods (R2).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Evaluation focuses on specific datasets/tasks; should include broader range (artistic style transfer, super-resolution) and diverse domains (video synthesis) to demonstrate versatility (R4). Only diffusion/score-matching models are discussed; no derivation for flow matching (SD3, FLUX) (R3).
- **Theoretical depth could be strengthened**: Concept of manifold constraints lacks rigorous mathematical framework; more detailed derivations showing benefits of operating within constraints would help (R4). CFG++ derivations for other solvers (Appendix A) are intuitive rather than derived from inverse problems and SDS loss as done for DDIM (R3). One reviewer (R3) also notes that CFG++ can be rewritten as a reweighted CFG with timestep-varying guidance, suggesting the claim that previous timestep-varying guidance works are “orthogonal” may be incorrect because CFG++ designs a different trajectory.
- **Scalability and computational efficiency not addressed**: No discussion or experiments on performance with larger models or datasets, nor any analysis of computational overhead (R4).
- **Lack of user-centric evaluation**: No user studies to assess perceived quality or usefulness of generated images (R4).
- **Questions about specific settings**: SDXL-Turbo does not use guidance_scale; validity of CFG/CFG++ in that case is unclear (R2). Whether CFG++ works for negative prompts is not discussed (R2).
- **Wording in abstract may overstate novelty**: “Contrary to widespread belief that these are inherent limitations…” could be reworded, as current work already recognizes CFG problems (R2).
- **Significant reviewer disagreement**: While R3 (rating 8) found no obvious weaknesses and R1/R2 (rating 6) found manageable issues, R4 (rating 1) raised fundamental concerns about experimental scope, theoretical rigor, scalability, and user evaluation, indicating a stark split in assessment of the paper’s contributions and validation.
