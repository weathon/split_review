## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the generalization of graph neural networks (GNNs) in graph classification, focusing on the relationship between expressivity and generalization. The main contribution is a framework that derives a \(k\)-variance margin-based generalization bound through an optimal transport (Wasserstein distance) perspective, bridging continuous GNN embeddings to discrete graph embeddings with higher separation power (e.g., 1-WL, homomorphism vectors). The bound involves intra-class concentration and inter-class separation trade-offs. The authors provide a concrete case study, empirical bounds (sharper than VC bounds on several real-world datasets), and experiments that generally support the theory. The work extends previous results (Chuang et al. 2021) to graph data and offers a new perspective on how expressivity can enhance generalization.

### Strengths
- The problem is pivotal and important to the GNN community (reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The theoretical results are sound and represent non-trivial progress (reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The proposed framework is empirically effective: the bound is given by the regularity and expressivity of the graph encoder itself, which are accessible in practice, offering potential to assess generalization of real-world models (reviewer 1).
- The experimental bounds are significantly sharper than VC bounds (reviewer 3).
- A concrete case study demonstrates how improved expressivity can benefit or harm generalization, which is interesting and potentially useful (reviewer 2).
- While one reviewer found the presentation clean and easy to follow (reviewer 1), others noted readability issues (see Weaknesses); the minority positive view on clarity is acknowledged.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited architectural scope.** The verification and discussion are restricted to MPNNs. The paper does not delve into how higher-order information (e.g., k-WL, attention mechanisms) quantitatively affect the B/S constants in the bound, how they impact separation of graph embeddings, or whether increased expressivity translates into a tighter bound or is offset by other factors. A reviewer questions whether an a priori analysis of how a specific aggregation scheme (e.g., k-WL, attention) influences the bound terms is possible (reviewer 1).
- **Readability and intuition.** Section 3 is full of equations and many terminologies (e.g., homomorphism, graph invariant) introduced without examples or figures, making it difficult to follow (reviewer 4). The insights behind the bound are not clearly explained: it is not intuitive why generalization error can be bounded by a discrete classifier with better separation power, or what role the Wasserstein distance and separation power play in the bound (reviewer 2). The paper would benefit from illustrative examples (reviewer 3).
- **Tractability and tightness of the bound.** The bound is sophisticated, intractable to compute, and potentially loose, making it hard to conclude that this upper bound reflects the real generalization gap (reviewer 2). The gaps in Table 2 appear nearly constant for several datasets (PROTEINS, SIDER, MUTAG), the VC bound is smaller than the proposed bound in PROTEINS and MUTAG, and the proposed bound does not clearly simulate trends in accuracy or loss gap; this raises concerns about practical utility (reviewer 4).
- **Lipschitz constant estimation.** The Lipschitz constants of graph encoders are needed but may be difficult to estimate for complex architectures such as graph transformers; the paper lacks a clear methodology for practically bounding or estimating these constants, limiting applicability (reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparison with other bounds.** The experiments should also compare with PAC-Bayesian bounds (e.g., Ju et al. 2023) rather than only VC bounds (reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete proofs.** Proofs of Corollary 4.5 and Proposition 5.2 are missing (reviewer 4).
- **Optimization not considered.** The derived bounds are static and do not account for training dynamics (optimizers, learning rate schedules) that can affect generalization; this could lead to overly pessimistic or optimistic estimates (reviewer 3).
- **Limited practical insights.** Section 6 shows that different graphs can lead to opposite effects on inter-class separation, but it does not provide concrete guidance on what to do in practice, and it seems irrelevant to intra-class concentration (reviewer 4). The experimental presentation is also questioned: why not show both loss gap and bound per dataset in Figure 2(a), and both \(W_1\) and \(\text{Lip}(f)\) per dataset in Figure 2(b)? (reviewer 4). Minor issues: line 482 likely refers to "Figure 2(b)" not "Figure 2(a)"; Figure 2 should be resized (reviewer 4).
