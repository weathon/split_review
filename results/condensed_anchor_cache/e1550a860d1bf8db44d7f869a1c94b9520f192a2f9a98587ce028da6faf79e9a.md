## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes continuity-preserving autoencoders (CpAEs) for learning continuous latent dynamical models from discrete image frames. It addresses the problem that standard convolutional autoencoders can produce latent states that are discontinuous in time. The key idea is to enforce Lipschitz continuity in the convolutional filters, supported by a mathematical formulation (Theorem 3.1) that gives sufficient conditions for continuous latent state evolution. A regularizer promotes filter continuity. Experiments across several dynamical systems show improved performance over baselines. The paper is well organized and presents an interesting theoretically grounded approach, though concerns exist about the generality of the assumptions, the strength of the experimental validation, and the lack of ablation studies.

### Strengths

- The paper identifies an important problem: standard autoencoders struggle to learn continuous latent states from discrete image observations of a dynamical system.
- It provides a rigorous mathematical formulation and theoretical analysis, including a key theorem (Theorem 3.1) that establishes conditions for continuous latent state evolution.
- The proposed CpAE architecture and the regularizer to promote filter Lipschitz continuity are novel and theoretically grounded.
- Empirical results demonstrate effectiveness across multiple datasets (damped pendulum, swing stick, two-body interaction) with clear improvements over baseline methods (e.g., standard CNNs and fully-connected networks).
- The theoretical framework could be useful for the community to build on.
- The paper is well organized and well written.

### Weaknesses

- The theoretical analysis is limited to rigid body motion in 2D (simple transformations like translation and rotation) and does not fully address non-rigid motion or more complex deformations. The assumptions (Assumption 3.1 about vanishing feature maps near image boundaries) may be too restrictive for real-world images, requiring significant zero padding that could lose information. The impact of this assumption on generalization is not thoroughly investigated. (Reviewer 1)
- The analysis requires large filter sizes (O(1/δ)), which could limit practical applications due to increased memory and computational time. The practical implications are not fully addressed. (Reviewer 1)
- There is a significant performance drop on the swing stick task (57.4% VPT vs 99.2% for damped pendulum), which is not thoroughly analyzed. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of factors such as data quality, dynamic complexity, and noise sensitivity. (Reviewer 1)
- The experiments attempting to demonstrate the importance of δ-continuity (e.g., Figure 6) are unconvincing: reconstructive ability is not clearly an appropriate indicator of latent continuity; using a fully-connected architecture as a baseline is questionable since it is known to be insufficient for complex image reconstruction (line 346); the conventional CNN baseline has comparable reconstructive ability to CpAE. A more appropriate experiment (e.g., Figure 10 paired with a quantitative assessment) would be more convincing. An additional experiment showing that a fixed dynamical model models CpAE latent states better than AE latent states would strengthen the paper. (Reviewer 2)
- There is a lack of comparison with autoencoders that are not based on standard CNNs (e.g., VAEs, Vision Transformers). The method is limited to CNNs. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- Ablation studies are missing on key components: (a) the effect of filter size; (b) the effect of the regularizer parameters λ_J and λ_R; (c) the contribution of the latent similarity loss (line 399); (d) how using smaller weights in initial layers impacts performance; (e) the impact of removing the VPNet regularizer. Without these, it is unclear whether improvements come from the CpAE architecture, larger filters, or the regularizer. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The mathematical formulation in Section 3.1 (projecting states to pixels) is key but difficult to follow. It would benefit from a simple running example (e.g., a single particle). Some notation is not introduced in the main text: Ω (Section 3.1), K (Section 3.2), λ_J (Section 3.5), λ and λ_R (Section 4). (Reviewer 4)
- The impact of the proposed method on more realistic tasks (e.g., with complex architectures like VAEs or ViTs) is unclear. Applying CpAEs to more complex architectures could improve performance on challenging experiments like the swing stick. (Reviewer 4)
- Several specific missing results: no image or quantitative results for the two-body data in Figure 6; no explanation of how filter sizes were determined for Table 1. (Reviewer 3)
- It is unclear why the authors compare to HNN architectures if the goal is to showcase the impact of the CpAE structure. (Reviewer 2)
- Reviewers disagree on the strength of the empirical results: most (score 6) find them good but with major gaps; one reviewer (score 8) finds them convincingly improved. Reviewer 2 questions whether the reconstructive ability metric is appropriate, while Reviewer 3 notes a lack of quantitative results for two-body data.
