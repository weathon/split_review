## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates scaling laws for LLM fine-tuning (full-model tuning, prompt tuning, and LoRA) on bilingual machine translation (WMT14 En-De, En-Zh) and multilingual summarization. The authors propose a multiplicative joint scaling law that incorporates LLM model size (1B–16B), pretraining data size, finetuning data size, and PET parameter size. Key findings: a power-law multiplicative fit works better than additive; LLM model scaling helps more than pretraining data scaling; PET parameter scaling is generally ineffective; the optimal tuning method is highly task- and data-dependent. The paper is primarily empirical, aiming to identify trends that could transfer to other domains.

### Strengths
- Extends scaling law investigations to fine-tuning, providing practical value for practitioners.
- Well-motivated and straightforward experimental design; authors clearly state where patterns hold and where they do not.
- Rich findings with thorough interpretation across many experiments.  
- Figures are clear and well-organized; excellent references to existing scaling law work.
- Correctly calibrates uncertainty and explains expected results (e.g., full-model tuning for abundant data, PET for limited data) with appropriate evidence.
- Insights about parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT) and its relationship with scaling axes are particularly useful for the community.
- Excellent analysis of scaling along different axes (pretraining data, model size, finetuning data).

### Weaknesses
- The multiplicative scaling law is strictly more expressive than the additive one tested; it is unclear why this comparison is surprising, and why even more expressive forms (e.g., with offsets, logarithmic terms) were not examined or controlled for complexity. (R1, R4)
- Table 2 reports only additive vs. multiplicative fits for one task (WMT14 En-De) and one metric (perplexity). Counterparts for other tasks and metrics are missing—the paper claims BLEURT–RougeL has “high correlation” with PPL, but Figure 7 in the appendix visually does not strongly support this claim; a quantitative correlation measure is needed. (R1)
- The practical guidance from the scaling law appears familiar (perplexity decreases for all methods; best method varies with data size). The paper does not demonstrate that the fitted law provides new, specific guidance beyond this general trend. (R1)
- For the 16B models, the multiplicative scaling law shows significant misfit for PET methods. The root cause is not investigated—whether the functional form breaks down at larger sizes, or whether including 16B in the fit changes conclusions. Alternative forms (power laws with offsets, logarithmic terms) were not explored. (R3, R4)
- The experiments in Figures 3 and 4 are limited to a 1B model; trends at larger model sizes are missing. It is unclear which model sizes are used in Figures 2–4 in the first place. (R2)
- Figure 4 does not extend finetuning data size to the same range as Figure 3. Without comparing PET on larger datasets, it is unclear whether PET methods plateau or continue improving relative to full-model tuning. (R4)
- Figure 5 (“critical point”) is ambiguous: it is not clear whether the plotted coordinates represent a performance crossover or a more nuanced relationship, nor how to interpret the axes. The practical implications for choosing between methods are not well explained. (R2, R4)
- Technical and writing errors: “propmt” in Figure 2 (correct: “prompt”); “infers” (should be “implies”); “there exits a critical point”; “pretty week” (should be “weak”). (R2, R4)
- The evaluation only covers machine translation and summarization. The representativeness for broader fine-tuning applications (e.g., classification, reasoning) is unclear; including more tasks or low-resource languages would strengthen the paper. (R2, R3)
- Training details are missing: the models are decoder-only; how was decoder-only training performed for translation? What prompts were used? Would scaling laws differ for an encoder-decoder architecture? (R3)
- Zero-shot evaluation on summarization (Figure 7) requires explanation: if the source language is unseen, how does the model achieve nontrivial performance? (R4)

*Note:* Reviewer scores ranged from 5 to 8. Reviewer 3 (score 5) raised major concerns about the misfit for 16B models and missing training details, while Reviewer 1 (score 6, raised after rebuttal) questioned the novelty and expressivity of the law. Conversely, Reviewers 2 and 4 (scores 8) found the experiments well-motivated, the analysis thorough, and the uncertainty well-calibrated.
