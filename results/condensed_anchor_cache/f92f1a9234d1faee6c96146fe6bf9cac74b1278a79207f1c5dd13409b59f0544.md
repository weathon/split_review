## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes IBGS, which injects an inductive bias into 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by computing normals from the distribution of neighboring densities rather than from independently trainable Gaussian covariances. The method parameterizes covariances using principal axes and variances derived from neighboring Gaussians, and applies regularization to ensure local surface smoothness. The approach is validated on multiple datasets and achieves competitive surface reconstruction among 3DGS-based methods.

### Strengths

- The core idea is novel and well-motivated. Using local neighbors to compute the orientation of Gaussians is a key distinction from previous 3DGS methods that optimize each Gaussian independently. It is an insightful link to traditional surface reconstruction, where neighborhood point distribution matters more than 2D rendering loss (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The method achieves state-of-the-art geometry reconstruction among 3DGS-based methods on several datasets (e.g., DTU), while maintaining faster training than implicit neural representation-based methods. Quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate improved surfaces with fewer holes compared to prior 3DGS works (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The sparsity-aware adaptive density control strategy is interesting and useful (Reviewer 2). The paper also introduces several regularization methods based on the proposed normal computation, forming a comprehensive workflow (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and provides sufficient implementation details for reproducibility (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Experimental validation includes both quantitative and qualitative comparisons as well as ablation studies of the regularization losses (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- The improvement over the closest 3DGS-based baseline (RaDe-GS) is marginal: only 0.02 improvement in Chamfer Distance on the DTU dataset, while the training time increases substantially (5–6× slower than RaDe-GS/original 3DGS). This sacrifices the key advantage of fast training in 3DGS for a small geometry gain (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The definition of the normal in Section 3.1 may be view-dependent because it uses the intersection point and the Gaussian center to determine the normal plane. This contradicts the paper’s argument that normals should be derived strictly from the density distribution and covariance of local neighbors. This fundamental issue is not addressed, and the reviewer asks whether the impact is negligible in practice (Reviewer 4).
- The sensitivity of the kNN parameter k (set to 5) is not sufficiently explored. There is no discussion of how k was chosen or whether it is optimal across scenes with different scales and geometric complexity. An inappropriate k could cause over-smoothing or under-smoothing. Additionally, constructing local covariance using KNN is problematic because Gaussians are anisotropic 3D signals (not zero-dimensional points) and their actual density extents should be considered when selecting neighbors (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Several regularization and splitting techniques appear similar to prior work: the minimization of the smallest eigenvalue λ₃ (Eq. 8) resembles NeuSG’s minimization of Gaussian scales; the sparsity-aware adaptive density control (Sec. 3.4) resembles VCR-GauS’s splitting in textureless regions. The paper does not provide detailed explanation or ablation studies to clarify the differences and contributions of these components relative to existing methods (Reviewer 1).
- No failure cases or limitations of the proposed approach are discussed. Reviewers explicitly ask for remaining failure cases, especially regarding scenes with complex geometry or varying surface complexity (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The paper lacks analysis of which part of the framework causes the lower training efficiency (e.g., KNN computation overhead, additional regularization, or number of primitives). The number of primitives used is not reported (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Missing details on mesh extraction: it is unclear how depth is integrated into the TSDF volume and whether the comparison methods use the same mesh extraction pipeline. The impact of this choice on reconstruction quality is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
