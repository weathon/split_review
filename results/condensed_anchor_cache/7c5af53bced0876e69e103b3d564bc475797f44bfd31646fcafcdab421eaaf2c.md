## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CADA (Continuously trAnsportable Domain Adaptation) to handle continuously shifting spurious features in domain adaptation. It combines causal transportability (inspired by VOOD) with continuous index adversarial alignment (inspired by CIDA). Three reviewers rate the paper 3 (weak reject); one reviewer rates it 6 (weak accept). The majority concerns are limited novelty (combination of existing methods), unclear definition of spurious features, missing experimental rigor, and insufficient causal grounding. The minority reviewer finds the problem important and the method well-written with strong empirical results.

### Strengths
1. **Interesting problem**: The paper identifies the important and underexplored challenge of continuously shifting spurious features across continuously indexed domains (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
2. **Theoretical attempt**: Provides theoretical insight into causality and mitigating distribution shifts (Reviewer 1) and aims to ensure causal transportability (Reviewer 3).
3. **Method description**: The method is detailed and well-written according to one reviewer (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also acknowledges a good job adapting previous approaches.
4. **Empirical performance**: Evaluated against multiple baselines on a semi-synthetic dataset (COLORED-MNIST) and two real-world medical datasets, showing performance improvement in the presence of continuously shifting spurious features (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and trivial combination**: The method is a simple combination of adversarial loss from CIDA (Wang et al. 2020) and causality learning from VOOD (Mao et al. 2022) without strategies specially designed for continuous spurious shift. Existing methods may already mitigate the challenge. (Reviewers 1, 4)
2. **Lack of precise definition of “spurious features”**: The term is not standard in causal literature; the paper does not define it explicitly. The illustrative example (respiratory rate) is confusing – the feature might still be useful if used jointly with domain index. (Reviewer 4)
3. **Unclear difference from previous approaches, especially CIDA**: Both CIDA and CADA extract invariant features across domains via adversarial alignment; the paper does not clearly contrast the novelty. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Missing comparisons**: 
   - Recent continuous domain adaptation baselines not included, e.g., Xie et al. 2024, Jin et al., Lin et al. 2023, Cai et al. 2024. (Reviewer 1)
   - No trivial baseline of pairwise domain adaptation for each discretized target sub-domain. (Reviewer 1)
   - Recommended additional datasets: RMNIST, Portrait, Cover Type. (Reviewer 2)
5. **Experimental setup issues**:
   - **Domain discretization**: Experiments discretize the continuous domain index (COLORED-MNIST created in discretized manner). It is unclear how this differs from standard domain adaptation; no evaluation on truly continuous index. (Reviewer 1)
   - **Missing details**: 
     - How is \(\lambda_d\) chosen? (Reviewer 3)
     - How are shifting spurious features introduced? (Reviewer 3)
     - Number of patients per domain and label distribution per subdomain not provided. (Reviewer 1)
     - No mention of multiple trials. (Reviewer 3)
   - **Probabilistic encoder**: Intuition for probabilistic encoder and Gaussian choices not explained; training stability and inference time compared to other methods not reported; no ablation on probabilistic encoder. (Reviewer 1)
   - **Discriminator**: \(D(k|v)\) is a regressor for domain index \(k\); how is log-likelihood computed? (Reviewer 1)
   - **Data sampler**: \(P(x')\) “uniformly randomly samples data from different domains with different labels” – unclear how labels are accessed in the target domain. (Reviewer 1)
6. **Causal theory and clarity issues**:
   - **Causal graph (Figure 1) unclear**: Why is \(U\) a dotted circle? What does the dotted arrow represent? A practical example corresponding to the graph is needed. (Reviewer 2)
   - **Theorem questionable**: Lemma 3.1 feels pieced together and unrelated to causal theory; claim \(P(y|do(x))\) is domain-invariant contradicts that \(k\) affects \(V\). (Reviewer 2)
   - **Proofs abstract**: Direct application of existing definitions on the specific causal graph, providing no further insight; more like observations than theorems. (Reviewer 1)
   - **Do operation not explained**: How the do operation is implemented in the neural architecture remains unclear; a model diagram is recommended. (Reviewer 2)
   - **Notations insufficiently introduced**: Concepts like do-calculus, transportability, identifiability, front-door criterion need citations and brief introductions (at least in supplementary). (Reviewer 1)
7. **Missing ablation studies**: No ablations studying the effect of choices such as source/target domains or how shifting spurious features are introduced. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Conceptual gaps**:
   - The paper does not discuss how the proposed spurious shift differs from classical shifts (covariate shift, conditional shift, target shift) and whether it is unique to continuous domain adaptation. Should cite Zhang et al. 2013 and Lipton et al. 2018. (Reviewer 2)
   - The illustrative example is questionable: why is “age” treated as a domain index rather than a feature? Using age as a feature would simplify to one-source-one-target adaptation. (Reviewer 4)
   - Paragraph on covariate shift (Line 160) should be expanded and connected to confounding. (Reviewer 4)
9. **Code not available** for reproduction. (Reviewer 1)
10. **Contrast of opinions**: One reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 6, confidence 3) finds the method well-written, with detailed description and good empirical evaluation, and does not raise concerns about novelty or causal grounding. The other three reviewers (scores 3) strongly question the novelty, clarity, experimental rigor, and causal validity. This disagreement is not resolved in the reviews.
