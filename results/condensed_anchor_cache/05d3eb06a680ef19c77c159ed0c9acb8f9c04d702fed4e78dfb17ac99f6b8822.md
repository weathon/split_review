## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies grokking in homogeneous neural networks with large initialization and small weight decay, proposing that grokking arises from a transition between early-phase kernel implicit bias and late-phase rich implicit bias. Theoretical results are proven for classification and regression settings, with corollaries for diagonal linear networks and matrix completion. Experiments verify grokking and misgrokking, and show grokking can occur without weight decay, though more slowly.

### Strengths
- Rigorous provable instance of grokking for simple models; first to succeed in proving grokking in relatively general settings; attributes grokking to transition from kernel to rich regime; interesting upper bounds on transition length.
- Explanation in terms of early/late phase implicit biases is elegant; restrictions (homogeneous nets, large init, small weight decay, gradient flow) are reasonable and motivated by prior empirical work (Liu, Michaud, Tegmark ICLR 2023).
- Analysis done for both classification and regression, and demonstrates "misgrokking" as a dual phenomenon.
- Empirically shows grokking can happen without weight decay, and provides experiments for classification with two-layer diagonal linear networks that verify theory.
- Most novel results are Theorems 3.3 and 3.7, showing small weight decay does not spoil kernel regime early enough and leads to a short transition.
- (Reviewer 2 finds the hypothesis intuitive and the paper a good reminder that theory can shed light on grokking; appreciates empirical examples of both grokking and misgrokking.)
- (Reviewer 3 highlights full proofs in appendix.)

### Weaknesses
- **Major concern (Reviewer 2, contrasting with positive views of Reviewers 1 and 3):** The core claim of a sharp transition in test accuracy is not supported by any analysis; theorems show different inductive biases but provide no generalization guarantees or risk bounds. Without such, the paper only shows models can fit training data and reach perfect training accuracy eventually, which was already done by Moroshko et al. This reviewer cannot support acceptance based on this lack.
- Limited discussion of extensions and limitations; results are in the limit of initialization scale going to infinity, and it is unclear if observations hold in more common settings. Additional experiments could clarify limitations (e.g., gradient flow assumption).
- The paper is dense and difficult to read due to multiple settings and complex theorem statements with little explanation. Theoretical results for regression tasks, especially overparameterized matrix completion, are not illustrated experimentally.
- Claim that homogeneous networks allow ReLU activation conflicts with Assumption 3.1 requiring C²-smoothness, which disallows ReLU due to non-differentiability at 0.
- Section 4.2 on sharpness reduction with label noise mostly restates results from Li, Wang, Arora (ICLR 2021) and does not contain much new material.
- Code was not submitted with the paper.
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms or missing experiments:**
   - Effects of stochasticity in SGD and large step size (deviating from gradient flow) are not studied.
   - The paper does not empirically validate transition on datasets commonly used in the grokking literature (e.g., modular arithmetic tasks beyond simple experiments).
   - The analysis requires data to be linearly separable for classification and the model to interpolate for regression; these assumptions should be stated more clearly in the main text.
   - No proof provided for Theorem 4.1 in the main text; details in appendix but unclear if fully proven.
   - Clarifications needed: Remark 3.11; learning rate and time scaling for Figure 2 (log(t) gives very large values); Lemma A.5/B.2 proof logic; notation q(θ) and rᵢ's; Figure 1 norm (L1/L2 for w or u,v); kernel feature in Corollary 3.5 (should be (2x,-2x)?). References for O(d) and O(k log d) sample complexities and for poor generalization of L2 margin should be provided.
   - The conclusion does not indicate directions for future work.
