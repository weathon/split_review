## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Cooperative Diffusion Recovery Likelihood (CDRL), which extends Diffusion Recovery Likelihood (DRL) by adding a cooperatively trained initializer model at each noise level. The initializer provides better starting points for MCMC refinement of the EBM, reducing required sampling steps and improving sample quality. CDRL is evaluated on unconditional and conditional generation (CIFAR-10, ImageNet 32×32), compositional generation, and OOD detection. The paper reports improved FID over DRL but still lags behind state-of-the-art diffusion models. Two reviewers were more positive (score 8), three moderately positive (score 6). Concerns center on incremental novelty, limited resolution, missing computational cost comparisons, and insufficient OOD evaluation.

### Strengths
- Clear exposition and well-organized paper (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- CDRL shows clear improvement over DRL in sample quality (FID) and reduced number of MCMC steps (R1, R2, R3, R5). Table 1 demonstrates improved FID on CIFAR-10 unconditional generation relative to DRL.
- Broad applicability: unconditional generation, classifier-free guided generation, compositional generation, image inpainting, and out-of-distribution detection (R1, R2). Many of these tasks were unexplored in the original DRL paper (R2).
- Ablation studies convincingly validate each component (R3).
- Classifier-free guidance for EBMs is empirically sound and verifies intuitions from diffusion models (R3).
- The presented techniques are likely new (R4). The main idea of combining DRL with cooperative training makes intuitive sense (R5).
- Strong empirical performance shows possibility of achieving FID on image benchmarks at least comparable to other generative models (e.g., GANs and diffusion models) (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and incremental contribution.** The method is a straightforward combination of DRL (Gao et al., 2021) and MCMC amortization/cooperative training (Xie et al., 2020) (R2, R3, R4). The combination is considered “hacky” and “cumbersome” (R3), and does not introduce new theoretical insights into the EBM framework (R2). The contribution is rated as incremental by three reviewers (R2, R3, R4). Two reviewers (R1, R5) were more positive, giving score 8 and emphasizing empirical performance and applicability.
- **Sample quality still lags behind state-of-the-art diffusion models.** On unconditional CIFAR-10, CDRL achieves FID in the mid-3s, while DDPM achieves low-2s (Table 1) (R1). In Table 3, CDRL is outperformed by DDPM++ with a significantly better FID, questioning competitiveness in high-fidelity generation (R1).
- **Experiments only on low-resolution images.** Evaluation is limited to CIFAR-10 (32×32) and ImageNet 32×32, with no results on higher resolutions such as 256×256 (R2, R3). This limits assessment of scalability and practical applicability (R2).
- **Missing computational cost comparisons.** The paper lacks comparison of inference time (number of function evaluations, wall-clock time) and training cost (GPU memory, training time, number of parameters) against baselines, despite CDRL requiring two networks versus one for diffusion or DRL (R2, R5).
- **OOD detection evaluation insufficient.** The OOD detection experiment (Table 4) uses only three test datasets, lacking diversity; SVHN, a challenging and standard OOD benchmark, is not included (R1). Comparison with the DRL baseline in this experiment is also missing (R5).
- **Clarity and positioning issues.** Related work is placed in the Appendix, hiding prior cooperative training work from the main paper (R5). The relationships between the proposed method, diffusion models, and specific equations (Eqs. (5), (6), (8), (9), (11)) need clearer elaboration (R4, R5). A more detailed discussion of advantages/disadvantages of this EBM compared to diffusion models (and when to use which) is needed (R5).
- **Unanswered questions that are missing experiments or critical comparisons:**
  - What are the practical benefits of CDRL over diffusion models, given that diffusion models achieve better FID and fast sampling (e.g., DPM-Solver with NFE=20 reaches 2.87 FID on CIFAR-10)? (R2, R5)
  - Would increasing MCMC steps significantly (e.g., 300 steps) in baseline DRL close the performance gap with CDRL? (R3)
  - The consistency of the model after adding the initializer is not discussed (R1).
  - For Figure 7, please report FID scores for K=0 and K=15 steps of Langevin refinement, and ideally for intermediate steps K=1,2,4,8 (R5).
  - A schematic overview of the approach (sequence of noise levels, one EBM and initializer per level) would help illustrate the idea (R5).
- **Minor issues:** Typos: Abstract “versons” → “versions”; Section 3.1 “y_t” should be “x_{t+1}”?; Section 1 “making prediction” → “making a prediction”; Section 3.2 “requires” → “require”; Algorithm 1 “noise level” → “noise levels”, “sampling step” → “sampling steps”; Algorithm 2 inconsistency between “L” and “K”; Section 4.4 “CDLR strong” → “CDLR achieves strong”; Appendix G.5 “doesn’t gives” → “doesn’t give” (R5).
