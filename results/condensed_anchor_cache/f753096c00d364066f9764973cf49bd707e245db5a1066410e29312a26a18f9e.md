## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Odyssey, a framework for LLM-based agents in Minecraft. It includes: (1) an interactive agent with a skill library of 40 primitive and 183 compositional skills; (2) a fine-tuned LLaMA-3 8B model (MineMA) trained on a 390k+ instruction dataset from the Minecraft Wiki; (3) a new benchmark covering long-term planning, dynamic-immediate planning, and autonomous exploration. Experiments compare against baselines like Voyager, GITM, DEPS, and VPT on programmatic tasks and the proposed benchmark. The authors open-source datasets, model weights, and code. Reviewer opinions vary: one reviewer (score 6) finds the decoupling of high-level and low-level control valuable for future work across LM scales, while others (scores 3,5,3) criticize limited novelty, unfair comparisons, and insufficient differentiation from prior work.

### Strengths
- Substantial engineering effort: collection of Minecraft-specific data, fine-tuning a large language model, building a Minecraft agent, comparing with numerous baselines, and designing three evaluation benchmarks. (R1)
- Paper is well-formatted, clear, and coherent; visual illustrations are appealing and stylish. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Research topic (open-world agents, LLMs) is relevant to the NeurIPS community. (R2)
- The proposed benchmark is interesting and somewhat comprehensive in terms of task diversity and complexity; it evaluates open-world capabilities. (R2)
- Performance of LMs under agentic frameworks like Voyager depends on the base model's code generation ability; Odyssey decouples evaluation of LMs as high-level vs. low-level controllers, enabling future work on "tool use" across LM parameter scales. This is a valuable community contribution. (R4)
- Experiments and analyses are thorough; models trained/evaluated with Odyssey are compared against relevant baselines. (R4)
- Code released is clean and easy to use. (R4)
- The appendix provides a thorough and detailed explanation of the methods. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and similarity to prior work**: The pipeline is highly similar to existing frameworks like Voyager, Optimus-1, ADAM, DEPS, and Plan4MC. The three contributions (skill library, fine-tuned LLM, benchmark) are loosely connected and do not clearly benefit open-world agents as a whole. (R2, R3) The concept of primitive skills has been introduced by DEPS, Voyager, Plan4MC; fine-tuned LLMs for Minecraft QA appear in OmniJARVIS; benchmarks are explored in BASALT, MineDoJo, Voyager, DEPS, GROOT, GROOT-2. The paper does not adequately distinguish its contributions from these. (R2) Hand-engineered skill libraries have a long history in robotics; Odyssey introduces no new techniques in task decomposition or skill representation. (R4)
- **Unfair baselines in Table 3**: Two of the three baselines (VPT, DEPS) use end-to-end control with native visual observations and keyboard/mouse action spaces, while Odyssey and GITM use Mineflayer with privileged information and scripted JavaScript skills. This comparison is invalid and cannot justify the effectiveness of Odyssey on programmatic tasks. (R1, R2, R3) Without an ablation controlling for this difference, results are not meaningful. (R2)
- **Fine-tuning unsurprising**: Fine-tuning on Minecraft-specific knowledge expectedly improves over untuned open-source models of equivalent size. Comparisons in Tables 4 and 5 should include models like GPT and Claude that possess robust Minecraft knowledge to demonstrate the significance of additional fine-tuning. (R1, R3)
- **Limited task range**: Odyssey does not demonstrate a broader task range than baselines; it remains at the diamond level already achievable by Voyager. More difficult tasks are not shown. (R3)
- **Reliance on predefined primitive skills**: Skills were generated by GPT-4, but GPT-4 can directly write JavaScript programs; relying on primitive skills limits the agent's ability to perform more complex and open-ended tasks like building. (R3)
- **Generalizability unclear**: The framework is designed specifically for Minecraft, and agentic performance is boosted through careful hand-engineering of low-level skills. It is unclear how well LM performance on Minecraft with Odyssey would transfer to other open-world environments like Web navigation. The paper lacks discussion of limitations and how the skill library might be adapted to other domains. (R4)
- **Architecture not novel, but detailed**: The paper repeatedly states the focus is not on a new LLM-based agent architecture, yet a significant portion details the architecture and lists it as a contribution. The architecture description would be better placed in the appendix. (R1) Consequently, ablation studies on a standard architecture are less meaningful. (R1)
- **Missing citations**: Several related works are not cited: Optimus-1, ADAM, OmniJARVIS (NeurIPS 2024), Steve-Eye (ICLR 2024). (R3)
- **Questions**: (R4) How well would even smaller LMs than LLaMA-3 8B perform under Odyssey? Have such experiments been conducted?
