## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a new loss function (“zephyr loss”) for GANs that is convex, smooth, and Lipschitz continuous, intended to improve training stability, robustness to outliers, and gradient control.  The resulting model is called ZGAN.  Theoretical properties are derived, including a claim that the generator objective minimizes a weighted total variation distance.  Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and SVHN are reported, showing improved Inception Scores compared to LSGAN and WGAN.  Multiple reviewers find the approach incrementally novel and the writing clear, but raise serious concerns about unsound theoretical derivations (particularly in Eq. 2 and 4 and the proof of Theorem 4), inadequate experimental evaluation (only low-resolution datasets, single metric IS, no high-resolution or few-shot tests, missing modern baselines and ablations), and insufficient justification of claimed stability benefits.  Two reviewers (scores 1) deem the paper unsound and the experiments far below current standards; three reviewers (scores 3) note incremental theory and weak validation.

### Strengths

- **Originality of loss function**: Proposing a new GAN objective based on a smooth approximation to the Huber loss is, to the best of Reviewer 1’s knowledge, the first such attempt.  The idea of using a more robust loss to address gradient instability and outliers is worth investigating (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is well written, the approach is properly related and contrasted with the GAN literature, motivations are clear, and the algorithm is easy to understand (Reviewer 1).  Reviewer 2 also states the loss presentation is clear and the paper is well organized.
- **Theoretical analysis**: Detailed proofs of convexity, smoothness, and Lipschitz continuity of the zephyr loss are provided, contributing to understanding of ZGAN’s properties (Reviewer 4).  Reviewer 1 notes the paper provides both theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation.
- **Experimental results on small datasets**: Inception Scores on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and SVHN show improvement over LSGAN and WGAN (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Unsound derivation and theory invalidation**:  The apparently unsound derivation in Eq. 2 and 4 invalidates the proof that the generator objective minimizes weighted total variation.  Specifically, the absolute value of an integral is not equal to the integral of the absolute value, and the three expressions in Eq. 2 are not equivalent (Reviewer 1).  The proof of Theorem 4 (Eq. 4) also appears to contain the same flaw (Reviewer 1).  Theorem 2 is framed as “approximately similar” rather than “equal”; lower and upper bounds should be provided instead (Reviewer 5).
- **Mismatch between paper and code**:  The implementation of the generator loss in the supplementary code (g_loss line 106) computes \( E_{x\sim p_r, x’\sim p_g} [ | D(x) - D(x’) | ] \), which does not correspond to either expression in Eq. 2 (Reviewer 1).  Reviewer 1 questions whether the code actually performs the claimed optimization.
- **Incremental theory**: Theorems 1-3 essentially reproduce standard GAN convergence results (from Goodfellow et al. 2016, Nowozin et al. 2016) for a specific loss, and share the same limitation of assuming an optimal discriminator at each step, which is unrealistic in practice (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing references**: The zephyr loss is effectively a smooth approximation to the Huber loss, but this connection is neither cited nor discussed (Reviewer 1).  The work also does not reference f-GAN or related frameworks (Nowozin et al. 2016, Kurri et al. 2022); the method likely is a specific instance of f-GAN (Reviewer 2).  The naming “zephyr loss” lacks prior research support (Reviewer 3).
- **Inadequate experimental evaluation**:  
  - Only Inception Score (IS) is reported; FID, KID, and LPIPS are not provided.  FID and KID are standard and more reliable, and should be added (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).  
  - Datasets are low-resolution (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, SVHN).  High-resolution datasets (LSUN, CelebA, ImageNet, FFHQ, AFHQ) and modern architectures (StyleGAN2, StyleGAN-XL) are needed to demonstrate effectiveness (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
  - Generated image quality is poor: Figure 4 shows unsatisfactory visual results, often completely broken, and IS is extremely low compared to modern standards (Reviewers 3, 5).  For CIFAR-10 the reported IS/FID (seeming to be ~2.9) is far below expected modern values (Reviewer 5).  The experiments use the original DCGAN setup; modern implementations with AdamW, EMA, and other improvements are required (Reviewer 5).  
  - Comparisons are limited to WGAN and LSGAN; missing WGAN-GP, WGAN-LP, and other recent methods (Reviewer 4).  Dirac-GAN experiments and convergence proof should also be included (Reviewer 5).  
  - No ablation studies on parameters \(\epsilon\) and \(\alpha\); the brief mention is insufficient.  Ablation results should be presented in figures or lists (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - Training with small datasets (e.g., Obama-100, Sketch-100, FFHQ-Sunglasses) is not conducted, yet the paper claims stable training – this should be tested (Reviewer 4).  
  - Number of training steps for each method in Table 1 is not specified (Reviewer 2).
- **Unsubstantiated claims about stability and robustness**:  
  - The paper claims ZGAN cures gradient instability and outlier sensitivity of LSGAN, but provides no evidence that LSGAN actually suffers from these ills in the presented settings (Reviewer 1).  
  - Convexity, smoothness, and Lipschitz continuity of the loss do not guarantee that GAN training dynamics themselves are Lipschitz continuous or stable; further analysis is needed (Reviewer 3).  
  - The gradient of zephyr loss exhibits a mutation near the center (Figure 2), whereas least squares loss remains smooth overall – this may indicate unstable gradients and requires discussion (Reviewer 3).  
  - The paper does not provide local stability analysis near Nash equilibrium points (Reviewer 3).  
  - As noted in WGAN, total variation distance is not continuously differentiable with respect to parameters; since ZGAN approximates this distance, this property is not ideal (Reviewer 5).  
  - Reviewer 1 notes that “flexibility in tuning with \(\alpha\)” is not a real advantage, as \(\alpha\) plays a role similar to the learning rate in all GANs.
- **Redundant section**: Section 5 appears redundant and is not supported by experiments; it could be moved to the experimental analysis in Section 6 (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing responses to alternative designs**: The authors did not test or compare with simply using the same zephyr loss for the generator as for the discriminator (i.e., the same kind of objective as LSGAN but with zephyr loss) (Reviewer 1, Q4).
