- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies how LLMs encode output sequence length in internal representations. On a summarization task (mainly Llama-2, also Llama-3 and Phi), it probes hidden units for length information, finds attention heads in early layers encode length, and shows that scaling those units can control length without substantially sacrificing semantic quality, as measured by compression ratio and ROUGE. A human evaluation and qualitative analysis are included. The work is considered original and addresses an important, open problem. However, the presentation and methodology are found to be unclear in several respects, results appear partially reported, and claims often lack statistical support. Two reviewers rated the paper 5 (confidence 2 and 3), two rated it 3 (confidence 4 each). The more positive reviewers highlight the contribution; the more critical reviewers stress that weak presentation and missing analyses undermine the conclusions.

### Strengths
- **Originality and importance**: First thorough experimental work on length representations in LLMs (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). The problem of controlling generation length is important and still open (Reviewer 4).
- **Useful mechanistic insight**: The focus on internal mechanisms and causally adjusting them is a nice contribution (Reviewer 1). The idea that length can be controlled more directly than with other methods, without heavily affecting quality, is valuable (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Broad experimental scope**: Experiments use multiple LLMs (Llama-2, Llama-3, Phi), increasing generalizability (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). The methodology includes probing, causal interventions, human evaluations, and qualitative analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Fine-grained control**: The paper shows that it is possible to tune the length in a fine-grained way without sacrificing the semantics of the output (Reviewer 2). Some evidence suggests that length information is partially separable from semantic information (Reviewer 1, 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear presentation of methods** (all reviewers):
  - The fine-tuning objective and data are not explained. The motivation for fine-tuning (and why with QLoRA 4/8-bit) is unclear (Reviewer 1, 2, 3). “I couldn’t find any explanation of what the fine-tuning objective was” (Reviewer 1).
  - The definition of the response variable in the probing (“generation time step”) is ambiguous: does it include prompt tokens? The variable \(n\) is not well specified. High \(R^2\) might simply reflect positional embeddings or attention masking rather than length representations (Reviewer 3, 4). “It looks like you’re predicting the ‘generation time step’ from a matrix of hidden states… couldn’t that just be inferred by the attention masking or the positional embeddings?” (Reviewer 3).
  - How individual neurons were selected by \(R^2\) is not clear: were new classifiers trained from single hidden units? At which token positions was scaling applied? (Reviewer 4).
  - The description of \(\Delta CR\) (delta-CR) is insufficient: “explained at a high level but not in detail, so it is hard to know what the numbers really mean and how to compare them” (Reviewer 1). Requested: explicit calculation (arithmetic difference or log ratio) and interpretation (Reviewer 1, 2).
  - Equations (1)–(4) use post-LayerNorm, but Llama-2/3 and Phi-3 use pre-LayerNorm. Are the equations wrong or was the architecture modified? (Reviewer 4). Also, \(S_{emb}\) should be the residual stream \(x\), not the embedding (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear presentation of results**:
  - Tables are hard to parse (especially Table 3; Reviewer 4). Table 5 caption says “word length” but means “output length (#words)” (Reviewer 1). In Table 4, “The numbers in parentheses indicate an index of hidden units from the second layer of the attention mechanisms”—unclear whether values are per hidden unit or averaged (Reviewer 2).
  - It is difficult to see how the method compares to alternatives (e.g., length priming, Table 2 approaches). For “Priming” results, is the prompt varied to produce different lengths to make a fair comparison with varying scaling factor? (Reviewer 1). The discussion of Figure 2 seems inconsistent: the text says “R-L scores slightly decrease” but the graphs appear to show increases for some prompts; this interpretation needs clarification (Reviewer 1).
  - The claim that neuron scaling genuinely decreases length is not clearly supported. Figure 2 suggests it works in fine-tuned settings but only with priming in non-fine-tuned settings. A histogram or distribution of length differences would be more informative (Reviewer 4).
  - The use of an MLP (rather than linear probe) is called “linear regression” in the text; this is misleading (Reviewer 3). The probing methodology has well-known pitfalls (e.g., decoding functionally irrelevant information) that are not discussed; engagement with the probing literature is lacking (Reviewer 4).
  - The claim that length information is “disentangled” is misleading because Figure 2 shows an interaction between length change (delta CR) and quality (ROUGE-L) (Reviewer 3).
- **Partial results and reproducibility issues**:
  - While Section 3 mentions experiments on Llama-2, Llama-3, and Phi, only Section 4 reports all three; Section 5 (including causal scaling and human evaluation) reports results only for Llama-2. Full set of results should be included (Reviewer 2).
  - No statistical significance or error bars are reported throughout. This makes it impossible to assess whether the findings are significant (Reviewer 2, 3). Specific requests:
    - Table 6 (human evaluations): differences in conciseness (e.g., 0.00–0.02) could easily be noise; standard deviations and significance values are needed (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
    - Table 5: if values are averages over \(N\), report standard deviation (Reviewer 2).
    - Figures 1–3: add error bars over random seeds; also scale y-axes to same range (Reviewer 2, 3).
- **Prompt design concerns** (Reviewer 3):
  - The “Length” prompt (Table 1) specifies a number of words to *delete*, not a desired summary length. Is the model actually following the intended instruction? For “Priming”, two user-provided numbers (target length and deletion count) may work against each other. It is not clear whether outputs actually adhered to the intended objectives, nor whether these prompts are sufficient to draw general conclusions about instruction–representation interaction (Section 5.1).
- **Human evaluation concerns**:
  - The differences in conciseness between scaled and baseline summaries are tiny (0.00–0.02) and not analyzed for statistical significance. Why is the gold standard conciseness not better than the baseline? (Reviewer 4). This casts doubt on whether the method actually changes conciseness meaningfully (Reviewer 3, 4).
- **Speculative and unsubstantiated claims** (Reviewer 3, 4):
  - The claim that “the increase in length representations in the final layer indicates that the model revisits this information to reinforce positional context” is speculative (Reviewer 4).
  - The analogy to Tenney et al. (2019) on semantic/syntax layers may be misapplied (Reviewer 4).
- **Other presentation issues**:
  - Figure 1: which model is shown? (Reviewer 4).
  - Block of questions at lines 240–245 should be rewritten (Reviewer 4).
  - Typos: “coherence and informativeness for texts” should be “of texts”; “multiplying positive numbers” should be “multiplying them by positive numbers” (Reviewer 4).
