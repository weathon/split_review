## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends spherical neurons to the orthogonal group O(n) for arbitrary n, constructing Deep Equivariant Hyperspheres via steerable filter banks based on regular n-simplexes. The architecture is O(n)-equivariant and can be composed into deep networks. The authors present theoretical proofs and evaluate on O(3)-invariant skeleton classification (UTKinect-Action3D) and synthetic O(5) regression and convex hull tasks, reporting favorable results in low-sample regimes but mixed performance on larger datasets compared to existing methods like CGENN, Vector Neurons, and EMLP.

### Strengths
- The extension of spherical neurons to O(n) with n > 3 is novel and requires non-trivial theoretical development, offering an alternative to MLP- or graph-convolution-based equivariant models.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow overall (Reviewers 1, 4), with clear mathematical notation and explanations.
- The theory (proofs of equivariance, composition, bias, normalization) is mathematically correct and rigorous.
- The proposed architecture achieves strong results in low-sample regimes compared to baselines like Vector Neurons and EMLPs on the tested tasks.
- The use of well-defined geometric tools (spherical neurons, steerable functions, n-simplexes) is appropriate for capturing O(n) symmetry.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental evaluation**: Experiments are restricted to small datasets (~1000 training samples), small models, and only one real-world dataset (O(3) action recognition). The main claim of O(n) for n>3 is not validated on any real higher-dimensional task, and practical applications for n>3 are not discussed (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Incomplete baseline comparisons**: Many relevant O(n)-equivariant/invariant architectures (EGNN, vector neurons, canonicalization/averaging, GVP, etc.) are either not included or tested only on a subset of tasks. The only non-synthetic experiment lacks comparisons to most equivariant methods (Reviewers 2, 4). CGENN outperforms the proposed method on O(5) regression, and on the convex hull task, Vector Neurons and GVP outperform when training set size increases (Reviewer 4). The paper does not provide theoretical or empirical reasons for these gaps.
- **Lack of computational/memory analysis**: Efficiency claims (e.g., a speed/performance trade-off) are not supported by any recorded time or memory comparisons with baselines (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Motivation unclear**: It is not articulated why spherical decision surfaces are inherently better than hyperplanes for these tasks, nor why cascading spherical neurons yields better representations than existing equivariant architectures. The isometry argument is not developed into a practical advantage (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty in the generalization to higher dimensions**: The extension from 3D to nD is described as a direct dimensional increase; potential challenges like curse of dimensionality, increased computational cost, and the need for careful design are not addressed (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 also notes the architecture description is hard to follow without prior knowledge of Melnyk et al. papers.
- **Practical applicability concerns**: The model requires training on canonically aligned data (one orientation) and relies on creating n+1 transformed copies via an n-simplex. In many real-world scenarios the group actions on data are unknown, making it unclear how the architecture would be trained without a canonical frame (Reviewer 3). This limitation is not discussed.
- **Presentation issues**: Some crucial concepts (steerability, background on spherical neurons) are insufficiently defined, making the paper hard to follow for readers unfamiliar with prior work (Reviewers 2, 3). The main body contains standard proofs (e.g., composition of equivariant layers) that could be moved to an appendix to free space for clearer method description and intuition (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameters and ablation studies missing**: The impact of key choices (number of spherical neurons, depth, activation functions) is not investigated. The paper would benefit from ablation studies to understand model behavior (Reviewer 1).
- **Performance plateaus and scaling issues**: In O(5) experiments (regression and convex hull), the method plateaus and is eventually outperformed by baselines when training samples increase; this suggests a fundamental limitation, possibly due to lack of higher-order interactions (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Figure 2 left also shows MLP Aug overtaking the method with more data.
- **Expressivity concerns**: The architecture may be less expressive than methods with higher-order interactions (like CGENN). A reviewer asks whether there is an expressivity gap; the paper does not address this theoretically or experimentally (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related work section**: The positioning relative to previous work is implicit and insufficiently structured, making it hard for readers to identify direct competitors and foundational works (Reviewer 1).
