## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes QuestCoT, where a model first asks itself how to start before proceeding with chain-of-thought reasoning. The motivation is that first-step correctness is critical for final answer accuracy, especially in smaller models which, when corrected, can solve tasks they otherwise struggle with. The authors demonstrate performance gains on GSM8K (+6), SVAMP (+9), ASDiv (+5), and MultiArith (+7). They also show that a larger model can guide the first step to boost smaller models (up to +24 on GSM8K for 7B models). The reviewers note a significant disagreement: one reviewer (score 5) commended the extensive experiments and analysis, while three others (score 3) raised concerns about novelty, experimental scope, and credibility of baseline numbers.

### Strengths
- Clear articulation of why the first step matters, supported by experimental results and concrete examples (Reviewer 1, 4).  
- Additional analysis on data leakage, comparisons between different types of CoT, and whether QuestCoT works with smaller models (Reviewer 1).  
- Well-written paper with clear prose; figures and tables are well-designed and effectively present data (Reviewer 2).  
- Key finding that smaller models can significantly improve when corrected by larger models is insightful and empirically investigated well (Reviewer 2).  
- Proposes a new framework (QuestCoT) to increase model robustness for solving mathematical word problems (Reviewer 3).  
- Demonstrates clearly that smaller models can reach the correct answer with multiple high-temperature sampling but fail to select the correct chain, and that providing the first step boosts performance (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- Extensive experiments addressing three main research questions about model capability, importance of the first step, and self-questioning; includes analysis of first-step guidance on longer chains and whether the first step leaks the final answer (Reviewer 3).  
- One reviewer (score 5) was notably more positive, praising the thoroughness of experiments and analysis, while other reviewers (score 3) expressed reservations (contrast preserved).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** QuestCoT is seen as more a new template for chain-of-thought (question-and-answer in the first step) than a fundamentally innovative approach (Reviewer 2). The first two research questions (small model capability, importance of first step) have been previously studied in [1] “Common 7B Language Models Already Possess Strong Math Capabilities” (Reviewer 4).  
- **Credibility of baseline numbers:** GSM8K performance for Gemma 2B in Table 2 is 7.5 versus the official 17.7 (gap 10.2), and Mistral 7B is 45.4 versus the official blog 52.1 (gap 6.9). These large gaps raise concerns about result credibility and need clarification (Reviewer 1).  
- **Limited experimental scope:** Only old datasets (GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDiv, MultiArith) are used; stronger models (e.g., Qwen2, DeepSeek) and harder/broader datasets (MMLU-Pro, GPQA, MATH, MATHBENCH) are needed to demonstrate robustness (Reviewer 1, 3). Missing comparisons with strong baselines such as Self-consistency [Wang et al. 2022] and Tree-of-Thought [Yao et al. 2024] (Reviewer 3).  
- **Confusing analysis in “Why QuestCoT works” (Section 3.3) vs. “What if subquestions are included at each step” (Section 4):** The reasoning in Section 3.3 would suggest that including subquestions at every step (Subques) should outperform QuestCoT, but results show the opposite; the paper lacks explanation for this discrepancy (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing ablation and evidence:** It is unclear how the self-generated questions are produced (the prompt used) and how question quality affects performance; an ablation on subquestions is needed (Reviewer 3). More evidence is required on why adding subquestions at every step increases error propagation compared to only at the first step (Reviewer 3).  
- **Insufficient support for the claim that the first step is more important than other intermediate steps** (Reviewer 4).
