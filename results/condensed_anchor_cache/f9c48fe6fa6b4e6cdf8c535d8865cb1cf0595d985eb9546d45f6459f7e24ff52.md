## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an iterative visual fine-tuning framework to improve large multimodal models (LMMs) in domain-specific visual classification by enhancing both cognition and explainability. The approach self-synthesizes interpretable training data: it generates answers containing human-verifiable visual features that are selected (based on expert-defined concepts) for alignment with image content, then filters the synthesized outputs using a reward model-free rejection sampling mechanism inspired by the Information Bottleneck principle. Iterative rounds of data synthesis and fine-tuning progressively improve accuracy and explanation quality without requiring extensive manual annotations. Experimental results on multiple datasets (e.g., CUB-200, Stanford Dogs) demonstrate effectiveness.

### Strengths
- **Clear problem identification and justification:** The paper identifies insufficient domain-specific alignment as the root cause of LMMs struggling with fine-grained classification and explainability, and provides a well-motivated solution.
- **Novel framework with theoretical grounding:** The framework is grounded in information theory (Information Bottleneck) and introduces a doubly robust rejection sampling strategy to assure self-generated data quality and reliability.
- **Significant accuracy improvement:** The method demonstrates substantial increases in classification accuracy across multiple iterations compared to training with labels alone (baselines NL, L+GE in Table 1).
- **Comprehensive evaluation:** The paper employs a multifaceted evaluation of explanation quality, including Explanation Existence (EE), Cognition Score (CS), Fluency Score (FS), and user studies, providing a thorough assessment.
- **Clear writing and presentation:** The paper is logically clear and easy to follow, with helpful figures and tables.
- **Important problem and general framework:** Improving interpretability of LMMs for challenging classification tasks is an important direction, and the proposed framework is general and promising.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope and generalizability concerns:**
  - Evaluation is conducted only on classification tasks; the impact on general QA capabilities is not assessed. Several reviewers recommend comparing general benchmark performance (e.g., MMVet, MMStar, SEED-Bench2) before and after fine-tuning.
  - The model’s ability to generalize to unseen categories (completely disjoint classes) is not addressed; experiments on datasets with novel categories are needed.
  - Cross-dataset transfer is not explored (e.g., does training on CUB-200 improve Stanford Dogs?).
  - One reviewer questions how much of the improvement stems from domain shift due to fine-tuning rather than genuine reasoning improvement, and whether this shift harms generalizability.

- **Missing baselines and ablation studies:**
  - No ablation study showing results using unfiltered data for training (to validate the data selection strategies). This is requested by multiple reviewers.
  - A baseline trained with explanations derived by prompting a vision-language model on each image (without the proposed filtering) is missing and would support a fair comparison.
  - Comparison with external works in explainable visual classification is limited; only self-created baselines (NL, L+GE) are used. Including an upper bound with manually annotated labels is suggested.
  - Ablation studies on different base LMMs (e.g., architectures beyond LLaVA-1.5) are missing; the method’s dependence on the base model’s capabilities is not analyzed.

- **Data synthesis, labels, and concept acquisition:**
  - It is unclear how a valid testing set is created when training labels are self-generated. The paper needs to clarify whether test set labels come from the original dataset and how overfitting to generated explanations is avoided.
  - The process of obtaining label-level concepts (expert-defined) is not fully outlined. If manual human annotation is required, this is a scalability bottleneck. The specific prompts used for eliciting concepts from experts or LLMs should be provided, and potential bias discussed.
  - The model may overfit to the specific explanation format used; experiments varying explanation styles or levels of detail are needed.

- **Analysis and methodology details:**
  - No quantitative analysis of how the iterative process improves explanation quality (beyond accuracy). One reviewer asks for analysis of how data selection affects explanation properties.
  - The trainable parameters during fine-tuning are not specified. If only the LLM side is fine-tuned, it is unclear whether this addresses deficiencies in the visual encoder (e.g., CLIP’s limited fine-grained perception).
  - No experimental runtime is reported.
  - Minor: In Table 1, highlighting best results would improve readability.
  - The impact of the choice of text encoder on data selection results is not investigated.
  - No qualitative examples demonstrating the effectiveness of the data selection strategy are provided.

- **Reviewer disagreement note:** While all reviewers rate the paper positively (score 6), one reviewer (Reviewer 4) assigns a lower soundness rating (2) and expresses a stronger concern that accuracy gains may largely reflect domain shift rather than genuine reasoning enhancement, and that generalizability could be compromised. This skepticism is not shared by the other reviewers, but the point about domain shift and missing baselines is echoed across multiple reviews.
