## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents RTop-K, a GPU algorithm for row-wise top-k selection optimized for tall-skinny matrices (fewer than 1000 elements per row, many rows). It uses a binary-search-based approach with warp-level parallelism and careful cache hierarchy use. An early stopping variant uses a fixed number of iterations to improve load balancing. RTop-K is compared to PyTorch’s RadixSelect on synthetic data (speedups of 2–6×) and integrated into MaxK-GNN models (GraphSAGE, GIN, GCN) on benchmark datasets, achieving 10–30% faster training. Early stopping preserves model test accuracy. The paper is well organized and easy to follow, with thoughtful consideration of hardware constraints. However, reviewers raised concerns about baseline selection, limited experimental scope, missing ablation and scalability analyses, unclear novelty, and insufficient discussion of broader applicability and resource management.

### Strengths

- The paper addresses an important computational bottleneck in GNN training with a practical, efficiency-oriented approach. (R1, R4)
- The algorithm is fairly original, taking a problem-specific perspective different from typical top-k methods. (R2)
- The design carefully considers appropriate levels of parallelism based on GPU hardware constraints and problem size. (R2)
- The experimental validation is comprehensive both as a standalone kernel and within GNN workflows, across models (GraphSAGE, GIN, GCN) and datasets. (R4)
- RTop-K achieves speedups of 4.25–9.51× over state-of-the-art GPU row-wise top-k implementations and improves overall GNN training by 9.76–31.53%. (R3, R4)
- The early stopping mechanism provides computational savings without significantly impacting model accuracy. (R3, R4)
- The paper provides detailed algorithm descriptions and pseudocode, improving reproducibility. (R4)
- The GNN test is well-conducted and rounds out an otherwise HPC-focused study. (R2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Baseline comparison insufficient.** The primary baseline is PyTorch’s RadixSelect, which solves the harder problem of sorting. Comparisons with other top-k algorithms—such as Shanbhag et al.’s method, radix select–based methods, Avg-TopK, and TopK-SGD—are missing. The assertion that these are “not suitable for GNN” is not sufficiently justified. Additionally, PyTorch’s top-k is sometimes reported to be slower than torch.sort, raising questions about its suitability as a baseline. (R1, R4)

2. **Limited applicability and scalability analysis.** Experiments focus only on MaxK-GNN (GraphSAGE, GIN, GCN). Broader impact on other deep networks—e.g., Mixture of Experts, large models like GPTs where top-k is used—is not discussed. Scaling to larger datasets (hidden dimensions, M up to 10⁴ or 10⁵) is not tested, leaving a gap in understanding when performance deteriorates or where to switch algorithms. (R1, R2, R4)

3. **Missing ablation and stage-wise performance breakdown.** The implementation has multiple stages (loading, searching, selecting), but no breakdown of each stage’s contribution to speedup. The individual contributions of the binary search and early stopping are not isolated via ablation. (R4)

4. **Insufficient theoretical analysis and hyper-parameter guidance.** The paper lacks a theoretical analysis of the early stopping strategy. Key hyper-parameters (precision ϵ′ in Algorithm 1, max_iter in Algorithm 2) are not accompanied by sensitivity analysis or guidance on how to set them for different tasks. (R1, R4)

5. **Hardware dependency and profiling missing.** Performance is demonstrated only on an A6000 GPU. It is unclear whether the optimizations generalize to other GPU architectures. Roofline analysis and Nsight profiling results are not provided, making it hard to assess how close the implementation is to hardware limits. (R1, R3)

6. **Bottleneck analysis missing.** The paper does not share a time breakdown of the overall GNN training to demonstrate that top-k is indeed the bottleneck; only the portion of the top-k part is given. (R1)

7. **Scalability on hidden dimensions not shown.** While varying K is studied, scaling over hidden dimensions (which can be large in some GNNs and non-GNNs) is not reported. (R4)

8. **Unclear novelty and parallel mechanism.** Integrating binary search into top-k selection is not inherently novel; the paper does not quantify the performance improvement specifically attributable to binary search over a non-binary-search baseline. The “parallel” mechanism is not clearly explained—it may simply be many independent single-row top-k selections executed on the GPU, which should be clarified. Claiming a summary of existing GPU top-k algorithms as a contribution is not sufficient. (R4)

9. **Limited discussion of approximation methods.** The algorithm uses an approximation (early stopping), but there is no discussion of how to overcome the limitations of approximation or how accuracy is controlled across varied data. (R3)

10. **Resource and memory concerns for large datasets.** RTop-K is optimized for limited-sized vectors; there is little discussion of resource consumption and memory management for very large datasets. (R3)
