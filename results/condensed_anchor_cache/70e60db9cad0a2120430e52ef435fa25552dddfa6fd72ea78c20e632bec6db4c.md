## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NeurPIR, a self-supervised contrastive learning method to learn intrinsic representations of individual neurons from population dynamics. It combines CEBRA (with surrounding information) and VICReg loss, employing adaptive average pooling to produce time-invariant embeddings. Evaluated on synthetic Izhikevich data, spatial transcriptomics (Bugeon dataset) with cell type annotations, and Steinmetz dataset with brain region labels. Compared against PCA, UMAP, NeuPRINT, and LOLCAT, reporting SOTA performance.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important and challenging problem: extracting individual neuron characteristics from population activity in a label-free manner (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Novel combination of CEBRA and VICReg with surrounding information (Reviewer 1). The approach is distinct from NeuPRINT (time-invariant look-up table) and LOLCAT (supervised) (Reviewer 2).
- Well-motivated, clearly written, with detailed methods and experiments aiding interpretability (Reviewers 1,4).
- Evaluated on three representative benchmarks: synthetic, real with ground-truth cell type, real with brain region (Reviewers 2,3). Out-of-domain evaluation on unseen mice increases soundness (Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrates convincing performance above alternatives (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewer 4 (score 8, the most positive) emphasizes successful extraction of neuron characteristics.
- Interest to the ICLR community given relevance to representation learning without labels (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Out-of-domain evaluation on Steinmetz dataset is questionable: the self-supervised model is trained on all mice including test mice (line 266), requiring retraining for new animals; can any part be reused? (Reviewer 1). More critically, predicting brain region (external property) does not directly support extraction of *intrinsic* neuron representations—even identical neurons in different regions may have different activity due to differing inputs, and the paper does not validate that region-related differences are smaller than intrinsic ones (Reviewer 4). A cross-animal generalization experiment on the cell-type dataset would be more relevant (Reviewer 3). For Steinmetz, evaluating on finer-grained spatial location would be better than coarse brain region (Reviewer 2).
- Model architecture is not clearly explained in figures or text. Key details missing: how VICReg pushes dissimilar neurons apart when it uses only positive pairs (Reviewer 1, also Reviewer 3 on similar point). How negative pairs are processed? Figure 1 lacks labels for model components (X, H, Z, P, F, CEBRA, VICReg) and its description is missing (Reviewer 1,4). The text does not clarify whether CEBRA is applied to single neurons or to population windows, nor how temporal aspects are handled for single-neuron segments (Reviewer 3). Other missing details: segment length, possibility of overlapping segments, dimensions of covariates ($X_{st}, X_{be}, X_{se}, X_{si}$), adaptive average pooling specifics, target value $\mu$ (line 202) (Reviewer 1). Provide hyperparameters, training time, time complexity vs baselines (Reviewers 1,2). 
- Ablation studies are missing: impact of different surrounding information in CEBRA; comparison with other contrastive methods besides VICReg; effect of VICReg loss itself (Reviewers 1,2,3). Effect of data shuffling, random initialization should be reported with error bars (Reviewers 1,2). Whether max-pooling would differ from mean-pooling (Reviewer 4).
- Results in tables and figures lack error bars; sensitivity analyses needed (Reviewers 1,2). Tables would benefit from aggregate metrics across all categories (Reviewer 4).
- Baseline comparisons may be unfair: LOLCAT fails to label any Sst neurons (Table 2), suggesting poor tuning. The paper should include detailed hyperparameter search space, cross-validation strategy, and ensure the same evaluation protocol is used for all baselines (Reviewer 3).
- Framing as "Platonic Representation Hypothesis" feels like hype chasing; the work does not meaningfully relate to PRH (representations of the world converging across domains). Request substantial revision of title, abstract, and introduction (Reviewer 3). The abstract is confusing: needs simpler wording with examples of intrinsic properties; "what information is conveyed" is usually about neural coding; "captures preset hyperparameters" overclaims (only categorical differences shown) (Reviewer 4). 
- Method is not significantly original—primarily combines CEBRA and VICReg, making it nearly a pure applications paper. This puts the burden of innovation on the value of scientific findings (Reviewer 3).
- Figures have barely readable label sizes and missing annotations; presentation quality should be improved (Reviewers 2,4).
- Conclusion crucially lacks a limitations paragraph (Reviewer 4).
- Reproducibility: manual labels for Bugeon dataset are not stated as provided; will exact labels be available? (Reviewer 4). 
- Further investigation needed on sampling strategy and amount of data required to guarantee convergence of the time-invariant embedding (Reviewer 2). 
- For Bugeon dataset, why was only mouse A used? (Reviewer 4). For Steinmetz, why not 10-fold cross-validation? (Reviewer 4). Would the method perform with many more Izhikevich types (10,20,30)? (Reviewer 4). 
- Cell type identification may require more than binned firing rate; spike train details could be informative (Reviewer 3).
