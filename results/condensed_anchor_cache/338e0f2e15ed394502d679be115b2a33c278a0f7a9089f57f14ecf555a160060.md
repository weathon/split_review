- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 6, 10

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SuperDiff, a training‑free framework for combining multiple pre‑trained diffusion models at inference time based on a superposition principle derived from the continuity equation. Two algorithms are introduced: a “logical OR” (fixed‑weight mixture of densities) and a “logical AND” (adaptive weighting to produce samples where the component densities are equal). The method is validated on CIFAR‑10 image generation, Stable Diffusion prompt‑conditioned editing, and unconditional protein *de novo* structure design, with claims of improved diversity, faithfulness, and designability.

### Strengths
- The theoretical foundation is solid and well‑motivated, with 9 propositions, 2 theorems, and 1 lemma that support the approach (R1, R2). The derivation from the continuity equation and the superposition of elementary vector fields is presented in a principled manner (R1).
- A key insight is the ability to evaluate the log‑density of each component distribution *without* computing the divergence of the score, enabling implicit and adaptive mixture weights (R3). The clever density estimator that avoids expensive divergence operations is highlighted.
- The method is training‑free and can combine diffusion models with different architectures (R2). It scales to large pre‑trained models (R1) and is designed to be efficient during inference (R1, though execution times are questioned – see Weaknesses).
- The connection to traditional logical OR and AND operators provides intuitive interpretability (R1).
- Experimental results convincingly demonstrate effectiveness across diverse domains (R3). In particular, the unconditional protein structure generation outperforms other baselines on designability, novelty, and diversity (R2). The concept interpolation and prompt‑conditioned editing examples show practical utility (R1, R2).
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (R1). The use of multiple theoretical results aids understanding (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Qualitative image quality and visual evaluation** – Reviewer 1 finds the generated images in Figure 1 and the appendix mediocre: they lack sharpness and detail, and concept combinations often appear blurry or distorted, which makes it difficult to assess the true capabilities of the method. Reviewer 3, in contrast, considers the experimental results convincing. This disagreement in perceived quality indicates that the visual demonstrations may not be compelling for all readers.
- **Inconsistent use of OR and AND operations** – Only the AND operation is shown in the qualitative results; the OR operation appears only in quantitative tables (R1). The absence of qualitative examples for OR (and vice‑versa missing quantitative benchmarks for AND) makes it hard to validate claims about both novelty (OR) and designability (AND) (R1). More critically, the practical implications of the OR operator remain unclear: Reviewer 2 asks whether OR simply selects one model with probability (i.e., trivial to sample without SuperDiff) or creates a more complex combination, and how it differs from simple averaging of outputs. Reviewer 3 notes that OR corresponds to a fixed‑weight mixture, so the results should be reproducible by a baseline that randomly picks a single model – if SuperDiff OR does better, the improvement may stem from orthogonal implementation choices rather than the theory. **Overlap with question: This essentially calls for an ablation or baseline comparison.**
- **Lack of quantitative evaluation on Stable Diffusion** – There are no quantitative metrics for the SD experiments. Obvious choices include CLIP Score (R1), TIFA Score (Reviewer 2 cites [1]), and Image Reward (Reviewer 2 cites [2]). The paper does not justify why these metrics were omitted (R2). This missing evaluation prevents a rigorous assessment of faithfulness and alignment (R2).
- **Missing comparisons against relevant prior work** – The paper does not compare against other inference‑time composition or combination methods. Reviewer 1 suggests comparing with “factorization diffusion” or “visual anagram” (Geng et al., 2024). Reviewer 2 references inference‑time score‑mixture methods [3‑6] that compose objects, styles, scenes, or improve text‑image alignment. A discussion (or experiments) is needed to clarify how SuperDiff differs and what its relative strengths/weaknesses are, especially in terms of generation quality, computational cost, and prompt following (R2).
- **Limited dataset diversity in experiments** – The conceptual combination experiments rely solely on CIFAR‑10 (with two disjoint 5‑class splits) (R1). Using more diverse datasets would strengthen the paper (R1).
- **Missing ablation study and insufficient baselines** – In the concept interpolation (AND), only one direction (A→B) is shown without changing the order or selecting the better comparison (R1). A systematic exploration of both directions would improve the ablation (R1). **Overlap with question: This is a methodological weakness.**
- **Execution times not reported** – The paper claims improved efficiency but provides no timing results. Reviewer 1 notes the claim is unsupported; this is critical given that one of the main contributions is training‑free, efficient inference.
- **Theoretical presentation clarity issues**:
  - Section 2.1 is too complicated and, for many readers, irrelevant to the core idea (R3). The main takeaway – that a mixture of clean densities leads to a mixture of noisy densities, and that ∇ log ∑ w_i p_i^t = ∑ w_i p_i^t ∇ log p_i^t / ∑ w_i p_i^t – is obscured by the use of general noise schedules (α_t, σ_t) and discrete‑time formulation (R3). Reviewer 3 suggests simplifying by stating the core result with the variance‑exploding SDE (α_t=1, σ²=t) in the main text and relegating the general case to the appendix.
  - The derivation of Proposition 7 would be more intuitive if presented in continuous time (using Itô’s formula on log q_t(x_t) where q_t solves the Fokker‑Planck equation) and then discretized (R3). The current discrete‑time exposition hides the simplicity of the result, which could be of independent interest.
  - Proposition 8 is cut off by a page break, making its full equation difficult to read; the individual terms of “Aκ = b + o(Δt)” should appear on the same page (R1).
  - The related work section appears only at the end of page 10, which disrupts logical flow; moving it earlier would improve coherence (R1).
- **Insufficient explanation of the AND operator’s target distribution** – The AND operator is defined implicitly: it produces samples such that q¹(x) = q²(x). It is unclear what underlying density is being sampled (R3). A more standard goal would be sampling from the normalized product q¹ q² / Z or geometric mean; the paper does not discuss this discrepancy or why the implicit definition is preferable (R3).
- **Missing explanation of protein generation metrics** – The terms “designability”, “novelty”, and “diversity” are used without a clear definition of their relevance in protein structure generation. The connection to the logical operators is also unclear (R1).
- **Questions about experimental setup**:
  - How are the mixing coefficients w_j set in practice? Is the model capable of adjusting them at test time? (R1)
  - How is N (the number of forward‑process steps) determined? (R1)
  - In Table 2, the parameters ω and T are not explained (R3).
  - FID on CIFAR‑10 is computed on the full dataset, even though the models are trained on class‑disjoint partitions – is this a fair evaluation? (R2)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Missing punctuation after “...a superposition of elementary vector fields” on p.3 lines 140‑141 (R1).
  - The abstract is overly long, taking up about 1/3 of the entire introduction (R1).
  - It would be interesting to see results as a function of the distance between the disjoint class sets (R1).
