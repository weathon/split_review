## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PTAD, a reconstruction-based tabular anomaly detection framework combining mask modeling (in data space and a disentangled projection space) with prototype learning (association prototypes for inter-feature and inter-sample dependencies), all formulated as optimal transport problems. The goal is to address representation entanglement and lack of global correlation modeling. Experiments on 20 tabular benchmarks show strong performance relative to baselines, with extensive ablation studies. Reviewer scores are 6, 3, 5, 3, indicating a split: R1 and R3 were positive about novelty and thoroughness, while R2 and R4 raised substantial concerns about reproducibility, statistical rigor, and methodological clarity.

### Strengths
- The framework is novel, combining mask modeling (inspired by MCM [4]) and prototype learning (inspired by PTaRL [5]) within an optimal transport formulation to improve tabular anomaly detection.
- The paper is well-structured, math is clearly set out, and individual components are justified with influence demonstrated.
- Extensive experiments on 20 tabular benchmarks show the proposed method achieves best performance compared to chosen baselines.
- Comprehensive ablation studies demonstrate the relevance of each added component.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and justification of combination**  
  Each component is based on existing work (MCM, PTaRL, NPT). The paper does not sufficiently highlight the unique challenges of combining them or why each component is essential for the whole framework.

- **Presentation clarity**  
  The framework is complex and not easy to follow; the style is sometimes wordy with several typos (e.g., line 235 “mask value … concerning about …”) and inconsistent notation.  
  * *Notation issues*: Mixing `N` and `n` in lines 135–154; from Eq. 1 it is unclear how to derive `Z` as defined in line 224. The projection-space learning should be presented first if basis vectors are needed for the mask. In Sec. 4.2, symbols such as `θ_E`, `σ_β^k` in `Q(B)`, `P(π)`, and the purpose of Eq. 7 are undefined or unclear. In Eq. 4, `(z_nh − β^k)^2` does not define `z_nh`; it is unclear how a scalar and a basis vector of dimension H are operated.  
  * The term “nominal” is used for normal samples, which is confusing; prefer “normal”.  
  * A detailed pipeline description (as in App. C.3 of [3] or App. B of [2]) is missing, especially for handling categorical features and the mask token.

- **Reproducibility and experimental rigor**  
  * Only 3 runs (3 seeds) are reported, which is low compared to papers that use 10–20 runs [1][2]. No statistical tests are performed; some bold results are unlikely to be significantly different (e.g., Wine AUPRC 0.9323 vs. MCM 0.9269).  
  * Results are not reproducible: (i) the grid search for learning rate does not specify the objective used for selection nor the obtained learning rate per dataset (only Cardio is given); (ii) ablation studies are not reproducible without excessive effort; (iii) full easy-to-run code for all experiments is not provided.  
  * Standard deviations are not reported; F1-Score (used in previous benchmarks) should also be included.

- **Experimental setup and baseline selection**  
  * Dataset selection is not justified: only 12 out of 30+ datasets from ODDS and 8 out of 57 from ADBench are used. Why not follow the benchmarks used in previous work [1][2]?  
  * Baseline selection omits strong methods from PyOD such as LUNAR, KPCA, GMM [3]; reasons for exclusion are not given.

- **Methodological concerns and missing analyses**  
  * **Risk of trivial masks**: In Eq. 3, learned weights `W_i` for mask construction are not constrained. MCM [4] includes a diversity loss to prevent identical or redundant masks; PTAD does not. Can the masks collapse to identical or trivial solutions (e.g., no masking)?  
  * **Prototype learning from masked representations**: If the data-space mask is diagonal (possible without constraints), how can basis vectors contain meaningful information? The number of basis vectors is fixed at 5 for all datasets, but the number of possible mask types may be much larger; can they still disentangle properly?  
  * **Double masking and inter-feature dependencies**: Learning inter-feature dependencies from representations that are masked twice (data space and projection space) seems particularly challenging. How is it ensured that the NPT itself does not already suffice to discriminate normal from anomalous samples?  
  * **Inference bias**: During inference, both normal and anomalous samples are used to compute basis vectors and prototypes. If anomalies originate from a similar (but distinct) distribution, basis vectors could capture anomalous behavior, polluting the anomaly score (“anomaly leakage”).  
  * **Categorical features**: It is unclear how the data-space masking (Eq. 3) handles categorical features, and whether a mask token is included (as in [2][3]).

- **Additional questions** (overlapping with criticisms)  
  * What is the specific advantage of soft masking in the data space?  
  * How is “anomaly leakage” defined? In what situations does it occur, and how does the masking strategy solve it?
