## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Add-and-Chill Experts (ACE) for continual pretraining (CPT) of Mixture-of-Experts LLMs, adding new experts and lowering the learning rate for existing parameters to mitigate forgetting. Experiments use Fineweb (general text) and The Stack v2 (code) with evaluations on Hellaswag and HumanEval. Reviewers note the method is simple and the writing is clear, but raise concerns over limited experimental scope, lack of novelty, missing comparisons, and insufficient analysis. Reviewer 2 is more positive (score 5), valuing the analysis of forgetting factors, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 3) find the work preliminary and lacking depth.

### Strengths
- Simple and easy-to-follow method for CPT of MoE LLMs.
- Paper is well organized and clearly written.
- Experiments include the Mistral model, adding some generality.
- Analysis of factors affecting LLM forgetting (pretrained data size, model size, replay data size, expert numbers) is valuable and provides insights.
- Experimental analysis is consistent with prior work and intuitive.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited experimental scope** – Only two datasets (Fineweb and Stack) and one domain shift (general text → code) are used. Only two evaluations (Hellaswag, HumanEval). Reviewers question whether results are from single runs (R1). More datasets and smaller LLMs should be tried if compute is a constraint.

2. **Missing comparison with Chen et al. (2023)** – This work is cited as the most relevant, but no performance or training efficiency comparison is provided to support the claim that ACE removes the need for regularization.

3. **Incomplete ablations** – Table 2 lacks the setting `ACE (new=10, old=0, rest=0.1)`, which would reveal whether chilling old experts is better than freezing them. The setting `ACE-freeze` with `rest=0` appears to be a sanity check; the authors should show results for `ACE-freeze` with `new=10, old=10, rest=0` and routing enabled (`rest={0.1, 1.0}`). Also missing: results for `ACE*` / `ACE+4 experts` with `10/0/1`.

4. **Method too simple and insufficiently analyzed** – No explanation of router or expert design; no in-depth discussion of advantages and disadvantages. The paper reads like a technical report, not a full academic paper.

5. **Lack of novelty** – Similar MoE+CL works exist (e.g., *Boosting continual learning of vision-language models via mixture-of-experts adapters*, CVPR 2024; *Mixture of Experts Meets Prompt-Based Continual Learning*, arXiv 2024; *Theory on Mixture-of-Experts in Continual Learning*, arXiv 2024) with well-known conclusions: adding experts reduces forgetting, replay reduces forgetting but hurts new task performance. ACE’s contributions are incremental.

6. **Related work insufficiently thorough** – Missing citations for many MoE+CL papers; understanding of existing CL methods is not deep enough.

7. **No discussion of limitations** – Adding experts increases parameters; compute-per-token comparisons are not provided. Compresssion or other efficiency techniques are not considered.

8. **Mistral experiment (Section 5.3) raises generalizability concerns** – Results are presented with opinionated language (“It is likely… we speculate…”) and indicate ACE is not robust across different pretrained LLMs, questioning whether findings generalize.

9. **Unclear or counterintuitive results** – Why does using more replay data (≥90%) degrade new-task performance (close to 0 on HumanEval) when intuition suggests it should approach joint training? (R2) The claim “More pretraining makes a model more prone to forgetting” seems contradictory to the expected benefit of stronger generalization; the domain difference may be a confounder.

10. **Experimental effect not significantly improved** – The method does not show clear improvement over baselines, and comparisons are limited.

*Minority contrast:* Reviewer 2 (score 5) found the analysis of forgetting factors valuable and the paper well organized, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 3) viewed the contributions and experiments as too limited.
