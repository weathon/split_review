## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a probabilistic unsupervised semantic segmentation framework that models uncertainty and uses spatial smoothing to improve robustness. It builds on existing pseudo-labeling methods (e.g., STEGO) and introduces a Spectral Normalized Gaussian Process (SNGP) post-processor to remove noisy segmentation. A new loss function leveraging pre-trained Vision Transformer features encourages intra-pixel similarity. The method is evaluated on COCO, Potsdam, and Cityscapes, showing competitive quantitative results and better calibration against baselines.

### Strengths
- **Uncertainty modeling**: Modeling prediction uncertainty, especially in ambiguous regions, is a significant and interesting direction for unsupervised semantic segmentation (R1, R3, R4).
- **Novel loss function**: The proposed loss encourages learning similarities within pixels and contributes to performance gains (R1).
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides theoretical justification for the proposed method, aiding understanding of its robustness (R1).
- **State-of-the-art performance**: The method achieves solid quantitative results and outperforms various baselines; it can be seamlessly integrated with existing methods (R1, R2).
- **Novel methodology**: Gaussian process-based post-processing (SNGP) is not well explored in unsupervised segmentation (R2).
- **Clear presentation of SNGP**: The SNGP development is presented with a solid theoretical foundation (R4).
- **Competitive on multiple datasets**: Shows efficacy across COCO, Potsdam, and Cityscapes (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and introduction**: The introduction does not clearly motivate why an unsupervised segmentor needs to be uncertainty-aware, nor does it state which components are novel. The contributions lack motivation and context (R2, R3).
- **Method presentation is hard to follow**: 
  - Jargon (e.g., random Fourier features, maximum correlation loss) is used without explanation; acronyms like SNGP are not defined at first use (R2, R3).
  - Missing citations for SNGP, RBG, and related work (R2).
  - Equations are not rigorous: in Equation 1, variables \(x, x^{knn}, x^{rand}\) are undefined; indices \(h,w,i,j,m,n,c\) are used without definition; Equation 2 has \(\mathcal{L}_{total}\) without arguments; \(w\) in Eq. 3 and \(n\) in Eq. 5 are unclear (R2).
  - The method section lacks logical flow: components (SNGP, pseudo-labels, GP with Laplace approximation) are introduced without explaining why they are needed (R2, R3).
  - The training loss is presented in Section 3.4 with unclear notations, but theoretical justification appears later in Section 4.2; it is unclear which loss term is novel (R3).
  - Fine-grained segmentation (Section 3.3) is not motivated beforehand, and its necessity is not ablated (R2).
- **Qualitative results and analysis**:
  - Figure 2a does not clearly show whether SNGP predictions are better than STEGO; better examples are needed to demonstrate the benefit (R2).
  - Figure 2b shows variance images with repeated grid patterns rather than smooth uncertainty, raising questions about the uncertainty measure (R2).
  - Visualizations in the Appendix show significant noise in predictions, questioning the effectiveness of spatial smoothing (R4).
  - No in-depth analysis of how uncertainty in pixel embeddings helps in challenging cases (R3).
  - Examples in Figure 2 are too simplistic to demonstrate benefits for ambiguous regions (R4).
- **Ablation and comparisons**:
  - Ablations are weak: they do not isolate each component of the loss function (e.g., the three loss terms), nor do they ablate the need for the fine-grained segmentor (R2, R3).
  - No comparison is made with SmooSeg, which uses a smoothness prior to improve segment coherence; such comparison is needed to evaluate the smoothing approach (R4).
- **Performance gains and novelty concerns**:
  - Improvements over baselines are modest; Tables 1-3 show similar or lower mIoU on unsupervised metrics; combining with existing methods (Table 4) yields minimal gains (R4).
  - The proposed work appears incremental, primarily integrating existing unsupervised segmentation techniques with SNGP; the loss closely resembles that of STEGO but introduces more hyper-parameters (R4).
  - Performance improvements may be attributed to additional tunable parameters rather than meaningful advances (R4).
- **Scalability and generalization**:
  - The effect of higher-dimensional probabilistic embeddings on computational cost and overfitting is not experimentally evaluated; solutions are not discussed (R1).
  - Generalization to datasets beyond the tested benchmarks (e.g., medical imaging) is not discussed (R1).
  - The model's sensitivity to hyperparameters (especially those related to the loss) is not ablated (R1, R4).
  - Test-time resource requirements and scaling with resolution/dataset size are not addressed (R1).
  - How many hyper-parameters are used and how they are tuned is not provided (R4).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Incomplete references (e.g., line 766, 810, 818 “Equation ??”); some appendix content could be moved to the main paper to keep it self-contained (R1). The acronym SNGP is not defined before its first use (R2).
