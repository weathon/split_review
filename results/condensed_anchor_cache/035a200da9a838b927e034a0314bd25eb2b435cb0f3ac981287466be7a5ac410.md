## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PreWorld, a semi-supervised, vision-centric 3D occupancy world model that uses a two-stage training paradigm: self-supervised pre-training (with 2D labels via an attribute projection head and volume rendering) followed by fully-supervised fine-tuning (with 3D labels). A state-conditioned forecasting module recursively predicts future occupancy and ego trajectory. Experiments on the nuScenes/Occ3D-nuScenes dataset demonstrate results across 3D occupancy prediction, 4D occupancy forecasting, and motion planning tasks.

### Strengths
- The self-supervised pre-training is simple and intuitive; it upgrades training strategy without introducing extra modules at inference time (R1).
- Extensive experiments and thorough evaluation across multiple tasks (occupancy prediction, 4D forecasting, motion planning) show competitive results (R1, R2, R3).
- Paper is well-written, well-motivated, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).
- The overall architectural design is comprehensive and the state-conditioned forecasting module is interesting (R4).
- The method effectively utilizes unlabeled data via pre-training (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Performance improvement from pre-training is marginal and not significant. In Table 1, pre-training sometimes hurts performance (e.g., IoU for driving surface and manmade drops), and overall mIoU gain over OccFlowNet is only ~2%. There is also no analysis of why certain categories (manmade, vegetation) underperform compared to prior methods (R1, R2, R3).
- It is unclear whether the observed improvement is due to accelerated convergence rather than genuine final performance gains. The paper does not provide experiments training both a randomly initialized model and the pre-trained model longer until convergence (R3).
- The ego state-conditioned module lacks sufficient analysis and comparison. Table 3 shows comparable results even without ego-state information; this needs elaboration (R1).
- Contributions appear incremental: the forecasting module aligns closely with existing practices [1][2], and the self-supervised volume rendering for 4D follows typical methodologies [3] (R2).
- Figures (especially 2 and 3) have insufficient captions; the difference between solid and dashed arrows is not explained, and it is unclear whether the two stages are trained sequentially or jointly (R2).
- The claim of “2D rendering as self-supervision” is undermined because the best results use depth and semantic labels, which are not self-supervision; this invalidates the “semi-supervised” label (R2).
- The paper only evaluates on nuScenes; it does not demonstrate the data-efficiency advantage expected from a semi-supervised approach. Missing comparison against a baseline trained solely on limited 3D data without 2D pre-training (R4).
- The two-stage training process increases model complexity; the paper lacks analysis of computational cost (parameters, FLOPs, memory) and inference time for real-time feasibility (R4).
- The effect of different tasks (occupancy prediction vs. motion planning) on each other is not discussed; no analysis of loss function weighting or trade-offs between modules (R4).
- Questions that overlap with missing analysis: Why train in two stages if they can be trained jointly? Table 6 setting for “Our Copy&Paste” is confusing (R1, R2). Provide category-wise performance analysis and training-convergence experiments (R3).
