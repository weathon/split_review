## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Geometry-aware Score Distillation to address the Janus problem in SDS-based text-to-3D generation. It introduces 3D-consistent noise by warping noises from nearby views using the current 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and a correspondence-aware gradient consistency loss to encourage multiview gradient coherence. The method is designed to plug into existing SDS variants with minimal added computation.

### Strengths

- **Addresses an important problem**: The work targets the well-known multiview inconsistency/Janus problem in SDS, offering a fresh perspective that looks at random noise sampling (R1, R3).
- **Well-motivated and intuitive design**: The approach is clearly motivated, and the overall pipeline is logically sound (R1). The noising method correctly generates i.i.d. Gaussian noise while maintaining inter-view correlations (R4).
- **Effective across multiple SDS variants**: The method is shown to work with 3DFuse, GaussianDreamer, ProlificDreamer, and MVDream, and provides noticeable improvements in 3D consistency and appearance details over these baselines (R1, R2).
- **Computationally efficient**: 3D consistency is incorporated by directly adjusting SDS gradients, without needing 3D data to fine-tune a 2D diffusion model, thus adding minimal overhead (R2).
- **Novel contribution to 3D generation**: Although an extension of existing integral noise work, the application to 3D (rather than video) is considered novel in the field (R3). The correspondence-aware gradient consistency loss serves as a useful regularization to mitigate geometry artifacts (R4).
- **Good presentation and visualizations**: The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and includes clear visual supports in Fig. 3, 4, and 5 (R1, R3).
- **Minority reviewer (R1, rating 6) was more positive**: R1 rated the paper higher than the other three reviewers (all 5), emphasizing the strong motivation and compatibility with variants. In contrast, the majority were more critical, especially regarding experimental rigor and theoretical justification for latent-space operation.

### Weaknesses

- **Dependency on point-cloud initialization**: The method requires an initial point cloud (3D GS) to establish correspondences. The effectiveness of the approach may be heavily influenced by the quality and density of this initialization. Prior work suggests that shape initialization already substantially mitigates the Janus problem, so the net benefit of the proposed noising is not properly disentangled. A comprehensive analysis of different point-cloud initialization strategies is missing (R2).
- **Concerns about comparative evaluation**: 
  - In the comparison with ProlificDreamer, the authors use 3D-Fuse (a method designed to reduce the Janus problem) as a baseline. This choice conflates contributions and makes it hard to isolate the specific effect of the proposed method (R2).
  - Figure 7 shows that the method primarily reduces floating artifacts rather than fundamentally improving 3D consistency relative to ProlificDreamer. Similar improvements can be obtained by tuning CFG weights (R2). These observations question the actual novelty and impact.
- **Latent diffusion model applicability is unsubstantiated**: 
  - All experiments use latent diffusion models, but 3D-consistent noise in latent space does **not** guarantee consistent gradients in pixel space due to the non-linear VAE encoder (convolutional layers, activation functions). The original integral-noise paper (Chang et al. 2024, Appendix E) explicitly states that their method fails in latent diffusion models for video, offering three reasons. The authors do not explain why integral noise should succeed in 3D despite these same LDM limitations (R3).
  - As a result, the entire theoretical grounding may be invalid for the latent-space setting used in the experiments (R3).
- **Missing limitations discussion**: The paper does not address the limitations of the proposed method (R3).
- **Experimental evaluation is weak**: 
  - Qualitative results in Fig. 6, 7, and 11 show low visual quality; generated results appear oversmooth and unrealistic. The outputs strongly underperform state-of-the-art implementations such as ProlificDreamer (VSD) and LucidDreamer (ISM), undermining the validity of the user study (R1, R4).
  - No quantitative metrics are reported except a CLIP score in Fig. 13, but the number of prompts and seeds used is not stated; at least 25 prompts are needed for a meaningful evaluation (R4).
  - No video demos are provided to validate the 3D quality of the generated objects (R1).
- **Motivation and justification of components unclear**: 
  - The reasoning behind why 3D-consistent noising specifically improves 3D generation is insufficient; the discussion seems disconnected from the 3D score interpretation of SJC (R4).
  - The *correspondence-aware gradient consistency loss* is difficult to grasp: the rationale that sharp changes are likely artifacts needs clearer intuition, and the ablation in Fig. 8 does not convincingly demonstrate its effect. More results with varied seeds/prompts are needed (R4).
- **Implementation limitations and algorithm ambiguities**: 
  - The noising method is confined to 3D Gaussian Splatting (point clouds) and cannot be applied to NeRF or mesh representations (R4).
  - The algorithm appears to ignore occlusion: Gaussian particles not visible from a given camera view still affect the noise computation, leading to questionable correlations compared to surface-warping (optical flow) used in integral noise (R4).
  - The paper lacks a pseudo-algorithm or optimization-level figure that highlights differences from standard SDS. It is unclear whether noise is regenerated after each batch of gradient updates and whether correlations are computed only within the same batch (R1, R4).
- **Missing related-work discussion**: The paper should explicitly distinguish itself from feedforward text-to-3D methods (e.g., LGM, 3DTopia) and discuss the relationship to the noise-recalibration scheme in “Diverse and Stable 2D Diffusion Guided Text to 3D Generation with Noise Recalibration” (R1).
