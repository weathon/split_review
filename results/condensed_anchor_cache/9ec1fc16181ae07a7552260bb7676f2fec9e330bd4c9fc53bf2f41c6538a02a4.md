- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 6, 6, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies uncertainty in reward models for conditional image generation as a key issue and proposes Ctrl‑U, which estimates uncertainty as the variance between two images generated under the same condition and uses it to reweight the reward loss (low uncertainty → high weight, high uncertainty → low weight). The method requires generating two images per training step and backpropagating through the UNet. The four reviewers (scores: 6,6,6,6) generally find the motivation sound and the approach easy to incorporate, but raise concerns about novelty, experimental comparisons, evaluation metrics, artifacts, computational cost, parameter choices, and clarity of the exposition.

### Strengths
- The method is intuitive, straightforward to incorporate, and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 2).
- It effectively addresses the critical problem of uncertainty in reward model training (Reviewers 3, 4).
- It avoids adding extra modules to assess reward uncertainty, relying only on the generative model’s own output variance (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Quantitative results outperform baseline methods by a large margin on controllability and generation quality (Reviewer 1). Gains are shown across multiple conditions (segmentation mask, edge, depth).
- The paper is well‑organized and clearly presented (Reviewer 2).
- The motivation is sound and the overall approach is reasonable for improving condition alignment (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Questionable evaluation metric**: mIoU from another segmentation model is used to evaluate quality, but this may penalize correct generations (e.g., the green masks for windows in Fig. 3 may be accurately generated objects, not errors) (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparison with auxiliary uncertainty estimation**: No ablation compares the proposed method to existing uncertainty estimation via an auxiliary network (e.g., “Learning model uncertainty as variance‑minimizing instance weights”, ICLR 2024). This is a significant oversight (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty and efficiency concerns**: The core idea (generate two images, compute variance, reweight loss) is considered trivial by one reviewer. It doubles per‑step training time with reportedly marginal quantitative improvement. The reviewer requests reported computational cost before/after the method and suggests an alternative: train a lightweight uncertainty predictor on paired generated images to avoid generating two images per step, which could improve efficiency and be adaptive to different models (Reviewer 3). Another reviewer asks whether only the control net parameters need backpropagation, or if the full UNet must be updated (Reviewer 4).
- **Unfair comparison**: The method is applied as post‑training on a pretrained model (e.g., ControlNet, line 292‑295) yet comparisons are drawn against pretrained baselines. Improvement over the base model should be reported for multiple base models to demonstrate generalization (Reviewer 3).
- **Artifacts and quality trade‑off**: The loss can cause artifacts (e.g., linerart distortions in Fig. 3). The method prioritizes condition alignment over image quality, leading to visible distortions when the condition is imperfect. The paper does not discuss maintaining visual plausibility while ensuring alignment (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear parameter choices**: The optimal Δt (Eq. 5, Tab. 5a) is not clearly identified; a small Δt (1% of 1000 timesteps) is used but its mechanism for encouraging diversity is unexplained. A rigorous analysis of Δt’s effect is needed (Reviewer 4). The range and optimality of t1 and t2 across datasets/tasks is also not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Inconsistent results across base models**: ControlNet on SDXL performs significantly worse than on SD1.5 in Tab. 1, without explanation (Reviewer 4).
- **Figure and equation ambiguities**: The annotation in Fig. 2 and Eq. 2 is ambiguous—does the KL divergence compute between input control (c) and c1/c2 separately (as Fig. 2 suggests) or only between c1 and c2 (as Eq. 2 suggests)? (Reviewer 1). The relationship between t in Eq. 7 and t1/t2 in Eq. 5 needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
- **Motivation and intuition gaps**: The statement at line 74 (“the denoised image at t=0 aligns well with the input condition”) is not intuitive and requires clearer explanation (Reviewer 2). Also, the authors claim that directly regressing uncertainty leads to indistinguishable predictions (line 215‑217), yet their own method also requires additional terms to prevent indistinguishable predictions (line 239‑240); given double training time, the advantage over a regressor is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Generalization concern**: It is unclear whether the consistency prediction generalizes to different distributions (e.g., different segmentation datasets) and to different reward model types (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental detail**: The re‑implementation of CLIP‑Score for ControlNet++ (line 360, marked with * in Tab. 3) needs elaboration on why and how it was redone (Reviewer 1).
- **Uncertainty visualisation**: It is unclear whether the uncertainty prediction aligns with the reward model’s inaccuracies—i.e., does uncertainty reduce loss weight precisely where the reward model predicts a wrong segmentation mask? A visualisation of inaccurate reward predictions and the corresponding uncertainty map would help (Reviewer 1).
