## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a spectro-temporal relational thinking framework for acoustic modeling. It extends prior work (Huang et al., 2020) from the temporal-only domain to joint time-frequency modeling by constructing probabilistic graphs over spectrogram chunks. The model takes wav2vec2 features and concatenates them with graph embeddings before a classification layer; parameters are trained using a variational lower bound. On TIMIT phoneme recognition, the method achieves 9.2% PER (7.82% improvement over baselines), and analysis shows it is more effective at recognizing vowels.

### Strengths

- Several reviewers note that the biological inspiration and the motivation to model relational thinking in the spectro-temporal domain are reasonable and valuable. The extension from time-only to joint time-frequency is a natural step given human auditory processing (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The experiments demonstrate that the proposed method works well on TIMIT, outperforming several baselines and achieving a 9.2% phoneme error rate (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). The detailed analysis and visualization of the learned graphs and their relationship with phoneme categories provide useful insights (Reviewer 2, 4).
- Reviewer 4 found the paper original and clearly written, appreciated the joint modeling of time and frequency, and saw the learning objective formulation as enabling further extensions. (This contrasts with other reviewers who found the paper difficult to read and the contribution incremental.)
- The observed parallelism between human vowel perception and the model’s improved vowel recognition is highlighted as a positive finding (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Incremental contribution and lack of novelty:** Several reviewers consider the technical contribution incremental, consisting primarily of extending the relational thinking network of Huang et al. from 1D to 2D input (Reviewer 2, 3). The paper does not adequately distinguish the proposed relational thinking mechanism from the well-established self-attention mechanism; mathematically, stacked self-attention layers also learn pairwise relationships. A detailed mathematical comparison and an ablation where the relational thinking module is replaced by a self-attention module are missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing baseline comparisons:** No direct comparison with a self-attention (e.g., Transformer/Conformer) model using a similar architecture is provided (Reviewer 3). The paper also lacks comparisons with other graph-based methods for phoneme recognition, as well as spectro-temporal features (e.g., Carlin 2012) and spectro-temporal HMM-based recognition (Reviewer 4). The reported wav2vec2 baseline (9.98% PER) does not match the original wav2vec2 paper (8.3% PER); the source of this number is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Dataset and task limitations:** The experiments rely solely on the small TIMIT dataset, which is an old and limited benchmark. This is insufficient to demonstrate that the model learns a robust representation; evaluation on larger, more diverse datasets and under complex conditions is needed (Reviewer 1, 2). Model size (number of parameters per layer) is not provided, making it difficult to assess whether performance gains justify added complexity (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity and completeness of exposition:** The paper is difficult to read, with unnecessary suggestive language (“Thinking”, “unconscious”, “mental impressions”) lacking rigorous justification (Reviewer 1). Other reviewers rated presentation as fair to good, but key details are missing: the explicit loss function is never stated (Reviewer 1), and 13 pages of appendices still do not fully explain the approach. In Figure 5, axis labels are missing and the meaning of line weights is unclear (Reviewer 1). The feature mapping in Eq. 1 is unspecified regarding whether it mixes within frequency bins and whether $\Lambda$ is diagonal (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient analysis and parameter exploration:** The trade-off study between temporal and spectral context (20.80 vs 20.66) yields a marginal difference that does not support the claim that higher frequency resolution is more beneficial; a wider range of settings and a statistical significance test are required (Reviewer 3). Important hyperparameters—window size $w$, temporal dimension $d^{(t)}$, spectral dimension $d^{(f)}$—are not explored (e.g., through grid search) (Reviewer 4). The analysis of phoneme classes shows the model is more effective on vowels but does not explain why; confusion matrix analysis, graph embedding visualization/clustering, and speaker-level PER analysis are missing (Reviewer 1, 4). Figure 6 shows only prediction proportions, not error rates per phoneme (Reviewer 1). The suggestion to repeat Figure 5 for the t1f8 and t8f1 models, and to impose left-to-right constraints for causality, were not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **Reviewer questions that align with missing experiments/ablations:** 
  - Have the authors conducted an experiment replacing the relational thinking module with a Transformer/Conformer? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why does the proposed method not perform well on non-vowel recognition? (Reviewer 3)
  - Are the baselines trained with cross-entropy objective? (Reviewer 4)
  - Would imposing a left-to-right constraint between time steps for causality be beneficial? (Reviewer 4)
  - Could PER analysis at the speaker level provide further intuition? (Reviewer 4)
  - Have other spectro-temporal features (e.g., Carlin 2012) or spectro-temporal HMM-based recognition been compared? (Reviewer 4)
