## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses Exemplar-Free Class Incremental Learning (EFCIL) with a focus on the “cold start” scenario where the first task has limited data. It proposes Elastic Feature Consolidation (EFC) using an Empirical Feature Matrix (EFM) to regularize feature drift in important directions, an Asymmetric Prototype Replay loss (PR-ACE) balancing new data and Gaussian prototypes, and improved prototype updates via the EFM. Experiments on CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-Subset, and ImageNet-1K show new state-of-the-art performance. The code is provided and reproduces results.

### Strengths
- Addresses a practical and more challenging problem: exemplar-free CL with cold start (Reviewer 1, 3).
- Novel contributions: combination of EFM+PR-ACE loss and prototype rehearsal (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Careful reasoning and theory: chain of motivation, supporting derivations, spectral analysis (Appendix B.2) (Reviewer 2, 3).
- Excellent clarity and presentation; enjoyable to read (Reviewer 2).
- Code runs out-of-the-box and reproduces results (Reviewer 2).
- Achieves new SOTA on both warm and cold start across multiple benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-Subset, ImageNet-1K) (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- Comprehensive ablation analysis (Reviewer 1).
- Figure 2 effectively illustrates motivation (Reviewer 3).
- Method builds on and extends existing ideas (prototype rehearsal and drift compensation), making the approach sound (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Cold start definition and motivation (Reviewer 4, in contrast to Reviewer 1 and 3 who find it well‑motivated):**  
  The paper lacks a quantitative metric to confirm that the first task is “insufficiently large”. The experimental protocol (e.g., 10 or 5 classes in Task 1 on CIFAR‑100) is standard in CL literature; no justification is given that this constitutes a cold start. Due to overparameterization, a model trained on 5–10 classes may already have high plasticity. The necessity of framing the work around “cold start” is not solid; the method might be better motivated by addressing strong regularization in existing methods. Also note that conventional “cold start” in machine learning typically means zero data, whereas here thousands of images are available.
- **Insufficient ablation analysis across datasets (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3):**  
  Ablations (e.g., Figure 4, Table 2) are only shown on CIFAR‑100 or Tiny‑ImageNet. The trend that EFM+PR‑ACE loss alone outperforms comparisons without prototype rehearsal needs to be confirmed on all datasets (ImageNet‑Subset, ImageNet‑1K).  
  Reviewer 3 also notes lack of experiments on ImageNet‑1K.
- **Explanation of EFM is unclear (Reviewer 1):**  
  The calculation and aggregation of the derivative of log output probabilities with respect to features to identify “significant” directions is insufficiently detailed. The connection between EFM and the Fisher Information Matrix is mentioned but not fully elucidated; the exact mathematical relationship and advantages of EFM over Fisher in this context need more explanation.
- **Poor cold start results for memory‑efficient variants in Table 2 (Reviewer 1):**  
  The paper does not sufficiently explain why fixing covariance matrices per class leads to a large performance drop in the cold start scenario compared to warm start. A detailed analysis of the impact of fixed covariance matrices on model adaptation in cold start is needed.
- **Unknown task boundaries (Reviewer 1):**  
  The method assumes known task boundaries to compute the EFM and save the previous model. The paper does not address scenarios where data arrives continuously without task delimiters, limiting real‑world applicability.
- **Assumptions in problem setup (Reviewer 2):**  
  The assumption that exemplar storage is expensive/unavailable is debated (citing arXiv:2305.09253); the paper could acknowledge this discussion.  
  The assumption of training from scratch rather than using pre‑trained models (e.g., ImageNet or self‑supervised) is common in CL but worth mentioning as a limitation or opportunity; the method could be tested with pre‑trained backbones.
- **Figure 2 details missing (Reviewer 4, also Reviewer 2 about font size):**  
  Figure 2 lacks details on dataset and experimental protocols; no mean/std or multiple runs provided. The impact of varying initial task size or number of classes is not investigated. Additionally, Figure 2 and Figure 4(a) have tiny fonts that are very difficult to read (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor (Reviewer 4):** Typo in Section 5: “with with” → “with”.
