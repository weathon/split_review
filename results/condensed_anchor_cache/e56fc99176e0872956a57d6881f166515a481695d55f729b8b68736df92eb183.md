## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose InverseBench, a unified framework and benchmark for evaluating plug-and-play diffusion prior methods on five diverse scientific inverse problems: black hole imaging, seismology, optical tomography, medical imaging (CS-MRI), and fluid dynamics. Fourteen representative algorithms spanning guidance-based, variable splitting, variational Bayes, and sequential Monte Carlo categories are compared against strong domain-specific baselines in terms of efficiency, accuracy, and adaptability. The work provides open-source datasets, pretrained models, and code to facilitate future research. While the benchmark is considered timely and important, concerns are raised about metric sufficiency for medical imaging, hyperparameter tuning rigor, and the breadth of the “scientific inverse problems” claim.

### Strengths
- **Important and timely contribution**: Fills a critical gap by evaluating diffusion models on scientifically meaningful, diverse, and challenging inverse problems beyond natural images, with potential to improve generalization and standardization across solutions.
- **Diverse and practically relevant forward models**: Problems include linear and nonlinear operators, with/without SVD decomposition, and unique structural challenges (e.g., sparse Fourier, radiative transfer, wave physics).
- **Comprehensive method coverage**: 14 representative diffusion-based algorithms are benchmarked across four categories, offering a strong starting point for model selection and baseline comparisons.
- **Extensive and thorough experiments**: Breadth of experimentation is larger than typically possible, enabling high-level insights across problems (e.g., bias toward prior with out-of-distribution data, strong performance vs. conventional baselines).
- **Open-sourced datasets, pretrained models, and codebase**: Provides a well-curated foundation for the community to build upon and reproduce results.
- **Well-written and concise**: Clear introductions to complex inverse problems, well-thought-out analyses, and effective presentation of results.

### Weaknesses
1. **Metric sufficiency in medical imaging (Reviewer 1)**: Using only SSIM and PSNR may be insufficient; hallucinations can lead to misdiagnosis. A method to quantify hallucination contribution should be considered.
2. **Title overclaim (Reviewer 1)**: The set of problems is extremely physics-heavy, with only MRI as a non-physics example. The title “Scientific Inverse Problems” should be toned down.
3. **Limited hyperparameter tuning and sensitivity analysis (Reviewer 2, more critical)**: Discussion on sensitivity to hyperparameters and initialization for each method is limited. It is unclear whether all methods were optimally tuned across tasks. Notably, Table 11 shows that Reddiff, DiffPIR, PnPDM, and DAPS have more hyperparameters and tend to perform better in Figure 1, raising the possibility that other methods are genuinely worse or simply undertuned.
4. **Out-of-distribution investigation insufficient (Reviewer 2)**: The study of out-of-distribution behavior could be elaborated (e.g., whether better sampling or trained prior is key). This is critical for real-world inverse problems.
5. **Terminology “plug-and-play” potentially misleading (Reviewer 2)**: Among the 14 methods, only one (Wu et al., 2024) is specifically a plug-and-play diffusion method. The broader definition used in the paper is questioned.
6. **Missing domain-specific MRI references (Reviewer 2)**: For compressed sensing MRI, the following domain-specific plug-and-play works should be cited and briefly introduced: Tezcan et al. 2018, Luo et al. 2020, and Luo et al. 2023.
7. **Lack of detailed cross-task analysis (Reviewer 3)**: The paper would benefit from a more detailed discussion explaining which methods or categories work best for each task, and why methods competitive on natural image tasks (inpainting, super-resolution) may not perform as well on scientific inverse problems.
8. **Synthetic data generation unclear (Reviewer 3)**: Line 316 states that synthetic images are generated “from a pre-trained diffusion model” for validation and test sets (e.g., 105 images). The exact procedure and whether the 50k training images were also used should be clarified.
9. **Robustness to forward model mismatch (Reviewer 3)**: As a future direction, systematic investigation of robustness when measurements come from forward model G but are reconstructed assuming ̃G is suggested. The CS-MRI results already show PnP methods are more robust than end-to-end baselines, but comparisons across different PnPDP algorithms are needed.
10. **Natural image priors not benchmarked (Reviewer 3)**: It is unclear whether diffusion models pre-trained on natural images (e.g., ImageNet) would be useful for the scientific tasks in InverseBench. Such an ablation would be interesting.
11. **Figure 2 visual quality limited (Reviewer 4)**: It is difficult to see differences between reconstructions, especially MRI reconstructions for PnP methods which appear identical. A zoomed-in region or error maps would be more informative.
12. **Posterior sampling question (Reviewer 4)**: Are the reported results (e.g., Table 4) based on a single posterior sample or a posterior mean? This should be stated.
13. **Minor typos** (Reviewer 4): L161: “generally intractable as so various approximations”; L508: “inverse scattering on sources that contain more than cells…”.
