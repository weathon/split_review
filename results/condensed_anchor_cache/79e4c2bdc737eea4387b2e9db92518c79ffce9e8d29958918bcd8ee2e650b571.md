## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Structure Language Modeling (SLM) for protein conformation generation, combining a discrete VAE (dVAE) to tokenize protein backbone structures and a conditional language model (BERT-like or GPT-like) to generate structure tokens. The framework is instantiated as ESMDiff, fine-tuning ESM3 with masked diffusion. Evaluated on BPTI equilibrium dynamics, conformational change pairs, and intrinsically disordered proteins, SLM achieves 20-100x speedup over prior 3D diffusion models. Reviewer scores are 8, 6, 8, 6, indicating generally positive reception but with notable concerns about evaluation rigor and clarity.

### Strengths
- The problem of efficient protein conformation sampling is important and timely (R2).
- The coupling of dVAE with language models for conditional generation in the structure token space is novel for protein conformation modeling (R1, R3).
- The paper is well-structured, with clear theoretical background and detailed appendices covering training, inference, and evaluation (R1, R2).
- Diverse experimental case studies demonstrate potential for wide practical application (R1).
- The framework naturally leverages pre-trained models like ESM3, offering an interesting alternative to 3D-aware generative models that are computationally heavy (R4).
- The reported 20-100x speedup is promising, and results are competitive with SOTA structure-based generative methods (R3, R4).
- The approach is accessible and likely to be of great interest to the community (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons (R1 major, R2, R4):** No comparison with pure language models that directly generate backbone coordinates as text (e.g., [a,b]), which could justify the added complexity of the dVAE. Also missing comparison with base ESM3 under iterative decoding (only Gibbs sampling was used), so the true benefit of fine-tuning ESMDiff is unclear. Including benchmarks from AlphaFlow/ESMFlow (Jing et al, 2024) and MD datasets like ATLAS and MD-CATH would better assess generalization beyond the small BPTI benchmark (R2, R4).
- **Mixed evaluation outcomes (R2, R4):** Zero-shot ESM3 outperforms ESMDiff in several instances, raising questions about the effectiveness of fine-tuning. The proposed method sometimes performs better and sometimes worse across tasks and metrics, and the number of metrics makes it hard to consolidate overall performance. The authors should identify the most practically relevant metrics or propose consolidated benchmarks (R4). In Table 2 the best SLM variant differs per cluster without insight (R3).
- **Misleading table formatting (R2 major):** In all tables, the best method is bolded only among the proposed SLM variants, not among all baselines. This gives a misleading impression of relative performance and must be corrected.
- **Discrepancy between theory and implementation (R2):** The formal variational ELBO is presented with statements such as “we begin by maximizing the ELBO … with respect to the encoder ψ and decoder φ,” but the dVAE are frozen from ESM3 and never trained. The decoder is formally conditioned on sequence tokens c, but the ESM3 decoder is not. This undermines the theoretical framing.
- **Unclear novelty of fine-tuning procedure (R2):** ESM3 is already trained at all mask levels; the only differences for ESMDiff appear to be explicit time-conditioning and stochastic unmasking. This incremental change may not warrant separate presentation as a new method.
- **Unjustified multi-stage training vs. joint training (R1 minor):** The dVAE and LM prior are trained in two separate stages, while prior work [c,d] trains them jointly. An ablation or theoretical justification for this choice is needed.
- **Only backbone coordinates (R1 minor):** The model is limited to backbone atoms (Cα coordinates), excluding side chains. This is a significant limitation for applications involving protein-protein or protein-ligand interactions and should be explicitly discussed.
- **Missing model details in tables (R3):** The tables do not report model sizes and pre-training data for all compared systems, making it hard to assess the significance of SLM’s results.
- **Overly formal exposition (R2):** The lengthy discussion of general discrete diffusion is unnecessary since only masked diffusion is used; the core method could be presented more succinctly.

*Note: The minority reviewers (R1 and R3, score 8) are more positive about novelty and efficiency, while R2 and R4 (score 6) raise more critical concerns about evaluation rigor, clarity, and the incremental nature of the contribution.*
