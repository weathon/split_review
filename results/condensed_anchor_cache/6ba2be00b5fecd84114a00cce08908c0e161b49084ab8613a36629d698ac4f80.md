- Scores: 6, 5, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dec-ODE, a neural-ODE-based framework for marked temporal point processes (MTPPs) that decouples the intensity function and mark distribution and models the influence of individual events via a latent process evolving through a neural ODE. Reviewers agree the idea is novel and promising, with potential for interpretability and parallel computation. However, reviews are mixed: one (score 8) is strongly positive, one (score 6) finds it sound but missing key validation, and one (score 5) raises multiple technical concerns and missing comparisons. All reviewers note the lack of computation time benchmarking and insufficient empirical evidence for the claimed advantages.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and promise**: The idea of using neural ODEs to model the per-event influence (triggering kernel) in MTPPs is novel and under-explored, with potential for high impact (R1, R2, R3).
- **Parallel computing**: The decoupled structure allows parallel computation of integrals, which is a practical benefit for training speed (R1: "more computationally efficient"; R2, R3: "beneficial" and "facilitates parallel computing").
- **Interpretability**: The per-event influence trajectories give some degree of explainability compared to black-box neural MTPPs (R1: "more interpretable model estimates", though this claim is contested by R2; R3 also notes interpretability as a strength).
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written and clearly presented (R1; R2 and R3 rate presentation 3/3 and 3/3 respectively).
- **Evaluation on real-world data**: The model is tested on five real datasets against several baselines (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing simulation studies / ground-truth validation**: No controlled simulation experiments are conducted to verify estimation accuracy of the latent process or model parameters when the true generating process is known. This makes it difficult to assess bias, variance, and how well the learned latent dynamics reflect ground truth (R1: major concern, confidence 4; also noted as a question by R1).
- **No computation time comparison**: The claimed computational efficiency from parallel integration is not empirically verified. No per-iteration or total training time comparison is provided against any baseline (e.g., THP, other ODE-based models). Both R2 (score 5) and R3 (score 8) independently request this comparison.
- **Explainability claim not validated against existing decoupled models**: The decoupled structure (intensity = sum of event-triggered terms) has been used extensively in earlier literature (e.g., RMTPP, other kernel-based approaches). The paper does not compare Dec-ODE against such models, so the claimed explainability advantage over them is unsubstantiated (R2).
- **Missing baseline RMTPP**: RMTPP is a standard benchmark in the MTPP literature and should have been included in the experiments; its absence is not justified (R2).
- **Inadequate experimental reproducibility**: Details such as activation functions, neural network architectures, and machine specifications are missing (R2).
- **Missing derivation of likelihood gradient**: The paper does not show the detailed equation for computing the derivative of the log-likelihood with respect to model parameters (R2).
- **No discussion of limitations**: The paper does not discuss limitations of the proposed approach (R2).
- **Technical inaccuracies** (multiple, noted by R2):
  - In Section 1, the Neural Hawkes process (Omi et al., 2019) is described as a marked TPP, but that work did not consider marks.
  - In Section 2, the argument that modeling the intensity is preferred over the pdf \(f^*(t)\) due to “complex dynamics depending on the past events” is not specific to \(f^*(t)\).
  - In Section 2, \(\int_0^{\infty} f^*(s) ds = 0\) should be 1.
  - Notation \(\mathcal{N}_g = \{t_i\}\) is unclear; it may not properly define the counting process \(\mathcal{N}_g(t_N)\) used in Eq. (2).
  - In Eq. (9), the function \(f_\theta(h,t)\) is not defined.
  - The reference for Transformer Hawkes process (THP) is cited as “Yang et al., 2020”, but the correct authors are Zuo et al. (2020). This error is also flagged independently by R3 as a question.
- **Minor note**: R3 (score 8) explicitly agrees with the need for computation time comparison but does not mention the other weaknesses, indicating a more positive overall view; this contrasts with R2’s more critical assessment and R1’s moderate stance. The disparities underscore that the paper’s claimed advantages (efficiency, explainability) are not uniformly convincing across reviewers.
