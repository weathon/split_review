## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CF-MSA, a counterfactual causal inference framework to mitigate unimodal bias in multimodal sentiment analysis. It differentiates treatment variables across modalities and uses a new optimization objective for integration. Experiments on MVSA-Single and MVSA-Multiple show state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths
- Proposes a new framework based on causal counterfactual reasoning, offering insights for multimodal sentiment analysis (R1, R4).
- Effectively communicates complex causal methodology with figures (e.g., Figure 3), clear definitions, and formulas with context (R2). The framework is simple and easy to understand (R3).
- Successfully mitigates unimodal biases and ensures effective integration of heterogeneous modalities (R4).
- Many experiments conducted with comprehensive implementation details (R1); strong experimental results showing improvements over existing methods on both MVSA datasets (R4).
- The modal-unbiased approach has significant implications for improving multimodal sentiment analysis (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited (R1, R4).** Causal inference methods have already been applied in other multimodal tasks (e.g., Counterfactual VQA [1] and Counterfactual Reasoning for OOD MSA [2]). The approach does not introduce significant innovations specifically tailored to MSA (R4). The methodology closely resembles existing works [1-3] but lacks a clear discussion distinguishing its contributions (R2).
- **Claim of being “first to introduce causal effects into MSA” is overstated/invalid** because prior work [2] (Sun et al., 2022) already applied causal reasoning within MSA (R2, R4). The authors fail to acknowledge and cite these relevant prior works (R4).
- **The Intermodal Discrepancy Treatment module is identical to that in [1]**, with Lines 286-291 mirroring descriptions from that work. The Integration Strategy module employs the SUM fusion approach from [1] without citation (R2).
- **The Intermodal Bias Mutual Elimination Optimization objective lacks direct evidence** through distribution visualization (R2).
- **Model used is overly simplistic**: a standard BERT-based text encoder, a ResNet-based image encoder, and a multi-head attention fusion module (R4).
- **Missing LVLM experiments**: Despite claiming model-agnostic applicability, the paper does not evaluate on closed-source LVLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3.5) or open-source LVLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2) (R2).
- **Outdated baselines**: All methods compared are from 2022 or earlier (R1). Missing comparisons to more recent work such as CLMLF (2022), MVCN (2023), and MDSE (2024) (R3).
- **Limited datasets**: Only validated on MVSA-Single and MVSA-Multiple from 2017. More datasets should be added (e.g., TumEmo, HFM, TWITTER-15, TWITTER-17) or new ones constructed (R3).
- **Inconsistency with modality label handling**: Section 4.1 states that image-text pairs with inconsistent modality labels are removed, yet Section 4.7 provides an example where the text label is negative and the image label is positive (R1). If labels are consistent after filtering, the need for modality bias elimination is unclear. Additionally, the dataset provides labels for both modalities, but Equation (20) does not use the corresponding labels for training (R3).
- **Text/appendix mismatch**: The models and parameters in the main text do not correspond to the details in Appendix B.1 and should be corrected (R3).
- **Motivation insufficient**: Line 57 lacks supporting evidence, such as examples or citations (R3).
- **Counterintuitive ablation result**: Removing only text bias yields optimal results, while removing both text and image biases yields suboptimal results – this is not explained (R3).
- **Code not open-sourced** to verify model performance (R3).
- **Illustration of causal inference should be clearer** (R1).
