## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a point-supervised contrastive variance method (PSCV) for medical image semantic segmentation, requiring only one pixel-point annotation per organ category. The segmentation network is trained end-to-end with a contrastive variance (CV) loss (exploiting unlabeled pixels) and a partial cross-entropy loss (on labeled pixels). Experiments on ACDC (MRI) and Synapse (CT) datasets demonstrate improvements over existing weakly supervised methods. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the approach is clearly motivated, but raise concerns about fairness of baselines, sensitivity to point location and hyperparameters, insufficient theoretical justification, and several experimental protocol issues.

### Strengths
- The targeted problem (reducing annotation cost in medical imaging) is important and well-motivated.
- The proposed contrastive variance loss uses pixel-level variance distribution maps instead of feature-level representations, avoiding information loss from unlabeled pixels.
- Combining inter-image pixel-level contrastive learning in a medical segmentation context is interesting.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; the overall structure is clear.
- The method outperforms the vanilla Mumford-Shah loss baseline.
- Evaluations are performed on two standard datasets covering both MRI (ACDC) and CT (Synapse) modalities.
- The paper includes ablation studies and comparisons with existing methods.
- The proposed loss functions (CV + partial CE) provide effective exploitation of limited point annotations.

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair Baseline Comparisons:** Several baselines (e.g., WSL4MIS) were originally designed for scribble supervision; their reported performance is much lower than in the original paper (e.g., 0.768 vs. 0.872). The paper does not clarify whether these results come from direct application or re-implementation, and does not include methods specifically designed for point-supervised segmentation (e.g., [1], [2]). Reviewer 2 is particularly concerned about unfairness, while Reviewers 1 and 3 also raise this issue.
- **Sensitivity to Point Annotation Location:** The method randomly selects one pixel per organ from the ground-truth mask. This location may greatly affect convergence and performance; e.g., boundary pixels or pixels near other organs could be problematic. Reviewer 3 notes no analysis of this variability. Reviewer 4 adds that the supplementary analysis was done on CT data only (more standardized) instead of MRI, and that repeated training with mean performance and extreme-point annotations (max/min intensities) should be reported. The strong assumption that organs occupy limited spatial regions reduces generalizability.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Figure 4 shows up to 10% performance variation with different hyperparameter settings, and no practical guidance is provided for optimal selection. Reviewer 2 highlights this as a practical reliability issue.
- **Insufficient Theoretical Analysis:** The paper lacks rigorous mathematical derivation showing how the contrastive variance loss minimizes intra-class variance while maximizing inter-class variance under point supervision. The connection to established contrastive learning frameworks is unclear. The novelty is incremental—mainly replacing intensity with variance maps—and the link to level-set methods is not deeply justified.
- **Experimental Protocol Issues:**
    - The Synapse dataset was split into only training and test sets; no validation set is mentioned. It is unclear how the optimized model was selected without seeing the test data.
    - A post-processing step filters fifty pixels near left/right edges of the prediction as background on Synapse only. The reasoning and consistent application across methods/datasets are not justified; this may artificially inflate performance.
    - Two datasets are insufficient to support generalizability claims; additional datasets are recommended.
    - Computational complexity compared to existing methods is not reported.
- **Missing Analysis:** Requested are: ablation on different similarity measures (cosine vs. others), failure examples, and evaluations on ACDC MRI data with the same protocol to demonstrate robustness across imaging modalities.
- **Minor Issue:** The claim that pixel-level variance maps eliminate inter-image variations from different acquisition equipment/conditions is not true—variance maps still vary across such conditions (Reviewer 2).
- **Weakness Noted by Minority Reviewer:** Reviewer 2 is more critical of the method’s novelty and practical reliability, while other reviewers found the methodology sensible. Specifically, Figure 3 shows marginal improvement over vanilla MS despite the reported gains.
