## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel out-of-distribution (OOD) detection method that enhances feature-level separation by leveraging the Neural Collapse (NC) phenomenon. Under NC, penultimate features of ID samples within a class collapse to nearly identical points aligned with the corresponding last-layer weight. The authors introduce a Separation Loss (OrthLoss) that encourages OOD features to lie in a subspace orthogonal to the principal subspace of ID features, thereby separating ID and OOD at the feature level rather than relying solely on output discrepancies. The method is straightforward and applicable to any Outlier Exposure (OE) based OOD detector. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet benchmarks without additional data augmentation or sampling.

### Strengths
- The concept of applying the NC phenomenon to directly manipulate feature spaces for OOD detection is innovative and a fresh perspective compared to traditional output-discrepancy methods. The motivation is well-founded on the strong analytical foundations of NC.
- The approach is conceptually straightforward, easy to implement, and broadly applicable to any OE-like OOD detection method.
- Empirical validation is robust: experiments cover multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), several architectures (ResNet, WideResNet, DenseNet), and include ablation studies that effectively address most concerns.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with good visualizations that illustrate how feature separation is achieved.

### Weaknesses
- **Inconsistency in performance across datasets**: The method performs well on ImageNet-1k (Table 1) but shows less consistent results on other benchmarks (Tables 2 and 3). The AUROC improvements on ImageNet are relatively marginal compared to CIFAR benchmarks. The paper provides insufficient explanation for this variability, raising concerns about generalizability.
- **Clu loss utility is unclear**: Ablation studies (Table 5) show that the Clu loss alone is ineffective; it only provides benefit when combined with Sep loss and performs well exclusively on CIFAR-100. Its marginal and dataset-dependent contribution contradicts the claimed effectiveness, and the paper fails to clarify conditions under which Clu loss yields tangible benefits.
- **Two-stage training heuristic**: The method requires a manual choice between two training strategies (direct all-losses vs. initial CE then proposed losses). The switch point is determined by monitoring training loss, introducing a heuristic element that reduces practicality. No automated criterion is provided, making the method user-unfriendly and potentially inconsistent across implementations.
- **Dependency on NC property**: The method relies heavily on NC, which may not hold in all architectures or training configurations (e.g., on test sets). Only ResNet, WideResNet, and DenseNet are tested; transformer-based architectures like ViT or Swin are not evaluated. This limits the claim of generalizability across architectures.
- **Limited comparison on ImageNet**: The experimental comparison on ImageNet (Table 1) includes only two methods, while the DAL paper (the baseline) compared five methods in its Table 16. This insufficient comparative analysis weakens the strength of the reported SOTA.
- **Missing OOD test distribution analysis**: Figure 2 only shows training OOD distribution changes; the distribution patterns of OOD data during the testing phase are not visualized. Similarly, Table 2 does not categorize OOD datasets into Near and Far OOD, which could provide insight into whether the method works better on far-OOD than near-OOD.
- **Visualizations limited to two classes**: The embedding space visualizations show only two classes plus OOD samples. A plot including all classes would provide a more comprehensive understanding of overall feature separation.
- **Potential exhaustion of orthogonal dimensions**: The method assumes OOD features fall on new orthogonal dimensions. For large-scale ID datasets, these dimensions may become exhausted; the paper provides only brief justification without further empirical or theoretical analysis.
- **Minority (Reviewer 1, score 5) raises stronger concerns**: This reviewer notes the Clu loss ineffectiveness and training heuristic as significant practical limitations, and finds performance inconsistencies across benchmarks particularly problematic. In contrast, the majority (scores 8,8) are more positive but still flag the marginal ImageNet gains and NC dependency as open questions.
- **Additional criticisms from Reviewer 2**: There are metric discrepancies between Table 1 and DAL paper’s Table 16; an inconsistency in decimal places for average AUROC of Energy between lines 381-382; and the method shares fundamental similarities with KNN (both classify based on feature distances) without clear distinction. Also, the method’s requirement on network feature dimensions is not analyzed.
- **Questions treated as weaknesses**: 
  - *Marginal ImageNet gains* (Reviewers 2,3) – overlap with the performance inconsistency weakness.
  - *Orthogonal dimension exhaustion* (Reviewer 3) – listed above.
  - *Use of FC weights vs. empirical class mean features in Table 9* – could affect alignment with NC motivation.
  - *Testing transformers/imbalanced datasets* (Reviewer 4) – overlaps with dependency on NC and limited architecture evaluation.
  - *OpenOOD framework for Near vs. Far OOD* – missing categorization and benchmark standard.
