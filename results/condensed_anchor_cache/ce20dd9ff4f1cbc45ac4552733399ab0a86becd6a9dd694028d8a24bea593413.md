## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Temporal Adaptive Convolutional Intervention Network (TACIN) for estimating time-varying counterfactual outcomes. TACIN employs convolutional layers with radial basis functions to capture temporal treatment interactions and uses an adversarial objective to achieve balanced representations, extending existing integral probability metric (IPM) approaches to sharpness-aware minimization (SAM). The method is motivated by generalization bounds derived from a domain generalization perspective. Experiments on synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets are reported.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important and challenging problem of counterfactual prediction over time.
- Explores an interesting connection between generalization bounds via IPM (developed for cross-sectional counterfactual prediction) and domain adaptation with SAM via adversarial distributions.
- The generalization bound is mathematically sound and based on known results (one reviewer with score 6 highlighted this; two other reviewers with score 3 found it technically plausible but questioned practical relevance).
- The method is motivated by and based on the theoretical results of the generalization bound.
- Considers the role of time and tries to capture temporal interactions; experiments include continuous intervention settings.
- Paper is well written and easy to read; hyperparameter tuning and dataset details for reproducibility are appreciated.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing causal assumptions and identification.** No explicit causal graph is provided. The paper makes strong assumptions (sequential ignorability, sequential overlap) that simplify the problem considerably; under these assumptions, any reasonable representation learning method may suffice, and the difficulty of hidden confounders is not addressed. There is no discussion of proper time-varying adjustment (e.g., g-computation, inverse probability weighting, marginal structural models). It remains unclear how counterfactual outcomes under fixed interventions are identified from observational data—Eq. (1) is correct but no causal estimand is derived. One reviewer (score 3) noted that Eq. (7) does not relate properly to the target (counterfactual under hard interventions; P_test should be a Dirac delta distribution).
- **Confounding bias and balancing.** The paper misunderstands confounding bias in the longitudinal setting, relying on heuristic intuition rather than formal adjustment. Balancing (IPM minimization) does not reduce confounding bias; it reduces estimation variance. Over‑balancing can actually induce representation‑based confounding (citing Melnychuk et al., 2024). Without invertibility of the representation (as in Shalit et al., 2017), confounding may persist.
- **Practical relevance of the generalization bound.** Lemma 3 holds for a fixed representation Φ, but the constant M_Φ may change when Φ is learnable, so the bound cannot be directly incorporated into the training objective (Eq. 17). The bound therefore lacks practical relevance; there is no way to bound M_Φ in practice. Minimizing the IPM is not an adjustment for time‑varying confounding.
- **Inconclusive ablation studies.** The ablation study in Fig. 4 is not convincing: TACIN with and without the proposed adversarial objectives shows almost equivalent performance. The ablation is only conducted on the tumor dataset with γ=4; experiments on other datasets and settings are needed to generalize the claim. The impact of the proposed interaction function (F(t)) is not ablated—it is plausible that F(t) learns similar interactions without the explicit intervention function if z already includes treatments. Additionally, the authors should ablate the choice of backbone (WaveNet vs. RNN/LSTM), as was done in the related ACTIN paper.
- **Lack of necessary baselines.** The paper does not compare to existing temporal treatment effect estimation methods (e.g., https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.02038, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10651). Without such comparisons, it is hard to gauge performance or justify claims. One reviewer (score 3) noted that using convolutions is not novel for time‑series and no clear benefit for counterfactual estimation is shown.
- **Unclear motivation for domain generalization.** The argument that the target domain (counterfactual outcomes) is unknown applies to any regression task; the paper does not provide a clear reason why counterfactual outcome regression is specifically a domain generalization problem. If the proposed method is intended for settings where train and test distributions differ, visualizations of distribution shifts and performance as shift grows should be provided.
- **No formalization of “complex treatment dependencies.”** The paper motivates capturing “complex temporal treatment interactions” but does not define what these are or explain how the proposed methodology approaches them.
- **Marginal contribution.** Overall, the contribution appears marginal compared with existing IPM balancing methods for counterfactual prediction (score 3 reviewer). One reviewer (score 6) saw meaningful novelty, but the majority (three reviewers with score 3) found the technical novelty limited and the empirical evidence insufficient.
- **Other issues.** (i) The purpose of modeling covariates (Eq. 16) in the main task of counterfactual outcome prediction is unclear. (ii) Hyperparameter λ in Eq. 17 is not justified. (iii) Typos exist (e.g., Section 4.3 line 470‑472, “strategie”).
