## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies that safety-aligned LLMs have category-specific vulnerabilities and a bias toward generating negative/rejective tokens regardless of context, harming helpfulness. It proposes Token-level Safety-Debiased Inference (TSDI), a learning-free method that estimates and subtracts this bias using randomly constructed prompts, aiming to improve the safety–helpfulness trade-off. Experiments show TSDI can enhance helpfulness while maintaining safety, but the evaluation is limited in scope and baseline comparisons.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written with clear graphs and captions (R1).
- The topic is timely and relevant (R1, R5).
- The proposed TSDI method is innovative and novel – a learning-free, cost-effective debiasing approach that requires no additional training (R2, R4, R5).
- The in-depth analysis of training parameters (KL penalty, iterations), dataset quality/quantity, and the finding that data cleaning (threshold 0.25) does not improve safety–helpfulness trade-off are enlightening (R1, R2).
- The paper presents both theoretical motivation and experimental results for category-specific vulnerabilities (R3).
- TSDI improves helpfulness while maintaining safety, which addresses a key trade-off in safety alignment (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**: Experiments focus almost exclusively on the “adult content” category. Claims of effectiveness across “ALL” safety categories are unsupported (R1, R5). The paper should test other categories with varying behavior and quality–quantity distributions (R1).
- **Unclear practical benefit**: The helpfulness win rate shows only marginal gains; it is unclear if the method provides a significant practical improvement, especially in low-helpfulness contexts (R1, R4). The improvement in helpfulness is not statistically substantiated (R3).
- **Lack of novelty in initial findings**: The challenges identified in Section 3 (safety–helpfulness trade-off, dataset biases) are well-known to the community (R1, R3).
- **Insufficient baselines**: No comparisons with standard safety baselines such as SmoothLLM, Llama Guard, in-context demonstration, self-reminder, or safe decoding (R3). Using SFT as the primary baseline is unfair because SFT’s goal is not safety (R3). The improvements over Beaver v3 and SACPO are hard to see from Figure 1 without statistical significance (R3).
- **Missing definitions and implementation details**:
  - “Helpfulness win rate” is not defined (R2, R5); the meaning of “actual helpfulness” from GPT-4 is unclear (R5).
  - The safety score is computed from two evaluators; it is not explained how their percentages are fused or whether evaluator biases across categories are mitigated (R5).
  - The choice of β=0.1 and the threshold 0.25 for data cleaning lack justification or sensitivity analysis (varying 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4) (R2, R1 question).
  - Figure 2(a) has ambiguity regarding λ/β vs β/λ; Figure 4 legend and x-axis labeling are unclear (R2).
  - The points in Figures 5 and 6 are not explained – are they different hyper-parameter settings? (R2, R3).
  - Line 267 mentions generation corruption due to small β/λ and excessive iterations, but no supporting evidence is provided (R2).
  - The reference model in TSDI is not described; how is it obtained? No ablation study on the choice of reference model (R2 point 10, R3).
- **Problem formulation issues**: The claimed “different type of vulnerability” is ambiguous – how does it differ from conventional safety vulnerabilities? (R5). Furthermore, all methods (including TSDI) still struggle with category 3 in Figure 1, which undermines the paper’s motivation (R3).
- **Methodological concerns**: Subtracting bias measured from random prompts is not obviously rational because the degree of bias likely depends on the safety scenario (e.g., harmful vs unharmful inputs) (R5). TSDI only adjusts the first L tokens and ignores hidden states; biases may arise from internal representations, limiting effectiveness in complex tasks (R4). The authors should compare with work on hidden-state safety (R4).
- **Missing evaluations**: General model capabilities are not benchmarked (e.g., MMLU, HellaSwag, ARC) to ensure no degradation (R2). The effect of increasing data quantity for the adult content category is not explored (R3, from cleaning discussion).
- **Missing ablation on L**: The sensitivity of safety score and compliance rate to the number of initial debiased tokens L is not shown (R2).
- **Reference missing**: Line 54 (“Previous studies … suffer from misdirected safety evaluation”) lacks a citation (R2).
- **Multi-category applicability**: It is not shown that TSDI’s debiasing works consistently across all safety categories (R4 question).
