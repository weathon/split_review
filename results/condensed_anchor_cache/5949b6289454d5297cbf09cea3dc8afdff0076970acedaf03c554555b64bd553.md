## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Amortized Control of continuous State Space Model (ACSSM) for irregularly sampled time series. It extends Doob’s h-transform to a multi-marginal setting to define a conditioned continuous latent dynamical system, then uses stochastic optimal control (SOC) to approximate the intractable transform and derive a variational evidence lower bound (ELBO). To enable tractable computation, ACSSM adopts affine linear SDEs with known Gaussian perturbation kernels, allowing simulation-free estimation, and uses transformer-based parallel inference. Empirical evaluations on real-world datasets show superior performance in classification, regression, interpolation, and extrapolation, with training times under 5 seconds.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical framework:** The paper introduces a multi-marginal Doob’s h-transform and links it to SOC, deriving a tight ELBO for continuous-discrete state space models. This formal grounding is a significant contribution.  
- **Strong empirical results:** ACSSM achieves lower Test MSE and faster training (<5 sec) than baselines (including RNNs, neural ODEs, and simulation-based SDE methods) across multiple irregular time-series tasks.  
- **Computational efficiency:** The simulation-free latent dynamics (via affine linear SDEs) and parallel transformer attention make the method scalable to high-dimensional data, unlike classical SDE-based approaches.  
- **Replication details:** The paper provides clear instructions for reproducing the experiments.  
- *Note on clarity:* One reviewer (R4) found the presentation clear; however, other reviewers (R2, R3) considered the exposition challenging (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing Related Work section:** The paper does not contextualize itself against existing irregular time-series methods, limiting the reader’s understanding of where ACSSM fits.  
- **Heavy formalism, insufficient intuition:** The multi-marginal Doob’s h-transform is not intuitively motivated (why it is needed, how it differs from the standard Doob’s transform). The Feynman-Kac model and the SOC cost function (Eq. 7) are introduced without accessible explanations. These sections rely on dense measure-theoretic language that is hard to follow even with the appendix. (One reviewer (R4) considered the presentation clear, but the majority found the exposition difficult.)
- **Simplifications and expressiveness trade-offs:**  
  - The affine linear SDE approximation, while efficient, may limit the model’s ability to capture non-linear dependencies in complex or high-dimensional latent dynamics. The practical impact of this trade-off is not investigated.  
  - No ablation compares a simple affine linear drift function vs. the learned neural network drift.  
  - No experiments on larger datasets or partially observable environments to test scalability and robustness.  
- **Full attention and fairness of comparisons:** The model uses full transformer attention, meaning the control $\alpha_{t_i}$ is informed by future observations $o_{t_j}$ ($j>i$). This gives ACSSM an advantage over methods that only use past information (e.g., CRU). The paper does not provide an ablation with masked (causal) attention, nor does it clarify when masked attention would be used.  
- **Unanswered specific questions:**  
  - How many learnable parameters did each method use? Were they matched?  
  - What is the quantitative contribution of parallel computation to the speed-up?  
  - Why is the cost function in Eq(7) chosen? What is the intuitive interpretation beyond theory?  
- **Presentation quality:** Several sentences contain syntax errors or missing words, reducing readability.
