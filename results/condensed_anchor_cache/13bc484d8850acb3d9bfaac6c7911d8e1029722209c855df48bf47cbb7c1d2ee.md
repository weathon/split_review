## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper attempts to reverse-engineer and open-source the data curation pipeline used for CLIP (WIT-400M). It constructs a metadata set from Wikipedia and WordNet, matches text entries from CommonCrawl, and applies a balancing strategy to diversify the data distribution. The resulting dataset (MetaCLIP) yields models that outperform CLIP and OpenCLIP on several benchmarks, including 70.8% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy (ViT-B) vs. CLIP’s 68.3%. Reviewers appreciate the effort to reproduce an opaque process and the promise to release code/data, but raise concerns about novelty, missing baselines, evaluation methodology, and depth of analysis.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles the important, long-standing problem of CLIP’s closed data curation process; reproducing it is well-motivated and of clear value to the community. (R1, R2, R4)
- The authors plan to open-source curation code, training data distributions, and possibly the full pipeline, which could advance open data research. (R1, R2, R4)
- Extensive empirical evaluation across different data sizes and model scales (ViT-B, ViT-bigG) with large computational resources. (R1, R2)
- The paper highlights and validates a key design choice from the original CLIP paper – entry balancing – that was largely overlooked by later works (e.g., LAION, DataComp). (R2, R3)
- Results are often superior to CLIP, OpenCLIP, and LAION-based models on many tasks. (R2, R3)
- Ablation studies on balancing steps and comparison of different implementation choices. (R4)
- Writing is clear and the experimental logic is coherent. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The pipeline closely follows the process already described (briefly) in Radford et al. 2021, without introducing substantially new technical components. One reviewer (R1) considers this a reproduction effort, while another (R3) notes that the key contribution – entry balancing – is similar to deduplication strategies used in prior works [1,2], making the novelty incremental. A third reviewer (R4) does not directly criticize novelty but flags missing comparisons with other reproduction efforts. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Missing comparisons with existing curation baselines**: The paper omits comparisons with other open data curation pipelines, especially those from the DataComp benchmark [1] and other methods for recreating WIT-400M. Such comparisons are essential to assess relative quality, computational cost, and generality. (R1, R4)
- **Evaluation metric concerns**: The paper relies on average accuracy across diverse datasets (e.g., Tables 4/5). This is problematic because datasets have different numbers of classes and sample sizes – e.g., misclassifying one class costs 10% on EuroSAT (10 classes) vs. ~1% on Flowers (102 classes). Per-dataset results and counts of wins should be reported instead. (R2)
- **Lack of analysis on mixed performance and potential overfitting to ImageNet**: The method improves on some downstream tasks but not others. The paper does not investigate why, nor does it verify that the curation is not overly tailored to ImageNet. A detailed analysis of tasks where performance does not improve (including distribution shifts and retrieval tasks) should be provided. (R4)
- **Insufficient detail on data preprocessing steps**: Several filtering steps (e.g., noise reduction via regular expressions, URL/text deduplication, NSFW image dedup) are only mentioned briefly or in the appendix. Their effects on dataset composition and model performance are not explained. (R2)
- **Ablation on metadata design is missing**: The choice of metadata sources (Wikipedia, WordNet) and hyperparameters like PMI threshold or view frequency is not probed. Only top‑20 entries are shown, and they appear generic. An ablation that removes certain sources or tunes thresholds would clarify their importance. (R2)
- **No exploration of model-based filtering rules**: Beyond CLIP’s information‑density rule, the authors could have tested pre‑trained image‑text matching scores to further improve pair relevance. Even though the goal is reproduction, trying such extensions would increase contribution. (R3)
- **No analysis of the source of improvement**: The paper does not disentangle whether gains come from the raw data source (different CommonCrawl snapshot) or the curation technique itself. (R3)
- **Post‑hoc pruning comment questioned**: The claim that post‑hoc data pruning has limited utility (page 3) is challenged: such pruning still saves resources when iterating on model hyperparameters. (R1)
- **Reproducibility of the full pipeline**: The paper only promises to release limited artifacts (curation data and distribution). One reviewer (R2) stresses that the most valuable part is the end‑to‑end code to go from Wikipedia/WordNet to the final balanced dataset, and its release would significantly benefit the community. (R2)
- **Impact of randomness**: The balancing stage introduces randomness; its effect on final results (e.g., variation across seeds) is not reported. (R2)
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: One reviewer (R1, score 5) finds the novelty and contribution limited, while three others (R2, R3, R4, scores 6–8) acknowledge the effort but still list substantial missing analyses and comparisons. The overall assessment is therefore mixed, with the main weakness being insufficient novelty and missing baselines. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
