## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates jailbreaking LLMs using five distinct methods (Multishot, Mirror Dimension, Cipher, “Wrong Question,” Textbook) combined with chain-of-thought prompting, and reports increased success rates. Most reviewers find the work lacks novelty, proper evaluation, and standard academic structure, with the majority rating it a reject (scores 1, 1, 1, 1). One reviewer (score 3) considers the topic important and the CoT approach interesting, but agrees on missing baselines and clarity issues.

### Strengths
1. **Timely and important topic** – LLM red-teaming is highly relevant (Reviewer 2).
2. **Innovative use of chain-of-thought prompting** to enhance jailbreaking success rate (Reviewer 2).
3. **Empirical effectiveness claimed for each method** – though setups are not described, the five approaches are reported to work within some unknown configurations (Reviewer 4; noted with low confidence).

### Weaknesses
1. **No novelty and insufficient contribution**
   - The five methods are already well-documented in prior work; the paper merely runs existing attacks (Reviewer 1). The approach is a straightforward combination of manually devised strategies already in the literature (Reviewer 3 cites Zeng 2024, Jin 2024, Deng 2024, Yang 2024). The overall contribution is unclear – it reads like a superficial survey of known attacks (Reviewer 5). No comparison with existing multi-turn jailbreaking approaches or security benchmarks is provided (Reviewer 4). The selection of jailbreaking approaches is unjustified (Reviewer 4).

2. **Missing evaluation and baseline comparisons**
   - No baseline comparisons to help understand strengths of the proposed techniques (Reviewer 2). Evaluation against existing defenses is absent (Reviewer 3). The whole framework is not evaluated (Reviewer 4). There is no results section except a brief subsection in the methods (Reviewer 5). Claims in Section 3 and Section 5 are not grounded by empirical evidence (Reviewer 4).

3. **Poor presentation and unclear figures**
   - Figures 2 and 3 lack critical details: what question set is used? What does “effectiveness” mean? Why is it binary (0 or 1)? Which LLM is evaluated in Figure 3, and is it the same model as in Figure 2? Why does ASR differ for each jailbreaking method before the attack? (Reviewer 2). The main figure is overly simplistic with unnecessary gridlines (Reviewer 5). Figure 1 needs improvement in clarity and presentation (Reviewer 3). There is inconsistency between text (five methods) and figures (six approaches including “reference”); textbook and reference appear different in Fig 2 and Fig 3 but are not clearly distinguished or introduced (Reviewer 4). The reported effectiveness being either 0% or 100% is counterintuitive (Reviewer 4).

4. **Format and structure issues**
   - The paper is disorganized and reads like an unfinished draft: incorrect reference formatting with placeholders and question marks in the introduction (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5). Standard academic style is missing: no dedicated evaluation section, no clear contributions at the end of the introduction, no setup description (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Section 3 is over-divided with subsections every 4–5 lines (Reviewer 3). Important works are missing from the background (e.g., Many-shot jailbreaking by Anil et al. 2024) (Reviewer 5). Named methods like PathSeeker are not cited (Reviewer 5). The structural flow does not match typical conventions (Reviewer 3).

5. **Unaddressed claims and unanswered questions**
   - The introduction attributes jailbreak attacks to LLM architectural features, but this is never followed up (Reviewer 5). Graphs suggesting the reference approach works 100% of the time raise the question of why attackers would need the whole framework (Reviewer 4). The manuscript needs to clearly state which components are contributions – if the five methods are not new, cite existing works; if they are, the contribution appears incremental and lacks benchmarking (Reviewer 4).

6. **Minority positive contrast** – One reviewer (score 3) found the use of CoT prompting innovative and the topic timely, but still noted the same weaknesses (novelty unclear, missing baselines, presentation issues). The other four reviewers (score 1) saw no strengths or very limited ones, with one explicitly stating “I can’t identify any strengths.”
