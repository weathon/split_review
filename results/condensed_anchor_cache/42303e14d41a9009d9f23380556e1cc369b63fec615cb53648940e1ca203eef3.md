## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents RDT-1B, a 1.2B-parameter diffusion foundation model for bimanual manipulation. It introduces a Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space to enable pre-training on diverse multi-robot datasets (1M trajectories) and a self-collected bimanual dataset (6K+ episodes). The model uses a diffusion transformer architecture with modifications for training stability and performance. Experiments on real robots show zero-shot generalization, few-shot learning, language following, and dexterous manipulation, outperforming baselines. Two reviewers rate it 8 (highly positive), two rate it 6 (positive but with notable concerns).

### Strengths
- **Novel and important direction**: The paper pushes the boundary of large-scale robot learning, addressing the absent foundation model for bimanual manipulation (Reviewers 1,3,4). Aligning different robot embodiments is a crucial question for pre-training on large-scale datasets (Reviewer 4).
- **Key technical contributions**: The diffusion transformer with unified action space enables large-scale pretraining on heterogeneous robot data (Reviewer 1). The unified action space, especially the padding technique, is simple yet effective (Reviewers 1,2,4). Using diffusion models to represent multi-modality is a creative extension for bimanual coordination (Reviewer 3).
- **Comprehensive real-world experiments**: The model is evaluated on 7 challenging real-world bimanual tasks, demonstrating zero-shot generalization to novel objects/scenes, few-shot learning (1–5 demos), and dexterous manipulation (Reviewers 1,2,3). The largest dataset for bimanual manipulation is collected for fine-tuning (Reviewer 1). The model follows language instructions (Reviewer 3).
- **Scaling behavior**: Larger model and pre-training with large data significantly boost performance (Reviewer 1). The paper presents strong performance in scaling up to 1.2B parameters (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair or incomplete baseline comparisons**:  
  - Baselines (e.g., OpenVLA) are not pre-trained from scratch on the same diverse dataset; they are first pre-trained on single-arm data then adapted for bimanual tasks, making it hard to attribute performance to the diffusion transformer architecture (Reviewer 4).  
  - Baselines are not trained on the complete fine-tuning dataset, so the comparison is not apple-to-apple (Reviewer 1).  
  - The paper does not provide sufficient details on how baseline models are adapted to bimanual tasks (input/output modifications, extent of additional pre-training) (Reviewer 4).

- **Insufficient evaluation breadth**:  
  - Each claimed capability (zero-shot, few-shot, dexterity) is validated on only ~one task; evaluations on more tasks and existing benchmarks are needed (Reviewer 1).  
  - The evaluation is limited to ALOHA hardware and the 6K-episode dataset; more varied and complex real-world tasks and other robot platforms would better validate robustness (Reviewer 3).  
  - The MLP decoder ablation (last linear layer → MLP) is only evaluated on one dexterity task (Reviewer 2).

- **Lack of sufficient ablation studies and analysis**:  
  - The claim that the model addresses “nonlinearity and high frequency” of robotic data is not substantiated. Adding an MLP decoder may seem ad hoc since the transformer already has high nonlinearity; comparison with the UNet’s Conv1dBlock decoder is missing (Reviewer 2).  
  - The unified action space contribution is not properly isolated: no direct comparison with prior methods that use robots with similar action spaces or zero-padding strategies (Reviewer 2).  
  - The necessity of RMSNorm and QKNorm is supported only by a loss curve without them, lacking detailed analysis of how they are integrated and address instability (Reviewer 2).  
  - Ablations on multi-modal encodings (masking, cross-attention) are absent—it is unclear how much each contributes to visual/language-conditioned tasks (Reviewer 3).  
  - The analysis is binary (with/without); more datapoints (varying model sizes, dataset percentages) would provide deeper insights (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing details and unaddressed concerns**:  
  - The potential limitations of the unified action space for robots with vastly different kinematics or loss of unique features during standardization are not discussed (Reviewer 3).  
  - The use of frozen vision encoders: no investigation of cases where they are insufficient, or how conditioning on control frequency works in practice (Reviewer 1).  
  - No rationale or comparison for choosing diffusion models over VAEs or GANs for bimanual manipulation (Reviewer 3).  
  - Several writing issues: long sentences (Reviewer 1), mistaken citation (DiT reference as “Xie et al. 2020” in lines 83 and 319) (Reviewer 1), typo “date” instead of “data” at L76 (Reviewer 3).

- **Clarifications needed on experimental details**:  
  - Does RDT-1B fine-tuning use the entire self-collected dataset, and is it not fine-tuned separately per task? (Reviewer 4)  
  - For the “wash cup” task, the success rate calculation appears inconsistent: “get water” SR=50, “pour water” SR=87.5, “place back cup” SR=87.5, yet overall SR=50; how is the total computed? (Reviewer 4)
