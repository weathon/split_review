## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ShareFormer, a Transformer for image restoration tasks (super-resolution, denoising, JPEG artifact removal). The core contributions are: (1) Shared Portion Stripe Attention (SPSA), which reuses attention maps between neighboring layers to reduce computation and latency (up to 2‑7× speedup); (2) residual connections on the Value of attention to maintain information flow and improve trainability; (3) a Combine SPSA with Gated Unit (CSGU) module. Experiments report comparable or improved results with smaller model size and faster inference.

### Strengths
- The references and related works cited are very recent and relevant (Reviewer 1).
- A comprehensive Lesion study (Sec. 4.1) shows that deleting or permuting self‑attention layers smoothly increases reconstruction loss, providing evidence that shared attention is an ensemble‑like behavior rather than a simple shallow‑network repetition (Reviewer 1).
- For large and lightweight super‑resolution, ShareFormer achieves notably enhanced performance with smaller model size, fewer computations, and faster inference than recent state‑of‑the‑art methods, though an unfair comparison is noted (see Weakness) (Reviewer 1).
- Major efficiency gains: latency reduction up to 2× compared to CNN models without accuracy loss, enabled by attention‑map sharing (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 3 additionally notes up to 7× speedup on stripe attention.
- Method enhances trainability over other transformers by introducing beneficial inductive biases, allowing faster convergence (Reviewer 2). However, the underlying mechanisms are not fully explained (see Weakness).
- Generalizability is shown by compatibility with different attention mechanisms such as shifted windows (Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 3 (more positive) stresses that SPSA reduces computational complexity, that residual connections on Value offset information‑flow obstruction, and that ShareFormer achieves similar or better performance with fewer parameters than other methods. (Yet Reviewer 3 also notes that DRUNet, a non‑transformer baseline, offers a practical speed‑accuracy trade‑off — see Weakness.)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The shared‑attention concept already appears in ELAN, which also explores sharing across multiple layers (relevant to Tab. 8) (Reviewer 1). Residual on V and attention‑reuse are reminiscent of Restormer and ELAN; the group‑split approach resembles EfficientViT (Reviewer 2). The work is perceived as a thoughtful amalgamation rather than a fundamentally novel architecture. Reviewers advise emphasizing unique aspects.
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:  
  – It is unclear where the attention sharing occurs: Eq. (4)–(5) suggest sharing between (l‑1)‑th and l‑th SPSA blocks, but Fig. 2 shows sharing from SPSA to CSGU, and Fig. 3 places it after the residual connection on Value (Reviewer 1, Q1).  
  – Fig. 2 omits labels (a) and (b) while the caption references them (minor) (Reviewer 1).  
  – In Tab. 1 caption, Restormer is incorrectly said to use window attention, and “MHDA” appears to be a typo (it should be MDTA) (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:  
  – No ablation studies isolating the contribution of SPSA, CSGU, or residual on V. Architecture variants should be tested; efficiency and effectiveness differences between the original GDFN and the proposed CSGU must be reported (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  – The claim that a concentrated ERF from residual connections on Value implies amplified locality bias is unsupported. The citation of ConViT does not provide direct evidence; visualizations or alternative justifications are needed (Reviewer 1, Q2).  
  – The source of improved trainability from residual connections lacks detailed analysis and intuition about the mechanisms behind faster convergence (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair comparison in super‑resolution**: Progressive Learning with training patch sizes 128–384 was used for large and lightweight SR, whereas comparative methods (SwinIR, ELAN, DLGSA) were trained on smaller patches (48×48, 64×64), raising potential unfair advantage (Reviewer 1, Q4).
- **Moderate or incremental improvements outside SR**: Gains on grayscale denoising, color denoising, and JPEG artifact removal are not significant (Reviewer 1). Overall advances over strong baselines (SwinIR, Restormer) appear incremental, not radically transformative (Reviewer 2). Notably, Reviewer 3 points out that in Tables 4–6, DRUNet reaches similar performance with nearly 5× lower latency (at a 0.1–0.2 PSNR loss) and would be preferred in practice—contrasting with the more positive assessment of ShareFormer’s performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Reproducibility**: Dataset splits (training, validation, testing) are not provided (Reviewer 2). Parameter counts for models in Tables 4 and 6 are omitted (Reviewer 3, Q1).
- **Concern about training stability and attention dilution**: Adding residual connections on Value and a gated unit increases system complexity, potentially raising the risk of training instability (Reviewer 3). Adding V directly to the output may give it disproportionate weight, diluting the attention scores. The reviewer suggests introducing a trainable scaling parameter on the residual connection (Reviewer 3, Q2).
- **Flawed Appendix D analysis**: The comparison of attention maps between layers 1 and 3 is not the most informative; since sharing is done in pairs (0,1), (2,3), …, comparing (0,1) or (2,3) would be more compelling. Moreover, the redundancy should be compared between “sharing employed” and “sharing not employed” within ShareFormer rather than with SwinIR cases (Reviewer 1, Q3).
