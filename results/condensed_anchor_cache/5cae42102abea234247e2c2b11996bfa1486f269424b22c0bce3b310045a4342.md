## Merged Review

### Summary
SubZero introduces a random subspace zeroth-order (ZO) optimization method for memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning. It uses layer-wise low-rank perturbations to estimate gradients, avoiding backpropagation. The authors provide theoretical analysis proving lower variance than traditional ZO methods and convergence guarantees. Experiments on several language tasks show improved performance and faster convergence over MeZO, and the method is integrated with LoRA. The paper is generally well-written and addresses an important problem.

### Strengths
- Clear and well-written overall; theoretical proofs and methodology are accessible, notations are thoroughly specified.
- Addresses the critical problem of high memory consumption during LLM fine-tuning by maintaining only six matrices, substantially reducing memory requirements.
- Novel ZO approach that avoids backpropagation, accelerates learning, and reduces gradient estimation variance without increasing memory.
- Comprehensive theoretical analysis with convergence guarantees; all claims are supported by proofs and empirical investigations.
- Pseudocode and detailed methodological descriptions ensure reproducibility.
- Thorough ablation studies validate the contribution of each component.
- The method is modular and integrates naturally with both full-parameter fine-tuning and parameter-efficient methods like LoRA; practical adjustments for integration are proposed.
- Evaluated on diverse downstream tasks and models (autoregressive and masked).
- Shows performance improvements over existing state-of-the-art ZO methods (e.g., MeZO) across multiple benchmarks.
- Memory efficiency is highlighted as a key practical advantage.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contributions**: A minority of reviewers (score 3) find that most improvements over MeZO and S-RGF are taken from GaLore (layer-wise, lazy low-rank update strategies). The integration into different fine-tuning schemes was also studied in prior work (Zhang et al., 2024). Only the reshaping trick is considered new.
- **Missing important baselines**:
  - No direct comparison with vanilla LoRA (standard first-order) or its variants (e.g., AutoLoRA).
  - GaLore and other methods that outperform LoRA on various tasks are not included as baselines (Tables 2, 3).
  - Missing comparisons with existing ZO optimizers, such as ZO-AdaMU or HiZOO, which should be evaluated in this work rather than deferred to future work.
  - Cannot compare with Adam or AdamW, which are the default optimizers for LLM fine-tuning (e.g., for LoRA). The paper treats this as a limitation, but multiple reviewers (1,3,4) require such a comparison, noting that SGD used in experiments lacks performance (e.g., a reported 3% gap between Adam and SGD in prior work).
- **Need for fair and consistent experimental setup**:
  - Computations in Table 1 (memory) should include AdamW and AdamW+LoRa for context; also unclear whether it uses batch size 1.
  - In Table 2, the best metric in the SST-2 column under ZO-FT is not correctly highlighted; highlighting should verify.
  - Averaging scores across different datasets (e.g., Table 2) is inappropriate; use average percentile improvement relative to a baseline.
  - No direct comparison of SubZero with first-order optimizers (AdamW+LoRa, AdamW+FullParameter) under the same compute budget, accounting for gradient accumulation to match batch sizes.
  - In Figure 1c, it is unclear whether wall-clock time includes ZO overhead, and methods appear to have different computational budgets, complicating convergence comparisons.
  - Tables 2 and 4: unclear which results are sourced from the MeZO paper; MeZO performance appears lower than in the original paper.
  - Table 5 shows no memory difference between FT and LoRa, which is contradictory (LoRa should reduce memory).
  - Table 11 grid search: missing validation that optimal hyperparameters are not at the endpoint; search spaces differ per method.
- **Statistical rigor and scope**:
  - Experiments likely run on only one random seed; multiple seeds and reporting of mean/std are needed to assess statistical significance (requested by reviewers 3 and 4).
  - The SuperGLUE benchmark is considered relatively easy; more complex benchmarks (e.g., CommonSense170K, MathInstruct) would better demonstrate generalizability.
  - The ReCoRD task disproportionately influences average performance; excluding it makes fine-tuning scores nearly identical across methods.
  - In Table 3, tasks are selected where SubZero significantly outperforms MeZO; CB dataset is very small (250 training samples) leading to high variance; results on more datasets are needed.
- **Theoretical and methodological concerns**:
  - Cosine similarity between SubZero gradients and backprop gradients is nearly 0 (Figure 1a, Eq 12/13), raising doubts about gradient estimation effectiveness. Additional evidence is needed.
  - The reshaping technique improves performance, but its impact on theoretical guarantees (e.g., convergence proofs) is not analyzed.
  - The claim that lower variance directly benefits fine-tuning performance lacks proof; the paper does not analyze the impact of different batch sizes on performance (another variance-reduction approach through gradient accumulation is ignored).
  - In the convergence theory (Theorem 3), projections are fixed like GaLore, but SubZero re-initializes them with random matrices (“lazy” update). How this lazy approach supports the theory is not adequately justified.
  - The relationship between variance reduction and accelerated convergence is presented as two separate points, but faster convergence is generally a direct consequence of lower variance – clarification is needed.
- **Missing details and reproducibility**:
  - No code submitted (reviewer 4).
  - Subspace dimension is inconsistently denoted as both q and r; standardize.
  - Experimental settings for Tables 6 and 7 are not provided.
  - The paper states the importance of batch size for ZO methods but does not study its impact. Gradient accumulation is not considered as an alternative to reduce variance or memory.
  - Discussion on why QR composition of two random matrices is used (vs. using weight matrices) and how block-diagonal projection differs from random projection is missing, but these are important for understanding the method.
- **Soundness and presentation**: Reviewers 3 and 4 rate soundness at 2, finding the experiments insufficient to support claims; reviewer 2 rates soundness at 2 due to missing baselines and inconsistent reporting. Contribution is rated 1 by two reviewers, while others rate it 3.
