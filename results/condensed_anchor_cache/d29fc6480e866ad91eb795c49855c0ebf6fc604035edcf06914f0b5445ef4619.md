## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis of steerable (equivariant) features in 3D geometric GNNs, focusing on k-hop architectures. A key lemma (Lemma 1) establishes a correspondence between equivariant and invariant features via an input-dependent group element, showing that any equivariant function can be rewritten as an invariant function times a group action. Using this, the authors argue that k-hop invariant GNNs lose geometric information between local structures, while equivariant GNNs do not. They further claim that the expressiveness of steerable features is primarily determined by the feature dimension, not by the highest type-\(L\) used, i.e., increasing \(L\) at fixed dimension does not essentially improve performance. Experiments on toy data, OC20 (IS2RE, S2EF) with eSCN, EquiformerV2, and MACE support the claims.

### Strengths
- **Topical and practically relevant**: The problem of geometric GNN expressivity is timely, and the paper gives prescriptive insights for hyperparameter selection (e.g., choosing \(L\) and channels). (Reviewer 1)
- **Novel theoretical approach**: The use of equivariant moving frames to connect invariant and equivariant features is creative and uncommon in the literature. (Reviewer 1)
- **Interesting core observations**: The correspondence between equivariant and invariant functions (Lemma 1) and the conjecture that high-order representations do not matter beyond dimension are novel and receive both theoretical and empirical evidence. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Clear and well-written**: The paper is generally clear, well-structured, and the theoretical claims are supported by large-scale experiments on cutting-edge architectures (OpenCatalyst). (Reviewer 1; partially echoed by Reviewer 3: "high quality and clarity")
- **Number of channels vs. order**: The idea of comparing models at fixed feature dimension is praised. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Corollary 3 overclaims**: The claim that representation choice is irrelevant up to dimension (Corollary 3, Remark 3) conflates information availability with **efficient computability**. Converting between representations may require computing Wigner D matrices of high \(L\), which could be arbitrarily complex for a given architecture. This distinction is critical and not addressed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Informal/vague core claims**: Many statements (e.g., “there is no fundamental distinction…”, “analyzing message passing using invariant features is reasonable”) lack formal precision. (Reviewer 1)
- **Correspondence lemma (Lemma 1) is non-constructive**: The mapping \(\lambda\) uses a canonical element (\(c\)) that exists set-theoretically but is not in general continuous or well-behaved. Hence an equivariant “nice” aggregation function corresponds to a “wild” invariant function. This limitation should be explicitly discussed. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Inconsistent narrative on invariant vs. equivariant**: The paper both claims a one-to-one correspondence (Lemma 1) and then asserts that equivariant features are superior. The logic that invariants lose geometric information while equivariants do not is not clearly justified given the mapping. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing related work and novelty concerns**:
  - The paper does not cite or compare to key prior work: **Villar et al. (2022)** (“Scalars are universal”)—which contains similar or identical results (Lemma 3 on invariant features)—**Puny et al. (frame averaging)**, **Smidt et al. (2021)** on equivariance symmetry constraints, and **Du et al. (2023) (LeftNet)** which uses moving frames for two-hop geometric GNNs. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
  - Reviewer 4 argues that the main contributions (separation of scalar vs. geometric information, expressivity of \(k\)-hop) largely overlap with [16] (likely Villar et al.), and that the conclusion about dimension-dependence is essentially “oversquashing” known from prior work, yet the term “oversquashing” is absent. (Reviewer 4)
- **Definitional clarity issues**:
  - **\(k\)-hop**: The role of \(k\) is never clearly defined, and it is unclear how a \(k\)-hop GNN differs from a 1-hop GNN with \(k\) layers. This is essential for the main claims. (Reviewers 1, 4)
  - **\(V_0^{\oplus d}\)**: \(d\) is not defined early enough; the notation is confusing. (Reviewer 4)
  - **Steerable features**: The paper should clarify what it means for a vector to be “steerable” (e.g., \(x=[1,2,5]\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)). (Reviewer 2)
  - **Faithfulness**: Remark 3 is vague; Corollary 3 does not require faithful action, yet faithfulness may be critical for the claim \(0<1=2=3=\dots\). (Reviewer 2)
  - **Action of \(\rho(g_X)\) on \(\lambda(X)\)**: It is unclear how \(\rho(g_X)\) acts on a tuple of scalars; the mapping should be made explicit (e.g., the scalars are viewed in \(V\)). (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - **\(2l+1\) vs. \(2l+2\)**: For O(3) one would expect an extra component; the authors should clarify. (Reviewer 4)
  - **Eq. 3bis/4 typo**: The gothic \(\mathfrak{g}\) appears inconsistently; the term \(g^{-1}\cdot x_{ij}\) appears equivariant, not invariant. (Reviewer 4)
- **Experimental weaknesses**:
  - **Table 2**: eSCN is SO(3)-equivariant while the task (Q9) is O(3); the red (interesting) cases may reflect this mismatch, not the paper’s point. Also, the table is not clearly distinguished from [16] Table 1. (Reviewer 4)
  - **Table 3 (IS2RE)**: Missing total number of parameters, training time, and GPU memory. The fixed-epoch comparison (epoch=12) could be supplemented with more thorough validation curves. (Reviewer 4)
  - **Table 4 (S2EF)**: No clear conclusion emerges. (Reviewer 4)
  - **Table 1 compute check**: Line 3 appears miscalculated (\((1+3+5+7+9+11+13)\times 256 = 12544\), not 7424). (Reviewer 4)
  - **Choice of \(L\) in experiments**: Why use \(L=2\) and higher if the paper claims \(L=1\) is sufficient? (Reviewer 3)
  - **EquiformerV2 (invariant) does well Table 2**, potentially contradicting the theoretical advantage of equivariant features. (Reviewer 2)
- **Other missing discussion**:
  - The paper does not address whether the choice of representation affects universality beyond dimension, which is a relevant theoretical question. (Reviewer 1)
  - The term “oversquashing” (used in [16]) should appear, or the authors should explain why their dimension-claim is distinct from oversquashing. (Reviewer 4)
  - The claim that “propagating steerable features of type \(\ge 1\) automatically captures geometry” is vague and already known from [16]. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Page 2: stabilizer is a group, not just a set. (Reviewer 2)
  - Page 3: “k-hop distinct” definition likely has typos; the last equality should be an inequality. (Reviewers 1, 2)
  - Page 3: “Given a geometric graph…” is not a full sentence. (Reviewer 1)
  - Lemma 1: \(V_0\) not defined; the action of \(\rho(g)\) should map \(V\) to \(V\) (here it maps \(V_0^d\) to \(V\)). (Reviewers 1, 2)
  - Page 4: “for simplicity we represent \(g\) as \(g_X\)” appears to swap \(g\) and \(g_X\). (Reviewer 2)
  - Theorem 2: \(V_{l,\text{aug}}\) not defined earlier. (Reviewer 2)
  - Page 9: “our work has evoked” wording is awkward. (Reviewer 2)
  - Calligraphic \(\mathfrak{g}\) (Lie algebra) is used for a group element; regular \(g\) is recommended. (Reviewer 1)
