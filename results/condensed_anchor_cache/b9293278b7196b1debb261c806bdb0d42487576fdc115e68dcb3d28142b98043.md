## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Conditional MAE, a multi-shot masking strategy for MAE that applies additional masking on hidden representations of the encoder, conditioned on previous unmasked representations. It presents an extensive empirical study of masking ratios, number of stages, and layer choices using ViT‑S/16 and ViT‑B/16 pretrained on ImageNet‑100, with downstream evaluation on classification (fine‑grained datasets), COCO object detection, and ADE20K semantic segmentation. Reviewers were split: one reviewer (score 8) praised the originality and thoroughness of the experiments, while three reviewers (scores 5, 3, 3) raised major concerns about novelty, theoretical depth, the limited experimental setup (ImageNet‑100, only two model sizes), and the practical significance of the gains.

### Strengths
- **Originality and systematic study**: The paper provides the first systematic empirical study of multi‑shot masking in MAE, addressing a commonly overlooked aspect of masked image modeling. Despite prior work on progressive masking (e.g., generative modeling) and adaptive strategies, the focused investigation on multi‑layer masking stages in MAE is novel. (R1, R2)
- **Extensive experiments**: The analysis covers multiple masking ratios, four equally‑spaced layers, two model sizes, and a wide range of evaluations: linear probing / fine‑tuning, Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) of representations, attention distance and entropy, robustness under occlusion and shuffling, and transfer learning to COCO detection and ADE20K segmentation. (R1, R2)
- **Clarity**: The paper is well‑written, with clear logic, well‑organized results, and carefully designed figures that effectively communicate the key findings and takeaways. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Significance**: The findings (e.g., one‑shot masking at 75% is optimal; two‑shot with large first and small second ratio is helpful) provide actionable insights and create potential to improve MAE‑based methods. (R1, R2)
- **Performance gains**: The proposed method shows improved performance on multiple downstream tasks compared to the standard MAE baseline. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and related work**:
    - No citation of prior work on progressive masking in generative models or adaptive masking strategies in vision/language. (R1)
    - Many existing studies on mask strategies for MAE are not compared; VideoMAE v2 already proposed multi‑shot masking in MAE, undermining claims of being first. (R2, R3)
    - The method is largely an empirical technique without a novel technical contribution; several findings (e.g., 75% optimal one‑shot) are well‑known in the community. (R4)
- **Lack of theoretical grounding**:
    - No theoretical analysis or discussion explaining why specific masking ratios and layer choices are optimal, or how multi‑shot masking affects information flow and receptive fields. (R1)
    - Observations such as the non‑positive correlation between linear probing and fine‑tuning performance are straightforward and already known. (R2)
    - The motivation in the introduction is confusing and contains unsupported claims about robustness (e.g., “interior robustness” of MAE). (R3)
- **Experimental setup limitations**:
    - Pretraining on ImageNet‑100 (not the standard ImageNet‑1K) limits the generalizability of conclusions; data scale and diversity significantly affect results. (R1, R3)
    - Scalability analysis uses only two model sizes, which is insufficient to draw robust conclusions. (R1)
    - Downstream tasks and datasets are not the most commonly used benchmarks; the experimental setting deviates from the original MAE conditions, making comparisons unclear. (R3)
    - The three‑shot masking setting is significantly worse than the baseline (Table 5), indicating a major limitation of the multi‑shot approach. (R4)
    - The impact of mask ratio tuning on different backbones is unclear; manual selection may not transfer across architectures. (R2)
    - A discrepancy in Figure 4 is noted: improvement from layer0‑6 to layer0‑9 is described as significant but the graph shows only a small difference from baseline, requiring explanation. (R2)
- **Minor issues**:
    - Grammar errors (e.g., “We need reconstruct two targets”, “pretaining loss”). (R1)
    - Typos: “fine‑grind” should be “fine‑grained”. (R4)
    - Unclear baseline description in a figure caption (the dashed line for one‑shot with ratios 0.75 and 0.9 is ambiguous). (R2)
