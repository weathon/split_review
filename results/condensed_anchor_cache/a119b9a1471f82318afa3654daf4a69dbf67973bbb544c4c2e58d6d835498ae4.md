## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PWM, a model-based RL algorithm that pre-trains a large multi-task world model on offline data and then learns continuous control policies via first-order gradient optimization through the differentiable model. The approach employs task embeddings and SimNorm activation (similar to TD-MPC2) for the world model and uses first-order optimization (similar to SHAC) for policy learning. The method is evaluated on single-task (DMC, Metaworld) and multi-task (80-task Metaworld) settings, achieving higher rewards than TD-MPC2 and, in some cases, outperforming SHAC which relies on ground-truth dynamics. The authors provide didactic examples to argue that a regularized world model prioritizing smoothness over accuracy is beneficial for policy learning.

### Strengths
- **Clear writing and informative exposition**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (reviewers 1, 4). The pedagogical examples help convey the key idea effectively (reviewers 3, 4). 
- **Comprehensive and solid experimental evaluation**: Experiments are extensive, covering single-task and multi-task settings with multiple baselines (reviewers 1, 2, 3). Comparison with a method using ground-truth dynamics (SHAC) is particularly valuable and compelling (reviewer 2). Ablation studies on environment dynamics and world model regularization are insightful (reviewer 2). 
- **Reproducibility**: Detailed experimental settings and code are provided, benefiting the community (reviewer 3). 
- **Promising sample efficiency**: Learning a task within 10 minutes (of computation) shows practical potential (reviewers 2, 4). 

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and unclear contribution boundaries**:
  - The method largely combines components from TD-MPC2 (multi-task world model, task embedding, SimNorm) and SHAC (first-order gradient policy learning) (reviewer 1). 
  - The idea of policy optimization via pathwise derivatives of a learned world model was already proposed in MAAC, which is not cited or differentiated (reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 expects analysis or experiments to show differences.
  - The actor-critic formulation (Equations 6–9) is almost identical to the Dreamer series (reviewer 4). The claim that world models rely on gradient-free optimization holds for TD-MPC2 but not for Dreamer, which has used first-order gradients since DreamerV1 (reviewer 4). 
  - Contributions to two subproblems—objective mismatch and multi-task RL—are not groundbreaking: objective mismatch is well-known since Lambert et al. (2020), and MBRL is already known to benefit multi-task learning (reviewer 4). 
- **Missing comparisons with DreamerV3**: 
  - DreamerV3 is a powerful baseline capable of handling multiple domains with a single configuration. It should be compared in the single-task setting (Figure 5) (reviewer 1) and in the multi-task setting (reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 also asks how PWM differs from Dreamer beyond implementation details. 
- **Contradictory results on Metaworld (Figure 6, left)**: In the MT80 setting, the left plot shows PWM achieving significantly lower reward than TD-MPC2, which contradicts the statement "PWM achieves higher reward than TD-MPC2 without the need for online planning." This discrepancy either arises from a drafting error or requires careful discussion (reviewers 1, 2, 4). 
- **Performance on high-dimensional tasks relative to SHAC (Figure 13)**: PWM underperforms SHAC on Anymal, Humanoid, and SHU Humanoid. This may suggest that world model accuracy (rather than smoothness) is more critical in complex environments, or that the world model lacks sufficient training data or regularization (reviewers 1, 2). The curves for some tasks are incomplete (reviewer 3). 
- **Missing total training time comparison**: The paper does not compare the total training time (including world model pre-training) against model-free methods like PPO and SAC (reviewer 3). The meaning of "10 minutes" (gameplay? computation? on one GPU?) is unclear (reviewer 2). 
- **Dataset origin and distributional shift concerns**: 
  - It is unclear what source algorithm generated the offline data (e.g., PPO) and how much results depend on that (reviewer 2). 
  - Since no online interactions are used for the target policy, PWM could be considered an offline method; it may suffer from distributional shift where the world model overestimates values for unseen state-action pairs (reviewer 3). 
  - It is not stated whether successful task demonstrations are present in the dataset and whether the task data must be included in the initial world model training (reviewer 2). 
- **Larger variances in Tables 3 and 4**: PWM shows much larger standard deviations than model-free methods. The reasons and potential mitigation are not explained (reviewer 3). 
- **Confusing didactic example (Figure 2)**:
  - The middle plot is unclear: what is the y‑axis? What does a negative value mean? The symbol θ may be overloaded (reviewer 2). 
  - The example appears to demonstrate a flat J (optimality gap) rather than a non-differentiable discontinuity, making the connection to contact dynamics confusion. It is not evident why SimNorm reduces the optimality gap (reviewer 2). 
- **Need for more detailed diagram**: A diagram illustrating the model architecture and policy training pipeline (more detailed than Figure 1) and clarifying differences from TD-MPC2 would be helpful (reviewer 2). 
- **Unclear notation and definitions**:
  - The variable V in Equation (4) is confusing because it seems unrelated to Equation (2) (reviewer 2). 
  - The means of m and n in Lines 348–349 are not defined (reviewer 1). 
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters**: It is unclear whether performance is sensitive to the SimNorm hyperparameters (reviewer 2). 
- **Scenarios where smoothness hurts**: The paper does not discuss scenarios where reducing world model accuracy for smoothness could be problematic (e.g., transfer across tasks with shared dynamics but different reward structures). The discrepancy on mw-stick-pull may be an example (reviewer 2). 
- **Unanswered experimental details**:
  - Does the baseline TD-MPC2 use the same horizon H=16 as PWM? (reviewer 4) 
  - Why does PWM benefit from smaller batch sizes? (reviewer 4) 
  - How is "high-dimensional" defined? "Hopper" is described as high-dimensional but has only 11‑dim observation and 3‑dim action (reviewer 3). 
- **Typos and formatting issues**: Multiple typos across the paper: Fig. 1 caption ("prpose"), line 188 ("optimality hap"), line 377 ("generalizble"), line 036 ("prpose"); incorrect citation format in Sec. 4.2; missing parentheses in Equation (2) (should be γ^{h-t+1}); missing ∇ symbols in Algorithm 1; missing ")" in Fig. 10 caption (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
