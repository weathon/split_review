## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper characterizes the theoretical expressiveness of invariant geometric models. It bounds the expressiveness of DisGNN (distance-based message-passing), showing unidentifiable cases are only highly symmetric point clouds. It then proposes GeoNGNN, a geometric counterpart of subgraph GNNs, which achieves E(3)-completeness. Using GeoNGNN as a tool, it shows that many subgraph GNNs and established models (DimeNet, GemNet, SphereNet) can also achieve E(3)-completeness. Empirical results on QM9, MD17, ModelNet40, and a synthetic dataset are provided.

### Strengths
- Addresses a crucial gap in understanding the expressive power of invariant neural networks, guiding future model design.
- Novel contribution: investigating geometric counterparts of subgraph GNNs (GeoNGNN) to break symmetries where DisGNN fails.
- Results extend beyond asymmetric point clouds to symmetric ones, broadening theoretical understanding.
- Introduces a novel conceptual framework for assessing invariance completeness, with extensive theoretical analysis and proofs.
- Provides a framework for developing future E(3)-complete invariant architectures.
- Extensive proofs accompany all theorems, with heuristic intuition and clear definitions that are well-thought-out.
- Symmetrically studies the problem of E(3)-completeness in geometric graph neural networks.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and structure:** The main text lacks key details on NGNN (backbone of GeoNGNN), such as core equations or an architectural diagram; these are relegated to the appendix. A comparison with DisGNN explaining why NGNN overcomes its limitations is missing. The introduction uses excessive bold text and lacks a clear outline, making contributions hard to discern. (Some reviewers found the presentation clear and definitions helpful, but overall the organization needs improvement.)
- **Definitions and terminology:** Some definitions are convolved and could be simplified. Terms like "completeness" (mathematically overloaded) and "isomorphism" (better phrased as same orbit under E(3)) may confuse readers despite being customary in ML.
- **Global connectivity assumption:** The theoretical analysis assumes fully connected graphs, which is often impractical due to computational cost in real-world molecular systems. Several studies (citations [1–4]) have explored sparse graphs; the paper does not extend analysis to sparse settings or discuss how completeness results vary with sparsity.
- **Sparsity analysis insufficient:** The theoretical claim that relaxing full connectivity improves GeoNGNN’s expressiveness over DisGNN relies heavily on a single example (Appendix C.2). There is no systematic exploration of different sparsity patterns or how local subgraph patterns translate to practical advantages.
- **Empirical evidence is weak:** 
  - Experimental results are marginal and lack comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art methods on diverse datasets. The main text does not provide direct evidence of GeoNGNN’s advantages over existing complete invariant architectures.
  - No comparative analysis involving node features generated by a complete invariant function is provided.
  - Datasets used (e.g., QM9) lack conformers and nearly isomorphic point clouds, which are the focus of the theoretical analysis. The proportion of indistinguishable structures within the symmetric subset of QM9 is not analyzed.
  - The noise study on QM9: the interaction between the applied scaling coefficient and the reported error (0.1Å) is unclear, making it hard to assess practical implications.
  - ModelNet40: it is unclear whether the full mesh or a sampled version is used; if sampled uniformly, symmetries may not be preserved. The dataset does not rigorously test claims about nearly isomorphic point clouds.
  - The synthetic dataset’s construction and relevance are not clearly explained—what each structure represents and why it is significant.
  - MD17 results are mixed; GeoNGNN performs well on benzene (highly symmetric), but how benzene behaves under the noise tolerance study is not shown.
- **Missing analysis on generalization:** It is unclear how the method for proving expressivity generalizes to other types of equivariant architectures (e.g., frame-based approaches or models using features beyond pairwise distances, such as dihedral angles). No insights are given on achieving E(3)-completeness for such alternatives.
