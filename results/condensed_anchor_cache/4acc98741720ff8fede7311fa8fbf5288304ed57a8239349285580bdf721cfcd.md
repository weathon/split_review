## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Weak-to-Strong Preference Optimization (WSPO), which transfers alignment signals from a weaker model (aligned via DPO/PPO) to a stronger model by learning the distribution differences before and after the weak model’s alignment. Experiments on Qwen2-7B-Instruct show win-rate improvements on Arena-Hard (39.70→49.60), AlpacaEval 2 (47.04 length-controlled), and MT-bench (7.33). The method avoids explicit reward model training and shows that even a poorly performing weak model (e.g., Qwen2-0.5B) can provide useful distribution shifts.

### Strengths
- **Novel, innovative approach**: WSPO introduces a unique method of transferring alignment capability from weak to strong models, offering an alternative to reward model training (RLHF, DPO). (Reviewers 1–4)
- **Strong empirical validation**: Significant improvements over baselines (DPO, SimPO, PPO) on multiple benchmarks (Arena-Hard, AlpacaEval 2, MT-bench, summarization, dialogue). Extensive experiments across tasks, datasets (UltraChat, Anthropic HH), and model sizes. (Reviewers 1–3)
- **Generalization beyond weak labels**: WSPO does not simply imitate the weak model—the strong model outperforms the weak supervisor, demonstrating amplification. (Reviewer 1)
- **Practical efficiency**: Eliminates repeated reward model training; alignment signal comes directly from the weak model, reducing computational cost compared to standard RLHF. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Effectiveness despite weak model limitations**: Even when the weak model (e.g., Qwen2-0.5B) scores very poorly on its own, the distribution changes from its alignment still provide meaningful guidance for the strong model. (Reviewer 4)
- **Comprehensive analysis**: Authors study impact of model size, hyperparameter γ, and provide insights into scalability and stability. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model-family evaluation**: All experiments use the Qwen2 family (0.5B, 1.8B, 4B, 7B). No results on other architectures (Llama3, encoder-decoder, different attention mechanisms) or tasks (code generation, mathematical reasoning). Several reviewers strongly request cross-model validation. (Reviewers 2, 4; questions from R2, R4)
- **Dependence on weak model alignment quality**: WSPO’s success hinges on the weak model’s alignment quality. The paper does not explore how varying degrees of weak model quality affect the strong model’s final performance, nor does it provide a mechanism to detect or mitigate poor weak-model alignment. (Reviewer 1; related question from R2)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Performance is sensitive to γ, but the paper lacks a systematic methodology or clear guidelines for selecting optimal γ across different model sizes, tasks, or datasets. No ablation on automated tuning methods. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Missing efficiency comparison and baseline**: The algorithm loads parameters from four models simultaneously (including two weak models), increasing memory/computation. The paper omits a comparison with efficient methods like SimPO, which could offer better performance and training efficiency. Also missing a PPO baseline in Table 1 (only DPO and SimPO). (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Lack of theoretical explanation**: The empirical amplification is demonstrated, but no formal theoretical analysis (optimization landscape, why weak-to-strong transfer amplifies alignment) is provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reliance on specific datasets**: Experiments heavily depend on UltraChat and Anthropic HH (dialogue/summarization). Generalizability to other alignment contexts (e.g., reasoning, coding) is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
- **Clarity and organization issues**: Section 3.2 does not explicitly detail how πᵣ_weak and π_ref_weak are obtained (readers must consult appendix to learn SFT/DPO/PPO derivation). Notation needs clearer first-introduction definitions. Section 4 tries to cover three sets of experiments simultaneously; it is recommended to move one experiment to appendix and expand details on the key ablation (Section 4.3). (Reviewer 4)
- **Complex theoretical derivations**: The objective and gradient derivations are dense and may be challenging for readers without strong RL background; more intuitive explanations or examples would help. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority reviewer concern (R1, score 5)**: One reviewer is significantly more critical, emphasizing that the method provides no safeguard against poor weak model alignment, that hyperparameter tuning is left unguided, and that efficiency comparisons are lacking. This contrasts with the other three reviewers (scores 8) who find these issues notable but less severe. (All reviews)

**Additional questions raised (included as weaknesses where they overlap with criticisms):**
- Would aligning the strong model directly be more beneficial than transferring from a weak model in some scenarios? (Reviewer 3)
- Is WSPO applicable to non-language tasks (e.g., RL agents, vision-language)? (Reviewer 3)
- Why is the MMLU score reported for Qwen2 in Table 2 in the 40s, which seems low? (Reviewer 4)
- What happens if the “weak” model is not truly weak (e.g., using the 7B model’s own SFT and DPO checkpoints as πᵣ and π_ref)? (Reviewer 4)
