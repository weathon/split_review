## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers acknowledge that the paper proposes reasonable algorithmic ideas (Mixed Hierarchical Oracle, Model Souping, Hierarchical Exploration) and a new MiniStar environment. However, two reviewers (scores 3) find major flaws in clarity, methodology explanation, experimental design, and missing details; one reviewer (score 5, low confidence) is more positive but also raises significant concerns. Core problems include lack of algorithm description, insufficient details on key components, paltry experiments limited to tabular settings, questionable novelty of MiniStar relative to SMACv2, and missing soundness analysis. The positive assessment of empirical results by one reviewer is not shared by the other two.

### Strengths
- The contributions (MHO algorithm combining Parallelized Oracle, Model Souping, and Hierarchical Exploration) are reasonable potential improvements to population-based algorithms for two-player zero-sum games (R1, R2).
- The idea of allowing lower-level policies to train against higher-level policies is a legitimate research question (R1).
- Model-souping and hierarchical exploration are sensible ideas to try (R1).
- The paper provides a clear introduction and helpful preliminaries (R2).
- One reviewer (R3, score 5) states the algorithms are sound and sensible and that empirical results look good (though with low confidence; this is contested by R1 and R2).
- One reviewer (R1) considers the MiniStar environment a potentially valuable resource for researchers.

### Weaknesses
- **Core algorithm never outlined:** No pseudocode or natural language description of MHO is given (R1). The overall structure, meta-strategy calculation (Section 4.1), and the difference between “MHO” and “MHO with PSRO” are not explained (R1).
- **Model Souping lacks details:** Section 4.2 gives only a high-level description; “parameter fusion” is not defined algorithmically or mathematically. No citation is provided if it is an existing method (R1, R2, R3). It should state exactly how models are combined (e.g., parameter averaging) (R3).
- **Hierarchical Exploration unexplained:** No explanation of how exploration vs. exploitation is controlled or switched (R1).
- **Missing solution concept:** The goal of finding an approximate Nash equilibrium with low exploitability is not clearly stated (R1).
- **Insufficient background on P2SRO:** The paper builds heavily on P2SRO but provides little background, making it hard to understand what changes MHO makes (R3).
- **Unclear terms and statements:** Introduction includes confusing statements about “use of EGTA”, “meta-games are open-ended”, and “meta-strategy derived from solvers like Nash equilibrium” (R1). The term “REFINED” appears in Section 5.1 without definition (R3).
- **Experiments paltry and poorly presented:** Only tabular experiments on AlphaStar888 are shown, limiting generalizability; matrix games may not be a suitable testbed (R1, R2). No details are given on how oracles are trained or how the meta-strategy is computed (R1). Hyperparameters for MHO and baselines are missing (R1). Results are presented in wall-time and “training steps” (Figure 2b x‑axis labelled “item”) without clarification of what the steps refer to; wall-time plots should also show iteration count (R1). The wall-clock comparison in Figure 2 is inconsistent because methods run for varying durations (R2).
- **Figure quality issues:** Figure 3 lacks a legend, does not indicate whether higher/lower values are better, and does not label which method corresponds to row/column player (R2). Figure 2 has legends, axis labels, and tick labels that are too small (R3).
- **MiniStar novelty questionable:** Two reviewers (R2, R3) note that SMAC and SMACv2 already provide smaller-scale combat scenarios; the paper fails to clearly articulate how MiniStar differs or offers unique advantages (R2, R3).
- **Missing soundness analysis:** No comparison of MHO’s convergence properties to PSRO, P2SRO, or DCH; the paper does not discuss that P2SRO avoids training lower-level policies against higher-level ones because it may not guarantee convergence to Nash equilibrium (R1).
- **Citations not parenthesized;** similarity to Self‑Play PSRO is not acknowledged (R1).
- **No discussion of limitations and future work** (R2).
