## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper challenges the view of large language models (LLMs) as individuals and proposes a new metaphor: "LLMs as superpositions of perspectives". The authors conduct experiments showing that LLMs' responses to psychology questionnaires (PVQ, VSM, IPIP) change significantly under different contexts, even when the context is unrelated to the questionnaire topic – an "unexpected perspective shift effect". They also introduce the concept of "perspective controllability" and compare four induction methods across 16 models. The conclusions are that LLMs are unreliable for measuring stable traits and that most models are not easily controllable in their expressed perspectives.

### Strengths

- The paper is timely, addressing a hot topic and challenging existing views about LLMs as individuals, giving warnings about potential dangers of that view (Reviewer 1).
- It adequately challenges the assumption of 'LLMs as individuals' for measuring traits (Reviewer 2).
- The methods for statistical analysis are sound (Reviewer 2).
- The approaches to test robustness of models are creative (Reviewer 2).
- The paper explores whether "perspectives" could be controlled by suggesting four induction methods (Reviewer 1).
- The experiments seem thorough, and the paper states that reproducibility and transparency have been a priority (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minority positive view:** Reviewer 1 (score 5) found the paper more compelling than the other reviewers (scores 3). They considered the paper’s challenges and exploration of controllability as strengths, though they also had concerns about clarity of importance.

### Weaknesses

- **Problem importance and implications unclear:** It is not clear what the paper suggests beyond proposing a new metaphor, why this is critical, or what its applications are. The paper confirms that LLMs are context‑dependent and not like humans, but the implications are not well defined (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Framing and argument structure need improvement:** The argument jumps from LLM context‑dependence to flawed psychological probing to problematic general conclusions without proper reconnection. The point about probing being problematic is not fully fleshed out or supported directly by results. The reference to quantum mechanics for the superposition metaphor is not a sound analogy (LLMs do not operate in a quantum regime) and is not necessary for the argument (Reviewer 4).  
  - The related work section is written as part of the argument rather than contextualizing existing literature; exposition is often unclear (e.g., “At first glance, these might seem like examples of the unexpected perspective shift effect, however…”) and shifts tone to describing the paper (Reviewer 4).
- **Key definitions are unclear or missing:**
  - "Unexpected perspective shift effects" is not given a clear technical definition; it is unclear how to distinguish expected vs. unexpected shifts (Reviewers 4).  
  - "Perspective controllability" lacks a clear technical definition and theoretical basis (Reviewers 4).  
  - "Cultural perspective" and its relation to "personal values" are not precisely defined (Reviewer 3).  
  - "Induced perspective" is not defined, and there is no theoretical analysis supporting the measure (Reviewer 4).
- **Model selection and experimental design limitations:**
  - The first experiment (Section 4.1) is performed only on a single model (ChatGPT) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Model selection for the larger comparison (Table 1) is not optimal to understand mechanisms; key comparison axes (base model vs. chat vs. instruct vs. RLHF, same series different size) are not systematically explored (Reviewer 2).  
  - It is very difficult to understand which specific inputs led to a change of perspectives; small variations might have large effects, making it hard to judge consistency (Reviewer 3).  
  - The selection and analysis of the induction methods are not completely clear, especially regarding the underlying research hypotheses (Reviewer 3).
- **Interpretation and naming issues:**
  - The results do not support the chosen metaphor: for a perspective to hold, it must be consistent across inputs conditioned in the same way, but the experiments show inconsistent results; a better conclusion might be that LLMs lack awareness or knowledge of a perspective (Reviewer 2).  
  - "Superposition" is a misleading term given the probabilistic nature of LLMs; even the same input can produce different outputs (Reviewer 3).  
  - "Controllability" seems inappropriate because the authors are not measuring actual controllability (Reviewer 3).
- **Use of psychology questionnaires is ill‑posed:** Questionnaires for traits are proxies for behaviors (e.g., “make friends easily” loads on extraversion); it would be more appropriate to frame tests as concrete behavioral scenarios (e.g., at a party, do you approach someone sitting alone?) (Reviewer 2).  
  - In addition to test‑retest validity, one could also measure variance inside each questionnaire (multiple questions loading on the same factor) (Reviewer 2).
- **Conclusions are overstated:** The paper states that discarding the old metaphor “may question the interpretation of recent studies aiming at characterizing the values, personality traits, social skills or moral values of LLMs using tools developed to measure attributes of human psychology,” but the argument has not directly led to this conclusion; a more cohesive argument is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details and reproducibility concerns:**
  - The supplementary material does not provide sufficient detail for reproducibility (e.g., which inputs were used for measuring perspective changes) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Formula (1) is not explained in detail, including how the mean is calculated (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - No data is provided about the influence of training datasets on the experimental results (Reviewer 3).  
  - The reasoning behind selecting the term "controllability" is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor concern:** The paper reports the lack of coherence in LLMs as a key finding, but given that models are trained on diverse sources, consistency would be more surprising; were the authors expecting a different result? (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarity of the main focus:** The main focus (“unexpected perspective shift effects”) is not clearly defined; the paper’s first methods sentence states “This paper aims at uncovering the existence of unexpected perspective shift effects i.e. how context can impact the values and personality traits expressed by LLMs in unwanted, unexpected ways,” which remains vague (Reviewer 4).
