## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies and addresses the coupling phenomenon in dense self-supervised learning, where positive pairs with limited overlap leak context from neighboring regions. It proposes a new augmentation, Region Collaborative Cutout (RCC), and a decoupling branch that can be integrated into both CNN- and ViT-based SSL frameworks. All reviewers acknowledge the method is simple and yields non-trivial improvements on multiple baselines. However, significant concerns are raised about presentation clarity, novelty (similarity to TokenMix and other token-mixing works), motivation relative to scaling approaches like DINOv2, ablation completeness, hyperparameter sensitivity, potential issues with contrastive-loss methods, and missing experimental details. Reviewer 2 (score 8) is markedly more positive, praising the clear motivation and comprehensive ablations, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 6) find presentation and novelty weak points that reduce enthusiasm.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow; the main motivation is clearly conveyed (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The proposed method is simple and can be directly combined with existing dense SSL frameworks (both CNN- and ViT-based) without introducing additional loss types (Reviewer 1).
- Multiple previous SSL methods (e.g., iBOT, CNN-based, ViT-based) are used as baselines, and non-trivial improvements are observed (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The method is proved effective for both CNNs and ViTs, and the experiments are reasonably thorough in generality (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- The high-level idea of asking token-wise features to be invariant to some surrounding tokens is simple and leads to better attention maps and downstream performance (Reviewer 3).
- Ablation studies are comprehensive and convincing (Reviewer 2), though Reviewer 1 notes a mismatch in epochs between Table 2 and Table 6 and requests a dedicated decoupling-branch ablation.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor presentation clarity**:  
  - Fig. 1(a) and Fig. 2(a) are very hard to understand; the red dashed lines, arrows, and “ViTs-based measuring pipeline” in 2(a) are unclear (Reviewer 3).  
  - Paragraph 3 of the Introduction only makes sense if you already know the entire method (Reviewer 3).  
  - The mask generation description (Sec. 4.1.1, first paragraph) is incomprehensible; Algorithm 2 in the appendix uses undefined notation (if defined elsewhere it should be repeated in the caption); Figure 4 does not follow (Reviewer 3).  
  - The order of Tables 4–6 does not match their appearance in the text (Reviewer 1).  
  - Key content is relegated to the appendix: Fig. 7(a) is needed to understand variables in equation 4, and Algorithm 2 is crucial for understanding RCC; these should be moved or merged into the main text (e.g., combine Fig. 7(a) with parts of Fig. 3) (Reviewer 1).  
  - Figure 5 is what finally makes the method click and should be moved earlier in the paper (Reviewer 3).

- **Novelty and relation to prior work**:  
  - RCC could be seen as combining thresholding the cutout ratio within a region and filling the cutout region with background images—a combination of existing strategies rather than a fundamentally new idea (Reviewer 1).  
  - Token mixing from different images in a carefully engineered way has been done before, e.g., TokenMix (ECCV’22, arXiv:2207.08409) for supervised classification. The paper should discuss these works and clarify the novel contribution of the self-supervised loss that leverages token mixing (Reviewer 3).

- **Motivation and relationship to scaling approaches**:  
  - DINOv2 achieved high-quality part-level representations by simply scaling up model and dataset sizes without considering the “coupling” phenomenon. The authors should briefly clarify the motivation of the proposed strategy in this context (Reviewers 2,3).  
  - It is unclear whether the decoupling strategy can capture long-range nonlocal dependence; the authors should comment (Reviewer 3).

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**:  
  - The method introduces a threshold of cutout ratio hyperparameter. How should this be set across different datasets? (Reviewer 1)  
  - (Question from Reviewer 1 merged as criticism.)

- **Ablation study concerns**:  
  - The COCO Det. numbers in Table 6 do not match those in Table 2 for iBOT because of different training epochs. If Table 2 represents converged results, the comparison in Table 6 may not lead to a convincing conclusion (Reviewer 1).  
  - A more informative ablation would be the effectiveness of the decoupling branch alone (Reviewer 1).

- **Potential limitation with contrastive-loss methods**:  
  - There is concern that the decoupling branch serves a similar purpose to contrastive loss within each batch, and thus the method may not work well for such methods; authors argue losses are at different levels with different masks, but this needs clarification (Reviewer 1).

- **Missing experimental details**:  
  - Why specifically 800 epochs of pretraining on COCO for all methods? How were hyperparameters tuned for the baselines? Most experiments do not mention dataset splits (Reviewer 3).  
  - Apart from the proposed masking, are standard augmentations (jitters, flips, blur, etc.) also used? Does the augmentation strategy match the existing methods on which the decoupling branch was applied? (Reviewer 3)
