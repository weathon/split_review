## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces DEPT, a pre-training framework that decouples token embeddings from the transformer body to alleviate negative interference when training on heterogeneous data (e.g., multiple languages or domains). Three variants are proposed: **Glob** (shared embedding matrix, close to standard pre-training), **Trim** (local embeddings per source with tokens restricted to a global vocabulary, aggregated via federated averaging), and **Spec** (fully decoupled with non-shared local embeddings). The transformer backbone is trained simultaneously across sources using a federated averaging inner loop. DEPT claims to reduce embedding parameters by up to 80% and communication costs by 675× for billion-scale models, and to improve generalization and plasticity. Experiments measure perplexity on multi-domain English and multilingual data, and test adaptation to new languages/domains. All three reviewers gave a score of 8.

### Strengths

- The problem of training on heterogeneous data mixtures is recognized as important and poorly understood (R1).
- The methodology is considered novel by two reviewers (R2, R3); one reviewer notes that model averaging after inner-loop training is not particularly novel but could have a large practical impact (R1).
- The value proposition is clear: DEPT reduces communication costs, avoids a shared global vocabulary, and enables custom per-source tokenization (R2).
- The experiments are extensive and address the stated research questions (R3).
- The results generally confirm improvements in generalization and plasticity (R3).
- The literature survey is satisfactory given the paper’s scope (R2).

**Minority contrast**: One reviewer (R3) found the paper well-written and easy to follow, whereas the other two (R1, R2) strongly criticized the writing quality (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and presentation are below required standards**:  
  - Method names are inconsistent (e.g., *SPEC* vs *SPED*, *Glob* vs *GLOB*, *GlOB*) (R2).  
  - Performance metrics are not clearly defined in any table (perplexity is used, but this is never explicitly stated; lower should be better) (R1, R2, R3).  
  - Tables lack summary statistics (e.g., averages) and bolding of best results; Table 1 is not referenced in the main text (R2, R3).  
  - Essential experimental details are missing: software/hardware, FLOPS, training time, hours (R1).  
  - Typos: line 278 uses “50257.0” instead of “50,257”; line 341 contains garbled text (“(b) tn the ?”) (R3).  
  - One reviewer (R3) considered the paper well-written, but this is a minority view.

- **Claims are overstated or insufficiently justified**:  
  - *M-T* (the mixed-source baseline) outperforms DEPT in 5 out of 11 datasets in Table 2 (R1).  
  - The claim that Trim and Glob perform identically is not convincingly justified (R1, citing L377).  
  - The issue of model divergence during training (which motivated the approach) is poorly explained; it may be due to hyperparameter choices or bugs rather than a general phenomenon. A better description and ablation study are needed (R2).  
  - The abstract treats “negative interference” and “curse of multilinguality” as parallel concepts, but the latter is a special case of the former (R3).  
  - The pipeline assumes that data sources are known and domain-consistent; otherwise manual or automatic clustering is required, which is not discussed (R3).  
  - The claimed communication cost reduction of 675× needs a clearer estimation basis (R1).

- **Important baselines and comparisons are missing**:  
  - Models trained on individual data sources (without any multi-source training) are not included as a baseline (R1).  
  - No external baselines from prior work are compared against (R2).  
  - No downstream task evaluations (e.g., NLU, generation) are performed on the resulting models (R3).

- **The inference methodology for SPEC models is not addressed**:  
  - Since each data source uses a separate tokenizer, it is unclear how to select the correct tokenizer at inference time for a given input (R1, R2). The paper feels incomplete without outlining inference procedures and their challenges (R2).

- **Missing analyses and ablations**:  
  - No hypothesis is offered for why GLOB (the least decoupled variant) works best in many settings (R1).  
  - Hyperparameter search methodology is not described (R2).  
  - Scaling experiments (e.g., model size vs. benefits) are not presented (R2).  
  - The multilingual data confounds language and domain: each language likely contains multiple domains, yet the cross-lingual generalization experiments do not control for domain. A natural question is whether the model can generalize to the same domain across languages (R3).
