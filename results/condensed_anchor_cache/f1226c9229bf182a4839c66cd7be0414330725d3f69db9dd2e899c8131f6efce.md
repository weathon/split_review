## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a generative deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework for the Location-Routing Problem (LRP) without predefined depot candidates. It consists of two modules: a Depot Generative Model (DGM) that generates depot locations either deterministically or as a multivariate Gaussian distribution for sampling, and a Multi-Depot Location-Routing Attention Model (MDLRAM) that takes the generated depots and customer requests to output routing solutions. The framework is tested on synthetic and real-world data.

### Strengths
- **Novelty:** Addresses a novel LRP variant without predefined depot candidates, using a generative approach for depot placement—a less-studied problem.
- **Clarity and discussion:** Provides a clear explanation of the framework, including model structures, objective functions, and training strategy, along with a good overview and discussion despite page limits.
- **Rigorous experimentation:** Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets across different problem scales, with comparisons against exact methods and several heuristics. All important parameters and experimental details are provided.
- **Significance:** Problem has potential relevance for real-world applications (e.g., emergency medical rescue, disaster relief logistics) where proactive depot generation is beneficial. The dual-mode generation (exact locations or Gaussian distribution for flexible sampling) offers adaptability.
- **Aims to apply ML/AI to overcome computational challenges of traditional optimization for a classical NP-hard problem, a worthwhile direction.**

### Weaknesses
- **Practical relevance questioned:** Several reviewers strongly argued that LRP without predefined depots is not practically meaningful. Real-world depots are limited to available discrete properties; solving LRP on a continuous plane ignores these constraints, and the error from placing depots at unavailable locations likely outweighs any routing accuracy gain. This fundamentally undermines the problem motivation.
- **Unsuitability of DRL for strategic planning:** The use of DRL for a strategic decision made infrequently (once per decade) with high sunk costs is criticized. Exact or robust optimization methods are preferred for such problems. Training requires 1,280,000 instances, which is unrealistic for strategic problems where historical data is scarce. The motivation for DRL over classical optimization is insufficiently justified.
- **Outdated baselines and inferior solution quality:** The paper compares against heuristics and exact methods from 2006, 2009, 2011—roughly 15 years old. The state-of-the-art for LRP has advanced significantly, so the reported gaps are likely much larger when measured against modern methods. The proposed MDLRAM is itself inferior to these outdated baselines on some metrics.
- **Flawed two-module design and lack of theoretical grounding:**
  - MDLRAM is a fixed model that cannot dynamically adapt to new depot locations generated by DGM, contradicting the claimed flexibility of proactive depot generation. The two modules are not integrated iteratively or end-to-end.
  - DGM is trained based on scores from MDLRAM, which is not a state-of-the-art routing solver. This creates evaluation bias: DGM is optimized for a suboptimal critic, so its performance may not generalize to truly optimal solutions. The separation of location and routing decisions in learning loses any optimality guarantee.
  - The design is largely heuristic-driven; no theoretical analysis of optimality bounds or algorithm efficiency is provided. Training strategies appear ad hoc without firm theoretical basis.
  - One reviewer questioned why the authors used MDLRAM rather than a SOTA method as the critic, or a more integrated end-to-end framework that dynamically generates depots and plans routes.
- **Weak baseline for DGM evaluation:** The baseline for the depot generative model is random depot generation. Reviewers noted this is too weak; heuristic methods for depot placement (e.g., from Related Work) would provide a more meaningful comparison and better demonstrate advantages of the DGM.
- **Missing descriptions and unclear presentation:**
  - For synthetic data, baselines (ALNS, GA, TS) are not described—acronyms are unexplained and specific implementations are not identified. Different baseline sets are used for synthetic vs. real-world data without justification.
  - The meaning of boldface and asterisks in Tables 1 and 3 is not explained in captions or text.
- **Reproducibility and formatting issues:**
  - Source code is not provided, hindering reproducibility.
  - Tables and figures are too small, with font sizes difficult to read; plots lack sufficient resolution.
  - Important content (limitations and future work) is placed in an appendix (Section B.4) rather than the main text.
  - The introduction lacks an overview of the paper structure.
- **Scalability and computational trade-off not analyzed:** The paper does not examine how the DRL method scales to very large instances or discuss the solution quality versus computational efficiency trade-off.
