## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates structured pruning combined with knowledge distillation to compress LLMs when original pretraining data is unavailable. It introduces a “teacher correction” phase (lightweight fine-tuning of the teacher on the target data distribution) and explores two pruning strategies: depth pruning (with a new saliency metric based on downstream task performance) and joint width pruning. The approach is applied to compress Mistral NeMo 12B to 8B and Llama 3.1 8B to 4B, and evaluated on commonsense, math, coding, instruction-following, and role-play benchmarks. Reviewers note the practical motivation and promising efficiency gains, but one reviewer (score 3) emphasizes significant overlap with prior work (Sheared LLaMA, Muralidharan et al.), lack of adherence to ICLR 2025 style guidelines, and insufficient analysis of the teacher correction mechanism.

### Strengths
- Teacher correction is an innovative solution for compressing models without access to the original training data (a common industry challenge). The approach is elegantly simple, requires only ~100B tokens for adaptation, and integrates smoothly into distillation pipelines. (R2)
- Impressive efficiency gains: MN-COMPRESSED-8B achieves SOTA with 40× fewer training tokens, and LLAMA 3.1-COMPRESSED-4B is competitive with 150× fewer tokens. The method delivers speedups of 2.7× (depth pruning) and 1.8× (width pruning) while maintaining strong performance across reasoning, coding, and instruction following. (R2)
- Detailed empirical investigation into the precise gains from each component (random student initialization, importance sorting, knowledge distillation) provides significant insight. (R1)
- Exhaustive evaluation on a wide variety of tasks (commonsense, math, instruction tuning, role-play, function calling). (R1)
- Authors provide the base model on Hugging Face, and results appear promising. (R3)
- Teacher correction is demonstrated to improve distillation effectiveness when original data is unavailable on two models (Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral NeMo 12B). (R1, R3)
- The paper is written in an easy-to-understand manner in most parts. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and overlap with prior work**: The core components (importance sorting, architecture selection, distillation loss) are very similar to Muralidharan et al. (2024) [1]. Domain adaptation of a teacher was also studied for BERT-like models [2], and the proposed teacher correction appears not significantly different. Dropping the last contiguous layers (except the last) has been studied in [3]; the authors did not clarify key differences. (R1) Reviewer 3 further notes substantial overlap with Sheared LLaMA, which first proposed efficient training using distillation and pruning, and this work is not cited. (R3)
- **Performance degradation despite modest compression**: For compressing Llama 3.1 8B to 4B (only 2× reduction), MMLU drops notably from 65.3% to 60.5% (width pruning) or 58.7% (depth pruning). This is concerning compared to models like MobileLLM-350M that achieve comparable performance to LLaMA-v2 7B with 20× smaller size. The additional fine-tuning required makes the approach less compelling for practical applications. (R2)
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: The paper does not compare against a broader spectrum of existing compression techniques, including other pruning approaches, quantization methods, or methods also using knowledge distillation. Comparisons to similarly-sized models trained from scratch are missing. (R1, R2) Additionally, no comparison is made with newly released small models (Llama 3.2 1B/3B, Ministral 3B). (R1 question)
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only two models are explored (Mistral NeMo 12B and Llama 3.1 8B); more aggressive compression scenarios (e.g., sub-1B models) are not studied. Evaluation is limited to a relatively small set of downstream tasks. (R2) The teacher correction phase uses only full fine-tuning, which does not scale to larger models (e.g., 70B) – the potential of PEFT schemes (LoRA, GaLore) is mentioned but not studied. (R1)
- **Clarity and completeness issues**: The paper lacks a cohesive algorithmic description of the final best-performing choices. Several points are ambiguous: “architecture related learnings” (line 167–168), how the student is designed, which calibration set is used and whether its domain matters (line 133–134), the definition of “continuously corrected teacher”, and the general scheme for selecting which contiguous layer indices to drop (line 312–313). (R1) Reviewer 3 notes the writing is somewhat unclear, with improper use of \citep/\citet and capitalization errors. (R3)
- **Reproducibility and dataset concerns**: Models are fine-tuned and distilled on a proprietary dataset; observations may not translate to public datasets (e.g., OpenWebText, Alpaca, Commonsense170K, Math10K). Code and experimental pipeline are not released. (R1)
- **Style guideline violation**: The paper does not follow the ICLR 2025 style guidelines, which could be a serious reason for desk rejection. (R3)
- **Lack of analysis for teacher correction**: The reasoning behind improvement from teacher correction is not clear; it is only applied to a single case. More analysis/experiments across different models or datasets are needed to establish generalizability. (R3) The hypothesis about sub-word token distribution differences lacks empirical validation. (R3 question)
- **Depth pruning saliency metric**: The choice of Winogrande as the downstream task for measuring importance is not justified. Sensitivity of the method to the choice of downstream task is not explored. (R2 question)
- **No on-device latency measurements**: The paper does not provide latency gains on specific hardware (e.g., A100/H100). (R1 question)
- **Minority reviewer perspective**: While two reviewers (scores 6) find the work practical and significant, one reviewer (score 3) rates it much lower, citing lack of novelty, style issues, and insufficient empirical justification – this contrast underscores substantial concerns about the paper’s contribution and presentation. (R3 overall tone)
