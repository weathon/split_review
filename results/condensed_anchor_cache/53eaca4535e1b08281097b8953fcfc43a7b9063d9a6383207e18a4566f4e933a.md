## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Reti-Diff, a latent diffusion model combined with a transformer for illumination degradation image restoration, using Retinex theory to generate reflectance and illumination priors. The method shows competitive performance on multiple tasks and downstream applications. Reviewer 1 is significantly more critical (score 6) than the other three reviewers (each score 8), who consider the work solid despite concerns.

### Strengths
- The problem of illumination restoration is important and relevant for downstream tasks. (R1)
- Combining the Retinex model with data-driven methods continues a line of work since RetinexNet. (R2)
- The coupling of latent diffusion model with Retinex priors and a Transformer is intriguing, and exploring robustness under illumination degradation with Retinex theory is meaningful. (R4)
- Compared to existing diffusion models in image enhancement, the method demonstrates relatively high computational efficiency and lower resource consumption while maintaining strong performance. (R4)
- Experiments are sufficient: thorough evaluation on four IDIR tasks, multiple datasets, and downstream tasks (detection, segmentation). (R2, R3, R4)
- Code is a plus. (R2)
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written. (R3, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty (minority reviewer R1):** The use of diffusion for decomposition and a transformer for reconstruction is seen as having hardly any contribution. Intrinsic image decomposition via Retinex is considered a dated idea; better methods exist (e.g., survey at https://www.elenagarces.es/projects/SurveyIntrinsicImages/). (R1)
- **Weak justification for downstream tasks (minority reviewer R1):** Detection and segmentation tasks are too easy to generalize without illumination enhancement; the paper does not convince the reader of the method’s merit. (R1)
- **Missing comparisons on no-reference datasets:** For low-light enhancement, results on LIME, NPE, MEF, DICM, and VV (no ground truth) are expected for a fair visual justification. (R2)
- **Missing citations:** Real-world low-light enhancement methods such as [1] Unsupervised Night Image Enhancement: When Layer Decomposition Meets Light-Effects Suppression and [2] Enhancing Visibility in Nighttime Haze Images Using Guided APSF and Gradient Adaptive Convolution are omitted. More related works like low-light image enhancement via clip-fourier guided wavelet diffusion should also be discussed. (R2, R3)
- **Lack of computational complexity discussion:** LDM is slow in inference; the paper lacks discussion on runtime and computational burden. (R2)
- **Insufficient analysis of Retinex priors and diffusion model relationship:** Further analysis is needed on why latent diffusion is used to extract Retinex priors (over a single Transformer or CNN) and on the consistency of Retinex theory in latent space. (R3, R4)
- **Unclear mechanism for cross-attention:** Authors should justify why cross-attention is chosen to model Retinex theory. (R3)
- **Ablation missing direct joint training:** The ablation compares removing the joint training stage but does not test whether training RLDM, RPE, and RGFormer together from scratch (without independent RLDM pre-training) would yield better results. (R3)
- **Missing verification of Retinex prior superiority:** Experiments should compare Retinex priors vs. sole RGB priors, and check whether the diffusion model serves as a better predictor than a common network structure. (R3)
- **No comparison with DiffIR [1]:** DiffIR uses a similar prior for image restoration; the necessity of using a Retinex-based prior over treating the entire enhancement as a prior should be demonstrated. (R4)
- **No prior-level evaluation:** Beyond enhanced image quality, prior feature errors when exchanging features between ground truth and low-light images should be assessed. (R4)
- **No convergence analysis:** Show loss or evaluation metrics on training/validation sets for the latent Retinex priors. (R4)
- **Reliance on Retinex decomposition reliability:** The method heavily depends on Retinex decomposition, which is ill-posed; little discussion on its reliability. Provide at least qualitative results of the decomposition, and discuss performance when decomposition fails (e.g., extreme bright light, strong shadows). (R4)
- **Performance under extreme conditions:** Questions remain about handling extreme noise, heavy color distortions, overexposed/underexposed images, and tuning noise variance parameters in the latent diffusion process. (R2, R4)
- **Failure case analysis:** Authors should analyze failure cases—whether they stem from a failure of one Retinex prior or from the RGformer integration. (R3)
- **Typos:** “ZR and ZL” in Supp. Line 90 need correction. (R3)
