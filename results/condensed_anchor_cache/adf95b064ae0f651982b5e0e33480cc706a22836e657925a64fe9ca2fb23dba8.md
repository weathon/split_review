## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces *homomorphism expressivity* (Definition 3.1) as a unified, quantitative framework for comparing the expressive power of GNN architectures, addressing limitations of the coarse, qualitative WL hierarchy. The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) characterizes the homomorphism counting ability of MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, Local k-GNNs, and Folklore-GNNs via *nested ear decompositions* (NEDs), with extensions to node-level (Theorem 3.6) and higher-order settings (Theorems 3.7, 3.8). The framework also yields subgraph counting results for moderate graph sizes (Proposition 4.4). Experiments on synthetic and real-world tasks (ZINC, Alchemy) verify the theory. Three reviewers assessed the paper as strong (scores 8,8,8) and recommended acceptance; one reviewer disagreed strongly (score 1) due to concerns about completeness, motivation, and missing extensions.

### Strengths
- **Fresh perspective on GNN expressivity**: Homomorphism expressivity provides a more fine-grained, quantitative alternative to WL-based comparisons, enabling direct architectural comparisons and analysis of subgraph counting ability. The framework recovers and extends several known results.
- **Well-written and polished**: The paper is extremely well-written, with clear motivation, results, and connections to related work. Despite some density, the presentation successfully formalizes extensive theoretical concepts and proofs.
- **Elegant theoretical connections**: The use of NEDs from graph theory yields simple, unified descriptions of expressivity for four GNN classes and establishes a strict hierarchy (share-point/strong/near-strong/general NEDs). The analysis of subgraph counting power is particularly impressive.
- **Clear proofs and synthetic experiments**: Proofs are well-structured, and synthetic experiments convincingly verify the theoretical results. Real-world experiments (ZINC, Alchemy) show alignment between proposed expressivity measure and practical performance.

### Weaknesses
- **Technical issues with homomorphism expressivity definition (Definition 3.1)**: The set \(\mathcal{F}^M\) is not guaranteed to be defined for all models \(M\), because there may exist graphs \(F\) where two input graphs have identical homomorphism counts from \(F\) but different representations according to \(M\). The definition is unclear on when \(\mathcal{F}^M\) is well-defined; the paper should clarify that for the considered GNN types it is always well-defined (as Theorem 3.3 indicates) and elaborate on this point to avoid confusion. The term “complete” measure is therefore misleading, especially because homomorphism expressivity is not defined for all architectures.
- **Overlooks vertex/edge features**: The framework assumes unique discrete vertex labels and disregards features, which often play a crucial role in practice. This limits the “completeness” claim and should be acknowledged.
- **Density and lack of intuition**: The presentation is quite dense, especially in Section 3.3. Reviewers suggested allocating more space to examples and intuitions for NED characterizations (e.g., which graphs belong to each NED class) and deferring some extensions to the appendix. The exact quantitative gap between NED classes (number of graphs in each class) is not characterized; even a rough scale would aid understanding.
- **Limited real-world experimental analysis**: Only two datasets (ZINC, Alchemy) are used, and no analysis correlates architecture performance with the ability to count specific subgraphs. A more refined study is expected.
- **Missing discussion of prior work and alternative methods**: The paper does not adequately relate its results to existing substructure-counting methods, such as equivariant polynomial GNNs or recursive-RNP GNNs. Specifically, how recursion in RNP-GNNs connects to NEDs is not discussed. Additionally, only one subgraph GNN variant (SWL(VS) from Zhang et al. 2023) is considered; the framework’s applicability to other variants (e.g., from Zhou et al. 2023 or Feng et al. 2023) is unclear.
- **Subgraph counting only for moderate sizes**: The subgraph counting results are proved only for graphs with ≤6 nodes or ≤8 edges. The reviewers questioned the obstacle to extending to larger sizes and whether the proposed condition is necessary for successful counting in general.
- **Missing motivation for homomorphism counting**: It is not convincingly argued why homomorphism numbers per se are important for practical applications (e.g., biology). While subgraph counts are clearly relevant, the connection to homomorphisms is less direct.
- **Necessity of connectedness assumption** (Definition 3.1): The need for the connectedness assumption should be explained.
- **Minor issues**: There is a missing “\(G \in \mathcal{G}\)” in the definition of \(\mathsf{Clo_{ind}}\) (page 5). The paper should also cite Dvořák (2010) for previous results on homomorphism-based graph recognition. Some sentences (e.g., “FM is maximal” at bottom of page 5) need rewording for clarity.
- **Unaddressed design implications**: The paper does not discuss how the framework can guide the design of new powerful and efficient GNN architectures, or how it could be applied to new GNN variants as they are proposed.
