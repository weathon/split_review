## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GLOMA, a Transformer-based global tracking method for video text spotting (VTS) that models the tracking problem as global associations and uses Gaussian Wasserstein distance to guide morphological correlation between frames. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on the ICDAR2015 video dataset (56.0 MOTA, +4.6 absolute improvement over previous SOTA, +8.3 over prior Transformer-based method). Experiments are conducted on public benchmarks. The writing is clear and the ablation study is detailed. However, several reviewers raise concerns about novelty justification, presentation clarity, lack of comparison with recent tracking and spotting methods, and missing details on design choices (e.g., why not use synthetic data, why not use arbitrary-shape detectors, how Wasserstein captures morphology, how embeddings are updated).

### Strengths
- Writing is clear and easy to understand (Reviewers 1,3,4). Figures and tables effectively illustrate key concepts and results (Reviewer 3).
- Strong motivation: Wasserstein distance is a better measurement for video text association because it captures both location and morphological similarity, especially under fast movement or interference (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The Transformer-based global tracking method associates multiple frames simultaneously, effectively utilizing global context information beyond local pairwise comparisons (Reviewers 2,3).
- Significant performance improvements: 56.0 MOTA on ICDAR2015 video dataset (+4.6 over previous SOTA; +8.3 over the best Transformer-based method) across benchmarks (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Extensive ablation study examines sliding window size, distance measurement, attention layers, and semantic embeddings, providing insights for model design (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental validation on public datasets demonstrates practicality and efficacy (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Justification for new Transformer and association mechanism**: It remains unclear why existing SOTA multi-object tracking algorithms (e.g., [1-4]) or single object tracking algorithms cannot effectively tackle VTS, given that current SOTA methods do not simply rely on IoU matching. The paper needs a much clearer justification for devising a specific Transformer and association mechanism, and why existing tracking methods are insufficient (Reviewer 4). Additionally, compared to TransDETR (which uses learnable queries), what is the advantage of the proposed association method? (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation clarity**: Figures 1 and 2 lack sufficient clarification. The colorful circles are not easy to interpret – do they refer to bounding boxes or masks? What is being associated? (Reviewer 4). In Figure 2, the three parallel modules (detection, recognition, tracking) are not clearly represented; distinct dividing lines and text descriptions, especially in the right-side process, would improve visualization (Reviewer 3). Figure 1 does not indicate the specific types of failure situations (e.g., target loss or target error) before introducing global associations; adding other failure types would enhance persuasiveness (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty and claim of "global"**: All employed technologies are well-known; the paper should explicitly state novel aspects and how the approach differs from simply combining existing techniques. The concept of "global" is not convincing given the limited number of frames considered – clarify what "global" means and how it is achieved (Reviewer 4). The innovations could be slightly expanded to better show their contributions to solving existing problems (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and outdated baselines**: The compared methods look kind of old; more recent related literature should be discussed and compared, including but not limited to [5][6] (Reviewer 4). The paper does not incorporate state-of-the-art text detectors/spotters for arbitrary shapes (e.g., DBNet++, Mask TextSpotter v3) – discuss the challenges of integration, such as computational cost or end-to-end training difficulties, and include a discussion on existing scene text spotting methods in related work (Reviewer 2).
- **Use of synthetic data**: TransDETR achieved 51.8 MOTA with synthetic data vs. 47.7 without. Why did GLOMA not leverage synthetic data for training? The paper should discuss potential benefits/drawbacks, including domain adaptation challenges and bias risk (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of explanation on Wasserstein distance**: The claim that Wasserstein distance captures morphological similarity lacks detailed explanation of how it specifically measures morphological features and its advantages over traditional IoU (Reviewers 3,4). Provide a more comprehensive justification by comparing with other distance metrics considered (Reviewer 4). In Equation (8), the hyperparameter α and function f are not sufficiently discussed – provide details on how α is chosen and its impact on performance, and the normalization process of f (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing details on embedding update and trajectory construction**: The embedding pool and trajectory association section lacks details on how embeddings are computed and updated, how the sliding window length is selected, and whether there is a trajectory drift problem. Elaborate on the embedding update strategy and trajectory construction details during inference (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing flow**: The abstract does not sufficiently emphasize the core role of temporal information and morphological features for VTS, and does not explain the specific advantages of Gaussian Wasserstein distance over IoU. The introduction has abrupt transitions – adding transition sentences to guide readers from existing work to the new model would be helpful. (Reviewer 3).
