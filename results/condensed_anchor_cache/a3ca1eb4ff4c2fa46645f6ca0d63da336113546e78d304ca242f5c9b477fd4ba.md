## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MicroCrackAttentionNeXt, an asymmetric encoder–decoder with attention mechanisms for microcrack detection from spatio-temporal wave field data. It builds upon SpAsE‑Net (1D‑DenseNet baseline) and examines different activation functions and loss functions; a combination of GELU activation and Combined Weighted Dice Loss (CWDL) yields 86.85% accuracy. The authors employ Manifold Discovery and Analysis (MDA) to visualize learned features. The dataset has severe class imbalance (5% crack pixels). Reviewers agree on clear writing and a relevant problem, but raise major concerns about limited novelty, insufficient comparisons, missing ablation studies, reliance on synthetic data, low‑resolution output, and inadequately reported evaluation details.

### Strengths
- Clear structure and writing; the paper is easy to follow.
- Accuracy improvement from 83.68% to 86.85% over the baseline.
- Consideration of the complex spatio‑temporal to spatial mapping required for crack detection.
- Asymmetric encoder‑decoder with attention mechanisms is a promising approach for handling spatio‑temporal data (R1, R2).
- Thorough exploration of multiple activation functions and loss functions, with GELU and CWDL identified as optimal (R1, R2).
- Application of MDA for feature space visualization provides qualitative insight into model behavior across layers and activations (R1, R2).
- Strong empirical evidence via comparisons to 1D‑DenseNet; theoretical foundation grounded in attention‑based deep learning (R1).
- The work addresses a critical problem in structural health monitoring and proposes a tailored solution to class imbalance (R1).
- R1 notes novelty in the architecture and activation‑function analysis; however, other reviewers strongly contest this (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty / incremental contribution**  
   - Most reviewers (R3, R4, R5) find the work lacks innovation: it merely applies well‑known convolutional and attention components in an architecture heavily based on SpAsE‑Net, with only activation/loss function investigations as extensions. R1 considered the architecture and activation analysis novel, but the majority view is that there is no fundamental new approach.  
   - No comparison to recent segmentation techniques (e.g., transformer‑based models, SAM2) is provided. R1 and R5 explicitly note this omission, and R5 asks whether an adaptation of SAM2 could work for this data and why such architectures are excluded.

2. **Insufficient experimental validation**  
   - Only one baseline (1D‑DenseNet) is quantitatively compared on a single dataset. Missing direct comparisons to other state‑of‑the‑art microcrack detection models (R2, R3, R5). R5 requests a table showing accuracies, DSC, and IoU for the proposed method vs. the baseline.  
   - No ablation study is conducted: the effects of kernel sizes, pooling layers, attention modules, or other architectural choices are not isolated (R1, R4). R1 recommends systematically varying these parameters.  
   - Visual comparisons (e.g., segmentation maps) are absent (R4).  
   - The specific modifications extending 1D‑DenseNet are unclear. R5 asks for a table or section explicitly listing similarities, differences, and which changes lead to performance improvements.

3. **Dataset concerns & generalizability**  
   - Training and validation rely solely on synthetic wave field data; no real‑world or field tests are performed (R1, R4). R1 notes that synthetic‑data dynamics may differ from practical engineering contexts, and R4 raises concerns about generalizability.  
   - The severe class imbalance (only 5% crack pixels) is acknowledged but not fundamentally addressed: no data augmentation (e.g., variations in crack morphology, orientation, scale) is explored (R2).  
   - Dataset preparation (whether real or synthetic) is not clearly described (R4).  
   - R2 questions how the model would generalize across different material compositions or wave propagation scenarios.

4. **Low‑resolution output segmentation**  
   - The paper itself mentions low output resolution, which can obscure fine crack details needed for accurate localization, length/width measurement, and connectivity assessment (R2). R2 asks how precise microcrack identification is ensured near the resolution limit.

5. **Weak interpretability contribution of MDA**  
   - MDA analysis is largely qualitative. R1 recommends adding quantitative measures (e.g., silhouette scores, inter‑class distances). R5 finds MDA insufficiently explained and not clearly more informative than standard evaluation metrics (DSC, accuracy) or direct attention maps. The contribution of MDA to understanding model behaviour is questioned.

6. **Missing evaluation details and metrics**  
   - Evaluation metrics beyond accuracy and DSC are not reported. R2 notes the absence of precision‑recall curves, AUC‑ROC, or statistical validation (confidence intervals).  
   - R3 states that evaluation metrics are not mentioned at all in the paper, making it difficult to assess contribution.  
   - R5 expected to see Focal Loss discussed given the class‑imbalance context.

7. **Scalability and computational cost**  
   - No analysis of the model’s scalability to larger datasets or higher spatio‑temporal resolutions (R1, R2). Temporal downsampling may discard valuable information, and the computational cost of attention mechanisms is not addressed (R2).

8. **Technical issues and unclear writing**  
   - “Figure X” appears without specification (R3, R5).  
   - Squeeze‑and‑Excitation layers lack a citation or definition (R5).  
   - The relationship between MicroCrackAttentionNeXt and Squeeze‑and‑Excitation layers is not explained (R3).  
   - Figures 3–5 are not described in the text (R3).  
   - It is unclear whether 1D or 2D convolutional layers are used; the conclusion mentions 1D but spatial data is 2D (R5).  
   - The loss function description (lines 66–69) is unnatural and could be clearer (R5).  
   - The soundness of the claim about UNet’s failure (lines 50–53) is unclear because the proposed architecture also uses residual connections (R5).  
   - Grammatical mistakes exist (e.g., run‑on sentence in the abstract) (R3).  
   - Performance reports cited in the related work (lines 133, 143, 148) are not confirmed to be on the same dataset, so their inclusion is unclear (R5).
