## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a test-time adversarial defense that uses diffusion-based purification along Opposite Adversarial Paths (OAPs) to excessively denoise adversarial inputs, pushing them away from decision boundaries. The plug-and-play purifier can be integrated into pre-trained models. It combines minimization of training loss (emulated via reverse diffusion when ground truth is unavailable) with opposite adversarial direction. Experiments on three datasets are performed, and multiple related works are compared.

### Strengths
- The plug-and-play purifier can be integrated into pre-trained models to improve robustness against adversarial attacks.
- The method increases the time required for attackers to generate adaptive adversarial examples, which is a novel practical advantage.
- The paper critiques the use of AutoAttack (Rand) for evaluating diffusion-based defenses and highlights the trade-off between attack effectiveness and computational complexity.
- The core idea of reducing the effect of adversarial perturbations by moving in the opposite adversarial direction is sensible; it pushes the adversarial input toward a non-adversarial region, and the method emulates this process at test time without ground-truth labels.
- Empirical results demonstrate effectiveness against strong attacks designed to defeat randomized defenses, such as EOT and BPDA.
- The method introduces a new combination of opposite adversarial path and reverse diffusion, which is a novel technical contribution.
- Experiments are conducted on three popular datasets and comparisons with multiple related works are provided.

### Weaknesses
- The overall method explanation is unclear. There is no algorithmic description for key sections (e.g., Section 3.3), making the procedure hard to follow.
- The explanation of Table 2 is not persuasive. As K increases, robustness decreases, which seems contradictory to the assumption in Figure 1. The authors attribute this to instability of the '-adv' process without ground-truth labels, but the training data \(x^K\) is also generated using ground truth \(y\). A more detailed analysis of stability across K values is needed.
- The algorithm is too complex for practical application. It uses multiple diffusion paths, which roughly doubles purification time (Table 6). The trade-off between complexity and performance is not discussed, and no simplification is offered.
- The improvements over DiffPure for transferability are small. The paper does not compare with the latest works: Robust Evaluation of Diffusion-Based Adversarial Purification (ICCV 2023) and Adversarial Purification with the Manifold Hypothesis (AAAI 2024).
- The evaluation is limited to gradient-based, non-targeted attacks (all derivatives of PGD/APGD). Experiments should also include attacks that converge to different adversarial examples from PGD, such as PGD-ODI and ACG, because PGD-based attacks highly depend on the initial point. This dependency is expected to also affect the minimization step used in OAP.
- The method only considers non-targeted adversarial attacks. For targeted attacks, the adversarial gradient varies with the target class, and a single opposite adversarial path appears insufficient.
- Optimization-based attacks (e.g., C&W, DDN), targeted attacks, and black-box attacks are not considered for a comprehensive evaluation.
- It is unclear in Tables 1–5 whether the adversarial attack is against the target model only or against both the model and the purifier as a whole. The described adaptive attack (BPDA, EOT) seems to target only the model, not a full white-box attack against the combined system. This must be explicitly stated.
- The aim of "Color OT" is not explained.
- The difference between solid and dashed lines in figures is not explained.
- Presentation issues: references should be enclosed in parentheses (e.g., "In DiffPure (Nie et al. 2022)" rather than "In DiffPure Nie et al. (2022)"); there are typos (e.g., in Figure 2, "Sec 3.xx"); a typo in Line 264: should be "Eq.(14)" not "(14) of guided diffusion described in Sec. 8 in Supplementary".
- There is a contradictory statement: Section 1.3 says clean accuracy decreases at K=1 (unstable), but Section 3.1.2 shows better performance at K=1. The explanation that ground-truth labels are not used in Table 2 is insufficient because training data \(x^K\) is generated step-by-step using labels. This inconsistency needs clarification.
- The impact of the number of diffusion paths on purification time is not quantitatively analyzed beyond stating it doubles.
- The experiments only evaluate against attacks that are derivatives of PGD, so the adversarial examples subject to purification are likely similar to each other. The method's performance against PGD-ODI and ACG is not shown.
