## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GLAN, a scalable instruction-tuning framework that generates synthetic data using a hierarchical taxonomy of human knowledge (inspired by education systems) rather than seed examples or existing datasets. The taxonomy is built by decomposing fields into sub-fields, disciplines, subjects, syllabi, class sessions, and key concepts, using GPT‑4 with human verification. From 10M instruction-response pairs, they fine-tune Mistral and evaluate on multiple benchmarks, achieving strong results. Reviewers acknowledge the approach’s coverage and flexibility, but raise concerns about novelty, cost, human effort, missing comparisons, and limited model experiments.

### Strengths
- **Novel data-generation paradigm**: The taxonomy-driven method ensures broad, systematic coverage across knowledge domains without relying on seed examples or existing datasets. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Scalability and customizability**: The structure allows easy addition of new fields/disciplines (R1, R4); can generate millions of samples (R2, R4). The curated 10M instruction-response pairs are considered a valuable community resource (R3).
- **Strong empirical performance**: GLAN fine-tuned on Mistral outperforms or matches state-of-the-art on math, coding, academic exams, and instruction following, without task-specific training data (R1, R2, R4). Detailed methodology and human oversight reduce hallucination (R1). Performance is demonstrated across multiple benchmarks (R1).
- **Clarity**: Well-organized, with clear explanation of the pipeline, helpful figures/tables, and detailed appendix (R1, R2). The hierarchical construction is intuitive (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work**: The idea of constructing knowledge structures to generate instruction data has been explored in earlier papers that are not discussed. Specifically, reviewers cite:
  - [1] *Enhancing Chat Language Models by Scaling High-quality Instructional Conversations* (question-first construction)
  - [2] *Instruction Tuning with Human Curriculum* (curriculum/education perspective)
  - [3] *A Survey on Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models* (subsection on Knowledge-Curation generating data “almost from scratch”)
  (R2, R3) argue this significantly reduces the paper’s novelty contribution. R1 and R4 find the method somewhat novel but acknowledge the overlap.
- **High computational cost and dependency on closed-source APIs**: The method relies heavily on GPT‑4/GPT‑3.5, incurring significant API costs. R3 estimates at least 42K USD for 10M queries. R2 notes that scaling to millions of API calls makes the approach expensive. R1 questions whether open-source models (e.g., LLaMA 3) could provide comparable performance; R4 states that GLAN is bounded by the generator’s knowledge (GPT‑4), impeding its use for self-training frontier models. Detailed cost/time/scalability analysis is missing (R1, R3).
- **Human verification effort under-explained and potentially large**: The taxonomy construction involves human verification, but the paper does not specify the number of annotators, instructions, criteria for pruning fields/disciplines, or how disagreements were resolved (R1). R3 estimates that ~3M key concepts would require human checking, contradicting the paper’s claim of “limited” human effort. R2 asks: “Is human evaluation really necessary?” for scalability.
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: The paper’s core contribution is the instruction generation method, yet no baseline generation methods (e.g., self-instruct, evol-instruct) are compared (R4). R4 also asks for comparisons given the same number of instructions to evaluate scaling trends.
- **Insufficient experiments**: The method is only evaluated on the Mistral model (R3). No experiments with other base LLMs (e.g., open-source frontier models) are provided (R4). R3 notes that Mistral-Instruct sometimes performs worse than the base Mistral (e.g., on MBPP, GSM8K, MMLU), which is not explained. R4 requests an in-depth analysis of why GLAN cannot beat the base model on non-STEM MMLU subjects.
- **Lack of evaluation on bias and ethical concerns**: The paper acknowledges potential amplification of biases but offers no detection or mitigation strategies (R4).
- **Missing multi-turn conversation data**: The generated dataset does not contain multi-turn conversations, which are crucial for many practical applications (R2).
- **Presentation of the taxonomy hierarchy is confusing**: The paper would benefit from an early figure/table defining and listing examples of each level (fields, subfields, disciplines, subjects, syllabus, class sessions, key concepts) and their counts (R3). R3 also asks whether the field-subfield-discipline hierarchy is discarded after collecting leaf discipline nodes.
- **Other specific questions raised as weaknesses**:
  - Table 1: How many instructions were used to train each model? (R4)
  - Table 2: Why does GLAN perform worse than the base model on several non-STEM MMLU subjects? (R4)
  - Scaling trends: How do GLAN instructions compare with alternative methods given identical quantity? (R4) Will performance saturate? (R4)
  - Instruction length: Does GLAN produce longer instructions than other methods? (R4) (suggested as a possible strength, but listed as an unanswered question.)
