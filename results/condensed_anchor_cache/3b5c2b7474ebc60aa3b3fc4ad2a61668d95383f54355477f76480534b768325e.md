## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores how long-term background factors (e.g., family environment, education, culture) and short-term pressures (e.g., instructions) influence LLM personality traits. Using sparse autoencoders (SAEs) and representation‑based methods, the authors extract features correlated with these factors and steer model responses. The steered outputs are tested on the TRAIT personality test (BFI and SDT dimensions) and SafetyBench. Two models are compared: Gemma‑2‑9B‑Instruct and Gemma‑2B‑Instruct. Results claim that the steering method effectively changes personality measures and safety scores.

### Strengths
- **Originality and significance:** The study is one of the first to use SAEs and representation‑based methods to steer LLM personality. It provides an empirical demonstration of LLM steering by directly manipulating features in model layers, contributing to AI interpretability.
- **Interesting and novel question:** The paper asks how model background descriptions lead to changes in model personality, grounding the work in social determinism theory and the emerging LLM psychometric literature.
- **Broad range of factors tested:** The experiments cover many background factors: gender, age, education, social ideology, family relations, professional commitment, socioeconomic status, emotional intelligence, AI familiarity, etc. (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Accessible presentation of results:** Tables present results clearly, making the data easy to parse (Reviewer 4).
- **Robust methodology:** The authors use validated (for humans) psychometric questions in prompts and structure their experiments in an interpretable fashion (Reviewer 3, 4).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 5) is considerably more positive, highlighting the originality and significance, while Reviewers 2‑4 (scores 3, 3, 1) express serious reservations about framing, validity, and experimental support. The following weaknesses reflect the majority critical view.

### Weaknesses
- **Anthropomorphization and framing of “personality”:** The paper equates LLM activations with human personality, which definitionally presumes personhood (APA). LLMs can solve personality tests without possessing underlying psychological constructs. The work conflates “persona” (outward projection) with “personality” (intrinsic biological/psychological features), citing Joshi et al. (2023a) incorrectly (Reviewer 4). The definition of personality is atypical; the paper should engage cognitive psychology literature (e.g., Mischel & Shoda, McCrae & Costa) and clarify whether “personality” is used as an analogy or metaphor. Alternative frameworks like “behavioral patterns” or “response tendencies” are recommended (Reviewer 4). This confusion extends to how an LLM can be said to have education, culture, age, or family relations – the equivalences to human backgrounds are never rendered explicit (Reviewer 1, 4).

- **Social determinism framework not convincingly applied:** The grounding in social determinism is superficial. The choice of background factors and social pressures is based on empirical findings from human psychology, but the paper does not articulate why these should apply to LLMs. No implications from theory or empirical predictions are inferred, and the psychological literature is not revisited in the findings or comparisons. Social determinism appears only to provide category labels, risking confusion and anthropomorphization (Reviewer 1).  

- **Unsupported and speculative claims:** Many findings lack statistical support (significance, effect size) and are presented as conclusions without proper evidence. Examples of unsubstantiated statements:
  - “Larger model exhibits more stable personalities and lower dark traits”
  - “Larger LLM is more easily shaped by external pressure, while smaller LLM is more sensitive to the background factor”
  - “Older and liberalism influence most on larger models while communism and uneducated influence most on smaller models’ personalities”
  - “Larger models are driven by self-motivations while smaller models are shaped by self-confidence in skills”
  These are based only on relative changes in scores after steering, with no statistical tests to quantify differences (Reviewer 1). The claim that SAE is better suited for long‑term influences and representation‑based methods for short‑term factors lacks empirical support – either method could apply to either factor set (Reviewer 2).  

- **Validity of steering comparisons:** It is unclear that results from different personality steers are directly comparable. Even when verifying monosemanticity relative to other tested factors, the degree to which each background is captured by a single SAE feature may vary (e.g., “poor” vs. “rich” feature vectors may not be equally monosemantic). Using the same steering coefficients across all concepts is therefore questionable; a coefficient of 200× for “poor” might be equivalent to 800× for “rich”. These validity issues undermine the paper’s conclusions (Reviewer 3).

- **Table labeling errors and data ambiguity:** Table 5 appears to mislabel which results correspond to each model when cross‑referenced with Tables 2‑4, casting doubt on the reliability of Section 5.2. For example, Narcissism is rated 4.3 for Gemma‑2B base in Table 5 but 4.3 for Gemma‑9B base in Tables 1‑4 (Reviewer 2, 4). Tables 2‑5 lack units and captions, making it difficult to interpret the numbers (Reviewer 3). The “base score” and what “largest difference” means are not explained (Reviewer 1). TRAIT is only introduced after the experimental results, but it should be described earlier (Reviewer 1).  

- **Research Question 2 (safety) not addressed:** RQ2 asks how personalities influence LLM safety, yet the main text does not engage with this question. The safety results and discussion appear only in the appendices, despite being presented as a main objective. The authors should either remove or revise RQ2, or include a dedicated analysis in the main body (Reviewer 1, 4).  

- **Literature gaps and citation issues:**
  - The literature review on “Personality and Trait Theory on LLMs” is superficial and omits extant findings that LLMs do not exhibit stable personalities and may be better considered as a cultural superposition of perspectives. This contradicts the paper’s framing and should be addressed (Reviewer 1).  
  - Line 051 claims previous works identify two primary strategies for endowing LLMs with personality, but no citations support this statement (Reviewer 4).  
  - Citation of Perez et al. (2023) for reliability concerns (misinformation, privacy) is incorrect: that paper does not discuss those issues; the relevant citations are Carlini et al. and Lin et al. All citations should be reviewed for direct support (Reviewer 4).  

- **Implicit interaction between long‑term and short‑term factors:** The introduction and abstract treat long‑term factors and short‑term pressures as interactional, yet the experiments investigate them independently. Conjoint analysis (e.g., “gregarious young person”, “gregarious male”) would be more appropriate and is recommended (Reviewer 4).  

- **Presentation and clarity issues:** The paper is confusing and repetitive; a clearer separation between theoretical framework, methods, results, and discussion would help. The safety results are relegated to appendices despite being presented as a main objective. Conclusions are often speculative and should be separated from result presentation (Reviewer 1). Minor issues: “trustworthy concerns” is an uncommon phrase (suggest “reliability concerns”); the title uses “warmth” as an adjective instead of a noun (“a low-empathy or warmth person”); no justification is given for choosing GEMMA over other open‑source models like Llama (Reviewer 4).  

- **Unanswered questions:**  
  - How were GPT‑4o sentence descriptions validated to represent the intended background factors? (Reviewer 3)  
  - What are the exact values in Tables 2‑3, and what do they signify? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Is the claim about larger models being more easily shaped by external pressure supported by tests on gender‑related questions directly (e.g., steering on gender and checking changes on gender‑related items)? (Reviewer 2)
