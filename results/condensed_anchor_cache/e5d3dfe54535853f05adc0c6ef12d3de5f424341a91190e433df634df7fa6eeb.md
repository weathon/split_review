## Merged Review

### Summary
SANA is a text-to-image framework that generates high-resolution images up to 4096×4096 with strong text-image alignment at high speed, deployable on a 16GB laptop GPU (under 1 second for 1024×1024). It combines four designs: (1) a deep compression autoencoder that downsamples images 32× (vs. typical 8×); (2) Linear DiT replacing vanilla quadratic attention with linear attention for high-resolution efficiency; (3) a decoder-only small LLM (Gemma2-2B-IT) text encoder with Complex Human Instruction (CHI) for alignment; (4) Flow-DPM-Solver to reduce sampling steps plus efficient caption labeling. SANA-0.6B is reported 20× smaller and 100+× faster in throughput than Flux-12B, with competitive quality.

### Strengths
- **Originality and breakthrough potential**: One reviewer (score 10) found the deep compression AE, Linear DiT, and 4K generation abilities to be innovative contributions that enhance quality/efficiency while reducing compute, and considered the work a breakthrough for diffusion text-to-image. The majority rated 8, noting high practical value.
- **Efficiency as a strong selling point**: The high compression AE and linear DiT enable high-quality generation even on a laptop GPU. The combination of four designs (AE, attention, text encoder, sampling) is well-motivated for resource-limited high-quality generation.
- **Effective use of LLM text encoder**: Decoder-only small LLM (Gemma2-2B-IT) with CHI pipeline boosts text-image alignment, outperforming larger but less efficient encoders like T5-XXL. This is noted as interesting and impactful.
- **Extensive and convincing experimental evaluation**: Ablation experiments on each component are sufficient and convincing (R3, R4). Experimental results at 512×512 and 1024×1024 show better or comparable performance vs. state-of-the-art methods, with much higher throughput and lower latency.
- **Practical application value**: Addressing high-resolution, fast text-to-image generation in resource-limited scenarios has great real-world relevance.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: Three core components (deep AE, linear DiT, LLM as text encoder) have been previously explored (R4 cites [1][2][3][4]; R2 calls them iterative upon recent advances). Combining them is not considered significantly novel by the majority. One reviewer explicitly rated contribution 2, while others gave 3.
- **Missing baseline comparisons and ablations**:
  - Need explicit component-by-component comparisons with recent high-efficiency models like PixArt-Σ and Playground v3 (which also use decoder-only LLMs like Llama3-8B) to better attribute SANA’s unique contributions (R2).
  - Should discuss or compare with UltraPixel (NeurIPS 2024) for high-resolution generation and Stable Cascade for efficient generation (R1).
  - Why does SANA perform significantly better than LUMINA-Next, even though both use the Gemma-2B model? (R1)
- **Incomplete evaluation at claimed 4096×4096 resolution**: Only speed comparison (Table 14) is reported for 4K. Need FID and image-text alignment metrics (e.g., HPSv2) for 4096×4096 to substantiate quality claims at maximum resolution (R3).
- **Text encoder analysis insufficient**:
  - The Gemma2-2B-IT model used in Table 9 should be explicitly explained for readers without LLM background (R1).
  - Comparison between Gemma2 and T5-XXL is limited to FID; need HPSv2 metrics on complex prompt benchmarks to demonstrate advantages of Gemma2 (R1).
  - Need to explore whether larger LLMs (e.g., Llama3-8B used in Playground v3) further improve generation quality, since only Gemma2 vs T5 was tested (R2).
- **Insufficient explanation of CHI pipeline**: Section 2.3 does not fully explain whether the Complex Human Instruction pipeline is the same as [5]. If similar, this further reduces novelty (R4).
- **Linear attention design lacks detail**: How does the linear attention block model long-range dependency and contextual relations between tokens, compared to original self-attention? The operation design needs a more detailed explanation (R3).
