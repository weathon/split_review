## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies diffusion models under the simplifying assumption that the data distribution is a centered Gaussian N(0,Σ). Using VP-SDE as the forward process, the authors derive closed-form solutions for the backward SDE (Proposition 2) and the probability flow ODE (Proposition 3). These exact expressions allow them to decompose and measure four error types — initialization, truncation, discretization, and score approximation — via the Wasserstein-2 distance. Experiments are conducted on a Gaussian distribution whose covariance is computed from CIFAR‑10 samples (a “gaussianized” CIFAR‑10) and on a learned score function with a U‑Net. The paper compares samplers (EM, Euler, Heun) and reports error contributions in Table 2 and eigenvalue dependencies in Figure 2.

Opinions diverge: one reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) finds the paper interesting and useful despite the Gaussian limitation, while two others (score 3, confidence 4 and 3) find the results too restricted to have clear significance for real-world data. A fourth reviewer (score 6, confidence 5) values the systematic analysis but questions practical relevance.

### Strengths
- Thorough analysis of initialization, truncation, and discretization errors for commonly used samplers (EM, Heun, Euler) in the centered Gaussian case, especially in Section 4 and Table 2 (R1, R4).
- Derivation of exact analytical solutions for the forward/backward SDE and probability flow ODE under the Gaussian assumption, enabling direct error computation (R4).
- Systematic decomposition of error sources (initialization, truncation, discretization, score approximation) and their individual quantification (R3, R4).
- Numerical experiments that are informative and illustrative, with comparisons showing Heun’s method introduces nearly no additional discretization error for Gaussian distributions (R2, R4).
- The paper is well written, easy to follow, and nicely presented (R1, R2, R3).
- Although limited to a specific case, the investigation is interesting and its findings could be useful for practical applications (R3).
- Heun’s method is demonstrated to be particularly effective for Gaussian distributions (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Gaussian assumption limits practical relevance**. The paper only considers Gaussian data distributions, which are rarely encountered in pure form; it is unclear how the insights generalize to complex, non‑Gaussian data with multi‑modality or heavy tails (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer notes that while the limitation is acknowledged, the paper does not address how the theoretical results would translate to non‑Gaussian data where the score function is no longer a simple linear operator (R4).
- **Lack of validation on real-world datasets**. The analysis relies solely on a “gaussianized” CIFAR‑10 dataset; no experiments on diverse real‑world data are provided, raising concerns about practical relevance (R4). Additionally, the ADSN data samples are out‑of‑distribution for the classifier used to compute FID scores (R1).
- **Missing citations for previously derived results**. Proposition 1 (score for linear diffusions) has been derived in Appendix A of [Albergo 2023] and in [Särkkä & Solin 2019] (eq 6.19‑6.20). Proposition 3 (solution to PF‑ODE) appears in Appendix B of [Khrulkov 2022]. These should be cited in the main paper (R1). One reviewer also asks whether the closed-form solutions were already available in earlier SDE textbooks (R3).
- **Significance of findings not clearly situated in the wider literature**. The paper claims (line 502‑503) that score approximation error is the most impactful source, but this is already known from works like [Chen et al. 2023] and [De Bortoli et al. 2021] (R1). The analysis of discretization error restates well‑known numerical sampling results (e.g., higher‑order schemes reduce error) without explaining how the Gaussian lens adds new insight (R1).
- **Questions about specific results and their generalizability**. 
    - In Figure 2, eigenvalues around λ = 1 cause sudden drops in error; similar drops occur at other eigenvalues. No explanation is provided for this behavior (R2).
    - The statement “for any Gaussian distribution Heun’s method introduces nearly no additional discretization error” – is this significantly influenced by the Gaussian assumption, or can it be stated regardless of the distribution? (R2)
    - In Section 5, the approximation of the Wasserstein distance between (pθ) and (pdata) uses eq (26) and assumes λ^{ξ,emp} = λ^{ξ,θ}. The derivation of Γ in Appendix F.3 is unclear (R2).
    - Would the Gaussian analysis agree that a higher‑order scheme like RK4 provides more accurate integration of the PF‑ODE than Heun? (R1)
    - Can the Gaussian analysis provide any theoretical justification for the robustness of SDE sampling over ODE sampling beyond empirical evidence? (R1)
- **Score approximation error not addressed**. While the paper notes that score approximation errors are significant in practice, it does not propose effective solutions for minimizing them (e.g., better architectures or training techniques) (R4).
- **Lack of discussion on alternative metrics and computational efficiency**. For non‑Gaussian distributions where Wasserstein error is not tractable, empirical metrics like MMD or kernel Stein discrepancies could be used but are not explored (R1). The paper also does not address computational efficiency when analytical solutions are unavailable for complex data (R4).
- **Limited coverage of sampling schemes**. The paper compares several schemes but does not discuss whether these are the optimal possible schemes (R4).
