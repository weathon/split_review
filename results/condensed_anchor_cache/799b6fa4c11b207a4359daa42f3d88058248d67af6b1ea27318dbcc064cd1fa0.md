## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a force-directed graph embedding method that reduces complexity from O(n²) to O(n log n) by limiting force computations to k‑hop neighborhoods and a few randomly sampled nodes. It uses a spring model without neural networks, aiming to position nodes at equilibrium under force‑based interactions. Reviewers find the general idea interesting but consider the manuscript incomplete. Three reviewers gave a rating of 3 (borderline); one reviewer (R4) gave a rating of 1 and is substantially more critical, citing limited novelty, shallow evaluation, and presentation issues.

### Strengths

- The overall idea of using a spring model for graph embeddings is interesting and offers a complementary perspective (R2).
- The proposed complexity reduction from O(n²) to O(n log n) is a notable scalability improvement (R4).
- The method demonstrates comparable or slightly better performance than some state‑of‑the‑art methods on two downstream tasks (link prediction and node classification), suggesting practical utility for large graphs (R4).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Most reviewers (R1, R2, R3) gave a rating of 3; Reviewer 4 gave a rating of 1 and is significantly more negative. While R4 acknowledges the above strengths, they view them as insufficient to offset the weaknesses below.

### Weaknesses

- **Incomplete presentation and errors:** The paper contains multiple signs of incompleteness. Placeholders such as “!!! A PICTURE TO BE INSERTED for CAMERA READY !!!” (Line 239) and “!!! TO BE ELABORATED ON for CAMERA READY !!!” (Line 452), and an empty Discussion section. Typographical errors: “topolofy” (Line 234), lowercase sentence start (Line 186). Broken reference (Line 301). Notation inconsistency: \( \mathbf{z}_{uv} = \mathbf{z}_v - \mathbf{z}_u \) introduced for brevity in Line 140, then used in Equation 3, not used in Equations 8 and 9, then used again in Equations 13 and 14. The abstract’s “log(n²)” (Line 027) should be O(n²). Proper inline citations are missing for well‑known methods like LINE, SDNE, DeepWalk, and Node2vec (Line 273). (R4)
- **Limited novelty:** The main contribution is an incremental improvement to the original method by Lotfalizadeh et al. (2023, 2024). The use of k‑hop neighborhoods and stochastic sampling for complexity reduction does not represent a fundamentally new idea in graph representation learning, and the paper offers no new theoretical contributions or analyses. (R4)
- **Ambiguous relationship to prior work:** It is unclear how this work fundamentally extends the force‑directed embedding approach from Lotfalizadeh et al. (R4, also asked as a question)
- **Insufficient evaluation:** The evaluation is limited to only two downstream tasks (link prediction and node classification). More powerful baselines such as DGI (Veličković et al., ICLR’19) and GraphZoom (Deng et al., ICLR’20) should be included, and experiments on larger graphs (over 1M nodes) are needed to demonstrate scalability improvements. (R3, R4) The paper should also consider additional tasks such as multilabel classification or clustering. (R4 question)
- **Marginal performance improvement:** Downstream task performance improvements over previous methods are marginal at best (Figures 3 and 5). (R4)
- **Questionable suitability for downstream tasks:** The method may not capture global and local structure in a way that enables proactive learning and adaptation of embeddings for downstream tasks. (R1)
- **Missing analysis of embedding quality:** The paper mentions reflecting graph topology (Line 234) but does not evaluate this property (e.g., with mean average precision). (R4 question)
- **Reproducibility:** Code is not provided. (R4 question)
